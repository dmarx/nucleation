# NOTE-tmpflmpr: Dispositions without conditionals

## Contribution

Dispositions are modal properties. The standard view individuates each disposition by a stimulus and a manifestation and captures its modality with a conditional, usually a counterfactual, from stimulus to manifestation. Vetter proposes instead that a disposition is individuated by its manifestation alone and that its modality is closest to possibility: fragility is being able to break easily. She develops the view in some detail and defends it against the main objections.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 1–6): nearly everyone assumes a conditional analysis of dispositions. Vetter separates "which modality?" from "how is it involved?" (reductive, modelling or grounding) and addresses only the first, as a serious alternative rather than a proven winner.
- §2.1 (p. 6) states the two theses: individuation by manifestation alone ("a disposition to M, full stop"), and possibility as the modality.
- §§2.2–2.4 (pp. 9–16) develop graded possibility ("easily", "more fragile than") and go beyond it.
- §3 (pp. 16–25) meets challenges: the apparent "disposition to M if C", the role of stimuli in causation, and whether some dispositions go beyond possibility. The natural properties of dispositional essentialism need a stronger modality than ordinary dispositions, so the view clashes only with one way of formulating that essentialism (p. 25).
- §4 (p. 25) concludes that ordinary disposition terms point toward the possibility conception, with a lesson about the status of linguistic intuitions in the dispositions debate.

## Open questions

- This is the foundational paper of Vetter's potentiality programme (Potentiality, OUP 2015), and it matters for any metaphysics that grounds modality in powers.
- The graded-possibility semantics is the technical core. Check how "can easily M" is made precise, and whether it avoids the finkish and masking counterexamples that motivated conditional analyses.
- It is relevant wherever capabilities or dispositions of systems (including AI systems) are analysed modally.
