# THEORY-1081: Di Biagio and Rovelli locate the Frauchiger–Renner contradiction and the violation of the Local Friendliness inequalities in one step, a superobserver using facts relative to a friend he holds in full quantum control as if they were his own, which is the classical sum rule applied where those facts are not stable for him; on that ground they base quantum ontology on facts relative to systems, with an absolute macroreality emerging only approximately and only relative to systems for which decoherence is strong

## Source

Di Biagio and Rovelli (2021), [LIT-1666](../literature.d/LIT-1666.md), §§2.1–2.3, pp. 7–11 of the
journal text; read in [NOTE-1226](../notes.d/NOTE-1226.md). It builds on [THEORY-1075](THEORY-1075.md).

## What was actually shown

**Why not stable facts (§2.1, p. 7).** Stable facts are the textbook link to
the world, and are ubiquitous for us because decoherence is. But "stability
is relational … Therefore one does not avoid relationalism by restricting to
stable facts", and "stability is only approximate in general. At no point
the interference terms perfectly vanish." "How stable does a fact need to be
before it is real? And with respect to what systems does it have to be
stable, in order to be real?" The alternative is to base the ontology on all
relative facts: "it is about real facts of the world and relative
probabilities of their occurrence … based on facts established at
interactions and are labelled by physical contexts" (p. 8).

**Frauchiger and Renner (§2.2.1, p. 8).** Their assumption (C), "If W,
applying quantum theory, concludes that F knows that L_S = a, then W can
conclude that L_S = a", becomes: W may reason as if a fact relative to F
were relative to W. That "holds only if every fact relative to F is stable
for W", and with full quantum control of the friend's lab, "by definition,
what is stable for the friends is not stable for W. Hence the contradiction
follows from inappropriately mixing contexts". That no contradiction
follows when the friends' labs decohere for W too is credited to [26, 27,
38].

**Local Friendliness (§2.2.2, p. 9).** Bong et al. show that absoluteness of
observed events, no superdeterminism and Bell locality imply inequalities
quantum theory violates, and the violation has been observed with photon
friends. "Absoluteness of observed events means that if W deems that F is an
observer, then W can use (3)—even if W has full quantum control on F",
which RQM denies. Dismissing photon friends "opens the problem of deciding
which systems give rise to facts".

**The measurement problem and macroreality (§2.3, p. 10).** Projection and
unitary evolution "refer to facts relative to different systems". Absolute
macroreality "only emerges approximately, relative to systems for which
decoherence is sufficiently strong." "Decoherence needs an ontology.
Relative facts provide such a general ontology that is well defined with or
without decoherence."

## What this does not say

- **That the no-go theorems favour RQM over its rivals.** Calling the Local
  Friendliness violation "evidence that the facts quantum theory deals with
  are facts relative to systems" (p. 9) outruns the theorem, which shows
  that one of three assumptions fails; QBism and Everett also deny
  absoluteness. The same overreach is noted for Rovelli 2021 ([THEORY-621](THEORY-621.md)).
- **That relative facts cohere across observers in general.** "Wigner will
  always agree with his Friend about the value of L_S once he too interacts
  with them" (p. 10) is argued from one entangled state's correlation
  ([THEORY-1080](THEORY-1080.md)).
- **How an agent should reason about an experiment on himself.** "has not
  been done within RQM yet" (pp. 8–9).
- **That an ontology of stable facts is impossible.** The argument is that
  its two questions have no satisfactory answer, not a proof that none
  could.
- **What makes something a system, or a context.** Both are taken as given.
