# LIT-150: What is emergence, after all?

Abbas K. Rizi (2025), *PNAS Nexus 5(2):pgag010 (2026; online 2026-01-19); arXiv preprint first* — [ARXIV-2507.04951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04951)

## Key takeaways

- Emergence can be made precise and non-mystical as many-to-one coarse-graining that preserves predictive information, with its onset located operationally by symmetry breaking, criticality and RG universality — weak emergence suffices, and strong emergence would require new causal principles unsupported by evidence.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The paper observes that "emergence" is increasingly used, often imprecisely and sometimes mystically, for collective behaviours such as flocking, synchronisation and collective intelligence. As a perspective piece, it recasts emergence as a measurable phenomenon grounded in physics. Using temperature, magnetism and herd immunity on social networks as examples, it shows how collective behaviour arises from local interactions under global constraints, and argues that a properly framed notion yields insight rather than mysticism.
