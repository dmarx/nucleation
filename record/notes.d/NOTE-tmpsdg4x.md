summary: >-
  A narrative review of maternal-to-offspring microchimerism (MMc). Cells
  cross the placenta both ways; maternal cells enter the fetus from the
  second trimester and persist into adulthood. They include T cells, B and
  NK cells, monocytes and haematopoietic and mesenchymal progenitors; levels
  depend on HLA compatibility, PAPP-A and possibly delivery mode; MMc may
  pass across generations (grandmother, siblings). Protective roles: immune
  maturation, early-life infection defence, possibly transfer of
  pathogen-specific T-cell memory, tolerance to non-inherited maternal
  antigens via MSCs. Harmful associations: type 1 diabetes, neonatal lupus,
  juvenile myopathies, Sjögren's and others (no association with coeliac
  disease). The authors conclude the role remains unclear and the evidence
  is hampered by detection limits, small samples and non-standard methods.

# NOTE-tmpsdg4x: Mpakosi and others (2024) — Deciphering the Role of Maternal Microchimerism in Offspring Autoimmunity: A Narrative Review

## Contribution

A clinical synthesis of MMc's dual role, with autoimmunity in the offspring as its focus.

## Key results

- §4.3.1: "Maternal cells are transferred to the fetus as early as the second trimester of
  pregnancy"; "It has been found that genetically different fetal cells remain in maternal tissues
  for many years after pregnancy and that maternal cells persist in offspring tissues, even into
  adulthood".
- §1: "Maternal microchimerism seems to play a decisive role in the development of the fetus by
  regulating both its immune system and the rest of the fetal organs".
- §4.3.3: "It is possible that MMc can be passed down through generations from the maternal
  grandmother."
- §4.3.4: "Furthermore, immune MMcs appear to include pathogen-specific T cells from previous maternal
  infections and vaccinations"; and (Iwai et al.) "MMc-transferred MSCs could induce immune
  tolerance".
- §7: "The primary limitation of studies investigating the role of maternal microchimerism in
  autoimmune disease development lies in the challenges associated with detecting and quantifying
  microchimeric cells, due to their low frequency and presence in healthy individuals."
- §8: "Conversely, no association of maternal cells with offspring celiac disease has been found." and
  "Therefore, the exact role of MMc in these conditions remains unclear, necessitating further
  research to elucidate its mechanisms and implications."

## Assumptions

Narrative (not systematic) review; associations from small, heterogeneous studies taken as
suggestive; mouse findings cited alongside human ones.

## Concepts

Fetal and maternal microchimerism (FMc, MMc); non-inherited maternal antigens (NIMA); HLA
compatibility; mesenchymal stem/stromal cells; graft-versus-host vs host-versus-graft readings of
autoimmunity; intergenerational microchimerism.

## Bearing on the record

**How far it stands in.** Partly: same phenomenon and the same "friend and enemy" balance (protective immunity and tolerance
vs autoimmunity), but two years older, centred on autoimmune disease, and without Malinská et al.'s
"special focus" on breast milk, which Mpakosi mentions only as a factor in infant MMc levels (Balle
et al.). It restates the persistence, tolerance and autoimmune parts of the original's abstract, not
the breastfeeding route or transplantation.

- **CLAIM-tmpn0ytb** (microchimerism bears on graded or processual individuality; separation not
  completed at birth). **Supports the premise**, independently of Chua et al. (LIT-tmpjik3n): maternal
  cells persist "even into adulthood", take part in regulation (tolerance via MSCs; immune
  maturation), and may cross generations, which adds a lineage dimension the claim does not state.
  Its autoimmune associations show functional incorporation can be pathological, which bears on
  A's "functional boundary" step and on whether participation is integration. It **leaves the
  gradedness conclusion open**, like the read sources (ARG-tmp51yxc). Candidate relation:
  `corroborates` on the claim's premise; nothing more.
- **QUESTION-297** (routes by which effects reach descendants), found in recheck: MMc's possible
  transfer of maternal T-cell memory and grandmaternal cells is a cellular route not among the
  question's four (germline epigenetics, womb, parenting, narrative); it is close to "the womb" but
  persists postnatally. Weak, worth a dated note.

  nine held microchimerism LITs is this review.

## Limitations

Skimmed; disease sections unread. Narrative review in a modest venue; the evidence it collects is
small-sample and associational, as it says itself.
