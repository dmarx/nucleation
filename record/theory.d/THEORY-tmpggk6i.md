# THEORY-tmpggk6i: Quantum mechanics does not entail agential realism: Bohmian mechanics reproduces its predictions with an ontology of individual particles on determinate trajectories, and relational quantum mechanics, the interpretation Barad names as closest, makes values relational while keeping the systems prior to their interactions

## Source

Faye & Jaksland (2021), [LIT-tmpstodb](../literature.d/LIT-tmpstodb.md), §5; read in [NOTE-tmpdn71t](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpdn71t.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Bohmian mechanics** "reproduces all the predictions of quantum
  mechanics, while promoting a metaphysics that is significantly different
  from that of agential realism": "individual particles with determinate
  position and (instantaneous) velocity" and pre-determined trajectories.
  It shares two features with agential realism (no ontological division
  between object and experiment; properties other than position produced
  in interaction), but its non-local interconnectedness "is an
  interconnectedness between individuals". So agential realism gives at
  best "the epistemological and ontological implications of one particular
  interpretation".
- **Relational quantum mechanics** (Rovelli 1996, [LIT-tmp57r0s](../literature.d/LIT-tmp57r0s.md)) has no
  observer-independent values and no inherent system–observer line, close
  to Barad. But its ontology is "a sparse ontology of (relational) quantum
  events happening at interactions between physical systems", "which means,
  of course, that the systems precede the interactions". Its observer is
  "any physical system"; Barad's measurement leaves "a mark on the
  measuring agency".

The argument is the existence of a counter-model, which could have failed
only if Bohmian mechanics did not reproduce the predictions.

## What this does not say

- That Bohmian mechanics is right; the authors do not promote it.
- That agential realism is inconsistent with quantum mechanics
  ([THEORY-tmpwbcq0](THEORY-tmpwbcq0.md)).
- That relational values imply relational relata. RQM is the case of the
  first without the second, which is [CLAIM-150](../claims.d/CLAIM-150.md)'s distinction between
  relational identification and constitution in a physical key.

## Note of 2026-10-27: Rovelli 1996 read

Rovelli's founding paper ([LIT-tmp57r0s](../literature.d/LIT-tmp57r0s.md), read in [NOTE-tmptc0hl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptc0hl.md)) bears out the
substance of the relational-quantum-mechanics half and corrects its source.
In 1996 the systems are prior to their interactions by assumption: "Assume
that the world can be decomposed (possibly in a variety of ways) in a
collection of systems" (§III.A, p. 10; [THEORY-tmpduhhr](THEORY-tmpduhhr.md)). But there is no
ontology of events in that paper. The "sparse ontology of (relational)
quantum events" Faye and Jaksland quote is Rovelli 2018. The decomposition
is also not unique, which the THEORY's "systems prior" should not be read to
deny.
