# ARG-tmp6sdu6: Kolmogorov's calculus of problems, by analogy from his problem to the record's question, supports reading a question as a problem, since both demand rather than assert, have no truth value and fix what counts as solving them; but narrowed, since a question with several admissible answers is a disjunction of problems and nothing in the calculus revises a problem, and widened, since a problem fully stated carries the means the record assigns to the inquiry

## The inference

[THEORY-tmpdsft8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpdsft8.md): a problem is something to be solved, not a statement to be
proved, and "für die Aufgaben hat man keinen dieser Richtigkeit
entsprechenden Begriff" (p. 61, n. 8). Each of Kolmogorov's examples fixes
what would count as its solution (four integers satisfying a relation, a
circle through three points), and a problem may admit many solutions.
[CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md)'s question likewise asserts nothing and "fixes which differences
would count as an answer". By analogy, a question in the record's sense is
a problem in Kolmogorov's, as [CLAIM-tmpnookt](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpnookt.md) says.

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are they alike, and is it the one the conclusion
  needs?** Both are demands, not assertions; both have no truth value of
  their own; both fix what counts as meeting them, and leave open which of
  the admissible things is supplied ([CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md)'s partiality, many
  solutions). Conditional problems (p. 59: "Vorausgesetzt, daß …") match
  questions under a presupposition, and a contentless problem, whose
  supposition is impossible, matches a question with a false
  presupposition. That is the respect the correspondence needs.
- **What relevant differences are there?** Three.
  1. *Answer space.* A Kolmogorov problem is solved or not; it has no
     space of answers. [CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md)'s question partitions states into
     answers, so "is the system stable?" has two admissible answers. In
     the calculus that is the problem a ∨ ¬a for this a, solved by solving
     a or refuting it, and Kolmogorov shows the general form is not a
     solved problem (p. 63). So a question corresponds to a disjunctive
     problem, each answer to a disjunct, not to a single problem. The
     mapping is the assistant's construction, not Kolmogorov's.
  2. *Revision.* A problem's sense is "von dem Zustand unserer Kenntnisse
     unabhängig" (p. 65), and a problem whose supposition fails is counted
     solved by the proof that it is contentless (p. 59). [CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md)'s last
     clause, that an inquiry can revise what an answer could be, and its
     bifurcation example, where a question with no adequate answer must be
     replaced, have no counterpart in the calculus: Kolmogorov closes the
     case as a solution where the record reopens it as a revision.
  3. *Means.* "Um ganz präzis zu sein, sollte man natürlich bei der
     Formulierung dieser Aufgabe die erlaubten Konstruktionsmittel angeben"
     (p. 59, n. 2). A problem fully stated carries its permitted means; the
     record puts the admissible interventions 𝓘 in the inquiry ([TERM-095](../terms.d/TERM-095.md)),
     beside the answer space, not in the question. In this respect a
     problem is wider than a question, nearer to a question with its
     admissible operations.
- **Is the domain the same?** No. Kolmogorov's problems are logical and
  mathematical, with generally valid solutions (p. 61). The record's
  questions include empirical ones, whose answers no hearer is bound to
  recognize. The analogy carries the structure, not the certainty.
- **What transfers?** That a question is a problem and not a statement,
  with its partiality, conditional form and the case of a false
  presupposition. Not the claim that the calculus models inquiry: it
  composes and solves problems and never changes one.

## Verdict

A sound analogy for the first correspondence, narrowed: a question is a
problem in Kolmogorov's sense when its answer space is read as a
disjunction of problems, and the calculus has nothing for a question's
revision. This does not meet [CLAIM-tmpnookt](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpnookt.md)'s defeater (b), a
correspondence failing on its own terms; it changes the correspondence's
statement. The claim's sentence "Kolmogorov read a proposition as a
problem" misattributes (p. 65, n. 17); that is a correction of wording,
in the dated note on the claim. Proposed.
