# CASE-tmp3euq0: Race-based power in the United States after abolition: the Thirteenth Amendment ends chattel slavery in 1865, a system of segregation and disfranchisement is upheld in Plessy v. Ferguson in 1896, and its legal form is dismantled by Brown v. Board of Education in 1954, the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965, with resistance and de facto segregation continuing after

## The events

As commonly dated, and not checked against sources here:

- **1865.** The Thirteenth Amendment abolishes slavery. The legal theory
  that made people property, and the theories of rights that excluded the
  enslaved, lose their legal footing. The Fourteenth (1868) and Fifteenth
  (1870) Amendments follow.
- **1870s–1890s.** Reconstruction ends. Southern states build a system of
  segregation and disfranchisement.
- **1896.** *Plessy v. Ferguson* upholds segregation as "separate but
  equal". Precedent now protects the norm.
- **1954.** *Brown v. Board of Education* overturns *Plessy* for public
  education.
- **1964–1965.** The Civil Rights Act and the Voting Rights Act.
- **After.** Organized resistance to desegregation, and residential and
  school segregation that outlasted the legal changes.

## What it is evidence for, and what it is not

- **For persistence after the original justification.** The norm, a
  race-based difference in power, outlived the justification the owner
  names by close to a century in law, and longer in practice.
- **For a discontinuous end.** It ended in law by overturned precedent and
  statute, not by fading.
- **For hysteresis after the legal change.** The practice continued well
  after its legal support was withdrawn.
- **Not, by itself, for the reliance loop.** The norm was also maintained
  by violence, by disfranchisement and by the interests of those it
  served. [CLAIM-tmpjg5gx](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjg5gx.md)'s mechanism, maintenance funded by what others
  had built on the norm, is one candidate among these, and the case does
  not separate them ([ARG-tmpqkfwb](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpqkfwb.md)).
- **Complicated by its history.** On one well-known reading (C. Vann
  Woodward's), Jim Crow segregation was largely built in the 1890s rather
  than carried over from slavery. On that reading, the case shows a norm
  of race-based power persisting while its form and its justifications
  were replaced: new rationales, among them "separate but equal",
  scientific racism and states' rights, took the place of the old ones.
