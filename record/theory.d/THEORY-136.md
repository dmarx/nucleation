# THEORY-136: A persistent or abrupt change is not evidence that a system has alternative stable states: field patterns such as jumps, multimodality and dual relationships also arise from a stepwise driver or a simple threshold, and only hysteresis, initial-state dependence or a lasting shift after a temporary disturbance, with slow return excluded, comes close to showing one

## Source

Scheffer & Carpenter (2003), [LIT-726](../literature.d/LIT-726.md), read in [NOTE-571](../notes.d/NOTE-571.md). The 2001
Nature paper it restates, [LIT-720](../literature.d/LIT-720.md), is unread.

## What was actually shown

Scheffer & Carpenter ([LIT-726](../literature.d/LIT-726.md)) review the theory and the evidence.

- **Theory.** Alternative attractors exist when a feedback is strong enough
  to make more than one basin of attraction at the same external
  conditions. As conditions change, a basin can shrink to nothing (a
  bifurcation), and returning needs a reversal past a second bifurcation:
  hysteresis.
- **What field data can and cannot show.** Jumps in time series, multimodal
  distributions and two-branched relationships to a driver all fit the
  theory. Each is also produced by a driver that itself jumps, or by a
  threshold that is not a bifurcation. Such data "can never be
  conclusive".
- **What experiments can show.** They can show different end states from
  different starting states under the same conditions, a lasting shift
  after a temporary perturbation (temporary fish removal turning lakes
  clear), and hysteresis (charophytes in Lake Veluwe as phosphorus rose and
  fell).
- **The confound.** A long return time looks like a new stable state.
  Biomanipulated Lake Zwemlust stayed clear for six years and then slipped
  back.
- **Feedback alone is not enough.** "Positive feedbacks only lead to
  alternative attractors if they are sufficiently strong."

## What this does not say

- **Not that alternative states are rare.** The review documents many
  cases and holds that they are part of the expected range of behaviour.
- **Not that persistence is irrelevant.** A state that regenerates itself
  over generations is evidence; one that simply has not yet decayed is not.
- **Not a recipe for large systems.** The experiments that discriminate are
  hard to run on oceans, and on organizations.

## Connections

- **Reef recovery ([THEORY-128](THEORY-128.md)).** Hughes et al. found the algal state on
  Great Barrier Reef plots vanished within 30 days once the cages excluding
  fishes were reopened. By this standard it was a reversible response, not a move
  between attractors. The review's Caribbean account is of resilience
  eroded slowly by fishing, nutrients and the loss of *Diadema*.
- **Self-reinforcing disorders ([THEORY-058](THEORY-058.md))** says disorders persist because
  their symptom networks maintain themselves and so outlast their
  triggers. Outlasting a trigger is one of the hints this account calls
  inconclusive. No relation is declared; the network account would need
  hysteresis or initial-state evidence to meet this standard.
- **Resilience ([THEORY-132](THEORY-132.md))** supplies the basin-of-attraction picture
  this test is built on.
