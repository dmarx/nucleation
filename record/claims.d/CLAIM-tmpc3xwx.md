# CLAIM-tmpc3xwx: No design in the programme includes an interlingual translation, and the defeat conditions are disjunctive, so English retellings and model chains could settle a thesis about what survives translation

## The objection

**What the designs use.** The stimulus sets the record's designs name are
these.

- The modernized refrains of Henley's Villon ([CASE-012](../cases.d/CASE-012.md)), English rewritings
  of an English text. [CASE-021](../cases.d/CASE-021.md)'s study takes "our Henley translations as
  stimuli", the exchange's own English renderings of Henley.
- The spending sentence, which [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s note calls "this case with its
  literary source removed", crossed with topic in [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md): English, and,
  as [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md) says, "every cell is an example label, not a stimulus".
- The language-model experiments ([CASE-005](../cases.d/CASE-005.md)). Experiment 2 chooses among
  "translations" but names no source text and no language pair.
- Text → image → caption → image chains ([CASE-009](../cases.d/CASE-009.md)), whose design is about
  modality ([CLAIM-087](CLAIM-087.md)).
- Recasts of trope instances from TV Tropes ([CASE-038](../cases.d/CASE-038.md)), an English
  catalogue.

The only interlingual cases in the record, Villon→Henley ([CASE-010](../cases.d/CASE-010.md)) and
Marot→Hofstadter ([CASE-031](../cases.d/CASE-031.md)), are put into no design. The nearest thing to an
interlingual design is [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md)'s cross-corpus version (A210 §5), which fits
topic models "to a source corpus and to its translation, paraphrase or
adaptation", and names no language pair. No entry in claims, cases or
questions uses the word "interlingual".

**Why it matters.** The defeat conditions are disjunctive. [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s ranges
over "translations, retellings or cross-modal renderings", and [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s
over "translations, recastings and media". A result on English paraphrase
alone can therefore defeat, or support, a thesis whose subject is what
survives translation. [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s second disjunct, failure "to transfer
across languages or media", is the one cross-language test in the line, and
it is a condition, not a design; the "or media" lets a cross-modal result
meet it.

What carries across paraphrase need not carry across languages. Languages
encode footing and politeness in grammar to different degrees: T/V pronouns,
honorifics, evidentials. A rendering into a language that marks the
speaker's relation to the addressee must choose a footing that an English
paraphrase can leave open. [CLAIM-033](CLAIM-033.md) already holds, after Jakobson, that
"Languages differ in what they must convey". Footing and stance, the
observables [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) names, are among the things languages force
differently, so evidence from paraphrase within English does not transfer to
them by default.

## What it does not say

- It does not say intralingual results are irrelevant. Paraphrase,
  retelling and cross-modal rendering are in the thesis's scope.
- It does not say any interlingual result would differ. It says the
  programme cannot tell.

## What would answer it

- An interlingual design: at least one language pair in which footing or
  politeness is grammatically marked on one side only, with judges on both
  sides.
- Or a restatement of the thesis's scope, so that the title's question is
  not settled by intralingual evidence alone.
