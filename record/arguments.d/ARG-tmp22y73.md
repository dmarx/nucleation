# ARG-tmp22y73: The works read on 2026-11-12 support the news claim's externality step and, narrowly, its selection for attention in wording, but not its central step that attention optimization costs investigative capacity; the readings it cited as grounds carry less than it cited them for

## The inference

From the readings of 2026-11-12 to [CLAIM-tmpifp69](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpifp69.md)'s three steps: that where
attention is the revenue, optimizing for it favours clickbait (step 1);
that investigative journalism's value falls largely to people who never pay
for it (step 2); and that its findings cannot be shown to be in demand
before they exist (step 3), so that attention optimization works against
it.

## Critical questions

- **Does the evidence support step 1?** Narrowly. Robertson and colleagues
  ([THEORY-tmp593fy](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp593fy.md)), a Registered Report on 12,448 randomized headline tests
  at one outlet, find that each negative word in a headline of average
  length raises click-through by 2.3%, a relative change on a base rate of
  about 1.4%. A firm optimizing clicks by such tests would select for
  negative wording. That is selection in packaging, not in assignments or
  beats, and says nothing about what is reported.
- **Does it support step 2?** Yes. Gao, Lee and Murphy ([THEORY-tmp9nkgq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp9nkgq.md))
  find county borrowing costs rising by about 5 to 11 basis points after a
  local daily closes, a cost borne by taxpayers whether or not they
  subscribed. Næss ([THEORY-tmpd4kon](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpd4kon.md)) finds willingness to pay for
  investigative journalism, summed over all who benefit, far above what a
  seller can collect, with exclusivity adding nothing.
- **Does it support step 3?** Indirectly. Scalco and colleagues
  ([THEORY-tmpp061k](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpp061k.md)) can detect an information void only where demand is
  visible as searches or page views; the investigative case is exactly the
  demand such a detector cannot see. That marks the limit of measurement
  the step relies on; it is not evidence that newsrooms act on it.
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its strongest rival?** No, and two
  readings favour the rival. The claim's own `defeated_if` names the
  collapse of the older advertising subsidy as the rival cause. Gao and
  colleagues' instrument is Craigslist's erosion of classified advertising,
  so the closures whose costs they measure are caused by that collapse.
  Bisiani and colleagues ([THEORY-tmpulte1](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpulte1.md)) document original local civic
  reporting halving across 27 papers of one publisher and being replaced
  by syndicated content, and treat cost-driven syndication under
  consolidation as the principal logic, with page-view targets one
  mechanism among several. Næss also attributes the decline to lost
  advertising revenue. None of the readings isolates attention optimization
  from subsidy collapse.
- **Do the claim's grounds carry what it cited them for?** Not as grounds.
  Robertson supports step 1 at the level of wording only; Bisiani documents
  the outcome but favours the rival cause; Hayes 2025 ([LIT-tmpk0xvf](../literature.d/LIT-tmpk0xvf.md)) could
  not be read and is Unavailable. Each is moved from the claim's grounds:
  Robertson's and Bisiani's accounts corroborate the claim instead, and
  Hayes is cited in its text only. [CASE-tmpd5eqf](../cases.d/CASE-tmpd5eqf.md) stays as a ground.

## Verdict

Proposed. Step 2 is supported by two independent readings, and step 1 at
the level of headlines. The claim's central step, that optimizing for
attention, rather than losing the advertising subsidy, costs a newsroom its
investigative capacity, is not shown, and the readings that bear on cause
lean to the rival. The claim stays Proposed, with corroboration in place of
grounds that carried less than they were cited for.
