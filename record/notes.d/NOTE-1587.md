# NOTE-1587: Evans, Silverberg & Santangelo — Lattice mechanics of origami tessellations

## Contribution

- A general framework for the kinematics and linear energetics of an
  origami tessellation treated as a crystalline lattice.
- An analytic decay length for localized deformations of the Miura-ori,
  checked against experiment.
- The identification of edge-localized soft modes, linked to topological
  mechanics.

## Key insight

Origami vertices are local mechanisms with several configuration branches,
and a tessellation is a lattice of them coupled through shared folds. Once
facet bending is admitted, a real Miura-ori is far floppier than the
one-degree-of-freedom count of rigid origami. But its extra inhomogeneous
freedom lives at its boundary: those soft modes decay into the bulk.

## Assumptions

- Linearized kinematics about the folded ground state.
- Nearly flat facets, with bending represented as extra folds.
- Crease energies as torsional springs of equal stiffness.
- A periodic tessellation, analysed in Fourier space.
- An experiment on laser-cut paper with α = π/3 and ε = π/2.

## Key results

- **Sec. I, the single vertex.** "the associated solutions are generically
  multi-valued. These results imply that there are multiple branches of
  configuration space for any given spherical polygon."
- **Sec. III, the Miura-ori.** Facet bending enters as two extra folds per
  vertex, and det[D(q)J] = 0 then gives
  cos²α/sin⁴(ε0/2) · sin²(qx/2) + sin²(qy/2) = 0 (Eq. 16). "The only real
  solution to this equation is q=0, indicating that an infinite origami
  tessellation does not admit spatially inhomogeneous solutions; only
  uniform deformations are allowed". Those are three uniform zero modes:
  contraction, twist and saddle.
  - For a finite sheet, qy = ±iκ(q) gives edge states with
    ℓ(q) = 1/(2|sinh⁻¹[cos α sin(q/2)/sin²(ε/2)]|) (Eq. 24).
  - A fourth, acoustic-like mode appears at finite q.
- **Fig. 5 caption.** "over a wide range the softest mode is the twisting
  mode (II)": a uniform, bulk mode.
- **Sec. III, experiment.** "Inhomogeneous deformations are created using
  both an external indenter to apply a displacement (Fig. 6B,C) and by
  placing reversible “pop-through defects” (Fig. 6D) [7]." "the strain
  decays exponentially away from the indenter with a decay length that is
  consistent (within error) with our theoretical predictions".
- **Fig. 6 caption.**
  - "in the presence of a “pop-through defect” (PTD) [7], the lattice
    distorts to accommodate the induced strain."
  - "Because the PTD induces an extensional distortion rather than a
    compression, the strain is oppositely signed."
  - For a PTD the decay length "can be estimated to within 100%".
  - "Within errorbars, this measurement is consistent with an inverse
    relationship between decay length and wave vector."
- **Sec. IV.** "these ``soft modes'' are edge states that cannot occur in
  an infinite tessellation", and "We can directly conclude from this that,
  unlike normal solids, the the number of degrees of freedom scale with the
  perimeter of a finite tessellation, rather than the area." (The doubled
  "the" is the source's.)

## The check

All of it is in scratch/formal/evans_miura_check.py, second-checked by the
coordinator on 2026-11-06.

- **The paper's own matrices.** J0 (Eqs. 12–15) and D^T(q) were
  transcribed from the source. Two entries of D^T are printed with a bare q,
  and only the reading q = q_x reproduces Eq. 16. With that reading:
  - det[D(q)J] vanishes on Eq. 16, with real roots at q = 0 only;
  - Eq. 24 follows from Eq. 16;
  - the small-q limit ℓ ≈ sin²(ε/2)/(|cos α| q) is the inverse law of the
    Fig. 6 inset.
- **Controls.** A κ with the cos²α/sin⁴ factor dropped fails at α = 9π/20
  and at ε = 2π/3. At α = π/3, ε = π/2 the factor is exactly 1, so the
  control cannot be run there.
- **Not checked.** The entries of J0 are stated without derivation ("Using
  the geometric relationships between the angles [35], we find").
- **The reviewer's own count.** This part does not use the paper's
  algebra. A bar-and-joint Miura-ori was built with each face split by one
  diagonal.
  - Folded, it has exactly B − 3 infinitesimal floppy modes, B being its
    boundary vertices, from 2 × 2 to 12 × 12 and at three aspect ratios.
  - Laid flat, the same net has a count that grows with area (the control).
  - So the perimeter scaling is plain Maxwell counting for a triangulated
    disc with no states of self-stress, generic to triangulated sheets. The
    paper does not make that point.

## Concepts

- **Fold and crease**: a fold is a hinge between unbendable, unstretchable
  sheets; a crease is a plastically formed fold with a preferred angle. The
  paper uses the two interchangeably.
- **Internal state s**: the N − 3 component vector fixing all a vertex's
  dihedral angles.
- **Constraint matrix D(q) and rigidity matrix R(q) = D(q)J.**
- **Pop-through defect (PTD)**: a reversible local inversion in the
  Miura-ori, cited from ref. [7] and not defined further here.
- **Soft modes and edge states**: inhomogeneous zero modes decaying from
  the boundaries with length ℓ(q).

## Connections

It cites Silverberg et al. (2014), [LIT-2044](../literature.d/LIT-2044.md), as ref. [7] for the
pop-through defect, and stands in for it, partly.

- **Of the 2014 paper's results, it restates**:
  - that Miura-ori sheets admit reversible pop-through defects;
  - that the strain a defect induces is localized and decays rapidly;
  - that a real Miura-ori has more freedom than rigid-origami counting
    gives, through facet bending.

  It adds several configuration branches at each vertex.
- **It does not restate**:
  - that each Miura-ori unit cell is mechanically bistable. Its one use of
    "bistability" is for single vertices, citing other work: "providing
    novel functionality such as complicated bistability [15–17]";
  - that switching cells reprograms the sheet's compressive stiffness;
  - how pop-through defects interact;
  - any analogy to vacancies, dislocations or grain boundaries; none of
    those words appear in it.

Silverberg et al. (2015), [LIT-2100](../literature.d/LIT-2100.md), makes the same move in the square
twist, modelling facet bending as virtual creases.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures, through this
  neighbour.** A's phrase "Miura-ori bistable cells" for the 2014 paper is
  not confirmed here. The neighbour calls pop-through defects reversible
  but says nothing of bistable cells. A's phrase about defects "analogous
  to vacancies, dislocations, and grain boundaries" is not confirmed
  either: the neighbour describes them only as local distortions. Both
  claims remain unchecked.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md) (scale separation makes constraints).** Related, weakly.
  The paper models facet bending, a stiffer mode, as extra folds. As in
  Silverberg et al. (2015), whether a mode counts as a constraint or a
  freedom depends on the energy scale probed. This paper does not make that
  point itself.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md) (composing interactions creates constraints).** Related.
  Vertex constraints compose through shared folds, and the composite's
  degrees of freedom sit at its boundary, not through its bulk. In a
  triangulated sheet, composing rigid triangles leaves exactly B − 3.
- **[CLAIM-495](../claims.d/CLAIM-495.md) (O(d) connection; assignments fixed by holonomies).**
  Unrelated in the linear form used here. The spherical-polygon
  construction encodes single-vertex closure, a loop-closure condition, but
  the paper does not put it in holonomy terms.
- **Edge states and topological mechanics.** The record holds:
  - Kane and Lubensky's boundary zero modes, [THEORY-1408](../theory.d/THEORY-1408.md), which this paper
    cites as [38];
  - Maxwell–Calladine counting, [THEORY-1414](../theory.d/THEORY-1414.md), which gives the perimeter
    scaling directly;
  - [QUESTION-391](../questions.d/QUESTION-391.md), of which this is a case: an effective constraint, the
    rigid facet, stops being a good approximation once facet bending
    matters;
  - [QUESTION-378](../questions.d/QUESTION-378.md): the freedoms a composition of rigid parts leaves sit at
    its boundary.
- **Filed**: [THEORY-1428](../theory.d/THEORY-1428.md). Its old promotion condition, an independent

  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- Linear theory, so small deformations about one ground state only.
- Bending coarse-grained as folds.
- One 8 × 8 paper sheet; the PTD decay length estimated only to within
  100%.
- J0 stated without derivation. D^T(q) prints a bare q in two entries,
  which must be q_x. The printed zero modes mix sign conventions.
