# THEORY-tmp15pfs: Dollar and Howe report that a four-fingered hand with compliant polymer joints and an adaptive tendon transmission, driven by one actuator under open-loop control, grasped household and test objects despite positioning errors comparable to the object's size, so that passive mechanics did work usually assigned to sensing and control

## Source

Dollar and Howe (2010), LIT-tmp8z7y4; read in NOTE-tmpb0ok0.

## What was actually shown

- **The design** (§2.2, p. 588): "A single actuator drives the four
  fingers (eight joints) of the hand", with compliance in parallel with the
  actuation.
- **Experiment 1** (§3.1.4, pp. 592–593): a grid of positioning offsets
  around a fixed cylinder and block, three trials each. Success over "a
  total allowable positioning error of over 100% of the object size in each
  direction" for the cylinder and over 45% for the block. The grid could
  have shown a narrow range; it did not.
- **Experiment 2** (§3.2.3, p. 596): four spheres located from one webcam
  image, twelve trials each, all successful under positioning errors of
  7.5 to 33.5 mm.

## What this does not say

- That the hand performs computation: "morphological computation" is the
  pasted synthesis's label, not the paper's.
- That one actuator is enough for precision or dexterous grasps: the hand
  has no pinch grip, and the authors expect more actuators to be needed as
  dexterity rises.
- That it beats a fully actuated hand: no such hand was tested in the same
  experiments.
- That tolerance is uniform: near the edge of the grasp range small changes
  of position switch the hand between jamming and slipping aside.
