# QUESTION-277: When formerly autonomous partners come to depend on one another, which of mutual dependence, reproductive cohesion, the distribution of essential functions across genomes, the host's regulatory control and the alignment of selection does each route to dependence bring about, and which of them, alone or together, make the partnership a new evolutionary individual?

## Why it is a question

The record has a theory of when a collective becomes an individual, and it
has almost no cases of how partners come to depend on each other. Its
readings set the conditions on the collective. Selection between collectives
must come to trump selection within them ([THEORY-141](../theory.d/THEORY-141.md)). Collaboration and
generatively entrenched interactions must give organizational closure
([THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md)). Darwinian and physiological individuality come apart
([THEORY-443](../theory.d/THEORY-443.md)), and the reading reports Godfrey-Smith placing aphid–*Buchnera* as both. [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md) holds that
constitutive dependence does not entail alignment of interests. What the
record lacks is the biology of the routes by which partners lose autonomy.
The exchange of 2026-10-27 named five, which "are not mutually exclusive":

- **Adaptive gene loss.** A costly, leaky function is lost because others
  supply it, and the loser becomes dependent (Black Queen, [LIT-1568](../literature.d/LIT-1568.md)).
  Selection acts on the individual, not on the collective.
- **Drift-driven decay.** Small, asexual, vertically transmitted populations
  accumulate mildly deleterious mutations (Muller's ratchet, [LIT-1573](../literature.d/LIT-1573.md);
  reviews [LIT-1559](../literature.d/LIT-1559.md), [LIT-1571](../literature.d/LIT-1571.md)). The resulting dependence need not
  benefit either partner.
- **Complementary loss across lineages.** Pathways are split among nested
  symbionts and the host ([LIT-1565](../literature.d/LIT-1565.md)), and a symbiont splits into two
  lineages with the functionality of one ([LIT-1563](../literature.d/LIT-1563.md)). The number of
  dependent parts grows without an obvious new collective function.
- **Gene transfer and import.** Genes move to the host nucleus and their
  products are imported back (*Paulinella*, [LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md); the nitroplast,
  [LIT-1562](../literature.d/LIT-1562.md); review [LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md)). This route gives the host control.
- **Host compensation.** The host maintains the collective's function by
  transport, regulation or rescue, including with genes taken from third
  lineages ([LIT-1569](../literature.d/LIT-1569.md)).

The exchange's thesis, in its words: "A system can become more
interdependent without becoming more cooperative, more unified, more
autonomous, or even more adaptive." Two of its cases show the routes do not
run one way. In mealybugs the inner symbiont has been replaced repeatedly
while host-encoded transferred genes stayed ([LIT-1572](../literature.d/LIT-1572.md)). In
*Monocercomonoides* a whole mitochondrion was lost once a bacterial Fe–S
system replaced its one essential job ([LIT-1567](../literature.d/LIT-1567.md), [LIT-1574](../literature.d/LIT-1574.md)). So
losing genes is not the same as losing function, losing autonomy or gaining
integration.

The exchange proposed scoring a partnership on five dimensions, as "a
conceptual schema, not an established metric":

- **D**, physiological dependence. It is directional: the host's dependence
  on the symbiont need not equal the symbiont's on the host.
- **G**, how essential functions are distributed across the genomes.
- **R**, reproductive cohesion: co-transmission, synchrony, bottlenecks.
- **C**, regulatory integration: the host's control of transport,
  expression, division and allocation.
- **S**, selection alignment: conflict within the collective relative to
  selection between collectives.

No single one is to stand in for individuality. Athreya, Czuppon and
Gokhale's model ([LIT-1570](../literature.d/LIT-1570.md)) gives the first formal separation, of D from
R. In it, mutual dependence evolves faster than reproductive cohesion even
when investments in the two are uncorrelated. With a carrying capacity for
collectives, complete dependence or cohesion may not evolve at all.

**What would count as an answer.** For each route, which of D, G, R, C and S
it moves, shown on worked systems, not assumed. Then a stated criterion,
tested on the same systems, for which combination makes a new evolutionary
individual. The criterion must say whether high D with low R is a candidate
individual or only a dependency. It must also say whether a partnership
whose constituents keep being replaced is one individual or a succession.
[THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md) predicts that metabolic complementation alone is not closure, and
the mealybug patchwork is a place to test that. Eukaryogenesis is a harder
test. The three phylogenomic programmes the exchange set together disagree
on whether it was one two-partner merger or a sequence of associations: late
mitochondrion after a complex host ([LIT-1566](../literature.d/LIT-1566.md)), mostly Asgard
inheritance ([LIT-1558](../literature.d/LIT-1558.md)), and several waves of bacterial transfer
([LIT-1561](../literature.d/LIT-1561.md)). [LIT-1564](../literature.d/LIT-1564.md) adds the chimeric metabolism. If the
programmes disagree, the event may not have a single route at all. [NOTE-159](../notes.d/NOTE-159.md)
already doubts that eukaryogenesis fits the transitions narrative.

Selection for extreme specialization within a group (Cooper & West,
[LIT-1225](../literature.d/LIT-1225.md), unread) is a sixth route. It works within a species, where the
exchange's routes cross lineages.

The question is the exchange's. The record takes no position on its answer
until the works are read. A2 put it as follows: "The biological literature
would not automatically settle these philosophical questions. It would,
however, constrain what a successful theory of individuality must explain."

## Note of 2026-10-27 (later): cases, and works for what the exchange named without citing

Six documented cases now hold the systems the question names. All are
Proposed, because their sources are unread.

- Aphid–*Buchnera* (CASE-tmpoa9h4): dependence produced largely by drift.
- The mealybug's nested symbionts (CASE-tmpir76a): genes from third
  parties, and constituents replaced.
- *Hodgkinia*'s splitting in cicadas (CASE-tmpx4tix): more parts and more
  dependence with no new function, and a host that compensates at
  transmission.
- *Paulinella* (CASE-tmpowpq1) and the nitroplast (CASE-tmp30dzl): control
  through gene transfer and import.
- *Monocercomonoides* (CASE-tmpa087v): an organelle lost.

The exchange named several things without citing a work. Works now
registered for each:

- **Gene transfer to the nucleus:** LIT-tmp1u989.
- **Host compensation:** LIT-tmp4u7hy.
- **Population-genetic models of gene loss:** LIT-tmpmnpe8 and
  LIT-tmprmgys.
- **Metabolic-network constraints:** LIT-tmp01c3k.
- **Multitype branching processes:** LIT-tmpj3ova. This is a general text;
  no endosymbiosis work using the method was found.
- **Mutualism theory:** LIT-tmpfubrx.
- **Host control and within-collective conflict, the S and C dimensions:**
  Frank's two models, LIT-tmpt00nh and LIT-tmpi201p.
- **The eukaryogenesis scenarios A1 listed:** syntrophy (LIT-tmp9k032,
  LIT-tmp4y7ls), and complexity before the mitochondrion (LIT-tmp8xhh2).

LIT-tmpi201p makes a point that bears on the answer. Reproductive cohesion
(R) can be imposed by the host's control (C), by sequestering a germ line
of symbionts. If so, the two dimensions are not independent, and an answer
must say which drives which.
