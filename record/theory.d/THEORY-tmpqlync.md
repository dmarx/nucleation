# THEORY-tmpqlync: Neural field recordings carry aperiodic 1/f-like activity with power at every frequency, whose exponent varies with age, state and task; so power in a band, correlated power changes across bands, and ratios of band power can arise from the aperiodic component with no oscillation changing, and 1/f structure is a ubiquitous background of field data rather than a mark of any particular organization

## Source

Donoghue, Schaworonkow and Voytek (2021), LIT-tmpz3w7i, pp. 9–19 and 39 of
the PsyArXiv preprint; read in NOTE-tmpsrba2.

## What was actually shown

Aperiodic activity, 1/f^χ with χ "often" between 0 and 4, is "a ubiquitous
and sometimes dominant feature of neural field data", linked to
postsynaptic potentials (p. 9). Because it has power at every frequency,
"any spectral measure … will always return a numerical value for power for
a given frequency band, even if there is no oscillatory activity present"
(p. 9). In simulation, a change in χ alone yields a difference in alpha
power (Fig. 3B), a pattern of changes across five bands (Fig. 3C–D) and a
change in relative alpha power (Fig. 3E). Empirically, the reported link
between ADHD and the theta/beta ratio "appears to be driven by aperiodic
activity" (p. 18, Robertson et al. 2019). The exponent varies with age,
diagnosis, sleep and task (p. 17).

## What this does not say

- **That 1/f activity is meaningless.** It is "itself a physiologically
  informative feature … reflecting processes distinct from neural
  oscillations" (p. 39).
- **That it is or is not a signature of a macro-level constraint.** The
  paper does not address emergence. What follows from it for
  THEORY-tmp6xjgq is narrower: since every field recording shows 1/f
  structure, its presence does not discriminate between accounts.
