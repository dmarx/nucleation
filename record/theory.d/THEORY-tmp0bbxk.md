# THEORY-tmp0bbxk: Reward has separable components: 'liking', its hedonic impact, measurable without report by orofacial taste reactivity in humans, apes, monkeys and rodents, and 'wanting', its incentive salience; brain stimulation and sensitization can raise 'wanting' without raising 'liking', so motivation can grow while pleasure stays flat or falls, and 'liking' reactions can occur without any subjective feeling of pleasure

## Source

Berridge and Kringelbach (2015), "Pleasure systems in the brain",
*Neuron* 86(3):646–664 ([LIT-tmpmp2nw](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmp2nw.md)), read in full in [NOTE-tmprub8s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmprub8s.md) from
the NIH author manuscript. It is unpaginated, so locations are by section.

## What was actually shown

A review of the authors' programme and others'.
- **Three components.** "liking (core reactions to hedonic impact),
  wanting (motivation process of incentive salience), and learning
  (Pavlovian or instrumental associations and cognitive representations)"
  (A neuroscience of pleasure).
- **A non-report measure of 'liking'.** Orofacial reactions to sweet and
  bitter tastes: "Homologous 'liking' orofacial expressions can be
  elicited also in apes and monkeys, and even in rats and mice" (Objective
  hedonic reactions).
- **'Wanting' without 'liking'.** "LH stimulation failed to enhance
  'liking' reactions to sweetness, even though it made the rats 'want' to
  eat at least four times more than normal amounts" ('Pleasure
  electrodes'). In addiction, incentive sensitization would "selectively
  magnif[y] cue-triggered 'wanting' to take the drug again, and so
  powerfully cause motivation even if the drug became less pleasant"
  (Resolving the cocaine puzzle?).
- **'Liking' without feeling.** "objective hedonic 'liking' reactions can
  sometimes occur alone and unconsciously in ordinary people without any
  subjective pleasure feeling at all" (Subjective versus objective
  levels).

What could have come out otherwise: manipulations that raise pursuit
raising taste reactivity with it.

## What this does not say

- That 'wanting' is not valenced. The review says "Motivational salience
  is never neutral, but its valence is not fixed" (Incentive salience as
  sham reward).
- That taste reactivity measures pleasure outside taste. It is a marker
  for one modality.
- That the dissociation has been read here first-hand. The record holds
  the review, not the primary studies.
