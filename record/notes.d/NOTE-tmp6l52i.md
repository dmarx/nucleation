# NOTE-tmp6l52i: Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven — Meaningful Human Control

## Contribution

A design-oriented, compatibilist analysis of control that is neither mere
presence in the loop nor mere causal influence, with "tracing" extended
from one agent's earlier choices to many agents along a design history.

## Key results

- **Presence is not control** (p. 4): "simple human presence or 'being in
  the loop' is not a sufficient condition for being in control of a
  (military) activity", for instance when the human task is "merely
  pushing a button in a reflex when a light goes on".
- **Tracking** (p. 7): "a decision-making system should demonstrably and
  verifiably be responsive to the human moral reasons relevant in the
  circumstances".
- **Necessary, not sufficient** (p. 8): "Meaningful human control is a
  necessary but not sufficient condition for a system to be morally or
  societally good."
- **Duties up and down** (p. 9): operators have a "meta-task
  responsibility" to check whether the system lets them do what they
  ought; "the designers, producers, and architects of these elaborate
  systems have an obligation to design the system in such a way that this
  type of inquiry by users is not made impossible or unduly difficult."
  Responsibility "may trace back to other agents upstream" when the
  operator's conditions fail through "no fault of the operator".
- **Tracing** (p. 9): outcomes traceable to "at least one human agent in
  the design history or use context" who "understands or is in the
  position to understand the capabilities of the system and the possible
  effects in the world of the its use" and "understands or is in the
  position to understand that others may have legitimate moral reactions
  toward them because of how the system affects the world and the role
  they occupy."
- **A gap without bad intent** (p. 11): programmers who "assume that
  military commanders will be able to discharge their duty" and commanders
  who "overestimate the capacity of the systems": "there is arguably a
  responsibility gap as no agent satisfies the tracing condition."
- **Design the environment** (p. 12): control can be kept by preventing
  hard encounters, e.g. "separate lanes for autonomous and traditional
  vehicles".

## Assumptions

- Compatibilism about moral responsibility.
- That Fischer and Ravizza's conditions transfer from the mechanisms of
  one person to extended socio-technical systems.
- That whose reasons are to be tracked is settled case by case.

## Concepts

- **Meaningful human control**: tracking and tracing together.
- **Tracking condition**, **tracing condition**, **guidance control**.
- **Meta-task responsibility**.
- **Responsibility gap**.

## Bearing on the record

Filed as THEORY-tmpnmvsj.

- **CLAIM-tmpb47ul**, the control-and-duty part, from theory: presence is
  not control (p. 4); designers owe operators a system whose fitness they
  can check (p. 9); responsibility traces upstream when the operator's
  conditions fail through no fault of hers. Nothing on where blame
  actually lands, nor on conscientiousness. Corroborates.
- **QUESTION-tmpvoufs**: the operator's meta-task responsibility and the
  designers' matching obligation are a pair of duties running both ways,
  but a duty, not an instrument by which agents hold principals to
  account. Evidence on the question, not an answer.
- The record's notes on Fischer and Ravizza's guidance control (e.g.
  NOTE-270, NOTE-1457, NOTE-548) hold the theory this paper adapts. Zeiser
  (LIT-tmp3iik8) says the framework "does not specifically focus on
  attributability".

## Limitations

- Mostly military; the civil application is a sketch.
- The tracking condition can be met by a system serving a wrongful
  commander.
