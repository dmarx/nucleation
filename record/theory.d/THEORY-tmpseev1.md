# THEORY-tmpseev1: Anodal transcranial direct current stimulation over the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, with the montage of the original study, does not raise reported mind-wandering in a sustained-attention task, and the original positive result was most likely a false positive

## Source

Boayue et al. (2019/2020), LIT-tmp9cpfz, read in NOTE-tmps966y: "we
conclude that the original result by Axelrod et al. (2015) was most likely
a false positive finding caused by strong variability and low sample size"
(§4).

## What was actually shown

In Tromsø, Amsterdam and Göttingen, 192 participants were randomised to 20
minutes of 1 mA anodal tDCS at F3 (cathode right supraorbital) or sham,
double-blind, during the original 40-minute SART with 24 probes. The
pre-registered sequential plan would have stopped early had the 95% HDI of
the effect excluded zero in the positive direction; it never did, and the
full sample was collected. The effect was d = −0.11 [−0.38, 0.17]; the
null was favoured over the predicted positive effect (BF 10.65) and the
original effect size was strongly disfavoured (replication BF 0.002). The
design could have found the effect: it was powered on the original's
estimate.

## What this does not say

- **That brain stimulation cannot change mind-wandering.** The authors
  say the failure "does not imply that tDCS is an ineffective tool for
  modulating mind wandering propensity", and point to parietal and medial
  prefrontal montages still to be replicated.
- **Why the first author's later replication succeeded.** It predates it.
- **Anything about meta-awareness or the agent's model of its control.**
  Only the original's single probe was used.
