# LIT-tmprlvo0: What is purely epistemic normativity, and why? A study in Wolfian epistemology

Richard Pettigrew (2025), *PhilArchive preprint (listed as "In Preparation" on the author's homepage)* — <https://philpapers.org/rec/PETWIP>

## Key takeaways

- What we ought to believe "epistemically speaking" is fixed by the norms whose broad adherence across an epistemic community would give the best epistemic results — a transposition of Susan Wolf's rule consequentialism — which explains why we separate epistemic from all-things-considered oughts.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Pettigrew asks what separates what we ought to believe simpliciter from what we ought to believe epistemically speaking, and why we draw that line. He motivates the question with examples and rejects existing answers. He proposes that purely epistemic norms are those whose general adherence across one's epistemic community would be best from an epistemic point of view — a version of Susan Wolf's rule consequentialism applied to belief — and argues that norms telling us to believe only what we epistemically ought to follow from it. A coda offers an account of the function of knowledge ascriptions that he presents as an improvement on Edward Craig's.
