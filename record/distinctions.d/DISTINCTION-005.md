# DISTINCTION-005: Three sources of a co-created delusion's themes

## The sides

- **A, the user's frame** ([CLAIM-1419](../claims.d/CLAIM-1419.md)). A sycophantic model doubles the
  user's own bias.
- **B, the model's priors** ([CLAIM-1444](../claims.d/CLAIM-1444.md)). The stories the model finds
  most coherent to continue.
- **C, the culture** ([CLAIM-1383](../claims.d/CLAIM-1383.md)). Narratives of machines carried by user
  and training text alike.

## Telling them apart

| Varied, others held fixed | A predicts | B predicts | C predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| the user's prior concerns | themes follow them | no change | no change |
| the model | no change | themes change | no change, if trained on the same culture |
| the culture | follows the user | no change | themes change |

The record has none of these contrasts. The most direct held evidence is
[THEORY-1339](../theory.d/THEORY-1339.md) (Lu et al.): drift off the Assistant persona follows the latest
user message, and goes to the non-Assistant end in AI-philosophy
conversations. That is A's driver with B's destination, which is why the
alternatives may combine ([ARG-270](../arguments.d/ARG-270.md)).

## What it does not say

That only one source acts. The defeater's first clause is the strongest
objection: B and C may not separate, since a model's priors are learned from
its culture's text. If so, the distinction narrows to A against B-and-C.
