# ADR-032: Argument development: QUESTION, CLAIM, CASE, TERM and ARG

## Context

The record documents other people's arguments well: LIT for works, NOTE for
readings, THEORY for accounts with their evidence, and `extends`, `rivals`,
`corrects` and `presupposes` between them. The owner wanted it to hold their
own argument as it develops: synthesis, objections anticipated and answered,
positions taken and given up.

The design was worked out in a session on 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07. It began
from established structures: IBIS (Kunz and Rittel, [LIT-758](../literature.d/LIT-758.md)), discourse graphs,
Toulmin, Dung's abstract argumentation and the AIF, ASPIC+'s three kinds of
attack, Walton's argumentation schemes with critical questions, Carneades and
IRAC from legal argument, and competitive debate. The design-rationale
literature on why such systems get abandoned ([LIT-759](../literature.d/LIT-759.md), [LIT-761](../literature.d/LIT-761.md), [LIT-756](../literature.d/LIT-756.md))
entered the record alongside it ([ADR-030](ADR-030.md)).

The decisive evidence was a real exchange: the owner's three-day development
of an essay with a chat assistant, 95 owner turns and some 300 replies, read
in full by seven parallel readers, which coded every argumentative move. What
it showed:

- **What went wrong was identity, not missing structure.** Concepts were
  redefined silently: will five or more times, concordance five times, the
  perspective and level schemes swapped turn to turn. A worked case changed
  identity six times. The permissiveness objection was re-derived six or more
  times as though new. Readings the record already held were recommended three
  times. Commitments were dropped and never retired. Each of these is what a
  code fixes.
- **Inference attacks are common, and mostly on analogies.** Roughly half to
  two-thirds of objections during development attacked an inference rather
  than a premise or conclusion, and nearly all the real ones were "same
  structure, not the same mechanism". In the final manuscript, 7 of about 30
  claims had more than one independent line of support.
- **Most "X does not establish Y" sentences deny inferences nobody makes.**
  There were more than 40 in the audit stretch alone. They are claims the
  record disowns, not attacks on arguments.
- **Credence and inclusion come apart.** The exchange's own audit rated the
  paper's central thesis "promising, needs direct defense" and made it the
  main contribution.
- **A line of argument had to be retracted whole.** The owner tried to fork
  the conversation upstream of where authenticity entered; the fork did not
  hold, and the retraction was done in-band, by audit.

## Decision

Five schemes, each with merge allocation and an index view.

- **QUESTION.** An open question. Statuses Open, Answered, Dissolved, Deferred
  and Superseded. `refines` another question. Claims and THEORY readings name it
  in `answers`, so its page holds both: the literature's accounts beside the
  record's own.
- **CLAIM.** The record's own proposition. `status` is credence, with the same
  words and meanings as on a THEORY. `role` is its voice: `thesis`, `counter`
  (a steelman or anticipated objection), `granted` (accepted, and against a
  thesis), `disclaimed` (explicitly not claimed) or `supposed`. A thesis must
  say `defeated_if`, and every claim names its `line`. Relations:
  - `rests_on` another CLAIM: the record's own derivation, and the spine a
    chain walks.
  - `grounds` a THEORY, CASE or LIT: evidence from outside the record's own
    argument. A LIT target means the work is cited without a reading, which is
    the weakest footing. The split from `rests_on` is THEORY's own, between
    `presupposes` and `source`.
  - `objects_to` a CLAIM (a rebuttal, or an undermining if it is a premise),
    and `undercuts` an ARG. The field says the kind of attack, so there is no
    `strikes:` field.
  - `superseded_by` a CLAIM, declared with a converse `supersedes`.
  - `complements` another CLAIM.
  - `uses` a TERM.
  - `answers` a QUESTION.

  **An objection is a claim**, so replies and counter-replies come free, and
  an answered objection is a counter gone Rejected.
- **CASE.** An example, thought experiment or historical episode, filed once
  so every claim it serves points at the same thing. `standing` is
  documented, stipulated or fictional. Relations: `illustrates` a claim
  (bears no weight), `counterexample_to` a claim, `variant_of` another case,
  and `substitutes_for` another case (a fictional company standing in for a
  real one).
- **TERM.** A concept or distinction. No credence: it has a definition, and a
  changed definition is a new version or a superseding TERM, so the claims
  stated in it (`uses`) can be found.
- **ARG.** An inference: premises in `rests_on` (claims) and `grounds`
  (readings, cases, works), at least one; `concludes` a claim; and `form`
  from a closed vocabulary whose blurbs carry Walton's critical questions.
  **Filed only when something must point at the inference** — a claim's second
  independent line of support, or an undercut. Premises of one ARG are linked;
  two ARGs concluding one claim are independent lines.

Alongside the schemes:

- **`lines`.** A named line of inquiry, so a line can be set aside as a whole
  and its dependents found through `rests_on`. It is the axis of the CLAIM and
  QUESTION indexes, which therefore group by line rather than by topic; the
  topic pages remain.
- **`works`.** The manuscripts a claim is marshalled into. Membership is
  independent of credence.
- **The workbench journal.** One dated entry per working session, saying what
  moved and why. The claims hold where the argument stands; the workbench
  holds how it got there.

**Chains** render what no single entry holds:

- `argument` walks `rests_on`, with `line` as its invariant. A step from one
  line into another is reported, because that is how a line set aside reaches
  back into one still standing: had the authenticity claims been resting
  under will-organization claims, the chain would have said so.
- `dialectic` walks `objects_to`, faceted by `role` and `status`. Each
  objection appears with its replies, and an open objection is visibly a
  counter still Proposed.
- `revisions` and `term-revisions` walk `supersedes`, oldest first.

The multi-scheme references this needs arrived in luria 0.36.0
(dmarx/luria#357), and CI is bumped to it. The far side of each relation is a
field of the same name on every scheme it names. THEORY gains `answers` and
`supports`; LIT gains `supports`.

**Demonstration.** The exchange is filed as it would have been documented,
from five workbench entries, one per session. Some of what it shows:

- the paper's thesis [CLAIM-tmp8swu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8swu5.md), Proposed but in the work;
- its two independent arguments [ARG-tmpuhkwv](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpuhkwv.md) and [ARG-tmpgkhf9](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpgkhf9.md);
- the permissiveness objection as one code, [CLAIM-tmp25zdy](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp25zdy.md), with its
  history;
- the definition of will superseded ([TERM-tmp1at0k](../terms.d/TERM-tmp1at0k.md) to
  [TERM-tmpn9tow](../terms.d/TERM-tmpn9tow.md));
- Northstar's variants as separate cases;
- the Fraenkel analogy undercut in all but its structural respect;
- the authenticity line deferred as a whole;
- open items as Proposed counters and unanswered critical questions,
  including two the final rewrite dropped.

## Alternatives considered

- **An objection scheme separate from CLAIM.** luria's own rule splits
  schemes when two kinds of claim must be able to disagree. An objection's
  status means what any claim's does — does the record believe it — so it is
  one scheme with a role.
- **An ARG for every claim** (AIF's information and inference nodes
  throughout). Principled, and it is what the design-rationale literature
  says gets abandoned: the capture cost lands on the author now and the
  benefit on a later reader. In the final manuscript most claims had a single
  linked line, so an ARG would be a second file saying what `rests_on`
  already says.
- **Claims only, with no ARG.** That leaves an undercut nothing to point at,
  and the exchange's most frequent real objection is an undercut of an
  analogy.
- **A `strikes: premise | inference | conclusion` field on objections.** With
  ARG available, the kind of attack follows from the kind of thing attacked,
  and a field would be a second copy of it.
- **Multi-scheme `rests_on` and `objects_to`** — one field holding a claim's
  premises whatever their scheme. This was the first draft. A chain walks a
  relation only when the chain's own scheme is its sole target, so the
  argument's spine and its objection threads could not be drawn as lines.
  Splitting by what the relation means (derivation versus evidence, rebuttal
  versus undercut) gives single-scheme spines without splitting one relation
  by target type.
- **Lines as topic tags.** The `topics` vocabulary is closed and says what a
  work is about. A line of inquiry is a different axis: it is retired as a
  whole, and it groups the record's own argument rather than its reading. A
  `line` field, named as the axis, groups without diluting the topics.
- **A chain over `refines` for question trees.** Nothing uses `refines` yet.
  Second use.
- **A full argumentation ontology** (AIF, Carneades). These are built to
  analyse finished arguments. This record develops unfinished ones.
- **Literature roles beyond premise** (foil, method, analogue, boundary
  marker), and **authorship** (owner or assistant). Both recurred. Prose
  carries them until a check needs them.

## Consequences

- **The filing rule is the record's own:** a thing gets a code the second time
  it turns up. The drift the exchange shows is exactly the set of things that
  recurred. When an agent does the filing, the risk is no longer that capture
  stops but that it floods, and this rule is the throttle.
- **The record now holds documents not in force on purpose.** A Proposed
  counter is an open objection; a Rejected one is answered history. Each
  citation of one carries an `inactive-ok` saying so.
- **`complements` stays between claims for now.** A symmetric relation that
  crosses schemes (CLAIM to THEORY) reads as one-sided from both ends under
  luria 0.36.0, and `luria link --fix` appends a duplicate edge on each run.
  That is a luria bug, fixed in dmarx/luria#358. Widen `complements` to THEORY
  once a release carries the fix. Until then a reconciliation with a reading
  is a `rests_on` plus prose.
- **The cross-line check was fired before being trusted** (2026-10-07). A
  will-organization claim was made to rest on the authenticity claim, and the
  lint reported "share no `line` (argument; each holds: authenticity /
  will-organization)". It is a report, not a failure: a cross-line step can be
  legitimate, and a person decides.
- **Lines of argument stop at an ARG.** A claim concluded by an argument rests
  on its premises through the ARG, and a chain does not cross schemes, so the
  main result appears in no line. Giving the claim a `rests_on` as well would
  be a second copy of the ARG's premises. A luria chain that can pass through
  an intermediate scheme would close this.
- **luria labels a second parent "also extends"** whatever relation a chain
  walks. On the dialectic page, an objection to two claims reads "also
  extends". Cosmetic, and an upstream fix.
- **Not yet built:** a report for a thesis with no counter against it, a
  check that a `granted` claim is Active, and a rendered argument map. Each
  waits for a second need.
