# THEORY-tmp5l4e4: Kelly argues that technology taken as a whole, the technium, is an interdependent, organism-like system whose self-reinforcing feedback gives it long-run tendencies independent of human wants, toward diversity, efficiency, complexity, ubiquity and interdependence, which on balance widen human choices

## The account

Kelly argues that "the technium", the interdependent whole of technology, has "its own agenda": "Its wants are not deliberations, but rather tendencies", an "inherent bias ... outside of our wants", arising from self-reinforcing feedback. The long-term bias is toward diversity ("More ways, more choices"), efficiency, complexity, ubiquity and interdependence. "We don't always have to do what technology wants", but should start from it.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpwvb8c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwvb8c.md), results 1–6; the prologue in [NOTE-tmptihtb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptihtb.md). Asserted from analogy, with the evidence promised for the book.

## What it does not say

What remains open to someone who declines a technology. Kelly's measure is the adopter's options, the measure [CLAIM-tmpbypt7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md) sets aside.
