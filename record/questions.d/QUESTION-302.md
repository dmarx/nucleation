# QUESTION-302: When a memory survives the rebuilding of the nervous system that formed it, as through insect metamorphosis or planarian head regeneration, what carries it across, and what does that show about whether the animal after is the one that learned?

## Why it is a question

The owner recalled work on memory that persists through metamorphosis, and
a child's experiment asking whether caterpillars he raised would remember
him as butterflies. The experiment is real, but its order is the other way
round from the recollection.

- **The publication came first.** Blackiston, Silva Casey and Weiss
  ([LIT-1680](../literature.d/LIT-1680.md)), in 2008, trained tobacco hornworm caterpillars to avoid an
  odour. Those trained in the last larval stage still avoided it as moths.
  Alloway ([LIT-1781](../literature.d/LIT-1781.md)) had reported the same in beetles in 1972.
- **The child's work followed it.** A 2026 report describes Jo Nagai, a
  schoolboy in Kobe, repeating the design with swallowtails and lavender
  oil, advised by Martha Weiss, and claiming the aversion passed to the
  butterflies' offspring. As of the reports seen on 2026-10-29 it had been
  presented to scientists in Japan but not published. The record files
  only the published work, and holds the offspring claim as an unverified
  report.

Planarians make the case sharper. A worm whose head is cut off regrows a
brain and, after a reminder, keeps a learned familiarity with its
surroundings (Shomrat and Levin, [LIT-1732](../literature.d/LIT-1732.md)). The authors' perspective
(Blackiston, Shomrat and Levin, [LIT-1707](../literature.d/LIT-1707.md)) collects such cases.

## Why it matters to the record

- **Persistence through the replacement of parts.** Philosophers'
  thought experiments about personal identity (Parfit, [LIT-469](../literature.d/LIT-469.md) and
  [THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md)) ask whether psychological continuity can survive a wholly
  rebuilt body. Metamorphosis is a natural case: the moth is continuous
  with the caterpillar in body, and partly in memory, through a
  reorganization of most of its nervous system. [CASE-098](../cases.d/CASE-098.md) is the
  social analogue, a rivalry that outlives every player who carried it.
- **Where the record's inheritance questions meet.** If a learned
  aversion passes through metamorphosis, the moth inherits its own past.
  If, as reported but unpublished, it passes to offspring, the case joins
  the epigenetic question ([QUESTION-297](QUESTION-297.md); Dias and Ressler,
  [LIT-1746](../literature.d/LIT-1746.md)). Bacterial cells already inherit a transient state from
  their mothers ([THEORY-179](../theory.d/THEORY-179.md)).
- **The self over time.** Hershfield ([LIT-1702](../literature.d/LIT-1702.md)) measures how continuous
  people feel with their future selves. The moth is a body continuous with
  a past self it cannot have experienced as its own, the legacy question
  ([QUESTION-301](QUESTION-301.md)) turned inside out.

## What would count as an answer

- **On the mechanism.** Evidence on what carries the memory: retained
  neurons (the mushroom bodies are partly preserved in metamorphosis),
  molecular states elsewhere in the body, or carried-over chemical cues,
  which Blackiston et al. tested against.
- **On what it shows.** An argument about whether a memory that survives
  total remodelling makes the later animal the same individual in any
  sense beyond bodily continuity, or shows only that information can be
  stored outside the tissue that formed it.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the works read

The moth study and the authors' perspective were read themselves.
Shomrat and Levin is read through the authors' own short post on it;
Alloway is Unavailable.

- **The moth keeps a memory through part of its brain** (Blackiston,
  Silva Casey and Weiss, [NOTE-1249](../notes.d/NOTE-1249.md)).
  - 77% of moths trained as fifth-instar caterpillars still avoided the
    odour, and 12 of the 15 tracked individually kept their larval choice.
  - Washing or scenting the pupae changed nothing, so residue on the
    pupal case does not carry it.
  - Aversions learned at the third instar survived two larval molts but
    not metamorphosis. That places the carrier in something the molts
    keep and metamorphosis replaces. The authors suggest late-forming
    mushroom-body neurons, by analogy with *Drosophila*, untested.
- **What carries it is still open** (Blackiston, Shomrat and Levin,
  [NOTE-1258](../notes.d/NOTE-1258.md)). The perspective calls the insect mechanism unsolved, and
  describes the mushroom-body lobe the 2008 paper relied on as only partly
  kept. Its proposal for planaria, memory stored outside the brain and
  written back into the new head, is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- **The planarian result is weaker than filed** (Levin and Shomrat's post,
  [NOTE-1283](../notes.d/NOTE-1283.md)). Worms recognised the training chamber after their heads
  regrew only after "a brief refresher". Neither source reports whether
  the refresher alone gives untrained worms the same result. Until it
  does, the case shows that something outlasts the head, not yet that a
  memory does.
- **What it shows about identity.** The moth keeps a thin,
  timing-dependent psychological connection to the caterpillar while
  plainly being the same organism. That is a partial connection under
  biological identity ([THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md)), not continuity through a wholly
  rebuilt body. The perspective recalls McConnell's report that both halves
  of a cut worm kept the training. If that held, planarian fission would be
  Parfit's fission case realised: two successors, each psychologically
  connected to one learner.
