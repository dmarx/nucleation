# CASE-261: A hospital network, stipulated, adopts an AI coordination system that improves diagnosis, cuts administrative cost and treats more patients, while patients cannot contest automated decisions, clinicians lose discretion, support workers are watched and contractors have no influence; the test is whether a justified objection to a hard-to-measure safety problem becomes effective in the organization, and whether it would if it came from patients or low-status workers

## The case

A23 §9 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6af6b47c-8b0c-83ea-ae04-3e3eb2649597):

> Consider a hypothetical hospital network implementing a powerful AI
> coordination system.
>
> The system improves diagnostic accuracy, reduces administrative costs,
> and increases the number of patients treated.

A's table of relationships, each with a potential improvement and a
potential problem:

| relationship | potential improvement | potential problem |
|---|---|---|
| hospital and patients | better diagnosis and treatment | patients cannot contest consequential automated decisions |
| hospital and clinicians | less paperwork and better information | professional discretion and independent skills diminish |
| hospital and support workers | more efficient scheduling | increased surveillance and unpredictable workloads |
| hospital and contractors | expanded economic opportunities | essential contributors receive little protection or influence |
| hospital and wider community | improved access to care | benefits or costs are distributed unevenly |
| hospital and future patients | accumulated knowledge and capacity | dependency on an irreplaceable provider increases fragility |

## The test

"The deeper test: What happens when interests conflict?" Clinicians find
"a recurring but difficult-to-measure safety problem"; "The system's
headline statistics remain excellent"; management has reason to keep the
system. A's questions:

> Does the clinicians' objection become legible?
>
> Can it exert effective authority?
>
> Can the organization investigate and correct the problem?
>
> Would its procedures preserve the same capacity if the objection came
> from patients or low-status workers?

The first three are [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)'s legibility, volitional efficacy and
correctability, asked of one objection. The fourth asks them again with the
party changed, which is what makes it a test of the organization and not of
the objection: an organization can pass for clinicians and fail for
contractors.

## What it shows, and what it does not

- **Illustrates [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md)**: "aggregate success cannot settle any one of
  these relationships". The case stipulates success and leaves each
  relationship open.
- **Illustrates [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)** and **[CLAIM-686](../claims.d/CLAIM-686.md)**: the objection's route to
  effect can fail at each of three points, and a failure at any of them,
  for a party's claims, leaves that party without effective voice. Exit
  (for patients, another hospital; for clinicians, another employer) is
  outside the test, as [CLAIM-686](../claims.d/CLAIM-686.md) says it is outside the three.
- **Bears on [QUESTION-461](../questions.d/QUESTION-461.md)**: the contractors are indispensable contributors
  whose contributions do not become claims; whether that is unjust is
  [QUESTION-463](../questions.d/QUESTION-463.md).
- It is stipulated and settles no fact about any hospital.

## Note of 2026-11-17: the economics of nominal oversight, a variant, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-tmp238zy CLAIM-tmpb47ul CLAIM-tmpe5u7i — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17 -->

A19 §2 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (2026-11-15) gives a
hospital AI variant of this case, with a deterioration-prediction tool and
clinicians who "retain responsibility for all treatment decisions". Its
added element is arithmetic: "Suppose management introduces a tool that
saves ten minutes of work but requires five minutes of verification." If
management "budgets only for the savings, without accounting for
verification time", the clinician must "either inspect the output
carefully and fall behind schedule, or work faster and risk missing an
error". "If they choose careful verification, their conscientiousness
subsidizes the organization. If they skip verification and something goes
wrong, they may be blamed for carelessness." "The system has converted
professional responsibility into an unbudgeted safety mechanism", and the
clinician is "**both an essential safety component and a convenient
repository of blame**".

For this case, that sharpens "clinicians lose discretion": they keep
formal discretion and lose the time to use it, which is how a justified
objection fails to become effective without any rule against it. The
mechanisms are filed from the same exchange: the unbudgeted subsidy
([CLAIM-tmpe5u7i](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe5u7i.md)), blame landing where failure is visible ([CLAIM-tmpb47ul](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpb47ul.md)),
and verification required and then cited as ownership ([CLAIM-tmp238zy](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp238zy.md)).

No status changes.
