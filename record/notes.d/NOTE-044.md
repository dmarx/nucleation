# NOTE-044: Grangier & Auffèves, What is quantum in quantum randomness?

## Contribution

Quantum randomness is often called ontological and classical randomness epistemological, but what quantization and discreteness contribute to the difference has seldom been addressed. The authors answer within their "contextual objectivity" ontology (CSM), where quantum randomness arises from contextuality together with quantization. They argue this changes what quantum theory is for. In particular, it undercuts reductionist programmes that try to derive the classical world from many quantum systems. A second part treats quantum physics and thermodynamics as two theories of randomness and traces their mutual influence. It ends with technological uses of quantum randomness in quantum thermodynamics.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction: Heisenberg-microscope "disturbance" accounts make quantum randomness look like classical coarse-graining. Bell violations removed local hidden variables, but no physical replacement explanation of randomness was offered. The paper is built on the authors' CSM approach (refs 13–15).
- "Contextual objectivity": systems and contexts are objective partitions of the world. A state is what licenses certainty about the repeated answer to the same question (an "ID card"). Classical phenomenology is non-contextual.
- "Randomness in a contextual world": the prescriptions for ontological randomness are (i) an elementary system with N exclusive modalities, embedded in a context, and (ii) a change of context. Adding (iii), that the context can change continuously, singles out quantum randomness. The probability matrix is then unistochastic, with the proofs in refs [14, 15]. The paper states the hierarchy reversals: randomness and non-unitarity come first and unitarity is a calculation tool; quantization comes first and interference second.
- The same section: because the wave function lives only at the system–context interface, dissolving the context into systems is "meaningless". This is contrasted with Relational QM.
- Thermodynamics section and conclusion: stochastic thermodynamics uses randomness to separate heat from work and to ground irreversibility. Measurement-induced "quantum heat" can fuel engines with no heat source, so quantum randomness becomes a resource beyond random-number generation.

## Open questions

- It is the Grangier–Auffèves CSM programme in brief. Its reliance on unistochastic transition matrices is a direct point of contact with Barandes' stochastic-quantum correspondence (c15), and a joint reading could compare the two routes to Born-rule probabilities.
- The derivation of quantum theory from (i)–(iii) is only cited, not reproduced. A deeper reading needs refs [14, 15] (Auffèves & Grangier, Sci. Rep. 2017, and related papers).
- The anti-reductionist claim, that the classical world cannot emerge from many quantum systems, is strong and should be checked against decoherence-based accounts.
