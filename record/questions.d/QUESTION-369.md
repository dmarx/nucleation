# QUESTION-369: What would show that an experience is a basic psychological need, something an organism's functioning requires, rather than something that reliably raises reported well-being, when self-determination theory identifies needs by their effects on well-being and treats well-being as the presence of need satisfaction?

## Why it is a question

Self-determination theory names three basic needs, autonomy, competence and
relatedness, and defends them by criteria. Vansteenkiste, Ryan and Soenens
2020 list nine ([NOTE-440](../notes.d/NOTE-440.md), Table 2). The "essential" and "distinct"
criteria are the theory's attempt at this question. If well-being is
measured by the satisfaction of the same needs, a need claim cannot be
tested without assuming the measure. [NOTE-439](../notes.d/NOTE-439.md)'s "Interdefinition" limitation
says so.

The record's evidence bears on it in four places:

- **Candidates that fail.** Martela and Ryan ([LIT-1983](../literature.d/LIT-1983.md), held
  second-hand in [NOTE-440](../notes.d/NOTE-440.md)) class beneficence a wellness enhancer, not a
  need, because its frustration adds no variance. Lubics et al. 2026
  ([LIT-1980](../literature.d/LIT-1980.md)) test it again.
- **Rival candidates.** Sheldon et al. 2001 ([LIT-1966](../literature.d/LIT-1966.md)) found self-esteem
  strong among ten candidates. [NOTE-440](../notes.d/NOTE-440.md) holds SDT's reply, that it is
  derivative.
- **Frustration as a separate test.** [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) holds that need frustration
  predicts ill-being apart from low satisfaction. Murphy et al. 2023
  ([LIT-1978](../literature.d/LIT-1978.md)) argue the frustration scale may largely measure item keying.
- **The measures themselves** (Kermavnar et al. 2024, [LIT-1988](../literature.d/LIT-1988.md)).

## What would answer it

A criterion for need-hood that does not pass through the same well-being
measure. For example: a functional deficit, not a report, that follows
thwarting of the candidate need and not of a matched enhancer, under
intervention rather than correlation. Or a showing that the interdefinition
is harmless because the nine criteria fix the needs independently.

## Bears on

[THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) and [CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md), the record's own use of frustration as a
primitive, which inherits whatever this question finds about need
frustration's measurement.

## Note of 2026-11-03: the readings

- **No functional measure exists.** All 31 need scales Kermavnar et al. map are
  self-reports ([NOTE-1509](../notes.d/NOTE-1509.md)), so none measures the functional deficit this
  question's answer calls for. Candidate needs are proposed because the three
  needs' frustration "may not adequately predict … ill-being": the well-being
  route itself.
- **The measures do not separate the needs.** In Patrick et al. 2007
  ([NOTE-1515](../notes.d/NOTE-1515.md)), corrected for unreliability, autonomy and competence
  correlate .96 and autonomy and relatedness .86. The frustration test sorts
  partly by keying (Murphy et al., [NOTE-1530](../notes.d/NOTE-1530.md)).
- **SDT's own researchers raised the question.** Sheldon et al. 2001
  ([NOTE-1502](../notes.d/NOTE-1502.md)) weighed treating self-esteem as an outcome, called that
  "imprudent", and concluded "there may be four fundamental psychological
  needs". [NOTE-440](../notes.d/NOTE-440.md)'s "derivative" reply answers them.
- **One criterion off the well-being route, weak.** Chen et al. find need
  satisfaction helps whether or not the need is valued or desired
  ([NOTE-1527](../notes.d/NOTE-1527.md)). It cannot tell a need from a universal enhancer.
- **Circularity elsewhere.** Niemiec et al. define intrinsic aspirations by their
  link to the needs ([NOTE-1520](../notes.d/NOTE-1520.md)), so their mediation inherits it.

Martela and Ryan and Lubics et al. are Unfetched, so beneficence is still held
second-hand. The question stays open.

## Note of 2026-11-03 (later): La Guardia et al., read

La Guardia et al. 2000 ([NOTE-1523](../notes.d/NOTE-1523.md)) give the interdefinition worry at the
level of an instrument. The need that best predicts attachment security,
relatedness, has items closest to security in content, so the authors repeat
every analysis without it. The results hold for autonomy and competence, which
is their answer. And the variance is mostly across partners, so a need's
satisfaction is a property of a person in a relationship, not of a person.
