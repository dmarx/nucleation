# THEORY-406: Self-presentations that differ by audience coexist without trouble while the audiences are kept apart by spatial, social and temporal boundaries, and collide when a medium removes the boundaries; networked publics make the collision frequent and often unseen by the person it happens to

## Source

danah boyd (2008), *Taken Out of Context: American Teen Sociality in
Networked Publics*, PhD dissertation, UC Berkeley, [LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md), read in part
in [NOTE-710](../notes.d/NOTE-710.md). The account of collapsed contexts is Meyrowitz's (1985)
account of broadcast media, extended to everyday people; Meyrowitz is not
held. This account rests on a skim.

## What was actually shown

**The dynamic (p. 34).** "Collapsed contexts: the lack of spatial,
social, and temporal boundaries makes it difficult to maintain distinct
social contexts."

**The paradigm case (pp. 36–37, after Meyrowitz 1985: 43).** Carmichael
used "very different rhetorical styles when speaking to blacks and
whites". He "was able to maintain distinct styles as long as he was able
to segment social groups, he ran into trouble when electronic media
collapsed those social groups". Broadcast left "no neutral speaking
style".

**Collisions before networked media (p. 38).** Weddings, "in which context
collisions are common, are frequently scripted to make everyone
comfortable". Meeting one's boss while out with friends is awkward, but
both parties see the collision and adjust. Online, "the performer is
unaware of audiences from different contexts, magnifying the awkwardness
and making adjustments impossible."

**Teenagers (pp. 145–146, 225).** Teens "feel more pressure when they are
forced to contend simultaneously with different audiences, such as peers
and parents", and manage it structurally (privacy settings, false
details) and socially. MySpace's Top Friends forced friends "from
different contexts ... into a single spot for comparison."

**Distinct contexts are not deception (p. 37).** Context helps people
"properly locate their performances", as bilingual speakers choose a
language by setting.

## What this does not say

- **That the effect is measured.** No count compares conflict with and
  without segregated audiences. The evidence is ethnographic.
- **That differing presentations are harmless in themselves.** The claim
  is conditional on segregation holding; it says nothing about
  presentations that differ for reasons other than audience.
- **That collapse began with social media.** Broadcast collapsed contexts
  for public figures first (Meyrowitz), and scripted occasions show people
  managing collisions long before either.
- **Anything beyond US teenagers of 2004–2008**, except through the
  Carmichael case, which the record has only at second hand.
