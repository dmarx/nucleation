# NOTE-832: Fricker — Epistemic Injustice (précis)

## Contribution

The précis states the book's two concepts and its virtue-theoretic answer
to them. The generic wrong is being "wronged specifically in his capacity
as a knower". It takes two forms, one in testimony and one in the shared
resources for making sense of social experience. Two corrective virtues of
the hearer answer them. The précis also argues that both virtues are at
once intellectual and ethical.

## Key insight

A hearer's prejudice that deflates a speaker's credibility is not only an
epistemic fault that blocks the flow of knowledge. It is also an ethical
wrong to the speaker. And a person can suffer a wrong of the same kind
with no prejudiced hearer present at all, when the concepts she would need
to understand her own experience are missing because people like her had
too little part in making them.

## Assumptions

- **Testimony is a main source of knowledge.** "Few would deny that an
  enormous amount of what we know is, at root, testimonially acquired."
- **Prejudice is the cause.** In testimonial injustice, credibility is
  deflated "owing to prejudice on the hearer's part". The précis says
  "prejudice", not "identity prejudice"; whether the book restricts it to
  prejudice about social identity cannot be checked from the précis.
- **Hermeneutical injustice is collective and social.** It concerns making
  sense of "a social experience", and the gap is "owing to the relative
  powerlessness of a social group to which the subject belongs".
- **A virtue is individuated by its immediate end**, not by its ultimate
  end. The hybridity argument depends on this premise.

## Key results

*Skim: the précis, pp. 69–71, read in full. No chapter of the book was
read.*

- **Generic definition (p. 69).** "The speaker who receives a prejudicially
  deflated degree of credibility from a hearer is thereby wronged—he is
  wronged specifically in his capacity as a knower. This idea of being
  wronged in one's capacity as a knower constitutes my generic
  characterization of epistemic injustice."
- **Testimonial injustice (p. 69).** "A speaker receives a prejudicially
  deflated degree of credibility from a hearer." It "wrongs the subject
  in his capacity as a giver of knowledge". Example: "a jury does not
  believe someone simply because of the colour of his skin."
- **Hermeneutical injustice (pp. 69–70).** It "occurs at a prior stage,
  when someone is trying to make sense of a social experience but is
  handicapped in this by a certain sort of gap in collective
  understanding—a hermeneutical lacuna whose existence is owing to the
  relative powerlessness of a social group to which the subject belongs.
  Such a lacuna renders the collective interpretive resources structurally
  prejudiced." Example: making sense of homosexual desire where gay
  people "were prevented from making a full contribution to collective
  resources for social meaning". The wrong is analysed as "situated
  hermeneutical inequality": "being unfairly disadvantaged in rendering
  one's social experiences intelligible, to others and possibly even to
  oneself".
- **Corrective virtues (p. 70).**
  - Testimonial justice: the hearer "corrects for any influence of
    prejudice by reinflating credibility to non-prejudiced levels". It
    "pre-empts the testimonial injustice altogether".
  - Hermeneutical justice: the hearer adjusts credibility "appropriately to
    the hermeneutical handicap incurred by the speaker". It comes "after
    the fact of the hermeneutical injustice itself, but it can ameliorate,
    even neutralize, the harms". The hearer picks up "that the speaker's
    lack of intelligibility is not her fault".
  - The précis twice writes "the virtue of testimonial injustice" and "the
    virtue of hermeneutical injustice" where the virtues of justice are
    meant, and once "of the hearer" where the speaker is meant. These are
    slips in the text, read here as intended.
- **Testimony as perception (p. 70).** Both virtues belong to the hearer's
  "testimonial sensibility". On this account "the virtuous hearer
  perceives her interlocutor in epistemic colour". This is offered to cut
  between inferentialism and non-inferentialism about testimony.
- **Hybridity (pp. 70–71).** Considered as intellectual, testimonial
  justice aims ultimately at truth. Considered as ethical, it aims
  ultimately at justice. Under both descriptions its immediate end is
  "neutralizing prejudice in one's credibility judgements". "Same
  immediate end, so same virtue." The same holds of hermeneutical justice,
  with understanding as its ultimate intellectual end. "Prejudice is at
  once an intellectual and ethical vice."

## Concepts

- **Epistemic injustice.** Being "wronged in one's capacity as a knower".
- **Testimonial injustice.** A prejudicially deflated credibility judgement
  by a hearer, wronging the speaker "as a giver of knowledge".
- **Hermeneutical injustice.** Being handicapped in making sense of a
  social experience by a lacuna in collective understanding that is owed
  to the relative powerlessness of one's social group.
- **Structurally prejudiced resources.** Collective interpretive resources
  with such a lacuna. The prejudice is in the resource, not in any
  person's judgement.
- **Situated hermeneutical inequality.** The lived experience of being
  unfairly disadvantaged in making one's social experiences intelligible.
- **Testimonial sensibility.** The hearer's "trained sensitivity to the
  multifarious signs of a speaker's degree of epistemic trustworthiness".

## Connections

The précis places the account in virtue epistemology and in an
Aristotelian ethical cognitivism, where the virtuous subject perceives
"the world in moral colour". It denies the Aristotelian separation of
intellectual from ethical virtue. Other works are not named.
[NOTE-098](NOTE-098.md)'s reading of Leonelli was until now the record's only route to
Fricker.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md)** was filed ahead of this reading. Its definitions
  hold against the précis, with three refinements:
  - **"Identity prejudice."** [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md) defines testimonial injustice as a
    deficit "caused by identity prejudice". The précis says "prejudice"
    and gives skin colour as its example. The word "identity" may be the
    book's, but it is not in the précis.
  - **The second position is narrower than the question states it.**
    [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md) says a contributor "whose kind of move fits no scheme is
    in the second position: the move is not disbelieved but cannot be
    expressed". In the précis, hermeneutical injustice concerns making
    sense of "a social experience", and the gap must be owed to "the
    relative powerlessness of a social group to which the subject
    belongs". A move that fits no scheme meets the definition only if
    the misfit traces to a group's exclusion from making the scheme. A
    lone contributor's misfit, or one owed to an assistant's role, does
    not meet it on the précis's terms. That settles [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md)'s "whether
    the gap tracks who the contributor is" in one direction: it must track
    group membership and powerlessness, not just who the contributor is.
  - **Part 1 is closer to testimonial than to hermeneutical injustice.**
    If whether a move is filed depends on who made it once content is held
    fixed, that is a deficit in uptake from a "hearer", the filer.
    Fricker's term for it is testimonial injustice, but only if the cause
    is prejudice.

## Limitations

- The précis states the book's conclusions and gives only the hybridity
  argument whole. The cases the book is known for, and its treatment of
  credibility excess, are not in it.
- The definitions come from a summary written for a symposium. The book's
  own wording may differ, for example on "identity prejudice".
- The précis says nothing on how to tell a group's "relative
  powerlessness" from other reasons a concept is missing.

## Open questions

- Does the book require identity prejudice for testimonial injustice, or
  any prejudice?
- Can hermeneutical injustice befall an individual whose group is not
  powerless, when the lacuna is local, for instance in a single
  institution's vocabulary? The précis's definition says no. Whether the
  book allows local or institutional versions would decide whether
  [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md)'s case can meet it.
- What are the commentators' objections, and Fricker's replies (Theoria 23(1),
  pp. 81–86)? They were not fetched.
