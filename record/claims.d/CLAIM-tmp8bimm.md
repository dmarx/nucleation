# CLAIM-tmp8bimm: Regulatory surplus can be captured asymmetrically

## The claim

A24 §8 widens what counts as the product of cooperation. Besides goods and
services, "cooperation also generates regulatory surplus": "If I can rely
upon you, I no longer need to expend the same resources preparing for your
unreliability. Those savings are real organizational benefits. The question
is how they are distributed."

| Before cooperation | After accommodation (A24 §8) |
|---|---|
| "Both verify information independently." | "One party becomes a trusted information source." |
| "Both maintain contingencies." | "One party handles interruptions." |
| "Both monitor the relationship." | "One party performs most of the monitoring." |
| "Both resolve their own failures." | "One party increasingly compensates for failures elsewhere." |

"One participant may capture the benefits of reduced regulatory
expenditure while another continues bearing—or increases—the costs of
maintenance."

A24 §9 gives the case of accommodation. Two parties meet recurring
disagreements; one is "more willing or able to accommodate"; the
relationship "develops around that participant's flexibility", and "The
other no longer needs to maintain comparable capacities for negotiation,
reflection or compromise." "The resulting arrangement appears harmonious.
But its harmony is asymmetrically produced." The figure's caption: "Both
arrangements can exhibit the same visible harmony." So the relationship
"can therefore become exploitative precisely because one participant has
become exceptionally good at maintaining it", and "The asymmetry has become
part of the cooperative organization itself", not a defection from it.

A22 §10 gives the norm-level form: one participant is bound by a norm it
maintains (reliability, reciprocity, honesty, patience, loyalty,
professional responsibility, openness to criticism, forgiveness), the
other relies on that and is less constrained by it. "The more reliable one
participant is at maintaining the relationship, the less another
participant may need to invest in its maintenance."

## Relation to the record

- [CLAIM-713](CLAIM-713.md) holds the commitment side: a moral commitment is exploitable
  because the committed party reliably acts on it. This claim names what is
  captured when it is: the regulatory work the other party no longer has to
  do.
- [CLAIM-700](CLAIM-700.md) holds the loop that hides it: conscientious compensation read
  as adequacy, and resources cut to what the present configuration needs.
  A22 §11's "hidden regulatory subsidy" (100 units of work, 85 funded, 15
  supplied by a constituent) restates that loop.
- [CLAIM-744](CLAIM-744.md) holds A22 §10's "Patience" row: whether waiting for a benefit
  another controls is good judgment depends on the controller's
  reliability.
- Gouldner's 1960 paper on the norm of reciprocity, which A22 §10 cites
  for connecting reciprocity, functional organization and exploitation, is
  registered ([LIT-tmp4wo8i](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4wo8i.md), not read).

## What it does not say

- Not that the asymmetry is exploitation. A22 §10: "That is not
  necessarily exploitation; it could be a fair division of labor. The
  distinction requires examining the terms, benefits, burdens and
  distribution of control." Exploitation is a verdict relative to a
  declared standard ([TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md)); A24 §8 calls this "a candidate mechanism
  for **regulatory exploitation**, under the appropriate normative
  conditions".
- Not that the accommodating party should accommodate less. A22 §20: "the
  response to exploitation of conscientiousness need not be less
  conscientiousness."
