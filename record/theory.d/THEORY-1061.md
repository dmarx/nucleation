# THEORY-1061: Flack makes downward causation operational as components tuning their behaviour to their own estimates of aggregate variables they collectively compute: weak (apparent) when at least one component tunes to a variable slower than the behaviour producing it, strong (effective) when the variable is predictive, robust, used nearly universally, agreed on and gaining mutual information with the microscopic behaviour; she speaks of a new level only at the strong form, and regeneration of the variable by its use is not among her criteria

## Source

Flack (2017), [LIT-1630](../literature.d/LIT-1630.md), §1, §3 and §7; read in [NOTE-1216](../notes.d/NOTE-1216.md).

## What was actually shown

**The move.** "I propose we move from simple feedback to downward causation
when components tune behaviour in response to estimates of coarse-grained,
aggregate properties" (§1). The aggregate is computed by the components
themselves, "endogenous coarse-graining" (§2). Because the components "are
tuning to patterns they perceive and hence are 'doing the work'", the
account claims to avoid the non-materialism objection (§1).

**Weak form.** "only a few components need to be tuning their behaviour to
estimates of aggregate properties for there to be some downward causation
in the system"; estimates need not be correct, predictive or shared (§1).
In the macaque case: the power distribution "changes more slowly than the
microscopic fight behaviour producing it and at least one individual uses
estimates" of it to tune behaviour (§3).

**Strong form** (§1, citing [8–10]): aggregates "predictive of the future
state of the system (slow variables)"; "robust to small perturbations";
estimates "used nearly universally by all components"; components "largely
agree in their estimates"; and "as estimates converge there should be an
increase in mutual information between the microscopic behaviour and the
macroscopic properties". "it makes sense to start talking of a new level of
organization when the criteria for effective downward causation are met"
(§1). §7 restates the level criteria with one addition: component estimates
must be "functionally good approximations of the aggregate properties as
computed by an observer with perfect information".

**What reinforces itself.** The convergence of estimates is more likely
"as a result of positive feedback induced by the downward causation itself"
(§3), and correlation among components "amplifies itself through effective
downward causation" (§7). No criterion in either list says that the
components' use of the variable preserves or regenerates it.

The reading could have come out otherwise: Flack could have made
maintenance by use a criterion, and her example would have allowed it,
since the signals that compose the power distribution are the members' own
behaviour, refreshed by continued fighting (§3). She does not.

## What this does not say

- That apparent or effective downward causation is causation under any
  formal account. "I will not discuss in this paper when the operational
  concept of downward causation I am proposing conforms to strict
  definitions of causality" (§1).
- That the macaque system meets the strong criteria. §3 states them for the
  system as "when/if", and the evidence is in [9,10,26,27], not here.
- That Flack denies regeneration matters. It is absent from her criteria,
  not argued against. Whether Flack 2012 ([LIT-1122](../literature.d/LIT-1122.md)) states it is unread.
- That the two lists (§1 and §7) coincide. They differ, and the paper does
  not reconcile them ([NOTE-1216](../notes.d/NOTE-1216.md), Limitations).
- That slowness has been shown necessary. It is a stipulated condition, with
  the reason "for tuning to be possible" asserted from [8–10] (§1).
