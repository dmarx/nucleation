# TERM-074: organization, and which sense: relational arrangement, organizational maintenance, collective institution or organizational closure

## Definition

"Organization" in this record means one of the following, and a claim
should say which.

1. **Relational arrangement.** A configuration of relations among
   distinguishable processes, components, roles or constraints, whose
   arrangement matters to some specified operation or outcome. Two
   engineering departments with the same specialists and information can
   differ in whether a safety engineer can stop a deployment; the
   difference is in their arrangement of access, authority, obligation and
   procedure, not in a further person above the specialists. Its boundary
   need not coincide with an employer, a team or a place. This is the
   default sense, and the sense of "organizational" in "organizational
   perspective" ([TERM-072](TERM-072.md) sense 1): a perspective constituted through
   organized relations, not the perspective of a formal organization.
2. **Organizational maintenance.** The activities that reproduce or
   change an arrangement: a school's teaching, recruitment, certification
   and correction. It is a separate fact from the arrangement, because an
   arrangement can be maintained from outside. [CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md) and [CASE-126](../cases.d/CASE-126.md) give
   the construction: two systems with the same arrangement, one
   maintaining it through its own activity and one reset from outside,
   that part under one perturbation.
3. **Collective institution.** A corporation, a university, an agency, a
   federation: an organization in the ordinary sense, with legal,
   institutional and social identity. Its persistence is not the
   persistence of any one perspective or arrangement within it. A party
   can stay legally continuous while its coalition and orientation change
   ([CASE-133](../cases.d/CASE-133.md)).
4. **Organizational closure.** The technical condition of [TERM-070](TERM-070.md): a set
   of constraints each of which depends on another in the set for its
   maintenance. It is a distinct, more demanding technical condition, not
   the top of a scale of organization: the four senses are not a
   hierarchy, and an institution is not a stronger case of arrangement
   than a maintained process. Closure is not the default, and does not
   follow from being organized or maintained, so that a disputed
   criterion of autonomy is not built into "organization" or into
   "perspective". [TERM-072](TERM-072.md) already declines to make self-maintenance part
   of sense 1 for this reason.

Senses 2 and 4 are related as general to particular: closure is one way
an arrangement can be maintained, by the arrangement itself.

## What it is not

- **Not unity, autonomy or central control.** "Organized does not mean
  unified, autonomous, or centrally controlled" (the relayed proposal). An
  arrangement in sense 1 can contain conflicting processes, have no
  controller, and be maintained from outside.
- **Not an agent.** An organization in any sense is a candidate for an
  agent only under a stated criterion of agency ([TERM-044](TERM-044.md), [TERM-063](TERM-063.md)), and
  meeting none is compatible with being organized. [CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md).
- **Not persistence.** Whether an organization persists, and in which way,
  is [TERM-071](TERM-071.md)'s question.
- **Not will-organization.** [TERM-023](TERM-023.md) is a particular arrangement, the one
  governing which of an agent's action-generating regimes is effective.

## What would settle it

Adoption is the owner's decision. The term would be shown wanting if a
claim needed a fifth sense, or if senses 1 and 2 could not be told apart
in a case, which [CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md) says they can. The relayed proposal's test for
every term applies here too, and is [CLAIM-278](../claims.d/CLAIM-278.md)'s objection: a vocabulary
flexible enough to describe every arrangement does not thereby explain
any. Each sense should let a claim say something that could be wrong.
