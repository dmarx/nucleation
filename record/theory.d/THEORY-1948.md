# THEORY-1948: Liedo, Van Grunsven and Marin argue that paid care workers' emotional labor is prone to exploitation because care duties are allocated by what Tronto calls privileged irresponsibility, that workers' satisfaction in caring can mask the exploitation, and that care robots assessed only by design, as Care-Centred Value-Sensitive Design assesses them, can sustain or newly enable that irresponsibility, letting employers and governments avoid reallocating care responsibilities

## Source

Liedo, Van Grunsven and Marin (2024), [LIT-2683](../literature.d/LIT-2683.md), read in full; the reading is [NOTE-2011](../notes.d/NOTE-2011.md).

## What was actually shown

An argument, not a study. Exploitation is benefiting "from the work of someone to the detriment of
her interests" (p. 6). Care workers' identification with caring "could function precisely as a way
of masking the exploitation" (p. 8). Privileged irresponsibility, reported from Tronto, is "a form
of power held by some groups of people that enables them to put too many responsibilities on the
shoulders of those who are supposed to perform care" (p. 9). In the wee-bot case, "the
unsatisfactory conditions of performing care are resolved by exploiting the commitment of PCWs to
the value of care" (p. 11). Empirical support is borrowed, chiefly Wright (2023) on Paro.

Corroborates [CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md): a party's commitment to care is what lets inadequate conditions be
covered at that party's cost. Bears on [QUESTION-468](../questions.d/QUESTION-468.md) (a standard and a source for identifying
exploitation) and [QUESTION-461](../questions.d/QUESTION-461.md) (why dependence on care workers does not become their authority).

## What this does not say

- Not that robots are harmful in themselves; the wee-bot "does seem to be contributing positively"
  (p. 11).
- No strong claim about gender exploitation in the care system as a whole (p. 6).
- Nothing about blame after failure or moral crumple zones.
- Its account of Tronto is second-hand and brief.
