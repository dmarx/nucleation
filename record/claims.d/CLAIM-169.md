# CLAIM-169: On Ainslie's account the population of interests is produced by desynchronized valuation, not given by a fixed architecture: an agent is many interests over a choice only as far as its valuations of that choice cross within the choice's horizon

## The claim

In [THEORY-067](../theory.d/THEORY-067.md) an interest is "selected by one kind of reward over one time
range". What makes two interests two is that each dominates at a different
delay, and that is possible only where the valuation curves cross. Under
exponential discounting the curves never cross, and every delay ranks the
options the same way. On Ainslie's own terms there is then one interest,
and nothing to bargain with.

So the population is not an inventory of standing agents. It is a
description of where, over a given choice and horizon, the agent's
valuations come apart. The same person is one interest over choices whose
curves stay apart, and many over choices whose curves cross. The
desynchronization ([TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md)) comes first, and the multiplicity is its
product.

This is why the owner's prompt can treat temporal discounting as the source
of the multi-agent self, "self" here in the integrated sense
([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md), sense 6), and synchronization as its cure. Anything that keeps
the curves from crossing, or that keeps a crossing from deciding action,
makes the agent act as one. [CLAIM-162](CLAIM-162.md) and [CLAIM-156](CLAIM-156.md) are the two
routes the prompt names.

## What it does not say

- **That every multiplicity of self is intertemporal.** IFS's simultaneous
  parts ([LIT-591](../literature.d/LIT-591.md)), model-based against model-free control ([THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md)) and
  one motive state checking another ([THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)) differ in method or
  content, not in delay. They are a second axis of multiplicity, and this
  claim is silent on them.
- **That the person is many persons.** [THEORY-067](../theory.d/THEORY-067.md) already denies this
  reading, and so does this claim.
- **That Ainslie's mechanism is true.** The claim is about how his
  account individuates interests, and it inherits the account's untested
  standing.
