# LIT-tmpe6ufg: Walking Together: A Paradigmatic Social Phenomenon

Margaret Gilbert (1990), *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 15(1): 1–14 — DOI-10.1111/j.1475-4975.1990.tb00202.x

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, under the topic
`social-ontology` that [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added, as the short statement of Gilbert's
plural-subject account. Whether this paper already states it in terms of

commentary on Tomasello, is unverified. The book-length version is *On Social Facts*
([LIT-tmpsf0sf](LIT-tmpsf0sf.md)).

What the record already holds about Gilbert's view is second-hand, from
the reading of Tomasello's target article and its commentaries ([LIT-125](LIT-125.md),
[NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md)):

- Tomasello uses "joint commitment" without defining it, and Gilbert's
  commentary supplies the definition.
- Pettit's commentary poses a dilemma between a "normatively austere"
  Bratman-style model of collaboration and a "normatively loaded"
  Gilbert-style one. Tomasello's reply offers a graded path between the
  two, which [NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md) rates weak (C8).

That is the disagreement this paper is the classic source for. On
Gilbert's side, obligations and entitlements are part of what acting
together is. On Bratman's side ([LIT-tmp73dsx](LIT-tmp73dsx.md), [LIT-tmpq3vga](LIT-tmpq3vga.md)), they may
accompany it but are not constitutive. Bratman's 2014 précis says so
directly: the interdependence of the participants' intentions "may, but
need not, be based on relevant mutual moral obligations", and it names
Gilbert, with Searle ([LIT-tmpr2slv](LIT-tmpr2slv.md)), as appealing to "basic new elements
over and above those involved in individual intentional agency".

The paper also bears on the group-agent strand. A plural subject is a
joint "we" of the kind Tomasello's account needs. List's paper ([LIT-401](LIT-401.md),
[NOTE-340](../notes.d/NOTE-340.md)) separates that kind of joint "we" from the corporate group agent
he asks about. So whatever Gilbert shows about plural subjects does not by
itself settle whether groups are agents or minds ([THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)).

No instruction for machine-learning practice is known, so nothing here
points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.**

- The two rejected analyses from shared personal goals, and exactly why
  each fails to deliver the walkers' rights and obligations.
- Whether joint commitment is defined here or only introduced, and
  whether it can be rescinded unilaterally.
- How the paper moves from walking together to the concept of a social
  group.
