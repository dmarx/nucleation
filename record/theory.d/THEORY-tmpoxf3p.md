# THEORY-tmpoxf3p: In the Iowa gambling task, every one of ten controls and none of six patients with bilateral ventromedial prefrontal damage developed anticipatory skin-conductance responses, and three patients who could state the right strategy still did not switch to the good decks; the controls' preference for the good decks became significant only once they reported a hunch, so the study does not show advantageous choice or deck-specific bodily signals before any awareness

## Source

Bechara, Damasio, Tranel & Damasio (1997), *Deciding Advantageously
Before Knowing the Advantageous Strategy*, [LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md) (*Science*
275:1293–1295), read in full in [NOTE-331](../notes.d/NOTE-331.md) with its notes.

## What was actually shown

**Design.** Ten normal controls and six patients with bilateral
ventromedial prefrontal lesions and real-life decision defects. Four decks:
A and B pay $100 a card with large unpredictable penalties and lose
overall; C and D pay $50 with small penalties and win. Play stopped after
100 cards. From card 20, and every 10 cards after, play was interrupted to
ask what the participant knew and felt about the game. The answers define
periods: pre-punishment (before the first loss, about card 10), pre-hunch
("no notion of what was happening"; controls roughly cards 10–50), hunch
(liking or disliking decks without certainty; controls roughly 50–80;
never reached by patients), and conceptual (can say which decks are good
and why; reached by 7 of 10 controls and 3 of 6 patients).

**Anticipatory SCRs (note 8).** All controls and no patients generated
them: Fisher's exact test, one-sided P < 0.001. In a group × period ×
deck ANOVA over the pre-punishment and pre-hunch periods, the reported key
effect is group × period, F(1,14) = 16.24, P < 0.001: SCRs rose from
baseline to pre-hunch only in controls. No deck effect or deck interaction
is reported for those periods. In the hunch and conceptual periods,
bad-deck SCRs were "generally higher" than good-deck ones, and the
difference "did not reach statistical significance".

**Choices (note 9).** Group × period × deck, F(1,14) = 6.9, P < 0.02.
Pre-hunch: patients chose significantly more bad than good cards (P <
0.01); controls chose more good than bad, "not statistically
significant". Hunch and conceptual: controls chose significantly more good
cards (P < 0.01). The three patients who reached the conceptual period
still chose more bad cards, not significantly; so "still chose badly"
means they did not switch.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Some patients could have developed
anticipatory SCRs, or the patients who could state the strategy could have
followed it. Neither happened. The controls' pre-hunch preference could
have been significant, and was not.

## What this does not say

- **That nonconscious biases guide behaviour before conscious knowledge.**
  That is the paper's inference (C5 in [NOTE-331](../notes.d/NOTE-331.md)), and it rests on its two
  weakest results. The preference became significant only when controls
  already reported liking and disliking decks. "Before the articulated
  strategy" is supported; "before any awareness" is not.
- **That controls responded to the risky decks before knowing they were
  risky.** The pre-hunch test reported is group × period, not bad versus
  good deck, and later deck differences are not significant either.
- **That the awareness probe was fine-grained.** Two open questions, from
  card 20, every 10 cards. Knowledge not volunteered is coded as absent,
  and nothing is asked in cards 10–20, when the SCRs are said to begin.
- **That the SCR causes the choice.** The two are measured together.
- **That the somatic marker hypothesis is confirmed.** The two-chain model
  and the ventromedial role in it (p. 1294, Fig. 2) are stated, not tested.
- **That the result generalizes beyond small samples.** Six patients,
  three in the conceptual-period result, and control demographics unstated.

  is the evidence for that sentence and is weaker than it.
- **Felt affect.** [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md) holds that felt affect is the experience of
  the body's regulatory state. The hunch period, liking and disliking decks
  without knowing why, is the kind of case it would describe; this study
  measured the SCR, not what was felt.
- **The awareness critique.** Maia & McClelland (2004), which re-examined
  what participants know in this task, is not held in either record and was
  not read.
