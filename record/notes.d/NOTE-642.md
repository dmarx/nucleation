# NOTE-642: Morphology of the Folktale

## Contribution

This reading is of the English translation. The record has already read
the book in Russian, in [LIT-828](../literature.d/LIT-828.md) and [NOTE-615](NOTE-615.md). In the two
chapters read, the 1968 English text carries Propp's method and its
result with nothing substantive lost: the unit (the function of a
character), the four theses, and the 31 functions with their
definitions, signs and subtypes. What the English edition adds is
apparatus. Wagner's notes check Propp's examples against Afanas'ev and
renumber them. Dundes's introduction places the book among structural
methods: it is the exemplar of "syntagmatic" analysis, against
Lévi-Strauss's "paradigmatic" kind.

## Key insight

The English edition is not only a translation but a corrected one.
Almost every page of ch. III carries a note by Wagner. Each gives
Propp's original sentence in transliterated Russian with its 1928 page
number, says where the cited tale is wrong, and gives the tale's number
in the later Afanas'ev edition. A reader of the English can check the
morphology against the tales; a reader of the 1928 text, as the
record's Russian reading was, meets Propp's citations uncorrected.

## Assumptions

- **The material, in the English numbering.** Ch. II keeps Propp's
  statement that the study runs from Afanas'ev's No. 50 to No. 151, "the
  first fairy tale of the collection" to the last. Wagner's note adds that
  "in the new system of enumeration, adopted for the fifth and sixth
  editions and utilized in this translation (cf. the Preface to the
  Second Edition, and Appendix V), the corresponding numbers are 93 to
  270". Every tale number in the running text is in that new numbering.
- **The class**, as in the Russian: "By 'fairy tales' are meant at
  present those tales classified by Aarne under numbers 300 to 749. This
  definition is artificial."
- **Function identity is fixed by consequence**, in the same words as
  the Russian: "Function is understood as an act of a character, defined
  from the point of view of its significance for the course of the
  action."

## Key results

- **The four theses**, verbatim from ch. II (pp. 21–23): "1. Functions
  of characters serve as stable, constant elements in a tale,
  independent of how and by whom they are fulfilled. They constitute
  the fundamental components of a tale. 2. The number of functions
  known to the fairy tale is limited. 3. The sequence of functions is
  always identical. 4. All fairy tales are of one type in regard to
  their structure." They match the four theses in [NOTE-615](NOTE-615.md).
- **The 31 functions** (ch. III), each given as a capitalised summary, a
  one-word definition and a sign. After the initial situation (α):
  absentation β, interdiction γ, violation δ, reconnaissance ε,
  delivery ζ, trickery η, complicity θ, villainy A, and lack a (numbered
  VIIIa, not counted separately). Then mediation ("the connective
  incident") B, beginning counteraction C, departure ↑, "the first
  function of the donor" D, the hero's reaction E, and provision or
  receipt of a magical agent F. Then "spatial transference between two
  kingdoms, guidance" G, struggle H, branding J, victory I, liquidation
  of misfortune or lack K (given no one-word definition), return ↓,
  pursuit Pr and rescue Rs. Last come unrecognized arrival o, unfounded
  claims L, difficult task M, solution N, recognition Q, exposure Ex,
  transfiguration T, punishment U and wedding W. Functions VIII–XV recur
  in a second move, marked "bis".
- **The conclusion of ch. III** in English: "Only some 31 functions may
  be noted"; they "all belong to a single axis"; "one function develops
  out of another with logical and artistic necessity"; functions come
  in pairs (interdiction–violation, struggle–victory and so on) and
  groups (ABC↑ "constitute the complication"; DEF); and the scheme is "a
  measuring unit for individual tales. Just as cloth can be measured with
  a yardstick… tales may be measured by the scheme."
- **Wagner's corrections.** Ch. II and III carry 41 notes signed
  [L.A.W.]. Most say that a cited tale lacks the episode, or holds it in
  a different form, and give the right tale. Two show a misreading
  rather than a slip. In one, the three daughters sent on
  reconnaissance belong to a different tale from the mother who sends
  them. In the other (tale new no. 155), the hero does not kill the
  lioness, and Wagner notes that "the same misinterpretation appears
  again" in the analysis of Appendix II. Some fix only the scope of a
  word: the bride who disappears "forever" (*navsegda*) is in fact found
  and married.
- **Dundes's introduction** (dated Berkeley, February 1968). Propp's
  analysis is "syntagmatic", following the order of the text, and so
  "empirical and inductive" and replicable. Lévi-Strauss's is
  "paradigmatic", regrouping elements under binary oppositions, and so
  "speculative and deductive". Propp "made no attempt to relate his
  extraordinary morphology to Russian (or Indo-European) culture". Dundes
  lists open extensions: to other tale types, to non-Indo-European tales
  (Paulme on African tales, his own work on North American Indian tales),
  to epic ("the last portion of the Odyssey is strikingly similar to
  Propp's functions 23–31"), to games, literature and film. He adds that
  the scheme "has been programmed for a computer (Dundes 1965)".

## Method

As in the Russian reading: name each constant action by a noun of action,
give it a sign and subtypes, and fix it by what it leads to. The English
text keeps Propp's instruction (ch. III, note 1) to read first only "what
is printed in capital letters" to follow the thread.

## Concepts

What the English makes of the terms [NOTE-615](NOTE-615.md) renders from the
Russian:

- **сказка / волшебная сказка.** The title says "Folktale". The class the
  text studies is "fairy tales" throughout, and "the tale" stands for
  both. The record's Russian reading says "wondertale". Dundes, in the
  edition itself: "The English title Morphology of the Folktale is
  misleading. Propp limits his analysis to only one kind of folktale,
  that is, to fairy tales or Aarne-Thompson tale types 300–749."
- **действующие лица.** "Dramatis personae" in the chapter title and
  in "functions of dramatis personae". But the theses say "functions of
  characters", and new figures are "personages" ("a new personage, who
  can be termed the villain"). The English uses three words where the
  Russian reading uses one ("characters").
- **функция**: "function", defined as above.
- **недостача**: "lack". Propp's remark that Russian has no exact word
  is kept, with the Russian words in transliteration: "the terms 'lack'
  (*nedostača*) and 'insufficiency' (*nexvatka*) are not wholly
  satisfactory… The word 'shortage' (*nedostatok*) sounds better". So is
  his image: "This lack can be compared to the zero which, in a series of
  figures, represents a definite value."
- **ход**: "move", as in the Russian reading.
- **Roles named in ch. III.** Villain, donor ("or, more precisely, the
  provider"), hero, of two types ("seekers" and "victimized heroes"),
  false hero, helper, and the dispatching parents ("the tale demands
  parent-senders"). The seven spheres of action themselves are in ch.
  VI, which was not read, so the seven roles could not be compared.
- **Signs.** The English signs are Latin and Greek, and they are not the
  ones in [NOTE-615](NOTE-615.md). That note writes the struggle–victory and
  task–solution pairs with Cyrillic letters (Б–П, З–Р). In English the
  same pairs are H–I and M–N, branding is J, and "B" is mediation.
  Symbols cannot be carried from one reading to the other without a
  table.

## Connections

structural analysis of myth is held as [LIT-775](../literature.d/LIT-775.md), unread.

## Bearing on the record

- **On translation.** This edition is a worked case for the record's
  question about translation. The method, the theses and the function
  inventory come through intact. The genre term and the character term
  shift: "folktale" in the title widens the scope, which the edition's
  own introducer flags as misleading, and "dramatis personae" theatricalises
  one Russian word that the text elsewhere renders "characters". The
  apparatus grows to correct the source. That is an observation from two
  chapters, not a THEORY.
- **1958 against 1968.** The renumbering of the tales and the
  corrections of Propp's citations are Wagner's. His note says the new
  numbering is "utilized in this translation (cf. the Preface to the
  Second Edition)", so the 1958 running text very probably cites the
  tales in Propp's old numbering. That is a difference in the
  references, not in the terms, the functions or the schemes. Whether
  Wagner also revised Scott's wording could not be told without the
  1958 text.
- No THEORY is filed: [THEORY-167](../theory.d/THEORY-167.md) already holds the content, and
  the translation observation rests on too little.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- Two of nine chapters, plus Dundes's introduction. The seven roles,
  the assimilations, the tale as a whole, the appendices and both
  prefaces were not read.
- One copy is a reader's annotated photocopy and the other a retyped
  transcript with at least one duplicated paragraph. Where the scan is
  obscured, the wording is the transcript's.
- The 1958 edition was not seen, so nothing here compares 1958 with
  1968 directly.
