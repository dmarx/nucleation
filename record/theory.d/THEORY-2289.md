# THEORY-2289: The Associated Press reports that the Tempe operator, charged with negligent homicide, pleaded guilty to endangerment and was sentenced to three years of supervised probation, and that prosecutors had declined to charge Uber as a corporation

The Associated Press (2023), [LIT-3188](../literature.d/LIT-3188.md), read in [NOTE-2389](../notes.d/NOTE-2389.md); with
Wamsley (2019, [LIT-3147](../literature.d/LIT-3147.md), [NOTE-2397](../notes.d/NOTE-2397.md)) and Wamsley (2020,
[LIT-3159](../literature.d/LIT-3159.md), [NOTE-2398](../notes.d/NOTE-2398.md)).

## The account

- **March 2019.** The Yavapai County Attorney's Office, acting for
  Maricopa County because of a potential conflict of interest, found "no
  basis for criminal liability for the Uber corporation".
- **September 2020.** The operator was charged with negligent homicide
  and pleaded not guilty.
- **28 July 2023.** She "pleaded guilty ... to endangerment", an
  "undesignated felony", and was sentenced to "three years of supervised
  probation". "Prosecutors previously declined to file criminal charges
  against Uber, as a corporation."

## Bearing

- **[CLAIM-751](../claims.d/CLAIM-751.md)**: corroborates its contrast. The claim's existence
  statement rests on the NTSB report; this shows the blame-based process
  on the same failure stopping where the no-blame one did not.
  [CLAIM-1005](../claims.d/CLAIM-1005.md) files the contrast, and [CLAIM-1033](../claims.d/CLAIM-1033.md) objects that the
  contrast is between criminal law and the NTSB, not between blame and
  its absence.
- **[QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md)**: bears on, as the blame-based counterpart of the
  blameless practice the question asks about.
- **[CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md)**: completes the case's "What it cannot show" on
  prosecution (dated note there).

## What it does not show

- Why prosecutors declined to charge the company or reduced the charge.
- What the civil side did, beyond a settlement with the family reported
  in 2019.
