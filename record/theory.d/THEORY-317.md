# THEORY-317: Epistemic injustice wrongs a person in her capacity as a knower, testimonially through a hearer's prejudicially deflated credibility judgement, and hermeneutically through a gap in shared interpretive resources owed to her group's relative powerlessness, with no hearer's prejudice required

## Source

Fricker (2007), *Epistemic Injustice*, [LIT-1004](../literature.d/LIT-1004.md), through her "Précis",
*Theoria* 23(1), 2008, pp. 69–71, read in full in [NOTE-832](../notes.d/NOTE-832.md). The book
was not read.

## What was actually shown

The précis states the account and argues one part of it.

**The generic wrong.** A speaker who receives "a prejudicially deflated
degree of credibility from a hearer is thereby wronged—he is wronged
specifically in his capacity as a knower" (p. 69). The précis argues for
the ethical side only by contrast with the epistemic one. The epistemic
fault is that "truths fail to flow from knower to inquirer". The précis
adds: "But this is not all."

**Testimonial injustice** is that deflation, caused by "prejudice on the
hearer's part". Example: a jury that disbelieves someone "simply because
of the colour of his skin" (p. 69).

**Hermeneutical injustice** occurs "at a prior stage". Someone trying "to
make sense of a social experience" is handicapped by "a hermeneutical
lacuna whose existence is owing to the relative powerlessness of a social
group to which the subject belongs" (pp. 69–70). The lacuna makes the
collective resources "structurally prejudiced". No individual hearer's
prejudice is involved. Example: homosexual desire where gay people "were
prevented from making a full contribution to collective resources for
social meaning". The wrong is "situated hermeneutical inequality".

**Each has a corrective virtue of the hearer.** Testimonial justice
pre-empts the wrong. Hermeneutical justice can only ameliorate it after
the fact (p. 70). Both are argued to be at once intellectual and ethical,
because a virtue is individuated by its immediate end, and each virtue has
the same immediate end under both descriptions (pp. 70–71).

What could have shown otherwise is not tested in the précis. It is a
conceptual account with two examples, offered in summary.

## What this does not say

> **2026-10-14, against a later source.** Fricker's 2006 paper
> [LIT-1046](../literature.d/LIT-1046.md) ([NOTE-859](../notes.d/NOTE-859.md)), which says it is drawn from the book's
> last chapter, defines hermeneutical injustice generically through
> "hermeneutical marginalization", systematic or incidental. On that
> text the first bullet below describes only the systematic case, and
> [THEORY-326](THEORY-326.md) is the rival statement. The book would settle which is
> Fricker's definition.

- **Not that every conceptual gap is an injustice.** The gap must be owed
  to the relative powerlessness of a social group to which the subject
  belongs, and it must bear on making sense of a social experience. A gap
  owed to novelty, to an individual's idiosyncrasy, or to a design choice
  that falls on no group, does not meet the précis's definition.
- **Not that every credibility deficit is testimonial injustice.** The
  deficit must be owed to the hearer's prejudice. An honest mistake about
  someone's reliability is not covered.
- **Not "identity prejudice".** The précis says "prejudice". If the book
  narrows it to prejudice about social identity, this account should be
  narrowed with it.
- **Not a reading of the book.** Its cases, its treatment of credibility
  excess and its arguments are not known here. The account is held at the
  strength of the author's own summary.
- **The hybridity argument is not filed.** No question in the record asks
  whether a virtue can be both intellectual and ethical.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md), part 2.** The question asks whether a project memory's
  failure to file a contributor's move would be a wrong in Fricker's
  sense or "only a bias in what persists". On this account it is a wrong
  only if two things hold. The gap in the record's grammar must be owed
  to the relative powerlessness of a social group the contributor belongs
  to, and it must bear on making sense of that contributor's social
  experience. An owner and an assistant are not obviously social groups
  in this sense. So the answer to part 2 is a definitional condition, not
  a verdict. Part 1, whether the asymmetry occurs, is untouched. If it
  occurs and is caused by prejudice in whoever files, it is the
  testimonial form, not the hermeneutical one.
- **[NOTE-098](../notes.d/NOTE-098.md)**, the record's reading of Leonelli, glosses hermeneutical
  injustice as "marginalisation of ways of knowing to the point of
  unintelligibility". That is wider than the précis, which ties it to
  making sense of a social experience and to a group's powerlessness in
  shaping the shared resources. The gloss is Leonelli's use, not
  Fricker's definition.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

The book ([LIT-1004](../literature.d/LIT-1004.md)) is still unread, and the promote_when says secondary
summaries cannot settle it. Maitra ([LIT-tmp8pt4c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8pt4c.md), read in [NOTE-tmp8uvyb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8uvyb.md))
reports the book, with its pages, as differing from the précis on both of
the promote_when's points: testimonial injustice needs identity prejudice
(book pp. 35, 42), and hermeneutical injustice is defined through
hermeneutical marginalization, which may be "systematic, or incidental"
(pp. 153, 158). With Fricker's own 2006 paper ([THEORY-326](THEORY-326.md)), two sources
now place the book's definitions where this account's caveats expected a
narrowing. If a reading of the book confirms them, this account is refuted
as a statement of Fricker, as its promote_when says.
