# CLAIM-348: No coarse-graining is normatively adequate in general because it preserves stability, prediction or aggregate performance: a description adequate for an institution's performance can merge states its members' claims must distinguish, so adequacy is fixed by the evaluative target, and the target cannot be justified by the description that preserves it

## The claim

A21 §18, Candidate 2: "A macro-description can preserve a functional or
normative judgment for one specified target while failing to preserve
another. Therefore no coarse-graining may be treated as generally
normatively adequate solely because it preserves organizational stability,
predictive accuracy, or aggregate performance." A21 gave its warrant as
"Demonstrable by finite counterexample, with an interpretive philosophical
conclusion".

The argument:

1. A coarse-graining preserves a deterministic judgment exactly when the
   judgment is constant on its fibres ([CLAIM-350](CLAIM-350.md)).
2. Which partition is sufficient depends on the target ([CLAIM-152](CLAIM-152.md)). Stability,
   prediction and aggregate performance are targets too: the whole's.
3. A whole's success does not establish its constituents' well-being
   ([CLAIM-060](CLAIM-060.md)). So the whole's judgments and a member's can have different
   level sets.
4. [CASE-110](../cases.d/CASE-110.md) builds such a pair. Total output decides an institution's
   performance and its survival, and merges a state in which a member works
   within an agreed cap with one in which that member works far past it.
5. So preserving the whole's stability, prediction or performance is no
   evidence that a description preserves a member's claims. Adequacy is
   settled one target at a time.

A21 §5 adds the clause that keeps this from becoming circular: "**The correct
coarse-graining depends on the evaluative target; the evaluative target must
not be justified solely by the coarse-graining that preserves it.**" A21:
"That second clause prevents a circular account of normativity." Choosing
the institution's description because it is the dynamically relevant one,
then judging by what that description keeps, would justify the
institution's standard by the institution's description. A21 §5's example
is "an institution whose aggregate performance remains unchanged when one
member is subjected to an unjust procedure", which A21 calls "a direct
challenge to any proposed criterion that simply treats the most dynamically
relevant scale as the normatively privileged scale."

A21 §5 also separates this from renormalization: "Normative relevance is
different. A microscopic feature can be ethically decisive even if it has
almost no effect on a macroscopic dynamical observable." RG relevance is a
target-free criterion of the kind the defeat condition names.

## What it does not say

- **Which target is right.** It says the description cannot decide. Whether
  the member's claim should constrain the institution is [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md)'s open
  ground and [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)'s question.
- **That institutional descriptions are worthless.** C is adequate for the
  institution's judgments, and the claim depends on that.
- **That there is no adequate coarse-graining.** One fitted to both targets
  exists in the case (C′), and the identity always does. The claim is about
  adequacy in general, chosen without a target.
- **That the whole and its members always come apart.** A member's claim
  that bears on the whole's persistence is preserved by any description
  sufficient for persistence. The case needs the claim to be idle for the
  whole.
- **That the logical point is new.** It is [CLAIM-152](CLAIM-152.md) and [CLAIM-060](CLAIM-060.md) together.
  What the exchange added is the coarse-graining form and the clause against
  circularity.
