# NOTE-tmpeop9z: Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems

## Contribution

A conceptual distinction that ecology lacked. Persistence is a different
property from return to equilibrium, it is located at the boundary of a
domain of attraction rather than at its centre, and it can be undermined by
the very management that maximises constancy.

## Key insight

How fast a system returns to its equilibrium tells you nothing about how
close it is to the edge beyond which it will not return. Optimising the
first can wear away the second.

## Assumptions

- Ecological systems are typically far from equilibrium and driven by
  random events, so equilibrium-centred analysis misses what matters for
  persistence.
- Realistic population models have lags, nonlinear functional responses,
  contagious attack and reproduction thresholds, which create bounded
  domains of attraction.

## Key results

- **Definitions.** Resilience "determines the persistence of relationships
  within a system" and measures their ability "to absorb changes of state
  variables, driving variables, and parameters, and still persist".
  Stability is "the ability of a system to return to an equilibrium state
  after a temporary disturbance".
- **Cases.** A small Italian crater lake that kept one state for millennia and
  flipped after the Via Cassia was built (after Hutchinson), Great Lakes fisheries, spruce budworm
  cycles, salmon runs jumping between levels, fire-driven forest sequences.
- **Trade-off.** Variable environments give low stability with high
  resilience, and homogeneous ones the reverse.
- **Measures.** Resilience by the area of the domain of attraction and the
  height of its lowest boundary point; stability by slopes and speeds near
  the equilibrium.

## Concepts

- **domain of attraction** — the region of state space from which
  trajectories return to a given equilibrium or cycle.
- **resilience** — the persistence of a system's relationships under change;
  a property of the domain, not of the equilibrium.
- **stability** — speed and smoothness of return to equilibrium.

## Connections

Builds on May's stability analyses and on Holling's own predation models,
and is the source of the resilience literature that Scheffer & Carpenter
([LIT-tmpak5ia](../literature.d/LIT-tmpak5ia.md)) work in.

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-tmp3clxf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp3clxf.md). Holling's resilience is about staying within a
domain of attraction. Aubin's viability ([THEORY-tmp4t87t](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4t87t.md)) is about staying
within a constraint set given apart from the dynamics. The two are not the
same: a regime can be resilient (hard to dislodge) while its constraint set
is being violated, which is the owner's protective lock-in. For the essay's
§6.1, Holling is the earlier and more widely cited source of "stability is
not persistence", and Aubin is the formal machinery.

## Limitations

Case histories, not controlled tests. The OCR text is poor, and the
quantitative measures are proposed rather than applied.

## Open questions

How to measure resilience in the field before it is lost, a problem
Scheffer and colleagues later took up as early warning signals.
