# NOTE-tmpvvxmy: Caruso — Skepticism About Moral Responsibility (SEP)

## Contribution

A survey, by one of the skeptics, of the arguments for and implications
of denying basic-desert moral responsibility. For the record it supplies
three things it lacked: Pereboom's definition of basic desert in his
words; hard incompatibilism set out as a case against each rival; and the
manipulation argument, with Pereboom's Case 2 quoted and the hard-line and
soft-line replies weighed. It also reports Levy's luck argument against
historical compatibilism.

## Key insight

Hard incompatibilism is an argument by elimination. Determination by
natural causes is no better than determination by manipulators, which is
the four-case argument. Event-causal indeterminism leaves the agent unable
to settle what happens, which is luck. Agent causation would do, but
there is no evidence for it. The four-case argument's force comes from the
soft-liner's predicament: any difference named between a manipulated and
an ordinary determined agent can be written into a new case.

## Assumptions

- **Basic desert is the target.** "For an agent to be morally
  responsible for an action in this sense is for it to be hers in such a
  way that she would deserve to be blamed if she understood that it was
  morally wrong … just because she has performed the action … and not,
  for example, merely by virtue of consequentialist or contractualist
  considerations" (Pereboom 2014a: 2, quoted in §1).
- **Free will is defined by responsibility.** Free will is "the control in
  action needed for moral responsibility" (§2). Fischer's
  semi-compatibilism is noted as an exception.
- **Survey stance.** The entry reports and does not adjudicate, except in
  §3, where it argues the skeptic's side.

## Key results

- **What survives skepticism (§1).** Attributability, answerability
  (Scanlon, Bok), grounded by Pereboom in "future protection, future
  reconciliation, and future moral formation", and Waller's take-charge
  responsibility.
- **Hard incompatibilism (§2.2).** "Whatever the fundamental nature of
  reality, whether it is deterministic or indeterministic, we lack basic
  desert moral responsibility." Against compatibilism: "no relevant
  difference between this prospect and our actions being causally
  determined by manipulators". Against event-causal libertarianism: the
  luck or "disappearing agent" objection. Agent-causal libertarianism
  "cannot be reconciled with our best physical theories".
- **The four-case argument (§2.2).** It "sets out three examples of
  actions that involve manipulation, the first of which features the most
  radical sort of manipulation consistent with all the leading
  compatibilist conditions, each progressively more like the fourth,
  which is an ordinary case of action causally determined in a natural
  way". The challenge is "a relevant and principled difference between
  any two adjacent cases". Case 2, quoted (Pereboom 2014a: 77): Plum is
  "programmed … at the beginning on his life so that his reasoning is
  often but not always egoistic", so that "he is causally determined to
  engage in the egoistic reasons-responsive process of deliberation and to
  have the set of first and second-order desires that result in his
  decision to kill White. Plum has the general ability to regulate his
  actions by moral reasons … Yet he does not decide as he does because of
  an irresistible desire." The conclusion: "it is simply irrelevant
  whether Plum's psychological states ultimately trace back to intentional
  agents or non-intentional causes".
- **Replies (§2.2).** Hard-line replies accept Plum's responsibility; the
  worry is conflict with intuition. Soft-line replies find a difference;
  the worry is that it "may be a difference that applies only to current
  manipulation cases", since agentless cases now exist and any named
  condition invites a revised vignette. "The challenge … is to show that
  there is some kind of requirement … that can be satisfied by agents in
  deterministic settings but which cannot (in principle) be satisfied by
  agents in manipulation cases." Deery and Nahmias (2017) is named as an
  attempt, and Capes as a reply.
- **The Basic Argument (§2.3).** Strawson: responsibility for action
  requires responsibility for how one is, which requires an infinite
  regress of chosen principles of choice. The objections are listed.
- **The Luck Pincer (§2.4).** Levy: every act is constitutively lucky,
  presently lucky or both, and each negates responsibility. Against
  *historical* compatibilism (Mele; Fischer and Ravizza), "the series of
  actions through which agents shape and modify their endowments … are
  themselves significantly subject to present luck … 'we cannot undo the
  effects of luck with more luck'". So "the actions whereby agents take
  responsibility for their endowment, either express that endowment …
  or reflect the agent's present luck, or both". Against non-historical
  accounts, the epistemic condition is demanding and itself lucky.
- **Scientific challenges (§2.5).** The Libet findings are widely
  rejected as an argument, including by skeptics. Situationism and
  automaticity suggest "shrinking agency".
- **Implications (§3).** Illusionism (Smilansky) is set against
  disillusionism. Pereboom replaces resentment and indignation with
  "moral concern, disappointment, sorrow, and resolve", and keeps
  axiological morality with an "ought of axiological recommendation".
  The quarantine model justifies incapacitation by the right of
  self-defence, constrained by least harm and a duty to rehabilitate.

## Concepts

- **Basic desert moral responsibility.** As quoted from Pereboom above.
- **Hard incompatibilism.** No basic desert whether determinism is true or
  false.
- **Hard-line and soft-line replies.** McKenna's terms: accepting the
  manipulated agent's responsibility, or finding a relevant difference.
- **Constitutive and present luck.** Luck in the agent's endowment, and
  luck in circumstances at or near the time of action (Levy).
- **Historical and non-historical compatibilism.** Whether how the agent
  came to be as she is matters to her responsibility.

## Limitations

- A survey by an advocate. Caruso co-writes with Pereboom, and §3 argues
  the skeptic's case.
- Only Case 2 is quoted. Cases 1, 3 and 4 are summarised in one sentence,
  in the 2014 wording, not the 2001 wording.
- Levy's argument is a paragraph-length report, and the replies (Talbert,
  Hartman, Hales) are only named.
- Published 2018, so it does not cover the 2020s literature on
  manipulation that [NOTE-tmp5wtbq](NOTE-tmp5wtbq.md) does.

## Open questions

- Whether a soft-line condition exists that manipulated agents cannot
  meet "in principle". The entry names Deery and Nahmias (2017) and does
  not assess it.
- Whether Levy's present-luck point applies under determinism, where
  "present luck" must be read as non-chancy.
