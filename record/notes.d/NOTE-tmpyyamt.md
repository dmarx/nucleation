# NOTE-tmpyyamt: Natali, Naiseh, Cabitza and Frischmann — Better AI with Designed Friction

## Contribution

It names and gathers a field, "frictional AI", for the hybrid human-AI
intelligence community, and sets an agenda for it. It contributes no
model or evidence of its own. Its use to the record is as a compact
statement of what "designed friction" means in human-AI interaction.

## Key insight

The friction proposed is friction on the user's thinking at the point of
interaction: it makes a person deliberate before accepting an output. The
organizational setting that fixes the person's authority, time and
incentives is outside the frame.

## Assumptions

- Overreliance (automation bias) is the main harm of frictionless AI, and
  it is a property of the individual user.
- Friction placed at the point of interaction is the lever.
- More deliberation by the user yields "accountability"; whose, and to
  whom, is not said.

## Key results

- **Definition** (abstract, p. 518): "designed friction—deliberate
  inefficiencies designed to foster engagement, critical reasoning, and
  user agency".
- **The problem** (§1, p. 518): "this frictionless approach can lead to
  automation bias—where users uncritically accept AI recommendations".
- **Genre** (§1, p. 518): "In our poster, we present designed friction".
- **The design problem** (§1, p. 518): "The challenge lies in calibrating
  friction effectively to enhance decision-making without causing undue
  frustration."
- **Mechanisms** (§2, p. 519): "Cognitive forcing functions can involve
  checklists, justification prompts, and alternative hypothesis
  generation"; in AI provocations, "systems challenge rather than obey the
  user, prompting them to justify their own AI-assisted decisions".
- **Culture** (§3, p. 519): people in low-context cultures "may perceive
  additional verification steps as bureaucratic barriers".
- **Claimed effects** (§4, p. 519): "frictional AI fosters deliberation,
  strengthens accountability, and preserves human agency by preventing
  passive automation reliance"; the community "can establish a culture of
  designer accountability".

## Concepts

Designed friction; frictional AI; prosocial friction (Frischmann and
Vardi); programmed inefficiencies (Cabitza et al.); cognitive forcing
functions (Buçinca et al., [LIT-2283](../literature.d/LIT-2283.md), the paper's reference [3]); seamful
design; microboundaries; AI provocations; automation bias; deskilling;
designer accountability.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmp238zy](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp238zy.md)** (cognitive friction differs from friction that makes
  the institution answerable). Corroborates its premise. Every mechanism
  the paper names acts on the user's thinking; none gives the user
  authority to refuse, suspend or escalate. The paper also shows the
  slippage the claim warns of: friction "strengthens accountability"
  without saying whose or to whom, and its one institutional term,
  "designer accountability", concerns designers, not answerability to the
  people affected. It is evidence of the framing the claim describes, not
  an argument against it.
- **[LIT-2283](../literature.d/LIT-2283.md)** (cognitive forcing functions) is the paper's reference [3],
  treated as one form of designed friction.
- Bainbridge's "Ironies of automation", its reference [2], is held
  ([LIT-2222](../literature.d/LIT-2222.md), [LIT-2225](../literature.d/LIT-2225.md)); [LIT-2224](../literature.d/LIT-2224.md) (AI assistance and skill decay) bears on
  its agenda item on skill retention.
- Frischmann and Benesch, "Friction-in-design regulation" (2023), the
  regulatory form of the idea, is cited and not held; it may be worth
  registering beside [CLAIM-tmp238zy](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp238zy.md).

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Partly faithful. A21 says the work "develops the idea of deliberately
introducing friction into AI systems to preserve critical reasoning and
human agency". The content is right: the abstract names "critical
reasoning, and user agency". "Develops" overstates a four-page poster that
defines the idea, surveys earlier work and lists questions; it neither
builds nor tests it.

## Limitations

A poster abstract with no empirical work and no worked theory. It gives no
account of when friction helps or hurts beyond "calibration". The
cross-cultural point is a hypothesis ("may perceive"). Its accountability
claims are stated, not argued.
