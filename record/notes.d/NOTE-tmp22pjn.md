# NOTE-tmp22pjn: Ostrom, Gibson, Shivakumar and Andersson — Aid, incentives and sustainability

## Contribution

An institutional (IAD) account of how outside supply changes the incentives
of the organization that receives it. Development is framed as solving
collective-action problems, the deficit as "missing institutions" rather
than missing money (Exec. Summ.). Aid enters an eight-actor system, the
"Octangle", that "lacks important error-correcting mechanisms", so an
incentive problem in one part spreads to the others. The mechanism the
report names is upkeep rather than formation: a recipient that expects the
supplier always to repair or replace has no reason to maintain.

## Key insight

The supplier's predictability is itself the cause of the dependence. In the
Samaritan's dilemma the donor helps whatever the recipient does, the
recipient therefore puts in low effort, and repeated, "the recipient loses
skill and motivation over time" (p. 31). What breaks it is not more help or
less, but a recipient with its own stake in the outcome: its own demand, its
own contribution, its own benefit and a share in deciding whether to stop.

## Assumptions

- Development is institutions that solve collective-action problems;
  material transfers matter only through the incentives they create.
- Actors respond to perceived rewards and penalties; donor staff are mostly
  well-motivated but constrained.
- The Samaritan cares and cannot commit to withholding help; that is the
  source of the dilemma.
- The games are ordinal and illustrative; no parameters are estimated.
- The evidence is one donor, Sida, around 2000: a literature review, 175-odd
  interviews (63 structured, with Sida staff) and five field cases in Zambia
  (aid-dependent) and India (less so), about ten person-days a site (p. 332),
  chosen "based on our early perceptions of their illustrative use" (p. xix).

## Key results

- **The Samaritan's dilemma** (§2.4.4, Fig. 2.3, pp. 30–31, after Buchanan
  1977; ordinal payoffs No Help 2,2 / 1,1, Help 4,3 / 3,4). The Samaritan's
  "dominant strategy is always to extend help"; the recipient's "best
  response is to expend a low level of effort". It is, they say, a
  problem of motivation, not of information. Repeated, "the recipient is
  actually in the more powerful position."
- **Upkeep of infrastructure** (pp. 31–32, citing a NORAD staffer via
  Catterson and Lindahl). Power stations "can last fifty to sixty years, if
  limited but constant efforts are made to maintain them", but "major
  rehabilitations are needed every ten to fifteen years". "It is the
  recipient whose actions make the difference in outcomes from sustainable
  to nonsustainable." Two remedies: "A more sophisticated Samaritan might
  try to condition their aid on meaningful participation by the recipient",
  or joint ownership.
- **Moral hazard against own revenue** (Exec. Summ., p. xviii): aid "helps
  to ensure incompetent governments from the results of their actions, thus
  weakening their incentive to find alternative revenue sources or better
  policies."
- **Accountability** (§4.2.2, p. 66): dependence on aid "reduces the
  accountability of the recipient government to its own people, since the
  leadership is not fiscally accountable to them."
- **Crowding-out and promises** (§5.2, pp. 82–83): aid "can crowd out
  domestic sources of funds", with contested magnitudes in footnote 2; "So
  long as donor countries are willing to lend funds, the recipient can get by
  with promises of commitments".
- **Bought conditionality** (§6.1, pp. 91–92, after Collier): where donors
  have bought reforms with programme aid, "they become, in effect, the
  owners";
  "In poor policy environments, therefore, aid can delay reform."
- **Perquisites** (§6.4.1, p. 104): project cars and equipment "can create
  an interest in prolonging the project".
- **Design against dependence** (KTS, p. 115): Sida requires local cost
  sharing against moral hazard and "limits the duration and financial volume
  of KTS projects, with the aim of avoiding aid dependency"; dispersed
  ownership "can compromise such incentive effects and lead to
  sustainability failures" (p. 116).
- **Sustainability defined** (§1.2.4, pp. 7–9): "the longevity of development
  cooperation's effects, rather than particular projects or activities",
  which "frees one from a sense that every project should be continued
  indefinitely".
- **Four dimensions of ownership** (§1.4.3, pp. 15–16): provision,
  production (contributions are "a costly signal"), consumption and
  alienation, the decision to continue or not. If the donor pays full costs,
  the implementing agency "becomes more of an owner than the beneficiary".
  "the expected situation at the end of the project should be a focus of its
  design in the first place"; terminate a project when, among other things,
  it fosters "an attitude of dependence". Ownership is "a necessary, though
  not sufficient, condition for aid sustainability".
- **Cernea's audit** (fn. 6, p. 12): "12 of the 25 projects actually achieved
  long-term sustainability", and "all of the sustainable projects involved
  very specific efforts to enhance the institutional capacities of
  beneficiaries".
- **Kafue Gorge, Zambia** (pp. 224–228): about SEK 300m from Sida 1970–94;
  "the government has continuously underfunded its maintenance" and donors
  stepped in; "GRZ remains completely dependent on outside funds for the
  continued operation of the KGS", and Sida's help "has not generated a way
  out of this dependence". One margin moved: a grant on-lent to the utility
  as a loan left it more motivated (p. 226).
- **Electricity Regulatory Board, Zambia** (pp. 222–223): SEK 7m over four
  years; once donors leave, "it is unlikely that this type of survival would
  include its effectiveness". A form can outlast its function.
- **Conservation Farming Unit, Zambia** (pp. 234–238): "The CFU is not
  self-sustaining and most likely will never be"; "we found no plans to have
  the farmers themselves pay"; yet its impacts may last if farmers' incomes
  rise. Results can outlast the organization.
- **Orissa forestry, India** (§9.3.3, pp. 195–196): the Forest Department's
  incentives are "to receive aid but not to reform"; its officials argue
  bureaucracies "slip back into passivity in the absence of an outside
  stimulus".
- **The donor's side** (Exec. Summ., pp. xxi–xxiv; Ch. 7 not read): about
  four-year postings, 47% with no contact with earlier projects, rising
  temporary staff, 2% saying promotion depends on past project performance,
  fourth-quarter disbursement peaks; "We found no examples of Sida having
  objected to receiving more funds because it compromised prospects for
  sustainability". Funding is "invariably de-linked from the sustainability
  of funded efforts. There are presently no institutions or competitive
  mechanisms that calibrate and reward the efficient delivery of sustainable
  aid" (p. 241).
- **Hardware** (§11.3, p. 242): "Providing infrastructure or hardware with no
  realistic plan of maintenance on the part of the recipient is a sure
  recipe for poor sustainability and donor dependence".
- **Ch. 11 recommendations** (pp. 239–252): the four ownership criteria;
  "the greater the number of owners, the smaller the stakes"; mid-term
  evaluations including beneficiaries, since "no effective demand exists for
  meaningful evaluations" (p. 247); rewards for sustainable projects;
  beneficiaries hiring consultants; untied aid; training recipients' own
  scholars in institutional analysis.
- **Appendix B, game theory** (pp. 302–314): the dilemma "arises as the Nash
  equilibrium of a repeated game" (the "Bad Folk Theorem"); a credible
  threat of withdrawal would neutralize it, but "recipient countries know
  that the threat is not credible", since for a power station "the donor
  country must be willing to let the capital stock deteriorate". Large
  capital projects "are ripe for generating aid dependence", and collusion
  of local officials and in-country donor staff "is a mechanism for
  supporting aid-dependency in the long run" (p. 311).

## Concepts

- **Samaritan's dilemma**: a carer who cannot commit to withholding help
  meets a recipient whose best reply is low effort (Buchanan).
- **Soft budget constraint, moral hazard, adverse selection, principal–agent
  chains**, with efficiency falling as layers are added (p. 307).
- **Fungibility and crowding-out**: aid replacing rather than adding to the
  recipient's own spending.
- **The Octangle**: donor government, aid agency, other donors, recipient
  government, sectoral agency, contractors, civil society on both sides, and
  beneficiaries.
- **Ownership**: provision, production, consumption and alienation, all four
  needed by beneficiaries.
- **Sustainability of effects** as against of projects or activities.
- **Bad Folk Theorem; credible withdrawal; aid tournaments** (Appendix B).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md)** (supplied maintenance): supports it in a social setting and
  qualifies a premise. Kafue Gorge is supply from outside with a recipient
  "completely dependent on outside funds for the continued operation", a
  rebuild every ten to fifteen years against a fifty-to-sixty-year maintained
  life. But the supply is not exogenous: its predictability lowers the
  recipient's own repair effort, so it suppresses the organization's own
  maintenance rather than only failing to build it.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md)** (independence from the establishing intervention): the
  report's answer is beneficiary ownership, with contribution, benefit and a
  say over continuation; results can outlive the organization (CFU) and a
  form its function (ERB). Short qualitative cases; no threshold.
- **[QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md)** (a productive nucleus becoming self-maintaining): partial.
  CFU's results last only if farmers' incomes rise, the activity feeding the
  resources that sustain it. No threshold or rate.
- **[CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md)** (assemble / persist / regenerate): consistent. Hardware
  assembles and persists (HVDC); the recipient's own regeneration is absent
  (Kafue). Sustainability of effects rather than of projects is a distinction the
  claim does not draw.
- **[CLAIM-516](../claims.d/CLAIM-516.md)** (turnover with production only by others): the social
  analogue, without a formal model.
- **[CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md)** (capabilities decay with disuse): supports it qualitatively,
  on both sides (the recipient's skill; the donor's memory under rotation);
  no decay rates.
- **[QUESTION-386](../questions.d/QUESTION-386.md)** (funding by observed activity): strongly relevant.
  Disbursement is rewarded, sustained results are not, and cheap,
  hard-to-observe upkeep and institution-building lose out.
- **[TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md)** (resource types): expenditure ceilings and annual
  disbursement deadlines are said to "favor rigid patterns of disbursement"
  (p. xxiv), bounding different things.
- **[CLAIM-520](../claims.d/CLAIM-520.md)** (keeping a corrective capacity): evaluations are the
  system's corrective capacity, and the case for mid-term over end-of-project
  evaluations is that they can still change decisions.
- **[CLAIM-598](../claims.d/CLAIM-598.md)** (uniform abundance): loosely supports it; more money with
  fixed staff worsens monitoring, and funds into a rent-seeking bureaucracy
  feed rent seeking.
- **[TERM-117](../terms.d/TERM-117.md)** (productive nucleus): the ERB and CFU are candidates whose
  influence is not yet self-maintained.
- **[TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md), [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md)**: analogy only. Kafue Gorge is supplied order, like
  the heat under a convection cell; ownership by one party in all four
  dimensions reads like closure of the constraints that keep a project going,
  an interpretive reading.
- Produces [THEORY-tmph35ra](../theory.d/THEORY-tmph35ra.md). Not an instruction for machine-learning
  practice.

## Limitations

- Five illustrative cases, about ten person-days a site, several incomplete
  when studied (Orissa Phase I; the HVDC terminal too new).
- No counterfactual or identification; sustainability is forecast from an
  incentive analysis, not observed after exit.
- Interview percentages rest on 63 structured interviews, some random, some
  chosen by Sida's evaluation unit; Chs. 7–8 were not read here.
- The games are ordinal and illustrative ("theorists' contrivance", p. 313).
- Fungibility evidence is mixed by the authors' own account.
- One donor around 2000; no threshold, rate or timing for handover.
