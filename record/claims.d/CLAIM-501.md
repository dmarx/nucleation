# CLAIM-501: Gutt's interpretive resemblance also predicts that judged pragmatic observables beat text similarity, so the central theses' defeat conditions cannot tell the thesis from a relevance-theoretic account either

## The claim

[CLAIM-233](CLAIM-233.md)'s version 2 named Gutt's interpretive resemblance among the rival
accounts that predict a pass on the central defeat test ([CASE-184](../cases.d/CASE-184.md)). The record
reads the account in Gutt's 1989 thesis (LIT-tmp1eyaz, NOTE-tmpfykfi). Two
utterances interpretively resemble each other "to the degree that the sets
of assumptions which each conveys in its own cognitive environment overlap,
that is, ... the degree to which they share their explicatures and/or
implicatures" (pp. 74–75). "Identity in propositional form between two
utterances is not a sufficient condition for identity in their
interpretations" (p. 72). A report that changes the words can resemble the
original more closely than a repetition of them (pp. 73–74). The
interpretation a translation is to keep includes irony (pp. 215–217),
intimacy (pp. 165–167), register (pp. 238–240) and the force of a greeting
(pp. 229–232). Even direct translation fixes its clues by their effect on
interpretation, not by their form (pp. 253–255). Judged stance and footing
are nearer to what the account preserves than embedding similarity is. On
renderings built to dissociate the two, it predicts the pass.

## What it does not say

- That Gutt predicts anything about human ratings. The thesis tests nothing
  on readers, and the prediction is the record's inference from the account.
- That context is idle. Fidelity is relative to a cognitive environment: the
  receptors' for indirect translation, and the original's for direct
  translation (pp. 164, 254). What the account predicts for a rater depends
  on the environment the rater brings.
- That the 1991 book ([LIT-784](../literature.d/LIT-784.md)), unread, keeps these definitions. The thesis
  is skimmed, and pp. 258–298, on partial resemblance and failure, are
  unread.
