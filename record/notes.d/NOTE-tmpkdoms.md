# NOTE-tmpkdoms: Strand and Burke (2012) — Polydnaviruses as Symbionts and Gene Delivery Systems

## Contribution

Polydnaviruses (PDVs) are viruses carried by parasitoid wasps. In bracoviruses the provirus sits in every wasp cell; it replicates only in calyx cells of the female ovary, and the virions are injected with the eggs into a caterpillar host, where their genes are expressed but never replicate. The genome has two parts in different places in the wasp genome: core replication genes (inherited from a nudivirus ancestor, vertically transmitted, never packaged) and packaged DNA segments carrying virulence genes that immunosuppress the host and alter its growth. Ichnoviruses have similar life cycles but no shared core genes: convergence.

## Key results

- PDV gene products "immunocompromise the host, which prevents it from killing the wasp's offspring, and 2) they alter host metabolism and growth, which promotes wasp development and ultimately causes the host to die"; "PDVs never replicate in the wasp's host."
- "Strikingly, the location of the core genes in the wasp genome differs from the location of the proviral DNAs packaged into virions."
- Origin: "BVs thus likely evolved from a nudivirus that established a latent infection in the common ancestor of microgastrine braconids"; microgastroids "evolved 100 million years ago" and number "more than 20,000 species".
- Replication of packaged DNAs "has shifted from control by viral core genes to control by the DNA replication machinery of the wasp".
- Convergence: "the similar life cycles of BVs and IVs reflect convergent evolution driven by the analogous roles they play in parasitism of hosts by wasps."
- "BVs today are obligate beneficial symbionts, which persist entirely from a proviral genome yet produce virions that efficiently deliver genes to other organisms wasps depend upon for survival."

- **QUESTION-277**: **bears directly**: a route to dependence through gene transfer into the host genome with control of replication handed to host machinery, plus vertical transmission, giving full reproductive cohesion between wasp and virus; and a shared origin (100 million years, one ancestral virus) that QUESTION-277's "alignment of selection" item needs.
