# LIT-628: Homeostatic feelings and the biology of consciousness

Antonio Damasio and Hanna Damasio (2022), *Brain* 145(7):2231–2235, an
Essay — DOI 10.1093/brain/awac194

## Key takeaways

- **What consciousness is.** It occurs when the contents of mind are
  "spontaneously identified as belonging to a specific owner": our own
  organism, located in its body. It "should not be confused with the mere
  integration of images, or with the scale of that integration, or with the
  attention accorded to some images in detriment of others."
- **Sensing is not minding.** Bacteria and plants sense and respond, with no
  nervous system and no representations. Minds are streams of mapped images
  built by nervous systems: mostly exteroceptive, and a less dominant
  interoceptive stream of homeostatic feelings.
- **The hypothesis.** The continuous flow of homeostatic feelings, salient
  (hunger, pain, malaise) or close to equilibrium (feelings of existence),
  identifies the mind with its organism. Each feeling "is itself
  spontaneously and automatically conscious", and feelings "lend"
  consciousness to exteroceptive images, which need feelings present to be
  conscious. Feelings were "the inaugural phenomena of consciousness",
  selected because they gave overt, not only covert, guidance of life
  regulation.
- **The mechanism.** Interoceptive axons are not myelinated, make frequent
  non-synaptic contacts and lack a systematic blood–brain barrier (Table 1),
  so viscera and circulating molecules act directly on the neurons that map
  them. Map and object "commingle", which no exteroceptive map can do with
  a landscape. That is why feelings are "hybrid".
- **Loss of consciousness.** Small lesions of the posterior brainstem
  (nucleus tractus solitarius, parabrachial nucleus, periaqueductal grey),
  where body signals are first integrated, cause coma, while "even extensive
  lesions of the cerebral cortex may not". General anaesthetics act on the
  membrane-level "sensing and detecting" below feeling, which is why they
  also halt plant activity.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request. No anthology topic holds a
theory of consciousness grounded in interoception, and the essay carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice. Its last claim, that
consciousness "cannot be found in inanimate objects, regardless of how
complex they may be", bears on machine consciousness only as a denial.

**It is the readable form of [LIT-387](LIT-387.md).** The Damasios' Neural Computation
paper (2023, Deferred, unread) has an abstract that states this essay's
opening summary nearly sentence for sentence: the identification of
contents with an owner, the two kinds of homeostatic feeling, the
inaugural-phenomena claim, the two-way interaction and the hybrid. The
Neural Computation paper was received in January 2022, before this essay,
so the two are parallel statements, not one building on the other. A
reader of this essay has the thesis of [LIT-387](LIT-387.md), but not whatever argument
or evidence that paper adds. The same authors also published
"Homeostatic Feelings and the Emergence of Consciousness" (Journal of
Cognitive Neuroscience 36(8), 2024), which the record does not hold.

**It carries forward Damasio and Carvalho (2013), [LIT-388](LIT-388.md) ([NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)),** to
consciousness. That paper made feeling the felt read-out of interoceptive
maps, placed the first whole-body maps in the upper brainstem and proposed
unmyelinated fibres as the root of sentience. This essay keeps all three
and adds two things: that feelings are what make any content conscious,
and that the unmyelinated, barrier-free physiology makes feeling a hybrid of
body and map rather than a map. It does not cite the 2013 paper (it cites
Carvalho and Damasio's 2021 Bioessays synthesis and Parvizi and Damasio on
brainstem coma), so no `extends` is declared.

**It rivals IIT 3.0, [LIT-445](LIT-445.md).** The essay names "integration of
information" (citing Tononi et al. 2016) among the processes that
prominent solutions study in place of subjectivity, and says consciousness
should not be confused with the integration of images. IIT identifies
consciousness with integrated information in any physical substrate; this
essay ties it to living bodies with nervous systems and denies it to
inanimate objects of any complexity. Each account excludes the other's,
and neither tests the other.

**It rivals the global neuronal workspace, [LIT-631](LIT-631.md).** The essay puts
Dehaene's Consciousness and the Brain with the solutions that target
attention instead of subjectivity, and makes three claims the workspace
review opposes: consciousness is not attentional selection; extensive
cortical lesions may spare it while small brainstem lesions abolish it,
where the review ties the level of consciousness to frontal-parietal hubs
and their long-range connections; and general anaesthetics act at the
membrane-level sensing below feeling, where the review argues their
common action is to disconnect the workspace. The review is about access
by its own stipulation; the essay is about the identification of contents
as one's own. They are rival accounts of what is lost in coma and under
anaesthesia.

How it bears on what else the record holds:

- **[THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)** (felt affect as the experience of the body's regulatory
  state) is sourced from Barrett ([LIT-542](LIT-542.md)) and Damasio and Carvalho. This
  essay supports its clause that such feeling is present "throughout
  waking experience": feelings of existence are "generated continuously in
  the awake state". On the theory's open question, map or prediction, the
  essay says nothing about prediction; its answer is a third option, that
  the feeling is partly constituted by the body state it maps.
- **[THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md)** (subcortical alarm consciousness) cites [LIT-388](LIT-388.md) for
  its subcortical clause. This essay adds the clinical form of that claim:
  posterior brainstem lesions cause coma and extensive cortical lesions may
  not. On the function clause it does not choose between alarm and
  [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)'s common currency: feelings give "knowledge" that guides
  regulation overtly, and its examples (pain, hunger, well-being permitting
  exploration) are corrections, not overrides or trade-offs.
- **Barrett ([LIT-542](LIT-542.md))** is not mentioned. Where Barrett makes feeling a
  prediction issued by cortex, this essay makes it a hybrid built from the
  brainstem down.
- **Chalmers's "Facing Up" ([LIT-406](LIT-406.md))** is the essay's first reference, cited
  for the notion that the problem "cannot be solved, at least within the
  realm of biology". The essay states its biological answer without
  engaging Chalmers's argument.
- **Seth ([LIT-135](LIT-135.md))** and this essay both tie consciousness to the regulation
  of a living body; Seth argues through prediction, this essay through the
  physiology of interoceptive fibres.
- **Plants ([LIT-193](LIT-193.md)).** The essay classes plant sensing as sensing without
  minding, and cites Baluška et al. on anaesthetics halting plant activity
  as evidence that anaesthetics act below feeling, not as evidence that
  plants feel.
