# QUESTION-159: Does an exact train-then-predict identity, of the kind proved for unnormalized linear attention and for softmax attention, hold for further sequence-layer classes such as delta-rule or mini-batch inner updates, and is there a standard attention layer whose dependence on its context admits no description as any learner's train-then-predict step?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-169](../theory.d/THEORY-169.md), narrowed on 2026-10-19, holds that for unnormalized causal
linear attention and for softmax attention the layer's output equals a
learner's train-then-predict step on its context, and that one linear
self-attention layer with constructed weights carries one gradient step of
in-context linear regression. Until that date its promote_when also asked
for a test of scope. At the owner's direction, from the promotion audit's
batch 024 ([ADR-047](../decisions.d/ADR-047.md)), the THEORY was narrowed and promoted on the proved
identities, which were re-derived, and that part of the condition moved
here unchanged.

It is open because the identities are for particular layers. Normalized
linear attention, TTT-Linear as built (mini-batch updates, a learned W₀,
LayerNorm) and delta-rule layers are not covered, and nothing the record
holds shows that some standard layer has no such description.

## What would answer it

The scope test formerly on [THEORY-169](../theory.d/THEORY-169.md), unchanged. An exact identity of
this kind for a further layer class, for instance a delta-rule or
mini-batch inner update, would confirm the pattern. A proof that some
standard attention layer's dependence on its context admits no description
as any learner's train-then-predict step would bound it. Restating the two
known constructions, or measuring trained models' similarity to gradient
descent, does not count.

## Note of 2026-11-15: Schlag, Irie and Schmidhuber (2021), read

<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1871 — Proposed; the reading of 2026-11-15 that defines the delta-rule layer this question names, cited with the filer's gradient-step reading left to the owner -->

The delta-rule layer the question names is defined in Schlag, Irie and Schmidhuber ([LIT-2642](../literature.d/LIT-2642.md), [THEORY-1871](../theory.d/THEORY-1871.md)), §4.2. The layer is v̄(i) = W(i−1)φ(k(i)), W(i) = W(i−1) + β(i)(v(i) − v̄(i)) ⊗ φ(k(i)), y(i) = W(i)φ(q(i)), with W(0) = 0 and β(i) generated by the model. The paper calls this "effectively a delta rule with a dynamic learning rate" and states no train-then-predict identity for it. Its appendix derivations were not read.

By the filer's arithmetic, not the paper's, the increment is −β(i) times the gradient in W of ½‖v(i) − Wφ(k(i))‖² at W(i−1). So the unnormalised layer's output is the prediction, at φ(q(i)), of a linear model trained from zero by one online gradient step per context token, at the learned rates β(i). That makes it a train-then-predict description by construction. But it is sequential, not [THEORY-169](../theory.d/THEORY-169.md)'s single batch step, and it does not cover the normalised layer (Eqs. 27–28).

Whether this counts as an exact identity of the kind asked, or is too immediate to count, is the owner's call. If it counts, it answers the question's first half for the unnormalised delta-rule layer. The second half is untouched. No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-19: readings for the exchange "Topological protection explained"

Akyürek and colleagues ([THEORY-2050](../theory.d/THEORY-2050.md)), read: leaves the question open. Their Theorems 1 and 2 show that chosen transformer weights can carry one gradient step, or one Sherman–Morrison update of a ridge solution, on in-context examples. These are existence constructions for particular weights, not identities for a layer class, and the question says that restating constructions of this kind does not count. Their measurements of trained models, which match ridge or least squares rather than one gradient step, are excluded by the question's terms as well.

No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-26: von Oswald et al. (2023), read

<!-- inactive-ok-block: THEORY-tmp8xq94 NOTE-tmpcktnn THEORY-2049 — new or Proposed; the reading this note reports and a held entry it names -->

Bears on the question and narrows it; does not answer it as posed.

- **The mesa-layer** (LIT-tmpuv16r, NOTE-tmpcktnn, Eqs. 4–8) is a sequence
  layer whose output is exactly the prediction of a ridge, or recursive
  least-squares, learner trained on the context so far. Like the delta-rule
  reading in the note of 2026-11-15, it holds by definition: the layer is
  built as the solution of the regression. Whether a layer defined as a
  learner counts is the same call that note left to the owner.
- **The delta rule.** The paper states, without derivation, that Delta-Net
  is "hardwired to do one gradient descent step per time point". That
  matches the arithmetic of the note of 2026-11-15.
- **Held entries this question's notes did not cite.**
  - NOTE-620 (LIT-801) reads DeltaNet as "TTT-Linear with b = 1 and no LN
    or residual", a delta-rule-as-learner identity.
  - THEORY-2049 and NOTE-2087 cover mini-batch test-time-training layers,
    the question's mini-batch clause.
- **Untouched.** Normalized linear attention, and the question's second
  half, whether some standard layer admits no learner description.

No status change.
