# CLAIM-tmpr2qzg: Originalism errs in assuming exhaustiveness

## The claim

A38's table names the "potentially inadequate assumption" of originalism:
"The formative specification of an organization sufficiently determines its
subsequent normative organization." A38 §1: "The inadequacy arises if the
authority of the formative specification is taken to settle questions about
the legitimacy, adequacy or realizability of the subsequent organization
that require additional arguments. This would be a kind of
formation–constitution conflation."

A40 §2 sharpens the target: "The strongest critique of originalism is not
necessarily a critique of its theory of meaning. It is a critique of the
transition from a theory of meaning to a theory of legitimate governance."
And A40 §2.2: "The target of our critique is therefore not historical
interpretation itself. It is specification exhaustiveness: the assumption
that recovering the formative specification is sufficient to resolve all
consequential questions about its developed realization."

## What A adds to it

- **Discretion relocated** (A40 §2.4). Recovering historical meaning is
  itself a resource-bounded inquiry (retrieval, verification,
  disambiguation, translation, authorization), so "A methodology can
  relocate discretion rather than eliminate it." A40 states this as a
  research question, not a finding; [LEAD-tmp2x04m](../leads.d/LEAD-tmp2x04m.md) holds it. A40 cites
  [CLAIM-760](CLAIM-760.md) for it, which holds the point for work, not discretion.
- **Interpretation, implementation and revision** (A40 §2.5). Without
  distinguishing enforcing, clarifying, updating an implementation, revising
  the interface and re-evaluating the requirement, "constitutional change
  may be disguised as interpretation, while legitimate implementation
  changes may be incorrectly characterized as unauthorized revision."
- **Even-handedness** (A38 §1). Living constitutionalism has the converse
  vulnerabilities: "Inferring authority from established practice",
  "Insufficiently constraining discretionary reinterpretation",
  "Privileging present realization". "The deeper problem is how to preserve
  the reliability of constitutional commitments while maintaining
  legitimate pathways for their correction."

## A's own qualifications

A38: "These are not definitions of the three traditions. Each contains
more sophisticated positions that avoid some or all of these assumptions."
A40 §2.2 names one that does: Balkin's framework originalism
([LIT-tmps708j](../literature.d/LIT-tmps708j.md)). A40 §2 reports that Solum separates semantic, legal and
normative claims and that Baude and Sachs ground originalism "in present
legal practice, rather than simply in the authority of historical actors"
([LIT-tmp3huft](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3huft.md)). A40 §10 then says the application to a theory "would
then require demonstrating that the targeted theory actually depends upon the
conflation in question". The exchange does not name a theorist who does.

## The objection

[CLAIM-tmpmp2wh](CLAIM-tmpmp2wh.md): originalism as its defenders state it is a claim about who
may change the constitution and by what route, not that the formative
specification settles every question. What survives of this critique is
the design question whether formal amendment is too costly for the
inadequacies the organization meets (A38 §1: "Our cost model asks whether
the available pathways for constitutional transformation remain
appropriate to the kinds and timescales of inadequacy the organization
encounters"), which is [CLAIM-571](CLAIM-571.md)'s reconfiguration threshold applied, and
unargued.
