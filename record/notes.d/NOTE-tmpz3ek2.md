# NOTE-tmpz3ek2: Banerjee, Duflo, Glennerster and Kothari: immunisation camps with and without incentives

## Contribution

A randomized comparison, in one non-governmental organization's villages,
of making a service reliably available against making it available and
paying a small incentive to use it, with costs per outcome. The authors
frame it as supply against supply plus demand.

## Key results

- **Arms (Methods).** A: "regular availability of immunisation services",
  a mobile team holding a camp on a fixed day every month (95 % of 1336
  planned camps held). B: "the same infrastructure as intervention A but
  in addition offered parents 1 kg of raw lentils per immunisation
  administered and a set of thalis ... on completion". 30, 30 and 74
  villages.
- **Effects (Table 2).** Fully immunised at age 1-3: 6 % control, 18 % A,
  39 % B; relative risk B against A 2.16 (1.54 to 2.78). At least one
  immunisation: 78 % A, 74 % B, no difference. "the impact of the
  incentive was mainly to reduce the number of children dropping out
  after three injections."
- **Costs (Costs).** "The average cost to Seva Mandir of fully immunising
  a child was $27.94 ... in the reliable camp with incentives and $55.83
  ... in the reliable camp without incentives". The reason: camps "had to
  be open from 11 am to 2 pm, regardless of the number of children
  present", and averaged 4.5 immunisations a day without incentives and
  13.4 with. The abstract has the two figures swapped; the correction:
  "It should be $56 for intervention A and $28 for intervention B."
- **Spillover.** Neighbouring villages of B camps: 20 % fully immunised
  against 11 % near A camps.
- **The authors' reading.** "improving the supply alone might not be
  enough: even a fully reliable supply system has a relatively modest
  effect on uptake"; parents "are not persuaded enough about its benefits
  to overcome the natural tendency to delay a slightly costly activity".

## Assumptions

- Self-reported immunisation, checked against BCG scars, cards and camp
  logbooks.
- Costs are Seva Mandir's; with existing government infrastructure the
  authors estimate $25.18 without incentives and $17.35 with.

## Concepts

- **Supply-side** against **demand-side** intervention.
- **Cost per fully immunised child**: total cost over completed courses,
  which falls as fixed capacity is used.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-562**, weakly. The money added on the demand side, on top of
  supply, doubled the outcome and halved its unit cost, so in this
  organization the binding constraint was not where the first
  intervention acted. But there is no arm on the demand side alone, and
  the totals are not equal: the comparison is of a supply arm against a
  superset of it. Where a reading puts the incentive "in a different
  place" in the structure, that is the filer's framing.
- **The second half of CLAIM-562** (adding can hurt): the nested arm
  did better, not worse. No evidence either way on whether adding can
  hurt.

## Limitations

- "we could not introduce an incentive programme without simultaneously
  making the supply more reliable."
- Very low baseline (under 2 % fully immunised), one area, one
  organization trusted in its villages.
- Cost figures per outcome, not per village or per arm in total;
  appendix 2 not read.
