# NOTE-1625: Lee Tin Wah et al. — Observing and controlling the folding pathway of DNA origami

## Contribution

A snapshot method for following origami folding molecule by molecule: at
each temperature of the ramp a piece of mica is dipped into the folding
solution for a minute, rinsed at that temperature, dried and imaged by
tapping-mode AFM, and image segmentation gives size and height
distributions from 50–150 particles. With it the paper gives a sequence
of intermediates for one 2D design, an equilibrium account of where
folding nucleates, and a way to move the nucleation site by staple
concentration without touching any sequence.

## Key insight

Where an origami starts folding is where its staples melt highest, and a
staple's melting temperature depends on its concentration as well as its
sequence. So the path can be steered by concentrations alone: "the
pathway is primarily defined by the distribution of the melting
temperature of staples bound to the scaffold, the origamis folding from
high to low melting temperature area" (Conclusion, p. 14).

## Assumptions

- **Adsorption and drying preserve the solution ensemble** at the sampling
  temperature; supported by agreement with bulk fluorescence and by a
  thermal estimate (SI section 10). Linear strands are drying artefacts
  that "probably do not exist in solution".
- **The ramp is slow enough** that each snapshot is near equilibrium.
- **One design**, the tall rectangle, with the west and east edge staples
  always omitted "to minimize the stacking interaction".
- **The model** (SI section 8) treats one two-site staple at a time, by
  mass action, without cooperativity.

## Key results

- **Standard pathway (abstract; pp. 5–6, Fig. 2).** "In standard
  conditions folding was initiated at the edges of the rectangle and
  progressed towards the center." Folded portions "appeared always on the
  top and bottom ends of the tall rectangle, and not in the middle,
  indicating that folding is not random but starts at well-defined
  positions". States in order: linear, entangled coil, half-width folded,
  full-width folded, broken rectangle, fully folded, between about 70 and
  55 °C; "Several of these structures coexisted at a given temperature".
  Standard conditions: 1 nM scaffold, 52 nM of each staple, 12.5 mM Mg
  acetate, 1 °C per 15 min.
- **Final yield, standard conditions (p. 6).** "85 % of the particles
  folded without defects, 5 % displayed large holes up to 1/2 of the tall
  rectangle surface, and 10 % were rectangular particles smaller than
  1/4".
- **No high-temperature step needed (pp. 6–7, Fig. S10).** After a quench
  from 95 °C to the sampling temperature, "we obtained well-formed
  rectangles down to Ti = 57", "a first indication that the folding
  process is under thermodynamic rather than kinetic control".
- **Concentration moves nucleation (pp. 10–12, Fig. 5; Methods p. 15).**
  With "the concentration of the central and border staples was changed
  from 52 nM to, respectively, 670 and 1.2 nM. In these conditions the
  folding pathway was dramatically changed and ... proceeded this time
  from the center towards the edges." The border set is "the outermost row
  staples (4 on the northern edge and 4 on the southern edge", the central
  set "the 9 rows of central staples". The onset of folding fell from 63
  to 59 °C, in bulk too. The Fig. 5 ramp was 1 °C per 30 min.
- **The final object under the change (p. 11).** Only: "normal rectangles
  were observed on top of a rugged surface, probably due to staple
  adsorption." In bulk, "the final fluorescence signal was also reduced"
  and "the interval of temperatures during which folding was observed was
  reduced". No yield or defect count.
- **More staple is not always better (SI 8.1–8.2).** In the model, "O
  increases with S0 only if K1 S0 < 1. For K1 S0 > 1 reaction 2 competes
  with reaction 3": a second copy occupying the other site blocks the
  fold. Order-of-magnitude melting-temperature estimates match the
  measured shifts in direction and roughly in size.
- **Melting departs from folding above 61 °C (pp. 9–10, Fig. 4).** Below,
  melting runs 2 °C above folding, "However, this difference is within the
  2" °C precision of the method. Above, "a new type of structure, called
  chain-like, was observed", read as out of equilibrium, its links slow to
  dissociate.
- **AFM against bulk (pp. 12–13, Fig. 6).** Folding temperatures differ by
  1–2 °C at high and medium folding and 4–7 °C at low folding, since "the
  bulk technique is proportional to the concentration of dsDNA, while AFM
  is sensitive to the formation of structures large enough to be detected
  (typically 10 nm)".
- **What each observable merges (pp. 7–8, 10).** Modal height shows "only
  two peaks suggesting that folding is a two-state process. However, this
  picture is incomplete"; median height resolves "at least four states";
  averaging after registration "is thus biased towards finding average
  rectangular shapes". Bulk fluorescence misses the chain-like hysteresis,
  because the states, "although structurally very different, had a
  similar number of base pairs". Lowering density to segment at higher
  temperature "would result in poor sampling statistics at lower
  temperatures".

## Concepts

- **Nucleation site**, located by imaging; **thermodynamic against kinetic
  control** of the pathway.
- **Melting-temperature distribution** over a structure, and its
  dependence on staple concentration.
- **Blocking by excess**: two copies of a two-site staple occupying both
  sites.
- **Folding–melting hysteresis**; out-of-equilibrium chain-like melting
  intermediates.
- **Order parameter choice**: modal height, median height, visual class,
  bulk fluorescence, each merging different states.

## Connections

The design is Rothemund's ([LIT-2120](../literature.d/LIT-2120.md), [NOTE-1631](NOTE-1631.md)). The authors cite
Dunn et al. ([LIT-2135](../literature.d/LIT-2135.md), [NOTE-1643](NOTE-1643.md)) as agreeing that early
connections from high-melting staples guide the path; Dunn changes
stabilities and shifts the final shape among near-degenerate ones, while
this paper changes concentrations and shifts only the path to one shape.
The quench result mirrors the narrow folding windows of Sobczak et al.
2012 ([LIT-2131](../literature.d/LIT-2131.md), its reference 17, Unavailable) and of Sobczak's thesis
([LIT-2138](../literature.d/LIT-2138.md), [NOTE-1644](NOTE-1644.md)). Schneider et al. ([LIT-2122](../literature.d/LIT-2122.md),
[NOTE-1641](NOTE-1641.md)) give an ordered binding of staple domains in a 3D brick;
this paper gives a spatial order for a 2D sheet. Against Dannenberg et al.
([LIT-2129](../literature.d/LIT-2129.md), [NOTE-1645](NOTE-1645.md)), hysteresis here is within precision below
61 °C. The observables that merge states echo Wagenbauer et al.
([LIT-2128](../literature.d/LIT-2128.md), [NOTE-1637](NOTE-1637.md)). The excess-blocking term of the model is
the single-fold blocked state that Majikes et al. 2021 ([LIT-2133](../literature.d/LIT-2133.md),
[NOTE-1633](NOTE-1633.md)) observe. Against the held simulation, [LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md) ([NOTE-1561](NOTE-1561.md),
[THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)): consistent with hierarchical growth from nucleation sites;
no global trap is seen in this 2D design, and the authors' equilibrium
reading contrasts with the kinetic traps the simulation finds in larger
3D designs.

## Limitations

- **One design**, and the two concentration changes made together (centre
  up about thirteenfold, edges down to near stoichiometry), so their
  effects are not separated, though the authors assign temperature shifts
  to each.
- **The total stock rose and the ramp halved in rate** under the altered
  condition; the paper does not treat the ramp difference.
- **No yield, defect count or misfold census** under the altered
  condition.
- **Segmentation works only at 59 °C and below**; above, particles are
  too dense.
- **A manuscript inconsistency**: the caption of Fig. S14 gives 12 nM for
  the edge staples, the main text and Methods 1.2 nM.
- **Hysteresis below 61 °C is within the method's precision.**
