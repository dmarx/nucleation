# LIT-144: Causal Exclusion and Downward Counterfactuals

Tuomas K. Pernu (2015), *Erkenntnis 81(5), 1031–1049 (2016)* — DOI-10.1007/s10670-015-9781-7

## Key takeaways

- The counterfactual theory of causation can answer the causal exclusion problem without being committed to downward causation, provided the semantics of counterfactuals is handled carefully. The exclusion problem itself, however, comes in several distinct forms.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

A leading response to Kim's causal exclusion problem appeals to a counterfactual account of causation. Critics have argued that this account cannot solve the problem because it commits one to downward causation. Pernu analyses those arguments and gives an account of counterfactual dependence. He argues that, with careful attention to counterfactual semantics, the counterfactual notion of causation need not be committed to downward causation. He also argues that the exclusion problem takes more than one form.
