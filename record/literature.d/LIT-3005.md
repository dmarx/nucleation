The first existence and tail result for the random difference equation X = AX + B: the stationary solution has a Pareto-like tail P(X > x) ~ c x^(−κ), κ solving E[A^κ] = 1, which forces E log A < 0 while A sometimes exceeds one. Kesten proved it for products of random matrices in d dimensions; the scalar case usually cited is Goldie's re-proof (1991). Here for the Kesten half of CLAIM-824.

# LIT-3005: Random difference equations and renewal theory for products of random matrices

Kesten (1973). Acta Mathematica. DOI 10.1007/BF02392040.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A names it at A3 for the Kesten process, multiplicative growth with a reflecting or additive term that gives a power-law tail. It bears on the mechanism behind the exchange's claim that multiplicative growth with a floor yields a power law rather than a lognormal. Not read: Unfetched since 2026-11-22 (Project Euclid behind a WAF challenge).
