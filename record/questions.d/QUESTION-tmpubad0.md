# QUESTION-tmpubad0: What evidence would show that a maintaining link, and not a regime's content, keeps a disorder in place: does the efficacy of a treatment that targets the link count, or only change in the link that is specific and comes before the outcome?

## The question

[QUESTION-045](QUESTION-045.md) asks whether a disorder that outlasts its trigger is a
protective lock-in, and what would show that the maintaining link, such as
safety behaviour, and not the content of a fearful belief, keeps it in
place. A maintaining link is a theory of change. This question asks what
evidence bears on such a theory.

## The answer

[THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md), Active, states the standard, from both wings of CBT
(Salkovskis et al. and Hofmann and Hayes):

- **Efficacy does not count.** Every theory of change predicts that the
  treatment it informs works, so a treatment's working cannot tell a
  link account from a content account.
- **What counts** is change in the theorised process that is specific to
  it, comes before the outcome, and moves the outcome more than the
  rival's process does.
- **The disconfirming case** is a treatment that moves the process and
  leaves the outcome alone. A treatment that fails to move the process
  has not tested it.

Mediation evidence of this kind is necessary, not sufficient. Whether any
disorder meets the standard, and so is a protective lock-in, remains
[QUESTION-045](QUESTION-045.md)'s.
