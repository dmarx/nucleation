# LIT-tmpappcx: Cognitive and attentional mechanisms in delay of gratification

Walter Mischel, Ebbe B. Ebbesen and Antonette Raskoff Zeiss (1972). Cognitive and attentional mechanisms in delay of gratification. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 21(2): 204–218. DOI 10.1037/h0032198.

## Key takeaways

- Children "waited much longer for a preferred reward when they were
  distracted from the rewards than when they attended to them directly"
  (abstract).
- Thinking about the hidden rewards cut the mean wait to 0.78 minutes,
  results that "utterly contradict the belief that ideating about the
  reward objects in their absence enhances voluntary delay" (Experiment
  III).
- The distraction was supplied by the experimenters, and children did not
  choose to cover the rewards before waiting ("quite randomly"); the
  reward contingency still governed.

Read on 2026-11-17 ([NOTE-tmpxyktj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxyktj.md)). The exchange's characterization is
partly faithful: A51 says those "who redirected their attention away from
the tempting reward often waited longer". The direction is right, but in
these experiments the experimenters redirected attention, which, if
anything, strengthens the case's point that waiting depended on how the
situation was organized.
