# THEORY-796: Question-order effects in surveys generally leave the probability of giving the same answer to both questions unchanged, the QQ equality that a projection model of judgment predicts, as Wang, Solloway, Shiffrin and Busemeyer propose

## Source

Wang, Solloway, Shiffrin & Busemeyer (2014), [LIT-795](../literature.d/LIT-795.md), whose title states
that question-order context effects "reveal quantum nature of human
judgments". The equality is derived in Wang & Busemeyer (2013), [LIT-834](../literature.d/LIT-834.md),
Appendix. The paper presents it as an a priori, parameter-free prediction
that holds for question-order effects in general.

## What is asserted

That for two back-to-back binary questions asked in both orders, the
probability of agreeing answers does not depend on the order, as a
regularity of survey and judgment data in general and not only of the
surveys tested.

## What the record holds toward it

[THEORY-166](THEORY-166.md), which is Active, holds the measured case: every Pew survey of
2001–2011 that varied the order of two questions shows order effects but no
departure from the equality, and 72 studies lie along the predicted line.
It also holds the limits: the equality breaks when information is read out
between the questions (Rose–Jackson), and a classical model with order
events can satisfy it.

## What this does not say

- That human judgment is quantum. The equality follows from projector
  symmetry under one update rule, and a constrained classical model meets
  it.
- That the data are contextual. Under the equality they are not ([LIT-264](../literature.d/LIT-264.md)).
- Anything outside back-to-back binary questions.
