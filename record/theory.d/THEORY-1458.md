# THEORY-1458: In Shah, Shafir and Mullainathan's working paper, players given small budgets of seconds or of guesses borrowed a larger share of their budget against later rounds than players given large ones and, when borrowing carried interest, scored lower than when they could not borrow, while large-budget players did not; the authors attribute this to constraint focus, attention shifted toward the binding resource at the cost of others, whose one direct test is a two-grid memory game with 33 players

## Source

Shah, Shafir & Mullainathan, working paper (undated, before 2012),
[LIT-2171](../literature.d/LIT-2171.md); read in [NOTE-1653](../notes.d/NOTE-1653.md). A stand-in for the unread Science
article, [LIT-2147](../literature.d/LIT-2147.md), whose Experiments 3 and 4 its Experiments 1 and 2
match number for number.

## What was actually shown

- **Time, Experiment 1** (pp. 4–6): the time-poor borrowed .22 of their
  budget, the time-rich .08; the time-poor scored z = .60 unable to
  borrow, .08 borrowing without interest, −.48 with; the time-rich did
  not differ (p = .15).
- **Time with debt, Experiment 2** (pp. 6–7): the time-poor's budgets
  shrank round by round (slope −.13 against −.01), and they scored .54
  without borrowing against −.49 with.
- **Guesses, Experiment 3** (pp. 8–9): run because "time pressure simply
  made it difficult to consider future rounds" could explain the time
  games; the poor borrowed .21 against .01, and scored .36 without
  borrowing against −.38 with; the rich did not differ.
- **The mechanism, Experiment 4** (pp. 10–12): within subjects, players
  scored less on rounds where one grid had three guesses and the other
  one (140.06) than where both had one (149.70), t(32) = 2.05.
- Each comparison could have come out the other way: the rich, with the
  same games and borrowing rates, are the control.

## What this does not say

- That the poor borrow more because of time pressure: the guess game has
  no clock on the resource and gives the same pattern.
- That the effect holds outside the lab, with money, or with real
  poverty: the parallel to payday loans is argued, not tested.
- That constraint focus causes the borrowing: the borrowing experiments
  do not measure attention, and the one attention test has no borrowing.
- That it survives replication at full size: the authors' own
  replications found smaller effects ([THEORY-1484](THEORY-1484.md)); none by another
  team has been made of these experiments.
- Anything about windfalls beyond one paragraph without numbers (p. 13).
