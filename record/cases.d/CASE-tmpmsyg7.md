# CASE-tmpmsyg7: Dolly's slip into ты in Anna Karenina I.4, and three English translations that carry it three ways

Found on 2026-10-10 by a search the owner asked for: documented cases for
[CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md), against [CLAIM-tmpfe37n](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpfe37n.md)'s objection that it grounds only on
the fictional [CASE-018](CASE-018.md).

## The case

In *Anna Karenina* Part I ch. 4, Dolly confronts Stiva over his affair. She
addresses him with вы, says one sentence with ты, and returns to вы
("скажите"). The narrator names the shift, and Stiva is grateful for it
(Tolstoy, *Анна Каренина*, I.IV, https://ilibrary.ru/text/1099/p.4/index.html):

> — Ты помнишь детей, чтоб играть с ними […]
> Она сказала ему «ты», и он с благодарностью взглянул на нее […]
> — […] Ну, скажите, после того... что было, разве возможно нам жить вместе?

The beat is a change of relation carried by a pronoun alone. English has one
pronoun of address, so a word-for-word rendering ("You remember…"; "She said
'you' to him") leaves the narrator's sentence pointing at nothing. Three
published translations handle it three ways.

- **Dole, 1886: the word kept and glossed.** "You think of the children,
  because you like to play with them […]" / "She had used the familiar tui
  (thou), and he looked at her with gratitude" (tr. Nathan Haskell Dole, New
  York: T. Y. Crowell, 1886, p. 17; archive.org `annakarenina00tolsiala`).
- **Garnett, 1901: a first-name vocative added.** "You remember the
  children, Stiva, to play with them […]" / "She had called him 'Stiva,' and
  he glanced at her with gratitude" (tr. Constance Garnett, Part One ch. 4,
  Project Gutenberg [#1399](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/1399)).
- **The Maudes, 1918: an inclusive possessive added.** "You think of our
  children when you want to play with them […]" / "But she had spoken of
  'our children', and looking gratefully at her he moved to take her hand"
  (tr. Louise and Aylmer Maude, Oxford World's Classics reissue 1998, Part I
  ch. 4, p. 11; archive.org `anna-karenina-maude`).

"Stiva" and "our" are not in Dolly's Russian line. Each is a target-language
marker of solidarity, and each translator rewrites the narrator's sentence to
point at it.

**Companion passages.** At I.20 Dolly's return to ты marks reconciliation:
«жена говорила с ним, называя его «ты», чего прежде не было»
(https://ilibrary.ru/text/1099/p.20/index.html). Dole: "called him tui
(thou), which had not been the case before" (pp. 80–81). Garnett: "addressed
him as 'Stiva,' as she had not done before". The Maudes: "addressed him
familiarly in the second person singular" (p. 69/70). At I.5 Stiva is «на
«ты»» with nearly everyone, and his low companions are his «постыдные
«ты»» (https://ilibrary.ru/text/1099/p.5/index.html). Dole: "the familiar
'tui' ('thou')" (pp. 22–23). Garnett: "called almost all of them by their
Christian names". The Maudes: "his 'disreputable pals'" (pp. 15–16).

**A translator's reasoning.** Nicolas Pasternak Slater, whose translation
with Maya Slater is announced from the Folio Society for 2026, reached
Garnett's I.5 solution independently and says why: "with someone like Stiva
Oblonsky—who's on easy terms with everyone and uses ty freely—I simply wrote
'he was on first-name terms with everybody.'" Maya Slater calls ty/vy "the
obvious challenge": "Each time, you have to think of a solution that makes
sense in English." (Interview by Olga Kenton, *Reading in Translation*,
21 July 2025.) Nicolas's remark that "an English reader is usually familiar
with French *tu* and *vous*, whereas they're not familiar with the Russian
equivalents" is made of a different episode, the lovers' move into French,
not of I.4 or I.5.

**The author on the dictionary equivalent.** At III.14 Karenin writes to
Anna in French, «употребляя местоимение «вы», не имеющее того характера
холодности, который оно имеет на русском языке»
(https://ilibrary.ru/text/1099/p.83/index.html). Garnett: "the plural
'*vous*,' which has not the same note of coldness as the corresponding
Russian form." At II.22 Vronsky speaks French to avoid «невозможно-холодного
между ними вы и опасного ты по-русски»
(https://ilibrary.ru/text/1099/p.56/index.html).

**The social coordinate.** Brown and Gilman 1960, p. 253: "The pronoun
'thou' is reserved, nowadays, to prayer and naive poetry". So Dole's
"(thou)" points a modern English reader toward reverence or archaism rather
than intimacy. Their p. 275 gives the reading the scene relies on: "The
momentary shift of pronoun directly expresses a momentary shift of mood."

## What it can show

It is a documented instance of the mechanism in [CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md). The address
system differs between source and target, so the coordinate that changes is
the code (k and g in [TERM-031](../terms.d/TERM-031.md)'s C = (u, s, a, k, g)), not the speaker. Two
published translators, seventeen years apart, added words that are not in
the source in order to carry a relation the source carries in a pronoun.
For I.5, two translators 124 years apart reached the same compensation, and
the later one gives his reasoning. The author attests the premise: the
lexical equivalent, вы as vous, does not keep the relational value.

So it meets two of the three conditions the search set: (a) a social
coordinate differs between source and target, and (b) a literal and a
compensating rendering of the same passage are attested, with reasoning for
one of the passages. That answers [CLAIM-tmpfe37n](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpfe37n.md) at the level of existence:
[CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md) no longer has only an invented case for its mechanism. This is the
translation arm of A24, not the reattribution arm of [CASE-018](CASE-018.md).

It bears weakly on the necessity half of [CLAIM-061](../claims.d/CLAIM-061.md). The narrator's
sentence changes its proposition in Garnett ("She had called him 'Stiva'"
is false of the Russian scene) while, on the translators' judgement, keeping
what the sentence does. That is the act judged kept by the translator, not
measured.

## What it cannot show

**Condition (c) is met only partly.** Every judgement here about what the
compensation conveys is a producer's or the author's: the translators'
choices, the Slaters' stated reasoning, the Maudes' footnote at IV.20 that
explains "thou" when they do keep it, and Tolstoy's statement about вы and
vous. Brown and Gilman supply the general sociolinguistics of English
"thou". None of it is evidence of what readers recover. No reader,
classroom or reception study was found, for these renderings or for T/V
compensation into English at all. That readers recover the relation better
from "Stiva" or "our children" than from "tui (thou)" is [CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md)'s
defeat condition, and this case does not touch it.

Further limits:

- **No bare literal is attested.** No published translation found says
  "She said 'you' to him". Dole's borrowing with gloss is the closest
  literal, and it is itself an explicitation. The literal arm of the
  contrast is the record's construction.
- **Not the same relation.** "Our children" turns a pronoun of intimacy into
  an appeal to shared parenthood, which may be a different act. "Stiva" is
  nearer, but English first-naming is a weaker and more ordinary mark than a
  marked ты among вы.
- **Nothing about proportion.** [CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md)'s "where it went" speaks of
  wording changing in proportion to the change of context. One beat and its
  companions say nothing about degree.

**The strongest reply a defender would make.** Translators are the
competent bilingual readers the claim is about: four of them, from 1886
to 2025, independently judged that keeping the word loses the relation, and
the author, a bilingual, judged the same of вы and vous. That is expert
judgement converging, which is evidence. The reply is right that it is
evidence that the literal fails for these producers. It is not evidence that
the compensation succeeds for target readers, which is what the claim says;
translators may compensate from habit or house style, and the Maudes'
footnote shows them unsure the literal would be understood, not sure the
compensation would be.

**What would close the gap.** A reader test built from these attested
texts. Give monolingual English readers I.4 in four versions: Dole's;
a bare literal ("She had said 'you' to him"), labelled as constructed;
Garnett's; the Maudes'. Ask whether Dolly's attitude changed in that line,
in which direction, and why Stiva is grateful. Use Russian readers of the
source as the reference. [CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md) predicts that the Garnett and Maude
groups match the Russian readers better than the Dole and literal groups.
The four versions could serve as items in the discriminating study,
[CASE-tmp1uzfn](CASE-tmp1uzfn.md), as attested renderings that change wording to keep the
relation, alongside [CASE-021](CASE-021.md)'s factorial design.
