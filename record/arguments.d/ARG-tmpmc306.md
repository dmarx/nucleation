# ARG-tmpmc306: Eyerman's cultural trauma and Volkan's chosen trauma are evidence for the owner's thesis only for chosen trauma, where an ancestral injury is taken up as an injury to a present group identity and felt as grievance when that identity is threatened; they do not tell an enlarged self from a switched one, and they leave mourning for particular dead open

## The inference

CLAIM-491 holds that what is called selflessness is concern for the good
of a self extended to include others or a group. QUESTION-297 runs it
backward in time: a descendant's grief over an ancestral injury would be
self-regarding, the injured self including the ancestors.

1. **Eyerman** (THEORY-1606, chapter 1 of his book): cultural trauma is a
   group's loss of identity and meaning, not an individual wound, and
   slavery became the cultural trauma of African Americans in retrospect,
   by shared fate rather than experience or descent. The injury is
   constituted as an injury to "us", and the individual is defined as a
   "race member" by it (NOTE-1246, p. 3).
2. **Volkan** (THEORY-1597): a large group's shared image of an ancestral
   injury, real or mythologized, becomes a marker of the group's
   identity, and a threat to that identity brings it up. A recent trauma
   still mourned is not one.
3. So, where a descendant feels an ancestral injury as their own, the
   feeling is roused by a threat to their present group identity and aims
   at restoring it. That is concern for a self that includes the group's
   past: the thesis's extended self, running backward.
4. And the self it extends to is a category, not a line of descent
   (Eyerman: "whether or not they directly experienced slavery or even had
   ancestors who did", p. 14). The thesis's "ancestral" should mean the
   group's past, not the family's.

## Critical questions

The evidence form asks three questions (ADR-061).

**Would the evidence be expected if the thesis were false?** Largely yes.
Both readings describe how a group's identity takes up a past injury; they
are accounts of collective memory, not of motive. A rival on which the
descendant cares about the injured as others, or about the group as a
good that is no self's, predicts the same commemoration and the same
grievance when the group is threatened. Volkan's trigger tells against
the first rival a little: the injury comes up when the present identity is
threatened, not when the ancestors' suffering is recalled. That is
self-regarding in timing. It is clinical impression, and nothing is
measured.

**Does it tell the thesis apart from its rivals?** Not from the nearest
one. CLAIM-491's own "What it does not say" separates a larger self from a
changed one: self-categorization's depersonalization is "a change of self,
not plainly a larger one". Volkan's members "subsume their personal
identities behind their large-group identities", the personal layer
receding (NOTE-1250), which is the changed self; his tent's two layers,
worn at once, fit the larger self. He uses both images and does not
reconcile them. Eyerman's "race member" is defined by the category, which
fits either. The readings therefore support "a group-level self is
served" and leave open whether it is the personal self enlarged.

**Does the measure measure what it is said to?** There is no measure.
Eyerman describes how an identity was articulated, not what members feel
(NOTE-1246: "Nothing in the chapter measures any individual's grief, or
whether it tracks identification"). Volkan's evidence is clinical work and
facilitated dialogue.

## Verdict proposed

The inference holds for a narrower conclusion than CLAIM-491 states:

- **Chosen trauma.** An ancestral injury, centuries old and no longer
  mourned, taken up as an identity marker and roused by threats to the
  identity, is self-regarding on a self that includes the group's past.
  What is felt is grievance and entitlement, and what is served is a
  self-concept kept intact, as in identity fusion (THEORY-1581), not a
  welfare.
- **Not mourning.** For a recent trauma still mourned, where descendants
  hold the pictures, belongings and stories of particular people, neither
  reading says whether the mourning is for the self or for the lost. That
  is the thesis's open case, as its note already says.
- **Not larger rather than switched.** The readings do not decide between
  them.

So the readings are weak evidence for CLAIM-491's shape, confined to
chosen trauma, and they correct its boundary from lineage to category.
They do not bear on its two objections beyond suggesting, for CLAIM-492, a
criterion of the self's extent apart from caring: membership ascribed by
others and then avowed (Eyerman), or an identity fixed in childhood and
carried by symbols (Volkan). Neither is measured.

If the owner adopts this verdict, the ARG goes Active as an inference to
the narrower conclusion, and CLAIM-491's note of 2026-10-29 can cite it in
place of "support its shape". If the owner holds that the readings support
the thesis as stated, the ARG's critical questions above are what the
ruling has to answer.
