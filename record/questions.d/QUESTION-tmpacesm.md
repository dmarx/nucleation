# QUESTION-tmpacesm: In a random sample of English-source human translations drawn without a named-entity or alignment filter, annotated by at least two annotators with agreement per category reported, how often per sentence does each language's translation add material for the target audience, and are measurement and entity explicitations the most frequent types?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1153](../theory.d/THEORY-1153.md) reports the PETra corpus ([LIT-1798](../literature.d/LIT-1798.md), read in [NOTE-1344](../notes.d/NOTE-1344.md)): among
additions a named-entity and null-alignment filter and a classifier
trained on its finds turned up, system transfers, chiefly unit
conversions, and entity changes are most of the labels. The filter
requires a named entity, so entity-linked additions were bound to be
common, three quarters of the pairs were found by the classifier, and no
agreement is reported. How often translators add material for their
audience, and of which kinds, outside that net is not known.

## What would answer it

An annotation of a random sample of English-source human translations,
drawn without a named-entity or alignment filter, by at least two
annotators working from the same guidelines with their agreement per
category reported, that gives per-language rates of explicitation per
sentence and says whether measurement and entity explicitations are the
most frequent types. Rates within PETra's own filtered pool, or classifier
counts, do not settle it.
