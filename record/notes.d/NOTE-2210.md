# NOTE-2210: Tomasello — Communicating Without Conventions: The Co-operation Model

## Contribution

The cooperation model of communication in compact form, stated by the
author as following the 2008 book: pragmatics first, conventions built on
it.

## Key results

1. **Pragmatics in the raw.** "With natural gestures, the veil is
   lifted and we see pragmatics in the raw (Tomasello, 2008)." (p. 3);
   "some original, non-conventional way of doing it must be available
   first; there must be something to conventionalize." (p. 4).
2. **Apes.** "Great apes do not point or gesture iconically for one
   another." (p. 8); "apes do not comprehend informative pointing because
   their communication is not structured cooperatively" (p. 9).
3. **Cooperation.** "human communication just is a collaborative
   activity" (p. 10); it is "structured most basically by motives of
   helping (both requesting and offering help) and sharing attitudes." (p.
   12).
4. **Common ground.** "In direct competition, shared common ground
   trumps individual personal relevance every time." (p. 14).
5. **Norms and reasoning.** Overt communication creates "actual social
   norms, whose violation is unacceptable" (p. 16); "we call the kind of
   practical reasoning characteristic of human communication cooperative
   reasoning." (p. 17).
6. **Origins.** Early humans "in fact became obligate collaborators"
   (p. 18); "The move to communicative conventions is thus, paradoxically,
   a natural one." (p. 19).
7. **Conclusion.** "This is not pragmatics as leftovers, but pragmatics
   as the foundation" (p. 25).

## Assumptions

Grice and relevance theory are the right frame; pointing is primordial;
the ape findings generalize; collaboration came before cooperative
communication; conventions need a prior non-conventional channel.

## Concepts

Natural gesture; referential and social intention; requestive, informative
and expressive motives; common ground and joint attentional frame;
communicative intention; mutual manifestness; cooperative reasoning; drift
to the arbitrary.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-444](../theory.d/THEORY-444.md)** (Grice), supports and grounds it: the communicative
  intention is central, made mutually manifest and grounded in shared
  intentionality, and natural gestures show it without convention.
- **[THEORY-511](../theory.d/THEORY-511.md)** (Strawson), a parallel: requests, informing and sharing
  by pointing with no convention.
- **[LIT-473](../literature.d/LIT-473.md)** (Lewis), a qualification: conventions presuppose a prior
  cooperative, non-conventional channel and arise by imitation and
  opacity, not agreement.
- **[THEORY-785](../theory.d/THEORY-785.md)** and **[THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md)** (rational speech act models), a
  contrast: reasoning is cooperative and tied to common ground, not one
  listener inverting an informative speaker; the claim that common ground
  beats egocentric relevance is testable in those paradigms.
- **[THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)**, a parallel: the same infrastructure, and overt
  communication creating norms.
- **[CLAIM-221](../claims.d/CLAIM-221.md)**, support: a developed account based on intention plus
  common ground.

## Limitations

An essay with no new data; the support is the author's lab's infant and
ape studies; a later restatement of the book; grammar left out.
