# LIT-tmpgo33i: A General Feedback Theory of Human Behavior: Part I

Powers, W. T., Clark, R. K. & McFarland, R. L. (1960). *Perceptual and Motor Skills* 11(1):71–88. doi:10.2466/pms.1960.11.1.71. Reprinted in *Living Control Systems: Selected Papers of William T. Powers* (Control Systems Group, 1989), pp. 1–24.

Read in part: the passage on the imagination connection, in the reprint.
The rest of the summary is what the work is standardly cited for.

## Why this is here

For [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) and [QUESTION-tmpr5tck](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpr5tck.md). Perceptual control theory has an internal loop with no action: "The imagination-signal makes it possible for our system to perceive reproductions of past perceptual signals ... to plan an action 'mentally' without actually performing it, to hallucinate, and as mentioned, to dream" (reprint p. 20). That loop is the synchronic, self-satisfying form, in which the reference "substitutes for the feedback-signal" and the "match" is "automatically ensured", the form [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) does not claim. The same passage gives the anchor problem: "the hierarchy could not perform very reliably in a real and sometimes dangerous environment if its actions were completely 'short-circuited' by the imagination connections". It also gives a remark on sensory deprivation, under which the imagination connection "apparently provides a great deal of information", which bears on [CASE-tmpxgcq2](../cases.d/CASE-tmpxgcq2.md).
