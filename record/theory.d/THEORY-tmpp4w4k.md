# THEORY-tmpp4w4k: Lee and colleagues report that mixtures of starfish embryos at two developmental ages, whose fluid-mediated interactions grow less nonreciprocal as they develop, pass abruptly from a traveling, flock-like collective state to a fluctuating, crystal-like one; weak nonreciprocity favours structural order and strong nonreciprocity fragments it, and at the passage both the rate of structural change and the estimated dissipation fall, while dissipation biases local rearrangements toward ordered motifs

## Source

Lee and colleagues (2025), "Topological flowscape reveals state
transitions in nonreciprocal living matter", arXiv 2511.11815, DOI
10.1103/tq6h-f5ws, LIT-2268, skimmed in NOTE-tmprzmfk.

## The account

Binary mixtures of embryos (24 and 48 hours post fertilization) interact
asymmetrically; as they develop the asymmetry falls. Topological
landscapes, distributions of local structural motifs, place crystalline,
self-propelled crystalline, flock-like and fragmented states in a state
diagram. Flowscapes, coordinates of divergence from reference motif
distributions, trace the experiment's passage from traveling to
fluctuating: a sharp turn near three hours, a fall in the rate of
structural change that coincides with the fall in polarization and in a
lower bound on entropy production, and a dissipation-dependent bias of
motif transitions toward the ordered motif, read as proofreading.

## Bearing

QUESTION-281: a measured, non-gradual passage between collective
organizations under a slow drift of interactions; the kind of transition
is not identified.
