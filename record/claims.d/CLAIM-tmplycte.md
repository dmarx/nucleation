# CLAIM-tmplycte: A defeat condition that says something cannot be done is met by an argument that it cannot, not by a record of failures, so the admissibility and correspondence theses can fail by their own conditions, though no study the programme runs can make them fail

## The claim

This is the record's reply of 2026-10-10 to the paragraph "Impossibility
claims" of [CLAIM-tmplkh2i](CLAIM-tmplkh2i.md). It is decisive and checkable: it is read off the
record's own rule for defeat conditions.

[CLAIM-tmplkh2i](CLAIM-tmplkh2i.md) says that two defeat conditions are modal universals, and
that "No finite record of failures shows that something cannot be done, so
the admissibility thesis cannot fail by its own condition." The first half is
right. The second does not follow, because a record of failures is not the
only thing that can meet a defeat condition. luria.yaml's blurb for
`defeated_if`: "what would defeat the thesis — a kind of result or argument".
An argument counts.

The two conditions:

- [CLAIM-077](CLAIM-077.md) is defeated if "No family of observable distinctions can be fixed
  independently of the transports being compared, so that the admissibility
  condition cannot be checked without assuming the fidelity it is meant to
  constrain."
- [CLAIM-143](CLAIM-143.md)'s second disjunct: "no correspondence can be validated
  independently of the fidelity it is used to measure ([QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) answered
  negatively)."

Each is met by an argument that shows the impossibility: for instance, a
proof that any procedure for fixing the distinctions or validating the
correspondence takes a fidelity judgement as an input. [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) asks
whether such a procedure exists, and a negative answer to it, argued, is
exactly what [CLAIM-143](CLAIM-143.md) names. Impossibility is shown this way throughout
mathematics: that no fraction p/q squares to 2 is not established by trying
fractions, but by an argument that any such fraction leads to a
contradiction (if p² = 2q² with p/q in lowest terms, p is even, p = 2m, so
4m² = 2q² and q² = 2m², so q is even too, against lowest terms).

So the two theses can fail by their own conditions. What [CLAIM-tmplkh2i](CLAIM-tmplkh2i.md) gets
right is narrower: no study the programme could run meets them. The
programme's experiments therefore do not test [CLAIM-077](CLAIM-077.md) or [CLAIM-143](CLAIM-143.md); only
argument does, and the record should say so where it lists what the
experiments test.

## What it answers

The fourth point of [CLAIM-tmplkh2i](CLAIM-tmplkh2i.md), which that entry's version 2 narrows on
this entry's account. The rest of that objection (comparative conditions
without margins, possibility theses with universal-negative conditions)
stands, and is met by restatements elsewhere.

## What it does not say

- It does not say an argument meeting either condition exists, or that the
  theses are true.
- It does not cover [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md)'s first disjunct, two realizations that agree
  "on every independently anchored relational invariant tried" and are judged
  different acts. That is a different case: it can be met by a study, but a
  study that meets it shows only that the invariants tried were incomplete,
  not that no invariant is complete, which is what [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md) says. Restating
  it is left to the owner, since [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md) is the owner's own position.
