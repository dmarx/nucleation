# CLAIM-730: Where a slowly revised governing level (a statute, an objective, a classification) constrains a fast process, the fast process can change the world before the slow level can be revised, so those it harms have no route to the rule as fast as its application reaches them, and the rule dominates them without anyone actively intervening; where the slow level is open to revision on demand, the same separation of timescales keeps the fast process accountable instead

## The claim

A33 §4 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):
eligibility decisions are made fast; the rules governing them change
slowly, and the statute more slowly still. "From the standpoint of a
caseworker making decisions throughout the day, those higher-level
structures function as constraints." Then:

> The slow level doesn't have to respond quickly to exercise enormous
> power. A statute, algorithmic objective, operating procedure, or
> administrative classification can remain relatively stable while
> governing millions of rapid decisions.

"**A slow variable can govern a fast process, while the fast process
changes the world before the slow variable can be revised.**" "The slow
level possesses enduring authority over the fast level. But affected
people may lack a sufficiently fast route to change the slow level when
its application becomes harmful." Hence "temporal domination without
constant active intervention by a controller": the rule, once set, needs
no one to act for it to keep producing consequences.

**The other side** (A33 §8). "Timescale separation can be
the basis of effective constraint rather than its destruction." An AI
agent's permissions, transaction limits and budget, set beforehand,
constrain fast execution without real-time review: "procedural friction
can be selectively *compiled into enforceable operating constraints*".
The distinction is "between **slow review after action** and **slowly
established rules that constrain action before it happens**". The
condition: "the slower governing process must itself remain open to
legitimate revision. Otherwise, historically contingent decisions become
entrenched as seemingly natural limits on what anyone may do."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)**: where some degrees of freedom settle fast relative to
  others, the relation they settle into is a constraint over the window
  between the two timescales. The claim reads a governing rule as such a
  constraint and asks who can revise it within the window. It rests on
  [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md), which is why it is supposed: the dynamical claim is not shown
  to hold of rules.
- **[CLAIM-423](CLAIM-423.md)** (the owner's): components at different speeds differ in
  which forms of influence they can accumulate and keep, and so in which
  holds authority over others. This is one case: the slow level holds
  authority because those it harms cannot reach it at its speed of
  application.
- **[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)** defines domination as a capacity, "used or not"; the claim's
  domination needs no use beyond the rule's standing operation.
- A33 §4 also says automation "accelerated one timescale without
  proportionately increasing another's capacity", so errors are produced
  faster than they are found; that is [CLAIM-462](CLAIM-462.md)'s action outrunning
  correction, recorded there as a note. [CLAIM-731](CLAIM-731.md) is the same
  asymmetry stated as cost. The owner's [CLAIM-710](CLAIM-710.md) is what compiled
  constraints preserve: points at which the process stays open.
- [CASE-285](../cases.d/CASE-285.md) proposes the study that would separate the two clauses.
- **[CLAIM-711](CLAIM-711.md)**, from the first share of the same exchange, holds this
  claim's second clause, from A33 §8 and A35 §7, as an objection to the
  "characterizes" reading of [CLAIM-710](CLAIM-710.md): slowly set constraints with fast
  actuators keep a process interruptible without delay. This claim is
  distinct in its first clause, from A33 §4, which that one does not hold:
  the same separation of timescales, with the slow level closed to those
  the fast process harms, is temporal domination with no one actively
  intervening. The two complement each other; which clause holds depends on
  whether the slow level can be reached.

## What it does not say

- Not that faster processes defeat slower ones. A33 §8: "It's worth
  resisting the idea that faster processes inevitably defeat slower ones."
- Not that slow rules are illegitimate. Its first clause is a hazard of the
  arrangement, its second the remedy, and which holds depends on whether
  the slow level can be reached.

## Note of 2026-11-17: The HKMA discussion paper, read in part
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1931 — new reading (Skimmed) and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

HKMA 2026, read in part ([NOTE-2018](../notes.d/NOTE-2018.md), [THEORY-1931](../theory.d/THEORY-1931.md)): it corroborates both halves of the claim independently ([THEORY-1931](../theory.d/THEORY-1931.md) carries `corroborates`). The harm: "Numerous individually minor actions may cause serious harm before review catches up"; "The last opportunity for human intervention may occur several steps before the harm becomes visible" (p. 18). The remedy: limits set beforehand in the agent harness, "velocity limits that are commensurate not only with technical constraints, but also with the demonstrated effectiveness of oversight arrangements" (p. 19), design that takes "account of detection time, decision time and stopping time" (p. 37), and human-in-command authority to "define, change, restrict, suspend or withdraw the deployment's mandate" (p. 7), the slow level kept open to revision. It is non-binding guidance, not evidence that the remedy works. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-22: CASE-285's test of the two clauses, run in a model
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CASE-tmp8riid CLAIM-tmpsaql8 — new; the executed study and claim of LEAD-068, filed 2026-11-22 -->

CASE-285 said "If precommitted limits restore it without pauses, CLAIM-730's second clause is supported over its first." In CASE-tmp8riid's model they did: precommitted limits won back the share of the lost intervention capacity they covered, keeping 0.92 to 0.99 of the fast arm's benefit, where pauses won it back only by giving up the speed. The second clause is thereby shown possible in a model. The first clause is not tested: the model has no affected party and never revises a limit, so it cannot show the domination the first clause describes or its absence. And when speed also multiplied the volume of actions, the limits' escalations of benign actions saturated the fixed reviewers, with held actions waiting months: the slow level kept the fast process answerable only while it had the capacity to hear what it held. CLAIM-tmpsaql8 states the result. No status changes.
