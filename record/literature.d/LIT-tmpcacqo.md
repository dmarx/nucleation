# LIT-tmpcacqo: The Depth of the Body

Christopher Register (2025), *Philosophers' Imprint 25: 47, 1–14* — DOI-10.3998/phimp.7576

## Key takeaways

- The body has a dimension of "depth" (how abstract or high-level its parts are) as well as spatial breadth. Register argues that body parts are fairly high-level objects, and bodily rights and interests concern only events that are not "high-level neutral". So a person's BCI software can be a body part while the cloud server implementing it is not.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

A body extends through space, so it has breadth. It also has depth: roughly, the level of abstraction of its parts. For moral purposes the body is characteristically fairly high-level, so individual molecules are not really within the "moral body", the body that figures in bodily rights and interests. Register develops this notion and uses it to resolve puzzles about brain–computer interfaces, some of whose computation runs on external or cloud hardware owned by third parties.
