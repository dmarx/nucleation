# THEORY-tmpkd2bw: In Hungary, 1994–2016, state-owned firms' advertising, and not private firms', shifted sharply toward print and online outlets when they became connected to the governing right and away when the 2015 Orbán–Simicska fallout ended a connection, at all five such events studied; and the outlets' share of articles on corruption scandals moved the opposite way at the three events with content data

## Source

Szeidl and Szucs, "Media Capture Through Favor Exchange", *Econometrica*
89(1): 281–310 (2021), [LIT-tmp6b6sj](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6b6sj.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmpefpic](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpefpic.md),
with Appendix A.2 of the supplement.

## What was actually shown

Monthly advertising by the 500 largest private advertisers and all
state-owned and government-agency advertisers (Kantar Media, list prices),
compared around five events.

- **Changes of government.** The right-connected daily's share, against the
  left-connected daily, of state-owned firms' advertising went from 16% to
  72% under the 1998–2002 right government and from 47% to 95% after 2010.
  Private firms' share tracked relative circulation. Regression: +26 to +34
  percentage points under right governments, relative to private firms.
- **Metropol, bought by a business partner of Simicska in 2011.** Its share
  of state-owned print advertising went from below 20% to above 50% at once,
  with private advertising unchanged.
- **The fallout, February 2015.** The share of state-owned advertising in
  Simicska's two dailies fell from above 60% to below 20%, beginning months
  before the fallout was public.
- **Origo, new editor (2014) and new connected owner (2016).** State-owned
  firms' share rose by 18–22 and then 57–61 percentage points relative to
  private firms. Visitor shares did not change.

Corruption coverage, as the share of an outlet's articles matching a
hand-built list of scandals: after the fallout Magyar Nemzet's rose by 1.27
points (s.e. 0.23) while the left daily's did not change. Relative to
Index, Origo's fell by 0.44 points after the new editor and 1.36 after the
new owner.

What could have come out otherwise: private advertisers could have moved
with the state-owned ones, as they would if audiences had changed. Online
audience demographics, and the politics of the 2013 readers' demographic
profiles, could have diverged. Neither happened. In the fallout, the
advertising moved before the coverage, which is the reverse of what an
audience-led explanation predicts.

## What this does not say

- **Not why the content moved.** The reduced form cannot tell an owner who
  shared the government's view from one paid by it. That split needs the
  structural model ([THEORY-tmplydnj](THEORY-tmplydnj.md), Proposed).
- **Not that left-wing governments did otherwise.** The authors take no
  stand on favoritism under left governments.
- **Not that advertising was the only favor**, nor corruption coverage the
  only slant.
- **Not a significance level for every comparison.** Origo's drop after the
  new editor has a standard error of 0.26 with period effects and 0.105
  with month effects.
- **Not beyond Hungary.** The authors' worldwide reading rests on a review
  of qualitative reports.
