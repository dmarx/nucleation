# THEORY-tmpxaj68: The brain's visceromotor and interoceptive regions form one intrinsic system that reproduces their tract-traced anatomy and contains the hubs of the default-mode and salience networks

## Source

- Jordan E. Theriault, Yuta Katsumi, Henning M. Reimann et al. (2025),
  "It's not the thought that counts: Allostasis at the core of brain
  function", *Neuron* 113(24), [LIT-996](../literature.d/LIT-996.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmp5s3k4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5s3k4.md):
  "The Allostatic Architecture of the Mammalian Brain" and Figures 1–2.
- Lisa Feldman Barrett (2017), [LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), read in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md): Figure 6, the
  3 T system from Kleckner et al., 280 participants replicated in 270.

## What was actually shown

As reported; the primary studies were not read here.

- **Seeds from anatomy.** 21 regions: 14 subcortical nuclei important for
  allostasis (among them the central amygdala, hypothalamic nuclei, PAG,
  parabrachial nucleus, VTA, raphe, locus coeruleus and the nucleus of
  the solitary tract) and the 7 cortical regions receiving their
  monosynaptic projections in non-human tract-tracing: the anterior
  midcingulate, pregenual and subgenual anterior cingulate, ventral
  anterior insula, dorsal mid and posterior insula, and hippocampus.
- **Intrinsic connectivity reproduces the anatomy.** At 7 T (N = 90),
  replicating 3 T (N = 550), resting-state connectivity "replicated 100%
  of the monosynaptic cortico-cortical connections, 94% of the
  monosynaptic subcortico-cortical connections, and 96% of the
  monosynaptic subcortico-subcortical connections". The system also
  takes in regions not seeded, including primary motor cortex and the
  cerebellum.
- **It contains the named networks' hubs (Fig. 2).** Its topography
  overlaps the central autonomic network, the skeletomotor network, the
  salience/cingulo-opercular network and a heat-pain signature. k-means
  clustering of the 21 seed maps yields a "default-mode-like" and a
  "salience/cingulo-opercular-like" motif, which overlap in the
  connectome's "rich club" hubs.

This could have come out otherwise: intrinsic connectivity could have
missed many of the tract-traced connections, or the seeds could have
formed a system separate from the networks studied for psychological
functions.

## What this does not say

- **Not that the brain's primary function is allostasis.** That is the
  paper's synthesis, and the authors write that their goal "was not to
  claim our 'allostasis-first' hypothesis is correct".
- **Not that the default-mode and salience networks do not serve their
  psychological functions.** Overlap of topography is not identity of
  function. The paper's stronger claim, that attributing psychological
  functions to those networks is "destined to fail", is an inference
  this account does not hold.
- **Not that resting-state activity is mostly interoceptive.** The paper
  asserts it from cited work; this account does not rest on it.
- **Not anything about feeling.** The anatomy says where regulation is
  organised, not whether or why its states are felt. It gives [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md)
  no case.
- **Not the computational reading of the laminar gradient.** That
  predictions flow outwards from limbic cortex is a separate, more
  speculative claim (C3 in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md), C3 in [NOTE-tmp5s3k4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5s3k4.md)).
