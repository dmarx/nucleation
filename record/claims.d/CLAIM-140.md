# CLAIM-140: The October thesis replaces what a receiver can reconstruct with what the analyst's probe system can identify as the test of what survives, and no turn argues the change

## The claim

The manuscript's thesis ([CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)) says what survives is what "a receiving
system can still reconstruct and act upon". The October thesis
([CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)) says "Fidelity concerns which independently identifiable,
decision-relevant relational distinctions persist through those
transformations." Identifiability in A218 is relative to the analyst's probe
system 𝒪. Its theorem target defines it: θ ∼_𝒪 θ′ when
P_θ(Y_𝒪) = P_θ′(Y_𝒪), and a signature s is identifiable "precisely when"
θ ∼_𝒪 θ′ implies s(θ) = s(θ′). Nothing in that definition mentions a
receiver.

The receiver did not leave in one step. A151 kept "which distinctions a
receiver can still recover and use", and outline v7 (A178) kept "appropriately
situated observers can recover, test, and act upon" ([CLAIM-133](CLAIM-133.md)). A203
kept "recoverable, usable". A214's thesis paragraph has no receiver, and A218
puts identifiability in its place. In A218 the receiver survives in two
places, outside the thesis: §8, "Receiver knowledge and adaptation enter
explicitly into the interpretation problem", and Ch13.5, "Receiver knowledge
and decoder side information".

The two tests come apart in both directions.

- **Identifiable but unusable.** A distinction can be identifiable from the
  analyst's "sparse, multi-view, context-indexed observation" (A218 Part III)
  and still not be recoverable by a receiver who has only the target text.
  A218's Ch13.6, "Identifiable but task-irrelevant factors", comes near this
  and keeps it outside the thesis.
- **Usable but unidentifiable.** A receiver with side information can recover
  a distinction that the analyst's probes cannot identify. This is Ch13.5's
  "decoder side information", and [CLAIM-074](CLAIM-074.md)'s point that fidelity is
  relative to the interpreter's learning history.

[TERM-034](../terms.d/TERM-034.md) ("relative to an interpreter") and [CLAIM-074](CLAIM-074.md) are on the
receiver's side. The October thesis keeps the receiver in the fidelity profile
and drops it from the criterion. No turn argues the change: U59 and U60 asked
for the argument to be tied together and for a new proposal, and the owner
raised nothing about the receiver.

## What it does not say

It does not say the analyst's notion is wrong. Identifiability is what makes a
fidelity measure non-arbitrary, which is [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md)'s worry about
building the preferred notion of fidelity into the measurement. It says only
that the thesis now needs both conditions, or an argument for dropping one. It
does not say the October thesis is false.
