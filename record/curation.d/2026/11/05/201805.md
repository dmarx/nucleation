After CLAIM-597 merged, the owner wrote: "this sounds like an opportunity
to connect to Nucleation's inquiries involving dissipative structure.
also, I'm wondering if social domains like international aid and
development might have some useful theory regarding how to evolve

taxation or exit. The assistant chose the works.

## What was read

- **Moore 2004**, revenues and state formation (LIT-tmpsb4vt): the fiscal
  contract, and rents and aid as unearned income.
- **Moss, Pettersson and van de Walle 2006**, the aid–institutions paradox
  (LIT-tmp4d1o2).
- **Ostrom, Gibson, Shivakumar and Andersson 2002**, Sida's Aid,
  Incentives, and Sustainability (LIT-tmpnb22c): the Samaritan's dilemma.
  Its NOTE is Skimmed, since chapters 3, 7 and 8 and appendix A were not
  read in full.
- **Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock 2013**, PDIA and capability traps
  (LIT-tmptn3yr), from the working paper, and its companion **Pritchett,
  Woolcock and Andrews**, Looking Like a State (LIT-tmphg6vu).
- **Azam, Devarajan and O'Connell 1999**, Aid Dependence Reconsidered
  (LIT-tmpahupp). It was read to check whether the assistant's

Dates for working papers that give only a month use the month's first
day. PDF creation dates were not used.

## What the readings change

- **The supply is not neutral.** CLAIM-597's supplied repair leaves the
  organization's own capacity alone. In every aid reading the supply
  lowers it, by the Samaritan's dilemma, by unused revenue machinery
  decaying, or by funding for form crowding out function.
- **What predicted lasting.** The Kenya summary names resources, capacity
  and motivation together, linkages formed early, and gradual exit in which
  participants operated independently before closure. Azam et al.'s
  optimal exit instead cuts aid up front, and their recipient is better off
  staying dependent.
- **Dated notes** go on CLAIM-597, CLAIM-566, CLAIM-558, QUESTION-398,
  QUESTION-399, QUESTION-386 and QUESTION-085, under "the readings on aid
  dependency and self-sufficiency".

## The assistant's contributions

- **CLAIM-tmpw4ai2** (demonstrated) connects CLAIM-597 to the dissipative
  structures. Upkeep that a structure draws in through its own intact
  state, as a flame does, has an ignition threshold, a supply level below
  which it collapses, and an outage after which it does not relight.
  Supplied upkeep has none of the three. It gives QUESTION-085 three
  signatures to look for in placement records.
- **CLAIM-tmpoh07b** (demonstrated) models aid crowding out capacity that is
  learned by doing.
  - Azam et al. already have kept capacity falling with aid's share, and
    the claim credits them.
  - Not in their paper: recovery after exit grows linearly with how long aid
    did all the work, because work is capped by capacity. Aid used after
    the organization's own capacity leaves its learning untouched.
  - Azam et al. call the harmful rule "gap-filling", so the benign one is
    named capacity-first.
- **Reproduction.** scratch/formal/supplied_maintenance_toys.py asserts
  every number in both claims.
