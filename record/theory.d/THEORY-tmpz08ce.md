# THEORY-tmpz08ce: After every function associated with consciousness is explained (discrimination, integration, report, access, attention, control), why these functions are accompanied by experience remains open, because those phenomena are defined by their functions and experience, what it is like for the subject, is not; so experience cannot be reductively explained by any mechanism, though it may be related to the physical by further laws

## Source

- Chalmers (1995), "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness", *Journal
  of Consciousness Studies* 2(3), 200–219, [LIT-406](../literature.d/LIT-406.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md) from the author's copy (sections reliable, pages the copy's).
- Nagel (1974), "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?", [LIT-096](../literature.d/LIT-096.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-089](../notes.d/NOTE-089.md) from a transcription of the *Mortal Questions* reprint, located
  by footnote.

## What was actually shown

**The criterion (Nagel).** "An organism has conscious mental states if and
only if there is something that it is like to be that organism." That
subjective character belongs to a type of point of view, not to an
individual. Functional, intentional and causal-role analyses are each
compatible with its absence, so none can be what it consists in ([NOTE-089](../notes.d/NOTE-089.md),
C1–C2). Reduction elsewhere succeeds by leaving a point of view for a more
objective description of the same thing, as with sound or lightning. For
experience that move leads away from the phenomenon (C4).

**Easy and hard (Chalmers §§2–3).** The easy problems are explaining
discrimination, integration, report, access to one's own states,
attention, deliberate control and the sleep–wake difference. Their
phenomena are functionally definable, so a mechanism that performs the
function explains them: "you have explained the verbal report function but
not reportability" is a conceptual mistake. "You have explained
discrimination, integration and report but not how it is experienced" is
not. The gene, life and learning have no such further question.

**No extra ingredient helps (§5).** Chaos, nonalgorithmic processing, new
neurophysiology and quantum mechanics are all more structure and dynamics,
and "from dynamics, one only gets more dynamics". Vitalism is
disanalogous: the vitalist doubted that mechanisms could perform life's
functions; here no function is in doubt.

**Case studies (§4).** Crick and Koch's 35–75 Hz binding and Baars's
global workspace would explain binding and accessibility. They leave "why
should global accessibility give rise to conscious experience?" open.
Edelman, Dennett and Jackendoff get the same verdict in a sentence each.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The test could have found some
other phenomenon, besides experience, for which "the function but not the
phenomenon" is not a conceptual mistake; Chalmers calls the hard problem
"almost unique" and names none. Or a mechanism could have been offered
whose explanation of the functions left those pressing the question with
nothing further to ask. The paper reports neither.

## What this does not say

- **That the gap is proved.** The step from "conceptually coherent that the
  process occurs without experience" to "experience is not entailed by the
  physical" is stated in §5 and deferred to *The Conscious Mind* ([LIT-441](../literature.d/LIT-441.md),
  unread), including the reply to a posteriori necessity, which appears
  here as a parenthetical "technical note" ([NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md), C4).
- **That physicalism is false.** Nagel's conclusion is that it is not
  refuted, only "not yet understandable", like "matter is energy" to a
  pre-Socratic ([NOTE-089](../notes.d/NOTE-089.md), C5). Chalmers goes further, to "naturalistic
  dualism": experience as fundamental, linked to the physical by
  psychophysical laws, with physics causally closed (§6). The gap does not
  by itself decide between them.
- **The positive programme.** Structural coherence (the structure of
  experience mirrors the structure of awareness), organisational invariance
  ([THEORY-tmpdz76a](THEORY-tmpdz76a.md)) and the double-aspect theory of information, which its
  author calls "more of an idea than a theory", are built on the gap and
  stand or fall separately. On structural coherence, a global-workspace
  theory is a theory of the mechanism of awareness, and so of a correlate
  of experience.
- **That neuroscience of consciousness is idle.** On this account it
  isolates correlates and explains the structure of experience; what it
  cannot do is explain why there is experience at all.
- **That the vitalism disanalogy is settled.** Dennett's reply ([LIT-397](../literature.d/LIT-397.md))
  holds that the analogy holds where it matters: in both cases the residue
  is defined as whatever survives the explanation of every function.
  Neither paper offers evidence that would decide between them ([LIT-397](../literature.d/LIT-397.md),
  Standing).

## Connections

- **Rival.** Illusionism ([THEORY-tmpspj51](THEORY-tmpspj51.md)) denies the explanandum: what
  survives the explanation of the functions is a conviction, produced by
  introspective representation, that something is left. The two cannot
  both be right about whether the further question has an answer to find.
  Chalmers's later meta-problem paper ([LIT-425](../literature.d/LIT-425.md), [THEORY-tmpltl13](THEORY-tmpltl13.md)) asks both
  sides to explain that conviction, and argues that only strong
  illusionism dissolves the hard problem.
- [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md) takes this gap as the background reason behavioural evidence
  underdetermines experience in AI. Block's "Harder Problem" ([LIT-639](../literature.d/LIT-639.md)),
  which is a source there, is the epistemic sequel: for a superficial
  functional duplicate on another physical basis we lack even a conception
  of the evidence.
- [THEORY-091](THEORY-091.md) (deficits cannot show a capacity is phenomenal consciousness's
  function) and [THEORY-094](THEORY-094.md) (report and no-report paradigms do not isolate
  experience) are the methodological form of the access–experience
  distinction this paper calls awareness and experience.
- IIT's identification of experience with integrated information sits
  oddly beside §2, which lists "the integration of information" among the
  easy problems. On this account Φ is either a measure of an easy-problem
  function or a proposed psychophysical law ([NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md), Connections; the
  pairing is the reader's).
