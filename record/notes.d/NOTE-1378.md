# NOTE-1378: Carstens and Land — Hyperstition: an introduction

## Contribution

It gives the CCRU's term a quotable definition, in Land's words, put for
a reader outside the collective: Carstens asks for it "in language that
normal academics (such as my supervisor!) can understand". It adds no
argument and no evidence. What it adds is the definition, three examples,
and the statement that the conditions under which the loop works are
unknown.

## Key insight

A hyperstition is a belief whose circulation is part of the cause of its
coming true. Land sets it against superstition: "Superstitions are merely
false beliefs, but hyperstitions – by their very existence as ideas –
function causally to bring about their own reality." The difference is
in the causal role of the belief, not in its truth at the time it is
held. Land says plainly that it can start false: hyperstition can, in
"favorable" circumstances, "transmute lies into truths".

## Assumptions

- **A loop through culture.** The definition needs a channel from the
  idea to the world. Land names two: investment, in markets and in
  cyberspace ("confidence acts as an effective tonic, and inversely"),
  and cultural and political investment, as in Jerusalem's case.
- **Sensitivity of the object to its postulation.** "a nonlinear
  complication of epistemology, based upon the sensitivity of the object
  to its postulation". He says this is not social construction: "The
  hyperstitional object is no mere figment of 'social constuction', but
  it is in a very real way 'conjured' into being by the approach taken
  to it."
- **No stated conditions.** The circumstances in which the loop runs are
  "'favorable' circumstances whose exact nature requires further
  investigation". Nothing in the interview narrows them.

## Key results

There are no results. The definitional answer (R3) holds four claims:

- **Definition.** "Hyperstition is a positive feedback circuit including
  culture as a component. It can be defined as the experimental
  (techno-)science of self-fulfilling prophecies."
- **Two directions.** Confidence in capitalist economics works "as an
  effective tonic, and inversely": the loop can run down as well as up.
- **Examples.** "The (fictional) idea of Cyberspace contributed to the
  influx of investment that rapidly converted it into a technosocial
  reality." Abrahamic monotheism, by treating Jerusalem as a holy city
  with a special destiny, "has ensured the cultural and political
  investment that makes this assertion into a truth."
- **From false to true.** "Hyperstition is thus able, under 'favorable'
  circumstances whose exact nature requires further investigation, to
  transmute lies into truths."

The other answers are of another kind. R1 says what apocalypse reveals
is "an alien order of time", shown in "coincidences" and in a
"cabbalistic pattern occulted in ordinary languages". R4+5 takes the line
from Carpenter's *In the Mouth of Madness*, "I thought I was making it
up, but all the time they were telling me what to write", as hyperstition
"at an extraordinary pitch", with human intelligences as "mere
incubators" for the Old Ones. R7 says capitalism "incarnates
hyperstitional dynamics at an unprecedented and unsurpassable level of
intensity". R8 puts hyperstition "equipoised between fiction and
technology".

## Concepts

- **Hyperstition**: as C1. Set against superstition, a "merely false"
  belief with no causal role.
- **Hyperstitional object**: an object sensitive to its postulation, not
  dissolved into "cognitive or semiotic structures" and still "conjured"
  by the approach taken to it.
- **Theory-fiction** is not used in the interview; R8's "equipoised
  between fiction and technology" is the nearest it comes. The LIT's
  keyword is from outside the text.

## Connections

Land gives the term as "the experimental (techno-)science of
self-fulfilling prophecies", so Merton's paper ([LIT-1398](../literature.d/LIT-1398.md), Unavailable)
is its acknowledged ancestor in substance, though the interview never
names Merton. Soros's reflexivity ([LIT-1397](../literature.d/LIT-1397.md), [NOTE-1362](NOTE-1362.md)) states the
same loop as a two-way feedback between "the participants' views and the
actual course of events", with its negative direction given equal place,
and as a limit on social science rather than a programme. MacKenzie's
performativity ([LIT-1395](../literature.d/LIT-1395.md), Unavailable) is the empirical treatment for
markets. Land's "confidence acts as an effective tonic, and inversely"
is the one place the interview admits a loop that can run against the
idea.

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-1396](../literature.d/LIT-1396.md)'s own body says the wrong thing about Merton.** It says
  Merton's prophecy "begins as a false definition of a present
  situation", whereas a hyperstition "is typically a fiction or a
  forecast, never false in that sense, only not yet true". The interview
  does not say that, and its Jerusalem example and its last sentence say
  the reverse: a hyperstition can begin as a lie ("transmute lies into
  truths") and make "this assertion into a truth". The LIT's distinction
  should be withdrawn or kept as the record's, not Land's. The second
  difference the LIT draws, that the mechanism is circulation, the
  interview supports: "by their very existence as ideas".
- **[CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md)** cites Land's definition, and the definition is as
  quoted. The interview gives no condition under which the loop runs, so
  it supports the claim's premise, that some judgements' circulation
  helps make them true, but gives nothing for telling such judgements
  apart from others. That is the part of [CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md) that bears weight.
- **[CLAIM-425](../claims.d/CLAIM-425.md)'s rule** (authority from grounds, not visibility) and the
  "inversely" of R3 together say a judgement's visibility can make what
  it says less likely too, which [CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md)'s title allows for only as
  "what it will bring about".

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- An interview, not an essay, by the term's leading user, for a
  student's thesis. No sources, no cases worked through.
- The conditions are left open by its author.
- The provenance is the archive's: dated 2009, place of first appearance
  not given.
- **Slips.** "'social constuction'" is misspelled on the page. Q6's
  "particularly in relation to the apocalypse meme, which is not at all"
  is garbled as printed.

## Open questions

- Under what conditions does an idea's circulation make it more likely,
  and under what conditions less? Land leaves this open, and it is what
  [CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md) would need.
