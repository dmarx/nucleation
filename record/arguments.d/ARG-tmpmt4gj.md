# ARG-tmpmt4gj: Broido and Clauset's test of 928 network degree distributions, as a source, supports for network degrees that a power law is weak evidence of its mechanism and that the tail must be fitted against the lognormal first, while naming no dynamical test

## The inference

[CLAIM-803](../claims.d/CLAIM-803.md), for network degree distributions. Ground: [THEORY-tmphcnj3](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphcnj3.md).

## Critical questions (source)

**Does the work say this, read in context?** Yes: "Because the shape of a
degree distribution imposes only modest constraints on overall network
structure, it represents relatively weak evidence when trying to
distinguish generating mechanisms ... even when the distribution's
functional form is clear" (Introduction); and "a weak discriminator
between mechanisms" (Discussion). It adds the converse: "some variations
of preferential attachment do not produce power-law degree
distributions", so the mechanism does not fix the law either.

**Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** For network degrees, and
for the fitting clause. Not for the dynamical tests.

**Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** No. The
dispute over the paper's rarity verdict turns on the definition of a
power law (pure above k_min against regularly varying); under either
definition the claim's point that the shape weakly discriminates
mechanisms is the paper's own, not a consequence of its tallies.

## Verdict

Support. [THEORY-tmphcnj3](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphcnj3.md) joins the claim's grounds.
