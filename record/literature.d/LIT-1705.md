summary: >-
  An overview of the SIDE model by two of its authors. Deindividuation theory held that anonymity and immersion in a group cut self-awareness and so released antinormative behaviour. SIDE reverses this. Anonymity within a group makes whichever identity is salient more salient. When that identity is a group identity, people conform more to its local norms, and behaviour is more socially regulated, not less. A second, strategic dimension concerns anonymity to an audience: being unidentifiable to a powerful out-group frees members to act on in-group norms it would punish. The model is applied to text chat and email, webcams, online communities and online collective action. The chapter argues that online and offline action share the same predictors: shared identity, efficacy and anger at injustice.
