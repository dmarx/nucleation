# THEORY-1603: Scheepers and Ellemers present social identity theory: social identity is the part of the self-concept that derives from group membership together with its value and emotional significance, people strive for a positive social identity through favourable comparison with out-groups, and members of low-status groups choose mobility, collective action or social creativity by permeability, legitimacy and stability

## The account

Social identity is the part of the self-concept derived from
membership "together with the value or emotional significance attached
to that membership" (Tajfel 1978). "Tajfel argued that people strive for
a positive social identity, just as they strive for a positive personal
identity" (p. 6); a positive social identity serves "basic human needs
like the need for certainty and the need for self-esteem" and the search
for meaning (pp. 7–8). Identification has cognitive, affective and
behavioural components. At work, identification is said to matter more
than the "selfish concerns" of interdependence. An imagined common
in-group raised children's helping of an immigrant classmate.

## Evidence

Summaries without effect sizes ([NOTE-1315](../notes.d/NOTE-1315.md)). The self-esteem strand is
contested elsewhere in the record ([NOTE-1278](../notes.d/NOTE-1278.md)). The common in-group result
is one two-study paper, with intentions and offers as outcomes.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) needs "a criterion of the self's extent that is
independent of what the agent cares about". Tajfel's definition puts
caring into social identity itself; the theory's measures separate
self-categorization from satisfaction and commitment; and the chapter
reports that commitment, the caring component, predicts behaviour
better. [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)'s note of 2026-10-29 records the same.
