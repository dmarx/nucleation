# LIT-090: Could wavefunctions simultaneously represent knowledge and reality?

Jonte R. Hance, John Rarity, James Ladyman (2021), *Quantum Studies: Mathematics and Foundations 9(3):333–341 (2022; online 2022-04-27); arXiv preprint first* — [ARXIV-2101.06436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06436)

## Key takeaways

- Harrigan and Spekkens' formal ψ-ontic and ψ-epistemic are contradictories only by stipulation. On the informal meanings (represents reality / represents knowledge), a wavefunction can be both, so PBR-type no-go theorems rule out overlapping support in state space, not epistemic readings as such.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

In quantum foundations, "ontic" (about what exists) and "epistemic" (about knowledge) have been identified with Harrigan and Spekkens' formal definitions within the ontological models framework, and those definitions are mutually exclusive. The authors argue that nothing in the informal notions prevents a wavefunction from representing reality and knowledge at once. They propose that the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem, and much else in the debate, be reconsidered in that light.
