# THEORY-2125: Moll and Kadipasaoglu argue that social perspective-taking, tracking what a partner has desired, witnessed or said, emerges at least a year before level-1 visual perspective-taking because joint attention builds a common ground with specific partners, while young children treat co-presence as shared perception, and that the two converge at level 2 at 4 to 5 years

## The account

Joint attention gives infants a common ground with particular people, so
they track what a partner has shared with them well before they can work
out what the partner can see. Co-presence misleads them into treating the
situation as shared. Only at level 2 do social and visual
perspective-taking proceed together.

## Evidence

A review of developmental experiments, mostly from the authors' paradigms.
