## What did not enter, and why

Nothing. All twelve readings became sources, which fits the owner's
hypothesis here. Three caveats apply.

- Most of the twelve are philosophical arguments, and their THEORYs are
  `Proposed` accounts whose `promote_when` names a kind of argument or
  construction, not an experiment. The data behind them are thin: Vetter's
  hand-coded corpus count, and Levin's cited experiments, which show that
  coupling changes outcomes, not that it changes a self.
- Three readings said "No THEORY" in their Bearing ([NOTE-125](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-125.md), [NOTE-137](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-137.md),
  [NOTE-112](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-112.md)). Each meant that no existing THEORY was supported. Each work
  states a definite thesis with its conditions, so each was filed.
- Varzi's entry gave two documents, one formal and one philosophical,
  because the result about axiom systems holds whatever "part" means and
  should not be cited as support for the verdict.

## Choices made

- **No relation edges to existing THEORYs.** `rivals`, `presupposes` and
  `extends` would have had `luria link --fix` write converses into
  documents this batch was told to leave alone. The candidates are named in
  each Connections instead: Levin against [THEORY-071](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-071.md) (minimal agency), which
  denies that a homeostat's individuality is agency; Hazelwood presupposing
  Woodward's interventions ([THEORY-138](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-138.md)); and Esfeld and Lam against the
  census in [THEORY-034](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-034.md).
- **No `answers` edge to [QUESTION-035](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)** from Lewis or from Smith and Varzi.
  Both classify or count the question's candidates, and neither supplies a
  criterion. The same for Schwitzgebel and [QUESTION-046](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-046.md).
- **Tags.** Vetter's THEORY adds `linguistics`, which [NOTE-125](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-125.md) called
  defensible because the positive case is built on '-able' semantics and a
  corpus count. Levin's leads with `mereology`, as his LIT does.
