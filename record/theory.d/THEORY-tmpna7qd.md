# THEORY-tmpna7qd: Privileged irresponsibility, on Tronto's account, is the capability of the relatively privileged to refuse unpleasant or burdensome responsibilities, a major source of injustice; a caring democracy would begin by realigning the privileges that keep unfair loads of care responsibility from being discussed, and hear everyone in the reallocation

Tronto (2018), LIT-tmpu3eh7, read in NOTE-tmp0tq2n.

## The account

"a major source of injustice in the world comes from the capability of the
relatively privileged to refuse to accept unpleasant or burdensome
responsibilities (privileged irresponsibility)" (p. 8). Caring Democracy's
first step is "to realign a system of privileges that make it impossible
to discuss how some people take on an unfair load of care
responsibilities", and "everyone's voice must be heard in making these
adjustments" (p. 14).

## What it does not show

- **How privileged irresponsibility works.** The 2018 article uses the
  term without the mechanism, examples or argument of Moral Boundaries.
- **Anything about blame**, or where it lands when an arrangement fails.
