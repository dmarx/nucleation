# THEORY-tmpqma2p: Halperin holds that the Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem excludes, at T > 0 in one and two dimensions and in equilibrium, true long-range order breaking a continuous symmetry whose generator is a sum of local operators, for interactions with finite second moments, and that it says nothing about quasi-long-range order or T = 0 and does not exclude orientational order in a two-dimensional crystal

## Source

Halperin (2018), "On the Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem and its
limitations", Journal of Statistical Physics 175, [LIT-tmp3zns2](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3zns2.md), read in
[NOTE-tmpuwmny](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuwmny.md).

## The account

Mermin and Wagner gave "a similar proof of the absence of long-range
order in one and two dimensions for a Heisenberg ferromagnet or
antiferromagnet, with SU(2) symmetry, or a magnetic system with U(1)
symmetry and an order parameter perpendicular to the symmetry axis"
(§I). Their argument "requires that these second moments be finite"
(§II). Its inequality "follows directly from the quantum version of the
fluctuation dissipation theorem" (§III). It "sheds no light on the
question of whether quasi-long-range order can exist" and "has nothing to
say" about T = 0; and orientational order in a 2D crystal escapes it,
since "the generator of these rotations cannot be written as an
unweighted sum of local operators" (§II). In a tethered membrane the
Gaussian divergence of orientational fluctuations is removed by
nonlinear coupling (§II).

## What it does not license

- "No continuous symmetry is broken in two dimensions", without the
  conditions.
- Any statement about quasi-long-range order, for which see
  [THEORY-tmpaojie](THEORY-tmpaojie.md).
