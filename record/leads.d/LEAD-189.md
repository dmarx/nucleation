- **The prosecution.** It proceeded against the operator and not the company. Prosecutors found "no basis for criminal liability for the Uber corporation" (March 2019); the operator was charged with negligent homicide (September 2020) and pleaded guilty to endangerment, with three years of supervised probation (July 2023). What this lead said would count, "proceedings against the operator alone support [CLAIM-751](../claims.d/CLAIM-751.md)'s contrast", is met for the criminal process. [CLAIM-tmp20uni](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp20uni.md) files the contrast, with [ARG-tmpql8m1](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpql8m1.md); [CLAIM-tmp64hmn](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp64hmn.md) objects that it is a contrast between the criminal law and the NTSB, since a settlement and the state's suspension of testing reached the company.
- **The blameless practice.** Neither Google text documents a postmortem that traces causes to decisions above the teams. The workbook's real exemplar names a staffing limit and routes no action item to it ([CASE-tmptkkgm](../cases.d/CASE-tmptkkgm.md), [ARG-tmp117cw](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp117cw.md), for [CLAIM-750](../claims.d/CLAIM-750.md)). The workbook puts questions about priorities set above the team in reviews across incidents, which [CLAIM-tmpauu5r](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpauu5r.md) files as an objection to how [CLAIM-750](../claims.d/CLAIM-750.md) is tested. So the other thing this lead said would count, "a documented blameless practice that traces causes upstream", is not found within a postmortem, and is left open across incidents.
