# NOTE-tmp2n8he: Rheinberg, Manig, Kliegl, Engeser and Vollmeyer — Flow at work, happiness in leisure

## Contribution

It replicates the paradox of work with flow measured by all its
components rather than by the challenge-skill quadrant, and it shows that
the goal-directedness of the current activity moves flow and
happiness/satisfaction apart: up for flow in both contexts, flat for
happiness at work and down for it in leisure.

## Key insight

Flow and happiness are reported at the same moment and go together a
little, but they answer to different things. Goal-directedness is what
lifts flow; it does not lift happiness, and in leisure it lowers it.

## Assumptions

- **Self-report at the signal is a report of the episode.** Participants
  rated "für jeden Zeitpunkt, an dem ein Signal eintraf" (for each moment a
  signal arrived) the activity under way; 83% of signals were answered
  within ten minutes and a further 9% within twenty (§3.4). Answering was
  on paper, so the timing rests on participants' word (§5).
- **Work and leisure are told apart by place**: "am Arbeitsplatz" against
  "zuhause" or "sonst wo" (§3.5).
- **Goal-directedness is dichotomized**: scale points 6 and 7 count as
  clearly goal-directed, because points 2 to 5 were rarely used (§3.5).

## Key results

- **Sample and design (§3.1-3.4).** 101 employees from Potsdam and
  Berlin, seven random signals a day for a week, 4,603 of 4,949 signals
  answered (93%). Flow: the Flow Short Scale (FKS), ten items (appendix),
  α = .84 over all reports. Happiness/satisfaction: two bipolar items from
  Schallberger's PANAVA, "glücklich vs. unglücklich" and "zufrieden vs.
  unzufrieden". Analysis by mixed models with person, day and time as
  nested random factors.
- **Paradox of work (§4.2).** Flow M = 5.24 at work, 4.81 in leisure,
  F(1, 3850) = 278.3; happiness/satisfaction 4.95 at work, 5.15 in
  leisure, F(1, 3756) = 15.3; both p < .001.
- **Flow and happiness at the same signal (§4.1).** The mean curves over
  the day run opposite, but "auf der Ebene der Einzelmessungen der
  Zusammenhang zwischen Flow und Glück/Zufriedenheit mit r = .36
  (Rohwerte) bzw. r = .30 (individuell z-standardisierte Daten) nicht
  negativ sondern leicht positiv" (at the level of single reports the
  relation is r = .36 raw, .30 within-person standardized: not negative
  but slightly positive).
- **Goal-directedness at work (§4.3.1).** Goal × scale interaction
  F(1, 1710) = 115.2; goal-directedness raises flow, F(1, 1276) = 213.9,
  and not happiness/satisfaction, F(1, 1258) = 0.9.
- **Goal-directedness in leisure (§4.3.2).** Interaction F(1, 2770) =
  203.3; it raises flow, F(1, 2124) = 121.8, and lowers
  happiness/satisfaction, F(1, 2084) = 19.2.
- **Frequency (§5).** 74% of work signals were clearly goal-directed; 54%
  of leisure signals were not. Hence the paradox "partiell als Effekt
  unterschiedlich häufiger Zielausrichtung" (partly as an effect of how
  often activities are goal-directed).
- **Happiness and satisfaction separately (§4.3.2).** They correlate .65
  raw, .47 within-person, and respond alike to goal-directedness.

## Concepts

- **Flow** (§1.1) — "das freudige Aufgehen in einer glatt laufenden
  Tätigkeit, die man trotz hoher Beanspruchung noch gut unter Kontrolle
  hat" (joyful absorption in a smoothly running activity kept under
  control despite high demand). The definition names joy; the scale does
  not: none of the ten FKS items asks about enjoyment or feeling.
- **Paradox of work** (§1.1) — flow more frequent at work than in
  leisure, while happiness and the wish to go on are lower there.
- **Zielausrichtung** (goal-directedness) — whether the current activity
  "soll ein Ergebnis herbeiführen/ein Ziel erreichen" (is meant to bring
  about a result or reach a goal).
- **Valenzgefälle zwischen Ist und Wunsch** (§5) — the authors' post hoc
  explanation: with a goal set, "Verglichen mit dem, was man gerade als
  erstrebenswert vor Augen hat (das Ziel also), ist die jetzige Lage (noch)
  unbefriedigend" (compared with the goal one has in view, the present
  state is still unsatisfying).

## Connections

It replicates Csikszentmihalyi and LeFevre (1989; LIT-tmp6jvaq) and
Schallberger and Pfister (2001; LIT-tmps1x50) with the FKS in place of the
quadrant model, which it criticizes for equating one component of flow
with the whole and for counting above-average demand as flow. Engeser and
Baumann (LIT-tmpu4yh1) report what appears to be the same Potsdam sample
with the full PANAVA and a lagged analysis.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-441, exit 3** (the positivity comes after the episode). Flow and
  happiness are rated at the same signal and correlate positively across
  reports, so on these data some of flow's positivity is in the episode.
  The data do not show that a flow episode is felt as positive: the
  correlation is pooled over all activities, flow is a continuous score
  with no enjoyment item, and work, where flow is highest, is where
  happiness is lower. Evidence against exit 3 as the whole story, with
  Engeser and Baumann's lagged test (NOTE-tmpoe2pz) the stronger part.
- **CLAIM-441, exit 1 and CLAIM-433** (which valence). No measure here
  separates intrinsic pleasantness from goal-conduciveness valence, and the
  happiness/satisfaction items are general valence. That goal-directedness
  raises flow and not happiness says flow's positivity, such as it is, is
  not carried by goal salience. It does not say what carries it.
- **CLAIM-440 and TERM-076** (sign as running ahead of or behind a
  reference course). A parallel only: the authors' post hoc gap between
  the present state and the goal in view is a sign-from-a-reference reading
  of why salient goals lower happiness, and they say it fails for work.
  No edge.
- **CLAIM-162** (no interest overridden in flow). Background: flow is
  highest where the activity is clearly goal-directed, which fits a
  coincidence of immediate and longer-run value, but nothing here measures
  override.

## Limitations

The flow-happiness correlation is a pooled correlation over reports, not
an analysis of flow episodes. Flow is a continuous FKS score, so high
scores are absorption and fluency, not Csikszentmihalyi's discrete
enjoyable state (the measurement point Abuhamdeh makes, NOTE-1429). Paper
forms; the sample lacks unskilled and industrial workers (§3.1); 70%
women.
