# CLAIM-tmp7cc3w: The contribution is not that translation is pragmatic or that speech acts change contexts, but a specified, testable account of which communicative distinctions a translation preserves

## The claim

A35 §9: "I wouldn't present the basic operator account of meaning as
unprecedented ... I'd treat it as a research hypothesis rather than a claim of
originality." A38's closing: "Each component has substantial prior art. The
promising contribution is their integration." A40's closing: Gutt's *Translation
and Relevance* ([LIT-784](../literature.d/LIT-784.md)) "is a direct challenge to claims of theoretical
novelty", and Krifka's 2024 paper "explicitly models speech acts using
informative and performative updates"; so the contribution lies "**not in
discovering that translation is pragmatic or that speech acts change
contexts**, but in giving a specified, experimentally assessable account of
*which communicative transitions a translation preserves*."

Manuscript §1: "Our contribution is the coupling of these ideas into one formal
and experimentally measurable account, not the claim that each individual
ingredient is novel."

## What was lost

Outline v3 (A48 §2.5) still had the positioning: "Acknowledge that neither
context-dependent meaning nor functional translation equivalence is novel". It
called Gutt "especially important" and Krifka "directly relevant". After the
owner's U19 instruction to withhold hedging ([CLAIM-tmps5hv6](CLAIM-tmps5hv6.md)), outline v4 (A52)
dropped the novelty section, and both works shrank to citations.

Krifka (2024), "Performative Updates and the Modeling of Speech Acts", was
called "particularly close prior art" at A38 and named again at A33, A35 and
A40. It is absent from the manuscript and from the record. So are dynamic
semantics (Stalnaker, Heim, Veltman) and the translation-studies works A38–A39
recommended (Nida, House, Reiss and Vermeer, Nord). Gutt is in the record
unread.
