# THEORY-tmp60gbs: Granted that a person survives a brain transplant and that half a brain would do, each product of a division stands in all that matters in ordinary survival, so what matters is not identity, which is one-one and all-or-nothing, but psychological relations that can branch and come in degrees

## Source

Parfit (1971), "Personal Identity", *The Philosophical Review* 80(1): 3–27,
[LIT-447](../literature.d/LIT-447.md), read in full in [NOTE-378](../notes.d/NOTE-378.md). The dilemma fission poses for
psychological views, as the current literature states it: Olson (2002;
substantive revision 2023), the SEP entry "Personal Identity", [LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md),
read in [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md).

## What was actually shown

**The premises.** "Most of us would agree, after thought, that the
resulting person is me. I shall here assume such agreement" (p. 5).
Footnote 5 says those who disagree need a different, multiple case, which
the paper does not give. People "have in fact survived with half their
brains destroyed", and each half is taken to be "exactly similar" (p. 5).
Attitudes can be criticised for resting on a false belief or for being
inconsistent (p. 9).

**Division (§I, pp. 4–10).** Each half of my brain is housed in a new
body. Of three descriptions, "I do not survive" and "I survive as one" are
"highly implausible" (p. 5). "I survive as both" is not absurd for a brief
case: with control over the bridge between hemispheres one could divide,
work two calculations and reunite, so a mental history "could be like a
river, with islands" (p. 7). With two bodies for good, "it would become
intolerable to deny that they were different people" (p. 7). The relation
to each resulting person "contains all that interests us—all that
matters—in any ordinary case of survival" (p. 10). The relation I bear to
each is the one I would bear to the only survivor had the other half been
destroyed, and that would be survival. So division lacks only uniqueness:
"To regard these as the same is to confuse nought with two" (p. 10).

**The inference (§§II, IV).** "Identity is a one-one relation. Wiggins'
case serves to show that what matters in survival need not be one-one"
(p. 10). "Identity is all-or-nothing. Most of the relations which matter in
survival are, in fact, relations of degree" (p. 11). Fusion, in which
compatible traits combine and incompatible ones cancel, is "not clearly
survival" and "not clearly failure to survive" (p. 19).

**The relations, described without identity (§III).** Q-memory is a belief
that seems like a memory belief, about an experience someone had, on which
it depends "in the same way (whatever that is)" as a memory depends on its
experience (p. 15). Connectedness, the holding of direct relations, is not
transitive and comes in degrees; continuity, overlapping chains of them, is
transitive (p. 20). A criterion survives branching: X and Y are the same
person if psychologically continuous and no contemporary person is
continuous with either (p. 13), offered as sufficient, not necessary
(fn. 17).

**The dilemma, as the survey states it.** Fission forces psychological
views either into multiple occupancy, on which two people were there all
along (Lewis, Perry), or into a non-branching clause, on which fission is
death. Non-branching makes survival depend on whether the other hemisphere
is destroyed, which the entry calls "mysterious", and it is why Parfit and
others conclude that identity is not what matters ([LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md) §5; [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md)'s
C5, strong as a dilemma).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The relation to each product could
have differed from the relation in a single transplant in something besides
number. By the construction of the case it does not, and that is what the
argument uses.

## What this does not say

- **That it binds those who deny transplant survival.** The paper assumes
  agreement and concedes in fn. 5 that dissenters need a case it does not
  supply.
- **That every question about personal identity lacks an answer.** That is
  one of the paper's targets, and it is made "implausible", not refuted:
  the author "cannot see how to disprove" the belief (p. 4).
- **That connectedness matters more than continuity.** Asserted: it "would
  need more arguments than I have space to give" (p. 21).
- **That self-interest has no rational force.** §VI argues this in two
  pages with an analogy to nationalism. The reading rates it weak.
- **That a copy, a recording or a transcript is a survivor.** Q-memory
  needs the later belief to depend on the experience "in the same way
  (whatever that is)" as memory does. The paper leaves that open, and it
  is where cases of re-instantiation are decided. Birch ([LIT-111](../literature.d/LIT-111.md), [NOTE-183](../notes.d/NOTE-183.md))
  argues that a transcript handed to a successor fails it. The paper says
  nothing about machines.
- **That "I" should be given up.** §V's successive selves, drawn by degree
  of connectedness and "left to the choice of the speaker" (p. 25), are a
  proposal about how we could talk, offered as such.

## Connections

- **Rivals.** Lewis ([THEORY-tmp9v34q](THEORY-tmp9v34q.md)) grants both premises of the inference,
  that identity is one-one and all-or-nothing and that what matters may
  branch and come in degrees, and denies the conclusion by comparing the
  relation among stages of one person, not identity, with the psychological
  relation. Williams ([THEORY-tmpgxi0r](THEORY-tmpgxi0r.md)), which Parfit's fn. 11 answers,
  argues that from the first person the question "shall I survive?" must
  have an answer, carries a risk, and leans to the body.
- **The criterion.** The non-branching clause is the repair, set out in
  [THEORY-tmpidotl](THEORY-tmpidotl.md), that lets Locke's psychological criterion survive
  division. Parfit's fn. 2 says division is "implicit" in Locke's
  II.xxvii.18.
- **Presupposed by** Dufner's map of identity and ethics ([THEORY-tmp8fvw1](THEORY-tmp8fvw1.md)),
  which takes the separation of identity from what matters as its premise.
- **Dennett's "Where Am I?"** ([LIT-405](../literature.d/LIT-405.md), [NOTE-338](../notes.d/NOTE-338.md)). The brain and its
  program in step are Parfit's divided mind before reunion; their drift is
  division without reunion.
- **The record's own claim.** [CLAIM-287](../claims.d/CLAIM-287.md) holds that organizational continuity
  "can branch and merge, so it is not an identity relation". That is this
  account's inference made for selves that persist through inactivity. The
  claim does not ground on it.
- **AI consciousness.** [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md) draws on Birch's Challenge One, which
  argues from Parfit's later Relation R that a chatbot character has no
  persisting bearer. This paper is where the relations and their branching
  are first set out.
