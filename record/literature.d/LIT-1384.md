# LIT-1384: Walking through Doorways Causes Forgetting: Further Explorations

Radvansky, G. A., Krawietz, S. A., & Tamplin, A. K. (2011). Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Further explorations. *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 64(8), 1632–1645. doi:10.1080/17470218.2011.571267.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1082](../notes.d/NOTE-1082.md). Pages are the journal's.*

- **Smaller displays and real rooms.** The effect held on 17-inch
  monitors (A′ .97 against .93) and in a real three-room lab with a
  modified task (A′ .86 against .82, p = .03).
- **Returning does not help.** "Memory did not improve by reinstating this
  context" (p. 1632): A′ .92 after a shift, .90 after going and returning,
  .86 after two new rooms. Memory tracked "the number of new areas
  entered" (p. 1640).
- **The account is interference.** A carried object is in two event
  models, "which compete with one another at retrieval" (p. 1633), "a kind
  of a fan effect".

## Why this is here

A37 cited the finding that returning to the original room did not restore memory, and drew from it that "our theory shouldn't reduce perspective to location". The case is [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** The return result shows that the
encoding location does not reinstate access. It does not show that the
relevant context is "goal-sensitive": no goal or task was varied, and on
the authors' account each room entered is a new event model, so the unit
is still room-bound. A37's own proposed variants (a doorway crossed while
a salient task continues; a change of task with no doorway) would test
that. See [NOTE-1082](../notes.d/NOTE-1082.md).
