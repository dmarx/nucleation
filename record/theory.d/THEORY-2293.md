# THEORY-2293: Epigenetic trauma transmission evidence is thin

## The account

Zhou and Ryan (2023), [LIT-3166](../literature.d/LIT-3166.md). MEDLINE and EMBASE were searched on 29
June 2023 for human studies using "intergenerational" or
"transgenerational" with epigenetic marks and trauma or stress. They found
22 publications from 19 studies, 2011–2023, with 21 to 896 participants.
All measured DNA methylation, mostly in the offspring's blood. 14
assessed the exposure before conception. Four considered both parents
and two fathers only, and one measured sperm. 19 of 22 reported a
difference. The authors call this "preliminary supporting evidence". They
say the separation from "other sources of parent–offspring transmission
(including shared genetics, environment, and behaviors) will continue to
be problematic and is something that cannot easily be resolved".

## Evidence

A search-term-limited scoping review. The authors name its blind spot:
studies that do not use the two words, such as a grandmaternal violence
study.

## What it does not show

- That the studies are sound, which was not assessed.
- That no separating design exists outside its terms. Donor-gamete and
  adoption designs, which do not call themselves intergenerational, fall
  outside the search ([NOTE-2409](../notes.d/NOTE-2409.md)).
