# THEORY-1625: Branand, Mashek and Aron report that in close relationships partners experience each other's resources, perspectives and identities as partly their own, that the inclusion-of-other-in-self circle scale is validated as a measure of subjective closeness, and that inclusion shows in cognitive signs such as slower self-judgements on traits where the partner differs and self–partner confusions in memory

## The account

Pair-bonding and inclusion of other in the self are two faces of one
thing: "the pair bond itself is an enduring feature of having
successfully included the other in the self" (p. 1). A partner's
"resources, perspectives, and identities" are experienced "as to some
extent one's own", so that "the cognitive construction of the other
merges with the cognitive construction of the self, and that person's
outcomes are shared" (p. 1). The single-item circle scale is the
standard measure, validated as subjective closeness. The model's motive
is expanding one's own efficacy by acquiring the partner's resources.

## Evidence

Reported, not presented ([NOTE-1276](../notes.d/NOTE-1276.md)): two cited studies of cognitive
signs (slower "me/not-me" judgements, memory confusions), an
independent validation of the scale (Gächter et al. 2015), and single
correlational studies tying overlap to positive regard for a partner
shown inferior, to sleep lost over a partner's pain, and to
honour-endorsing men's aggression toward a partner's moral violation.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) names "perceived overlap (Aron's
inclusion-of-other-in-self scale)" as a candidate "criterion of the
self's extent that can come apart from caring". The review reports the
scale validated as closeness, which is close to caring, and offers the
cognitive signs as measures caring alone does not obviously predict.
