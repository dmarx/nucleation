# QUESTION-257: When an organizational perspective stops being enacted, what distinguishes a temporary loss of activation, a loss of the conditions for enacting it, an interruption of its social reproduction, and irreversible loss or later reconstruction, and what survives when a practice survives but the arrangements that authorized, interpreted and reproduced it do not?

## Why it is a question

A37: "**Under what conditions does an interruption change only the
accessibility of an organizational perspective, and under what conditions
does it transform or destroy the processes required for that
perspective's continued reproduction?**" A39 put it for the record: "When
an organizational perspective ceases to be enacted, what distinguishes
temporary loss of activation, loss of the conditions needed for its
enactment, interruption of its social reproduction, and irreversible loss
or subsequent reconstruction?" and added the question it found "especially
valuable": "**What survives when a practice survives but the social
arrangements that previously authorized, interpreted, and reproduced it
do not?**"

[CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md) names the kinds. It does not say how evidence tells them
apart, and the cases show why that is hard:

- In the doorway effect ([CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md)), the same forgetting has been read
  as event-model updating and as interference at retrieval, and in some
  replications does not occur.
- In Chinese martial traditions after the Cultural Revolution
  ([CASE-139](../cases.d/CASE-139.md)), A37 found "selective destruction, selective
  preservation, institutional substitution, and subsequent
  reconstruction" in one period, and separated "Reinstatement of practice"
  from "Reinstatement of authority". A practice may keep its historical
  continuity and lose the authority to represent that history.

An answer would give, for each kind of interruption, the evidence that
shows it, and a test for reinstatement against reconstruction. A37's
"Returning Tradition" states the hard case: two traditions, one reinstated
through concealed but continuous teaching, one rebuilt from archives and
neighbouring practices, "indistinguishable under a given set of tests" in
the present. [TERM-071](../terms.d/TERM-071.md) separates historical from reinstatable persistence,
and [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md) makes persistence a matter of causal transmission; [TERM-027](../terms.d/TERM-027.md)'s
cultural attractor warns that a stable reconstructed form can erase
information about its origin. The question asks how those apply when the
transmission was interrupted, concealed or replaced.

## Note of 2026-10-27: the question restated, and a third case

A3 of the exchange of 2026-10-26 proposed, as "an authentic open problem in
the present Nucleation framework": "Under what conditions does forced
disruption of a population's interaction structure destroy an existing
organizational perspective, merely interrupt its enactment, or induce the
formation of historically continuous but organizationally distinct
perspectives—and how do these outcomes relate to the capacity for
collective self-governance?" Its first part is this question; A3 cited
[CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md) and not this. The second part is new here and is [CLAIM-424](../claims.d/CLAIM-424.md)'s
distinction: A2: "A tradition can therefore display considerable
historical persistence while its bearers lack collective self-governance
over crucial conditions of its future."

The case is [CASE-169](../cases.d/CASE-169.md), a people expelled from its islands and dispersed
across three countries, beside the doorway ([CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md)) and the martial
traditions ([CASE-139](../cases.d/CASE-139.md)). It gives the question two things the others lack:

- **A comparison across carriers.** Communities in Mauritius, the
  Seychelles and Britain differ in environment, migration history and
  institutions. A3: process tracing, archives and participant accounts,
  not group differences, because migration routes, resources and cohorts
  select who lives where.
- **A defeater for transmission.** A3: "evidence that apparent continuity is
  explained almost entirely by independently acquired public
  representations of Chagossian culture, rather than demonstrable
  transmission from preceding participants", against "detailed
  intergenerational relationships linking practices, interpretations, and
  responses to novel situations". That is the reinstatement-or-
  reconstruction test asked for above, in a documented setting.
