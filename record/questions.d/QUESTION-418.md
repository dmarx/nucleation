# QUESTION-418: Does a meta-analysis pooling randomized evaluations of cash transfers in low- and middle-income countries find a pooled effect on household alcohol and tobacco spending that is zero or negative, with an upper confidence bound that excludes more than a trivial increase, and no significant difference between conditional and unconditional programs?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1494](../theory.d/THEORY-1494.md) reports a vote count of 44 non-independent estimates, only 17
of them randomized, with no pooled estimate or interval. It says such
spending is almost never raised; it does not bound the effect.

## What would answer it

A meta-analysis of randomized evaluations with a pooled effect at or below
zero and an upper bound excluding more than a trivial increase, with no
difference between conditional and unconditional programmes, would answer
yes. A further count by sign would not settle it.
