# THEORY-tmp16ugb: A person's mind is normally a system of parts that take protective roles, managers, exiles and firefighters, forced on them by experience and not part of their nature; beneath the parts is a Self, distinct from all of them and present from the start, which appears with calm, curiosity and compassion when the parts step back and which heals by leading the system

## Source

Schwartz, "Evolution of the Internal Family Systems Model", [LIT-579](../literature.d/LIT-579.md), read
in full in [NOTE-453](../notes.d/NOTE-453.md) with two companion pages from the IFS Institute.
Schwartz and Sweezy, *Internal Family Systems Therapy*, [LIT-591](../literature.d/LIT-591.md), of which
only chapter 1 (pp. 3–23) was read, in [NOTE-457](../notes.d/NOTE-457.md). That reading is a skim in
the scheme's sense: one chapter of twenty. Both NOTEs declined a THEORY
comparing IFS with ACT, for want of process studies. This document states
IFS's own account, which both readings report in Schwartz's words, and
files it as Proposed for the same reason.

## What was actually shown

**Parts ([LIT-591](../literature.d/LIT-591.md), ch. 1; [LIT-579](../literature.d/LIT-579.md)).** Clients with bulimia described a
"cacophony" of parts: a critic, a defender, a sad part, a part that "took
over" to binge. Other clients told "remarkably similar stories". Parts take
common roles: managers that control and protect, exiles that carry pain,
firefighters that suppress it in emergencies. The roles "could be changed
if one intervened carefully and respectfully". Extreme parts are protective,
not pathological: Roxanne's cutting part had the "two-pronged job" of taking
her out of her body during abuse and holding back a dangerous rage.

**What fails and what works.** Coercing a part ("I badgered it for 2
hours") was followed by worse self-injury. Asking with curiosity revealed
its purpose. Going to vulnerable parts too fast was followed by accidents,
fevers and migraines, which the clients' protectors later "explained".

**The Self.** Asking an angry part to "step back" produced "a calm, caring
voice" ("Diane" in [LIT-579](../literature.d/LIT-579.md), "Cora" in the book). Repeated with other
clients, the procedure yielded a state they called "who I really am". The
Self "clearly did not need to develop over time". It "wasn't just a passive
witness state … but could also be an active healing presence". It is
recognised by eight qualities: "calmness, curiosity, clarity, compassion,
confidence, creativity, courage, and connectedness".

**What could have come out otherwise.** Clients could have reported no
stable state when parts stepped back, or one that differed from client to
client. By Schwartz's account they did not. That is the evidence, and it is
clients' reports interpreted by the model's developer.

## What this does not say

- **That the Self exists as a mechanism of change.** On [THEORY-053](THEORY-053.md)'s terms,
  that clients improve when the Self is accessed is evidence for it only if
  a measure of Self-access changes first and specifically. None is reported.
- **That everyone has a Self.** That is generalised from the author's
  practice (C2 in both NOTEs).
- **That parts are real functional units.** It is an assumption that
  clients' reports of parts are data ([NOTE-457](../notes.d/NOTE-457.md), Assumptions).
- **That IFS is effective.** The SAMHSA rating the chapter mentions was not
  checked, and the research chapter (19) was not read.
- **That the backlash is caused as described.** Three anecdotes, with the
  causal reading supplied by the clients' parts ([NOTE-457](../notes.d/NOTE-457.md), C3).

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-034](../questions.d/QUESTION-034.md)** asks what makes a population of interests act as one:
  bargaining, a change in which may act, or agreement. IFS gives a
  different answer from all three: leadership by a Self that is not one of
  the interests. That is what sets it apart from Ainslie's bargaining
  interests ([LIT-586](../literature.d/LIT-586.md)) and Minsky's society of mind ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md)).
- ACT's self as context ([THEORY-044](THEORY-044.md), [THEORY-051](THEORY-051.md)) is a contentless
  perspective learned from deictic relations. IFS's Self is innate and has
  qualities. Both hold thoughts and feelings without fusing with them. A
  claim that this is a common active ingredient would need process studies
  the record does not hold ([NOTE-453](../notes.d/NOTE-453.md)).
- Firefighters (bingeing, drugs, self-harm) are close to experiential
  avoidance in the psychological-inflexibility account ([THEORY-050](THEORY-050.md)).
