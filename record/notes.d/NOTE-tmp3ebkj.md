# NOTE-tmp3ebkj: Lovett and Riedener (2021) — Group Agents and Moral Status: What Can We Owe to Organizations?

## Contribution

An argued account of the moral patiency of group agents, as distinct from their responsibility. It explains organizations' apparent claims without giving them moral status, and adds that a person's welfare is partly constituted by group activity.

## Key results

1. The puzzle (Abstract, p. 221): "Organizations have neither a right to vote nor a right to life. But we can owe them to keep our promises or show them gratitude."
2. The verdict (§1, p. 223): "But in the final analysis, the moral status of organizations is merely apparent: they themselves can make no claims on us at all."
3. Against Individualism (§2, p. 224): "when organizations change their members, Individualism can’t explain why claims stick with the organization rather than move with the (former) members."
4. Against Collectivism (§3, p. 226): "Single-source theories tend to grant organizations full moral status or none at all."
5. The assumption that carries the asymmetry (§4.a, p. 228): "We now make an important assumption. Organizations do not have welfare." Welfare needs phenomenal consciousness, which organizations lack.
6. The interest (§4.a, p. 229): "We’ll call it an ‘interest in participation in successful group agency.’" It holds only for endorsed participation; of an enslaved chef, "The lesson here, we think, is that participation in group agency is only good for you when it is endorsed."
7. No right to life (§4.b, p. 230): "organizations don’t themselves have an interest in their own continuation."
8. Assumption of claims (§4.c, p. 233): members "assume these claims merely by willingly participating in the relevant group agent."
9. Political obligation (§5, p. 235): "Only citizens in democratic states properly participate in their state’s agency; those in autocratic states do not."
10. Conclusion (§6, p. 236): organizations "don’t have any moral status whatsoever".

## Assumptions

- Organizations are group agents in List and Pettit's sense (p. 223; the record holds that account as THEORY-139).
- An interest theory of claims, assumed and not defended (p. 228).
- Welfare requires phenomenal consciousness, and organizations lack it (p. 228).
- What benefits you non-accidentally gives you reason to want it to keep its valuable properties (p. 231).

## Concepts

Fragmented and full moral status; directed obligation; Individualism and Collectivism (the authors' coinages); single- and dual-source theories of status; the Group Interest View; interest in participation in successful group agency; legitimate interest; transference, assumption and discharge of claims; moral subject and moral object.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-tmp9bbpk** (what a whole owes its parts): the paper is about organizations in TERM-074's sense 3, collective institutions that are group agents, with persons as members. It asks the opposite direction and never what an organization owes its members. It bears on the question only for sense-3 wholes, in two ways. If an organization has no welfare and no standing, what is owed around it is owed by and to persons, so the owner's question becomes what members, and those who act for the whole, owe the parts. And the members' interest in willing participation is one the whole's arrangements can serve or frustrate; coerced participation gives them nothing of value (p. 229). Neither transfers to the question's default sense, an arrangement (TERM-074 sense 1) whose parts need not be persons and which need not be an agent. Filed as `bears_on`, with a dated note on the question. A12's description of the paper ("organizational agency alone does not give the rights or moral status owed to persons") is accurate but understated: the paper denies organizations any moral status.
- **CLAIM-075** (viability does not establish what ought to persist): corroborated on another route. An organization has no interest in its own continuation (p. 230), so its persistence carries no weight of its own. Filed as `corroborates`.
- **TERM-083** (moral standing): an example of standing denied to an agent because its ground, welfare, is absent, the separation of agent from patient the term draws. No relation written; the term does not use the reading.
- **QUESTION-118, CLAIM-329**: claims staying with an organization through complete turnover is one of the paper's data (p. 224), explained by the new members' interests (pp. 232–233) without a persistence criterion for the organization.
- **CLAIM-tmpwn3wr, CLAIM-603, CLAIM-104**: not addressed.

## Limitations

The interest theory is assumed. The welfare assumption carries the whole asymmetry. The endorsement condition is stated briefly. The paper covers claims on outsiders only, not what an organization or its officers owe members.
