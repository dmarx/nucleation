# THEORY-tmpitywd: McAuliffe, Blake, Steinbeis and Warneken argue that children show the signatures of fairness in order, refusing to get less than a peer by about four, punishing unfairness to self and others at a cost by six to eight and refusing advantageous inequality by about eight, varying by culture, and that three-year-olds already state equal division as a norm but do not follow it when it costs them

## The account

Fairness develops as a sequence of costly choices against equality: first
against getting less, then punishing unfairness, last against getting
more. Knowledge of the equality norm comes early, by 3; acting on it at a
cost comes later, with behavioural control, and its last signature depends
on local norms.

## Evidence

Review of developmental experiments with behavioural-economic games across
several societies.
