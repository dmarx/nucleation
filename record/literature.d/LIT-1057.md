# LIT-1057: Adapting the Environment Instead of Oneself

Kirsh, D. (1996). Adapting the environment instead of oneself. *Adaptive Behavior* 4(3–4), 415–452. DOI 10.1177/105971239600400307

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-872](../notes.d/NOTE-872.md)). A conceptual paper. It models a task as a directed graph of states and actions with a cost metric on transitions, and classifies how an agent can improve its payoff by actions external to the task: pruning hard initial states, routine maintenance, scouting ahead, tools (which change the graph), and actions that make a space more "cognitively congenial" (which leave the graph intact and reduce mental operations). It names amortization once, as a parenthetical gloss on chimps leaving nut-cracking stones in place, and gives an investment rule for maintenance (future benefit against present cost times an "interest factor" for risk) in words only. There is no cost model with reuse frequency, retrieval cost or staleness; mental cost lies outside the graph, and the paper says there is "no general theory" of congeniality. The empirical figures are unpublished pilot results.

## Why this is here

Turned up by the citation search on [QUESTION-095](../questions.d/QUESTION-095.md) (amortized retrieval as amortized computation) and registered at the owner's request. The closest statement of the owner's intuition from inside distributed cognition: leaving tools in place to "amortize the cost of search", named and, by the search's reading, not modelled.

The reading ([NOTE-872](../notes.d/NOTE-872.md)) corrects the description from the citation search. "Amortize" occurs once, in an aside about chimpanzees leaving stones where they are useful. The paper's economics is a verbal rule: do work now if its future benefit exceeds present cost times an "interest factor" for risk, the risks being that the result is not used, that it is not "still present later", and that the agent does not survive. No cost model.
