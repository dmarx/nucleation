# LIT-101: Moral disciplining: The cognitive and evolutionary foundations of puritanical morality

Léo Fitouchi, Jean-Baptiste André, Nicolas Baumard (2021), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 46 (online 2022-09-16; issue 2023); preprint on PsyArXiv* — DOI-10.1017/S0140525X22002047

## Key takeaways

- Societies moralise harmless bodily pleasures because people hold folk theories that indulgence erodes the self-control cooperation requires, so puritanism is part of cooperative morality rather than a separate purity/disgust domain.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The authors ask why many societies condemn apparently harmless pleasures — lust, gluttony, alcohol, drugs, music and dance — and exalt temperance, modesty and piety, which existing theories treat as outside the cooperative function of morality. They argue that puritanical norms arise from lay beliefs that cooperation requires self-control and that indulgence weakens it, so restraining pleasure is moralised as a way of protecting cooperation. They derive testable predictions for each behaviour in the "puritanical constellation" and use the theory to explain why puritanism declines in rich societies.
