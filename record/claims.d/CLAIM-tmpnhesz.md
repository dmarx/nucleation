# CLAIM-tmpnhesz: Answerability is directed and role-specific: an actor owes an answer to a party with a claim concerning the matter, on one of several grounds that need not presuppose the actor's legitimate authority, among them interference with a protected interest, entrusted authority, collective rule, consequential dependence and control over recourse; the party owed, the recipient of reasons, the forum that judges and the party that remedies can differ; and what is owed is indexed to the claim, not to how much the party is affected

## The claim

A83 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), at the
owner's U36. Four parts.

**1. Several grounds** (A83 §3):

| Ground | Why an answer may be owed | Principal recipient |
|---|---|---|
| **Protected-interest interference** | The actor affects something another has a justified claim to control or protect | The claim-holder |
| **Assumed or entrusted authority** | The actor exercises a role whose powers exist for particular purposes | Those to whom the role's duties are owed |
| **Collective rule or public coercion** | The actor imposes binding rules on people who need not have personally consented | The governed, through suitable direct and representative forums |
| **Consequential dependency** | The actor controls essential conditions of another's participation and alternatives | The dependent parties, as warranted by their claims |
| **Control over recourse itself** | The actor can determine whether challenges to its conduct become effective | Those with standing to challenge, and appropriately independent oversight |

"These grounds are neither exhaustive nor mutually exclusive."

**2. No presupposition of authority** (A83 §3A): "**duties arising from
exercising power must not be restricted to actors who possess legitimate
authority**". Authority_N(A, B, x) = 0 does not imply
Answerability_N(A, B, x) = 0: "An actor who lacks the authority to
perform \(x\) can nevertheless owe B a justification, an explanation, or
redress because the actor performed it." A83 §9 Case B, an unauthorized
but beneficial intervention, is the example: "This distinguishes the
justification of an outcome from the justification of who was entitled to
determine it."

**3. Roles that can differ** (A83 §4): "Claim-holder", "Justification
recipient", "Accountability forum". A child is owed consideration through
a representative; future people "cannot presently demand reasons, but that
does not establish that their morally relevant interests can be ignored".
A90 IV adds the remedier: "the bearer of a right, the recipient of an
explanation, the institution authorized to adjudicate it, and the actor
responsible for remedying its violation can be different parties".

**4. Indexed to the claim** (A83 §4, §10): "The obligation must therefore
be indexed to a *particular claim* and *the particular role of the person
to whom it is owed*." A's schema,

    Ground_N(B, x) ∧ Nexus_N(A, x) ∧ RequiresAnswer_N(B, x, φ) ⇒ Ans_N(A, B, x, φ),

is, in A's words, "a schema, not an independent derivation of moral truth.
Its third premise carries substantive normative content." It is a form
for stating which answer (notice, reasons, review, redress) is owed, not a
derivation of it.

A83 §5 adds when: constitutive, prospective, ongoing, retrospective and
reflexive forms, and "a *standing duty of answerability*" that does not
wait for a complaint. These are the temporal forms the grounds take, not
further grounds.

## How it sits with the record

- **CLAIM-687** (Proposed) scales accountability with indispensability.
  That is the dependence ground with an intensity. A83 §6 lists
  dependence among four "intensifiers" with discretion, irreversibility
  and control over recourse: "The grounds of answerability determine why
  something is owed; vulnerability and power asymmetry help determine what
  protections are required for it to be adequately honored." That
  distinction is noted on CLAIM-687.
- **CLAIM-682** (Proposed): A83 §3D cites it and adds "Dependence by itself
  does not establish domination."
- **CLAIM-624** (Proposed) states the second-order duty to keep the means
  of answer effective; A83 §11 restates it ("Ans_N … does not entail
  Feasible"). This claim is upstream: when there is a first-order answer
  to keep effective.
- **TERM-083** (Proposed), moral standing apart from agency and legal
  personhood, is what lets the party owed differ from the party able to
  demand.
- **CLAIM-tmpcpijj** files the fifth ground's consequence: authority over a
  decision does not carry authority over the conditions of its challenge.
- **CLAIM-tmpyqfrf** files the limit A83 §9 Case A first states, "**Being
  affected by another's exercise of agency does not confer general
  authority to govern that agency.**", from its later form in part 7 of the
  exchange. Part 4 here depends on it: an index to claims, not to effects.

## What it does not say

- Not why anyone is owed reasons at all. A83 §12 calls that a moral
  premise the formal account "cannot answer"; Darwall, Forst and Pettit,
  whom A83 §2 relays, are three candidate grounds the record has not read.
- Not that every affected party has a veto. A83's opening: "An affected
  person can be owed consideration or reasons without possessing a veto."
- Not that a forum must exist for an answer to be owed. A83 §1: "One can
  owe an answer even when no adequate forum currently exists to demand
  it." CASE-229 shows a matched pair with and without effective review.
