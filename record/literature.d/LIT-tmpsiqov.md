  cognitive science should explain, among other things, narrow and wide
  meaning, what makes expressions meaningful, the relativity of meaning to
  representational system, learning, and how inherited meaning (of words on
  a page) relates to autonomous meaning (of the language of thought).

# LIT-tmpsiqov: Advertisement for a Semantics for Psychology

Ned Block (1986), *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 10 (*Studies in the
Philosophy of Mind*), pp. 615–678 — DOI-10.1111/j.1475-4975.1987.tb00558.x.

The brief's citation (Midwest Studies 10, 1986) is correct. The running
head of the scan reads "MIDWEST STUDIES IN PHILOSOPHY, X (1986)"; the
"1987" in the DOI is Wiley's.

## Key takeaways

- **Two factors (pp. 627–628).** Meaning has "a conceptual role component
  that is entirely 'in the head' (this is narrow meaning) and an external
  component that has to do with the relations between the representations
  in the head ... and the referents and/or truth conditions of these
  representations in the world". Conceptual role is "total causal role,
  abstractly described", abstracted to the relations that mediate inference,
  decision and the like, as herring have a culinary role inside their
  total causal role.
- **Narrow meaning is motivated by explanation (pp. 619–625).** Two people
  thinking "I am in danger" leap aside for the same reason; Teen and her
  Twin Earth duplicate share a thought about "Michael Jackson"; my twin and
  I both jump into the pool when our pants are on fire. What they share
  explains behaviour and fixes the function from context to referent. An
  analysis of variance over a bus of Earth and Twin Earth tourists would
  otherwise have to credit variance to a factor with no physical effect
  on them (p. 625).
- **Conceptual role fixes the kind of reference, not the referent
  (pp. 643–644).** Which theory of reference is true (causal, not
  descriptive) is a fact about how names function in our thinking, so
  conceptual role "determines the nature of the referential factor"; the
  same role maps to H₂O here and XYZ on Twin Earth.
- **Harman's long-arm roles collapse into two factors (pp. 635–637).** To
  handle the visitor to Twin Earth, the elm/beech case and borrowed
  reference, long-arm roles must import "normal context", experts' roles
  and causal chains into the past. "Chop off" the part outside the skin and
  what is left is the short-arm role; what is chopped off is a theory of
  reference.
- **The competitors (pp. 649–660).** Non-reductionist semantics
  (possible worlds, situations, Davidson, Katz) do not aim to say what makes
  an expression meaningful. Searle's "equivalent causal powers" is not
  naturalistic. Dretske–Stampe indicator semantics falls to Fodor's
  disjunction problem, and Fodor's own theory fails on both its "wheels":
  epistemic idealisation cannot be specified naturalistically for
  theoretical sentences, and evolutionary teleology does not guarantee
  mechanisms aimed at truth. Without compositional structure, it cannot
  tell "the electrical conductivity is rising" from "the thermal
  conductivity is rising" (the Wiedemann–Franz law, p. 660).
- **Functionalism for intentionality, not experience (p. 635).** Block
  explains why he advocates a functionalism after "Troubles with
  Functionalism": he will settle for a theory of human meaning, and his
  liberalism arguments "were strongest against functionalist theories of
  experiential mental states". Pain is "a composite state consisting of a
  nonfunctional qualitative state together with a functional state".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, with Block's other papers on
functionalism, meaning, AI and perception. A theory of meaning and mental
content as philosophers pose it is `philosophy-of-language`; no anthology
topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for machine-learning
practice.

It is the record's first full statement of functional (conceptual) role
semantics, which the record had cited only at second hand.

- **[NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md) (Drayson, [LIT-112](LIT-112.md))** names "Block 1987" as a counter-model to
  Toon's mind-first metasemantics: functional role semantics on which mental
  representations take content partly from social and communicative use.
  Drayson's reference list gives that as "Functional Role and Truth
  Conditions" (1987), held here as [LIT-tmp1az05](LIT-tmp1az05.md), not this paper. This paper
  is where the theory Drayson leans on is stated in full, and its p. 630
  leaves the social dimension of meaning to the referential factor or to "a
  distinct social factor". [NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md)'s limitation, that Block 1987 is "named,
  not developed", can now be checked against both.
- **[NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md)'s RTM core.** [NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md) takes RTM's core to be that mental
  representations are individuated apart from attitudes, with structure
  inessential. Block's representationalism is minimal in a similar way: he is
  committed only to representational states that "constitute a combinatorial
  system", contingently and empirically (pp. 641–642). If CRS met serious
  empirical problems because of that commitment, he "would pursue a
  nonrepresentationalist version", and he names Loar's and Schiffer's
  functionalist Gricean programme as one such avenue (p. 642).
- **Searle** ([LIT-400](LIT-400.md), [NOTE-349](../notes.d/NOTE-349.md)). CRS gives the systems reply a theory: the
  Chinese symbols would have autonomous meaning if they had the right
  conceptual roles. Block separates *simulating* a Chinese speaker's
  processing from *emulating* it, and says only emulation is understanding
  (pp. 665–666).
- **Teleosemantics** ([LIT-149](LIT-149.md), [NOTE-165](../notes.d/NOTE-165.md)). Martínez's teleofunctional account
  of content is in the family whose teleological "wheel" Block argues cannot
  be naturalised by appeal to evolution, because selection does not
  guarantee mechanisms aimed at truth (meiotic drive, pp. 659–660). That
  pairing is mine.
- **Interpretationism** ([LIT-206](LIT-206.md), [NOTE-138](../notes.d/NOTE-138.md)). [NOTE-138](../notes.d/NOTE-138.md)'s C7 contrasts
  reward-based representationalism with interpretationism; Block's p. 645
  rejects truth-maximising charity and derives coherence-based charity from
  conceptual roles, which is a third position in that dispute.
- Neighbours held only as seeds: Prinz's two wide contents ([LIT-134](LIT-134.md)) and
  Brandom's inferentialism ([LIT-106](LIT-106.md)).

No relation is declared. The paper argues against Searle, Fodor, Dretske and
Harman, none of whose primary statements of these views is in the record
except Searle's, and against Searle it diagnoses an ambiguity rather than
refuting the argument.
