# CLAIM-tmpsk25g: Blame and accountability are not the same thing: an agent's diminished culpability for the reasons it acts on does not diminish the wrongfulness of what it does, the claims of those it harms, or the need for correction, because the grounds of correction are not exhausted by the grounds of blame

## The claim

The owner's U47 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), 2026-11-17,
quoting A108 §5's "JoJo's moral incapacity is meant to complicate our
assessment of blame":

> perhaps "blame" and "accountability" aren't the same thing. It might not be JoJo's "fault" that they hold the reasons that they do, but they are still accountable for the effects of acting on those reasons. "blame" gives us provenance for holding upstream actors accountable as well. Moral incapacity does not change the necessity for correction once a harm has been created, it simply identifies the harm as unintentionally counter-normative.

Three of its parts are this claim: blame is not accountability; an agent
not at fault for its reasons is still accountable for the effects of
acting on them; incapacity leaves the need for correction where it was.
The fourth, blame as provenance for upstream actors, is CLAIM-tmpixmhp.

A110 §1 states the owner's point as three judgments that can hold together
of JoJo's wrongful order to imprison someone: the act is wrongful, his
blameworthiness is diminished, and correction "may still hold". Its
reasons: "The imprisoned person's claim to release does not become weaker
merely because JoJo lacks ordinary moral competence", and "The
institutional duty to end an unjustified detention does not depend on
whether JoJo is an appropriate target of resentment." Its summary:
"**Blameworthiness is agent-sensitive; the wrongfulness of an action is not
necessarily dependent on the culpability of the agent who performed it.**"
A110 §12 puts it shortest: "**the grounds of correction are not exhausted
by the grounds of blame**".

## Which "accountability": a usage note (A110's introduction)

A110 separates four questions, "What happened, and whose conduct produced
it?", whether it was wrongful, who deserves blame, and "**What must now be
done, and who bears responsibility for doing it?**", and says "The fourth
question cannot be answered simply by resolving the third." It then marks
the owner's word: "In parts of the philosophical literature,
*accountability* is used more narrowly to mean eligibility for blame or
sanction", and proposes "**remedial and institutional accountability**"
for "responsibilities that survive diminished culpability".

The record follows that note. "Accountable" in the owner's sentence is
TERM-tmp8pnnr's **remedial** sense: who owes prevention, relief, repair or
explanation. It is not THEORY-147's accountability (Wolf's: answerability
to the reactive attitudes, needing only that the agent acted wrongly and
was in a position to know it), and not Watson's accountability face. In
those senses the claim would be false or trivial. A110 attributes the
three-way split of attributability, answerability and accountability to
"Gary Watson and David Shoemaker" jointly. Watson's "Two Faces of
Responsibility" (1996) has two faces, attributability and accountability;
the three-way split is Shoemaker's (2011).

## Unwitting counter-normativity (the owner's phrase, A's amendment)

The owner's last clause: incapacity "simply identifies the harm as
unintentionally counter-normative". A110 §3 amends it, openly: "I would
slightly modify your phrase *unintentionally counter-normative*." JoJo may
order the imprisonment intentionally; what he lacks is recognition of its
wrongness. So A separates "Intentional action" from "Intentional violation
of a recognized norm", and names the case "**unwitting
counter-normativity**, or more precisely **normatively unrecognized
wrongdoing**". It "does not by itself establish an excuse", and A adds
that institutions show the same pattern: "officials knowingly perform
actions whose wrongful significance their governing framework fails to
represent."

The amendment is right and the claim uses it: what incapacity changes is
the agent's recognition of the act's significance, not the act's
intentionality, so the harm is unwittingly, not unintentionally,
counter-normative. The coinage is the owner's; the correction is A's.

## Three assessments, and relief that need not wait (A110 §12)

A110 §12 organizes the result as three "analytically independent
assessments": normative (what is wrong or owed), historical responsibility
(who made the conduct and its mechanisms what they were; "Assign blame only
where its conditions are met"), and prospective responsibility (who is now
obligated and able to correct). Its consequence for urgent cases bears on
this claim directly: "correcting the ongoing violation should not have to
await a complete determination of everyone's historical culpability", and
"designing a system that makes immediate relief conditional on first
resolving every dispute about blame could itself create a procedural
obstacle to honoring the claimant's rights". That last is CLAIM-464's
mechanism, a procedure that makes a correction costlier, applied to blame
itself. It is carried here as a consequence, not filed as a claim of its
own.

## How it sits with the record

- **Not new to the exchange, new in its use.** A17 §9 (2026-11-15) had
  already drawn "an interesting distinction between *accountability* and
  *blame*": "A person can be required to explain their decisions and
  participate in correcting a failure without necessarily being morally
  culpable for everything that happened." The owner's U47 takes that
  distinction to the case where the agent's moral competence itself is
  impaired, and adds the provenance clause.
- **THEORY-147** (Wolf's 2013 lecture, LIT-747) holds that attributability
  and accountability are independent kinds of responsibility. That is a
  neighbouring split in a different sense of accountability (above).
- **THEORY-207** (Floridi) allocates responsibility for distributed
  outcomes "without fault or intention". It is a reading that the
  allocation of remedial responsibility can be faultless, and would
  corroborate this claim for networks of agents.
- **QUESTION-068** (Deferred) asks whether accountability needs normative
  competence. On the remedial sense this claim answers no; on Wolf's
  (THEORY-122) the question stays where it is.
- **CLAIM-624**'s duty of answerability already holds a correction owed
  without fault for organizations that keep a harm unrecognized; this claim
  is the general statement for any agent, persons included.
- The responsibility graph and the non-substitution principle built on it
  are CLAIM-tmp4o53g; the self-induced incapacity is CLAIM-tmp0wx69.

## What it does not say

- Not who owes the correction. A110 §1: "not every wrongful outcome
  generates a duty of complete restitution borne by its immediate causal
  author. Duties of repair require their own grounds." The non-culpable
  agent may owe nothing; the claim says only that someone may.
- Not that every harm is a wrong (A110 §12: "A harmful outcome is not
  necessarily a rights violation").
- Not that incapacity changes nothing. A110 §1: it "changes what kinds of
  response—punishment, restraint, correction, assistance, or
  rehabilitation—can be justified".
- Not that challengers get unlimited powers. A110 §12: accountability
  without blame "cannot simply mean treating every harm as an occasion to
  impose unrestricted restorative obligations on whichever actor is most
  accessible".
