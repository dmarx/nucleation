# NOTE-2112: Suzuki, Li and Zurek 2026 — Machine learning topological defect formation: When are the defects made?

## Contribution

A simulation study (arXiv 2508.20347 v2, 10 Sep 2026; Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 157301, 2026) of kink formation in a noisy, overdamped one-dimensional φ⁴ (Landau–Ginzburg) field quenched linearly through its symmetry-breaking transition. A recurrent network is trained to map short windows of the field's evolution (11 time steps) to the final field at ε = 1. Its accuracy as a function of when the window is taken shows when information about the final defects (their number and locations) first becomes present in the field. That time is near the critical point, inside the Kibble–Zurek "impulse regime" and well before the freeze-out time. The authors explain it by an attenuation–instantiation–amplification sequence, and the onset of predictability collapses across quench rates when time is rescaled by the freeze-out time.

## Key results

- **Main finding** (abstract): "we show that a recurrent neural network can predict locations of topological defects from short-time dynamical data deep within the impulse regime. We thus demonstrate that defects are sown in the immediate vicinity of the critical point."
- **Before the defects are visible** (abstract): this is before the freeze-out time "and well before the order parameter assumes its final symmetry-broken configuration." At that time the seeds are "exponentially small" (abstract), "which makes it impossible to identify defect seeds with the" naked eye (p. 1).
- **Some information even earlier** (p. 1): "Remarkably, the ML results suggest that limited information about the defect locations is present even before the critical point is traversed." (Fig. 4: "the ML model can predict part of the final topological defect configuration even from input data obtained shortly before the critical point is traversed".)
- **Number and place** (p. 3, Fig. 4): "the model can predict both the number and final locations of topological defects with high precision." The location error "is computed only when the model correctly predicts the number of defects".
- **Mechanism** (p. 3): before the transition the restoring force suppresses fluctuations, "This is why the pre-transition configuration from t < 0 is erased." Near t = 0 the potential is flat and noise imprints fluctuations. After it, "Perturbations of ϕ(x) imprinted by noise on ϕ(x) near t = 0 become amplified more than these introduced later."
- **What carries the information** (p. 3): long-wavelength structures "subsequently evolve into the final domains and determine the defect locations, whereas short-wavelength fluctuations are largely transient and relax away."
- **Universality** (p. 5, Fig. 5): validation-loss curves for τQ = 128, 256 and 512 nearly coincide in t/t̂. "We therefore interpret the rapid decrease in prediction error as a signature of the relevant information becoming accessible to the RNN."
- **Not an artefact of the network** (p. 5; Supplement): "the same prediction behavior is observed with LSTM and Transformer architectures". With larger noise (θ = 10⁻²), "although larger noise can affect the predictability of the final topological defect configuration, the main result remains unchanged".
- **Claim relative to KZM** (p. 5): the result is "consistent with the Kibble–Zurek mechanism (KZM), but goes beyond its standard predictions by identifying when information about the final defect configuration becomes encoded in the order parameter."

## Assumptions

- A 1+1D real scalar field, overdamped (η = 1) Langevin dynamics, V(ϕ) = (ϕ⁴ − 2εϕ²)/8 with ε = t/τQ ramped from −1.5 to 1. White noise at θ = 10⁻⁸ (also 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻²). L = 512, 1024 grid points, periodic boundaries.
- Defects are kinks, the zeros of ϕ in the final configuration at ε = 1. Their net charge is fixed (zero) by the periodic boundaries. Their number and positions vary between noise realizations.
- 3000 samples (80/20 training/validation split), 100 test samples, 60 epochs, averages over five runs. The network's accuracy is used as a lower bound on the information present in the input window.
- The interpretation rests on the authors' "deconstruction" of KZM (their ref. [32]), which was not read.

## Concepts

- **Kibble–Zurek mechanism (KZM)**: critical slowing down near a second-order transition creates an "impulse" interval [−t̂, +t̂] in which the order parameter cannot follow the changing potential. Defects form, and their density scales with τQ.
- **Freeze-out time t̂**: the end of the impulse regime, when the order parameter catches up with the new minima. In the overdamped case it scales as the square root of ητQ.
- **Attenuation–instantiation–amplification (aia)**: before the critical point, fluctuations are damped. At it, noise imprints them. After it, they are amplified by the inverted potential.
- **Prediction onset as a timestamp**: the time at which a trained predictor's error drops marks when the future configuration has become encoded in the present field.

## Bearing on the record

- [QUESTION-281](../questions.d/QUESTION-281.md): supports, for a stated system, the separation the question asks about. The form (the kink pattern) is made by instability amplifying small differences: noise imprinted at the flat potential near t = 0 is selectively amplified. That is one of the question's listed routes. Its creation, meaning the moment the pattern is determined, comes near the critical point. Its stabilization, meaning settling into the new minima, comes at freeze-out t̂, measurably later, and pre-transition differences are erased rather than carried forward. So for this system creation and stabilization are distinct and dated, and a future structure is predictable before it forms. The paper says nothing about a constitutive role.
- [CLAIM-694](../claims.d/CLAIM-694.md): supports. With the same boundary conditions and charge sector, the kinks' number and positions differ between noise realizations and are set by fluctuations imprinted near the critical point. Which kinks there are and where they sit is fixed by history, not by the charge. This is independent, formation-side evidence for the claim's statement that the charge does not locate a kink and does not fix the count of tokens. Net charge is fixed at zero only on a periodic domain.
- [CLAIM-606](../claims.d/CLAIM-606.md): bears on the topological row's "how it forms". Kinks form by a quench through a symmetry-breaking transition, seeded by noise and fixed in place before they are visible. Persistence after formation is not studied: the run ends at ε = 1.
- [CASE-269](../cases.d/CASE-269.md): bears on it. The paper's protocol (overdamped Langevin φ⁴, linear quench, noise) is the testbed's φ⁴ kink with a formation readout. Its prediction-onset method is a way to measure when a structure becomes determined, which could join the testbed's readouts (probability of formation).

## Limitations

- One model: 1+1D, overdamped, real scalar, periodic boundaries. No 2D vortices, no underdamped case, no experiment.
- The predictor gives a lower bound on available information. Absence of predictability before t ≈ 0 could reflect the network's limits, though three architectures agree.
- Error on locations is computed only when the defect count is right. The count accuracy is shown separately, as a colour scale.
- The explanatory account (aia, deconstruction) is from the authors' earlier paper [32], not shown here.
