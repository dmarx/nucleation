# THEORY-tmplydnj: Under Szeidl and Szucs's structural model and its identifying assumptions (an owner's ideology fixed across episodes; the government's online advertising budget not driven by the outlets' slant), both owner ideology and favor exchange drove the Hungarian portal Origo's reduced coverage of corruption in 2013–2016: favor exchange alone cut it by 29–44%, the new owner's ideology alone by 63% after 2016, and the two together by 74%, so they are substitutes; and a ban on targeted government advertising would have raised it by 41–79%

## Source

Szeidl and Szucs (2021), [LIT-tmp6b6sj](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6b6sj.md), read in [NOTE-tmpefpic](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpefpic.md): Section 4.4
(identification), Table IV (estimates) and Table V (decomposition).

## What was actually shown

The model of [THEORY-tmpwiydx](THEORY-tmpwiydx.md) was estimated by simulated method of moments
on three online episodes (before Origo's new editor, after it, after the new
owner) and two print episodes (before and after the fallout). δ is
identified from the co-movement of the connected outlet's relative slant
and government advertising. β is identified from periods in which the
owner, and so by assumption the ideal point, is unchanged but the reward
for slant changes: online, a rise in the government's budget under
Telekom; in print, the end of Simicska's connection. Pooled estimates:
δ = 441, γ = −69, β = 1.50, with bootstrapped intervals excluding zero.
Letting Simicska's ideal point change at the fallout gives a small,
insignificant change.

Decomposition for Origo, against a world with no targeted advertising and
Telekom as owner throughout: favor exchange cut corruption coverage by 29%,
44% and 42% in the three periods. The new owner's ideology alone cut it by
63%, and the two together by 74%, less than the sum. Setting δ = 0 would
have raised coverage by 41%, 79% and 42%.

What could have come out otherwise: β could have been zero, meaning slant
did not respond to the reward with the owner fixed, so that all content
favors were ideology. It was not, in any specification.

## What this does not say

- **Not free of the identifying assumptions.** If Simicska's ideology
  changed at the fallout for reasons other than lost favors, or the
  government's online budget responded to Telekom's slant, β is
  misidentified. The authors give arguments against both, not tests that
  settle them.
- **Not about rewards other than advertising**, or slant other than
  corruption coverage.
- **Not a prediction for a temporary ban.** The authors note that a pause
  expected to end may be absorbed by a flexible exchange.
- **Not a dynamic account.** Each episode is a separate equilibrium.
- **Not beyond Origo and Magyar Nemzet.**
