# CLAIM-tmpahrqb: A delusional aha is closure reached on an unfinished Gestalt

## The claim

Conrad read the beginning of schizophrenia through Gestalt psychology.
Recognition in healthy perception unfolds in stages: a diffuse background,
then flickering figures whose expressive qualities dominate, then the
actual form, recognized with relief. Mishara's review reports him likening
the delusional patient to someone held at the middle stage. There the
pattern's expressive or "physiognomic" quality stands in for its structure,
as a tree trunk at night is seen as a crouching figure. The patient also
cannot step outside the current frame and see the situation as others
would. The "aha" that ends the delusional mood brings the relief of
closure, but it is closure on an unfinished form.

Mathematical insight is also felt as closure: everything fits, and the
tension of not yet seeing goes. On this reading the two feelings need not
differ. What differs is whether the form that closed was the structure or
only its expression. That would explain Nash's report that his delusional
ideas came to him the same way his mathematical ideas did, and it is
[CLAIM-276](CLAIM-276.md)'s separation of coherence from fidelity, inside one mind.

## What it predicts

- Mathematical delusions will be held on the strength of how a pattern
  looks and feels: its symmetry, its rightness, numbers recurring. Checking
  its structure will be weak or refused ([QUESTION-tmpao6hx](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpao6hx.md), part 2: the
  dismissal of standard methods).
- Restoring the ability to shift frame should dissolve the certainty
  without changing the feeling's memory.

## What it does not say

That every mathematical delusion is schizophrenic; grandiose discovery
delusions are common in mania. Nor that Conrad's stage order holds. In
Hambrecht and Häfner's sample of first admissions, only the first stage was
well confirmed.

## Note of 2026-11-27: a mechanism for the shared feeling
