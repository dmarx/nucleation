# CLAIM-671: Where an organizational transformation increases a constituent's vulnerability to the discretionary choices of a responsible agent, and that vulnerability grounds special obligations under an independently justified principle, the transformation can strengthen that agent's responsibilities toward the constituent

## The claim

The owner's review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488) on 2026-11-13 stated it:

> Where an organizational transformation increases a constituent's
> vulnerability to the discretionary choices of a responsible agent, and
> that vulnerability grounds special obligations under an independently
> justified principle, the transformation can strengthen that agent's
> responsibilities toward the constituent.

The review: "This identifies the actual normative mechanism: increased
vulnerability to another's discretionary power, rather than integration
itself. It also prevents the claim from being read as saying that the
more completely an organization absorbs its members' capacities, the more
morally authoritative it becomes."

It replaces A's version (A12 §1, on Goodin): "The whole's
responsibilities could therefore grow as its integration deepens."
Versions 1 to 3 kept that wording, as a supposition on Goodin's
vulnerability principle.

## Integration and vulnerability

The review: "Even granting Goodin's vulnerability principle, deeper
integration need not monotonically increase vulnerability. Integration
could create credible alternatives, shared governance, enforceable
guarantees, or mechanisms for distributing responsibility." So
integration is one route by which a transformation can increase
vulnerability, not the mechanism. [CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md) gives that route: a capability
lost because another reliably supplies it is favoured to the one who
loses it, and one-way supply leaves a dependence that nothing in the
dependent's dynamics limits. Its reciprocal form, each partner supplying
what the other gave up, is integration that need not increase one side's
exposure, though [CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md) warns that mutual dependence can still be
organized as coercive leverage.

## Who bears the responsibility

A "responsible agent", the review's phrase. The claim no longer says the
whole bears it. Under [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) the arrangement transformed can be any
sense; the bearer must be something that can owe, an agent or a role, and
the constituent owed must have standing ([TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)). For an organism and
its cells, or an ecosystem and its populations, the claim names no bearer;
whether the arrangement grounds a norm there, and who would bear it, is
the owner's [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md).

## Why it is filed as supposed

The inference from dependence to obligation is one the record has refused
before. The authenticity line's reciprocal agency ([CLAIM-096](CLAIM-096.md)) was deferred
as needing "independent moral argument", and an earlier exchange put the
refusal as "interdependence ⇏ obligation" ([CLAIM-096](CLAIM-096.md)'s note of 2026-10-18;
[QUESTION-007](../questions.d/QUESTION-007.md)). The claim does not derive obligation from organizational
dynamics. It holds only under "an independently justified principle", one
the record does not hold. Goodin's is the principle in the sources reached
(the note of 2026-11-12 below). With the principle granted, the claim says
where the strengthened responsibility falls: on the agent to whose
discretion the transformation exposes the constituent.

## Admission

A12 §7 to §10 was a synthesis turn (curation entry of 2026-11-12). What
passes is a discriminating prediction. Given the principle, the components
leave open whether responsibility tracks integration or the constituent's
vulnerability to someone's discretion. The claim says it tracks the
vulnerability. So a transformation that integrates while bringing
alternatives, guarantees or shared governance predicts no strengthening,
and one that leaves the constituent exposed to an agent's discretion
predicts it.

## What it does not say

- **That the principle holds.** Goodin is unread here.
- **That integration strengthens responsibility.** Only through increased
  vulnerability to discretionary choices.
- **What the strengthened responsibilities require.** Kittay's caution
  against domination by supporters (note below) bears on it.
- **That every constituent is owed it.** Only parts with standing
  ([TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)); A12 §5: "A human constituent can possess moral claims that a
  cell, enzyme, or organizational subprocess does not possess in the same
  sense."

## Note of 2026-11-12: Fineman, read

Fineman's "The Vulnerable Subject and the Responsive State"
([THEORY-1818](../theory.d/THEORY-1818.md), read in [NOTE-1899](../notes.d/NOTE-1899.md)) is about one sense-3 whole, the
state, toward persons. It does not support this claim as titled.
Vulnerability on her account is universal and constant ("Vulnerability is
posited as the characteristic that positions us in relation to each other
as human beings and also suggests a relationship of responsibility between
state and individual", p. 255); it is not produced by the whole's taking
over its parts' functions, and the state's responsibility does not wait on
integration. It follows the state's role in constituting the institutions
that confer resilience ("To the extent that these legally constituted
institutions distribute significant social goods, they should be
monitored by the state", p. 272). That supports the governance clause A12
§10 stated, not the growth with integration in the title. The judgement is
[ARG-099](../arguments.d/ARG-099.md), Rejected. Her derivative dependency, "neither inevitable, nor
... universally experienced" (p. 264), is the nearest thing in her account
to integration-made vulnerability. The claim's defeat conditions are not
met: she grounds a special responsibility, in the state's constitutive
role. No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Goodin and Kittay, as far as reached

Goodin's book ([LIT-2531](../literature.d/LIT-2531.md)) and article ([LIT-2535](../literature.d/LIT-2535.md)) are Unavailable;
his own text was not read. The claim stays Proposed. What the sources
reached show, all about sense-3 wholes and persons ([TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)): families,
clients, compatriots, the state.

- **The supposition is Goodin's.** The APSR abstract: "it is the
  beneficiary's vulnerability rather than any voluntary commitment as such
  on the part of the benefactor that generates these special
  responsibilities", and agents may be vulnerable to us "individually or
  collectively".
- **The consequent is not shown to be his.** His cases are families,
  friends, clients, compatriots, the state, foreign aid and future
  generations; none is a whole and its own parts. A restatement by Carroll
  and Crutchfield (2022, CQHE 31(3)) reads the duty's strength as varying
  "according to the degree of dependence", which fits the claim only
  insofar as integration raises dependence, as the claim already says.
- **A route the defeaters did not list**, now added: the same restatement
  lets the duty be discharged "by transferring the child’s vulnerability to
  another person". The contents' chapter "Invulnerability as an
  Alternative" may hold a cognate route, reducing dependence instead of
  protecting within it. Unread.
- **A qualification from the abstract**: "the welfare state can satisfy
  the criteria of a morally acceptable dependency relationship". Goodin
  appraises dependencies themselves, not only the responsibilities they
  generate, so a whole's response to growing dependence would be judged by
  whether the dependence is acceptable. The criteria are in the unread
  body.
- **A qualification from Kittay** ([THEORY-1823](../theory.d/THEORY-1823.md), her 2026 interview):
  the tension "between insisting on support and seeing to it that there is
  non-domination on the part of those who give support", and "keeping the
  state pretty far away from the actual dependency relationships
  themselves". Growing responsibility is not warrant for growing control.
  Love's Labor itself ([LIT-2519](../literature.d/LIT-2519.md)) is Unavailable.

No status change.
