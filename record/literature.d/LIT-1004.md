# LIT-1004: Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing

Fricker, M. (2007). *Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing*. Oxford University Press. DOI 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198237907.001.0001.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of the author's précis, [NOTE-tmpaz74m](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpaz74m.md). The book was not read.*

- **The generic wrong.** A speaker given a prejudicially deflated degree
  of credibility "is wronged specifically in his capacity as a knower".
  That is epistemic injustice, and it is an ethical wrong as well as an
  epistemic dysfunction.
- **Testimonial injustice** is that deflation, caused by the hearer's
  prejudice. The précis says "prejudice", not "identity prejudice".
- **Hermeneutical injustice** needs no prejudiced hearer. It is a
  "hermeneutical lacuna" in collective understanding, "owing to the
  relative powerlessness of a social group to which the subject belongs",
  that keeps her from making sense of a social experience.
- **Corrective virtues.** Testimonial justice pre-empts the wrong by
  correcting credibility for prejudice. Hermeneutical justice can only
  ameliorate after the fact, by allowing for the speaker's handicap.
- **Hybridity.** Each virtue has the same immediate end under an
  intellectual and an ethical description, so each is both.
