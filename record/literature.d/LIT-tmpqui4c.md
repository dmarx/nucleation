**The exchange's characterization, through the stand-in: partly faithful.**
A19's "organizations and individuals can obscure their moral
responsibility by inserting technological processes between themselves and
consequential decisions" is the core (p. 137), and A's cases (targeted
advertising, teacher evaluations, gig-work platforms, criminal-risk
assessment) are the chapter's. A leaves out what makes it laundering rather
than delegation: the ascription of "morally relevant qualities" to the
process and the failure to "adequately account" (conditions 3 and 5), and
that two of the cases, Uber's goal reminders and COMPAS in Loomis, are not
laundering on the authors' account. A's loan example, where "each statement
could be factually correct", is laundering only if the account given is
inadequate; as A tells it, it is nearer Democratic Chair. The authors also
say the mechanism "need not be technological at all". A's "their 2021
work" is the book; the concept dates from 2019.
