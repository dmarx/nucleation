# THEORY-tmpkqzrf: Constructor theory puts constraint in two places: a principle is a law that constrains other laws and is tested through its rivals, and a constructor is a physical thing that keeps its ability to cause a transformation; a task is possible when the laws set no limit, short of perfection, on how well it could be performed and that ability retained, so possibility needs no actual constructor, and constructor-theoretic statements mention none

## Source

Deutsch & Marletto (2015), LIT-tmpp0mod, §1, §2 and footnote 1; read in
NOTE-tmpbcj9n.

## What was actually shown

**Principles constrain laws.** The conservation of energy–momentum is the
model. "It is a *principle*—a law of physics that constrains other laws
rather than the behaviour of physical objects directly" (§1). Principles "do
not make direct assertions about the outcomes of measurements" but are
testable: "a principle *P* is refuted if some law violating *P* survives
experimental tests, whereas all rival laws conforming to *P* are refuted"
(§2). Footnote 1 gives the pattern: beta decay kept energy conservation by
way of the neutrino, and parity invariance was refuted "in just that way".

**Constructors are realized and approximate.** The constructor is the agent
"whose defining characteristic is that it remains unchanged in its ability
to cause the transformation again" (§2). "No perfect constructors exist in
nature. Approximations to them, such as catalysts or robots, have non-zero
error rates and also deteriorate with repeated use."

**Possibility needs no constructor.** A task is possible "if the laws of
nature impose no limit, short of perfection, on how accurately *A* could be
performed, nor on how well things that are capable of approximately
performing it could retain their ability to do so again" (§2). Subsidiary
theories must measure the accuracy of approximate constructors "(including
its own maintenance)". "Constructor theory is the theory that the (other)
laws of physics can be expressed *without* referring explicitly to
constructors."

**Role, not kind.** "Loops are excluded, because a substrate on a loop is a
constructor" (§2). Whether a system is a constructor or a substrate depends
on where it sits in a network of tasks.

## What this does not say

- That principle I is true: that every law can be stated as possible and
  impossible tasks. That is the conjecture of the whole programme, and the
  paper does not argue it beyond its uses.
- That a law of possibility is a constraint "realized and maintained by
  something", or that it is not. The paper treats possibilities as facts
  about the laws and says nothing about what realizes a law. Whether this
  makes it a case of a constraint realized by nothing is CLAIM-tmpmand1's
  question, not the paper's.
- That a constructor is a constraint in Montévil and Mossio's sense. The
  definitions are alike, but constructor theory has no time: "the notion of
  time does not appear" (§2). Its persistence is a limit over repetitions,
  not conservation at a time scale.
- Anything about loops beyond the one sentence. Closed networks of
  constructors are not treated.
