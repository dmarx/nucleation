# NOTE-013: Quasi-quantization

## Contribution

The paper argues that much of quantum theory follows from one departure from classical physics: a fundamental limit on which probability distributions over physical states can be prepared, i.e. on what any observer can know. It surveys such "epistricted" theories for continuous and discrete degrees of freedom and shows that one restriction, classical complementarity, defined via the symplectic structure of phase space, begins to unify them. Because it applies to any classical degree of freedom it acts as a "quasi-quantization" scheme, yielding only a subset of quantum theory (and, for bits, a distorted one). The author proposes classifying quantum phenomena as weakly or strongly nonclassical according to whether they can arise in an epistricted theory.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Table II sorts phenomena: noncommutativity, superposition, collapse, complementarity, no-cloning/no-broadcasting, interference, teleportation, key distribution, dense coding, much of entanglement theory, Naimark/Stinespring/Choi-Jamiolkowski all arise in epistricted theories; Bell and noncontextuality-inequality violations (and any computational speed-up) do not (§I.B, Table II).
- The metric of success is argued to be the number of "distinctively quantum" phenomena reproduced, not the fraction of Hamiltonians covered (Gaussian epistricted mechanics only has at-most-quadratic Hamiltonians) (§I.B).
- For continuous variables and odd-prime dimension, the quadrature epistricted theory is operationally equivalent to the quadrature (stabilizer/Gaussian) quantum subtheory, with the Wigner representation as the bridge (§IV.A, App. A: stabilizer states = quadrature states).
- For bits/qubits the two theories are structurally isomorphic (states, measurements, transformations) yet make different predictions, because qubit Wigner functions go negative; the qubit stabilizer subtheory shows contextuality/nonlocality while the toy theory cannot (§IV.B).
- The author calls this qubit/bit gap "perhaps the most interesting product" of the programme and suggests the quantum innovation can be described possibilistically rather than probabilistically (§IV.B conclusion).
- Proposes extending the scheme to electrodynamics and gravity via a Lagrangian quasi-quantization, left open (§I.A).

## Open questions

- Strongest claim: the weak/strong nonclassicality split. Check the exact definition of "classical complementarity" (§II.A) and whether Table II's placements (e.g. teleportation) rest on the stabilizer-restricted versions of those phenomena, as the text concedes.
- The odd-prime equivalence is a known result (Gross's discrete Wigner function); the deeper read should isolate what is new here versus Spekkens 2007 toy theory and Bartlett-Rudolph-Spekkens 2012.
- ML link: none direct. Loosely, it is a worked example of "a restriction on admissible distributions generates rich structure", and positivity of quasiprobability representations reappears in item 71; do not overstate this.
