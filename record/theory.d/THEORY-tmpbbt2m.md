# THEORY-tmpbbt2m: Asano shows that a passive rimless wheel's gravity-powered gait on a slope is asymptotically stable and period-1 because its impact posture and the energy gravity restores each step are fixed by geometry, so the return map's eigenvalue is cos²α, independent of the step period, and the gait exists only above a minimum slope tan²(α/2)

## Source

Asano (2012), LIT-tmpckrd7; read in NOTE-tmpbpr6m. A neighbour of McGeer
(1990), LIT-tmpv6ctd, which was not read.

## What was actually shown

- **Convergence** (eq. 1): K⁻ᵢ₊₁ = εK⁻ᵢ + ΔE with ε = cos²α and ΔE fixed
  by geometry and slope, so the gait "always becomes asymptotically stable
  and 1-period" (abstract).
- **The eigenvalue** (§IV-B, p. 4): the stance-phase error ratio "is equal
  to cos α; the stability property is determined only by the
  configuration", so the return map's eigenvalue is cos²α.
- **The minimum slope** (§IV-B): the energy margins "must be positive in
  stable passive-dynamic gaits", which in the linearised model holds for
  ϕ ≥ tan²(α/2); the step period diverges as ϕ approaches it.

## What this does not say

- That walkers with a swing leg, McGeer's two-legged models among them, are
  stable for the same reason; the extension is promised, not given.
- Where the gait ends on steep slopes: the onset of jumping is not
  identified.
- Anything about McGeer's own results, which the paper cites and does not
  restate.
- That the bounds hold exactly in the nonlinear model.
