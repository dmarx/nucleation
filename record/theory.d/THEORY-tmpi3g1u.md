# THEORY-tmpi3g1u: Seamster and Charron-Chénier define predatory inclusion as giving a marginalized group access to a good, service or opportunity it was historically excluded from, on conditions that jeopardize the access's benefits while dominant actors profit, and argue from Survey of Consumer Finances data that Black households' education debt rose faster than white households' from 2001 to 2013 and exceeded it by 2010, a gap that educational attainment and socioeconomic controls do not explain

## The account

Seamster and Charron-Chénier define predatory inclusion as giving a marginalized group access to a good, service or opportunity it was historically excluded from, on conditions that jeopardize the access's benefits while dominant actors profit, and argue from Survey of Consumer Finances data that Black households' education debt rose faster than white households' from 2001 to 2013 and exceeded it by 2010, a gap that educational attainment and socioeconomic controls do not explain

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpvf8za](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvf8za.md). [ARG-tmpou39l](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpou39l.md) says how far it supports [CLAIM-tmp16mkz](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp16mkz.md).

## What it does not say

That the lending produced the need, or built reliance on the lender: the need predates the access, and what it leaves is debt. Nor which lenders drive the gap, which is conjectured.
