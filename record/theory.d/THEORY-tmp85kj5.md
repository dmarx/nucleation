# THEORY-tmp85kj5: Implementation intentions affect performance only when the goal intention they serve is strong and activated, so the cue-triggered response is gated by the goal and does not fire on the cue alone

## Source

Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), LIT-1497, read in NOTE-1124, p. 103; and
Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024), LIT-1488, read in NOTE-1131, Table 2
and p. 177. What the two showed is THEORY-904, which this entry extends.

## The claim

Gollwitzer and Sheeran state the gate as a general property of
implementation intentions: "action control by implementation intentions
does not involve a mechanistic elicitation of action in the presence of
environmental cues but rather respects the presence versus absence of
activated strong goal intentions" (p. 103; NOTE-1124, C6). An if-then plan
delegates the response to its cue, but whether the cue produces it is
decided by the goal the plan serves. Sheeran, Listrom and Gollwitzer read
their 2024 moderator the same way: planning works when motivation to reach
the goal is high, and not when it is weak (NOTE-1131, C6).

The evidence is narrative in 2006, from a handful of studies, and a
between-test difference in 2024 (d = .79, k = 8), with a null in the weak
cell only among the physical-activity tests. No crossed test of plan
against goal is reported, and the authors leave open whether the gate
survives the plan's being acted on many times.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, which is QUESTION-314: plan crossed with an activated or
deactivated goal, in samples that could show a null in the weak cells,
preferably repeated after many executions of the plan. Between-test
comparisons in a meta-analysis cannot settle it.
