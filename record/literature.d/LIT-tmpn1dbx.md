# LIT-tmpn1dbx: What is better for animal conservation translocation programmes: Soft- or hard-release? A phylogenetic meta-analytical approach

Resende, P., Viana-Junior, A., Young, R. & de Azevedo, C. (2021). What is better for animal conservation translocation programmes: Soft- or hard-release? A phylogenetic meta-analytical approach. *Journal of Applied Ecology* 58: 1122–1132. doi:10.1111/1365-2664.13873. Initials as Morris et al. give them for the same team's 2020 review, unverified for this paper.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. From the Crossref abstract, unverified until read.*

- **Soft release about 40% better overall**, estimate 0.44 (95% CI
  0.11–0.76), from 61 effect sizes in 17 studies of 17 species.
- **Mostly reptiles and movement.** The advantage is driven mostly by
  terrestrial reptiles; for birds and mammals, and on the other success
  metrics, soft and hard release had similar effects.
- **Origin did not matter** to the soft-against-hard comparison: captive
  and wild animals alike.

## Standing in the record

Unavailable. It stands against Morris et al. (2021), LIT-tmph8zqa, read
as its preprint, which found no benefit of soft release on reported
success and higher population growth after hard release. The two need not
conflict: by its abstract this one's advantage rests mostly on reptiles
and on movement metrics, such as site fidelity after release, not on
population growth. Nothing should be cited from it as read until its text
is obtained. It bears on CLAIM-597 and QUESTION-398: whether a support
given at release and then withdrawn leaves the released animals better
able to do without it.
