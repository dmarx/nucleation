# NOTE-tmp2mtd7: Guida, Minutola and Presti — Propagation, not aggregation

## Contribution

It names the step between an individual intervention and an
organizational change as a separate problem, propagation, and gives it
two conditions: the leverage of the members whose flexibility rose and
the value content of the culture. It recasts Bond's organizational
flexibility model (the "orgflex", Bond 2018) as one case of that
mechanism, the case where the people who change are the leaders. It is
a theory paper: the authors call the propositions "hypotheses, not a set
of findings" and say the two measures a test needs do not yet exist.

## Key insight

Flexibility does not add up. A raised repertoire in one member persists
and recurs in untrained colleagues only if that member actually changes
the reinforcement and punishment those colleagues meet, and only inside
a culture that does not penalize the action. The mediator to be measured
is a change in consequences, not a change in what members report.

## Assumptions

- **Functional contextualism.** Behaviour is act-in-context;
  psychological flexibility is a repertoire of six processes (the
  hexaflex), not a trait, and fusion is a position on a continuum set by
  context.
- **Multilevel selection.** Through the Prosocial framework (Atkins,
  Wilson and Hayes 2019), with Ostrom's core design principles as
  generalized by Wilson, Ostrom and Cox (2013), selection by consequences
  is applied to the interlocking action of many people.
- **Culture as a contingency structure.** Culture is "the consequences
  members deliver to one another, together with the rules and shared
  statements of purpose that modulate their function". §5.3 calls this
  "a deliberate simplification".
- **Values versus goals.** In the ACT sense values are inexhaustible
  directions and goals exhaustible targets; profit is treated as a goal.
- **Cross-level parallel.** Defusion changes the function of a private
  event at Level 1; culture changes the function of an adverse
  consequence at Level 3. The paper says the parallel is functional, that
  "no proposition rests on it", and that it is "assumed rather than
  demonstrated".

## Key results

There are no empirical results. The propositions (§4):

- **P1.** Individual flexibility is positively associated with worker
  health. Stated as the established premise, not a contribution, and in
  §5.3 as resting "largely" on cross-sectional evidence.
- **P2.** Applying the Prosocial method turns individually held values
  into jointly held ones, measured by a self-report index and a
  behavioural convergence index scored against target repertoires taken
  from a source independent of the trained group. The one proposition
  that claims the effect of a specific intervention.
- **P3.** A value-based culture moderates the flexibility–health link.
  What moderates is value content, not resources or job control; it is
  falsified if a high-control, low-value-content setting converts
  flexibility into health as fully as a value-based one. It is set
  against Bond and Flaxman (2006), where job control moderates.
- **P4 (the central one).** Retention and spread of a rise in individual
  flexibility depend jointly on leverage and culture. Leverage is
  "effective control over the consequences others encounter, not
  position on the organizational chart", vertical (appraisals, resources,
  tasks) or horizontal (peer accountability, Ostrom's principle 3 and
  graduated sanctions). Falsified if training low-leverage members in a
  penalizing culture yields action that is retained and taken up by
  others. Leverage must be measured a priori, "through an index of who
  controls whose consequences", or the proposition becomes circular.
- **P5.** The resulting flexibility shows as group-level functioning
  (purpose-consistent action under pressure, adaptation that keeps
  purpose, coordination that survives disruption). Not yet testable: the
  measure must be built first and kept independent of P4's.
- **Design (§5.1).** Leverage high/low crossed with culture
  value-based/extractive, in three studies: a negative control, leverage
  varied with culture held, culture varied with leverage held.
- **Diagnostic (§3.3, §5.2).** Compliance and values-consistent action
  look alike while members are watched; they separate when oversight
  relaxes.

## Concepts

- **Propagation**: "the retention over time of a values-consistent
  repertoire in those who acquired it, under conditions in which that
  repertoire carries a local cost, together with its recurrence in
  colleagues who were not themselves trained".
- **Leverage**: effective control over the consequences colleagues meet;
  vertical or horizontal.
- **Organizational flexibility**: a property of how members' actions
  interlock (Glenn's interlocking behavioural contingencies), not a state
  of any member nor a property over and above them.
- **Value-based culture**: one whose shared purpose changes what
  reinforces members, so that action holds without surveillance.
- **Niche construction**: an organization's insulation of itself from
  extractive market selection by governance, ownership or market
  segment; "insulation of degree, not immunity".

## Connections

Its individual-scale premise is ACT's psychological flexibility (Hayes et
al. 2006), for which the record holds Kashdan and Rottenberg (LIT-553) and
THEORY-050. Its organizational antecedent is Bond (2018), a chapter in
Wilson, Hayes and Biglan (eds.), *Evolution and Contextual Behavioral
Science*, pp. 131–148, which the record has not registered. Its collective
level is the Prosocial framework and Ostrom's principles. Its account of
culture is Glenn's (2004) cultural selection. It cites no work on
opinion dynamics or network influence, though its leverage index is a
directed graph of who sets whose consequences.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-349.** The claim's note says Bond's model "could not be
  identified uniquely". The paper identifies it: Bond, F. W. (2018),
  "Organizational flexibility: creating a mindful and purpose-driven
  organization". The paper agrees with CLAIM-349 that flexibility is
  context-sensitive and that more control is not more flexibility, and
  its compliance-versus-value test is close to the claim's RO-DBT
  translation (less disclosure under tighter control). It does not
  separate access to a signal from its authority over action; its unit is
  a repertoire and the consequences it meets, not a signal. So it is a
  prior transfer of ACT to the organization, as the claim already says,
  but not a prior statement of the claim's three-way split.
- **The influence model.** P4's leverage is close to what the record's
  influence canvas (CLAIM-297, CLAIM-282) computes: who weights whose
  output. The paper offers no dynamics for it. A reduction of P4 to a
  weighted influence network would be the record's contribution, and the
  paper's demand that leverage be measured independently of spread is
  the same circularity guard QUESTION-117 needs.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **No data, and no measure.** The two constructs on which every test
  depends, an a priori leverage index and a group-level index of systemic
  functioning, do not exist; the authors say so.
- **The motivating evidence is thinner than stated.** Fleming (2024) is
  cross-sectional and, as the paper describes it, found that
  participants in individual-level interventions were "no better off"
  than non-participants on wellbeing. That bears against individual
  benefit, the half of the premise the paper takes as settled, as much
  as against organizational transfer, which it did not measure. Calling
  it "direct evidence" that individual interventions "do not carry
  through to the organization" overstates it.
- **Errors and slips.** Two empirical sentences in §2.1 ("Systematic
  reviews and meta-analyses report consistent reductions …" and "A
  large-scale study across three Scandinavian countries …") carry no
  citation. Bakker and Demerouti's "Job demands–resources theory" is
  listed as 2016 with volume 22 of the *Journal of Occupational Health
  Psychology*, which is the 2017 volume (not checked against the
  journal). In the PDF the commentary under Proposition 2 is laid out
  after the heading of Proposition 4.
- **Normative stance.** Worker health is treated as a value in its own
  right throughout; the authors say so, and it shapes P3 and §5.2.

## Open questions

- Can leverage be measured before and independently of spread, as P4
  demands? A network map of who sets whose appraisals, resources and
  assignments is proposed but not built.
- Does P4 reduce to a known model of weighted social influence, in which
  case its predictions follow from the network alone?
