# THEORY-864: When a generative model reproduces a prejudiced narrative learned from its training data, the resulting testimonial injustice runs through a credibility excess assigned to the model, so that hearers believe the amplified account over the testimony of those it discredits, rather than through a hearer's prejudiced judgement of a present speaker

## Source

Kay, Kasirzadeh and Mohamed (2024), *Proceedings of AIES* 7: 684–697,
[LIT-1338](../literature.d/LIT-1338.md), read in full in [NOTE-1049](../notes.d/NOTE-1049.md) from the publisher's PDF.
The statement is the paper's, in §4 under "Amplified Testimonial
Injustice".

## What was actually shown

Nothing was measured. The paper argues that a model trained on web data
"inherit[s] and reproduce[s] the prejudices and biases embedded within
those sources", that deployment scale and "the perceived objectivity and
authority of AI-generated content can lend credence to these narratives",
and that the wrong therefore takes a different form from Fricker's
interpersonal case: "In the conventional human-only environment,
testimonial injustices involve a credibility deficit assigned to someone's
account of truth based on the prejudices of the listener. In the
algorithmic setting, the injustice requires a credibility excess assigned
to the algorithm; that is, humans believe the account amplified through the
technology over the individual or group who is discredited."

Its illustration is NewsGuard's test of GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 on 100 false
narratives (80% and 100% followed), which shows that models produce the
narratives on request. It does not show that anyone believed them over the
people they concern, which is the step the account needs.

What could have come out otherwise: the account predicts that attributing
a claim to a model raises belief in it relative to the same claim from a
human source of the discredited group. If model attribution lowers
credibility, the excess the account requires is absent.

## What this does not say

- **That users do overtrust models.** That is the premise the promote_when
  asks for.
- **That the prejudice has gone.** The prejudice is carried by the
  training data. The account moves it, from a hearer judging a speaker to
  a corpus shaping what the model says. It still needs it, which is why it
  does not supply what [CLAIM-406](../claims.d/CLAIM-406.md) says the loop lacks.
- **That deliberate fabrication with a model is testimonial injustice.**
  The paper says so for its "manipulative" configuration. Fricker
  ([THEORY-327](THEORY-327.md)) treats deliberate manipulation of credibility as a distinct
  wrong.
- **That the amplification is a self-reinforcing loop.** The paper says it
  "creates a feedback loop" and gives no condition. [CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)'s loop and
  [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md)'s condition are the record's, not this paper's.
