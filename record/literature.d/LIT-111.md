# LIT-111: AI Consciousness: A Centrist Manifesto

Jonathan Birch (2025), *PhilArchive preprint (BIRACA-4); v4 acknowledges a special issue of Philosophy and the Mind Sciences "in which this article appears"* — <https://philpapers.org/rec/BIRACA-4>

## Key takeaways

- Two challenges — users misattributing human-like consciousness to chatbots, and the real but untestable-for-now possibility of profoundly alien AI consciousness — must be met together. Birch argues this means nudging users out of the "persisting interlocutor illusion" without denying that AI could be conscious, while building research programmes, via comparative animal-consciousness science, that could test both computational-functionalist and biological-naturalist claims.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Birch identifies two urgent challenges. First, very many users will soon attribute human-like consciousness to AI companions and assistants on the strength of mimicry and role-play, and no one knows how to stop this. Second, AI might genuinely achieve profoundly alien forms of consciousness, and consciousness science is too immature to say either way. "Centrism" is the position that both must be taken seriously. They pull against each other: countering misattribution by calling conscious AI impossible undermines the second project, and taking AI consciousness seriously can feed misattribution. The manifesto tries to set out mutually consistent strategies for both.
