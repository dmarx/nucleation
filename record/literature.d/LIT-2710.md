# LIT-2710: The Influence of Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender and Insincere Apologies on Perceptions of Sexual Assault

Sarah J. Harsey and Jennifer J. Freyd (2023). The Influence of Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender and Insincere Apologies on Perceptions of Sexual Assault. *Journal of Interpersonal Violence* 38(17–18): 9985–10008. DOI 10.1177/08862605231169751.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of 2026-11-17; the reading of 2026-11-16 is below.*

- "Scores on all variables except victim responsibility (H1b) were
  statistically different between DARVO and No DARVO conditions"
  (Results).
- Support for punishing the perpetrator: 82% without DARVO, 60.7% with it
  (Discussion).
- The effect on the perpetrator's rated responsibility is "a value that is
  generally categorized as a small effect size" (Limitations).
- "Insincere apologies had minimal impact on ratings" (Abstract).

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read ([NOTE-2020](../notes.d/NOTE-2020.md) draft). The exchange's account is right in direction; 235 took part (230 in the analysed data), there
was one scenario in four variants, and effects were small. The exchange's own caveat, that such experiments give no
test of who is lying, holds: the perpetrator is guilty by design. Part of the DARVO contrast is with a condition in
which the accused said nothing. The registration named [CLAIM-709](../claims.d/CLAIM-709.md) and [CASE-279](../cases.d/CASE-279.md); the reading finds no bearing
on either. It bears instead on the record's epistemic-injustice readings, [THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md) and [THEORY-317](../theory.d/THEORY-317.md) (notes only).
