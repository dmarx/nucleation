# LIT-tmp07mjg: Procedural Skills Decay in Emergency Medicine: A Scoping Review

Oskar, K., Prehoda, E., Sapp, R., Qi, X., Botticelli, B. & Palaganas, J. C. (2026). Procedural Skills Decay in Emergency Medicine: A Scoping Review. *Cureus* 18(3): e104804. doi:10.7759/cureus.104804.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpw1i6o. Cureus pages are unnumbered; places are the review's sections.*

- **Decay is rarely the outcome.** "Few studies were explicitly designed
  to isolate and analyze skill decay as a primary outcome" (Theme 1).
- **Short follow-up.** "Only two of the included studies (5.5%) described
  skill retention or decay beyond a one-year timeframe" (Theme 2), with
  timeframes "driven primarily by study feasibility".
- **Narrow and simulated.** 43% of studies involved ultrasound; "Nearly
  all studies used simulation-based technologies" (Themes 3–4).
- **Attendings unstudied.** "only five articles (13.5%) reported a study
  population of exclusively attending EM physicians" (Theme 5).
- **The upshot**, in the abstract's words: "a literature base that is
  heterogeneous in design, limited in range and scope, and still
  evolving".

## Standing in the record

The pasted bibliography on emergency readiness cites it under maintaining
capabilities that are rarely exercised. A's quoted sentence is A's
paraphrase, fair in substance. The review is evidence that the decay of
rarely used clinical skills is poorly measured, and it carries the HALO
framing (high-acuity, low-occurrence procedures need training beyond
clinical shifts), which it takes from cited frequency studies, not from
its own synthesis. It says nothing about measuring capabilities by
utilization or about organizational slack. Filed from it: THEORY-tmpkplf4.
