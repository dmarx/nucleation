# THEORY-1296: Deliberate thought selects and sustains candidates from a stream of unintended thoughts that mind-wandering generates, and whether the conscious model of oneself as a knower that accompanies the selection causes it, enables it or only accompanies it is undecided by current evidence

## Source

Metzinger (2017), [LIT-1945](../literature.d/LIT-1945.md), §§3.2–3.4, pp. 11–17, read in
[NOTE-1496](../notes.d/NOTE-1496.md).

## What was actually shown

**The hypothesis.** "Could there be something like an exclusively internal
affordance competition? Let me propose that mind wandering ... may be
exactly this — the creation of a constant flow of possible mental actions
... Call this the 'cognitive affordance hypothesis'" (p. 11). "Every
spontaneously occurring 'task-unrelated' mental event is a potential task
in itself, a cognitive affordance ... The mind wandering network sets up
this task domain, and sometimes frontal regions of the brain latch onto the
best candidate, and create a transient functional integration with the
EAM" (p. 12). "Put differently, if conscious perception can be described
as a form of controlled hallucination, conscious thinking is a process of
controlled mind wandering" (p. 12). With M-autonomy the system can "not
only ... 'reactively pick up currently available affordances' ... but ...
actively sculpt its own internal affordance landscape" (pp. 12–13).

**The analogy behind it.** A sleep-walker who "briefly 'comes to'" and takes
control, then returns to autopilot: "first we are mental sleep-walkers;
then we episodically turn into genuine mental agents by 'seizing control'"
(p. 11). Mind-wandering is "model-free epistemic self-control", mental
agency "model-based" (p. 15).

**The open clause.** "on one end of the spectrum we might treat the EAM as a
mere epiphenomenon devoid of any functionally relevant properties; at the
other end it could be exactly the missing computational element that
constitutes epistemic, model-based predictive control on the mental level.
An intermediate position could be that most mental actions begin without
an EAM ... but end by transiently culminating in a conscious model of
agentive second-order knowledge. Here, the EAM would not constitute, but
represent. It might causally enable relevant second-order functional
properties like veto control ... Currently available empirical data do not
help to decide between these three metaphysical options" (pp. 13–14).

**What could have failed.** Nothing is tested. H1–H4 (pp. 14–15) are
proposed tests; the chapter cites Axelrod et al. (2015) and Broadway et al.
(2015) as "early examples of relevant studies" for H1, not read here.

## What this does not say

- **What makes a candidate "best".** Selection could follow the candidates'
  own epistemic value (content) or relations that govern which may be taken
  up. The chapter does not say, and its goal, epistemic value (p. 17), is a
  property of the candidates.
- **That mind-wandering exists for this.** "a central function", among
  others the chapter lists: incubation, memory consolidation, maintaining
  the autobiographical self-model (p. 3).
- **How attentional action begins.** "we may have a model of action
  initiation for CA, but not for AA" (p. 22).
- **That the 2015 principle holds.** [LIT-1941](../literature.d/LIT-1941.md) states that an ability is
  deliberately exercised only when represented ([THEORY-1300](THEORY-1300.md)). This
  chapter leaves open whether the representation does anything.
