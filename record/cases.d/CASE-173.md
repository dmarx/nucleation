# CASE-173: Aphids and Buchnera: an obligate nutritional symbiosis in which neither partner reproduces alone, and the symbiont's genome reduced to a seventh of E. coli's

## The case

Almost every aphid species has 60 to 80 bacteriocytes holding *Buchnera*,
a close relative of *E. coli*, transmitted maternally to eggs and embryos.
The abstract of the genome paper ([LIT-1596](../literature.d/LIT-1596.md)) says the mutualism "is so
obligate that neither can reproduce independently". The 640,681-bp
chromosome is a seventh of *E. coli*'s. It keeps genes for the amino acids
the host cannot make and lacks those for the ones it can, as well as genes
for cell-surface components, regulators and defence. The authors read
*Buchnera* as viable only in the bacteriocyte.

Its genes evolve faster than free-living relatives', with unusually many
replacement substitutions and A+T-rich changes. Moran ([LIT-1573](../literature.d/LIT-1573.md)) explains
this by Muller's ratchet in small asexual populations, with mutational bias,
so much of the reduction is not selected.

In the record, [NOTE-114](../notes.d/NOTE-114.md) reports Godfrey-Smith's placement of aphid–*Buchnera*
as both an organism and a Darwinian individual. The entry it reads, Wilson
and Barker, misplaces *Buchnera* in a human gut.

## What it can show

- **Dependence through degradation.** The symbiont's dependence was
  produced largely by drift, by the abstracts' account. That separates the
  route to dependence from any selection for the collective ([QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)).
- **Constituent autonomy lost while the whole thrives.** *Buchnera* cannot
  live outside the host. The partnership is one of the most successful in
  insects. It illustrates [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md): the whole's success does not preserve a
  constituent's viability on its own.
- **High cohesion by transmission.** Vertical transmission couples the
  partners' reproduction. The abstracts report the two genomes as
  complementary, and report no gene transfer to the host. Whether any
  occurred is for a reading to check. Compare the mealybug ([CASE-171](CASE-171.md))
  and *Paulinella* ([CASE-174](CASE-174.md)).

The case is documented in works the record has not read. What it shows
rests on their abstracts until they are read.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (later): a host protein inside *Buchnera*, and what the model says of the host

- **Host-encoded protein in the symbiont.** McCutcheon et al. ([LIT-1571](../literature.d/LIT-1571.md),
  read in [NOTE-1213](../notes.d/NOTE-1213.md)) report that host-encoded proteins have been
  identified by immunofluorescence "in the cytoplasm of aphid and
  mealybug endosymbionts". The study for the aphid looked at a single
  protein, and the mechanism of import is unknown. The paper does not say
  that the protein comes from a gene transferred from *Buchnera*: it
  speaks of horizontally transferred bacterial genes on host genomes in
  general. So the case's statement, that its sources report no gene
  transfer to the host, still holds of those sources. But the host
  already reaches into the symbiont, which bears on control (C) in
  [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md). *Buchnera* APS's genome (641 kb) is well above the
  400 kb and 200 kb thresholds of [THEORY-1049](../theory.d/THEORY-1049.md).
- **The host's side, on Athreya et al.** [LIT-1570](../literature.d/LIT-1570.md) (read in
  [NOTE-1211](../notes.d/NOTE-1211.md)) lists aphid–*Buchnera* as mutually obligate and
  vertically transmitted. Its model predicts that the faster-evolving
  partner, the symbiont, becomes obligate first, and that the host's
  dependence follows only if partners die inside the collective more than
  they are born there ([THEORY-1051](../theory.d/THEORY-1051.md)). That turns the case into a
  measurement: the demography of *Buchnera* within its host. Nothing in
  the sources reports it.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (batch 2): the genome paper read in full

[LIT-1596](../literature.d/LIT-1596.md) has been read in full ([NOTE-1236](../notes.d/NOTE-1236.md), [THEORY-1103](../theory.d/THEORY-1103.md)). So has
the review [LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md) ([NOTE-1241](../notes.d/NOTE-1241.md)). The case's first paragraph mostly
stands. Six corrections:

- **"Neither can reproduce independently" is cited, not shown.** It is the
  paper's opening sentence, cited to earlier work. The genome shows the
  symbiont's side only.
- **Almost all, not all.** The non-essential amino-acid genes are "almost
  completely" missing, and cysteine is still made, from reduced sulphate.
  The host's glutamate and aspartate are precursors of *Buchnera*'s
  essential amino acids, so the pathways interlock as well as complement.
- **The drift attribution has no source here.** The genome paper does not
  mention drift, Muller's ratchet or population size. "Dependence ...
  produced largely by drift" rests on [LIT-1573](../literature.d/LIT-1573.md) alone, which is Unfetched.
- **On gene transfer to the host, the genome paper says nothing.** It does
  not study the host genome. The host-encoded protein reported in
  *Buchnera* (the note above) is RlpA4. Its gene came to the aphid from
  another bacterium, not from *Buchnera*. Proteomics found no host protein
  reliably enriched in *Buchnera* ([LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md)). The paper itself proposes
  that the host supplies phospholipid, or the enzymes to make it, "like
  mitochondria do". That is untested.
- **Loss of self-regulation, not host control.** *Buchnera* lacks nearly
  all its own transcriptional regulators and keeps the genes they once
  regulated. That bears on C as lost self-regulation, not as host control.
- **Envelope loss above the threshold.** At 641 kb, *Buchnera* APS has
  already lost phospholipid and LPS synthesis. That is above
  [THEORY-1049](../theory.d/THEORY-1049.md)'s 400 kb boundary for complete envelope loss, and consistent
  with its remark that erosion begins in larger genomes.

Campbell et al. ([LIT-1590](../literature.d/LIT-1590.md), Table 1) cite 886 to 8,223 *Buchnera* cells per
aphid egg across three aphid species, from earlier work.
