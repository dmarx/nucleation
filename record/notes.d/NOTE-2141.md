# NOTE-2141: Howard and Ruder — Universal Language Model Fine-tuning for Text Classification

## Contribution

A recipe for transfer to text classification, and the authors' framing
that "not the idea of LM fine-tuning but our lack of knowledge of how to
train them effectively has been hindering wider adoption" (p. 1).

## Key results

1. **Labelled-sample cost (§5, Fig. 3).** "On IMDb and AG, supervised
   ULMFiT with only 100 labeled examples matches the performance of
   training from scratch with 10× and 20× more data respectively, clearly
   demonstrating the benefit of general-domain LM pretraining." With
   unlabelled task text, "at 100 labeled examples, we match the
   performance of training from scratch with 50× and 100× more data on
   AG and IMDb respectively". The abstract's 100× is the semi-supervised
   figure. Single runs, validation error, no variance.
2. **Pretraining, at full data only (Table 4).** "Pretraining is most
   useful for small and medium-sized datasets"; validation error on IMDb,
   TREC-6 and AG falls from 5.63, 10.67, 5.52 without pretraining to
   5.00, 5.69, 5.38 with it.
3. **The method matters (Tables 6, 7).** Fine-tuning only the last layer
   "severely underfits and is never able to lower the training error to
   0"; on TREC-6 it is worse than training from scratch. "ULMFiT is the
   only method that shows excellent performance across the board".
4. **Forgetting, as the authors mean it (§3.3, Fig. 4).** "Overly
   aggressive fine-tuning will cause catastrophic forgetting, eliminating
   the benefit of the information captured through language modeling";
   full fine-tuning's error rises after early epochs "as the model starts
   to overfit and knowledge captured through pretraining is lost". No
   retained capability is measured: forgetting here is loss of the
   pretrained benefit on the target task.
5. **Pretraining's cost.** "While this stage is the most expensive, it
   only needs to be performed once and improves performance and
   convergence of downstream models." (p. 3). Not counted in the
   comparison.

## Assumptions

Language modelling as the source task; layers from general to specific;
hyperparameters tuned once on IMDb; baselines with early stopping.

## Concepts

Discriminative fine-tuning; slanted triangular learning rates; gradual
unfreezing; supervised and semi-supervised low-shot learning;
catastrophic forgetting as loss of the pretrained benefit.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-756](../claims.d/CLAIM-756.md).** The first reading on record that measures prior learning
  lowering the labelled-sample cost of a later task to a stated level:
  evidence for the claim's premise, and for the owner's point at U37 in
  cost terms. Qualified: the saving is joint between pretraining and the
  fine-tuning method, and pretraining and unlabelled data are uncounted.
  Nothing on the claim's own content, valuation.
- **[CLAIM-758](../claims.d/CLAIM-758.md).** A terminological caution: ULMFiT's "catastrophic
  forgetting" is not collateral loss of held capabilities, the forgetting
  version 2 counts as cost.
- **[CASE-294](../cases.d/CASE-294.md).** Samples to match a comparator is a ready form for the
  primary estimand; the fine-tuning protocol alone moves the cost a great
  deal, so it must be fixed in advance.

## Limitations

Single runs; plot readings; an unspecified from-scratch baseline;
forgetting not measured on what was pretrained; text classification only.
