# NOTE-tmpivq5d: Moll and Kadipasaoglu — The primacy of social over visual perspective-taking

## Contribution

A short review arguing the developmental primacy of social over visual
perspective-taking, and explaining it by joint attention.

## Key results

1. **The thesis.** "we argue for the developmental primacy of social over
   visual perspective-taking" (p. 1).
2. **Ages.** "level 1 perspective-taking as demonstrated by tests using
   interactive methods emerges between the second and third birthday" (p.
   4); level 2 "emerges between 4 and 5 years" (p. 3).
3. **Common ground.** "Through joint attention, infants construct a common
   ground (Clark, 1996) with specific other persons" (p. 3). "Contra Clark
   and Marshall (1981) and contra intuition, immediate physical
   co-presence does not necessarily facilitate the delineation of common
   ground." (p. 4).
4. **Over-attribution.** "young children have a proclivity to treat social
   interaction as a sufficient condition for shared perceptual
   availability" (p. 5); "A more synchronous pattern is found at level 2"
   (p. 5).
5. **Joint attention first.** "Joint attention thus directly paves the way
   to early forms of social, but not visual perspective-taking." (p. 6).

## Assumptions

Reference-disambiguation tasks measure perspective-taking; the paper
concedes looking-time findings weaken the role of joint attention.

## Concepts

Social versus visual perspective-taking; common ground with specific
persons; overestimation of shared perception.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's question**, support for common ground as primitive: what
  young children treat as mutually given comes from a shared history with
  a partner, not from recursive inference from co-presence (the Clark and
  Marshall route is rejected for young children).
- **THEORY-tmpqxwyk** (Schelling), a qualification of its
  mutual-expectation reading for young children.
- **THEORY-495**, support: perspective-taking serves cooperation before
  competition.

## Limitations

Short and programmatic; mostly the authors' paradigms; the drawing
argument is interpretive.
