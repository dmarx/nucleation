# THEORY-507: Any system with the same fine-grained functional organisation as a conscious system has qualitatively the same experiences, with natural though not metaphysical necessity, because otherwise a neuron-by-neuron replacement would yield either a rational subject systematically wrong about its fading experience or experience that flips with a switch nobody could notice; the principle covers only isomorphs, so it says nothing about systems merely similar in kind to a conscious one

## Source

- Chalmers (1995), "Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia", in
  Metzinger (ed.), *Conscious Experience*, [LIT-392](../literature.d/LIT-392.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md) from the author's text (sections, no page numbers).
- Chalmers (1995), "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness", [LIT-406](../literature.d/LIT-406.md),
  §7, read in [NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md): the same principle as one of two nonbasic
  psychophysical laws, argued in one page.

## What was actually shown

**The principle (§1).** "Given any system that has conscious experiences,
then any system that has the same functional organization at a fine
enough grain will have qualitatively identical conscious experiences." A
functional organisation is a set of abstract components, their possible
states, and the dependencies among them and the inputs and outputs; "fine
enough" means fine enough to fix behavioural dispositions, and the two
systems must be in corresponding states.

**Fading qualia (§3).** If a silicon isomorph lacked experience, then along
a one-neuron-at-a-time replacement series experience either vanishes at
one step, which would need a brute discontinuity in laws that compound
only continuous dependencies, or fades. Halfway, "Joe" says "bright red"
while experiencing "tepid pink", and is "utterly out of touch with his
conscious experience". Everyday fading, in drowsiness, comes with a
matching change in function; Joe's does not. Searle's variant, with true
but impotent new beliefs, is rejected: an unchanged organisation has "no
room" for them.

**Dancing qualia (§4).** If an isomorph had inverted qualia, some two
systems ten percent apart along the series would differ noticeably.
Install the second's circuit beside the first's with a switch. Flipping it
changes experience and no function, so the subject is "not suddenly
disposed to say 'Hmm! Something strange is going on!'". The same
construction serves against absent qualia.

**Populations (§3).** The series runs equally with neurons replaced by
tiny homunculi, or by normal-sized ones linked by radio, "ending up with a
network of homunculi that is essentially equivalent to the population
controlling a robot", and on to one homunculus working from written
records. The system has the experiences; the homunculus "is merely acting
as a sort of causal facilitator".

**The modality (§5).** Fading and dancing qualia are intelligible, and the
premise that some brains are conscious is empirical, so the conclusion is
natural, not logical, supervenience: "nonreductive functionalism", "just
as compatible with certain forms of property dualism … as with certain
forms of physicalism".

**What could have come out otherwise.** The conditionals (C2, C4 in
[NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md)) could not, given the series. What carries the weight is the
rejection of their consequents, and that is a judgment, not a result: "these
arguments do not constitute conclusive proof" (§2).

## What this does not say

- **That any actual AI system, animal or group is conscious.** The principle
  quantifies over fine-grained isomorphs of something already conscious.
  The paper leaves open "just what sort of organization gives rise to
  experience" (§5). No current AI system is an isomorph of a human, and
  the United States is an isomorph of nothing, so the principle does not
  license Schwitzgebel's step to it by similarity of kind ([NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md),
  Bearing). Schwitzgebel's Leo case ([LIT-459](../literature.d/LIT-459.md)) applies the invariance style
  of argument to a group without an isomorph, but only against Kammerer's
  principle on a loose reading.
- **That experience is functional organisation.** The supervenience is
  natural, not metaphysical, and the view is offered as compatible with
  property dualism. It sits inside the naturalistic dualism of
  [THEORY-694](THEORY-694.md), not against it.
- **That coarser realisations are covered.** The series is built neuron by
  neuron, and "the arguments generalize" is asserted. Block's main China
  system, a machine table with a line per person, is reached only by that
  assertion; his note-17 case, a billion people each simulating a neuron,
  is reached directly.
- **That the loopholes are closed.** Speed (a much faster or slower
  isomorph cannot be swapped in), history, very mild inversions and
  unattended qualia are conceded as coherent but called unmotivated (§4).
- **That self-knowledge is reliable outside the systems it was observed
  in.** An opponent can hold that it is, which is the question at issue
  ([NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md), Limitations).

## Connections

- **Anti-nesting ([THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md), [QUESTION-046](../questions.d/QUESTION-046.md)).** If invariance holds, a whole
  whose parts are minded people can have experiences of its own while the
  parts keep theirs, so Putnam's stipulation and IIT's exclusion, where it
  would deny the whole, fail for isomorphs. Kammerer's functional
  principle survives, since a homunculus in a neuron's role is one a chip
  could fill. The dispute over a modular mind and a nation becomes wholly
  architectural, which invariance cannot decide because neither is an
  isomorph of the other or of us. That is the partial answer to
  [QUESTION-046](../questions.d/QUESTION-046.md): a constraint on any criterion, not a criterion.
- **IIT** holds that a functional equivalent can differ in Φ and lack
  experience ([LIT-445](../literature.d/LIT-445.md), [NOTE-365](../notes.d/NOTE-365.md), C7), which denies this principle for
  isomorphs. The two cannot both be true.
- **Opposite conclusions from one premise.** Dennett's "Quining Qualia"
  ([THEORY-415](THEORY-415.md)) also turns on a change in experience the subject could
  not notice, and concludes that qualia separable from reactions are
  incoherent. Chalmers concludes they are fixed by organisation.
- [THEORY-252](THEORY-252.md) (Gidon, Aru & Larkum) argues that on computational
  functionalism an idle clamp that changes no actual activity can change
  consciousness. It reads itself as the converse of fading qualia: this
  argument holds organisation fixed and changes the substrate, the clamp
  holds substrate and activity fixed and removes unused organisation.
  [THEORY-095](THEORY-095.md) (Block's Inverted Earth) denies that long-arm functional role
  fixes phenomenal character, and says it must not be cited against this
  principle, which is about internal organisation.
- [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md): the principle is the conditional the functionalist side of
  the AI dispute assumes, and it reaches no system that is not an
  isomorph.
