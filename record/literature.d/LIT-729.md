# LIT-729: Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning

James G. March (1991), *Organization Science* 2(1):71–87 —
DOI-10.1287/orsc.2.1.71

## Key takeaways

- **The trade-off** (p. 71). Exploration is search, variation,
  experimentation; exploitation is refinement, selection, execution.
  Exploring without exploiting gives "too many undeveloped new ideas";
  exploiting without exploring leaves systems "trapped in suboptimal
  stable equilibria".
- **Why exploitation wins** (p. 73). Exploration's returns are less
  certain, later and organizationally further from the decision. Learning
  from feedback therefore improves exploitation faster, and competence in
  an inferior activity can shut out a better one.
- **Mutual learning** (pp. 74–76). Members learn from an organizational
  code (rate p1), and the code learns from members who know more than it
  does (rate p2). Members and code converge on a stable, not necessarily
  accurate, belief. Equilibrium knowledge is highest when the code learns
  fast from members who are socialized slowly.
- **Mixed learners and turnover** (pp. 76–80). A mix of fast and slow
  learners beats a uniform population with the same average rate. Modest
  turnover improves the code: recruits know less, and "The gains come from
  their diversity".
- **Turbulence** (p. 80). When reality changes and there is no turnover,
  converged beliefs stay fixed "regardless of changes in reality" and
  decay to chance. Moderate turnover avoids this, unless recruits are
  chosen to fit the code.
- **Competition** (pp. 81–84). Where only first place counts, variance
  matters more than mean as competitors multiply, and reliable knowledge
  costs a chance of primacy.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a supplement to the essay on
organizational will. It gives §5.4 a mechanism for lock-in without anyone
choosing it: an organization that socializes its members fast stops
learning from them. It gives §8.2 an organizational counterpart of the
transient-diversity result in epistemic networks ([THEORY-148](../theory.d/THEORY-148.md)). The
account is [THEORY-142](../theory.d/THEORY-142.md).
