# NOTE-184: SEP — Units and levels of selection

## Contribution

Evolution by natural selection is biology's crowning theory, yet which entities are selected is contested. Lloyd clarifies the debates by distinguishing four research questions, often confused, and two schools of multilevel genetics. She then reviews particular debates — group selection, species selection, genic selectionism, genic pluralism, transitions in individuality, holobionts — showing which questions, alone or combined, drive each.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2.1: the interactor question (Hull: an entity interacting as a cohesive whole with its environment so that replication is differential), which is the oldest form of the debate (Lewontin 1970).
- §2.2: the replicator/reproducer question, tied to linkage disequilibrium — with strong linkage, single genes cannot act as separate replicators (Lewontin's whole-genome parameters).
- §§2.3–2.5: the manifestor-of-adaptation and beneficiary questions; an ambiguity in "adaptation" (a product of selection vs an accumulated engineering design) has shaped both the group and species selection debates.
- §3: the evolutionary-change school (contextual analysis, quantitative genetics) vs the adaptationist/kin-selection school (ESS, inclusive fitness); following Wade & Goodnight, they ask different questions and make different modelling assumptions.
- §4: Williams's 1966 attack was on group benefit, not group selection; species selection was tangled with demands for "emergent" species-level adaptations; genic selectionism is really about the beneficiary question; §§4.5–4.6 cover ETI and holobionts.
- §5: "We should not treat different answers as competitors if they are answering different questions."
