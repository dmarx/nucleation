# ARG-tmplptzc: A collective whose members are all replaced continues because each newcomer takes its organization from the collective as found, and an unrelated maintainer restores a thing from the organization it finds in the thing, so by analogy replacing a maintainer leaves a causal path from the earlier relations to the later through the thing, and a car that changes garages is a continuation, not the defeater of the transmission criterion

## The inference

1. [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md) (Active): a collective that replaces all its members persists
   "by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by
   resemblance". No member need pass anything to its own replacement. The
   newcomer is socialized into the organization it finds, so the path
   from the earlier organization to the later runs through the collective.
2. [CLAIM-280](../claims.d/CLAIM-280.md) (Active): the persistence of a product and of the
   organization producing it are separate facts. A maintainer is such a
   producer.
3. A car that passes from one garage to an unrelated one is restored by
   the second to the arrangement it finds in the car: the parts in place,
   their fits, the record the car carries of its own repairs, and the
   type's manual for the rest.
4. So, as with the newcomer, the restored relations are caused by the
   earlier relations through the car. Nothing of the first garage's own
   organization need pass to the second. The car's continuation is
   transmission in [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s sense, and the admission case of [CLAIM-615](../claims.d/CLAIM-615.md)
   is not "a succession in which nothing of that organization was
   transmitted".

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are they alike, and is that the respect the
  conclusion needs?** In each, an agent with no tie to its predecessor
  takes up an organization from the thing that carries it, and its work
  re-produces that organization. The conclusion needs only that this is a
  causal path from the earlier organization to the later, which is
  [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s test against an organization that "arises independently".
- **What relevant difference is there?** A collective's newcomers become
  part of it, and a garage does not become part of the car. The
  conclusion does not need the channel to be inside the individual:
  [CLAIM-615](../claims.d/CLAIM-615.md)'s layer 4 lets "the transmitting organization" lie outside it,
  and [CLAIM-280](../claims.d/CLAIM-280.md) separates product from producer. A second difference: a
  collective's organization is carried largely by its members, and a car's
  by its standing parts. That makes the car the easier case. The thing
  carries more of what the new agent needs.
- **What transfers, and what does not?** What transfers is that a change of
  agent is not a break when the new agent works from the thing. It does not
  transfer where the thing carries too little of its organization for a
  new agent to work from, and the rest was held by the old agent. A
  project's maintainers hold conventions and plans its code does not
  carry. Whether losing those is a break in some respect is where reading
  (a), [CLAIM-tmp5koor](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp5koor.md), and reading (b), [CLAIM-tmp68okc](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp68okc.md), part. This
  argument decides neither. It concludes both because both need it.
- **Is the causal path real or a redescription?** The test is
  counterfactual. Had the car's arrangement differed, the second garage
  would have restored that arrangement. A replica built from the type
  with no tie to the token fails the test, since nothing in it varies
  with the token. That replica is the failure case [CLAIM-615](../claims.d/CLAIM-615.md)'s admission
  can use in place of the garage.
