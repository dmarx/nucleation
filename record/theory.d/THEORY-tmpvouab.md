# THEORY-tmpvouab: In a preregistered analysis of a non-elite US cohort, preschool marshmallow-test performance does not reliably predict age-26 achievement, health or behavior, its few bivariate associations being largely explained by demographics and the early home environment

Sperber, Vandell, Duncan & Watts (2024), [LIT-2723](../literature.d/LIT-2723.md).

## The analysis

Nine preregistered adult outcomes against 54-month performance. Only
educational attainment (r = .17) and BMI (r = −.17) were associated
bivariately, and "almost all regression-adjusted coefficients were
nonsignificant" (abstract). The preregistration fixed the test before the
adult data were seen, so a surviving association could have been found.

## What it does not show

- **That no outcome is predicted.** A non-preregistered binary model kept
  a BMI association with full controls (β = −.17, p = .03), uncorrected
  for multiple comparisons.
- **What the task measures.** The authors suggest a "screener for broader
  developmental advantages"; that is a reading, not a finding.
