# LIT-1102: Lower Bounds on Near Neighbor Search via Metric Expansion

Panigrahy, R., Talwar, K. and Wieder, U. (2010). Lower bounds on near neighbor search via metric expansion. In *Proc. FOCS 2010*, pp. 805–814. DOI 10.1109/FOCS.2010.82

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-914](../notes.d/NOTE-914.md)). Cell-probe lower bounds for near-neighbour search follow from the expansion of the graph joining points within distance r: a deterministic t-probe structure with m cells of w bits must have (mwt/n)^t at least the vertex expansion, and a randomized one must have m^t w/n at least the robust expansion at measure 1/m^t, on random data sets with weakly overlapping neighbourhoods. For the hypercube this recovers known bounds and gives, for example, space n^{1+Ω(1/(ct))} for approximation c and n^{Ω(1/(tε²))} for 1 + ε; for symmetric (Cayley) graphs a one-probe structure matches the bound, and for dynamic low-contention structures the update time must be at least the robust expansion over poly(t). The bounds are average case, for approximate decision search, and weak in t: they fall off exponentially in the number of probes, so they are tight only for a constant number of probes and say little about structures that probe n^ρ cells.

## Why this is here

[QUESTION-114](../questions.d/QUESTION-114.md) and [THEORY-340](../theory.d/THEORY-340.md): cell-probe lower bounds for near-neighbour search from the expansion of the metric, the similarity analogue of Yao's lower bound. Unlike Yao's, it allows any cell encoding; it is average case on random data and approximate, where Yao's is worst case and exact.
