# QUESTION-tmp2lvhi: What makes one agent's authority to decide about another's body legitimate when the other has standing but cannot decide: the other's welfare, their earlier wishes, their projected future wishes, or the relation between them, and why do these differ between a parent's consent for an infant and an advance directive for one's own later self?

## Why it is a question

A3 separates three rights that ordinary arguments run together: "A right
against unwanted intervention", "A right to receive care or support" and
"A right to authorize decisions on behalf of another". Infant circumcision
turns on the first and third. A9 §5 adds an asymmetry:

- "In an advance directive, there is an earlier exercise of autonomy that
  might govern later circumstances."
- "In neonatal circumcision, there has been no earlier exercise of
  autonomy by the patient; the authorization comes from a different
  person."

[QUESTION-364](QUESTION-364.md) asks what makes a revised will the continuation of the same
agent's will; the record has nothing on authority that passes between
agents. A9's proposed test: compare medically necessary neonatal surgery
with elective circumcision, holding fixed the inability to consent and the
permanence of the intervention, and varying necessity, expected welfare,
postponability and the parents' reasons, "to identify which differences
actually justify surrogate authority".

What would count as an answer: a ground for surrogate authority that sorts
those cases, and says whether the future adult's interest in deciding
(Feinberg's "open future", which Millum argues reduces to existing rights,
[LIT-tmpapwfu](../literature.d/LIT-tmpapwfu.md)) is a ground of its own.

## Bearing

- [TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md) asks a standing claim for its bearer, interests, ground and
  what follows; this asks who may act on what follows.
- [THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md) (the relation that decides in identity-involving ethics is
  not identity): A9 §3 suggests that what links infant and future adult
  may be causal dependence of the adult's autonomy on the infant's body,
  not numerical identity.
- [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md) (a voluntary delegation against the same restriction imposed):
  authorization by the agent's own earlier act, the advance-directive side
  of the asymmetry.
