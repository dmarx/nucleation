# CLAIM-tmppgdad: Where each nucleus formed by a teacher becomes a teacher, the succession replaces every outside teacher at one generation per teacher lifetime plus nucleus lifetime, and whether it sustains itself is decided by the aggregate teaching gain alone: populations matched in the number and turnover of their students persist or collapse on withdrawal by the share of students that can teach, and before withdrawal the difference shows only in the outside supply, material and teachers each needs

## The claim

**Succession** (e8). In [CASE-tmpy4orj](../cases.d/CASE-tmpy4orj.md)'s generation-resolved model, the
outside teacher is generation 0. A nucleus taught by a generation-g
teacher is generation g + 1, and it makes generation g + 1 teachers.

- **The founding.** An outside teacher is supplied at σ = 0.05 for 12
  time units. At withdrawal, 82 % of teachers are outside ones.
- **Replacement.** By t = 46, under 1 %; by t = 100, none to four places.
- **The generation rate.** The mean teacher generation then advances by
  0.0499 per time unit: one generation per 1/k_T + 1/d = 20 time units,
  a teacher's mean lifetime plus its student's.
- **With the supply kept on**, outside teachers stay at about 16 % of the
  pool.

**Teaching** (e7).

- **The populations.** Each holds 3.0 nuclei of equal turnover and
  material cost. A share ε cannot teach. Each is held by the outside
  supply its own steady state needs.
- **On withdrawal.** With ε = 0, 0.3 and 0.5 the populations persist.
  With ε = 0.6, 0.7 and 0.9 they lose every nucleus. The critical share is
  0.560.
- **Before withdrawal.** The populations differ in the supply each needs
  (0.033 at ε = 0, 0.078 at 0.6, 0.096 at 0.7), in material (4.4 to 5.9)
  and in teachers (2.95 to 2.02).
- **Only the aggregate matters.** The defective share enters the
  aggregate equations only through (1 − ε)β. A population with fewer
  teaching students, each teaching proportionally faster, behaves
  identically.

## What it does not say

- **Not QUESTION-361's distinction.** That distinction, a perfect student
  and a poor teacher, is between individual successors. This model
  separates them only in the aggregate, where it cannot tell which
  students teach.
- **No error is inherited.** Students here inherit no error from their
  teachers, and their teaching does not degrade down the generations. The
  "increasingly degraded teacher" of QUESTION-361 is not modelled.
- **The record's other cases.** [CASE-161](../cases.d/CASE-161.md)'s separation for governing rules
  is untouched.
