# LIT-tmpo461k: Cancer across the tree of life: cooperation and cheating in multicellularity

C. Athena Aktipis, Amy M. Boddy, Gunther Jansen, Urszula Hibner, Michael E.
Hochberg, Carlo C. Maley & Gerald S. Wilkinson (2015), *Philosophical
Transactions of the Royal Society B* 370(1673):20140219 —
DOI-10.1098/rstb.2014.0219

## Key takeaways

- **The framing** (Introduction). "Multicellularity requires the
  suppression of cell-level fitness in order to promote organism level
  fitness", and effective multicellularity needs mechanisms for
  suppressing conflict as well as cooperation. Cheating is defined
  functionally, as breaking shared rules for a cell-level advantage, not
  as an evolved strategy.
- **Five foundations.** Proliferation inhibition, controlled cell death,
  division of labour, resource allocation and transport, and maintenance
  of the extracellular environment. Their breakdown gives uncontrolled
  proliferation, inappropriate survival, deregulated differentiation,
  resource monopolization and a degraded environment.
- **The survey.** Cancer-like phenomena (abnormal proliferation or
  differentiation, a deliberately broad notion) are reported across the
  lineages with complex multicellularity, which evolved independently at
  least seven times. All or nearly all bilaterian animals look
  susceptible, and cnidarians suggest the problem is ancient. Gaps, such
  as sponges and Ulvophyceae, may be lack of study.
- **What generalises** (Discussion). Cheating on division of labour is
  present in all forms of multicellularity; resource and environment
  cheating are documented only in animals and plants so far. Every report
  surveyed showed both "demographic" and "economic" cheating.
- **Suppression.** Complex multicellularity "represents a highly
  sophisticated cheater detection and suppression system", working
  cell-autonomously, through tissue architecture and systemically.
  Proliferative units such as intestinal crypts may be an intermediate
  level of selection that makes suppression modular.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a supplement to the essay on
organizational will. It is the biological case of the essay's §6.3
"capture from below": lower-level units gaining at the collective's expense,
and the collective as a system for suppressing them. Okasha's overview of
the major transitions ([LIT-tmpxuqbt](LIT-tmpxuqbt.md)) supplies the general principle, and
cautions against applying the language of cheating where no collective
action problem has been shown. The account is [THEORY-tmp66bwr](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp66bwr.md).
