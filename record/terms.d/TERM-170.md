# TERM-170: institutional sociopathy

## Definition

Two conditions, both needed:

- **Legibility.** The needs and claims of those it affects are legible to
  it: it understands the harm. Not a failure of knowledge.
- **No answerability.** Under a declared standard of their standing, their
  claims carry no effective weight in what it does, neither through their
  own authority nor through any process that represents them.

Like domination ([TERM-124](TERM-124.md)), it is relative to a declared standard: whose
claims count, and for how much, is part of the judgment.

## How it differs from its condition

Asymmetric legibility ([TERM-169](TERM-169.md)) is the structural situation: the
institution sees those it affects, and they hold no direct authority over
it. The owner, 2026-11-24, separated the two: an institution in that
situation "can still respect the standing and claims of its constituents
without giving them direct authority", as "effective representative
governance" does. Their claims then carry weight through the processes that
represent them. Institutional sociopathy is that route failing.

## How it typically arises

The owner's contrast is "effective representative governance vs.
captured". A representative process is captured when it is driven by the
interests it was meant to check more than by the claims it was meant to
carry, which is interpretive capture ([TERM-066](TERM-066.md)) applied to representation.
So the common path runs from asymmetric legibility, through capture of the
representing processes, to claims that are legible and carry no weight.

## Use

- The end of A46's chain from measurement naivety ([TERM-173](TERM-173.md)) through
  greedy agency ([TERM-179](TERM-179.md)).
- Shareholder primacy as the owner states it ([CLAIM-1257](../claims.d/CLAIM-1257.md), U30) is a
  case where the representing structure, the board's duty, is defined to
  carry one class's claims only.
- Its wrong, beyond any one harm, is a breach of the second-order duty of
  answerability ([CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)).
- Whether such an institution shows remorse is separate ([QUESTION-265](../questions.d/QUESTION-265.md);
  [CLAIM-431](../claims.d/CLAIM-431.md), [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md)).

## Not to be confused with

- Asymmetric legibility ([TERM-169](TERM-169.md)), its condition.
- Incompetence or ignorance: the institution understands the harm.
- Malice: it need not aim at the harm.
