# LIT-tmpt2jbv: Précis of The Border between Seeing and Thinking

Ned Block (2024), "Précis of *The Border between Seeing and Thinking*",
*Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 110(1), 2025, pp. 273–283,
online 18 December 2024 — DOI-10.1111/phpr.13126.

## Key takeaways

- **The thesis (p. 273).** "Perception is constitutively iconic,
  non-conceptual and non-propositional while cognition is paradigmatically
  (but not constitutively) discursive (non-iconic), conceptual and
  propositional." Cognition can use perceptual material "in a cognitive
  envelope", as in reasoning with imagery. Block also accepts "a moderate
  level of modularity of perception".
- **The book's route (pp. 273–274)**, as the précis lists it: armchair and
  scientific indicators of perception (adaptation, speed, pop-out, illusory
  contours); low- versus high-level perception; infant colour perception as
  nonconceptual; core cognition as "a mongrel notion" to be split between
  perception and cognition; perception studied irrespective of
  consciousness, against cognitive theories of consciousness, which Block
  now takes "more seriously" in their "pointer" versions.
- **The cognitive envelope (pp. 274–275).** The global neuronal workspace,
  "often offered as a theory of consciousness", is "better thought of as a
  theory of how perceptual representations are conceptualized". Broadcasting
  in the workspace is "the main mechanism of 'working memory'", and its use in
  reasoning makes the represented content "in part discursive, conceptual and
  propositional".
- **Working memory is not held perception (pp. 276–279).** Nine differences:
  resistance to masking; recoding outside visual cortex; bias toward colour
  category centres; dependence on colour vocabulary; coarse grain; no
  divisive normalisation (Bloem et al. 2018); no iconic-memory partial-report
  effect across saccades; different decay ("sudden death"); task-dependent
  transformation (Kwak & Curtis 2022). Duan and Curtis (2024): V1
  working-memory codes are "abstractions of percepts". So "even the most basic
  perceptions ... are recoded when used in substantial reasoning".
- **Iconic format (pp. 279–281).** Block relies on a Shepard-derived notion:
  families of representations track degrees of environmental difference,
  change what they represent by degree, and mirror relations among the
  represented properties. Infant parallel-individuation and entropy-matching
  results show iconic representation in thought too, but within a cognitive
  envelope.

thesis and the book's plan in Block's words. It does not carry the book's

colour argument, the neural evidence for nonconceptual perception), which
remain unread here.

`cognition` primary, for the architecture of perception and thought;
`neuroscience` for the working-memory and decoding evidence; `consciousness`
for the reading of the global workspace. No anthology topic holds it, and it
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.

**Where it bears.**

- *Ivy & Mroczko-Wąsowicz* ([LIT-099](LIT-099.md), [NOTE-150](../notes.d/NOTE-150.md)) side "with Green (2020) and
  Block (2023), who keep a perception/cognition border without committing to
  penetration". The précis confirms Block keeps a border defined by format,
  not by encapsulation alone. [NOTE-150](../notes.d/NOTE-150.md)'s "Block (2023)" is very probably the
  book ([LIT-tmphk2iw](LIT-tmphk2iw.md)); Ivy's reference list was not checked.
- *Sustained perception and prefrontal content* ([THEORY-tmpwyfwm](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwyfwm.md)). That
  account finds content carried in ventral temporal cortex for as long as an
  image stays on, while prefrontal and parietal content is an onset event. On
  Block's reading the prefrontal ignition is not where the percept lives but
  where it is conceptualised, so the account's finding is what the
  cognitive-envelope view would predict for a task without report. That
  reading is mine; the précis does not discuss the study.
- *Report without laminar cortex* ([THEORY-tmprpyvx](../theory.d/THEORY-tmprpyvx.md)). The crow nidopallium
  caudolaterale is the prefrontal analogue that carries report. On Block's
  view such a region is a candidate for the envelope that conceptualises a
  percept for report, which is why that account's own promotion test asks
  for a no-report paradigm. No more is claimed.
- *Consciousness readings.* Block's consciousness papers, filed separately,
  are where the "pointer" versions of cognitive theories and the critique of
  prefrontal decoding are argued.
