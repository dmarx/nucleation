# NOTE-2034: NTSB (2019) — Collision Between Vehicle Controlled by Developmental Automated Driving System and Pedestrian, Tempe, Arizona (HAR-19/03)

## Contribution

An official causal account of the case A17 §4 uses for the moral crumple zone. It names both the
frontline operator's conduct and the organization's design and oversight conditions, and it finds
that the conditions contributed to the operator's own failure.

## Key results

1. p. v: "The system design precluded activation of emergency braking for collision mitigation,
   relying instead on the operator’s intervention to avoid a collision or mitigate an impact."
2. p. v, probable cause: "the failure of the vehicle operator to monitor the driving environment
   and the operation of the automated driving system because she was visually distracted
   throughout the trip by her personal cell phone"; ATG's failures contributed, "all a
   consequence of its inadequate safety culture" (p. vi), as did "the impaired pedestrian’s
   crossing of N. Mill Avenue outside a crosswalk" and Arizona's oversight.
3. p. vii: "Had the vehicle operator been attentive, she would likely have had sufficient time to
   detect and react to the crossing pedestrian to avoid the crash or mitigate the impact."
4. p. vii: her distraction was "a typical effect of automation complacency"; p. 44: "humans are
   very poor at this task".
5. p. viii: ATG's safety culture "created conditions—including inadequate oversight of vehicle
   operators—that contributed to the circumstances of the crash and specifically to the vehicle
   operator’s extended distraction during the crash trip".
6. p. 45: ATG "had the means to retroactively monitor the behavior of vehicle operators and their
   adherence to operational procedures, it rarely did so"; p. 29: the move to one operator was
   "an operational decision that allowed the company to increase the number of test vehicles".
7. p. 27: "She was suspended after the crash and was laid off after ATG ceased Tempe operations in
   May 2018."
8. Title verso: "The NTSB does not assign fault or blame for an accident or incident".
9. Landsberg, pp. 62-63: "since everything was going so well and the tagging process was made
   easier, ATG decided that a dedicated observer was not needed."

## Assumptions

Causal, not moral, attribution; the human-factors account of automation complacency taken as
settled.

## Concepts

Probable cause and contributing factors; safety culture; safety management system; automation
complacency; layers of safety redundancy.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md)**: a clean specimen of its structure, in causal terms: the operator answers for
  her conduct, and the conditions-setter is found to have contributed to that conduct itself
  (result 5).
- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: the case A17 §4 uses. It qualifies the claim's blame clause: the official
  account did attribute contribution upstream, though its probable cause names the operator and
  she was suspended and laid off. It also meets the claim's own exclusion, since the operator had
  adequate time (result 3). It says nothing of public or legal blame.
- **[CLAIM-422](../claims.d/CLAIM-422.md)**: ATG had the policies and the cameras but not the maintained capacity that would
  make them work (results 6-7); an instance of procedure without maintained capacity.
- **[CLAIM-700](../claims.d/CLAIM-700.md)**: an analogue, not an instance. Apparent success was read as licence to remove
  a redundancy (result 9), but the success was the automation's, not members' compensation.
- **The exchange** (A17 §4) is accurate except that it calls the operator's distraction
  contributing when it was the probable cause, and omits the pedestrian's impaired crossing.

## Limitations

A safety investigation that disclaims fault; one case; it cannot show how blame was distributed.
