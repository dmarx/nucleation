# CLAIM-tmp1qybv: That a mechanism realizes a capacity can mean that the capacity is produced through it over time or that the capacity consists in it at a time, and interventions show only the first: a pathway whose disabling changes the capacity after a delay is a cause, can be intervened on surgically, and can be located relative to a declared boundary, whereas every intervention on a capacity that consists in its parts also changes some part along another path, so that relation is underdetermined by intervention data and is inferred by abduction or from simultaneity and settled parthood; and which of the two a given dependence is depends on the time resolution at which a description treats the parts' response as instantaneous

## The claim

The record says "realized" in two places where it matters: CLAIM-522
("where maintenance is realized") and CLAIM-515's third question ("what
mechanisms realize them, shown by interventions that discriminate
implementations"). The constitution debate after Craver forces a choice
between two readings.

1. **Produced through: diachronic, and shown by intervention.** In
   CASE-189, ċ₁ = 3c₂²/(1 + c₂²) − (1 + d₁)c₁. Removing the c₂ → c₁
   production changes ċ₁ at once and c₁ only over the following interval.
   On Baumgartner and Gebharter's own criterion (M**), a variable is a
   cause when some intervention on it at t changes the other at a later t′
   (NOTE-tmpeljd8, p. 17). So the pathway is a cause of the later
   capacities. Interventions on it can be surgical: bottom-up interventions
   "may well exist" (their footnote 8). CLAIM-522's separation, disabling
   the pathway inside the boundary or interrupting the controller outside
   it, is of this kind. So is CASE-163's identification of a mechanism from
   probes within a catalog. The fat-handedness result does not reach them
   (ARG-tmp39a6w).
2. **Consists in: synchronic, and not shown by intervention.** If the
   capacity at t is what its parts are doing at t, every cause of a change
   in it is also a cause of a change in some part along another path. No
   finite set of manipulations then tells a model in which the capacity
   consists in those parts from one in which the experimenter merely moves
   both (THEORY-tmp3pqnm). The inference is abductive, from repeated failure
   to find a cause that spares the parts (THEORY-tmpy9dyw). Or it follows
   from one intervention that is surgical within each level, given that
   realization is simultaneous and that parthood and levels are already
   settled (THEORY-tmpv5g3z). CASE-189 has a dependence of this kind: the
   coupling L(c) is a function of the capacities, so no intervention on c
   leaves L alone.
3. **The line moves with the time scale.** Both negative results and the
   positive one draw the line between causation and constitution at
   simultaneity: "Simultaneous changes cannot be causally related"
   (NOTE-tmpz7pqv, p. 6). CLAIM-480's chain gives a dependence that falls
   on either side depending on the description. If the interior x₂
   equilibrates fast, the boundary sees the memoryless coupling E_eff, a
   change at the boundary is matched at once in the interior, and the
   dependence is synchronic. If x₂ relaxes slowly, eliminating it leaves a
   memory kernel, and the interior's response follows the boundary's
   change, as a cause's effect follows it. So whether a part's dependence
   on its whole is constitution or causation, in the debate's own sense,
   is fixed by the time resolution at which the description treats a
   response as instantaneous, not by the system alone. This is CLAIM-405's
   criterion-relativity applied to the causation–constitution line.

## Why it matters for the record

- **CLAIM-522 stands as stated.** Its conclusion is about where a cause
  sits relative to a declared boundary, which is reading 1.
- **CLAIM-515's third question should say which reading it means.** Read
  as 1, "shown by interventions that discriminate implementations" is
  right. Read as 2, it is not available even in principle, and the
  question becomes which abductive or principled route applies.
- **QUESTION-284** gets a partial answer for one relation. Part–whole
  constitution is not shown by any finite set of manipulations; a
  second-order pattern, or simultaneity with settled parthood, can support
  it. Neither route supplies the boundary.

## What it does not say

- **That constitution is unknowable.** Abduction and horizontal
  surgicality are routes to it; they are just not first-order intervention
  data.
- **That the record's organizations have constituents in the mechanists'
  sense.** Whether a maintained organization's capacity "consists in" its
  production network at a time is a further question. The record's
  constructions are stated as dynamics, which is reading 1.
- **That the time-scale point refutes the debate.** Its authors assume
  simultaneity as an idealization of the macroscopic sciences. Part 3 says
  the idealization is a choice of resolution, and that a verdict of
  constitution is relative to it. Krickel 2018 (LIT-tmp5bf7n, unread)
  reportedly reads mutual manipulability causally, between a component
  and temporal parts of the phenomenon, which would be the same move from
  the other side. Nothing here rests on that report.
