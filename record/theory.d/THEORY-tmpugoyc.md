# THEORY-tmpugoyc: Recognising one's body, face or voice as one's own is a probabilistic inference in which multimodal association cortex explains away unimodal prediction errors with a high-level prior that this object is "me", the most parsimonious cause of correlated signals across the senses; the rubber-hand, enfacement and rubber-voice illusions are that prior shifting toward another object under synchronous stimulation, by perception and learning without action, and context gates the shift

## Source

Apps & Tsakiris (2014), "The free-energy self: a predictive coding
account of self-recognition", *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*
41:85–97, [LIT-tmpetw17](../literature.d/LIT-tmpetw17.md). Read in full in [NOTE-tmp69t3e](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp69t3e.md) from the Europe PMC
author manuscript. The publisher's version and the cited studies were
not checked.

## What was actually shown

Nothing new was shown. The paper is an argument from the free-energy
framework to a reading of existing results.

**The phenomena it cites.** Ownership of a rubber hand, a seen full body,
another's face or a stranger's voice follows synchronous multisensory
stimulation, and fails or weakens when the object is non-corporeal, of a
different skin tone or wrongly placed. Self-stimuli activate face, body
and voice areas that respond to everyone, and several multimodal areas,
none exclusively. Predicted self-stimuli attenuate responses.

**The reading.** Each of these is redescribed as explaining away. The
self-label is a high-level prior; synchronous stimulation makes "the
rubber hand is me" the best explanation of the felt touch; context is the
prior; attenuation is a predicted error. The real hand's lower
probability of being "me" is read into reports of its cooling and raised
histamine reactivity.

**Its distinctive commitments.** Any sensory channel can explain away
surprise in any other, so self-recognition is not tied to motor efference.
No region is self-specific. The update happens without action.

What could have come out otherwise: little within the paper. The
authors' own §7 says that standard fMRI cannot separate prediction from
error signals, that no direct test of probabilistic self-representation
exists, and that "empirical data neither largely supports nor refutes our
account".

## What this does not say

- **That ownership is computed this way.** No model was fitted, and the
  account explains the illusion and its failure by priors fixed after
  the fact.
- **That the real hand is rejected.** The cooling and histamine reports
  are cited, not examined. A later study, not read here, reported no
  consistent cooling.
- **Anything about phenomenal selfhood.** The account is about which
  object is labelled "me". Why the label is experienced as being someone,
  Metzinger's transparency ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md), [LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md)), is not addressed.
- **That body ownership is the simplest form of self-awareness.** The
  paper treats mirror self-recognition as the behavioural marker, and does
  not rank ownership against it ([NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md), C6).
- **That the questionnaire reports are veridical.** The account takes
  them at face value, and expectancy could enter it as one more prior.

  paper's text.
