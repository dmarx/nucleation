# THEORY-1616: Hurlstone et al. report that in a threshold climate game 9 of 10 groups reached the target when the saved endowment paid the players the next day, 2 of 10 when it paid for trees for future generations, and 7 of 10 when messages meant to activate the legacy motive were added, with no control message and no manipulation check

## The account

Climate cooperation faces two tragedies at once, the commons and the
horizon. When the benefit of cooperation goes to future generations instead
of the players, group cooperation in a collective-risk game collapses, as
Jacquet et al. (2013) found. Three roughly 250-word messages, on death
awareness, on the powerlessness of future generations and on paying forward
a past generation's sacrifice ("We Are One With Future Generations"),
largely restore it.

## Evidence

- Reaching the target: short-delay 9/10, intergenerational 2/10, legacy
  induction 7/10; legacy against intergenerational, β = 2.23, z = 2.13, p =
  .033.
- Group investment: $116.80, $77.20 and $105.20; F(2, 27) = 4.07, p = .029.
- Legacy induction raised fair-share choices, not altruistic ones (strategy
  counts, p. 7).
- Each message raised investment over the same rounds of the
  intergenerational game, F(3, 36) = 2.95, p = .044; the messages were not
  compared with one another.

Ten groups per condition; no control message and no manipulation checks, as
the authors state (p. 9); the per-message test treats sets from the same
groups as independent ([NOTE-1279](../notes.d/NOTE-1279.md), Limitations).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md)'s note of 2026-10-29 cites the reading: "Hurlstone et al.'s
legacy messages ([NOTE-1279](../notes.d/NOTE-1279.md)) bundle identification with generations, a moral
appeal and one's own death, so they cannot tell the thesis's reading from
Pitcher's." The reading is evidence that legacy messages raise cooperation
with the future, and bears on the question without separating its readings.
