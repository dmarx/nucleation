# THEORY-tmpzuov4: Zahavi argues, reconstructing Husserl, that seeing oneself as one of us requires second-person address and taking over the other's perspective on oneself, which keeps the self–other difference while downplaying it, and that a we is not an enlarged I

## The account

Truly social acts need an I–Thou relation of reciprocal, communicative
address. In it I am aware of myself as addressed, and by taking over the
other's apprehension of me I "fit myself into the family of man ... It
is only now that I am, in the proper sense, an Ego over against an other
and can then say 'we'" (p. 10, quoting Husserl). One can group-identify
"only ... if the difference between self and others is present but
somewhat downplayed" (p. 10). "A we, a first-person plural, is not an
enlarged I. The social unification and integration of the involved
subjects never amounts to fusion" (p. 10). Particular identifications
need more: "shared norms, values, emotions, goals etc." (p. 11).

## Evidence

Exegesis and argument (NOTE-1289); the developmental findings against
Schmid are cited, not analysed. The parallel with self-categorization
theory's self-stereotyping is stated as "obvious" (p. 12).

## What it bears on

QUESTION-300 names this paper for how one comes to see oneself as
one of us. Its answer is through being addressed and taking over the
addresser's view of oneself, and on it identification changes how one
apprehends oneself rather than enlarging the self, for the face-to-face
case only.
