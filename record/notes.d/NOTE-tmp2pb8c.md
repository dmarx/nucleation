# NOTE-tmp2pb8c: Bovens and Zouridis — From Street-Level to System-Level Bureaucracies

## Contribution

A typology (street-level, screen-level, system-level), an organizational
mechanism (automating one step forces the formalization of the steps
before it) and two cases. As discretion moves from the case to the
system, the constitutional question moves from legality to political
accountability for design, and rigidity replaces partiality as the
characteristic failure.

## Key insight

Automation does not remove discretion; it relocates it upstream, to the
people who write the decision rules, where the accountability mechanisms
built for caseworkers do not reach.

## Assumptions

- A continental (Dutch) legality tradition with low-threshold
  administrative courts.
- Scope: high-volume "decision-making factories" applying legal
  frameworks, not teachers, nurses or police.
- The transformation is driven by ICT together with legal experts, system
  designers and efficiency-minded managers.

## Key results

- **Abstract** (ms p. 1): "The street level bureaucrats have vanished." …
  "System analysts and software designers are the key actors in these
  executive agencies."
- **Mechanism** (ms p. 9): "automation of a step in a process demands the
  standardization and formalization of the preceding steps".
- **The residual human role** (ms p. 9): officers "in practice they merely
  pressed a button to indicate their acceptance of the computer's
  decisions"; (ms p. 10) "Only if the student lodged a formal objection
  was the application referred for assessment by a living person."
- **Who holds discretion** (ms p. 14): "The hundreds of individual
  case-managers have all vanished. Their pivotal role in the organization
  has been taken by the system and process designers."
- **Designers as policymakers** (ms p. 17): "This is mainly a matter of
  political control and accountability rather than of legality and the
  rule of law."; "Who checks the developers and their systems?"
- **Zenith** (ms p. 16): "the system-level bureaucracy may be regarded as
  the zenith in legal rational authority", though n. 6 notes errors where
  "civil servants relied too heavily on the system".
- **Rigidity** (ms p. 18): "Here, arbitrariness is not a question of a
  lack of impartiality, but of excessive rigidity."
- **Remedies** (ms pp. 18–19): "If the construction and linking of
  information systems is not a mechanical implementation activity, but is
  in fact a form of legislation, this should be subject to public
  accountability."; hardship clauses, so that citizens can "draw attention
  to specific circumstances that do not fit within the existing
  algorithms".
- **How it happened** (ms p. 20): "The process of transformation from
  street-level bureaucracy to screen-level and system-level bureaucracy
  in our cases was an insidious one."

## Concepts

Street-level bureaucracy (Lipsky); screen-level and system-level
bureaucracy; infocracy (Zuurmond); administrative discretion; legality
and transparency as constitutional ideals; conditional versus
goal-oriented legal programming; digital rigidity; hardship clauses;
informatization review.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-410** (governing positions eliminated, distributed or
  transferred). Corroborates its "transferred" case: discretion in
  applying rules leaves the caseworker and passes to designers, and the
  accountability owed changes with the locus ("political control and
  accountability rather than ... legality"). The traffic-fine case also
  eliminates the prosecutor's and judge's case-level discretion.
- **CLAIM-tmp11xvc** (reducing procedural friction removes places where a
  process could be stopped or redirected). Corroborates. In both cases
  the human points at which a case could be weighed or redirected are
  gone; the only one left is one the citizen must open by objection or
  appeal. Hardship clauses and panels are attempts to put such points
  back. The paper does not use the language of friction.
- **CLAIM-671** (a transformation that raises vulnerability to a
  responsible agent's discretion can strengthen that agent's duties).
  Related: vulnerability moves from the caseworker's discretion to the
  designer's, and the authors argue new accountability for designers from
  it. Not filed as a relation.
- **LIT-tmp7wp1i** (Citron) is the US counterpart; the two converge on
  audit or supervision, public access to the system's logic and channels
  for exceptions.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Faithful. A21: computerization "could move discretion from officials
handling individual cases to analysts and software designers". The paper
reports that it did, in two cases.

## Limitations

Two Dutch cases, described qualitatively; generality is conjectured. A
manuscript, not the version of record. The remedies are sketched, not
evaluated. The verdict "zenith" rests on one cited finding.
