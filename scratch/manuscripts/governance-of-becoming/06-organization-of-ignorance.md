# Article 6. The Organization of Ignorance

## Heuristics, reflexive control, self-confirming governance, and protected evidence

**Abstract.** An organization can be kept ignorant without being kept from information. It can be governed through what its procedures receive, through the criteria they weigh it by, through the situations its sound values meet, and, on a Proposed claim, through how its procedures form. It can stop looking for what would count against it because looking only costs it, and it can certify itself clean against a specification that never names the harm. This article sets out what the Nucleation record holds about those arrangements, and with what standing. Its formal and computational results are Active and hold in stipulated models. In one population model, weighting evidence by known admission probabilities undoes lock-in from selective admission and leaves lock-in from self-scoring as it was. In a model institution penalized for every violation it recognizes and unrewarded for discovering requirements, inquiry never raises its score while the hidden requirements do not otherwise come to light, and it learns to make none. In a simulated sentry, a protected investigator corrects the belief while protection outlasts the correction in most runs, and a habit of matched memory reproduces every intervention that model can receive. In a simulated sensor network, an anchor the revision cannot rewrite or refuse restores accuracy, and the same anchor, miscalibrated, fixes the network's error at its own. The owner's principle that self-correction turns on how evidence, evaluation and authority are coupled, and on what the system cannot rewrite or reject, is Proposed and interpretive, and an Active objection stands against it. The article then sets familiar heuristics and biases beside these mechanisms as candidates, not reductions. The record holds the proposals and the tests that would decide them, and no test has been run on human judgement.

### 6.1 Ignorance as an arrangement

The article's opening point is that ignorance can be a property of how an organization's evidence, evaluation and authority are arranged, not only a lack of facts. Four held entries say so in different ways, and none says it of any real institution.

- **Governed without a decision made for it.** "An organization can be governed from outside without any of its decisions being made for it, through the evidence its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh evidence" (CLAIM-404, Active, interpretive; from the owner's U20 and U21, the wording A's). Its edge: "It does not say that any regulator, newsroom or expert body is captured." Two readings hold models of the owner's cases. In Besley and Prat's model of news media, under capture voters, "though fully rational and not deceived, learn nothing" (THEORY-837, Active). Short, Hill and Brown hold that capture "can operate through the evaluative framework a regulator adopts rather than through its decisions" (THEORY-859, Proposed), which is the criteria route of §6.2.
- **Less informed with no one censored.** "An organization can become normatively less informed without censoring anyone by two distinct routes, selective disappearance of the harmed from the population it observes and costly discovery that keeps it from investigating the questions that would reveal unmet obligations; neither needs deliberate suppression" (CLAIM-657, Proposed, interpretive; the owner's). The claim adds that both routes differ from the counter-corrective weakening of challenge (CLAIM-619, Proposed, interpretive; A's), and that they should not be conflated, "particularly when attributing responsibility". It is held back because route A rests on CLAIM-652 (Proposed, demonstrated).
- **A description adequate for performance can merge what members' claims must distinguish.** A coarse-graining preserves a judgment "exactly when the judgment is constant on its fibres" (CLAIM-350, Active, demonstrated, granted). Adequacy "is fixed by the evaluative target, and the target cannot be justified solely by the description that preserves it" (CLAIM-348, Active, interpretive).
- **Legibility is relative to the observer and its task.** It is "whether the observer, with the rules, categories and resources it actually has, can recover the distinction from what it is given and bring it to bear on the task" (TERM-064, Active; the owner's U22).

A's agnotological character (TERM-tmp9mag8, Proposed) proposes a profile of a perspective's "organized epistemic limitations" in four components, legibility, epistemic standing, practical efficacy and revisability. It is "Not a verdict": a limitation is ignorance only relative to "an independently specified epistemic or normative task". The article uses the term only in §6.8.

### 6.2 Routes of reflexive control, counted

The record holds four routes by which one party can govern another's decisions without making them. Three are Active. The fourth is Proposed, and half of it is unsupported. A49's Article 6 lists "Three routes of reflexive control" (the record's audit, curation entry of 2026-11-24). A51's backbone names CLAIM-404 and CLAIM-703 and nothing about formation. Both leave out the fourth route, though A49's own formal centre tests exactly that route.

1. **Evidence.** For a fixed objective, the decision moves with the evidence supplied, "even if the regulator reasons correctly on the evidence it receives" (CLAIM-404).
2. **Criteria.** "Even perfect information about both properties does not ensure the regulator will consider the second" (CLAIM-404, quoting A26). The two are "distinct and each is possible without the other" (CLAIM-404's title). THEORY-859 (Proposed) is a reading of this route in a regulator.
3. **Circumstances.** "The manipulation changes the situation the target's sound values respond to, without deceiving it or changing its criteria" (CLAIM-703, Active, interpretive; A13's, at the owner's U7, after Keller, LIT-2670). Stated bare, the route "is trivial, since anyone who changes the world changes what others decide". What makes it a way of being governed is the condition that one party controls the trigger and another bears the cost, and that condition is CLAIM-713's (Proposed). CLAIM-404's note of 2026-11-15 leaves open whether "circumstances" is a third route or a case of "evidence".
4. **Formation.** "A transmissible expression the target takes up can seed interpretive, evidence-selecting and authority-allocating dispositions that the target then maintains itself, so the influence outlasts its source, reaches circumstances the intervention never presented, and leaves formation provenance outside while maintenance is the target's own" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw, Proposed, interpretive; A7's, at the owner's U6). It rests on CLAIM-404 and on the owner's CLAIM-tmp1ng5k (Proposed, U2).

**A miscount, now corrected.** CLAIM-404's note of 2026-11-24 says "This claim's three routes act on a decision's conditions". CLAIM-404 holds two routes, and CLAIM-703 holds the third. CLAIM-404's note of 2026-11-25 records the correction. CLAIM-tmpp0ufw's summary counts them correctly: "CLAIM-404 holds two routes of outside governance (evidence, criteria) and CLAIM-703 a third (circumstances)". The same summary says all three act on a decision "by a procedure that stays what it was". That fits the evidence and circumstance routes. It sits uneasily with the criteria route, which changes the weights the procedure applies. The body's wording, an influence "on the standards a procedure applies while the influence lasts", is the more careful one. CLAIM-tmpp0ufw's note of 2026-11-25 records this second point (G6.5).

**What supports the fourth route.** ARG-tmp2kh2e (Proposed; the assistant's) separates two halves.

- *The possibility half* is that an influence on formation can leave an organization the target maintains itself. CASE-191 (Active, construction, fictional; built at the owner's request) and CASE-123 (Active, thought experiment, stipulated) carry it. In CASE-191, a capacity installed by an outside pulse reaches the same maintained state (S = 2.618) as one formed by the agent's own frustration. "Upheld is told apart afterwards. Own and pulse are not: after formation they are the same organization, and only a knockout made during formation separates them." That is a result of the construction, not a law about formation. TERM-100 (Active) states it for knockouts: "after formation no knockout tells it from a self-formed one". In CASE-123's one-time-perturbation variant, "The influencing actor need not continuously control the regulator's decisions", and of real regulators it shows nothing.
- *The carrier half* is that the influence can be a transmissible cultural expression. It is not supported: "Nothing built on the record has a cultural expression as the installing influence" (ARG-tmp2kh2e). "So the inference holds for the possibility half and is open for the carrier half." The nearest held statement of a carrier is that a meme can carry "a portable specification of a governance or inquiry protocol" (CLAIM-tmpdq0ah, Proposed, interpretive; A's). It is not built, so it leaves the verdict as it was.

**Marks, not a definition.** The route predicts two marks: persistence after the source stops, and transfer to circumstances never presented, "the transfer by which CLAIM-532 tells a formed capacity from a temporary state" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw). CLAIM-532 (Active, interpretive; A33's, at the owner's U33 of "Assess SDT Coverage") states a sufficient condition for self-formation, "as shown by transfer to discrepancies of the same class after the inducing conditions are gone and by interventions on the processes that produce the capacity while it forms". So persistence and transfer are evidence of formation, not what formation is. Persistence alone is a weak mark: "Persistence and nucleating productivity in the committor sense vary independently" (CLAIM-595, Active, demonstrated). The fourth route is also not a charge of wrongdoing. A7 §8 says: "external formation is not sufficient to establish manipulation, and endogenous maintenance is not sufficient to establish freedom" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw).

**The test that has not been run.** LEAD-tmpuxizu (Open, idea; A7 §5's question, the assistant's design) would extend Weatherall, O'Connor and Bruner's propagandist (THEORY-855, Active): policy makers pass a selection rule to newcomers, and the propagandist is removed after a time. The companion reading gives the model's lever: belief depends only on the cumulative excess of successes, so "whoever chooses what the audience sees chooses that number" (THEORY-845, Active). *Expectation, stated in the lead before any run:* "If the false belief decays once the propagandist stops, the distortion was maintained from outside (CLAIM-404's route only). If it persists because the transmitted rule keeps selecting, the route of CLAIM-tmpp0ufw has a built instance with a cultural carrier". Knocking out the rule's transmission during and after the campaign separates the two. The lead names a question to settle first. If the rule is only copied, and nothing selects for it, it drifts.

### 6.3 Selection against self-scoring (A51's first centre)

A51 opens with CASE-162. The record holds the run and the claim drawn from it. CASE-156 (Active, construction; fleshed out at the owner's U18–U19) is its held precursor, a legibility filter of selectively admitted evidence: "Selection-aware inference is the control that the pathology has to beat".

**No criterion before the run.** Neither CASE-162 nor CLAIM-466 records a criterion set before the run for what would count as different remedies. The reading below was drawn from the results.

**The run.** CASE-162 (Active, executed study, stipulated) is the Perspective Population Lab, v1.3, at dmarx/PerspectiveLab@7e2edc2. It has 32 networked perspectives, admission of cues that depends on the local record, and 64 world seeds per condition. The outcome counted is a wrong incumbent at the end of 1,400 updates. With no self-scoring (c = 0):

| selectivity s | 0 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive credit | 0 | 31 | 41 | 41 |
| exact inverse-propensity weighting | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5 |

At s = 0.75 the raw cue mean is −0.193 and the mean of the admitted cues is +0.184, so selection has reversed the sign of the available evidence. "Self-scoring alone, c = 0.5 with s = 0, leaves 39 of 64 wrong. Nothing is censored there, so the correction changes nothing." With both present, the correction removes the part due to selection (41 to 34 at c = 0.5, s = 0.5). A51's figures agree with these.

**The claim.** "Lock-in from selectively admitted evidence and lock-in from self-scoring need different remedies: weighting admitted evidence by known, positive admission probabilities undoes the first and leaves the second, so correcting for how evidence was selected is not correcting how it is scored" (CLAIM-466, Active, demonstrated). It reads CASE-162 against CLAIM-404: the routes are distinct, "this says their remedies are too".

**What the record holds less strongly than A51 says.**

- *"Establishes."* A51: "CASE-162 establishes the distinction". The result holds for a stipulated population, and its figures are read from stored results. The status review of 2026-10-30 re-read them against the entry's figures, and they agree. No session has rerun PerspectiveLab, and the reruns of 2026-11-25 could not, since the code is not in this checkout.
- *"Different remedies."* The record holds half of this. Weighting by propensity repairs selection and is idle on self-scoring. In CASE-162 the remedy for self-scoring is not tested: "Independent scoring is the natural one ... the runs do not test it" (CLAIM-466, "What it does not say"). Elsewhere the record holds a test in another model. CLAIM-342 (Active, interpretive) states the principle: "What keeps a judgement–observation loop from confirming itself is evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests". In a simulated single-mind loop, CASE-353 (Proposed, executed study) finds that a score or weight the loop's own authority does not set prevents lock-in, while evidence that arrives unselected "keeps it sighted but does not anchor it" (CLAIM-931, Proposed, empirical). One of those scores, commitment before the evidence, is qualified by a held result: "Scoring operators on predictions committed before the evidence is not by itself Regime B", since in the linearized operator model "commitment removes capture only when the interpretation bends the reading of the evidence no more than it bends the predictions" (CLAIM-340, Active, demonstrated). Which "independent scoring" CLAIM-466 means is open between TERM-147's two senses (Proposed).
- *Governance against routing.* Inside CASE-162 itself, "CASE-162's run with the router's audit delay matched still had the router ahead" of the learned allocation (CLAIM-467, Active, interpretive). So "whatever governance adds over routing has to be shown where evaluation fails" (CLAIM-467's title).
- *The correction.* "The correction is an oracle" (CASE-162), "with known propensities" (CLAIM-466). A procedure for unknown or zero propensities is what the case cannot show.

**A toy of confirmation feedback.** A11 offered a sampling model of confirmation bias with a threshold at κ > 2/3. The assistant's derivation shows that in that model "the unbiased state is unstable for every κ > 0; the threshold κ > 2/3 shown with it comes from the illustration's decay factor 0.92, and a decay λ puts the threshold at κ = λ/a" (CLAIM-tmpu5cap, Active, demonstrated; made Active at the assistant's discretion). So in the toy, an accumulator that does not correct for its own selection has no threshold: a small lean grows at any coupling. A nucleation reading of confirmation bias, with a threshold to cross, therefore needs a restoring term. What the claim does not say is "Anything about people." Two held cautions bear on the name. "Retrieval that depends on current belief is not a bias in itself … calling a search biased needs a standard outside the search" (CLAIM-292, Active, demonstrated, granted), so reading the toy as confirmation *bias* needs such a standard. And a toy's conclusion can read a parameter as the concept it stands for (CLAIM-tmpyxemn, Proposed, interpretive).

### 6.4 Inquiry suppressed by its own score, and self-certification (A51 §3)

**The incentive.** "For a fixed arrangement, expanding the recognized demand set cannot improve conjunctive compliance and cannot decrease the count of recognized violations; where governance penalizes newly recognized violations without rewarding their discovery, an organization may acquire an incentive not to improve its identification of its obligations" (CLAIM-662, Active, demonstrated). It is A44's, as narrowed by the owner's review of #485 on 2026-11-12. Its dynamic form, for one model institution: inquiry that only reveals requirements "never raises its score when the hidden requirements do not otherwise come to light: on every path it adds only penalized violations and spent budget, so an institution acting on that score makes no inquiry" (CLAIM-1064, Active, demonstrated; checked path by path in the study's tests). Two conditions carry the result: discovery is unrewarded, and the hidden requirements do not otherwise come to light. A51's gloss, "the conditions under which inquiry cannot improve the institution's operative score", keeps them.

**The criterion, before the run.** LEAD-181 (Done; the assistant's) set what would count: "Less inquiry under the penalty supports CLAIM-662; none defeats it."

**The run.** CASE-396 (Active, executed study; the assistant's, for LEAD-181) has 30 requirements, 10 visible and 20 found only by inquiry, and a budget split among inquiry, remediation and output. It runs five regimes in four settings, with 16 learners per regime and setting.

- "Under the penalty the institution makes no inquiry. The optimum is a = 0 in every setting, uniquely". The learners end between 0.01 and 0.36 units of 10.
- The two scorecards part. The internal count ends at 0.00 (S1, S2) or 0.10 (S3, S4) recognized violations, "the independent count, at 6.08 and 3.36".
- Scored by the independent count, the institution inquires at 5 to 6 units and ends with no violations.
- The negative result A51 rightly keeps: "removing the penalty on newly recognized violations does not by itself bring inquiry back, because inquiry still earns the institution nothing". Rewarding discovery restores some inquiry (1 to 4 units).

The learners are told only their score, so the avoidance comes "without any rule against inquiry and without suppressing anyone". That is "CLAIM-657's second route produced by learning" (CASE-396), and it models no exit (CLAIM-657's note of 2026-11-23). The learners learn whether and how much to inquire, not which questions to ask. Which comparison answers the lead's test is filed two ways, as alternatives for the owner. Against the independent count, the penalty suppresses inquiry (CLAIM-1120, Proposed, empirical). Against internal scoring without it, "the penalty lowers inquiry only where inquiry has some other value to the institution" (CLAIM-997, Proposed, empirical). LEAD-181 was closed as Done on 2026-11-25, and its history says the choice "stays the owner's; the closing does not choose".

**Self-certification.** CASE-397 (Active, executed study; an arm of CASE-412's model, the assistant's, for the manuscript governance-of-possibility and item 3 of LEAD-226) brings in mid-run a harm the specification does not name. Its finding: "Never entering the demand gives zero violations against D0 and against the organization's own specification, in every regime, while the harm runs for all 120 periods"; "Only the true demand set ranks the two correctly. That follows from how the scorecards are built." It is an instance of CLAIM-727's limit (Active, interpretive; A's), that an actor's "own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has".

**What the record holds less strongly than A51 says.** A51 calls CASE-397 "a constructive demonstration of the mechanism we called normative agnotology". The case says of itself: "The policy is an input. Nothing in the model makes the choice between excising and revising." So it shows self-certification *given* a policy of excision. It does not show ignorance produced by the organization's own dynamics. The case names where that is learned: CASE-396 "is the record's case in which that choice is learned". One arm comes closer. "Under the peak rule, excision happens by default": a review that needs capacity is never funded, so "The organization has not chosen to excise; its budget rule leaves it unable to revise." Even there the policy given is revision, and the budget rule blocks it. The term A51 uses is A48's, held in CLAIM-tmpa61rf (Proposed, interpretive), which is not the same idea as A6's transmitted character (§6.8). No run joins the two cases, in which an institution learns to excise a demand and then certifies itself clean against the specification it controls (G6.1).

### 6.5 Belief correction against practical correction (A51's second centre)

**The owner's thesis.** "Some persistent anxiety may be governance-maintained rather than belief-maintained". Anticipated threat grants defensive processes authority over action, attention and evidence acquisition, and "the regime can outlast a corrected belief" (CLAIM-446, Proposed, interpretive). It is the owner's, from 2026-10-23: "I think you may have inadvertently landed on a formalization of anxiety". Its general form, also the owner's: "A protective regime, in a person, an institution or a system of states, becomes self-maintaining when the interventions it authorizes shift the evidence used to judge its necessity", and "it is a failure only where the regime has become less responsive to evidence that it is no longer needed" (CLAIM-445, Proposed, interpretive).

**What human data the record has read.** The thesis has readings beneath it, and none of them tests its distinctive part. In Goodson and colleagues' meta-analysis, reducing safety behaviours lowered symptoms (d = 0.43) and increasing them raised symptoms (d = 0.65), mostly in young non-clinical samples (THEORY-903, Active). Patients with OCD or PTSD avoided a safety cue more than controls and, after effective avoidance, still expected the aversive picture more (THEORY-912, Active). A review of 116 studies favours expectancy and inference-based accounts of avoidance (THEORY-900, Active). A rival reading of "the belief corrected, the practice persists" is that extinction "does not erase the original threat association but adds a competing inhibitory one" (THEORY-914, Proposed). The human tests are open questions: QUESTION-325, QUESTION-326, QUESTION-327 and QUESTION-331 (all Open).

**The criterion, before the run.** CASE-153 (Active, proposed study), the design CASE-346 carries out, set its own check before the run: "if a Bayesian learner with matched information costs, or a habit model with matched memory, reproduces every intervention response, the extra authority state has not earned its place. That outcome is to be reported, not tuned away."

**The run.** CASE-346 (Active, executed study; the assistant's, for LEAD-076) is a sentry in a hidden world that is dangerous, then safe for 400 steps, then dangerous again, with 1,000 paired seeds per condition.

- With authority rewarded by the apparent success of protection, and the sentry restricting inspection, "no run leaves protection in 400 safe steps and 43 % of runs never even correct the belief".
- "A protected investigator corrects the belief within a median 6 steps, yet protection outlasts it in 87 % of runs". The belief is corrected in 100 % of runs. A51's "corrects the belief in every simulated run" and its 87 % agree with these.
- Restriction adds needless protection under success feedback beyond what it adds under independent evaluation, an interaction of 99 [95, 103] units.
- Every regime still protects when danger returns.

**The check's outcome.** A51 states the limit rightly: "we must not claim that every such pattern uniquely identifies will-organization." The case's check "comes out mostly against" the authority state. The Bayesian with matched cost does not reproduce the responses. The habit does: under success feedback the authority update is the habit trace, and "a habit of matched memory reproduces every intervention it can receive" (CASE-346's title). The interventions are belief correction, authority reset and restored evidence access. "Only interventions on the evaluation tell the two apart": an independent audit, and reinforcement made independent, under which 100 % of runs leave protection (median 44 steps). CLAIM-924 (Proposed, empirical; the assistant's) states this. Whether it meets CLAIM-446's defeater "depends on whether an outcome-gated habit can receive reinforcement correction" (CLAIM-446, note of 2026-11-22). That reading is set out for the owner. What the case cannot show is "That a governance account beats a habit account anywhere outside this model."

**What it separates.** Within the model, appraisal and practical correction come apart: the belief is corrected and protection persists. A51's non-equivalence, "Accurate appraisal does not establish corrective governance", is this case's in one stipulated model. A49 and A51 both point to persistence after the inducing context is gone as the formal mark. CASE-191, CASE-123 and CASE-346 each carry it, for a scalar capacity, a stipulated institution and a sentry. Human judgement is §6.7's question.

### 6.7 Heuristics and biases: candidate manifestations

A51 asks the article to "situate familiar cognitive biases as candidate manifestations of these distinguishable mechanisms, not claim that they have all been experimentally reduced to them". The record holds the candidates, A's from U10 to U12, with the assistant's notes and tests. None has been tested on human judgement. For a single mind the record's own question is what, there, plays the part of independent scoring (QUESTION-121, Open). For the operator model the formal half is held: commitment before the evidence is not by itself independent scoring, and removes capture only under CLAIM-340's condition (CLAIM-340, Active, demonstrated; §6.3).

**The programme and its condition.** "Several conventionally distinct cognitive biases can be observable marks of one organizational constraint ... so the catalogue of biases is not a catalogue of mechanisms; the thesis earns standing only if one intervention on such a constraint, measured independently and with the information held fixed, moves several bias measures together" (CLAIM-tmp5atd3, Proposed, interpretive; A11–A12, at the owner's U10–U11). Its rivals are named: Hilbert's noisy-processing unification (LIT-tmpngp4x, Deferred) and resource-rational analysis (THEORY-359). *Expectation, from the claim's defeater, before any run (LEAD-tmpr4xwc, Open, idea):* an intervention on the shared constraint, measured apart from the biases with the information held fixed, moves several bias measures together, and more than the rivals predict from manipulations of "noise, memory or processing cost alone". *Falsifier:* the measures move one at a time, or no more than the rivals predict, or the constraint "can be identified only from the co-variation it is meant to explain". The held form of that worry is CLAIM-402 (Active, interpretive): "a category that speakers or institutions act on can produce the regularities that confirm it, and contexts that share its use are not independent". The lead's design checks the reliability of the bias measures before it reads co-variation.

**Biases as mismatched contrasts.** Relative to "a separately justified standard", many biases are "spurious sensitivity" or "spurious invariance" (CLAIM-tmpei0cf, Proposed, interpretive; A11 §§4–5). The assistant's note shows that both classes are "the one difference R⁻_P − R⁻_*". It also shows a limit: a bias that departs from the standard by a constant offset "has Δ_P(g,x) = Δ_*(g,x) for every g in the family, so no response contrast shows it". "Overconfidence measured as an excess of confidence over accuracy that is the same across the item sets compared is such a bias." Separately, a perspective's character "detects the transformations it is blind to, not the states it merges" (CLAIM-tmpqbaic, Active, demonstrated; the assistant's, made Active at its discretion). So a profile of transformation contrasts cannot by itself reach biases of level or ignorance at the level of merged states. Each limit is stated in its own entry, and the article sets them side by side (J6.5). No lead tests the classification (G6.2).

**Anchoring.** The record's empirical entry: "the shift is established, the accessibility step is inferred and has not been measured" (CLAIM-315, Proposed, empirical). Harris et al.'s five preregistered experiments found nothing, "with tasks that also missed direct picture priming", so the direct evidence for selective accessibility "is removed, not reversed" (THEORY-335, Active). A11 sets a third mechanism beside insufficient adjustment and selective accessibility, nucleation: the anchor helps a response organization form that then maintains itself (CLAIM-tmpggu4v, Proposed, interpretive). The mechanisms part "on what follows the anchor's removal". The claim does not say that ordinary anchoring in a one-shot numerical estimate is nucleation. *Expectation (LEAD-tmp3nqe0, Open, idea; the assistant's plan), before any run:* in a repeated-judgement task with the anchor withdrawn, nucleation predicts persistence, hysteresis or transfer beyond what accessibility, measured in each session, accounts for. *Falsifier, the claim's defeater:* decay with further adjustment in every condition, no path dependence and no transfer, or persistence fully carried by measured accessibility. Persistence alone would not mark nucleation (CLAIM-595, §6.2). The rival's own step is unmeasured (QUESTION-323, Open), so the test runs against an account whose mechanism is itself open.

**Governance heuristics.** "Beside heuristics that select what to do there are governance heuristics, learned priors over which perspective or procedure determines what to do; intuitive expertise and entrenched bias can then be one learned allocation of authority" (CLAIM-tmpn754o, Proposed, interpretive; A12–A13, at the owner's U11–U12). It rests on CLAIM-443 (Active), whose guard holds that persistence alone does not demonstrate amortization. The assistant's note gives the prediction that separates a governance heuristic from an action heuristic: after "clear feedback in one" task, "the shift toward deliberation generalizes across the tasks that share the cue, including those where intuition is still right". No lead holds it (G6.3). CLAIM-tmpysg95 (Proposed; A16) reads such a heuristic as "a compiled contract". CASE-346's identification limit does not carry to that test. In CASE-346 an allocation learned on success follows a habit's law only because the protecting posture masks the outcome that would judge it: "Under protection the outcome is the same in both states" (CASE-346). The note's test gives clear feedback, so the limit bears only on tasks whose governing procedure masks its own outcome. CASE-346 has one task and no channel for deliberation, so it says nothing of cue-generalization (J6.4).
