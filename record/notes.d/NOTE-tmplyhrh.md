# NOTE-tmplyhrh: Tomasello — Why Don't Apes Point?

## Contribution

The core of the book's chapters on pointing, before the book: why apes do
not point, explained by one lack.

## Key results

1. **The fact.** "And yet there is not a single reliable observation,
   by any scientist anywhere, of one ape pointing for another." (ms p. 2).
2. **Intentions.** Apes "do not understand the embedded structure of
   informing or communicative intentions" (ms p. 6).
3. **Motives.** Human pointing "may be decomposed into two basic
   underlying motives: helping and sharing." (ms p. 10).
4. **Gestures.** "chimpanzees use and understand their gestures as
   one-way procedures for getting things done, not as intersubjectively
   shared, bidirectional coordination devices or symbols." (ms p. 13).
5. **Conclusion.** Human communication rests "fundamentally on a
   foundation of uniquely human forms of collaborative engagement
   involving shared intentionality." (ms p. 17).

## Assumptions

Captive and human-raised apes are the relevant contrast; the philosophers'
accounts of shared intentionality apply to infants; ritualization and
imitation can be told apart.

## Concepts

Object-choice task; imperative, declarative and informative pointing;
joint attentional frame; ritualization; role-reversal imitation; shared
intentionality.
