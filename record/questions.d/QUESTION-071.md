# QUESTION-071: Can a will-organization be manipulated without anything in its present structure showing it, and if so, what in its history would have to be recorded to tell a manipulated will from one that is merely effective?

## The question

The will line describes an agent's practical efficacy by how its regimes
are organized now. [CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md) already says that such an explanation does not
settle whose the will is. Manipulation is the sharpest case. In Pereboom's
manipulation cases ([LIT-1006](../literature.d/LIT-1006.md)), an agent's present structure is produced by
someone else and matches an unmanipulated agent's in every respect the
account names. That fits the manipulation cases, not all four: the case at
the compatibilist end is ordinary determination, where nobody else
produces the structure. The book was read only in part ([NOTE-823](../notes.d/NOTE-823.md),
Skimmed; [THEORY-316](../theory.d/THEORY-316.md)), and the reading stopped before Chapter 4, which
holds the four cases, so this description is not confirmed first-hand.

*Note of 2026-10-14, later.* Two surveys read in full as alternatives
([LIT-1047](../literature.d/LIT-1047.md), [NOTE-862](../notes.d/NOTE-862.md); [LIT-1045](../literature.d/LIT-1045.md), [NOTE-856](../notes.d/NOTE-856.md)) confirm the
description in outline, at survey strength ([THEORY-328](../theory.d/THEORY-328.md)). Case 1
(mid-life intervention) and Case 2 (programming from the start of life)
are quoted in Pereboom's 2024 and 2014 wordings, and each stipulates that
Plum meets reasons-responsive and hierarchical conditions. Case 3 is an
egoistic upbringing. Case 4 is ordinary determination. The 2001 text is
still unread. For part 2 they report a difficulty. An original-design
case (Mele's Ernie) leaves no bypassed capacity, so a history that
records how capacities were formed, or whether they were bypassed, does
not separate it from an ordinary agent. A natural-force case leaves no
manipulator to record.

The question has two parts:

1. **Is manipulation invisible to present structure?** [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md) says yes
   for every structural condition in the record's readings. Does the will
   line's notion of organization, with its governance among regimes and its
   capacity to transform, escape that, or is it one more structural
   condition?
2. **If it is invisible, what history would show it?** [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md) says a
   regulation acquired under controlling conditions is introjected, not
   integrated, so how a value was acquired fixes how autonomous acting on
   it is. Pettigrew ([LIT-202](../literature.d/LIT-202.md)) makes the criterion "the space and resources
   those mechanisms leave for the free exercise of agency". [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md)
   makes the mechanism the agent's own only once she has taken
   responsibility for it. Each names a historical fact. The question is
   which of these facts could be recorded, and by whom. Susser, Roessler
   and Nissenbaum ([LIT-1002](../literature.d/LIT-1002.md)) give a candidate of another kind: whether
   an influence manipulated depends on whether the agent was, or could
   easily have become, aware of it, a fact about her access to the causal
   history of her state ([NOTE-745](../notes.d/NOTE-745.md)). They argue it for single
   decisions, not for global manipulation.

## Why the record needs it

- **[CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md)** becomes testable. If no present-structure fact separates
  the cases, explanation of efficacy and ownership come apart exactly as it
  says.
- **[CLAIM-016](../claims.d/CLAIM-016.md)** (Deferred) is defeated by "a manipulation case that
  satisfies every condition of integration and answerability". This
  question asks for that case's structure on a line that is active.
- The analyses of manipulation now filed, Noggle ([LIT-1007](../literature.d/LIT-1007.md)),
  Susser, Roessler and Nissenbaum ([LIT-1002](../literature.d/LIT-1002.md)) and Coons and Weber
  ([LIT-1003](../literature.d/LIT-1003.md), unread), say what makes an influence manipulation, as
  against persuasion or coercion. The four-case argument assumes an
  account of the manipulation it uses, and the record has not stated one.
  The two read so far do not supply it: both treat ordinary manipulation
  of single decisions, not the installing of a whole character, and
  Noggle says the relation between the two has rarely been examined
  (§1.1; [NOTE-744](../notes.d/NOTE-744.md), [NOTE-745](../notes.d/NOTE-745.md)).

## What the record holds

- **[THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md):** present-structure conditions are met by the manipulated
  agent, and no historical condition in the readings is shown to escape.
- **[THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md):** controlling conditions of acquisition produce
  introjection. This is empirical, from self-determination theory.
- **[THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md):** ownership of the mechanism through taking
  responsibility, a historical condition the four-case argument targets.
- **[THEORY-235](../theory.d/THEORY-235.md):** covert, vulnerability-exploiting influence harms
  autonomy by making the agent opaque to herself. Its criterion is
  historical and epistemic: whether she was, and could easily have become,
  aware of what shaped her decision. Its limit: argued for single
  decisions, not for the installed character of the four-case argument.
- **[THEORY-225](../theory.d/THEORY-225.md):** no single-mechanism analysis of ordinary
  manipulation fits the clear cases. Its limit: it is about ordinary
  manipulation, so it does not supply the account of global manipulation
  the four-case argument assumes.

## What would answer it

Either a feature of will-organization that differs between the
manipulated agent and its unmanipulated twin. Or an argument that none
can, together with a statement of what a record of the will's formation
would have to contain.

## Note of 2026-10-21: reflexive control, and part 1 restated twice

**At the scale of an institution.** A20 of the exchange of 2026-10-20, on
reflexive control: "An intervention from outside the organization can
alter its internal allocation of practical authority, after which the
organization reproduces the altered allocation through processes that
appear to be its own", and "even identical present-day procedures could
have been constituted through different processes." That is this
question at the scale of an institution. Under [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md), if the
target's loop is unstable, a single perturbation m₀ + ε suffices and
needs no continued control, so the present structure shows nothing.
[QUESTION-070](QUESTION-070.md) holds the state-channel version. The institutional route is
filed as [CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md), which bears on this question without answering
it.

**Part 1 restated twice.** A16 §11 and A27 §5 restated part 1, for
[QUESTION-011](QUESTION-011.md), as "a present-organization indistinguishability argument":
"A present-state or future-response criterion cannot distinguish
historical modes of constitution that leave its inputs unchanged."
Neither cited this question. As stated, the argument stipulates that the
twins' present organization is identical, so it is true by definition. It
is not this question's "argument that none can", which would have to show
that will-organization cannot differ between a manipulated agent and its
twin. A27's five things an ownership theory might need are, in the
record's terms:

- current control: [CLAIM-174](../claims.d/CLAIM-174.md) and [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md);
- provenance: [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md) and [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md);
- "the agent's understanding of how its commitments were constituted":
  [THEORY-235](../theory.d/THEORY-235.md);
- "capacity for informed revision or contestation": [CLAIM-364](../claims.d/CLAIM-364.md);
- the normative conditions: [CLAIM-016](../claims.d/CLAIM-016.md).

Its feminist-epistemology point, that endorsement given "without having
had meaningful opportunities to understand or challenge" its assumptions
does not show ownership, is adaptive preference ([THEORY-025](../theory.d/THEORY-025.md)) and
[QUESTION-057](QUESTION-057.md)'s point that avowal is one more attitude. Not filed as a
claim.

## Note of 2026-11-02: the stipulated indistinguishability a third time, and what a path can show

In the exchange "Assess SDT Coverage" (2026-11-02), A22 §4 built a double well with a slow reinforcing variable, in
which a self-initiated, an outside and a noise-driven displacement leave the
same end state and dispositions. As A16 and A27 did before it (the note of
2026-10-21), it is true by construction: the inputs enter only through their
sum. This is the third time. Two points are new.

- **The transition path, not the end state** (A21 §3). In a gradient system
  with small isotropic noise, the most likely escape follows the
  time-reversal of the relaxation path from the saddle (Freidlin–Wentzell;
  [LIT-1881](../literature.d/LIT-1881.md); Heymann and Vanden-Eijnden, [LIT-1981](../literature.d/LIT-1981.md), registered). A directed
  transition need not, unless the controller imitates the noise path. So
  history can record more than the end state. This holds only in the
  frozen-slow-variable limit, since the toy is not a gradient system and its
  noise is degenerate.
- **From the owner's U19**, on malicious reflexive control:
  - Self-formation can be causally the agent's without being autonomous: an
    outside input at a sensitive moment, amplified and then written into
    slow dispositions by the agent's own processes (A19 §5).
  - Comparing the agent's channel with an outsider's for the same transition
    by their Gramians gives one measurable component (A20 §4;
    [CLAIM-542](../claims.d/CLAIM-542.md)).
  - Capture needs no instability. Near threshold, [CASE-099](../cases.d/CASE-099.md)'s mixed mode is
    also its slow one, so a one-time perturbation lasts about −1/ln λ₊ steps.
    That is persistence, not self-maintenance ([CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)).

The question stays open.

## Note of 2026-11-03: a construction for part 1, and why it matters

[CASE-191](../cases.d/CASE-191.md) builds part 1 for self-formation. An agent whose capacity was
installed by a single outside pulse and then maintained by its own cycle is,
after formation, the same organization as one that formed the capacity itself,
under every knockout of production or maintenance. Only a knockout during
formation separates them. So for part 2, what history must record is whether
the producing process that formed the capacity was the agent's own while it
ran. That is the historical condition [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md) and [THEORY-1118](../theory.d/THEORY-1118.md) say
structural accounts need, the vulnerability [CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md) and [CASE-123](../cases.d/CASE-123.md) describe
for reflexive control, and the place where self-determination theory's
history of internalization ([THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)) would have to leave a trace in the
capacity's relations to the rest of the organization, since it leaves none in
the capacity.

The question stays open.

## Note of 2026-11-03 (later): the readings

- **The path test, confirmed and bounded.** Heymann and Vanden-Eijnden
  ([NOTE-1504](../notes.d/NOTE-1504.md)) confirm the note of 2026-11-02 for gradient drift with
  identity noise, and the degenerate-noise caveat. Gradient is sufficient, not
  necessary: in the Maier–Stein model the noise path stays the reversed
  relaxation path up to β = 4, though the drift is gradient only at β = 1. And
  the path evidence is a small-noise statement; a controller can compute and
  follow the noise path.
- **The same problem for one act.** On Sánchez-Cañizares's irruption model
  ([NOTE-1506](../notes.d/NOTE-1506.md), p. 15), a change of mind-set parameter cannot be told from one
  set by a stimulus, by internal dynamics or by another party.
