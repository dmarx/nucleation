# LIT-668: Bridging Mode Connectivity in Loss Landscapes and Adversarial Robustness

Pu Zhao, Pin-Yu Chen, Payel Das, Karthikeyan Natesan Ramamurthy and Xue Lin
(2020), International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2020) —
[ARXIV-2005.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00060)

## Key takeaways

- **Path connection repairs tampered models.** Given two models backdoored
  with the same poisoned data, a quadratic Bezier path trained on a small
  bonafide set has models near its ends (t = 0.1 or 0.9) with near-original
  clean accuracy and backdoor success near 0% (Fig. 2). On CIFAR-10 VGG with
  2,500 clean samples the model at t = 0.1 has 88% clean accuracy and 1.1%
  backdoor success. With 50 samples it has 63% and 2.5%, against 46% clean
  accuracy for fine-tuning (Table 2).
- **The same holds for injected weight errors.** On the path between two
  fault-sneaking-attacked models, injection success is 0% at every data size
  tested while clean accuracy stays at 88–96% (Table 3).
- **Robustness has a barrier where loss does not.** On paths trained with
  the standard loss between two regular models, a regular and an
  adversarially trained model, or two adversarially trained models, the
  standard loss is flat but the PGD robustness loss rises mid-path. Its
  shape tracks λ_max of the input Hessian, with Pearson correlations 0.71,
  0.88 and 0.86 (Fig. 4). The authors read the regular–robust barrier as
  geometric evidence for the "no free lunch" view of adversarial
  robustness.
- **An adaptive attacker** who trains a poisoned path and releases its
  endpoints does not break the defence: models at t ∈ [0.25, 0.75] are still
  repaired (Appendix J).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 with the owner's mode-connectivity batch ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). It
is the batch's earliest work after the foundations, and its only one on
security. The record has no word for adversarial robustness or model
tampering, so `loss-landscapes` is the only subject tag. That gap is
reported with the batch rather than filled by stretching
`society-and-governance`, whose "security" is institutional.

It extends Garipov et al. ([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)). It trains Garipov's quadratic
Bezier curve with Garipov's uniform-t objective (its Eqs. 2 and 4), and its
contribution is what lies along such a curve when the endpoints are
tampered. It names Draxler et al. ([LIT-653](LIT-653.md)) among the works showing
flat paths between minima. Later papers in the batch cite it as the
robustness application of mode connectivity: Lubana et al. ([LIT-669](LIT-669.md)),
Sonthalia et al. ([LIT-659](LIT-659.md)), Bo Zhao et al. ([LIT-655](LIT-655.md)) and Tran et
al. ([LIT-670](LIT-670.md)). Its repair result, that leaving a basin along a trained
curve sheds an unwanted behaviour, anticipates Lubana et al.'s argument
that staying linearly connected to a model keeps its mechanism.

It carries an instruction for practice (repair a suspect pretrained model
by path connection with a little clean data), so it is flagged for the
anthology.
