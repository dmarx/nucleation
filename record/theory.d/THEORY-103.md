# THEORY-103: Perceptual consciousness overflows cognitive access: in partial-report tasks more items are experienced than the roughly four that working memory holds, though any one of them can be accessed when cued

## Source

- Block (2007), *Consciousness, accessibility, and the mesh between
  psychology and neuroscience*, [LIT-646](../literature.d/LIT-646.md), read in [NOTE-497](../notes.d/NOTE-497.md):
  §§8–12 (the method, Sperling, Landman et al., Sligte et al., the
  refrigerator-light reply and working-memory capacity). Target article
  only.
- Block (2011), *Perceptual consciousness overflows cognitive access*,
  [LIT-640](../literature.d/LIT-640.md), read in [NOTE-500](../notes.d/NOTE-500.md): the restatement, the generic and
  fragment illusions, fragile VSTM, Soto et al., Box 2 and the two
  fallacies.

## What was actually shown

**The data both sides accept.** Block (2007), [LIT-646](../literature.d/LIT-646.md), §9, reports
that in Sperling's arrays of 9–12 letters subjects "said that they could
see all or almost all of the characters" and could report only about 4,
yet when a tone cued a row after the array went off they could report "all
or almost all the characters in any given row". In Landman et al.'s task
with 8 rectangles, capacity (Cowan's K) is 4 when the cue comes with the
test array, almost 8 when it comes with the first array, and "between 6
and 7 for up to 1.5 seconds after the first stimulus has been turned off"
when it comes in the blank. Sligte et al. extend this to 32 items and 4 s
(§10). Working memory, across many paradigms, holds about 4 items, with
"neither visual working memory system [having] a capacity higher than four"
(§12). So subjects hold specific information about more items than they
can access at once. That much is not in dispute.

**The surplus is not retinal.** Block (2011), [LIT-640](../literature.d/LIT-640.md), splits the old
"iconic memory" into a rod-driven retinal persistence of a few hundred
milliseconds and a cortical *fragile visual short-term memory*. With cues
up to 4 s after offset, its capacity is "7 of 8 items at 1 second, 6 of 8
at 2.5 seconds and 5 of 8 at 4 seconds – while working memory remains
steady at 4 items". A light flash before the cue affects only the earliest
cue, and Sligte et al. image the persisting representation in V4, not
V1–V3. Withdrawing attention, by three methods, and a TMS pulse to right
dorsolateral prefrontal cortex reduce working memory and leave fragile
VSTM almost intact. So the high-capacity store is a distinct system,
neither the retina nor working memory.

**The step that makes it overflow.** The claim is that the surplus is
*experienced*. Block's case is cumulative ([LIT-646](../literature.d/LIT-646.md), §11;
[LIT-640](../literature.d/LIT-640.md)). Subjects' introspective judgement that they see every item
does not depend on being asked about any item. It predicted partial-report
superiority, which was borne out. Subjects in Landman et al. recover size
and orientation together, as from an image. The rival reading, that only a
generic letter-likeness or fragments are conscious and the specific
information sits in an unconscious store until the cue amplifies it,
needs "highly detailed unconscious iconic memory", for which Block finds
"no independent evidence". The one direct test of unconscious working
memory he cites (Soto et al.) gives comparisons "slightly above 55%" at
5 s for a single grating rated unseen, far below what the Amsterdam tasks
require. Hence "it is reasonable to tentatively accept the overflow
interpretation" ([LIT-640](../literature.d/LIT-640.md)).

**Accessible, not accessed.** Block (2011) is explicit that overflow "does
not claim that any of the items in the array are cognitively
inaccessible, but rather that necessarily most are unaccessed", as not
everyone can win a lottery that each can win. The title keeps that
reading. Block (2007), §9, already notes that this argues for less than
his 1995 paper ([LIT-643](../literature.d/LIT-643.md)), which took Sperling to show inaccessible
phenomenal states directly.

What could have come out otherwise: cued capacity could have fallen to
working-memory level within the first few hundred milliseconds, as a
retinal afterimage would, or could have been reduced by the same
attentional and prefrontal manipulations that reduce working memory. It
did neither.

## The rival accounts

The sparse or illusion view, that subjects mistake potential access for
actual experience (Dehaene's "refrigerator light"), or a generic image
for a specific one (Kouider et al.; Cohen and Dennett), is the account
this one excludes. The record holds no theory stating it. It holds
Dennett's position only as works: Dennett and Kinsbourne's "Time and the
Observer" ([LIT-431](../literature.d/LIT-431.md)) and "Quining Qualia" ([LIT-433](../literature.d/LIT-433.md)). Block's 2007 paper
rivals both at the level of the work. [LIT-431](../literature.d/LIT-431.md) argues that whether a content
was experienced and then revised, or revised before it was experienced,
has no answer, because every datum fits both. Block argues that the
parallel question for Sperling, whether the uncued letters were seen
before the cue, has an answer that inference to the best explanation gives
([LIT-646](../literature.d/LIT-646.md), §§7–8). [LIT-433](../literature.d/LIT-433.md) concludes from the subject's inability to
tell two hypotheses apart that the notion they share is empty, the move
Block rejects by name. Neither is a theory, and Cohen and Dennett's
"Consciousness cannot be separated from function" (2011), Block's main
target in 2011, is not held, so no `rivals` is declared against Dennett.

The workspace side of the sparse view is now held as a theory:
[THEORY-096](THEORY-096.md), from Mashour et al. ([LIT-631](../literature.d/LIT-631.md)), which takes global
availability to be what is experienced and reads experience without access
as untested. That is this account's rival, and the relation is declared on
that file. The two agree on the capacity data and disagree on whether the
unaccessed surplus is experienced, which is the premise each adopts.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say the surplus is inaccessible.** Any one item can be
  accessed when cued. It says that, necessarily, most are not.
- **It does not rest on reports alone, but it does rest on them.** The
  capacity data are not in dispute. That the surplus is experienced rests
  on reading subjects' reports at face value, which is exactly what the
  illusion view denies. Block's case against the illusion is cumulative,
  and he calls the issue "certainly still open" ([LIT-640](../literature.d/LIT-640.md)).
- **It does not say fragile VSTM is shown to be conscious.** Block concedes
  that the choice of the cued object "could" be unconscious in principle,
  and answers that "we will not get anywhere in consciousness research by
  accepting the less plausible account". That is a plausibility argument
  (C5 in [NOTE-500](../notes.d/NOTE-500.md), weak to moderate).
- **It does not say where experience is in the brain.** The step from
  overflow to a claim about the neural basis is the mesh argument, filed
  separately as [THEORY-098](THEORY-098.md).
- **The evidence is second-hand.** Every experiment is as Block reports it,
  in an opinion article and a target article. The 32 BBS commentaries,
  where the objections were made, were not read.
