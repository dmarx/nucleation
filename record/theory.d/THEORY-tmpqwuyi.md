# THEORY-tmpqwuyi: Dirty hands are coherent, on Eggert's account: when two actual duties conflict, an act that is all things considered permissible, even required, can remain wrong, because the overridden duty survives as a moral remainder that makes remorse apt and can ground duties of apology or compensation

Eggert (2023), LIT-tmp0l030, read in NOTE-tmp50e3o.

## The account

"a wrong action, such as the transgression of an actual, intact right, may
be permissible under certain circumstances, without this rendering it any
less wrong" (p. 14). The remainder is "why the agent's hands are dirty, why
it would be apt for her to experience remorse, and why she may owe a duty
to apologize" (p. 15). Conflicts arise from the world: "Duties conflict as
a result of things that happen, including other people's actions" (p. 17).

## What it does not show

- **That creating a predicament wrongs the person placed in it.** The
  paper says nothing of the arranger. Whether dirty hands are "always
  generated by others' wrongdoing" is "a discussion for another day"
  (p. 18).
- **How rectificatory duties are fixed**, or to whom they are owed.
