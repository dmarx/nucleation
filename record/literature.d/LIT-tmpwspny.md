# LIT-tmpwspny: The Constitution of Organization as Informational Individuation

Bencherki, N., & Iliadis, A. (2021). The constitution of organization as informational individuation. *Communication Theory*, 31(3), 442–462. doi:10.1093/ct/qtz018. (Online 29 November 2019.)

Read in full, from the authors' manuscript, in NOTE-tmp62e4t.

## Key takeaways

- **Information** is "a differential in action potential that emerges
  from disparity in ordering between domains". **Communication** is "the
  kin to kin propagation of action that seeks to resolve disparity and,
  in doing so, organizes a domain as it circulates through it"
  (proposition 1).
- **Levels coexist.** Organizations are not at a later stage of
  individuation than their members but at "different informational
  levels"; "An employee is not prior to the organization, nor is the
  organization an abstraction of its employees" (proposition 2).
- **Meaning is reordering.** "what information means ... corresponds to
  the way it reorders a domain" (proposition 3).
- **Knowledge is individuation.** Knowing an organization "is knowing how
  I have changed along with it as I have come to know it" (proposition
  4).

## Why this is here

The owner asked for the works Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy (2024,
LIT-tmp3mudg) lean on. Bencherki is the first author of both. The parent
takes from this paper:

- **Individuation as "the constitution of new entities or collectives"**
  (parent p. 1355, citing p. 445). The words are verbatim in the
  manuscript (ms. p. 8).
- **Individuation as the "processual emergence of physical, technical,
  psychological, or collective entities"** (parent p. 1355, citing p.
  448). Verbatim in the manuscript (ms. p. 13). The two journal page
  numbers could not be checked.
- **Cross-level action and a new conception of communication** (parent
  pp. 1351, 1364). "see also Bencherki & Iliadis, 2021" for treating
  "cross-level" granularities as a continuum and for communication as
  "the continuity of action through beings". Faithful: proposition 2,
  and the paper's claim that the scaling-up question is "not a matter of
  sequential physical steps" (ms. pp. 14–15).
- **Information's dual sense** (parent p. 1357): meaning as how an
  action affects the continuation of a field, and information as the
  in-forming of the new field. Faithful to proposition 3 and to ms. p.
  16: "what an action means corresponds to the ordering that action
  brings about in a given domain."
- **Unattributed borrowing.** The parent's "an action differential that
  may reorganize those individuation processes" (p. 1356) is this
  paper's "information – as an action differential that may take many
  guises" (ms. p. 13). Same first author; not a misuse.

The parent (pp. 1354–1355) groups this paper with work that "tend[s] to
mobilize [Simondon's] ideas to theorize the constitution of
organizations without theorizing the vital role of agency". That is
accurate for this paper. It equates communication with the propagation
of action. It says nothing of agency or affect, and its one empirical
excerpt is read for ordering, not for who acts. THEORY-tmp4o1dd states
the levels claim the parent leans on.
