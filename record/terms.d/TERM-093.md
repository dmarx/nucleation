# TERM-093: correspondence structure, and how it is held: the maps that say which local states or descriptions of interacting systems count as agreeing, distinct from the physical coupling between them, and held as formed, discovered or adapted

## Definition

A **correspondence structure** on a family of locally described systems is the
family of maps ρ_{i,e}: V_i → W_e that send each system's local description
into a space shared with a neighbour. Two local states agree on the edge e when
ρ_{i,e}(x_i) = ρ_{j,e}(x_j). Collected over the network, the maps define a
coboundary δ, and the jointly compatible assignments are its kernel, the global
sections (Hansen and Ghrist, [NOTE-1233](../notes.d/NOTE-1233.md)). "Compatible", "glued" and
"frustrated" are all relative to a correspondence structure.

It is held in one of three ways ([CLAIM-471](../claims.d/CLAIM-471.md)):

| how held | what it is | example the record has read |
|---|---|---|
| **formed** | physical interconnection fixes the maps: joining systems identifies some of their variables, so which local quantities must agree, and on what shared space, is set by how they are connected, while the law that holds at a junction is fixed beforehand | Baez and Fong's wiring, [NOTE-1223](../notes.d/NOTE-1223.md): a map of terminals such as {1, 2} → {3} sets the joined potentials equal and adds the currents (Example 5.17), under the same Kirchhoff condition at every junction |
| **discovered** | measurement estimates a correspondence that already held; it is established only where the estimate is checked against evidence the measurement did not produce ([CLAIM-143](../claims.d/CLAIM-143.md)) | Singer's relative angles, [NOTE-1224](../notes.d/NOTE-1224.md); Robinson's geometric maps, [NOTE-1221](../notes.d/NOTE-1221.md) |
| **adapted** | an apparatus revises its own rules of comparison | discourse sheaves, [NOTE-1222](../notes.d/NOTE-1222.md) |

Forming sets which maps hold where. The joint behaviour they admit is their
kernel, a constraint on states, and that is what [CLAIM-471](../claims.d/CLAIM-471.md)'s physical
constraint formation names; the term keeps the two apart as map and kernel.
Whether interaction among systems already connected ever changes the maps
themselves, rather than which fixed kind of map holds on which edge, is
[QUESTION-282](../questions.d/QUESTION-282.md).

## The distinctions it draws

- **From coupling.** The coupling is what moves the states, for instance a
  dissipative Laplacian flow. The correspondence structure is what counts
  as agreement. A coupling attracts to the specified compatible space only
  if it enforces that structure ([CASE-179](../cases.d/CASE-179.md)). Setting the two apart is
  what makes the proposed study informative.
- **From a representation of it.** An apparatus's model of the
  correspondences is not the operative structure ([CLAIM-471](../claims.d/CLAIM-471.md); A23 §IX:
  ĉ_t ≠ c_t in general). An adapted structure changes the apparatus.
  It changes the systems compared only if it is implemented.
- **Between the three ways of holding it.** Only formation bears on the
  thesis that interaction generates constraints. Discovery is knowledge of
  a correspondence, and its adequacy needs evidence the correspondence does
  not itself produce ([CLAIM-143](../claims.d/CLAIM-143.md)). Adaptation can succeed at agreement by
  changing what is compared ([THEORY-1088](../theory.d/THEORY-1088.md)).

## Why it was proposed

The word was doing three jobs in one claim before the owner's review separated
them, and four entries filed the same evening use it. [QUESTION-282](../questions.d/QUESTION-282.md) asks
when interaction changes the operative structure, and it needs the term to ask
it. What would settle it: adoption if claims cite it to say which sense they
mean; rejection if "gluing" and "compatibility" carry the distinction without
it.
