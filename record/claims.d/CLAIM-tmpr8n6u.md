# CLAIM-tmpr8n6u: Preference reversal is not yet disunity: interests that govern a choice at disjoint times glue, and a self is divided over a choice only on an overlap, which the agent's prediction of its own later valuation creates

## The claim

[CLAIM-169](CLAIM-169.md) holds that an agent is many interests over a choice only as
far as its valuations of that choice cross within the choice's horizon.
Put the interests in the sheaf picture of [CLAIM-153](CLAIM-153.md). An interest is a
section over the contexts of choice in which its valuation ranks first.
The question is whether these sections glue into one self
([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)<!-- ref::uses: TERM-045 -->), in the self-governing sense.

**Without an overlap they glue.** Suppose the interest in the diet governs
the mornings and the interest in the cake governs the evenings, and
nothing in a morning bears on an evening. Then the windows are disjoint,
and by [CLAIM-tmpk4fd7](CLAIM-tmpk4fd7.md) the sections glue: the global section is a
preference indexed by time of day. This is the "tall" example over time.
A reversal of preference, on its own, is no more a division of the self
than "tall" is a division of English.

**Division needs an overlap.** The interests conflict only on a choice
that both bear on at once. The evening's cake is that choice only if the
morning's interest is present in the evening's context, or the evening's
in the morning's.

**Self-prediction makes the overlap.** On Ainslie's account
([THEORY-067](../theory.d/THEORY-067.md)) the agent comes to see the present choice as a forecast
of its later choices in the same series. The present choice is valued as
the whole series' expected value. This brings the later interest's
valuation into the present context. Bargaining, side bets and personal
rules all happen on this overlap, and nowhere else.

So the population of interests that [CLAIM-169](CLAIM-169.md) describes becomes a
divided self only where the agent models itself. Where it does not, the
interests take turns and do not contend.

## What it answers

[CLAIM-163](CLAIM-163.md) objects that "synchronization" of interests
([TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md)<!-- ref::uses: TERM-050 -->) has no coupling, no order parameter and no
transitions. It names self-prediction as "a candidate" coupling. This
claim says where that coupling acts: on the overlap, and only there.
Interests with disjoint windows are uncoupled, as the objection says,
because there is nothing for them to be coupled through. The objection's
other two parts, an order parameter and transitions, are not answered
here. The objection is answered in part and stays open.

## What it does not say

- **That an agent without a self-model has no conflict.** It may have
  conflict in a context both interests reach for other reasons, such as
  two drives cued by the same stimulus. The claim is that intertemporal
  division of the Ainslie kind needs self-prediction. It does not say
  that every conflict does.
- **That the cover is settled.** Which contexts of choice count, and how
  finely, is the open piece [CLAIM-tmpk4fd7](CLAIM-tmpk4fd7.md) names, and [CLAIM-153](CLAIM-153.md) leaves
  cover-dependence open as well.
- **That self-prediction is conscious.** It is the agent's forecasting of
  its own later valuations, however that is implemented.
