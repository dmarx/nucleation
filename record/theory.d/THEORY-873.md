# THEORY-873: Claims to be conscious are produced by an automatic internal model, and since such models are schematic, the intangible, what-it-is-like essence people report is that model's caricature of a real process, proposed to be attention, or the deep selective processing of information that attention, the global workspace and integration share; a theory that explains how neurons produce a feeling without explaining how the feeling comes to be encoded for report (Arrow B) does not explain the reports

## Source

Graziano (2024), *eNeuro* 11(10), [LIT-1358](../literature.d/LIT-1358.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1071](../notes.d/NOTE-1071.md): "From Self Model to Speech" (p. 4), Hypotheses 1–6
(pp. 4–12) and Figures 2–4.

## What was actually shown

An argument and a proposal. No new data.

- **The encoding premise.** If Bob says he has conscious experience,
  "whatever conscious experience is or is not, Bob must have a neural
  embedding that represents it" (p. 4).
- **Arrow B.** A feeling produced by neurons explains the report only if it
  in turn causes that embedding. That needs neurons that respond to "the
  emergent feeling itself" (p. 5). Without it, "the conscious feeling that
  emerges from the brain cannot be the consciousness that we say we have".
  With it, what is reported "is entirely dependent on the information
  embedding" (p. 5).
- **Subtle illusionism.** Brain models are schematic, so "Subtle
  illusionism is necessarily true of all models in the brain and must be
  true of consciousness as well" (p. 7). Hard illusionism, a model of
  nothing, is set aside as evolutionarily improbable.
- **The target.** An Item 1 should affect neurons, resemble the reported
  consciousness in outline, and covary with report (p. 9). Attention is
  offered as meeting all three, on a century of cited covariation, with
  laboratory dissociations called "difficult, though possible" (p. 10). The
  attention schema depicts attention as "an amorphous, mental essence, a
  mind vividly possessing something in the moment" (p. 10).
- **Deep illusionism.** Attention, the workspace and integrated information
  are "different descriptions of the same underlying object—the deep,
  selective processing of information in the cerebral cortex" (p. 11).

What could have come out otherwise is the covariation premise. Reports of
awareness could have followed something other than attention. The paper
reports that they mostly do not.

## What this does not say

- **That nothing real is going on.** Item 1 is a real process, and the
  paper's word is "caricature" (p. 2). But what it keeps real is the
  process, not the phenomenal property: "The subjective, phenomenal
  experience that Bob describes is a distorted construct" (p. 7).
- **That every schematic model is false.** The argument's premise, that
  "nothing is exactly as the brain represents it", is true of any model and
  does not by itself show that a depicted property is absent from its
  target. That step is made on causal-closure grounds, not from the
  premise.
- **That the attention schema has been found.** Its location is "not
  important to the present argument" (p. 4), and the paper reports no
  manipulation of the model apart from attention.
- **That attention, workspace and integration are one thing.** This is
  asserted from overlap. The integration cited, Treisman's, is not IIT's.
- **That machines are or are not conscious.** The framework "could have
  applied to artificial as well as biological consciousness" (p. 12), and
  the topic is set aside.

  model.
