The owner asked for the papers-feed tracker's snapshot to be checked for more
philosophy to file, after Nagel ([LIT-096](../../record/literature.d/LIT-096.md)). The owner also decided
that everything from the sweep stays here, and asked for anthology candidates
to be flagged with a tag. That tag is `anthology-candidate`, from
[ADR-005](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-005.md).

## The funnel

| stage | count |
| --- | ---: |
| papers in the snapshot (`gh-store-snapshot.json`) | 7,800 |
| scored as possibly philosophy | 217 |
| kept by hand, with duplicate captures merged | 132 |
| already held (the SEP entry on structural realism) | 1 |
| seed dossiers written, by six agents | 131 |
| dropped as not philosophy | 3 |
| merged as the same work under another capture | 9 |
| **filed, all `Deferred`** | **119** |

95 were skimmed from a full text and carry a `Skimmed` NOTE. 24 are seeded from
the abstract alone.

**How the score worked:**

- **Journal names:** *Synthese*, *Mind*, *Philosophy Compass*, *Biology &
  Philosophy*, *Minds and Machines* and the like.
- **Source URLs:** PhilPapers, PhilArchive, PhilSci-Archive, the SEP and JSTOR.
- **Keywords:** philosophy words in the title and the first 1,500 characters of
  the abstract.

*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society* matches the journal filter
and is a science journal. Its hits were removed by hand.

**The score misses philosophy that carries no signal:** an arXiv paper in a
physics category, say, that never uses a philosophy word. A second pass over
the low scorers would find more. Nobody has made that pass.

**Three drops:**

- a political-science paper on propaganda technologies;
- a mathematics manuscript on Connes' rigidity conjecture, hiding behind an
  unresolved PhilPapers code;
- a JSTOR chapter on Hero of Alexandria's automata.

## Traps

- **PhilPapers, PhilArchive and Taylor & Francis are behind Cloudflare** from
  here. The tracker had captured about twenty of them as "Just a moment..." or
  as a bare hash, with no title.
  - The agents identified them from the PhilPapers code (an author-surname +
    title stem) and web search.
  - They read the texts through Wayback copies (`web.archive.org/web/2026id_/…`),
    author pages, PhilSci-Archive with a plain `curl` user agent, and
    `rd.springer.com`.
- **Duplicates only show once titles are resolved.** Seven of the nine merges
  were an unresolved PhilPapers capture of a work already in the batch under
  its journal URL: Wallace, Vetter, Floridi, Drayson, Šekrst, the
  distributional-semantics chapter, and the epistemic-injustice paper. So
  deduplicate *after* the seed, by DOI, arXiv id and title, never only before
  it. Reading time is pooled across the captures.
- **The dossiers cross-referenced each other by batch id** (`p078`). The filing
  script copied those into 150 files as-is. They were rewritten to the record's
  codes before commit.

## The vocabulary

Two words were added by [ADR-006](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-006.md):

- **`epistemology`**, used by fourteen works. One had no topic at all, and
  the rest had the nearest wrong one.
- **`logic`**, used by two.

A `philosophy-of-mind` word was considered and not added. The mental-causation
papers are honestly `metaphysics`.

**Two works are flagged `anthology-candidate`:**

- *Borges and AI* (Bottou & Schölkopf);
- Terziyan et al., on ethics carried across generations of AI.

The borderline cases the dossiers kept here are:

- Freeborn's *A Model of Understanding in Deep Learning Systems*;
- the three philosophy-of-unsupervised-learning papers;
- Mallory's *LLMs Are Stochastic Measuring Devices*.

## Dates

- **Pettigrew's two "in preparation" drafts** (*Knowledge is not always more
  valuable than mere true belief* and *What is purely epistemic
  normativity?*) carry the tracker's capture date, 2025-10-29, as an upper
  bound. The PhilArchive record, which would give the upload date, was
  unreachable.
- **Weyl** carries 1949, the English edition, with no month. **Two more** have
  a year only. Each `access:` line says which.

## Reading order

These are the high-priority deeper readings, as the dossiers ranked them:

1. **The AI-consciousness cluster:**
   - Birch's *Centrist Manifesto*;
   - Seth's *Conscious AI and biological naturalism*;
   - Schwitzgebel's *AI and Consciousness*;
   - Goldstein & Lederman's *What Does ChatGPT Want?*.

   These sit beside Butlin et al. (`LIT-056`) and Nagel (`LIT-096`).
2. **Structural realism:** Wallace's *Stating Structural Realism*, Morganti,
   and Chakravartty. They sit beside the SEP entry (`LIT-045`).
3. **Philosophy of biology:**
   - Baedke et al. on reciprocal causation, to be read with Hazelwood's
     reply;
   - the SEP's *Biological Individuals*.
4. **Evidence:** Douglas on inductive risk, and Hartmann & Trpin on
   coherence-based explanatory power.
