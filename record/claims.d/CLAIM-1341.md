# CLAIM-1341: A heuristic can be a compiled contract

## The claim

A16 §9: "If processes repeatedly negotiate recurring conflicts, they may
eventually establish a durable protocol that makes repeated negotiation
unnecessary": recurring conflict, accommodation, maintained protocol. "A
heuristic can then be understood, in some cases, as a **compiled result of
previous governance work**." "This can explain why heuristics are
efficient. It also suggests a mechanism for some cognitive biases: a
compiled governance arrangement continues to operate under circumstances in
which the assumptions supporting it no longer hold."

A16's table of candidates, which it calls "hypotheses about candidate
mechanisms": anchoring ("Early allocation of authority persists beyond its
useful jurisdiction"), status quo bias ("Existing commitments are expensive
to renegotiate"), confirmation bias ("Evidence-selection arrangements favor
the regime they support"), sunk cost ("Historical commitments are treated
as continuing requirements"), loss aversion ("Protective guarantees receive
asymmetric precedence"), availability ("Accessible evidence sources receive
default epistemic authority"), rigidity ("Contracts are maintained despite
changes invalidating their assumptions"), and intuitive expertise ("Learned
local agreements efficiently coordinate appropriate responses").

A18 §9 adds a second route: "Some biases may arise through characteristic
contractual relationships between affective and investigative processes."
"a threat-sensitive process may enjoy broad authority to recruit attention
and inhibit competing inquiry. Such an arrangement may be valuable under
dangerous circumstances while producing distorted sampling or premature
closure elsewhere. Affect claims precedence. The epistemic constitution
determines how that precedence is granted."

## Where the record stands

[CLAIM-358](CLAIM-358.md) holds that a recurring conflict admitting a stable accommodation
can be settled locally at lower amortized cost; [CLAIM-443](CLAIM-443.md) models the
learned prior over which processes govern. The claim adds the reading of
the settled accommodation as a contract with assumptions ([CLAIM-1239](CLAIM-1239.md),
A16 §6's A), so that a misfire is the contract run outside them. The
exchange's earlier turns on heuristics and biases (U10–U12) are filed with
their own codes; the closing pass relates this to them.

## What it does not say

It does not say that every heuristic is a compiled contract or that every
bias is a misapplied one; A16 says "in some cases" and "some cognitive
biases".

## Note of 2026-11-24: the same idea filed earlier, the closing pass

A16 §9's "compiled result of previous governance work" is A13 §VI's
"amortized governance", held as [CLAIM-1303](CLAIM-1303.md) from A12 and A13: a
learned allocation of which process governs, kept past the regime that
made it adequate, so that expertise and bias are one mechanism. This
claim restates it in the vocabulary of contracts. What it adds is A16's
table of candidate mappings from named biases to compiled protocols and
A18 §9's second route, through the precedence affect is granted. The
earlier entry is kept as the idea's home and this one complements it.
Whether to retire either is the owner's call. The wording is the
assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
