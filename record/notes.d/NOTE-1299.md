# NOTE-1299: Belonging to a community of European fans? European common in-group identity formation through football fandom

## Contribution

The first survey measure, by the paper's account, of identification with
a *European* community of football fans as distinct from club and
national fan communities. It shows the three are related but that the
European one separates from the other two in a factor analysis. It
reports what the European identification goes with, among people
already sampled as fans.

## Key insight

"The fandom" has more than one level. Fans of rival clubs can still
answer yes to "I consider myself a part of a bigger community of fans in
Europe", and that answer goes with trusting fans from elsewhere in
Europe, not with liking the institutions that run European football.
The community a fan feels part of and the governance a fan criticizes
come apart.

## Assumptions

- **Social identity in Tajfel's sense**, with Ashmore et al.'s
  multidimensional components (p. 2), and the **common in-group identity
  model** (CIIM): contact under equal status, cooperation and common goals
  recategorizes subgroups into a superordinate group, possibly with a
  "dual identity" (Dovidio et al. 2003; pp. 2–4).
- **The sample is fans.** Of 7,040 respondents, those who self-categorized
  as fans, or scored football interest ≥ 7/10, were kept: 3,685 cases
  (p. 4). The panel is a commercial online panel with quotas on gender,
  age and education; the paper calls it representative.
- **One item per level.** The dependent variable is agreement (0–10) with
  "I consider myself a part of a bigger community of fans in Europe",
  with parallel single items for club and nation (p. 4).

## Key results

- **Levels (Table 3, p. 6).** Means are club 6.13 (SD 2.50), nation 5.57
  (2.58) and Europe 5.29 (2.61). Correlations are nation–Europe .78,
  club–nation .69 and club–Europe .58. The text reports 1,962 respondents
  (49.3%) at ≥ 6 for Europe and 833 (20.93%) at ≥ 8. Those percentages
  imply a denominator of about 3,980, not the 3,685 analysed; the paper
  does not say which.
- **Dimensionality (Table 4, p. 7).** A one-factor solution explains 70%
  of shared variance, with loadings .72–.96. A two-factor solution
  explains 78%: club and nation load on one factor, Europe alone on the
  other (.931). The nation loading on factor 1 is 1.011 with uniqueness
  .012, a Heywood case the paper does not remark on. A three-factor
  solution "fails to provide additional explanatory value".
- **Correlates (Table 5, OLS, R² = .37, n = 3,685).**
  - *Positive:* trust in a European fan from another country (0.336,
    the largest); watching foreign leagues (occasionally 0.55, regularly
    0.80, very often 1.19, against rarely); positive perceptions of fans
    (0.231); attachment to Europe (0.159); fan-interest representation
    (0.112); football interest (0.098); watching European competitions
    regardless of club (0.195); medium (0.37) and high (0.27) education
    against low; right-leaning self-placement (0.045); interpersonal
    trust (0.040).
  - *Negative:* age (−0.020 a year); male (−0.197); Germany (−0.47) and
    Norway (−0.71) against Spain; cosmopolitan values (−0.056).
  - *Null:* income; negative perceptions of fans; football universalism;
    critique of football governance.
- **Interpretation (pp. 8–10).**
  - The European identification is "not simply an additive extension"
    of club or national attachments but "a related, but qualitatively
    distinct form of belonging", consistent with CIIM and a "marble cake"
    of nested identities.
  - The null for governance critique suggests "a conceptual separation
    between the community of fans and the institutions that regulate
    football".
  - The paper flags its own limits: no causal inference; four countries;
    no direct measure of cross-border contact.

## Concepts

- **European fan community identification**: the single item above.
- **Europeanisation** (of football): changes in football's institutions
  and practices attributable to European integration. It runs top-down
  (downloading), bottom-up (uploading) and horizontally (crossloading)
  (p. 4).
- **particularized trust**: trust directed at a defined in-group, here
  "a hypothetical football fan that is a European citizen not from the
  respondent's home country" (p. 5).
- **dual identity / marble cake**: holding superordinate and subgroup
  identities at once (Dovidio et al.; Risse 2015).

## Connections

- **Reimer et al. ([NOTE-1251](NOTE-1251.md)).** Their chapter's common in-group and
  dual identity models are the frame here. Their caution, that
  recategorization can subsume minorities and that in-group projection
  makes the majority's attributes the default, is not taken up. The
  finding that identification is lower among fans of the dominant German
  league is suggestive and unexplained.
- **Self-categorization theory ([THEORY-1114](../theory.d/THEORY-1114.md)).** SCT predicts the
  European level becomes salient when the comparison frame includes
  non-Europeans or non-fans. The survey measures standing identification
  with no frame manipulated, so it cannot test that.
- **Giulianotti ([NOTE-1244](NOTE-1244.md)).** Cited (p. 3) for the varied modes of
  fandom. The heavy-foreign-viewing correlate fits Giulianotti's
  "followers" and "flâneurs" more than his "supporters"; the paper does
  not draw this.
- **Pettigrew ([LIT-1408](../literature.d/LIT-1408.md))**, unread, is the contact theory behind CIIM.

- **[CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md) and [QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md).** The governance null bears on the case
  (fans "hating" an owner) only obliquely. It suggests that judgment of a
  sport's institutions and identification with fellow fans are separate
  attitudes, which is A2's reason for not reading one off the other.
  That is a European-level finding and an inference to the club level.
- **"The fandom" has no single boundary.** It adds evidence for the
  exchange's point that a fandom has nested scales.
- **No ML instruction.**

## Limitations

- **Single-item measures** for all three identification levels. The
  "distinct dimension" claim is a factor analysis of three items, and the
  two-factor solution includes an out-of-bounds loading.
- **Cross-sectional associations**, as the paper says.
- **The percentages do not match the analysed n.**
- **Fans only**: identification relative to non-fans is not measured, and
  the inclusion rule mixes self-categorized fans with the merely
  interested.
- **Four countries**; country effects (Germany, Norway lower) are
  explained post hoc.
- **The significance note in Table 5** marks p < .1 with one star while
  the text treats p = .031 as significant. This is a labelling slip, not
  a substantive one.

## Open questions

- Does the European factor survive multi-item measurement, and does it
  shift when the comparison frame is manipulated (fans of European versus
  non-European football; fans versus non-fans)? That would connect the
  survey to SCT's prediction.
- Does cross-border contact precede the identification? A panel design or
  a contact measure would settle direction.
