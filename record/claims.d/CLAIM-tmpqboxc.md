# CLAIM-tmpqboxc: Grice's factivity test shows that a designed alarm's content can be false: its sounding means, factively, that the detector crossed its threshold, and carries "fire" only as a non-factive content, so a false alarm's error lies there; what makes "fire" the content it is supposed to carry is a separate question, answered for this designed alarm by its designer's intention and left open, between selection history and organizational maintenance, for other systems

## Two questions

The review of #483 the owner relayed on 2026-11-12 asked for these to be
kept apart:

- **(a) Can the alarm's content be false?** This is a question of
  correctness conditions.
- **(b) What makes that content the one the alarm is supposed to
  carry?** This is a question of the source of the norm.

## (a) Correctness: Grice's factivity test

Grice's first test for natural meaning is that "x means that p entails p"
(NOTE-735, pp. 377–378; THEORY-444). Applied to the owner's smoke detector
(CASE-tmpv6i22):

1. **Natural meaning.** The alarm's sounding means that the detector
   crossed its threshold. This entails its content, and no one means it.
   When steam sets the detector off, this content is true.
2. **"Fire".** "The alarm means there is a fire" does not entail that
   there is one. It fails the first test, so it is not natural meaning.
3. **Where the error is.** The steam alarm is in error only with respect
   to "fire". So a false alarm needs a non-factive content.

A4 §3 said "**Meaningfulness is not the same as truthfulness.**" On this
reading that holds of the non-factive content only. The natural meaning
cannot be false.

## (b) The source of the norm: not settled by (a)

That "fire" is non-factive shows only that the factive account cannot
say what makes a false alarm false. It does not say where the norm comes
from. The review: "non-factivity shows only that the factive account is
insufficient". Candidates:

- **A designer's intention.** For CASE-tmpv6i22 this is the source: the
  alarm is stipulated to be designed to warn of fire. Grice's red light
  points the same way for designed signs, "some sort of reference to
  somebody's intentions" (NOTE-735, p. 385). That is a fact about this
  case, not a consequence of (a). Inferring it from non-factivity alone
  is the step ARG-tmp27nss rejects.
- **Selection history.** A biological warning system could have a
  non-factive function grounded in what its ancestors' signals were
  selected for. That is teleosemantics (Millikan), unread here, and the
  reading is owed.
- **Organizational maintenance.** A self-maintaining system's own
  anticipations can be false by its own lights (Bickhard, THEORY-220).
  CLAIM-tmph9egz takes this up.

## Where it sits

- **It bears on QUESTION-128.** Question (b) is that question asked of a
  signal. The claim answers it only for a designed alarm, and only by the
  case's stipulation.
- **It places the owner's claim** (CLAIM-tmpkr3ti). The debugger's
  absence is evidence about the mechanism, and is factive only under a
  validated guarantee. The sprinkler deploying for steam is an error
  with respect to "fire".

## What it does not say

- That Grice classified alarms. He did not. The red light is his nearest
  case, and he leaves it unresolved.
- That "fire" is non-natural meaning in Grice's full sense, which needs
  an intention to be recognized by an audience. The claim needs only
  that "fire" fails the factivity test.
