# NOTE-159: SEP — Philosophy of microbiology

## Contribution

The entry asks why philosophers should pay attention to microbes, given philosophy's focus on humans and philosophy of biology's historical focus on animals. It argues in four steps: microbes do most of the planet's biological work and underlie every major transition; microbiology gave rise to fields such as modern medicine, molecular biology and genomics; microbes serve as material models of general phenomena; and microbiome research bears on human nature, individuality and causality.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1: the neglect of microbes came from contingent disciplinary history (the field's founders were zoologists and evolutionists), not active bias.
- §2.2: three philosophical reasons — the biological species concept fits microbes poorly (§2.2.1); lateral gene transfer challenges the tree of life (§2.2.2); major transitions look different from a microbial vantage (§2.2.3).
- §4: microbes as model systems, their virtues (§4.1) and alleged limits (§4.2), such as predation, ageing and kin recognition, which were first defined by animal traits.
- §5.1: microbiome individuality is approached with evolutionary criteria (are host and microbes one Darwinian unit?) rather than traditional mereology; claims that microbiomes change the self are called contentious.
- §5.2: microbiome causal claims as a test bed for interventionism and causal-law accounts; causal-role vs selected-effect function.
- §6: microbes bear on identity, selfhood, human nature and mind, not only on philosophy of science.
