  any theory of groups must meet: members can differ across times and
  worlds, groups exist contingently and temporarily, are located, and

  identity at a time needs the same members and the same relations. A

# LIT-tmpgr5qe: What are groups?

Katherine Ritchie (2013), *Philosophical Studies* 166(2):257–272 (online 10 October 2012) — DOI-10.1007/s11098-012-0030-5

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the works filling out
the record's coverage of social ontology under the topic [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added. With
the 2018 paper ([LIT-tmppeiud](LIT-tmppeiud.md)) it is the record's first account of what a group
*is*, as opposed to what a group can do.

[NOTE-tmplq4uh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplq4uh.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. The criteria section and the four refutations are the contribution.
The positive view is a sketch: it leaves open how fine-grained structures are
(three options, none chosen), defines node occupation by a condition that
mentions occupation of every node, and treats intentions as an optional extra
condition that "might be added" to separate social groups from other material
objects. The 2018 paper keeps the view, calls these groups "organized social
groups" and amends the membership definition (its n. 22).

**Bearing on what the record holds.** The paper never mentions agency, minds
or consciousness. What follows is this record's reading of what its
metaphysics implies for the group-agency works.

- **List & Pettit ([LIT-391](LIT-391.md)) and List ([LIT-401](LIT-401.md)).** List & Pettit's publisher
  says group agents exist "over and above" their members. Ritchie gives one
  precise, cheap sense of "over and above": a group is not identical to its
  members, since two groups with the same members at every time can differ
  in structure (her Supreme Court and a committee of all the Justices). That
  distinctness holds for every structured group, agent or not. So "over and
  above" in the sense of numerical distinctness from the members is settled
  by a metaphysics of groups before agency comes up, and List & Pettit's
  claim, read as a claim about agency, says something further.
- **Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md)) and his reply to Kammerer ([LIT-459](LIT-459.md)).** [NOTE-381](../notes.d/NOTE-381.md)
  records Schwitzgebel's worry that it is unclear which entity "the United
  States" names, so that citizens may not represent the entity whose
  consciousness is at issue (its C2). Ritchie's Coincidence criterion makes
  that worry structural: a set of people can realise several distinct
  structures at once, each a distinct group. Which of them a citizen
  represents is a further question. This sharpens the reply; Ritchie does
  not discuss it.
- **[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md).** The view makes a group's identity depend on its
  organisation, which suits the functionalist premise [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) starts
  from. But Ritchie's structures are roles and functional relations of the
  baseball-team kind, not causal or informational architecture, so the
  paper supplies none of the criteria [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s `promote_when` asks for.
  It does not move it.

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary. `metaphysics` and `mereology` hold its
argument against fusion and aggregate theories of composition, and
`individuation` holds its persistence, modal and coincidence criteria and its
identity condition.
