# ARG-tmpn6xou: That a cooperative solution changes its participants and so creates new problems shows, by contrast with morality-as-cooperation, that morality is more than the solutions to cooperation problems

## The inference

Premise (A24 §2, on [THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md)): "A cooperative solution can alter the
participants and their circumstances so substantially that it creates a new
problem requiring moral evaluation. The solution becomes part of the
environment of the next problem." Conclusion ([CLAIM-tmpreipd](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpreipd.md), in part):
morality is not only the set of solutions to cooperation problems; it
evaluates the terms on which cooperation is formed, maintained and
transformed.

## Critical questions

1. **Does the contrasted account deny what the premise asserts?** No.
   [THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md) says morality is the solutions to recurrent cooperation
   problems, and its source expects new games to add domains ([NOTE-408](../notes.d/NOTE-408.md),
   "extending the foundations"). That solving one problem changes the
   players and poses another is a dynamic the account can take on without
   strain: the problem posed is still one of cooperation.
2. **Are the new problems outside the contrasted account's domains?** The
   ones A23 and A24 name are not. Hold-up after relationship-specific
   investment (A22 §9) is a problem of dividing a joint surplus when
   outside options have shifted; [THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md)'s division domain is fairness
   in such divisions. A party's reliable commitment captured by another
   ([CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md)) is a failure of reciprocity in exchange. Control over the
   terms of continued cooperation is a contest over a resource. Each could
   be argued to escape the seven domains, but A does not argue it.
3. **Is the contrast drawn at the same level?** No. [THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md) is an
   account of what morality is, functionally and historically: which
   dispositions and norms a culture values, and why. [CLAIM-tmpreipd](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpreipd.md)'s
   conclusion is about what moral verdicts evaluate and what makes them
   right ("Moral principles can govern how cooperation itself is
   constituted ... But their justification need not be exhausted by their
   contribution to cooperation", A23 §6). A23 §11 keeps genesis and
   persistence apart from validity; the contrast in A24 §2 lets one stand
   for the other.
4. **Is there a route that does separate the two?** Yes, and A gives it
   elsewhere. Consideration owed to those who do not or cannot cooperate
   (A24 §10: "Animals, infants, incapacitated individuals"), cooperation
   that wrongs outsiders, and refusal that is right (A23 §5) are cases
   where the moral verdict and any cooperative solution come apart.
   Consideration owed to animals tells against [THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md)'s closing clause,
   "behaviour unrelated to cooperation is morally neutral", directly. Killen
   ([THEORY-2110](../theory.d/THEORY-2110.md)) gives the developmental form of the refusal case.

Verdict: Rejected as support for [CLAIM-tmpreipd](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpreipd.md). The conclusion is not
refuted; it is carried, if at all, by the routes under question 4, and
the record holds nothing that settles whether the problems cooperation
creates are themselves cooperation problems. That would need a case,
stated in advance, of a problem produced by successful cooperation that
none of the seven domains covers.
