# LIT-2174: Aid, Incentives, and Sustainability: An Institutional Analysis of Development Cooperation

Ostrom, E., Gibson, C., Shivakumar, S. & Andersson, K. (2002). *Aid, Incentives, and Sustainability: An Institutional Analysis of Development Cooperation. Main Report.* Sida Studies in Evaluation 02/01. Stockholm: Swedish International Development Cooperation Agency. ISBN 91-586-8836-6.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1647](../notes.d/NOTE-1647.md). Page numbers are the report's printed pages.*

- **The Samaritan's dilemma** (§2.4.4, pp. 30–31, after Buchanan 1977). A
  Samaritan's "dominant strategy is always to extend help"; knowing it, the
  recipient's "best response is to expend a low level of effort". Repeated,
  "the recipient loses skill and motivation over time".
- **Upkeep is the type case** (pp. 31–32). Power stations "can last fifty to
  sixty years, if limited but constant efforts are made to maintain them",
  yet "major rehabilitations are needed every ten to fifteen years"; "It is
  the recipient whose actions make the difference in outcomes from
  sustainable to nonsustainable."
- **Kafue Gorge** (pp. 227–228). After about SEK 300m from Sida over 1970–94,
  "GRZ remains completely dependent on outside funds for the continued
  operation of the KGS".
- **Ownership in four dimensions** (§1.4.3, pp. 15–16): provision,
  production (contributions are "a costly signal"), consumption, and the
  decision to continue or stop. Where the donor pays full costs the
  implementing agency "becomes more of an owner than the beneficiary".
- **Sustainability of effects, not of projects** (§1.2.4, pp. 7–9): "the
  longevity of development cooperation's effects, rather than particular
  projects or activities".
- **The donor's side** (Exec. Summ.; Ch. 11). Funding is "invariably
  de-linked from the sustainability of funded efforts"; staff rotation,
  disbursement pressure and late evaluations keep it so.
- **Withdrawal** (Appendix B, by Gardner and Waller). A credible threat to withdraw would
  neutralize the trap, but "recipient countries know that the threat is not
  credible".

## Standing in the record

The record's first work on foreign aid, read at the owner's request after
[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (supplied maintenance) to ask whether development theory says how
an aid-dependent organization becomes self-sufficient. It is the same
school as [LIT-719](LIT-719.md) (Ostrom's Nobel lecture) and [LIT-737](LIT-737.md) (*Governing the
Commons*), whose account of durable user-made institutions is [THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md);
this report applies it to aid and finds the positive half of its ownership
criteria there. Its distinctive addition to [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) is that the outside
supply is not exogenous: its predictability lowers the recipient's own
repair. Filed from it: [THEORY-1480](../theory.d/THEORY-1480.md).
