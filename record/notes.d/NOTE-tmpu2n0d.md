# NOTE-tmpu2n0d: Le Doussal, skimmed for what became of the KPZ letter's claims above one dimension

## Contribution

"We give a brief overview of the seminal paper which introduced the
Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation as a paradigmatic model for random growth in
1986" (abstract). For the record, a check by hindsight on the letter's
claims for d ≥ 2. The reading is [THEORY-tmpdcfxq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpdcfxq.md).

## Key results

1. **The flow** (p. 4, eq. 10): ∂_ℓ g = (2 − d)g + g² + O(g³), so "for
   d ≤ 2 the nonlinear coupling g flows to large values into the strong
   noise phase", while "for d > 2, there is an intermediate fixed point,
   at g = g* ≃ d − 2, which separates a weak noise phase where the
   nonlinearity is irrelevant, and the strong noise phase".
2. **The symmetry** (p. 4): "Galilean invariance (i.e. the STS symmetry)
   ensures the relation χ = 2 − z."
3. **The conjecture failed** (p. 4): "The conjecture χ = 2/(d + 3) was put
   forward by Kim and Kosterlitz … More recent numerics show that it is
   not exact, with, in d = 2, χ = 0.3869(4) for stationary RSOS
   interfaces".

## Assumptions

A review; it relies on the cited numerics and calculations.

## Concepts

Weak- and strong-noise phases; statistical tilt symmetry (STS);
roughening transition above two dimensions.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-841](../claims.d/CLAIM-841.md)**: **qualifies the KPZ row.** Above two dimensions KPZ
  scaling is reached only on one side of a tuned fixed point, while both
  phases it separates are scale invariant. The row is scoped to d ≤ 2
  ([ARG-tmp2xbl4](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp2xbl4.md)).
- **[CLAIM-tmpvq33w](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpvq33w.md)**: its KPZ case, and its "d ≥ 3 for small λ" as a
  perturbation that puts a tuned point back, rest on item 1; item 2 is
  the tilt symmetry's role, stated by the retrospective, not the 1986
  letter.

## Limitations

Read for one passage only; the cited numerics and the non-perturbative
RG predictions were not read.
