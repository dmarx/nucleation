# LIT-tmpufm8a: Double-Loop Learning, Teaching, and Research

Chris Argyris (2002), *Academy of Management Learning & Education*
1(2):206–218 — DOI-10.5465/amle.2002.8509400

## Key takeaways

- **The definitions.** "Learning may be defined as the detection and
  correction of error. Single-loop learning occurs when errors are
  corrected without altering the underlying governing values." "Double-loop
  learning occurs when errors are corrected by changing the governing
  values and then the actions." The example is a thermostat that asks why
  it is set to measure temperature.
- **Espoused theory versus theory-in-use.** Espoused theories vary widely.
  Theories-in-use, Argyris says, do not. The prevalent one, Model I, has
  four governing variables: be in unilateral control, strive to win and not
  lose, suppress negative feelings, act rationally. It requires defensive
  reasoning, with premises kept tacit and tests that are "self-serving and
  self-sealing".
- **Skilled incompetence.** Theories-in-use are learned early and run
  skilfully, so people are "unaware and unaware that they [are] unaware".
- **Organizational defensive routines.** These are practices that prevent
  embarrassment and also prevent discovering its causes. Their logic: send
  an inconsistent message, act as if it is not inconsistent, make that
  undiscussable, and make the undiscussability undiscussable. They and
  individuals' Model I sustain each other, so neither can be changed alone.
- **Model II.** Its governing values are valid information, informed choice,
  and vigilant monitoring of implementation. It works by "productive
  reasoning": premises and inferences explicit, conclusions testable by
  logic independent of the actor's.
- **The case.** Thirty-four CEOs advising a role-played failed COO ("Andy")
  reproduced the abstract, untestable advice and outward blame they
  diagnosed in him. They blamed Andy as Andy blamed his board.
- **A proposed test.** Part III proposes hypotheses, scoring categories, time
  series and comparison groups that would turn the workshop into a
  quasi-experiment. The paper does not report running one.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, in place of the unread 1977
*HBR* article ([LIT-tmpnuvqj](LIT-tmpnuvqj.md)), for the owner's essay on organizational
will. The essay's §8.3 separates changing policies from reconsidering "the
standards through which policies acquire authority". That is single- versus
double-loop learning, and the essay should name it. The essay also says
recursive revision does not guarantee normative adequacy. Argyris supplies
a mechanism for why organizations that espouse revisability fail at it:
defensive routines make the governing values undiscussable. The account is
