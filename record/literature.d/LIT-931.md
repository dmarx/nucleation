# LIT-931: Generalized Stability Theory. Part I: Autonomous Operators

Brian F. Farrell and Petros J. Ioannou (1996), *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences* 53(14), 2025–2040 — `doi:10.1175/1520-0469(1996)053<2025:gstpia>2.0.co;2`

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp9vrtb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9vrtb.md).*

- **Eigenvalues govern only the long run.** If A is normal, ‖e^{At}‖ =
  e^{λ^R_max t} and the spectrum decides stability for all time. If not,
  growth over time t is ‖e^{At}‖, the top singular value of the
  propagator. The eigenvalues bound it from below, not above (eq. 6).
- **Two limits and the interval between.** As t → 0 the growth rate is
  the numerical abscissa, the top eigenvalue of (A + A†)/2, which can be
  positive when every mode decays. As t → ∞ the leading mode wins, but
  its best excitation is its adjoint vector, not the mode. The physically
  relevant times lie between, where the SVD of the propagator gives the
  optimal perturbations.
- **Large growth with every mode damped.** A damped Eady model, stable at
  the chosen wavenumber, grows about a hundredfold in energy over four
  days. A channel with only decaying modes grows its two-day optimal
  112-fold.
- **Forced and stochastic responses are larger.** Resonance is bounded by
  κ(E)/dist(iω, Λ(A)), not 1/dist. Under white noise the variance
  exceeds the sum over the poles, with equality only for normal A (eq.
  22, cited to Ioannou 1995). In the 2 × 2 example, ⟨‖u‖²⟩ = 3/4 +
  cot²θ/12, of which 3/4 is the normal response.
- **Norm-relative.** Non-normality depends on the inner product, and some
  (usually unphysical) norm always makes the operator normal. The paper
  uses the energy norm.

## Standing in the record

Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmp9vrtb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9vrtb.md). It is the compact statement of
non-modal stability for a fixed operator, and [THEORY-tmpwdx4q](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwdx4q.md) is filed
from it with Trefethen et al. ([LIT-932](LIT-932.md)): the eigenvalues of a non-normal
operator govern only the long-run limit, and finite-time growth and
forced variance can exceed what they predict by large factors.

It bears on [CLAIM-186](../claims.d/CLAIM-186.md), whose note "settling does not rule out bursts"
states its central result at the right strength. Two things the reading
adds to that note. Under continual small disturbance a non-normal system
holds more standing variance than its spectrum predicts, so the effect
is not only bursts. And non-normality is relative to a norm, so a
reading across to an argument has to say what its measure of drift is.
The note's further step, that directed coupling such as `rests_on` is a
standard source of non-normality, is not in this paper. Its source is
background shear; its 2 × 2 example is a one-way coupling and fits the
reading, but states no rule.

Part II ([LIT-929](LIT-929.md)) extends the theory to operators that change in time,
where the conclusion changes: transient growth can become asymptotic
instability ([THEORY-tmpyhatw](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyhatw.md)). Not held in the Anthology of the SOTA; it
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.
