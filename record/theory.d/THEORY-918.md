# THEORY-918: Across 642 experimental tests to September 2019, forming implementation intentions reliably improved behaviour, cognitive performance, affect regulation and social judgement, but publication bias was substantial: the pooled d = .36 falls to a model-averaged d = .15 under robust Bayesian meta-analysis, with large heterogeneity

## Source

Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, [LIT-1488](../literature.d/LIT-1488.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1131](../notes.d/NOTE-1131.md) from the version of record in KOPS: pp. 168–173, Tables 1
and 2, and p. 183.

## What was actually shown

- **Pooled (p. 170).** d = .36 [.33, .40] over 642 tests from 294
  reports, experimental designs only.
- **Bias (p. 170).** Egger's regression b = 1.06, above the threshold for
  "substantial" bias; trim and fill .35; robust Bayesian meta-analysis:
  extreme evidence for an effect, for publication bias and for
  heterogeneity, model-averaged d = .15 [.08, .22]. "Thus, implementation
  intentions appear to have a robust impact on outcomes though the effect
  size is small according to conventional guidelines" (p. 170).
- **By outcome (Table 1, p. 171).** Behaviour .27 (k = 301), cognitive
  performance .47 (k = 204), affect regulation .66 (k = 26), social
  judgement .52 (k = 20). Vaccination and voting did not differ reliably
  from zero.
- **By setting (Table 2).** Lab .49, field .27; in person .53, online .31;
  objective outcomes .43, self-report .28.

What could have come out otherwise: the bias correction could have left
no effect, as it has for some behavioural interventions in the debate the
authors cite (p. 170).

## What this does not say

- **That the per-outcome figures are corrected.** Only the overall
  estimate was corrected; Tables 1–4 are uncorrected.
- **That the effect is up to date.** The search ends in September 2019
  and was not updated (p. 168).
- **How plans work.** No mechanism was tested in this review.
- **That the 2006 estimate was wrong in kind.** The two sets differ in
  inclusion (2024 excludes correlational designs and includes schedule
  plans and non-behavioural outcomes), so the comparison of .65 with .36
  is not like for like.
