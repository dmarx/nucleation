# THEORY-063: The unity of experience is the integration of one global world-model into a single functional cluster, and it does not require a self-model

## Which unity this is about

**Phenomenal unity**: the unity of experience. Of the four unities the
record keeps apart ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)), this account is about that one only, and its
second clause separates it from the self. The unity of a conscious world is
achieved, on this account, before and without a self-model. What makes
experiences one *subject's* is a further question, answered, if at all, by
the self-model and the modelled perspective ([THEORY-064](THEORY-064.md)). The précis
says so: "the question remains how such a cluster becomes tied to an
individual first-person perspective" ([LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), p. 9). The functional
integration here is not behavioural integration either: it is the binding
of representational contents into one model, not the coordination of
control systems into one agent.

## Source

- Metzinger (2005), [LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), read in full in [NOTE-460](../notes.d/NOTE-460.md), §§1.1.4,
  2.1 and 2.6. Page references are to the PSYCHE PDF.

## What was actually shown

**Globality.** Minimal consciousness is "the presence of a world": "the
activation of a coherent, global model of reality ... within a virtual
window of presence ... a model that cannot be recognized as a model by the
system generating it". All such a system would experience "would be the
presence of one unified world". Neither inner structure, subjective time
nor "the perspectivalness going along with a first-person point of view
exists at this point. We could call this 'Selfless Snapshot
Consciousness'" (p. 3). Metzinger calls this minimal notion "probably
empirically empty" (p. 14): it is a conceptual floor, not a reported state.

**Functional level.** Phenomenal representata "are operated on by a
highest-order integrational function", which "dynamically unifies a large
number of microcausal relations into a single and distinct causal role"
(p. 7). This "highest-order binding" is called a "speculative concept".

**Neural level.** The correlate "must be a distributed process which can be
described as the realization of a functional cluster, combining a high
internal correlation strength between its elements with the existence of
distinct functional borders", as in Tononi and Edelman's dynamic core.
The prediction: "any system operating under a conscious model of reality
will be characterized by the existence of one single area of maximal causal
density within its information-processing mechanisms", and "what you
subjectively experience upon experiencing your world as coherent is the high
internal correlation strength between a subset of physical events in your
own brain" (p. 8). There may be many integrated bundles, "and typically
there will be one single, largest island of maximal causal density
underlying the current conscious model of the world" (p. 9). "Currently no
detailed theories concerning the possible neural correlates ... do exist"
(p. 7).

**Without a perspective.** "Perspectivalness is not a necessary condition
for the ascription of conscious experience." In certain spiritual
experiences and "fully depersonalized states during severe psychiatric
disorders ... no conscious self and no consciously experienced first-person
perspective exist", and these are "non-subjective consciousness" (p. 17).
They are global experiential states, so the world-model is still one.

So the précis argues the first clause on the representational and
functional levels and offers the neural level as a hypothesis borrowed from
others. It supports the second clause with a conceptual case and with
phenomenological inference from reported states, which is all it claims.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say the dynamic-core hypothesis is established.** The précis
  borrows it, and says the neural detail is unknown.
- **It does not say what makes the cluster subjective.** "The philosophical
  question remains of what it is that makes this cluster into the subjective
  world the organism lives in" (p. 9). The account is about unity, not about
  why the unified cluster is experienced at all.
- **It does not say selfless experience is common or settled.** The
  evidence for non-subjective consciousness is phenomenological inference
  from rare states, and reports of having had no self face an obvious
  objection the précis acknowledges elsewhere.

## Connections

- **[THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)** (minds made of agents). "One single area of maximal causal
  density" has the shape of the anti-nesting principles that account
  examines, closest to IIT's exclusion postulate, in which only the local
  maximum of Φ is conscious. It is motivated by the unity of experience
  within one brain, not by a verdict about groups, which is what [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)
  asks of such a principle. But it is a prediction, not a computation, and
  "typically" and "the largest" leave room for exactly the cases that
  account cares about. It does not meet [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)'s `promote_when`.
- **The self-model ([THEORY-064](THEORY-064.md)).** That account is about the self; this
  one is about the world the self is later modelled in. They come from the
  same précis and are kept apart because the record keeps the unities apart.
- **Korsgaard ([THEORY-065](THEORY-065.md)).** Korsgaard grants that a natural
  individual is "the seat of a unified consciousness" and denies that this
  gives unity of agency. This account says what that given unity is, on one
  theory. Together they mark the line [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md) draws: phenomenal unity is
  given by integration; unity of agency, on Korsgaard's view, has to be
  achieved.
