# NOTE-091: Pernu — Mental causation via neuroprosthetics?

## Contribution

Several authors (List and Menzies, Menzies, Woodward) have cited research on brain–computer interfaces as evidence that mental states are genuinely causally efficacious. Pernu reviews the problem of mental causation, the difference-making (interventionist) response, and how neuroprosthetics works. He grants that neuroprosthetics can be read as supporting mental causation, but only if the mental is identified with the neural. So, contrary to what these arguments assume, neuroprosthetics cannot ground nonreductive physicalism, and if anything it illustrates mind–brain identity.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 5160–5161): the main lesson is that difference-making arguments have been "equivocal" on whether the mental and the physical are distinct at all. Preferring coarse-grained causal hypotheses does not by itself save nonreductive physicalism. That would also require showing that mental states are multiply realised.
- §2–3 (pp. 5161–5164): the causal exclusion argument and the interventionist reply, followed by a "crash course in neuroprosthetics" from Helmholtz and Fetz's operant conditioning of cortical units through modern cortical decoding.
- §4 (pp. 5164–5168): the reconstructed argument. It rests on Musallam et al. 2004's ensemble recordings of cognitive (intention) signals. Many single-neuron patterns give the same ensemble pattern, so the counterfactual holds for the intention but not for any one neural realiser.
- §5 and Fig. 2 (pp. 5168–5171): Woodward calls the BCI case "about as clear a case of mental causation as one could imagine". Pernu replies that what is multiply realised is the *cortical* variable N (by single-neuron states N1 and N2), not the intention M. Genuine multiple realisation would need M realised by distinct cortical states N and Z, which neuroprosthetics does not show.
- §6 Conclusion (pp. 5171–5172): future work should keep apart two difference-making defences, the substantial one (distinctness plus multiple realisability) and the deflationary one (mental causes are physical causes). Neuroprosthetics supports only the latter.
