# QUESTION-504: Does help that maximizes the user's long-horizon influence, as a stabilizing shared-autonomy assistant does, erode the user's unassisted capacity, and can command-to-outcome information taken with the assistance present ever show it?

## What makes it open

A stabilizing assistant raises the user's influence over the episode by
doing part of the work, keeping the lander out of the crash region
([CLAIM-787](../claims.d/CLAIM-787.md)). [CLAIM-675](../claims.d/CLAIM-675.md) holds that where a capacity is learned by doing
and decays with disuse, support that does the work can leave a party with
less capacity later than support that does not. The user's own landing skill
may be such a capacity. Command-to-outcome information measured with the
assistant present scores the joint channel, so it cannot tell a channel the
user can carry alone from one the assistant carries for them. [CLAIM-532](../claims.d/CLAIM-532.md)'s
test, removing the inducing conditions and presenting a new case of the same
class, is the one that would tell. First Contact itself does not run it
([NOTE-2236](../notes.d/NOTE-2236.md)). The nearest held evidence is [THEORY-1521](../theory.d/THEORY-1521.md): automation that
does a subtask can let the operator's skill in it decay unnoticed.

## What would count as an answer

A study that trains users with and without the stabilizing assistant to the
same assisted score and then removes it: lower unassisted performance in the
assisted group, with equal assisted scores, would show erosion invisible to
the score. A design that keeps the user's capacity, for instance assistance
withdrawn as skill grows, would be the repair to test beside it.

## Note of 2026-11-22: a removal arm in a scroll study, from the exchange "Explain First Contact RL"

A8 §3B of that exchange applies this question to an adaptive scroll
interface: a high score "measures the human-machine system *with the
assistance present*" and "cannot necessarily tell us which participant
supplies the capability", so a study should measure performance with
adaptation on and after temporarily restoring standard scrolling. That
arm is in [LEAD-tmp4eo5e](../leads.d/LEAD-tmp4eo5e.md), on the design of [CASE-tmptzhse](../cases.d/CASE-tmptzhse.md). It is a cheaper
instance of the study this question names, not a different one, and scroll
skill may decay less than landing skill, so a null there would answer less.
