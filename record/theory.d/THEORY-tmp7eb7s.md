# THEORY-tmp7eb7s: When people can choose their cooperation partners and have equal outside options, the stable division of jointly produced gains is in proportion to each partner's contribution, and partner choice selects for a genuine sense of fairness as proportionality

## Source

Baumard, André & Sperber (2013), [LIT-508](../literature.d/LIT-508.md) (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences*
36(1)), read in [NOTE-407](../notes.d/NOTE-407.md), with the commentaries and the authors' Response.

## What was actually shown

**The argument (sect. 2.1.4).** With freely chosen partners, nobody accepts
less than they could get elsewhere, so biased splits are not evolutionarily
stable. With equal outside options, payoff should be proportional to the
resources each invests. The pairwise result is cited from André & Baumard
(2011a). For effort and talent, "a formal proof will require further
modeling" (p. 67).

**Genuine fairness (sect. 2.2.1).** Being chosen rewards a reliable,
intrinsic sense of fairness over calculated fairness. This is a verbal
argument.

**Games, reread (sect. 3).** When dictators earned the money, 79% and 70% gave
nothing, against 19% and 15% when they had not (Cherry et al. 2002). When
recipients earned it, modal offers matched what they had earned (Oxoby &
Spraggon 2008). Modal dictator allocations matched contribution in 30 of 73
cases (Frohlich et al. 2004). About 80% found it fair to reward effort and
70% to reward talent (Cappelen et al. 2007). Framing a game as Maasai
*osotua*, need-based mutual help, lowered transfers (Cronk 2007).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Earned and unearned money could have
been divided alike. They were not.

## What this does not say

- **That the games test it.** None was designed to separate mutualistic
  fairness from altruism or other accounts, and the article says such
  experiments "can and should be devised" (p. 77). Kirkby, Hinzen & Mikhail
  note that proportionality to "rights" is close to a truism.
- **That everyone shares one sense of fairness.** In controlled games with
  shared information, people split into egalitarians, meritocrats and
  libertarians (Cappelen & Tungodden's commentary).
- **That fairness is all of morality.** The authors give two positions in the
  Response (R4) and do not settle them. On the broad one, kin care,
  coalitions, hierarchy and purity are moralized where fairness regulates
  them. On the narrow one, Sperber's, they rest on other dispositions and are
  not moral proper.
- **That the theory can fail on purity or loyalty.** If what does not fit is
  not moral, it cannot (Machery & Stich; Ramlakhan & Brook).
