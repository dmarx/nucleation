# LIT-483: Groups as Agents

Deborah Perron Tollefsen (2015), *Polity, Key Concepts in Philosophy (hardback ISBN 9780745684833; paperback ISBN 9780745684840; 184 pp.)* — https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Groups+as+Agents-p-9780745684840

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the works filling out
the record's coverage of social ontology under the topic [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added. List's
paper ([NOTE-340](../notes.d/NOTE-340.md)) named it among the group-agency works the record did not hold.

`Deferred` because no lawful full text was reachable, so it is not read and no
NOTE is filed. What follows is only what the publisher says.

**What the publisher says.** "We say for example that 'Google intends to
develop an automated car', 'the U.S. Government believes that Syria has used
chemical weapons on its people', or that 'the NRA wants to protect the rights
of gun owners'. … But could groups literally intend things? Is there such a
thing as a collective mind? If so, should groups be held morally responsible?"
The book "offers a careful survey of contemporary philosophers' answers to
these questions, and argues for the unorthodox view that certain groups should,
indeed, be treated as agents and deserve to be held morally accountable", and
"shows the reader how a belief in group agency can be reconciled with our
understanding of individual agency and accountability".

**Contents** (Wiley):

- Preface and Acknowledgements
- Introduction
- 1 Group Belief
- 2 Group Intention
- 3 Group Agency
- 4 Group Cognition
- 5 Interpreting Groups
- 6 The Moral Responsibility of Groups
- Conclusion; Notes; References; Index

**Where it would sit, from what was seen.** It is on the same side as List &
Pettit ([LIT-391](LIT-391.md)) and List ([LIT-401](LIT-401.md)): groups can be agents. Its examples (a
company, a government, an advocacy organisation) are all organised groups in
Ritchie's sense ([LIT-486](LIT-486.md)). Its chapter titles cover what [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) calls
the uncontested half, agency, and add "Group Cognition", where Huebner
([LIT-426](LIT-426.md)) is the held neighbour. A chapter called "Interpreting Groups"
suggests a view of group agency through interpretation, which would bear on
Dennett's intentional stance ([LIT-440](LIT-440.md), [LIT-398](LIT-398.md)); that is a guess from a
chapter title and is recorded only as something to check. Whether the book
addresses group phenomenal consciousness, [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s actual question, is
unknown. Nothing read lets this record say more about what Tollefsen's view
implies for [LIT-391](LIT-391.md), [LIT-401](LIT-401.md), [LIT-159](LIT-159.md) or [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md).

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary: whether groups can be agents or
minds, in the topic's own words. `agency` holds chapters 2–3, `cognition` holds
chapters 1 and 4 (group belief and cognition), and `ethics` holds chapter 6.

No instruction for machine-learning practice appears in what was seen, so
nothing here points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.**

- Which groups count as agents, and by what criterion: functionalist like List
  & Pettit's, interpretivist, or another.
- What chapter 4 says about collective minds, and whether it touches
  experience as well as cognition.
- Whether chapter 6's case for group responsibility depends on group agency
  alone, as List & Pettit's does by List's account ([NOTE-340](../notes.d/NOTE-340.md)), or on more.
