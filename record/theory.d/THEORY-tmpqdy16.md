# THEORY-tmpqdy16: Kaur, Jain and Berman report that representing animal posture across many time resolutions at once lets a transfer operator recover slow metastable behavioral states, one linear arm per basin around a central hub in its slow eigenvectors, which in flies persist for minutes against leg movements at tens of hertz and show heavy-tailed residences; where the gap between fast and slow rates is wide, a single-resolution operator finds no slow modes at all

## Source

Kaur, Jain and Berman (2026), "Using timescale as a state coordinate
reveals the metastable geometry of behavior", arXiv 2605.24135, [LIT-2257](../literature.d/LIT-2257.md),
skimmed in [NOTE-tmpj6fck](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpj6fck.md).

## The account

Wavelet amplitudes of joint angles across dyadic frequency bands are
delay-embedded and clustered; a transition matrix at a chosen lag is the
transfer operator. Its leading non-trivial eigenvectors, read with the
spectral theory of metastability and its non-reversible extension, place
clusters on linear arms from a hub, one per basin. In flies this gives four
basins that shift over minutes and retain predictive information past
100 s; in worms, two (run and pirouette). With a wide fast–slow gap a
single-resolution operator's slow eigenvectors localize on a few clusters
and show no collective structure.

## Bearing

[QUESTION-281](../questions.d/QUESTION-281.md): measured behavioral states as metastable basins with
heavy-tailed residences, a case of distinctions that persist and give way;
the transitions themselves are not identified.
