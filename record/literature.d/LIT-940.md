# LIT-940: Optimal scaling laws in learning hierarchical multi-index models

Leonardo Defilippis, Florent Krzakala, Bruno Loureiro and Antoine Maillard (2026), *arXiv:2602.05846*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-805](../notes.d/NOTE-805.md).*

- **Thresholds set by the target's weights.** With x ~ N(0, I/d),
  n = αd, and f⋆ = Σ_k a_k g_k(⟨w_k, x⟩), take orthogonal w_k, even links
  of generative exponent two, and a_k ~ k^(−γ). Then the Bayes estimator
  first correlates with w_k at α_k = Θ(a_k^(−2)) = Θ(k^(2γ)) for γ > 1/2
  (Thm. 3.2).
- **Optimal rates.**
  - For γ > 1/2, the weighted subspace error is Θ(α^(−1+1/(2γ))) while
    α ≪ m⋆^(2γ), and Θ(m⋆/α) after.
  - For γ < 1/2, it is Θ(1), then Θ(m⋆/α).
  - The error splits into the error on the k_α ≍ α^(1/(2γ)) directions
    already out, plus the weight of the rest.
- **A spectral method attains them.** The top eigenvectors of
  Σ T(y_i)x_ix_iᵀ, for any bounded preprocessing T and cut at an
  eigengap, match both the rates and the thresholds (Thm. 3.6). Each
  spike correlates with one direction, because the even, separable links
  make E[zzᵀ − I | y] diagonal.
- **A network attains them in excess risk.** A two-layer net whose first
  layer is that estimator times a random matrix, with a ridge readout fit
  on a second half of the data, reaches Θ(MMSE) + O(n^(−1/2)) for
  polynomial links (Thm. 3.8).
- **The cascade is in samples.** No training trajectory is analysed.
  Early gradient descent on the first layer from a small sphere is a
  power iteration on that matrix only by Remark 3.5, which cites
  Bonnaire et al. and Zhang et al. (2025).
