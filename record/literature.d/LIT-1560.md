# LIT-1560: Protein import into bacterial endosymbionts and evolving organelles

Sørensen, M. E. S., Stiller, M. L., Kröninger, L. & Nowack, E. C. M. (2025). Protein import into bacterial endosymbionts and evolving organelles. *The FEBS Journal* 292(12): 2992–3013. doi:10.1111/febs.17356.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1241](../notes.d/NOTE-1241.md).*

- **Import at scale in two systems.** Proteomics of isolated
  endosymbionts found hundreds of host proteins in *Paulinella*'s
  chromatophore and more than 350 in the nitroplast, filling gaps left by
  lost genes.
- **No gene transfer needed.** The imported proteins come "mostly from
  the host itself" in *Paulinella*, and no EGT at all was found in the
  nitroplast's host: "EGT is not a precondition for the genetic
  integration of an endosymbiont" ([THEORY-1100](../theory.d/THEORY-1100.md)).
- **Single proteins elsewhere.** Six or seven targeted proteins in a
  diatom and a trypanosomatid, and one or two each in the aphid's
  *Buchnera*, two weevils' symbionts and the mealybug's *Moranella*,
  mostly found with antibodies. The mealybug protein is in *Moranella*,
  not *Tremblaya*.
- **A proposed order.** Effectors first, then dependence on one
  redundant imported protein, then a general import pathway. Rhizobia,
  targeted by hundreds of host peptides but not vertically transmitted,
  are the authors' case of control without integration ([THEORY-1107](../theory.d/THEORY-1107.md)).
- **Caveats.** It is a review. The scenario is called plausible on
  limited data, and the import mechanisms are almost entirely unknown.

## Why this is here

A1 ranked it third, for the mechanisms of host–endosymbiont integration and for why protein import complicates a sharp symbiont/organelle boundary. It bears on [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)'s regulatory-integration dimension.
