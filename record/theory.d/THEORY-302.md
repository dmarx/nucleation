# THEORY-302: Coordinated action is controlled by tuning relatively autonomous coordinative structures, not by commanding muscles: a higher level specifies an equivalence class of acceptable states and sets the parameters of self-regulating units, leaving the realized details to them

## Source

Turvey (1977), "Preliminaries to a theory of action with reference to
vision", [LIT-904](../literature.d/LIT-904.md), read in [NOTE-826](../notes.d/NOTE-826.md) in its preprint form, Haskins
Status Report SR-41 (1975), pp. 1–49: the argument against the keyboard
executive (pp. 5–6), coordinative structures (pp. 7–9), executive
ignorance (pp. 14–15), tuning and pretuning (pp. 18–30), and vision and
tuning (pp. 32–38).

## What was actually shown

**The argument.** Innervation and movement are equivocally related: for a
limb segment, I·d²α/dt² = F(E, α, dα/dt) + G(α), so the same impulses give
different movements in different states and loads. The controlling system
has far fewer degrees of freedom than the controlled one. So the executive
cannot command muscles. It sets "the modes of interaction" of lower units,
"reflexes and functional combinations of reflexes", which are "relatively
autonomous". What it specifies is not a state but an equivalence class,
a "ballpark" from which the lower level brings the state in by itself.

**The evidence at the spinal level** (reviewed, not new).

- Tendon and H-reflexes probed between command and movement are enhanced,
  specifically in the muscles about to act and nonspecifically elsewhere,
  beginning about 60 msec before agonist activity (Gurfinkel et al.;
  Gottlieb et al.; Kots).
- Reciprocal inhibition of the antagonist is enhanced in the last 30 msec
  without any change in the antagonist's own excitability (Kots and
  Zhukov).
- The reorganization goes from diffuse to localized as movement nears.
- A joint behaves like a spring, F = −S₀(l − λ₀): its equilibrium is set by
  parameters fixed beforehand and is independent of initial conditions,
  even when loads are removed unpredictably before the movement (Asatryan
  and Fel'dman; Fel'dman).
- In one data set the reflex changes do not track the EMG, so tuning and
  activation appear to be separately controlled (Gottlieb et al.).

What could have come out otherwise: the probes could have shown no change
before the first EMG sign of movement, which would have made the
pretuning only the start of activation. They did not.

## What this does not say

- **Not shown above the spinal level.** That every coordinative structure,
  at every level, is a "biasable, self-regulating vibratory system" tuned
  the same way is extrapolated "on the assumption that the regulatory
  principles for large systems follow very much the pattern of small
  systems" (p. 30).
- **Not shown for environment-related acts.** Turvey says there were "no
  experiments on segmental pretuning for acts that depend on the
  detection of environmental properties" (p. 22). That optical properties
  set tunings "without the intervention of executive procedures" is
  argued from the cat's leap and the mantis's strike, not measured.
- **Not a model.** The equation m·x″ + k·x′ + s·x = 0 is "given only to
  illustrate a principle" (fn. 7).
- **Not the source of the term.** Turvey borrows "coordinative
  structure" from Easton (1972a) and widens it. Nor is reciprocal
  compensation, later taken as its signature ([LIT-902](../literature.d/LIT-902.md)), part of this
  account.
- **Not about more than one body.** The units are reflexes and their
  combinations within one organism.
