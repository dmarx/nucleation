# LEAD-253: Read originalism's, unitary executive theory's and conservatism's defenders in their own statements, to check the strawman marks on the exchange's three critiques and the three objections filed against them

## The idea

Read, for each objection, the statements it attributes:

- **[CLAIM-1301](../claims.d/CLAIM-1301.md) (originalism).** Solum on fixation, constraint and
  construction; Baude and Sachs ([LIT-3201](../literature.d/LIT-3201.md)); Balkin ([LIT-3249](../literature.d/LIT-3249.md));
  Scalia, "Originalism: The Lesser Evil" (1989); McGinnis and Rappaport on
  supermajoritarian legitimacy.
- **[CLAIM-1313](../claims.d/CLAIM-1313.md) (unitary executive).** Calabresi and Prakash, "The
  President's Power to Execute the Laws" (1994); Calabresi and Yoo, The
  Unitary Executive (2008); Federalist No. 70 ([LIT-3203](../literature.d/LIT-3203.md)), read in full.
- **[CLAIM-1266](../claims.d/CLAIM-1266.md) (conservatism).** Burke's Reflections ([LIT-3255](../literature.d/LIT-3255.md));
  Oakeshott, "On Being Conservative"; Hayek, "Why I Am Not a Conservative".

Only those already registered were cited by the exchange; the rest would be
registered when the reading starts.

## What it would settle

Whether each critique's target is held by the theory's defenders (reviving
the critique, against [ARG-258](../arguments.d/ARG-258.md) or [ARG-259](../arguments.d/ARG-259.md)) or is a strawman (as
the objections mark it); and whether the objections state the defenders
fairly. A second reading, of popular and political statements of the three,
would settle whether those make the conflations the theorists do not.
