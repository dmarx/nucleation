# NOTE-tmpjem53: Schuhr — An experiment on reward bundling

## Contribution

It isolates bundling from precommitment and from stake size in humans
with real payouts and structural estimation, by comparing a free series
of decisions with a forced one and with single decisions at matched
delays and magnitudes.

## Key insight

Presenting a choice as one of a series made patience more likely even
when nothing tied the choices together, and forcing them together did
not help. On the thesis's reading, bundling is something the chooser
does, not something the design imposes.

## Assumptions

- **Two latent processes.** Choices are a mixture of an exponential
  (patient) and a hyperbolic (impulsive) discounter, and bundling shows as
  more weight on the first (pp. 33-37).
- **Simultaneous series stand for future choices.** "The theory of reward
  bundling is concerned with behavior that involves decisions about the
  future. This does not necessarily mean that those decisions have to be
  made in the future" (p. 35). The sentence is split across a line break
  in the converted text and was checked there.
- **No deception.** The thesis rejects Kirby and Guastello's suggested
  condition because it "reports to subjects as a fact something that is
  not established, and indeed likely is not a fact" (p. 3, footnote 1).

## Key results

- **Design (ch. 7, Table 1).** Georgia State University undergraduates,
  August-September 2013, one session per treatment: T0 baseline (n = 35),
  T1 one-day front-end delay (29), T2 tripled amounts (36), T3 35-day
  front-end delay (35), T4 two free decisions per row (37), T5 two forced
  (33), T6 three free (31), T7 three forced (30). Multiple price lists,
  ten screens of seven rows, principals $10-$300, horizon 28 days, one
  row paid by die roll, high amounts paid with probability 1/10, PayPal.
- **Free and forced (pp. 35-36, ch. 7).** In T4 and T6 "nothing
  restricted choice behavior across the two decisions"; in T5 and T7
  choosing in one decision "automatically activated the choice of the
  corresponding decision in the same row".
- **First choices (ch. 8).** "the fraction of LL choices is indeed higher
  among the first choices in bundling treatments than the comparable
  choices in treatment T0." The descriptive probit gives an average
  marginal effect of bundling from about 4 points at a $10 principal to
  12 points at $300 (p. 95).
- **Mixture model (Table 24, pp. 128-129).** BUNDLED raises the
  probability of exponential discounting by 0.307 (p = 0.019); FORCED
  lowers it by 0.330 (p < 0.001); SCALE is not significant; SECOND and
  THIRD are negative, so the effect is in the first choices.
- **Reading of FORCED (p. 130).** "Reward bundling in human subjects is a
  psychological process prompted by the presentation of each decision as
  part of a series of comparable decisions."
- **Conclusion (p. 132).** "The results offer strong support for the
  reward bundling hypothesis."

## Concepts

- **Free versus forced bundling treatment**: a series of decisions with
  no link imposed, against one whose decisions must match.
- **Precedent effect**: Kirby and Guastello's name, as the thesis gives
  it, for treating a present choice as a test case for later ones.
- **Mixture probability of exponential discounting**: the share of
  choices the model assigns to the patient process.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-067.** Corroborates the bundling half of the account in humans
  with real money: a series of choices, not imposed as one, raises the
  weight on patient choice. It does not meet `promote_when` (1): the
  decisions in a row are made at the same moment, so nothing is predicted
  about a later chooser; and the choice set changes (two or three pairs,
  all paid, against one). It is evidence for bundling, not for bundling by
  self-prediction.
- **Imposed bundles.** That forcing lowered patient choice cuts against
  reading the imposed-bundle studies (Rung and Madden, NOTE-1440) as the
  mechanism's best evidence.
- **Khan and Dhar (NOTE-tmpq33n0).** The contrast is in when the later
  choice is made. Here it is made now, beside the first, and patience
  rises; there it is left to a later self, and optimism lowers it. A
  dated note on THEORY-067 sets the two side by side.
- **CLAIM-169.** Indirect: more support for bundling, the mechanism the
  claim waits on, but not for self-prediction.

## Limitations

- Treatments are sessions, one each, on different days; assignment is by
  session, not by person.
- Each treatment has about 30 subjects; the free and forced effects rest
  on the pooled bundling treatments and the mixture model's assumptions.
- The thesis says "no attempt has been made" to test bundling in humans
  by experimental economics' standards and does not cite Hofmeyr et al.
  (LIT-tmpqbo3w), a UCT study by Ross's group with Ainslie, which tested
  suggested and forced bundling with real money.
- Why forcing lowers patient choice is not shown; the thesis reads it as
  confirming that the bundling is the chooser's, which is one reading.
