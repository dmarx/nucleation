# ARG-tmpu5yj6: Transtrum and colleagues' review supports the compression half of the claim that a model has no single practical dimension, through its hopping model seen at long times, and is silent on the half that has resolved directions grow with the data

## The inference

CLAIM-tmpj0k4q: the number of directions a data set resolves grows with
the data, and zooming out and demanding precision move it in opposite
ways. The review (THEORY-tmpd7a4j) reports, after Machta et al. (LIT-1299),
that the hopping model's manifold is a "hyper-blob" after one step and "as
time proceeds, the model manifold contracts onto a hyper-ribbon", with the
continuum parameters stiff and "all other microscopic parameter
combinations ... increasingly sloppy". That is the compression half: fewer
directions resolved under coarser observation.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** The
  compression result is THEORY-774's, which the claim already grounds on;
  the review restates it and adds the Ising case (THEORY-777). It is
  expected whether or not more data resolve more directions, so it bears
  on one half only.
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its rivals?** The rival is a fixed
  effective dimension. The compression half excludes it under changes of
  observation scale; the review says nothing of changes in sample size.
  Its nearest remark is the EGFR figure's cut at "eigenvalues less than" 1, a
  resolvability threshold at one data set, of the nλ ≳ 1 kind the claim
  uses, without the dependence on n.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** The review counts stiff
  directions by eigenvalue in log parameters, or by widths; the claim
  counts by nλ_j ≥ 1 at a fixed eigenbasis. These agree where the
  eigenvalues approximate the widths (dimensionless parameters).

## Verdict

Supports the compression half; silent on the growth half, which rests on
the claim's own derivation and check. The review was cited as ground for
the setting; it carries less than a ground, and corroborates. Proposed.
