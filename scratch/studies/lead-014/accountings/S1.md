## Ideas of the source (ledger)

- **C1.** Definitions: parameter-space symmetry; data-dependent vs
  data-independent; loss vs network symmetry (§2, Fig. 5). **NOTE.**
- **C2.** Identifiability via the realization map: fibers, transitivity,
  completeness of a symmetry group; ReLU networks have hidden symmetries
  and non-constant functional dimension within a fiber. **NOTE;**
  bears on THEORY-018.
- **C3.** Continuous symmetries make minima positive-dimensional
  manifolds; discrete (permutation) symmetries multiply minima
  factorially. **THEORY** (with C4).
- **C4.** Quotienting out symmetry gives a more compact landscape: more
  linear mode connectivity, more monotone loss along paths, easier
  sampling. **THEORY** (Proposed), with G4.
- **C5.** Points on one orbit have different gradients, so dynamics from
  them differ; teleportation and symmetry-invariant optimizers exploit
  this. **NOTE** (method detail left to the anthology).
- **C6.** Conserved quantities of gradient flow from continuous symmetry
  (imbalance, norm differences, angular-momentum analogue); they label
  trajectories and parameterize minima, formalising one kind of implicit
  bias; SGD with noise or weight decay biases toward balanced solutions.
  **THEORY** (Proposed), the reading's main content for the record.
- **C7.** Open question: whether all conserved quantities come from known
  symmetries. **NOTE**; candidate QUESTION only if the record's argument
  needs it (it bears on CLAIM-093).
- **C8.** Symmetry confounds sharpness; symmetry-aware (quotient)
  sharpness restores its correlation with generalization (da Silva et al.
  2025). **NOTE.**
- **C9.** Counting only functionally distinct parameters tightens
  covering-number bounds by factorial factors in width (Shen 2024).
  **NOTE.**
- **C10.** Which symmetries to keep and which to break is open; breaking
  permutation symmetry can enable linear mode connectivity (Lim et al.)
  and prevent capacity collapse (Ziyin et al. 2025). **NOTE.**
- **C11.** Symmetry in data preserved in parameters under GD with
  symmetric initialization (R1). **NOTE**, in R1's dated note.

## Gaps in the source's own argument

- **G1.** "Similarly, in neural network training, parameter space symmetry
  implies the existence of conserved quantities" is stated as a Noether
  analogue without the conditions: gradient flow (no finite step, noise,
  momentum or weight decay) and a continuous group. §7 concedes SGD
  breaks them, but §5's opening does not.
- **G2.** Fig. 8a's "matching ∇Q to a symmetry direction": both are
  orthogonal to ∇L, but orthogonality to one vector does not make them
  match; the correspondence needs the specific construction of Zhao et
  al. 2023 and is stated loosely.
- **G3.** Flat directions from symmetry confound sharpness only for
  continuous symmetries; permutations create no flat direction. The text
  speaks of "symmetries" without that split.
- **G4.** Two claims pull against each other without being reconciled:
  continuous symmetry "explains" mode connectivity (connectedness of
  minima in parameter space), and removing symmetry "creates more
  connected minima". They are about different spaces (orbits in Θ versus
  the quotient). And orbits only join functionally equivalent
  parameters, while independently trained networks are generally not
  functionally equivalent, so symmetry alone cannot explain their
  connection.
- **G5.** "Conserved quantities formalise implicit bias" holds for the
  part of the minimum they parameterize; which point of that manifold is
  reached still depends on initialization, so the survey's claim that
  they "reveal how initialization and symmetry constrain the final
  solution" is a restatement, not a mechanism of generalization.
- **G6.** A survey: no result of its own is derived; every substantive
  claim is unverified until its cited source is read (the NOTE should say
  so per claim).
