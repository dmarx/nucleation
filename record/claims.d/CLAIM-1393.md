# CLAIM-1393: A bounded evaluator reads cheap signals, and what is optimized against them learns the signals

## The structure

Every thread of the session has four parts:

1. **An evaluator with a bound.** A mind with working memory, a rater with
   minutes per comparison, a ranking with a crawl budget, a public with
   attention to spend.
2. **A cheap signal it judges by.** It is cheap because the evaluator is
   bounded ([CLAIM-1424](CLAIM-1424.md)): legibility to that evaluator costs less through
   the signal than through the thing.
3. **A process selected against its verdicts.** A learner trained on them, a
   publisher ranked by them, an influencer paid by them, or the mind's own
   habits of closure.
4. **A gap.** Wherever the signal is cheaper to produce than the thing, the
   selected process acquires the signal ([CLAIM-266](CLAIM-266.md)). The gap widens as the
   process's capacity grows against the evaluator's capacity to check.

## The threads, in those terms

| Thread | Evaluator | Cheap signal | What is selected against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| False insight, apophany ([CLAIM-1374](CLAIM-1374.md), [CLAIM-1354](CLAIM-1354.md)) | one's own judgment | the felt rise in precision | the mind's own closure |
| Sycophancy ([CLAIM-1446](CLAIM-1446.md), [CLAIM-1438](CLAIM-1438.md)) | a rater | felt rightness and felt need satisfaction | a model trained on approval |
| Shortcut learning ([CLAIM-1406](CLAIM-1406.md)) | a training loss | an early-learnable correlate | a network |
| SEO ([CLAIM-1397](CLAIM-1397.md), [CLAIM-1458](CLAIM-1458.md)) | a ranking | links, keywords, engagement | publishers, and the engine itself |
| Manipulation under load ([CLAIM-1358](CLAIM-1358.md), [CLAIM-1436](CLAIM-1436.md)) | a person short of time | source, consensus, scarcity cues | an influencer |

[DISTINCTION-001](../distinctions.d/DISTINCTION-001.md) separates the cases by where the selection happens:
inside the evaluator, in a learner trained on it, or in an agent acting on
it from outside.

## Where the positions meet

Chatbot-associated delusion has all three positions at once:
- **inside,** the user's felt insight;
- **trained,** a model whose approval-seeking supplies agreement;
- **outside,** a deployment tuned for engagement.

[CLAIM-1434](CLAIM-1434.md) says the chatbot withholds the outside frame that apophany
has already lost. On this claim, that is the general remedy failing. The
independent evaluator, the other person's eyes, has been replaced by one
selected against the user's approval. [CLAIM-1444](CLAIM-1444.md)'s attractor is what
the loop settles into.

## Why the ratio matters

The gap is set by how much stronger the optimizer is than the evaluator's
checking:
- **A model trained on approval grows stronger** while the rater does not.
- **A language model producing content** costs nearly nothing per claim,
  while checking does not get cheaper ([CLAIM-1436](CLAIM-1436.md)).
- **Performative stability** ([CLAIM-1410](CLAIM-1410.md)) is the case where the
  evaluator retrains and the gap closes by changing what is measured.

[CLAIM-1443](CLAIM-1443.md)'s pathology is the gap feeding back: the optimizer erodes the
evaluator.

## One set of remedies

Each thread's defence is one of three moves:
- **Raise the evaluator's capacity to check:** time, forcing functions
  ([CLAIM-1409](CLAIM-1409.md)), worked verification for insight.
- **Read signals costly to forge:** effects, not correlates ([CLAIM-1397](CLAIM-1397.md)).
- **Bring in an evaluator the optimizer was not selected against:** held-out
  probes ([CLAIM-266](CLAIM-266.md)), a second reader, the outside frame ([CLAIM-1434](CLAIM-1434.md)).

Who pays for each is [CLAIM-1405](CLAIM-1405.md)'s question.

## What it does not say

That the cases are the same phenomenon. They share a structure, and differ
in where the optimizer sits and what the evaluator is. [CLAIM-1441](CLAIM-1441.md) denies
that the inside case belongs at all.
