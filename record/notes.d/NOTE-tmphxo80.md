# NOTE-tmphxo80: Wamsley — Uber Not Criminally Liable In Death Of Woman Hit By Self-Driving Car, Prosecutor Says

## Contribution

The prosecutor's decision on the company, in the prosecutor's words as
quoted, and what remained open after it.

## Key results

- **The declination.** "After a very thorough review of all the evidence
  presented, this Office has determined that there is no basis for
  criminal liability for the Uber corporation arising from this matter,"
  the Yavapai County Attorney's Office wrote to the Maricopa County
  Attorney's Office. "Tempe is in Maricopa County, but Yavapai County took
  the case due to a potential conflict of interest."
- **The operator.** The letter "recommends expert analysis of the
  collision video that would show what the driver "would or should have
  seen that night given the vehicle's speed, lighting conditions, and other
  relevant factors.""
- **What stayed open.** "Uber ... could still be sued in civil court and
  be forced to pay damages. The government could also potentially pursue
  criminal charges against managers or employees of Uber."
- **Civil settlement.** "Herzberg's family reached a settlement with the
  company shortly after the crash."
- **A caution.** Bryant Walker Smith: "It's not necessarily exculpatory —
  it doesn't exonerate Uber or put the company's conduct then or now
  beyond criticism".

## Assumptions

- A report of a letter; the letter's reasoning is not given.

## Concepts

- Corporate criminal liability, as distinct from charges against an
  organization's managers or employees.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-751](../claims.d/CLAIM-751.md)** (bears on). With [LIT-tmpum8to](../literature.d/LIT-tmpum8to.md), it shows that the criminal
  process declined to reach the company. The later reports read mention
  no charge against anyone but the operator; whether managers were ever
  considered is not reported.
- **[CLAIM-tmp64hmn](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp64hmn.md)** (supplies the objection). The settlement with the
  family is a liability process that did reach the company, outside the
  criminal law. Its terms are not reported.

## Limitations

- The letter's reasons are not reported; "no basis for criminal
  liability" may reflect the legal elements of corporate liability in
  Arizona as much as any finding about the company's conduct, and the
  record cannot tell which.
