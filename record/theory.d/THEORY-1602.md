# THEORY-1602: Dias and Ressler report that male mice fear-conditioned to an odour before mating had sons and grandsons more sensitive to that odour, and not to another, with more olfactory neurons for its receptor, a change that held in offspring conceived by IVF, and they read what passes as sensitivity to the cue, not fear

## The account

Males conditioned with acetophenone or propanol paired with foot shock
before mating had sons, never exposed to either odour, who startled more
to the odour their father learned and not the other, a double
dissociation across two odours and two strains, and who detected it at
lower concentrations. Sons of acetophenone-trained males had more
olfactory neurons carrying its receptor (M71) and larger M71 glomeruli.
Grandsons showed the same behaviour and anatomy. Offspring conceived by
IVF and carried by surrogates showed the anatomy; their behaviour was not
tested. Mothers conditioned before mating gave the same results, which
survived cross-fostering. Olfr151 was less methylated in F0 and F1 sperm,
not in the olfactory epithelium.

The authors read startle "as an enhanced behavioral sensitivity to
acetophenone, not necessarily an increase in fear", and conclude that
generations "inherit information about the salience of specific stimuli in
ancestral environments". The mechanism is speculative: "we cannot and do
not claim to know what fraction" of sperm carry the information.

## Evidence

The paper ([NOTE-1281](../notes.d/NOTE-1281.md)): double-blind automated behaviour with groups of 9
to 16; blind anatomy, large and consistent across F1, F2, IVF and fostered
offspring; no general anxiety or fear-learning differences. The sperm
methylation difference is modest (P = 0.03 in F0, n = 12 against 10) and
rests on four against four in F1, with no causal test. Effect sizes are
not reported.

## What it bears on

- [QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md), the germline route: evidence for transmission of a
  cue-specific sensitivity, not of trauma or fear. What passes needs no
  representation of the parent, the event or a group, and cross-fostering
  shows rearing by a trained mother does not transfer it.
- [QUESTION-302](../questions.d/QUESTION-302.md) names the work where a learned aversion in insects
  would pass to offspring: on this paper's showing, what passed would be
  heightened sensitivity to the odour, not the learned avoidance. That is
  beside its question, which asks about the animal that learned, so it
  takes no edge.
