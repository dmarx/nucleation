# LIT-122: The Representation of Time and Change in Mechanics

Gordon Belot (2005), *Handbook of the Philosophy of Science: Philosophy of Physics, ed. J. Butterfield & J. Earman (Elsevier/North-Holland, 2007), pp. 133–227; preprint on PhilSci-Archive ([#2549](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/2549)) from December 2005* — DOI-10.1016/B978-044451560-5/50005-1

## Key takeaways

- The "problem of time" in general relativity is that, unlike in well-behaved Hamiltonian/Lagrangian theories, time is not represented by a flow on a symplectic space and change is not represented by functions on a space of states — not that GR shows change to be an illusion; privileging a "geometric time" is the natural but costly way around it.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The chapter examines how classical (non-quantum) physical theories represent time and change. Its main aim is to clarify the nature and scope of the "problem of time" — the knot of technical and interpretive difficulties rooted in general covariance that obstruct the quantisation of general relativity. It approaches GR by first working through better-understood cases, from Newtonian particle mechanics through field theories, gauge theories and time-dependent systems, each preparing a feature of GR responsible for the problem.
