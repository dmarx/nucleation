# ADR-034: pragmatics and myth-and-folklore join the topics

## Context

Registering the bibliography of What Survives Translation? brought in two
clusters that no word fitted.

- **Pragmatics.** Before this batch the record held:
  - Austin ([LIT-785](../literature.d/LIT-785.md));
  - Goffman ([LIT-780](../literature.d/LIT-780.md));
  - Du Bois ([LIT-779](../literature.d/LIT-779.md)).

  The batch added:
  - Searle, Stalnaker, Krifka, Heim and Veltman;
  - Brandom;
  - three rational-speech-act papers.

  These were filed under `linguistics`, whose blurb is "the study of
  language itself … as linguists pose them", and `philosophy-of-language`,
  which is scoped to philosophy. The RSA reader reported the gap.
- **Myth and folklore.** Lévi-Strauss ([LIT-775](../literature.d/LIT-775.md)) was already held, and the
  batch added Propp, Descola, and Doja, Capocchi and Santucci. All four were
  filed under `social-science`, whose blurb names anthropology but not myth
  or folktale. The structuralism reader reported the gap.

The owner authorised extending the vocabulary as the record sees fit on
2026-10-09 ([ADR-031](ADR-031.md)).

## Decision

Add two topics.

- **`pragmatics`.** It covers:
  - speech acts and illocutionary force;
  - assertion and common ground;
  - presupposition;
  - dynamic and update semantics;
  - footing and stance in discourse;
  - probabilistic models of speakers and listeners.

  It comes first on works whose subject is language in use: the RSA papers,
  Krifka, Searle, Stalnaker, Austin, Goffman and Du Bois. It comes second
  on Heim, Veltman and Brandom, whose primary subject is semantics or
  philosophy.
- **`myth-and-folklore`.** It covers myths and folktales and their variants
  as objects of study, whether analysed structurally or computationally. It
  comes first on Propp, Lévi-Strauss, Descola and Doja et al.

The THEORYs filed from these readings take the same words.

## Alternatives

- **No new words.** The closed list exists to stop works being filed under
  the nearest wrong word. Two readers reported independently that this was
  happening.
- **`narrative`.** Considered for myth. It invites confusion with
  `narrative-unity`, which is about selves. The works here study myths and
  folktales, not narrative as such.
- **`semantics`.** Heim and Veltman are as much semantics as pragmatics.
  But the reason the record holds them is what an utterance does to its
  context, which is the pragmatics question, and `linguistics` already
  covers semantics as linguists pose it.
