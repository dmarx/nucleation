# THEORY-tmp800bi: Spectators' identification with football clubs varies on two independent axes, how central the club is to the self (hot or cool) and whether the tie rests on local tradition or on market consumption, giving supporters, followers, fans and flâneurs; and commodification since the late 1980s has shifted identification from supporters towards flâneurs

## Source

- Giulianotti (2002), [LIT-1150](../literature.d/LIT-1150.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmp0bj7o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0bj7o.md) from the
  Author's Original Text in Loughborough's repository.

## What the account says

Two questions about a spectator's tie to a club are independent: how much
the club matters to who they are, and whether the tie comes from place
and inheritance or through the market. The four combinations are
distinct ways of being a spectator, with different motives (obligation,
participation, mediated intimacy, sensation) and different relations to
the ground (home, stage, distance, non-place). Since the late 1980s,
commercial pressure has moved spectators along the diagonal from
supporter to flâneur.

## What was actually shown

Nothing empirical. The types are argued from the sociology of Taylor,
Critcher and Bryan Turner and illustrated with British and European
examples. The trend is inferred from the political economy (pay-TV,
club flotation, the G14) rather than from data on spectators.

## What this does not say

- **Not that any spectator is a pure type.** They are ideal types, with
  thicker and thinner variants within each.
- **Not that flâneurs are illegitimate.** The paper's normative leaning
  is towards supporters, but its argument is that the two types depend on
  each other.
- **Not that member-owned clubs** (in Germany, Spain or Latin America)
  follow the same trend; they are named as partial exceptions.

## Bearing

- **[QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md) and [CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md).** If a fanbase is a mixture of these types,
  a public stance credited to "the fans" is likely voiced by the hot types
  and silent among the cool. Composition by type is then one source of
  the gap between public stance and private aggregate, before any
  sampling bias.
- **[CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md).** The supporter's topophilic tie to the ground predicts
  that supporters' continuity follows place, not franchise, when a club
  relocates.
