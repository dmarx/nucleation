# CASE-138: Challenger to Columbia: NASA corrected its launch decisions and its safety organization after 1986, and the Columbia board found in 2003 that related organizational failure mechanisms had recurred, while its lessons-learned system was little used in between

## The case

**Before.** O-ring erosion on the solid rocket boosters' field joints had
been seen on earlier flights and accepted flight by flight. On the eve of
the launch of 28 January 1986, Morton Thiokol's engineers recommended
against launching in the cold; Thiokol's management reversed the
recommendation after NASA pushed back. Challenger broke apart 73 seconds
after launch. The Rogers Commission ([LIT-1393](../literature.d/LIT-1393.md)) traced the failure to
that history (chapter 6, "An Accident Rooted in History") and called the
safety program "silent" (chapter 7).

**After.** NASA created a headquarters safety, reliability and quality
assurance office in 1986 and a NASA Safety Reporting System in 1987. A25
called the first a "constitutional correction", a change in who can
object, beside the technical correction of the joint. In 2002 GAO
([LIT-1378](../literature.d/LIT-1378.md)) found NASA's lessons-learned system little used by its
managers and no assurance that lessons were applied.

**Columbia.** Columbia broke up on re-entry on 1 February 2003. The
Columbia Accident Investigation Board found that Challenger's
organizational causes had not been fixed. In the record's reading of its
chapter: evidence that the design was not performing as expected was
reinterpreted as acceptable, each survived anomaly justified the next
flight, and the safety organization depended on the program it oversaw.
The record holds this as [THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md) (Proposed), from the board's chapter
([LIT-734](../literature.d/LIT-734.md)). Diane Vaughan's account, which named the normalization of
deviance, is [LIT-715](../literature.d/LIT-715.md) (Unavailable).

## What it shows

- **A warning's standing changes as it moves.** A25: "The warning didn't
  simply fail to enter the institution. Its evidential and practical
  standing changed as it passed through the institution." That is
  [CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md)'s separation of admission, recognition, credibility, uptake
  and a part in revising the standards, in one night's teleconference.
- **A correction recorded is not a capacity maintained.** The rules and
  the office changed after 1986; the capacity they were for had eroded by
  2003. A25: "Institutional learning is not complete when an organization
  changes its rules." This is [CLAIM-422](../claims.d/CLAIM-422.md)'s case.
- **Success is evidence relative to the regime that scored it.** Each
  survived flight was read as evidence the joint was acceptable. A37:
  "outcomes evaluated under particular conditions can be overgeneralized
  into claims about the competence of the organization itself"
  ([CLAIM-416](../claims.d/CLAIM-416.md)).

## What it does not show

- One unchanged cause. The board's finding, as [THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md) reads it, is a
  recurrence of related organizational failure mechanisms over twenty
  years, not a single mechanism that sat unaltered between the two
  accidents. (Title narrowed the same day after a review the owner
  relayed.)
- Why the correction decayed. [THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md) gives the board's answer; this
  case does not test it.
- The exchange's details that were not checked. Its "2001 GAO review" and
  "January 2002 review" are taken to be one report, GAO-02-195. Its name
  for the 1986 office differs slightly from the office's name at the time.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the two reports, read

- **Where the launch decision is.** The teleconference and the decision to
  launch are in the Rogers report's chapter V, "The Contributing Cause of
  The Accident"; chapter VI is the history and chapter VII "The Silent
  Safety Program", whose body says the safety staff "was never mentioned"
  ([LIT-1393](../literature.d/LIT-1393.md), p. 152).
- **The 1986 office and the 1987 reporting system are not in Rogers.** The
  report only recommends "an Office of Safety, Reliability and Quality
  Assurance to be headed by an Associate administrator, reporting
  directly to the NASA Administrator" (p. 199). That NASA created it, and
  the Safety Reporting System, need another source.
- **GAO-02-195 is not about Challenger.** It was prompted by the 1999 Mars
  failures and never discusses Challenger or the post-1986 reforms. What
  it supports is narrower than "little used": NASA "monitors the system to
  determine the number of times it is accessed ... but does not collect
  information on the number of times lessons are applied to a program or
  project" ([LIT-1378](../literature.d/LIT-1378.md), p. 21). The exchange's "2001 GAO review" is probably the
  separate interim report GAO-01-1015R, which the 2002 report cites.
- **The normalization of deviance, first hand.** [THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md), held from the
  Columbia board, has its Challenger half in the Rogers report: NASA and
  Thiokol "accepted escalating risk apparently because they 'got away with
  it last time.'" (p. 148), with safety offices "under the supervision of
  the very organizations and activities whose efforts they are to check"
  (p. 161). The report could be added as a second source of [THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md).
