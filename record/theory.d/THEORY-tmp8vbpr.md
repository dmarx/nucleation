# THEORY-tmp8vbpr: Morality is the set of solutions to recurrent problems of cooperation, so each of the seven cooperation problems game theory identifies yields a moral domain valued in every culture, and behaviour unrelated to cooperation is morally neutral

## Source

Curry (2016), [LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md), the founding chapter, read in full in [NOTE-408](../notes.d/NOTE-408.md).
Its two tests: Curry, Mullins & Whitehouse (2019), [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md), read in
[NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md); and Curry, Jones Chesters & Van Lissa (2019), [LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md), read in
[NOTE-412](../notes.d/NOTE-412.md).

## What was actually shown

**The account ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md)).** Morality is "a collection of biological and
cultural solutions to the problems of cooperation and conflict recurrent in
human social life" (pp. 29, 44). Nonzero-sum game theory gives seven such
problems: kin allocation (Hamilton), coordination (Lewis, Schelling,
Skyrms), exchange (Trivers, Axelrod), hawkish and dovish contest displays,
division (Nash, Skyrms) and possession (Gintis). Each gives a domain:
family, group loyalty, reciprocity, bravery, respect, fairness, property.
The chapter reports no data. Its evidence is quotations chosen to show each
solution praised.

**The predictions (pp. 39–40).** The seven are morally good, and their
failures bad, "in every human culture, at all times and in all places".
"Behaviour that has nothing to do with cooperation … will be regarded as
morally neutral", and there is no culture where morality "is about
something other than cooperation — say, aesthetics or nutrition". Sexual,
political and religious morality, food taboos included, will turn out to be
cooperative.

**The positive half, tested.** In the ethnographic record of 60 societies the
seven are described as good in 961 of 962 valenced mentions ([LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md);
[THEORY-tmpvm5mu](THEORY-tmpvm5mu.md)). Questionnaire items written for the seven form seven factors as
relevance ratings in the UK, the US and Turkey ([LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md), [LIT-513](../literature.d/LIT-513.md); [THEORY-tmpdn4s2](THEORY-tmpdn4s2.md)).

**The negative half, untested.** Neither test codes any non-cooperative
content. The only place the programme's data meet it is the MFQ's purity
items in [LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md), Studies 2 and 3a. Those items are rated as morally relevant
as loyalty and authority, which the authors say "appear[s] to contradict"
the theory (p. 27; [THEORY-tmps5brs](THEORY-tmps5brs.md)).

**What could have come out otherwise.** A society could have condemned one of
the seven, and non-cooperative content could have been rated morally
irrelevant. The first is barely detectable by the coding used. The second
did not happen.

## What this does not say

- **That morality is only about cooperation.** That is the claim, and the
  evidence for it so far is evidence for the positive half alone.
- **That there are exactly seven domains.** The count is the list of games
  known in 2016, and new games are expected to add domains (p. 40). A further
  cooperative domain does not count against the account; only a
  non-cooperative one does.
- **Why cooperation is moralized rather than merely liked.** The chapter
  identifies morality with the solutions, so it has no account of what makes
  a solution moral. Bloom's question in [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s comments stays open.
- **That the falsifier is live yet.** The page that names nutrition as a
  domain no morality is about also predicts that food taboos will turn out
  cooperative. Until a cooperative account of a given taboo is written and
  tested, that prediction cannot fail.
- **That the seven are a combinatorial system.** Moral Molecules
  ([LIT-509](../literature.d/LIT-509.md), [NOTE-399](../notes.d/NOTE-399.md)) fits a concept to each of 21 pairs by search for
  confirming instances, with no protocol, and says the theory does not
  predict which molecules exist. It is not evidence for this account.

## Connections

- **Rivals.** Moral Foundations Theory ([THEORY-tmphcyt1](THEORY-tmphcyt1.md)) keeps Sanctity as a
  foundation with a non-cooperative, pathogen-avoidance origin. The two
  lists differ in both directions: MAC has bravery and property, MFT has
  Sanctity and care for non-kin. Baumard, André & Sperber ([THEORY-tmp7eb7s](THEORY-tmp7eb7s.md)) start from
  cooperation too but count only fairness as moral proper, defining kin
  altruism and group loyalty out.
- **Rescues.** Fitouchi, André & Baumard ([THEORY-tmpbzrkk](THEORY-tmpbzrkk.md)) argue that puritanical norms
  are cooperative after all. That rescues "morality is about cooperation"
  for some purity content. It does not rescue "morality is these seven
  domains", since it reduces puritanism to harm and fairness. Rai & Fiske
  ([THEORY-tmpreimy](THEORY-tmpreimy.md)) put purity under Unity, a relational motive, which is another
  cooperative home for it.
- **The hawk–dove attribution.** The chapter credits hawk–dove contests to
  Maynard Smith & Price (1973). That paper ([LIT-505](../literature.d/LIT-505.md), [NOTE-404](../notes.d/NOTE-404.md)) has no
  Hawk–Dove game, so the display and deference domains need a later source.
  What it does show is filed as [THEORY-tmp8lv8d](THEORY-tmp8lv8d.md).
- **The games it borrows.** The kin, exchange and reciprocity domains rest on
  results the record files separately: Hamilton's inclusive fitness
  ([THEORY-tmp15rjw](THEORY-tmp15rjw.md)), Trivers' reciprocal altruism ([THEORY-tmpx3n05](THEORY-tmpx3n05.md)), the
  stability of TIT FOR TAT ([THEORY-tmpxorvn](THEORY-tmpxorvn.md)) and Nowak's five mechanisms
  ([THEORY-tmpkifk7](THEORY-tmpkifk7.md)). Each holds under its own conditions, which this account
  does not restate.
