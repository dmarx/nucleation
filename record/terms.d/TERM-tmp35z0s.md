# TERM-tmp35z0s: constitutional capability

## Definition (A4 §12)

"**Productive capabilities** allow the organization to accomplish its
purposes. **Constitutional capabilities** allow its constituents to
participate effectively in determining, contesting, evaluating and
revising those purposes and the arrangements through which they are
pursued. The distinction is functional and context-dependent, not an
intrinsic classification of skills."

| Activity | Productive role | Potential constitutional role |
|---|---|---|
| Programming | Building software | Inspecting and changing governing infrastructure |
| Statistics | Analyzing data | Independently evaluating institutional claims |
| AI-assisted research | Accelerating inquiry | Revising the criteria by which inquiry is governed |

Constitutional capability "can be distributed. It need not reside in every
individual human. It might be maintained through accountable
institutions, collective practices, independent experts, open
infrastructure, or human-governed AI systems."

## Why it is needed (A4 §11)

"What may distinguish AI is the range of functions that it could
externalize simultaneously, including the very functions by which
externalization is evaluated and governed." The concern "is that AI could
absorb both productive functions and the **meta-functions by which humans
govern their delegation**." "Civilization can externalize enormous
amounts of productive capacity without necessarily undermining human
agency. The more delicate question concerns externalization of
constitutional capacity."

## The population form (A5 §3)

A5 adds a third frontier beside the operational and developmental:
"**Constitutional frontier** What aspects of the social order can they
effectively contest and transform?" "A society could expand one frontier
while contracting another", and A5 crosses operational with
constitutional capacity:

| Operational capacity | Constitutional capacity | A5's reading |
|---|---|---|
| Increasing | Increasing | Emancipatory development |
| Increasing | Decreasing | Productive domination |
| Decreasing | Increasing | Liberation with transitional costs |
| Decreasing | Decreasing | Organizational deterioration |

## What it must not be confused with

- **[SYNTHESIS-105](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-105.md)'s inquiry frontier**, what a system can formulate
  questions about. A constitutional capability is a capability to change
  the arrangement one is part of, not to inquire into it.
- **[CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s governance of one's own governability**, an agent's
  relation to its own governing relations. A constitutional capability is
  a constituent's relation to an organization's.
- **Rank.** The term sorts by role, not by value: losing a productive
  capability can matter more to a person than losing a constitutional one.

## Limits

- **Role is relative to an arrangement.** Statistics is constitutional
  where institutions make claims that statistics can test and where the
  tester's finding can count. The term needs the arrangement declared, as
  [TERM-074](TERM-074.md)'s sense of "organization" does.
- **"Effectively" carries the weight.** Participation that changes nothing
  is not a constitutional capability; what counts as effective is the
  question [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)'s three achievements answer for correction.
