# NOTE-1499: Metzinger — The Elephant and the Blind

## Contribution

The book presents the free-text answers of a 2020 online survey of
meditators (pp. xvi, 12–20). Respondents described an episode in which
"awareness of awareness itself" was salient. Of 1,403 usable respondents,
841 gave usable reports, and more than 500 are printed. They are grouped
into 34 loosely thematic chapters, each followed by commentary. Around this
material the book states Metzinger's later account of consciousness. The
simplest conscious state, minimal phenomenal experience (MPE), is said to
lack a self, a perspective, time and space. It is modelled as the
organism's model of its own capacity to know. The first-person perspective
is said to be content of consciousness, not a condition of it
(pp. 457–459). The book calls itself a "prolegomenon", not a monograph
(pp. xxvi, 455).

## Key insight

Consciousness, on this account, is "knowing that one is alert" (p. 37). It
is a model of the organism's own epistemic openness. A self, or "epistemic
agent model", is a further layer, and it can drop away while that model
runs (pp. 302–306). On the account's own terms, though, what drops away is
the egoic self-model, not self-modelling. The model of epistemic openness
is itself "a single, integrated model of its own epistemic openness in the
form of a highest-level but nonegoic self-model" (p. 477). The book's
slogan is that consciousness is "not subjective". Read closely, its claim
is narrower: consciousness needs no model of a *knowing self*. It does not
say consciousness needs no model of the system at all.

## Assumptions

- **Naturalism and representationalism.** Pure awareness "is not literally
  contentless, but ... actually represents something" (p. 366). Its
  content is the system's epistemic capacity. Metaphysics is set aside:
  "I am not interested in metaphysics" (p. 463).
- **Probabilistic, not definitional, concepts.** "Pure awareness" has
  "probabilistic, not definitional structure", a family resemblance with a
  "hard prototypical core" (p. xxv). MPE is "a region in or path through
  the phenomenal state space" (pp. 463–464).
- **Report as access to the target.** The book takes the reports, together
  with semantic analysis of traditional texts and the survey's factor
  structure, as triangulating on one phenomenon. It names the method
  heterophenomenology, "not taking the reports as authoritative beyond the
  seeming itself" (p. 20).
- **Predictive processing.** Wakefulness is "a statistical hypothesis in
  the brain, a neural representation of the probability that veridical
  perception will actually occur" (p. 41). The minimal model is sketched
  in active inference (Figure 34.1, pp. 475–477, with Sandved-Smith).

## Key results

- **The survey** (pp. xvi, 12–20). Online, early 2020, in five languages.
  Over 3,500 respondents, 1,403 with usable data, a mean age of 52, and
  more than 77% meditating regularly. There are 92 items rated on 0–100
  sliders, and twelve factors chosen "under joint conceptual and
  statistical considerations" with "only limited explanatory power"
  (p. 12). Item 84, "The experience of 'pure awareness' is the simplest
  kind of conscious experience I know", had a median of 80 (p. xix).
- **The phenomenological core** (pp. 468–469). Simplicity, silence,
  wakefulness, clarity and "the signature of knowing". Wakefulness
  appears in two factors, factor 4 (Wakeful Presence) and factor 3
  (Self-Knowledge, Autonomous Cognizance and Insight). Factor 8,
  "Emptiness and Nonegoic Self-Awareness", has its items for
  self-knowing "negatively correlated with the phenomenology of
  selfhood" (p. 466). The factor loadings themselves are not printed in
  the pages read.
- **States and modes** (pp. xxi–xxii, 470–472). MPE occurs as a local state
  ("my mind was crystal clear") and as global modes: witnessing, nondual
  awareness, bodiless body-experience and "virtuality". A
  "full-absorption episode" is one after which awareness itself "is the
  only phenomenal feature that can later be reported" (p. xxiii). It is
  "concurrently ineffable" (p. xxi).
- **Five senses of "purity"** (pp. 460–461). Contentlessness, no thought,
  clarity, suchness and nonduality.
- **The epistemic agent model** (pp. 301–306). It is the layer of the
  human self-model in which a knowing, attending, thinking self is
  represented. It "is what creates subjectivity in the sense of a strong
  first-person perspective" (p. 302), and it "is precisely what is
  completely missing in all full-absorption episodes" (p. 306). On a
  conservative estimate, people lack a stable one for about two-thirds of
  the 24-hour cycle (Figure 25.1, p. 305; from Metzinger 2015,
  "M-Autonomy").
- **The contraction principle** (pp. 79–82). Phenomenality "is a
  subpersonal property, a property of certain functionally integrated
  brain states". Ordinary brains misrepresent it "by contracting it into a
  transparent conscious self-model, which then forms the origin of a
  first-person perspective" (p. 80). Nondual states are offered as
  "well-documented counterexamples" (p. 80). "There is a methodological
  primacy to subpersonal state consciousness: It can occur without system
  consciousness, but not vice versa" (p. 81). "The subpersonal world-model
  in the person's head is what is truly conscious" (p. 82).
- **Transparency** (pp. 362–369). Being No One's transparency is
  restated: contents are "represented as realities" because earlier
  processing stages are unavailable (pp. 362–363). It is applied to pure
  awareness: if the model of epistemic openness is transparent, its
  certainty is "transferred to the content" and a real world appears. If
  it is opaque, "there would be an explicit experience of emptiness and
  bare wakefulness" (p. 369). "Transparency creates realness; opacity
  creates knowingness" (p. 368).
- **The minimal model** (pp. 476–477). Level 4 of a nested hierarchy models
  "the system's global 'epistemic openness'". "It is level 4 that really
  endows the content generated on all lower-order levels with
  phenomenality" (pp. 476–477). A full-absorption episode is "inference at
  the highest level, without any perceptual inference at the levels below
  it" (p. 477). The sketch is "a hypothetical starting point" (p. 477).
- **Two fallacies** (pp. 416–418). The E-fallacy takes a felt sense of
  knowing for knowledge. The C-fallacy takes a state that feels like the
  nature of consciousness for a finding about it. The C-fallacy has a
  mirror image: "Just because something is reported as feeling not like an
  experience at all, this doesn't license the conclusion that it isn't"
  (p. 417).

## Method

A three-way approach (pp. xx, 14): semantic constraints drawn from
traditional texts (Metzinger 2020, [LIT-1946](../literature.d/LIT-1946.md)), exploratory factor
analysis of the 92 items, and a "soft qualitative analysis that doesn't
satisfy academic criteria for systematicity and completeness" (p. xxvi).
The grouping of reports reflects "the implicit phenomenological
intuitions of someone who has meditated twice a day for more than
forty-seven years" (pp. 12–14).

## Concepts

- **Minimal phenomenal experience (MPE)**: the simplest conscious
  experience. The working hypothesis is that it lacks "time
  representation; self-location ...; ownership, agency, and
  autobiographical self-awareness; and a phenomenally experienced
  first-person perspective" (p. xviii).
- **Arousal, tonic alertness, wakefulness**: a physical, a functional and
  a phenomenal property. Wakefulness "can be interpreted as a special way
  of representing tonic alertness" (pp. 36–37).
- **Epistemic openness**: "a global form of phenomenal character ... a
  specific form of openness to the world" (p. 41), the "sheer potential to
  know" (p. 43).
- **Epistemic agent model (EAM)**: the self-model layer of a knowing self.
  "A subject is an epistemic agent model that functions as the unit of
  identification" (p. 306).
- **Unit of identification**: whatever experiential content one
  experiences oneself as. It can be the EAM, the body, or "the space of
  wakeful awareness" (pp. 361, 470–471).
- **Zero-person perspective**: experiencing from MPE, without egoic
  self-awareness (p. xix).
- **Contraction**: the misrepresentation of a state property as a system
  property (p. 80).
- **Full-absorption episode**: an episode after which only awareness
  itself can be reported. Its types are I, entered from waking; II, from
  NREM sleep; III, from a lucid dream (pp. xxiii, 220).

## Bearing on the record's own argument

- **[THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) (unity of experience without a self-model): it qualifies the
  second clause rather than confirming it.** The précis's minimal case was
  "Selfless Snapshot Consciousness", the presence of "one unified world"
  with no perspective. The précis itself called it "probably empirically
  empty". This book offers full-absorption episodes as what such states
  are like in fact. Three things change. First, the minimal case is not
  a world experienced as one. In it "the distinction between world and
  self is not yet represented", and there is no world-model content at
  all (p. 477). The 2020 paper adds that it lacks any "phenomenal quality
  of singularity" ([LIT-1946](../literature.d/LIT-1946.md), p. 13). Second, the model that remains
  is described as "a highest-level but nonegoic self-model" (p. 477). So
  what the case would show is unity without an *egoic* self-model, or an
  epistemic agent model, which is narrower than "no self-model". Third,
  the cases nearer to [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)'s selfless world are the nondual
  *modes* (pp. 470–471), in which a world is present and subject/object
  structure is absent. These, too, are known only by report, and the
  pages read give no measure of integration for them. [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)'s
  `promote_when` asks for such states "established by methods that meet
  its own performative worry". The book states the worry and leaves it
  open (p. 19). The inference is the record's.
- **[THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) (transparent self-model).** The book restates transparency
  as Being No One gave it (pp. 362–363), and extends it from the
  self-model to the model of epistemic openness (pp. 368–369). On
  [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)'s prediction that a fully opaque self-model would end
  selfhood, the book offers no test. Its opaque cases are opacity of
  *awareness*, not of the self-model. It reads meditation as "opacity
  control" (p. 366, after Sandved-Smith et al.), a mechanism of the kind
  that prediction needs. It gives no measure of opacity.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md) (what report evidence can establish).** The book is a
  worked example of report-only evidence, and it states the limits
  itself. It names self-selection, theory contamination, memory and
  "response drift" (pp. 16–20), concurrent ineffability (p. xxi), and the
  autobiographical-memory problem: a report that one was selfless is
  "highly dubious from a methodological perspective", since what is
  remembered is remembered as a self's (p. 19). It also names the
  E-fallacy and the C-fallacy (pp. 416–418). The neural programme it
  proposes, the Triple Triangulation Project (pp. 220–221, 473), would
  look for "minimally sufficient neural correlates" common to absorption
  entered from waking, NREM sleep and lucid dreams. Every one of those
  episodes would still be identified by report. The summary nonetheless
  says that "full-absorption episodes, nonegoic self-awareness, and nondual
  modes of consciousness are empirically established phenomena" (p. 457),
  and that the strong reading of subjectivity "is false" (p. 459).
  [THEORY-1295](../theory.d/THEORY-1295.md) states the gap.
- **[QUESTION-046](../questions.d/QUESTION-046.md) (whether an organized whole is a subject of experience).**
  The contraction principle answers part of the question with a
  stipulation, not a criterion. The bearer of phenomenality is "the
  subpersonal world-model", and system consciousness is a representation
  of it (pp. 80–82). Phenomenality "can occur without system
  consciousness, but not vice versa" (p. 81). If that is right, the
  criterion the question asks for would have to find the conscious states
  inside a whole, not the whole. Same structure as the record's
  distinction between an organization and its members' states, not shown
  same mechanism.
- **[QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md) and [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) (experience as perspectival interpretive
  organization).** [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md)'s part 3 requires the organization to be
  perspectival. On this book's account the simplest experience is
  aperspectival, though it is still an organization: a model of the
  system's own epistemic capacity. If the account were right, part 3
  would need either a sense of "perspectival" that the zero-person
  perspective satisfies, or a weaker condition. [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md)'s sense 1, an
  organizational perspective, may be that sense. The book's "level 4"
  (p. 477) is a candidate statement of what makes lower-order content
  experiential. It is stated without report-independent evidence, which
  is what [QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md) asks for.
- **[TERM-082](../terms.d/TERM-082.md) (senses of consciousness).** The three-level split of arousal,
  tonic alertness and wakefulness (pp. 36–37) refines [TERM-082](../terms.d/TERM-082.md)'s sense 4,
  wakefulness or arousal. The book cites unresponsive wakefulness, a
  sleep–wake cycle without awareness, as tonic alertness without
  phenomenal wakefulness (p. 37). On its usage, "wakefulness" names a
  phenomenal property, so [TERM-082](../terms.d/TERM-082.md)'s sense 4 as worded would be split
  between the functional and the phenomenal.
- **[TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md) (moral standing) and [THEORY-554](../theory.d/THEORY-554.md), [THEORY-677](../theory.d/THEORY-677.md).** The epilogue
  separates consciousness from suffering. Artificial consciousness "may
  involve a consciously experienced sense of self that may in turn
  generate artificial suffering". Yet MPE "involves no psychological
  suffering whatsoever", and the book asks whether a machine that "reliably
  remains in a single full-absorption episode" would be wrong to build
  (pp. 484–485). The book does not ask whether such a machine would have
  standing. That is filed as [QUESTION-368](../questions.d/QUESTION-368.md). The epilogue also flags
  an information hazard: the simplest conscious state "may be the easiest
  to implement" (p. 484).
- **[LIT-1354](../literature.d/LIT-1354.md) (Chandia-Jorquera et al.).** Their "pure awareness" scale is
  defined by this framework's MPE. The book's own measure, the
  questionnaire's sliders, has the same structure: practitioners rate
  episodes against the experimenters' description of the target. The
  survey's instructions defined the target as "awareness of awareness
  itself" (p. xvi). The worry [NOTE-1058](NOTE-1058.md) raises about a defined scale
  applies here too, and more strongly: the reports are of past episodes,
  some from decades earlier (p. 18).
- **THEORY filed.** [THEORY-1299](../theory.d/THEORY-1299.md) states the account, with the 2020
  paper. [THEORY-1295](../theory.d/THEORY-1295.md) states the evidential gap.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **Read in part**; see the history. The phenomenological chapters
  between 5 and 33, most of the book, were not read through, and some
  of the theory sits in their commentaries (chapters 5, 17, 22, 24, 27,
  29, 30).
- **Report only.** No physiological or behavioural measure is part of the
  study. The factor analysis is exploratory, its loadings are not printed
  in the pages read, and the author says future confirmatory work may
  give "very different results" (p. 12).
- **A count that does not match.** The introduction says 841 usable
  reports and "more than 500" printed (p. xvi). The limitations section
  speaks of "the 1,183 written phenomenological reports presented in this
  book" (p. 17). The acknowledgments thank 1,187 meditators (p. xi).
- **The summary claims more than the methods allow** ([THEORY-1295](../theory.d/THEORY-1295.md)).
- **A participant-researcher.** The grouping is the author's, a
  practitioner of 47 years, and he grants that it carries biases "I am
  completely unaware of" (p. 14).

## Open questions

- Would a report-independent marker separate full absorption from
  dreamless sleep without report? The Triple Triangulation Project needs
  one, and the book names none.
- Does factor 8's negative relation between nonegoic self-knowing and
  selfhood survive confirmatory analysis in a sample not recruited through
  contemplative networks?
- Is the "nonegoic self-model" of p. 477 a self-model in Being No One's
  sense, one the system identifies with, or only a model of the system?
  The answer decides how [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)'s second clause should read.
