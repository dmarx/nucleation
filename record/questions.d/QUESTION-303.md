# QUESTION-303: After cued traumatic recall, do people with persistent PTSD sample disambiguating peripheral context less than matched trauma-exposed controls, and does restoring that sampling during the same exposure produce lasting change that the exposure alone does not?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-285](../theory.d/THEORY-285.md) shows, in illustrative simulation, how an overly precise prior
that one is in a harmful context can keep itself up by making exploratory
sampling look uninformative. The paper fits no data. Whether people with
persistent PTSD do this is open, and it is what [QUESTION-045](QUESTION-045.md)'s lock-in
reading of disorder would need at the individual scale.

One reading in the record leans against the sampling account in people,
on a different task. In De Kleine et al. ([THEORY-912](../theory.d/THEORY-912.md)), patients with OCD or
PTSD "sampled as often as controls and drew less from the outcomes". That
task is avoiding a safety cue, not sampling context, so it does not answer
this question.

## What would answer it

Evidence from people, not from the model. Either of two kinds would
support it. First, the authors' own prediction confirmed: after cued
traumatic recall, people with PTSD attenuate peripheral evidence about
context more than focal evidence, against matched trauma-exposed controls,
with context-sampling measured directly (for example by eye movements
toward disambiguating periphery). Second, an intervention contrast matched
on exposure: restoring context sampling during the same exposure produces
lasting change that the same exposure without it does not, while a
precision-lowering drug reduces symptoms that return on withdrawal. The
account is refuted if people with persistent PTSD sample disambiguating
context as much as resilient controls and still keep the harmful-context
belief. More simulations with other parameters cannot settle it, and nor
can symptom improvement alone.

<!-- inactive-ok-block: LIT-tmp5bqfy LIT-tmpocdt2 LIT-tmpizs88 LIT-tmpgjodp LIT-tmp4oubb LIT-tmpg13ib — Deferred; registered by the lead search of 2026-11-22, not read -->

LEAD-106 searched Europe PMC for both kinds of evidence this question
names. **Neither has been collected.** No study measures context sampling
after cued traumatic recall in PTSD against trauma-exposed controls, and
the 2026 scoping review of eye tracking in PTSD (THEORY-tmp8n4s7) includes
none after recall. No trial runs the same exposure with and without
restored context sampling.

**The nearest, first half** (THEORY-tmp1ov9x, read): with a cue specific
to each participant's trauma set in a neutral background, people with PTSD
first fixated the background later than trauma-exposed controls and the
cue as fast. It lacks the recall manipulation, disambiguating periphery
and a measure of how much is sampled. Whether it counts as evidence here
is filed as alternatives: CLAIM-tmpcug8h, leaned to, reads it as weak,
indirect support for the predicted direction, with ARG-tmpc0892;
CLAIM-tmpc7tmg reads it as no evidence, since the background carried no
information to withhold. Unread studies of how widely people scan point
both ways: shorter scanpaths in PTSD in visual search (LIT-tmp5bqfy), but
more scanning of ambiguous scenes with hypervigilance (LIT-tmpocdt2,
LIT-tmpizs88) and more gaze to emotional backgrounds with symptoms
(LIT-tmpgjodp).

**The nearest, second half** (registered, not read): the EMDR protocol
with an external visual focus beat the same protocol without one, pre to
post (LIT-tmp4oubb); the component is not context sampling and lasting
change is not reported in the abstract. Stimulus-discrimination training
before analogue trauma films reduced intrusions against suppression
(LIT-tmpg13ib); it has no matched exposure.

The refutation condition, equal sampling with the belief kept, is untested.
The question stays Open.
