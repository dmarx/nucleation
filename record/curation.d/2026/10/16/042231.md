The owner asked for the later works relevant to the random-projection
readings to be read and filed. Three were named by the readings
themselves. Bourgain (1985) and Linial, London and Rabinovich (1995) are
in [NOTE-tmpmv42v](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpmv42v.md)'s Connections, flagged as unchecked. Alon and Klartag
(2017) is in [NOTE-tmp4mti8](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmp4mti8.md)'s report of later work. The assistant added a
fourth, Lee and Naor (2005), as the later work on Johnson and
Lindenstrauss's extension problem ([THEORY-tmp7nqd6](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp7nqd6.md)). No reading names it,
and its LIT's history says so.

## What entered

- **Bourgain (1985)**, [LIT-tmpwy0he](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpwy0he.md), doi:10.1007/BF02776078. `Unavailable`:
  OpenAlex lists only the closed Springer copy, and web searches found no
  author-posted or course-hosted copy. Linial, London and Rabinovich state
  its theorem and reprove its distortion bound, and the record reads it
  through them.
- **Linial, London and Rabinovich (1995)**, [LIT-tmp9g20z](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp9g20z.md), read in
  [NOTE-tmpetxcq](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpetxcq.md) from the authors' February 1995 manuscript on an MSU
  course page. Filed as [THEORY-tmpbseab](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpbseab.md): O(log n) dimensions and O(log n)
  distortion for every n-point metric, with expanders needing Ω(log n).
- **Lee and Naor (2005)**, [LIT-tmp2k8rj](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp2k8rj.md), read in [NOTE-tmpd7vjw](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpd7vjw.md) from a
  January 2004 preprint on James Lee's page. Filed as [THEORY-tmpi8kfq](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpi8kfq.md),
  C log n/log log n for extension into any Banach space, and
  [THEORY-tmpvy222](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpvy222.md), extension from padded random partitions.
- **Alon and Klartag (2017)**, [LIT-tmp08fvg](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp08fvg.md), read in [NOTE-tmpfnjjj](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpfnjjj.md) from
  arXiv v4. Filed as [THEORY-tmpup7x5](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpup7x5.md), the bit counts and the dimension
  lower bound for every ε ≥ 2/√n, and [THEORY-tmpbh0v2](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpbh0v2.md), the bipartite JL
  lemma.

## What the readings changed

- Problem 4 of Johnson and Lindenstrauss is answered in the negative, by
  the record's inference from Linial, London and Rabinovich. The
  sharpness of their √(log n) extension bound stays open, as Lee and Naor
  say ([THEORY-tmp7nqd6](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp7nqd6.md), version 2).
- Larsen and Nelson's report of Alon and Klartag needed qualifying: the
  bound is additive, for ε ≥ 2/√n, and only its lower half is proved
  ([THEORY-tmpfo89o](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpfo89o.md), version 2).
- [NOTE-679](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-679.md) relays a miscitation from Sala et al. The paper credits Linial,
  London and Rabinovich with a Euclidean lower bound for trees that they
  do not contain. [NOTE-679](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-679.md) is annotated.
- [QUESTION-tmpa0dq9](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-tmpa0dq9.md) goes to version 4: Alon and Klartag's proof makes its
  proposed shape literal, a log of a family's size.

## Boundary

None of the four is held in the anthology. A grep of its `record/` for
the authors' names and identifiers found nothing. All four are
mathematics. Linial, London and Rabinovich also carry `operations-research`
for multicommodity flow, and Alon and Klartag carry `information-theory`
for their bit counts. None carries an instruction for machine-learning
practice.
