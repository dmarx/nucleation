# CLAIM-033: In published translations, renderings that lose distinctions along one dimension commonly gain specificity along another, so loss is not one-dimensional

## The claim

A75: "A translation that discards distinctions may map a relatively narrow
region into a broader one. A translation that introduces a highly specific
stance may narrow the target region along certain dimensions. And both can occur
simultaneously: the translation can broaden conceptual possibilities while
narrowing interpersonal interpretation. That is a more expressive way to think
about what our refrains do."

## Where it went

Not into A81's outline, C6 or the manuscript. The manuscript's §5 says equal
entropy does not give equal relational structure, and §11 that "Referential
information may survive while pragmatic invariants break". Neither says that a
rendering can gain specificity on one dimension while it loses it on another.
"The high life eats your paycheck" ([CASE-012](../cases.d/CASE-012.md)) would be a case: it widens
the reference and fixes the speaker as a critic.

## Prior statement

Jakobson (1959) makes the same point at the level of grammar ([LIT-786](../literature.d/LIT-786.md),
[NOTE-614](../notes.d/NOTE-614.md), [THEORY-170](../theory.d/THEORY-170.md)). The Russian rendering of "I hired a worker"
must fix aspect and the worker's sex, which the English leaves open, and it
drops tense and definiteness, which the English fixes. Languages differ in
what they must convey, so a translation adds information along one dimension
while losing it along another.

## Note of 2026-10-19: the title restated as prevalence

The promotion audit's batch 034 found the title behind its own defeat
condition. Since version 2 the condition tests a rate over a sample of
published translations, after [CLAIM-238](CLAIM-238.md) showed that a "never" condition
no study meets. The title still said a translation "can" do this, which
Jakobson's example alone settles. The title now states the rate the
condition tests. The possibility is not given up: it is Jakobson's
point, [THEORY-170](../theory.d/THEORY-170.md), and the examples above are its instances. The warrant
stays interpretive, because no sample has been drawn.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

Its title is a claim of prevalence, and no sample of published
translations has been drawn by a rule stated in advance and rated by
independent judges, which is what its defeat condition tests. Jakobson's
examples (THEORY-170) and CASE-012 show only that such a rendering occurs.
