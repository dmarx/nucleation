# NOTE-tmp7qron: Griffith, Redding and Van Reenen — Mapping the two faces of R&D

## Contribution

The first systematic cross-country industry estimate of the "second face"
of R&D, the role the authors take from Cohen and Levinthal's 1989 paper:
besides producing innovations, R&D makes it easier to absorb technology
from the frontier. They separate R&D's innovation return from its
absorptive return, and argue that U.S.-based studies, where the gap to the
frontier is small, understate R&D's social return.

## Key insight

How much of the knowledge on offer a party can take up depends on its own
work in the same field. The frontier's technology is available to every
industry behind it, yet industries behind it catch up faster the more R&D
they do, and the further behind they are, the more of R&D's return comes
through catching up.

## Assumptions

- **Lagged R&D is predetermined** in the productivity equation (eq. 7);
  serial correlation is tested and t−2 lags used, and IV with second lags
  gives similar results (fn. 33). They concede "hardly any papers" have
  found good external instruments for R&D.
- **Distance to the frontier** (the TFP gap) proxies the potential for
  transfer; TFP is a superlative index, adjusted for skills and hours.
- **Separability** of R&D from other inputs (fn. 9), and small
  depreciation of R&D knowledge (fn. 10).
- **The level** is the country-industry; firm mechanisms are inferred,
  not observed.

## Key results

*Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **The mechanism, asserted (§I, p. 883).** Tacit knowledge is "hard to
  acquire without direct investigation. By actively engaging in R&D in a
  particular intellectual or technological field, one acquires such tacit
  knowledge and can more easily understand and assimilate the discoveries
  of others." Arrow's example: jet-engine plans supplied by the British
  "took ten months for them to be redrawn to conform to American usage."
- **Prior firm-level evidence they cite (fn. 2).** Jaffe: "high-R&D U.S.
  firms benefit most" from his spillover pool; Geroski, Machin and Van
  Reenen: "U.K. firms with a history of innovation were those most likely
  to benefit from the innovations of other firms."
- **The model (§II, eq. 4).** The speed of technology transfer is
  δ1 + δ2(R/Y), so the return to R&D is ρ1 + δ2 ln(A_F/A_i), rising with
  distance behind the frontier.
- **The estimates (Table 2, p. 889; 1801 observations, within-group).**
  Column (3): R&D × gap δ2 = 0.596 (s.e. 0.333), "positive and
  statistically significant at the 10% level". Column (5), skills- and
  hours-adjusted: δ2 = 1.00 (0.346). Column (6), with human capital:
  ρ1 = 0.473 (0.172), δ2 = 0.815 (0.350), human capital × gap 0.464
  (0.137), and the gap term alone 0.013 (0.021), against 0.080 (0.014) in
  column (1). Column (8), R&D lagged t−2 (1695 observations): δ2 = 0.814
  (0.385).
- **The reader's inference, not the authors'.** Since the gap term alone
  falls from 0.080 to 0.013 once the R&D and human-capital interactions are
  in (column 6), little catch-up is left that does not run through the
  party's own R&D and skills. The authors do not draw this conclusion.
- **Quantification (Table 3, p. 891; text, p. 890).** Technology transfer's share of
  R&D's total effect: 14% United States, 23% Germany, 40% Italy, 48%
  Norway, 54% Finland. In Finland "less than half of the estimated effect
  is due to innovation—absorptive capacity is quantitatively more
  important."
- **The return (pp. 890–891).** An innovation return "of around 40% is
  consistent with existing U.S.-based studies", read causally only "Under
  the assumption that the association between productivity growth and R&D
  is causal".
- **Conclusions (§VI, p. 893).** "R&D stimulates growth directly through
  innovation and also indirectly through technology transfer." Trade "had
  a statistically weak effect". Left open: why countries behind the
  frontier do not invest more, perhaps "underdevelopment of financial
  markets or inappropriate government policies."

## Concepts

- **Two faces of R&D**: innovation, and the absorption of others'
  technology (absorptive capacity, taken from Cohen and Levinthal).
- **Technology frontier and TFP gap**: the most productive country in an
  industry, and the log distance of each other country from it.
- **Tacit knowledge**: knowledge hard to codify, acquired by direct
  investigation.
- **Social and private return to R&D**.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)** (when transient support can be withdrawn safely):
  qualifies its flux condition. Outside knowledge is a flux that stays
  available, yet its use scales with the recipient's own R&D (δ2 > 0), so
  a flux that stays available is not enough without the party's own work.
  Supports the condition that the party could do the work and did it
  throughout. No support is ever withdrawn, so nothing on persistence after
  it ends.
- **[CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md)** (capacity-first order of supply): supports it in spirit.
  The capacity to use outside knowledge comes from "actively engaging in
  R&D". Substitution of outside supply for own work is not tested.
- **[CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md)** (upkeep drawing on a flux in proportion to intact state):
  weak and indirect. Uptake of the frontier's flux scales with own state,
  the structural feature the claim models, but the model is linear, with no
  threshold, collapse or hysteresis.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md)**: nothing directly; no intervention is withdrawn. At
  most, dependence on an outside source of knowledge is modulated by own
  R&D, which a withdrawal test would need to hold fixed.
- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md), [CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md), [CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md), [CLAIM-601](../claims.d/CLAIM-601.md)**: nothing. The frontier is

## Limitations

- Country-industry aggregates: firm mechanisms, Cohen and Levinthal's
  level, are inferred, not observed.
- A correlational panel; the causal reading rests on predetermined R&D,
  and δ2 is significant at only 10% in column (3).
- No case of R&D stopped, cut or outsourced; no horizon of loss; no
  lockout, cumulativeness or path dependence tested.
- The sample ends in 1990 and holds twelve rich countries.
