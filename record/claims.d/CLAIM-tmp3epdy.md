# CLAIM-tmp3epdy: Selection alone can make organizations converge on power

## The claim

A6 §5: "Bostrom's instrumental convergence thesis concerns the tendency
for agents with different final goals to pursue similar intermediate
goals, such as resource acquisition and self-preservation." Then:
"Organizations pursuing very different purposes can nevertheless converge
on similar behaviours ... This need not result from explicit instrumental
reasoning. Organizations that fail to acquire and preserve the resources
necessary for their continuation may simply become less prevalent."

| Mechanism | Explanation (A6) |
|---|---|
| Deliberative | An agent recognizes that power helps it achieve its objective. |
| Selectional | Organizations that accumulate power are differentially reproduced. |

"The latter does not require a sophisticated agent at all."

## What the claim needs (the assistant)

Selection yields a tendency only if three things hold: organizations
vary in it, the variation affects their survival or spread, and it is
retained by the organization across the turnover of its members, as
routines, rules or structure. A6 states the second. The third is what
makes the convergence the organization's and not its current members',
and it is the condition the defeater names. Where it fails, the pattern
is a sequence of agents each reasoning instrumentally, the deliberative
route again. The record's organizational-ecology readings are where the
retention condition would be checked. This section's wording is the
assistant's (Claude, in this session).

## What it does not say

- **That organizations seek power.** A selected tendency need not be
  represented as a goal by anyone, which is A6's point.
- **That the two mechanisms are exclusive.** A6 links the selectional one
  to "the Black Queen and organizational individuation lines": "Selection
  can stabilize a larger organization whose apparent interests are not
  identical to those of its constituents." That link is A's analogy and
  carries no weight here.
