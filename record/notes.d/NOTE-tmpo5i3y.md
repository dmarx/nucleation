# NOTE-tmpo5i3y: Tomasello — Precís of A Natural History of Human Thinking

## Contribution

The book's architecture in five pages, with its claimed contribution:
distinguishing joint from collective intentionality, a second-personal
middle step before culture.

## Key results

1. **The contribution.** "distinguishing between joint intentionality
   and collective intentionality" (p. 60).
2. **Apes.** They represent situations "as perceptual abstractions in
   iconic format" (p. 61); given that, "the evolution of propositional
   thought is a bit less mysterious" (p. 62).
3. **Joint step.** "This dual-level structure of joint agency" yields a
   shared world of distinct perspectives (p. 62), and its products are
   "(i) perspectival cognitive representations; (ii) recursive inferences;
   and" social self-monitoring (p. 63).
4. **Collective step.** Modern humans became "group-minded creatures"
   (p. 63), leading "to an objectification of the group’s social and
   institutional norms" (p. 64), with "(ii) self-reflective inferences;
   and (iii) generically normative" self-monitoring (p. 64).
5. **Method.** On borrowing philosophers' concepts out of context he
   pleads "guilty to this charge" (p. 64).

## Assumptions

The great-ape starting point rests on experiments interpreted in certain
ways; the evolutionary scenario is hypothetical; infants are an analogy
for early humans not to be pushed too far.

## Concepts

Individual, joint and collective intentionality; joint goal; joint
attention; dual-level structure; perspectival and objective
representation; recursive inference; social and normative self-monitoring;
cultural common ground.
