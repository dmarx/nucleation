# CLAIM-1299: A collaboration record's attributions are only as reliable as the step that makes them: this record attributes each contribution by quotation checked against a verbatim transcript the filer did not write, and a record kept by the student alone, with no such transcript, is self-report that leaves an adopted LLM proposal indistinguishable from the student's own

## The claim

[CLAIM-668](CLAIM-668.md) says a shared record can represent who contributed a proposition.
It can represent it truly or falsely, and nothing in a record's grammar tells
which. In Nucleation, attribution is anchored outside the record: an
exchange is filed from its verbatim text, each owner's or assistant's turn is
quoted, and the quote is rechecked against that text before filing
([ADR-068](../decisions.d/ADR-068.md)'s fifth invariant: "Every quote that a title, a claim or a status
change leans on is rechecked against the source text by whoever files"). The voice
line ("The wording is A's ...") is then a claim about a source anyone can
reread.

A student keeping a record of their own collaboration with an LLM has no such
anchor unless the transcript is kept by something other than the student, for
instance the tool the student used, and the record's attributions are
checked against it. Without that, the record is self-report, and the case the
owner's proposal is meant to catch, a student adopting an LLM's proposal and
filing it as their own, leaves no trace: a proposal restated in the
student's words looks like the student's. So the educational use of
[CLAIM-1213](CLAIM-1213.md) needs two things the publishing use mostly has already: a
transcript the contributor did not write, and an attribution step checked
against it.

## What it does not say

- Not that a transcript settles authorship of ideas: a student can bring an
  idea from elsewhere into a prompt. It settles who said what in the
  collaboration, which is what the record attributes.
- Not that the check must be complete; this record checks quotes, not every
  paraphrase.
