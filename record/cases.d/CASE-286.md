# CASE-286: Voluntary submission against alienation of standing: two informed, voluntary delegations of consequential decisions, one limited and still governed by the delegator's protected claims, the other purporting to give the recipient unlimited authority to determine whether the delegator keeps any rights at all

## The case

A85 §11 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):

> **Case A: Voluntary submission versus alienation of standing.** Two people agree to delegate consequential decisions to another actor. In one case, the delegation is limited and remains governed by protected claims. In the other, the recipient purports to acquire unlimited authority to determine whether the delegator retains any rights at all. Both delegations are stipulated to be informed and voluntary. Does inalienability invalidate the second even when procedural consent is perfect?

Held fixed: the parties' information, the voluntariness of the agreement,
and the procedure by which consent is given. Varied: what the delegation
purports to confer. In the first, authority over particular decisions; in
the second, authority over the delegator's standing to make any claim
against the recipient.

A85 §4 gives the case in an employment form: "suppose someone agrees never
to question their employer's authority, regardless of what the employer
later demands. Would the agreement remove every subsequent ground for
objection?"

## What it can show

It isolates the inalienability premise. Any theory on which valid consent
is sufficient for valid authority treats the two delegations alike; a
theory with [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md)'s limit on the holder's power to confer
treats the second as conferring nothing beyond what the first does. The
case does not decide between them. It shows what each commits to, so
that a reader who finds the second delegation invalid has accepted a
limit consent cannot cross.

It is the self-binding case [CASE-128](CASE-128.md) (Active) taken to its limit. There a
person gives a friend a password and is refused it under temptation, and
the record holds that the delegation can be the person's own and hard to
revoke. Here the delegation would remove the standing from which the
person could later object that it had gone too far.

Being stipulated, it cannot show that anyone makes such agreements or
what courts do with them. A85 marks its contract version "a proposed moral
argument, not a claim that every broad waiver or arbitration agreement is
legally invalid".
