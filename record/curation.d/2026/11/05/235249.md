The owner asked the assistant to "proceed systematically filling all of
those identified gaps". This branch takes medicine, the most quantitative
case of supply suppressing a system's own production and of tapering.

- **Corticosteroids.**
  - Broersen et al. 2015 ([LIT-2214](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2214.md)), read from the reprint in
    Broersen's open Leiden thesis.
  - The 2024 ESE/Endocrine Society guideline ([LIT-2218](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2218.md)).
- **Ventilation.**
  - Levine et al. 2008 ([LIT-2212](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2212.md)) is Unavailable. It was read through
    Jaber et al. 2011 ([LIT-2211](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2211.md)) and Goligher et al. 2020
    ([LIT-2216](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2216.md)).
  - Boles et al. 2007 ([LIT-2213](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2213.md)) is Unavailable. It was read through
    Shah, Hart and Kaltsakas 2024 ([LIT-2217](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2217.md)) and Alía and Esteban 2000
    ([LIT-2215](../../record/literature.d/LIT-2215.md)).
- **Attributions corrected by the lead.** The lead's first drafts of the
  notes credited three weaning figures to the wrong neighbour. The figures
  are 75 % abrupt withdrawal, about 13 % reintubation, and the equivalence
  of 30- and 120-minute trials. They are Alía and Esteban's, and the notes
  now say so.
- **What changed.**
  - Supply that does a gland's work suppresses it, more with dose and
    duration. The guideline's cure is to cover the shortfall: capacity
    first.
  - Full ventilatory support atrophies the diaphragm within days, and
    effort has a band, not just a floor.
  - For most patients, abrupt removal after a passed readiness trial is
    the course. The method that tapers machine breaths did worst.
  - No corticosteroid taper scheme is shown to be safe or efficient.
  - These agree with the assistant's capability-trap claim, filed
    separately in dmarx/nucleation#405, on abrupt cuts of substitutive
    support.
