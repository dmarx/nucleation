The owner sent 33 readings. Most are on mode connectivity and model
merging; the rest are on statistical-mechanics and random-matrix accounts
of generalization, and on where in a network generalization is carried.

Nucleation held none of them. The anthology holds two, Git Re-Basin
([ANTH-LIT-333](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-333.md)) and Entezari et al. ([ANTH-LIT-251](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-251.md)), which are filed here as
[ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md) second readings. This record's question is how the solutions
training finds are arranged in weight space, and what that arrangement
says about what a trained network is. Every work carries
`anthology-candidate`.

No topic covered that question, so `loss-landscapes` was added
([ADR-027](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-027.md)).

## What was filed

33 LITs and 33 NOTEs, every one read from a lawful copy: 31 from arXiv, one
from PMLR, and one from the NeurIPS Bayesian Deep Learning workshop site.
Three LITs are Proposed rather than Active, because the reading found
them too thin to stand on:

- Yao et al. on generative and contrastive models: one pair of modes per
  model, and inconsistent numbers;
- Li et al. on GNNs: no proofs, and an unfilled placeholder in the text;
- Zhang & Blei on the ELBO: no results.

The groups were:

- **Foundations** (8): Garipov, Draxler, Frankle, Kuditipudi, Entezari,
  Git Re-Basin, Ferbach, Benton.
- **Extensions** (9): mechanistic connectivity, layerwise feature
  connectivity, geodesic and star-shaped connectivity, the symmetry
  account, star domains, transformers, mixtures of experts, and
  adversarial robustness.
- **Merging and other settings** (9): weight-space regions of fine-tuned
  models, ZipIt!, TIES-Merging, continual and class-incremental learning,
  generative models, GNNs, federated learning, and the ELBO.
- **Landscape theory and generalization** (7): Yang et al.'s taxonomy,
  Martin & Mahoney, Liao et al. and Dereziński et al. on double descent,
  and three papers on where in depth generalization is carried.

## Findings for the anthology, reported not edited

- **[ANTH-LIT-251](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-251.md) / [NOTE-131](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-131.md) (Entezari) state a "no dead neurons"
  condition.** The paper's only theorem (3.1) has no such condition. It is
  for one-hidden-layer networks at random initialization, and it bounds
  the output difference, not a loss barrier. The paper's own permutation
  search did not lower barriers for VGG or ResNet.
- **[ANTH-LIT-333](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-333.md) / [NOTE-116](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-116.md) (Git Re-Basin) describe weight matching as
  greedy, layer by layer.** The paper uses coordinate descent over all
  layers, and its Appendix A.7 argues against greedy single-pass matching.
  It also has a merge-many algorithm. The quoted figures ("within 1–2%",
  "2–5×", "H > 10,000") were not found in the text.
- **[ANTH-THEORY-010](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/theory.d/THEORY-010.md) (most of the barrier is permutation).** Its
  promote_when asks for a transformer demonstration. Theus et al. give
  one. Permutation alignment alone removes 63–90% of the transformer
  barrier, which supports the theory, but reaching zero needs an
  orthogonal map on the residual stream, which is not a permutation. Lubana et al. bound the theory with
  barriers that reflect real differences in mechanism.
- **[ANTH-SOTA-217](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/practices.d/SOTA-217.md) (align permutations before averaging).** ZipIt! narrows
  it to models trained on the same task.
- **[ANTH-SOTA-012](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/practices.d/SOTA-012.md) (sharpness correlates with test error).** Yang et al.
  give counterexamples.
- **The anthology holds no task-arithmetic paper.** TIES-Merging's main
  baseline is therefore absent from both records.

## Gaps noticed

- **Vocabulary.** There is no word for statistical mechanics of learning,
  random-matrix theory, ensembling, pruning, or adversarial robustness.
  TIES-Merging argues about parameter updates, not connectivity. A second
  work like it would meet [ADR-027](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-027.md)'s condition for a separate
  `model-merging` word.
- **Unfilled works the readings lean on:**
  - Martin & Mahoney's heavy-tailed self-regularization (1810.01075),
    which Papyan ([LIT-617](../../record/literature.d/LIT-617.md)) names;
  - Fort & Jastrzębski on "tunnels";
  - Juneja et al. 2023;
  - Annesi et al. 2023;
  - Seung, Sompolinsky & Tishby 1992;
  - task arithmetic.
