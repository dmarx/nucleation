# CLAIM-182: Storage is not anchoring, addressability is: a structure that is kept but cannot be pointed at part by part does not stop re-derivation, and the same content becomes an anchor once its parts get addresses

## The claim

Both exchanges that this record reconstructed were stored the whole time,
as shared transcripts (workbench 2026/10/07/011440 and 2026/10/09/025539
give the links). The participants could have scrolled back. They
re-derived instead. The permissiveness objection came back six or more
times as though new ([ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md)). The passive/active distinction came back
after the manuscript dropped it ([CLAIM-004](CLAIM-004.md)). The relativity analogy came
back after it was rejected ([ARG-004](../arguments.d/ARG-004.md)). Storage did not stop any of it.

What changed things was giving the content addresses. The reconstructions
labelled every turn, U for the owner and A for the assistant, and filed the
recurring moves under codes. From then on a claim could cite "A125" or
[CLAIM-017](CLAIM-017.md), and a later entry could rest on it, object to it or supersede
it. The transcript's content did not change. Its parts became addressable.

The same holds for a manuscript draft ([CLAIM-178](CLAIM-178.md)). A draft persists,
but its claims cannot be pointed at one by one. Nothing rests on them, so
when a draft drops one, no reference breaks and nothing reports the loss.

So for anchoring ([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)), the trigger that matters is the one
that gives a thing an address. Persistence is necessary but not enough.

## What it does not say

- It does not say storage is useless. Without the transcripts nothing
  could have been reconstructed. Storage is what makes addressing possible
  later.
- It does not say an address is enough. A code that nothing points at
  anchors nothing. Stable identity and discoverability may matter too
  ([QUESTION-038](../questions.d/QUESTION-038.md)).
- The comparison is not controlled. Re-derivation inside a live exchange
  and citation after reconstruction happened at different times, for
  different reasons.
