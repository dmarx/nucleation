# LIT-tmph6kst: Zipf's Law for Cities: An Explanation

Gabaix (1999), Zipf's Law for Cities: An Explanation, Quarterly Journal of Economics 114(3), 739–767, DOI 10.1162/003355399556133. Newman ([LIT-2976](LIT-2976.md)) cites it, with two other works, for the point that "random multiplication processes can also generate perfect power-law distributions with only a slight modification: if there is a lower bound on the value that the product of a set of numbers is allowed to take … then the behaviour of the process is modified to generate not a log-normal, but a true power law".

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-22 while filing Newman's review, as a gap the filing found: the record's floor mechanism ([CLAIM-824](../claims.d/CLAIM-824.md)) and its question on the tail of city sizes ([QUESTION-506](../questions.d/QUESTION-506.md)) both turn on the mechanism Newman cites it for, and the record held no copy of it. Clauset, Shalizi and Newman ([LIT-2987](LIT-2987.md)) find the US city tail indistinguishable from a lognormal in cross-section, so [QUESTION-506](../questions.d/QUESTION-506.md) needs a dynamical account; whether this paper gives one in a testable form is for its reading to say. Not read.

barrier, is of the max form X = AX ∨ B, for which Goldie's Theorem 5.2,
as Kevei restates it (LIT-tmpk12vu, NOTE-tmpv02cb, THEORY-tmpsmrhx, p. 4),
gives P{X > x} ∼ c x^(−κ) with κ solving EA^κ = 1. Zipf's exponent 1
would then be the case κ = 1, E[A] = 1. Kevei does not cite Gabaix; the
link is the filer's. This work stays Deferred.
