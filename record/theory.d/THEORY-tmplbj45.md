# THEORY-tmplbj45: For a real scalar field with an analytic potential, every localized oscillating state except the 1+1 dimensional sine-Gordon breather radiates, at a rate exponentially small in its amplitude parameter but nonzero; in 3+1 dimensions such oscillons are stable only above a minimum amplitude and decay suddenly once radiation has carried them below it, after a few thousand oscillations at most, while in 1 and 2 dimensions the loss rate falls exponentially as the amplitude falls and they never decay

## Source

Fodor (2019), LIT-2197; read in NOTE-1698 (Chapter 2, the introduction and
the summary). Split from THEORY-1502 when the twenty-ninth status review
narrowed that entry to what the review derives.

## What was actually shown

The review states this thesis (Ch. 1, §2.7). What it derives is narrower,
and is THEORY-1502: no one-parameter family of exactly periodic localized
solutions shrinking to the vacuum except the sine-Gordon breathers (§2.3);
an exponentially small leading-order radiation (§2.9); and stability by
dimension from the core energy (§2.8). Against isolated solutions it offers
a counting argument, "Three conditions for each mode for coupled second
order equations is generally too much" (§2.7.1), and numerics: "the
existence of isolated breathers is unlikely, even for large amplitudes"
(§2.3).

## What this does not say

- Whether fine-tuned resonant oscillons are permanent, which Gleiser &
  Krackow (2019) argue and the review denies.
- Anything for non-analytic potentials, complex fields with a conserved
  charge, coupled environments or oscillatons.

## Note of 2026-11-06: what holds it back

The review derives the family result, not the absence of isolated breathers, which its own
numerics make only "unlikely"; and its leading radiation coefficient vanishes at isolated
parameters (ν3 = 0 at g5 = 0.26495, §2.9.4), where the next order it derives carries a factor
d − 1, so in one dimension the orders computed give no nonzero rate. The 1 and 2 dimensional
clause holds for small and moderate amplitudes only, and "a few thousand oscillations at most" is
stated, not derived. A proof for isolated solutions, or a source deriving a nonzero rate in those
cases, is what must come first.
