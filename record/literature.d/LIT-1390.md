# LIT-1390: The Majority Illusion in Social Networks

Lerman, K., Yan, X., & Wu, X.-Z. (2016). The "majority illusion" in social networks. *PLOS ONE*, 11(2), e0147617. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0147617. arXiv:1506.03022.

Read in full, [NOTE-1085](../notes.d/NOTE-1085.md). The summary was checked against the text and
stands.

## Why this is here

The owner's U39 asked how a perceived plurality of opinion, with no real one behind it, shapes an individual's perspective. A39 and A40 cited this as a mechanism. Bears on [CLAIM-415](../claims.d/CLAIM-415.md).

**Corrected 2026-10-22, from the reading.** The paper is a mechanism for
a perceived majority, but not for the one U39 described. Its mechanism is
degree: the attribute's holders are seen by many because they are
connected to many, not because they are loud, and each node sees its
neighbours correctly. It measures no belief, conformity or self-conception,
so it bears on [CLAIM-415](../claims.d/CLAIM-415.md)'s attributed collective and not on how the
attribution organizes a person. It cites Prentice and Miller ([LIT-1370](LIT-1370.md))
and offers itself as an explanation of pluralistic ignorance, but the two
are different errors: a skewed sample seen accurately, against a
misjudgement of what others hold. The structural finding is filed as
[THEORY-887](../theory.d/THEORY-887.md); it sits beside [THEORY-242](../theory.d/THEORY-242.md), whose model finds the
topological effect small under a different measure, without contradicting
it. See [NOTE-1085](../notes.d/NOTE-1085.md).
