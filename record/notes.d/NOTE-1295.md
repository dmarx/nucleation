# NOTE-1295: Reconceptualizing Individuals, Categories, and the Social Group: Self-Categorization Theory 2.0

## Contribution

It is the first sustained internal critique of the mechanism of
self-categorization theory, as opposed to its scope or its motivational
claims. It separates two things the theory ran together: seeing oneself
as a member of a category, and seeing oneself as part of a group. It
argues that meta-contrast cannot produce the second and is too costly to
produce the first in general. For this record it also does a second job.
It fixes the theory as the plain reading of Turner et al. 1987, chapter 3
and Oakes's chapter 6, and quotes the book's assumptions and hypotheses
with page numbers. That gives the record second-hand access to the
unread book ([LIT-1723](../literature.d/LIT-1723.md)).

## Key insight

A category is a set, and a set is internally differentiated, so it is
not a unitary thing. Making its members look alike, by accentuation,
makes them interchangeable, not one. "Trees" do not become a forest by
looking more like one another. A forest is picked out by its own
configuration, as a face is picked out by the arrangement of its parts
rather than their similarity. Groups, on this view, are perceived when
people are seen to be bound by shared mental states, not when they are
seen to be alike.

## Assumptions

- **What counts as SCT** (p. 2): the plain reading of Turner et al.
  (1987), chapter 3, with Oakes (1987), chapter 6, for salience. Turner
  et al. (1994) is "a supplementary statement" that does not alter the
  theory. Later work is about the theory, not constitutive of it.
- **Fodor's conceptual atomism and a language of thought** (pp. 31–39).
  The paper adopts these against prototype and inferential-role
  semantics. Much of critiques 4 and 5 stands or falls with them.
- **Modular, context-impenetrable perception** (pp. 35, 46), after
  Firestone & Scholl (2016) and Pylyshyn. If perception were
  context-penetrable, the argument that structural fit must operate only
  after perception would lose its force.
- **Tractability as a constraint on cognitive theory** (p. 25), after
  van Rooij (2008): a cognitive function must be computable in plausible
  time.

## Key results

**The book, as quoted (pp. 2–7).** Page numbers are Turner et al.
1987's.

- SCT is a theory of "the functioning of the social self-concept" and of
  "how individuals are able to act as a group" (p. 42).
- The self-concept is the "set of cognitive representations of self
  available to a person", and "any individual possesses multiple
  concepts of self" (p. 44).
- Self-categorizations are "cognitive groupings of oneself and some class
  of stimuli as the same ... in contrast to some other class of stimuli"
  (p. 44).
- There are "at least three levels of abstraction": human, in-group
  versus out-group, and personal (p. 45).
- Meta-contrast: "within any given frame of reference ... any collection
  of stimuli is more likely to be categorized as an entity ... to the
  degree that the differences between those stimuli on relevant
  dimensions of comparison ... are perceived as less than the differences
  between that collection and other stimuli" (pp. 46–47).
- Salience accentuates "intra-class similarities and inter-class
  differences" (p. 49). There is a "functional antagonism" between
  levels (p. 49).
- Depersonalization is "'self-stereotyping' whereby people come to
  perceive themselves more as the interchangeable exemplars of a social
  category" (p. 50). "The depersonalization of self-perception is the
  basic process underlying group phenomena", including "social
  stereotyping, group cohesiveness, ethnocentrism, cooperation and
  altruism, emotional contagion and empathy, collective action, shared
  norms and social influence processes" (p. 50). The book itself calls
  this the point of the whole argument.
- Groups form psychologically to the degree that differences among
  people are less than differences between them and others present
  (pp. 51–52).
- Salience is accessibility × fit (p. 54). Accessibility is set by input
  required, past learning and current motives (p. 55). Fit is structural
  (similarities and differences "correlate with [that] classification")
  and normative (p. 55).
- The group is seen "as one whole entity ... as if they were real and
  unitary psychological beings" (p. 3).

**Critique 1: categorization is neither sufficient nor necessary for
group entities (pp. 9–15).** Not sufficient: accentuation produces
perceived interchangeability, not unity. Oakes et al.'s (1994) own
analogy between kinds of vegetation and nationalities shows this, since
"trees" are a category, not a thing. Not necessary: families,
orchestras and governments are groups whose members are by definition
dissimilar. Resolution: groups are holistic perceptual entities,
analogous to faces, emerging from configural relations among members'
mental states.

**Critique 2: "self-categorization" is underspecified (pp. 15–20).**
"Self" is defined circularly and "stimuli" is overinclusive.
Resolution: self-categorization is the categorization of *intentional
agents*, and groups require *collective intentionality*. The evidence is
Skorich et al. (under review), four experiments described in one
paragraph (p. 20). Groups defined by mental states (chosen T-shirt
colour; seeing a duck or a rabbit) drew more in-group favouritism and
were tracked more in who-said-what tasks than groups defined by physical
properties (assigned T-shirt colour; digit ratio).

**Critique 3: the person level is underexamined (pp. 20–24).** Following
Mavor, persons are categories of "person aspects" (Alex at home, Alex at
university), and person and group processing should be equivalent by
default. Two studies are reported. Skorich & Mavor (2013): with memory
confounds removed, neither group nor person processing is privileged
under load. Skorich et al. (2021): individual identity is extracted from
faces as fast as age, race and gender, and shows SCT's prototypicality
effects.

**Critique 4: meta-contrast is implausible for entity formation
(pp. 24–33).**
- *Inapplicable to holistic entities*, which come from configuration,
  not similarity.
- *Computationally intractable.* Taken as a formula for selecting the
  best in/out split, it is a subset-selection problem with 2ⁿ
  candidates. At 1,000 operations a second, 5 people take about 32 ms, 25
  take about 9.3 hours, and 35 take about 13 months (pp. 25–26). The
  arithmetic checks: 2²⁵ ≈ 3.4 × 10⁷, 2³⁵ ≈ 3.4 × 10¹⁰. The obvious reply
  is that readiness and normative fit prune the candidates. The paper
  answers that pruning presupposes already-formed entities, so
  meta-contrast may still select among them (salience) but cannot form
  them (pp. 27–28).
- *Prototype semantics.* Prototypes do not compose. "Black woman" does
  not inherit the prototypes of "Black" (a Black man) and "woman" (a
  white woman), as Crenshaw's intersectionality requires. Context-specific
  prototypes cannot be known for a novel compound ("Gabonese-Inuit emu
  farmers") before meeting one (pp. 28–31).
- Resolution: Fodor's atomism. Entities are "fingers of instantiation"
  triggered by "abductive templates", bound to a mental token, with
  content in an "object file" (pp. 31–33).

**Critique 5: comparative and correlational relations are
overemphasized (pp. 34–40).** Context effects on perception are
weaker than SCT needs, so structural fit must work after perception, as
inference. Comparison is then one propositional relation among
indefinitely many. Resolution: a language of thought.

**SCT2.0, "Self-Conceptualization Theory" (pp. 40–45).**
- Kept from SCT: groups are psychologically real and part of the self;
  perception is dynamic; the same explananda.
- Changed: self-categorization is of intentional agents only; category
  membership is distinguished from group membership, and category
  effects (out-group homogeneity) from group effects (crowd behaviour).
- Meta-contrast has "little role" in entity formation or salience.
  Perception is "not inherently and universally comparative": an
  in-group need not appear against an out-group.
- Without comparison there is no need for strict levels, and so no
  "coherent notion of functional antagonism" (p. 44).
- SCT1.0 becomes a sub-theory: the special case of equivalence classes
  under comparison.

**The three sub-processes (pp. 46–58).**
- *Salience* is staged: an impenetrable perceptual triggering, then
  inference using goals and background knowledge.
- *Depersonalization* hides two shifts: away from the person (keep the
  word) and towards a group entity ("collectivization").
- *Prototypicality* is a match between contextually activated object-file
  content and a member. It need not be comparative ("students during
  spring break"). It applies to categories, not to group entities,
  whose members are parts, not exemplars. A part can only be *diagnostic*
  (Ronaldo for the Portuguese team) or *outsized* (Eisenhower).

## Concepts

- **SCT1.0 / SCT2.0 / S-ConcepT**: the 1987 theory; the proposed
  revision; its other name, "self-conceptualization theory".
- **intentional agent**: an object perceived to generate its own actions,
  and so to have a point of view, mental states and a sense of self
  (pp. 17–18).
- **collective intentionality**: intentionality spread across individual
  agents (Tomasello & Rakoczy 2003); held necessary for perceiving a
  group (p. 19).
- **category versus entity**: a set of separately instantiated things,
  versus a bounded, holistic, internally undifferentiated thing (p. 10).
- **person aspects**: the instances of one person across situations,
  after Mavor (p. 22).
- **FINST, abductive template, object file**: an attentional index
  triggered when a configuration of low-level features matches a stored
  holistic pattern, with an associated store of content (pp. 31–33).
- **collectivization**: the shift towards seeing oneself as part of a
  group entity, distinct from depersonalization, the shift away from the
  person (pp. 49–53).

## Connections

- **Turner et al. 1994 ([NOTE-1284](NOTE-1284.md)).** Skorich treats it as
  supplementary. Two of its points are the ones he attacks: metacontrast
  setting content and prototype (critiques 4–5), and the self as
  generated in context. He keeps the latter's dynamism but moves the
  context effects from perception to inference.
- **Hornsey 2008 ([NOTE-1253](NOTE-1253.md)).** Hornsey reports a softening of
  functional antagonism; Skorich's SCT2.0 removes it.
- **León 2023 ([NOTE-1309](NOTE-1309.md)).** Cited for the point that close
  relationships are groups without interchangeable members (p. 14). The
  two papers converge on that and diverge on the remedy: perceptual
  collective intentionality here, socially extended personhood there.
- **Collective intentionality.** [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md) already distinguishes I-mode
  from We-mode collective emotion by collectively intentional appraisal.
  Skorich makes collective intentionality the condition of perceiving a
  group at all. These are different claims that use the same notion.
- **Fodor and Pylyshyn.** The paper's positive theory imports conceptual
  atomism and the language of thought wholesale. The record holds no
  reading of them.
- **Self-models.** "Persons as categories of person aspects" sits near
  [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) (self-model) and [THEORY-691](../theory.d/THEORY-691.md) (self as abstractum) without
  engaging either.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-1114](../theory.d/THEORY-1114.md).** This is its strongest rival. Skorich accepts that
  self-categories vary with context, but denies that meta-contrast is the
  mechanism of entity formation and gives it "little role" in salience.
  The THEORY's promote_when already asks for a frame-only manipulation.
  This reading adds a sharper test: whether comparison effects survive
  when the comparison group is absent, which SCT2.0 says is possible and
  SCT1.0 does not. Noted on that THEORY.
- **Source of [THEORY-1112](../theory.d/THEORY-1112.md)** (Proposed): perceiving a group as an
  entity requires cues to shared mental states, not similarity.
- **[CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md) and [QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md).** Skorich's line between a category and a
  group entity cuts the fanbase case in two. "The fans" can be a category
  (people who support the club) without being a group entity, which
  would need perceived shared mental states (a chant, a boycott, a
  supporters' trust). His notion of an individual "diagnostic" or
  "outsized" in a group (Eisenhower) is an alternative to the
  prototypicality reading of a vocal minority. These are inferences, not
  claims of the paper.

## Limitations

- **Unreviewed.** A preprint. Its key evidence for the positive proposal
  (Skorich et al., under review) is not available and is summarized in
  one paragraph.
- **The intractability argument assumes exhaustive search.** It shows
  that meta-contrast cannot be computed by brute force over all subsets.
  It does not show that no tractable approximation, such as clustering
  on a similarity space, could do what the theory needs. The paper
  answers only the pruning reply.
- **The positive theory is heavily borrowed.** Conceptual atomism,
  context-impenetrable perception and the language of thought are each
  contested, and the paper does not defend them beyond citation.
- **The supplementary predictions were not read**, so how SCT2.0 would be
  tested is not recorded here.

## Open questions

- Do comparison-dependent effects on self-stereotyping and prototypicality
  appear when no comparison group is present, or does salience require
  one? This is the experiment that separates SCT1.0 from SCT2.0.
- Do mental-state groups reliably beat physical-property groups in
  favouritism and tracking? The under-review studies, once published and
  read, would settle C3 for the record.
