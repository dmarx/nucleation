# NOTE-tmp1125m: Three types of phenomenal consciousness and their functional roles: unfolding the ALARM theory of consciousness

## Contribution

It extends the authors' 2023 two-level ALARM theory with a third phenomenon,
reflexive self-consciousness, and argues that it is not a third kind of
experience but general alertness carrying metacognitive, self-relational
content. It sets out a function for each of the three (Table 1), and it argues
that IIT, higher-order theories and GNWT all leave out the evolutionarily
oldest form.

## Key insight

Ask what consciousness did when it first evolved, and the answer is not
integration, broadcast or monitoring but alarm: a felt signal that takes over
the whole organism when a homeostatic variable leaves its range, because a
reflex alone stops at withdrawal while a pain that persists keeps the injury
protected and teaches avoidance in one trial. Later forms are layered on
that, and they work only while the alarm is not overriding them.

## Assumptions

- **Phenomenal consciousness in wakefulness** is the target; vegetative
  states and Block's access/phenomenal distinction are set aside
  (Introduction).
- **Phenomenality is independent of content**: experience is explained by
  how a content is processed, not by what it is (Introduction; Vosgerau,
  Schlicht & Newen 2008).
- **Consciousness is an adaptation, or part of one**; epiphenomenalism is set
  aside without argument here (Introduction).
- **Different realisers across taxa**: birds and fish may be conscious with
  different neural mechanisms, so no single NCC cluster is expected
  (Introduction).
- **Opt-out paradigms indicate metacognition** (fn. 2).
- **The pattern theory of self**: no self as substance (fn. 4).

## Key results

These are positions argued, with cited evidence.

- **Basic arousal is subcortical ("Two framework observations", a).**
  Evidence cited: Merker's hydranencephalic children, who seem to have
  affective experiences of comfort and distress; Bjursten et al.'s cats
  decorticated from infancy, which orient by vision and touch and solve a
  T-maze visual discrimination; Redinbaugh et al. (2020), where central
  lateral thalamus stimulation in anaesthetised macaques works as an on–off
  switch to a state with pain and perceptual responses but no capacity for
  attention-demanding tasks. NCCs for basic arousal lie in the upper
  brainstem and thalamus. Merker's claim that this holds for all
  consciousness is rejected.
- **Four advantages of felt over unfelt alarm** (same section): pain keeps
  the hand away; it prompts care that supports healing; it generalises to new
  situations; it allows one-shot learning, which unconscious associative
  learning does not.
- **General alertness ("Two framework observations", b)**: attention-guided
  consciousness, the ability to select and switch between sensory signals;
  everyday human consciousness, and "clearly proven" in mice (citing Wimmer
  et al. 2015).
- **Reflexive self-consciousness can be tested without language
  ("Reflexive self-consciousness", a)**: mirror self-recognition at 18 months
  and in apes, elephants, dolphins and magpies; and opt-out metamemory tasks
  (Hampton 2001). The text describes the subjects of the latter as both
  "chimpanzees" and "monkeys"; Hampton's 2001 paper, by its title, used
  rhesus monkeys.
- **Reflexive self-consciousness is general alertness plus metacognitive
  self-relational content ("Reflexive self-consciousness", b).** Self-relational
  content comes with every sense of ownership, agency and perspectivity, and
  each develops from basic to complex forms. With basic arousal it is
  pre-reflexive self-consciousness (four- to nine-month-olds treat a live
  image of themselves differently from a mimicking other); with general
  alertness and metacognition it is reflexive (18-month mirror
  self-recognition, the 4-year explicit false-belief task). No third kind of
  experience is posited, on three grounds: parsimony, no phenomenological or
  neural evidence for one, and the neural overlap between attributing
  beliefs to oneself and to others.
- **Critique of three theories ("The underestimation of the plurality of
  functions").** All three make neocortex essential (IIT posterior, GNWT
  prefrontal, Lau's HOT prefrontal and parietal; citing the adversarial
  collaboration of Ferrante et al. 2025). IIT: on an a priori identity
  reading, integration is not a function of consciousness but what it is;
  its axioms are introspective and evolution plays no part in them; Φ is not
  constrained by cost; modular minds are a challenge. HOT: Rosenthal grants
  mental-state consciousness little or no function; Lau's perceptual reality
  monitoring either needs no experience (blindsight, dual visual streams) or
  amounts to general alertness. GNWT: a theory of access, giving a
  synchronic function (Dehaene's keep, compress, transfer) that the authors
  accept for general alertness, but with no phylogenetic account.
- **Functions ("The various functions of phenomenal consciousness"; Table
  1).** Basic arousal gives the extreme signal "100% weight" over slow
  homeostatic updating, with rigid programmes and no choice. General
  alertness organises costs by choice: holding a hot plate rather than
  dropping it at a greater cost. Reflexive self-consciousness weighs one's
  reputation: holding the plate to be seen bearing the pain. A "managerial
  analogy" orders them: each later manager works only while the older one
  does not override it.

## Concepts

- **basic arousal**: level-1 phenomenal consciousness; sensitivity to pain,
  pleasure and other basic affect without focused attention; identified with
  Denton's primordial emotions; realisable subcortically.
- **general alertness**: level-2 phenomenal consciousness; attention-guided,
  selective and goal-directed; cortical with thalamic modulation.
- **reflexive (self-)consciousness**: general alertness whose content is
  meta-representation of an agent's mindset, one's own or another's.
- **mindset**: the cluster of a person's mental states and processes
  (emotions, desires, beliefs).
- **self-relational content**: information about the agent produced alongside
  information about the world, in the senses of ownership, agency and
  perspectivity.
- **pre-reflexive self-consciousness**: self-relational content experienced
  with basic arousal only; self–other differentiation without an explicit
  self-representation (fn. 3).

## Limitations

- No new data; the subcortical case rests on three cited findings.
- What separates basic arousal from unconscious alarm is argued from felt
  pain's advantages, which presuppose that it is felt. No marker is offered
  that would tell a conscious alarm from an unconscious one in an animal.
- The critique treats GNWT as essentially prefrontal-cortical; a GNWT
  reading as connectome architecture (as in Maldarelli & Güntürkün) escapes
  it.
- The metamemory example is described inconsistently ("chimpanzees" and
  "monkeys").

## Open questions

- What finding would separate basic arousal from unconscious homeostatic
  alarm in a non-verbal animal?
- Does ALARM predict that one-shot avoidance learning is absent where basic
  arousal is absent, and is that testable in decorticate or thalamically
  silenced animals?
- If GNWT can be met without cortex, does ALARM's objection to it survive?
