# ARG-007: Stability does not entail viability, and the emergency regime keeps its operation and its governance while its transformation capacity degrades, so effective action and the capacity to govern among regimes are different achievements

## The inference

The viability distinction supplies the formal possibility; the emergency case
shows the three persistences separating; together they give two achievements
where the operative-will picture saw one.

## Critical questions

- **Is the case coherent?** Yes.
- **Does it separate what the conclusion says comes apart?** Yes, if the
  mapping holds.
- **The mapping premise.** Applying viability needs a constraint set K, and the
  paper says K is "always indexed" but gives no non-arbitrary way to choose the
  maintenance constraints for an agent's regulatory capacities. Unanswered,
  and it is the weakest step: "precarious" is relative to that choice.

## Note of 2026-10-18: a second instance, in training

A31 of the exchange of 2026-10-17: "acquiring capacities ≠ governing the
acquisition of capacities". A conventionally trained network gains capacities
while the governance of its updates stays with the optimizer and the trainer
([CLAIM-tmpw4w7a](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpw4w7a.md)). That is this argument's conclusion, effective action and
the capacity to govern among regimes as different achievements, in a case
where the second is held outside the system altogether. It is an instance,
not a new premise, and the mapping question above applies to it unchanged:
which capacities count as the network's to maintain.
