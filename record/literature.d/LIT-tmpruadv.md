# LIT-tmpruadv: Phase transition in a dryland shrub–grass ecosystem: Emergence through local trade-offs

Jingyao Sun, Kailiang Yu, Xinrong Li, Max Rietkerk, Paolo D'Odorico, Ning
Chen, Yanli Wang, Libin Zhang and Guirui Yu, "Phase transition in a dryland
shrub–grass ecosystem: Emergence through local trade-offs", *Proceedings of
the National Academy of Sciences* 123(46), e2618656123 (2026),
doi:10.1073/pnas.2618656123. CC BY 4.0.

## Key takeaways

*Taken from the abstract (Crossref's deposit), not a reading. Each item is
the authors' claim, unverified until the text is read.*

- **The frame.** "Phase transition theory, a classic paradigm in physics,
  links local interaction rules to emergent changes in macroscopic system
  states, offering a formal language for understanding cross-scale
  dynamics."
- **Three hallmarks.** "First, abrupt shifts separate different phases of
  deserts, shrublands, and grasslands. Second, shrublands and grasslands
  self-organize into Turing-like phase patterns, contrasting with
  homogeneous deserts. Third, special phenomena emerge near-transition
  threshold, including patch enlargement and abrupt decline at
  desert–vegetation interface, and fairy circles within the shrub–grass
  transition zone."
- **The mechanism.** A cellular automaton "built on a fundamental trade-off
  between extensive and intensive resource foraging strategies (EI
  trade-off)" shows "how kernels encoding local trade-offs integrate these
  seemingly disparate phenomena within a unified phase transition
  framework."
- **The check.** "this EI trade-off was empirically validated through
  analyses of plant functional traits and spatial patterns of plant
  patches."
- **The conclusion drawn.** "dryland ecosystem dynamics reflect universal
  self-organization principles."

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-11-14 at the owner's request. The record's nearest holdings
are on regime shifts and their warnings: Scheffer & Carpenter on

alternative states, Kadowaki and colleagues ([LIT-2165](LIT-2165.md), [THEORY-1468](../theory.d/THEORY-1468.md)); and,
in the record's own argument, the question of the dynamical transitions by
which distinctions come into being ([QUESTION-281](../questions.d/QUESTION-281.md)). None of these holds
spatial self-organization, pattern formation in vegetation or fairy
circles. This work would be the record's first on how a spatial pattern,
and not only an aggregate state, changes across a transition.

What the abstract leaves for the reading to settle:

- whether "phase transition" is used in the strict sense (a singularity in
  a macroscopic order parameter as a control parameter is varied, with
  scaling near it) or for any abrupt shift between states. A fold of the
  kind [THEORY-1315](../theory.d/THEORY-1315.md) describes is abrupt without being a phase transition in
  the first sense;
- what the control parameter is (aridity, rainfall, grazing) and whether
  the shifts are shown along it in the field or only in the model;
- whether the "Turing-like" patterns come from a scale-dependent feedback
  (short-range facilitation, long-range competition) or from competition
  alone, which the record's stand-in Martinez-Garcia and colleagues
  separate;
- how the trait analysis tests the trade-off and not only agrees with it.

The two stand-ins, read in its place, are listed in the curation entry of
2026-11-14. Neither restates the EI trade-off, the cellular automaton or
the shrub–grass data, so neither stands in for the paper's own result.
