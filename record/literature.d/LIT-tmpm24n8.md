# LIT-tmpm24n8: Property Rules, Liability Rules, and Inalienability: One View of the Cathedral

Guido Calabresi and A. Douglas Melamed (1972). "Property Rules, Liability Rules, and Inalienability: One View of the Cathedral". *Harvard Law Review* 85(6): 1089–1128. DOI-10.2307/1340059.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading (NOTE-tmpv28va).*

- Entitlement first: law decides "which of the conflicting parties will be
  entitled to prevail", then makes "second order decisions" about how the
  entitlement is protected (pp. 1090–92).
- A property rule gives "the seller a veto"; a liability rule lets an
  entitlement be destroyed on payment of "an objectively determined value";
  inalienability forbids transfer "between a willing buyer and a willing
  seller" (p. 1092). "most entitlements to most goods are mixed" (p. 1093).
- A fourth rule: the polluter entitled, but only under a liability rule
  (p. 1116).
- §V: charging the thief only the value taken "would be to convert all
  property rule entitlements into liability rule entitlements" (p. 1125);
  criminal sanctions deter "future attempts to convert property rules into
  liability rules" (p. 1126).
