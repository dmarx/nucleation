# THEORY-335: Selective accessibility, the account that comparing a target with a plausible anchor makes anchor-consistent knowledge more accessible and that this knowledge carries anchoring, fits the moderations of the effect by applicability, representativeness and plausibility that Strack and Mussweiler found, but its accessibility step is inferred, not measured: Harris et al.'s repetition of the recognition test of it, four times by lexical decision and once by continuous identification, found nothing in five preregistered experiments, with tasks that also missed direct picture priming

## Source

Strack & Mussweiler (1997), [LIT-1064](../literature.d/LIT-1064.md), read in [NOTE-877](../notes.d/NOTE-877.md). Harris et al.
(2019), [LIT-1067](../literature.d/LIT-1067.md), read in [NOTE-876](../notes.d/NOTE-876.md). Mussweiler & Strack (1999), [LIT-1078](../literature.d/LIT-1078.md),
is Unavailable. Mussweiler & Strack 2000a, the studies Harris et al.
replicate, is not held; what is said of it is Harris et al.'s account.

## What was actually shown

**The account.** The comparison is solved by testing whether the target
has the anchor value, which builds a model of the target from
anchor-consistent knowledge; that knowledge stays accessible for the
absolute estimate. In the abstract's words, "anchoring is a special case of
semantic priming". The paper proposes it for plausible anchors; for
implausible ones it allows an adjustment-like route.

**The moderations (Strack and Mussweiler, N = 32, 32, 67).** With the
anchor number fixed, the effect was large when the estimate was on the
dimension compared and small when it was on another (Study 1, interaction
F(1, 28) = 10.50, n = 8 per cell), and reversed to contrast when the
estimate was about a very different object (Study 2, interaction
F(1, 28) = 6.30). With plausible anchors the comparison was slower and the
estimate faster than with implausible ones (Study 3, interaction
F(1, 66) = 72.81). A numeric-priming account predicts none of these, and
the paper argues against it on that ground. No study measures what was
retrieved.

**The direct test, repeated.** Harris et al. ran Mussweiler and Strack
2000a's test (does a high or low anchor speed recognition of
anchor-consistent words?) five times, preregistered, N = 128 each, four by
lexical decision and one by continuous identification. The anchor ×
word-type interaction was null every time (η²G ≤ .004), and an exploratory
meta-analytic Bayes factor found the data "21.10 times more likely under
the null hypothesis than the alternative". The originals had interactions
at p = .043 and .016, with none of four simple effects significant. Then
the same tasks failed to detect direct picture priming whose manipulation
check was strong. The authors' conclusions: "these are not good signature
tests", the results do not "necessarily provide additional evidence for or
against that theory, which we still consider a good explanation", and past
support from the method "is not a strong part of the evidence base".

## What this does not say

- **That selective accessibility is refuted.** The failed test cannot
  detect priming at all, so its null says nothing about retrieval either
  way. What is rejected is the earlier lexical-decision result as evidence,
  not the account.
- **That accessibility has been shown.** Every remaining line of support
  infers it: moderation by applicability and representativeness (small
  samples), latencies (which time the estimate, not what is retrieved),
  coded free reports ([LIT-1078](../literature.d/LIT-1078.md) and 2000a, unread), and an overlap result
  whose explanations, Harris et al. note, rest on "presumably". Harris et
  al.'s continued preference for the theory rests on its fit with
  hypothesis testing and priming, a theoretical virtue, not evidence.
- **That accessibility fixes the judgement.** The account has two stages.
  Accessibility is "not sufficient to guarantee its use and determine the
  direction of its influence", and Study 2 turned the same activated
  knowledge into contrast. A frame acts on retrieval and on evaluation, and
  these designs do not separate the two.
- **That it covers all anchoring.** Implausible anchors anchored at least
  as strongly, and the paper assigns them to adjustment. Insufficient
  adjustment is not ruled out. Harris et al.'s leading account of durable
  anchoring, a provisional answer formed at the comparison and recalled, is
  neutral between mechanisms.
- **That the retrieval is selection from a fixed store.** The paper's
  mechanism is construction: judges "retrieve or generate" and "construct a
  mental model"; the biasing information "is generated by judges
  themselves".
- **That hypothesis-consistent retrieval trades accuracy now for bias
  later.** The paper says the positive test strategy "seems to generate
  information that improves the validity of comparative judgments but
  distorts subsequent judgments for which no specific hypothesis has been
  provided". That is the authors' reading of their model, not tested.
