# THEORY-1952: In CLINIC v. Rubio the Southern District of New York held that the State Department's 2026 policy ordering consular officers to refuse immigrant visas to nationals of 75 countries, whatever individualized assessment found of their eligibility, made those determinations irrelevant to the outcome, was contrary to the bar on nationality discrimination and to the rule that refusals rest on grounds the law sets out, and exceeded the Secretary of State's authority, and vacated it with the refusals based solely on it

CLINIC v. Rubio, ECF No. 83, [LIT-2727](../literature.d/LIT-2727.md).

## The holding

"The Court holds that the Policy is contrary to law and was issued in
excess of Secretary Rubio's statutory authority" (p. 2): it requires
officers "to discriminatorily refuse visas solely on the basis of the
applicant's nationality" (p. 34), mandates the refusal of eligible
applicants against § 1201(g) and 22 C.F.R. § 40.6, and takes from consular
officers a decision § 1104(a) gives them. "The outcome is predetermined"
(p. 42). The Policy and the refusals based solely on it are vacated.

## What it does not show

- That the Policy violated the public-charge provision: the court held it
  did not (p. 32).
- That notice-and-comment was required: judgment for the Government.
- Anything about the Policy's moral justification, or the ruling's fate on
  appeal.

## Note of 2026-11-18: what holds it back
