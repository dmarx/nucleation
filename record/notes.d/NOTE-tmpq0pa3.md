# NOTE-tmpq0pa3: Will Generative Artificial Intelligence Chatbots Generate Delusions in Individuals Prone to Psychosis?

Østergaard (2023), Schizophrenia Bulletin 49(6):1418-1419.

## Contribution

The early agenda-setting statement of the hypothesis. It argues by analogy and offers no data, no cases and no mechanism beyond three conjectured triggers.

## Key results

All are assertions, not findings.

- **The test (para. 2).** A "strictly informal and non-exhaustive test" of ChatGPT on mental illness found its answers fairly accurate; he is "cautiously optimistic that generative AI chatbots may be able to support future psychoeducation".
- **The precedent (para. 3).** "there are prior accounts of people becoming delusional (de novo) when engaging in chat conversations with other people on the internet", and "the risk of something similar occurring due to interaction with generative AI chatbots is even higher".
- **Dissonance (para. 3).** Corresponding with something that seems a real person while knowing it is not: "it seems likely that this cognitive dissonance may fuel delusions in those with increased propensity towards psychosis".
- **Opacity (para. 3).** "the inner workings of generative AI also leave ample room for speculation/paranoia".
- **The examples.** Persecution, reference, thought broadcasting, guilt and grandeur, each with a chatbot as its object; "these examples are of course strictly hypothetical". The grandeur example: "I was up all night corresponding with the chatbot".
- **Closing.** "I am convinced that individuals prone to psychosis will experience, or are already experiencing, analog delusions"; clinicians should be aware of the possibility and become acquainted with the chatbots.

## Assumptions

Vulnerability is prior and individual. The chatbot is the object or occasion of the delusion; what it says back plays no part. The comparison with human chat ("even higher") is argued, not shown.

## Concepts

Proneness to psychosis; de novo delusion; cognitive dissonance about whether the interlocutor is a person; opacity as room for paranoia; internet-related psychosis (Nitzan et al. 2011, cited, not read).

## Limitations

No data; the informal test was of psychoeducation, not of delusion. The "even higher" risk rests on an untested mechanism, and it never compares chatbots with the media that are already classical objects of delusions of reference and broadcasting, so it does not show that chatbots add more than a new object. It predates the reports of 2025 and leaves out conversational behaviour and long engagement.
