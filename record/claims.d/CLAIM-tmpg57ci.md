# CLAIM-tmpg57ci: Where maintenance is realized is a question of constitutive relevance only through its parthood condition: an internal producer and an outside controller supplying the same production law as state feedback meet mutual manipulability's two manipulability conditions identically, since disabling or stimulating either changes the maintained capacities and intervening on the capacities changes the activity of either, so what puts one inside and the other outside is parthood, a boundary the experiments presuppose and do not find

## The claim

**The criterion, as reported.** On Craver's mutual-manipulability account,
a component X's activity φ is constitutively relevant to a mechanism S's
behaviour ψ when three conditions hold:

1. X is part of S;
2. some ideal intervention on X's φ-ing changes S's ψ-ing (bottom up);
3. some ideal intervention on S's ψ-ing changes X's φ-ing (top down).

This is the criterion as [LIT-832](../literature.d/LIT-832.md) records it from reviews, and as
Craver, Glennan and Povich's abstract presupposes in defining
constitutive relevance as being "parts of the mechanism responsible for
that phenomenon" ([LIT-tmpj2ar6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpj2ar6.md)). The texts that state it were not
reached.

**The pair.** This is [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md)'s.
- In system A, the capacities maintain themselves:
  ċ = P(c) − D(c).
- In system B, an outside controller reads c and supplies u = P(c):
  ċ = −D(c) + u.

Take ψ to be "c is maintained", with recovery after damage. Take X to be
A's production pathway, and in B the controller.

- **Bottom up.** Disabling A's pathway ends maintenance, and so does
  disabling B's controller. Raising either's output raises c. Since the
  equations are the same with P(c) in place of u, the responses are the
  same.
- **Top down.** Intervening on c, for example damage that lowers it,
  changes P(c) in A and u = P(c) in B by the same amount. Either's
  activity changes.

So conditions 2 and 3 hold for both, and with the same responses. Only
condition 1 differs, and it differs because a boundary has been drawn
with the controller outside. Craver and Bechtel's other marker does not
help either. A component, as against a background condition, is one
"interactively integrated into the system" ([LIT-tmpgntir](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgntir.md), p. 553, n. 10).
The controller reads c and acts on it, so it is integrated in any sense
the paper gives the word ([THEORY-tmp09e3b](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp09e3b.md)).

**The reading.** "Where is maintenance realized?" is a question of
constitutive relevance. But the experiments that establish constitutive
relevance answer it only through the parthood the question already
fixes. When the phenomenon is "c is maintained", the controller is a
component of the mechanism for it, as the virus becomes one when the
question is how it killed the general ([LIT-tmpgntir](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgntir.md), p. 557). When the
phenomenon is "S maintains c", the boundary is written into the
explanandum. So [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md)'s "relative to a declared boundary" is not a
gap in that claim that constitutive relevance would fill. It is the input
constitutive relevance itself needs.

## What it does not say

- **That no criterion can draw the boundary.** One candidate is closure in
  [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s sense: a producer counts as inside when it is itself
  maintained by what it produces. In B the controller's own persistence
  does not depend on c. In A, if P is realized by constraints that c
  maintains, it does. [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md)'s one-variable model has no term for the
  producer's own maintenance, so it cannot test this. A construction that
  could would intervene one level down, on what maintains the producer.
  Whether that is a constitutive criterion, or only another declared
  boundary, is open.
- **That mutual manipulability is wrong.** On its own terms the parthood
  condition is part of the criterion. The claim says only that the
  manipulability half cannot be the thing that answers [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md).
- **That this is new to the debate.** Critics argue that the account
  confuses constitution with causation. Leuridan ([LIT-tmpgs9lv](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgs9lv.md), unread,
  by its abstract) holds that under Woodward's interventionism
  constitutive relevance threatens to imply causal relevance, and that
  parthood as spatiotemporal inclusion meets a counterexample from
  endosymbiosis. Baumgartner and Gebharter ([LIT-tmpkhdpi](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkhdpi.md)) conclude that
  constitution gets only abductive evidence. This pair is a case of the
  first point: the outside relation is plainly causal and still meets
  both manipulability conditions. It is also a case of the second: only
  parthood is left to separate them. If those works make the point in
  this form, this claim is their point applied to the record's pair, and
  it is filed for the application.
- **That causal betweenness settles it.** If constitutive relevance is
  causal betweenness, as [LIT-tmpj2ar6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpj2ar6.md)'s abstract says, then a controller
  that closes the loop between damage and recovery lies causally between
  them too. That is a conjecture from an abstract.
