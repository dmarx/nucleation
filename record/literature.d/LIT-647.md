# LIT-647: Neural traveling waves in cortex: Network mechanisms and potential roles in neural computation

Lyle Muller, Alexandra N. Busch, Zachary W. Davis and John H. Reynolds (2026), *Neuron* 114(17):3156–3174 — DOI-10.1016/j.neuron.2026.06.019

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the abstract and keywords, not a reading. What follows is
what the abstract says.*

- Neural traveling waves, first found in anaesthetised animals, are now
  seen throughout the brains of awake animals, where they "influence
  neural excitability and behavior". They can arise in ongoing activity or
  be triggered by sensory input and behavioural events.
- By structuring activity within a cortical region, they introduce
  "spatiotemporal dependencies across sensory maps that are not naturally
  captured by purely feedforward or feedback processing".
- The review defines the waves, outlines circuit mechanisms that generate
  them (its keywords name horizontal and patchy connections), and argues
  that waves can implement computations such as predicting upcoming input
  by embedding sensory history in a region's evolving activity. It closes
  with a conceptual framework for "spatiotemporal, generative processing"
  by waves travelling over sensory maps.

Where it would bear, once read:

- **Xu et al. ([LIT-638](LIT-638.md), [NOTE-502](../notes.d/NOTE-502.md))** find rotating waves across
  the whole cortex in fMRI and cite Davis, Muller et al. (2020) on
  spontaneous travelling waves gating perception in behaving primates.
  This review covers waves within single areas, measured
  electrophysiologically. A reading should say whether it treats rotating
  waves and phase singularities, and whether it takes the fMRI spirals as
  the same phenomenon.
- **Verzhbinsky et al. ([LIT-633](LIT-633.md))** put long-range coordination in
  zero-lag synchronous ripples. A reading should note what the review
  says about synchrony against propagation.
- **Pattern formation.** Cross and Hohenberg ([LIT-527](LIT-527.md)) class travelling
  waves as type I_o instabilities, and Turing ([LIT-535](LIT-535.md)) has them as his
  case (e). The keyword "horizontal connections" suggests the review's
  mechanism is the conduction delay along cortical axons, not a
  reaction–diffusion instability; a reading should check.

**Priority for a reading: moderate.** It is the batch's only review, and
the only work here on the computational role of waves.
