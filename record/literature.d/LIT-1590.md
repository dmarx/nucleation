# LIT-1590: Changes in Endosymbiont Complexity Drive Host-Level Compensatory Adaptations in Cicadas

Campbell, M. A., Łukasik, P., Meyer, M. C., Buckner, M., Simon, C., Veloso, C., Michalik, A. & McCutcheon, J. P. (2018). Changes in Endosymbiont Complexity Drive Host-Level Compensatory Adaptations in Cicadas. *mBio* 9(6): e02104-18. doi:10.1128/mBio.02104-18.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmp6a57o.*

- **The problem.** With random transmission, the cells needed to give
  every egg every *Hodgkinia* lineage grow steeply as lineages multiply
  and grow uneven: over 500 per egg for one cell of each of
  *T. chilensis*'s six lineages, 4 to 5 times more at *Magicicada*'s
  complexity.
- **The host's answer.** *Hodgkinia* cells per egg range from 4,889 in
  a single-lineage cicada to 30,154 in *M. septendecim*; *Sulcia* stays
  between 2,572 and 5,643. Only *M. septendecim* differs significantly
  (THEORY-tmpjel7l).
- **Transmission complete.** Every sampled egg carried every tracked
  lineage, and eggs share their mother's lineage proportions. Rare losses
  could escape detection, the authors say.
- **Same route.** Under the light microscope, transmission looks the same
  in a three-lineage *Tettigades* and in *Magicicada*.
- **A possible feedback.** Larger transmitted populations mean weaker
  bottlenecks, which the authors speculate could reduce host control and
  feed further splitting.

## Why this is here

A2 listed "host compensation" ("selection to maintain collective functionality", giving "transport, regulation, or metabolic rescue") as a mechanism, and named no work for it. This is a documented case of the host compensating at the level of transmission, so it bears on reproductive cohesion (R) in [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md). The reading finds that the host maintains cohesion as the parts multiply, rather than raising it, and that the authors suspect the same change may weaken the host's control.
