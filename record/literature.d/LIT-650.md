# LIT-650: Conscious birds

Gianmarco Maldarelli and Onur Güntürkün (2025), *Philosophical Transactions
of the Royal Society B* 380(1939):20240308, theme issue "Evolutionary
functions of consciousness" — DOI-10.1098/rstb.2024.0308

## Key takeaways

- **Report without cortex.** In crows trained to report whether a
  near-threshold stimulus appeared, single neurons in the nidopallium
  caudolaterale (NCL), the functional analogue of mammalian prefrontal
  cortex, fired with the *report*, whether or not a stimulus was shown, and
  were silent on "not seen" reports. A later study found separate NCL
  populations coding reported presence and reported absence. The NCL is
  nuclear, not layered: the cortex-like layers and columns found in birds
  (Stacho et al. 2020) are confined to sensory pallium. So, if birds are
  conscious, a correlate of conscious report does not need laminar
  architecture.
- **Theories as checklists for a different brain.** The pigeon forebrain
  connectome has a few densely interlinked hubs (NCL and four others)
  joined to local modules, the arrangement global neuronal workspace theory
  (GNWT) requires, so GNWT's anatomical requirements are "largely met".
  Recurrent connectivity in the visual pallium and NCL meets recurrent
  processing theory's (RPT's) anatomy, though recurrence was never switched
  off experimentally. IIT's requirements are unmet "(yet)": nobody has
  measured the perturbational complexity index (PCI) in birds or found the
  grid-like microcolumn lattice IIT is linked to in mammals.
- **The mark test underrates birds.** Only magpies (contested by a failed
  replication) and Indian house crows pass it. Pigeons and roosters fail it,
  but pigeons under food competition, and roosters warning of a predator,
  treat their mirror image differently from a conspecific. The authors read
  self-awareness as graded, multidimensional and fitted to each species'
  ecology.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, with Newen & Montemayor
([LIT-626](LIT-626.md)) from the same theme issue, for the record's holdings on
animal consciousness and its theories. No anthology topic holds it, and it
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.

It is the record's first work on consciousness in a non-mammalian brain and
its first direct use of GNWT and RPT on an animal. It connects as follows.

- **Butlin et al. ([LIT-056](LIT-056.md))** derive "indicator properties" from the same
  theories (RPT-1–2, GWT-1–4) and apply them to AI systems. This paper makes
  the same theory-heavy move on a bird, without using their list. The
  parallel is instructive: the paper finds the two functionalist theories'
  requirements met by a brain built differently from ours, which is what a
  computational functionalist would expect. That is my reading, not the
  authors' claim.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)** says AI consciousness cannot currently be settled. Its
  `promote_when` asks for a comparative programme across animals in which a
  biological property predicts where consciousness markers fall. Avian
  evidence of this kind is that programme's raw material. The NCL result
  counts against cortical lamination as such a property. It says nothing
  either way about life or metabolism, the properties Seth's biological
  naturalism ([LIT-135](LIT-135.md)) turns on.
- **IIT 3.0 ([LIT-445](LIT-445.md)) and Barrett et al. ([LIT-185](LIT-185.md)).** The paper proposes PCI
  as "a good substitute" for Φ in birds. Barrett et al. argue that PCI is a
  proxy, not an approximation, capturing at most two of IIT's six axioms. So
  even a positive avian PCI would test IIT less directly than this paper
  implies.
- **Newen & Montemayor ([LIT-626](LIT-626.md))**, in the same issue, read GNWT as
  needing prefrontal cortex and argue that basic consciousness is subcortical.
  This paper reads GNWT as a connectome property that a nuclear pallium can
  meet. The two disagree about what GNWT requires. Neither cites the other.
  The two also use the mirror test differently: Newen & Montemayor count
  magpies among the species that pass it, while this paper notes a failed
  replication and doubts the test.
- **Veit ([LIT-105](LIT-105.md))** argues for a gradualist, evolutionary approach to animal
  consciousness. This paper ends on the same gradualism ("not ... an
  'all-or-nothing' cognitive function") and the same adaptationist
  inference. It is consistent with Veit's approach but offers no account of
  why consciousness evolved, only evidence of where it is.

No relation is declared. It tests no theory the record holds, it compares
itself against nothing the record holds, and it argues for no account of
consciousness that another entry excludes.
