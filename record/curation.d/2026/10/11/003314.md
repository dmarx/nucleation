The owner asked to connect the self as a multi-agent system to
synchronization through temporal discounting, mindfulness and flow (the
workbench entry of the same date). The record held the discounting side,
Ainslie and Schelling read, and the mindfulness side through ACT. It held
nothing on flow, and no study of mindfulness and discounting. Five works
were registered so that the claims made in that session cite something,
and each is marked as the weakest footing.

## What entered

All registered Deferred and unread, with metadata checked against Crossref:

- **Nakamura & Csikszentmihalyi (2002), "The Concept of Flow"**
  ([LIT-tmpwvter](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpwvter.md), doi:10.1093/oso/9780195135336.003.0007). The anchor for
  flow. Paywalled; no lawful open copy was found.
- **Dietrich (2004), "Neurocognitive mechanisms underlying the experience
  of flow"** ([LIT-tmpqpq9d](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpqpq9d.md), doi:10.1016/j.concog.2004.07.002). Transient
  hypofrontality, the rival reading of flow's effortlessness.
- **Bruya (ed.) (2010), *Effortless Attention*** ([LIT-tmpjoaq1](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpjoaq1.md),
  doi:10.7551/mitpress/9780262013840.001.0001). The phenomenon that
  effort-cost accounts of control must meet.
- **Dixon et al. (2019), brief mindfulness and momentary impulsivity**
  ([LIT-tmp8dmxy](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp8dmxy.md), doi:10.1016/j.jcbs.2018.11.003). n = 23; discounting fell
  after a 5-minute exercise. Abstract known only as summarised on the
  repository record page.
- **Ge et al. (2024), brief online mindfulness, RCT** ([LIT-tmpz3v2y](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpz3v2y.md),
  doi:10.1007/s12671-024-02479-3). As summarised: some impulsive behaviour
  reduced, discounting not improved, nothing sustained.
