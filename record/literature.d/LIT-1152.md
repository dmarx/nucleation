# LIT-1152: Extending Lipschitz functions via random metric partitions

James R. Lee and Assaf Naor (2005), "Extending Lipschitz functions via random metric partitions", *Inventiones Mathematicae* 160(1): 59–95 — [doi:10.1007/s00222-004-0400-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-004-0400-5)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-927](../notes.d/NOTE-927.md).*

- **The method (Lemma 2.1, Theorem 4.1).** A K-gentle partition of unity
  gives extensions of constant 3K into any Banach space, linear in the
  map. (ε, δ)-padded random partitions at every scale give K = O(1/(εδ)),
  and Lemma 3.8 moves them from X to any superspace.
- **Absolute extendability.** ae(X), the extension constant over every
  superspace and every Banach target, depends on X alone. Theorem 5.1:
  ae(X) ≤ C log λ(X) for doubling X; ≤ C r² for K_r-minor-free graph
  metrics; ≤ C(g+1) on genus-g surfaces; e(ℓ₂ᵈ, Z) ≤ C√d.
- **n points (Theorem 1.10).** ae(X) ≤ C log n/log log n, improving
  Johnson, Lindenstrauss and Schechtman's C log n by spreading the
  smoothing over about log log n scales. For Banach targets only.
- **Hilbert domains (Theorem 1.12).** eₙ(L_p, Z) ≤ C(p)(log n)^{1/p} for
  1 < p ≤ 2, C√(log n) at p = 2, by Johnson–Lindenstrauss dimension
  reduction to d of order log n and e(ℓ₂ᵈ, Z) ≤ C√d, averaged over the
  random map.
- **A lower bound (Lemma 1.13, sketched).** eₙ(L₂, L_p) ≥
  C(log n/log log n)^{(p−2)/p²} for 2 < p < ∞, so supₙ eₙ(L₂, Z) can be
  infinite.
- **Open (§1.4).** Whether eₙ(X, Z) = O(√(log n)) for every metric X and
  normed Z: "All known lower bounds fail to beat the '√(log n) barrier.'"
- **Scope.** Constants are universal and unspecified; Theorem 1.10 does
  not extend to barycentric targets; the excluded-minor results are
  sketched; separated domains are not discussed.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-16 as later work on the extension problem of Johnson and
Lindenstrauss's Theorem 1 ([THEORY-392](../theory.d/THEORY-392.md)), and read the same day in
[NOTE-927](../notes.d/NOTE-927.md). The registration placed it as the work on the sharpness
Johnson and Lindenstrauss left open. The reading corrects that. For maps
from n points into Hilbert space the paper's bound, C log n/log log n, is
weaker than their K√(log n); what it improves is the bound for arbitrary
Banach targets, and it states the √(log n) question as open in a wider
form. Its √(log n) for Hilbert domains into any Banach space (Theorem
1.12) is the dual of their Theorem 1 and uses their lemma. It says nothing
about separated domains ([THEORY-398](../theory.d/THEORY-398.md)); the reading notes that
Benyamini's 4D/ε there is itself an absolute extendability bound that the
paper's §1.2 overlooks. Filed as [THEORY-396](../theory.d/THEORY-396.md) and [THEORY-400](../theory.d/THEORY-400.md).
For [QUESTION-120](../questions.d/QUESTION-120.md) it is marginal: the random linear map in Theorem
1.12 needs its lower side for every pair and its upper side only on
average. The paper is mathematics, not ML practice.
