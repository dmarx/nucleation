# THEORY-1332: In Bishop's account, chaos is a property of deterministic dynamics, so sensitive dependence is not indeterminism; under determinism it may model deliberation but is not sufficient for incompatibilist freedom, and under indeterminism the incompatibilist (Kane) must still show that agents can harness amplified indeterminism; and amplification itself may fail, since damping, the sign of the local Lyapunov exponents when a perturbation arrives, and the brain's parallel redundancy limit it

## Source

Bishop, "Chaos", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, substantive
revision of 11 October 2024, [LIT-1961](../literature.d/LIT-1961.md), §§4, 6.1, 6.4; read in
[NOTE-1535](../notes.d/NOTE-1535.md).

## What was actually shown

An argument, not data.

- **Determinism.** "Mathematically, chaos is a property of dynamical
  systems which are deterministic"; Popper's step from unpredictability to
  indeterminism "identifies determinism (an ontological property) with
  predictability (an epistemic property)" (§6.1).
- **Amplification is conditional.** The SD argument needs a settled
  interpretation of QM; "damping due to friction can place constraints on
  how quickly amplification … takes place before being completely washed
  out"; a perturbation arriving "when the local Lyapunov exponents are
  negative" will "dissipate rather than amplify" (§4).
- **The brain.** "Large amounts of parallel redundancy in brain regions and
  neural networks make changes in one neuron's firing unlikely to be
  amplified to any appreciable degree" (§6.4).
- **Free will.** Under determinism, SDIC "wouldn't be sufficient for
  incompatibilist notions of freedom"; under indeterminism, "the challenge
  remains for incompatibilists, such Robert Kane (1996), to demonstrate
  agents can effectively harness such indeterminism" (§6.4).

## What this does not say

- **That harnessing is impossible**, or that Kane's account fails; the
  challenge is stated as open.
- **That the brain is not chaotic.** "The presence of chaos in the brain …
  is an empirical matter that's hotly debated" (§6.4).
- **What harnessing would require.** The entry gives no criterion; the
  record's attempt at one is [CLAIM-533](../claims.d/CLAIM-533.md)'s coupling of control to the
  amplifying directions.
- **Evidence for the redundancy point**, which is uncited.
