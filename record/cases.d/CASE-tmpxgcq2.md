# CASE-tmpxgcq2: Locked-in syndrome: consciousness preserved with anarthria and quadriplegia; in the classic form, vertical eye movement and blinking remain and carry communication, and in the completely locked-in state no motor channel remains

## The case

Locked-in syndrome "consists of anarthria and quadriplegia while
consciousness is preserved. Classically, vertical eye movements or blinking
allow coded communication. Given appropriate medical care, patients can
survive for decades" (Bruno et al. 2011, [LIT-tmp8g2to](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8g2to.md)). The literature
distinguishes classic, incomplete and total forms (Laureys et al. 2005,
[LIT-tmpx97h7](../literature.d/LIT-tmpx97h7.md), not read). In the completely locked-in state (CLIS),
usually reached through ALS, no motor channel remains.

- **Lucid life on one eyelid.** Bauby dictated *Le scaphandre et le
  papillon* ([LIT-tmppvr8q](../literature.d/LIT-tmppvr8q.md)) by blinking his left eyelid as a partner
  read out an alphabet.
- **Lucid over years.** Of 65 chronic patients with usable answers in
  Bruno et al.'s survey, 47 reported happiness, and a longer time in LIS
  correlated with happiness. The survey was self-selected and answered
  through the spared channels.
- **The completely locked-in state.** Kübler and Birbaumer
  ([LIT-tmpxcw33](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxcw33.md)): none of seven CLIS patients regained yes/no
  communication by BCI. They proposed that goal-directed thinking is
  extinguished when contingency between intention and outcome is lost, and
  that "a single CLIS patient who learns to communicate with a BCI or any
  other communication method will disprove our hypothesis". Chaudhary et
  al. ([LIT-tmpzuemc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzuemc.md)) report such a patient, who spelled phrases with
  intracortical signals after auditory neurofeedback training. The
  authors' earlier CLIS papers were retracted after a DFG misconduct
  finding, and outside researchers urged caution about this one.

## What it can show

- **Classic LIS separates lucid judgement from limb and speech action,
  not from action.** Friston et al.'s action is first of all eye movement:
  their simulations are of oculomotor control ([LIT-tmp670rc](../literature.d/LIT-tmp670rc.md)). So
  classic LIS leaves active inference the action it models first, and
  cannot by itself decide [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) against it. It can show that
  judgement keeps a built model working, over years, with world-directed
  action reduced to a trickle.
- **CLIS is the test, and it is asymmetric.** Evidence of lucidity in CLIS
  has to cross some channel, and a channel the patient drives is an action
  in active inference's sense. Chaudhary et al.'s patient communicated
  only once neurofeedback restored a contingency between intention and
  outcome. So the case can show the loop running down (Kübler and
  Birbaumer's prediction, [CLAIM-tmpki5gp](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpki5gp.md)), but it cannot show judgement
  running with no action at all, which is [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md)'s defeater (b)
  turned around. This is the evidence-for-experience line's standing
  result applied to the case: "Every kind of evidence the record holds
  about experience reaches it through access" ([QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)).
  Command-following, the usual marker, marks access, not experience
  ([THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md)). No-report markers still have to be validated through
  report or executive control ([THEORY-298](../theory.d/THEORY-298.md)).
- **Command-following by brain imaging.** Owen et al. ([LIT-tmp22anc](../literature.d/LIT-tmp22anc.md), read
  in [NOTE-tmpx40wa](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpx40wa.md)): a patient who met the criteria for the vegetative
  state, five months after injury, imagined playing tennis or moving
  around her home on request, and responded "through her brain activity,
  rather than through speech or movement". Monti et al. ([LIT-tmpp8ob6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpp8ob6.md), read
  in [NOTE-tmpy3j64](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpy3j64.md)): 5 of 54 patients with disorders of consciousness
  modulated their brain activity at will. Two "remained behaviorally
  unresponsive at the bedside, even after the functional MRI results were
  known", but they were not asked questions. The one patient who answered
  yes/no questions, 5 of 6 correctly, showed on retesting "reproducible,
  but inconsistent, responses indicative of a minimally conscious state".
  So what this shows is narrower than it first looked:
  - **The answers left with no action on the world**, but the patient who
    gave them kept some motor contingency. The questions (his father's
    name, for example) were about his life before the injury, so they test
    a model built while acting, not one kept in touch without it.
    Defeater (b) of [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) is not tested.
  - **The channel is imagined action** ("imagine standing still on a tennis
    court and to swing an arm"), read from a motor region. That fits
    [CLAIM-tmp9cjyo](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp9cjyo.md)'s "action moved inward" literally, though the answer it
    carries is a judgement, put into imagery by a code the investigators
    set.
  - **Negative results are uninterpretable.** When a question got no
    signal, "Whether the patient fell asleep ... or lost consciousness
    cannot be determined". The asymmetry above holds.
  - **The scoring was blind**: "the investigators would not know the correct
    answers before judging the functional MRI data". That is
    [CLAIM-tmpt568z](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt568z.md)'s anchor, but for the observers' reading, not for the
    patient, who got no feedback.
- **Not every contingency goes.** Paralysis removes action on the world.
  It does not remove the body's regulation of itself. Barrett's action is
  visceromotor ([NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)), and on Damasio's account feelings may need the
  body to act on the neurons that map it ([QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md)). Damasio also
  holds that an "as if body loop", in which body states are re-enacted
  when an option is imagined, has largely superseded the body loop in
  adults ([THEORY-tmpddd8b](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpddd8b.md); asserted, not shown). If so, the mechanism
  that would keep judgement working in locked-in syndrome is already
  internal, and the case cannot remove every contingency between
  intention and outcome, as [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md)'s defeater (b) asks.
- **Extinguished or dormant.** A coalition that falls silent can remain
  available for reactivation ([CLAIM-287](../claims.d/CLAIM-287.md)). Kübler and Birbaumer's patients
  may have lost the capacity for goal-directed thinking, or only the
  channel that would show it. Chaudhary et al.'s patient fits the second
  reading, though with the cautions above.
- **What it cannot show.** Every patient here built their model while
  acting. The case bears on the loop that keeps a model in touch, not on
  the one that builds it.
