# ARG-227: Three learners → no scalar learning optimum

## The inference

[CLAIM-1197](../claims.d/CLAIM-1197.md) lists four conditions: a declared task law, one valuation, a
finite value and attainment. A condition is not implied by the others if
some coherent learner meets the others and lacks a scalar optimum. Three
stipulated learners do this.

- **Two selves and three selves** ([CLAIM-1036](../claims.d/CLAIM-1036.md)). Finitely many policies
  and outcomes, a fixed and declared result for each policy, every value
  finite, every self's utility bounded. What fails is the one valuation:
  each policy is valued by the self it produces. No rule fixed by those
  valuations alone picks one best policy, since each construction is
  symmetric under relabelling policies and selves together.
- **Invest, then exploit** ([CLAIM-1130](../claims.d/CLAIM-1130.md)). A declared and deterministic
  task law, one valuation (discounted payoff), countably many policies,
  every value finite. What fails is attainment, with the supremum infinite
  when γg > 1, and in the bounded version, with the supremum finite and
  still not reached.

## Critical questions

- **Is each case coherent?** Yes. Each is a finite or countable list of
  policies with stated outcomes and values, and `scratch/studies/lead-011/`
  checks the arithmetic of each, in exact fractions where it matters.
- **Does it separate the very things the conclusion says come apart?** Yes,
  with one qualification. In the selves' constructions, the condition that
  is dropped is exactly the one valuation; the others hold. In the
  invest-then-exploit learner with γg > 1, a finite value for each policy
  holds and attainment fails, so it separates attainment from the rest;
  the bounded version separates attainment from a finite supremum as well.
  It does not separate a finite value for every policy from the rest. That
  separation comes from the remark in [CLAIM-1130](../claims.d/CLAIM-1130.md) that, without the
  commitment, an alternating policy has infinite value when γ²g ≥ 1, which
  keeps the other conditions.
- **Are the cases ones of learning that changes what can later be learned?**
  Yes, and they have to be. In the selves' constructions what is learned
  changes what is valued, in the invest-then-exploit learner it changes
  what later periods can earn. Without that, the learner faces one
  valuation and γ·1 < 1 and the conditions hold.
- **Does the conclusion claim more than the cases show?** It claims
  independence of the conditions, not that they are each necessary in
  every sense: an ε-optimal policy exists in the bounded case, and a
  declared evaluator restores an optimum in the selves' case. Both claims
  say so.
