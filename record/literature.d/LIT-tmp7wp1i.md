# LIT-tmp7wp1i: Technological Due Process

Danielle Keats Citron (2008). Technological Due Process. *Washington University Law Review* 85(6): 1249–1313. https://journals.library.wustl.edu/lawreview/article/id/6697/.

## Key takeaways

- "This century's automated decision making systems combine individual
  adjudications with rulemaking while adhering to the procedural
  safeguards of neither" (abstract, p. 1249).
- Programmers "in fact" make rules when encoding policy (p. 1254), "a
  troubling delegation of legislative power" (p. 1288).
- Automation bias makes a mixed system little different from a fully
  automated one (p. 1272), and a Mathews analysis of one case captures
  "only a tiny fraction" of the benefits of fixing a system (p. 1285).
- Remedies: audit trails (p. 1305), hearing officers who explain their
  reliance on the system (p. 1307), released source code (p. 1308) and
  testing (p. 1310).
