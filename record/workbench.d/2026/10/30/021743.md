**What the pass took.** The worklist on main after the ADR-047 kind-1 sweep
had nothing in its problem sections. The reliance ranking is flat, so the

- what pass 15 left;

Five reviewers read. The rulings were given here, and each reviewer applied
its own cluster. Each cluster was checked before commit.

**What freed the claims.** Most of the promotions came from one move: a
Proposed reading that a claim cites as an instance, a parallel or a
precedent is corroboration, not a ground (ADR-058). Once those readings move
to `corroborated_by`, the claims stand on what they actually use.

- CLAIM-402, CLAIM-352, CLAIM-341, CLAIM-296, CLAIM-273, CLAIM-363 and
  CLAIM-288 were freed this way.
- CLAIM-356 was freed by dropping an edge that only marked an attribution.
- CLAIM-175 was freed by an Active split of THEORY-579 that states what
  Clark and Chalmers argue, on THEORY-1110's pattern. It is the one split
  this pass made, because it was the one that freed a claim.

CLAIM-321 is freed for a later pass. CLAIM-172 is one premise short
(THEORY-069), and CLAIM-242's edges are cleaned, though it still grounds on
the Proposed THEORY-159. The blocker notes say what each waits on.

**Checks run here.**

- **The THEORY-265 model** was re-run from NOTE-773's equations. Results:
  - Learned phases reach the global optimum in 50 of 50 test runs, and so
    does synchrony matched to the modules and imposed by hand.
  - Random phases and shuffled phases reach it in 0 of 50.
  - Global synchrony reaches it in 5 to 19 of 50, as a transient
    system-wide reset rather than modules responding to one another.
  - The self-coupling variant behaves the same way.
  - The effect holds only where modules are isolated enough to lock in:
    random phases already reach the optimum once the coupling across
    modules is 0.1 or more.
