# LIT-193: Better to be a Pig Dissatisfied than a Plant Satisfied

Ethan C. Terrill, Walter Veit (2024), *Journal of Agricultural and Environmental Ethics, 37, article 4* — DOI-10.1007/s10806-024-09922-4

## Key takeaways

- If sentience comes in degrees and moral status comes in degrees, plants may be "quasi-sentient" and have partial moral status while animals still have prima facie more — so granting plants some moral status does not force treating eating plants and eating pigs alike.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Responding to Segundo-Ortin and Calvo's case that plants should be taken seriously as candidates for sentience, the authors note that critics tend to assume that because plants matter less than animals they must not matter at all. They propose a gradualist alternative: sentience and moral status are both matters of degree, so plants may be quasi-sentient and carry partial moral status. That lets one hold both that plants and animals have moral status and that animals have prima facie more of it.
