# NOTE-1621: de Oliveira, de Sá and Martins — Multi-period bi-objective ambulance location in Belo Horizonte

## Contribution

Two multi-period location–allocation models for an emergency medical
service with several service types, and a bi-objective form of each
(§§3–4), applied to the SAMU of Belo Horizonte (§5).

- **FLEET-ICt** (§3.1, eqs. 20–27), from Schilling et al.'s FLEET:
  maximize the demand of each type covered by an ambulance of that type
  within its standard, period by period. The allocation x^t_ju carries
  the period; the base variable z_j does not.
- **LR-MEXCLP-ICt** (§3.2, eqs. 28–38), from Sorensen and Church's
  LR-MEXCLP: the same, with each node's demand weighted by the
  reliability q^t_iuk = 1 − (b^t_iuk)^k of the k ambulances in its
  neighbourhood.
- **The bi-objective form** (§4.1, eqs. 39–44): minimize the number of
  bases as a second objective, solved by the ε-constraint method.

§2, a survey of the probabilistic covering models they build on, is
summarized under Connections.

## Key insight

Relocation here is a per-period allocation with no cost: "let ambulances
change their locations at the end of a period to achieve higher coverage
in the next period" (p. 4). Left free, the multi-period models open many
bases (226 and 179 against 28 and 27 for the static ones), which is why
the bases-against-coverage objective is added; coverage flattens past
about 28 to 30 bases (§5.5).

## Assumptions

- **Of the models** (§3, pp. 13–14): coverage by service type is
  independent, "a demand point may be covered by one type of ambulance
  without the obligation of being covered by the other type"; an
  ambulance of type u serves only type u; travel times are deterministic
  ("do not consider the travel time variation between time periods");
  "once a base is opened, it must remain open until the end of the
  planning horizon and ... there is no limit to the number of bases"; a
  fixed fleet per type and a capacity per base.
- **Of the probabilistic one**, as §2 says of LR-MEXCLP: server
  independence and locally constrained service areas, the busy fraction
  "assumes that ambulances are only allowed to answer demands within its
  coverage zone" (p. 29).
- **Of the probabilistic models in general** (p. 10): uniform server
  busyness, server independence, locally constrained service areas.
  "Due to the model's probabilistic nature, stochastic elements of the
  EMS systems result in non-linear mathematical models."

## Key results

- **Abstract (p. 2).** "The proposed multi-period models introduce the
  concept of relocation to the local EMS, which allows ambulances to
  move among bases in different periods to raise the system coverage";
  "This paper also proposes a bi-objective approach aiming to minimize
  the number of bases and maximize the coverage of demands".
- **Data (§5.1, pp. 21–22).** 427 demand nodes, 1,527 candidate sites, 24
  one-hour periods, 7 ALS and 21 BLS ambulances, at most two per base,
  standards of 10 minutes (ALS) and 8 (BLS), 26,024 demands, kmax = 3.
- **Table 2, FLEET-ICt (p. 24).** Total coverage 60.6% (2019
  configuration), 70.0% (2019 bases, one period), 70.5% (2019 bases, 24
  periods), 89.9% (optimized bases, one period), 91.6% (optimized
  bases, 24 periods, 226 stations).
- **Table 3, LR-MEXCLP-ICt (p. 26).** 59.0%, 68.5%, 68.2%, 83.5%, 83.6%
  (179 stations). With the 2019 bases, "the best total rate of coverage
  under the conditions established by scenario S2, when the problem is
  treated as static, without reallocation".
- **Pareto sets (§5.5, p. 27).** For FLEET-ICt, 28 → 226 stations raises
  the objective from 23,476 to 23,775, "an increment of less than 1.3%";
  for LR-MEXCLP-ICt, 30 → 179 stations adds 2.45%.
- **What the reported gains compare** (this reading, by
  scratch/formal/oliveira_tables_check.py). The abstract's "up to 31%"
  and "up to 24%" are S1 → S5 in percentage points. Its relocation
  gains, "up to 21%" and "up to 15%", match only comparisons that also
  replace the 2019 bases by optimized ones; for LR-MEXCLP-ICt, two static
  runs (S2 → S4) already differ by 15.0 points. Relocation alone adds
  +0.5 and +1.7 points to FLEET-ICt and −0.3 and +0.1 to LR-MEXCLP-ICt.
- **Inconsistencies in the text.** LR-MEXCLP-ICt's printed coverage
  rates are not objective / 26,024, though §5.4 says they are (S5: 72.1%
  by that ratio, 83.6% printed); the text gives 24 stations for S1–S3,
  the tables 25; the ε = 226 Pareto value (23,775) is below Table 2's S5
  optimum (23,827) at 226 stations; (43) is said to be subject to
  (21)–(27) where (31)–(38) is meant; the conclusion says increments
  "narrow below 30 installed stations", the abstract "above 30".
- **From §2.** "Simplifying assumptions regarding the uniform server
  busyness and the independence of the servers are made in MEXCLP"
  (p. 10). "The MALP model also brings the assumption of server
  independence and includes the locally constrained service area
  assumption, but it relaxes the assumption of uniform ambulance
  availability" (p. 10). "In MALP, ReVelle & Hogan (1989) introduce a
  method to compute an estimate of area-specific busy fractions
  associated with each demand point" (p. 10). "Either MEXCLP or MALP
  might request to allocate more than one ambulance to cover demand
  points, depending on its demand level and the number of vehicles
  available" (p. 10). "Sorensen & Church (2010) argue that the MALP's
  α-reliability objective function may not be appropriate in some EMS
  contexts and may lead to inferior results" (p. 11). LR-MEXCLP (eqs.
  12–19, pp. 11–12): b_{i,k} = Σ_{j∈N̄i} d̄_j / k, q_{i,k} = 1 − (b_{i,k})^k,
  maximize Σ_i Σ_k d_i q_ik y_ik.

## Concepts

- **Busy fraction** — the fraction of time a server is unavailable.
- **Area-specific busy fraction** — estimated from the demand in a
  node's neighbourhood; the MALP device, by this paper's account.
- **Reliability q_ik** — the chance that at least one of the k covering
  servers is free, under independence.
- **Independent coverage** — coverage by each service type counted
  separately, after Guimarães et al. (2020).
- **Multi-period relocation** — a location plan per period, as against
  real-time relocation after each dispatch (§2, after van Barneveld et
  al.).
- **ε-constraint method** — one objective kept, the other made a bound
  and varied.

## Connections

A neighbour of Daskin (1983), [LIT-2098](../literature.d/LIT-2098.md), and of ReVelle and Hogan (1989),
[LIT-2075](../literature.d/LIT-2075.md), read in their place, through §2 only; §§3–6 add nothing about
those models' own formulations.

- **Of Daskin (1983)** it restates what MEXCLP maximizes (expected
  coverage given that servers may be busy), its two simplifying
  assumptions, and that it may place several ambulances over one demand
  point; LR-MEXCLP generalizes its objective. It does not restate
  MEXCLP's own objective formula, the properties Daskin proves, his
  heuristic or his computational results.
- **Of ReVelle and Hogan (1989)** it restates MALP's aim (demand covered
  with minimum reliability α), its assumptions, that it introduced
  area-specific busy fractions, and Sorensen and Church's objection to
  its α-reliability objective. The busy-fraction and reliability
  formulas it gives are those of LR-MEXCLP, credited in part to MALP,
  not MALP's own formulation. It does not restate the zero–one program,
  how α becomes a count of servers, the distinction between MALP's two
  versions, or the Baltimore results.

## Limitations

- The authors, citing Sorensen and Church: relaxing one assumption
  "often leads to another imposition" (p. 13).
- One city; exact solutions, not validated by simulation, which the
  authors name as future work (p. 30); relocation costless and
  unlimited, which inflates the multi-period base counts.
- The abstract's relocation gains mix relocation with base optimization,
  and the probabilistic coverage rates cannot be reproduced from the
  stated objective and total demand (Key results).
- A preprint submitted to Expert Systems with Applications; v1 only.
- This reading's own observation: b = Σd̄/k can exceed 1, making q
  negative; the paper does not say how that case is handled.
