summary: >-
  Reed & Hughes (2002), From gene families and genera to incomes and internet file sizes: Why power laws are so common in nature, Physical Review E 66, 067103 (Brief Report), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.66.067103. If a process growing exponentially "in expectation" is killed, or observed once, at a time with a constant hazard ν, the killed state has power-law behaviour "in one or both tails". Deterministic growth e^(μt) gives a pure Pareto with survival x^(−ν/μ). Geometric Brownian motion gives a double Pareto, its exponents α and −β the roots of ½σ²s² + (μ − ½σ²)s − ν = 0. A killed multiplicative process, a killed birth-and-death process (density exponent 1 + ν/(λ − δ) for λ > δ, a stretched exponential at λ = δ) and a killed supercritical Galton-Watson process (exponent 1 + κ, κ = ln[1/(1 − p)]/ln μ, with log-periodic wiggles) follow. Offered as the explanation of tails in incomes, settlement sizes, web-site and file sizes, genera and gene families, on log-log plots alone. Here as item 6 of the record's catalogue of feedback-free mechanisms (exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws", A5).
