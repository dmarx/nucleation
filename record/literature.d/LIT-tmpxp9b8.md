# LIT-tmpxp9b8: Examining the Society of Mind

Push Singh (2003), *Computing and Informatics 22(6), 521–543* (revised
manuscript received 4 November 2003) — journal article page
https://www.cai.sk/ojs/index.php/cai/article/view/467, open access

## Key takeaways

- **History.** The theory began in Minsky and Papert's discussions at the
  MIT AI Lab in the early 1970s. Their hand-eye "copy-demo" robot showed
  them that "no single method ever worked well by itself". Frames with
  attached procedures (1974) were "the ancestor of the concept of an agent".
  Minsky and Papert drafted a book in 1976, the unpublished "Brazil" drafts,
  and abandoned it. The theory then appeared piecemeal in Minsky's 1977
  and 1980 papers.
- **Mechanisms.** Agents and agencies; K-lines, which turn on a remembered
  set of agents; nemes, which represent, and nomes, which control, with
  polynemes, micronemes, isonomes, pronomes and paranomes. Frames,
  frame-arrays and transframes. Difference-engines. Censors and suppressors.
  A- and B-brains. Protospecialists, predestined learning, and learning from
  attachment figures. Papert's Principle and the Principle of
  Non-Compromise.
- **Communication without a shared code.** Agents are "too simple to
  understand the languages of most other agents". They communicate by
  arousing other agents through K-lines. They use randomly connected "bus"
  wires whose meanings are learned, after Calvin Mooers's superimposed
  coding. They use paranomes, which update parallel representations so that
  no message is sent. Ambiguity is tolerated, not removed.
- **Since 1986.** The theory resembles probabilistic graphical models (its
  ring-closing recognition is like belief propagation), case-based reasoning
  (derivational analogy "came directly from the K-line theory"), blackboard
  systems, Cyc's microtheories, Soar (chunking resembles K-lines) and
  multiagent systems. Each lacks something the theory needs: nomes,
  managerial knowledge, or reflection. "The Society of Mind theory has not"
  seen a series of implementations.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for Minsky's Society of Mind
programme. The brief asked for it as a sympathetic exposition by Minsky's
student, and that is what it is. Singh says he was given the unpublished
1976 drafts and discussed the history with Minsky and Papert. The draft of
*The Emotion Machine* ([LIT-tmpr3qe6](LIT-tmpr3qe6.md)) cites this paper for how the two books
relate.

It is the record's best reachable account of *The Society of Mind*
([LIT-tmpqepho](LIT-tmpqepho.md)), which is unread here. Read it for the map, and for the
history it quotes. Do not read it for evidence that the theory works: it
says the theory has never been built. No anthology topic holds it. Its
survey of AI techniques is a comparison of architectures, not advice for
machine-learning practice.
