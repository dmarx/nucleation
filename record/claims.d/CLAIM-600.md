  The assistant's, at the owner's request of 2026-11-05 to characterize
  "what differentiates transient support that induces dependency and is
  hazardous to remove from transient support that builds new capabilities
  and is safe to remove", with reintroduction and ecological recovery
  proposed as cases. It draws together the branch's three domains: DNA
  origami, aid and development, and ecology. Three conditions decide it.
  First, the support starts a self-sustaining process on an ambient flux
  instead of doing the upkeep. Planted tree islands, weeded for 2.5
  years, kept their advantage to 16–18 years because established trees
  keep drawing seed dispersers (Holl et al.; Schubert et al.). Supplied
  repair, by contrast, never becomes optional ([CLAIM-597](CLAIM-597.md)). Second, the
  threat it stood in for is gone. Conservation-reliant species stay
  reliant while fire is suppressed or habitat threats persist (Mussmann
  et al.; Brown et al.). Third, the party did the work throughout.
  Support that does the work wears capacity down by disuse
  ([CLAIM-558](CLAIM-558.md)), by slack effort (Ostrom et al.) or by selection for the
  supported conditions, which costs hatchery steelhead fitness in the wild
  within one generation (Christie et al.). Captive-bred translocated

  must be shown after withdrawal: impact at exit and judged success both
  overstate it. Revised the same day after two counterpoints the owner
  asked for. Evidence against the lazy-poor stereotype finds no slack
  effort in poor households: cash transfers barely change work, and
  one-off graduation packages raise it. So that route is confined to help
  that is assured and permanent. A household asset threshold (Balboni et
  al.) adds a condition on size: support too small decays with no
  dependence at all. Urban development adds three cautions. Some public
  inputs are a standing part of a coproduced service (Evans; Watson).
  Forty years of persistence can be slow reversion (Kline and Moretti).
  And a place can last while the people it served are dispersed
  (Collins and Shester; LaVoice). The wording is the assistant's

# CLAIM-600: Transient support can be withdrawn safely when it starts a process that keeps itself going on a flux that will stay available, when it is large enough to carry the supported party past any threshold that process has, when the threat it stood in for has gone, and when the party could do the work and did it throughout; inputs the party cannot produce must stay supplied as a standing part of the arrangement, support that fails these leaves dependence or decay, and persistence after withdrawal is needed to tell which, though slow reversion can mimic it

## The three conditions

1. **The support starts a process that keeps itself going on an
   available flux. It does not do the upkeep.**
   - **Supplied upkeep never becomes optional** ([CLAIM-597](CLAIM-597.md)). Origami that
     self-heals from a strand pool and replicators fed fresh monomers are
     independent of their seed and never of their supply.
   - **Applied nucleation is the counter-case.** Holl et al.
     ([NOTE-1676](../notes.d/NOTE-1676.md)) contrast perches and bat boxes, which "have
     short-term impacts", with planted trees, which "once established ...
     attract dispersers and modify microsite conditions continuously". In
     the Costa Rican experiment ([NOTE-1660](../notes.d/NOTE-1660.md)) weeding stopped at 2.5
     years, and planted islands still recruited more later-successional
     species at 16–18 years.
   - **What the system depends on afterwards is the flux, not the
     provider.** Recovery still relies on seed from the surrounding
     forest. This is [CLAIM-604](CLAIM-604.md)'s distinction between dependence on a
     flux and dependence on a provider. The fiscal contract is the state's
     version (Moore, [NOTE-1663](../notes.d/NOTE-1663.md)).
2. **The threat or suppressed process the support stood in for has
   gone.**
   - **Support against a persisting threat cannot end.** Mussmann et al.
     ([NOTE-1670](../notes.d/NOTE-1670.md)) find translocating prairie chickens while the habitat
     threat persists gives "ambiguous or transitory" gains. Leaving
     reliance needs the extrinsic threat and the population's own state
     "alleviated in tandem".
   - **Supply that replaces a suppressed process is permanent while the
     suppression lasts.** The Kirtland's Warbler lives on plantations that
     replace suppressed wildfire, and Brown et al. ([NOTE-1674](../notes.d/NOTE-1674.md)) expect
     it to remain reliant.
   - **Removing the pressure is often not enough.** In Kadowaki et al.'s
     cases ([NOTE-1677](../notes.d/NOTE-1677.md)), reintroducing fire alone does not restore a
     fire-suppressed wetland, because hardwoods now stop the fire.
3. **The supported party did the work throughout.** Support that does
   the work wears capacity down by three routes, at different speeds.
   - **Disuse.** A capability not exercised decays ([CLAIM-558](CLAIM-558.md)). Where
     capacity is learned by doing, substitution lowers what is kept, and
     recovery after full substitution grows with its length
     ([CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md)).
   - **Slack effort.** Assured help makes low effort the recipient's best
     reply, the Samaritan's dilemma (Ostrom et al., [NOTE-1647](../notes.d/NOTE-1647.md)). The
     loss is favoured to the one who loses ([CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md)).
   - **Selection for the supported conditions.** This is the fastest
     route. In Hood River steelhead (Christie et al., [NOTE-1652](../notes.d/NOTE-1652.md)) one
     generation of hatchery rearing selected for success in captivity.
     Offspring of the wild broodstock most successful in the hatchery had
     "71% lower per capita reproductive success" in the wild. Araki et al.
     ([NOTE-1668](../notes.d/NOTE-1668.md)) judge relaxed selection, the route of disuse, too
     slow to explain the loss.
   - **The counter-practice in every domain is doing the work while the
     support remains.** In the Kenya exit study ([NOTE-1650](../notes.d/NOTE-1650.md)),
     participants who "operate independently prior to project closure"
     kept activities going. Under [CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md)'s capacity-first rule,
     the support covers only what the party cannot yet do and withdraws
     itself.

## What the windfall readings add

The owner suggested lottery winners as cases of large, sudden,
unconditional money. Three readings test both of the revision's
conditions.

- **Slack effort, in the setting it was confined to.** A lottery prize
  is large, unconditional and permanent. Work does fall, but modestly
  and smoothly.
  - Swedish winners earn about 1.1 % of the prize less each year, from
    the first year and for more than ten (Cesarini, Lindqvist,
    Notowidigdo and Östling, [NOTE-1648](../notes.d/NOTE-1648.md)).
  - US winners' households earn about $2.3 less a year per $100 won
    (Golosov, Graber, Mogstad and Novgorodsky, [NOTE-1671](../notes.d/NOTE-1671.md)).
  - Neither finds a threshold. In both, the effect per unit won is, if
    anything, smaller for larger prizes.
- **A stock does not start a process.** Cash larger than the median
  heavy debtor's debt halved bankruptcy filings among Florida winners
  for two years. Filings then rose in years 3–5, and over five years no
  difference was detected (Hankins, Hoekstra and Skiba, [NOTE-1673](../notes.d/NOTE-1673.md)).
  US winners save most of a win and draw it down slowly. Wealth raised
  only low-profit self-employment, not profitable businesses (Golosov et
  al.).
- **What this says about size.** A large windfall is not enough on its
  own, because size counts only relative to a process that keeps going.
  Balboni et al.'s cow crossed a threshold in a productive process.
  Florida cash was a stock that ran down. That is the first condition
  again: support must start something that keeps itself going, or what
  it supplies is spent.

## What the counterpoints changed

Read after the owner asked for a counterpoint to the dependency material
("e.g. the myth of the lazy poor") and suggested urban development and
city planning. Six changes, each reflected in the title.

1. **Slack effort is not a general property of support.** The household
   evidence contradicts it.
   - Across seven government cash-transfer trials, Banerjee, Hanna,
     Kreindler and Olken ([NOTE-1685](../notes.d/NOTE-1685.md)) find a pooled change in work of
     about five minutes a week. Cash transfers "do not induce the "bad"
     behaviors that are often attributed to them".
   - Baird, McKenzie and Özler ([NOTE-1684](../notes.d/NOTE-1684.md)) find little or no change in
     prime-age adults' work. Work falls only for pensioners and where
     transfers are large and prolonged. They expect impacts "are likely to
     be different when transfers are dependable and permanent".
   - Graduation packages raised work during and after support ([NOTE-1669](../notes.d/NOTE-1669.md),
     [NOTE-1667](../notes.d/NOTE-1667.md)).
   - None of 17 randomized estimates found more spending on alcohol or
     tobacco (Evans and Popova, [NOTE-1649](../notes.d/NOTE-1649.md)).
   - Haushofer and Fehr ([NOTE-1681](../notes.d/NOTE-1681.md)) offer a rival cause for
     short-sighted choices: poverty itself, which transfers relieve.

   Slack effort survives only where help is assured, permanent and
   relieves no binding constraint. That is Ostrom et al.'s setting of
   repeated donor rehabilitation of state assets, not the households'.
2. **Size matters relative to a threshold.**
   - In Bangladesh, Balboni et al. ([NOTE-1654](../notes.d/NOTE-1654.md)) estimate an asset
     threshold of about 9,300 taka, near the value of one cow. Households
     the transfer left just below it lost assets, while those lifted above
     it kept accumulating for eleven years.
   - Small unconditional grants faded within two years (Baird et al.).

   Support too small to cross a threshold fails without creating any
   dependence. That is a second hazard beside dependence, and it is why the
   title adds a condition on size. It also qualifies [CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md)'s "not
   the amount of supply": under capacity-first support the amount does not
   cause dependence, but it can decide whether the process starts.
3. **The party must be able to do the work, and the support must come
   before a competing configuration sets in.**
   - In Tanzania (Michaels et al., [NOTE-1651](../notes.d/NOTE-1651.md)), plots and infrastructure
     laid out on empty land drew private building that kept the
     infrastructure up, for 35–40 years.
   - Upgrading existing slums at a similar cost left housing no better, and
     the infrastructure decayed for "lack of maintenance".
   - The authors' model needs enough owners able to invest, and they warn
     that credit-constrained residents may not sustain it at scale.
4. **Some inputs are a standing part of the arrangement.**
   - In Brazil's condominial sewers, residents built and kept up the block
     feeders while the agency kept the trunk system. Watson ([NOTE-1659](../notes.d/NOTE-1659.md))
     finds that handing feeder upkeep wholly to residents worked poorly even
     in the best cities. Where the systems worked, more resident
     responsibility went with more contact with the agencies, not less.
   - Evans ([NOTE-1665](../notes.d/NOTE-1665.md)): "Active citizens are
     hamstrung unless their governments dependably supply them with inputs
     that they cannot produce on their own".
   - What could safely end was the founding team's organizing effort, not
     the agency's part. This is [CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md)'s reciprocal form, and the
     first condition does not ask that such inputs be withdrawn.
5. **Persistence after withdrawal can be slow reversion.**
   - The Tennessee Valley Authority's manufacturing gains were still
     growing "forty years after TVA became financially self-sufficient",
     well past a turnover. Yet Kline and Moretti's model ([NOTE-1646](../notes.d/NOTE-1646.md))
     finds a constant agglomeration elasticity, no threshold and no second
     equilibrium, and expects the effects "will eventually die out".
   - Persistence through a turnover is therefore necessary evidence, not
     sufficient evidence. Telling a self-sustaining process from slow
     reversion needs the shape of the feedback, as in [CLAIM-604](CLAIM-604.md)'s
     threshold.
   - Neumark and Simpson ([NOTE-1656](../notes.d/NOTE-1656.md)) conclude that even the best
     evidence on place-based policy "does not establish that they create
     self-sustaining economic gains".
6. **Whose persistence counts.**
   - US urban renewal raised cities' incomes and property values for
     decades after funding ended (Collins and Shester, [NOTE-1683](../notes.d/NOTE-1683.md)).
     It also cleared over 300,000 families, mostly nonwhite. LaVoice
     ([NOTE-1680](../notes.d/NOTE-1680.md)) finds rents rising across low-income neighbourhoods.
   - The TVA's local gains net to about zero nationally.
   - A place can become independent of the support while the community it
     was meant to serve is dispersed. The conditions apply to a stated
     unit, and the unit must be the one the support was for.

## How to tell which kind it was

Support that fails a condition can look like success while it lasts.
- Impact at exit did not predict sustainability (Kenya, [NOTE-1650](../notes.d/NOTE-1650.md)).
- Captive-bred translocations were judged successful as often as wild-sourced ones, yet their mean population growth was negative (Morris et al., [NOTE-1657](../notes.d/NOTE-1657.md)).
- Only 24.8 % of reported translocation successes reached the field's own standard, a population that persists "without further augmentation".

Schröder, Persson and De Roos ([NOTE-1666](../notes.d/NOTE-1666.md)) give the test: a state is independent of an intervention once it lasts a full turnover of its parts after the intervention ends. A's withdrawal measure for [QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) is the experimental form of that test.

## What the readings do not support

- **That thresholds and hysteresis are common.**
  - Since revised: the clearest empirical threshold on the record is now a household one, Balboni et al.'s asset threshold ([NOTE-1654](../notes.d/NOTE-1654.md)). Regional evidence finds none: Kline and Moretti find a constant agglomeration elasticity, and Neumark and Simpson report EU Structural Funds needing no minimum transfer.
  - [CLAIM-604](CLAIM-604.md)'s ignition, collapse and unrecoverable outage are demonstrated in a model. In the field they are rarer. Schröder et al. find alternative stable states in most conclusive laboratory experiments and few field ones, and few cases where removing a pressure failed to restore.
  - Cutting the Kirtland's Warbler's supplied habitat gives lower plateaus, not collapse.
  - Hawaiian waterbirds returned to a marsh once it was managed again (Underwood et al., [NOTE-1672](../notes.d/NOTE-1672.md)).
  - Thresholds are real in founder numbers and nucleus size: success plateaus above about 20–50 animals, and about 64 m² is cited as the minimum for a tree nucleus. They are not the rule for withdrawal.
- **That longer support is always worse.** Morris et al. find longer release programmes with fewer failures. That is continued supply, not substitution, and it does not test the third condition. The first version's "deepens with its length", dropped in the revision, is shown for substitution in [CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md)'s model and for generations in captivity. It is not shown for support in general.
- **That the evidence is strong.**
  - The aid evidence is review essays and short case studies.
  - Of the ecology, the captive-rearing result rests on one pedigree. The nucleation result rests on one experiment, and the conservation-reliance cases are three species.
  - Several key originals were read only through neighbours: Scott et al. 2010, Araki et al. 2007, Corbin and Holl 2012, Suding et al. 2004 and Rogers and Coates 2015.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on persistence after shocks and support, and on people against places

Read to fill a gap the supplied-maintenance filing left: whether persistence after withdrawal is self-sustaining or slow reversion. The works were chosen by the assistant.

- **Both poles, now on the record.**
  - Japanese cities returned to their pre-war relative size within 15–20 years of WWII bombing, Hiroshima and Nagasaki included (Davis and Weinstein, [NOTE-1696](../notes.d/NOTE-1696.md)). The companion search for multiple equilibria finds "no support for the existence of multiple equilibria" ([NOTE-1690](../notes.d/NOTE-1690.md)).
  - US cities at historical portage sites are still 60–100 % denser than comparable sites a century after portage stopped mattering, with no decay (Bleakley and Lin, [NOTE-1697](../notes.d/NOTE-1697.md)). The case passes their tests against slow reversion: matching on past density, the age of housing stock, and prices.
  - Against these two, the Tennessee Valley Authority ([NOTE-1646](../notes.d/NOTE-1646.md)) is the ambiguous case. The claim's caution that slow reversion can mimic persistence now has its contrast cases.
- **Size, again.** Bleakley and Lin find almost no effect at the smallest watersheds, an echo of the condition on size.
- **The unit, from the other side.** In Moving to Opportunity (Chetty, Hendren and Katz, [NOTE-1695](../notes.d/NOTE-1695.md)) the gain was carried by children who moved young, into adult earnings, while parents in the same places gained nothing. That is the mirror of urban renewal, where the place gained and its people were dispersed. The voucher was standing support, not transient; what ended was childhood.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-06: the readings on solitons

Supports and sharpens the caution that slow reversion can mimic persistence ([CLAIM-606](CLAIM-606.md)). An oscillon shows almost no outflow for 10³–10⁴ times the linear lifetime and then ends abruptly (Copeland, Gleiser and Müller, [NOTE-1699](../notes.d/NOTE-1699.md)). A damped soliton keeps its shape while its amplitude runs down at a rate set by the kind of loss (Grimshaw, Smyth and Stepanyants, [NOTE-1687](../notes.d/NOTE-1687.md)). A cavity soliton switched on by a transient writing beam then persists on its standing supply (Grelu and Akhmediev, [NOTE-1688](../notes.d/NOTE-1688.md)): founding support withdrawn, feeding support kept. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-06: Suding, Gross & Houseman, "Alternative states and positive feedbacks in restoration ecology", read

Read in full ([LIT-2169](../literature.d/LIT-2169.md), [NOTE-1700](../notes.d/NOTE-1700.md)), the review, previously among the originals read only through neighbours, bears on two of the claim's points. On combined interventions, actions on several constraints at once "(e.g. burning and adding native seeds) often prove more successful than would either approach alone", and those on strong feedbacks may have to come first; the evidence is cited cases, and comparisons of sequence are "limited". On withdrawal, it expects "little or no further management intervention" only where a single constraint existed; otherwise a successful shift leads to maintenance by "very different actions than those required to shift states", a management that partly stops (the shifting work ends, upkeep of the regime such as fire or hydrology continues), not a withdrawal. It measures no case after withdrawal, so the claim's persistence test is untouched; its recommended repeated characterization after the constraints are addressed is the nearest thing to it. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings in medicine, on suppression, disuse and weaning

Read to fill a gap the supplied-maintenance filing left; the works were chosen by the assistant.

- **Doing the work has an upper bound.** The diaphragm needs effort within a band (Goligher et al., [NOTE-1704](../notes.d/NOTE-1704.md)): too little atrophies it, and too much may injure it. The condition that the party did the work throughout needs that qualification.
- **Withdrawal is tested, not assumed.** In weaning from ventilation, readiness is tested by a short, reversible spontaneous breathing trial. Success is judged only after a window following extubation (Shah, Hart and Kaltsakas, [NOTE-1706](../notes.d/NOTE-1706.md)). Ventilation can be stopped abruptly in about 75 % of patients whose underlying cause has improved, and immediate extubation after a successful trial is the recommended course (Alía and Esteban, [NOTE-1701](../notes.d/NOTE-1701.md)).
- **Tapers, where the evidence exists.** Among patients who fail the first trial, the method that tapers machine breaths did worst in both trials Alía and Esteban review ([NOTE-1701](../notes.d/NOTE-1701.md)). For corticosteroids, the authors of the meta-analysis find "insufficient evidence to prove any withdrawal scheme after steroid use to be efficient or safe" ([NOTE-1703](../notes.d/NOTE-1703.md)). The guideline's schedule is consensus.
- **Recovery is slow and graded.** Adrenal recovery takes from months to about four years, and a minority never fully recover ([NOTE-1705](../notes.d/NOTE-1705.md)).

The original consensus statement on weaning, Boles et al. 2007 ([LIT-2213](../literature.d/LIT-2213.md)), and Levine et al. 2008 on diaphragm atrophy ([LIT-2212](../literature.d/LIT-2212.md)) were not reached. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: Knack, "Aid Dependence and the Quality of Governance", read

Knack's cross-section of 80 aid recipients, 1982–95 ([NOTE-1707](../notes.d/NOTE-1707.md)), finds higher mean aid going with smaller gains in governance, an effect that roughly doubles when instrumented. That supports the clause that support failing the conditions leaves decay, for aid that substitutes for a state's own work. It is evidence on support in progress only. There are no withdrawal episodes, so the withdrawal conditions go untested. It adds a complication the synthesis lacks: steady aid harms more than erratic aid of the same mean. The state-level evidence the synthesis lacked is still thin: Bräutigam and Knack 2004 ([LIT-2221](../literature.d/LIT-2221.md)) and Ahmed 2012 ([LIT-2220](../literature.d/LIT-2220.md)) were not reached, and Knack's own estimates rest on instruments whose exclusion is untested. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on assistance and skill

Read to fill a gap the supplied-maintenance filing left; the works were chosen by the assistant.

- **The third condition is qualified in its unit.** Under automation the operator keeps doing the job while the subtask the automation performs decays (Macnamara et al., [NOTE-tmpfgu7k](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfgu7k.md)). The condition that the party did the work throughout must hold for each subtask, not only for the role.
- **A withdrawal the claim did not consider.** Automation hands work back at its failures, when the system is least predictable, in Ahn's reading of Bainbridge ([NOTE-tmpxhylb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxhylb.md); Bainbridge's own text, [LIT-tmph5vta](../literature.d/LIT-tmph5vta.md), was not reached). That is an unplanned withdrawal at the hardest case, unlike the planned exits the claim's conditions assume.
- **Timing and grading.** Koedinger and Aleven ([NOTE-tmp607yf](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp607yf.md)) find that early withholding is not safe by default, and their tutors withdraw help gradually as competence is shown.
- **The flux is not enough on its own.** Griffith et al. ([NOTE-tmp7qron](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7qron.md)) find outside technology stays available but is used only in proportion to a party's own R&D.


## Note of 2026-11-06: Cohen and Levinthal, "Absorptive Capacity", read

Cohen and Levinthal (1990), now read ([NOTE-tmpraw7b](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpraw7b.md)), support the condition that the party could do the work and did it throughout. On their account the capacity to use an outside input is built only by the party's own doing, so supply that replaces that doing leaves it unable to use later supply. They qualify the claim with lockout: withdrawal can do harm that the return of supply does not undo. "once a firm ceases investing in its absorptive capacity in a quickly moving field, it may never assimilate and exploit new information in that field, regardless of the value of that information" (p. 136), and it may not pay to rebuild "even after major advances in the field" (p. 137). The cause is expectations as well as a lost stock. The firm's beliefs about the field "will tend not to change over time", and catching up costs more later (p. 136). So for a capacity in a fast-moving, cumulative field, the claim's safe-withdrawal condition needs the party's own work to continue through and after withdrawal, not only before it. Lockout is argued from psychology and illustrations (Monsanto: "once off the technological escalator it's difficult to get back on", p. 137), not measured. No persistence or recovery after withdrawal is observed. No status changes.
