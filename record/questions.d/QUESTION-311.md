# QUESTION-311: Do synaptic eligibility traces on the scale of seconds carry learning from delayed outcomes in behaving animals, so that interfering with the trace between action and outcome abolishes learning from the delayed outcome but not from immediate ones?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-909](../theory.d/THEORY-909.md) holds that, in five papers reviewed by Gerstner et al., mostly
slice experiments with one laboratory per region, a pairing changed the
synapse when a neuromodulator or plateau potential followed within one to
five seconds and not later. The reviewers grant that persistent network
activity or short-term plasticity could bridge the same gap. [CLAIM-443](../claims.d/CLAIM-443.md)'s
schematic three-factor rule would need such a trace to learn from delayed
outcomes, though it claims no neural implementation.

## What would answer it

The seconds-scale trace shown in behaving animals, not only in slices: an
experiment in vivo in which interfering with the trace's mechanism (for
example CaMKII in striatal spines) during the interval between action and
delayed outcome abolishes learning from that outcome while leaving learning
from immediate outcomes intact, and independent replication of at least the
striatal and cortical time constants. Refuted if learning from delays of
seconds survives the abolition of synaptic traces, carried instead by
persistent network activity or short-term plasticity. Further slice
experiments with the same protocols cannot settle it.
