The owner asked for the two groups left out of the entry of 00:59 to be
filed. Six works were filed, each read in full.

**Philosophers running rubber hand and self-location experiments.**

- **Hohwy & Paton 2010** ([LIT-1272](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1272.md)), from PMC. After the illusion
  starts, touch is reported as caused by a finger hovering over a rubber arm,
  or on a cardboard box. Before it starts, a box takes no touch. It is also
  the study Apps & Tsakiris cite for cooling of the real hand. That result
  is 0.13 °C in one comparison of four, one-tailed, under a real foreign
  arm. [THEORY-769](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-769.md) now says so; its claim and status are unchanged. No
  item asks about ownership, and expectations were not measured.
- **Alsmith & Longo 2014** ([LIT-1266](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1266.md)), from the published PDF on
  Longo's lab site. People pointing "directly at you" divide between the
  upper face and the upper torso, and which one wins depends on where the
  pointer starts. It gives [THEORY-768](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-768.md)'s self-location a measure other
  than drift, one a task factor moves. Its reanalysis prints "p = .53" for
  an F that gives about .053.

**Folk-concept experimental philosophy.** These sit near the topic rather
than building on the rubber hand. Each NOTE says what a folk judgement can
and cannot establish ([QUESTION-048](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)).

- **Starmans & Bloom 2012** ([LIT-1265](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1265.md)), from the Yale lab site. The
  record's citation is *Cognition* 123(2); the brief had given 124(3).
  Children and adults judge a drawn character to be "closer" to things near
  its eyes. Read beside Alsmith & Longo's first-person result, which puts
  the torso alongside the face.
- **Nichols & Bruno 2010** ([LIT-1269](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1269.md)), from a copy linked on Nichols's
  own site. First- and third-person tellings of Williams's case give the
  same verdict, so Williams's explanation of his two arguments by
  grammatical person does not hold for this sample.
  - The reader proposed that [THEORY-531](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-531.md) note this in "What this does not
    say". [THEORY-531](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-531.md) predates this batch, so the proposal is left for its
    own pass.
  - A reply, Berniūnas & Dranseika 2016, is named in the NOTE and not
    filed.
- **Reuter, Sienhold & Sytsma 2019** ([LIT-1278](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1278.md)) and **Borg, Harrison,
  Stazicker & Salomons 2020** ([LIT-1270](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1270.md)), from repository manuscripts.
  - The Borg copy is labelled accepted but headed "Revise and resubmit".
  - They take opposite sides on whether the folk concept of pain is
    bodily, and both use implicature as their tool.
  - Neither bears on the rubber hand THEORYs.

**A vocabulary gap.** The topics list has no word for experimental
philosophy or for the method of intuitions. The folk-concept works are
tagged with what they are about (personhood, consciousness, philosophy of
language, epistemology), not with how they work. If the record collects
more x-phi, a word for it is a decision to make, not a tag to improvise.
