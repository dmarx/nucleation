# QUESTION-079: Is the coordination among an agent's regimes better described by discrete switches between metastable states or by continuous reorganization, and does the answer change what would count as a transition in the test of the synchronization objection?

## The question

From an interlocutor's reading of [LIT-638](../literature.d/LIT-638.md) against [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md), relayed by the owner on 2026-10-12: "One further connection worth investigating is [LIT-638](../literature.d/LIT-638.md)'s explicit rivalry with [LIT-080](../literature.d/LIT-080.md)'s discrete-metastable-state account. Whether cognitive coordination is better described by discrete switching, continuous spatiotemporal reorganization, or both at different scales could materially affect how [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)'s proposed transition tests should be formulated."

## Why the record needs it

[CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)'s third part asks for transitions that "would behave as switches between stable patterns", with "hysteresis, and critical slowing in the order parameter before a lapse". That test presupposes discrete switching. [NOTE-502](../notes.d/NOTE-502.md)'s C5 records [LIT-638](../literature.d/LIT-638.md)'s claim that "Transitions between functional network states are continuous, not discrete switches between metastable states", rated weak because it was not tested against a state-switching model. [LIT-080](../literature.d/LIT-080.md) (Vohryzek et al.) holds the discrete-metastable-state view, and [LIT-638](../literature.d/LIT-638.md) and [LIT-080](../literature.d/LIT-080.md) are recorded as rivals. If the coordination is continuous, the switch test could come out negative while coordination is present.

## What would answer it

For a population of interests, or for the neural dynamics proposed to carry one, a comparison of a state-switching model and a continuous-reorganization model on the same data. Then a statement of [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)'s transition test for whichever wins, or for both at their scales.

*Filed as advice from the interlocutor's reading; the record takes no position.*
