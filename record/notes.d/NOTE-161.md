# NOTE-161: Cartwright — Rigour vs evidential diversity

## Contribution

Cartwright argues for evidential diversity and the mixed methods it requires, focusing on singular causal claims about what a cause will do, or did, in a specific setting. She offers a template that catalogues the kinds of evidence such claims need, generated by asking what must happen for a causal process to carry through from cause to effect. Unlike the usual mixed-methods argument, which triangulates on one overall claim, her template targets the many subsidiary claims that must hold for the overall claim to be true. The methods needed seldom meet today's standards of rigour, so she asks what "rigour" actually means and argues that emphasizing it can be counterproductive. RCTs, the paradigm of rigour, are used to show what rigorous methods can and cannot deliver.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (Rigour, RCTs and pToCs) documents calls for rigour in education and policy (NRC 2002, among others). Footnote 1: her point is not only that RCTs cannot rigorously support singular claims, but that they often fail to secure even an unbiased estimate of an average treatment effect in the study population.
- §3 asks what makes RCTs rigorous. Controlling for statistical bias licenses the inference from a difference in expectations to an unbiased ATE only under a potential-outcomes equation and substantial metaphysical assumptions (following Deaton & Cartwright 2018).
- §4 (Causal-process-tracing theories of change): following Salmon, she focuses on the process between C and E. A "pToC" sets out each step, together with the support factors and derailers at each step, which shows what evidence is needed. She aligns this with the EBM+ call for mechanistic evidence.
- §5 (In sum): no single method, or small set of methods, can warrant a singular causal claim. Use rigour where you can have it, but do not let it dictate which methods are admissible. Her image is the Jacana bird's nest woven from diverse materials, with Neurath's raft as the parallel.

## Open questions

- Transfers to ML evaluation: a benchmark gain, like an RCT average, does not warrant the claim that a method will work in your setting. That needs evidence about the steps connecting intervention and outcome in the deployment context.
- Check the full pToC template (§4 and its figures) for an explicit checklist of evidence types that could be adapted.
- Read alongside Worrall ([LIT-175](../literature.d/LIT-175.md)) and Reiss ([LIT-118](../literature.d/LIT-118.md)) on evidence hierarchies and pragmatist evidence.
