# LIT-tmpvp0b1: Algebra of Conscience

Lefebvre, V. A. (1982). *Algebra of Conscience: A Comparative Analysis of Western and Soviet Ethical Systems*. With a foreword by Anatol Rapoport. Theory and Decision Library 26. Dordrecht: Reidel. doi:10.1007/978-94-010-9051-3. Revised edition with a Second Part ("Moral Choice") and a new foreword by Rapoport, Theory and Decision Library A 30, Kluwer, 2001, doi:10.1007/978-94-017-0691-9.

Not read: no lawful copy of the chapters was reached (see the status note).
What follows is from the publisher's free front matter of the two editions,
which is the author's and Rapoport's account of the book, not the book. A
reading should check it first. OCR line-break hyphens and letter errors in
the 1982 scan are corrected in the quotes.

## What the front matter says

**The formalism.** In the 1982 introduction (pp. xvi–xvii), "A formula with
a special hierarchical structure is called an 'individual'. An individual has
an 'inner world', which may consist of images of this individual, of his
partner, and of the relationships between them (confrontation or compromise).
These images, in their turn, may have their 'inner worlds', etc." Images can
be correct or incorrect, and an individual can doubt their correctness. The
formula is a machine as well as a description: "The formulae representing
individuals are considered as transformers of a current of the environment's
demands into a current of individuals' agreements or disagreements" (p. xvii),
and "By changing a formula we can change both the behavior and the structure
of the inner world of an individual" (p. xiv).

**What this means for [MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md)'s level-k reading.** The images are not
only beliefs about what the other will do. They are the parts of the formula
that compute the agent's own choice, and they include an image of the
relationship and evaluations of it as good or evil. The choice rule is "a
postulate that by making a decision an individual tends to maximize the
ethical status of his image of himself" (p. xix). The 2001 preface (p. xiii)
puts it as the choice of union or confrontation being "determined by the
individual's self-esteem, as this is automatically generated by his cognitive
system", "a state of affairs which is not affected either by practical utility
or by moral evaluations performed by the 'other'". So influence on such an
agent can act on what it values in its image of itself, not only on what it
believes about the world. Whether the chapters bear this out is for a reading.

**Fixed points and limits.** Two statements bear on whether the nesting has a
resting state. Appendix 3, on environments generated by the individuals'
own activity: "for some quite 'natural' conditions an individual is either in
a balanced state or asymptotically approaches a balanced state" (p. xxiv). And
a limit on self-description: the "perfect individual", whose ethical status is
1, "cannot 'see' himself as perfect. Thus, 'perfection' is always an outer
characteristic of an individual and cannot be a fragment of his own
self-description" (p. xx). Appendix 4 treats "the limiting case, where the
number of levels tends toward infinity" (pp. xxiv–xxv).

**The 2001 edition.** Part Two, "Moral Choice", adds chapters on
"Metachoice", "Modeling of Awareness", "The Prisoner's Dilemma", "Imitation
of the Other" and "The Subject Controlling His Relationships with Another
Subject" (contents, pp. v–vi). Rapoport's new foreword (p. ix) says the model's
"mannikin is supposed to possess an internal world and thus to be able to
generate subjective intentions", and that "when the actor describes the
situation by a non-linear model, his behavior becomes chaotic" (p. x).

## Why this is here

Reflexive control is the influence the record's anchor question did not anticipate: conveying to a target prepared information so that it reaches, by its own reasoning, the decision the controller wants. In the record's terms it acts on the judgement–observation loop of [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md) by shaping the observer, and it makes [QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md)'s 'unselected' evidence selected by someone else ([CLAIM-tmpa2zsl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2zsl.md)).
