**What it is said to argue**, attributed: *per Harvard University Press*,
it "offers the most detailed account to date of the evolution of human
moral psychology. Based on extensive experimental data comparing great
apes and human children, Michael Tomasello reconstructs how early humans
gradually became an ultra-cooperative and, eventually, a moral species."
The press lists the APA's Eleanor Maccoby Book Award (2018) and a PROSE
Award (2017).

**Relation to [LIT-125](LIT-125.md)** (inference from the chapter titles and from
[NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md), not from the book). Tomasello's "The moral psychology of
obligation" ([LIT-125](LIT-125.md)) has the same two-step shape as chapters 3 and 4: an
obligation that arises first inside joint commitment with a collaborative
partner, and then extends to in-group members through collective
identification ([NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md), summary). That BBS paper appeared three years
later and confines itself to obligation; the book is the full natural
history of which it is one strand. [NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md) records that in 2016 the
author conceded that reciprocity's tie to obligation is culturally
contingent (its claim C6, citing the paper's note 4); whether that
concession is in this book is for a reading to check.

**Relation to morality-as-cooperation** (inference, labelled). Both say
human morality evolved from cooperation, and Curry 2016 ([LIT-tmpcsgn6](LIT-tmpcsgn6.md))
lists Tomasello's earlier statement with Vaish (2013) among the
"previous cooperative views of morality". They differ in three ways a
reading should check against the text. MAC derives morality from seven
types of cooperation game; Tomasello, on his chapter titles, from one
route, interdependence in collaboration (mutualism), through shared and
then collective intentionality. MAC treats reciprocity as a moral domain;
Tomasello's later paper argues that reciprocity cannot generate
obligation ([NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md), C6). And "cooperation-plus" says morality is
cooperation *with* something added (on [LIT-125](LIT-125.md)'s account, a self-regulating
"we" and its normative force), where MAC identifies morality with the
solutions to cooperation problems. MAC's contest, division and possession
domains have no chapter here.

**Neighbours.** Trivers ([LIT-tmppstp9](LIT-tmppstp9.md)) is the reciprocity account
Tomasello sets aside. Alexander ([LIT-tmp0b4eb](LIT-tmp0b4eb.md)) and Boehm ([LIT-tmpwfq9u](LIT-tmpwfq9u.md))
also give natural histories, by reputation and by social selection. The
joint-commitment philosophy [LIT-125](LIT-125.md) draws on is in the record through
[LIT-125](LIT-125.md)'s commentaries ([NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md)) and Gilbert ([LIT-477](LIT-477.md), [LIT-492](LIT-492.md)).

**Topic.** `moral-psychology` first. `cognition` for shared and collective
intentionality as cognitive capacities, `social-ontology` for joint and
collective agency (as on [LIT-125](LIT-125.md)), `social-science` for the developmental
and comparative experiments. `ethics` is not added: nothing reachable
says the book argues what is owed, although it offers, in Kitcher's
endorsement on the press page, "a philosophically relevant genealogy of
morality".
