# NOTE-tmpoed22: The Random Hierarchy Model

## Contribution

A synthetic family of classification tasks, generated by a random
context-free grammar, in which the number of examples a network needs can
be measured and in part predicted. Before it, depth was known to help
*represent* hierarchically compositional functions with few parameters
(Poggio, Mhaskar et al.; Schmidt-Hieber), and to support a polynomial
information-theoretic bound, but not to help networks *trained by gradient
descent* learn from few examples. The paper shows by experiment that it
does here: P* ≈ n_c m^L for deep CNNs in a feature-learning regime, against
a constant fraction of P_max (exponential in d) for two-layer and lazy
networks. It ties that number to two others, the onset of invariance to
synonyms in the hidden layers and the detectability of patch–label
correlations, and shows the correlations are necessary.

## Key insight

A hierarchical task is learnable from few data when its low-level parts
carry a detectable trace of the label, because the trace is the same for
parts that are interchangeable. Counting how often each s-tuple appears with
each class groups synonymic tuples together (same parent, same counts), and
collapsing each group to one symbol reduces the input from s^L to s^(L−1)
positions; repeating this L times reaches the label. The counts become
reliable at about n_c m^L examples, and that is where deep networks start
to generalise.

## Assumptions

- **Grammar.** n_c classes, L levels, vocabulary v at every level, branching
  s, multiplicity m; constant across levels "for ease of exposition".
  Each class and each level-ℓ symbol rewrites to m distinct s-tuples of
  level-(ℓ − 1) symbols, chosen uniformly at random; no s-tuple has two
  parents, so m v_{ℓ+1} ≤ v_ℓ^s. Production rules are equiprobable: every
  datum of a class is equally likely, and the training set is P data drawn
  without replacement, uniformly.
- **Input.** One-hot over v symbols at each of d = s^L positions, whitened
  over channels.
- **Networks.** Over-parametrised (width (4–8)·v^s, increased until test
  accuracy stops changing), maximal-update parametrisation so that features
  are learned as width grows. CNNs have depth L + 1 and non-overlapping
  filters of size s, i.e. matched to the tree; fully connected networks of
  depth L and 6 are also run. SGD on cross-entropy, batch 128, learning
  rate 0.1 annealed to 0.01, stopped at training loss 10⁻³; results
  averaged over 10 draws of grammar and initialisation.
- **The theory's regime.** The signal estimate is for v ≫ 1 with
  m = q v^(s−1), and large n_c and P (Appendix C); the main text's version
  assumes the m^L choices along a path are independent. The one-step
  model is a two-layer network on the first patch only, with the tuple
  one-hot over v^s values (orthogonalised inputs), first-layer weights all
  set to 1, fixed Gaussian readout, mean-field width limit, one full-batch
  gradient step.

## Key results

- **Eq. 3, 6.** Data per class m^((d−1)/(s−1)); P_max = n_c m^((d−1)/(s−1)),
  v^(s^L) in the maximal case n_c = v, m = v^(s−1).
- **Fig. 2.** Two-layer fully connected networks: the P needed for test
  error 0.7 ε_rand grows linearly with P_max. *Holds in:* L = s = 2,
  n_c = v = m, v from 4 to 32.
- **Eq. 7, Figs. 3, 4, 12.** Depth-(L + 1) CNNs: P* (test error below
  ε_rand/10) ≈ n_c m^L, i.e. P*/n_c ≈ d^(ln m/ln s), independent of v.
  *Holds in:* s = 2, L = 2–4, m from 3 to 32 with m ≤ v, n_c from 8 to 64
  (Fig. 12 at v = 256, m = 23, L = 2). An empirical law.
- **Fig. 13.** Deep fully connected networks of depth L or 6: the same law
  with an extra factor of about 2^L (s^L in the caption), independent of
  depth.
- **Fig. 14.** Depth-(L + 1) CNNs in the lazy limit (analytic NTK, kernel
  logistic regression): error stays finite up to P_max. They follow the
  feature-learning curves for P ≪ P*, using local correlations, but do
  not converge to zero.
- **Eq. 8, Figs. 5, 6.** S_{k,l} = ⟨‖f_k(x) − f_k(P_l x)‖²⟩/⟨‖f_k(x) − f_k(y)‖²⟩
  falls from 1 to 0 around P*; S_{2,1} curves collapse on P/(n_c m^L).
  Invariance to level-l swaps is reached from layer k = l + 1, and all
  levels together. *Holds in:* L = 3, s = 2, n_c = m = v = 8 for Fig. 6.
- **Eq. 10, C55.** Relative signal σ_f² = v/m^L (Eq. C49, for v, n_c ≫ 1);
  relative sampling noise v n_c/P (Eq. C54); equal at P_c = n_c m^L.
- **Eq. 13, Fig. 8.** After one gradient step, unit h's response to tuple µ
  is Σ_α a_{hα}(N̂(µ; α)/P − N̂(µ)/(n_c P)); since the true N(µ; α) depends
  on µ only through its parent (Eq. C7), it becomes synonym-invariant as P
  grows, with S_{1,1} collapsing on P/(n_c m^L). The hidden weight matrix
  then has rank tending to v.
- **Weight sharing** over all s^(L−1) patches scales signal and noise by the
  same factor, so does not change P_c in the one-step setting.
- **Appendix D, Fig. 10.** Clustering the n_c-dimensional one-step
  representations separates synonyms at P ≈ √n_c m^L, a factor √n_c below
  P_c; a layer-wise GD-plus-k-means procedure collapses on v^(L+1/2), not
  v^(L+1), at n_c = m = v, L = 3.
- **Fig. 9.** The uncorrelated RHM (every tuple equally likely in every
  class at every level; parity at m = v = n_c = 2): depth-(L + 1) CNNs stay
  near chance for P/P_max up to 0.9 when v > 2.
- **Fig. 15.** A ResNet18 on subsets of CIFAR-10, set beside the estimate
  P* ∈ [10³, 3 × 10⁴] for n_c = 10, L = 3, m ∈ [5, 15]. An illustration of
  magnitudes; m and L for CIFAR-10 are guesses.

## Concepts

- **Synonyms** — the m s-tuples a single higher-level symbol rewrites to;
  interchangeable for the label. Not linguistic synonymy.
- **Synonymic sensitivity S_{k,l}** — Eq. 8, layer k's response to swapping
  every level-l tuple for a random synonym, relative to swapping the whole
  datum.
- **Signal** — the deviation of the class probability given a tuple in a
  given patch, f_j(α|µ), from 1/n_c, over random draws of the rules.
- **Sample complexity P*** — the smallest P with test error below
  ε_rand/10, ε_rand = 1 − 1/n_c.
- **Maximal case** — n_c = v, m = v^(s−1): every string occurs.

## Connections

It descends from Mossel's hierarchical generative models (input–label
correlations needed for learnability) and Malach and Shalev-Shwartz's
layer-wise clustering algorithm, and positions itself against the classical
routes past the curse of dimensionality: low-dimensional manifolds,
smoothness, and dependence on a few linear projections. The maximal RHM has
none of the three. It cites Saxe, McClelland and Ganguli's semantic
development model ([LIT-862](../literature.d/LIT-862.md)) and DeGiuli's random language model as earlier
physics models of structured data. The lazy-regime contrast is the kernel
picture of [THEORY-086](../theory.d/THEORY-086.md) seen from the failure side: a fixed kernel can use
local correlations but cannot compose them.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md).** Named as the next step by [LIT-863](../literature.d/LIT-863.md), but it does not
  answer the question. The RHM's hierarchy is of constituency: an s-tuple
  in a position implies its parent symbol, and so on up to the class. It
  has no words with attributes, no co-occurrence of words with contexts,
  no PMI and no linear directions; the paper's embedding-like object, the
  vector of class frequencies of a tuple, is shown only to be equal for
  synonyms. Two things carry over. First, the mechanism by which the latent
  structure is read off is the one [THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md) relies on, latent structure
  showing in first-order co-occurrence statistics, here between a tuple
  and a class; second, the RHM's statistics are not independent across
  levels, so a derivation for [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md) built on it would need
  attributes defined as ancestors, which the paper does not attempt. An
  answer is more likely to come from a tree of attributes over items such
  as [LIT-862](../literature.d/LIT-862.md)'s.
- **[THEORY-186](../theory.d/THEORY-186.md).** Both are models of learning hierarchically generated
  data with a sharp, sigmoidal onset. In [THEORY-186](../theory.d/THEORY-186.md) the stages come from the
  singular-value spectrum of a tree of attributes and are learned coarse to
  fine; here every level is learned at the same P, and the onset is set by
  sampling noise against a random-rule signal. The two are not in conflict,
  being about different quantities (time against sample size) and different
  data, but they should not be cited as one account of "learning
  hierarchies".
- **[CLAIM-119](../claims.d/CLAIM-119.md) and [LIT-267](../literature.d/LIT-267.md).** No bearing beyond [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md)'s.
- **New account.** [THEORY-tmpzjtfu](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpzjtfu.md), Proposed: the sample-size law and its
  tie to detectable correlations.
- **Practice.** The paper gives no instruction. Its CIFAR-10 estimate is a
  magnitude check, and the layer-wise clustering result is an existence
  claim about an algorithm, not a recommendation. Its subject, what
  structure of data makes learning possible, is what the anthology's
  `signal-structure` topic holds, hence the flag on the LIT.

## Limitations

- **P* ≈ n_c m^L is fitted, not derived for the trained networks.** The
  derivation is of P_c, a statistical threshold, and the link to trained
  deep networks is the agreement of collapses plus a one-step model of one
  patch in a two-layer network. The authors say whether end-to-end training
  can reach the clustering rate "remains open".
- **Narrow ranges.** s = 2 throughout the experiments; L ≤ 4; P_max is small
  enough to enumerate. The threshold for P* (ε_rand/10) is a choice.
- **The CNNs are told the tree.** Filter size s, non-overlapping patches and
  depth L + 1 match the generative process; the fully connected runs
  remove the matching and show an extra factor of about 2^L.
- **Uniform rules.** Every production is equally likely and every symbol
  appears equally often, unlike language; the authors name non-uniform
  frequencies and noisy rules as extensions.
- **Synonymy here is exact interchangeability under a known grammar.** The
  paper's suggestion to test invariance to synonym swaps in language
  models is not carried out.

## Open questions

- A proof that gradient descent on a deep network reaches P* ≈ n_c m^L, or
  a counter-example in which correlations are detectable earlier than the
  network learns.
- What sets P* when production probabilities are non-uniform (Zipfian), so
  that different tuples become detectable at different P.
- Whether a co-occurrence embedding of the RHM's tuples, or of symbols with
  their ancestors as attributes, has linear directions for those ancestors,
  which is the form in which it would bear on [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md).
