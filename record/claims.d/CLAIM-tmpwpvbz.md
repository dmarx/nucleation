# CLAIM-tmpwpvbz: A process with no procedural friction can still be interruptible: standing constraints set slowly in advance and enforced by mechanisms faster than the process can foreclose alternatives keep it open to intervention without delaying it, so an opportunity to intervene does not consist in friction

## The claim

A33 §8 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c):
an AI agent processes transactions rapidly, but "the agent's permissions,
maximum transaction size, spending budget, and categories of authorized
action can be established beforehand". "The distinction is between
**slow review after action** and **slowly established rules that
constrain action before it happens**." So "procedural friction can be
selectively *compiled into enforceable operating constraints*", on a
condition: "the slower governing process must itself remain open to
legitimate revision."

A35 §7 restates it as two architectures, reactive oversight (fast action,
then consequences, then slow review) against prospectively constrained
execution, and draws the condition: "The slow authority must have fast
enough *actuators*, even if its deliberative processes remain slow."

## Why it objects

The owner's claim ([CLAIM-tmpuiaop](CLAIM-tmpuiaop.md)) has two readings. On "exposes", friction is where an
intervention opportunity becomes visible, and removing friction can remove
it. On "characterizes", an opportunity to intervene is a piece of
friction. The standing-constraint architecture is a process in which an
exception halts before crossing a consequential boundary and no step waits
on anyone, so it is interruptible without friction. If that is coherent,
the "characterizes" reading is false and the "exposes" reading is not
touched: friction is one place opportunities are exposed, and there are
others.

What carries the opportunity, on this account, is a relation of speeds: the
enforcing mechanism must act before the process forecloses the
alternatives, whatever the speed of the deliberation that set the limit.
That is [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)'s deadline applied to the
actuator, not the deliberation, and
[CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)'s point that what settles slowly can act as a
constraint on what moves fast.

## What it does not say

- **That standing constraints suffice.** They interrupt only the
  transitions anticipated when they were set. A novel failure still needs
  a channel through which someone can stop the process, and that channel
  may need the friction the owner's claim describes. A35 §5 makes the same
  point about specification lag.
- **That the constraints are legitimate.** A35 §7: "The second design isn't
  automatically legitimate, of course. Its constraints may be poorly
  chosen, unjust, or ineffective."

## Note of 2026-11-16: Hong Kong Monetary Authority, read

The HKMA's discussion paper (LIT-tmpo6res, NOTE-tmphnawz) is where the exchange
found this objection's architecture: A23 §3 cites it, and A33 §8 and A35 §7
develop it. Read, the paper specifies the architecture, but it does not draw
the objection's conclusion.

- **Supported.** Limits set beforehand and technically enforced:
  boundaries "should be defined in advance, technically enforced and changed
  only through the AI’s" change management (§7.1, p. 35), and a failed
  deterministic check is "prevented, escalated or routed for human review."
  (p. 36). Fast actuators: intervention is timed by "detection time,
  decision time and stopping time" (§7.2, p. 37), and stopping must be shown
  to work in substance, not "on paper" (p. 38). Revision held by slow
  authority: "human-in-command" persons may "define, change, restrict,
  suspend or withdraw" the mandate and boundaries (p. 37).
- **The claim's own caveats, confirmed.** Standing limits miss what was not
  foreseen. An action "within its authority" that "complies with defined
  rules" can be inappropriate in the circumstances, and needs
  transaction-specific validation before it takes effect (§4.4, p. 20).
  Boundaries drift: "A deployment may also move along this spectrum without
  a deliberate decision." (§3.2, p. 13).
- **Not supported.** The paper never treats standing limits as removing the
  need for friction. It keeps approval gates before consequential actions
  (p. 37). Its main answer to speed is to slow the agent to what oversight
  can handle: velocity limits commensurate "with the demonstrated
  effectiveness of oversight", and where oversight fails, "authority, scope
  or velocity may need to be constrained." (§4.3, p. 19). That is
  deliberately reintroduced delay, which favours the owner's claim
  (CLAIM-tmpuiaop) more than the objection.

Relation: none to add as corroboration; the paper is not independent of
A's argument. The filer may cite LIT-tmpo6res in this claim as the
documentary source of its architecture. No change of status.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Alfrink, read

The framework names both of the objection's routes to contestability before a decision takes effect. One is ex-ante contestation of design choices, "as part of AI system development processes", an "agonistic debate" over data and modelling: the objection's slowly set standing constraints. The other is built-in safeguards and "formal constraints on system behavior" enforced on each decision. The paper offers both alongside post-hoc review and intervention requests, never as replacing them. That fits the objection's condition that the slow governing process stay open to revision, and gives no evidence that the arrangement suffices.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Frey and Schneider, read

Among the mechanisms: "Make a community’s rules binding on administrators.", possibly by "rules that are
enforced computationally, placing hard limits on behavior" (§3), as blockchain protocols bind founders. A
standing constraint that keeps those in authority interruptible without delaying them, as this claim says
is possible. Proposal, not demonstration. Relation: bears_on.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Citron, read

Citron's technological due process is a set of standing, upstream constraints: audit trails that "record"
the facts and rules of each decision (p. 1305), "testing suites" run before launch and whenever policy
changes (p. 1310), open code and IT review boards. They keep automated decisions open to correction
without slowing each decision, as CLAIM-tmpwpvbz says is possible. Relation: corroborates CLAIM-tmpwpvbz
(as an institutional proposal, not a demonstration that it works).
