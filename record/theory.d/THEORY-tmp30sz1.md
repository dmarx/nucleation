# THEORY-tmp30sz1: Statements that are visibly false to anyone who checks, such as 'long-term' for a three-day study or 'assault' contradicted by video, persist in science, paid testimony and official statements when the community that hears them accepts them as terms of art and attaches no cost to them, so that those willing to make things up gain over those who are not

## Source

Gelman (2025), [LIT-014](../literature.d/LIT-014.md), "Reckless disregard for the truth coming from cops,
doctors, and scientists: A rant", read in [NOTE-019](../notes.d/NOTE-019.md). Section numbers are the
post's.

## What was actually shown

Nothing is measured. The post argues from examples in three domains.

- **Scientists (§1).** A paper described as "long-term" studied effects
  over three days; a power-pose abstract said people "instantly become more
  powerful" with no measure of power. Gelman's concern is that "the authors
  involved never even saw the problem".
- **Paid testimony (§2).** Ludmerer testified in 2002 to "no opinion" on
  whether smoking contributes to lung cancer.
- **Official statements (§3).** DHS called Lander's conduct "assaulting law
  enforcement" and Padilla's "lung[ing]", accounts quoted journalists say
  video disputes. Bump could not get ICE to source a "413 percent increase
  in assaults" claim.

**The account (§4a–d).** False claims are added when the real arguments
are not good enough (motive). A channel carries them: journals that let
"just about anything" into a title given significant p-values, courts, and
media repeating official statements (mechanism). Inside the community the
phrases "function as terms of art", and critics are outsiders (acceptance).
There are no consequences beyond "the occasional angry columnist or
blogger" (§4d). By "a form of Gresham's Law, liars win" in the short run,
and lying is negative-sum. Bystanders who tolerate it tip the incentives.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Nothing in the post could. The
examples were chosen to illustrate it.

## What this does not say

- **How common it is.** Three to five anecdotes, chosen, not sampled
  ([NOTE-019](../notes.d/NOTE-019.md), Limitations). [NOTE-019](../notes.d/NOTE-019.md) rates the persistence claim (C5) weak.
- **That the speakers know they are lying.** §4c says "I don't even think
  the people saying these false statements recognize them as lies"; §4a
  says of Ludmerer that "he was lying". The account works either way only
  if acceptance, not intent, carries it, and the post does not say which.
- **That the channel works in every case.** Gelman concedes the testimony
  case probably did not persuade anyone (§4b).
- **The legal standard.** "Reckless disregard for the truth" is a
  commenter's phrase, not the defamation-law actual-malice test.
- **That the police examples are established.** They rest on quoted
  journalism; neither the author nor this reading examined the video.
