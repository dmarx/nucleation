# CLAIM-191: Restating an argument across memoryless sessions is iterated transport with only a weak channel back to the source: without anchors, restatements drift toward the collaborators' own framings rather than at random, and a code restores continuity of transmission

## The claim

The nucleating-argument line cited the pragmatic-transport line only as
data: its theses [CLAIM-017](CLAIM-017.md), [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) and [CLAIM-133](CLAIM-133.md) as instances of drift.
It did not use the transport line's results. Read as transport, an
argument restated across sessions by collaborators who keep no memory is a
chain of retellings, [CASE-015](../cases.d/CASE-015.md)'s telephone game. Each session reconstructs
the argument from what reaches it, and passes on its reconstruction.

Three transport results then apply:

- **Convergence is not preservation** ([CLAIM-081](CLAIM-081.md)). A chain can settle on
  a stable form while forgetting where it started. A thesis that keeps
  coming back in some form is not thereby the same thesis.
- **Drift has a direction.** Serial transmission is "filtered by the
  transmitters' inductive biases" ([CLAIM-090](CLAIM-090.md)), and Bayesian learners who
  sample from the posterior converge to their shared prior ([THEORY-155](../theory.d/THEORY-155.md)).
  Read across, unanchored restatements should drift toward the
  collaborators' stock framings. That is a prediction [CLAIM-180](CLAIM-180.md) does not
  make: it treats drift as retrieval failure outside a critical distance,
  which by itself has no direction.
- **Local errors propagate by the chain's dynamics** ([CLAIM-056](CLAIM-056.md)). The same
  small restatement error can be damped, accumulate or be amplified. That
  is the transport form of what [CLAIM-186](CLAIM-186.md)'s note takes from generalized
  stability theory.

A code changes the chain. [CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md) says identity across retellings is a
family of relations, artifact, continuity of transmission and similarity of
function, and that "descent is not similarity". Re-derivation is identity
by similarity alone, which is all a memoryless collaborator has
([CLAIM-180](CLAIM-180.md)). A code supplies continuity of transmission: later entries
reach the claim by descent, by its code, not by resemblance. That is why
storage does not anchor and addressability does ([CLAIM-182](CLAIM-182.md)).

## What it does not say

- It does not say the chain is memoryless. The transcripts persist
  ([CLAIM-182](CLAIM-182.md)), and the owner carries memory across sessions and
  reintroduced the relativity analogy ([CLAIM-177](CLAIM-177.md)). The convergence
  theorems assume transmitters with no access to the source, and
  [CLAIM-066](CLAIM-066.md) limits Griffiths and Kalish's result to learners that sample
  from the posterior. The channel back to the source is weak, not absent.
- It does not say developing an argument aims at preservation. It does
  not; see the counter, [CLAIM-189](CLAIM-189.md).
- The direction prediction is untested. [CLAIM-177](CLAIM-177.md)'s evidence was coded by
  readers from the same assistant family ([CLAIM-085](CLAIM-085.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-12: a measured case for the direction of drift

[CASE-068](../cases.d/CASE-068.md), filed in PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121), is now a ground of this claim. In two
serial-reproduction studies of risk messages, the transmitters' measured
risk perception predicts the direction in which the evaluative signal
changes, and re-exposure to the balanced source did not undo the drift.
That is a measured instance of drift toward the transmitters' bias, and of
a return to the source failing to pull a chain back. Its limit is
[CLAIM-234](CLAIM-234.md)'s: the record's chain evidence covers evaluative stance,
not footing, and not the restatement of an argument. The chains are of
human retellers, not memoryless sessions, and in one study the prior was
measured after the experiment.

## Note of 2026-10-12: the regimes this claim leans on

The third point leans on [CLAIM-056](CLAIM-056.md)'s regimes. [CLAIM-056](CLAIM-056.md) carries an Active
objection, [CLAIM-208](CLAIM-208.md), which says a sensitivity needs many retellings
of each of several inputs at every generation, "where a chain gives one".
The record conceded it for [CLAIM-059](CLAIM-059.md) and [CLAIM-078](CLAIM-078.md) and left [CLAIM-056](CLAIM-056.md)'s
condition unchanged. [CLAIM-059](CLAIM-059.md) version 2 now asks for "several independent
retellers per generation", in a metric other than total variation. A
sequence of memoryless sessions gives one restatement per generation, so
testing the third point in this claim's own setting would need that
design.
