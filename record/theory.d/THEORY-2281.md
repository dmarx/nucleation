# THEORY-2281: Frequency differences between remote clocks estimated by two satellite techniques agree with each other and with a simultaneous optical-fibre comparison within their combined uncertainties

## The account

Riedel et al. ([LIT-3144](../literature.d/LIT-3144.md), read in [NOTE-2371](../notes.d/NOTE-2371.md)) compare remote
optical and fountain clocks through local hydrogen masers and a satellite
link between them. Two satellite techniques run at once: TWSTFT, a
bidirectional exchange of coded signals that cancels the transit times,
and GPS PPP. Each gives an estimate of every clock pair's relative
frequency difference.

- The two techniques' estimates agree within their combined uncertainties
  (p. 11 for the optical clocks, p. 13 for the fountains).
- For LNE-SYRTE and PTB, where an optical-fibre link joins the same
  clocks, the satellite estimates agree with the fibre comparisons, the
  strontium one run simultaneously with the campaign (pp. 12–13).
- The authors take the satellite techniques to be "completely
  independent", so that comparing them can expose systematic effects in
  either (p. 13).

## Why it is here

It corroborates [CLAIM-510](../claims.d/CLAIM-510.md). The frequency difference between two remote
standards is a correspondence among local descriptions that measurement
estimates, and here it is checked against channels that took no part in
producing the estimate. The case is [CASE-387](../cases.d/CASE-387.md).

## Limits

The channels share the clocks and the redshift correction, so the
agreement tests the links and not the clocks or the geodesy. Only one pair
has a fibre check. The uncertainty budgets that decide "agree" were not
read (§3 and the appendix were skipped).
