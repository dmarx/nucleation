# THEORY-tmpnfh8u: A public ranking can raise its own validity through the reactions it provokes: where a unit's published rank affects the inputs to its next rank, as when applicants choose by rank and their choices feed the selectivity score, or raters who know little but the last rank fill the reputation survey, rank differences that began as measurement noise can become real differences that the ranking then correctly reports

## Source

Espeland, W. N. & Sauder, M. (2007), [LIT-tmpngpsy](../literature.d/LIT-tmpngpsy.md), read in [NOTE-tmpxq56d](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxq56d.md).

## What was actually shown

The definition: "We define self-fulfilling prophecies as processes by
which reactions to social measures confirm the expectations or
predictions that are embedded in measures or which increase the validity
of the measure by encouraging behavior that conforms to it." No false
belief is needed.

The channels named, with the evidence the paper gives:

- **Rank → applicants → selectivity.** Raw scores are "tightly bunched".
  Small rank differences move applications and yield (Sauder and
  Lancaster 2006, cited). These change the selectivity score "and in this
  way reinforce the original differences which were often largely a
  product of measurement noise." The illustration is two elite schools: A
  went from losing about two thirds of cross-admits to B to winning a
  reported 80–90%, after years of ranking slightly higher.
- **Past rank → reputation survey.** Raters know little about most
  schools. Stake (2006, cited) "finds that reputation scores slowly become
  aligned with overall rank", and past rankings are the strongest
  predictor of current reputation score. Footnote 14: Schmalbeck (2001)
  finds no effect of a one-year drop on the next survey among the top 50.
  The authors read the effect as slow and strongest for lower-ranked
  schools.
- **Conformity.** Schools become "more like what rankings measure, which
  reinforces the validity of the measure". The access school weighs
  moving its low-LSAT admits out of the counted program.

## What this does not say

- **Not that rankings are invalid.** On this account the ranking can come
  to report real differences correctly. The point is where they came
  from.
- **Not measured here.** Both quantitative channels are cited. Their
  identification (rank against quality) is not in this paper, and the
  record has not read the studies.
- **Not a model of the dynamics.** Nothing says when the loop amplifies a
  small difference and when it damps one. The cross-admit example is one
  pair of schools.
- **Not, by itself, an argument about kinds.** [CLAIM-tmpeftzb](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpeftzb.md)'s condition
  (the evidence for a category must not pass through the category's use)
  is what this channel would violate. Showing that it does so in a given
  case needs the identification that promote_when asks for.
