# THEORY-1920: AI decisions should be contestable throughout

## Source

The new LIT for Alfrink et al. (Minds and Machines 33:613–639), read in the open-access publisher PDF as a partial stand-in for Harid (2026), [LIT-2676](../literature.d/LIT-2676.md).

## What was actually shown

A systematic review and thematic synthesis, not a test. The framework's features are built-in safeguards (including a second system every decision passes, flagging disagreement for human review), interactive control, explanations, human review and intervention requests, and tools for scrutiny. Its practices are ex-ante safeguards, agonistic development, quality assurance during and after development, risk mitigation and third-party oversight. The authors state that "Requests for human intervention are necessarily post-hoc", and that validation "was not part of this paper".

## What this does not say

- That any feature works: most source papers "are not based on empirically validated interventions".
- Anything about friction, delay or their costs, or about who bears them.
- Harid's construct: it is filed as a neighbour for the concept of pre-execution contestability only.
