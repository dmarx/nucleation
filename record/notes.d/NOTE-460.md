# NOTE-460: Précis: Being No One

## Contribution

The précis compresses the theory of [LIT-467](../literature.d/LIT-467.md) into its three targets:
consciousness (§2), the phenomenal self (§3) and the first-person perspective
(§4). It does not follow the book's order. Its method is "a
constraint-satisfaction approach to phenomenal experience". Six of the book's
ten constraints are each analysed on four levels: phenomenological,
representational, informational or functional, and neural. The précis adds no
evidence. What it adds to the record's reading of [LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md) is the constraint
machinery itself, the graded account of transparency, and the PMIR.

## Key insight

What makes a self-model into a self is something missing: information about
the model's own construction. If a system cannot attend to the earlier
processing stages that build its model of itself, it cannot experience that
model as a model. It therefore experiences it as itself. Selfhood is "a
function realized by a lack of information", and a "naive-realistic
self-misunderstanding". On the same logic a world-model that cannot be seen as
a model is experienced as the world. The unity of that world is the unity of
one integrated functional cluster.

## Assumptions

- **Representationalism and functionalism.** Phenomenal content is a kind of
  representational content with specific functional properties, analysed
  level by level. Phenomenal content "locally supervenes" on the brain: "A
  bodiless brain in a vat could certainly enjoy the phenomenal experience of
  holding a paper like this one".
- **Consciousness as global availability.** Phenomenal information is "that
  subset of currently active information in the system, of which it is true
  that it is globally available for many different processing capacities at
  the same time". The précis notes one known limit: fine-grained sensory
  contents are available for attention and motor control but not for
  concept formation.
- **A working definition of transparency.** "Transparency holds if earlier
  processing stages are unavailable for attentional processing." This is
  offered as Metzinger's own definition, against the standard one
  (introspective access to content properties only).
- **The Conant–Ashby theorem** is cited for the claim that "every regulator
  of a complex system will automatically and by necessity become a model of
  that system", the starting point for the self-model.
- **Interest-relative terminology.** Whether transparency is necessary for
  phenomenality is "in part interest-relative". It is needed for a robust
  phenomenal self. It is not needed if one counts selfless states, such as
  depersonalization or certain spiritual experiences, as experiences.

## Key results

There are no formal results. The précis's claims, by section:

- **§1.1 Minimal consciousness.** Globality, presentationality and
  transparency together give "the presence of a world": a coherent global
  model in a window of presence that cannot be recognised as a model.
  Metzinger calls this "Selfless Snapshot Consciousness", and says the
  minimal notion is "probably empirically empty".
- **§1.2 The phenomenal self.** "No such things as selves exist in the
  world." The self is "not a thing, but an integrated process", the content
  of a phenomenal self-model (PSM). Organisms "neither have nor are a self";
  "we are systems that constantly confuse themselves with the content of
  their PSM".
- **§2.1 Globality and unity.** Phenomenal representata are bound by
  "highest-order binding" into one causal role. On the neural level this
  predicts "one single area of maximal causal density" (the dynamic core).
  Its physical make-up changes "from millisecond to millisecond" while it
  stays one functional cluster.
- **§2.2 Presence.** "All conscious experience is a form of memory." The
  experienced Now is "a simulational fiction from the third-person
  perspective", generated by elementary integration units that delete
  information about the system's own temporal processing.
- **§2.3 Graded transparency.** "For every phenomenal state, the degree of
  phenomenal transparency is inversely proportional to the introspective
  degree of attentional availability of earlier processing stages."
  Examples: sensory experience is the paradigm of transparency, thought of
  opacity, and emotion lies between. Lucid dreams are globally opaque except
  for the dreamer's self, which "still looks entirely real".
- **§2.6 Perspectivalness is not necessary for experience.** Fully
  depersonalized states and some spiritual experiences are "non-subjective
  consciousness". The centre of the perspective is anchored by a "single
  embodiment-constraint" and by continuous internal input (vestibular, body
  schema, visceral, brain stem).
- **§3.3 From system-model to self.** "Any machine can do self-directed
  modeling." The step to selfhood is transparency, which is also "an
  evolutionary viable strategy" for halting the regress of self-modelling:
  the "principle of necessary self-reification". It yields subject-formation,
  not object-formation. A transparent PSM makes a system "maximally
  egotistic". "You become a person by having the right kind of subpersonal
  self-model."
- **§3.5 Process, not thing.** The phenomenal self is "highly
  discontinuous", interrupted by sleep, and the experience of an enduring
  self comes from "the invariance of bodily self-awareness and
  autobiographical memory". "We are not things, but processes."
- **§4 The PMIR.** A phenomenal subject is "a model of the system as acting
  and experiencing". The PMIR co-represents "the representational relation
  itself". A second-order PMIR gives "introspective intelligence": awareness
  of agency, of attention, and, with concepts, of having a first-person
  perspective. A PMIR aimed at another agent gives "social intelligence", up
  to mutual acknowledgement "as persons".

## Concepts

- **PSM (phenomenal self-model)**: the conscious part of a self-model, "an
  integrated representation of the system as a whole", combining
  self-presentation (sensory), self-simulation (top-down hypotheses) and
  self-representation.
- **Transparency**: attentional unavailability of earlier processing stages.
  A phenomenological concept, not an epistemological one, and unrelated to
  referential transparency or to transparency in media.
- **Autoepistemic closure**: "a structurally anchored deficit in the
  capacity to gain knowledge about oneself"; the epistemological side of
  transparency.
- **Mineness**: the higher-order phenomenal property, "often also called the
  'sense of ownership'", that binds contents into the self-model.
- **PMIR (phenomenal model of the intentionality relation)**: a conscious
  model whose content is "an ongoing, episodic subject–object-relation".
  Distinguished from Brentano's "Good Old Fashioned Intentionality".
- **Convolved holism**: nested part–whole structure of experience, with "no
  decontextualized atoms".

## Connections

The précis's own lineage is the book ([LIT-467](../literature.d/LIT-467.md)), with earlier work cited for
highest-order binding (Metzinger 1995) and the PMIR (1993, 2000). It builds on
Baars's and Chalmers's global availability, Tononi and Edelman's dynamic core,
Llinás's thalamocortical model, Pöppel's elementary integration units and the
Conant–Ashby theorem, all as cited. It overlaps [LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md) almost entirely in its
conclusions; the difference is in method and detail.

Against the record's other selves. Gallagher's minimal self ([LIT-453](../literature.d/LIT-453.md)) has the
same two parts, ownership and agency, though the précis treats ownership
("mineness") as the binding property. Dennett's narrative self ([LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md))
shares the denial of a substantial self. Metzinger's self is pre-narrative,
and the précis mentions narrative only through autobiographical memory.
Minsky's self-models ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md)) are the "system-models" the précis says "any
machine can do". On Metzinger's terms they are not yet selves unless they are
conscious and transparent.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-044](../theory.d/THEORY-044.md)** (the self in relational frame theory). Two conflicting
  claims sit close together here. That account holds that the self as context
  is "a context for verbal knowing, not the content", and so cannot be found
  as content. The précis holds that what is experienced as the self is
  content (a transparent self-model), and that the point of view is itself
  modelled (the PMIR). They may not conflict: "self as context" may name what
  the précis calls the transparent partition, which cannot be attended to as
  a model. A theory relating the two would need the RFT account to say
  whether the perspective is represented. As far as the record shows, it
  does not.
- **[THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)** (felt affect). The précis anchors the self-model in
  homeostatic and visceral input and likens the transparent PSM to Damasio's
  core self. That is consistent with the account. It adds no evidence for it.
- **A candidate claim, now filed** as [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md), with C1 filed as
  [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md). The précis supplies one side of a
  dispute the record could hold as a THEORY: whether phenomenal unity is the
  unity of a self-model's world, a functional binding, or a narrative unity.
  The narrative side is McAdams ([LIT-595](../literature.d/LIT-595.md)) and Schechtman. Proposed only,
  since the précis gives the functional side as hypothesis.

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice. The question it
raises about machine self-models is philosophical, and is already discussed
at [LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md).

## Limitations

- It is a summary. Its arguments are sketched and refer to the book for
  detail. The case studies that test the theory (agnosia, neglect,
  blindsight, depersonalization) are explicitly left out.
- The neural sections say repeatedly that little is known. "Currently no
  detailed theories concerning the possible neural correlates ... do exist";
  "not much is presently known about the neural underpinnings of the
  transparent self-model".
- The central ontological claim, that there are no selves, depends on
  identifying "self" with a substantial, essential, indivisible individual.
  A process view of the self would not be refuted by it, and the précis
  itself says "we are not things, but processes".
- The circularity worry about transparency (it is defined only for
  phenomenal states) is answered by a terminological decision, not an
  argument.

## Open questions

- What would test C2? The précis names depersonalization and "system
  consciousness" as candidate states with an opaque system-model, but reports
  of them face its own "performative self-contradiction" objection.
- Whether graded transparency (C3) can be measured as attentional
  availability of earlier processing stages, and whether it covaries with
  reported selfhood.
