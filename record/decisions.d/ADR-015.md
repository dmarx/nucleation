# ADR-015: self-and-personhood joins the topics, and identity becomes individuation

## Context

The owner asked how the record covers identity, personhood and selfhood. 39
works carried `identity`, and they were about three different things:

- **Individuation and identity of things:** structural realism, quasi-sets,
  dualities, the Yoneda lemma, criteria of identity.
- **Biological individuality:** Bourrat, Wilson, Griesemer, Lloyd, the
  organism.
- **Personal identity and the self:** Dufner, Floridi, Velleman, Frankfurt,
  Dennett, Hofstadter, Minsky's self-models, Levin's selves, the extended
  self.

A reader browsing `identity` for the self met quasi-set theory first. The
self material was hard to find and hard to see the gaps in. The record held
almost none of the personal-identity literature its cognitive-science and
AI readings answer to. The owner asked for that literature to be filed and
for this word to be added.

## Decision

Add `self-and-personhood` to `topics`, in the `philosophy` group beside
`identity`, `consciousness`, `cognition` and `emotion-and-affect`. Its
blurb names personal identity, the narrative, minimal and bodily self,
self-models, agency over one's own life, and machine or group selves.

`identity`'s blurb narrows to "individuation, individuality and persistence
of things of any kind". It points the self and the person to the new word.

**`identity` is renamed `individuation`.** Once the self has left it, what
the word holds is the individuation of things: what makes something one
thing and the same thing, numerically identical to or distinct from
another, an individual organism, persisting through change. "Identity" read
alone invites the personal sense the decision just moved out, and a reader
browsing for the self would still land there first. The word's 41 bearers
change in place, in the same tag position; no work gains or loses a topic,
so no relation's tag invariant changes. The blurb keeps numerical identity
and "across descriptions" by name, because the structuralist and quasi-set
readings are about exactly that. Notes and curation entries written before
the rename keep saying `identity`; they record what the tag was called when
they were written.

**Retagged.** No tag was removed, so no relation's tag invariant loses a
value.

- **First tag:** [LIT-130](../literature.d/LIT-130.md) (Dufner), [LIT-136](../literature.d/LIT-136.md) (Floridi), [LIT-405](../literature.d/LIT-405.md) ("Where Am
  I?"), [LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md) ("Narrative Gravity"), [LIT-423](../literature.d/LIT-423.md) (*I Am a Strange Loop*),
  [LIT-413](../literature.d/LIT-413.md) (*The Mind's I*).
- **Second tag:**
  - [LIT-142](../literature.d/LIT-142.md) (Meincke, on pregnancy);
  - [LIT-292](../literature.d/LIT-292.md) (Velleman) and [LIT-293](../literature.d/LIT-293.md) (Frankfurt);
  - [LIT-416](../literature.d/LIT-416.md) (Hofstadter, "Is there an 'I' in AI?");
  - [LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md) (*The Emotion Machine*'s self-models);
  - [LIT-439](../literature.d/LIT-439.md) (Levin's selves);
  - [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) (the extended self).
- **Last tag:** [LIT-437](../literature.d/LIT-437.md) (*GEB*), whose "I" is a strange loop but whose
  subject is wider.

The personal-identity literature filed in the same contribution takes the
new word.

## Alternatives

- **Leave personal identity under `identity`.** That keeps one word for
  three subjects. The biology readings already show the cost: individuality
  of organisms and personal identity share a vocabulary and almost nothing
  else.
- **Two words, `personal-identity` and `self`.** The persistence question
  and the "what is a self" question are argued in the same papers. Parfit,
  Schechtman and Dennett's "Narrative Gravity" each answer both. Splitting
  them would split those papers.
- **Keep the name `identity`.** It is the established word in metaphysics,
  but in this vocabulary it now sits beside `self-and-personhood`, and the
  everyday reading of "identity" is the personal one. The name would keep
  sending the wrong reader to the wrong page.
- **Move biological individuality out too.** Bourrat and Wilson are about
  what makes something one individual, which is what `identity` now says.
  They stay, and are much of why `individuation` is the right name.
