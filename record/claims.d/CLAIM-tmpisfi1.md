# CLAIM-tmpisfi1: Resolving an ambiguity that no arrangement yet settles forms a contract, and the same act has two outcomes: resolved jointly, it is a productive misunderstanding that reorganizes the parties around a new distinction; resolved by one party who controls when and how, it is exploitation through ambiguity

## The join

- **CLAIM-tmp80ny9** (A's): of the three kinds of relational ambiguity,
  constitutive indeterminacy, where "no arrangement yet fix[es] what the
  relationship requires", is "resolved only by forming one". Resolving it
  forms a contract.
- **CLAIM-tmpupcws** (the owner's): a misunderstanding is productive when
  clarifying it detects a distinction that now matters and reorganizes the
  parties around it.
- **CLAIM-tmpro73c** (A's, extending the owner's U17): who controls
  resolving an ambiguity, when, and at whose cost can matter more than how
  ambiguous it is.

None of the three says that productive misunderstanding and exploitation
through ambiguity are the same act. This claim does: both are the
formation of terms where none were fixed. They differ in who sets the
terms and when.

## Admission (ADR-075)

By a discriminating prediction. If the claim holds, then across
resolutions of the same kind of ambiguity, the share of benefit each party
takes from the new terms tracks how far that party controlled the timing
and terms of the resolution, not how ambiguous the relation was beforehand.
The rival, that exploitation needs an ambiguity of a special kind (larger,
or about a more valuable matter), predicts the opposite dependence.

## What it does not say

- Not that every one-sided resolution is exploitative. The defeater names
  the case that would show it.
- Not that joint resolution is always productive. CLAIM-tmpgx2ej: leaving
  the ambiguity unresolved can be what is productive.
