# NOTE-tmp3uqu5: Surviving Death, Again

## Contribution

A change of view by the author of *Surviving Death*. He gives up the 2010
account of the self, on which what we are is partly fixed by our own
future-directed attitudes. He replaces it with a non-reductionist account:
we are embodied wills that endure through embodiments that perdure. The
argument for the change is ethical: reductionism about persons makes moral
life incoherent.

## Key insight

If a person is any maximal sum of suitably related stages, then within one
life there are countless person-like sums, and each has as good a claim to
moral status. A child drilled in scales for a musical future she, as a
seven-year-long sum, will never enjoy is wronged. A sinner's innocent
first decade goes unrewarded. Ethical life becomes "damned if you do,
damned if you don't". Only an enduring will, one bearer of moral status
per embodiment, restores what Johnston calls ethical singularity.

## Assumptions

- **Moral status is intrinsic** (p. 273): two beings that are moment by
  moment duplicates over a period both have moral status in that period.
  The fellow traveler argument runs on it.
- **A neo-Aristotelian ur-essence.** Having an essence is having one
  characteristic from which all and only one's essential properties flow,
  like a tone's loudness, pitch and timbre.
- **Theism**, for the account of where wills come from and for the hope of
  re-embodiment.

## Key results

- **The 2010 view and its withdrawal** (pp. 263–264). *Surviving Death*
  argued that the Christian hope is dashed by the conditions of personal
  survival and by what justice owes to innocent "personites". "In place of
  that standard Christian hope, I there offered a this-worldly analogue or
  surrogate, a way of living on in the onward rush of humankind—a
  surrogate developed out of the idea that we are the 'higher-order' kind
  of thing whose deepest future-directed attitudes determine which
  this-worldly future embodiments will be ours." Note 6 sets as an exercise
  finding a fellow traveler problem for that view.
- **Four fellow traveler problems** (pp. 271–273, 304–305):
  - personites in four-dimensionalism, and their sub-process analogue in
    Parfit;
  - R-variants: other continuity relations that build rival person-like
    things from the same events;
  - Dion and the "Theons": many overlapping candidates for the subject of
    one pain;
  - plural modal profiles under a plenitudinous view of essence.
- **The new account** (pp. 273–279). We are embodied wills, "embodied
  conscious valuers of value" able to act to secure those values. That is
  also the ground of moral status. Wills endure and their embodiments, as
  biological processes, perdure. Being somehow embodied follows from the
  ur-essence; this embodiment does not, so another is possible.
- **Against emergent substance dualism** (pp. 281–285). A fundamental law
  of soul emergence would be either non-Markovian or oddly self-defending,
  and "neurocentric" among quantitative laws. Theists should drop the fig
  leaf and say God creates and embodies each will.
- **Mind and brain** (pp. 291–304).
  - Identity is not reduction, since reduction is anti-symmetric.
  - A mental event m is reduced to a physical event p if m occurs wholly
    in virtue of p's nature. By that test the mental does not reduce.
  - Instead, neural events subserve passive mental events, implement
    mental acts, or are interdependent with them. These relations allow
    difference-making with no "oomphy" mind–body causation.
- **Conclusion** (pp. 308–309). Re-embodiment is possible, not shown
  actual. Hope for it is livable only "in a community of grace, where the
  members of that community in their life together have escaped the bounds
  of self-involvement".

## Concepts

- **fellow traveler**: a being within a person's spatiotemporal envelope,
  very like the person, not derivative of it, with different persistence
  conditions.
- **personite**: a non-maximal but long-lived sum of person stages.
- **ethical singularity**: being the only bearer of moral status within
  one's envelope.
- **embodied will**: an embodied agential subject, a conscious valuer of
  value able to act to secure it.
- **ur-essence**: a single individual characteristic from which all and
  only a thing's essential properties flow.
- **subserving / implementing / interdependence**: non-reductive
  dependence of a mental event on a physical one, of a physical event on a
  mental act, and essential co-occurrence.

## Bearing on the record

- **How the record cites the book.** CLAIM-tmpjylq8 says Johnston "takes
  the same premise to the opposite moral: the good person's self is as
  large as humanity". This paper confirms the premise as Johnston held it
  in 2010: what we are is fixed by our deepest future-directed attitudes,
  and the good live on in "the onward rush of humankind". It also shows he
  has given the premise up, so the book is a view its author no longer
  holds. A dated note is added to the claim.
- **A new objection to an attitude-fixed self.** Johnston rejects it
  because it multiplies bearers of moral status, not because it is
  trivial (CLAIM-tmprudek). If the extent of my self is set by what I
  identify with, there are as many candidate selves as ways of drawing
  that line, each with its own interests. Whether the thesis's enlarged
  self inherits the problem is an open question, and the paper does not
  raise it.

  record's current questions.
