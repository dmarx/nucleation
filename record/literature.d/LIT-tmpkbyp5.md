summary: >-
  Wickersham et al. (2020). An online experiment with 988 American adults aged 18 to 87, sampled evenly by age. Writing about what one wants future generations to remember one for (adapted from Zaval et al. 2015), or about a beloved place and climate change, raised pro-environmental intentions (η² = .030) and hypothetical donations to a tree-planting charity (η² = .009) against a scrambled-eggs control. Age did not moderate either effect. The legacy writing raised scores on an eight-item Legacy Motivation Scale, which statistically mediated part of its effect; but the place writing raised legacy motivation too, the legacy writing raised love of nature, and in a model with both mediators love of nature carried as much of the donation effect. The scale mixes wanting to be remembered with self-regard and felt responsibility to future generations, so the study does not show that wanting to be remembered is what moves people.
