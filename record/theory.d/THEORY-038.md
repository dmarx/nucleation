# THEORY-038: Category theory gives radical ontic structural realism no formal support: generalized elements exist in every category, morphisms relate objects the category presupposes, and Bain's physical cases eliminate spacetime points, not relata

## Source

Lam & Wüthrich, [LIT-222](../literature.d/LIT-222.md) (preprint, §§3–8), read in [NOTE-194](../notes.d/NOTE-194.md). The target is
Bain, [LIT-223](../literature.d/LIT-223.md) (§§2–6), read in [NOTE-193](../notes.d/NOTE-193.md). The Yoneda lemma, [LIT-221](../literature.d/LIT-221.md) (read in
[NOTE-195](../notes.d/NOTE-195.md)), supplies the mathematics. The synthesis is in the curation entry
of 2026-09-26, "The Yoneda lemma, and whether it supports ontic structural
realism".

## What was actually shown

**Bain's argument.** In set theory a relation is a subset of X × X, so it
presupposes its relata. Bain proposes defining "structure" as "object in a
category", taking elements to be arrows 1 → A. He argues that these are
"not essential" when the objects are not structured sets ([LIT-223](../literature.d/LIT-223.md) pp.
1623–1625). He offers two cases: sheaves of Einstein algebras for GR with
asymptotic boundaries, which may have no global sections, and nCob/Hilb for
TQFT. He concludes that radical OSR "avoids the charge that it rests on an
incoherent claim" (p. 1634). "Essential" is never defined.

**The rebuttal, point by point ([LIT-222](../literature.d/LIT-222.md)).**

- *Arrows-only presentations only relabel.* Identifying objects with
  identity arrows still needs composable pairs and a composition operation
  (§3, fn. 11).
- *Elements are not eliminated.* Generalized elements X → A exist in every
  category, so terminal-object elements were never the only kind (§4 p.
  10). By Yoneda these probes, with their functoriality, fix A up to
  isomorphism ([LIT-221](../literature.d/LIT-221.md), Cor. II). The rebuttal is therefore stronger than
  its authors state it. Bain's claim that elements "only make sense" in
  categories with terminal objects is false.
- *Morphisms relate C-objects.* Those objects are structures or models, not
  the physical relata. The morphisms "only distinguish isomorphism classes
  of structures" (§5 p. 11). The relations the categorial reading supplies
  are therefore between presupposed objects.
- *Where elements vanish, relations can vanish too.* Set-style relations
  need products, and a category can lack both terminal objects and
  products (§4).
- *The physics eliminates points only.* Einstein-algebra models drop
  manifold points "in name only", because points correspond to maximal
  ideals. Without global sections, local sections remain as local relata.
  nCob and Hilb have manifold points and vectors (§§6–7). Bain's own
  closing claim, "structure devoid of objects", goes beyond his §3
  ([NOTE-193](../notes.d/NOTE-193.md)).

The readings also found errors on both sides. Sections are not maximal
ideals (Bain p. 1629). Hilb has direct-sum products, so both papers are
wrong that it lacks them. Lam & Wüthrich mis-define a topos. None of these
errors bears on the conclusion.

## What this does not say

- That radical OSR is incoherent. Lam & Wüthrich close one route to it, and
  Chakravartty's argument leaves eliminative OSR "untouched" ([LIT-196](../literature.d/LIT-196.md), p.
  200).
- That balanced (non-eliminative) OSR is vindicated. Lam & Wüthrich assume
  it is coherent and do not defend it ([NOTE-194](../notes.d/NOTE-194.md)). That horn is the
  subject of [THEORY-034](THEORY-034.md), on Chakravartty's dilemma.
- That category theory is idle for structuralism. It changes what
  "structure" means, and Corfield's programmatic use ([LIT-115](../literature.d/LIT-115.md)) is untouched.
- That Bain, Lam or Wüthrich invoke the Yoneda lemma. None of them does.
