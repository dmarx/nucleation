# LIT-2687: Better AI with Designed Friction: Theories, Applications and Research Agenda

Chiara Natali, Mohammad Naiseh, Federico Cabitza and Brett Frischmann (2025). Better AI with Designed Friction: Theories, Applications and Research Agenda. In *Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence and Applications* (IOS Press). DOI 10.3233/FAIA250680.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of 2026-11-17; the reading of 2026-11-16 is below.*

- Designed friction is "deliberate inefficiencies designed to foster
  engagement, critical reasoning, and user agency" (abstract), proposed
  against the automation bias that frictionless design produces (§1).
- Its forms are all at the point of interaction: cognitive forcing
  functions ("checklists, justification prompts, and alternative
  hypothesis generation", §2), slow and seamful design, and AI
  provocations, where "systems challenge rather than obey the user".
- The design problem is calibration, "without causing undue frustration"
  (§1); the paper is an agenda, not a test.

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read in full in the publisher's open-access PDF. It is a two-page poster. It defines designed friction as "deliberate inefﬁciencies designed to foster engagement, critical reason[ing], and user agency", introduced "at critical points" of a human–AI interaction against automation bias, lists the mechanisms proposed in HCI (prosocial friction and speed bumps after Frischmann and Vardi, seamful and slow design, programmed inefficiencies, cognitive forcing functions, AI provocations), and sets an agenda for calibrating friction against cognitive load. It reports no data. Its friction is the inefficiency (delay) sense that [TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md) sets aside, located in a single user's interaction, not in a process between parties, so it does not bear on [CLAIM-710](../claims.d/CLAIM-710.md)'s institutional claim except as a neighbouring sense. The exchange (A21 §2, A23 §7) describes it accurately and rightly classes it as cognitive friction. The evidence it relies on, Buçinca et al. 2021, is held ([LIT-2283](LIT-2283.md), [THEORY-1765](../theory.d/THEORY-1765.md)). It cites Frischmann and Benesch, "Friction-in-design regulation" (Yale J.L. & Tech. 25, 2023), not held, as a likelier source for regulatory friction. See the NOTE and THEORY filed from this reading.
