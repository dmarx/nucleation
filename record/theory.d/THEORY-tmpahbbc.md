# THEORY-tmpahbbc: Seeing one's own back stroked on live video 2 m ahead, in time with felt strokes, raises agreement that the seen body felt like one's own and makes people walk back to a point some 17–25 cm toward it; the questionnaire separates a body from a box but the walking error does so only weakly, the error is not felt as drift, and nothing like leaving the body occurs

## Source

Lenggenhager, Tadi, Metzinger & Blanke (2007), "Video ergo sum:
manipulating bodily self-consciousness", *Science* 317:1096–1099,
[LIT-tmpeggnf](../literature.d/LIT-tmpeggnf.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmp9c2fc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md) with the supporting online
material and the Science e-letters of June 2008.

## What was actually shown

**The procedure.** Participants watched live 3D video of their own back,
filmed from 2 m behind, on a head-mounted display, while their back was
stroked for 60 s. In Study II a mannequin or a white box took the back's
place. Afterwards they rated seven statements from −3 to +3 and, blindfolded
after being walked backwards, walked to where they had stood.

**Study I (n = 14).** After synchronous stroking, "it felt as if the virtual
body was my body" scored 2.1, against negative scores after asynchronous
stroking. Drift toward the seen body was 24.1 ± 9.0 cm (SEM), against
12.5 ± 8.5 cm for asynchronous stroking (paired p = 0.01).

**Study II (n = 14).** Synchronous drift against a no-vision baseline was
17.1 cm for the own body, 25.4 cm for the mannequin and 14.3 cm for the box
(p = 0.02, 0.01 and 0.07). Q3 scored 2.1, 0.6 and −0.4. Only the mannequin
showed a significant synchronous–asynchronous drift difference, and only
the mannequin–box contrast was tested among synchronous conditions
(p = 0.04).

**The limits, as the paper reports them.** No participant reported
disembodiment or a change of viewpoint. The questionnaire item for felt
forward drift (Q4) was answered negatively in every condition.

What could have come out otherwise: synchrony could have left Q3 and drift
unmoved, and the box could have scored like the bodies. Q3 came out as
predicted. Drift came out only partly: the box's 14.3 cm is close to the
own body's 17.1 cm.

## What this does not say

- **That the self was relocated outside the body.** The measured
  quantity is a walking error of about a tenth of the 2 m distance, and
  participants did not report feeling it. Calling it "self-location" is
  the paper's interpretation.
- **That an out-of-body experience was induced.** The authors say it was
  not.
- **That ownership, rather than self-recognition, was induced.** Ehrsson
  and Petkova's reading, recognising oneself on what amounts to a CCTV
  monitor, fits the questionnaire as worded. They also failed to induce an
  illusion with a real mannequin at 2 m. Neither possibility is tested
  here.
- **That the effect survives demand control.** No expectancy measure was
  taken, and blinding of the drift measurement is not reported.
- **That body-part ownership and global ownership are one mechanism.** The
  paper says they rely "at least partly" on similar integration. That is
  an analogy between two measures, not a shared mechanism shown.
