# CLAIM-tmph4j5r: How much of a person's time is taken by activities they cannot reasonably refuse limits what they can viably pursue apart from their income and rights, so that two people with the same resources can differ in substantive freedom by their time, and productivity gains taken wholly as output can leave that freedom unchanged or smaller; the contrast that matters is between time committed to demanding activity and time surrendered to necessity, not between work and leisure

## The claim

A13 §VII.1 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6af6b47c-8b0c-83ea-ae04-3e3eb2649597), "The distribution of time is a distribution of
freedom":

> A person may possess considerable material wealth while having little
> discretionary time.
>
> Another may possess adequate income but face unpredictable schedules
> that make relationships, education, and community involvement
> difficult.
>
> Both situations involve restrictions on capabilities.

and the contrast: "The distinction is between the freedom to commit
oneself to demanding activity and the necessity of surrendering most of
one's time to activities one cannot reasonably refuse." A11 §18 draws the
consequence for productivity: "Economic productivity should therefore be
evaluated partly by whether it expands discretionary time, rather than
simply by whether it increases output."

## Why it holds, and what is A's

[CLAIM-352](CLAIM-352.md) maps the opportunity aspect of Sen's freedom onto what an agent
can viably reach. Time under necessity is a constraint on that set that
income and rights do not state: the same resources reach less when most
hours are already committed to what cannot be refused, and unpredictable
hours remove the activities that need a schedule (A's relationships,
education and community involvement). Sen's own readings on the record
([THEORY-tmpj32pb](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpj32pb.md)) make resources an insufficient measure through
conversion factors of body, climate and society; that time is one more
such factor is A's step, not Sen's, and is not checked against his texts.

## An example from the exchange

A6 §VII sets two futures for AI side by side, an "automation-centered"
one and an "agency-centered" one, and observes that the difference
"cannot be resolved solely at the level of product design":

> An excellent AI tutoring system operating in an economic structure that
> leaves people without time to learn may accomplish little.

That is this claim's point applied to assistance: what assistance can add
to a person's capability ([CLAIM-tmp6yhlh](CLAIM-tmp6yhlh.md)) depends on the time they have to
use it. The two futures illustrate; they do not argue.

## What it does not say

- Not that less work is better. A13 §VII.1: "this doesn't imply that the
  ideal society is one in which people work as little as possible. Work
  can be meaningful."
- Not that every job should be meaningful. A13 §VII.3 asks for "a right to
  meaningful opportunity, not a guarantee of meaningful employment", which
  is [CLAIM-tmpfyxes](CLAIM-tmpfyxes.md)'s line between cultivating conditions and imposing
  commitments, and A13 §VII.2's principle, that "People's economic
  indispensability should not determine their entitlement to dignity", is
  noted here and not filed.
- Not how productivity gains should be divided. A11 §18 leaves it "an
  empirical and political question".
