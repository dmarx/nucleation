# NOTE-tmpdccjf: Evangelion and the desperate search for meaning

## Contribution

There is no published abstract. The chapter reads Neon Genesis Evangelion as an extended treatment of the oldest question in philosophy: why do we exist, and what gives life meaning? It follows how the young EVA pilots and those around them look for meaning through work, recognition and relationships, bringing in Camus on absurdity and revolt, Schopenhauer's porcupine dilemma, Heidegger's "They", and the post-Kantian idealists on self and other. It argues that the series' climax rejects the dissolution of individual selves as a route to meaning.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- The opening (pp. 3–4) poses the question through the characters' motives. It invokes Camus: suicide is not an acceptable response to meaninglessness, and one revolts by making one's own meaning.
- pp. 6–8 use Schopenhauer's porcupines (the "Hedgehog's Dilemma", title of the fourth episode) for the tension between intimacy and pain, and read the AT fields as the barriers between selves.
- "The Human Instrumentality Project" (pp. 8–9) reads Instrumentality as losing the self into the They, in the sense of Heidegger's inauthentic being. Following Fichte and Hegel, Shinji rejects it because "you can't see yourself unless others are with you".
- pp. 9–11: Rei's identity is formed through her interactions with others, and Shinji's relationships with Misato, Asuka, Rei and Kaworu are what let him "find himself and save humanity". Meaning comes through connection that keeps some distance.
