# NOTE-2339: Manik, Timme and Witthaut — Cycle flows and multistability in oscillatory networks

## Contribution

A review with new bounds: moving the analysis of phase locking from node
phases to edge flows and cycles, so that coexisting locked states appear as
states differing by cycle flows, counted by winding numbers.

## Key results

1. **Method (Discussion).** "The main methodological advancement is to
   split the calculation into two parts: First, we calculate the flows
   which satisfy the continuity equation at all nodes. Then we single out
   the the specific solution which leads to consistent phases of the
   oscillators."
2. **Geometric condition (§III).** Phases are single-valued only if "the
   phase differences over every simple cycle (as defined in Definition 2)
   in the network must add up to an integer multiple of 2π"; Theorem 7
   makes fixed points equivalent to conserved flows with integer winding
   vector for some branch partition.
3. **Their sense of frustration (Definition 8).** "An oscillator network
   is said to be geometrically frustrated if a solution of the dynamic
   conditions (11) exits, but all solutions are incompatible to the
   geometric conditions (20) such that no fixed point exists."
4. **Rings (Corollaries 3–4).** "ring networks RN with N ≤ 4 do not have
   multiple stable fixed points"; with homogeneous coupling, rings of seven
   or more nodes are multistable above a coupling threshold. "the number of
   stable fixed point is particularly large if (a) the cycle is long, (b)
   the edge strength are large and (c) the power sources are distributed
   homogeneously" (Discussion).
5. **Braess's paradox (§IV.D, Fig. 2).** On a four-node ring, increasing
   one edge's capacity raises the critical coupling: "an increase of local
   transmission capacity reduces the ability of the network to support a
   phase locked fixed point".
6. **Composition (§V.E, Fig. 5).** Two motifs with three and one stable
   fixed points, fused, have none; two rings with none, joined by an edge,
   gain one. Bounds per cycle do not multiply for cycles that share edges.
7. **Extremes are unique (Discussion).** "both tree networks and fully
   connected networks have at most one stable fixed points"; a claim of
   Jadbabaie and colleagues of uniqueness at strong coupling is shown
   wrong.

## Assumptions

Symmetric nonnegative coupling, sine coupling without phase lag, fixed
points in a co-rotating frame (Σωⱼ = 0); results for "normal operation",
all edge differences below π/2 in size, which guarantees stability
(Corollary 1). Second-order (power-grid) and first-order models share
fixed points and their stability.

## Concepts

Dynamic condition (flow conservation with capacities); geometric condition
(cycle consistency); cycle flow; winding number and vector; normal
operation; geometric frustration in the authors' sense, which is not the
record's: here the local flow balance is solvable and no consistent phases
exist, with no offsets on the couplings.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md) and [CLAIM-935](../claims.d/CLAIM-935.md).** The graph case of the bound of five:
  a ring with four or fewer nodes has one stable locked state, and the
  twisted states of the unfrustrated ring are the windings [CLAIM-935](../claims.d/CLAIM-935.md)
  uses. Their split into a linear local condition and a global cycle
  condition is the same split [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md) makes between local consistency
  and monodromy.
- **[QUESTION-282](../questions.d/QUESTION-282.md).** Two parallels, not answers: strengthening one link,
  a change of the coupling structure from outside, can remove every
  locked state (Braess), and fusing two motifs can create or destroy
  locked states that neither had. Both are changes of the operator made by
  the modeller, not by interaction.
- **[TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)** (a homonym to keep apart). "Geometric frustration" here is
  conserved flows with no consistent phases, a third sense beside the
  record's two.

## Limitations

General networks are hard: lower bounds are given for rings and planar
graphs, and the authors say generalizing is hard. Basins of attraction are
cited, not computed. The arXiv version calls itself an overview and
reproduces results from earlier papers by the group.
