# THEORY-tmp3wg6o: Johnston withdraws the account of his Surviving Death, on which our deepest future-directed attitudes fix which future embodiments are ours, arguing that every reductionist account of personal identity breeds fellow travelers with moral status, and holds instead that we are essentially embodied wills whose present embodiments are not essential

## The account

Johnston recalls the 2010 view: "In place of that standard Christian
hope, I there offered a this-worldly analogue or surrogate, a way of
living on in the onward rush of humankind—a surrogate developed out of
the idea that we are the 'higher-order' kind of thing whose deepest
future-directed attitudes determine which this-worldly future
embodiments will be ours" (pp. 263–264).

He now rejects it. If a person is any maximal sum of suitably related
stages, then within one life there are many person-like sums
(personites, R-variants, overlapping candidates for one pain, modal
variants), each with as good a claim to moral status. Ethical life then
becomes incoherent. Only an enduring will, one bearer of moral status
per embodiment, restores what he calls ethical singularity. We are
embodied wills, "embodied conscious valuers of value", who endure while
our embodiments perdure. Being somehow embodied is essential, this
embodiment is not, so re-embodiment after death is possible, though not
shown actual (pp. 273–279, 308–309).

## Evidence

- Four fellow traveler problems are set out (pp. 271–273, 304–305); the
  argument depends on moral status being intrinsic (p. 273), and its
  detail is in the author's papers of 2016–2019.
- The positive account assumes a neo-Aristotelian ur-essence and, for
  where wills come from, theism. Emergent substance dualism is called a
  "fig leaf" for divine creation (pp. 281–285).
- Mental events are argued not to reduce to neural ones, standing
  instead in relations of subserving, implementing and interdependence,
  which the author calls "mysterian" (pp. 291–304).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md) was opened by Surviving Death's good person who lives on in
humanity's onward rush, and its text records that "Johnston has since
given that account of the self up ([NOTE-1257](../notes.d/NOTE-1257.md))". The reading is the
record's source for that withdrawal and for its reason: a self whose
extent is fixed by one's attitudes multiplies bearers of moral status.
Whether a self enlarged across people or time, as [CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md) has it,
inherits that problem is open; the paper does not raise it.
