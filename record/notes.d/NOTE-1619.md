# NOTE-1619: Kang, Kim, Yang and Ryu — Physics-informed neural networks for programmable origami

## Contribution

A data-free, physics-loss network for the energy curve of conical, not
only cylindrical, Kresling origami; inverse design from minimal
specifications, two stable heights and a barrier, with the barrier's
location inferred; and passive sequential deployment of multi-layer
stacks by ordering their barrier heights, checked in FEA and in a
prototype.

## Key insight

Under a slow monotone load, the cell with the lowest barrier snaps first.
If the barriers of a stack can be set independently, the order of folding
is designed in, and no active control is needed.

## Assumptions

- **Truss abstraction**: creases as linear axial springs standing in for
  facet bending, twisting, shear and torsion.
- **Quasi-static loading** along the minimum-energy, torque-free path.
- **Geometric feasibility** (triangle inequality, positive valley length,
  angle range, flat-foldability) by a large penalty.
- **Normalization**: barriers in the prototype compared after normalizing
  to the bottom layer.

## Key results

- **Data-free (abstract).** Forward prediction and inverse design "without
  requiring pre-collected training data".
- **Architecture (2.2.1).** "The neural network architecture, shown in
  Figure 2(A), is a fully connected feedforward model with two hidden
  layers of 128 tanh-activated neurons."
- **Validation (2.4).** "The simulation employed a beam-truss model
  consistent with the truss-based formulation described in Section 2.1."
- **Non-uniqueness (3.2).** Several geometries fit one target, "reflecting
  the one-to-many mapping between energy landscape and geometry"; "Our
  framework currently selects one feasible solution".
- **Feasibility (3.3).** "not all target barrier values can be physically
  realized".
- **Design intent (3.4).** "This capability enables complex shape changes
  without active control, as each layer folds in a predetermined order
  dictated by its barrier height."
- **Prototype (3.4).** "the relative errors between normalized
  experimental values and PINN predictions are -7.93% for the middle layer
  and -16.16% for the top layer"; residual energy remains after each fold
  "rather than a full return to zero as predicted by the model".
- **Design rule (Discussion).** "maintain a sequence margin between
  adjacent barrier heights that exceeds expected experimental deviations".

## Concepts

- **Conical Kresling origami**: Kresling cells whose top and bottom
  polygons differ in radius, coupling contraction and twist.
- **Torque-free equilibrium path**: ∂U/∂φ = 0 at each height.
- **Bistable energy programming**: inverse design from two stable heights
  and a target barrier, its location found by the model.
- **Sequence margin**: a gap between adjacent barriers larger than the
  expected experimental deviation.

## Connections

and the Kresling-origami literature on bistability to conical cells. The

## Bearing on the record

- **The synthesis's description.** The pasted synthesis on constraint
  architectures gives only the bibliography under origami as
  resource-constrained reconfiguration, with nothing to check; the
  bibliography is right. It is the clearest of the synthesis's works on
  ordering transitions by barrier heights, but it does not treat
  resource-constrained reachability: the load is unbounded and monotone,
  and the question is order, not whether a path exists under a cap.
- **Caution on validation.** Agreement with FEA checks the optimizer and
  network against the same truss mechanics; only the prototype tests the
  mechanics, where the bottom layer's measured barrier (0.0875 mJ) differs
  from the predicted 0.05 before normalization.

## Limitations

- **Truss model** without crease rotation stiffness, frame bending, panel
  buckling, friction or contact; dissipation leaves residual energy.
- **One of many geometries** returned, with no secondary criteria.
- **Quasi-static only**.
- **Validation**: FEA with the same abstraction; one three-layer prototype,
  three tests per specimen; data on request.
