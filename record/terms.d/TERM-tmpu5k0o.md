# TERM-tmpu5k0o: destructive lock-in, of an industrial ecosystem against a declared family of future demands: specialization that removes or makes unaffordable the pathways to alternatives those demands need while current operation stays successful, and whose loss the benefits of consolidation do not justify; its opposite is productive specialization

## The sense

A48 §11 distinguishes productive specialization, which concentrates
resources on what current demands need while keeping the pathways to
alternatives a declared family of future demands requires, from
destructive lock-in, which removes or prices out those pathways while
current operation stays successful. It is [CLAIM-758](../claims.d/CLAIM-758.md)'s overconsolidation
relative to a family, with [CLAIM-679](../claims.d/CLAIM-679.md)'s transition loss as its mechanism
and [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)'s stable-but-unreorganizable system as its result.

## Limits

The justification clause has no standard: whether consolidation's
benefits "justify" a loss is the open part, near [CLAIM-684](../claims.d/CLAIM-684.md)'s rising
burden of justification.
