# NOTE-tmp0o5hp: DeChurch and Mesmer-Magnus — Measuring shared team mental models: A meta-analysis

## Key results

1. Abstract, p. 1: "only methods that model the structure or organization of knowledge are
   predictive of process."
2. Abstract: "SMMs were positively related to team performance regardless of the manner of
   operationalization."
3. Results: the strongest process effects with Pathfinder PFNets (ρ = .31, SDρ = 0); when
   the method did not enable the representation of structure, there was essentially no
   observed effect between SMMs and team process (ρ = .05, SDρ = .28). (ρ and minus signs
   are garbled in the PDF text layer; values checked against the text.)
4. Results: studies using an index of within-group agreement showed no relation between
   SMMs and team process (ρ = −.06, SDρ = .35).
5. Discussion, p. 9: "knowledge content is predictive of team performance but not of team
   process. Knowledge structure is predictive of both team process and team performance."
6. Introduction, quoting the definition: shared team mental models are "knowledge structures
   held by members of a team that enable them to form accurate explanations and expectations
   for the task".
7. Limitations: small-k cells; "we urge against drawing more fine-grained conclusions about
   particular comparisons across methods until additional research is available."

## Assumptions

Similarity (congruence) of members' models is the construct; accuracy and complementarity
are not covered (Limitations). Hunter–Schmidt corrections.

## Concepts

shared team mental model; elicitation (similarity ratings, card sorting, concept mapping,
questionnaires); structure representation (Pathfinder, MDS, UCINET); representation of
emergence (Pathfinder C, consistency, within-group agreement); knowledge content vs
structure.

## Bearing on the record

It cumulates 23 studies including Mathieu et al. (2000) and restates the general claim that
shared mental models relate to team process and performance. It does **not** restate
Mathieu et al.'s specific results (task- vs team-model distinction; full mediation by team
process). Mathieu's method (paired comparisons analysed with a network algorithm) falls in
the measurement class this meta-analysis finds most predictive of process.

- **CASE-145** (Active): *bears on measurement, leaves the case open*. If shared perspective
  is to be measured apart from collective decision, this meta-analysis says the measure must
  capture the structure of members' knowledge, not agreement ratings (results 1, 4).
- **TERM-tmp8deza / CLAIM-tmpyp3u3**: *contrasts*: a shared mental model is a
  property of members' heads (result 6); the record's term is deliberately not that.
- **QUESTION-tmpxq2sb**: *bears*: if a record helps a team, one route would be by
  making members' knowledge structures converge; this meta-analysis gives the measurement
  that would detect it.
