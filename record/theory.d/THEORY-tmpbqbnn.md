# THEORY-tmpbqbnn: Körding, Beierholm, Ma, Quartz, Tenenbaum and Shams propose that in auditory-visual localization the observer infers whether the two cues share a cause and weights fused against separate estimates by the posterior probability of a common cause, so that an ideal observer of this kind predicts human localization responses and the fall of crossmodal bias with disparity better than forced fusion, segregation or interaction-prior models

## Source

Körding, Beierholm, Ma, Quartz, Tenenbaum and Shams (2007), LIT-tmpb2oz9;
read in NOTE-tmp53s7s.

## What was actually shown

- **Experiment 1**: a dual-report ventriloquist task, five azimuths, 19
  analysed subjects. Four parameters fit 250 data points, "The causal
  inference model accounts for the data very well (R2 = 0.97" (p. 3);
  parameters fitted on half the data give R2 = 0.96 on the other half (p.
  8). Forced fusion gets R2 = 0.56, and segregation fails on the bias data.
  This could have come out the other way: either special case could have
  fitted.
- **Rivals** (Table 1): per-subject log likelihood favours causal inference
  over a MAP rule, full integration, full segregation and the Roach et al.
  and Bresciani et al. interaction priors, "even when correcting for the
  number of parameters".
- **Experiment 2**: a refit of Wallace et al.'s (2004) published group data,
  with two parameters imported and two fitted; it explains 72% of the
  variance of unity judgements and 87% of the bias.

## What this does not say

- That the observer commits to one causal structure: it averages, and a
  commit rule was not tested.
- How p_common is set or learned; it is a constant fitted per dataset.
- That the inference is automatic or effortless; that is asserted, not
  manipulated.
- That the model is hierarchical in the sense of shared, learned
  higher-level knowledge; the paper never uses the word.
- Anything about body ownership; THEORY-769 borrows the model class, and
  this paper supplies no evidence for that use.
- That there were two new experiments: there was one.
