summary: >-
  Costa, Yetter & DeSomer (2018). Among 19,903 children of Union Army veterans born after the Civil War and surviving to 45, sons of men held in Confederate camps in the no-exchange period (July 1863 to July 1864), when conditions were worst, had an older-age mortality hazard 1.11 times that of sons of non-POWs (P = 0.009) and about 1.09 times that of sons of exchange-period POWs. Daughters were unaffected. Within families, sons born after the war to no-exchange POWs died at 2.23 times the rate of their brothers born before it (P = 0.056). The effect was 1.20 among sons born in the second quarter and absent among those born in the fourth, which the authors read as adequate maternal nutrition reversing it. Paternal and maternal socioeconomic controls and maternal lifespan did not change the result. By elimination, the authors judge an epigenetic route most consistent, while granting they cannot reject psychological or cultural transmission.
