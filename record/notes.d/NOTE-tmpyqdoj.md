# NOTE-tmpyqdoj: Dennett & Kinsbourne — Time and the Observer

## Contribution

The paper names a hidden assumption, Cartesian materialism, and shows it at
work in the interpretation of four temporal illusions and in the arguments of
both sides of the Libet controversy. It offers the Multiple Drafts model in
its place. The new tool is the distinction between the time a content is
represented and the time it represents, applied to the brain's timing. With
it the paper argues that some questions about when an experience happened
have no answer at fine scales.

## Key insight

A letter dated before the battle can arrive after news of the battle, and a
reader who sees the date still knows the order. The brain can likewise
represent "A before B" without representing A first. So the experienced
order of events is a content that some process concludes, not the order in
which the vehicles of content arrive anywhere. Once there is no place where
arrival counts, "was this conscious before or after the revision?" can lose
its answer.

## Assumptions

- **Materialism, and no central gateway.** "Nothing in the functional
  neuroanatomy of the brain suggests such a general meeting place" (p. 185).
  This is asserted with the state of neuroanatomy in 1990, not argued.
- **Subjective sequence is accessed through report.** "Only through the
  subjects' verbalizations … do we gain access to a perspective from which
  the anomalies can appear" (p. 187). Note 2 sets aside the solitary
  experimenter and languageless animals.
- **Causes precede effects** throughout: what the model rules out is
  "magical" or precognitive filling in (p. 190).
- **Scale.** The claims about indeterminate timing are restricted to
  intervals "of a few hundred milliseconds" (p. 200). At ordinary scales
  the experienced order of thoughts is granted to be their actual order.

## Key results

- **Four phenomena (§1.2).**
  - Colour phi (Kolers and von Grünau 1976): a red spot followed by a green
    one 4° away is seen to move and change colour in mid-path.
  - The cutaneous rabbit (Geldard and Sherrick 1972): taps at the wrist and
    elbow are felt as evenly spaced hops up the arm.
  - Libet's backward referral: skin stimuli are felt as earlier than
    cortical stimuli that precede them.
  - Libet's delay of intention: reported intentions lag the readiness
    potential by 350–400 ms.
- **Representing vs represented time (§2.1).** The Battle of New Orleans
  example (the battle was fought on 8 January 1815, fifteen days after the
  truce). "Content-sensitive settling", by analogy with synchronising a
  soundtrack to film and with random-dot stereograms, shows how order can be
  inferred without date stamps. Mellor's claim that "perceptions of
  temporal order need temporally ordered perceptions" is rejected, with
  what is right in it kept: timing matters at the start, when it fixes
  content, and at the deadline, when content must be in time to control
  behaviour.
- **The temporal control window (§2.1).** Within the window bounded by the
  earliest arrival of information and the latest time it can still control a
  behaviour, the vehicles' timing can be moved freely.
- **Orwellian vs Stalinesque (§2.2).** Two accounts of colour phi:
  - Stalinesque: an editing room before consciousness waits for the green
    spot and splices in intermediate frames.
  - Orwellian: the subject is briefly conscious of the stationary red spot,
    and memory is rewritten.
  The button-press latency favours the Orwellian story. The Stalinesque
  theorist replies that the press was unconsciously triggered. Both fit all
  verbal, behavioural and first-person data, and metacontrast gives the same
  standoff. So "there is really only a verbal difference between the two
  theories" (p. 193).
- **No filling in (§2.2).** The brain "just adjusts to the conclusion that is
  drawn"; it need not construct the intervening frames, "for no one is
  looking" (p. 194).
- **Libet re-read (§3).** His account of cortical stimulation is called
  Stalinesque, and Churchland's "go" reaction-time critique Orwellian; both
  assume an absolute time of experience. In the intention experiments the
  clock-position judgment is "an artifact of the experimental situation"
  (p. 198). A cortex-wide "mode shift" model of consciousness is "still the
  Cartesian Theater" if its objective timing is taken as definitive
  (p. 199).
- **Grey Walter's carousel (§3.3).** Patients whose motor-cortex signal
  advanced the slides felt the projector anticipate their decisions. The
  paper reads this as a violated expectation of feedback, not as a timing of
  the conscious decision.

## Concepts

- **Cartesian materialism / Cartesian Theater.** The view left when
  Descartes's dualism is dropped but a central "place where it all comes
  together" is kept (p. 185).
- **Multiple Drafts model.** Distributed, once-only discriminations,
  continually revised; probing at different times yields different
  narratives; no "final" or "published" draft is canonical (p. 185).
- **Orwellian revision.** Post-experiential falsification of memory.
- **Stalinesque revision.** Pre-experiential falsification, a staged "show
  trial" that is then accurately remembered.
- **Content-sensitive settling.** Inferring order by aligning contents, as an
  editor slides a soundtrack against film (p. 189).
- **Temporal control window.** The interval within which a representation's
  timing does not matter, bounded by causation at one end and the deadline of
  the behaviour it controls at the other (p. 190).

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md).** Three points, all my inferences, since the paper
  does not discuss groups or nesting:
  - *Not a society of agents.* The model shares the denial of a centre with
    Minsky, Selfridge and Brooks, but its parts are not agents and it says
    nothing about whether they could be minds. It places it on the
    downward side of the THEORY without adding to it.
  - *A regress argument against one kind of nesting.* A conscious central
    subsystem would duplicate the work of the whole's other parts (p. 185).
    That is close to Kammerer's functional principle: what is excluded is a
    part that consciously represents for the whole in a role nothing else
    could fill. It does not exclude minded parts that do not play that
    role. So it gives the THEORY's first refusal a second, independent
    motivation for the intra-brain case, the motivation Kammerer's version
    lacks. It still does not reach the United States. This is a candidate
    for the THEORY's text, since the promotion condition asks for
    "an anti-nesting principle justified by something other than the
    absurdity of group minds".
  - *Against definite borders in time.* IIT's exclusion postulate is derived
    from an axiom that experience is definite, and the record's reading of
    IIT 3.0 ([NOTE-tmpxik42](NOTE-tmpxik42.md)) has it pick a definite spatio-temporal grain,
    the one at which integration peaks. This paper's C3–C4 deny that there is a fact about which content
    is conscious at an instant within a few hundred milliseconds. If C4
    holds, the axiom fails for time, and with it one of the THEORY's
    anti-nesting routes. C4 is the paper's weakest step.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md).** No bearing beyond the general point that any
  architectural indicator timed by onset is, by this paper's argument, the
  Cartesian Theater again.
- **No new THEORY.** The regress argument would strengthen [THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md),
  if its author judges it independent of the group verdict; it is not
  enough for a THEORY of its own.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Commentary not read.** The issue carries open peer commentary and a
  response to p. 247; I read one page of one commentary. Antony's opening
  page argues that the most charitable reading of the paper commits it to
  eliminativism about consciousness, and that other passages say
  consciousness exists. How the authors answer that is unread.
- **The C3-to-C4 step.** Indistinguishability by every available test is
  taken to show that there is no fact. Harnad's contrary view is reported
  fairly in note 11 and is not refuted.
- **Libet's data.** The paper accepts Churchland's technical criticisms of
  Libet's cortical-stimulation studies ("very few subjects … inadequately
  controlled … not replicated", p. 196) and does not re-analyse them.
- **No experiment.** The Grey Walter follow-up (a variable delay before the
  slide advances) is proposed, not run.

## Open questions

- What is the delay at which Grey Walter's "precognitive carousel" effect
  disappears? The paper says the experiment was never done.
- Does Antony's charge of eliminativism stand after the authors' response?
  Reading pp. 201–247 would settle what the paper is committed to.
- Can the Orwellian/Stalinesque standoff be broken by a measure the paper
  did not consider, for instance a no-report paradigm? The paper's argument
  covers every measure that the subject's judgments mediate.
