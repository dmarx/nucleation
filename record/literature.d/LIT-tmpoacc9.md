# LIT-tmpoacc9: Semiorders and a Theory of Utility Discrimination

R. Duncan Luce (1956), *Econometrica* 24(2):178–191 — DOI-10.2307/1905751

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* What follows is from the abstract only.

- **Intransitive indifference is admitted.** "In this paper intransitive
  indifference relations are admitted and a class of them are
  axiomatized." The title gives the motivation as discrimination. The
  usual gloss, which is the record's and was not checked against the
  paper, is that options whose utilities differ by less than a
  just-noticeable amount are not told apart, while a chain of such small
  differences can add up to one that is.
- **The representation is by thresholds.** "This class is shown to be
  substantially equivalent to a utility theory in which there are just
  noticeable difference functions which state for any value of utility the
  change in utility so that the change is just noticeable." The paper's
  own axioms and the exact form of its representation were not seen.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the correction to CLAIM-tmp85p0g, part 3a. That
part shows that a threshold override set on the *level* of a cost, alarm
on above c*, still has a numerical representation. An override that
fires on a *difference* in cost larger than a discrimination threshold
is a different structure. Its indifference is not transitive, so it is
not a weak order, and part 1's construction does not apply to it even on a
finite set. Luce's semiorder is that structure in one dimension, and
Tversky's lexicographic semiorder (LIT-tmpzq0ry) puts it in
front of a second dimension. The bearing is set out in
LIT-tmpzq0ry.
