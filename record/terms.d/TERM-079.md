# TERM-079: belief, of a process: a maintained, truth-directed inferential commitment, held to the degree that the process uses a proposition as a premise in interpreting observations, generating expectations and evaluating counterfactuals across the contexts it bears on, together with the profile by which that commitment is revised; distinct from entertaining an interpretation, from asserting it, and from granting it authority over action

## Definition

A process believes p to the extent that it uses p as a premise when
interpreting observations, generating expectations and evaluating
counterfactual possibilities across the contexts p bears on, and maintains
or revises that use through its epistemic learning. *Truth-directed*
marks p's role as a claim about how things are. It does not require the
belief to be true, rational, explicit, conscious or easily revised.

Three parts carry the definition.

- **Inferential commitment.** Interpretation alone is not belief
  ([CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md)'s interpretive operators can entertain a frame). An
  interpretation is belief-like when it is maintained and reused as a
  premise across anticipations, inferences and revisions. Strength is how
  broadly and robustly p is so used, not a universal threshold of credence
  ([THEORY-647](../theory.d/THEORY-647.md)'s context-relative thresholds are one formal option, not the
  definition).
- **Revision profile.** Two processes can agree now and diverge under
  evidence: [CLAIM-294](../claims.d/CLAIM-294.md)'s agents with P(H) = 0.9 each, and P(H | C) = 1.0 and
  0.8. A belief state is the pair of present commitment and disposition to
  revise. That is operational, not a claim that each belief stores its own
  update rule.
- **Relative to a process.** Two processes can believe differently about
  one proposition without the organization being incoherent. The
  organization's effective belief is the commitment maintained through its
  governance and legibility, which need not be any aggregate of the local
  credences ([THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md), [THEORY-536](../theory.d/THEORY-536.md)). Private belief, organizational belief
  and official assertion can diverge.

**Probes.** Anticipation (forecasts of p's observable consequences under
independent scoring, not inference from approach or avoidance);
inferential generalization (does the process use p to predict consequences
it was not trained on?); epistemic intervention (supply diagnostic
evidence or remove an assumption, and see what changes, keeping access
apart from uptake). Then vary incentives: a report that moves with reward
while independent predictions do not is a reporting or governance effect.
The diagnostic is fallible: finite behaviour underdetermines belief.

## What it is not

- **Wanting** ([CLAIM-447](../claims.d/CLAIM-447.md)): what draws pursuit. **Liking**: how an
  experienced outcome is evaluated. **Feeling** ([CLAIM-439](../claims.d/CLAIM-439.md)): an affective
  evaluation made available to the system's further processing.
  **Authority**: which evaluations become consequential. An agent can
  believe an activity safe, want to avoid it, enjoy it when it does it,
  and be governed by its defensive processes.
- **[CASE-124](../cases.d/CASE-124.md)'s m**, a scalar lean in one interpretive dimension, which
  summarizes the content of the prevailing interpretation and is not the
  whole belief state.
- **Dennett's intentional stance** ([THEORY-538](../theory.d/THEORY-538.md)), which earns belief
  attribution from predictive success across contexts. The candidate adds a
  process-level hypothesis about what organization sustains the pattern.
- **Knowledge.** The exchange went on to ask whether belief and knowledge
  can be told apart in the model, and the owner set the distinction aside
  (U26–U27). Nothing here defines knowledge.

## What would settle it

Adoption as stated, a narrower definition, or a rival. It would be adopted
if claims about belief in the will and legibility lines can be stated in
it, and the three probes distinguish cases the record needs (an accurate
belief denied authority, [QUESTION-270](../questions.d/QUESTION-270.md); a false belief kept by a
legibility regime, [CASE-156](../cases.d/CASE-156.md)).
