# THEORY-1950: Smith argues that answerability is the only kind of moral responsibility: attributability without answerability is not responsibility, accountability is answerability where a specific obligation to others has been violated, and differences in warranted response belong to the ethics of blame, not to distinct conceptions of responsibility

## Source

Smith (2012), Ethics 122: 575–589, read in full in [NOTE-2008](../notes.d/NOTE-2008.md) as a neighbour
for Watson's "Two Faces of Responsibility" ([LIT-2718](../literature.d/LIT-2718.md)).

## What was actually shown

An argument from cases. Against attributability: Shoemaker's condition
would make agents responsible for OCD or depression, and the aretaic labels
it is meant to license ("cowardly") are withdrawn on learning that the
agent is not answerable. Against a separate accountability: "He’s the same
guy, doing the same thing, in the same way; all that has changed in these
two cases is Martha’s reaction to his failure." The differences belong to
"the ethics of blame".

`rivals: THEORY-147` because [THEORY-147](THEORY-147.md) (from Wolf, [LIT-747](../literature.d/LIT-747.md)) holds that
attributability and accountability are independent kinds of deep
responsibility, and Smith holds that neither is a kind distinct from
answerability. Both cannot be right. The filer should check [THEORY-147](THEORY-147.md)'s
text before writing the relation.

## What this does not say

- Anything first-hand about Watson's paper beyond the gloss in n. 2.
- That an agent's history is irrelevant to how she should be treated. On
  Smith's view it matters, as part of the ethics of blame.
- How answerability is to be analysed. Smith leaves its conditions open.
