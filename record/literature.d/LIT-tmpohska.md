# LIT-tmpohska: Inadequacy of entropy and entropy derivatives in characterizing the steady state

Rolf Landauer (1975), *Physical Review A 12(2), 636–638* — DOI-10.1103/PhysRevA.12.636

## Standing in the record

Seeded on 2026-10-02 with the critics of Prigogine's extremum principles,
the maximum-entropy-production (MEP) literature and the fluctuation
theorems, at the owner's request to fill out the record's coverage of
dissipative structures. It was routed here because no anthology topic can
hold a result in nonequilibrium physics read for itself.

`Deferred` because no lawful full text could be reached, so no NOTE is
filed. The abstract makes two separate claims, and they are the substance
of Landauer's objection to Prigogine's programme as far as the record can
state it.

- **Selection between steady states is kinetic, not local.** "In bistable
  systems the transition kinetics between the two locally stable states can
  be altered without changing the behavior in the immediate vicinity of the
  two favored steady states." So "quantities which characterize only the
  vicinity of the favored states cannot determine the most probable state".
  This bears on any extremum principle that selects among steady states.
  - Read at face value, it bears on MEP, which Kleidon presents as just
    such a selection principle ([LIT-tmp8wfl8](LIT-tmp8wfl8.md)).
  - It bears equally on an entropy-based criterion of relative stability.
  - Whether the paper means entropy production in particular, and how it
    makes the argument, needs the text.
- **Minimum entropy production fails even in a simple case.** "Even in
  monostable linear circuits the steady state need not correspond to
  minimum entropy production." The text would have to say which circuits,
  and which of the minimum principle's conditions they violate.

The two claims reach different targets. The first is broader than
Prigogine's minimum principle, and is not answered by the distinction
Kleidon draws between relaxation (minimum principle) and selection (MEP).
If it holds, it is an objection to selection principles as such. The
second is a counterexample to the minimum principle itself, and the
abstract places it in linear circuits.

It should be read together with:

- Martyushev and Seleznev's survey ([LIT-tmp9n5sf](LIT-tmp9n5sf.md)), also Deferred, which
  reviews MEP's relation to Prigogine's principle;
- Dewar's derivation ([LIT-tmpbf3wy](LIT-tmpbf3wy.md)) and Grinstein and Linsker's critique of
  it ([LIT-tmpflluj](LIT-tmpflluj.md)), the contested foundation of the selection principle the
  first claim would undercut.

This is not the record's Landauer 1961 paper on erasure ([LIT-328](LIT-328.md)). The
author is the same; the subject is not.
