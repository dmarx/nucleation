# NOTE-tmpzc2v0: Lanier (2010) — You Are Not a Gadget, the publisher's excerpt

## Contribution

The book's own words, five pages of them, on the stance its predecessor essay ("One Half a Manifesto", LIT-tmpgxxnv) stated: cybernetic totalism named and opposed by a technologist, and the claim that philosophies spread through software design.

## Key results

Pages are the book's.

1. p. 24: "THE IDEAS THAT I hope will not be locked in rest on a philosophical foundation that I sometimes call cybernetic totalism. It applies metaphors from certain strains of computer science to people and the rest of reality." (The proof hyphenates "philosophical" across a line.)
2. p. 26: "The digital hive is growing at the expense of individuality."
3. p. 27: "Since implementation speaks louder than words, ideas can be spread in the designs of software."
4. p. 28, Word's automatic outlining: "you end up having to work more than you would otherwise in order to manipulate the software’s expectations of you."
5. p. 28: "information doesn’t deserve to be free"; "Information is alienated experience."

## Assumptions

Persons, not information, are where meaning lies; design choices carry and spread philosophies; lock-in, argued elsewhere in the book, can freeze such choices.

## Concepts

- **Cybernetic totalism**: computer-science metaphors applied to people and reality.
- **The digital hive**: collective aggregation that grows at the expense of individuality.
- **Information as alienated experience**: information means nothing without a person to decode it.

## Connections

The book itself, not a neighbour. It confirms that "One Half a Manifesto" (LIT-tmpgxxnv, THEORY-tmpvajjd) carries the book's stance: chapter 2 opens with the same "cybernetic totalism". Lock-in is named in the first sentence only; the book's chapter on it (chapter 1, on MIDI) is not in the excerpt. The New York Times review on the Edge page says Lanier warns that design may be "frozen into place by a process known as lock-in"; that is the reviewer's report, not text read here.
