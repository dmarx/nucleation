# LIT-2223: Ironies of Generative AI: Understanding and mitigating productivity loss in human-AI interactions

Simkute, A., Tankelevitch, L., Kewenig, V., Scott, A. E., Sellen, A. & Rintel, S. (2024). Ironies of Generative AI: Understanding and mitigating productivity loss in human-AI interactions. arXiv:2402.11364.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1711](../notes.d/NOTE-1711.md). Sections are the paper's.*

- **The irony, cited to Bainbridge** (§1): the ironies "capture the idea
  that the more advanced an automated system is, the more important the
  human operator may be."
- **Production to evaluation** (§2.1): users' roles shift from producing
  output to evaluating it, and monitoring can "paradoxically impose a
  considerable workload on humans".
- **Polarization** (§2.4, cited to Lee and Seppelt): "easy tasks are easier
  to automate, and so the more difficult tasks tend to remain under manual
  control, albeit alongside the additional task of monitoring automation".
- **Remedy** (§3.5): "clearly specifying how tasks are allocated between
  the human and system, particularly during high workload periods".

## Standing in the record

Read as a stand-in for Bainbridge (1983), [LIT-2225](LIT-2225.md), and the weakest of
the three on the record's gap: it is about productivity, not about the
skill the support lets decay. What it carries is the shape of the hand-back:
support that takes the easy share leaves the party the hard residue and a
new monitoring task, so "the party could do the work and did it throughout", a condition of
[CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md), can hold for the hard part while the easy part is no longer done.
