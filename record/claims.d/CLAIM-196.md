# CLAIM-196: Roles only respectively available in disjoint contexts cannot conflict

## The claim

**The passage.** MoM's v1 "Sheaf Semantics of Context" §11.5, "Identity as
Global Section", says: "Not only words but **selves** are contextual". It
takes a person's roles in family, professional and social contexts, and
continues: "If these local identities glue coherently, the sheaf has a
global section: a stable self. If they do not (contradictory roles,
fractured coherence), identity fails to unify."

**As written it is wrong, in the way [CLAIM-197](CLAIM-197.md) identifies.** If the
family context and the professional context do not meet, the roles agree
on every overlap because there is none, and they glue. A person who is
deferential at home and authoritative at work holds no contradiction by
that fact.

**The defensible version.** Roles conflict only on an overlap: an occasion
on which audiences from two contexts are present together and the person
must present one way to both. The cover is not given. People build and
maintain it.

- **Segregating audiences keeps the contexts disjoint.** Different styles
  for different groups do no harm while the groups are kept apart.
- **Scripting manages the overlaps that must happen.** Weddings, "in which
  context collisions are common, are frequently scripted to make everyone
  comfortable" ([LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md), p. 38).
- **Collapse creates overlaps nobody chose.** Broadcast and networked
  media bring the audiences together. Carmichael "was able to maintain
  distinct styles as long as he was able to segment social groups, he ran
  into trouble when electronic media collapsed those social groups"
  ([LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md), pp. 36–37; [CASE-052](../cases.d/CASE-052.md)).

So a presented self ([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)<!-- ref::uses: TERM-045 -->) is a global section relative
to a cover the person partly controls. Integrity of presentation can be achieved two
ways: by sections that agree where contexts meet, or by keeping contexts
from meeting. The second is not deception. boyd makes the point that
distinct contexts are how people "properly locate their performances".

## Version 2: availability, not disjointness

The owner's qualification puts the condition where the argument needs it.
Disjoint audiences do not suffice. A role can travel to a context where its
audience is absent:

- by memory or anticipation of the other audience, which version 1's
  defeater already excepted;
- by **identification**. In [LIT-tmpdbb0m](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdbb0m.md) ([NOTE-tmptx4q4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptx4q4.md)), a scene-limited
  role is entered "only in the course of the session limited in time",
  which is a role only respectively available. Identification is "present
  also when the role is not assumed" and reaches "everyday life", which is
  a role made available beyond its context.

A carried role meets the person's other roles wherever it is carried. So it
can conflict with them while the audiences stay apart. The "double life" in
that study protects its holder only as far as the role stays put.
Segregating audiences prevents overlaps of the first kind, between
audiences, and not of the second, a role carried across.

## Why this record holds it

It is the synchronic partner of [CLAIM-198](CLAIM-198.md). There, interests over time
conflict only where self-prediction makes an overlap. Here, roles across
audiences conflict only where audiences meet. [CLAIM-153](CLAIM-153.md) supplies the
mathematics once overlaps exist: with pairwise overlaps, two contexts
always glue, and the obstruction is a loop.

The sheaf picture adds one thing to the sociology. It says where conflict
can be: on an overlap. That makes a prediction the sociology states but
does not derive. Segregation strategies should target the overlaps, the
shared channels and the occasions where audiences meet, and should not
target the roles themselves.

## What it does not say

- **That role conflict is rare.** Overlaps are common, and for networked
  publics boyd describes them as constant.
- **Anything about the other senses of "self".** The manuscript's
  "identity" is the presented self, sense 7 of [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md). A person whose
  presentations collide is not thereby less a subject, less integrated or
  less the same over time.
- **That contradiction does no harm unseen.** A person may feel the
  contradiction while the audiences are apart, by remembering one audience
  in front of the other. That is an overlap made by memory, as for the
  film joke in [CASE-048](../cases.d/CASE-048.md), and the defeat condition is written to allow it.

## Note of 2026-10-12: documented cases of context collapse

[CASE-064](../cases.d/CASE-064.md), filed in PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121), now illustrates this claim. A photograph
posted for friends who knew a running joke reached strangers four weeks
later, who read it as contempt for the war dead, and Stone lost her job.
It is a documented collapse of the kind this claim describes, where
[CASE-052](../cases.d/CASE-052.md) is stipulated. [CASE-061](../cases.d/CASE-061.md) is a neighbouring documented case:
one broadcast phrase heard as faith by an in-group and as boilerplate by
others, with the split measured. It shows a speaker addressing two
audiences at once in one wording, not a conflict between roles, so it is
not linked here.

## Note of 2026-10-15: split under [ADR-043](../decisions.d/ADR-043.md)

[ADR-043](../decisions.d/ADR-043.md)'s classification found this claim compound: an interpretive
reading of a person's roles as sections over contexts, which locates role
conflict on an overlap, and the empirical sociology of audiences it draws
on, from boyd (2008) and [CASE-064](../cases.d/CASE-064.md). The empirical part, that presentations
which differ by audience do no harm while the audiences are kept apart,
that people keep them apart and script their meetings, and that broadcast
and networked media bring them together, is now [CLAIM-317](CLAIM-317.md), on which this claim
rests. Its formal part was already [CLAIM-197](CLAIM-197.md). This claim keeps the rest,
the sheaf reading and its prediction that segregation strategies target
the overlaps, and stays interpretive. Its title states the sociology only
as what keeps or creates an overlap, so it was kept.

## Note of 2026-11-06: boyd, read further

boyd supports the premise in her own words: "teens want to be able to
interact with their peers differently from how they interact with their
parents" (p. 287, [NOTE-1432](../notes.d/NOTE-1432.md)). Her healthy-household cases, where parents
join teen space with "little conflict" (p. 251), fit the claim's
condition. An overlap is needed for role conflict but does not bring it
by itself. No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-26: Niebudek and Iniewicz (2023), read


A case. In [LIT-tmpdbb0m](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdbb0m.md) ([NOTE-tmptx4q4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptx4q4.md)), Table 1 records a participant who "Leads a double
