# LIT-tmpn694x: The Meta-Problem of Consciousness

David J. Chalmers (2018), *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 25(9–10), 6–61.
Imprint Academic. The target article of a JCS symposium; the replies
followed in a 2019 issue (an editorial introduction, "Debates on the
Meta-Problem of Consciousness", is at Ingenta, imp/jcs/2019/00000026), and
the author's reply, "How Can We Solve the Meta-Problem of Consciousness?",
appeared in JCS 27 (2020), pp. 201–226 (per his papers page). Neither was
read.

## Key takeaways

- **The problem (intro, §1).** Explain *problem intuitions*: dispositions
  to judge that there is a gap between physical processes and
  consciousness. They come in four central kinds: explanatory (gap,
  anti-functionalist), metaphysical (dualist, fundamentality), knowledge
  (Mary; other minds) and modal (zombies, inversion). Neighbouring kinds
  include distribution intuitions, such as "robots are not conscious". It
  is an easy problem because problem reports are behaviour. The
  explanation must be topic-neutral, meaning it does not mention
  consciousness, though it may be representational, rational, historical
  or structural.
- **Thirteen candidate solutions (§2).** Introspective models; phenomenal
  concepts; independent roles; introspective opacity; immediate knowledge
  (with acquaintance, presentation and revelation); primitive quality
  attribution (Pereboom); primitive relation attribution (cf. Graziano's
  attention schema); introjection; the user illusion (Dennett); the
  use–mention fallacy; underestimating the physical; historical and
  cultural explanations; others. A recurring test is "the problem of
  belief": a proposal that would generate problem intuitions about beliefs
  too explains nothing distinctive.
- **The proposed picture (end of §2).** Introspective models with opaque
  concepts, largely independent of physical ones, attribute primitive
  mental relations to primitive perceived qualities, with a sense of
  immediate knowledge and acquaintance. The paper calls it neutral between
  realism and illusionism, and "closer to a fleshed-out explanandum … than
  an explanans".
- **The meta-problem challenge (§3).** If mechanism M is the basis of
  consciousness, M should play a central role in producing judgments about
  consciousness. This tells against IIT: a zero-Φ simulation would make the
  same reports. It also tells against biological theories, whose silicon
  duplicates would report alike. It is an open question for global
  workspace and higher-order theories, a further aspect of the combination
  problem for panpsychism, and a failure for analytic functionalism.
- **Six reactions (§4) and debunking (§5).** Non-reductive: no solution,
  correlationism, realizationism. Reductive: strong illusionism, or
  consciousness as a lower-order or a higher-order meta-problem state.
  A debunking argument for illusionism and a coincidence argument are each
  set out, and three replies are given. Realizationism is favoured: on it,
  consciousness causally realises the meta-problem processes. But "it is
  hard to avoid a sense of coincidence entirely".
- **Illusionism (§§6–7).** Weak illusionism does not dissolve the hard
  problem, since that problem needs only "the basic fact that there is
  something it is like to be us". So illusionists who want to dissolve it
  must be strong illusionists. Strong illusionism is coherent, because an
  illusion can be a judgment and not an experience, but it is "empirically
  false": people feel pain.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request for David Chalmers in this
record. It is the hard problem's sequel. It reframes the problem as a
tractable question about cognition, which is why `epistemology` and
`cognition` follow `consciousness` in its tags.

**On the hard problem.** Its introduction restates [LIT-tmpbxqmy](LIT-tmpbxqmy.md)'s analysis, and it
turns it into a programme. It is the record's fullest treatment of
illusionism. Frankish's illusionism, the target of §§6–7, is the stance of
[LIT-103](LIT-103.md), which charges Veit with leaving the hard problem "as hard as
ever". This paper's §6 is the general version of that charge, aimed at weak
illusionists. Hofstadter's *I Am a Strange Loop* ([LIT-tmpltilo](LIT-tmpltilo.md), unread) is
cited in footnote 13 as "a sort of illusionism based in self-models and
self-reference, primarily directed at illusions about the self". Minsky's
*Emotion Machine* draft ([LIT-tmpr3qe6](LIT-tmpr3qe6.md), read in [NOTE-tmp95bt1](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp95bt1.md)) is not cited.
By my reading it belongs with the deflationary proposals the paper
surveys.

**On AI consciousness ([THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)).** Footnote 26 bears directly on it. A
simple theorem-prover agent (Muehlhauser and Shlegeris) "mirrors at least
some aspects of our phenomenal reports, while being so simple that most
people would deny that it is conscious". If that pattern continues, "we
should probably be cautious" about tests that take puzzled phenomenal
reports as evidence of machine consciousness. That is the mimicry worry in
[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) (after Birch, [LIT-111](LIT-111.md), and Schwitzgebel, [LIT-191](LIT-191.md)), applied to
the one kind of behaviour often proposed as hardest to fake. The §3
challenge also applies to the indicator approach of [LIT-056](LIT-056.md). An indicator
taken from a theory whose basis M does not explain our reports inherits
the challenge. That application is mine.

**On the society-of-mind bridge ([THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md)).** Two bearings, both my
inferences:

- *Against IIT as the architectural criterion.* The THEORY counts IIT's
  verdicts (exclusion; modular networks splitting into small complexes) as
  one of the architectural routes. This paper's §3 argues that Φ plays "no
  essential role in explaining phenomenal intuitions", since a zero-Φ
  computational isomorph reports alike. That is a reason, independent of
  the group case, to doubt that Φ is the basis of consciousness. It
  supplies part of what the THEORY's `promote_when` asks for: a ground for
  or against a criterion that does not come from its verdict on groups.
- *A meta-problem test for groups.* By the §3 challenge, a criterion for
  phenomenal consciousness should go with the mechanisms that produce
  problem reports. Whether a nation produces problem reports of its own,
  over and above its citizens' reports, is a question none of the
  readings asks. It might separate a society of mind, which on Minsky's
  account produces its owner's reports, from a nation, whose "reports"
  about consciousness are its members'. The paper does not discuss groups.

**On the extended mind ([LIT-097](LIT-097.md)).** No direct bearing. The paper's
problem intuitions include "self intuitions", but it leaves them aside.

Kammerer is cited for an illusion meta-problem (the "resistance problem")
and as a possible illusionist (2016). Neither is the Kammerer paper held
here ([LIT-tmpdpmaj](LIT-tmpdpmaj.md), 2015, on the US). The paper's own earlier solution to
the meta-problem is in *The Conscious Mind* ([LIT-tmpwl6af](LIT-tmpwl6af.md)).

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice. Its research
programme includes computational models of phenomenal reports, and
footnote 26 cautions against report-based tests for machine consciousness.
Neither instructs ML practice, and it does not belong in the anthology.
