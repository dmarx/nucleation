# THEORY-1921: Tronto holds that a major source of injustice is the capability of the relatively privileged to refuse unpleasant or burdensome responsibilities, which she calls privileged irresponsibility, and that a caring democracy requires realigning the privileges that keep the unfair load of care responsibilities from being discussed, hearing everyone's voice in reallocating them, and organizing care democratically

## Source

Tronto (2018), SAJHE 32(6): 6-18 (the neighbour's new LIT; `source:` to be set to its code), read
as a stand-in for [LIT-2708](../literature.d/LIT-2708.md) and [LIT-2669](../literature.d/LIT-2669.md). The reading is [NOTE-2010](../notes.d/NOTE-2010.md).

## What was actually shown

Statements, not an argument. Privileged irresponsibility: "a major source of injustice in the world comes from the capability of
the relatively privileged to refuse to accept unpleasant or burdensome responsibilities (privileged
irresponsibility)" (p. 8). Caring Democracy's steps are "to realign a system of privileges that make
it impossible to discuss how some people take on an unfair load of care responsibilities; second,
that everyone’s voice must be heard in making these adjustments; third, that democracy is better
when caring is organized democratically and that caring is better when it is organized
democratically" (p. 14).

## What this does not say

- Nothing about organizations distributing blame, or about moral crumple zones.
- Nothing about "passes" or a "privilege of not noticing".
- It does not argue either thesis; for the argument, the books, which remain unread.
