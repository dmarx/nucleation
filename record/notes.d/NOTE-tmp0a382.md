# NOTE-tmp0a382: Conscious birds

## Contribution

A compact review from the Bochum avian-cognition group. It sets the two
avian electrophysiology studies of sensory consciousness, the avian
connectome and the new cortex-like-circuit anatomy beside the requirements
of three theories of consciousness, and it argues that bird mirror studies
show the mark test's limits rather than birds' lack of self-awareness. It
adds no data.

## Key insight

The question "could a bird be conscious under theory T?" splits into an
anatomical requirement and a physiological one, and the avian brain answers
them separately. The anatomy that matters for GNWT is the connectome's shape,
not cortical layering, and birds have it in a nuclear pallium. The physiology
GNWT predicts, report-tracking activity in the prefrontal analogue, has been
recorded. IIT's markers have simply not been looked for.

## Assumptions

- **Consciousness is not uniquely human**, and theories of it must account for
  both its neural basis and its behavioural consequences, "because evolution
  can only operate by selecting an animal's behavioural output" (§1).
- **NCL is the functional analogue of mammalian prefrontal cortex** (§2,
  citing Güntürkün 2005; Herold et al. 2011).
- **Report tracks experience**: a neuron that follows the animal's "seen"
  report rather than the stimulus is a correlate of subjective experience
  (§2).
- **PCI is "a good substitute"** for Φ (§2).
- **Mirror self-recognition and its ecological variants measure
  self-awareness**, graded rather than all-or-nothing (§4).

## Key results

- **Bistable perception (§2).** Pigeons shown apparent-motion stimuli report
  alternating horizontal and vertical motion (Vetter et al. 2000).
- **Crow NCL (§2).** In a delayed detection task at threshold, NCL single-unit
  activity followed the "seen" report regardless of stimulus presence, and was
  silent for "not seen" (Nieder et al. 2020). Separate NCL populations coded
  reported presence and reported absence when absence was trained as a
  reportable category (Wagener & Nieder 2024). The authors say the data
  "could be explained by any of these theories". The absence-coding result
  could "expand or challenge" GNWT, since absence too produced ignition-like
  activity.
- **Avian anatomy (§3).** 3D polarised-light imaging and tracing show layers,
  columns and repeated canonical circuits in avian *sensory* pallium (Stacho
  et al. 2020). NCL lacks them and is nuclear. The pigeon telencephalic
  connectome has hubs (NCL, arcopallium dorsale and intermedium, area
  parahippocampalis, area corticoidea dorsolateralis) that are densely
  interlinked and tied to local modules (Fig. 1B, from Shanahan et al. 2013).
  Recurrent connectivity is found throughout the visual pallium and NCL.
- **Theory scorecard (§3 summary; §5).** GNWT: anatomical requirements
  "largely met", with NCL activation correlating with awareness. RPT:
  anatomical requirements met, physiological ones unproven without switching
  recurrence off. IIT: requirements "not met (yet)"; no PCI measured and no
  grid-like lattice found, although sensory pallium's cortex-like modularity
  is the precondition for one.
- **Self-awareness (§4).** Magpies (Prior et al. 2008, but a 2020 replication
  failed) and Indian house crows pass the mark test; many corvids fail it,
  though crows and ravens show self-directed behaviour and contingency
  checking. Pigeons discriminate live from delayed self-video (1–7 s delays)
  and show less stress-related behaviour to their mirror image than to a food
  competitor. Roosters give alarm calls to a visible conspecific but not to
  their mirror image, even with a hidden conspecific behind the mirror
  (Hillemacher et al. 2023; Fig. 2).

## Concepts

- **NCL (nidopallium caudolaterale)**: the associative avian pallial region
  treated as the functional analogue of prefrontal cortex; nuclear in
  organisation.
- **sensory awareness vs self-awareness**: the paper's two headings for
  evidence of consciousness in birds.
- **"seeing" vs "knowing" (RPT)**: recurrence confined to sensory areas gives
  a basic, non-reportable experience; recurrence extending to prefrontal
  areas gives reportable experience with working-memory access.
- **mirror-audience test**: a rooster's alarm calling toward a mirror image
  versus a conspecific, used as an ecological self-recognition test.

## Bearing on the record

- Candidate source for a THEORY that conscious report in vertebrates does not
  require laminar cortex. It would need the primary crow studies read first,
  and should state that report-based paradigms do not separate access from
  phenomenal consciousness.
- `self` is justified by §4, which is a third of the paper and is about
  self-recognition.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- A review: the evidence is second-hand, and one lab's crow studies carry
  the sensory-awareness case.
- Report-based: it cannot separate phenomenal from access consciousness,
  a limit the paper does not discuss.
- IIT is judged unmet only because it is untested.
- Binocular rivalry and flash suppression, milestone paradigms in humans,
  have not been run in birds, as the authors note.

## Open questions

- Is activity in primary and associative visual areas the same on seen and
  unseen trials, with NCL added only on seen ones? That is the paper's
  statement of what GNWT predicts (§3), and it is untested.
- What does avian PCI look like across wake, NREM sleep and anaesthesia?
- Does the avian sensory pallium have a grid-like lattice of cell-type
  columns?
