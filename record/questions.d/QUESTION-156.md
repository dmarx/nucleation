# QUESTION-156: Which published models of concept combination reproduce overextension by dropping conjunction-as-meet, and which by dropping an order-preserving membership weight?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-255](../theory.d/THEORY-255.md) holds the impossibility. If membership is an order-preserving
weight on a lattice and "and" is the meet, then µ(A and B) ≤ min(µ(A),
µ(B)), whatever the negation. On 2026-10-19 it was narrowed and promoted
at the owner's direction, carrying out the promotion audit's batch 018
(record/promotion-audit/decisions/[THEORY-255](../theory.d/THEORY-255.md).yaml, [ADR-047](../decisions.d/ADR-047.md)). Its former
promote_when also asked for a reading of a second quantum model of the
concept-combination data, to confirm that it too puts the guppy-type
deviations outside the lattice meet.

That confirmation is now a consequence: every model that reproduces an
overextended item drops conjunction-as-meet or the monotone weight. What
is open is which one each published model drops. That matters because
the two choices make different claims about concepts. Dropping the meet
says a conjunction is a new concept, as Aerts's emergent subspace does.
Dropping monotonicity says membership in a narrower concept can exceed
membership in a broader one.

## What would answer it

A reading of at least one model other than Aerts's that reproduces
Hampton's overextended items, such as Busemeyer and Bruza ([LIT-316](../literature.d/LIT-316.md), if a
copy is found) or Franco's conditional-probability model. It should state
which of the two assumptions the model gives up. A better fit to
Hampton's data does not answer it, because the question is where the
deviation lives, not how well it is fitted.

A model that reproduces overextension with conjunction as a lattice meet
and an order-preserving weight would refute [THEORY-255](../theory.d/THEORY-255.md) instead.
