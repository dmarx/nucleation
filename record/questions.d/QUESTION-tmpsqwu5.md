# QUESTION-tmpsqwu5: What record of an organization's or an animal's conduct shows that one ordering prevails in the negotiation among its perspectives, which conflicts it wins, for how long, and whether it returns after interruption, and does potential measured on conflicts actually won give the same verdicts as potential read as a disposition?

## Why it is a question

TERM-tmp8gbrt makes valence a regime of potential and reads potential
through Frijda's marks of control precedence (THEORY-055). Every
consequence CLAIM-tmpz8lli draws, and every defeater it names, needs
potential to be measurable: which ranking wins the pairs on which the
perspectives disagree, whether it keeps winning, and whether it returns
after a perturbation.

Three candidate measures are in reach.
- **Conflict record.** For an organization, its decisions on the pairs
  where its perspectives disagree, with the perspective each decision
  followed (for Wells Fargo, CASE-151, where the sales model's ranking
  repeatedly overrode risk and HR). For an animal, choices in conflict
  tasks.
- **Out-prediction.** A ranking prevails when it predicts the system's
  next moves better than its rivals, the evidential grade CLAIM-427 asks
  for.
- **The coupled meaning–influence model.** Past the instability
  threshold of CLAIM-330, one direction grows and mixes meaning with
  authority: an ordering coming to prevail, in the model's own terms.

Three established tools are nearer.
- **Resumption: attractor strength.** Kuppens, Oravecz and Tuerlinckx's
  DynAffect (LIT-tmpwkuys, unread beyond the abstract) fits an "affective
  home base, a baseline attractor state around which affect fluctuates",
  with fluctuations "tied back to the home base by the attractor
  strength". Fitted to a different series, the share of conflict
  decisions that follow a candidate ranking, it would estimate that
  ranking's resumption. Whether the model carries over is unchecked.
- **Persistence: hysteresis.** Lange and colleagues (LIT-tmp39gs5,
  NOTE-tmp7qgo0) give a network of emotion components in which strong
  connections mean that "it becomes more difficult to switch back to a
  deactivated state", and "In less strongly connected networks, hysteresis does not
  occur" (p. 460). Their network ranks nothing, so it has no conflicts to
  win and no analogue of priority.
- **Which ranking: devaluation.** Moors (LIT-tmp18qgb, NOTE-tmpyam8d)
  supplies the interventional test of goal-directed control: devalue an
  outcome, or degrade its contingency, and see whether conduct still
  follows the ranking that values it. A ranking that keeps winning after
  its outcome is devalued is not the one prevailing.

Two measures from emotion research are near but not the thing. Scherer's
synchronization of response components (LIT-tmpfjacn, NOTE-tmpsx1uk) and
Lewis's predicted theta phase synchrony (LIT-tmpj284p, NOTE-tmpz7wab) are
correlates that potential would produce. Neither is signed, neither says
which ranking is winning, and neither is given as a measure per episode.
The component process model proposes two coherence measures, neither
signed: an EEG gamma and beta contrast between felt and unfelt episodes
(Grandjean, Sander and Scherer, LIT-tmpgh72l, p. 489), and a
function-on-function regression of EEG against EMG (Scherer and Moors,
LIT-tmpjaacn, p. 738). For organisms, the physiological split between
intrinsic valence (facial EMG) and goal congruence (skin conductance and
skin temperature; LIT-tmpjaacn, p. 730) separates the two levels of
valence CLAIM-tmp5lpdu distinguishes. The componential measurement
literature since does not close the gap.
Mohammadi and Vuilleumier (LIT-tmp7rygg), Meuleman and Rudrauf
(LIT-tmp5ejuc) and Meuleman et al. (LIT-tmppjo0s) measure
multi-componentiality as structure across a sample of ratings, not as
coordination within an episode. Sander, Grandjean and Scherer
(LIT-tmpzf3da, NOTE-tmphslle) say what synchronization should be in the
brain and call outlining it "a major challenge" (p. 228); they also keep
control precedence, "some action tendencies with control precedence take
priority over other potential action tendencies" (p. 223), apart from
synchronization, as this question does. The nearest to a per-episode
measure is Mohammadi, Van De Ville and Vuilleumier's latent processes
(LIT-tmp9qnrb, NOTE-tmpocnqb), defined for each 12-second event, but
their scores rest on reported tendencies and are bipolar, so they cannot
show "continue" and "stop" both high, the ambivalent case this question
must separate from neutrality. Meuleman and Rudrauf's virtual-reality
paradigm, whose headset tracking the paper does not use, is the kind of
setting in which a conflict record of enacted tendencies could be
kept.

The second half of the question is the eraser. Priority, persistence and
resumption are dispositional. If potential is read from conflicts
actually won over an interval, a clamp that never acts cannot change it;
if it is read as a disposition, THEORY-252's argument applies to it.

**When to read it.** Kahneman, Wakker and Sarin (LIT-tmpd3ww1,
NOTE-tmpj69i0) separate decision utility from experienced utility and
show later choices following remembered utility, which neglects duration
and weights peaks and ends: "In many other situations the sovereign
masters that determine what people do are not pleasure and pain, but
memories of pleasure and pain" (p. 385). Potential during an episode
must therefore be read from the conflicts won during it, not from later
choices about it, which measure a different ordering, the remembered one.

## A procedure: the Hodge decomposition of the conflict record

With the rename to "potential" (TERM-tmp8gbrt) the question has a
candidate procedure. Record, for each contested pair, which side the
negotiation's decision took, and treat the record as a flow on the graph
of options. HodgeRank (Jiang, Lim, Yao and Ye, LIT-tmp54cwg, NOTE-tmp7wl76;
THEORY-tmp4zhyp) splits it into a gradient part, the least-squares global
ranking, and a cyclic part, curl for local inconsistency and harmonic for
global. Then:
- **which ordering prevails** is the gradient's ranking, compared with
  each perspective's own;
- **how far it prevails** is the agreement of the gradient with one
  perspective's flow, not the gradient share, which measures only
  coherence (CLAIM-tmp7df5r, part 3);
- **ambivalence** is the cyclic share above the baseline a transitive
  binary record already leaves, (n − 2)/3n on n options (CLAIM-tmp7df5r,
  part 2), or the circular triads counted directly;
- **persistence** is the stability of the decomposition across windows,
  and **resumption** the return of the gradient's ranking after a
  perturbation.

Three cautions from the readings. The shares are cardinal and depend on
the weights given to pairs (by default, how often a pair was contested),
on how repeated contests are aggregated, and on the norm; binary records
enter as the net frequency of each side winning (NOTE-tmp7wl76). Their
paper's Table 1 reports a cyclicity ratio above 1, which orthogonality
forbids, so a reported figure is not taken on trust. And the
decomposition does not separate a lexicographic override from a
compensatory potential on a finite record (CLAIM-tmp85p0g).

## What would count as an answer

A procedure that, applied to a record of conduct, returns which ranking
prevails and how strongly, with a case where it separates ambivalence
(no potential ranking, or split potential) from neutrality (potential with
no movement), and a statement of whether it measures actual or
dispositional potential.

## What would not answer it

A measure of how strongly a system pursues one goal, without the
conflicts it wins against the others.
