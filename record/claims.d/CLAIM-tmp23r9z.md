# CLAIM-tmp23r9z: Retrieval that depends on current belief is not a bias in itself: adaptive search can be rational when inference accounts for how the evidence was selected, and calling a search biased needs a standard outside the search

## The claim

A21 §4: "adaptive retrieval is not inherently irrational or biased. In an
ideal sequential Bayesian design, search policy can depend on current
beliefs, and inference can account correctly for how observations were
selected." The pathology lies elsewhere: treating selected evidence as
representative, ignoring explanations the queries excluded, treating
conjectures as constraints, or stopping because one's own results agree
with each other.

And the measure: "Neither measure alone certifies bias. We'd need an
external standard of task relevance or correctness." That is [CLAIM-190](CLAIM-190.md)'s
point about the record's own metrics, applied to search.

## Why it is granted and Active

It is a standard result of Bayesian design that the selection rule does
not bias inference when the likelihood accounts for it, and it cuts
against the strong reading of [CLAIM-tmps7uw8](CLAIM-tmps7uw8.md). A20 also said it of a single
retrieval: "Cue-dependent retrieval does not imply bias by itself."

## What it does not say

Not that real agents account for their selection. Whether they do is
[QUESTION-tmph2dsr](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmph2dsr.md).
