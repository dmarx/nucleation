# CLAIM-275: An intervention can come before the interpretation it makes available, so in an organization of processes meaning, policy and influence can co-evolve, and none of them is upstream of the others in every case

## The claim

A4 §6: "We initially imagined agents reading an evolving meaning canvas,
constructing interpretations, and then deciding which transformations or
actions to undertake." The record holds two accounts of emotion that order
these differently. Frijda puts the appraisal of concern-relevance upstream
of action readiness ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)). Barrett puts predictive organization of
action at the centre of constructing emotional meaning, with categories
assembled by the perceiver ([THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md)). A4: "Our model need not choose one
order universally. Instead, meaning, policy, and influence can co-evolve.
An operator may act not because it already possesses an interpretation, but
because its intervention creates the conditions under which a particular
interpretation becomes available. For example, an operator might re-noise a
settled region before it knows which new interpretation it wishes to
establish."

The owner, at U5, to the first sentence: "yes, exactly". To the second: "so
much yes." A4's consequence: "That makes **exploration, disruption, and
action potential sources of meaning**, rather than mere consequences of
meaning", and the meaning canvas must hold "action dispositions,
affordances, unresolved tensions, and partially constructed interpretations".

[CLAIM-255](CLAIM-255.md) separates intending, performing and being recognized as having
performed an act. This claim adds that the interpretation itself may come
after the act. A3's re-noising in order to look for alternatives is the
plainest case.

## What it does not say

- It does not say interpretation never comes first. A4: "need not choose one
  order universally."
- It does not decide between Frijda and Barrett. It says the model should
  not assume either order.

## Note of 2026-10-29: promoted

The claim stands on A4 §6, the owner's assent at U5 and A3's re-noising, and
it uses [TERM-094](../terms.d/TERM-094.md) (Active). It has no objection, and nothing the record holds
meets `defeated_if`. [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)'s elicitation thesis would bear on its
emotional-action half if it were held, because an appraisal of pertinence
comes first there; but that appraisal is not the interpretation the act
makes available, and [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md) is Proposed. [CASE-143](../cases.d/CASE-143.md) is an instance: the
favour came before the liking.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the contact literature, read

Pettigrew's 1998 review, [LIT-1408](../literature.d/LIT-1408.md) ([NOTE-1408](../notes.d/NOTE-1408.md)), and Pettigrew and
Tropp's meta-analyses of 2006, [LIT-1413](../literature.d/LIT-1413.md) ([NOTE-1407](../notes.d/NOTE-1407.md)), and 2008,
[LIT-1405](../literature.d/LIT-1405.md) ([NOTE-1405](../notes.d/NOTE-1405.md)), are now read. Contact before revised attitude
is the claim's order, and they bear on it in three ways.

- **The review states the order.** Among contact's four processes:
  "Behavior change is often the precursor of attitude change", with
  dissonance as the mechanism. That is the shape of [CASE-143](../cases.d/CASE-143.md). It is
  argued, not tested.
- **The 2006 meta-analysis cannot mostly order the two.** It is the
  strongest support the record holds for contact changing attitudes, but
  most of its samples are cross-sectional surveys of reported contact.
  The ordered evidence is the 36 experimental samples (r = −.336) and the
  samples with no choice of contact (−.280), and both give larger effects
  than the rest. The authors' explanation, mere exposure and reduced
  anxiety, is an encounter acting before any revised view, which fits
  the claim; they argue it from other literatures and do not test it.
- **The 2008 meta-analysis puts feeling before belief.** Reduced anxiety
  and increased empathy carry more of the contact–prejudice association
  than knowledge of the outgroup does. Its mediation is cross-sectional
  and statistical, and the sequence it suggests, anxiety falling first,
  is proposed, not shown.

The claim is unchanged.
