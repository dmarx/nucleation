# LIT-676: Unveiling Mode Connectivity in Graph Neural Networks

Bingheng Li, Zhikai Chen, Haoyu Han, Shenglai Zeng, Jingzhe Liu and Jiliang Tang (2025), arXiv preprint — [ARXIV-2502.12608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12608)

## Key takeaways

- **Connected, but not linearly.** For GCNs trained for node classification
  from different seeds or data orders, the straight line between solutions
  often shows a clear loss barrier: small on Cora and Citeseer, large on
  Coauthor-CS and Squirrel. A quadratic Bezier curve fitted as in Garipov et
  al. removes most of it (§3.1, Figs. 1–2). Graphs from similar domains give
  similar interpolation curves (Observation 2).
- **The graph, not the architecture.** Barriers for GCN, GraphSAGE and GAT
  are similar, while an MLP on the same features differs markedly
  (Observation 3, Fig. 4). On CSBM graphs, barriers fall as density and
  feature separability rise, and are highest at middling homophily, with
  both strongly homophilous and strongly heterophilous graphs better
  connected (Observation 4, Fig. 5).
- **Theory and uses, stated without proofs.** Theorem 3.2 bounds the barrier
  by layer-wise weight differences, weight norms and an effective
  propagation factor tied to the spectral gap; Proposition 3.5 gives a CSBM
  version in which the bound falls with density and with (h − ½)². Theorem
  4.1 bounds the generalisation gap by the barrier. A Wasserstein-1 distance
  between two graphs' interpolation-loss curves is claimed to bound the
  domain-adaptation gap and to correlate with it empirically (Fig. 7).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the owner's batch on mode connectivity and model merging, under
`loss-landscapes` ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). `network-science` because its explanatory
variables are graph properties (density, homophily, spectral gap, the
stochastic block model). `learning-theory` because it proposes a
generalisation bound in terms of the loss barrier.

`Proposed`, not `Active`. The empirical observations are plausible and
interesting; the theoretical half cannot be checked. Every proof is referred
to "Appendix" with no section, and the appendix holds only figures, settings
and datasets. §3.2 still reads "[add more experimental details here]". The
barrier values behind Figs. 4–5 are not given as numbers.

It extends Garipov et al. ([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)): its central finding, non-linear
connectivity, is established by fitting their quadratic Bezier curve between
GNN solutions, "following Garipov et al. (2018)" (§3.1), and its loss-barrier
definition is applied along their curve as well as the line. It cites Frankle
et al. ([LIT-654](LIT-654.md)) for linear connectivity in fully connected networks,
and argues from its own results that their pruning-connectivity link may
not carry to heterophilous graphs.

Within the batch, it is one of three works testing connectivity outside
image classifiers, with [LIT-660](LIT-660.md) (diffusion and CLIP models) and
[LIT-677](LIT-677.md) (the ELBO of LDA). Like Zhou et al. on federated learning
([LIT-666](LIT-666.md)), it makes the barrier a function of data structure.
