# Article 1. The Identity of Organization

## Individuation, maintenance provenance, and the limits of behavioural equivalence

**Abstract.** Organizations are usually told apart by what they do, where their boundaries lie, how tightly their parts hang together, or how long they last. This article argues that these criteria pick out different things, and that the difference that matters most for governing an organization, whose processes keep it in being, is invisible to all of them. It argues first that "individuation" has several senses, which are not stages, so that a pattern can be an individual while being kept going entirely from outside; and that the achievements running from interconnection to autonomy form a partial order, not a ladder. It then shows, in a simulated population and in a series of exact constructions, that arrangements with identical ordinary behaviour can be maintained in different ways; that coupling statistics, informational closure and recovery from perturbation do not reveal which; and that maintenance is located only by interventions on the maintaining mechanism, relative to a boundary declared in advance and not discovered by the experiments. In the one construction of formation available, no intervention after formation tells a self-formed organization from one installed from outside and then self-maintained; an intervention during formation does. What persists through the replacement of every part is a lineage carried by causal transmission, not a resemblance, so a character two unrelated organizations share is a kind and not the persistence of either. The article ends with what these separations forbid: self-production is not robustness, identification works only within a known catalogue, no single closure measure fixes identity, and individuation in any sense establishes neither agency, autonomy, experience nor standing. Every result is a result in a model, and the article says where its premises are conjectures.

---

### 1.1 Introduction

When are two organizations the same organization, and when is one organization still the one it was? The ordinary answers appeal to behaviour (it does the same things), to boundaries (it has the same members, or the same edges), to coherence (its parts remain coupled), or to persistence (it has lasted). Each answer is useful, and each is what a governance arrangement typically observes: auditors see outputs, regulators see legal boundaries, analysts see coordination, and historians see continuity. The question this article asks is whether these criteria agree, and, where they do not, which of the facts they miss would matter to anyone deciding how an organization should be governed.

The fact the article puts at the centre is maintenance: whose processes keep an organization, or a governing rule inside it, in being. Two organizations can behave identically while one keeps its rules going by its own reciprocal production and the other has them reset from outside. The difference matters because the two fail differently, respond differently to the withdrawal of support, and stand differently towards claims that the organization governs itself. If ordinary observation cannot see the difference, then a governance regime built on ordinary observation is governing something it cannot identify.

The argument proceeds in the order its steps depend on each other. Section 1.2 separates the senses of "individuation", and 1.3 orders the achievements from interconnection to autonomy. Section 1.4 gives the central executed result: three ways of keeping two governing rules that agree in ordinary operation and come apart under intervention. Sections 1.5 and 1.6 explain why the usual diagnostics do not see the difference and what does: coupling and closure measures in 1.5, interventions on state and the declared boundary in 1.6. Section 1.7 separates maintenance from formation and assembles the results into a design for telling them apart. Section 1.8 asks how a maintained form comes to be and when a supporting process can be withdrawn. Section 1.9 turns to a domain where dependence and individuality are documented in organisms rather than constructed, and 1.10 to what persists through change of every part. Section 1.11 sets out what representation theory and resource-bounded profiles can and cannot contribute, and 1.12 why individuation is not agency. Section 1.13 states what the argument does not establish and takes up the strongest objection to it, and 1.14 concludes.

**A note on method.** The article is written from a research record in which each step is held in its own entry, cited by its code in parentheses. A TERM is a definition, a CLAIM an assertion with its argument, a CASE a construction, a simulation run or a documented specimen, a QUESTION an open question and a LEAD a planned piece of work. The standing of each entry, who filed it and when, the reviews the draft has had, and the record's own bookkeeping are in the article's apparatus and the manuscript's README, not here. Where the firmness of a step matters to the argument, the text says so. All constructions and runs cited are models whose populations, boundaries and laws the modeller stipulates; none is an observation of a real organization.

### 1.2 Which individuation?

To say that something is an individual can mean at least four things (TERM-088). It can be *descriptive*: "an inquiry can distinguish and track it". It can be *dynamical*: "its organization persists under specified dynamics and perturbation, with robust identity and boundaries". It can be *constitutive*: "its relational organization helps fix its identity and boundary". And it can be *organizational autonomy*, "the further achievement of contributing to the maintenance of its own constitutive constraints". Autonomy here is closure of constraints, where closure is "a set of constraints each of which depends directly on another in the set and has another depending on it" (TERM-070), a notion taken from the organizational account of biological function (Mossio, Saborido and Moreno 2009; Montévil and Mossio 2015; THEORY-072).

The senses "are not stages, and not degrees of existence. A pattern can be dynamically individuated while sustained wholly from outside, like a convection roll fed by an imposed gradient. That is individuation without autonomy" (TERM-088). The point is easy to lose and the rest of the article depends on it: "A persistent externally sustained pattern may still be a dynamically individuated entity" (TERM-088; CLAIM-474). Whether an organization is an individual is one question. Whose processes keep it going is another.

The four senses name what is claimed, "not how it is measured" (TERM-088). Boundaries, coupling strength, coherence and persistence are criteria, and none of them is a sense. Criteria are also relative to a purpose. "An individuation is well defined only once its criterion is fixed (the target it serves, the transformations its unity must survive, the scale), and that dependence does not make it arbitrary: once the criterion is fixed a verdict can be false" (CLAIM-405). The relativity follows from a more basic fact: "Any individuation built on sufficiency is relative to the target it is sufficient for" (CLAIM-152). One candidate criterion for when a combined form is one system is a block of nearly decomposable dynamics whose spectral gap beats an Occam factor (CLAIM-157), a proposed answer to the open question of when composition yields a unit (QUESTION-035). It is a measure of the dynamical sense, and section 1.5 shows what it cannot see.

There is a fifth use of the word that TERM-088 sets aside: "the genetic, or operational, sense of Simondon's readers, individuation as the operation by which a metastable system resolves a disparity and yields a provisional individual". That sense names "the process that would produce a candidate, not a claim about one", and "a claim that means it says so" (TERM-088). In organization studies it is the sense in which research into individuation follows "the being in its genesis" (Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy 2024, after Simondon; THEORY-1628). This article uses it once, in section 1.8, where it asks how a form comes to be, and says so there.

The four senses together suggest a thesis that is tempting and should be resisted in its strong form: that organizational identity is a plurality of related achievements rather than a single equivalence relation. On a weak reading, that no single relation fixes identity across the senses and criteria just distinguished, the thesis follows from them and adds nothing. On a strong reading, that identity is a plurality even once sense and criterion are fixed, the thesis has no stated content that a case could contradict. The article does not assert it. What it does assert, in section 1.10, is one criterion of identity over time.

### 1.3 Achievements in a partial order

The relations between interconnection and individuation can be set out "as a research program with distinct testable transitions, rather than a cluster of philosophically suggestive analogies" (CLAIM-474):

> From interconnection to individuation, the relationships are distinct achievements that form a partial order, not a nested sequence: none implies the next, and a later one need not require every earlier one (CLAIM-474).

Interconnection fixes what joint behaviour is possible. Compatibility fixes which local descriptions admit a coherent whole. Dynamics fixes which organizations persist; individuation, which have "sufficiently robust identity and boundaries"; organizational autonomy, which "help maintain their own constitutive constraints"; and inquiry, which can revise their own models under resource bounds, with "inquiry and reconstruction occurring without autonomy" (CLAIM-474). Two of the failures of implication bear directly on this article. "Persistence without individuality. Synchrony can come from a common drive". And "Persistence without autonomy. A pattern can be maintained entirely by external supply" (CLAIM-474).

The claim was first stated as a nested sequence and was revised into a partial order under a rule of method that this article follows: "derive the dependency structure of the achievements separately from the order in which an inquiry met them" (CLAIM-474). The order of the sections here is an order of exposition, not a claim about which achievement comes first in any organization.

The partial order is an interpretive claim, and its empirical support is one run. The experiment it calls for manipulates coupling, correspondence maps and their revision independently (CASE-179), so that the transitions "can be made to come apart on purpose" (CLAIM-474). The first run of that design, in an oscillator network, did so (CASE-333). Under a shared drive, uncoupled and coupled networks lock alike and their offsets glue alike. Displacing one member's drive moves the others by 0.2 per cent of its shift without coupling and by 30 to 58 per cent with it. After the drive is withdrawn, order is 0.47 without coupling and 0.996 with it (CLAIM-903). So a common drive produces the appearance of an integrated collective, and only withdrawal shows whether the coupling was doing the work. That pattern, appearance under ordinary operation and difference under intervention, recurs through the rest of the article.

The partial order is of achievements of organization. It is not an order of how ideas or memes come to organize people, and a proposal to use it that way was rejected on the ground that "CLAIM-474 orders other things, and the hypothesis needs no such order" (ARG-tmpeukxb). Whether contracts among an organization's processes are a further achievement in the order has been proposed and not argued (CLAIM-474); Article 3 takes contracts up.

### 1.4 Matched behaviour, different maintenance

The central result is a simulation. A population of 32 members keeps two governing rules, an audit rule and an admission rule, in three ways: by external reset, by reciprocal production between two producer teams, or by feedback without production (CASE-161). The ordinary trajectories are identical. The arrangements come apart only under intervention. Reciprocal production "repairs the loss of one rule and not of both". A producer outage of 80 updates ends both rules in 48 of 48 reciprocal runs and leaves the other arrangements unaffected; the audit team's outage is survived at 32 updates and not at 36, the admission team's at 36 and not at 40, and "These are grid points under these laws, not a critical duration". After the same audit pulse, an intervention on the state, the next audit integrity is "1.00 under external reset, 0.18 under reciprocal production and 0.00 under feedback only" (CASE-161). The figures come from stored results that were read again and checked, not from regenerated runs.

The general claim behind the run is that "Matched ordinary trajectories can conceal differences in an interpretation's efficacy, in the allocation of that efficacy, and in the maintenance of its enabling constraints", and that in two finite constructions "a specified intervention exposes the difference that passive observation of the matched trajectories does not" (CLAIM-414). Put generally: "Agreement in ordinary operation does not fix how a system responds to an intervention on its mechanism" (SYNTHESIS-079). Whether two systems with matching behaviour and persistence can be told apart by whether their own processes generate and revise the constraints that keep them going, "and by what intervention?", is the open question this article advances and does not close (QUESTION-086).

If the candidate mechanisms are known, they can be identified. A companion study asks which of four known mechanisms maintains the rules (CASE-163). With histories matched, a model fitted under reciprocal production predicts 0.97055 for both reciprocal production (actual 0.97093) and self-dependent production (actual 0.00000), so the fit to history cannot tell them apart. Without probes the class is identified in 25.0 per cent of cases; with a complete audit pulse and a short producer outage, in 100.0 per cent, and the responses to interventions withheld from fitting are predicted. The result holds only "within a known catalog and with every probe run on a counterfactual reset": "A posterior over a known catalog is not identification of an unknown law" (CASE-163). This study is less firm than its companion: its stored results have not yet been re-read as the first study's were.

The run also says something against the arrangement one might be inclined to favour:

> Maintaining a governing rule internally, by the organization's own reciprocal production, can be more fragile than having it reset from outside: arrangements identical in ordinary operation can differ in which losses are repairable, and the self-produced rule can be lost to a double loss or an interruption of the work that produces it which outside reset survives (CLAIM-456).

"It says nothing against self-governance, only that self-production is not robustness" (CLAIM-456). And the run says where its boundary comes from: "The producer teams, schedules and production law are assigned by the modeller, so 'internal' means inside the simulated population by stipulation" (CASE-161). That is the boundary section 1.6 argues the inference needs, and it is why the run does not show a population drawing its own.

The same shape appears in other quantities. Matched output can hide a difference in readiness rather than in maintenance: in a matched-performance model of regulation, "every regime produces the same output until a contingency arrives, while readiness at a deadline of six periods ranges from 0.147 to 0.894" (CASE-412). And the concealment can be kept in place by the questions asked: of two ecosystems "equivalent under the routine queries", the one "that optimized away capacities only an unasked query needs is rewarded" (CLAIM-1054), an interpretive claim. Article 2 takes up readiness.

### 1.5 Coupling, closure and reciprocal maintenance

Why do the ordinary diagnostics miss the difference? Three results answer for three of them.

**Coupling does not register who made it.**

> A gap or coupling criterion cannot tell a boundary a system produces from one imposed on it: a membrane the cell makes and a beam joining two pendulums pass alike, so the block test cannot by itself supply the individuality minimal agency needs (CLAIM-194).

"The criterion has no term for who made the coupling" (CLAIM-194). The claim is an objection to using the block criterion of section 1.2 (CLAIM-157) as a test of individuality, and it is limited: it does not say that production is measurable wherever coupling is (CLAIM-194). The view that places the line at production, that organisms differ from dissipative structures by "several distinct constraints that produce one another at different time scales" (THEORY-072), is a reading the argument uses and does not defend.

**A persisting capacity needs a productive cycle, and a cycle is not closure.** Where every constraint capacity turns over and is produced only by others, "capacities whose production graph is acyclic all decay to zero: a capacity that persists needs a productive cycle among its ancestors". The cycle "is necessary and not sufficient, and a one-member cycle is not closure" (CLAIM-516). So a productive cycle is the minimal structure a persisting capacity needs, under that claim's hypotheses, and having one does not make an organization internally maintained; closure in the sense of TERM-070 needs two or more constraints. Entrenchment is no substitute. An entrenched core "can sit at the root of an acyclic dependency order, and where interactions turn over such an order persists only while something outside supplies its core" (CLAIM-531). The self-dependent production graph of section 1.4 shows the same thing in the population: it "does not regenerate a zeroed rule" (CASE-161).

**Informational closure is not closure of constraints, and it does not keep intervention responses.** "Closure" has several senses, and a claim should say which (TERM-068). Informational closure, a description whose own past predicts its future as well as the full state does, is a fact about prediction, and it can hold while a description loses what an intervention would reveal:

> A coarse-graining exactly informationally closed under a process's ordinary dynamics need not preserve its responses to an intervention on the mechanism (CLAIM-407).

The measure is the deficiency Γ = I(S; Y^(I) | T(S), I) for a declared distribution of interventions, and "Γ = 0 exactly when, for every intervention ρ charges, the response kernels are constant on T's fibres over the states of positive probability" (CLAIM-407). This is "an application of ordinary causal sufficiency, not new information theory" (CLAIM-407). An exactly known organization of 192 states, with the three maintenance modes of section 1.4, is a second witness (CASE-164). On it, "closure on a support, global lumpability and sufficiency for a declared intervention mixture give different verdicts". The description by agents and phase is informationally closed on the intact support; its strong-lumpability defect is 0.42 globally and 0 on that support ("Closure on a support is not lumpability of the chain"); and its deficiency is 0.55·H₂(1/3) ≈ 0.5051 bits for every description that omits the maintenance mode and 0 for the full state. Every verdict is relative to a declared stationary law, since the chain has none of its own, and in finite samples "a finite-sample sign test of closure fails in both directions at the sample sizes tested" (CASE-164). Nor does refinement order closure: a deficiency of sufficiency "cannot increase under refinement", while informational closure "is monotone in neither direction" (CLAIM-409). The construction claims nothing about real organizations; it says it cannot show "Anything about real organizations" (CASE-164).

**Present coupling need not reveal the stability of what maintains it.** Two constructions of reciprocal maintenance make this exact (CASE-189). In the first, two constraints that produce each other hold a three-unit coupling, which survives damage up to 0.4 and meets a saddle-node at damage 0.5. In the second, "a transient encounter … forms a connector enabling that reciprocal production, after which the connector is maintained by what it enables, every single-pathway knockout ends it, and the regime ends in a saddle-node at damage ≈ 0.691 where the coupling's gap is still ≈ 0.989" (CASE-189). "The coupled units are as tightly integrated at the edge of collapse as well inside the regime" (CLAIM-521). Hence: "An organization's present coupling strength need not reveal the stability of the process maintaining it" (CLAIM-521). The dissociation is generic for such folds, "built in rather than surprising" (CLAIM-521), and it is exactly what a coupling criterion would miss.

One caution on the word "realizes". "That a mechanism realizes a capacity can mean that the capacity is produced through it over time or that the capacity consists in it at a time, and interventions show only the first" (CLAIM-528). In this article "maintained by" means produced through, the causal sense, which interventions can test. When constraints only characterize an entity and when they partly constitute it is open (QUESTION-284).

### 1.6 What interventions on state cannot show, and where the boundary comes from

If coupling, closure and passive observation do not locate maintenance, perhaps perturbation does: knock the system and see whether it recovers. It does not, in the limiting case.

> Recovery under interventions on state cannot show where maintenance is realized: an outside controller that measures the constraint capacities and supplies the internal production law as state feedback yields the same trajectory under every perturbation of state, damage and recovery included, even with every capacity observed exactly; only interventions on the production mechanisms, relative to a declared boundary, separate the two (CLAIM-522).

"Full observation of c changes nothing." What separates the two systems is "disabling a production pathway inside the declared boundary (A loses it, B does not), or interrupting the controller (B loses it, A does not)" (CLAIM-522). This is a demonstrated result about an ideal case. It tells against a natural first design for studying organizational identity, one that removes initiating supports, exchanges components and perturbs relational constraints. Exchanging components and perturbing constraints are interventions on state, and removing an initiating support acts on the mechanism that initiated the organization, not on the one that maintains it.

The result is scoped to an ideal controller, one that reproduces the whole production law. A controller that reproduces less of the law can be found out by interventions on state, and the degree to which it can be is ordered. In a two-variable mutual-maintenance model, "a constant outside controller holding r at its maintained value gives the same equilibrium but recovers from c = 0.01, where the mutual system collapses" (CASE-265), so the constant controller "is found out by perturbing the state". A ladder of controllers in the same model runs from a constant supply, through the production law applied to a delayed reading, to the law applied to the present reading. "A brief knock to the structure separates the first two from internal production, the second by less as its delay shrinks, and no intervention on state separates the third until the internal pathway is cut" (CASE-268). The largest gap under the knock falls from 0.146 for the constant controller to 0.010 for a delay of 0.03, and to 0 for the exact law. The general form is that "The interventions needed to find that a structure's maintenance comes from outside grow with how much of the internal production law the outside maintainer reproduces" (CLAIM-693). It is shown for the delay family only; "That the ordering holds beyond that family is the claim's general reading and stays open" (CLAIM-693). Two executed studies fit the lower rungs: a constant-rate outside service is told apart by zeroing the state, with 0 of 64 second corrections against 64 of 64 (CASE-332), and in the population of section 1.4 the audit pulse parts external reset from the other arrangements (CASE-161). Neither is a counterexample to the ideal case (CLAIM-522).

The constructions on this ladder are models with illustrative parameters, and none has yet been rerun independently of its author. Their direction is what the argument needs: the closer an outside maintainer comes to reproducing the internal law, the less a state intervention can show, and at the limit it shows nothing.

**The boundary is not found by the experiments.** Every one of these separations presupposes that one knows which mechanism is inside. That is fixed by a declared boundary, "and a gap criterion cannot supply it (CLAIM-194)" (CLAIM-522). The mechanist account of constitution reaches the same place. Under mutual manipulability (Craver 2007; Craver, Glennan and Povich 2021), an internal producer and an outside controller of this kind meet the two manipulability conditions identically, "so what puts one inside and the other outside is parthood, a boundary the experiments presuppose and do not find" (CLAIM-530). Maintenance provenance is therefore relative to a boundary. By CLAIM-405 that does not make it arbitrary: once the boundary is declared, a verdict about whose process maintains the organization can be false. But the declaration comes first, and it is not an output of the inquiry.

The steps of this section and the last are separate results: passive observation fails, interventions on state fail against an ideal controller, interventions on mechanism succeed relative to a boundary, and the boundary is declared. Whether they form a single ladder of what separates, or whether a productive cycle could itself supply the boundary, are further questions the article does not settle.

### 1.7 Formation provenance and a three-arm design

#### 1.7.1 Formation is a fact about history

Maintenance is not the only provenance an organization has. Three senses should be kept apart (TERM-100):

| sense | when | what can test it |
|---|---|---|
| formation: "whose process produced it" | the history of production | "an intervention on the producing process while it runs, or a record kept then (CASE-191)" |
| maintenance: "whose process keeps it in being now" | the present | "knockouts of the processes that produce and uphold it (CLAIM-522)" |
| source: what it was made from | the history of a transport | a fidelity profile against the source |

"The first two come apart" (TERM-100). A construction shows how (CASE-191). It compares three agents: one whose own frustration at two demands produces a capacity; one whose capacity was installed by an outside pulse and is then maintained by the agent's own cycle; and one whose capacity is upheld from outside. "A capacity upheld from outside is told apart by knockouts afterwards, but one installed by an outside pulse and then self-maintained is told apart only by a knockout during formation". "After the fact the two are the same organization" (CASE-191).

So a study run on matched organizations that have already formed can reach maintenance provenance by knockouts of the maintaining mechanism. In this construction no knockout after formation reaches formation provenance; a knockout during formation does, and so does a record kept at the time (TERM-100; CASE-191). That is shown for one fictional construction with a scalar capacity, and the generalization is not claimed. Nothing shows that formation leaves no other trace after the fact. Where a trace might be in a richer object, self-determination theory gives a hint: a regulation taken in under controlling conditions is "introjected rather than integrated" (Deci and Ryan 2000; THEORY-047), and autonomy is graded by integration "with one's other values and needs" (Ryan and Deci 2000; THEORY-054). If that is right, an outside-installed and a self-formed capacity "must differ somewhere this model lacks", "not in the capacity, which is identical, but in its relations to the rest" (CASE-191). Whether a richer object can be told apart after formation in that way is open.

The distinction has a practical edge. Reflexive control can "seed interpretive, evidence-selecting and authority-allocating dispositions that the target then maintains itself", which "leaves formation provenance outside while maintenance is the target's own" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw). An organization can then pass every test of self-maintenance and still owe its constitution to someone else. Article 6 takes this up.

#### 1.7.2 Intervention or history

The same structure appears in an objection to a non-domination criterion for integration:

> Processes conditioned never to object coordinate without override at the same behaviour and supervisory cost as processes that negotiate, so the two part only under intervention, when a constituent dissents, or in their history (CLAIM-364).

"Every discriminating test is an intervention or a history" (CLAIM-364). It is tempting to read the two discriminators as the two provenances: intervention for maintenance, history for formation. That reading is half wrong. Removing the supervisor is a knockout of an outside upholder, and both kinds of coordination survive it, so the two share their maintenance provenance. The correct statement is:

> Conditioned and negotiated coordination share their maintenance provenance, since both persist when the supervisor is removed, so the interventions that part them test how the constituents' own processes answer dissent, not whose process keeps the arrangement, and removing the supervisor fails to discriminate because it is the one maintenance-provenance test among them (CLAIM-tmp9ia3w).

The history half holds: history reads formation provenance (TERM-100; CLAIM-tmp9ia3w). And the claim can be defeated only relative to a boundary "declared in advance (CLAIM-522, CLAIM-194)" (CLAIM-tmp9ia3w). The lesson generalizes. An intervention that parts two arrangements does not thereby test maintenance; it tests whatever mechanism it acts on, and one must say which.

#### 1.7.3 The design

The results so far assemble into a design for telling provenances apart. It has three arms, matched on ordinary behaviour:

- **(a) self-formed:** the organization's own processes produced the maintained object and maintain it;
- **(b) installed, then self-maintained:** an outside process produced it, and the organization's own processes maintain it;
- **(c) upheld:** an outside controller supplies the internal production law as state feedback, the ideal case of CLAIM-522.

For a scalar capacity the design has already been run: the three agents of CASE-191 are its three arms, and the upheld agent's controller supplies "P(G) and P(S) as feedback", the ideal case (CASE-191; CLAIM-522). For governing rules in a stipulated population, the studies of section 1.4 run its maintenance arm (CASE-161; CASE-163). The steps, with what holds each, are these.

1. **Declare the boundary before any intervention** (CLAIM-522; CLAIM-194; CLAIM-530). The simulated studies declare it by construction.
2. **Intervene on the maintaining mechanisms after formation.** Disable a production pathway inside the boundary, and separately interrupt the outside controller. In CASE-191 the internal knockout leaves (a) and (b) at 0 and (c) at 2.618; the outside knockout does the reverse. This step holds by the definition of arm (c), which has no internal maintenance and is defined so that no state probe parts it from (a). A state probe that did separate them would show only that the controller was not ideal (section 1.6).
3. **Intervene on the producing process during formation, or keep a record then.** Only this separates (a) from (b) (TERM-100; CASE-191). In CASE-191, knocking out frustration-driven production from the start leaves (a) at 0 and (b) at 2.618, and no later knockout separates them. A knockout after formation that separated (a) from (b) would falsify the expectation; for an object richer than a scalar, that possibility is live (section 1.7.1).
4. **Withdraw the initiating support,** and read persistence after withdrawal over a horizon fixed in advance, since "slow reversion can mimic it" (CLAIM-600). In CASE-191 the pulse arm's supply ends at t = 2 and the capacity persists. The oscillator run withdraws a shared drive (CASE-333), and section 1.8 times a withdrawal (CASE-365). For memes the same test has been proposed: whether a self-fulfilling idea's product is maintained by its own processes "is a fact about maintenance provenance, told apart by withdrawing the circulation once the realization has formed" (CLAIM-tmpyp5bu), an interpretive claim.
5. **Run state interventions too,** as a control. Under the ideal controller they cannot separate (a) from (c) (CLAIM-522), and they do part non-ideal controllers (CASE-265; CASE-268; CASE-332; CASE-161).

So steps 3 and 4 carry the design's test, and one question it cannot answer from inside: whether any real system realizes arm (c).

The design has not been run for anything richer than a scalar capacity or a governing rule. Its extension to a maintained perspective, first in artificial agents, with interventions on the maintaining mechanism inside a declared boundary and dispositional models as the rival, is planned and not run (LEAD-tmpu1jj5). The plan states its expectation in advance: the knockout "degrades it while state interventions do not", and the dispositional rival "predicts no difference between arm 2 and the control" (LEAD-tmpu1jj5). It must also declare where on the ladder of section 1.6 its outside controller sits (LEAD-tmpu1jj5; CLAIM-693). It carries a warning from an executed study: "a habit of matched memory reproduces every intervention it can receive", so the design "needs an intervention inside the boundary that a habit cannot receive" (LEAD-tmpu1jj5; CASE-346). A related study, transplanting a perspective between carriers, is proposed and not run, and its own design warns that "No transfer experiment can settle numerical identity merely by demonstrating causal continuity" (CASE-148).

### 1.8 Coming to be and staying

This section uses "individuation" in the genetic sense that section 1.2 set aside. It asks how a form comes to be and stays, not whether a candidate is an individual. The question is open:

> By which dynamical transitions do the distinctions individuation needs come into being and pass away (instability that amplifies small differences, nucleation across a finite barrier from a locally stable state, bifurcation of the set of attractors, noise- or rate-induced tipping), and are a form's creation, its stabilization and its coming to have a constitutive role three separate achievements? (QUESTION-281)

**Assembling, persisting and regenerating are three things.** "That a form assembles, that it persists over a specified horizon and that the system regenerates it after turnover are three properties with separate probabilities" (CLAIM-566). The parts of the argument about what can be realized meet at this separation (SYNTHESIS-043). Persistence is not productivity either: "Persistence and nucleating productivity in the committor sense vary independently" (CLAIM-595; SYNTHESIS-078). Conway's Game of Life gives the three achievements of QUESTION-281's second clause in exact form: a block created and destroyed two generations later, a lone block stabilized with no role, and the blocks of Gosper's gun in a constitutive role (CASE-260; CASE-259). Two of the three achievements come apart, each way (QUESTION-281). But "Taking on a constitutive role can change which of these holds", so stabilization and role, "though they can come apart, are not independent" (CLAIM-672).

**Founding and feeding differ.** Where every replacement part is supplied from outside, an organization "can become independent of what founded it but never of what feeds it" (CLAIM-597). In the mutual-maintenance model, "a founding pulse can be withdrawn and the supply cannot", and the survival threshold "is a separatrix in the joint state and not a level of c" (CASE-265). Where repair draws on a flux at a rate proportional to the intact share, as a flame's upkeep does, there is "an ignition threshold below which it goes out with the supply still on", a supply level below which it collapses, and an outage after which restoring supply does not relight it; supplied repair "has none of the three" (CLAIM-604).

These three signatures do not appear in every model of reciprocal maintenance. The two constructions of section 1.5 are, in their own words, "a bistable system pushed across a separatrix", in which "Which terms are internal is stipulated, the substrates are assumed available" (CASE-189). They have no supply variable, so of the three signatures they show only the threshold; collapse appears in the damage parameter, not in a supply; and an outage of supply is undefined. With a supply added to the production terms of the second construction, all three appear: an interior saddle with the supply on, a supply fold below which the connector goes out rather than declines (s* = 0.649 at d = 0 when the supply also feeds the connector), and an outage of about 1.5 time units after which restoring the supply does not relight it. Supplied repair matched to the same kept state shows none of them, and where the supply enters only the capacities' production its fold is the damage fold relabelled (CLAIM-tmpv6tw7). This last result is a demonstrated computation that only its author has so far checked.

Even where all three signatures are found, they do not certify that upkeep is self-sustaining. An outside crew delivering repair at the same state-dependent rate gives the same equation. The inference from the signatures to self-maintenance is therefore an instance of section 1.6's result and needs a declared boundary (CLAIM-522; SYNTHESIS-048).

**Dependence comes in kinds.** A self-sustained and a damped oscillator "give the same drive-locked output"; an inhibitory control holds the self-sustained one at rest while it still answers the drive; withdrawing the drive alone lets the output die, and withdrawing drive and control "restores the limit cycle", "so dependence for timing, dependence for performance and suppression of a latent capacity are three things". "The output under drive does not tell the three apart; only the withdrawals do" (CASE-225). It is the point of sections 1.4 and 1.6 in an oscillator.

**Withdrawing a transient support is safe only under conditions.**

> Transient support can be withdrawn safely only if it started a process that keeps itself going on a flux that will stay available, was large enough to carry the supported party past any threshold that process has, outlasted the threat it stood in for, and left the work to the party throughout (CLAIM-600).

The conditions are necessary, and "meeting them all is not enough"; "persistence after withdrawal is needed to tell which, though slow reversion can mimic it" (CLAIM-600). A test that would settle it, establishing a nucleus with external support and withdrawing the support at matched intermediate states, is an open question (QUESTION-398). One kinetic model times the moment (CASE-365). Nuclei form fast only on a teacher and, once formed, make teachers. The outside teacher can be withdrawn once the joint state crosses a separatrix, which happened "with nuclei at 0.02 % to 30 % of their maintained number and a self-made share of 0.2 % to 65 %", so neither count marks the moment (CLAIM-953). Material supply is still needed. For anyone deciding when to withdraw support from an organization, the result is unwelcome: the observable quantities one would naturally watch do not say when it is safe.

**A seed works only in a window.** In a model of a seeded fold, "a transient seed leaves a maintained organization only inside the bistable window and only by carrying the state past the unstable threshold". Because the seed enters as a scalar forcing, "the model expresses the environment's receptivity but not which organization a seed nucleates" (CLAIM-tmpq1lyz). That limit matters for three conjectures that would extend the account to ideas. One is that "persisted alternative perspectives serve as conceptual nucleation sites for parallel depth pursuit" (CLAIM-1004). A second is that a meme can be "a seed around which beliefs, practices and relations organize, and not only a unit that copies itself" (CLAIM-tmp1ng5k), with "the persistence of the seed, of the organization it nucleates and of the perspective it carries" distinct (CLAIM-tmpgvsgg), so that "a widely copied meme can organize little and a rarely copied one much" (CLAIM-tmp4uj40). A third is that "the collapse of irrelevant distinctions into equivalence classes is the mechanism that underlies reification of those equivalence classes into useful new distinctions", "substantive only where the classes are closed under the system's own operations and are reused" (CLAIM-762). All three are interpretive conjectures. The one-dimensional model bounds what can be said of the second: it can say whether a seed takes, not what it nucleates. Whether memetic nucleation can be told from threshold contagion is a planned study (LEAD-tmpiktom), and Article 7 takes it up.

No answer to the first clause of QUESTION-281 has been given for a form whose individuation is settled. Whether the Life block is an individual at all has two readings, and neither has been chosen (CLAIM-871; CLAIM-870). The third conjecture above may offer a fifth route to a distinction beside the four transitions the question lists, but that is a suggestion and not a result (QUESTION-281).

### 1.9 Symbiosis: dependence without a new individual

The constructions so far are invented. Symbiosis is the one domain in which the separations of this article are documented in organisms. When formerly autonomous partners come to depend on one another, which properties each route to dependence brings about, and which make the partnership a new individual, is open:

> the documented symbioses show dependence, reproductive cohesion, the distribution of functions across genomes and host control coming apart, dependence directional, and integration undone as well as deepened (SYNTHESIS-053).

The question asks, for each route by which partners lose autonomy, which of "mutual dependence, reproductive cohesion, the distribution of essential functions across genomes, the host's regulatory control and the alignment of selection" it brings about, "and which of them, alone or together, make the partnership a new evolutionary individual" (QUESTION-277). The thesis that frames it is that "A system can become more interdependent without becoming more cooperative, more unified, more autonomous, or even more adaptive" (SYNTHESIS-053).

Six specimens bear on it: aphids and *Buchnera* (CASE-173); the mealybug's nested symbionts (CASE-171); *Hodgkinia* in cicadas, where "dependence and the number of parts grow with no new collective function, and the host pays" (CASE-177); *Paulinella* (CASE-174); the nitroplast (CASE-167); and *Monocercomonoides*, "the negative control: loss of function dissolving an integrated compartment, not deepening dependence on it" (CASE-168). One route to interdependence that is not individuality is adaptive gene loss, in which a function one partner supplies is lost by the other, which becomes dependent (Morris, Lenski and Zinser 2012; THEORY-1048; QUESTION-277). The sequence evidence has limits of its own: "Gene ancestries alone cannot tell a symbiotic partner from a source of transferred genes" (CLAIM-508), and with stem lengths it cannot time an acquisition either (SYNTHESIS-053).

These specimens rest on works that are mostly unread, so the domain is evidence that the dimensions come apart in nature, and not yet an answer to which of them make an individual. What would answer the question is a criterion tested on the worked systems, and none has been stated (SYNTHESIS-053). For this article the domain supports section 1.3: dependence is one achievement and individuality another, and here they come apart. A tentative mapping of the question's five dimensions onto TERM-088's senses and CLAIM-474's achievements places four of them, and leaves the alignment of selection, on which "a new evolutionary individual" turns, on none of TERM-088's senses (QUESTION-277). An answer would need an evolutionary sense of individuation beside the four, or aligned selection read as a further condition.

### 1.10 Persistence: product, producer and lineage

Organizations can preserve their characteristic relations through change of components. That much is right. What it does not license is the inference that persistence consists in the characteristic relations.

**Persistence does not need fixed components.** In the collaborative canvas r/place, an artwork defended for days has its pixels repeatedly replaced, its defenders come and go, and its attackers keep working, "yet the image stays recognizable" (CLAIM-280).

> A pattern can persist while every constituent is replaced and some constituents work against it, as a high-turnover regime of damage and repair, so the persistence of a product and the persistence of the organization producing it are separate facts (CLAIM-280).

The claim does not say "that the pattern, or the coalition, is a self" (CLAIM-280).

**What persists through turnover is carried by transmission, not resemblance.**

> A collective that replaces all its members persists, when it does, by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by resemblance: an organization of the same kind that arises independently is not its continuation (CLAIM-329).

The formal counterpart is that "Isomorphism is not persistence, in either direction", so "which morphisms count as continuations is structure added by assumptions about the domain" (CLAIM-593). A broader thesis, still a proposal, carries this to individuals generally: identity over time is "carried by the causal transmission of the relations that keep its repertoire realizable, not by its constituents or by any structure invariant across its realizations", so that "two structurally identical copies can be two individuals" (CLAIM-615; SYNTHESIS-046). What must stay continuous, and which of several successors continues the original, is open (QUESTION-118).

It follows that "characteristic relationships" cannot carry persistence by being characteristic. A character, in the precise sense of section 1.11, classifies "kinds, not tokens or positions" (CLAIM-132), and two unrelated organizations can share one (CLAIM-417). If a character were what persisted, persistence would be resemblance, which CLAIM-329 excludes. What persists is the lineage, a causal chain of realizations (CLAIM-329; CLAIM-615; CLAIM-417). This is the criterion of identity over time the article asserts, and it is one criterion, not a plurality.

Two further integrations are tempting and are not made here. One is that a collective's product, carrier and perspective each persist by a criterion of their own; no case yet separates a product from a perspective. The other is that the lasting of a block of nearly decomposable dynamics (CLAIM-157) is what persistence by transmission measures; that would need a criterion of what makes a later block "that block", which nothing supplies.

### 1.11 Character, realization and resource-bounded profiles

#### 1.11.1 Five objects kept apart

The earlier vocabulary of organizational "selves" is better replaced by "organizational perspectives" (CLAIM-417), a proposal whose value lies in what it keeps apart:

1. "A perspective as realized", in one carrier or distributed across several (TERM-072);
2. "Its character, a pattern by which realizations are recognized as of one kind. Two unrelated traditions can share one";
3. "Its lineage, a chain of causal transmission from one realization to the next";
4. "Its representations: self-descriptions, narratives, reputations, which can misdescribe the other three" (CLAIM-417).

"Resemblance ≠ Historical descent ≠ Numerical organizational identity" (CLAIM-417). Character and lineage are read as relations among realizations (TERM-072). A fifth object belongs beside them, a dispositional basis: "The cessation of an organizational perspective's enactment need not imply the loss of the dispositions, capacities or reproductive relationships from which it can be reinstated" (CLAIM-421).

The proposal has a live competitor: habitus and transposable schemas may explain the same transitions, and no model yet out-predicts them at matched complexity (CLAIM-417). Habitus is a theory of a dispositional basis, so the competitor is pitched at the fifth object, and it is the rival the planned provenance study names (LEAD-tmpu1jj5). Until a model out-predicts it, the vocabulary of perspectives is a proposal, not a result.

#### 1.11.2 What character theory gives, and on what condition

The proposal that characters are more essential than the representations built from them is held as classificatory priority:

> A communicative kind is identified by an invariant that does not depend on any one realization, as a group character identifies a representation up to isomorphism, once one symmetry group is identified across the realizations by its action on the situation and not by labels of a medium, so that invariant has classificatory priority over any particular realization (CLAIM-137).

The mathematics runs the other way from the informal statement: a representation decomposes into irreducible representations, and its character is computed from it. But the priority survives the correction (CLAIM-137). It is priority in classification, not constitution, and "No such invariant has yet been built for any communicative case" (CLAIM-137).

Three limits bound the analogy. Characters classify kinds up to isomorphism, and only in a narrow setting: finite-dimensional representations of a finite or compact group "only over a field of characteristic zero". They "forget the isomorphisms, fix neither the group nor a permutation action, and classify kinds, not tokens or positions" (CLAIM-132). Identifiability comes before automorphisms and characters (SYNTHESIS-077). Second, "the same character" depends on a chosen correspondence: comparing characters across realizations "needs one group identified on both, which is fixed only up to automorphism, and automorphisms permute the irreducible characters, so 'the same character' is relative to a chosen correspondence" (CLAIM-249). Third, the word is reserved: "character" is used literally only "when χ is a trace character of a specified representation", and otherwise the object is a "character-like structural signature" (TERM-041). This article follows that rule.

The step from groups to communicative kinds is made on a condition that is still unmet (CLAIM-137), and the step from communicative kinds to perspectives is "new territory, with CLAIM-249 standing against any easy identification" (CLAIM-417). The first thing any application needs is a declared set of transformations that count as the same in both realizations: "Say which changes of situation count as the same transformation in both populations, the correspondence CLAIM-249 says comparison needs, before comparing profiles" (LEAD-tmpqucxc).

#### 1.11.3 What a character can see

Where the condition is met, a demonstrated result limits what a character can be used for:

> The character of a perspective's representation detects the transformations it is blind to, not the states it merges: its kernel is the observational invariance subgroup when the projection is equivariant and its image spans, but a projection can merge states no transformation relates while its character shows no blindness at all (CLAIM-tmpqbaic).

The result concerns one representation, the permutation representation of a group on a perspective's observations, and is "Not defeated by another choice of representation" (CLAIM-tmpqbaic). Where a perspective's representation is of that kind, its character profiles its invariances under the chosen transformations. It does not record every distinction the perspective fails to draw.

The limit bears on a conjecture that memes are "characteristic values of perspectives: each records how a perspective responds to one kind of transformation of a situation, so a family of memes samples a perspective's character without being it" (CLAIM-tmp4mk0t), and on its companion, that "what a meme carries between carriers is a realization-independent invariant by which a perspective is recognized, not a particular content" (CLAIM-tmphi3mx). Both owe a group before they can be tested (CLAIM-249). If a family of memes is read as a group character, it inherits the limit: "If it means a group character, CLAIM-tmpqbaic shows the character sees only the transformations a perspective is blind to, not the states it merges" (CLAIM-tmpjlxfy). What remains open is which representation a meme belongs to. A looser thesis, that "memetic fidelity can be the preservation of characteristic relations rather than the copying of content" (CLAIM-tmpjitli), and the conjecture that "persistent reproduction of a meme can nucleate related but distinct perspectives (e.g. 'death of the author' type phenomena)" (CLAIM-tmp590sw), go to Article 7.

Contracts belong here for the same reason. "An organization's characteristic structure can include the contracts maintained among its processes, which its ordinary behaviour does not reveal" (CLAIM-tmpersgx; CASE-tmptpna1). "Characteristic structure" in that claim is meant in the plain sense, nearest a governance-response profile (TERM-067), not the reserved sense of TERM-041. The claim is one more instance of section 1.4: matched ordinary behaviour, parted only by intervention. Article 3 develops it.

#### 1.11.4 Resource-bounded profiles

A second formal tool is a profile of a system under a budget of probes. "Distinctions drawn by restricted Yoneda profiles only grow as nested probe families grow", so "an equivalence a richer regime breaks was relative to its regime, not false", and "the monotonicity needs an every-probe comparison, which an averaged distortion is not" (CLAIM-574). "Which category is chosen changes which configurations count as the same": in one configuration space every two configurations are uniquely isomorphic in the groupoid, while in the category enriched in peak footprint two folded states are 1.866 apart, "so equivalence of possibilities is not equivalence of resource-bounded capabilities" (CLAIM-590). And observing and acting budgets run in opposite directions, "so a system's budgeted individuation runs in opposite directions from outside and from inside and is indexed by a pair of budgets, not one" (CLAIM-557).

So profiles index the descriptive sense of individuation by a budget. They do not settle persistence: "neither isomorphism nor a profile settles persistence" (SYNTHESIS-040). Whether a profile can tell two organizations apart that are matched on their ordinary trajectories, and at which budget, has not been tried (LEAD-tmp81wwz). The article reports the profiles' limit, not their use.

### 1.12 Individuation is not agency

Individuation, in any of the four senses, is compatible with the failure of every further achievement a governance argument might want from it. Each separation is held on its own.

- **Not autonomy.** "Persistence without autonomy. A pattern can be maintained entirely by external supply" (CLAIM-474). The convection roll of section 1.2 is individuated without autonomy (TERM-088).
- **Not agency.** "Possessing, enacting or being shaped by an organizational perspective does not establish an independent agent, and perspectival, agential and institutional boundaries need not coincide" (CLAIM-418). "Recognizing an organizational perspective does not license attributing desires, intentions, authority, ownership, or experience to that perspective without independent justification" (CLAIM-417).
- **Not reflexive governance, and not governing well.** Reflexive governance is "a capacity some organizations of perspective have and others lack, not what makes something a perspective; and the capacity to revise those standards is not the capacity to revise them well" (CLAIM-424). It is not autonomy in TERM-088's sense, which is a relation among constraints.
- **Not experience.** A phenomenal perspective, "A lived point of view", is a separate sense from the organizational one (TERM-072), and the subject sense of "self" "stays a separate question" (CLAIM-417).
- **Not standing or legitimacy.** "Viability does not establish what ought to persist": "An unjust institution may be highly viable" (CLAIM-075). For moral standing, "The grounds and extent of standing remain questions for ethical argument, not conclusions of the definition" (TERM-083).

Later articles in the series must establish each of these that they need on its own evidence. Article 4 takes up will and authority.

### 1.13 Discussion: what the argument does not establish, and the strongest objection

**What not to infer.** The separations of this article are also limits, and each is a limit within its own model.

| Result | Entries | What not to infer |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocal production fails after joint loss and after long producer outages, where outside reset survives | CLAIM-456; CASE-161 | That endogenous maintenance guarantees regeneration, or robustness |
| Probes identify the mechanism only within a known catalogue | CASE-163 | That an unknown organization is identifiable through a few probes |
| Closure on a support, global lumpability and sufficiency for interventions give different verdicts | CASE-164; CLAIM-407; CLAIM-409 | That one closure measure establishes organizational identity |
| No intervention on state finds an ideal outside controller | CLAIM-522 | That recovery from perturbation shows self-maintenance |
| No knockout after formation separates self-formed from installed-then-self-maintained | CASE-191; TERM-100 | That a present intervention reads formation history |
| A habit of matched memory reproduces "the responses to belief correction, authority reset and restored evidence"; "Only interventions on the evaluation tell the two apart" | CASE-346; CLAIM-924 | That persistent activity under intervention identifies an organization's maintenance |
| A coupling criterion passes an imposed boundary | CLAIM-194 | That coordination or coupling establishes individuation |
| Dependence and the number of parts "grow with no new collective function, and the host pays"; interdependence can arise by adaptive gene loss | CASE-177; QUESTION-277; SYNTHESIS-053 | That interdependence establishes individuality |

Read together, these say that behaviour does not identify organization and that coordination does not establish individuation, each in its stated model. The table adds nothing to its rows.

**What the argument does not establish.** It does not show that any real organization differs in maintenance or formation provenance from a behaviourally matched one. It does not show that any real system realizes the ideal controller of arm (c). It does not show that a character-like signature, or a resource-bounded profile, of any perspective or organization exists. It does not show that the design of section 1.7.3 works beyond scalar capacities and governing rules in a stipulated population. And it does not establish that organizational identity is a plurality of achievements rather than one relation: what it holds are several senses of individuation that are not stages (TERM-088), a partial order of achievements (CLAIM-474), verdicts relative to a criterion (CLAIM-405) and sameness relative to a category (CLAIM-590), together with one criterion of identity over time, causal transmission (CLAIM-329; CLAIM-615), kept apart from resemblance and descent (CLAIM-417).

The firmness of the steps is uneven, and it is uneven in a pattern. The formal results (CLAIM-516, CLAIM-407, CLAIM-409, CLAIM-522, CLAIM-597, CLAIM-604, CLAIM-132, CLAIM-249, CLAIM-tmpqbaic) are demonstrated within their hypotheses. The executed studies are simulations whose laws the modeller wrote, and several of them have not yet been checked by anyone other than their author; the run that anchors section 1.4 has had its stored results re-read but not regenerated. The interpretive claims that organize the results (TERM-088's senses, CLAIM-474's partial order, CLAIM-329's criterion) are argued rather than shown. And the extensions to perspectives, memes and contracts rest on conjectures.

**The strongest objection.** The objection that goes deepest is that the central distinction is stipulated. Every separation of maintenance provenance in this article is relative to a declared boundary (CLAIM-522; CLAIM-530), and in every executed study the boundary is the modeller's (CASE-161). If the experiments presuppose the boundary and do not find it, then "internal maintenance" looks like a label one attaches to whichever processes one has decided to count as the organization's own, and the claim that two behaviourally matched organizations differ in maintenance looks like a claim about two choices of label.

The reply has two parts, and only the first is complete. First, relativity to a boundary is not arbitrariness. Once the boundary is fixed, the verdict about whose processes maintain the organization can be false, and the interventions of section 1.7.3 can show it false (CLAIM-405; CLAIM-522). The same holds of every individuation criterion (CLAIM-152): a theory of organizational identity that required boundaries to be discovered rather than declared would demand more than any account of constitution offers (CLAIM-530). Governance has its own sources of boundaries, legal, contractual and constitutional, and the argument shows what follows once one of them is adopted.

Second, the objection has an empirical form that the argument has not answered. A dispositional theory, of habitus and transposable schemas, may explain the same transitions without the vocabulary of maintenance provenance, and no model yet out-predicts it at matched complexity (CLAIM-417). An executed study shows how strong the rival is: a habit of matched memory reproduces the responses to every intervention but one on the evaluation (CASE-346; CLAIM-924). If the dispositional rival predicts everything the provenance account predicts, the distinction between installed and self-formed organization does no explanatory work outside the models. The planned study is designed to decide this, and it has stated what each side predicts before running (LEAD-tmpu1jj5). Until it is run, the objection stands.

### 1.14 Conclusion

The criteria by which organizations are ordinarily told apart, behaviour, boundary, coherence and persistence, do not pick out the same thing, and none of them picks out maintenance. In the models examined here, arrangements identical in ordinary operation differ in which losses they repair and which interruptions they survive; the internally maintained arrangement can be the more fragile; coupling cannot say who made it; informational closure can discard exactly the responses an intervention would reveal; and an ideal outside controller is invisible to every perturbation of state. Maintenance is found only by acting on the maintaining mechanism, inside a boundary the inquirer declares. Formation is a further fact, about history, which in the one construction available only an intervention during formation reads. What persists through change of every part is a lineage, and a character two organizations share is a kind.

For governance the lesson is a negative one with a positive edge. An arrangement that observes outputs, coordination or survival cannot tell whether the organization it governs keeps itself, is kept, or was made by someone else and now keeps itself. It can find out only by declaring what it counts as the organization and then intervening on what it suspects does the maintaining. The first step beyond these models is to run the provenance design on something richer than a scalar capacity, against a dispositional rival, and that has not been done.
