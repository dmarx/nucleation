# THEORY-791: The colour-naming systems of the world's languages lie near the information-bottleneck bound for compressing perceptual meanings into words, and differences in one trade-off parameter place each language along it, as Zaslavsky, Kemp, Regier and Tishby propose

## Source

[LIT-810](../literature.d/LIT-810.md), [LIT-338](../literature.d/LIT-338.md). Filed by the promotion audit's narrowing-loss sweep
(batch 066, [ADR-047](../decisions.d/ADR-047.md) v4) on 2026-10-20, to carry the wider claim that the
narrowing of [THEORY-163](THEORY-163.md) dropped. [THEORY-163](THEORY-163.md) is Active and is this
THEORY's evidence; this one stays Proposed.

## What was actually shown

Treating a colour lexicon as an encoder from Gaussian perceptual
meanings to words, the authors fit each World Color Survey language and
English to the information-bottleneck curve: all lie near it, at β just
above 1, nearer than hue-rotated variants of themselves (93% beat all 39),
and soft, inconsistent naming is what IB optima look like at finite β.
[THEORY-163](THEORY-163.md) states this, with the caveat that the need distribution that
makes the fit best was estimated from the naming data cross-validated
over languages. The authors write of colour naming across languages in
general, and of its evolution as a path along the bound.

## What would settle it

The promote_when is inherited from [THEORY-163](THEORY-163.md)'s before it was
narrowed, and [QUESTION-214](../questions.d/QUESTION-214.md) asks the same question.

## What this does not say

It does not carry the authors' further reading that languages are
driven to the bound by a pressure for efficient compression: the paper
gives no process that would take a language there, and its "evolution"
is a path through optima, not a dynamics ([NOTE-631](../notes.d/NOTE-631.md)). That reading would
be a separate thesis. Rotations are one family of alternatives only.
