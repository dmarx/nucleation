# LIT-2706: The New Presidential Adjudication

Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar (2026). The New Presidential Adjudication. *The Yale Law Journal Forum* 135: 597–653 (Essay, 9 February 2026). https://yalelawjournal.org/collections/the-new-presidential-adjudication.

## Key takeaways

- Presidential adjudication: fact-specific presidential decisions about
  particular parties, with limited judicial review.
- It has grown through doctrine, eroded norms, weakened oversight and lost
  agency capacity.
- Risk: executive coercion. Response: a "harder look" from existing doctrine,
  plus civic friction.

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read from the publisher's open PDF, body in full and footnotes skimmed
([NOTE-2029](../notes.d/NOTE-2029.md)). The exchange's account of the essay (A25) is accurate:
particularized, consequential presidential decisions with constrained
independent scrutiny, made possible by stronger control of agencies,
weakened norms and diminished oversight. The essay's own subject is
arbitrariness and coercion, and its remedy is judicial review. It says little
about speed: decisions "often must be made quickly," (p. 643), seriatim
attacks cost "those targeted in the meantime." (p. 647), and civic actors can
"slow down White House edicts" (p. 648). The "temporal dimension" is A's
addition, and A marks it as such. The registration line "on the speed and
reach of presidential decisions" overstated speed.

Standing in the record: corroborates [CLAIM-462](../claims.d/CLAIM-462.md) ([THEORY-1923](../theory.d/THEORY-1923.md)). For [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md),
it gives the timing condition (harm before remedy), not the mechanism. A
specimen reading of [CASE-278](../cases.d/CASE-278.md): the "harder look" needs "final agency
action" (p. 645), the threshold the government disputed at Hains Point.
[CASE-276](../cases.d/CASE-276.md) is not discussed.
