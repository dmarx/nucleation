# SYNTHESIS-tmp5p7lm: An argument's nucleation threshold is whatever makes a structure persist, relative to identity criteria the record stipulates by decision; addressability, not storage, makes a kept structure something later structure is built on; and being built on is not being productive, which is judged against a target, comparator and horizon the record does not declare for an argument

*A part account of the nucleating-argument line (ADR-tmpyihxy, version 2); the line's synthesis is built over it and its two sibling parts.*

## The account

- **The threshold.** CLAIM-185 (the owner's reframing, interpretive,
  Proposed): an argument's nucleation threshold is whatever makes a
  structure persist, because later structure grows only on anchors.
- **The triggers.** Recurrence is one (CLAIM-176, the assistant's reading of
  ADR-032's rule, interpretive, Proposed); CLAIM-185's body lists others.
- **What persisting means.** CLAIM-195 (the record's, interpretive, Active):
  what counts as the same claim persisting is stipulated by an amendable
  decision.
- **What makes an anchor.** CLAIM-182 (the assistant's, empirical,
  Proposed): storage is not anchoring, addressability is.
- **How fast.** CLAIM-186 (the assistant's, interpretive, Proposed): drift
  tracks how fast material arrives against how fast it is anchored.
  CLAIM-176's note of 2026-10-11 joins it to the trigger.
- **Persistence is not productivity.** CLAIM-595 (A's, with the assistant's
  proof; demonstrated, Active): persistence and productivity vary
  independently in the committor sense. TERM-117 (A's, Active) defines a
  productive nucleus against a declared target, horizon and comparator.
  CLAIM-185's note of 2026-11-06 sets the two apart: "a persisting trap is
  still built on". QUESTION-038 (Open) keeps addressability as the standing
  candidate for the question as asked, and its note of 2026-11-07 says why
  the two are not joined.
- **Open questions.** QUESTION-064 (Open): does an anchor also need
  endorsement? QUESTION-397 (A's, Open): does a partial organization help by
  changing transition dynamics or only by a head start? QUESTION-201 (Open)
  holds Hopfield's capacity at large N.
- **The measures.** CASE-183 (Active): inbound references per code were
  counted on 2026-11-06, and almost every code is cited; turns per filed
  entry are uncounted. ARG-028 (Proposed): measures counted in the record's
  grammar cannot by themselves certify that drift fell.

## Admission

Unadmitted: each link above is in the entry cited for it. Three candidates
were tested.

- **Built-on and productive anchors joined:** a free parameter, the target,
  comparator and horizon QUESTION-038 names. Filling them with CLAIM-182's own
  comparison gives back CLAIM-182's defeater; filling them with correctness
  gives back CLAIM-075, which CLAIM-185's note already holds.
- **One criterion, persists, addressable and reused:** in the built-on sense
  the reuse clause is already CLAIM-182's; in the productive sense
  CLAIM-595's trap defeats it as a sufficient condition.
- **CASE-183's count as silent on productivity:** trivial, one step from "a
  persisting trap is still built on".

## Not stated on the record

- An argument's target, comparator and horizon, without which TERM-117
  cannot be applied to an argument.
- Whether CLAIM-186's damping by anchoring trades timeliness, as CLAIM-595
  shows for slowed exits.
- Which entries QUESTION-397 bears on in this line.

## What it does not say

- That persistence is the threshold: CLAIM-185 is Proposed.
- That any anchor of the record is productive, or that the counts show
  anchoring (ARG-028).
- That the crystal or committor models are evidence about arguments.
- Anything with standing of its own.
