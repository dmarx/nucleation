# CLAIM-tmp3lv11: The only design for the October thesis builds its outcome in: the act is fixed by whoever writes the items, and the topic-only baseline is built to fail on the critical cells

## The objection

[CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md) is the design A218 "names as the first experiment to run". It
crosses topic (spending, relationships) with act (teasing, reprimand), and
[CLAIM-129](CLAIM-129.md) is defeated if "On held-out, correlation-breaking items of a design
that crosses topic with act ([CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md)), a topic-only factorization of lexical
co-occurrence recovers the act distinction as well as factorizations fitted to
interpretive-judgement or uptake data do."

**The act is assigned.** [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md) "makes the act a manipulated factor rather
than an outcome", and "every cell is an example label, not a stimulus". The
items do not yet exist, and whoever writes them decides which are teasing and
which are reprimand. A factorization of judges' responses that recovers the
act then recovers the writer's intent as the judges read it. That is close to
a manipulation check: it shows the items were written as intended, not that
the act is a structure the judgements reveal and the words hide.

**The baseline is built to fail.** The critical items are held out and
"break the familiar correlation": "test whether the model correctly
distinguishes a joking use of admonitory vocabulary from a serious
reprimand." A joking item written with admonitory words has, by design, the
vocabulary of a reprimand. A topic-only factorization of lexical
co-occurrence sees only the vocabulary, so on these items it must assign the
act the words suggest, which is the wrong one. [CLAIM-129](CLAIM-129.md)'s defeat condition
can then scarcely be met, and a pass shows that the items were built as
intended.

**The baseline is weak.** It is "topic-only NMF". [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s own defeat
condition names "topic-factor, embedding or surface similarity", and A218's
Chapter 15, as [CLAIM-078](CLAIM-078.md) quotes it, asks for prediction "beyond standard
lexical, embedding, and factorization baselines". A supervised classifier of
the text, or a language model, reads wording in context rather than
co-occurrence counts, and is the rival a critic would field on "a joking use
of admonitory vocabulary".

**What it reaches.** [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md) illustrates [CLAIM-129](CLAIM-129.md) and [CLAIM-135](CLAIM-135.md), and
[CLAIM-135](CLAIM-135.md) is a premise of [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s epistemic clause. So the only planned
evidence for that clause is a design whose outcome is fixed by its
construction.

[CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md)'s "What it cannot show" names two limits, that it has not been run
and that its interventions manipulate a described speaker ([CLAIM-127](CLAIM-127.md),
[CLAIM-130](CLAIM-130.md)). It does not name these.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the design is useless. As a first pilot it shows whether
  judges and models can be made to separate topic from act at all.
- It does not say [CLAIM-129](CLAIM-129.md) is false. A topic is plausibly not an act. It
  says this design cannot be the evidence for it.

## What would answer it

- Acts fixed independently of the item writers: naturally occurring items
  whose act is labelled by raters who did not write them, or by observed
  uptake.
- Critical items whose vocabulary is not chosen to mislead the baseline, or
  a report of how the baseline does on items where the lexical cue is
  neutral.
- A baseline at least as strong as a supervised text classifier or a
  language model, alongside topic-only NMF.
