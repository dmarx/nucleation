# NOTE-400: Haidt & Joseph 2004, intuitive ethics

## Contribution

- A proposal that moral intuitions, not moral reasoning, are where evolved
  psychology meets culture: "the golden spike" between the two (p. 57).
- A list of four candidate moral modules, each with an adaptive challenge,
  a proper domain, an actual domain, characteristic emotions and the
  virtues built on it (Table 1, p. 59).
- A link from those modules to virtue theory: virtues are culturally
  constructed, but built on and constrained by the prepared intuitions
  (pp. 61–63).
- An account of four ways cultures vary on a shared base (pp. 63–64), and
  advice for moral education and for political disagreement (pp. 65–66).

## Key insight

Morality is innate as a small set of prepared intuitions, and socially
constructed as sets of interlocking virtues. Evolution supplies a few
"social receptors", like the tongue's five taste receptors, that make some
patterns easy to moralize. Culture decides what else triggers them, which
ones it builds on, and what virtues it assembles from them.

## Assumptions

- **Two systems.** Most moral judgment is intuitive and fast; reasoning is
  slow and mostly comes after (p. 57, citing Haidt 2001, [LIT-511](../literature.d/LIT-511.md)).
- **Preparedness, not hard-wiring.** Innate here means a readiness to learn
  some associations quickly, as with fear of snakes (p. 58).
- **Modularity in a weak sense.** A module is a fast input–output routine
  for a recurrent problem; the essay follows Sperber and Hirschfeld's
  partial modularity (fn. 10).
- **Method.** "Meta-empirical": the authors listed the social situations
  five works said people (or chimpanzees) evaluate, and counted how many of
  the five mentioned each (p. 58). Two works describe universals (Brown
  1991; Fiske 1991), two cultural variation (Schwartz & Bilsky 1990;
  Shweder et al. 1997), and one primate precursors (de Waal 1996).

## Key results

There are no empirical results. The tally and the table are the content.

- **The tally (p. 58).** Suffering/compassion, reciprocity/fairness and
  hierarchy/respect appeared in all five works.
- **Purity (pp. 60–61).** Added on the strength of "our research and that
  of others". The module evolved against microbes and parasites; its
  proper domain is rotting corpses, excrement and scavengers; its emotion
  is disgust. Cultures elaborate it into rules on diet, hygiene and sex.
- **Table 1 (p. 59).**

| | Suffering | Hierarchy | Reciprocity | Purity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| proper domain | suffering and vulnerability of one's children | physical size and strength, domination, and protection | cheating vs. cooperation in joint ventures, food sharing | people with diseases or parasites, waste products |
| actual domain | baby seals, cartoon characters | bosses, gods | marital fidelity, broken vending machines | taboo ideas (communism, racism) |
| emotions | compassion | resentment vs. respect/awe | anger/guilt vs. gratitude | disgust |
| virtues | kindness, compassion | obedience, deference, loyalty | fairness, justice, trustworthiness | cleanliness, purity, chastity |

- **Evolutionary stories (pp. 58–59).** Suffering from maternal care of
  dependent young; hierarchy from making the most of rank in primate groups
  (de Waal); reciprocity from reaping the gains of cooperating with non-kin
  (Trivers 1971, fn. 9).
- **Ingroup (fn. 15).** "The best candidate for a fifth": a module whose
  proper domain is the co-residing kin group, extended now to ethnic
  groups, teams and hobbyist gatherings.
- **Cultural variation (pp. 63–64).** Cultures widen actual domains (racism
  taught through the purity and reciprocity modules); weight the modules
  differently (American Muslims and conservatives value kindness, respect
  for authority, fairness and spiritual purity, while liberals lean on
  suffering and reciprocity); give a virtue different intuitive bases
  (loyalty in reciprocity or in hierarchy; honor as integrity, chivalry or
  chastity); and form unique virtue complexes (the Arab *hamasa*).

## Concepts

- **moral intuition** — "feelings of approval or disapproval pop into
  awareness as we see or hear about something someone did" (p. 56).
- **module** — a processing system designed for problems that recurred in
  the ancestral environment, giving fast automatic responses to specific
  triggers (pp. 59–60).
- **proper domain / actual domain** — the stimuli a module evolved to
  handle, and everything that now happens to trigger it (Sperber's terms,
  p. 60). Later MFT texts call them original and current triggers.
- **virtue** — a disciplined, socially tuned sensitivity of perception,
  emotion and response; "virtues are social skills" (p. 61).
- **virtue complex** — a culture's characteristic cluster of virtues, not
  logically bound together (p. 64).

## Connections

- **Morality-as-cooperation.** [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md), read in [NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md), argues (pp.
  49–50 of that paper) that MFT omits kin altruism, reciprocal altruism,
  bravery and property, and that purity has "no explicated connection to
  cooperation". Against this text:
  - *Kin.* The suffering module's proper domain is one's own child, and its
    story is maternal care. The kin-selection grounding is there; what is
    missing is a separate duty-to-family module, and the actual domain is
    widened to any suffering.
  - *Reciprocity.* The module is named reciprocity and grounded in Trivers.
    [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s footnote 1 retreats to the narrower charge that MFT conflates
    reciprocity with fairness, which fits this table: "fairness, justice,
    trustworthiness" are the reciprocity module's virtues.
  - *Bravery and property.* Neither is a module here. Hierarchy covers
    domination and deference together, where morality-as-cooperation
    separates hawkish from dovish displays.
  - *Purity.* [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) is right that purity is grounded here in disease
    avoidance, not cooperation. It is wrong that the essay calls purity odd
    because it is not about how we treat others: the essay calls it odd to
    Westerners who think morality is only about that (p. 60).
- **Moral disciplining.** [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md) (read in [NOTE-132](NOTE-132.md)) proposes that the
  puritanical part of this purity module, temperance, chastity and piety, is
  cooperative morality fed by beliefs about self-control. It excludes the
  contamination part, which is exactly this essay's proper domain.
- **Relationship regulation.** Rai and Fiske ([LIT-507](../literature.d/LIT-507.md)) take Fiske's
  relational models, one of the five works surveyed here, and fold purity
  and ingroup into a single motive, Unity.
- **The later theory.** Graham et al. 2013 ([LIT-499](../literature.d/LIT-499.md)) restates the
  theory with five foundations (ingroup promoted from fn. 15, suffering
  renamed Care) and criteria for adding more.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md)'s claim C7 (MAC is broader and more precise than MFT).** This
  essay supports half of it. The derivation here is a count over five
  books, which is less principled than a derivation from game theory, so
  "more precise" fits. "Broader" does not fit as stated: the essay grounds
  its modules in kin care and in Trivers' reciprocity, and it moralizes
  purity, which the seven cooperative domains leave out.
- No THEORY document cites it. No ML instruction; nothing here belongs in
  the anthology.

## Limitations

- No data. The tally that selects three of the four modules counts mentions
  in secondary works, and the fourth is added from unreported research.
- "Module" is used loosely. The essay does not say what would show that
  two of its patterns are one module, or that one is two.
- The evolutionary stories are sketches. Hierarchy and reciprocity are
  primate-general; nothing explains why only humans moralize them through
  third parties, a point the essay itself raises (p. 58).
- The political claims (C6) and the parenting advice (C7) rest on no
  evidence given here.

## Open questions

- What would decide between purity as a pathogen module (this essay),
  purity as a cooperative concern about self-control ([LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md)), and purity
  as a Unity motive in communal relationships ([LIT-507](../literature.d/LIT-507.md))? A study that
  separates contamination purity from puritanical purity, with
  relationship context varied, would discriminate.
- Is the reciprocity module one thing or two? The MFQ-2 later split
  fairness into Equality and Proportionality ([LIT-500](../literature.d/LIT-500.md)), and
  morality-as-cooperation splits it into reciprocity and division.
