# QUESTION-130: Does transport of evaluative representations between contexts, with the evaluated situation held fixed, admit a connection fixed by independently established relations rather than chosen, and is its holonomy around a loop of contexts ever nontrivial?

## Why it is a question

The owner, at U19: "this might be a stretch, but given our framework already
leverages fields and flows, are we perhaps moving towards a gauge theoretic
account of something here?"

A19 put local evaluative representations v_c at each context c, transport
maps T_{c′←c}: V_c → V_{c′}, and the covariance law T′_{c′←c} = g_{c′}
T_{c′←c} g_c⁻¹ under changes of local coordinates. Around a loop A → B → C →
A the holonomy is H_A = T_{A←C} T_{C←B} T_{B←A}. Under a local change of
coordinates it goes to g_A H_A g_A⁻¹, so its conjugacy class does not depend
on representation. A19's sharper hypothesis: "**Even when the underlying
evaluated situation is held fixed, there may be nontrivial path dependence
in translating locally coherent evaluative representations across
organizational frames.**"

A19's assessment, kept: gauge freedom in relational representations
"Established"; gauge-covariant comparison "Mathematically constructible"; a
natural connection "Open"; nontrivial normative holonomy or curvature
"Speculative". A21 later demoted gauge to "a possible language of
representation and transport", with "natural transport rules" open.

## The first objection: arbitrary transport makes the curvature vacuous

A19 named it: "Where does the connection come from? ... Otherwise, we could
manufacture arbitrary curvature by choosing arbitrary transport maps."

The record already rejected a work built this way. [LIT-038](../literature.d/LIT-038.md) (Rejected; read
in [NOTE-050](../notes.d/NOTE-050.md)) puts each agent's causal model at a node of a network, a shared
abstraction on each edge, and transport by alternating restriction and
extension. [NOTE-050](../notes.d/NOTE-050.md): "The result is path-dependent and generally lossy". And
its verdict: "The formal apparatus adds little to it, because the stalks are
all measures and the maps are arbitrary affine maps." The extension maps
"are *any* affine measurable maps", and Theorem 2 "holds for any pushforward".
Path dependence produced by maps no relation fixes says nothing about what
is transported. A connection for evaluative transport has to be derived from
relations established independently of it (A19's candidates: "mappings
between shared commitments, invariant causal effects, or recognized
institutional roles"), or the holonomy is the modeller's.

## Distinctions any answer must keep

- **A change of viewpoint is not a gauge transformation.** A19: "Changing
  from the perspective of a host to the perspective of a parasite can change
  whose interests are at stake. That is ordinarily a substantive change, not
  merely a re-description of the same normative fact." Only a change that
  preserves the evaluated relations is gauge. [CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md)'s warning governs:
  such a gauge "is not a symmetry of the relational system".
- **Holonomy is not contextuality, and neither is learning.** A19:
  "contextuality, curvature, and holonomy are not synonyms". A failure to
  glue local judgments ([CLAIM-038](../claims.d/CLAIM-038.md), [THEORY-012](../theory.d/THEORY-012.md)) is a different obstruction
  from path dependence of transport. If the agent learns or changes its
  commitments along the loop, the difference is "substantive state
  evolution rather than geometric transport".
- **A frame change can be coordinates.** [CLAIM-144](../claims.d/CLAIM-144.md): observers who disagree
  about temporal order differ in coordinates, not in a failure to glue.
- **Commitments carried across contexts may glue.** [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md): interests
  that govern at disjoint times glue, and division needs an overlap. That is
  the record's nearest result about one agent's commitments across contexts,
  and it is a gluing result, not a transport one.

## What would count as an answer

A19 §8's finite construction: three overlapping evaluative contexts, each
with a local representation of the same commitments; the comparisons shared
on each overlap; transport derived from those; the loop's holonomy. The
first target is the null: "three contexts with different local coordinates
but trivial holonomy. That would prove that **perspectival plurality does
not imply nontrivial gauge curvature**." A nontrivial holonomy counts only
if its transport was not chosen to produce it, and only if it predicts a
discrepancy that is not an artefact of coordinates.

Sarkar, Mukherjee and Sato (2026, [LIT-1820](../literature.d/LIT-1820.md)) take this construction
further for relation transports in O(d): under per-entity frame freedom,
transports interact only around cycles, a loop's invariants are class
functions of its holonomy, and an equivariant update has the form
Ψ(H)Tᵢⱼ ([THEORY-1140](../theory.d/THEORY-1140.md)). That develops A19's "gauge-covariant
comparison". It does not touch this question's open part, because the
transports are supplied, and its companion paper calls the frame freedom
itself "a chosen inductive bias" ([LIT-1803](../literature.d/LIT-1803.md)). For d > 1 a connection
can be frustrated in some directions and compatible in others
([CLAIM-495](../claims.d/CLAIM-495.md)), so a nontrivial holonomy need not mean that no
commitment survives the loop.

Prior work on gauge ideas in neuroscience exists (Sengupta et al. (2016),
named by A19); A19: "These are relevant predecessors, but they do not
establish a gauge theory of normativity." The relational normative realism
this would formalize is [TERM-059](../terms.d/TERM-059.md).

## Note of 2026-10-29: a natural connection of the wrong kind

Sengupta, Tozzi, Cooray, Douglas and Friston's neuronal gauge theory,
[LIT-1215](../literature.d/LIT-1215.md), the nearest published gauge-theoretic treatment of a
self-organizing system, which A19 cited at U19, is now read
([NOTE-1350](../notes.d/NOTE-1350.md)). A19 asked where its connection comes from. It comes from
the Fisher metric: it is the Levi-Civita connection of the belief
manifold, so it is fixed and not chosen, and its holonomy is the
manifold's curvature (−1/2 for the univariate normal, checked in the
reading). But what it transports is a descent direction between tangent
spaces of one agent's belief manifold, not an evaluation between contexts
with the situation held fixed. The curvature belongs to the statistical
family, whatever is evaluated. So the paper supplies a connection that is
non-arbitrary and nontrivially curved, and not about context. It is not
an answer. The question is unchanged and stays Open.
