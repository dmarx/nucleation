# CLAIM-736: An opposition can be independent as an actor, in its leadership, aims and speech, while its capacity to change the arrangement it opposes depends on another party's control of its resources, standing, corrective authority or time; that controlled incapacity, and not incapacity or failure as such, is what makes a contained opposition a mark of domination

## The claim

A37 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b)
answers the owner's U18, "relate our discussion to controlled opposition".
Its candidate proposition (§10):

> **An organization can preserve opposition as a legible and internally autonomous process while exercising second-order control over the conditions of its efficacy. Where another actor controls the opposition's access to resources, institutional standing, corrective authority, or time available for intervention, the opposition's apparent independence need not imply independence in its capacity to change the governing arrangement.**

"Its distinctive contribution would be to separate **opposition's
independence as an actor** from **opposition's independence over the
conditions determining its effectiveness**." §11 names three autonomies,
"organizational autonomy, expressive autonomy, and corrective autonomy",
the third being "the capacity to make warranted objections effective,
including when doing so conflicts with the preferences of those presently
holding power."

**Controlled incapacity.** §10 separates "**incapacity**: opposition
cannot produce the desired change because no feasible coalition, legal
remedy, or institutional transition currently exists" from "**controlled
incapacity**: an actor with authority over the surrounding arrangement
maintains or manipulates the constraints that prevent opposition from
becoming effective." "The second is a substantially stronger claim. And
if we want to identify domination, it is often the more relevant one."
The claim makes that the criterion: in [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)'s terms, another holds the
capacity to set the terms on which the opposition can contest, and the
capacity is not answerable to it.

**A's formalization**, with O an opposition, G the arrangement and J a
discrepancy from a justified standard: an expressive opposition can have
Legibility(O, J) = 1 while J ∉ 𝒞_O(G, B, H), where 𝒞_O is the set of
corrective outcomes reachable through the opposition's actual
institutional powers under budget B and horizon H. A: "This is a proposed formalization using
Nucleation's concepts, not an established result from the repository."
Controlled incapacity adds that another party chooses what bounds 𝒞_O.

## Its parts

- **Three phenomena** (A37 §1): "**Manufactured opposition**",
  "**Co-opted opposition**" and "**Structurally contained opposition**",
  of which only the first needs deliberate orchestration. The diagnostic
  question changes from whether the opposition is sincere to "Who governs
  the conditions under which this opposition can be effective?" The claim is about the third kind and about co-optation
  by dependence.
- **Three outcomes** (A37 §4): "**Accommodation**", "**Expression without
  accommodation**" and "**Containment:** The organization of opposition
  actively reduces the likelihood that grievances will be pursued through
  more effective alternative channels." "Only the third is containment in
  the stronger causal sense", and "We shouldn't infer containment simply
  from immediate failure."
- **Evidence** (A37 §9). For deliberate control: "undisclosed funding,
  documented directives, organizational dependence, coercive leverage,
  infiltration, or coordination". For structural containment: "the
  organization's genuine powers, access to resources, intervention
  channels, dependence on incumbents, and demonstrated capacity to change
  consequential decisions." "The goal is to identify mechanisms of
  control, not infer hidden controllers from disappointing outcomes."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-686](CLAIM-686.md)** (Proposed): where a dependent's claims fail of
  legibility, volitional efficacy or correctability, it lacks effective
  means of influence. This claim adds who holds the failure. An
  opposition can fail [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)'s third achievement with no one
  responsible (incapacity) or because another keeps it failing
  (controlled incapacity).
- **[CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)** (Active). A37 §2's heading relays it, "Opposition can be legible
  without being effective", which is right, but A glosses correctability as
  a change the organization can implement, dropping the claim's condition
  that the revision be carried out within deadline and resources, and its rider that none of the three
  achievements shows the demand is justified.
- **[CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md)** (Active): an organization can be governed from outside
  through the evidence and criteria its procedures use, with no decision
  made for it. This claim is the converse arrangement: the opposition's
  decisions are its own and the conditions of their effect are not.
- **[CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md)**, **[CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md)** and **[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)** are the parents A names.
  The claim is [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)'s domination with the party an opposition and the
  terms those on which it contests.
- **[CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md)** (Proposed): conditioned and negotiating processes part
  only under intervention. A37 §7's test of an opposition's standing,
  whether it can impose constraints when the institution "*doesn't want
  to accommodate it*", is that claim's test.

## What it does not say

- Not that ineffective opposition is controlled. A37 §9: "An opposition
  movement can be ineffective without being compromised", and
  "**Institutional participation is not equivalent to institutional
  capture.**" The concept "becomes self-sealing: any opposition that fails
  is regarded as controlled".
- Not that contained opposition does no good. A37 §4: a protest that fails
  "may nonetheless build networks, reveal information, or increase future
  bargaining power."

## Note of 2026-11-17: Arnstein, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1968 — new theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Arnstein 1969, read from an OCR scan ([NOTE-2037](../notes.d/NOTE-2037.md), [THEORY-1968](../theory.d/THEORY-1968.md)): it corroborates the claim independently ([THEORY-1968](../theory.d/THEORY-1968.md) carries `corroborates`), with three instances of controlled incapacity: citizen majorities on Model Cities boards where "city councils have final veto powers even when citizens have the majority of seats on the CDA Board" (p. 222); community control that can be "a new Mickey Mouse game for the have-nots by allowing them to gain control but not allowing them" sufficient resources (p. 224); and placation's dependence on "the quality of technical assistance they have in articulating their priorities" (p. 220). Her conditions for real partnership, own funds, own technicians and ground rules "not subject to unilateral change" (p. 221), are the converse. The participants are citizens' groups, not an opposition party. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Szakonyi, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1953 LIT-2731 — new theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed; Semenov Unavailable -->

Szakonyi 2025, read ([NOTE-2056](../notes.d/NOTE-2056.md), [THEORY-1953](../theory.d/THEORY-1953.md)), which the claim lists under "Works A relied on" as A's counterexample: read, it corroborates the claim ([THEORY-1953](../theory.d/THEORY-1953.md) carries `corroborates`; the claim does not rest on it). It supports the defeat condition's warning: an opposition inside autocratic institutions produced measurable change there, ruling-party deputies earning "roughly 22% less income" on opposition-held councils (p. 2), so inclusion is not itself evidence of containment. And it documents the claim's mechanism one level up: "the opposition did not win enough seats to overcome the municipal filter" (p. 5), "phony criminal charges were filed against nine deputies from the opposition" (p. 5), and in 2022 "the regime blocked opposition candidates from running and used fraud" (p. 15). Local efficacy with the larger arrangement held by another is the claim's controlled incapacity; the author does not frame it so. His finding that systemic and nonsystemic councils did not differ significantly once governing (p. 15) bears on whether that distinction marks containment. Semenov ([LIT-2731](../literature.d/LIT-2731.md)) stays unread. No status changes.
