**What the reading corrected.** Each correction is recorded in the work's
LIT.
- The implementation-intention effect is d = .65 in 2006, uncorrected for
  publication bias, and about .15 after the 2024 review's correction.
- Edwards et al.'s associations are with falling urges, not behaviour, and
  phone coaching did not predict outcome.
- Friston et al.'s learned prior is over action policies, grows by
  frequency with no reward gate, and shows persistence, not amortization.
- Three-factor rules are established only at the hour scale of synaptic
  consolidation.
- Friedkin et al.'s discussions follow Friedkin–Johnsen, not DeGroot, and
  the predicted end state was not reached.
- Brown et al. is a narrative review of fear-based disorders.
- Mancinelli and Bach covers healthy volunteers only. It names expectancy
  theory as best supported and finds that avoidance offered and withdrawn
  can raise threat expectancy.
- Zorowitz et al. is simulation only. The misbelief is about future
  control, not danger.
- In Chou et al., less exploration fits only state or somatic anxiety and
  foraging tasks.
- De Kleine et al.'s effects hold in two of three patient groups each, and
  relief was already higher before avoidance was available.
- Goodson et al.'s bias-corrected effect is d = 0.31.
- Juhász's protection was a war blockade, not a tariff.
- Bjørnskov et al.'s cases are suspected unlawful, from news and NGO
  coding.
- The IDI summary gives no method and reads the channels as deliberate.
- Ku and Finch's publics keep routines against the anxiety, while the
  narrative is held latently.
