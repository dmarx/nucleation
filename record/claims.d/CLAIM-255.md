# CLAIM-255: Intending to perform an act, performing it and being recognized as having performed it are distinct, coupled variables: intended meaning is fixed at production, the act partly by roles, standing and norms, and recognition by uptake, so competent uptake is constitutive for some acts, evidential for others and irrelevant to the existence of still others

## The claim

The reading: "The critical distinction is between intending to perform an act, performing that act, and being recognized as having performed it." Two cases carry it. "A person might intend to issue an authoritative reprimand but lack the standing to do so. Their intention is historically fixed, yet whether they successfully reprimanded someone can depend on institutional and interpersonal relations." And "a successful communicative act need not wait for an actual reader. A formal notice may acquire legal force upon issuance, even if unread."

So: "I'd model these as distinct, potentially coupled variables rather than competing definitions of one variable." Its recommendation, recorded as advice, is to "Retain [CLAIM-160](CLAIM-160.md) for intended meaning, retain [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) for socially constituted act identity, and weaken [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md)'s implication that competent uptake directly fixes all relevant act identity. Competent uptake can be constitutive for certain acts, evidential for others, and irrelevant to the existence of still others."

It partly answers [QUESTION-073](../questions.d/QUESTION-073.md): teasing and reproach are individuated by intention as intended acts and partly by the frame and standing as performed acts.

## What it bears on

- [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md) separates kind, position and token. The reading's decomposition adds a referent [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md) has no slot for, intended meaning at production, and a fourth level, "Operational recovery".
- [CLAIM-241](CLAIM-241.md) already holds that competent uptake is "a defeasible standard of a communicative kind, not the criterion" where institutional conditions diverge. The formal-notice case is of that kind.

## What it does not say

It does not decide which variable [QUESTION-029](../questions.d/QUESTION-029.md) asks about, so it leaves open whether [CLAIM-160](CLAIM-160.md) answers that question.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

Two of its three clauses are premises the record does not hold: intended
meaning fixed at production is [CLAIM-160](CLAIM-160.md), and the act partly constituted by
roles, standing and norms is [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md), both Proposed.

## Note of 2026-10-31: a theory that merges the variables, and a critique that needs them apart

<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmpuh0eb — Proposed; a reading filed on 2026-10-31 and set beside this claim -->

Bencherki 2017 (LIT-tmp1rpyl, read in NOTE-tmp4ysch and THEORY-tmpuh0eb)
makes authorship an outcome: deciding on an author "is the outcome of
individuation, not its starting point" (p. 786). And: "a being only
possesses its components from a particular perspective ... possession is
never complete or univocal" (p. 782). That merges performing an act and
being recognized as its author into one variable, settled by which
individuations the action is allowed to count toward. Table 1 (p. 787)
crosses two such allowances: whether the action may count toward the
person's individuation, and whether it may count toward the
organization's.

The paper's critical case needs this claim's split. In the daycare
excerpt, care work done by military spouses as volunteers is credited to
the army, and the paper calls this usurpation (pp. 786–792). Usurpation is
a wrong only if the spouses performed the act and the organization's
recognition misassigned it. So the case has force only if performance is
fixed apart from recognition. THEORY-tmpuh0eb's "What this does not say"
makes the same point from the reading's side. NOTE-tmp4ysch's first open
question asks how channelling is told from usurpation "without a prior
standard of whose action it rightly was". This claim's decomposition
gives that standard a place: performance is fixed partly by roles,
standing and norms, and recognition by uptake, here the institution's.

The parent paper (LIT-tmp3mudg) removes the place altogether: "Who or what
has agency is a premature question" (p. 1357). Its responsibility section
(pp. 1366–1367) then needs a standard for which individuations ought to
continue, and NOTE-tmpfw3f7 found none supplied.

What this adds is an outside instance, from organizations rather than
conversation, of an account that merges performance and recognition and
then needs them apart to say anything critical. It is an argument from a
reading's internal tension, not evidence for the decomposition. The data
are a documentary, watched as second-hand ethnography with one side heard
(pp. 784–785).

No status, title or warrant changes.
