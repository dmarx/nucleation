# NOTE-1614: Chen and Santangelo — Branches of triangulated origami near the unfolded state

## Contribution

Rigid origami folded a little from a crease pattern can be studied by
linearizing its constraints, but not at the flat state, where every
vertical displacement of every vertex is a first-order motion. The paper
expands the length constraints of a triangulated sheet to second order,
ties the resulting quadratic constraints to the self stresses of the flat
network, and counts the branches of nearly-flat configurations that meet at
the flat state: exponentially many in the number of internal vertices.

## Key insight

At the flat state the constraints that matter are curvature constraints,
one per internal vertex, and each is a quadratic form with one negative
eigenvalue, so each vertex chooses independently to pop up or pop down.
The branches of the whole sheet are, in every case computed, labelled by
these choices, two per sign pattern. A self-folding sheet has to pick one
of 2^Vi branches from a point where they all meet.

## Assumptions

- **Ideal rigid origami** (§II): rigid triangular plates and ideal hinges.
  Self-intersection is not excluded by the equations; only neighbourhoods
  of the flat state, where none occurs, are considered.
- **Generic planar triangulations of a disk**, with no fold joining two
  boundary vertices.
- **Second order stands for all orders.** The authors treat second-order
  solutions as configurations, and say solutions to second order "may not
  be a solution at all orders" (§II). "However, we know of no such general
  guarantee". Transversality of the vertex hypersurfaces is assumed and
  checked numerically.
- **One degree of freedom.** The counts are for four boundary vertices,
  D = 1. D > 1 is not treated.

## Key results

- **Why linearization fails** (abstract): "the degeneracy of the unfolded
  state makes a linear approach impossible there". The generic count is
  "D = Ve − 3." (§II C, eq. 9), but the flat state admits D + Vi linear
  motions.
- **Self stresses give the constraints** (§II A): "each self stress of the
  unfolded configuration of the origami gives an equation (5) that
  constrains the vertical displacements hn to second order", a special case
  of Connelly and Whiteley's second-order theory.
- **Curvature** (§II B, App. B): "the wheel stress constraint is precisely
  Gaussian curvature preservation". One N-fold vertex has a quadratic form
  with one negative eigenvalue; its second-order motions form a double cone
  whose nappes are the popped-up and popped-down states.
- **The count** (§III A): the second-order system has 2^(Vi+1) Bézout roots;
  with a few exceptions "all the solutions of our system are distinct and
  isolated" and all are real, so B = 2^Vi branches. Numerical, for Vi from
  2 to 8, at up to 690 digits of precision. The exceptions contain
  triangulated triangles.
- **Sign patterns** (§III B): "there are exactly two branches with each
  such pair of sign patterns", a pair being a vertex sign pattern and its
  global reversal. And (§I) "The branches are not necessarily
  distinguished by the mountain and valley assignments of the folds".
- **A proof for a special class** (§IV): for triangulations built by
  Henneberg-1 moves, "their configuration spaces consist of 2Vi
  intersecting 1D branches" (2^Vi), to all orders. These are rare: their
  fraction falls from 1 at Vi = 3 to 0.0077 at Vi = 8.
- **Self-folding** (§V): "once an origami structure begins folding along
  the wrong branch, it is potentially very difficult to return it to the
  desired branch". Tachi and Hull's condition for a driving force, that it
  be perpendicular to every undesired branch, fails: "it is impossible to
  satisfy the above orthogonality constraint for even modest numbers of
  internal vertices!"
- **The authors' conclusion** (abstract): "a generic structure is likely
  to become trapped in a “misfolded” state", and "controlling the popping
  state of the vertices may be one possibility".

## Concepts

- **Degrees of freedom D**: the dimension of the configuration space at
  nonsingular points.
- **Branch**: one of the B cones through the flat state, a component of the
  projective second-order solution set; for D = 1, a line.
- **Wheel stress**: the self stress supported on the star of one internal
  vertex.
- **Popped up / popped down**: which side of the sheet a vertex's spherical
  trace bounds with area below 2π.
- **Vertex sign pattern**: the ±1 popping state of each internal vertex.

## Connections

It builds on Connelly and Whiteley's second-order rigidity ([LIT-2080](../literature.d/LIT-2080.md),
filed in this batch), on Kapovich and Millson for single-vertex
configuration spaces, and on Tachi and Hull for self-foldability. Hull and
Tachi (2018), [LIT-2033](../literature.d/LIT-2033.md), restate the self-foldability criterion it tests.
Berry, Lee-Trimble and Santangelo (2020), [LIT-2024](../literature.d/LIT-2024.md), extend this
second-order theory to vertices carrying curvature. On second-order
rigidity at the flat state it covers, by self stresses and quadratic forms,
ground that Hull's chapter "Rigid Foldings" ([LIT-2039](../literature.d/LIT-2039.md)) covers by
reciprocal diagrams; it is not Hull's treatment and does not stand in for it.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis's description.** A says second-order rigidity is
  needed "because linearization is insufficient near the degenerate
  unfolded configuration". That sentence is A's paraphrase: it is not in
  the paper, which says "the degeneracy of the unfolded state makes a
  linear approach impossible there". The substance is right. A states
  none of the paper's results: the 2^Vi count, the sign-pattern labelling,
  the curvature reading of the wheel stresses, and the self-folding
  consequence. The last bears most on A's "Feasible target ⇏ reachable
  target" and on branch selection.
- **A's framing.** The four-way distinction among existence, continuous
  reachability, singular passage and branch selection is A's. The paper
  studies the neighbourhood of one singular point and says "We did not
  attempt to compute the global structure of the configuration space, e.g.
  how the branches join."
- **[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md) (frustration as an ordinal fact; the compatibility
  complex).** Qualifies, by analogy. Each vertex's curvature constraint is
  a local demand, but every branch satisfies every constraint. The
  multiplicity is non-uniqueness of the solution set at a singular point,
  not a failure of joint satisfiability: this is selection among jointly
  feasible realizations, which the complex does not see.
- **[CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md) (structural infeasibility against realized violation).**
  Qualifies, by analogy. Misfolding is failure to reach a feasible target,
  caused by branch selection rather than infeasibility.
- **[CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md) (stability does not entail viability).** Loose analogy only:
  a branch can be entered and be hard to leave. Not engaged in substance.
- **[CLAIM-495](../claims.d/CLAIM-495.md) and [CLAIM-538](../claims.d/CLAIM-538.md)** (holonomy and cycle compatibility): not
  engaged. The constraints here are vertex curvature conditions, not loop
  transports.
- **[QUESTION-281](../questions.d/QUESTION-281.md)** (dynamical transitions, nucleation across a barrier):
  a different mechanism. The branching is kinematic, not a barrier crossed.
- Filed: [THEORY-1427](../theory.d/THEORY-1427.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Numerical and conjectural** beyond H1 triangulations, which are a
  vanishing fraction of all triangulations.
- **Second order is not shown to be all orders** in general, by the
  authors' own account.
- **D = 1 only**, four boundary vertices.
- **Kinematic.** Energetics enter only in the self-folding discussion
  (eqs. 11–12); that misfolding is likely is inferred from the count, not
  measured.
- **Local.** Nothing on how branches join away from the flat state.
