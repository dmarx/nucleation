# THEORY-1749: Singh (1999) models a social commitment as a four-place relation C(x, y, G, p), in which a debtor x is committed to a creditor y, in the context of a group G, to bring about a discharge condition p evaluated in the world rather than in any agent's mind, manipulated by the operations create, discharge, cancel, release, delegate and assign under social policies, which are higher-order commitments, and holds that such commitments cannot be reduced to internal commitments or mutual beliefs and that metacommitments are the norms that constitute a society

## The account

Singh (1999) models a social commitment as a four-place relation C(x, y, G, p), in which a debtor x
is committed to a creditor y, in the context of a group G, to bring about a discharge condition p
evaluated in the world rather than in any agent's mind, manipulated by the operations create,
discharge, cancel, release, delegate and assign under social policies, which are higher-order
commitments, and holds that such commitments cannot be reduced to internal commitments or mutual
beliefs and that metacommitments are the norms that constitute a society.
