# LIT-2071: Rigid Foldability is NP-Hard

Akitaya, H., Demaine, E. D., Horiyama, T., Hull, T. C., Ku, J. S. & Tachi, T. (2020). Rigid Foldability is NP-Hard. *Journal of Computational Geometry* 11(1): 93–124. arXiv:1812.01160.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1579](../notes.d/NOTE-1579.md). Section and theorem numbers are the arXiv version's.*

- **Two results** (§1): "deciding rigid foldability of a given crease
  pattern using all creases is weakly NP-hard by a reduction from
  Partition", and with optional "creases is strongly NP-hard by a reduction
  from 1-in-3 SAT".
- **The loop condition** (§2): rotations about the creases crossed by any
  simple closed vertex-avoiding curve multiply to the identity, "for all
  such curves γ (see [5])", [5] being belcastro and Hull; "A necessary
  (but not sufficient, since the paper may cross itself)" condition, and
  near flat "a necessary and sufficient condition for rigid foldability".
- **Where the hardness lies** (§1): "the complexity comes from the
  combinatorial behavior of the different possible rigid folding
  configurations at each vertex"; and "a mere intersection of such single
  vertex condition does not lead to useful condition of multi-vertex
  rigid-foldability".
- **Faces close** (Corollary 11): a mountain–valley assignment of a
  flat-foldable degree-4 mesh folds exactly when the speed coefficients
  multiply to 1 around every face.
- **Scope** (§2): the question is whether a path exists, "not whether it
  is mechanically feasible to, say, program a rigid, self-folding material
  to do so".
