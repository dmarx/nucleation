# ARG-tmpv5t3t: Van Lange and Bergmann's model of intergroup cooperation does not establish that cooperation within groups and the coexistence of groups are distinct targets that can trade off: it shows that the processes building in-group cooperation can fuel conflict between groups, and says nothing on whether groups persist, go extinct or are owed impartial concern

## The inference

[CLAIM-tmpjnqe9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjnqe9.md) separates three achievements: cooperation local to a group,
the persistence of several groups whose interests are opposed, and concern
for those outside one's group. It says none secures the next, and that the
first two can trade off. Its exact instance is Nowak's group-selection
rule. As first filed, it cited Van Lange and Bergmann ([LIT-tmpblw9a](../literature.d/LIT-tmpblw9a.md), then
unread) as the human case. The reading is [THEORY-tmpmr4g4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmr4g4.md), from
[NOTE-tmpeqzkm](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeqzkm.md).

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** In part, and this is the
  question that fails for the claim as titled.
  - **Said:** "the same processes that can establish ingroup cooperation
    can also fuel intergroup conflict" (p. 554). Under negative
    interdependence, "ingroup cooperation can require disadvantaging
    outgroup members" (p. 554). Group-based morality has "little or no
    regard for the quality of outcomes for outgroups" (p. 555).
  - **Not said:** whether groups persist or go extinct, which is the
    claim's second target. The nearest the review comes is that groups
    may "compete, cooperate or coexist independently" (p. 555). It also
    says nothing on impartial concern as a target, beyond the contrast
    between individual-based and group-based morality.
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** No. The review's
  subject is cooperation and conflict between groups. A cost to the
  out-group is not the out-group's failure to persist.
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than the reading holds?**
  It did, by citing the review as the human case of the trade-off. That
  citation is withdrawn, and the claim keeps the corroboration of its
  first link.

## What survives

- **The first link, as corroboration.** Within-group cooperation can work
  against relations between groups. That is [THEORY-tmpmr4g4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmr4g4.md) corroborating
  [CLAIM-tmpjnqe9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjnqe9.md) ([ADR-058](../decisions.d/ADR-058.md)). The claim does not rest on it, because its
  instance is Nowak's model.
- **A finding the claim had not foreseen.** The trade-off is conditional.
  Favouritism "need not imply ... any harmful behaviour towards outgroups"
  (p. 553), and harm needs conflicting interests, real or perceived (p.
  554). And the review's own success case, merger "into larger
  collectives" (p. 555), is a second way to fewer groups. Both are in the
  claim's note of 2026-11-04.
