# LIT-tmpbjnsz: Material Beings

Peter van Inwagen (1990), *Cornell University Press* — DOI-10.7591/9781501713033

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- **The Special Composition Question.** When do some things compose something?
  The book names it and sorts the possible answers: never, always, or under
  some restricted condition.
- **Its answer, organicism.** Some things compose something if and only if
  their activity constitutes a life (or there is only one of them). There are
  organisms and simples arranged tablewise, but no tables.
- It is standardly cited as the source of the question and as the main
  restricted answer.

## Standing in the record

Filed for [QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md), when a pattern of coordination constitutes an
additional agent, which is a special-composition question for agents. The
survey of the Mathematics of Meaning corpus found that corpus asking the same
question and setting the literature aside in a clause ([MOM-LIT-015](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-015.md)).

**It is a rival to the record's answer.** The record makes a combined form one
system only relative to a stated target ([CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md), [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)), while van
Inwagen gives a target-free condition, life. A reading should say whether his
"activity constitutes a life" can be stated as a condition on dynamics, and so
compared with the gap criterion, and how he answers the vagueness argument
against restricted composition (Lewis 1986, Sider 2001; not filed).
[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md) is the other question it bears on.
