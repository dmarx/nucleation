# THEORY-tmp0hhij: Cottingham reports that the third lecture of Scheffler's Death and the Afterlife holds that a life must be temporally bounded for its goods to have the status of values, so that personal immortality is no remedy, and that among the commentators Frankfurt doubts the concern for humanity's continuation is altruistic and Wolf denies that impending extinction would or should undercut our projects

## The account

As reported, the third lecture holds that "it is essential to our idea
of a life that it is temporally bounded" (p. 100), so what we cherish
has the status of values "because of their role in our finite and
bounded lives" (p. 100), and agrees with Williams that personal
immortality would not be desirable (p. 107). Death is needed for
confidence in what we value, yet that confidence depends on others being
alive after we die (p. 109), a conclusion Scheffler calls "strange and
unsettling".

## Evidence

- Frankfurt suggests the significance of the desire for humanity to
  continue may come to little more than that "natural selection has
  ensured that we have those desires" (p. 138).
- Wolf holds we could "snap out of it" (p. 128), with time and reason
  still to "create beauty, gain wisdom and help each other" (p. 127).
- Shiffrin and Kolodny are named only; the replies are mentioned, not
  summarized.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-301's note of 2026-10-29 cites this review with the lectures
("Scheffler is less hard a case than filed (NOTE-1298; NOTE-1306)"). It
adds the third lecture's pairing, our own death for our values and
others' lives after it for our confidence in them, and the two
commentators' objections from either side of the question.
