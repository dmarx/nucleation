# NOTE-1493: Baumgartner & Gebharter — fat-handedness

## Contribution

It shows that Craver's mutual manipulability criterion (MM), read with
the interventionist definitions it was built on, cannot do the job it was
built for. Under Woodward 2003, MM plus supervenience plus non-causal
constitution entails that no part is ever constitutively relevant to a
whole (pp. 10–13). Under Woodward's 2011 relaxation it entails that whole
and part are causally related (pp. 15–17). A temporal clause on causation
removes both absurdities (pp. 17–18). Then a new problem appears: every
mutual manipulation is a common cause of whole and part, so the
manipulations give no evidence of constitution (pp. 18–20). The paper
closes with a second-order, abductive criterion, fat-handedness (FH).

## Key insight

Since a whole cannot change without some part changing, nothing can act on
the whole except by also acting on a part, along a different path. Every
"top-down" manipulation is a common cause of whole and part. A common cause
of two variables tells you nothing about how they are related to each
other. So mutual manipulability, which looks like the experimental mark of
constitution, is equally well explained by "the experimenter wiggles both"
(Fig. 5b). What a constitutive model explains and the common-cause model
does not is a pattern across experiments: no cause of the whole is ever
found that leaves every part alone (pp. 20–21).

## Assumptions

- **(Sup)** "Every change in the supervening behavior is necessarily
  accompanied by a change in the supervenience base" (p. 5). The base is
  taken to be the constituents' behaviours, not their causal organization,
  which under a difference-making theory adds nothing (pp. 5–6).
- **(SC)** constituents are spatiotemporal parts of the macro level;
  **(NR)** a part that never makes a difference is not a constituent (p. 6).
- **(NC)** constitution is non-causal (p. 7). The majority view, assumed
  standardly, with the consequences of dropping it examined (pp. 13–15).
- **Stability.** The mechanism is "completely stable", with compensatory
  responses cancelled by further interventions or the setup (p. 10).
- **Variables as modelling devices.** The ontology of macro and micro
  levels is left open; Ψ and the Φᵢ are "different random variables such
  that changes in the values of Ψ supervene on changes in V1" (p. 6).
- **Parthood is not analysed.** (SC) is used as given; its problems are
  referred to Leuridan's §6 ([LIT-1929](../literature.d/LIT-1929.md)) and Franklin-Hall (p. 6).

## Key results

- **MM as stated (p. 8).** Φᵢ(Xᵢ) is constitutively relevant to Ψ(S) iff
  Xᵢ is a spatiotemporal part of S, some possible ideal intervention on Φᵢ
  changes Ψ, and some possible ideal intervention on Ψ changes Φᵢ. The
  authors read it as necessary and sufficient, against Couch (footnote 4).
- **(INEX) under Woodward 2003 (pp. 10–13).** Suppose I_Ψ is an
  intervention variable for Ψ w.r.t. Φᵢ that changes both. By (IV.i) and
  (M), I_Ψ causes Ψ; by (Sup), every change it induces in Ψ comes with a
  change in some Φⱼ, so I_Ψ causes Φⱼ too. Of the three structures in which
  one variable causes two (Fig. 3), (NC) leaves only the common cause (C).
  But (IV.iii) requires every path from I_Ψ to Φᵢ to pass through Ψ. So
  either I_Ψ is not an intervention, or it does not change Φᵢ. Condition
  (†), that each Φᵢ be changeable by a top-down intervention, is
  unsatisfiable "for any mechanism" (p. 13). Hence: "There do not exist
  any relationships of constitutive relevance between macro phenomena and
  their spatiotemporal parts." Checked: the argument uses only (Sup), (NC),
  (M) and (IV), and fails if (NC) is dropped, as the authors say.
- **Dropping (NC) (pp. 13–15).** Ψ and Φᵢ can then sit on one path, (†)
  becomes satisfiable, and MM yields "constitutive causation": bidirectional,
  instantaneous, whole-to-part causal loops (Fig. 4). This is Leuridan's
  conclusion (his p. 424), which the authors grant follows but reject as
  unlike any causal structure.
- **Under Woodward 2011, interventionism* (pp. 15–17).** (IV*) lets a path
  from I to Y bypass X if it passes through X's supervenience base; (M*)
  stops requiring supervenience bases to be held fixed. Common causes of Ψ
  and Φᵢ now count as interventions, and MM is satisfiable, but (M*) then
  rules mutually manipulable Ψ and Φᵢ to be in causal feedback, which
  contradicts (NC).
- **(M**) (pp. 17–18).** X causes Y only if some intervention sets X at t
  and changes Y at a strictly later t′. Because (SC) makes macro and micro
  changes overlap in time, no constituent is ever a cause of its whole, and
  (NC) follows instead of being assumed. This is the first consistent
  embedding of MM.
- **The evidential problem (pp. 18–20).** Under (M**) every intervention
  that satisfies MM is a common cause of Ψ and Φᵢ. "Model 5(a), which
  features constitutive dependencies ... and model 5(b), which does not
  ... imply the same correlations under manipulations. They are
  empirically indistinguishable." The asymmetry that matters: one can
  change the micro level without changing the macro level, but not the
  reverse (p. 20; also footnote 8: bottom-up interventions "may well
  exist").
- **(FH) and (CR) (p. 21).** (FH): every (IV*)-intervention on Ψ is a
  common cause of Ψ and some Φᵢ ∈ V. (CR): V's elements are constitutively
  relevant to Ψ iff MM and FH hold. FH has universal form, so "compliance
  with (CR) can, in principle, only be established inductively and is,
  thus, always prone to error" (p. 22).

## Concepts

- **Fat-handed intervention** — "an intervention that influences its
  ultimate effect along two (or more) different causal paths" (footnote 11,
  after Scheines 2005).
- **Specific variable** — a variable for the behaviour of a particular
  entity, Ψ(S), Φᵢ(Xᵢ), after Spohn (p. 4).
- **Surgical cause** — a cause of Ψ that is not a common cause of Ψ and
  some constituent; FH says there are none (p. 20).

## Bearing on the record's own argument

- **[CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md): left standing, and its "declared boundary" clause is
  sharpened.** The paper's problem is synchronic: whole and part change at
  the same time, so nothing can act on one without the other. [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)'s
  separating interventions are diachronic. Knocking out a production
  pathway in [CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md) (removing c₂ → c₁) changes ċ₁ at once and c₁ only
  over the following interval, so by the paper's own temporal criterion
  (M**) (p. 17) the pathway is a cause of the later capacities, not a
  constituent of them, and interventions on it can be surgical. The paper
  allows this explicitly: bottom-up interventions "may well exist"
  (footnote 8, p. 13). So the fat-handedness result does not reach
  [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)'s conclusion. What it does reach is the step [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md) already
  declines to take: whether the producer is part of the system. The paper
  takes parthood as given (SC) and refers its problems to Leuridan (p. 6).
  Intervention data do not supply a boundary here either, which is
  [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)'s "relative to a declared boundary" and [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s point.
  [CLAIM-528](../claims.d/CLAIM-528.md) and [ARG-050](../arguments.d/ARG-050.md) set this out.
- **[CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md) holds a fat-handed dependence of exactly this kind.** The
  coupling L(c) = Bᵀ diag(a₁, a₂)B with aᵢ = max{cᵢ − ½, 0} is a function of
  the capacities, changing whenever they do. Any intervention on c is an
  intervention on L, so "c maintains the coupling" and "the coupling is
  realized by c" cannot be separated by intervening on c. [CLAIM-521](../claims.d/CLAIM-521.md)'s
  comparison of coupling and maintenance is made on the model, which is
  what makes it possible. Same structure, not shown to be the paper's
  part–whole relation: L is a parameter, not a whole.
- **[CLAIM-515](../claims.d/CLAIM-515.md)'s third question, "Realized ... shown by interventions that
  discriminate implementations".** If "realized" means that the capability
  consists in the mechanism, synchronically, this paper says intervention
  data cannot show it: the interventions that change both are common
  causes (C5), and only a pattern across many experiments supports it
  (C6). If it means that the capability is produced through the mechanism,
  diachronically, interventions can show it. [CASE-163](../cases.d/CASE-163.md)'s identification of
  a mechanism from probes within a catalog is of the second kind. The
  claim does not say which it means ([CLAIM-528](../claims.d/CLAIM-528.md)).
- **[QUESTION-284](../questions.d/QUESTION-284.md): an answer for one kind of constitution.** [QUESTION-284](../questions.d/QUESTION-284.md)
  asks "whether any evidence distinguishes ontological constitution from
  ordinary causal modification". For part–whole constitution in
  mechanisms, this paper's answer is: no single manipulation does (C5),
  because every manipulation of the whole is a causal modification of some
  part. A second-order pattern does (C6), and only inductively. That is
  [CLAIM-150](../claims.d/CLAIM-150.md)'s underdetermination with a way out, and the way out is
  abductive. The question's "constitute" is about relations fixing the
  identity of relata, which is not this relation; the bearing is the form
  of the evidential problem, not shown to be the same problem.
- **[CLAIM-388](../claims.d/CLAIM-388.md) and [CLAIM-386](../claims.d/CLAIM-386.md): the asymmetry the closure tests use.** The
  paper's asymmetry, that the micro level can change without the macro
  level changing but not the reverse (p. 20), is exactly what a test of
  dynamical closure uses. [CLAIM-388](../claims.d/CLAIM-388.md)'s sum is closed because changes in the
  distribution of activity among units that leave the sum fixed do not
  change the sum's future; [CLAIM-386](../claims.d/CLAIM-386.md)'s closure defect QA(I − VQ) is the
  aggregate's response to such within-block redistributions. Those are
  bottom-up interventions inside a fibre of the supervenience map, and they
  are the surgical ones. Closure of a collective variable is thus testable
  by the interventions this paper allows, which constitution is not.
- **[CLAIM-526](../claims.d/CLAIM-526.md): coexisting levels without inter-level causation.** The
  paper's (M**) makes macro and micro "two closely related, but still
  distinct" relata of a non-causal dependence (pp. 22–23), which is
  [CLAIM-526](../claims.d/CLAIM-526.md)'s coexistence. The levels differ, though: the paper's are
  levels of parthood; [CLAIM-526](../claims.d/CLAIM-526.md)'s are levels of description fixed by
  closure. [CLAIM-405](../claims.d/CLAIM-405.md) already says such criteria need not agree.
- **[TERM-088](../terms.d/TERM-088.md).** The term's "constitutive" sense (relational organization
  helps fix identity and boundary) is not this paper's constitutive
  relevance (a part's behaviour realizing a whole's behaviour). A claim
  citing this debate should say it means the latter.

## Limitations

- **(NC) and (Sup) carry everything.** Drop (NC) and the first reductio
  fails (Leuridan's route). Make the base the organized activities rather
  than the behaviours and the argument for using (b) depends on
  Woodward's difference-making view of causal structure (pp. 5–6).
- **(M**) is offered "only" for causation in mechanisms (footnote 10), and
  the strict temporal overlap of macro and micro changes is assumed, not
  argued. Where a part's response takes time, (SC)'s overlap is an
  idealization ([CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md)'s point; [CLAIM-528](../claims.d/CLAIM-528.md) part 3).
- **No case is analysed.** The Mimosa example is illustrative; the authors
  say they have not tested whether practice fits (CR) (p. 22).
- **FH is untestable as stated** except inductively, and the paper gives
  no account of how large a variable-set expansion must be.

## Open questions

- Does any real constitutive inference in neuroscience or biology proceed
  by searching for a surgical cause of the whole and failing to find one?
  A reconstruction of one would test FH.
- When the micro response lags the macro change, is the dependence causal
  by (M**)? If so, the line between constitution and causation moves with
  the time resolution of the description.
