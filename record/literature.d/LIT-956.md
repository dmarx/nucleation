# LIT-956: Back and forth between algebraic geometry, algebraic logic, sheaves and forcing

Tarek Sayed Ahmed (2018), *arXiv:1811.01754*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-817](../notes.d/NOTE-817.md).*

- **Sheaf duality for lattices with operators.** A bounded distributive
  lattice with join-preserving idempotent operators is the algebra of
  continuous sections of a sheaf whose stalks are its quotients by the
  prime ideals of its fixed-point part (Theorem 4.1). With two-element
  stalks this is Stone duality; for algebras of formulas, a theory is the
  gluing of its complete extensions. The theorem is stated without proof.
- **Forcing from a site.** Conditions are the objects of a small site and
  names are presheaves on it (Theorem 5.1). The Kripke–Joyal clauses are
  listed for ∧, →, ∨, ∀ and ∃, but not for ¬, and the ∨ and ∃ clauses
  omit the covering families.
- **Fuzzy forcing.** I ⊩ φ ⊗ ψ iff some monomorphism J → I has
  I ⊗ J ⊩ φ ∧ ψ, and many-valued theories are interpreted in a
  quasi-topos whose Heyting reduct is an "almost" subobject classifier
  (§§5.1–5.2, stated).
- **Epimorphisms.** Under amalgamation, a non-surjective epimorphism
  between strongly regular algebras forces one among the simple algebras
  (Theorem 7.6, the one result proved in full).
- **Not the expected survey.** It is a 36-page research note with one
  reference, distributive throughout, and it never treats contexts or
  writes a pseudo-complement.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for [QUESTION-055](../questions.d/QUESTION-055.md), as the
machinery behind the option that negation is a Heyting pseudo-complement
over contexts. Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-817](../notes.d/NOTE-817.md), which finds that it
does not supply that machinery: its Heyting material is standard,
asserted rather than proved, and about forcing posets, not contexts. It
cannot promote [THEORY-037](../theory.d/THEORY-037.md). No THEORY is filed from it. A textbook account
of sheaves and the Kripke–Joyal semantics would serve the question
better.
