The owner is considering extending this record with argument-development
schemes: questions, claims and objections, in the manner of IBIS. Systems
of that kind have a fifty-year history, and most of it is a history of
not being used. The owner asked for that literature to be held here, as
the cautionary evidence the extension should be weighed against. The
failure modes it documents are these:

- the cost of capture falls on the author while the benefit goes to a
  later reader;
- users will not commit early to a formal structure;
- explicit structure has a cognitive overhead.

## What entered

Nine works, all registered and none read, so none has a NOTE ([ADR-025](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-025.md)):

- the notations and tools: Kunz and Rittel's 1970 working paper that
  introduced IBIS ([LIT-tmpf7m81](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpf7m81.md)), gIBIS ([LIT-tmpqlgz0](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpqlgz0.md)) and QOC
  ([LIT-tmpv7ysw](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpv7ysw.md));
- the success case: Conklin and Burgess Yakemovic's field trial, where an
  indented-text IBIS captured more than 2,300 decisions ([LIT-tmp4w33e](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp4w33e.md));
- the failure modes: Grudin on who does the work and who benefits
  ([LIT-tmpjgnbb](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpjgnbb.md), [LIT-tmpeup5u](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpeup5u.md)), Buckingham Shum and Hammond on what
  argumentation-based rationale is for and what it costs ([LIT-tmpntmge](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpntmge.md)),
  Lee's seven practical issues ([LIT-tmpnt23h](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpnt23h.md)), and Shipman and Marshall
  on why users will not formalize ([LIT-tmp7332z](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp7332z.md)).

The owner's list had eight. I added Conklin and Burgess Yakemovic,
because a body of failure evidence needs the case that did not fail, and
its abstract says what made the difference: capture with little
disruption of the normal process. I considered Grudin's chapter
"Evaluating opportunities for design capture" in Moran and Carroll's
*Design Rationale* (1996), and left it out: the only DOI is the 2020
reissue's, and the 1996 first appearance could not be dated from a
source.

No topic held these works, so `design-rationale` joins the topics
([ADR-030](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-030.md)). They are not anthology material. None is about machine
learning, and the anthology's nearest topic, `human-ai-interaction`, is
about working with a model. A grep of the anthology's record for every
author and DOI found nothing.

## How deeply

Every bibliographic detail was checked against Crossref, and the dates
follow [ADR-002](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-002.md). What the entries say about content comes from abstracts.
For Kunz and Rittel, the abstract came from the eScholarship scan, whose
title page was seen. For Grudin 1988, Buckingham Shum and Hammond, and
Shipman and Marshall, publishers refused this client. Their takeaways
come from secondary descriptions of the abstract, or, for Shipman and
Marshall, from an outline of the authors' web version, and each entry
says so. Two dates need a word:

- Grudin 1988 takes the CSCW '88 opening day, because Crossref gives
  the paper only a year.
- Grudin 1994 takes 2 January 1994, which is the date Crossref gives.

## What a reading should check

Whether the record's own experience bears out the first failure mode.
Here the person filing an entry is usually also its later reader, which
is the condition Grudin's argument says is missing. Buckingham Shum and
Hammond, and the Conklin and Burgess Yakemovic field trial, are the
places to look for whether that changes the cost-and-benefit balance.
