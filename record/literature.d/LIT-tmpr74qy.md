summary: >-
  Bostrom (2013). Defines existential risk as a threat of premature extinction of Earth-originating intelligent life or of the permanent and drastic destruction of its potential for desirable development: risks of at least crushing severity and pan-generational scope. Gives four classes (human extinction, permanent stagnation, flawed realisation, subsequent ruination), the last two defined normatively. On an aggregative, time-neutral view even Earth alone could hold 10^16 future lives, so cutting the risk by a millionth of a percentage point is worth a hundred million lives. Hence the maxipok rule of thumb for impersonal moral action: maximise the probability of an outcome that avoids existential catastrophe. Other ethical views (Adams' shared project of humanity, preference satisfaction, custodial duties to ancestors and descendants) concur to differing degrees. Under moral uncertainty, keeping options open recasts sustainability as a trajectory. The risk is neglected: in Scopus, papers on "human extinction" number fewer than those on dung beetles. It is also undersupplied, as a pan-generational public good that even a world state could only partly capture.
