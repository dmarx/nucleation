# CASE-107: Locked-in syndrome: consciousness preserved with anarthria and quadriplegia; in the classic form, vertical eye movement and blinking remain and carry communication, and in the completely locked-in state no motor channel remains

## The case

Locked-in syndrome "consists of anarthria and quadriplegia while
consciousness is preserved. Classically, vertical eye movements or blinking
allow coded communication. Given appropriate medical care, patients can
survive for decades" (Bruno et al. 2011, [LIT-1183](../literature.d/LIT-1183.md)). The classic, incomplete and total forms are Bauer et al.'s (1979), as
Bruno et al. cite them ([NOTE-961](../notes.d/NOTE-961.md)). Laureys et al. 2005 ([LIT-1198](../literature.d/LIT-1198.md))
reviews them but could not be read. In the completely locked-in state (CLIS),
usually reached through ALS, no motor channel remains.

- **Lucid life on one eyelid.** Bauby dictated *Le scaphandre et le
  papillon* ([LIT-1193](../literature.d/LIT-1193.md)) by blinking his left eyelid as a partner
  read out an alphabet.
- **Lucid over years.** Of 65 chronic patients with usable answers in
  Bruno et al.'s survey, 47 reported happiness, and a longer time in LIS
  correlated with happiness ([NOTE-961](../notes.d/NOTE-961.md)). But most of the sample was
  not on one eyelid: 55% had recovered some speech and 70% some limb
  movement, answers were given "aided by the patient's proxy", and
  recovered speech was the strongest correlate of happiness (OR 20.47).
  The survey shows lucid lives with restricted action, not without it.
- **The completely locked-in state.** Kübler and Birbaumer ([LIT-1199](../literature.d/LIT-1199.md),
  read in [NOTE-948](../notes.d/NOTE-948.md)): none of seven CLIS patients regained yes/no
  communication by BCI. All seven were already in CLIS when training
  began. They proposed that patients "may loose the perception of the
  contingency" between the required brain response and its consequences,
  and that "passive sensory information processing is intact in CLIS even
  at the most complex semantic processing levels". The hypothesis is
  about contingency, not evidence. It also predicts its own exception:
  "learning BCI-control or any other contingency before onset of CLIS,
  which can be transferred from LIS to CLIS should prevent extinction in
  CLIS". It is stated as what "might be responsible", from seven patients
  and an analogy with curarized rats.
- **Chaudhary et al.'s patient fits that exception** ([LIT-1200](../literature.d/LIT-1200.md), read in
  [NOTE-951](../notes.d/NOTE-951.md)). He had used an eye-movement BCI in LIS, and his speller
  was "identical to the one used previously", the one he had used while he
  still controlled his eye movements. He had been in CLIS only "a few weeks before and also
  after implantation", and he produced intelligible output on 44 of 107
  days. So he does not refute the extinction hypothesis. He is the case
  it predicts. The article carries no editor's note or correction as of
  2026-10-16. The authors' earlier CLIS papers were retracted after a DFG
  misconduct finding (STAT, 2022-03-22, not rechecked).

## What it can show

- **Classic LIS separates lucid judgement from limb and speech action,
  not from action.** Friston et al.'s simulations begin with oculomotor
  control, and add a two-joint arm, a mountain car and Lorenz trajectories
  ([NOTE-960](../notes.d/NOTE-960.md)). So
  classic LIS leaves active inference the action it models first, and
  cannot by itself decide [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md) against it. It can show that
  judgement keeps a built model working, over years, with world-directed
  action reduced to a trickle.
- **CLIS is the test, and it is asymmetric.** Evidence of lucidity in CLIS
  has to cross some channel, and a channel the patient drives is an action
  in active inference's sense. Chaudhary et al.'s patient communicated
  only once neurofeedback restored a contingency between intention and
  outcome. So the case can show the loop running down (Kübler and
  Birbaumer's prediction, [CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md)), but it cannot show judgement
  running with no action at all, which is [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md)'s defeater (b)
  turned around. This is the evidence-for-experience line's standing
  result applied to the case: "Every kind of evidence the record holds
  about experience reaches it through access" ([QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)).
  Command-following, the usual marker, marks access, not experience
  ([THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md)). No-report markers still have to be validated through
  report or executive control ([THEORY-298](../theory.d/THEORY-298.md)).
- **Command-following by brain imaging.** Owen et al. ([LIT-1178](../literature.d/LIT-1178.md), read
  in [NOTE-962](../notes.d/NOTE-962.md)): a patient who met the criteria for the vegetative
  state, five months after injury, imagined playing tennis or moving
  around her home on request, and responded "through her brain activity,
  rather than through speech or movement". Monti et al. ([LIT-1192](../literature.d/LIT-1192.md), read
  in [NOTE-964](../notes.d/NOTE-964.md)): 5 of 54 patients with disorders of consciousness
  modulated their brain activity at will. Two "remained behaviorally
  unresponsive at the bedside, even after the functional MRI results were
  known", but they were not asked questions. The one patient who answered
  yes/no questions, 5 of 6 correctly, showed on retesting "reproducible,
  but inconsistent, responses indicative of a minimally conscious state".
  So what this shows is narrower than it first looked:
  - **The answers left with no action on the world**, but the patient who
    gave them kept some motor contingency. The questions (his father's
    name, for example) were about his life before the injury, so they test
    a model built while acting, not one kept in touch without it.
    Defeater (b) of [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md) is not tested.
  - **The channel is imagined action** ("imagine standing still on a tennis
    court and to swing an arm"), read from a motor region. That fits
    [CLAIM-337](../claims.d/CLAIM-337.md)'s "action moved inward" literally, though the answer it
    carries is a judgement, put into imagery by a code the investigators
    set.
  - **Negative results are uninterpretable.** When a question got no
    signal, "Whether the patient fell asleep ... or lost consciousness
    cannot be determined". The asymmetry above holds.
  - **The scoring was blind**: "the investigators would not know the correct
    answers before judging the functional MRI data". That is
    [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)'s anchor, but for the observers' reading, not for the
    patient, who got no feedback.
- **Not every contingency goes.** Paralysis removes action on the world.
  It does not remove the body's regulation of itself. Barrett's action is
  visceromotor ([NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)), and on Damasio's account feelings may need the
  body to act on the neurons that map it ([QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md)). Damasio also
  holds that an "as if body loop", in which body states are re-enacted
  when an option is imagined, has largely superseded the body loop in
  adults ([THEORY-500](../theory.d/THEORY-500.md); asserted, not shown). If so, the mechanism
  that would keep judgement working in locked-in syndrome is already
  internal, and the case cannot remove every contingency between
  intention and outcome, as [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md)'s defeater (b) asks.
- **Extinguished or dormant.** A coalition that falls silent can remain
  available for reactivation ([CLAIM-287](../claims.d/CLAIM-287.md)). Kübler and Birbaumer's patients
  may have lost the capacity for goal-directed thinking, or only the
  channel that would show it. Chaudhary et al.'s patient does not decide
  between these, since he carried a contingency over from LIS.
- **What it cannot show.** Every patient here built their model while
  acting. The case bears on the loop that keeps a model in touch, not on
  the one that builds it.
