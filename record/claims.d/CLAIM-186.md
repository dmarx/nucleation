# CLAIM-186: How fast an argument grows sets how it grows: drift tracks how fast new material arrives relative to how fast it is anchored, so a fast, prolific exchange branches, as a crystal grown far from equilibrium does, and a project memory stabilizes an argument by keeping anchoring up with arrival

## The claim

A crystal grown slowly, close to equilibrium, is faceted. Its shape is
reproducible and does not depend on the seed's outline. A crystal grown
fast, at high supersaturation, loses its flat faces to an instability:
bumps grow faster than the surface around them, and the crystal branches
into dendrites. Their form records fluctuations in the growth conditions.

An exchange with a chat assistant can run at very high supersaturation.
Every reply offers more connections, terms and proposals than the turn
asked for. The translation exchange shows what the branching looks like:

- In 51 minutes on 2026-10-10 (U49–U56, workbench 2026/10/10/040457), the
  thesis moved twice, to [CLAIM-133](CLAIM-133.md) and then to the first form of
  [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md). At U50 a passing remark about FID was escalated into a
  THEORY, a new metric, three LITs, a CLAIM, a QUESTION and chapter
  placements, until the owner pulled it back at U51.
- Each of the two crystallized arguments (U38, U56; [TERM-052](../terms.d/TERM-052.md)) came
  at the end of a run of fast turns. The first lost material when it was
  drafted into C7 ([CLAIM-324](CLAIM-324.md)). The second, A203, left out Tannaka
  reconstruction ([QUESTION-036](../questions.d/QUESTION-036.md)) and [CLAIM-125](CLAIM-125.md)'s two open problems, and nothing in the exchange says whether they
  dissolved as the argument matured or were not carried forward.

The record acts on growth by anchoring, not only by pausing. Under the
owner's reframing ([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)), what lets structure grow in order is a
persistent anchor for it to grow on. Drift then happens where material
arrives faster than it is anchored: proposals that are never coded have to
be restated, and restating is where redefinition creeps in. Filing between
turns, the second-occurrence rule ([CLAIM-176](CLAIM-176.md)), and the other triggers
of persistence each raise the anchoring rate. Pace matters because it sets
how fast material arrives, and the ratio is what decides whether the
argument faces over or branches.

The ratio can be measured. In a reconstructed exchange, count the turns in
a stretch against the entries filed for it, and set that against the
re-derivations and unannounced changes of thesis the workbench records for
the same stretch.

## What it does not say

- It does not say fast exchanges are bad. Dendrites grow fast, and the
  branching is where new material is found. The claim is about stability,
  not yield.
- It does not say the physics carries over. Morphological instability is
  an analogy here, and the claim is that drift tracks pace. That can be
  tested without the physics.
- One exchange, and no slow one to compare it with. The comparison that
  would test it does not exist yet.

## Note of 2026-10-11 (later): settling does not rule out bursts

Generalized stability theory ([LIT-931](../literature.d/LIT-931.md), [LIT-929](../literature.d/LIT-929.md), and Trefethen
et al., [LIT-932](../literature.d/LIT-932.md), read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-831](../notes.d/NOTE-831.md), [NOTE-842](../notes.d/NOTE-842.md)
and [NOTE-839](../notes.d/NOTE-839.md); [THEORY-323](../theory.d/THEORY-323.md), [THEORY-325](../theory.d/THEORY-325.md), [THEORY-304](../theory.d/THEORY-304.md))
adds a qualification. When a
system's operator is non-normal, it can be stable in every mode and still
amplify a disturbance by a large factor for a while before the disturbance
decays. Trefethen et al. conjecture ("we speculate"), from a 2 × 2 model, that
shear flows judged stable by their eigenvalues turn turbulent because a
transient amplification hands the disturbance to nonlinear dynamics,
which bootstraps it.

Read across, an argument that settles in the end can still show a burst of
drift after a disturbance, and asymptotic settling is not evidence against
the bursts. A record's dependencies are directed: claims rest on claims.
The record takes directed, asymmetric coupling to be a source of
non-normality; that is its own reading, not the papers' (Farrell &
Ioannou's source is a background shear, though their 2 × 2 example is a
one-way coupling and fits it). If it holds, a change to a claim many
others rest on could be amplified through them before the argument
settles. Two consequences for this claim:

- Drift should be measured over the short run after a disturbance, not only
  as an average. An average can hide the burst.
- An argument under development is the nonautonomous case ([LIT-929](../literature.d/LIT-929.md)).
  Its structure changes as it is worked on, so there are no fixed modes to
  appeal to even in principle. Worse, time variation of a non-normal
  operator can make the system asymptotically unstable while it is stable
  at every instant, removing settling altogether, not only adding bursts.
  In the paper's deterministic examples variation much faster than the
  transient growth is harmless, and damping stabilizes; anchoring is a
  candidate for damping.

The bearing is the assistant's reading. Neither paper is about arguments.
