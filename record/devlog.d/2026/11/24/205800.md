**Going right.** Nine filers, one group of turns each, in one checkout
(ADR-139), each owning the ideas first introduced in its turns and listing
handoffs for the rest, then one reconciler. The ownership rule kept
duplicates to three pairs across 130-odd entries, and the handoff lists
gave the reconciler a worklist instead of a search. Committing each
group's listed files as it reported, and pulling CI's repair only between
workers, cost no lost edits.

**Going right.** The hidden code under ChatGPT's turns held the full text
of a manuscript the share page shows only as a download card. The decoder's
"not shown on the share page" blocks are worth reading for any turn that
says it wrote a file.

**Going wrong.** Staging had not taken main's concretized codes (the
skipped back-merge, devlog of 20:19), and ChatGPT browses main. So every
filer met codes the exchange cited that did not exist in its tree, and
each worked out the mapping by title on its own. The back-merge was done
mid-filing, which then produced seven merge conflicts and a second round
of repair. Check that staging contains main before briefing filers.

**A wrong theory, found out by the owner.** The assistant's gap fill on
the norm model read "the justification disappears" as the model's
baseline falling past collapse, and reported that the model "says less
than ChatGPT claims". The owner's counterexample (segregation after
abolition) prompted a recomputation showing the opposite: the model lets
an established norm outlast the loss of most of its baseline support. The
error was conflating a parameter with the concept it was taken to stand
for; the check computed the right numbers and read them under the wrong
mapping.

**Departure.** The lead did not write the idea ledger before briefing, as
the skill's step 2 says; each filer wrote its group's. At this size a
single ledger would not have fitted one context. Whether step 2 should
say so is for the retro.
