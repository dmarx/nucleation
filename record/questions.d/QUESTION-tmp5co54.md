# QUESTION-tmp5co54: By what mechanism is an organization's classification of signal against noise revised, and what observation would measure its responsiveness to reasons for revising that classification, apart from its responsiveness within it?

## What makes it open

[CLAIM-tmp76xaw](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp76xaw.md) (Active) holds that what an organization's filtering treats
as noise is itself governed, and that the capacity to recognize a demand
as demand-worthy "must stay open to correction". It does not say how the
classification is revised, by whom, or on what evidence. [CLAIM-719](../claims.d/CLAIM-719.md)
(Proposed) separates responsiveness within a governing mechanism from
responsiveness to reasons for revising it, and its admission says the
second-order measure "is named, not defined". The manuscript reads the
classification of noise as the object of that second-order responsiveness
(its join JB4), so the two gaps are one.

What the record has toward a measure: [CLAIM-719](../claims.d/CLAIM-719.md)'s note of 2026-11-18
proposes counting completed mechanism-revising action items in
postmortems apart from instance fixes, and its note of 2026-11-22 records
that the pilot for [LEAD-119](../leads.d/LEAD-119.md) ([CASE-335](../cases.d/CASE-335.md)) could not measure completion from
published documents. [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) says what the revising evidence would need:
an arrival or scoring that does not pass through the classification it
tests.

## What would count as an answer

A mechanism (who revises the classification, on what evidence, by what
procedure) and an observation that separates second-order from
first-order responsiveness: for instance, the lag between the first
arrival of a class of input later shown to carry demand and its
reclassification, against the lag in responding to inputs already classed
as demand.
