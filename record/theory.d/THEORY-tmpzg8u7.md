# THEORY-tmpzg8u7: Naudts, Helberger, Veale and Sax (2025) argue that because recommender systems on very large online platforms perform a public function, their optimization goals should not be left to the platform: people should have a right to constructive optimization, a participatory say in those goals directed at self-development and self-determination, with publicly reasoned refusals and the burden of proof on the platform, whereas the Digital Services Act treats recommenders as proprietary and fundamental rights only as a limit.

## The account

Naudts, Helberger, Veale and Sax (2025) argue that because recommender systems on very large online platforms perform a public function, their optimization goals should not be left to the platform: people should have a right to constructive optimization, a participatory say in those goals directed at self-development and self-determination, with publicly reasoned refusals and the burden of proof on the platform, whereas the Digital Services Act treats recommenders as proprietary and fundamental rights only as a limit.
