# QUESTION-486: Can rights that no institution creates or can extinguish, the Founders' "unalienable rights", serve as the institution-independent criteria the record's normative judgments of organizations need, and what would such rights have to be to do so?

## Why it is a question

The owner, at U37 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):

> what if we leverage the Founders' notion of "inalienable rights" as independent normative criteria to build on?

The record judges organizations only relative to a declared standard
([CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)). Whether such standards can be objective about themselves is
[QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md), and how an organization's demand set can be fixed when its
own governance shapes it is [QUESTION-456](QUESTION-456.md). The owner's move is to take as
the standard rights that, by their own description, no institution
creates and none can remove. If that works, the institution under judgment
cannot set the terms of its judgment.

On spelling: the Declaration of Independence as adopted reads "certain
unalienable Rights"; the Virginia Declaration of Rights §3, in the
National Archives transcription, gives "a majority of the community" an
"indubitable, inalienable, and indefeasible right to reform, alter, or
abolish" an inadequate government. The owner's word and A's are
"inalienable" throughout; the sense is the same.

## What A85 adds to the question

A85 §3 separates two things the proposal could be asking for:
"Institution-independent normative warrant" and "Philosophically
unquestionable normative warrant". "The first does not require the
second." The Founders' assertion does not make
the rights real, and the Founding's own exclusions (enslavement, the
exclusion of women from equal political authority) show, in A's words,
"why the standards must be capable of **criticizing the very institutions
and authors that proclaim them**". So the question is not whether the
historical list is authoritative. It is whether some rights can be
independent of institutions and still be justified, and what follows for
the record's apparatus if they are.

A85's own development is filed elsewhere: inalienability as a limit on
what a delegation can confer ([CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md), tested by [CASE-286](../cases.d/CASE-286.md)),
and the persistence of a claim whose remedy has been made unreachable
([CLAIM-722](../claims.d/CLAIM-722.md)). A100 later proposes Scanlon's contractualism as the
justification layer, with rights as "outputs of a justificatory inquiry";
how feasibility evidence bears on that is [QUESTION-484](QUESTION-484.md).

## What would count as an answer

- **Yes, with a justification.** An account of why some rights hold
  independently of any institution's recognition (natural-rights,
  contractualist, interest or status-based), with a test that tells such
  rights from rights an institution may validly create, waive or
  extinguish. A85 §12 lists this as "substantial" remaining work.
- **Yes, as a provisional premise.** A showing that the record can take a
  candidate set of rights as a declared standard, in [CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)'s sense,
  and that what it then concludes (about suppression, persistence,
  adequacy) does not depend on which justification is later accepted.
  That would make the rights independent of institutions without
  answering [QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md).
- **No.** An argument that any criterion strong enough to judge
  institutions must be specified through some institution or practice, so
  that "institution-independent" rights collapse into the standards of a
  particular community; or that rights of this kind are too indeterminate
  in content to fix a demand set.
