# LIT-029: Long-Term Cognitive and Neuropsychiatric Consequences of Repetitive Concussion and Head-Impact Exposure

Thomas McAllister, Michael McCrea (2017), *Journal of Athletic Training (vol. 52, no. 3, pp. 309–317)* — DOI-10.4085/1062-6050-52.1.14

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-858](../notes.d/NOTE-858.md).*

- **Short term is known.** "The overwhelming majority of athletes achieve
  a complete recovery in self-reported symptoms and performance-based
  measures … by 1 to 2 weeks". Recurrent concussion is rarer than a first
  one (3.1 against 22.2 per 100,000 athlete-exposures in high school) and
  worse acutely.
- **Exposure without effect, mostly.** Football players take hundreds of
  head impacts a season (median 257–438 per college team), and
  season-long studies mostly find no cognitive effect of impact counts.
  One study found 24% of contact athletes against 3.6% of non-contact
  athletes below prediction on verbal learning.
- **Long term is unmeasured.** "To date, no prospective, longitudinal
  studies of well-defined cohorts over longer periods … have been
  conducted". The evidence is surveys of retired players (5 times the MCI
  prevalence with three or more reported concussions), brain-bank
  autopsies and case series.
- **CTE is uncertain.** Its pathology may mark exposure rather than the
  clinical syndrome, and some critics doubt it is a distinct disease.
- **What would settle it.** Prospective cohorts of concussed,
  exposed-but-unconcussed and unexposed athletes with baselines and long
  follow-up.
