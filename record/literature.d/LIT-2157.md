# LIT-2157: Replications of Shah, Mullainathan, and Shafir (2012)

Shah, A. K., Mullainathan, S. & Shafir, E. (2018). Replications of Shah, Mullainathan, and Shafir (2012). Write-up posted on the Open Science Framework, osf.io/vzm23. 6 pp.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1686](../notes.d/NOTE-1686.md).*

- **Over-borrowing replicated, for shots and for time.** "scarcity
  itself leads to over-borrowing. Second, this is true for multiple kinds
  of resources" (p. 5). Effects smaller: the borrowing-by-scarcity
  interactions fell from ηp2 = .12, .09 and .10 to .04, .04 and .01
  (Table 1).
- **Focus replicated.** Poor players spent longer aiming the first shot,
  "F(1, 972) = 115.79" (Table 1).
- **Neglect weaker.** Aiming time and later borrowing, original "r(38) =
  .34, P < .05", replication "r(616) = -.007, P = .87"; previews
  interaction "not significant" (Table 1).
- **Study 1 reversed.** "we find no evidence in Study 1 for the hypothesis
  that scarcity-induced focus leads to cognitive fatigue on subsequent
  tasks. Indeed, the results run opposite to our initial finding" (p. 6).

## Standing in the record

Filed as its own work, the authors' second test of their claims, and as
a stand-in, so far only, for the unread Science article ([LIT-2147](LIT-2147.md)):
its Table 1 restates every original analysis, and for Studies 1, 2 and 5
it is the only text read that carries the published numbers. `corrects`
the article on Study 1, which the authors now call a false positive in
their comment on the SSRP report (read with Camerer et al. 2018,
[LIT-2162](LIT-2162.md)). Filed from it: [THEORY-1484](../theory.d/THEORY-1484.md).
