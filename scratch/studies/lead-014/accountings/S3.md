## Relations to held entries

- **R1. THEORY-013** (Proposed: once context-dependent marginals are
  separated from contextuality, word-meaning data published as
  contextual show none). The paper's own results **support** it for the
  Bell-type phrase sets (signalling; non-contextual in CbD), and its
  bootstrap weakens the two CbD-contextual cases: the chance of a
  non-contextual resample is > .56 for adopt–boxer and > .08 for
  throw–pitcher. Neither is significant at conventional levels. So the
  paper is consistent with THEORY-013 and is not a counterexample to it.
  A support judgement that is contested (the paper's abstract says
  contextuality "is also possible"): **ARG** from this reading to
  THEORY-013's scope.
- **R2. CLAIM-037** (formal contextuality needs CbD when marginals
  shift): the paper is an instance of exactly that move (sheaf analysis
  blocked by signalling, CbD applied). **Illustrates.**
- **R3. CLAIM-044** (ambiguity and contextuality are different: an
  ambiguous utterance admits several global assignments, a contextual one
  none). The paper treats sense selection across phrases as measurement
  and finds a possibilistically contextual set ({tap, box} × {pitcher,
  cabinet}): lexical ambiguity resolved by phrase context yields no
  global sense assignment. This **qualifies** CLAIM-044: the two are
  distinct, but ambiguity at the word level is the raw material of a
  contextual model at the phrase level. A dated note on CLAIM-044 and an
  ARG (contested support).
- **R4. CLAIM-069** (the 2016 CbD criterion being replaced by
  multimaximal couplings): the paper uses the dichotomised CbD 2.0
  procedure with pairwise maximal couplings (§8), so its verdicts are
  under the criterion CLAIM-069 discusses. **Bears on**; note only.
- **R5. CLAIM-041** (context sensitivity and order effects do not by
  themselves establish nonclassical probability): the signalling results
  are such a case. **Illustrates.**

## Ideas of the source (ledger)

- **C1.** Ambiguous two-word phrases as bipartite Bell-type scenarios:
  each word a measurement, senses as outcomes, phrases as contexts.
  **NOTE; THEORY** (the method).
- **C2.** Possibilistic results: {coach, boxer} × {lap, file} is
  possibilistically signalling, so not judged; {tap, box} × {pitcher,
  cabinet} is possibilistically non-signalling and contextual;
  {press, box} × {can, leaves} non-signalling and non-contextual.
  **NOTE; THEORY.**
- **C3.** Probabilistic (corpus) variants from BNC and UKWaC: all become
  signalling, so the sheaf-theoretic test cannot be applied. **NOTE;
  THEORY.**
- **C4.** There is no reason to expect non-signalling in language; the
  analogy of words in a phrase to entangled qubits that can
  "communicate" (§7). **NOTE.**
- **C5.** CbD analysis: Bell-type phrase sets non-contextual (the
  press/box system by a 105 × 2^16 linear program); the subject-verb /
  verb-object pairs adopt–boxer and throw–pitcher CbD-contextual, degrees
  1/30 and 7/30, bootstrap as in R1. **NOTE; THEORY.**
- **C6.** Footnote 3: no Bell-scenario-like model has been found
  CbD-contextual by this corpus method, though no theoretical reason
  forbids it. **NOTE.**
- **C7.** The subject-verb / verb-object design: identifying "adopt" across
  the two grammatical contexts is a modelling choice that makes the
  systems signalling and only CbD-analysable. **NOTE**, and gap G3.
- **C8.** Claim that contextuality frameworks can "formally study the
  effect of context on interpretation". **Left** (programmatic).

## Gaps in the source's own argument

- **G1.** The headline that CbD-contextuality "is also possible" rests on
  point estimates whose own bootstrap gives > .56 and > .08 chances of a
  non-contextual system; the conclusion section does not carry that
  caveat.
- **G2.** Senses were assigned to corpus occurrences by the authors by
  hand; no annotation protocol or agreement is reported.
- **G3.** Counts are very small (proportions such as 2/3 and 7/74 imply
  single-digit counts in some cells) and per-context sample sizes are not
  tabulated in the text.
- **G4.** The CbD-contextual cases are exactly those built by the design
  choice C7; contextuality there may be an artefact of identifying a
  word's variable across grammatical roles.
- **G5.** Attribution that does not check: the conclusion conjectures that
  the bus sense of "coach" is rare because the corpora are British and
  that sense is "mainly American". The bus sense of "coach" is the
  British usage (American English says "bus"), so the conjecture runs
  the wrong way.
- **G6.** The meaning inventories were truncated by the authors (two
  senses per word, no metaphor), which shapes the supports on which the
  possibilistic verdicts rest.
