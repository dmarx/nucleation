# THEORY-tmpavfyh: Martinengo and colleagues find that spaced digital education for health professionals improves post-intervention knowledge more than massed digital education does, with a small effect, and promotes clinical behaviour change, while the evidence for spaced over massed digital simulation for skills rests on two studies of low certainty

## Source

Martinengo et al. (2024), LIT-tmpu5jbd; read in NOTE-tmpv5axa.

## What was actually shown

- **Knowledge:** nine studies, spaced against massed online education,
  SMD 0.32 (0.13–0.51), I² 66%, moderate certainty (Abstract).
- **Behaviour change:** spaced online education against massed online
  education and no intervention, SMD 0.67 (0.43–0.91), I² 5%, moderate
  certainty (Abstract).
- **Simulation skills:** two studies, SMD 1.15 (0.34–1.96) in the
  abstract and 1.24 (0.84–1.64) in the Results, I² 74%, low certainty;
  time to complete and errors not significantly different.

Each pooled estimate could have crossed zero; the simulation time and
error outcomes did.

## What this does not say

- That spacing improves retention of skills: it analyses immediate
  post-training results; only knowledge retention, in two studies, is
  pooled.
- That spaced simulation is reliably better: two studies, one of them a
  nursing case-management task pooled as "surgical skills".
- What spacing interval is best: none of the studies justified theirs.
- Anything about maintaining a rarely used capability over long non-use.
