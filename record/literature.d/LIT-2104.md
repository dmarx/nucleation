# LIT-2104: On the complexity of reconfiguration problems

Ito, T., Demaine, E. D., Harvey, N. J. A., Papadimitriou, C. H., Sideri, M., Uehara, R. & Uno, Y. (2011). On the complexity of reconfiguration problems. *Theoretical Computer Science* 412(12–14): 1054–1065. doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2010.12.005.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1595](../notes.d/NOTE-1595.md). Section and theorem numbers are the journal's.*

- **The general picture** (abstract): "We demonstrate that a host of
  reconfiguration problems derived from" "NP-complete problems are
  PSPACE-complete, while some are also NP-hard to approximate." "In
  contrast, several reconfiguration versions of problems in P are solvable
  in polynomial" time.
- **Membership** (§2.1): "any reconfiguration version of a problem S in
  NP, can be solved in" polynomial space, by Savitch's theorem; "Theorem 1.
  Power supply reconfiguration is PSPACE-complete."
- **Easy cases** (§3): matroid bases connect by |B0 \ Bt| exchanges
  without exceeding the weight bound; "Theorem 3 ([3]). Satisfiability
  reconfiguration for linear, Horn, dual Horn and 2-literal clauses are all
  in P", [3] being Gopalan et al.
- **The worst intermediate state** (§4): "Theorem 4. Maxmin clique
  reconfiguration cannot be approximated within any constant factor unless
  P = NP"; "Lemma 3. Minmax power supply reconfiguration is strongly
  NP-hard"; "Theorem 6. There is a linear-time 2-approximation algorithm
  for minmax set cover reconfiguration."
- **Decision only** (§2.1): the problems ask whether a path exists; they
  do "not ask for an actual reconfiguration sequence."

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on resource-bounded realizability cites
"complexity-theoretic reconfiguration problems" to ScienceDirect without a
title; this paper is the probable referent, not a certain one. It bears A
out that reconfiguration makes finding a valid state and navigating
between valid states precise, with different complexities. A's further
point, that the complexity can differ dramatically "even for closely
related constraint systems", is better supported by Gopalan et al.,
[LIT-2114](LIT-2114.md), whose st-connectivity result this paper quotes. A omits §4,
which is A's own bottleneck B*, the least over paths of the peak resource,
posed as a computational problem: the worst intermediate state of the
minmax problems is that bottleneck, and computing it is hard in some cases.
It bears on [CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md) (supports and extends, adding that the
threshold can be hard to compute), on [TERM-106](../terms.d/TERM-106.md) (Theorem 6 bounds the
slack of set-cover reconfiguration by the larger endpoint cost) and on
[CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md). Filed from it: [THEORY-1375](../theory.d/THEORY-1375.md). No instruction for
machine-learning practice.
