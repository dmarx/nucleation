# Hirschman's exit and voice, in which members denied effective voice can still exit, carries over to a fork as the construction of a new organization through which a disposition the parent kept ineffective gains efficacy

## The inference

1. [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md): when an organization deteriorates, its members' exit and
   voice are both recuperation mechanisms. An organization needs floor
   levels of both for feedback, and its managers "may encourage the
   response least dangerous to their power rather than the one that gives
   the most feedback" (the reading's paraphrase of Hirschman).
2. A50 §6: "Contributors may object strongly without possessing the
   practical capacity to change the decision." "They may then organize a
   fork, creating a new channel through which those previously
   ineffective dispositions can become consequential."
3. A contributor community whose governance grants dissent no effect is an
   organization holding voice below its floor. A fork is the members'
   exit.
4. So a fork can be exit that builds a new organization through which the
   disposition the parent kept ineffective gains efficacy
   ([CLAIM-tmp7fzb9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7fzb9.md)).

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are they alike, and is that the respect the
  conclusion needs?** In both, members dissatisfied with an organization's
  course respond by leaving it when speaking up inside it does not work,
  and the organization's managers shape which response is available. That
  is the respect for the claim's condition, "when voice inside the whole
  is denied a channel". It is not the respect for the claim's conclusion,
  which is about what the leavers build. The analogy carries the first and
  needs a second premise for the second.
- **What relevant difference is there?** Three.
  - **Exit to what.** Hirschman's exit is to an existing alternative: a
    competing supplier or another party. [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md) has
    dissatisfied customers who "churn among equally bad suppliers". The
    claim's fork is exit into an organization the leavers found, "**the
    construction of a new organization through which a previously
    constrained disposition gains efficacy**". The reading the record
    holds has no founding.
  - **Exit for whom.** For Hirschman exit is a recuperation mechanism, a
    feedback channel to the declining organization. In the claim the
    fork's point is the leavers' own efficacy, whether or not the parent
    recovers. A fork that signals nothing to the parent and still lets the
    dissenters' objection govern is the claim's case, and it is outside
    Hirschman's frame.
  - **The cost of exit.** Hirschman stresses that exit can be costly. In
    open source the code can be copied at no cost under its licence. The
    name, the infrastructure and the community cannot. So the fork is
    cheap exit for one part of the organization and costly for the rest,
    a split the reading does not have.
- **What transfers, and what does not?** What transfers is the condition
  and its cause: voice held below its floor, by managers who favour the
  response least threatening to them, leaves exit. That supports the
  claim's "when voice inside the whole is denied a channel", and its
  defeat condition, forks "in which the forking party's objections were
  already effective in the parent". What does not transfer is the claim's
  distinctive clause, that the exit builds an organization in which the
  disposition governs. The claim stands on [CLAIM-047](../claims.d/CLAIM-047.md) and [CLAIM-287](../claims.d/CLAIM-287.md) for
  that, not on Hirschman. Hirschman's loyalty, which raises the cost of
  exit so that members voice rather than exit, has no counterpart in the
  claim, and the reading says nothing about what loyalty does in a split.

Proposed. The analogy gives the claim its condition and leaves its
distinctive clause on its other premises. It could become a fuller
second line if a reading of [LIT-748](../literature.d/LIT-748.md), the 1970 book, found exit into a new
organization there. [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md) is itself Proposed and argued from
examples.

## What it does not show

- That a fork succeeds, or that gaining efficacy is gaining legitimacy.
- Which bearer holds the disposition, the community or its dissenters.
  [TERM-tmp6ersv](../terms.d/TERM-tmp6ersv.md) requires a claim to declare it.
