# LIT-412: Supersizing the Mind: Embodiment, Action, and Cognitive Extension

Andy Clark (2008), Oxford University Press, 286 pp. by NDPR's count
— DOI-10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195333213.001.0001

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, with Chalmers's
extended-mind programme. `Deferred` because the full text is not lawfully
readable online, and so no NOTE is filed. What is reachable, to scope it
until somebody reads it:

- **The publisher's description.** The book surveys work on "embodied,
  situated, distributed, and even 'extended' cognition", asks "exactly what
  kind of link is at issue" between thought and body, action and
  environment, and argues for an approach that "combines the use of
  representational, computational, and information-theoretic tools with an
  appreciation of the importance of context, timing, biomechanics, and
  dynamics". "More controversially, it depicts some coalitions of
  biological and non-biological resources as the extended cognitive
  circuitry of individual minds."
- **Contents (Crossref, with pages).** Foreword by David Chalmers (ix–xvi);
  Introduction: BRAINBOUND Versus EXTENDED (xxv–xxx); Part I, "From
  Embodiment to Cognitive Extension": 1 The Active Body (3–29), 2 The
  Negotiable Body (30–43), 3 Material Symbols (44–60), 4 World, Incorporated
  (61–82); Part II, "Boundary Disputes": 5 Mind Re-bound? (85–110), 6 The
  Cure for Cognitive Hiccups (HEMC, HEC, HEMC…) (111–139), 7 Rediscovering
  the Brain (140–166); Part III, "The Limits of Embodiment": 8 Painting,
  Planning, and Perceiving (169–195), 9 Disentangling Embodiment (196–217);
  10 Conclusions: Mind as Mashup (218–219); Appendix: The Extended Mind
  (220–232). The part titles are from the NDPR review, which does not
  list chapters; the grouping of chapters into parts is inferred from the
  page gaps after chapters 4 and 7.
- **What it answers.** Part II is the reply to the critics this record now
  holds: chapter 6's title uses Rupert's labels for the embedded (HEMC) and
  extended (HEC) hypotheses ([LIT-422](LIT-422.md)), and Chalmers's foreword says he
  agrees "with almost everything in Andy's authoritative treatment of
  these objections in part two". Adams and Aizawa's critique
  ([LIT-438](LIT-438.md)) is the other target, and Clark's earlier reply to it is
  held as [LIT-403](LIT-403.md).
- **What it contains that is already here.** The appendix reprints [LIT-097](LIT-097.md).
  The foreword is [LIT-421](LIT-421.md), read in full.
- **Chapter 8 and consciousness.** The foreword says Clark there allows
  only "a weaker sort of dependence of consciousness on the environment".
  Clark's fuller case that "the machinery of consciousness is (probably)
  still in the head" is a 2009 Mind paper, not held here.

A deeper reading should check whether Part II answers Rupert's dilemma on
the past-endorsement criterion and his generation-effect and interference
arguments directly, or only Adams and Aizawa. It should also check whether
chapter 8's "weaker dependence" bears on the group-consciousness question
of [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md). It carries no instruction for machine-learning
practice so far as the description shows.
