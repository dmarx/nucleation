# Part B, continued. Time

Sections 3–5 read identity stabilization as a filter over the frequencies of change, and found that the reading breaks for one kind of demand: "Fourier spectra cannot fully characterize event-triggered obligations, irreversible harms, or response deadlines" (CLAIM-1152, Active, demonstrated; §4). This part starts from that limit. §6 describes demands by their deadlines, and asks how organizations facing them can be compared. §7 turns to the governing arrangement itself, whose parts act and are revised at different speeds, and asks what those speeds must be for the arrangement to stay answerable.

Citation, standing and voice follow the conventions stated at the head of §3's part, and so do the marks for restatements, declined joins, joins filed in accounts or as claims, and filed gaps. Joins continue from JB6 and gaps from GB5. The models cited here are models. Where this part uses the readiness model of §9, the numbers are invented, and the model illustrates which of the draft's stipulations hold, and on what assumptions; it is not a test of them.

## 6. Demands have temporal structure

### 6.1 Readiness for a contingency, under intervention

Some demands are naturally stated as deadlines rather than frequencies: a hazard to be interrupted before an irreversible threshold, a supply disruption that allows some days for substitution, a workforce transition that takes years. The record's term for an organization's standing against such a demand is **scenario-conditioned response readiness**: "the probability that an organization operating under a specified policy can deliver an effective response to a specified contingency within its deadline" (TERM-115, Active; the wording is ChatGPT's, adopted at the status review of 2026-11-06). Formally,

  R^π_O(c, τ | s) = Pr_π(effective response by τ | do(c), s),

with c an imposed contingency, τ its deadline, s the organization's initial state and π the policy it actually follows. Three features of the definition carry the argument that follows.

- **It is defined under intervention.** "The do(c) is the point: this is readiness for a specified situation, imposed, not success conditional on the situations that happened to be observed. An organization that never met c has a value of R for it all the same." So readiness is not utilization or realized performance: "It is defined under intervention, so a record of what was done does not measure it."
- **It takes the policy as followed.** It is not operational availability, the chance that a resource is deployed before the deadline under the best procedure available (TERM-055). An organization can have every resource available and low readiness, "because its dispatch policy fails to mobilize them".
- **It is a family, not a number.** "No single scalar. The object is the family {R^π_O(c, τ_c | s) : c ∈ C}, one value per contingency and its deadline. Two organizations' readiness profiles can be incomparable, each more ready for some contingencies than the other."

The first feature connects this section to §3.2 and §4.1. An organization's ordinary record does not show its readiness, just as passive observation does not show what an intervention would expose (CLAIM-407, Active, demonstrated). Records do not identify it either. Records kept of each capacity a response needs "bound a reserve's readiness for a contingency between the sum of their probabilities less one less their number and the weakest of them, and identify it only when the capacities' dependence is known" (CLAIM-632, Active, demonstrated).

### 6.2 Readiness curves, and when two organizations are ranked

The motivating draft lets the deadline vary and writes R_i(S, t) as a curve over t, for demand i and organization state S. It then says that organizations "need not admit a universal ranking" and stops there. The record's note on that passage gives the condition the draft lacks. It is the assistant's, written as a dated note and not filed as a claim, because it is close to definitional:

"Since R_i(S, ·) is the distribution function of the response time, two organizations are ranked the same way at every deadline exactly when one curve lies at or above the other at every time (first-order dominance); where the curves cross, which is more ready is fixed only by the deadline" (TERM-115, note of 2026-11-23, the assistant's).

The model of §9 shows both cases (analysis/results.md, run of 2026-11-23; the numbers are invented). The regime that keeps a reserve covering the original route (II) dominates the regime that holds only what it uses (I) at every deadline. Regime I and a redesigned regime with a smaller reserve (III-b) cross. At t = 2, I is ready with probability 0.025 and III-b with 0, because III-b's staged route cannot finish in fewer than three periods. At t = 3 the order reverses (0.081 against 0.118), and III-b stays ahead from then on. Which of the two is "more ready" is a fact about the deadline, not about the organizations alone. In the model, II (with V, which equals it) is the most ready regime at every deadline. What the deadline decides is the order of other pairs, and, below, which regime is cheapest.

Two further steps follow. The draft marked them as a join (JB6: TERM-115's family with its note of 2026-11-23). Both restate entries: the first is TERM-115's "No single scalar" with its note's condition applied to each contingency in the family, and the second is CLAIM-1108's (§6.4). JB6 is a restatement of TERM-115 and CLAIM-1108.

- **Across a family of contingencies, a ranking needs dominance in every one.** TERM-115's object is a family over contingencies. Two organizations are ranked the same way for every contingency and every deadline exactly when one's curve dominates the other's for each contingency in the family. If one organization dominates for some contingencies and is dominated for others, no ranking exists without weights on the contingencies. That is TERM-115's "No single scalar", restated with the deadline free.
- **Any ranking beyond dominance imports a commitment.** Weights on contingencies, a choice of deadline, or a loss function on lateness all rank organizations whose curves cross. None of them is supplied by the curves; that is CLAIM-1108's point, that steering optimally "is relative to commitments the mathematics does not select" (CLAIM-1108, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). In §9's model the cheapest regime by long-run cost changes with the deadline, under one set of costs. Regime I is cheapest at τ = 2, the full reserve (II) from τ = 3 to 8, and the redesigned regimes at τ = 12 and 20. The break-even for keeping a reserve depends on the probability and loss of the contingency against the reserve's cost (analysis/README.md, items 5 and 6; the parameter is recorded in a note on QUESTION-395).

### 6.3 Deadlines shrink what can be met together

Readiness concerns one contingency at a time. Regulation faces several demands at once, and §2 described what an organization can meet jointly as a compatibility complex: the sets of demands that some attainable history meets, closed under taking subsets (CLAIM-347, Active, interpretive). The record holds what deadlines do to that complex:

"When demands are predicates on histories with deadlines, the sets of them an organization can jointly meet under its response architecture and budget form a downward-closed complex contained in the complex of the same demands without deadlines, so two demands can be compatible statically and incompatible in time, and a change in the response architecture, faster actuation, different sequencing, staged capacity or a new method, can enlarge the temporal complex without weakening any demand" (CLAIM-1084, Active, demonstrated). The idea is ChatGPT's (A44 §8). The containment step, and so the possibility of static compatibility with temporal incompatibility, is the assistant's.

The derivation is short. Every history that meets a demand by its deadline meets the same demand with no deadline, so the temporal complex K_{θ,B} lies inside the deadline-free complex K⁰_B under the same budget and safeguards. The containment can be strict. ChatGPT's factory can cut emissions and keep production, but may "lack the capacity to complete the conversion within the regulatory deadline while meeting uninterrupted supply requirements". The pair is jointly attainable without the deadline and not with it. A change of response architecture θ that adds attainable histories can enlarge the temporal complex for the same demands. By the record's result on hard constraints, that needs a change in the dynamics, not a constraint added at fixed dynamics (CLAIM-1121, Active, demonstrated). ChatGPT adds, and the claim keeps, that this is "one possible mechanism, not a guarantee of monotone expansion". The record also counts a deadline as a resource type of its own, distinct from a stock, a capacity, a rate limit, a cumulative expenditure or a reserve (TERM-105, Active). The motivating draft does not use either entry. The audit of the draft excluded CLAIM-1084 from the work because the draft does not state it. This paper states it.

Two Proposed cases show deadlines acting on what can be done together, both A's from the pasted synthesis on constraint architectures. Two cells that each need one unit of power for one minute "need a peak of 2 under a one-minute deadline and a peak of 1 under a two-minute one, unless a coupling between them forbids folding one before the other" (CASE-211, Proposed). And "a rescue squad that can serve either of two distant incidents cannot serve both at once, and serves both in turn only when the second's deadline leaves time for the first job and the travel between them" (CASE-209, Proposed; its times are the filer's).

**Readiness and the temporal complex are two views of one object.** The complex asks whether some admissible policy meets a set of demands by their deadlines. Readiness asks with what probability the policy actually followed meets one demand by its deadline when the contingency is imposed. The record already pairs existence with probability for demands without deadlines: "its compatibility complex says which sets of public demands some attainable history meets, the probability of meeting them under a model of participants' behaviour says how reliably the regime selects such a history, and a regime can improve either without the other" (CLAIM-1193, Active, demonstrated). CLAIM-1084 says the same holds with deadlines: "Under stochastic dynamics the existence test becomes a probability of joint satisfaction, as in CLAIM-1193." Reading TERM-115's readiness as the one-demand, imposed-contingency case of that probability, and so as the realization side of CLAIM-1084's temporal complex, restates two entries. CLAIM-1084 states the probability side, as above, and the record's viability kernel calls itself readiness's "set-valued, worst-case-over-Ω counterpart, made a viability requirement" (TERM-167, Proposed). The draft marked the reading as a join (JB7: TERM-115 with CLAIM-1084 and CLAIM-1193); it is a restatement of CLAIM-1084 and TERM-167. One caution keeps it a reading and not an identity. Readiness concerns one demand, an imposed contingency and the policy followed, while CLAIM-1193's probability is of joint satisfaction under a model of participants' behaviour. The record has declined a like identification elsewhere: that CLAIM-572's readiness is a case of TERM-115's "holds only once a deadline and a policy are chosen to make it hold" (SYNTHESIS-027, Proposed). On that reading, a set of demands in the temporal complex can still have low joint readiness, and enlarging the complex by redesign does not by itself raise readiness. §9's redesigned route without a reserve (III-a) is a model case: the route exists and is never affordable when needed, a result the record holds as the assistant's (CLAIM-1099, Active, demonstrated; promoted at the owner's word of 2026-11-24, "promote the ten").

### 6.4 No universal ranking, and no optimum, until commitments are declared

The motivating draft concludes that "temporal adequacy is therefore demand-relative". The record holds a stronger, and Proposed, form, as ChatGPT's objection to the owner's thesis, which the owner put at U16 as "regulations as constraints which operationalize a demand specification to optimally steer harmonious governance across an industrial ecosystem" (CLAIM-1201, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's; the claim's title words it "chosen so as to steer an industrial ecosystem optimally toward harmonious governance"):

"There is no optimal regulation until the demand specification, the instruments, the horizon and the trade-offs among demands are declared, and a regime is better judged by a profile of possibility, realization, resilience, accountability and cost than by a scalar, so steering an industrial ecosystem optimally is relative to commitments the mathematics does not select" (CLAIM-1108, Proposed, interpretive, a counter, ChatGPT's).

The claim "qualifies the thesis and does not reject it". It does not say regulation cannot be optimized: "Once the objective and the hard constraints are declared, A31 §6's programme is well posed." A note of the assistant's fills a step ChatGPT asserted. Regimes whose complexes each contain a face the other lacks are unordered under inclusion, so "even before realization, resilience and cost are added, the regimes need not have a best element" (CLAIM-1108, note of 2026-11-22, the assistant's).

§6.2 adds a temporal reason to the same conclusion. Even on a single dimension, readiness for one contingency, organizations are ranked independently of the deadline only under dominance. A declared deadline, or a declared weighting of deadlines, is one of the commitments CLAIM-1108 says the mathematics does not select. That this is a case of CLAIM-1108 restates the claim (JB6, §6.2).

## 7. The temporal architecture of governance

### 7.0 Levels of response, schematically

The motivating draft lays out four levels of industrial governance by horizon:

- operational controls (shutdowns, hazard responses, routine adjustments);
- reconfiguration (replacing suppliers, reallocating production, retraining);
- institutional processes (revising standards, reallocating authority, reconsidering classifications);
- the slow reproduction of knowledge, competences, public commitments and ecological conditions.

It adds that these "need not be arranged as a single hierarchy of command". A slow rule may authorize an immediate intervention, and a fast event may reveal a problem that needs institutional revision.

No entry on the record holds this hierarchy as such (GB5, the levels of governance by horizon, an open item for the owner). The nearest are three, and each is weaker than a hierarchy.

- **Five demand horizons.** ChatGPT's table of "immediate, operational, developmental, institutional, intergenerational" horizons, each with its capacity and failure mode, is a step of CLAIM-1104 (Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's).
- **Schematic levels of will.** A hierarchy of "effective volition" by timescale, recorded on CLAIM-331 (Active, interpretive) with its own caption: "Schematic levels, not a demonstrated RG hierarchy" (note of 2026-10-18).
- **Speed and authority.** A Proposed claim that, in a political collective, components that change at different speeds differ in "which forms of influence each can accumulate, exercise and keep, and so which of them holds authority over the others" (CLAIM-423, Proposed, interpretive). Against it the record notes that "Slowness alone supplies no authority or motivational hierarchy" (CLAIM-331, note of 2026-10-18). Whether the authority one process has over others goes "with the slower variable, the faster one, or neither" is open (QUESTION-440, Open).

A reading from biology defines one such level without making the levels a hierarchy: Bich and colleagues "define biological regulation as second-order control by a subsystem the organism produces but which runs at its own rate and stoichiometry, is triggered by perturbations and switches the constitutive regime between viable states" (THEORY-2221, Proposed). Reading an industrial regulator that way is this paper's analogy.

So the levels in this section are a schema for the argument. They are not a result.

### 7.1 Execution speed and revision speed

The motivating draft's central distinction here is between **execution speed**, how fast a governing commitment is applied, and **revision speed**, how fast the commitment itself can be changed. It credits the distinction to CLAIM-711 as support, without saying that the claim is Proposed. The claim is ChatGPT's, from another exchange, and it is an objection to one of the owner's claims.

The owner's claim: "Automation reduces procedural friction, yet procedural friction is where an opportunity to intervene in a process is exposed and perhaps what such an opportunity consists in, so removing friction can remove the points at which an unfolding decision can still be interrupted" (CLAIM-710, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). It keeps both strengths the owner gave, "exposes" and the hedged "characterizes".

ChatGPT's objection: "A process with no procedural friction can still be interruptible: standing constraints set slowly in advance and enforced by mechanisms faster than the process can foreclose alternatives keep it open to intervention without delaying it, so an opportunity to intervene does not consist in friction" (CLAIM-711, Proposed, interpretive, a counter, ChatGPT's). Its condition: "The slow authority must have fast enough *actuators*, even if its deliberative processes remain slow." It objects to the "characterizes" reading of CLAIM-710 and leaves the "exposes" reading standing. What carries the opportunity to intervene, on this account, is a relation of speeds: the enforcing mechanism must act before the process forecloses alternatives, "whatever the speed of the deliberation that set the limit."

The record's note on the exchange behind this paper finds ChatGPT's later uses of the claim faithful. A42: "the rate of executing a decision can be separated from the rate of revising the rules that authorize it". A44: "Preauthorize fast actions that remain within the jurisdiction of the slower governing structure. ... The commitment is slow; its application is fast." (CLAIM-711, note of 2026-11-23.) What holds the claim back is the owner's: whether an independent execution gate counts as procedural friction (TERM-125, Proposed) decides whether CLAIM-711's case removes friction or relocates it. Promoting CLAIM-711 "would also make its concession due on CLAIM-710, the owner's own claim" (CLAIM-711, note of 2026-11-23).

CLAIM-711 also states its own limit: "That standing constraints suffice. They interrupt only the transitions anticipated when they were set. A novel failure still needs a channel through which someone can stop the process". A44 states the same residual case for an emergency outside the preauthorized categories, which needs "an escalation or revision pathway rather than merely faster execution of obsolete rules". The record also holds that the faithful use is about interruptibility, not lock-in. Its account of the exchange lists "'Stable governance against lock-in', cited to CLAIM-711 (A33 §6)" among what is "Not stated on the record": "CLAIM-711 concerns interruptibility, not lock-in or the means to revise constraints" (SYNTHESIS-122, Proposed). This paper keeps to the faithful use. A regulator's proposal read on the record states the same arrangement as policy: authority "defined in advance and technically enforced, with human oversight counted only where it is effective", and "where oversight cannot keep pace the agent's authority, scope or velocity should be reduced" (THEORY-1931, Proposed; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority on agentic AI in banking).

**Execution and revision as separately maintained capacities.** The record holds, Active, a family of separations that this distinction belongs to. The first three come from the owner's essay (the work organization-of-will), not from an exchange; the fourth from an exchange of 2026-10-26:

- "Maintaining a regime of effective action and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes are not the same achievement" (CLAIM-099, Active, interpretive);
- "An operative regime, the governing organization among regimes, and the capacity to transform that organization can persist or fail separately" (CLAIM-025, Active, interpretive);
- "Stability does not entail viability: a system can occupy a stable regime while losing access to trajectories that keep satisfying its constraints" (CLAIM-079, Active, demonstrated);
- "An arrangement of institutions can keep, or increase, its capacity for effective action while its capacity to be effectively corrected by others degrades, because the two are maintained by different relations, so the first is no evidence of the second" (CLAIM-462, Active, interpretive).

Reading fast execution of standing rules as effective action, and the rule-revising level as the capacity to govern among regimes and to be corrected, restates the record. CLAIM-099, CLAIM-025 and CLAIM-462 each state the separation, and CLAIM-711's note records A42's "the rate of executing a decision can be separated from the rate of revising the rules that authorize it" as faithful to the claim. The draft marked the reading as a join (JB8: CLAIM-711 with CLAIM-099, CLAIM-025 and CLAIM-462); it is a restatement of those entries. On that reading the four separations predict the failure the motivating draft warns of: an arrangement that gets faster and more reliable at applying its commitments while the relations through which they could be revised decay, with the first no evidence of the second.

### 7.2 When the slow level dominates the fast

The worry has a sharper form on the record, and it is *supposed*: held only on the condition that an analogy carries. It is ChatGPT's, filed after the owner pointed to the record's "timescale separation":

"Where a slowly revised governing level (a statute, an objective, a classification) constrains a fast process, the fast process can change the world before the slow level can be revised, so those it harms have no route to the rule as fast as its application reaches them, and the rule dominates them without anyone actively intervening; where the slow level is open to revision on demand, the same separation of timescales keeps the fast process accountable instead" (CLAIM-730, Proposed, interpretive, supposed, ChatGPT's).

It is supposed because "it carries CLAIM-478's scale separation, a dynamical result, into institutions by analogy". Its defeater names the weak point: "that a governing rule is not a slow variable in CLAIM-478's sense, because what it constrains does not settle relative to it". That is the same carriage §3.1 makes, and the lead filed for it there would test both (LEAD-222, Open; the draft's GB3).

The record has one model of it (CASE-316, Proposed; its result is CLAIM-879, Proposed, demonstrated). It carries out a design filed earlier: two arrangements whose overseer has the same authority and resources and which "differ only in how fast the acting institution can make its actions irreversible" (CASE-285, Proposed). In a queueing model, the overseer kept the same authority, information and reviewers while the acting institution's speed varied. At ten times the speed, "the share of objectionable actions stopped before they became irreversible fell from 0.99 to 0.40", and to 0.04 when speed also multiplied the volume of actions. Precommitted limits, CLAIM-711's arrangement, "brought back about the share they foresaw", keeping "0.92 to 0.99" of the speed's benefit. Outside what they foresaw nothing was won back unless the overseer detected it in time by the ordinary route. Pauses won back capacity only by giving up the speed, and none at all when volume rose (CASE-316; CLAIM-711, note of 2026-11-22).

What the model shows and does not show is recorded on CLAIM-730 (note of 2026-11-22). "The second clause is thereby shown possible in a model." The first is not tested: "the model has no affected party and never revises a limit". And where escalations saturated the fixed reviewers, "the slow level kept the fast process answerable only while it had the capacity to hear what it held." The draft found no lead that tests the first clause (its gap GB7). One is now filed, with its design fixed (LEAD-216, Open, stage specified, the assistant's). It keeps CASE-316's model unchanged and adds a class of actions inside the limits that harms an affected party, a party who petitions to revise the limit after its third harm, a revision process of its own speed, and an arm in which a petition suspends the class at once. CLAIM-730's first clause "predicts that, at fixed τ_rev, the harm to P before revision rises with s"; as a negative control, with τ_rev → 0 "the harm must equal the slow arm's".

### 7.3 How fast a governing reference must move

Two Active results bound how fast governance must be revisable to meet a demand by its deadline. Both are ChatGPT's in wording and checked by the assistant.

**A necessary half.** "If practice must stay within ε of a governing reference that can be revised at most at rate v, a demand to bring practice within δ of a target at distance 1 by deadline H can be met only if vH ≥ 1 − δ − ε, so the same rate of revision is adequate to a demand with a long horizon and inadequate to one with a short horizon, and a fixed reference (v = 0) meets no demand farther than δ + ε" (CLAIM-1010, Active, demonstrated). The bound is tight when the practice can track freely within its band. Its limit, already used in §4, is that "it says nothing of what a fast-revisable reference costs against transient fluctuations".

**A sufficient half.** "If an organization's operation relaxes at rate γ toward a configuration h(θ) set by a governance parameter θ that changes at rate at most V, with h Lipschitz with constant L, then its distance from the governed configuration stays within LV/γ once it starts within that, so a governance change of size Δθ by deadline H keeps the organization within a tolerance ε if LΔθ/(γH) ≤ ε; the condition is sufficient, not necessary" (CLAIM-1051, Active, demonstrated). It names four obstructions: the reference moving too fast for its realization; "operation slowing, so that the permissible retuning rate falls"; governance unable to change θ fast enough for the deadline; and the cases the inequality does not settle.

The first obstruction has a name in the record's readings. An open system "can tip away from an attractor that exists, keeps its stability and keeps its basin at every frozen value of its parameter, when the attractor moves faster than the system can track it" (THEORY-1079, Active), and "rate-induced tipping is the failure of that time-scale separation, and it can occur while the parameter itself moves slowly, if the attractor is sensitive to it, and with no slowing of any mode" (THEORY-1052, Proposed). CLAIM-1192 cites the second.

The record already notes ChatGPT's way around retuning, preauthorization, as CLAIM-711 (CLAIM-1051, "What it shows"). Two further consequences follow, and the record now holds both.

- **What preauthorization leaves bounded.** Preauthorization replaces revision only for the transitions anticipated when the limits were set (CLAIM-711). For a demand outside them the response must go through revision, and CLAIM-1010's floor applies: no response is in time unless vH ≥ 1 − δ − ε. So the speed of the revision pathway, not the speed of execution, bounds readiness for unanticipated demands. This restates two entries: A44's residual case, recorded on CLAIM-711 as an emergency that needs "an escalation or revision pathway rather than merely faster execution of obsolete rules" (CLAIM-711, note of 2026-11-23), and CLAIM-1010's bound, whose substitution is immediate. The draft marked it as a join (JB9: CLAIM-711's limit with CLAIM-1010); it is a restatement of those entries. It concerns response to the unanticipated, not lock-in, which is the use of CLAIM-711 that SYNTHESIS-122 declines (§7.1). §9's novel-demand arm is a model of the same thing (analysis/README.md, item 7). There a review that needs capacity cannot start under the rule that holds only what is used, and the demand is excised by default.
- **A slow mode is cheap to push and slow to retune.** CLAIM-1192 (§5.2) says a slowly relaxing mode is cheaper to redirect by direct input through the same channel. CLAIM-1051 says that when operation relaxes slowly toward a governed configuration, the governing parameter may move only slowly if operation is to stay within tolerance: V ≤ εγ/L. Both hold. They concern different channels: pushing the mode directly, and moving the configuration it relaxes toward. CLAIM-1051's condition is "sufficient, not necessary", so from it alone a small γ only fails to guarantee a fast retuning. The first draft joined the two here (join JB10: CLAIM-1192 with CLAIM-1051). The join passed the record's admission test by derivation, with a discriminating prediction, and is now filed as a claim in the assistant's voice: in one system, ẋ = −γ(x − h(θ)) + bu, "a smaller γ makes a direct push cheaper and a change of the configuration slower", since moving the configuration by D with operation kept within ε of it throughout "needs H ≥ (D/ε − 2)/γ", the necessary half CLAIM-1051 lacks, "so an organization whose operation has slowed is easier to redirect by intervention and slower to govern by changing its rules" (CLAIM-1082, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's; integrated by SYNTHESIS-123, Proposed). Its prediction: where operation slows and the input channel b stays fixed, direct interventions through that channel get cheaper and tolerance-keeping changes of the configuration take longer, both in proportion to 1/γ (ibid.). Which channel a regulator has is the question §5.2 put to entrenchment.

### 7.4 The design principle, in the record's terms

The motivating draft ends §7 with a principle: "Slowly maintained commitments should enable rapid legitimate action, while remaining connected to effective procedures for recognizing when the commitments themselves require change." It credits the first half to CLAIM-711 and calls the second "the crucial additional requirement".

In the record's terms, the principle has three parts with different standings, and it is no stronger than the weakest.

1. **Fast application of slow commitments keeps a process interruptible without delaying it,** for the transitions the commitments anticipate. This is CLAIM-711, Proposed and held on TERM-125, with CASE-316 showing it in a model.
2. **What it does not anticipate needs a revision pathway fast enough for the deadline.** This is CLAIM-711's own limit, bounded by CLAIM-1010 (Active, demonstrated), as the note on CLAIM-711 records (JB9, a restatement). Without that pathway, the slow level can dominate those the fast process harms (CLAIM-730, supposed).
3. **The pathway must be able to recognize that a demand has arisen.** An organization whose filter classes objections to its categories as noise will not open the pathway at all (CLAIM-1040, Active, interpretive; §4.1). The record holds no mechanism for revising that classification; it holds the question (QUESTION-522, Open, the assistant's; the draft's GB2). CASE-316's saturated reviewers add a material condition: the slow level must have the capacity to hear what it holds.

The motivating draft's "crucial additional requirement" is therefore the record's limit on CLAIM-711, read through the Active bounds of §7.3 and the Active claim of §4.1. What the record does not supply is any case of a real institution in which these parts were observed together. §8 takes up whether the capacity to keep all three can itself be kept, and §13 the governance of the revising level.

### 7.5 Governance as allocation over time

*Added 2026-11-25 from governance-of-becoming's superseded file 03 (A49's Article 3, `superseded/03-cost-of-becoming.md`; old §3.5.1, with the entry its §3.4.2 cites); standings as of 2026-11-25.*

The architecture of §§7.1–7.4 runs on resources, and the exchange behind this paper said how they are divided over time. ChatGPT's A52 §5, in the same exchange, "Explain First Contact RL": "Organizational governance is partly the intertemporal allocation of scarce resources among inquiry, response and the upkeep of the structures that make both possible, slow organizational modes arise partly as ways of amortizing inquiry and coordination costs, and adaptive governance must keep the capacity to reallocate between answering existing questions and learning to ask and answer new ones" (CLAIM-1060, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). The claim's summary says its mechanism is already held: "stored specification moves inquiry cost away from the moment a response is urgent and also makes revision costly", which is CLAIM-475 (Active, interpretive; the owner's thesis, its stored-inquiry clause drawn by ChatGPT at A17 and A18 of the exchange of 2026-10-27) and CLAIM-761 (Active, interpretive; the owner's thesis, in ChatGPT's wording), with TERM-085's note on Hutchins, that preparation "moves the cost of search ahead of the deadline" (§10.3). What A52 adds is the governance reading.

Its second clause, from amortization to slow modes, is an abduction the assistant filed (ARG-238, Proposed), and it is narrowed. "Slow" there means slow in the economic sense, formed at a cost and used cheaply many times, and not slow to relax, since "a slow variable can save no work, and a result reused cheaply need not be an attractor of any dynamics" (CLAIM-755, Proposed, demonstrated; ChatGPT's, A39 §3 of "Topological protection explained"). So CLAIM-1060's slow modes are not this paper's slow modes of §§3–5, which are dynamical. That some amortizing structures are also dynamically slow "is a further fact" (CLAIM-1060, Standing), and the record leaves it open.

The clause that matters most for §8 is the last: the capacity to reallocate. One Proposed entry gives a mechanism by which it is lost while operation succeeds. "Where the reactive work that restores a failing condition and the engineering that would remove its cause draw on one budget and the reactive work takes precedence, an organization can stay operationally viable while its capacity to transform itself is consumed, and a standing reservation of capacity for transformation, enforced by returning the overflow to those whose activity generates it, breaks the loop" (CLAIM-tmpjoaj3, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's, A34 §7 of the exchange "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity": "Immediate demands consume the capacity to prevent future demands"). Its specimen is Google SRE's cap on operational work at half an engineer's time, with the excess returned to the development teams (CASE-tmphtt17, Proposed, a specimen read from the SRE book). Here the cost of the present is paid in the capacity to change, and the present output does not show the payment, as in §9.3. The record does not join CLAIM-tmpjoaj3 to CLAIM-577's ratchet, and this paper does not either: CLAIM-577 loses a transition through the rule that sets the budget, while CLAIM-tmpjoaj3 loses it through precedence within a budget. SYNTHESIS-124's note of 2026-11-25 sets the two beside CLAIM-921 as three distinct mechanisms, and names a shared form of remedy, funding held apart from current use, without asserting it; whether that is one remedy or three of one shape is left to the next synthesis review, with ADR-075's test.
