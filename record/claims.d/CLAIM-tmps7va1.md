# CLAIM-tmps7va1: Sharing a kind is symmetric and blind to which work is the source, so the October thesis cannot say a translation is of its original, against the line's own directed, graded fidelity and identity through descent

## The objection

[CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md): what makes the spoken, translated and visual realizations of a
teasing remark one act is "a shared invariant", "a common structure of
speaker–audience relations, normative expectations, and permitted
conversational responses", "not resemblance to an original". [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)
grounds identity in "relational organization rather than in any expression
or encoding", and its defeat condition is stated on "Human judgements of
shared communicative kind across translations, recastings and media".

Realizing a common kind is symmetric, and for any one kind K, "both
realize K" is an equivalence relation: if x and y realize K, so do y and
x, and so does any third realization of K. It carries
no direction and no history. Two cases that the kind criterion cannot tell
apart make the point.

- S: a friend at breakfast says "You blow it all on sex and booze."
- T: a translation of S into French, made from S.
- I: at another table, with no knowledge of S, someone teases a friend in
  French about spending, in an utterance of the same kind.

S, T and I realize one kind. T and I agree on every relational invariant
[CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md) asks for, and a judge asked "same communicative kind?" answers
the same of (S, T) and (S, I). On the kind criterion I is as good a
rendering of S as T is. But T is a translation of S and I is not. What
separates them is descent: T was made from S.

The line holds that this matters, in two Proposed theses no `objects_to`
links to the October theses.

- **[CLAIM-106](CLAIM-106.md)**: fidelity is "a graded, potentially asymmetric property of
  transport, not an equivalence relation", and the manuscript's transport
  "is intentionally directed". Sharing a kind is symmetric, and
  as [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s defeat test uses it, a yes-or-no one.
- **[CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md)**: a text's identity across retellings includes "continuity
  of transmission", which is why "a distant retelling can be recognizably
  descended from an original it no longer resembles", and "descent is not
  similarity".

[CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s fidelity clause speaks of distinctions that "persist through
those transformations", which presupposes a source and a transformation.
So direction is in the fidelity clause, but it is presupposed, not
supplied: the identity clause and the defeat test have no place for it.

The record touches this in two places and states it in neither.
[CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md)'s second defeat disjunct, "judges reliably privilege one
realization, an original", is an empirical test of whether judges care
about originals. This objection is not empirical: "is a translation of" is
a fact of descent whatever judges privilege. The workbench entry for the
2026-10-08 evening session lists "descent against similarity ([CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md))"
among the manuscript's distinctions dropped by omission, in prose only.

## What it does not say

It does not say descent must be part of fidelity, nor that the kind
criterion is wrong as a criterion of kind. Two utterances can be the same
act without one being a translation of the other. It says [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md), as
stated, cannot say what makes a rendering a rendering of its source, and
that the line's earlier theses say it must.

## What would answer it

- A directed clause beside the kind criterion: fidelity as the
  preservation of a kind along a transformation from a given source, with
  descent required ([CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md)) and the distortion measured in one
  direction ([CLAIM-106](CLAIM-106.md)). The kind criterion would then fix what is
  preserved and the transport would fix what it is preserved from.
- Or an argument that descent is irrelevant to fidelity. Its consequence,
  that I is as faithful to S as T, would then have to be stated and owned,
  and [CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md) and [CLAIM-106](CLAIM-106.md) revised.
