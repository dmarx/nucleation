# THEORY-tmpaw08o: Russian military writers of 2015–2020 use 'hybrid war' for a category of war marked off by its objective, reshaping a target state's governance, geostrategic orientation and worldview, in which every action up to conventional military force is subordinate to an information campaign, and not, as in Western usage, for a set of means below the threshold of armed conflict

## Source

Clark (2020), [LIT-023](../literature.d/LIT-023.md), "Russian Hybrid Warfare" (Institute for the Study
of War, September 2020), read in [NOTE-012](../notes.d/NOTE-012.md).

## What was actually shown

**The definition (pp. 8, 11, 15).** Hybrid war is "a strategic-level effort
to shape the governance and geostrategic orientation of a target state in
which all actions, up to and including the use of conventional military
forces in regional conflicts, are subordinate to an information campaign".
It is not a descriptor for all future war, not an operational approach
inside conventional war, not a set of means and not "gray zone" activity
(p. 15). The core ("all efforts, including military operations, are
subordinate to an information campaign") is footnoted to one Military
Thought article (Chekinov & Bogdanov, January 2017); the objective half
comes from Bartosh and Kiselev (nn. 22–25).

**The threshold is the objective (p. 16).** A conflict is a hybrid war only
if the aggressor aims to reshape the target's strategic orientation and
"worldview". "Hybrid means" can be used outside one.

**Conventional force is inside it (p. 20).** Russian analysts are said to
see conventional forces as most often "the concluding step", frequently
under "diplomatic cover" such as peacekeeping.

**The reversal (pp. 21–22).** Kinetic operations now support information
campaigns and "cannot succeed" without them. The figure that non-military
efforts have "four times the impact" of military ones is second-hand:
Bartosh citing a closed Gerasimov speech (n. 82).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The writers could have defined
hybrid war by its means, as the Western literature Clark criticises does,
or kept conventional war outside it. On Clark's reading of his sources
they do neither.

## What this does not say

- **That the definition is doctrine.** No single doctrinal text is quoted;
  the skim's "quotes the Russian military definition" is corrected in
  [NOTE-012](../notes.d/NOTE-012.md).
- **That it is the institutional view.** That rests on the standing of the
  outlets and authors, with no sample and no count of dissent (C2,
  weak–moderate). A handful of pieces carries much of the weight.
- **That Russia avoids conventional war.** The narrower claim is that it
  avoids "major conventional wars between great powers" and force-on-force
  conflict with NATO (pp. 12–13). The 2022 invasion bears on the reported
  view that conventional war is a "legacy" form, and does not cleanly
  falsify the thesis as stated.
- **That the US policy recommendations follow.** They are ISW advocacy
  with no assessment of cost or escalation (C10).
- **That the Kremlin's belief that the West wages hybrid war on Russia is
  sincere.** Clark argues it by elimination and offers a fallback (the
  "fake tail … wagging the dog", p. 18). That question is the subject of
  [THEORY-tmpiqvkw](THEORY-tmpiqvkw.md).
