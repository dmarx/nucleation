summary: >-
  Van Oosten (2002) sketches the history of realizability from Kleene's
  1945 paper to the effective topos. He states Kleene's definition clause
  by clause: a realizer of an existential is a pair of a witness and a
  realizer of its instance, a realizer of an implication or universal a
  program, a realizer of a true atomic sentence the number 0. Realizability
  was "designed to handle 'information' about formulas rather than
  proofs", and Kleene set aside Heyting's proof interpretation to build it.
  Kreisel's modified realizability (1959) types the realizers by the
  formula's form, and in Hyland's effective topos Kleene's realizability is
  the truth definition for arithmetic. Read as the stand-in for Kleene 1945
  ([LIT-tmp9cdlq](LIT-tmp9cdlq.md)) for [LEAD-tmp3xevc](../leads.d/LEAD-tmp3xevc.md); it bears on [CLAIM-tmpnookt](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpnookt.md) and on the

# LIT-tmpegqz0: Realizability: a historical essay

Jaap van Oosten (2002). *Mathematical Structures in Computer Science*
12(3): 239–263. DOI-10.1017/S0960129502003626. Author's copy:
https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~ooste110/realizability/history.pdf

## Key takeaways

- Kleene's 1945 definition, clause by clause (pp. 5–6), and its soundness
  theorem for Heyting arithmetic.
- Realizability handles information about formulas, not proofs, and is
  stated in classical terms a classical mathematician can check (p. 4).
- Typed realizability (Kreisel 1959) predates "formulae as types" (p. 11).
- In the effective topos, truth for arithmetic is Kleene's realizability
  (p. 15).
