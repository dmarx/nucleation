## What A got wrong

- **The CSP dichotomy.** It classifies decision problems over fixed finite
  languages, not "computational systems", and says nothing about how
  solutions connect. The polymorphism algebra A calls "the algebraic
  component" does not decide navigation: in Gopalan et al.'s phrase, the
  boundaries "cut across Boolean clones". The lead checked by enumeration
  that two languages with the same trivial clone fall on opposite sides.
- **Eppstein.** NP-completeness of flat folding is earlier work. A missed
  the paper's Galois-theory result (folded shapes not expressible in
  radicals), which qualifies A's dismissal of Galois groups.
- **Resource composition.** A's cost laws do not come from Coecke, Fritz
  and Spekkens, whose conversions are free. A's "resource-graded" category
  is the standard graded (locally graded) category. Multiparameter
  persistence is of wild type, a stronger limit than A states.
- **Akitaya et al.** "Infinitesimal" should be small finite motions from
  flat, and the result holds for general crease patterns.
- **The askable set.** A presented it as new; CLAIM-475 holds it.
