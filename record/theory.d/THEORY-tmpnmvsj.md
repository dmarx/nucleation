# THEORY-tmpnmvsj: Meaningful human control over an autonomous system, on Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven's account, requires that the system track the human moral reasons and facts relevant in the circumstances, and that its outcomes trace to at least one human in its design or use who understands its capabilities and that others may hold them answerable; presence in the loop is not control

Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven (2018), [LIT-2705](../literature.d/LIT-2705.md), read in
[NOTE-tmp6l52i](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6l52i.md).

## The account

Tracking (p. 7): "a decision-making system should demonstrably and
verifiably be responsive to the human moral reasons relevant in the
circumstances". Tracing (p. 9): outcomes traceable to "at least one human
agent in the design history or use context" who understands the system's
capabilities and "that others may have legitimate moral reactions toward
them because of how the system affects the world and the role they
occupy." Both are necessary, not sufficient (p. 8). "simple human presence
or 'being in the loop' is not a sufficient condition for being in control"
(p. 4).

## What it does not show

- **Where blame lands** when the conditions fail; only that a gap opens
  ("no agent satisfies the tracing condition", p. 11).
- **A worked civil application**; the driving section is a sketch.
