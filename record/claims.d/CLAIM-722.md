# CLAIM-722: Succeeding in making a remedy unreachable gains a wrongdoer no normative power over what is owed: a claim still valid under the standard persists while the correctability of its fitting remedy falls to zero, and an acceptance or settlement reached under the conditions the violation created does not by that fact authorize the violation

## The claim

A85 §5 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), with
r_i^N = 1 meaning that i holds right r under N and α_r the capability to
obtain its protection or remedy: an institution can leave r_i^N unchanged while
α_r falls, and "In an extreme case", r_i^N = 1 and α_r ≈ 0. "The moral
right remains valid. Its protection becomes practically inaccessible."

A87 §2 puts it in Hohfeld's terms, separating CanPhysically(A, x),
Power_N(A, Δr_B) and Immunity_N(B, A, Δr_B): "**The wrongdoer changes the
world without necessarily possessing the normative power to change what
the injured party is owed.**" A87 §10's case: contractor A is bound not to
demolish B's structure without B's authorization, and demolishes it. The
state goes s₀ → s₁, but "it does not follow that"
Power_N(A, ReplaceObligation) = 1. If restoration is impossible and
compensation feasible, α_J for restoration "may be low or zero even while
B's normative claim remains valid. We should not infer that because a
particular remedy has become impossible, the original violation has been
retroactively legitimized."

A90 V gives the thesis: "**A wrongful act does not become legitimate
merely because it makes the restoration of the violated entitlement
impossible.**" And A90 VII its consequence for consent obtained
afterwards: of the histories proposal → authorization → change and
unilateral change → foreclosed alternatives → acceptance, "The claimant's
later acceptance does not necessarily retroactively authorize the earlier
interference."

Three parts, then:

1. **Persistence.** A claim valid under N persists through the loss of
   its fitting remedy. What changes is correctability ([TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)), not the
   claim.
2. **No power gained.** Producing that loss is not an exercise of a
   normative power, so it gives the wrongdoer none over what is owed,
   including the power to substitute its own terms.
3. **No retroactive authorization.** An acceptance or settlement reached
   under conditions the violation produced does not, by being accepted,
   authorize the violation. It may still settle the remedy.

The scope is A90 V's: "The persistence thesis applies to the claims that
remain valid under \(N\), not to every legal relation irrespective of
circumstances." Property can be validly transferred and duties
discharged; those are changes by a valid power, which [CLAIM-743](CLAIM-743.md) keeps
apart from suppression.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)** (Active) is the causal half this rests on: the
  intervention changes what correction would take "while the standards
  that govern that outcome stay the same". This claim adds what stays the
  same on the injured party's side.
- **[CASE-170](../cases.d/CASE-170.md)** (Active), the East Wing demolished before the challenge
  was decided, has the structure: the remedy left was to stop new
  construction, "not to keep the old building". Whether any claim
  persisted there is a legal question the case does not settle, so it is
  cited, not taken as grounds.
- **[CLAIM-716](CLAIM-716.md)**, remedial hold-up: the breach lowers the injured
  party's disagreement point and gives the breacher leverage over the
  settlement. Part 3 here is the normative complement: the leverage is
  real, and the settlement it produces does not authorize the breach.
- **[CLAIM-734](CLAIM-734.md)**: an irreversible act converts consent-based
  protection into compensation in practice. "In practice" is part 1's
  α; this claim says the conversion is practical, not normative.
- **[CLAIM-720](CLAIM-720.md)**: inalienability as a limit on powers. This claim
  needs no inalienability premise; it holds for any claim still valid
  under N.
- The second wrong A87 §10 names, "Litigation by attrition", is the
  owner's siege, [CLAIM-748](CLAIM-748.md).

## What it does not say

- Not what remedy is owed once restoration is impossible. A87 §10: "Nor
  should we infer that every possible substitute remedy is adequate.
  Those are separate normative questions."
- Not that the injured party may refuse every settlement; only that
  accepting one does not authorize what preceded it.
- Not that the claim's persistence makes the arrangement adequate. A85 §5:
  "The right's persistence is not the institution's adequacy".
