summary: >-
  León (2023). A we-identity is one a subject has as a group member, is at times aware of, endorses, and cares about. Self-categorization theory explains we-identities by depersonalization, and phenomenologists (Zahavi, Salice) have adopted it as an account of the "we" in general. But the we-identities of close relationships arise by mutual individualization: partners grasp each other as non-substitutable and complementary. León proposes a socially extended account, drawing on Husserl and integrationist extended-mind criteria.

# LIT-1721: Being one of us: we-identities and self-categorization theory

León, F. (2023). Being one of us: we-identities and self-categorization theory. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*, 25(1), 97–121. doi:10.1007/s11097-023-09923-0.

Read in full in [NOTE-1309](../notes.d/NOTE-1309.md).

## Why this is here

It quotes Turner et al. 1987 ([LIT-1723](LIT-1723.md)) at second hand: pp. 29, 42, 44–46, 49–51, 66, 103 and 204, including the claim that group size is no theoretical restriction. It also tests the theory's claim to be a general account of group belonging against a case it fits badly. That bears on the record's self and social-ontology topics, and on [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md)'s We-mode collective emotion.
