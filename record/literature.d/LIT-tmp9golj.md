# LIT-tmp9golj: Examining Domains of Psychological Flexibility and Inflexibility as Treatment Mechanisms in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: A Comprehensive Systematic and Meta-Analytic Review

Jenna A. Macri and Ronald D. Rogge (2024), *Clinical Psychology Review*
110:102432 — DOI-10.1016/j.cpr.2024.102432

## Key takeaways

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the
work says about itself.*

- **Scale.** "Analyses of 77 records (67 unique studies; Ntotal = 9123
  participants)" from searches of several databases.
- **Both sides of the model move.** ACT "led to reduced inflexibility
  (i.e., lowered global inflexibility, lack of present moment awareness,
  cognitive fusion, experiential avoidance, self-as-content, & inaction)
  and increased flexibility (i.e., committed action/contact with values,
  global flexibility/acceptance, & defusion)". The changes held against
  waitlist and active treatments and "were significantly linked to
  corresponding drops in psychological distress".
- **Moderators.** Student samples, excluding clinically symptomatic
  people, and comparison with other active treatments weakened the
  effects. Individual ACT and excluding people in extreme crisis
  strengthened them.
- **Recommendations.** Measure flexibility and inflexibility as distinct
  mechanisms, with multidimensional scales (CompACT, MPFI). Broaden
  outcomes to well-being, assess repeatedly during treatment, study
  non-specific factors such as the alliance, and test mechanisms in
  effectiveness studies.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the current quantitative
test of the ACT process model. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries
no instruction for machine-learning practice.

`Deferred` because it was not read (see the status note).

What it tests, from the abstract. The six inflexibility domains it lists
are the six inflexibility processes of the ACT model stated in the 2006
review ([LIT-tmpxkxq0](LIT-tmpxkxq0.md)), one of which is the experiential avoidance introduced
in 1996 ([LIT-tmpsjur3](LIT-tmpsjur3.md)). Its recommendation to measure flexibility and
inflexibility separately, with multidimensional scales, answers the
measurement complaint of Kashdan and Rottenberg ([LIT-tmpt6rok](LIT-tmpt6rok.md)) in part.
They objected to global self-report, and this review still pools
self-report scales, only more finely divided.

No `extends` relation is declared until it is read. The abstract shows what
it tests, but not which works it builds on. A reading should add `extends`
to the 2006 review if, as expected, its model is the one tested.

What a reading should check:

- Whether the mediation is temporal. The abstract says changes "were
  significantly linked" to drops in distress. The 2006 review's own caveat
  was that mediators were often measured after outcomes had already moved.
- Whether flexibility and inflexibility separate as factors, which is the
  open question the 2006 review left on the AAQ's single factor.
- How the effect against active treatments compares with the effect against
  waitlist. The abstract says the active comparison weakened the effects.

Hayes and Hofmann's process-based therapy (2019), filed in parallel by
another agent, is the programme this kind of mechanism meta-analysis serves.

**Priority for a reading: high.** It is the evidence a THEORY on
psychological inflexibility would rest on.
