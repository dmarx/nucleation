# CLAIM-tmpevykb: Jurisdiction, not the objective, sets what an optimizer can harm

## The claim

A6 §3: "There is nothing inherently problematic about wanting to produce
paperclips. ... The problem arises when an objective acquires authority
over decisions for which it is not an adequate normative criterion." And
"Even seemingly benevolent objectives can become pathological when
granted unrestricted jurisdiction. This suggests that the search for the
*correct* objective function may itself be misguided as a general
solution to alignment."

A6 §4, after defining the term: "The objective alone tells us remarkably
little about the consequences of the process." And: "A relatively
unintelligent optimization process with enormous jurisdiction can be more
socially consequential than a superintelligent process with tightly
constrained jurisdiction. This would make the distribution of causal
authority at least as fundamental an alignment consideration as
intelligence or motivation."

Three theses, in order of strength:

1. the same objective has different consequences under different
   jurisdictions;
2. benevolence of the objective does not prevent overreach;
3. jurisdiction is at least as important a variable as the optimizer's
   capability or motivation.

The first two are close to definitional once jurisdiction is defined; the
third is the claim's empirical content.

## What it does not say

- **That objectives do not matter.** Within a jurisdiction the objective's
  adequacy is the whole question ([TERM-108](../terms.d/TERM-108.md)'s proxies).
- **That intelligence does not matter.** The comparison is "at least as
  fundamental", and a capable optimizer can widen its own jurisdiction,
  which [CLAIM-1287](CLAIM-1287.md) holds for narrow optimizers and A6 §6 calls
  "optimization that acquires authority over the mechanisms intended to
  regulate it". So the two variables are not independent: capability is
  one route to jurisdiction.

## Where it stands

Interpretive. Its third thesis needs jurisdiction measured apart from the
harm it is meant to explain, or it is true by construction. That is
[LEAD-tmpxcj7n](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpxcj7n.md).
