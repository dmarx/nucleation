## Note of 2026-11-18: the reading pass

Not read; Unavailable (status note). **King (2013)**, [LIT-tmpcg293](LIT-tmpcg293.md), stands
in for what it quotes of this paper by page: attributability as appraisal
"of the individual as an adopter of ends" (229); accountability, which
"involves a social setting in which we demand (require) certain conduct
from one another and respond adversely to one another’s failures to comply
with these demands" (229); its fairness condition, "[i]t is unfair to
impose sanctions upon people unless they had a reasonable opportunity to
avoid incurring them" (237); and the standing of the one who holds
accountable (the hijacker, 237). It does not stand in for Watson's own
arguments, and King warns he uses the distinction "potentially in ways to
which Watson himself would object".

**The exchange's characterization is partly supported, through the
neighbour.** A9: "Something can genuinely express who you are without your
necessarily deserving the ordinary degree of blame for it." King: "one can
have something attributable to them that one is not accountable to others
for. Watson makes this plain." But "the ordinary degree of blame" runs the
faces together: on Watson's view as King reports it, attributability
carries its own appraisal, so the point is a different kind of response,
not a lesser degree of one. The nearest first-hand discussion the record
holds is Wolf's lecture, [LIT-747](LIT-747.md) ([NOTE-565](../notes.d/NOTE-565.md)), which works from this paper.
