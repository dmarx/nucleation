# NOTE-1239: Santana-Molina, Williams, Snel & Spang — Chimeric origins and dynamic evolution of central carbon metabolism in eukaryotes

## Contribution

It traces, enzyme by enzyme, where the eukaryotic central carbon
metabolism (CCM) came from. The textbook view was that archaea gave
eukaryotes their informational genes and bacteria, chiefly the
mitochondrial ancestor, gave them metabolism. The paper finds archaeal,
and specifically Asgard, contributions in glycolysis and the pentose
phosphate pathway (PPP). It finds alphaproteobacterial contributions
concentrated in the TCA cycle, and further contributions from other
bacteria. It then maps how those enzymes were lost, replaced and
retargeted across 207 eukaryotes after LECA. It is a pathway-scale study:
64 gene families, each tree inspected by hand.

## Key insight

Syntrophy models say the archaeal and bacterial partners fed each other.
If so, the eukaryotic metabolism they left behind should carry genes from
both, not only from the bacterium. It does. The archaeal enzymes sit in
the cytosol and in the oxidation of sugars, and the alphaproteobacterial
ones sit in the mitochondrion and the TCA cycle. That matches a picture in
which the archaeon fermented sugars and passed reducing equivalents to a
bacterium that burned them. In the authors' words, modern metabolism
"preserves information about the nature of the original
asgardarchaeal–alphaproteobacterial interactions". The trace is faint,
because most of the Asgard enzymes have since been replaced in many
lineages.

## Assumptions

- **A LECA clade is a clade with two of three supergroups.** Eukaryotic
  clades with members from at least two of Excavata, Amorphea and
  Diaphoretickes are "potential LECA clades". The species tree is unrooted;
  "our interpretations of gene family origins do not assume a particular
  root position".
- **Donor by sister group.** The prokaryotic donor of a LECA clade is its
  sister group in a maximum-likelihood tree (IQ-TREE, LG + C20 + G + F,
  ultrafast bootstrap). Trees were "manually rooted", by outgroup where
  possible, otherwise arbitrarily "to ease visualization". When sisters are
  mixed, the taxa in them are tallied (Fig. 3e).
- **KEGG defines the pathways.** Gene families come from KEGG orthology
  annotations, with homologous KO families merged by HMM comparison.
- **Targeting predicted, not measured.** Subcellular localization is TargetP
  prediction.
- **Sampling.** A core set of 483 archaeal, 487 bacterial and 224 eukaryotic
  proteomes (2021). Potential LECA clades were then searched against
  187,681 prokaryotic and 44,889 viral proteomes, adding the top 15 hits per
  class up to 150 sequences.

## Key results

- **LECA had the full CCM.** Nine of ten glycolytic (Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas)
  steps, seven of eight PPP steps and all ten TCA steps have putative LECA
  clades. The Entner–Doudoroff enzymes came later, in photosynthetic
  lineages. Pyruvate dehydrogenase, acetyl-CoA synthetase and lactate
  dehydrogenase were in LECA; the anaerobic alternatives (pyruvate formate
  lyase, POR, ADP-forming acetyl-CoA synthetase) are mostly post-LECA,
  though "others (such as POR) may have been present in LECA". LECA had
  redundant isofunctional enzymes for several steps. The authors:
  "these phylogenies unambiguously show that LECA had the complete set of
  enzymes needed for the CCM".
- **Asgard contributions (Fig. 3a,d).**
  - Clear: ADP-dependent glucokinase (ADPGK, 76% bootstrap),
    2,3-bisphosphoglycerate-independent mutase APGM (89%), enolase (ENO,
    39%), ribose 5-phosphate isomerase A (RPIA, 97%).
  - Ambiguous: GPMI_1 (a few Asgard sequences sister to eukaryotes) and
    pyruvate kinase PK_5 (mainly Amoebozoa nested in Asgard). For PK_5 and
    for a Lokiarchaeia–Fornicata group of ACDA (100%), the authors give two
    readings: present in LECA and later lost, or a post-LECA transfer.
  - Tentative and not Asgard: ATP-citrate lyase (sisters in DPANN and
    Thermoplasmatota) and malate dehydrogenase MDH1/2 (TACK and
    Baldarchaeota sisters, "long branches"). The Discussion still calls
    these "even more salient" possible "archaeal host contributions" to the
    mitochondrial TCA cycle.
  - The Discussion counts six Asgard contributions: ADPGK, GPMI, APGM, ENO,
    PK, RPIA. This contradicts earlier claims of none (Eme et al. 2023) or of
    enolase alone. Except for enolase, they are "patchily distributed in
    extant eukaryotes, apparently because of independent horizontal
    replacement events".
- **Alphaproteobacterial contributions (Fig. 3b).** Seventeen: ALDO and TPI
  (glycolysis), PRPS (PPP), PDHA/B/C/D (pyruvate), and ten in the TCA cycle
  (IDH1, SUCA/B, LSC1/2, SDH1–4, FUMC); counts checked here. Most are
  mitochondrial; ALDO, TPI and PRPS are not. Signals are "not always
  unequivocal": TPI and PRPS are sister to Alpha- and Gammaproteobacteria
  together, and other taxa intrude in LSC2 and ACS.
- **Cyanobacterial contributions** to plastid-bearing eukaryotes in
  glycolysis, the PPP and pyruvate conversion, and PDH trees that separate
  the green and red secondary plastid events.
- **Other donors (Fig. 3c,e).** Some with good support (> 95%): Chlamydia
  (TAL), Planctomycetota–Verrucomicrobiota (PGK, G6PD, RPE), Fusobacteriota
  (PK), Dependentiae (two LDH acquisitions), Chloroflexota (CS). Sister-group
  tallies show recurrent Myxococcota–Desulfobacterota, Bacteroidota and
  Acidobacteriota. "no additional dominant source of gene donations is
  apparent."
- **Unresolved (Fig. 2).** At least 12 phylogenies give no clear LECA clade.
  For GPI, conserved intron positions suggest it was in LECA anyway, and a
  recombination between nuclear and plastid paralogues may explain its tree.
- **Remodelling after LECA (Fig. 4).**
  - Asgard-derived ADPGK, APGM and RPIA were replaced in some lineages by
    bacterial HK, PGMA/B and RPIB.
  - Enzyme repertoires correlate partly with lifestyle (|phi| ≥ 0.5): plastid
    status with glycolysis and the PPP, anaerobiosis with pyruvate and TCA
    enzymes.
  - Targeting does not follow origin. ALDO and TPI are alphaproteobacterial
    but not mitochondrial. CS, ACNB and IDH1/2 are not alphaproteobacterial
    but are mitochondrial. "The targeting of proteins is not always in
    agreement with their origins."
  - In Archaeplastida, plastid glycolysis and PPP come mostly from duplicated
    nuclear genes relocated to the plastid, "which might have promoted the
    genome reduction of the endosymbiont". The mitochondrial glycolysis of
    some SAR lineages is retargeting, not an endosymbiotic origin of
    glycolysis.
- **The scenario (Fig. 5).** Host and symbiont contributions plus continuous
  gene transfer. The compartment split "is consistent with symbiogenetic
  models", and "Specifically, our results support the view of syntrophic
  interactions between host and endosymbiont, in which the archaeal partner
  produced reducing equivalents by the degradation of organic substrates via
  glycolysis which, in the absence of a suitable electron acceptor, were
  shuttled to a bacterial symbiont which contributed a TCA cycle and an
  electron transport chain". Other donors "may be due to transient
  interactions with other prokaryotes before and during eukaryogenesis".

## Concepts

- **Central carbon metabolism (CCM)**: here, glycolysis (Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas
  and Entner–Doudoroff), the pentose phosphate pathway, pyruvate and acetate
  conversion to acetyl-CoA, and the TCA cycle.
- **Potential LECA clade**: a eukaryotic clade with members from at least
  two of Excavata, Amorphea and Diaphoretickes.
- **EGT**: endosymbiotic gene transfer, from an endosymbiont to the host
  nucleus.
- **Syntrophy**: metabolic cross-feeding in which one partner depends on the
  other, here "as an external electron sink".
- **Isofunctional enzymes**: unrelated enzymes for the same reaction, such as
  the four phosphoglycerate mutases.

## Connections

- **Syntrophy models.** It cites the reverse-flow model (Spang et al.), the
  revisited syntrophy hypothesis (López-García & Moreira) and Imachi et al.'s
  cultured Asgard archaeon ([LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md), unread here) among the models it
  supports. It sets itself against the view that "the archaeal host
  metabolism was replaced by counterparts from the endosymbiont", for which
  it cites Martin & Müller's hydrogen hypothesis ([LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md), unavailable
  here).
- **Tobiasson et al.** ([LIT-1558](../literature.d/LIT-1558.md), read in [NOTE-1242](NOTE-1242.md)) cite this paper for
  alphaproteobacterial associations with mitochondrial carbon metabolism.
  The two agree on that. They are harder to square on Asgard's share. In
  this pathway set, alphaproteobacterial contributions (17) outnumber the
  Asgard ones (six, two ambiguous), while Tobiasson et al. report Asgard
  leading across "large parts of the core metabolic network". The
  measures differ (counts of hand-inspected trees here, likelihood weights
  there), so this is a tension, not a contradiction. The inference is mine.
- **Bernabeu et al.** ([LIT-1561](../literature.d/LIT-1561.md), read in [NOTE-1237](NOTE-1237.md)). The same two phyla
  that paper names as major donors turn up here: Planctomycetota–
  Verrucomicrobiota as sister to three LECA enzymes, and Myxococcota–
  Desulfobacterota recurring in sister groups. This paper reads them as
  minor, "no additional dominant source". The two also differ on LECA's
  anaerobic enzymes. Bernabeu et al.'s reconstruction lacks
  pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase (PFO, the same enzyme as POR here);
  this paper allows that POR "may have been present in LECA".
- **The Fe–S parallel.** The authors compare their mosaic to Fe–S cluster
  synthesis, with alphaproteobacterial mitochondrial steps and cytosolic
  steps of mixed origin, the pattern Tobiasson et al. also report.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md).** The record cited this work for "the chimeric
  metabolism", and for discriminating syntrophy-first from complexity-first
  scenarios ([LIT-1564](../literature.d/LIT-1564.md)'s "Why this is here"). The reading supports the
  first and not the second.
  - **On discrimination.** The paper does not address timing: nothing in it
    says whether the host was complex before the mitochondrion. Its
    syntrophy support rests on a compartment split that a host taking up a
    TCA-bearing bacterium by any route would also leave. What it does count
    against is the view that the host's metabolism was simply replaced by
    the symbiont's.
  - **G, the distribution of essential functions across genomes.** This is
    the paper's dimension. In LECA, carbon metabolism was split by origin
    between the partners, and all of it is now nuclear-encoded. Origin and
    compartment have since come apart through retargeting.
  - **C, host control through import.** Mitochondrial TCA enzymes of
    non-alphaproteobacterial origin (CS, ACNB, IDH1/2) are a case of the
    host supplying the symbiont's compartment with its own proteins. That
    is [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)'s gene transfer and import route, and its host
    compensation route, at the scale of one pathway.
  - **Host compensation as a cause of symbiont reduction.** In
    Archaeplastida, host genes were duplicated and sent to the plastid,
    which "might have promoted the genome reduction of the endosymbiont".
    That is a mechanism the question's routes do not name: the host's
    supply makes the symbiont's genes redundant. The authors offer it as a
    possibility only.
  - **Replacement.** Asgard-origin enzymes replaced by bacterial ones in
    many lineages show the theme of [CASE-171](../cases.d/CASE-171.md) and [LIT-1572](../literature.d/LIT-1572.md) (constituents
    replaced, function kept) at the level of single genes.
  - **D.** The syntrophy reading implies an initial metabolic dependence
    of the archaeon on a bacterial electron sink. That is inferred from
    present gene origins, not observed.
- **The live disagreement.** This paper sits with the two-partner reading:
  "we could not identify a third dominant donor lineage". Its other donors
  are "transient interactions".
- **Monocercomonoides.** The metamonads' mitochondrial PDHD and FUMC
  branch within LECA clades, consistent with secondary loss of
  mitochondria. That bears on [CASE-168](../cases.d/CASE-168.md)'s lineage, though not on
  *Monocercomonoides* itself.
- Filed as [THEORY-1109](../theory.d/THEORY-1109.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **Single-gene trees over deep time.** The authors call resolution
  "sometimes limited". Enolase, the one Asgard contribution earlier work
  agreed on, has 39% bootstrap here. The trees themselves are in the unread
  supplement.
- **Hand curation.** LECA clades and orthogroups were "manually selected",
  and trees manually rooted. That is careful but not reproducible as an
  algorithm, and the paper gives no estimate of curator error.
- **A small sample for donor questions.** 64 families can establish a
  donor's presence but not its absence or its rank.
- **The syntrophy inference outruns the data**, as above. The authors list
  the distinct predictions syntrophy models make ("types of metabolites
  exchanged, the origin of eukaryotic cell membranes, the timing of
  mitochondrial acquisition, the mechanism of mitochondrial uptake and the
  origin of the nucleus") and test none of them directly.
- **The species tree is uncertain at points.** Ancyromonadida and the
  Anaeramoebae move between analyses, and the PhyloBayes chains "did not
  converge". The authors say gene-origin calls do not depend on the root.
- **Slips.**
  - "four enzymes of the EMP" introduces a list of five (GPI, ALDO class II,
    FBA, GAPDH, PGK).
  - The six cyanobacterial PPP contributions include EDD and EDA, which the
    paper elsewhere assigns to the Entner–Doudoroff pathway.
  - "Amoprhea" in the Results; "Mailin" for Malin in one place.

  None changes a result.

## Open questions

- Would other pathways show the same archaeal-cytosol, bacterial-mitochondrion
  split? The authors expect so.
- Which of the syntrophy models' distinct predictions can gene phylogenies
  test? The exchanged metabolites might be one, since the donors'
  hydrogenases and electron carriers have trees of their own.
- Were the patchy Asgard enzymes in LECA and then replaced, or are some later
  transfers from Asgard archaea? PK_5 and the Lokiarchaeia–Fornicata ACDA are
  the test cases.
- Does host retargeting of its own enzymes into an endosymbiont generally
  precede and promote the symbiont's gene loss? The plastid case is offered,
  not shown.
