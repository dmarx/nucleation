# CLAIM-439: A regime of valence is felt, in the sense of attended and available to the system's other processes, when the system that is in it also models it: when a deeper process infers which ordering wins its negotiation, which way the system is moving along it and how strongly it prevails, and the inference is used elsewhere in the system; unfelt valence is the potential without that model, as unfelt fluency is a change in precision without the inference about it

## The claim

[CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md) makes valence a functional regime: an ordering of the
system's own states, by their bearing on its maintenance, prevails in the
negotiation among its perspectives. It says nothing about when that
regime is felt. This claim proposes the condition.

**The condition.** The regime is felt when the system also models it. A
process at a deeper level infers three things about the negotiation: which
ordering is winning, which way the system is moving along it against its
reference course, and how strongly it prevails. The inference is used
elsewhere in the system, to guide attention, report, or further
regulation.

**Where it comes from.** Brouillet and Friston ([THEORY-662](../theory.d/THEORY-662.md)) separate
unfelt fluency from felt fluency. In the record's reading of them
([NOTE-785](../notes.d/NOTE-785.md)), unfelt fluency is "the subpersonal recognition of a change in
precision, which orients attention", and felt fluency is "a hierarchically
deeper inference about it". Hesp and colleagues ([LIT-1468](../literature.d/LIT-1468.md), [NOTE-1111](../notes.d/NOTE-1111.md)) put valence in a
deeper inference: "agents infer their valence state based on the
expected precision of their action model—an internal estimate of overall
model fitness ("subjective fitness")" (p. 399). The precision γ weights
the preference-driven ranking of policies against the habitual prior, so
"higher γ values afford a greater influence of the expected free energies
of each policy entailed by one's current action model" (p. 408): in this
record's terms, γ is a potential's strength, and the deeper level models
it. They add that "this type of explicit valence representation is also a
necessary condition for self-reportable experience of valence" (p. 429).

**A precedent and a warning in Scherer.** The component process model
gives feeling "a special status … as it monitors and regulates the
component process", and requires it to "centrally represent all
information about the continuous patterns of change and their coherence
in all other components" ([LIT-1451](../literature.d/LIT-1451.md), [NOTE-1113](../notes.d/NOTE-1113.md), §3e). That is a
model of the system's own regime, as this claim requires, though of the
components' coherence rather than of which ordering wins. Scherer makes a
second, separate claim, that "it is the degree of synchronization of the
components … that will generate conscious experience", which is nearer
Veit's identity than to a self-model view, and he places the monitoring
representation only partly inside consciousness. So the model alone may
not suffice for feeling, which is the gap [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md) marks. He also
concedes that "all we can currently measure is the individual's verbal
account", the limit [QUESTION-266](../questions.d/QUESTION-266.md) is meant to get past.

**What it would answer.** [CLAIM-411](CLAIM-411.md) asks which further organizational
properties make an interpretation experiential, under the owner's
constitutive thesis ([CLAIM-412](CLAIM-412.md)). For affect, this is a candidate: the
system's model of its own potential. It answers [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md), whether a
state meeting a functional characterization of affect must be felt, in
part: no; it is felt when it is also modelled, and an organization may or
may not have such a model.

**For an organization.** The model is the organization's reporting, to
those who govern it, of what wins its conflicts. On this reading Wells
Fargo's board ([CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md)), told that sales-integrity terminations were "in
the range of 200-300", had a model of the bank's potential that
misrepresented it. If the claim holds, that is mis-felt valence, not
unfelt valence. Whether the misrepresentation was motivated is the
report's open question, not this claim's.

## Its relation to illusionism

The claim fits Graziano's account ([THEORY-873](../theory.d/THEORY-873.md)), on which reports of
consciousness come from a schematic model of attention. Here the model is
of potential, not attention, and it may be schematic in the same way. The
claim does not say whether such a model explains feeling or explains it
away. It adopts [THEORY-662](../theory.d/THEORY-662.md)'s sense of "felt", attended and available, and
[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)'s distinction between access and experience stands: if
"felt" is given a phenomenal sense beyond access, the claim's third
defeater applies.

## Its rival in the component process model

Grandjean, Sander and Scherer ([LIT-1452](../literature.d/LIT-1452.md), [NOTE-1115](../notes.d/NOTE-1115.md)) hold that
"consciousness of emotional reactions emerges when lower levels of
processing are not sufficient to cope with the event and regulate the
emotional process, particularly when the degree of synchronization
between the components reaches a critical level and duration" (p. 484),
and that then "higher level regulation strategies, such as reappraisal,
must kick in. These strategies require high-level representational
operations and are therefore usually conscious" (p. 492). Scherer and
Moors restate it: awareness follows "after a second threshold of
coherence has been reached" ([LIT-1457](../literature.d/LIT-1457.md), p. 737).

- **Where they agree.** Feeling is a representation that higher
  regulation uses, which is this claim's condition in other words.
- **Where they differ: the trigger.** For them the regime's own degree
  and duration recruit consciousness. This claim can adopt that as a
  hypothesis about when the self-model is recruited: strong and lasting
  potential, too costly to regulate below.
- **Where they differ: the content.** Their representation is of the
  components' coherence, which has no sign and does not say which
  ordering wins. This claim's model is of which ordering prevails.
- **The case that would decide.** Strong, lasting synchronization that
  no further regulation uses. Grandjean et al. predict it is felt; this
  claim predicts it is not. Their data split felt from unfelt episodes by
  report, so they do not decide it.

## A second rival: the beautiful loop

Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria
([LIT-1363](../literature.d/LIT-1363.md), [NOTE-1063](../notes.d/NOTE-1063.md), [THEORY-882](../theory.d/THEORY-882.md)) separate local "parametric depth", a
second-order inference about one precision such as "policy precision",
for which they cite Hesp et al., from epistemic depth, a system-wide
recursive loop over all precisions. They hold that "even complex
metacognition can potentially be unconscious or conscious depending on
epistemic depth" (p. 20). This claim's condition, a deeper process that
infers potential and is used elsewhere, is parametric depth in their
terms, and on their view it is not sufficient for awareness. Two
readings follow. If "felt" keeps the thin sense adopted here, attended
and available, their view is about something stronger and does not
conflict. If "felt" is meant as awareness, their view predicts this
claim's second defeater, and the claim would need strengthening: the
model of potential must be part of a global, recursive precision loop.
Their proposal has no data and no simulation ([NOTE-1063](../notes.d/NOTE-1063.md)), so it decides
nothing, but it names the distinction the claim must take a side on.

## Felt what?

Berridge notes that 'wanting' can be appraised as pleasure by the person
who has it ([NOTE-1110](../notes.d/NOTE-1110.md)), and Hesp et al. concede that their precision
signal sits nearer 'wanting' than pleasure (p. 430). If the potential this
claim's model is of is motivational, as [CLAIM-438](CLAIM-438.md) argues, the claim
is a theory of felt motivation. Whether it is also one of felt pleasure
depends on where 'liking' is in the account, which [CLAIM-438](CLAIM-438.md) leaves
open.

## What it does not say

- That any particular system feels. The claim is a condition, and its
  test is [QUESTION-266](../questions.d/QUESTION-266.md).
- That the model must be accurate. A model that misrepresents potential
  gives felt valence that misrepresents the system's state, which is what
  the claim predicts of denial and of mis-reported organizations.
- That the deeper process must be a cortex, or a person. Any process in
  the system that infers its potential and is used elsewhere qualifies.

## Note of 2026-10-24: what the self-model models

Asked "what's our current characterization of what it means for an affect
to be 'felt'?" (workbench entry of 2026-10-24, U24), the exchange found a
gap. If the self-model represents only the ordering that prevails, the
claim explains felt motivational precedence better than felt pleasure
from a perspective that does not prevail, and [CLAIM-437](CLAIM-437.md) makes such
perspectives common. A suggested extension, not yet the claim: felt affect
is an accessible, consequential self-representation of a process-relative
affective evaluation, prevailing or not. That allows pleasure felt without
governing and motivation governing unfelt, and separates having an
affect, feeling it, understanding it and letting it govern. Legibility
([CASE-124](../cases.d/CASE-124.md)) bears on which affective signals are registered and whether the
representation has influence; it does not make the represented state
phenomenal. An earlier turn (U5) proposed [CASE-129](../cases.d/CASE-129.md), the alarm that learned
against the alarm that was disabled, as this claim's test case: accurate
local evaluations can fail to reach the process that models the
organization, and a model can represent the organization as calm when
consequential signals were suppressed, so self-modelling is no infallible
route from evaluative organization to felt affect. The three mechanisms,
evaluation, the gate on its influence and the model of the state, can be
manipulated separately, which is [QUESTION-266](../questions.d/QUESTION-266.md)'s design. [THEORY-570](../theory.d/THEORY-570.md)'s
Minsky, for whom feelings are descriptions of organizational cascades, is
the nearest antecedent.
