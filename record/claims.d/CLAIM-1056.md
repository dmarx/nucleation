# CLAIM-1056: Reconstructions cannot test code protection

## The claim

[CLAIM-180](CLAIM-180.md)'s defeater asks two things of "the reconstructed exchanges".
Are the re-derivations the restatements furthest from their originals?
Are coded items re-derived about as often as uncoded ones? [CLAIM-182](CLAIM-182.md) asks
for a count of re-derivation before and after the same content got
addresses. Each of these is a rate, and a rate needs the restatements
that were recognised as well as those that were not.

The reconstructions do not report the recognised ones. [CASE-393](../cases.d/CASE-393.md)
coded every restatement event they report. Of 265, 76 are re-derivations
as new. One is a retrieval by citation, and it cited the wrong entry.
Two carry both cues, among them the record's single clean report of a
coded item restated with its code: [CLAIM-122](CLAIM-122.md), "re-derived again, this
time cited". The other 186 say only that something recurred.

A reconstruction is written to find what went wrong, so a recognised
restatement is not news. P(re-derived | coded) comes out at 0.99, and
P(re-derived | uncoded) at 1.0. Neither number measures anything. Only 16
events quote the restatement beside a single code, none of them a
retrieval, so the distance comparison has nothing to compare.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the reconstructions are wrong. They do what they were
  written for, which is to find what went wrong.
- It does not say the tests cannot be run. They need a different source:
  a verbatim transcript in which every restatement is coded, recognised
  or not, and paired with its earlier statement.
- The coding is one coder's, by rule. [CASE-393](../cases.d/CASE-393.md) gives the rules'
  errors as their author found them.
