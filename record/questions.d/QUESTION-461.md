# QUESTION-461: When an organization comes to depend on the contributions of participants it subordinates, under what conditions does that dependence increase their effective authority over it, and under what conditions does the organization instead reproduce the arrangements that keep their indispensable contributions from becoming effective claims on it?

## The question

A6 §8, after connecting Powers's and Prest's colonial theatre to the
record at the owner's request (U3):

> Under what conditions does an organization's growing dependence upon
> subordinated participants transform those participants' authority over
> the organization, and under what conditions does the organization
> instead reproduce the arrangements that prevent their indispensable
> contributions from becoming effective claims upon it?

A9 §1 put it again, after Ray ([LIT-2536](../literature.d/LIT-2536.md)): "Under what conditions does
organizational reliance upon a subordinated population increase that
population's effective authority, rather than reinforce the hierarchy
through which its labor is organized?" A9 added that Ray "provides a
strong foundation for asking that question" and that the record "would
need to provide a more precise causal or counterfactual answer". The
wording is A's.

## Why it is a question

What the record holds bounds it without answering it.

- **Dependence does not fix its organization.** The same material
  interdependence can be organized "as mutual opportunity, as competitive
  vulnerability or as coercive leverage", and payoff coupling alone does
  not settle which ([CLAIM-428](../claims.d/CLAIM-428.md)).
- **Stable coexistence is not cooperation** ([CLAIM-546](../claims.d/CLAIM-546.md)), and viability
  does not establish what ought to persist ([CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)).
- **Admission is not authority.** A contribution's admission,
  recognition, credibility, uptake and its contributor's part in revising
  the standards of uptake are separable, so an organization "can widen
  access while every channel by which a less authoritative contribution
  could change the criteria of authority stays closed" ([CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md)); an
  exception can leave the rule for others unchanged ([CASE-146](../cases.d/CASE-146.md)).
- **What feeds an organization it never becomes independent of**
  ([CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md)), and reliable supply can produce a dependence that nothing
  in the dependent's dynamics limits ([CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)). Those claims treat the
  organization as the dependent. This question turns the dependence
  round: the organization depends on its subordinated parts.
- **Reproduction of non-legibility** is [CLAIM-614](../claims.d/CLAIM-614.md) and [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md), and the
  disposition to answer a challenge by reducing the capacity for future
  challenge is [CLAIM-619](../claims.d/CLAIM-619.md).

None says when an organization's need of a party gives that party
bargaining power or a voice in the rules, and when it does not.

## A's candidate answers

A6 §8, "not mutually exclusive": an organization "might become more
inclusive because dependence gives participants bargaining power. It
might preserve exclusion because its governing coalition retains control
over alternatives and institutional rules. It might create exceptional
positions that permit a few individuals to advance without changing
general conditions. Or change might require intervention from
institutions outside the organization."

A6 §2 offered a formulation of the second: "The transition from
dependency to domination occurs not simply when one party needs another,
but when one party acquires disproportionate authority over the
conditions under which that need can be satisfied." That is [CLAIM-428](../claims.d/CLAIM-428.md)'s
coercive leverage, with the party who holds influence named; it is not
filed separately.

A9 §2 added a mechanism by which the dependence itself can be produced:
[CLAIM-669](../claims.d/CLAIM-669.md).

## Tests A proposed

A6 §6: comparisons "in which one dimension changes while others remain as
similar as possible": two theatres with similar artistic outputs and
different labour arrangements, to separate exploitation from
performance; two periods with similar Black artistic participation and
different rules of admission, to separate individual recognition from
institutional change; and two institutions facing comparable challenges
and responding differently, to separate ordinary resistance to change
from reproduced barriers to accountability ([CLAIM-619](../claims.d/CLAIM-619.md)'s test). A6 adds
the limit: "Archival absences are often systematic, and missing evidence
cannot simply be treated as proof that a mechanism existed."

## Note of 2026-11-12: Prest, "Performing the Racial Scale", and the H-France Forum on Prest, read

Both readings are about a collective institution, [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)'s sense 3, the
public theatre of colonial Saint-Domingue, and bear on this question for
that kind of organization.

- **The H-France Forum** ([THEORY-1820](../theory.d/THEORY-1820.md), [NOTE-1897](../notes.d/NOTE-1897.md)), standing in for
  Prest's book: the theatre was "reliant on the world of enslaved people
  of African descent" (Row, quoting p. 187), whose labour its records
  nearly hid. And from 1775, "during a financial crisis", free Black women
  "were offered access to the theater", at first "restricted to standing
  in the corridor at the back" (Sabee). That is A's first candidate
  answer, dependence giving bargaining power, in its weakest form: the
  institution's need for revenue widened access for a subordinated group,
  with no sign of a part in its rules. The dependence there is on
  spectators' money, not on enslaved labour; for the enslaved
  contributors the readings show dependence without any authority.
- **Prest, "Performing the Racial Scale"** ([THEORY-1816](../theory.d/THEORY-1816.md), [NOTE-1896](../notes.d/NOTE-1896.md)):
  Minette's rise, "significant, though ultimately limited", attests A's
  third answer, an exceptional position that changes nothing general;
  Prest adds that Minette "was not an active pioneer for actors of colour".
  Whether the theatre depended on her is not argued.

Both carry `bears_on` this question. The question is unchanged.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Kittay, as far as reached

Kittay's 2026 interview ([THEORY-1823](../theory.d/THEORY-1823.md)), standing in for Love's Labor,
is about persons and society, sense 3 ([TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)). Unpaid dependency work is
the paradigm of an indispensable contribution kept from becoming a claim:
"unless it's remunerated, we're really exploiting those who we put on the
pedestal". Her answer is not the contributors' bargaining power but a
public principle, doulia, support owed by the wider society. That is A's
fourth candidate answer, change through institutions outside the
relation. Filed as `bears_on`. The question is unchanged.
