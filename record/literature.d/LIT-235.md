# LIT-235: When and How Does Known Class Help Discover Unknown Ones? Provable Understanding Through Spectral Analysis

Yiyou Sun, Zhenmei Shi, Yingyu Liang, Yixuan Li (2023), *Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2023), PMLR 202:33014–33043* — [ARXIV-2308.05017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05017)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-833](../notes.d/NOTE-833.md).*

- **The graph.** Vertices are augmented views. Edges join views of one
  unlabelled image and views of two labelled samples of the same known
  class. Known and novel data meet only through shared views. The
  rank-k factorization of the normalized adjacency is the optimum of a
  contrastive loss with five terms (Theorem 4.1).
- **The residual, exactly.** Split the eigenvectors into kept (top k)
  and discarded, and each into labelled and unlabelled rows. A novel
  labelling y then has residual R = ‖(I − P_{L♭})U♭ᵀy‖² (Theorem 5.5,
  printed as ≤, proved as = in C.1). The 0–1 linear-probe error is at
  most 2R.
- **Help and harm (toy, §5.2).** Unlabelled red and blue cubes and
  spheres, with shape linked more strongly than colour. A known red
  cylinder makes "red" recoverable (R = 0). A known gray cylinder leaves
  R = 1. A known gray cube makes it worse still (Lemma 5.4).
- **The general bound is approximate.** Theorem 5.6 bounds the error by
  the "ignorance degree" times one minus squared "coverage", plus
  ‖Ȧ − Ā‖₂/(σ_k − σ_{k+1}). Ā averages all labelled rows, so known
  classes act as one block. The quantities are not estimated on data.
- **Experiments.** ResNet-18 on CIFAR: 64.0% novel clustering accuracy
  on CIFAR-100-50 against 53.4% for the best baseline, without error
  bars.
