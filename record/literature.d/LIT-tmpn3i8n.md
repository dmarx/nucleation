# LIT-tmpn3i8n: Programmable branched flow of light

Chang, S.-s., Xiong, D., Zheng, Z.-h., Wang, L.-W., Lu, Y.-q., Chen, L.-J., Jiang, J.-H. & Chen, J.-h. (2026). Programmable branched flow of light. arXiv:2601.13623. Published as "Programmable Branched Flow of Light", *Physical Review Letters* 137(11): 113802. doi:10.1103/2fxf-jr9b.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp5yuxz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5yuxz.md); the account is [THEORY-tmp6ntgy](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp6ntgy.md).*

- **Designed disorder.** A director pattern digitized into 18 angles and
  imprinted by photoalignment sets the potential's strength (ε ≈ 0.05),
  anisotropy A = ly/lx and rotation α, film by film.
- **The anisotropic law** (Eqs. 3–4, from Degueldre et al. 2017):
  df ∝ ε^−2/3 A^4/3 lx at α = 0, and
  df ∝ ε^−2/3 A^4/3 lx (cos²α + sin²α/A²)^5/6 under rotation; at A = 1 it
  reduces to the isotropic law of [LIT-tmpv09vj](LIT-tmpv09vj.md).
- **Measured** (p. 4): "Each curve represents an ensemble average over
  approximately ten independent realizations", and the first-branch
  distance "shows quantitative agreement with both the analytical scaling
  law from equation (3) and numerical simulations".
- **Channeling** (Fig. 4): at A = 1/1000 the caustics stay along the
  principal axis for tilts up to about π/9, and the velocity-angle spread
  grows with an exponent that "is smaller than 1/2".

## Standing in the record

Filed with the optics and holography batch of 2026-11-15 from A's turn on
wave mathematics and learning. A's account, that liquid-crystal structures
"deliberately control those branches" by "adjusting the anisotropy and
orientation of the optical potential", holds, and so does A's venue. But A
credits this paper with the scintillation index and with relating the
first-branch distance to correlation length and disorder strength; both
are Patsyk et al. (2020), [LIT-tmpv09vj](LIT-tmpv09vj.md), which this paper cites for them.
What is new here is the dependence on anisotropy and orientation and the
channeling regime.

The paper's "universal scaling law" is a confirmation of an existing
prediction, not a discovery, and the quality of the fit is in the unread
Supplemental Material. Like its predecessor it is linear wave transport;
that a designed potential steering light into chosen channels models how
weights route information is A's analogy, and the paper does not make it.
