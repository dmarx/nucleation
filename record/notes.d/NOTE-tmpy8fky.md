# NOTE-tmpy8fky: Kilteni, Maselli, Kording and Slater — Over my fake body

## Contribution

It collects about fifteen years of ownership-illusion experiments, from the
rubber hand to full virtual bodies, and sorts them by what was manipulated:
visuotactile, visuomotor and visuoproprioceptive agreement, and the
semantic properties of the fake body. From that it states which conditions
appear sufficient and which necessary. It then proposes that the
illusion is what Bayesian causal inference, as used for the ventriloquist
effect, would deliver when asked whether one body part or two produced the
signals, and sketches neural-network models as a possible implementation.

## Key insight

Signals from one's own body are tied together by physics, so the brain
rarely has to ask whether a seen and a felt event share a cause. The
illusions work by separating the sources and then making them agree.
Ownership, on this view, is the brain's answer to a causal question,
"one hand or two?", and the answer can be pushed to "one" by agreement in
time, place and form.

## Assumptions

- **Questionnaire scores are the main measure.** "Our criterion about the
  impact of the manipulation ... is primarily based on the most common
  measure, the subjective scores collected through questionnaires" (p. 4),
  with other measures where no questionnaire was given.
- **Scope.** Only illusions with visual input; somatic and auditory
  versions and interoception are excluded (p. 15).
- **The model's evidence is conditionally independent given the cause**
  (Eq. 2), each likelihood perhaps normal "in the correspondent metric"
  (fn. 1).
- **A threshold.** "The illusion is then experienced when this posterior
  probability exceeds a given threshold (Wozny et al., 2010)" (p. 13).
  Its value is not given.
- **The prior** includes midline position, "personality traits including
  suggestibility to illusory perceptions" and "previous experience of the
  illusion" (p. 13).

## Key results

There are no new data. The review's conclusions, by section:

- **Body illusions (pp. 2–4).** Distortion illusions show the body's
  perceived shape is computed flexibly; out-of-body illusions
  (Lenggenhager et al. 2007; Ehrsson 2007) show felt self-location can be
  detached from the body; ownership illusions make an object felt as part
  of one's body and the source of its touch. Questionnaire scores have
  been shown to correlate with proprioceptive drift, brain activity and
  cooling of the real hand (citations only). Full-body illusions
  (Petkova and Ehrsson 2008 and later) are said to have "physiological and
  proprioceptive correlates analogous to those of the RHI"; Petkova and
  Ehrsson supplied only the physiological one.
- **Visuotactile (pp. 4–5).** Synchrony at homologous places induces it;
  spatial mismatch (index against little finger) abolishes it; delays
  under about 300 ms still induce it (Shimada et al.). Summary: agreement
  is sufficient, but with a realistic fake body superimposed on the real
  one ownership is reported even under asynchrony (Longo et al. 2008a;
  Maselli and Slater 2013), so it is not necessary there. Petkova and
  Ehrsson is cited for a mannequin owned under synchrony "but not when
  asynchronously", which matches its Experiment 1; its Experiment 5
  reported a weaker illusion surviving asynchrony, which the review does
  not mention.
- **Visuomotor (pp. 5–7).** Moving homologous parts in time induces it,
  for fingers, hands, arms, legs and full bodies; delays of about 500 ms or
  more, independent movement, or a different finger abolish it. Apparatus
  delays of 70–100 ms are under the 150 ms detection threshold.
- **Visuoproprioceptive (pp. 7–9).** Hand offsets of 10 to over 40 cm
  toward the midline are tolerated (no difference at 15 against 45 cm);
  outward offsets can prevent it (Preston 2013); a vertical gap of 43 cm,
  not 27.5, attenuates it. A full body seen from a viewpoint shifted 25 cm,
  or tilted 30°, is still owned. Sight of a spatially coincident hand or
  body alone can induce ownership; a non-coincident one cannot. Summary:
  coincidence is not necessary with congruent stimulation, and may be
  sufficient without it; the "consensus" that congruent stimulation is
  necessary "may specifically apply to conditions that include
  visuoproprioceptive mismatches" (p. 9).
- **Semantics (pp. 9–11).** Shape: checkerboards, sticks, hand-textured
  non-hands, a wooden block with sculpted fingers, and Petkova and
  Ehrsson's box all weaken or abolish it; Armel and Ramachandran's "table
  ownership" came after a rubber-hand condition, and Hohwy and Paton show
  a prior illusion can carry over to cardboard. Texture modulates. A hand
  rotated 180° prevents it. A third-person view of a full body at 100, 80,
  75 or 40 cm abolishes it, while a slightly shifted first-person view
  does not; a third-person view through a mirror works (Bertamini et al.
  2011). Size, a long arm up to three times normal, and extra limbs are
  partly tolerated; a rubber foot for a hand is not. Different stroking
  materials make no difference. Summary: the fake body must satisfy, to
  some extent, an "abstract and not self-specific body model", with "the
  exact boundaries of tolerable violations ... still to be determined".
- **Earlier models (p. 12).** Botvinick and Cohen's three-way interaction;
  Makin et al.'s two-stage integration; Tsakiris's "test-for-fit" in the
  right temporo-parietal junction. Schematic, without "the underlying
  computational problem".
- **Predictive coding (p. 12).** The RHI as minimising the error between
  seen and felt touch and between the rubber and the real hand's look
  (Apps and Tsakiris; Seth), which fits the attenuation of somatosensory
  processing in the real hand. Judged "formulated mainly at a conceptual
  level".
- **Causal inference (pp. 12–14).** Eq. 1: p(C = 1 | m_V, x_V, x_P, s_V^→τ,
  s_T^→τ) by Bayes's rule; Eq. 2 factors the likelihood into semantic,
  visuoproprioceptive and visuotactile terms given C = 1. The illusion is
  felt above a threshold. Mapped qualitatively onto: slow onset
  (evidence accumulates); asynchrony and spatial mismatch (evidence for
  two hands); semantic violations (a less important likelihood);
  distance from the midline (lower likelihood and prior); coincidence
  (visuoproprioceptive likelihood high enough to make touch unnecessary).
  An iterative extension, where having the illusion raises the prior and
  widens the likelihoods, is offered for Hohwy and Paton's persistence
  under later conflict and for the attenuation of touch on the real hand,
  "conceptually analogous to minimizing prediction errors". Predictions,
  stated as untested: more complex or less predictable congruent
  stimulation should give faster, stronger illusions; visuomotor
  agreement, carrying more information, should give faster onset.
- **Network models (pp. 14–15).** Visuotactile networks with Hebbian
  learning reproduce peripersonal-space effects; a hybrid with Bayesian
  semantic gating is suggested.
- **Open (p. 15).** Whether the constraints change once the illusion is
  established, and why somatoparaphrenic patients disown their limbs.

## Concepts

- **Body ownership illusion (BOI)** — the illusory perception of a
  non-bodily object as part of one's body and as the source of its
  sensations (p. 3). Distinguished from body distortion and from
  out-of-body (self-location) illusions.
- **Full BOI** — ownership of a whole artificial body.
- **Semantic congruence** — how far the fake object resembles a human body
  part in shape, texture, posture and structure; for body illusions, its
  fit to an "abstract and not self-specific body model".
- **1PP / 3PP** — first- and third-person visual perspective on the body.
- **Causal inference** — inferring whether signals share a cause (C = 1)
  or not (C = 2) before combining them (Körding et al. 2007).
- **Test-for-fit** — Tsakiris's gating of integration by a body model.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md), C6.** The review classes Lenggenhager et al. 2007 as an
  out-of-body illusion of self-location, apart from ownership illusions,
  and reports direct comparisons in which a third-person view of a body
  abolishes full-body ownership. Lenggenhager et al. showed the body from
  behind. So C6's "body ownership can be manipulated by multisensory
  conflict", cited to Lenggenhager et al., is right about the literature
  (ownership is manipulable) and leans on the wrong study for it: the
  ownership evidence is the rubber-hand and first-person full-body work.
  The review says nothing about ownership as "the simplest form of
  self-awareness"; it cites Blanke and Metzinger 2009 for the question of
  own-body perception and for self-location, not for that claim. My reading. The readings of the two sources ([NOTE-tmp9c2fc](NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md),
  [NOTE-tmpyzsg9](NOTE-tmpyzsg9.md)) agree that Lenggenhager et al. measured self-location and
  a weak, mannequin-dependent ownership rating, and that "simplest" is
  Blanke and Metzinger's proposal about global ownership.
- **Metzinger ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md), [LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md)).** The review's "abstract and not
  self-specific body model" that gates ownership is close to what
  Metzinger calls the unconscious self-model that the PSM draws on; the
  review does not cite Metzinger's theory beyond Blanke and Metzinger.
  The pairing is mine.
- **Active inference ([LIT-1181](../literature.d/LIT-1181.md), [NOTE-960](NOTE-960.md)).** The review sets
  predictive-coding and free-energy accounts beside causal inference and
  calls the iterative extension "conceptually analogous to minimizing
  prediction errors". Neither framework, as reviewed, includes action as a
  way of resolving the conflict: the visuomotor illusions are explained
  by evidence accumulation, not by action fulfilling proprioceptive
  predictions as in [NOTE-960](NOTE-960.md). The comparison is mine.
- **Demand characteristics (Lush 2020, [LIT-tmpozu5y](../literature.d/LIT-tmpozu5y.md), [NOTE-tmptyehi](NOTE-tmptyehi.md)).** The review grades
  evidence mainly by questionnaire and does not discuss demand
  characteristics, response bias or expectancy. Its model puts
  "suggestibility to illusory perceptions" into the prior. So a finding
  that trait suggestibility or expectancy predicts reports would be
  absorbed by the model rather than counting against it; what it would
  count against is the review's reading of questionnaire differences as
  differences in perception.
- **Petkova and Ehrsson ([NOTE-tmpb1ult](NOTE-tmpb1ult.md)).** Cited accurately for
  Experiments 1 and 4; credited with a proprioceptive correlate it did not
  measure. The review supplies the third-person comparisons (Petkova et
  al. 2011b and others) the 2008 paper's necessity claim lacked.
- **[NOTE-325](NOTE-325.md) (phantom pain as a body-schema prior).** The review's
  prior and its "abstract body model" give the record a second statement
  of a body prior, here one that is overridden by evidence within
  minutes. The two do not conflict; they bound the prior's strength from
  opposite sides.
- Filed as [THEORY-tmp5h02h](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5h02h.md).
- **For the Anthology of the SOTA:** no instruction for ML practice. The
  network models it mentions are models of neurons, not learning systems
  to build.

## Limitations

- No systematic search, no effect sizes and no meta-analysis; the authors
  say the review "is not exhaustive".
- Graded by questionnaire, with no discussion of what questionnaires
  measure or of control statements.
- The model is not fitted, its threshold is unspecified, the C = 2 term is
  not written out, and with the prior and the iterative extension it can
  accommodate persistence, abolition and after-effects alike.
- Several of the "not necessary" findings are the authors' own studies.
- The reviewed studies were not checked here.

## Open questions

- Fit the causal-inference model to graded data (delay, offset, rotation)
  within one participant group, with the threshold and prior fixed in
  advance, and test whether it predicts held-out conditions better than a
  model that only adds up congruence cues.
- Do the predicted effects of stimulation complexity and of visuomotor
  information on onset hold?
- How much of the questionnaire evidence the review grades by survives an
  expectancy-matched control?
