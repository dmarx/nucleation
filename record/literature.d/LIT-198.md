# LIT-198: Rule Consequentialism

Brad Hooker (2003), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism-rule/>

## Key takeaways

- Rule-consequentialism — selecting rules by the good of their general acceptance and judging acts by those rules — survives the classic collapse, incoherence and rule-worship objections, provided it is formulated in terms of acceptance by less than everyone and includes a prevent-disaster rule.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry defines full rule-consequentialism as a theory that chooses rules solely by the goodness of their consequences and lets those rules determine which acts are wrong, which decision procedure to use, and when blame is apt; Berkeley is cited as its first proponent. It surveys the consequentialist family (utilitarianism, theories of welfare, non-welfare goods, global consequentialism) and the choices in formulating rule-consequentialism: actual vs. expected good, compliance vs. acceptance, complete vs. incomplete acceptance. It gives three ways of arguing for it and defends it against the long-standing collapse-or-incoherence dilemma and the charge of rule-worship before reviewing newer objections.
