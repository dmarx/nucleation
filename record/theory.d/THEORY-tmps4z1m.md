# THEORY-tmps4z1m: Standard unitary quantum theory and the general quantum theory of open systems are intertranslatable on their common domain, so neither dependence nor justification ranks them; the open theory comes out more fundamental only through premises about interpretation, that orthodox and Everettian readings exhaust the complete readings of the state vector, and that density operators precede state vectors in how states are assigned

## Source

Cuffaro & Hartmann (2021), [LIT-037](../literature.d/LIT-037.md), read in full in [NOTE-077](../notes.d/NOTE-077.md).

## What was actually shown

**The two frameworks.** ST: normalised state vectors, Hermitian Hamiltonians
and unitary evolution, with open-system dynamics obtained by tracing out an
environment. GT: density operators under general linear maps, in general
non-unitary and not necessarily completely positive (§2.2.2, after
Sudarshan–Mathews–Rau and Jordan–Sudarshan). Each instantiates a "view":
the closed view models an open system by coupling it into an isolated
whole; the open view writes the environment into the system's own law.

**The tie (pp. 29, 35–36).** On the descriptions both frameworks make sense
of, ST's dynamics re-describe losslessly in GT via ρ = |ψ⟩⟨ψ|, and GT's
completely positive dynamics derive from ST by Stinespring dilation.
"Neither dependence nor determination can help" (OntFund-1). Under
generalized Nagel–Schaffner reduction each justifies the other (EpFund-1).
The paper sets aside the greater expressiveness of GT (non-CP maps) as a
premise, because using it would be circular (pp. 29, 33, 35).

**The break.** OntFund-2 asks whether a framework's objects actually
represent their targets as its motivating metaphysics says. On orthodox
readings a state vector encodes an open system's possible manifestations in
interaction with a further system; on Everettian readings reduced density
operators are as real as the universal state, and a non-unitary universal
density operator is consistent with Everett (§3; pp. 30–33). So ST's objects
match the open view, and GT is ontically more fundamental. EpFund-2 orders
concepts by which is constructed from which in practice: a pure state is an
idealisation of ρ = (1−ε)|ψ⟩⟨ψ| + εσ (Eq. 4.2), and a global state is
reached only through subsystems (pp. 36–38).

**Explanation.** ExFund-3 on its face favours ST, whose explanations are
simpler; it is rejected by one reductio (Newton against GR). ExFund-1 is
deflationary, and the authors suggest discarding the notion (p. 41).

**What could have come out otherwise.** OntFund-1 or EpFund-1 could have
found an asymmetry, a translation that loses content in one direction on
the common domain. They did not, which is why the verdict rests on
interpretation.

## What this does not say

- **That the open theory is more fundamental tout court.** The ontic verdict
  holds only relative to the partition in §3, which counts Everettian and
  orthodox readings as the candidate-fundamental ones and sets hidden-variable
  and collapse theories aside as different frameworks (fn. 41). "There are
  no other options" (p. 26) is asserted ([NOTE-077](../notes.d/NOTE-077.md), C5 weak, C9 weak).
- **That Everett favours non-unitary universal dynamics.** The paper shows
  only that they are consistent with Everett, and concedes nothing makes
  that description "any more likely to be true" (p. 32).
- **That epistemic order confers fundamentality.** EpFund-2 rests on an
  epistemic fact about us, imperfect preparation. The paper disclaims
  foundationalism but does not show why the order should rank frameworks.
- **That some phenomenon forces the general theory.** The authors concede
  that "no phenomena are known that would absolutely force us" to a
  not-CP or non-Markovian fundamental dynamics (fn. 83).
- **That arrow-of-time or information-loss puzzles are solved.** §5.1 asserts
  they lose their conceptual difficulty and leaves the details to others.
