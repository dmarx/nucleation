# THEORY-tmpvtotj: Gallo, Lynch and Tomy find that firms under deferred or non-prosecution agreements that must take an on-site government-appointed monitor commit 18 to 25 percent fewer penalised violations than such firms without one while the monitor is on site, and not after the agreement ends, and that a measure of ethics and compliance norms in managers' speech rises and falls with it

## Source

Gallo, Lynch and Tomy (2023), "Out of Site, Out of Mind? The Role of the
Government-Appointed Corporate Monitor", *Journal of Accounting Research*
61(5), LIT-tmpaya40, read in NOTE-tmpejwjy.

## The account

Monitors are imposed on firms already exposed for wrongdoing, under
agreements that defer or forgo prosecution. Against N/DPA firms without a
monitor, matched on the agreement's terms, governance and text, monitor
firms' penalised violations fall by 18–25% during the agreement's term and
return to their earlier level after it. The fall runs through a change in
ethics-and-compliance norms, measured from managers' unscripted speech,
which also does not outlast the monitor, except where the monitor had done
the job before or stayed longer. Mandated board changes are associated with
fewer violations after the term, but the monitor's effect does not run
through them.

## What it does not show

- That harm to others is weighed or not weighed: the outcome is penalised
  violations, which a sanction itself produces.
- That the firms without a monitor were changed by something other than
  sanction: both arms are under an agreement.
- That reputation or benefit is held fixed.
