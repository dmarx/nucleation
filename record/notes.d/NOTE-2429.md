# NOTE-2429: Between a role and identification. Understanding BDSM practices from practitioners' perspective

Niebudek and Iniewicz (2023), Psychiatria Polska 57(1), 121–146.

## Contribution

A small descriptive study. It names a distinction, identification versus role, and asserts a continuum between them, in practitioners' own words. It does not test its model.

## Key results

- **The distinction's source (p. 122, citing Ortmann and Sprott).** Identity
  is "relatively permanent in comparison with the role which may become
  fluctuant, related to the context, specific situation and/or activity".
- **The authors' terms (p. 128).** "identification (as a process of
  developing an identity) and a role (assumed in a given situation)".
- **Role as identity (p. 130).** For many participants "the role is part
  of their identity rather than a label describing a set of behaviours in
  a specific context". M1: "I am a master, and only a master."
- **Role as a game (p. 131).** M1: "We're playing a game, I assume a role".
  F6: "You need to earn it".
- **The continuum (p. 130).** "it might be assumed that their
  understanding of BDSM practices would be on a continuum – from just
  assuming a role in the relationship to identification, present also when
  the role is not assumed." Then: "This assumption was confirmed in the
  responses of the participants".
- **The two poles (p. 141).** Identification "also has reference to
  everyday life". A role is entered "only in the course of the session
  limited in time". "it is rather a continuum between them".
- **The model (pp. 137–141).** "a multidirectional process which can lead
  to identifying with a role in BDSM, also in a non-sexual context". The
  starting point "was gender identity and sexual orientation". And
  "Identification with sexual practices is not a condition necessary to end
  this process."
- **Attribution and stigma.**
  - Men at community meetings assume F3 is submissive "because you are a
    female" (p. 129).
  - "internalized kinkphobia" (p. 139).
  - Concealment and coming out (pp. 139, 141, 143).
  - Table 1 notes that one participant "Leads a double life".

## Assumptions

- **Sample.** Twelve participants aged 20 to 49, self-selected through
  online forums. Six women and six men by designated gender; one
  genderfluid; no non-heterosexual men.
- **Interviews.** One unstructured interview each, lasting "from 15
  minutes up to 1.5 hours", in settings the participants chose.
- **Coding.** Two coders whose codes were "joined and compared". No
  agreement statistic, no check with participants, and saturation only
  asserted.
- **Terms.** Used "descriptively", with "no reference to a specific
  theoretical concept".

## Concepts

- **Identification:** a process of developing an identity, which reaches
  everyday life.
- **Role:** assumed in a situation, limited to a session.
- **Identity:** left undefined beyond "relatively permanent".
- **The model's stages:** confrontation with the need; belonging to the
  community; then identification or role.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-260](../questions.d/QUESTION-260.md) (supplies cases and a candidate marker; does not
  answer).** Identification, "present also when the role is not assumed"
  and reaching "everyday life", against a role confined to a scene, is a
  person-level analogue of a constitutive perspective against an
  influencing one. The candidate marker is **scope of enactment**: scene,
  relationship, everyday life, self-description. The continuum does not
  object to the dichotomy. At most it makes constitutiveness a matter of
  degree. Two cautions:
  1. The data are self-descriptions, represented perspectives in
     [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md)'s sense 2, not evidence about practical organization.
  2. The same participants describe their role both ways (M1: "only a
     master", and "a game"). That fits two dimensions, self-description
     and scope of enactment, better than one continuum.
- **[QUESTION-257](../questions.d/QUESTION-257.md) and [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md) (supply cases).**
  - A role confined to scenes is held but not engaged between scenes, by
    arrangement. That is a loss of activation with the conditions intact,
    because the scene is the condition.
  - Participants between partners are closer to having lost the
    conditions, while their identification continues.
  - The community stage is where social reproduction would sit.
- **[TERM-075](../terms.d/TERM-075.md) (cases of each sense, by report).**
  - Held: others' attribution of submissiveness to F3 because she is a
    woman.
  - Imagined audience: F3's feared "social group".
  - Appropriated: "internalized kinkphobia".
  - Concealment under an expected judgement the person has not taken on is
    the pattern of [CASE-142](../cases.d/CASE-142.md).
- **[CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md) (a case).** A "double life", and concealment against coming
  out, are roles kept in disjoint contexts, and those contexts then
  collapse.
- **[TERM-099](../terms.d/TERM-099.md) (a parallel).** Participants frame their path as discovery,
  so the paper cannot tell self-formation from a disposition held all
  along.
- **[CLAIM-543](../claims.d/CLAIM-543.md) and [CLAIM-454](../claims.d/CLAIM-454.md) (parallels).** F6's "You need to earn it"
  shows one partner's role conditioning the other's, so the roles in a
  dyad are not exchangeable. And one label can be one person's identity
  and another's game.
- **[QUESTION-043](../questions.d/QUESTION-043.md) (a case, no measure).** F3 re-reads her childhood games as
  early dominance, a life story absorbing new content.
- **[TERM-166](../terms.d/TERM-166.md):** no bearing.

## Limitations

- **A continuum of what is never said.** Identification is a process and a
  role is a situational state, so the two poles are not of one kind. The
  candidate axes (duration, scope of contexts, self-description) are never
  separated.
- **The continuum was assumed before it was confirmed (p. 130).** The
  confirmation shows two kinds of answer, not intermediate cases, and no
  participant is placed on the continuum.
- **The stages are proposed, not traced.** Each person gave one
  retrospective interview. "Multidirectional" (p. 138) sits uneasily with
  "stage-like" (p. 144). The stated starting point, gender identity and
  orientation, is not in the diagram.
- **Thin validity checks.** A small, self-selected sample, single
  interviews (some 15 minutes long), only a two-coder comparison, and no
  statement of the researchers' own position.
- **Reporting errors.**
  - p. 129 says "Three out of four" women were heteroflexible; Table 1
    shows four of six.
  - p. 144 says "no individuals identifying as non-heteronormative
    volunteered", though one participant is genderfluid and four are
    heteroflexible.
