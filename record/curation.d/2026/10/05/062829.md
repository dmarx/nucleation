The owner's essay on organizational will (§§5–9) cites four bodies of work
the record did not hold: Aubin on viability, Longino on social knowledge,
Ostrom on polycentric governance, and "research on epistemic networks".
The owner asked for them to be filed. Neither record held any of them.

## What was read and what was not

Each author's main book is closed, so each was registered `Deferred` from
Crossref, and a shorter lawful work by the same author was read in its
place:

- **Aubin.** *Viability Theory* (1991) is behind Springer's bot challenge,
  and the HAL copy of a later paper is behind one too. Neither was passed.
  His 1999 UC Davis mini-course notes are on arXiv. Their introduction and
  chapters 1–2 give the definitions first-hand: viable, invariant, the
  viability kernel and the capture basin.
- **Longino.** Her two books are closed. Her own SEP entry, which she wrote,
  states the argument and the four community norms.
- **Ostrom.** *Governing the Commons* is closed. The Nobel lecture, read in
  full from the Nobel Foundation's PDF, gives the design principles in
  their updated form and defines polycentricity.
- **Epistemic networks.** The essay names no work, so the founding papers
  were filed: Zollman 2007 and 2010, his 2013 survey, all from his own
  site, and the robustness critique by Rosenstock, Bruner & O'Connor
  (2017), from O'Connor's site. Longino's entry pointed to the critique.

## What the readings say about the essay

- **Aubin.** The notes define viability but never compare it with
  stability. Their "stability properties" are about limits of sets. The
  essay's §6.1 contrast follows from the definitions, but it is the
  essay's step. It also needs the essay to say what the constraint set is
  for an organization.
- **Longino.** Her norms concern how a community comes to know, not how it
  decides. The essay's list of what makes criticism "consequential" maps
  onto her norms, and uptake is the one it most needs. Naming them would
  turn §8.2's suggestion into a test.
- **Ostrom.** Her principles describe users governing a resource they
  share. A firm governing itself is a further step. Her own last lesson is
  "no panaceas", so polycentricity is not good in itself.
- **Epistemic networks.** The essay's sentence is as strong as the evidence
  allows, and no stronger. Sparse networks help only where inquiry is hard.
  Even there, Rosenstock et al. prefer an evidence standard to less
  communication. The robust result is Zollman's transient diversity.

One correction to the record's own summary of a source. Longino's entry
says Zollman "proves" the network result; the 2007 paper is a simulation
study. The NOTE on the entry records it.

## Theories

- [THEORY-tmp4t87t](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp4t87t.md), Active. Viability is relative to a constraint set
  fixed apart from the dynamics; the viability kernel is its largest viable
  part. The contrast with stability is marked as not the source's.
- [THEORY-tmpv9j17](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpv9j17.md), Proposed. Longino's account of objectivity
  through the four norms. Proposed because the books are unread and the
  norms' benefit is argued, not shown.
- [THEORY-tmpsk5aw](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpsk5aw.md), Active. Users can govern commons durably, and lasting
  institutions share design principles, not rules. It says the principles
  are correlates, not causes, and are not shown for firms.
- [THEORY-tmpgo526](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpgo526.md), Proposed. The Zollman effect, stated with
  Rosenstock et al.'s scope: it holds only where evidence is scarce.
- [THEORY-tmpqnfk9](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpqnfk9.md), Proposed, extending the last. Transient diversity.

No new topic was needed. The networks papers take `epistemology`,
`network-science` and `philosophy-of-science`. Ostrom takes
`society-and-governance`, `social-science` and `game-theory`, and Aubin
takes `mathematics` and `complex-systems`.
