# NOTE-tmpy2v8a: Clark and Brennan — Grounding in communication

## Contribution

The chapter is a theoretical synthesis that extends the contribution model of Clark and Schaefer. It makes two claims:

- grounding depends on both purpose and medium;
- media can be compared by which grounding techniques they allow, and at what cost.

## Key results

- **p. 127.** "All collective actions are built on common ground and its accumulation."
- **pp. 128–129, grounding criterion.** "The contributor and his or her partners mutually believe that the partners have understood what the contributor meant to a criterion sufficient for current purposes."
- **p. 131, Evidence in Grounding.** "people ultimately seek positive evidence of understanding."
- **p. 141, Constraints on Grounding.**
  - "Reviewability: B can review A’s messages."
  - In email and letters, "an utterance stays behind as an artifact that can be reviewed later by either of the partners—or even by a third party."
- **p. 140, scope.** The chapter sets aside "broadcastable media as books, newspapers, television, and radio".
- **Medium and Purpose Interact.**
  - "Fault costs and repair costs will be higher when a group is involved."
  - "Any medium that supports cooperative work can be evaluated in terms of the techniques it allows for grounding."

## Assumptions

- The analysis covers dyadic, two-way personal media. Broadcast media and multi-party groups are mentioned only briefly.
- People minimize collaborative effort.
- Understanding is graded and relative to the current purpose.

## Concepts

- common ground
- grounding and the grounding criterion
- the contribution model: presentation and acceptance phases
- positive and negative evidence
- least collaborative effort
- referential installments and trial references
- the eight medium constraints, including reviewability and revisability
- grounding costs

## Bearing on the record

The LIT says A cites the chapter for the idea that collaboration requires establishing and updating mutual understanding. That is accurate, and it is the chapter's opening thesis.

A's own gloss is: a record "makes candidate common ground durable, but reading and acknowledgement remain necessary". This is A's extension, but the chapter's terms support it well:

- A record has reviewability, and perhaps revisability.
- Reviewability alone does not meet the grounding criterion, which requires *mutual belief* reached through positive evidence such as acknowledgment or a relevant next contribution.

Two caveats bear on any appeal to this chapter:

- The chapter's analysis is dyadic.
- It sets aside broadcast media.

A shared record read asynchronously by many agents falls outside its stated scope.

- **[TERM-tmp8deza](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8deza.md)** (shared epistemic state, defined as not shared beliefs, not shared memory, and not common knowledge). **Supports the distinction.**
  - Clark and Brennan's common ground is mutual knowledge, beliefs and assumptions, and it requires grounding.
  - On their account, a publicly inspectable record is not common ground.
  - The term is right to separate the two.
- **[CLAIM-tmpyp3u3](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpyp3u3.md)** (a record can represent who contributed a proposition and what standing it has, but not that collaborators believe it or all know it; access is a precondition, not evidence). **Supports, by a separate route.**
  - Grounding requires positive evidence of understanding, and the absence of complaint is not enough.
  - Mere availability of a reviewable artifact is therefore not evidence of mutual understanding.
  - This complements the Halpern–Moses route via [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md) and [ARG-tmpbuocw](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpbuocw.md).
  - It also suggests what *would* count as evidence in a record: acknowledgments, uptake as the relevant next contribution (an entry that builds on another), or citations that answer.
- **[QUESTION-tmpxq2sb](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpxq2sb.md).** The cost framework gives one candidate explanation of where a record helps: it lowers start-up, delay and reviewing costs. It also gives one of where it does not: acknowledgment is costly in asynchronous media. That speaks to the question's "recognition" and "uptake" stages.

## Limitations

- The source is a scanned copy, and the OCR has artifacts. The quotes are verbatim from the text layer and should be checked against the page images.
- The chapter is theoretical, with illustrative excerpts from corpora and cited studies.
- It is dyadic and predates networked collaborative records.
