# QUESTION-088: Does modelling psychological organization as processes that allocate influence to one another predict habit, rumination, rigidity, akrasia and insight better, with one shared set of organizational variables, than models built for each phenomenon or around a central executive?

## Why it is a question

The owner, at U5: "a standalone question is just: 'do we gain something
just by framing a "self" this way?' this feels like an extremely useful model
for explaining a lot of psychological phenomenon." A5 §8: "**Does the model
unify phenomena that are ordinarily described using different psychological
vocabularies?**" Its candidate variables were the persistence of the
influence topology, responsiveness to feedback, diversity of influential
processes, concentration of authority and cross-regional transport. A7 made
it H8, "A common set of organizational variables predicts multiple behavioral
phenotypes". A10's Question 5: "Does the resulting theory explain
psychological integration, conflict, self-control, identity, and
transformation better than competing models?"

The last filing called this [QUESTION-056](QUESTION-056.md) and filed nothing. It refines 056
instead. 056 asks whether an agent-level description implements or replaces
the mechanisms described under it. This asks whether one particular
agent-level description earns its keep by prediction. The objection is the
same, [CLAIM-024](../claims.d/CLAIM-024.md): "Nothing here is new". A7 §14 granted the risk: "A
sufficiently flexible operator framework could redescribe almost any
dynamical process."

The record already reads several phenomena this way. Procrastination is
[CASE-013](../cases.d/CASE-013.md) and [CLAIM-047](../claims.d/CLAIM-047.md). Disorder as lock-in is [QUESTION-045](QUESTION-045.md). Preference
reversal is [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md). An answer is a comparison on held-out phenomena, or
interventions, with flexibility penalized, in which the shared variables do
better or do not.

## Note of 2026-10-26: the lab is not yet the comparison

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CASE-162 CASE-161 CASE-163 — Proposed; the lab's runs filed 2026-10-26, cited as tools for the comparison, nothing here rests on them -->

The Perspective Population Lab (dmarx/PerspectiveLab, through v2.2 at
@9f82bfd) is one engineered lineage in which authority, selected
evidence, practice that depends on access, maintained rules and
diagnostic interventions run together ([CASE-162](../cases.d/CASE-162.md), [CASE-161](../cases.d/CASE-161.md),
[CASE-163](../cases.d/CASE-163.md)). Its v1.2 README lists correspondences to habit and agenda
control as hypotheses. It has predicted no independently measured habit,
rumination, rigidity, akrasia or insight from shared variables, and has
been compared with no model built for one phenomenon or around a central
executive. It is a tool for this question's comparison, not an answer.

The lab's v2.6 (dmarx/PerspectiveLab@74621fb
experiments/affective_dissociation/) adds an affective domain: five
stipulated causes of continued pursuit after an outcome is devalued,
habit among them as one of the rivals. That is one phenomenon, not
several, and habit in it is a class written into the generator, not a
phenotype predicted from shared variables. Its comparison is between
measurements within one known catalog. Interventions predicted from
probes and readouts beat those predicted from passive choice (mean MSE
0.00230 against 0.03496), a conventional model of the same law makes the
same predictions, and a hybrid left out of the catalog is assigned to a
catalog class in all 40 of its test worlds. It is still a tool, not this
comparison. The question is unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-26 (later): habit fitted, and reframing built

The lab's v2.7 (dmarx/PerspectiveLab@c54617e
experiments/trained_affect/) is the closest it has come to this
question's comparison, and it is within one phenomenon, continued pursuit
after an outcome is devalued. Predictors built for learned value, habit
and cue sensitization are fitted beside one with an organizational uptake
term, on the same recorded choices, and scored on synthetic worlds held
out from fitting. The predictors built for a phenomenon win where it
dominates: a cue-and-habit predictor scores 0.01152 in the habit world and
0.00896 in the sensitization world, against 0.02581 and 0.01466 for the
uptake predictor. In the governance world a generic predictor with no
interaction beats the uptake predictor. The uptake predictor wins in one
held-out hybrid. All eight worlds are one logistic law with different
coefficients.

v2.8 and v2.9 (experiments/perspective_engine/ and
experiments/endogenous_frames/) build reframing, a perspective reading
retained evidence under a new frame. The frame is written by hand in v2.8
and selected in v2.9 from a 15-rule grammar on labelled outcomes. Neither
predicts insight, rumination or any other phenomenon; each scores a
synthetic forecast. The lab is still a tool for this comparison, not an
answer. The question is unchanged.
