# THEORY-tmpt62hk: Perceptual constancies are invariants of transformations that act within experience, not only of physical transformations or planar projections, since perceived shape and depth match no linear projection and the transformations can be realized with no physical change; on Isaac's structuralist account, after Cassirer, it is these invariants, co-present in experience with the varying appearances they abstract from, that make experience represent an objective world and so distinguish subject from object

## Source

Isaac (2025), *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 6, LIT-1361, read in full
in NOTE-tmpn1hle from the publisher's open-access PDF. §2.2 ("Perceptual
constancies: Group-theoretic structure") with footnotes 3–8 and Figure 1,
and §3 with the Cassirer quotation and Figure 3. The argument for
objectivity is Cassirer's, "The concept of group and the theory of
perception" (1944), quoted at length, and is not reconstructed beyond
what Isaac quotes.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested in the paper. It argues in two steps.

**The transformations are internal.** Constancy is "something about its
phenomenal character" that "remains 'constant' across gross
transformations in the signal incident at the retina": colour, size,
shape and timbre. One could model it as an artefact of physical
transformations, rotation groups or the projective group acting on planar
projections, with no structure in experience (Gibson, Noë). Isaac argues
that this is wrong, for two reasons.

- Experience of shape is not flat. Converging tracks and a carpet that
  turns trapezoidal down a hallway are contracted *in depth*, and
  perceived angles measured with an adjustable compass "do not match those
  of a linear projection" (Erkelens 2015; Wagner et al. 2018, cited). So
  the invariant cannot be that of planar projections. It "must instead be
  invariant over some internal analog to such transformations".
- The transformations occur with no physical analogue. Two static images
  at suitable intervals are seen as one object rotating or translating
  (Shepard 1984), and same–different judgements take time linear in the
  angle between the two orientations (Shepard and Cooper 1982).

The projective invariants the paper uses as the physical alternative are
correct as stated. Under random projective maps, the line cross-ratio
(EG × FH)/(FG × EH) of footnote 7 and Niall and Macnamara's planar
cross-ratio [area(EFC) × area(GHC)]/[area(FGC) × area(EHC)] of footnote 3
were invariant to a relative error below 10⁻¹¹ in 2,000 trials each,
checked here. For the planar ratio this follows because each triangle's
area is multiplied by the same determinant over the product of its
vertices' homogeneous weights, and every vertex appears equally often
above and below the line.

**The invariants make experience objective.** Colour experience has two
levels at once: the shadowed and sunlit parts of a wall differ in
"apparent, subjective" colour, and the wall is seen as uniformly coloured.
Instructions can move subjects between the two (Radonjić and Brainard
2016), and the painter's attitude reaches the first. Cassirer: wherever
there is "an opposition and separation between an 'ego' and the 'world'",
perception "is a process of objectification, the characteristic nature
and tendency of which finds expression in the formation of invariants. It
is within this process that the distinction between 'reality' and
'appearance' emerges." Isaac concludes that the group-theoretic structure
of perception "ensures phenomenal experience constitutes a genuine
representation of an objective world", and the abstract that it "induces
a distinction between subject and object".

What could have failed: the first half could be refuted, modality by
modality, by showing that a constancy is fully explained by an invariant
of the stimulus, with no internal transformation. For shape in depth the
cited angle measurements count against that. Nothing in the paper could
show the second half false.

## What this does not say

- **Not that constancy is a group.** The paper calls the link between
  invariance and group theory "conceptual", not empirical, and its own
  cases are not groups: constancy is "at best approximate", Krantz's
  structures for metamers are groupoids (footnote 5), and in the
  illumination toy model the inverse of adding a light is defined by
  filtering the white baseline, so it is an inverse only from that
  baseline. "Group-theoretic" means invariance under transformation.
- **Not a test of objectivity.** No criterion separates a process that
  extracts invariants, which a camera pipeline with white balance or any
  object recognizer also does, from one for which there is a
  subject–object distinction. The co-presence of the two levels is offered
  for introspective verification (footnote 6).
- **Not about whether a state is conscious.** Like the rest of the paper,
  it concerns the character of experience.
- **Not a claim about mechanism.** Isaac takes "no position" on whether
  perception involves multiple stages of representation (footnote 6). The
  internal transformations are functional analogues, not identified
  processes.
- **Not that illusions are errors, or that they are not, in general.** The
  checker-shadow case is read as veridical recovery of reflectance; the
  paper says "many", not all, illusions are mislabelled.

## Connections

- **QUESTION-254 and CLAIM-412.** The record filed the paper for this
  layer, as a candidate for what a perspective adds to an interpretation.
  It offers a structural mark of perspective, a varying level and an
  invariant level present together, and no criterion that would keep a
  perspective-free invariant extractor out. So it narrows the question
  and does not answer it. CLAIM-411's gap, why any such organization is
  conscious, is outside it.
- **THEORY-880.** Lyre's similarity structuralism places an experience by
  its position in a quality space. This account adds a layer above the
  geometry that Lyre lacks, and denies that geometry alone generates
  meaning. THEORY-867's finding that colour-space geometry does not mark
  the unique hues does not touch it.
- **QUESTION-255.** The two levels are a case of one experience holding
  two incompatible-seeming descriptions of the same surface (different
  and the same colour), which is not a gluing failure: one global state
  holds both, indexed by level. The reading is the record's.
