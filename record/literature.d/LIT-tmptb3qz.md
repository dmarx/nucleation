U.S. District Court for the District of Rhode Island, Judge Mary S. McElroy (2026), *U.S. District Court for the District of Rhode Island* — <https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.rid.62049/gov.uscourts.rid.62049.38.0.pdf>

## Key takeaways

- The court quashed a DOJ administrative subpoena (issued under 18 U.S.C. § 3486) for records of minors who received gender-affirming care at Rhode Island Hospital, on three independent grounds: the subpoena lacks a congressionally authorized purpose, it was issued for an improper purpose in bad faith, and it would violate the children's Fourteenth Amendment right to informational privacy. The court also enjoined DOJ from receiving or using any patient-identifying information produced under it.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The Rhode Island Child Advocate and Rhode Island Hospital (RIH) each moved to quash a DOJ HIPAA administrative subpoena. Issued in July 2025 as part of a nationwide investigation, the subpoena sought years of identifying and clinical records for every minor who had received gender-affirming care at RIH. According to the order, DOJ stopped negotiating with RIH and then, without notice, obtained an enforcement order from the Northern District of Texas. The court finds that the Child Advocate has standing and that the collateral-attack doctrine does not bar relief, because it quashes the subpoena itself rather than the Texas enforcement order. The court also sharply criticizes DOJ's candor toward both courts and toward the negotiating parties.
