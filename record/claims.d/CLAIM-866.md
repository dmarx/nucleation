# CLAIM-866: A kept channel is part of will-organization

## The claim

*An alternative, the one that names a feature. Its rival is
[CLAIM-867](CLAIM-867.md).*

[TERM-023](../terms.d/TERM-023.md) defines will-organization as the maintained organization of
"relations governing the conditions under which an agent's available
action-generating regimes acquire, retain, lose, or alter practical
efficacy". A channel by which another party can move the agent's
precedence, jurisdiction or revision is such a relation: it governs the
conditions under which a regime acquires efficacy, whether or not it is
used. [CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md) already says so in its last clause, counting domination as
"another's capacity to govern those same relations, used or not", and
[CLAIM-868](CLAIM-868.md)'s premise 1 counts couplings among what fixes
will-organization.

On this reading, Pereboom's Case 1 is not [CASE-310](../cases.d/CASE-310.md) but its variant
1. The neuroscientists "have the ability to manipulate Professor Plum's
neural states at any time" ([NOTE-856](../notes.d/NOTE-856.md), quoting the 2024 wording), and they
keep it. Plum and his twin then differ in a present feature of
will-organization: what an outside channel reaches of his governing
relations. It is testable now, in two ways:

- **by intervention**: an input on the channel moves his precedence, and
  the same input on his twin, who has no such channel, does nothing;
- **by reach**: comparing the outside channel's controllability Gramian
  with the agent's own, for the transitions that matter, gives what the
  outsider can reach and at what least energy, which systems of the same
  sensitivity can differ in arbitrarily ([CLAIM-542](CLAIM-542.md)).

So present structure sees manipulation that keeps its channel. Only the
manipulation that withdraws it, [CASE-310](../cases.d/CASE-310.md) proper and Pereboom's Case
2, is invisible, and there [CLAIM-868](CLAIM-868.md)'s record of formation is needed.

## What it does not say

- **That the feature shows manipulation happened.** It shows the channel.
  A twin exposed to an equally capable, unused channel held by someone
  benign matches Plum in this feature. What it detects is domination in
  [CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md)'s sense, a standing vulnerability, not a past act.
- **That Case 1 Plum is not responsible**, or that the channel's
  existence makes his will not his own. That is Pereboom's argument
  ([THEORY-328](../theory.d/THEORY-328.md)), and [CLAIM-022](CLAIM-022.md) still holds: explaining efficacy, now
  including the outside channel, does not settle whose the will is.
