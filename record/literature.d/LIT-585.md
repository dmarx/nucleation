# LIT-585: The Expected Value of Control: An Integrative Theory of Anterior Cingulate Cortex Function

Amitai Shenhav, Matthew M. Botvinick & Jonathan D. Cohen (2013), *Neuron*
79(2):217–240 — DOI-10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.007

## Key takeaways

- **Control has three component functions.** *Regulation* is the control
  signal acting on lower-level processing. *Specification* is deciding which
  signal (identity) and how strong (intensity). *Monitoring* is detecting
  the information that specification needs. The theory gives dACC monitoring
  and specification, and gives regulation to lateral PFC and other
  structures.
- **The decision maximizes the expected value of control.** EVC is the sum
  of the outcomes' values weighted by their probabilities, with value defined
  recursively and future rewards discounted, minus a cost of control that
  rises with signal intensity. The control system picks the signal with the
  highest EVC.
- **Control is costly.** "[M]ental effort is assumed to carry intrinsic
  disutility." People avoid control-demanding tasks and will forgo reward to
  do so. dACC's output can be read as "a willingness-to-pay signal in the
  currency of cognitive control".
- **Default override is the general case.** Stroop colour-naming,
  exploration, foraging and patient intertemporal choice all require
  overriding what would happen without control. The EVC model predicts dACC
  engagement in each, and the review cites evidence for each.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, for the record's coverage of
behavioural control. It is the record's main reading of the view that
control is a distinct decision process with a dedicated substrate: a
specification system that weighs payoffs against effort costs and directs
separate regulators.

**The boundary.** The paper is a theory of a brain region and of human
cognitive control. Its formalism borrows from reinforcement learning and
optimal control, and it notes that EVC "generalizes what is referred to as
a Q-value". It gives no instruction for machine-learning practice, so it
belongs here.

That makes it the record's clearest counterweight to [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md). The theory
holds that regulation is one motive state checking another, with "no
distinct regulating system above them". EVC posits exactly a distinct
system that decides control, though not one that regulates: specification
is kept apart from regulation. The theory is about emotion and EVC is about
cognitive control, but the paper lists "modulators of emotion" among the
parameters a control signal can set, and treats override of
emotion-driven automatic responses as within dACC's remit. The two accounts
are not tested against each other, so no `rivals` is declared.
[NOTE-446](../notes.d/NOTE-446.md) sets out where they meet.

It also stands beside Ainslie's précis ([LIT-586](LIT-586.md)). Both treat patience as
overcoming a default preference for immediate reward. EVC treats it as
costly control allocated by a separate decision. Ainslie treats it as
bargaining among interests, with no organ of will, and his précis argues
against "organ" models of exactly this kind.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
