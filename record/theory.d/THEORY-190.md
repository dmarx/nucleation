# THEORY-190: Under geometric coarse-graining of the S¹ hidden-metric network model, which merges blocks of r angular neighbours into supernodes linked if any of their members were, the supernodes' link probability approaches the S¹ form with β kept, κ′ = (Σκ^β)^{1/β}, μ′ = μ/r and R′ = R/r, its ratio to that form tending to 1 as their separation grows large against the block width and the coupling μκ′κ″; between nearby supernodes it exceeds that form

## Source

García-Pérez, Boguñá and Serrano (2017; Nature Physics 2018),
[LIT-869](../literature.d/LIT-869.md), read in [NOTE-673](../notes.d/NOTE-673.md).

## What was actually shown

The model is S¹: nodes on a circle of radius N/2π with angles θ and hidden
degrees κ, linked with probability 1/(1 + (RΔθ/(μκκ′))^β). Its hyperbolic
form is the ℍ² model of [THEORY-188](THEORY-188.md). The transformation merges blocks
of r consecutive nodes on the circle into supernodes, linked if any of
their members were.

- **Closure.** The probability that two supernodes are linked is one
  minus the product of the r² member pairs' non-link probabilities. Cut
  after its first-order term, and with every member pair treated as at
  the blocks' separation, it is again of the S¹ form, provided
  κ′ = (Σκ_j^β)^{1/β}, μ′ = μ/r, R′ = R/r and β′ = β. The supernode angle
  is any order-preserving mean; the source picks a power mean that
  composes. This could have failed in simulation. On a synthetic network
  of about 225,000 nodes (γ = 2.5, β = 1.5), the empirical connection
  curve stays on the theoretical one through eight layers, and mean
  clustering stays flat (Fig. 3A).
- **Degree exponent.** A sum of r variables κ^β stays power-law, and γ is
  asymptotically kept, when (γ−1)/2 < β. Otherwise scale-freeness is lost
  along the flow.
- **Average degree.** ⟨k⟩′ = r^ν⟨k⟩. If the renormalized hidden degrees
  stay a pure power law, which the source says is not true in general,
  then ν = 2/(γ−1) − 1 where (γ−1)/β < 1 and ν = 2/β − 1 where
  1 < (γ−1)/β < 2. So ν > 0, and the flow goes to a complete graph, if
  γ < 3 or β < 2. ⟨k⟩ is kept on γ = 3 (β ≥ 2) or β = 2 (γ ≥ 3), an
  unstable fixed line between small-world and non-small-world. ν < 0, and
  the flow goes to a ring that loses the small world, if γ > 3 and β > 2.
  An exact series for r = 2 agrees with this for large γ or β.
- **Links kept are long-range.** Links that survive to a coarser layer join
  nodes at larger angular separation.

## What this does not say

- **Not that real networks have hidden metric spaces.** The source finds
  six embedded real networks self-similar under the flow and calls this
  new evidence that hidden metric spaces underlie real networks. The
  account here is about the model. In the data, the collapse is judged by
  eye over two to five layers, with no null ordering of the circle to
  show that geometric blocks are what produce it.
- **Not exact.** Closure holds to first order with equidistant blocks, and
  that is weakest for adjacent supernodes. The closed forms for ν rest on
  a further approximation. No error bound is given.
- **Not a hierarchy found in the data.** Each node sits in one supernode
  per layer, so the layers nest, but the nesting is set by the procedure
  (contiguous blocks on the circle), not discovered. The ℍ² radius is
  popularity, as in [THEORY-188](THEORY-188.md). Neither is a hierarchy in which one
  kind implies another, which is what [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md) asks about.
- **Not unique.** Other choices of block (equal arcs, connected pairs only)
  are said to give the same closure. The supernode angle is a convention,
  and its power-mean form depends on where the angle origin is placed.

## Note of 2026-10-20: the closure at large separation, and where it fails

Worked for the promotion audit (batch 048).

**The bound.** In S¹, members i and j at arc distance d, with
a_ij = μκ_iκ_j, link with p = 1/(1 + (d/a_ij)^β), so
(a/d)^β(1 − (a/d)^β) ≤ p ≤ (a/d)^β. Let two blocks of r members each span
an arc of at most w, with supernode angles inside their blocks, at arc
distance D = RΔθ′. Every member pair then has d_ij in [D − w, D + w]. The
supernodes link with the union probability U, and by the Bonferroni
inequalities Σp_ij − Σ_{pairs} p p′ ≤ U ≤ Σp_ij. Since
Σ_ij a_ij^β = μ^β(Σκ_i^β)(Σκ_j^β) = (μκ′κ″)^β with κ′ = (Σκ^β)^{1/β},

U = (μκ′κ″/D)^β (1 + ε), |ε| ≤ βw/D (1 + o(1)) + O(r² max(a_ij/D)^β).

After coarse-graining, μ′ = μ/r and R′ = R/r give R′Δθ′/μ′ = D/μ, so the S¹
form is p′ = (μκ′κ″/D)^β(1 + O((μκ′κ″/D)^β)). Hence U/p′ → 1 as D grows
large against w and μκ′κ″, with β unchanged.

**Where it fails.** Two-node blocks of equal hidden degree with μκ² = 1,
members at unit spacing (positions 0, 1 and s, s + 1), supernode
separation s. Then U = 1 − Π(1 − 1/(1 + d^β)) over the four member pairs
and p′ = 1/(1 + (s/2^{2/β})^β). The ratio U/p′ at s = 2, 4, 8, 16, 64 is
1.32, 1.18, 1.07, 1.03, 1.003 for β = 1.5; 1.42, 1.17, 1.05, 1.01, 1.001
for β = 2; and 1.86, 1.23, 1.05, 1.01, 1.001 for β = 3. The excess is
largest between neighbouring supernodes, which is where most of a node's
links lie, so a degree flow derived from the closed form inherits it.
