# LIT-1449: Control Theory, Goal Attainment, and Psychopathology

Charles S. Carver (2018), *Psychological Inquiry* 29(3):139–144 —
DOI-10.1080/1047840X.2018.1513681

## Key takeaways

- **What cybernetics adds to goal talk.** Goal pursuit also needs
  perception of the present state and a comparison of it with the goal:
  "action occurs in service to changing the input" (Powers, [LIT-1179](LIT-1179.md)).
- **Psychopathology as distorted loop parameters.** The input can be
  biased or attend to the wrong channel. The reference value can be
  wrong, too lofty, or "inimical to one's life-space of goals". The
  comparison can be too precise or too sloppy. The output can be
  missing, or blocked by doubt.
- **Valence.** Valence "has a different origin and a different meaning
  than most other people seem to think". An approach system and an
  avoidance system can each do well or badly, so each can produce
  positive or negative affect (citing Carver & Scheier 1990, 1998).
  Valence is neutral at a reference value, and "these are all seen in
  terms of velocity of goal attainment rather than states".
- **Engagement against progress.** Engagement is high near the
  criterion and somewhat below it. Effort falls off when things go "too
  badly", and ceases when the incentive is lost.
- **Competition and priority.** Behaviour can be guided by two competing
  sources, one reactive and one deliberative. Which level of Powers's
  hierarchy is "functionally superordinate" varies by situation and
  person. Many goals are in play at once, with "continual juggling of the
  priorities afforded to each". Turning from one goal to another may be
  a change in priority ranking rather than giving up (n. 3).
- **Doubt is outside the loop.** Nothing in control theory predicts
  disengagement: "A thermostat that can't get the furnace to keep up …
  doesn't quit trying". Expectancy-value theory supplies what is
  missing.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the record's proposal that hedonic valence is a
regime in which one ordering of a system's own states prevails in the
negotiation among its perspectives ([CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md), [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md)). It is
the only first-hand statement of Carver and Scheier's affect account that
the record could reach lawfully. The 1990 paper ([LIT-1441](LIT-1441.md))
and the 1982 review ([LIT-604](LIT-604.md)) are both Unavailable. The bearing is set
out in [NOTE-1102](../notes.d/NOTE-1102.md). In short:

- the proposal's sign clause is Carver and Scheier's, with one
  difference that matters: their neutral point is a reference rate, not
  rest;
- their account puts a valence on every goal loop and has no single
  potential ordering;
- their priority juggling is the nearest thing in the tradition to the
  proposal's potential.

Its loop vocabulary is Powers's ([LIT-1179](LIT-1179.md), [LIT-1188](LIT-1188.md), [LIT-1197](LIT-1197.md)).
