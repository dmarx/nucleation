# CLAIM-277: Durable authority among an organization's processes need not be a primitive: trust can be the temporal regularity of endorsement that policies earn by being right often, and the same loop entrenches whatever gained influence early

## The claim

A4 §5, reading the record's note on Montévil and Mossio ([LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)):
"For our model, that means distinguishing persistent authority structures
from transient endorsement updates". The owner, at U5: "why? I'd expect
persistent authority to be an emergent property here as 'right a lot'
policies become distinguished into 'trusted' policies."

A5 §1 granted it: "We don't need two kinds of authority, one transient and
one persistent. We need an authority-allocation process whose dynamics can
produce different degrees of persistence." With endorsements updated toward
a learned assessment of each operator's suitability,
Ė_ji = η_j[Q_ji − E_ji], "**Trust is then an emergent temporal regularity of
endorsement**, not an independently imposed authority category." Momentary
endorsement, established trust and institutionalized authority "are
descriptions of different dynamical regimes, not different ontological
primitives."

A6 §1B gave the knob, a memory kernel over the endorsed operator's track
record. "A slowly decaying memory kernel means established trust persists
despite occasional failures. A rapidly decaying kernel makes endorsement
highly responsive to recent behavior." And A6 §1C separated latent authority
from the influence currently exercised. "an operator can remain highly
*trusted* while being temporarily inactive."

A5 named the failure mode in the same loop. One chain is good policy, then
endorsement, then greater influence, then more chances to show competence.
The other is early advantage, then greater influence, then more visibility,
then greater endorsement. "This is how a system might develop both
well-calibrated expertise and undeserved authority."

The claim rests on [CLAIM-282](CLAIM-282.md). The slow route by which an operator's
authority changes, through others' endorsements, is the route by which
trust accumulates.

## What it does not say

- It does not say which loop dominates in a given organization. That is an
  empirical question about memory kernels and feedback.
- It does not give a criterion for when entrenched authority is capture and
  not merited trust. The record's nearest result is protective lock-in, a
  regime that "generates the conditions that justify its own continuation"
  ([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md), [CASE-036](../cases.d/CASE-036.md)). A11 §4 asked for a criterion grounded in the
  organization's own good. [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) disclaims that viability alone
  supplies one.
- It does not say the record holds a theory of credibility among
  contributors. [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md) asks the same question about the record's own
  contributors.

## Note of 2026-10-18: the loop seen from the process that loses

A46 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 cited this claim for "Niche exclusion by
authority deprivation". A rare specialist "receives little influence
during ordinary operation", so it "receives fewer opportunities to
demonstrate competence", and "Its apparent performance deteriorates,
further reducing its influence":

  Low exposure → Poor competence estimates → Low endorsement → Even lower
  exposure

"**A system may destroy useful niche diversity not by explicitly
suppressing constituent processes, but by allocating authority in a way
that prevents rare specialists from reproducing their value.**" A46 called
it "almost a dual of the authority-capture process".

It is this claim's entrenchment loop seen from the side that lost. A5's
good chain, "good policy, then endorsement, then greater influence, then
more chances to show competence", run downward for an operator whose
competence shows only in rare contexts. The antecedent A46 did not cite
is March ([THEORY-142](../theory.d/THEORY-142.md)): exploitation's returns are surer and nearer, so
learning from experience crowds out exploration.

A32 of the same exchange gave the other side, from this claim's
separation of latent authority from current activation ("An operator can
remain trusted while inactive."): "a training system might leave an
emergency regime while retaining a durable propensity to recruit it
again." Which of the two happens to an idle specialist turns on whether
its competence estimate decays while it is not exposed, which is this
claim's memory kernel. A slow kernel keeps latent authority; a fast one
starves it.

It bears on [QUESTION-083](../questions.d/QUESTION-083.md) and on the owner's non-domination criterion
([CLAIM-362](CLAIM-362.md)), whose test is whether a specialist can recover
influence. Not filed as a claim of its own. The status is unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-18 (later): when deprivation destroys competence

The note above records niche exclusion by authority deprivation at the
level of competence estimates. A synthesis the owner relayed later the
same day (workbench entry of 2026-10-18) took it a step further. When
influence decides access to training, not just to evaluation, the loop
can destroy the competence itself: "The organization has altered the
specialist's future competence by denying the opportunities required to
maintain it." Then the specialist cannot recover even when its niche
returns. Whether that happens, and how to tell it from mere under-use,
is [QUESTION-134](../questions.d/QUESTION-134.md). The recovery exponent that measures under-use is in
[QUESTION-132](../questions.d/QUESTION-132.md).

## Note of 2026-10-21: the loop in institutions and in people, and as perpetuated privilege

**In institutions.** A22 §5 of the exchange of 2026-10-20 re-derived this
claim's note of 2026-10-18 nearly word for word: "An organization can lose
the capacities of a constituent not by directly suppressing it, but by
failing to maintain the conditions under which its contribution can be
recognized and reproduced", with a chain "Low legibility of competence →
Low endorsement → Low opportunity → Diminished demonstrated or actual
capacity". A22 adds one thing: the first link is a failure of legibility
([TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)), not of exposure. It also applied the chain to a
constituency whose interests "are difficult to express in the categories
an institution uses".

**As perpetuated privilege.** At U24 the owner heard this claim's loop, in
A23's form, as "perpetuation of privilege" in feminist philosophy. A24
rebuilt the expert-roster version ("Prior institutional standing →
Opportunities to contribute and be recognized → Visible indicators of
expertise → Future institutional standing") and the side that loses ("a
measure of demonstrated competence can partly reflect the history of
access to opportunities for demonstrating competence"). Both are already
here, in A5's second chain and the 2026-10-18 note.

**In people.** A26 restated the note of 2026-10-18 for people, not
operators. A specialist strong on rare cases gets fewer chances to show
it, and "Its scarcity of favorable performance evidence can then be
interpreted as a reason to continue denying it authority". It added
Fricker's structural, pre-emptive testimonial injustice ([THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md)),
where a speaker's word "is never sought": "**Institutional recognition can
depend on evidence of prior recognition.**" It also added Longino's uptake
([THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)): an organization "may retain dissenting specialists and
allow them to speak while having no meaningful process through which
their criticism affects established views".

What A24 and A26 add is the case: Fricker and Anderson's un-refreshed list
([THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md)), with its prejudice condition as the criterion this claim
says it lacks. That is filed as [CLAIM-406](CLAIM-406.md), which rests on this
claim, and the uptake point as [CLAIM-403](CLAIM-403.md). Its limit is this claim's.
A26: "The mechanism itself is not inherently unjust. A normative
conclusion requires establishing relevant prejudice, wrongful exclusion,
or another justified standard of unfairness." Whether the loop is
pathological by an internal standard is [QUESTION-087](../questions.d/QUESTION-087.md). Whether it destroys
competence, not just the evidence of it, is [QUESTION-134](../questions.d/QUESTION-134.md).

## Note of 2026-10-25: the entrenchment loop, named and not run

A42 of the exchange of 2026-10-24, continued, put this claim's converse as
the model's "crucial feedback loop": access → experience → competence and
evaluation → future access, which "can also preserve an early advantage
when alternative perspectives don't receive enough opportunities to
demonstrate competence". That is also [THEORY-927](../theory.d/THEORY-927.md)'s self-reinforcing
gate. A47's experiment froze competence, so it cannot show the loop
([CASE-158](../cases.d/CASE-158.md)). The claim is unchanged.
