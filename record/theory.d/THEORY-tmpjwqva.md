# THEORY-tmpjwqva: In rodents, gut microbes can drive stress and affect-related behaviour through vagal, immune, endocrine and metabolite routes, and faecal transplant from depressed people partly transfers the phenotype; in people, probiotics have so far shown at most small reductions in subjective stress in healthy volunteers, and strains with strong preclinical records have failed in controlled trials

## Source

Forssten et al. (2022), [LIT-915](../literature.d/LIT-915.md), "One Giant Leap from Mouse to Man", read in
full in [NOTE-703](../notes.d/NOTE-703.md) from the Europe PMC text. The authors were all employed by
IFF Health & Biosciences, which "manufactures and commercializes
probiotics". The review is narrative, with no search method.

## What was actually shown

**Routes, in animals.** "The vagus nerve is the most direct route of
communication between the gut and the brain". In mice the effects are lost
after vagotomy. Germ-free animals show an exaggerated stress-hormone
response that colonisation normalises, replicated across rodent strains.

**Transplant.** Faecal transplant from depressed humans to rodents gives
abnormal behaviour "indicating at least partial transfer of the clinical
psychiatric phenotype". The donors were few, and "the gut microbiome is the
only humanized component of the rodent".

**People.** "A recent meta-analysis demonstrated that probiotic consumption
could result in a reduction of subjective stress levels in healthy
volunteers". Single trials report reduced reactivity to sad mood, perceived
stress and anxiety, some only under stress or in subgroups.

**Failures.** L. rhamnosus JB-1, after "comprehensive preclinical evidence",
"was not found to have an effect on mood, anxiety, stress, sleep quality,
memory performance, attention, or anti-inflammatory cytokine levels
compared with the placebo." Another strain did not relieve exam stress. A
combination left depression scores identical to placebo.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The strains with the best rodent
records could have worked in people. The trials the review reports say
they did not.

## What this does not say

- **That probiotics treat depression.** The evidence is small trials and
  nulls (C4, weak). The authors note that "most findings from these
  clinical trials cannot be extrapolated to patients with clinically
  diagnosed depression."
- **That one's own microbiome moves everyday mood.** In people the
  evidence is about adding strains, mostly in healthy volunteers, with
  small effects on subjective stress.
- **That the routes are shown in humans.** Vagal, immune and endocrine
  signalling are documented in animals and asserted in humans (C1).
- **That the review is complete.** It has no search method, and its
  optimistic close is warmer than its own failures warrant ([NOTE-703](../notes.d/NOTE-703.md)).
