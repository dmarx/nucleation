# NOTE-tmpouif2: Conceptualization of the Holobiont Paradigm as It Pertains to Corals

## Contribution

A short review, written after an NSF "Reintegrating Biology" meeting, which
argues that coral research should treat the coral as a consortium. It adds
no data. Its contributions are a definition of the coral holobiont, four
methodological demands under the heading "a holistic approach requires",
and three open research questions. It is useful mainly as a compact
statement of the position with its worked examples.

## Key insight

A partner's trait is not a property of the partner. Whether a Symbiodiniaceae
genus is "thermally tolerant", or whether a coral "bleached", depends on the
combination it is in and on which partner the measurement looked at. So the
unit that has the phenotype is the combination, and a study that measures
one partner can misattribute what it sees.

## Assumptions

- **Holobiont by location.** The coral holobiont is the coral plus the
  microbiota in its body, mucus and skeleton (Introduction, after Rohwer et
  al. 2002). Associated animals are excluded "even if they engage in
  mutualisms with corals". Margulis's 1991 definition, a "symbiont compound
  of recognizable bionts", is quoted, with the caveat that not every
  symbiont is part of a holobiont. No criterion of integration or
  dependence is used.
- **Coral** covers scleractinians and octocorals together.
- **The cited results are taken as given.** The review does not reanalyse
  them.

## Key results

The paper's evidence, every item cited from other work:

- **Mutual benefits (Constituents section).** Corals gain photosynthate and
  enhanced calcification from Symbiodiniaceae, and the algae take up the
  coral's nitrogenous waste and are protected. Bacteria may supply carbon,
  nitrogen and sulfur and protect against antagonists. For archaea, viruses
  and fungi the roles are "reviewed" possibilities.
- **Changing membership (Specificity section).** Coral species keep specific
  Symbiodiniaceae genera and types under stress (Goulet 2006, 2007), with
  shuffling of proportions (Berkelmans & van Oppen 2006). Bacteria are both
  core and transient, and vary by geography and microhabitat. Hence the
  claim that "the same coral colony may represent a different holobiont at
  different times and/or under different environmental conditions".
- **Symbiont affects host fitness.** Juvenile Acropora millepora and A.
  tenuis with Cladocopium grew up to three times faster than with
  Durusdinium (Little et al. 2004). The authors infer an effect on
  survivorship and on the onset of reproduction.
- **Bleaching mechanism contested.** Whether the host expels the algae or
  the algae abandon "a sinking ship" is "still debated". In some species
  bleaching varies with the Symbiodiniaceae hosted, and in others it does
  not.
- **Non-additivity (in an anemone).** In Exaiptasia pallida, Florida
  anemone–Symbiodinium holobionts had higher oxygen flux under heat than
  Bermuda anemones with their native Breviolum. A laboratory pairing of
  Bermuda anemones with Symbiodinium gave higher flux than either natural
  combination at 32 and 34 °C (Goulet et al. 2005). This is the abstract's
  "evidence that the holobiont properties are not necessarily the sum of
  its parts".
- **Two routes to one bleaching outcome.** Octocoral branches at +3 °C lost
  Symbiodiniaceae: 26% in Eunicea tourneforti and 35% in Pseudoplexaura
  crucis. In P. crucis lipid also fell 19%, so symbionts per unit host lipid
  did not change significantly. The reading: E. tourneforti lost symbionts
  per host cell, while P. crucis lost host cells (McCauley et al. 2018).
- **Stable consortia under perturbation.** In seven Caribbean octocorals,
  in Acropora digitifera and A. muricata, and in six Red Sea corals, the
  Symbiodiniaceae and dominant bacteria were stable while bacterial
  abundances or mucus communities shifted.

## Concepts

- **holobiont** — after Margulis, a "symbiont compound of recognizable
  bionts". For corals, the coral plus the microbiota in its body, mucus and
  skeleton.
- **coral consortium** — the paper's synonym for the coral holobiont.
- **specificity** — a coral species hosting particular Symbiodiniaceae (or
  bacterial) taxa consistently.
- **shuffling** — a change in the proportions of symbiont types already
  present, as opposed to acquiring new ones.
- **core microbiome / transient microbiota** — bacteria consistently present
  versus those that come and go.
- **Adaptive Bleaching Hypothesis (ABH)** — Buddemeier and Fautin's 1993
  proposal that bleaching lets a holobiont lose ill-suited symbionts and
  take up better-suited ones.

## Connections

- **The bleaching review ([LIT-tmpx0l32](../literature.d/LIT-tmpx0l32.md)).** Boilard et al. build their second
  stage of bleaching on the ABH and describe it in host-centred terms ("the
  coral host has the ability to regulate the density and diversity of their
  associated Symbiodiniaceae"). That is the framing this paper warns about.
  Neither cites the other; they appeared a month apart.
- **Lloyd ([LIT-160](../literature.d/LIT-160.md)).** C7 is Lloyd's §4.6 question. On her anatomy, C4 is
  evidence relevant to holobionts as interactors, a combination-level
  character, and says nothing about holobionts as reproducers or as
  manifestors of adaptation.
- **Wilson & Barker ([LIT-168](../literature.d/LIT-168.md)) and O'Malley & Parke ([LIT-210](../literature.d/LIT-210.md))** report the
  holobiont's individuality as unsettled. This paper supplies coral cases
  for that dispute and does not take a side.
- **Baedke ([LIT-167](../literature.d/LIT-167.md))** rejects the holobiont as organism on anti-nesting and
  epistemic grounds. C2, that membership changes, is an independent
  difficulty for treating the holobiont as one persisting thing. This paper
  does not draw it.
- **Montévil & Mossio ([LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)).** Their tendency to closure is a proposed
  way to draw boundaries between interdependent organisms. The location
  definition used here is the kind of observer-drawn boundary that closure
  is meant to replace.

## Bearing on the record

- It is the conceptual source for the composite-system framing of corals.
  It is cited, with the bleaching review, in the theory that coral
  bleaching is a breakdown of a regulated host–symbiont exchange reached by
  more than one route ([THEORY-tmpwypmh](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwypmh.md)). C5 is the evidence for "more than
  one route".
- No ML instruction; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **A review with no data**, and short: about 3,700 words of body text.
- **The non-additivity evidence is not coral evidence** and covers one
  physiological variable.
- **The holobiont definition is spatial.** It gives no way to decide
  whether a transient bacterium is a member, a question the authors raise
  themselves ("what is considered transient needs to be defined").
- **Unit of selection is posed, not argued.**
- The octocoral inference (host-cell loss versus symbiont loss per cell)
  rests on lipid as a proxy for host biomass, and the review does not
  discuss the proxy.

## Open questions

- What makes a microbe a member of the holobiont rather than a visitor? The
  paper asks for a definition of "transient".
- Does the holobiont's fitness depend on the specific consortium or only on
  the roles its members fill? The paper asks this; a functional-redundancy
  result would answer it.
- Is the non-additivity of C4 found in corals, and for traits closer to
  fitness than oxygen flux?
