# QUESTION-tmpzblpf: Which observable diversity index, if any, tracks an ecosystem's richness, and under what conditions on how constituents' capacities combine, what they share and how a demand reaches the one that can meet it does diversity remain a generally accurate measure of it?

## Why it is a question

The owner holds that diversity is "generally" an accurate measure of
richness (CLAIM-tmp6ik2v); A60 that it is "a generally informative proxy"
(CLAIM-tmp6thmv). Neither says when. A60's examples of failure are
redundant participants and participants that consume what existing
capabilities need. The assistant's check of A60's coverage model
(CLAIM-tmp7iie0) adds three conditions on which the answer turns:

- **how capacities combine**: under single selection differentiated
  constituents out-cover similar ones; under parallel independent attempts
  the order can reverse;
- **what they share**: under a shared budget an added constituent can
  lower coverage;
- **how a demand reaches the right constituent**: a selector that cannot
  tell questions apart erases the advantage of differentiation.

And which diversity is meant matters: a count of kinds, an evenness, or a
measure that weighs how different the kinds are in what they can ask and
answer, which is the one A60 §2 calls for ("diversity of consequential
capabilities").

## What would answer it

- A model, or a body of evidence, in which a stated diversity index and
  richness in TERM-tmp9lu6c's sense can be measured on the same
  ecosystems, with the three conditions varied, saying where the index
  tracks richness and where it fails.
- Or a reading of what ecology and social epistemology have found on the
  same question (LEAD-tmptd829), set against the conditions above.

## What would not answer it

- More examples of diverse ecosystems that are rich, or of redundant ones
  that are not. Both sides concede both kinds exist.
- THEORY-148 alone: it bounds the duration of diversity of pursuit, not
  which index tracks richness.
