# NOTE-tmpxhylb: Ahn — From Bainbridge to bedside

## Contribution

A framework for medical curricula that takes Bainbridge's ironies of
automation, written for industrial control and aviation, as warnings for
students who will work with AI: each irony, in Ahn's reading, is paired with
an educational countermeasure.

## Key insight

The order of support and skill matters: build the skill without the support
first, then bring the support in, and train separately for the rare case in
which the support fails and hands the work back.

## Assumptions

- Bainbridge's ironies of industrial control transfer to LLM-assisted
  medicine.
- Ahn's paraphrase of Bainbridge is faithful. It cannot be checked here: he
  quotes no sentence of hers and gives no page.

## Key results

*Every point attributed to Bainbridge is Ahn's wording.*

- **§2, deskilling, attributed:** Bainbridge "warns of the “deskilling”
  effect where automation can erode skills that remain essential,
  particularly during system failures or unusual situations."
- **§3, monitoring, attributed:** "the increased difficulty of monitoring
  tasks without active engagement, the degradation of vigilance due to
  boredom", and new error types of human–automation interaction.
- **§4, the central irony, attributed:** automation "often takes over the more
  straightforward, routine tasks, leaving human operators primarily
  responsible for managing complex, atypical situations, and edge cases";
  with deskilling, this "can paradoxically make the remaining human roles
  more demanding".
- **§5, the hand-back, attributed:** "it positions the human to intervene
  during unexpected or complex failures, precisely when the system is least
  predictable and intervention is most critical"; "An operator who has been
  “out-of-the-loop” may lack situational awareness and find their manual or
  cognitive skills degraded from disuse".
- **§2, Ahn's remedy for sequencing:** "Implementing dedicated “AI-free”
  learning phases"; "Once students have solidified these core abilities, AI
  tools can then be introduced".
- **§5, Ahn's remedy for the hand-back:** training to "prepare students for
  these high-stakes, low-frequency events", by simulations in which flaws are
  deliberately injected into an LLM's output.
- **Evidence:** none tested.

## Concepts

- **Deskilling by offloading.**
- **The monitoring trap.**
- **Out-of-the-loop hazard**, and the "worst possible time" to regain control
  (a phrase Ahn quotes without a source).
- **AI-free learning phases.**

## Connections

- **Bainbridge (1983)**, [LIT-tmph5vta](../literature.d/LIT-tmph5vta.md), unread. This is the closest of the
  three stand-ins: it restates the deskilling irony, the monitoring problem,
  the operator left the hard cases, and the hand-back at failure. It does
  not restate her wording, her remedies or any numbers, and its two quoted
  phrases, "deskilling" and "worst possible time", are not located in her
  text. Its remedies are Ahn's, not hers.
- **Macnamara et al. (2024)**, the companion stand-in, carries the evidence
  on skill decay under automation, second-hand, which Ahn does not.
- **Simkute, Tankelevitch et al. (2024)** carry the monitoring problem and the
  easy-tasks-easier, hard-tasks-harder pattern for generative AI.
