# NOTE-tmp8pbdm: Nickel and Etinson — Human rights

## Contribution

A survey of what human rights are, how they are said to exist, how they
are justified and which rights qualify; a map rather than an argument for
one grounding.

## Key results

- **Independence** (§1): "People have human rights independently of
  whether such rights are present in the practices, morality, or law of
  their country or culture."
- **Not inalienable by definition** (§1): an inalienable right is one whose
  "holder cannot lose it temporarily or permanently by bad conduct or by
  voluntarily giving it up", and "It is doubtful that all human rights are
  inalienable in this sense."
- **Political function** (§1): "a theorist might see them as the norms of a
  highly useful political practice that humans have constructed", a view
  the entry does not make part of the concept.
- **Duties** (§1): "The duties associated with human rights often require
  actions involving respect, protection, facilitation, and provision."
- **Existence** (§2.1): "The best form of existence for human rights would
  combine robust legal existence with the sort of moral existence that
  comes from being supported by strong moral and practical reasons."
- **Grounds** (§2.2): "These include prudential reasons; linkage arguments
  (Shue 1996); agency and autonomy (Gewirth 1996; Griffin 2008); basic
  needs (D. Miller 2012); capabilities and positive freedom"; "The
  discussion here is limited to two approaches: agency/autonomy and
  dignity." For agency "some additional ground pertaining to fairness and
  equality seems to be needed" (§2.2.1); welfare, autonomy and fairness may
  "partially constitute the idea of human dignity rather than being
  derived from it" (§2.2.2).
- **Shue's linkage** (§3.2): "He argued that security and subsistence are
  so indispensable to the full realization of other rights that anyone who
  endorses the realization of any other right must also endorse ESRs".

## Assumptions

Human rights may be moral or legal, with no single theory required of both.

## Concepts

The four defining features; inalienability and forfeiture; minimalism;
orthodox and political conceptions; legal, conventional and justified
existence; agency and dignity grounds; rights inflation; linkage arguments;
economic and social rights.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-tmptinzk](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmptinzk.md).** The entry counts independence from "the practices,
  morality, or law" of a country as part of a human right's universality,
  the property the question wants of "unalienable rights". It qualifies the
  owner's framing: it doubts that all human rights are inalienable, a right
  being forfeitable by bad conduct, and it holds that rights grounded only
  in reasons have a thin existence that legal embodiment completes.
- **[TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md).** The entry's duties are "respect, protection, facilitation,
  and provision", not the triad [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md) attributes to Shue (see the
  reading of Shue, [LIT-tmpog11n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpog11n.md)).
- The exchange's A87 is partly faithful: agency and dignity are the two
  grounds discussed; "interests" is not a ground the entry lists, and
  "international practice" figures as a mode of existence and a political
  view, not as a ground surveyed.

## Limitations

An encyclopedia survey, limited by its own statement to two grounds. §4
(relativism) not read closely.
