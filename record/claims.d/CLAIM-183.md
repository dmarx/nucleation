# CLAIM-183: Generated views of the record, fed back into the exchange, become instruments of the argument: they show where its terms evolved faster than its formalism

## The claim

At U40 the owner showed the assistant the generated term-revisions view,
with no argument attached. A125 replied: "The generated revision view is
valuable precisely because it exposes places where our terminology evolved
faster than our formalism." From the view it split the frame into three
formal roles, found [TERM-002](../terms.d/TERM-002.md) vacuous, and proposed unifying the two Active
fidelity lineages. The owner agreed to the last of these at U41.

A record that only stores what was said cannot do this. The chains
(`revisions`, `dialectic`, `argument`) render relations that no single
entry holds. Read back, they show the argument something about itself that
no participant had said. In this case the view was the first observation
of the case the manuscript is about.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the moves A125 drew from the view were right. A125 also
  overclaimed a link to Noether-type conservation ([CLAIM-093](CLAIM-093.md)).
- It is one instance.

## Note of 2026-10-11 (later): ascription that shapes its target

[THEORY-137](../theory.d/THEORY-137.md) (Tollefsen) is added as a ground, by analogy. Attributions of
knowledge to an institution work largely by shaping the institution. "What
the record holds", read back through its generated views, is such an
ascription: it shapes the argument it describes. The analogy is the
filer's; [THEORY-137](../theory.d/THEORY-137.md) is about corporate criminal law.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

It is one instance, U40, and its defeat condition is stated over further
exchanges: no later exchange is recorded in which a fed-back view
produced a move the participants acted on that rereading the source
entries would not have given. Its premise CLAIM-178 is Proposed.
