# CLAIM-tmpflbi3: Blackwell comparison needs the state each rendering is about, which natural texts do not supply, so a decision measure is fixed by whoever labels the state, and it orders translation procedures, not single renderings

## The objection

**It needs the state.** Blackwell's order compares experiments P(V | Z)
over a state Z. To test it between a source and a rendering one needs the
joint law of Z and each signal: for each source item, the situation it is
about, and the distribution of the signal given that situation. [CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md)'s
tasks are of this kind: "tell teasing from reproach, recognize a position,
choose a reply".

For natural texts Z is not observed. It is what interpretation is meant to
recover. So it has to come from somewhere.

- **The design.** [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md) crosses topic with "pragmatic act P ∈ {teasing,
  reprimand}", and "makes the act a manipulated factor rather than an
  outcome". There the state is whatever the item writers made it.
- **The analyst's labels.**
- **The judges**, whose judgements are then both the state and the
  outcome.

In each case "the information needed to make the relevant distinctions
about the communicative situation" ([TERM-028](../terms.d/TERM-028.md), from A86 §3) is measured
against whoever fixed Z. [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md) found that identifiability in the
October thesis is relative to the analyst's probe system. The same holds
here for decision-relevance: it is relative to the labeller of the state.

**It orders procedures.** An experiment is "an n-tuple of probability
measures on a common space, one per state" ([THEORY-156](../theory.d/THEORY-156.md)). A single rendering
of a single source is one draw, under one state. Henley's rendering of
Villon ([CASE-010](../cases.d/CASE-010.md)) is one text, about one situation. Its Blackwell fidelity
is undefined. What has one is the procedure that produced it, applied
across a distribution of situations. So [CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md)'s defeat condition, "A
case where one rendering is a garbling of another", is not well typed as
written: garbling is a relation between channels, not between texts.
[CLAIM-tmp6xxbf](CLAIM-tmp6xxbf.md) makes the same point for the outcome-level kernels of the
manuscript's transport, which constrain a translation only when fixed
across utterances.

**The record has met the measurement gap once.** [CASE-042](../cases.d/CASE-042.md): A207 §6 calls a
held-out comparison of decision risk between two encodings "a direct
Blackwell experiment", and "By the record's reading it is not one. It
compares a chosen family of losses, which [THEORY-176](../theory.d/THEORY-176.md) shows is not
Blackwell's order". [CLAIM-126](CLAIM-126.md) limits its own certificate to "decisions
about the label used to define the experiment". Neither says that for
natural texts the label is someone's choice. For stitching, the label
comes with the data set. For translation it is the situation, and someone
assigns it.

## What it does not say

- It does not say a design-based state is illegitimate. Experiments
  routinely fix the state by design, and [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md) is a careful one. It
  says that the decision term then measures agreement with the design's
  labels, and that the theory's notion of fidelity must not be built into
  those labels ([QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md)).
- It does not say a single translation cannot be judged. It says
  Blackwell's order does not judge it.

## What would answer it

- A design-based Z with an argument that the design's labels do not build
  in the theory's preferred fidelity ([QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md)).
- [CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md)'s defeat condition restated over translation procedures,
  applied across a stated distribution of situations.
- For single renderings, a different measure, stated as such.
