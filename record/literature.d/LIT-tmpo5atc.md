# LIT-tmpo5atc: The physics of symbols: bridging the epistemic cut

Pattee, H. H. (2001). *BioSystems* 60(1–3):5–21. doi:10.1016/S0303-2647(01)00104-6.

Read in full in [NOTE-tmp7d44m](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7d44m.md), from the author-posted text on the
Binghamton CASCI site (casci.binghamton.edu/publications/pattee/pattee.html).
That copy has no page numbers, so locations are sections.

## Why this is here

Filed as measurement-first prior art for [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md), on the
expectation that for Pattee measurement, not action, separates a living
system's records from its dynamics. The reading does not bear that out.
Measurement and control are converses across one cut: "measurement
transforms physical states to symbols in memory, while memory-stored
controls transform symbols to physical states" (§5). The cut "must first
arise at the genotype-phenotype control interface", and "control of
construction came first" (§10, note 6). Measurement presupposes control:
"in order to perform a measurement the subject must have control of the
construction of the measuring device" (§10). That is closer to the
action–perception shape the claim rejects.

What the claim can use is narrower. The first new distinction in a cell is
made where the system reads its own memory, in protein folding (§10), and
semantic closure, in which the reader of the symbols is built from them,
has the shape of the claim's diachronic loop. But Pattee's control is
construction of the system itself, and he does not separate operations on
the self from action on an environment, so he neither supports nor
refutes the claim's exclusion of action. His objectivity condition, that
"the act of measurement does not influence the form of the laws, and ...
the laws do not influence the act of measurement" (§6), is close to
[CLAIM-tmpt568z](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt568z.md)'s anchor. Measurement is not a record internal to the
system as opposed to a physical act: it is a physical constraint coupling
to the dynamics, whose function no dynamical description keeps.
