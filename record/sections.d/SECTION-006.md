# SECTION-006: Objective but nonunique organizational boundaries

## Manuscript text

The boundary-relativity of provenance invites a familiar worry: if we specify the candidate unit in order to study its maintenance, are organizational individuals merely artifacts of our questions? That does not follow. The criteria under which a phenomenon is measured may depend upon scientific aims while the world objectively determines whether the criteria are met. A collection may fail to support the claimed closed dynamics, or its supposed internal production may turn out to depend entirely on excluded processes. <!-- ref::asserts: CLAIM-405 -->

It is useful to distinguish three senses of boundary plurality. *Vagueness* concerns nearby candidate boundaries for what is ordinarily treated as one object, as in Lewis's problem of the many. *Criterion plurality* concerns different causally meaningful decompositions of the same physical situation. *Constitutive plurality* concerns different kinds of organization potentially sharing constituents, as when distinct social groups are constituted by distinct authority relations even though their members overlap. These are different problems, and success at one does not imply the existence of multiple numerically distinct objects at every descriptive level.

The boundary of a maintained product need not coincide with the boundary of the processes maintaining it. A perspective may be enacted by multiple agents. A tradition can be reproduced through distributed practices without one sovereign organization coordinating its transmission. An institution's explicit rules may persist while the capacity to apply them depends on a wider set of relationships. These cases suggest *crossing* as well as nested organizational units. <!-- ref::asserts: CLAIM-tmpx8nob, CLAIM-418 --> <!-- ref::discusses: CLAIM-422 -->

The modest conclusion is realism about objectively ascertainable organizational properties and the possibility of more than one warranted individuation under specified criteria. The stronger conclusion that every warranted individuation identifies a metaphysically distinct individual requires additional ontological commitments. The paper should not slide between these claims. Its positive contribution is to make the evidential commitments of each individuation explicit and to show why productive provenance introduces a further dimension of assessment, not a new universally privileged scale. <!-- ref::asserts: CLAIM-405 -->
