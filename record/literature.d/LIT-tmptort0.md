# LIT-tmptort0: The Appraisal Basis of Anger: Specificity, Necessity, and Sufficiency of Components

Peter Kuppens, Iven Van Mechelen, Dirk J. M. Smits & Paul De Boeck (2003).
*Emotion* 3(3), 254–269. DOI 10.1037/1528-3542.3.3.254.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmp0e1ym.*

- **A test of necessity and sufficiency.** Componential theories assume
  strong links between emotions and appraisals, and some (Smith and
  Lazarus; Roseman and Smith; Frijda's definition of emotion as change in
  action readiness) imply necessity. Instructing people to recall
  situations where a component was absent allows a test.
- **Anger has a distinctive pattern.** Other accountability (specific to
  anger), unfairness and, in study 2, wanting to express opposition go with
  anger; control does not.
- **No component is necessary or sufficient.** Asymmetric phi indices were
  well below 1 for every component, in both studies and under every
  dichotomization.
- **At least one is needed.** In study 2, every situation reported as
  angry had at least one of the five components present.
- **Self-report throughout.** The manipulation is an instruction about what
  to recall; appraisals, action tendency and emotion are all self-rated.
