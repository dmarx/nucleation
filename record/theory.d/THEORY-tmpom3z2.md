# THEORY-tmpom3z2: Body ownership draws on interoceptive as well as exteroceptive signals: a visual cue synchronous with one's heartbeat increases ownership of a virtual hand and identification with a virtual body, and lower interoceptive sensitivity goes with greater susceptibility to the rubber hand illusion

## Source

Seth (2013), [LIT-tmp2v8qs](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2v8qs.md), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 17(11), read in
full in [NOTE-tmpi27ob](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi27ob.md): "Interoception and the rubber hand" (p. 569), the
cardio-visual rubber hand and Figure 3 (pp. 569–570), and Box 3 (p. 571).
The studies the claim rests on were not read.

## What was actually shown

Seth reports three results, all from cited studies:

- **The cardio-visual hand.** Suzuki et al. (2013), from Seth's own group,
  built an augmented-reality hand whose colour pulsed toward red over 500 ms,
  either in or out of time with the participant's heartbeat. Synchronous
  feedback "enhanced the experience of ownership of the virtual hand", by
  questionnaire and by proprioceptive drift. Control questions showed no
  effect (pp. 569–570, Fig. 3).
- **The cardio-visual body.** Aspell et al. (2013), from Blanke's group,
  applied the same feedback to a virtual body seen from behind. Synchrony
  "increased identification with the virtual body", objectively and
  subjectively (p. 570).
- **Sensitivity and susceptibility.** "Participants with lower IS are more
  susceptible to the RHI" (Tsakiris et al. 2011), where interoceptive
  sensitivity is measured by heartbeat detection (p. 569).

Seth's conclusion is that "statistical correlations between interoceptive
(e.g., cardiac) and exteroceptive (e.g., visual) signals can lead to
updating of predictive models of self-related signals ... just as may
happen for purely exteroceptive multisensory conflicts" (p. 570). The cardiac
result could have come out otherwise: a pulse out of time with the heart
could have done as well as one in time. As reported, it did not.

## What this does not say

- **It does not separate the heartbeat from any periodic signal.** The
  asynchronous condition breaks the timing relation; it does not show that
  the signal must be interoceptive. A visual cue locked to a non-bodily
  rhythm at heart rate would separate the two. No such control is
  reported, and [NOTE-tmpi27ob](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi27ob.md) names this as the open question.
- **It is second-hand.** All three results are Seth's report of studies
  not read here. One of the two cardio-visual studies is the author's own,
  and the text gives no effect sizes.
- **The cardiac cue is weak beside movement.** In Suzuki et al., when finger
  movements were mapped to the virtual hand, visual–proprioceptive
  coherence "overshadowed the influence of cardiac (interoceptive)
  prediction errors" (p. 569). The claim is that interoceptive signals
  contribute, not that they dominate.
- **It does not establish the precision reading.** Seth reads low
  sensitivity as "possibly reflecting lower precision-weighting of
  interoceptive prediction errors". The finding is equally a result about
  heartbeat-counting performance, and the article does not discuss the
  validity of heartbeat detection.
- **It is not the emotion claim.** Seth's thesis that felt emotion is the
  content of interoceptive prediction ([NOTE-tmpi27ob](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi27ob.md), C1) is a separate
  hypothesis, which he says has no direct test. This THEORY carries only
  the ownership half.
- **The autonomic results are not part of it.** Cooling and histamine
  changes in the real hand are called "compatible with" active inference,
  not predicted by it. They are left out here.
