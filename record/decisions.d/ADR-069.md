# ADR-069: LIT carries a full author list, from which first_author is derived

## Context

Each of the 1,947 LITs has `first_author: 'Surname'`. That is the record's
only author fact. `luria.yaml` does not declare the field: it appears only
in the LIT alias template, `LIT-{first_author}-{published:.4}-{number:03d}`.
It reaches `luria facts` and `luria export` as a plain value, which has
three costs:

- **Co-authors do not exist.** "List & Pettit", "Moreno & Mossio" and
  "Bechtel & Abrahamsen" are only their first author, so a senior author
  in second position, such as Pettit, Mossio or Friston, is undercounted.
- **Surnames collide.** "Simon" matched a 2023 ML first author as well as
  Herbert Simon, and "Taylor" matched two different people.
- **NOTEs carry no authors**, though they derive `tags` and `published`
  from their LIT.

The owner raised this on 2026-11-01, during a survey of which authors the
record leans on but has not read. That survey had to join on bare
surnames and disambiguate by hand.

## Decision

1. **LIT declares `authors:`**: many-valued, each value `Surname, Given`
   as the byline gives it (initials if that is all it gives), in byline
   order.
2. **`first_author` is derived** from the surname of `authors[0]` ([DP-003](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-3)).
   A LIT without `authors` keeps its hand-written `first_author` until the
   backfill reaches it.
3. **NOTE derives `authors`** from `paper`, as it derives `tags`.
4. **Backfill** from each LIT's source:
   - the arXiv API for `arxiv:`;
   - Crossref for `doi:`, with no email address in any query parameter,
     header or body ([ADR-008](ADR-008.md));
   - by hand for `url:`-only works, mostly books.

   Where the source's first author disagrees with the existing
   `first_author`, the backfill reports it and writes nothing for that
   LIT.
5. **Optional, then required.** `authors` is optional while the backfill
   runs, and becomes required on LIT once it nears completion, by a later
   decision that states the coverage.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave `first_author` as the only author fact.** Every by-author
  question then keeps needing manual disambiguation and keeps missing
  co-authors.
- **An AUTHOR scheme with one entry per person, or ORCID identifiers.**
  This solves identity, which strings do not. But it is a larger change,
  and nothing yet needs it. Strings in byline order get most of the value,
  and a scheme can later be built from them.
- **Keep `first_author` hand-written beside `authors`.** That gives two
  copies of one fact, which will drift.

## Consequences

- By-author analysis can count co-authors, and a given name cuts most
  collisions.
- The backfill sends about 1,947 requests to arXiv and Crossref, and some
  `url:` works need reading by hand. The work is deferred for API budget.
- Derivation from `paper` gives NOTEs authors with no hand edits.
- The same person spelled two ways stays two strings until an identity
  decision.
