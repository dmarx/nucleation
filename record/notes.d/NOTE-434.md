# NOTE-434: The theory of constructed emotion: an active inference account of interoception and categorization

## Contribution

The paper restates Barrett's conceptual act theory, renamed the theory of
constructed emotion, from the brain up rather than from emotion words down. It
derives the theory from three premises: the brain's job is allostasis, it does
that by prediction, and its predictions are concepts. It then maps the
architecture onto cortical cytoarchitecture (Barbas's structural model) and
onto three intrinsic networks, and lists the neuroscience it takes to
disconfirm the classical view (Table 1) and to support its own (Table 2).

## Key insight

Perception, action and emotion are one process. A brain that must regulate its
body before needs arise has to predict, and to predict it has to use past
experience to categorize what is coming. "Predictions are concepts. Completed
predictions are categorizations". When the concept doing the categorizing is an
emotion concept, the result is an instance of that emotion. So an emotion is
"constructed the same way that all other perceptions are constructed", and
"Emotions are constructions of the world, not reactions to it" (Conclusions).

## Assumptions

- **The brain's core task is allostasis** ("What is a brain for?"),
  following Sterling: anticipating and meeting the body's needs. Regulation of
  autonomic, immune and endocrine systems runs under every other function.
- **Predictive coding** as the brain's computational scheme ("An internal
  model is predictive, not reactive"). The paper calls it "an increasingly
  popular hypothesis" and treats it as the frame.
- **Barbas's structural model** of corticocortical connections. Prediction
  signals flow from the deep layers of less laminated (agranular, limbic)
  cortex to the upper layers of more laminated cortex. Prediction error flows
  the other way and is compressed on the way.
- **Degeneracy and population thinking** (Edelman & Gally, after Darwin's
  Origin as read by Mayr). A category is a population of variable instances
  grouped by a goal, and its summary representation "need not exist in nature".
- **Perceiver dependence.** Emotion categories are conceptual categories of
  "social reality", real in the way money is (point 1 of the list of claims
  not made; footnote 15).

## Key results

The paper reports no new experiment. Figure 3 (simulation BOLD, N = 16, from
Wilson-Mendenhall et al. 2013) and Figure 6 (a resting-state allostasis and
interoception system, from 280 participants and replicated in 270, from Kleckner
et al.) are the closest to original data, and both are cited from other papers.
What it gives is the theory's structure:

- **Affect and interoception.** "Interoceptive sensations are usually
  experienced as lower dimensional feelings of affect", and valence and arousal
  are "basic features of consciousness … that, importantly, are not unique to
  instances of emotion".
- **Concepts as whole-brain predictions.** A concept is "a collection of
  embodied, whole brain representations that predicts what is about to happen
  in the sensory environment, what the best action is to deal with these
  impending events, and their consequences for allostasis" (Glossary). Concepts
  are ad hoc and goal-based. "happiness" is constructed anew each time
  relative to the situation's goal.
- **Architecture** (Figure 5):
  - Agranular limbic regions (ACC, ventral anterior insula) send visceromotor
    predictions to the hypothalamus and brainstem, with efference copies to
    motor cortex and all sensory cortices.
  - Primary interoceptive cortex (mid/posterior insula) sends sensory
    predictions to the better-laminated exteroceptive cortices, and motor
    cortex sends its own (hypotheses, so labelled).
  - The amygdala signals uncertainty, not emotion, and ventral striatal
    prediction errors mark allostatically relevant input.
- **Networks.** The default mode network is "necessary for the brain's
  internal model", hosting multimodal summaries. The salience network sets
  precision, choosing which prediction errors to learn from. The frontoparietal
  network holds simulations over longer timescales. Hence "Emotion perception
  is event perception, not object perception."
- **Against the classical view** (Table 1). Emotion categories cannot be
  consistently localized to regions or intrinsic networks. MVPA patterns for a
  category need not occur in any instance. Intracranial stimulation finds no
  emotion-specific receptive fields. Amygdala lesions have variable effects
  (patients SM, AM and BG, Box 1). Rodent "fear" circuits show many-to-one and
  one-to-many mappings.
- **Pattern classifiers misread.** Classifiers that separate emotion
  categories do not replicate across studies, and a classifier pattern is "a
  statistical summary of a highly variable set of instances", not the brain
  state for the emotion. Taking it as such is an "essentialist error".
- **Mental inference fallacy.** Equating an action, such as freezing, with an
  emotion such as fear. The paper says this "has wreaked havoc" with the
  accumulation of knowledge. Decorticate "sham rage" is pattern generators
  without the conceptual system.
- **Appraisal theories.** Because "meaning does not trigger action, but
  results from it", classical appraisal theories that "assume that a response
  derives from a stimulus that is evaluated for its meaning (e.g. Lazarus,
  1991; Scherer, 2009; Roseman, 2011)" are "highly doubtful". "Appraisals as
  descriptions of the world (e.g. Clore and Ortony, 2008), however, are
  produced by categorization with concepts".
- **Non-human animals.** "'Is the fly fearful?' is not a scientific
  question", but whether a human perceives fear in a fly ("yes") and whether
  the fly feels fear ("no") are. The fly "may feel affect".

## Concepts

- **allostasis**: "Regulating the internal milieu by anticipating
  physiological needs and preparing to meet them before they arise".
- **interoception**: the representation and use of sensations from the
  internal milieu; "at the core of the brain's internal model".
- **affect**: valence and arousal, the felt consequence of allostatic
  predictions about the body. Present in every waking moment, not only in
  emotion.
- **concept / category**: a category is a population of events treated as
  similar for a goal in a context, and a concept is the population of
  representations of them (footnote 15). Here a concept is a cascade of
  whole-brain predictions.
- **degeneracy**: "the capacity for biologically dissimilar systems or
  processes to give rise to identical functions". Distinguished from
  redundancy.
- **classical view of emotion** (Figure 1): basic emotion theories, causal
  appraisal theories, and black-box functionalism. Each assumes that every
  emotion has an essence: a Lockean one (an underlying mechanism, neural or
  appraisal-based) or a Platonic one (a fingerprint in face, physiology or
  appraisals).
- **mental inference fallacy**: treating an observed action as evidence of a
  specific emotion.
- **precision signals**: predictions of which prediction errors are worth
  learning from. The paper assigns them to the salience network.

## Connections

- **Lazarus, Emotion and Adaptation ([LIT-550](../literature.d/LIT-550.md)), Deferred.** Named as the
  first example of a causal appraisal theory (claim C6). The paper engages the
  type, not the book's specific arguments, and quotes nothing from it.
- **Frijda ([LIT-540](../literature.d/LIT-540.md), read; [LIT-546](../literature.d/LIT-546.md), Deferred).** Not cited.
  Frijda's late statement agrees with this paper on several points. Motives
  are "set to expect a particular input", as in predictive coding; concern
  pertinence is appraised automatically; and actions are anticipated through
  their exteroceptive and interoceptive sensory consequences. But it keeps
  appraisal as what *elicits* action readiness, and keeps modes of action
  readiness that "correspond to certain common emotion names". On this
  paper's terms, that is a Lockean essence.
- **Gross ([LIT-554](../literature.d/LIT-554.md)), read.** This paper cites Gross (2015) and Gross &
  Barrett (2011), not the 1998 review. Its hypothesis C8 keeps reappraisal
  and gives it a mechanism: changing the predictions that categorize. It
  does not keep the 1998 model's sequence, in which situations generate
  meanings and meanings generate responses.
- **Seth ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md); [NOTE-169](NOTE-169.md)), read.** Both papers take interoceptive predictive
  processing that serves allostasis as the base layer, with shared sources

  consciousness in it, while this paper grounds affect in it and calls affect
  a basic feature of consciousness. The two are compatible. Neither paper

## Bearing on the record

- It sources one side of a THEORY proposed in this batch's report: that emotion
  categories are not natural kinds with neural essences, but populations
  constructed by categorization. That stays Proposed while the record holds
  only the theory's own statement and no independent test or reply. Adolphs's
  companion piece (nsw153) is the obvious reply to file.
- It bears on the interoception cluster. [LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md), [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md) and this paper agree
  that interoceptive, allostatic prediction is the layer feelings report.
  That agreement could ground a THEORY of affect as felt allostatic
  prediction, sourced from all three, which is also proposed in the report.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **Hypotheses are the body of the paper.** "I hypothesize" introduces most
  architectural claims: the motor-to-sensory and insula-to-sensory
  predictions, the amygdala as uncertainty signal, the roles of the networks.
  The paper calls itself "a mere sketch".
- **The case against the classical view rests on disconfirming essentialist
  readings.** It is weaker against appraisal theories that never claimed a
  neural fingerprint per emotion. The paper's Figure 1 nonetheless counts
  causal appraisal theories as positing a Lockean essence ("a set of
  evaluative mechanisms called 'appraisals'"), and does not engage any
  specific appraisal theory's predictions.
- **The positive evidence (Table 2) is largely consistency.** Degeneracy and
  neural reuse are predicted by the theory, but they are also compatible with
  many other non-modular accounts.
- **Predictive coding is assumed, not argued.** The theory inherits that
  framework's open questions.

## Open questions

- What would disconfirm the theory? The paper says new paradigms raise "more
  questions than they answer", and lists no test that the classical view
  passes and constructionism fails.
- Can a constructed emotion concept be shown to change interoceptive and
  physiological prediction, and not only report? Figure 3's simulation result
  is suggestive.
- How does the account handle appraisal theory's best-supported finding, that
  appraisal profiles predict which emotion people report? The paper's answer,
  that appraisals are descriptions produced by categorization, is stated, not
  tested.
