# QUESTION-tmpnxt9o: In a second, preferably pre-registered sample with a cost attached to avoiding, do anxiety-disorder patients again avoid a safety cue more than controls but danger cues no more, keep a higher threat expectancy after effective avoidance, and report more relief at omissions, with rated aversiveness matched or controlled?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-912](../theory.d/THEORY-912.md) holds De Kleine et al.'s single cross-sectional study, in
which avoiding cost nothing and patients rated the pictures as more
aversive. A replication with matched aversiveness and a cost would say
whether the differences belong to learning or to appraisal.

## What would answer it

[THEORY-912](../theory.d/THEORY-912.md)'s former promote_when, unchanged:

A second sample, ideally pre-registered and with a cost attached to
avoiding, comparing anxiety-disorder patients with healthy controls on
the same kind of avoidance task, and finding again that patients avoid a
safety cue more, avoid danger cues no more, keep a higher expectancy of
the aversive outcome after effective avoidance, and report more relief
at omissions, with rated aversiveness controlled or matched. It is
weakened if the differences disappear when aversiveness is matched, for
example by raising aversiveness in healthy participants as the authors
propose. The authors' mechanism (excess relief reinforcing avoidance
without action-safety learning) is not carried by this THEORY, and a
result about it neither promotes nor refutes this one.
