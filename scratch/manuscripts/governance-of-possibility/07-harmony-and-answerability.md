# Part D. Harmony and the governance of governance

Part D takes up two questions that the earlier parts leave open. §§3–11 say what a regulatory arrangement must maintain if an industrial ecosystem is to stay able to reorganize: routes to alternative configurations, reserves that keep those routes affordable, a diagnosis of which kind of failure is at hand. They do not say what the reorganization is for, beyond meeting a declared set of demands (§2), or who declares the demands and corrects the declarer. §12 asks what a regulator should conserve when the aim is described, as the owner's thesis describes it, as "harmonious governance" (CLAIM-tmpz67ew, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's, U16). §13 asks who governs the regulator.

Each step cites the entry that holds it, with its standing on 2026-11-23: Active (the record accepts it) or Proposed (filed and argued, not adopted); demonstrated (shown by construction or proof), empirical (observed) or interpretive (argued). Voices are marked: the owner's theses as the owner's; entries in ChatGPT's wording, from the exchange "Explain First Contact RL" (2026-11-22 to 23), as ChatGPT's; entries the drafting assistant (Claude) put forward as the assistant's. Where this part joins entries the record keeps apart, it says so and marks the join "(join JD*n*: …)"; where the argument needs a step the record does not hold, it marks a gap "(gap GD*n*: …)". Both are collected in §15.

The two sections are weaker than §§2–10 and should be read that way. Their negative steps (harmony is not coexistence; capacity to act is no evidence of capacity to be corrected) are Active. Most of their positive steps are Proposed, open, or shown only in small models of systems that are not regulators.

## 12. Harmony as conserved capability

### 12.1 What harmony is not

The motivating draft's §12 starts from a correct observation: an ecosystem of firms, suppliers, evaluators and authorities can persist together without anything worth calling harmony. A dominant firm and dependent suppliers can coexist in a stable arrangement that is exploitative. The record holds three results that make this exact, all Active.

- **Coexistence is not cooperation.** "Negative frequency dependence that keeps two types in coexistence does not make their interaction cooperative." In Hawk–Dove with cost above value the two types coexist at the interior equilibrium, "with Hawks still taking the whole resource from Doves", so "whether types persist together and whether they cooperate are separate questions, and a coexistence criterion answers only the first" (CLAIM-546, Active, demonstrated).
- **Relief is not reduced competition.** Niche differentiation matters to an organization "as possible relief of structural frustration among its constituents' persistence demands", a strict enlargement of what they can jointly realize, "and not as reduced competition". It follows that "the exclusion of a constituent or a redistribution of influence relieves no structural frustration of the original demands, and specialization can relieve frustration where nothing competes" (CLAIM-554, Active, interpretive). *Structural frustration* is §2's term: the demands that cannot be met together, read off the complex of jointly satisfiable demand sets (CLAIM-347, Active, interpretive).
- **Viability is not legitimacy.** "Viability does not establish what ought to persist" (CLAIM-075, Active, interpretive). Its source puts it bluntly: "An unjust institution may be highly viable." CLAIM-075 is filed in the role the record calls *disclaimed*: a boundary on what the record claims, not a premise from which anything positive follows.

Two further Active entries sharpen the third point for this paper. A demand's satisfaction condition "does not carry its warrant", so "the joint satisfiability of demands cannot certify that any deserves fulfilment, and an institution's maintained requirements cannot certify their own authority" (CLAIM-725, Active, interpretive). And cooperation within groups and the coexistence of groups "are distinct targets that can trade off": in one of Nowak's rules, cooperation spreads inside groups because other groups are driven extinct (CLAIM-547, Active, interpretive).

The motivating draft concludes that ecological organization must be evaluated "against independently justified public demands". The record supports the negative half of that sentence: neither viability nor joint satisfiability supplies the justification (CLAIM-075, CLAIM-725). It does not supply the positive half. Nothing on the record says where a public demand's justification comes from (§13.1; gap GD1, below).

### 12.2 The record's definition of harmony

The motivating draft never defines harmony. The record has a definition, filed from the same exchange: **harmonious integration** of a plurality S of declared constituents (perspectives, members, species, firms) over a horizon H is the conjunction of three conditions (TERM-tmppvlfi, Proposed, ChatGPT's wording, A24 §4):

- **Viability_H(S):** "the histories the organization can produce over H meet every constituent's persistence requirement at once", in CLAIM-554's form;
- **Accommodation_H(S):** the constituents' interactions "sustain compatible activity without requiring their differences to disappear";
- **Correctability_H(S):** "a constituent can still make a consequential intervention when the organization needs to change".

The definition says what it is not: not coexistence (CLAIM-546), not agreement, not relief of frustration, and not stability, since "A team might look well integrated because one dominant perspective has made the others quiet" (TERM-tmppvlfi). Such an organization is viable and accommodating and fails correctability.

This paper uses the definition, and the reader should know three things about it.

1. **It is Proposed, and fails its own condition for adoption.** The term says adoption "needs each conjunct operationalized for a declared plurality and horizon", accommodation "by a measure not reducible to either" of the others. Its note of 2026-11-23: "accommodation has no measure not reducible to viability or correctability, and no declared plurality and horizon has had the three conjuncts operationalized." Its source called the conditions "proposed conditions, not an established ecological theorem" (TERM-tmppvlfi). (Gap GD2: a measure of accommodation.)
2. **Its correctability is a constituent's, not an arrangement's.** The term identifies its third conjunct with "continued access to consequential roles" (CLAIM-362, Proposed, interpretive), and its ecological counterpart with recovery when rare (QUESTION-132, Open). That is not the record's main sense of correctability, which is a property of an arrangement: "the chance that the arrangement, starting from the description of the problem it actually has, finds, authorizes, coordinates and carries out a plan achieving the objective before the deadline and within its resources" (TERM-085, Active). The two meet where a constituent's consequential intervention is the arrangement's route to correcting itself.
3. **Its correctability conjunct is what joins §12 to §13.** The motivating draft's §12 drops correctability and its §13 brings it back as "effective answerability". Read through TERM-tmppvlfi, the two sections are one condition seen from two sides. §12 asks whether the constituents of an industrial ecosystem can still intervene consequentially when it must change. §13 asks whether the regulator, as one participant in a wider institutional order, can still be intervened upon. *The join is this paper's* (join JD1: harmony's correctability conjunct read as the answerability of §13; TERM-tmppvlfi with TERM-085, CLAIM-362 and CLAIM-462). No entry makes it, and it carries the distinction in point 2 across: a constituent's access to consequential roles is not the same quantity as an arrangement's chance of carrying out a correction.

The definition also makes the owner's "harmonious governance" precise in one way. CLAIM-tmpz67ew's own gloss of the phrase uses joint satisfiability (CLAIM-347, CLAIM-554), coexistence not taken for cooperation (CLAIM-546) and viability not taken for legitimacy (CLAIM-075), and points to TERM-tmppvlfi as "The exchange's own definition of harmony". Using the definition brings in a Proposed term with an unmet condition. The paper does so with that cost stated.

### 12.3 Unity without convergence

The exchange that produced the definition also proposed a reading of what kind of unity harmony is. "A self-like organization need not be unified by convergence on a single perspective: it can be unified ecologically, by a historically maintained regime in which differentiated perspectives stay viable, mutually consequential and able to reorganize their relations" (CLAIM-tmp37le7, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's, A24). Its correspondence table pairs "Rare species can recover when conditions favor them" with "A marginalized perspective can regain influence when relevant", and its gloss says that "A healthy plurality is not merely one in which every constituent currently exists. It is one in which constituents retain the capacity to become consequential again." A companion claim holds that harmonious integration and organizational singularity (one effective perspective integrating every other) are independent (CLAIM-tmp31b3v, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's).

CLAIM-tmp37le7 was stated of selves and teams, and its note of 2026-11-23 says what holds it back: no case on the record shows a self-like organization unified this way. This paper carries it to an industrial ecosystem governed by a regulator: the ecosystem's unity, so far as it has one, would be the maintained regime in which differentiated producers, evaluators and authorities stay viable and able to become consequential again, not convergence on one firm's or one agency's perspective. *The carriage is this paper's* (join JD2: CLAIM-tmp37le7's ecological unity, from a self or team to an industrial ecosystem). It adds nothing that §12.2's correctability conjunct does not, except a name for the kind of unity it yields.

### 12.4 Conserve a capability, not its occupant

The motivating draft's positive proposal for §12 is that a regulator protecting an ecosystem's differentiated capabilities should keep "a capability and its conditions of reproduction, not a particular organizational occupant". The record now holds it, filed from the draft itself: "What regulation should conserve in an industrial ecosystem is often a capability and the conditions of its reproduction, its infrastructure, expertise and access, and not the organization that now performs it"; "Keeping the occupant is one way to keep the capability, and not the only one" (CLAIM-tmpyakra, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). It rests on CLAIM-556 (Active, interpretive), whose last diagnostic level is "a failure of regeneration, the capability not surviving turnover, repaired by maintenance or reproduction" (§10), and on CLAIM-075 for why viability alone does not choose what to conserve.

The claim states something the record did not hold before, and it is useful: it separates protecting a function from protecting an incumbent, which an assessment of present performance cannot tell apart. But it has an assumption it does not state. To conserve a capability *apart from* the firm that holds it, one must be able to say which capability it is without naming the firm. The record's open question on the same contrast says this cannot yet be done.

- **The question.** "Can a regulation work in an industrial ecosystem as a stabilizing mechanism does in coexistence theory, so that a capability reduced to rarity recovers when demand for it returns without continuing support, and what separates that from regulation that holds visible diversity in place or that protects incumbent firms?" (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a, Open, ChatGPT's wording, A33). A *stabilizing* mechanism, in Chesson's sense, makes each type limit itself more than it limits others, so a type reduced to rarity can increase again; an *equalizing* one only narrows differences in average fitness (QUESTION-371).
- **What it says is unsettled.** "The bearer of recovery. Is it a firm, a capability, or a pathway for producing an essential good? The answer changes what a rare-invasion test measures. A33 says capabilities, and the record has no account of how a capability is individuated apart from the firms that hold it." It also asks how stabilization by changed dynamics is told apart from "diversity kept by continuing external support", and warns that "Stable coexistence can keep an exploitative relation (CLAIM-546), so recovery when rare is not by itself harmony" (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a).

So CLAIM-tmpyakra presupposes the individuation that QUESTION-tmpn9t5a says is missing. Its own defeater, "A case where the function cannot be reproduced apart from its present occupant", is the question's first unsettled item seen from the other side. The question's note of 2026-11-23 records the claim as a partial answer to it; this paper reads it as an answer conditional on that individuation. (Gap GD3: an account of how a capability is identified apart from its holders.) The model that would decide it is stated as a lead: heterogeneous producers, a shared infrastructure bottleneck, an externality and declared public demands, comparing a uniform conduct constraint with a regime that also changes infrastructure access and "measuring the compatibility complex, realized violations and the recovery of rare capabilities" (LEAD-tmpy0jkr, Open, stage idea). It needs, in its own words, "a declared bearer of recovery, a firm or a capability". The lead is not built.

Reading CLAIM-tmpyakra beside QUESTION-tmpn9t5a gives it a test, which is this paper's and not either entry's: conserving a capability, rather than its occupant, would mean that the capability has positive growth when rare once demand for it returns and support is withdrawn, whoever comes to hold it. A regime that keeps the incumbent alive by continuing subsidy would fail that test even if the capability survives (join JD3: CLAIM-tmpyakra's conservation target read as QUESTION-tmpn9t5a's recovery when rare, with CLAIM-556's regeneration). Two Proposed entries bear on the dependent-supplier case of the motivating draft. A capability lost because another reliably supplies it is "dependency or division of labour" according to the supply, not the loss: one-way supply set from outside leaves a dependence "that withdrawal punishes", reciprocal loss leaves interdependence (CLAIM-603, Proposed, interpretive). That is a criterion for which losses of capability a regulator should treat as harm, on the condition that the capability can be named.

### 12.5 Norms that stabilize

The motivating draft cites QUESTION-371 for the idea that regulation can stabilize coexistence "by altering the dynamics through which competing groups persist". QUESTION-371 (Open) asks something narrower: whether "a moral norm" can work "among cooperating groups as a stabilizing mechanism does in coexistence theory, limiting a dominant group more than it limits its rivals so that a group reduced to rarity can increase again". It distinguishes an equalizing norm ("a cap, a transfer"), a stabilizing one ("limits on dominance, protections for minorities, rules against exclusion") and a loyalty norm, which is neither. Its note of 2026-11-22 says the industrial counterpart, "does a rule that alters access to essential infrastructure stabilize, where subsidies to entrants only equalize?", is a sibling filed as QUESTION-tmpn9t5a, and "does not answer it". QUESTION-371 is also explicit about what it does not ask: "Not whether such norms are justified" (citing CLAIM-075).

The paper therefore cites QUESTION-371 as the moral-norm form of §12.4's question, not as support. The motivating draft's sharpest sentence here, "Compensating participants for permanent exclusion is not equivalent to removing the mechanisms that make exclusion unavoidable", is the equalizing–stabilizing distinction of both questions, and both are open. What the record holds Active is the negative part: excluding a constituent relieves no frustration of the original demands (CLAIM-554).

### 12.6 A homonym: self-regulation is not public regulation

One entry near this section uses the word "regulatory" in another sense. CLAIM-916 (Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's) gives a rule for the constraint set of "A regulatory capacity": the set holds "each constraint the capacity's own constraints depend on for their production", so that "once the agent and the capacity are named only the agent's boundary is left to choose". Its "regulatory capacity" is an agent's capacity to regulate itself, the object of viability theory's constraint set (THEORY-134, Active), not a public regulator's authority over an industry. CLAIM-tmpyakra's defeater names CLAIM-916 as an entry that might already state it. On this paper's reading it does not. Its fourth step, a union over "substitutable routes", gives one capacity several realizers, which is close to a capability with several possible occupants, but it presupposes the capacity named ("C is fixed by the claim under test") and so presupposes the individuation that §12.4 lacks. Its open choice of boundary is filed as two readings, narrow (CLAIM-917, Proposed) and wide, in which "a capacity supplied from outside is held by the agent and is precarious with its supply" (CLAIM-915, Proposed). The wide reading is the formal shape of the dependent supplier's position, and this paper only notes the parallel.

### 12.7 What §12 establishes

- **Active.** Coexistence is not harmony, exclusion is not relief, and viability does not fix what ought to persist (CLAIM-546, CLAIM-554, CLAIM-075). Joint satisfiability does not certify that a demand deserves to be met (CLAIM-725).
- **Proposed.** What harmony is (TERM-tmppvlfi, with an unmet condition), what kind of unity it yields (CLAIM-tmp37le7), and what to conserve (CLAIM-tmpyakra, which presupposes the missing individuation).
- **Open.** Whether regulation can stabilize a rare capability's recovery rather than equalize or hold it in place (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a, QUESTION-371), and where public demands get their justification (§13.1).

The positive target the motivating draft proposes, "the recoverability of differentiated capabilities", is therefore a well-posed research target, not a result.

## 13. The governance of governance

### 13.1 Who specifies the demands

The motivating draft asks: "Who specifies the demands? Who determines which capacities are worth maintaining? Who decides whether a new form of industrial organization constitutes legitimate adaptation or unacceptable deviation?" It answers that "These questions cannot be answered by control theory alone." That matches the record exactly, and it should be read as the statement of an open question, not a result. The record holds five open questions on it:

- "From where does a relationship, a capability or a restriction derive its legitimacy, and from where its adequacy?" (QUESTION-485, Open, the owner's, U31 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse").
- "When is answerability required of an authority or governing capability: under what conditions is justification owed, to whom, and why?" (QUESTION-480, Open, the owner's, U36 of the same exchange).
- "Under what conditions is the allocation of authority to evaluate and constrain an actor sufficiently independent of that actor's capacity to influence the allocation?" (QUESTION-482, Open, ChatGPT's wording).
- Whether "relationally indexed normative judgments" can "be objective about the standards and perspectives themselves" (QUESTION-129, Open). CLAIM-746 quotes the exchange it came from: "I don't think compatibility and reachability can derive the moral authority of a norm from nothing."
- When an agent's own processes revise its will-organization, "what makes the result the continuation of that agent's will rather than a replacement authored by something else" (QUESTION-364, Open). For a regulator that rewrites its own mandate, this is the question whether the result is still the institution that was authorized.

The owner's thesis says the same of itself: "It does not say who sets the demand specification, or what makes it legitimate" (CLAIM-tmpz67ew, Proposed, the owner's). The pieces the record does hold are negative: viability does not settle what ought to persist (CLAIM-075), a demand's satisfaction condition does not carry its warrant (CLAIM-725), and a standing distinction among four judgments of an authority, "the warrant of the obligations it is to serve, its jurisdiction over the matter, its arrangement's capacity to realize those obligations together, and its capacity to reach compliant arrangements and correct failures by permitted means", of which "neither of the first two follows from any of the others" (CLAIM-746, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's as corrected by the filer). (Gap GD1: who legitimately sets a demand specification, and what makes it legitimate.)

The analysis beside this draft adds one thing to the question of who specifies, within its model (§9 describes the model). When a harm arises that the specification does not name, an arrangement that never enters it into its specification "scores zero violations on D0 and on its own specification, while the harm runs for all 120 periods", and "Only the true demand set ranks the two correctly" (analysis/README.md, item 7). That is CLAIM-361's point that excising a demand "has changed the comparison and has not been satisfied" (CLAIM-361, Active, interpretive), measured: an arrangement that scores itself against the specification it controls cannot see what it has excised. It is also, on this paper's reading, an instance of CLAIM-727's last clause, below: where authority is to be constrained by independently justified claims, "its own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has" (join JD4: the model's excision result read as CLAIM-727's self-certification limit, through CLAIM-361). The same run shows that under the peak budget rule a review that needs capacity "can never start, so the demand is excised by default, not by choice". The result is not yet on the record (gap GD7: file the novel-demand result as a CASE).

### 13.2 Capacity to act is no evidence of capacity to be corrected

The motivating draft rests its §13 on one entry, correctly cited: "An arrangement of institutions can keep, or increase, its capacity for effective action while its capacity to be effectively corrected by others degrades, because the two are maintained by different relations, so the first is no evidence of the second" (CLAIM-462, Active, interpretive). Its source separated four capacities that "are not different degrees of one quantity": effective action, restraint, correction and reconstruction. The relations that hold the capacity to be corrected are ones the arrangement's action does not need: "a court's orders reaching an enforcer who will act on them, information reaching a reviewer". "The institutions are formally continuous. The organization through which they exercised mutual constraint is not necessarily continuous." The entry's cases (CASE-176, CASE-175 and CASE-170, all Active) are real institutional episodes, and it says of them: "The cases show the pattern, not its frequency."

Three Active entries give the mechanism.

- **Within one organization.** "Maintaining a regime of effective action and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes are not the same achievement" (CLAIM-099, Active, interpretive), and "A successful corrective act, an executable procedure and a maintained organizational capacity are three different things" (CLAIM-422, Active, interpretive). CLAIM-462 rests on both.
- **Action that moves its own correction.** "An intervention can change what it would take to correct it: by altering the distribution of control, access, resources or enforcement before it is reviewed, it can make a specified corrective outcome costlier or unreachable while the standards that govern that outcome stay the same" (CLAIM-464, Active, interpretive). In §§6–9's language, an intervention can move the corrective configuration out of reach at the reviewer's budget without any rule changing.
- **Authority over authority.** "A constraint may govern conduct without governing the conditions under which it remains authoritative" (CLAIM-086, Active, interpretive).

For a regulator these say that the motivating draft's worry, "Its revision procedures can become ineffective even while formal rules remain unchanged", has an Active form, and that a regulator's demonstrated ability to change its own rules is no evidence that others can correct it. The second-order form, that an institution can be "reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons for revising that mechanism", is Proposed (CLAIM-719, Proposed, interpretive), and the record has no case of it: the one candidate examined, CASE-289, turned out on reading the court's opinion to be a first-order failure (SYNTHESIS-104, SYNTHESIS-038, both Proposed accounts). (Gap GD6: a case of a regulator responsive at the first order and unresponsive at the second.)

### 13.3 The structure of challenge

The motivating draft says the governing arrangement "must also preserve appropriate pathways through which affected parties and independent institutions can challenge its decisions". Two Active entries say what such a pathway is made of.

- **Control of the challenge needs its own warrant.** "Authority over a decision does not carry authority over the conditions on which it may be challenged: control of the cost, timing, evidence, forum or remedy of a challenge needs its own justification, and where an actor's authority is meant to be constrained by independently justified claims, its own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has" (CLAIM-727, Active, interpretive).
- **Three entitlements, each failing without the next.** For a party affected by a decision, explanation, contestation and interruption "are separate entitlements, and each without the next fails in its own way: explanation without contestation is transparency without accountability, contestation without interruption a right that arrives too late, and interruption without reasons an arbitrary veto" (CLAIM-740, Active, interpretive).

CLAIM-740's middle clause is the timing point of §§6–7 stated for challenge: a contest that cannot suspend execution arrives after the intervention has moved the corrective outcome out of reach (CLAIM-464). The record files the Proposed extension of this, that "A protected objection is effective only if six achievements hold together" (CLAIM-732, Proposed, interpretive), and the term for the maintained capability, *protected contestability*, "robust to a declared class of opposition; not a guarantee of success, a veto or costless adjudication" (TERM-130, Proposed).

### 13.4 Effective answerability: which sense

The motivating draft concludes that the response architecture "must include not only industrial control and institutional revision, but effective answerability", and contrasts a "technically adaptable regulatory institution" that is "politically unanswerable" with a "formally contestable institution" that cannot "implement corrections within meaningful deadlines". The record uses "answerability" in two senses and warns against reading them as one.

- **The normative relation, by dimension.** *Answerability* is "the normative relation in which the actor owes the party some form of answer, an account, reasons, responsiveness, an effective opportunity to contest, or judgment and redress", and it has four dimensions: normative ("Does the actor owe reasons, consideration, or correction to this party?"), institutional, effective ("Can the party's protected claims actually become consequential under foreseeable conditions?") and maintained. "These dimensions come apart. A defendant can owe a claimant a remedy even when the claimant cannot afford to pursue it." The bare term is the normative dimension; "the others are said with their qualifier" (TERM-126, Proposed). It is directed: "the party owed, the recipient of reasons, the forum that judges and the party that remedies can differ" (CLAIM-739, Proposed, interpretive).
- **Reachability plus correctability.** CLAIM-746's fourth judgment, which its source labelled "dynamic answerability", is an authority's "capacity to reach compliant arrangements and correct failures by permitted means". CLAIM-746 warns: "'Answerability' in A74's label means reachability plus correctability, not the directed normative relation of TERM-126 and CLAIM-739. The record should not read the two as one." TERM-126 says the same from its side: A74's sense is "the effective and maintained dimensions without the normative one".

**This paper means TERM-126's effective and maintained answerability**: that the claims of parties to whom the regulator owes an answer can actually become consequential, and stay so, under foreseeable conditions. It presupposes the normative dimension (who is owed what), which §13.1 leaves open. The motivating draft's other half, a regulator able to "implement corrections within meaningful deadlines", is correctability in TERM-085's sense, or CLAIM-746's dynamic judgment, and this paper calls it correctability, not answerability. The draft's two failures are then the two coming apart: correctable and not answerable, answerable and not correctable. *Joining the two as conditions on one regulator is this paper's* (join JD5: TERM-126's effective and maintained dimensions with TERM-085's correctability and CLAIM-740's entitlements, as two separate requirements on a regulator, kept apart as CLAIM-746 asks). It also connects §12: the correctability conjunct of harmony (TERM-tmppvlfi) is closer to effective answerability, a constituent's claims becoming consequential, than to TERM-085's correctability (JD1).

### 13.5 Three achievements, and a regulator's own failures

The paper's §10 diagnoses why an ecosystem's response fails. The same diagnosis applies to the regulator. For a normatively relevant discrepancy to change what an organization does, "three distinct achievements are needed and any can fail while the others hold: legibility, by which the discrepancy becomes recognizable; volitional efficacy, by which a corresponding demand can move action; and correctability, by which a fitting revision can be carried out within deadline and resources; none establishes that the demand or the revision is justified" (CLAIM-523, Active, interpretive). The entry adds two cautions that bear on §13.1: "A nonempty intersection does not show the agent will choose such a history", and "An empty intersection does not show the agent is beyond criticism", since its earlier actions may have destroyed the capacities required. It does not say "Who has the authority to decide that a change is warranted", which it hands to QUESTION-364.

A regulator can therefore fail to be corrected in three ways that need different remedies: criticism of it never becomes legible to it, becomes legible and is given no authority, or is given authority and cannot be executed in time. The motivating draft names all three ("Its standards can be incomplete. Its indicators can become detached from their underlying purposes. Its authority can be captured.") without separating them. The record also holds that capture can work through the evaluative framework a regulator adopts rather than through its decisions (THEORY-859, Proposed), and that an organization "can be governed from outside without any of its decisions being made for it, through the evidence its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh evidence" (CLAIM-404, Active, interpretive). The second is the record's account of the motivating draft's point that "The regulator is not an omniscient observer standing outside the ecosystem". It sits uneasily with the owner's thesis, whose gloss reads the ecosystem as governed "from outside by a regulator who is not one of the constituents whose demands are specified" (CLAIM-tmpz67ew). This paper follows the motivating draft and CLAIM-404: the regulator is a participant, and its evidence and criteria are channels through which it is governed.

### 13.6 The owner's correction-coupling principle

Since the motivating draft was written, the owner has stated a principle that gives §13 a mechanism where the draft has an aspiration ("institutions capable of being changed by evidence, legitimate challenge, and changing public demands"):

> "A system's ability to correct itself depends not simply on having critics or feedback, but on how evidence, evaluation, and authority are coupled—and what the system is prevented from rewriting or rejecting." (CLAIM-tmp4h9rc, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's, 2026-11-23.)

The owner gave four constructions as its instances. Three are Active and demonstrated, each in a model, and one is Proposed.

- **Protected evidence.** "In a simulated sensor network whose correspondences drift, revision improves prediction on positions the network did not choose only when it answers to beacons whose position and channel the revision can neither rewrite nor refuse; revision by the sensors' disagreement alone restores consistency and not accuracy" (CLAIM-tmpvqmwc, Active, demonstrated; CASE-tmp33vf4, Active).
- **Scoring apart from agreement.** In a population on the coupled meaning–influence model, "grants indexed by role with the critic role scored by observed relevance leave neither belief factions nor capture, while scalar grants fail one way or the other"; "indexing by role without a separate score buys nothing" (CLAIM-tmp5wozo, Active, demonstrated).
- **An identifiability limit.** "Where no process holds evidence on a block of tasks that does not come from the proposals themselves, a governance rule that treats its members alike cannot tell honest dissent from an attack that pushes the same block as hard" (CLAIM-tmpxoxys, Active, demonstrated).
- **Capture through a return channel.** The loop between an evaluator and the party it evaluates "can capture the evaluator's reading" even when "the evaluator's conferral reads no interpretation at all"; "informational independence does not prevent it while a resource channel runs, and what does is a return channel too weak to close the loop" (CLAIM-tmpz8q70, Proposed, demonstrated).

The assistant's reading, in the entry, is that the evidence half is already held, "What keeps a judgement–observation loop from confirming itself is evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests" (CLAIM-342, Active, interpretive), and that what the principle adds is two further conditions: "authority's coupling" (the return channel; grants indexed by role and scored apart from agreement) and "protection" (what the revision can neither rewrite nor refuse). The principle's negative half, that critics or feedback do not suffice, follows from the four components, each of which exhibits them with correction failing. Its positive half is a prediction that has not been tested: "The claim, read strictly, predicts separate requirements. In one model that hosts all four, removing any one alone should defeat correction while the others hold." "If one condition is found to entail the others, the claim reduces to that one" (CLAIM-tmp4h9rc). The model is proposed as a lead: one population with four switches (evidence from outside the proposals, its protection from revision and refusal, a return channel with a strength, scoring by relevance or by agreement), scored "on ground truth the population never sees" (LEAD-tmpdzbyn, Open, stage idea, the assistant's).

Two cautions on how far the principle reaches here. First, its components are a sensor network and populations of agents on a meaning–influence model, not regulators. Carrying them to a public regulator's relation to industry, courts, auditors and affected parties is *this paper's* step, not the record's (join JD6: the correction-coupling principle and its model components carried to a regulatory arrangement). On that carriage, a regulator's protected evidence is evidence about the industry it cannot rewrite or refuse (independent measurement, mandatory disclosure); its authority's coupling is how standing and resources flow back from the regulated to the regulator (the return channel of capture); and its scoring is whether objections gain weight by their relevance or by agreement with the regulator's current view. Second, whether the conditions are separate requirements is open, and the account that maps them says so ("Whether each condition is needed when the others hold is open, and LEAD-tmpdzbyn would test it": SYNTHESIS-111, Proposed).

### 13.7 A protected anchor that is wrong: the regress measured once

The motivating draft ends §13 on a paradox: a regulator "must maintain enough identity to preserve legitimate commitments and institutional responsibility", but "must not stabilize its own interpretive and authority structures so thoroughly that criticism cannot become consequential". The record holds a result that measures one form of it, in one model.

The assistant ran the sensor network of CLAIM-tmpvqmwc with a protected beacon that is itself miscalibrated. "What a system is prevented from rewriting or rejecting bounds what it can correct as well as enabling correction" (CLAIM-tmpw11e6, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's; a counter to CLAIM-tmp4h9rc). Over 40 seeds, with the beacon correct the network's late error is 0.044; with the beacon miscalibrated by |δ| = 0.361 it is "late error 0.360 (0.328 to 0.393), radius 0.114": the network converges on the beacon's error and is as internally consistent as with a correct beacon. A rarer protected survey of the beacon, used only by a slower revision of its calibration, brings the error to 0.122 when it comes every 100 steps and 0.293 every 500. "An unrewritable, unrefusable anchor imports its own error whole, and nothing inside the system can see it, since consistency is unaffected." "This is CLAIM-342 again, one level up. The regress stops at whatever is not itself revised." The reason nothing inside can see it is held Active: the miscalibration "is unidentifiable from inside the sensor network by a symmetry", since a world with a miscalibrated beacon and a shifted world with a correct one "give every report identically", so "correcting it needs evidence that breaks the symmetry from outside" (CLAIM-tmpklggb, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's).

On this paper's reading, this is the motivating draft's reflexive problem in a measured form (join JD7: CLAIM-tmpw11e6 and CLAIM-tmpklggb read as the regress of the governance of governance). What protects a regulator's correction (evidence it cannot rewrite, commitments it cannot drop) also fixes what that correction answers to. If the protected anchor is wrong, the regulator converges on its error while staying consistent, and only a further, slower protected channel, at that channel's rate, corrects it. Each level of governance answers to something it does not itself revise. The owner's principle says protection enables correction; the assistant's counter says it also bounds correction. The paper's reflexive conclusion needs both.

Three cautions keep this in proportion.

1. **It is a counter to the owner's claim, and Proposed.** Its note of 2026-11-23: promoting it "would make its concession due on CLAIM-tmp4h9rc, the owner's own claim (ADR-059), and writing it in is the owner's". This paper does not write that concession. It reports the counter as the assistant's and the principle as the owner's, unrevised.
2. **It is one model.** The claim's title says the error falls "only through a further, rarer protected channel", and its note concedes this "reaches beyond the one model". It "does not show that every protected anchor must be revisable". LEAD-tmpdzbyn would test it in a second model.
3. **Who places the anchor is untested.** The case says it cannot show "Anything about who places the beacons. Here the world draws them" (CASE-tmp33vf4). For a regulator, who places the anchors is §13.1's question of who specifies the demands. The measured regress says what happens once an anchor is placed; it says nothing about whether it was placed rightly. (Gap GD8: a model in which the anchors are placed by a party with interests, the regulatory analogue of who specifies.)

### 13.8 What §13 establishes

- **Active.** An arrangement's capacity to act is no evidence of its capacity to be corrected by others (CLAIM-462, CLAIM-099, CLAIM-422), and an intervention can make its own correction costlier or unreachable under unchanged standards (CLAIM-464, CLAIM-086). Control of a challenge needs its own warrant, and explanation, contestation and interruption are separate entitlements (CLAIM-727, CLAIM-740). Legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability can each fail alone (CLAIM-523). Three of the four components of the owner's principle are demonstrated in models (CLAIM-tmpvqmwc, CLAIM-tmp5wozo, CLAIM-tmpxoxys), and a protected anchor's error is unidentifiable from inside (CLAIM-tmpklggb).
- **Proposed.** Answerability as a term (TERM-126), the four judgments of an authority (CLAIM-746), the owner's correction-coupling principle (CLAIM-tmp4h9rc) and the assistant's counter to it (CLAIM-tmpw11e6).
- **Open.** Who specifies the demands and what makes the specification legitimate (QUESTION-485, QUESTION-480, QUESTION-482, QUESTION-129, QUESTION-364); whether the owner's conditions are separate requirements (LEAD-tmpdzbyn).

The motivating draft's closing formula, "a historically continuous organization of public authority that retains the capacity for effective correction", is on this account a conjunction of two requirements the record keeps apart, correctability and effective answerability, under a demand specification whose source the record does not yet account for.
