# CLAIM-tmpts8ee: A record used to evaluate research should make legible the distinctions the evaluating decision needs and not every detail: publication count is adequate to a question about throughput and inadequate to one about contribution to knowledge

## The claim

A24 §4 of the exchange "Hyper article interpretation"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b064f1b-9ea8-83e8-951e-634ed41675b6) restates
CLAIM-535's condition correctly. For an acceptable policy based only on
observations h(w) to exist, the circumstances w that give each observation o
must share an acceptable action: the intersection of A(w) over all w with
h(w) = o is non-empty. A24 then applies it to evaluation.

> Suppose two researchers each published four papers. One has developed
> and maintained a highly influential theoretical framework. The other has
> produced four genuinely independent empirical contributions. The
> publication count makes them look identical.

Whether that matters depends on the decision. For throughput the count
suffices, and for contribution it does not. A contribution record adds the
missing distinctions, but "the record shouldn't necessarily expose every
internal detail." Hence A24's design goal, "more precise than maximal
transparency": "Provide the distinctions required for justified action,
with sufficient provenance to support reconsideration when the relevant
decisions change."

## What it does not say

- The goal speaks of "the distinctions required", as if one decision fixed
  one set. By CLAIM-535 it need not. Where acceptable actions overlap, the
  coarsest sufficient observations are several and incomparable, and which
  one suffices depends on the action committed to. CLAIM-tmpu6e4u works
  this through.
- A24 also says "Different audiences require different observations of the
  same underlying work". That is the owner's CLAIM-tmpkvi10, which this
  claim does not argue.
