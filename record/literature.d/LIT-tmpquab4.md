# LIT-tmpquab4: Increasing propensity to mind-wander with transcranial direct current stimulation

Axelrod, V., Rees, G., Lavidor, M. & Bar, M. (2015). Increasing propensity
to mind-wander with transcranial direct current stimulation. *Proceedings
of the National Academy of Sciences* 112(11): 3314–3319. DOI
10.1073/pnas.1421435112.

Read in full in NOTE-tmp7gplv.

## Key takeaways

- **The effect.** Prefrontal anodal tDCS, "but not of the occipital cortex
  or sham stimulation, increased the propensity of participants to
  mind-wander (i.e., TUTs)" (Discussion). Experiment 1: 2.39 against
  2.05 on the 1–4 scale, t(13) = 2.63, P = 0.021. Experiment 2: 2.93
  against 2.12 (sham) and 2.17 (occipital), F(2,28) = 5.85, P = 0.007.
- **No cost to the task.** Target detection did not fall and rose
  slightly, not significantly; the authors read this as "an enhancement
  (extension) of cognitive capacity" and conclude that "executive control
  is an integral part of the mind-wandering cognitive mechanism".
- **Spatial limits stated.** The montage may reach the left DLPFC, medial
  frontal regions or the right frontopolar cortex under the cathode; "our
  study was not designed to establish unequivocally what regions within
  the frontal lobes are involved".

## Standing in the record

Registered and read for LEAD-141, because Metzinger's 2017 chapter
(LIT-1945, p. 14) cites it, with Broadway et al.'s commentary, as an "early
example of relevant studies" for his H1, that "selectively blocking [the
epistemic agent model's] introspective availability should suffice to
trigger a mind wandering episode". The reading finds it is not a test of
H1, for the reasons in NOTE-tmp7gplv and ARG-tmpqszyu. Its main effect is
contested: replicated by its first author (LIT-tmpg3vlm), not replicated in
a pre-registered three-lab study of 192 participants (LIT-tmp9cpfz).
