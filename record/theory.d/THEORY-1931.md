# THEORY-1931: Oversight counts only if it can keep pace

## Source

Hong Kong Monetary Authority, "Discussion Paper on Agentic A.I. Governance in
the Banking Sector", November 2026 ([LIT-2696](../literature.d/LIT-2696.md)), read in [NOTE-2018](../notes.d/NOTE-2018.md).

## What was actually shown

A regulator's proposal, not evidence. Boundaries "should be defined in
advance, technically enforced and changed only through the AI’s" change
management (§7.1, p. 35). "A formal approval step is not, by itself,
meaningful human oversight." (§7.2, p. 37). Intervention design "should take
account of detection time, decision time and stopping time, so that /
intervention can occur before harm arises or becomes irreversible." (p. 37).
And where oversight fails under stress, "the deployment’s / authority, scope
or velocity may need to be constrained." (§4.3, p. 19). Velocity limits
should be commensurate "with the demonstrated effectiveness of oversight"
(p. 19).

It corroborates [CLAIM-705](../claims.d/CLAIM-705.md) qualitatively, as a regulator's judgement:
where actions outrun review, the approval step stays in place and stops
working ("approval fatigue", §4.4, p. 20). It gives no rates and no
derivation, and the exchange's A had read it (A23 §3) before A35 stated
the throughput claim, so it is only partly independent. It bears on
[QUESTION-471](../questions.d/QUESTION-471.md): it puts the stopping points before consequential
actions take effect and ties the rate of action to the capacity of review.

## What this does not say

- That standing limits make friction unnecessary. It pairs them with
  approval gates and transaction-specific validation, and its answer to
  speed is to slow the agent down. So it is the documentary source of
  [CLAIM-711](../claims.d/CLAIM-711.md)'s architecture, not support for that claim's conclusion
  that an opportunity to intervene does not consist in friction.
- That the controls work. "practical evidence remains limited" (§4.1); it is
  a discussion paper that creates no obligations (§2.3).
- Anything beyond banking agents under the HKMA.

## From the second reading (2026-11-17)

The second reading states the same proposal from its organizing principle,
"intent-anchored accountability" (p. 5): an agent acts only for an
approved business intent within delegated authority, and accountability
stays with identified owners and "cannot be passed to customers" (p. 27).
It adds two relations, to claims that do not rest on it: it corroborates
[CLAIM-730](../claims.d/CLAIM-730.md) (a slow governing level over a fast process) and
[CLAIM-715](../claims.d/CLAIM-715.md) (scrutiny differs from authority to act on it). One limit:

- **A right of the affected to suspend.** Customers may request review;
  interruption stays with the bank.
