# A rule whose outcomes change when classification moves to another group, with the rule unchanged, is evidence that jurisdiction over a concept's contexts partly fixes whether its uses are compatible

## The inference

1. CASE-002: "If an incident is classified as severe, deployment must
   stop." The product team controls classification, and moving it to an
   independent reliability group "changes outcomes without changing the
   rule".
2. CASE-002's note of 2026-11-07 reads A43 §3's two institutions, "with
   the same recorded classification of a particular patient", as this
   case with a concept in place of an incident's severity.
3. CLAIM-197: "which families count as covers decides whether anything
   glues".
4. So whoever holds jurisdiction over a concept's contexts partly fixes
   the cover, and so partly settles whether its uses are compatible
   (CLAIM-tmpxe1ih). The claim says of these premises: "Joined,
   they give this claim".

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** Yes.
  CLAIM-tmpxe1ih's defeat condition states the alternative: the cover is
  fixed by the observations, "so that changing who holds jurisdiction
  changes which section is acted on but never whether the sections glue".
  CASE-002's result is what that alternative predicts. Under either
  classifier one classification of each incident governs. Moving
  classification changes which incidents count as severe, the section. The
  incidents and the deployment decisions, the contexts, stay as they were.
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** No. To bear on
  compatibility, a case needs a family of uses over overlapping contexts
  whose agreement is in question, and a change of jurisdiction that
  changes which contexts are in the cover. CASE-002 has one classifier at
  a time, so the family has one member and glues trivially under either
  rule. Senior management's "business critical" override is suspension,
  in TERM-017's sense. It withholds a consequence in some contexts and
  does not change whether two uses of "severe" agree.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** No, and this is the
  question that fails. CASE-002 measures outcomes: whether a deployment
  stops. The claim needs a compatibility verdict. Its own test, the
  reader's, says so: "two institutions with identical case data and
  different jurisdiction rules. Check whether the compatibility verdict,
  and not only the action taken, changes under the cover each rule
  fixes." CASE-002 reports only the action taken.

Rejected. CASE-002 shows that jurisdiction acts on outputs, which ARG-001
already holds: precedence, jurisdiction and suspension come apart. The
claim says "not only on its outputs", and that is the part the case does
not reach. The claim's distinctive clause rests on CLAIM-197 and
CLAIM-057, and on a bridging premise that no entry supplies: that
jurisdiction over which contexts count as relevant is a choice of cover,
not a choice of section. The claim itself is not defeated. It needs
another route.

## What would bear on the conclusion

A variant of CASE-002 with two classifiers whose uses of "severe" overlap
on some incidents, and a jurisdiction rule deciding whether the second
classifier's contexts count. Under one rule the cover has one member, and
the uses glue. Under the other it has both, and on the shared incidents
they may disagree. With the same incidents and the same classifications,
the compatibility verdict would then change with the rule. A43 §3's
Institution B, where "the label automatically determines which
information is considered relevant", points this way. A43 §10's design,
which varies "the mechanisms through which classifications acquire
authority", measures "subsequent judgments, actions, correction, and the
long-term stability of the category". It would need a compatibility
measure added.
