summary: >-
  Castano, Yzerbyt, Paladino & Sacchi (2002). One experiment with 48 Italian students. Those who wrote about the emotions their own death aroused, rather than about reading a book, then identified more with Italians (6.19 vs 5.09), saw Italians as more of an entity (5.40 vs 4.77) and showed more in-group bias against Germans (1.01 vs 0.45), the last entirely through rating Italians higher. Identification and entitativity, which a factor analysis separates (r = .59), each mediated the effect on bias, through what they share. The authors propose, without testing it, that a group seen as a real, lasting entity lets people project themselves beyond their own death; the standard reading, defence of a worldview the group shares, is not ruled out.

# LIT-1719: I Belong, therefore, I Exist: Ingroup Identification, Ingroup Entitativity, and Ingroup Bias

Castano, E., Yzerbyt, V., Paladino, M.-P. & Sacchi, S. (2002). *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 28(2), 135–143.

Read in full in [NOTE-1297](../notes.d/NOTE-1297.md), from the published version on co-author Yzerbyt's UCLouvain page.

## Why this is here

Joins the legacy angle to the social one: death reminders enlarge the self into a group seen as lasting. For [QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md) and [TERM-096](../terms.d/TERM-096.md) (a group entity versus a category).
