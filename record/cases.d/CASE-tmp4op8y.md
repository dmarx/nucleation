# CASE-tmp4op8y: The U.S. fire incident reporting guidance on cancelled dispatches, 2023: units assigned, arrived and turned back are recorded, while a call a department could not answer for lack of resources is not an incident at all, so the data omit the failures that most concern readiness

## The case

The U.S. Fire Administration's NFIRSGram "Dispatched and Canceled en
Route", posted 19 June 2023 (LIT-tmpnhw2v, read in full in NOTE-tmpain0u), is
coding guidance for the National Fire Incident Reporting System (NFIRS).
It fixes what the incident data can and cannot carry.

**What is recorded.**

- An incident is coded 611 only when no one arrives: "This incident type
  should only be used when the fire department is dispatched to an
  incident but does not arrive on the scene because they were canceled
  prior to arrival."
- Units turned back from an incident that others reached are recorded
  unit by unit: "The use of the Apparatus/Personnel Modules can show what
  resources were available, even though they never arrived on scene."
- In the Basic Module, Section G1, "all apparatus and personnel that were
  assigned to the incident for the reporting fire department should be
  recorded regardless of whether they arrived or were canceled before
  arrival."
- In the page's second scenario, a department sends four units "Based on
  information provided by dispatch"; the engine finds a false alarm, and
  "The fire department cancels the tanker/tender and the rescue vehicle
  before their arrival on scene." A department can also track
  cancellations of its mutual aid: "The fire department can analyze how
  often they were canceled en route to other districts using the results
  from this field."

**What is not.** "It is not an NFIRS incident if: Your resources were not
dispatched and did not leave the station. The fire department was not
aware of the call. The fire department was aware of the call but did not
have resources to respond." And: "While these situations are not
reportable in NFIRS, it is recommended the fire department record them
outside of NFIRS in a separate database."

Held fixed: the page is a coding rule. It says nothing about how often
any of these happen, and nothing about whether departments keep the
separate record it recommends.

## What it shows

- **A reporting system that sees committed capacity and not unmet
  demand.** Within an incident, NFIRS distinguishes what was assigned,
  what arrived and what was turned back, a reporting-level form of
  CLAIM-296's distinctions between what a system has and what it deploys.
  The failure of deployment for want of a resource, the one outcome that
  most directly measures readiness, is the case the system defines out:
  it has no incident to attach to.
- **Under-representation by construction, not by error.** An analysis of
  NFIRS data alone would find dispatched-but-unused units, which can read
  as surplus, and would not find the calls nobody answered, which read as
  nothing. Only a record the guidance recommends, and does not require,
  would show them. Whether the omission matters in practice depends on how
  many departments keep that record, which the page does not say.
- **Cancellation is recorded as an event, not as a value.** A cancelled
  unit's record says it was assigned and turned back. It does not say
  what its dispatch was worth under the uncertainty at the time; that is
  the question of A's case of provisional dispatch (CASE-tmpt6fm7).

## What it does not show

- That any department lacks resources to respond, or how often, or with
  what consequence.
- That NFIRS data have been used to cut capacity. The link to A's
  proposition, that measuring a capability by observed use can destroy it,
  is the filer's and A's, not the page's.
- A heavy rescue squad. The pasted bibliography's running example is not
  in the page, whose cancelled units are a tanker/tender and a rescue
  vehicle.
- Anything about NFIRS's successor system, which the page does not
  mention and the reading did not check.
- `standing: documented` is the guidance's: the rules are as stated on
  the page as fetched on 2026-11-04.

The wording is the filer's. Adopting it is the owner's call.
