# CLAIM-tmpgmxs0: Framework can diagnose conservative theories

## The claim

The owner's words, U25: "I think this positions us to characterize
extremely precisely what is wrong/deficient with purported "originalist",
"unitary executive", and even more broadly, "conservative" theories,
respectively." "This" is the framework as the exchange had developed it
through A34 (site reliability engineering) and A36 (American governance as
a distributed architecture of normative relationships). "Respectively" asks
for a different deficiency in each, not one charge against all three.

The claim is about the framework's reach: that it can locate the
deficiency precisely. It does not itself name the deficiencies.

## Two readings

- **(a) This entry.** The framework yields precise critiques of the three
  theories.
- **(b) [CLAIM-tmp9v9yl](CLAIM-tmp9v9yl.md).** "Purported" marks theories that call themselves
  originalist, unitary-executive or conservative, and the framework shows
  where they fall short of what they purport. A38 §8 ends on this reading.

## What followed

A38 and A40 took it up. Each separated a general proposition from its
application to a theory, and A40 §10 said the application "would then
require demonstrating that the targeted theory actually depends upon the
conflation in question":

- the general propositions: [CLAIM-tmpazcha](CLAIM-tmpazcha.md) (formative specification and
  continuing constitution), [CLAIM-tmp1wlu2](CLAIM-tmp1wlu2.md) (coordinated unity and unity of
  authority) and [CLAIM-tmpa8tdg](CLAIM-tmpa8tdg.md) (structure and capacity, and the cost of
  the status quo);
- the critiques: [CLAIM-tmpr2qzg](CLAIM-tmpr2qzg.md) (originalism), [CLAIM-tmplc4m8](CLAIM-tmplc4m8.md) (strong
  unitary executive theory) and [CLAIM-tmplc0vv](CLAIM-tmplc0vv.md) (preservationist
  conservatism);
- the three in tension with one another ([CLAIM-tmpawbfn](CLAIM-tmpawbfn.md)), and A's positive
  term, constitutional regeneration ([TERM-tmpx4zke](../terms.d/TERM-tmpx4zke.md)).

## What the filing found

The filing checked each critique against how the theory's defenders state
their own positions, and filed the strongest reply the exchange omits as an
objection: [CLAIM-tmpmp2wh](CLAIM-tmpmp2wh.md) (originalism), [CLAIM-tmpqhxsz](CLAIM-tmpqhxsz.md) (unitary executive)
and [CLAIM-tmpdzcj6](CLAIM-tmpdzcj6.md) (conservatism), with the arguments [ARG-tmptjsx2](../arguments.d/ARG-tmptjsx2.md),
[ARG-tmpz2gmo](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpz2gmo.md) and [ARG-tmpx1cys](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpx1cys.md). The general distinctions stand as A states
them. The attributions are weakest for originalism and conservatism, where
the conflation charged is one that the theorists the turns themselves cite
do not hold, and strongest for strong unitary executive theory's control of
correctives inside the executive. So far the filing bears on the claim's
first defeater, and on the theories as stated by their theorists; whether
popular or political versions of the three make the conflations was not
checked. [LEAD-tmpdy0f8](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpdy0f8.md) would check the defenders' statements against their
texts.

## What it does not say

That any of the three theories is wrong as a whole, or any conclusion of
constitutional law. A38 §2: "Our framework supplies an architectural
analysis, not a substitute for that constitutional inquiry."
