# CLAIM-551: Read in DBT's sense, cognitive dysregulation is a consequence of emotion dysregulation, not a failure to allocate attention: a perspective with high gain, intense response and slow return to baseline has a small and lingering tolerance, can stay active when the current discrepancy no longer warrants it, and while active supplies the interpretation that governance acts on, so the effective will becomes mood-dependent; it predicts impairment that follows arousal and its slow decay, where the attentional reading predicts impairment that follows competing demand on a fixed budget

## The two readings

The owner's U29: "apply our framework interpretively to explain maladaptive
behaviors, cognitive dysregulation, etc." A29 §7 read the phrase as
attention and working memory "become contested resources": allocations
rᵢ with Σᵢ rᵢ ≤ Bₜ, and dysregulation as a failure of governance in
allocating them, kept apart from "attention limitations due to capacity"
([CLAIM-537](CLAIM-537.md)'s note of 2026-11-04). That reading stays on the record.

In DBT, "cognitive dysregulation" is a term of art, and A29 never named
it. Linehan's borderline personality is "primarily a disorder of emotion
dysregulation", which is "(a) heightened emotional sensitivity, (b)
inability to regulate intense emotional responses, and (c) slow return to
emotional baseline", and her construct of emotion includes
"emotion-linked cognitive process" ([LIT-2005](../literature.d/LIT-2005.md), p. 496). Cognition is
dysregulated as a consequence: reactions to emotional situations "result
in distorted information processing, difficulties regulating actions to
achieve non-mood-dependent goals, problems controlling emotion-linked
behavior, and shutting down/freezing" (p. 502).

## The claim, in the framework's terms

Take [CLAIM-550](CLAIM-550.md)'s perspective, with activation z = b(c) + α(c)d rising
with discrepancy d, acting with probability σ(κ(z − θ)), and tolerating
the discrepancy (θ + κ⁻¹ logit ε − b)/α. Linehan's three properties are
three of its parameters:

| DBT | in the framework | effect on tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| heightened sensitivity | high gain α | smaller: a small discrepancy activates |
| intense response, not regulated | high κ, and governance unable to lower z | activation reaches extremes and is not damped |
| slow return to baseline | b(c, t) raised by an earlier event and decaying slowly | smaller, or negative, long after the event |

A negative tolerance is [CLAIM-550](CLAIM-550.md)'s mark of activation "driven by
something other than the discrepancy". Here it is driven by the history
of activation itself.

Cognitive dysregulation is then what an active perspective does to the
rest of the organization. While it is active, its interpretation of the
situation is the one governance acts on. That is "distorted information
processing", a failure at the locus of interpretation, and the same
authority over attention and evidence that [CLAIM-446](CLAIM-446.md) gives defensive
processes. The effective will then follows the activation of whichever
perspective is active, not the maintained arrangement. That is the
"difficulties regulating actions to achieve non-mood-dependent goals":
a will that is mood-dependent.

## How the two readings differ

| | the attentional reading (A29 §7) | the DBT reading |
|---|---|---|
| locus | allocation of a fixed budget among perspectives | activation dynamics, then interpretation |
| impairment follows | the number and size of competing demands | arousal, and its slow decay after the event |
| outlasts the event | no, once demand falls | yes, until activation returns to baseline |
| what restores it | reprioritizing, or more capacity | lowering activation; DBT's distress-tolerance and emotion-regulation skills |

[CASE-192](../cases.d/CASE-192.md) builds the two as organizations equally impaired during an
episode and separates them by time course and by intervention.

They can both hold in one individual, and an episode can involve both.
The DBT reading adds what the attentional one cannot say: why the
impairment lingers after the demand is gone, and why it is selective for
emotional situations.

## What it does not say

- **That this is DBT's whole account.** The pages read restate the
  biosocial theory. Linehan's grouping of borderline criteria into five
  areas of dysregulation, with cognitive dysregulation among them, is
  known here only by report; her 1987 paper and the skills manual are
  held unread ([LIT-1221](../literature.d/LIT-1221.md), [LIT-1492](../literature.d/LIT-1492.md)).
- **That the skills work by this mechanism.** The record's DBT data,
  [THEORY-922](../theory.d/THEORY-922.md) and [QUESTION-307](../questions.d/QUESTION-307.md), relate skills homework to falling urges,
  not to cognition.
- **That the dysregulated perspective is pathological.** As in A29, its
  concern may be legitimate. What is dysregulated is its activation and
  the authority it then holds.
- **That high gain is a defect.** It is a context's parameter, and in
  some contexts a sensitive perspective is the right one to have.
