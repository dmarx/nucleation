# CLAIM-547: Cooperation within groups and the coexistence of groups are distinct targets that can trade off: in the group-selection rule of Nowak's five, b/c > 1 + n/m, cooperation spreads because a group that grows to size n splits and another group is driven extinct, so the mechanism that stabilizes cooperation inside groups works by removing groups; cooperation local to a group, the persistence of several groups whose interests are opposed, and concern for those outside one's group are three achievements, and none of them secures the next

## The claim

Nowak's fifth rule ([LIT-501](../literature.d/LIT-501.md), [THEORY-567](../theory.d/THEORY-567.md)) is group selection. There are m
groups, and a group that reaches size n splits in two. "In this case,
another group becomes extinct in order to constrain the total population
size." Cooperators are favoured when b/c > 1 + n/m. Groups of cooperators
grow faster and split more often, so they drive other groups out. Within
the model, then, what stabilizes cooperation inside groups is the
elimination of groups.

That makes the synthesis's §7 exact for one case. It names three things:

- **local cooperation**, among partners or within a group;
- **coexistence at the level of the system**, several groups with opposed
  interests all persisting;
- **impartial concern**, commitments that reach past partners, kin and
  group.

None secures the next. The group-selection model has the first without
the second. A society of coexisting groups that are each indifferent to
outsiders has the second without the third. Morality-as-cooperation's
group-loyalty domain ([THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md)) is a solution to the first problem,
and it can work against the second. The synthesis cites Van Lange and
Bergmann for the human case. Read, the review corroborates the first link
only ([THEORY-1352](../theory.d/THEORY-1352.md)); [ARG-052](../arguments.d/ARG-052.md) gives the verdict.

The record's other entries put this in two places:

- [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md): viability does not establish what ought to persist. A
  cooperative arrangement that evolved, or that persists, is not thereby
  justified. The synthesis says the same.
- [CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md)'s last clause: domination is another's capacity to govern
  the relations that make a will free. A group whose internal
  cooperation lets it set the terms for others is that case at the scale
  of groups.

## What it does not say

- **That between-group competition must be lethal.** In the model it is,
  by construction.
- **That impartial concern is the right standard.** Which of the three a
  moral framework should serve is open ([QUESTION-371](../questions.d/QUESTION-371.md)).
- **That the review shows the trade-off for groups' persistence.** See
  the note below.

## Note of 2026-11-04: Van Lange and Bergmann, read

Read in [NOTE-1537](../notes.d/NOTE-1537.md), with [THEORY-1352](../theory.d/THEORY-1352.md) from it. It supports one link:
"the same processes that can establish ingroup cooperation can also fuel
intergroup conflict" (p. 554). Its contrast of group-based and
individual-based morality (p. 555) backs the remark that [THEORY-468](../theory.d/THEORY-468.md)'s
group-loyalty domain can work against the coexistence of groups. Three
things qualify the claim.

- **The review is about cooperation and conflict between groups, not
  their persistence.** It says nothing on extinction or on impartial
  concern. It does not take up Nowak's rule.
- **The trade-off is conditional.** In-group favouritism "need not imply
  ... any harmful behaviour towards outgroups" (p. 553). Costly competition
  needs conflicting interests, real or perceived (p. 554). That fits "can
  trade off, not must".
- **A second route to fewer groups.** The review's success case is groups
  that cooperate until they "merge into larger collectives that transcend
  former group boundaries" (p. 555). Cooperation between groups can
  therefore also fail to secure their coexistence, by merger rather than
  extinction. This is the coexistence-to-integration step the synthesis
  draws from endosymbiosis, met again at the scale of groups.
