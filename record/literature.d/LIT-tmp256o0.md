# LIT-tmp256o0: Disenshittify or die! How hackers can seize the means of computation and build a new, good internet that is hardened against our asshole bosses' insatiable horniness for enshittification [DEF CON 32 talk]

Cory Doctorow (2024), *DEF CON 32, Track 1, Las Vegas (talk; video on the DEFCONConference YouTube channel; author's text on Pluralistic, 2024-08-17)* — <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4EmstuO0Em8>

## Key takeaways

- Doctorow argues that platform decay ("enshittification") was not inevitable. It followed from removing four forces that once disciplined tech firms: competition, regulation, interoperability and tech-worker power. Restoring them, especially the legal right to reverse-engineer and interoperate, is how a "new, good internet" gets built.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The talk opens by asking what happened to the "old, good internet" and describes the enshittification cycle. Platforms are first good to users and lock them in. They then shift value to business customers, and finally claw value back from both. Doctorow names the mechanism "twiddling": altering a service's back end to change prices, rankings and terms. He argues that firms were once restrained by competition, regulators strong enough to discipline them, users' ability to interoperate and modify, and scarce tech workers who could refuse. Antitrust retreat, regulatory capture, IP law and mass layoffs removed each restraint. He closes by asking hackers to fight for interoperability rights and to organise as workers.
