# LIT-tmpxww5n: Asymptotic Methods in Statistical Decision Theory

Lucien Le Cam (1986), New York: Springer-Verlag, Springer Series in
Statistics, xxvi + 742 pp. — DOI-10.1007/978-1-4612-4946-7

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read*: no lawful full text was reachable (see the history
note). What follows restates the book's own front matter ("To the Reader"
and the summary of contents), which Springer serves freely, labelled as
such; none of it was checked against the chapters.

- **The theme, in the author's words.** The statistician faces a complex
  experiment ℰ = {P_θ} and looks for a known, tractable experiment ℱ close
  enough that procedures for ℱ transfer to ℰ "without dire consequences".
  Closeness is a pseudo-distance between experiments.
- **Two distances, one number.** The preface says that measuring closeness
  by risk functions under losses bounded by one, and by how closely each
  experiment can be mimicked by a randomisation of the other, give
  distances that are "not only related, but exactly equal" (Chapter 2,
  Theorem 2, from Le Cam 1964). Specialised to distance zero this is the
  Blackwell–Sherman–Stein equivalence, which Chapter 3, Section 7 states in
  a form.
- **Structure.** Chapters 1–8 are general (experiments as vector lattices
  and transitions, deficiencies, likelihood-ratio distributions,
  sufficiency and insufficiency, contiguity, limit theorems, invariance);
  9–14 develop Gaussian, Poisson and exponential-family approximation,
  including local asymptotic normality, global asymptotic normality and
  posterior convergence; 16–17 recover classical results for independent
  and i.i.d. observations. Chapter 15 is an aside on sums of independent
  variables.
- **Its stance on limits.** "Limit theorems 'as n tends to infinity' are
  logically devoid of content about what happens at any particular n";
  the author urges reading the results as approximations.
