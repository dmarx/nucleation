# LIT-tmpogxg7: Eye-tracking and cognitive patterns in post-traumatic stress disorder: a scoping review

Diotaiuti, Spica, Di Siena, Cavicchiolo, De Souza, Andrade and Mancone
(2026), *European Journal of Psychotraumatology* 17(1): 2703319. DOI
10.1080/20008066.2026.2703319.

## Key takeaways

- Seventeen studies met the criteria, from 132 records in PubMed,
  PsycINFO and Scopus.
- "The comparatively most recurrent pattern was greater sustained
  allocation of gaze to negatively valenced, threatening, or
  trauma-related stimuli" (Discussion).
- Reduced exploration (Blekic et al. 2025; Roy et al. 2022) and delayed
  attention to context (Zidda et al. 2025) rest on one to three studies
  each, and the authors call reduced dwell and shorter scanpaths "not
  process-pure measures".
- No included study views stimuli after recall of the trauma, and the
  authors call gaze measures of change in treatment "hypothetical".

## Standing in the record

Read for [LEAD-106](../leads.d/LEAD-106.md) as a check on the search: if an eye-tracking study of
context sampling after cued recall in PTSD existed to 2025 and used the
standard terms, it should be in this review. None is. The review misses
at least two eligible studies the lead's search found (Milanak et al.
2018, [LIT-tmpgjodp](LIT-tmpgjodp.md); Kimble, Cappello and Fleming 2023, [LIT-tmpizs88](LIT-tmpizs88.md)), so
it is a check, not proof of absence.
