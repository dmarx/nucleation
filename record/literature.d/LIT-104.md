# LIT-104: Philosophy of Mathematics from Descartes to Kant

Emily Carson (2026), *Cambridge University Press, Elements in the Philosophy of Mathematics* — DOI-10.1017/9781108982405

## Key takeaways

- The early-modern debate over whether metaphysics can be demonstrated with geometric certainty turned on how Euclidean demonstration secures certainty, and the Element traces the answers from Descartes through Locke, Leibniz, Wolff, Lambert and Mendelssohn to Kant.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

In the 17th and 18th centuries, philosophers argued over whether metaphysical truths admit the kind of proof that geometrical truths do, and whether they can be known with the same certainty. Settling that comparison required an account of how Euclidean demonstration yields certainty. Carson examines how Descartes, Locke, Leibniz, Wolff, Lambert, Mendelssohn and Kant handled this question. The focus is on the metaphysical and epistemological status of geometrical demonstration in that period.
