summary: >-
  Victor R. Lesser and Lee D. Erman (1977), Proceedings of IJCAI-77. The
  Hearsay model attacks "errorful" knowledge-intensive problems with many
  separate knowledge sources (KSs) that interact only through a global,
  multi-level data structure, the blackboard. In Hearsay-II each level
  (segment, syllable, word, phrase) holds hypotheses with a uniform
  attribute-value structure: level, label, time span, AND/OR links to
  supporting hypotheses, validity ratings summarized as a rating state
  (Unrated, Neutral, Verified, Guaranteed, Rejected), and any attributes a
  KS chooses to add. KS preconditions declare which blackboard changes
  interest them and are triggered by them; a scheduler ranks pending KS
  activations. After two configurations (C1, January 1976; C2, September
  1976, 85% of sentences correct on a 1,000-word vocabulary, OCR'd as
  "857."), the authors judge: storing partial results on the blackboard
  avoids recomputation; uniform structure made KSs easy to write and replace
  (C2, almost entirely new, built in six months); the blackboard is a bad
  scratchpad for a KS's internal work; hypotheses' state is too local to
  show the state of the network they belong to, which hampers scheduling;
  and goals (requests for work) fit badly into the hypothesize-and-test
  format. Registered on 2026-11-12 as a stand-in for LIT-tmpwza8d; by two of
  the four authors, it restates the blackboard architecture, its knowledge
  sources and their control, with what experience taught, including
  credibility propagation tried and dropped.
