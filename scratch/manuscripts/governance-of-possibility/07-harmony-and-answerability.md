# Part D. Harmony, the governance of governance, and limits

Sections 12 and 13 take up two questions that the earlier parts leave open. §§3–11 say what a regulatory arrangement must maintain if an industrial ecosystem is to stay able to reorganize: routes to alternative configurations, reserves that keep those routes affordable, a diagnosis of which kind of failure is at hand. They do not say what the reorganization is for, beyond meeting a declared set of demands (§2), or who declares the demands and corrects the declarer. §12 asks what a regulator should conserve when the aim is described, as the owner's thesis describes it, as "harmonious governance" (CLAIM-tmpz67ew, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's, U16). §13 asks who governs the regulator.

Each step cites the entry that holds it, with its standing on 2026-11-23: Active (the record accepts it) or Proposed (filed and argued, not adopted); demonstrated (shown by construction or proof), empirical (observed) or interpretive (argued). Voices are marked. The owner's theses are named as the owner's; several of the oldest entries used here (CLAIM-075, CLAIM-086, CLAIM-099) come from the owner's essay *The Organization of Will* (work organization-of-will, drafted 2026-10-04 to 2026-10-06), and CLAIM-362 and CLAIM-523 are also the owner's. Entries in ChatGPT's wording (called A in the record) come from several exchanges, not one: §12's mostly from "Explain First Contact RL" (2026-11-22 to 23), §13's mostly from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (2026-11-15 to 17: CLAIM-727, CLAIM-740, CLAIM-746, TERM-126, QUESTION-482). CLAIM-462 and CLAIM-464 are A's, the assistant of the exchange of 2026-10-26. Entries the drafting assistant (Claude) put forward are named as the assistant's.

Where this part joins entries the record keeps apart, it says so and marks the join "(join JD*n*: …)"; the joins are collected in §15. Where the review of 2026-11-23 (REVIEW-2026-11-23.md, Part B) found a join already stated by an entry, the text cites that entry and the label is kept as a restatement; where an account declines a join, the text says so and does not state it as held. The joins no entry states are now filed in accounts of the record, under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict of the record's admission test (ADR-075, step 5): JC6, JD1, JD2 and JD4 in SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, and JD6 in SYNTHESIS-111 (version 3). The two sections are weaker than §§2–10 and should be read that way. Their negative steps (harmony is not coexistence; capacity to act is no evidence of capacity to be corrected) are Active. Most of their positive steps are Proposed, open, or shown only in small models of systems that are not regulators.

## 12. Harmony as conserved capability

### 12.1 What harmony is not

The motivating draft's §12 starts from a correct observation: an ecosystem of firms, suppliers, evaluators and authorities can persist together without anything worth calling harmony. A dominant firm and dependent suppliers can coexist in a stable arrangement that is exploitative. The record holds three results that make this exact, all Active.

- **Coexistence is not cooperation.** "Negative frequency dependence that keeps two types in coexistence does not make their interaction cooperative." In Hawk–Dove with cost above value the two types coexist at the interior equilibrium, "with Hawks still taking the whole resource from Doves", so "whether types persist together and whether they cooperate are separate questions, and a coexistence criterion answers only the first" (CLAIM-546, Active, demonstrated).
- **Relief is not reduced competition.** Niche differentiation matters to an organization "as possible relief of structural frustration among its constituents' persistence demands", a strict enlargement of what they can jointly realize, "and not as reduced competition". It follows that "the exclusion of a constituent or a redistribution of influence relieves no structural frustration of the original demands, and specialization can relieve frustration where nothing competes" (CLAIM-554, Active, interpretive). *Structural frustration* is §2's term: the demands that cannot be met together, read off the complex of jointly satisfiable demand sets (CLAIM-347, Active, interpretive).
- **Viability is not legitimacy.** "Viability does not establish what ought to persist" (CLAIM-075, Active, interpretive). Its source, the owner's essay, puts it bluntly: "An unjust institution may be highly viable." CLAIM-075 is filed in the role the record calls *disclaimed*: a boundary on what the record claims, not a premise from which anything positive follows.

Two further Active entries sharpen the third point for this paper. A demand's satisfaction condition "does not carry its warrant", so "the joint satisfiability of demands cannot certify that any deserves fulfilment, and an institution's maintained requirements cannot certify their own authority" (CLAIM-725, Active, interpretive, ChatGPT's). And cooperation within groups and the coexistence of groups "are distinct targets that can trade off": in one of Nowak's rules, cooperation spreads inside groups because other groups are driven extinct (CLAIM-547, Active, interpretive).

The motivating draft concludes that ecological organization must be evaluated "against independently justified public demands". The record supports the negative half of that sentence: neither viability nor joint satisfiability supplies the justification (CLAIM-075, CLAIM-725). It does not supply the positive half. Nothing on the record says where a public demand's justification comes from (§13.1; GD1, now the assistant's note of 2026-11-23 on QUESTION-485, Open).

### 12.2 The record's definition of harmony

The motivating draft never defines harmony. The record has a definition, filed from the same exchange: **harmonious integration** of a plurality S of declared constituents (perspectives, members, species, firms) over a horizon H is the conjunction of three conditions (TERM-tmppvlfi, Proposed, ChatGPT's wording, A24 §4):

- **Viability_H(S):** "the histories the organization can produce over H meet every constituent's persistence requirement at once", in CLAIM-554's form;
- **Accommodation_H(S):** the constituents' interactions "sustain compatible activity without requiring their differences to disappear";
- **Correctability_H(S):** "a constituent can still make a consequential intervention when the organization needs to change".

The definition says what it is not: not coexistence (CLAIM-546), not agreement, not relief of frustration, and not stability, since "A team might look well integrated because one dominant perspective has made the others quiet" (TERM-tmppvlfi). Such an organization is viable and accommodating and fails correctability.

This paper uses the definition, and the reader should know three things about it.

1. **It is Proposed, and fails its own condition for adoption.** The term says adoption "needs each conjunct operationalized for a declared plurality and horizon", accommodation "by a measure not reducible to either" of the others. Its note of 2026-11-23: "accommodation has no measure not reducible to viability or correctability, and no declared plurality and horizon has had the three conjuncts operationalized." Its source called the conditions "proposed conditions, not an established ecological theorem" (TERM-tmppvlfi). The missing measure is now a lead: operationalize the three conjuncts "for one declared plurality and horizon, with a measure of accommodation not reducible to the other two", checked by "a pair of configurations equal in viability and correctability that differ in the candidate accommodation measure" (GD2: LEAD-tmppp23a, Open, stage idea, the assistant's).
2. **Its correctability is a constituent's, not an arrangement's.** The term identifies its third conjunct with "continued access to consequential roles" (CLAIM-362, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's, as refined in the exchange of 2026-10-17), and its ecological counterpart with recovery when rare (QUESTION-132, Open). That is not the record's main sense of correctability, which is a property of an arrangement: "the chance that the arrangement, starting from the description of the problem it actually has, finds, authorizes, coordinates and carries out a plan achieving the objective before the deadline and within its resources" (TERM-085, Active). The two meet where a constituent's consequential intervention is the arrangement's route to correcting itself.
3. **Its correctability conjunct is what joins §12 to §13.** The motivating draft's §12 drops correctability and its §13 brings it back as "effective answerability". Read through TERM-tmppvlfi, the two sections are one condition seen from two sides. §12 asks whether the constituents of an industrial ecosystem can still intervene consequentially when it must change. §13 asks whether the regulator, as one participant in a wider institutional order, can still be intervened upon. No entry makes this join (join JD1: harmony's correctability conjunct read as the answerability of §13; TERM-tmppvlfi with TERM-085, CLAIM-362 and CLAIM-462). The account of correcting a regulator lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, an interpretive mapping": "It sets two definitions side by side and states no inference" (SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, Proposed). The account adds that the fit is partial: "TERM-tmppvlfi's conjunct needs no party to be owed an answer, while TERM-126's dimensions presuppose one" (ibid.). So the paper offers it as a reading that connects the two sections, not as a step. What it keeps is the distinction in point 2: a constituent's access to consequential roles is not the same quantity as an arrangement's chance of carrying out a correction, which the account finds is "TERM-tmppvlfi's own pairing with CLAIM-362 set against TERM-085's definition" (ibid.).

The definition also makes the owner's "harmonious governance" precise in one way. CLAIM-tmpz67ew's own gloss of the phrase uses joint satisfiability (CLAIM-347, CLAIM-554), coexistence not taken for cooperation (CLAIM-546) and viability not taken for legitimacy (CLAIM-075), and points to TERM-tmppvlfi as "The exchange's own definition of harmony". Using the definition brings in a Proposed term with an unmet condition. The paper does so with that cost stated.

### 12.3 Unity without singularity

The exchange that produced the definition also proposed what kind of unity harmony is, and it separated unity from singularity first. Its term for *singularity* distinguishes three senses that come apart, carrier, organizational and phenomenal, and defines organizational singularity as "one overarching organizational perspective within which every perspective that makes a difference is integrated" (TERM-tmpm0s7g, Proposed, ChatGPT's wording, A22). Unity is something else: "Unity is a property of the relations and dynamics that maintain coherent activity across a plurality of processes. Singularity is the stronger claim that this unity belongs to one encompassing perspective—or, in an entirely different sense, one subject of experience" (TERM-tmpm0s7g, quoting A22 §7).

Two claims build on that distinction. "A self-like organization need not be unified by convergence on a single perspective: it can be unified ecologically, by a historically maintained regime in which differentiated perspectives stay viable, mutually consequential and able to reorganize their relations" (CLAIM-tmp37le7, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's, A24). Its correspondence table pairs "Rare species can recover when conditions favor them" with "A marginalized perspective can regain influence when relevant", and its gloss says that "A healthy plurality is not merely one in which every constituent currently exists. It is one in which constituents retain the capacity to become consequential again." And "Harmonious integration and organizational singularity are independent: a correctable plurality with no overarching perspective is harmonious and not singular, and a unified organization that silences its internal differences is singular and not harmonious" (CLAIM-tmp31b3v, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's, A24 §6). The second claim is about entailment: "An argument that real organizations tend to have both or neither does not defeat it."

CLAIM-tmp37le7 was stated of selves and teams, and its note of 2026-11-23 says what holds it back: "no case in the record shows a self-like organization (integrated governance of its discrepancies, persistence through turnover) unified by harmonious integration without a single perspective". This paper carries the unity it describes, unity without organizational singularity, to an industrial ecosystem governed by a regulator: the ecosystem's unity, so far as it has one, would be the maintained regime in which differentiated producers, evaluators and authorities stay viable, mutually consequential and able to become consequential again, with no firm's or agency's perspective integrating the others. The regulator, on this reading, is one constituent among them and not the encompassing perspective (§13.5 takes the same view for other reasons). No entry states the carriage (join JD2: CLAIM-tmp37le7's ecological unity, which is unity without organizational singularity in TERM-tmpm0s7g's and CLAIM-tmp31b3v's sense, carried from a self or team to an industrial ecosystem). The account of correcting a regulator lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, a carry": CLAIM-tmp37le7's own note finds no case of a self-like organization unified this way, the record holds no industrial case, and since the carriage adds nothing to §12.2's correctability conjunct but a name, "That makes it a recap of TERM-tmppvlfi as well" (SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, Proposed). The paragraph above is therefore an illustration, not a step.

Three things JD2 does not say. It does not say that an industrial ecosystem is a self or a *pseudo-self*, the owner's word for a multi-perspectival organization "causally integrated relative to a declared probe family and history, reproducing some of the integration, persistence and adaptive governance associated with selves" (TERM-tmpxwa7g, Proposed); CLAIM-tmp37le7 itself does not say "That a team or an ecosystem is a self". The record's account of the exchange declines that reading: "'A polity as an ecological pseudo-self' (A31). No component states that an industrial ecosystem satisfies TERM-tmpxwa7g's integration condition. Left as an analogy" (SYNTHESIS-tmp4zamw, Proposed). The account's note of 2026-11-23 sets the scope of that decline: it "tests the link against the pseudo-self term only", and a link "carrying that ecological unity to an industrial ecosystem, the manuscript's join JD2, needs no integration into one overarching perspective, so the decline does not reach it". Second, JD2 does not say that the regulation thread and the self thesis are one framework, which the same account also declines ("They share vocabulary: compatibility complexes, harmony, pseudo-selves. No entry derives one from the other"). Third, it is not held: the same note says JD2 "is not stated on the record either: no component states that an industrial ecosystem meets CLAIM-tmp37le7's conditions, and the record holds no industrial case." JD2 adds nothing that §12.2's correctability conjunct does not, except a name for the kind of unity that conjunct yields: unity kept by the relations among constituents, not by one of them. It is now mapped, as not stated and a carry, in SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i.

### 12.4 Conserve a capability, not its occupant

The motivating draft's positive proposal for §12 is that a regulator protecting an ecosystem's differentiated capabilities should keep "a capability and its conditions of reproduction, not a particular organizational occupant". The record now holds it, filed from the draft itself: "What regulation should conserve in an industrial ecosystem is often a capability and the conditions of its reproduction, its infrastructure, expertise and access, and not the organization that now performs it"; "Keeping the occupant is one way to keep the capability, and not the only one" (CLAIM-tmpyakra, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). It rests on CLAIM-556 (Active, interpretive), whose last diagnostic level is "a failure of regeneration, the capability not surviving turnover, repaired by maintenance or reproduction" (§10), and on CLAIM-075 for why viability alone does not choose what to conserve.

The claim states something the record did not hold before, and it is useful: it separates protecting a function from protecting an incumbent, which an assessment of present performance cannot tell apart. But it has an assumption it does not state. To conserve a capability *apart from* the firm that holds it, one must be able to say which capability it is without naming the firm. The record's open question on the same contrast says this cannot yet be done.

- **The question.** "Can a regulation work in an industrial ecosystem as a stabilizing mechanism does in coexistence theory, so that a capability reduced to rarity recovers when demand for it returns without continuing support, and what separates that from regulation that holds visible diversity in place or that protects incumbent firms?" (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a, Open, ChatGPT's wording, A33). A *stabilizing* mechanism, in Chesson's sense, makes each type limit itself more than it limits others, so a type reduced to rarity can increase again; an *equalizing* one only narrows differences in average fitness (QUESTION-371).
- **What it says is unsettled.** "The bearer of recovery. Is it a firm, a capability, or a pathway for producing an essential good? The answer changes what a rare-invasion test measures. A33 says capabilities, and the record has no account of how a capability is individuated apart from the firms that hold it." It also asks how stabilization by changed dynamics is told apart from "diversity kept by continuing external support", and warns that "Stable coexistence can keep an exploitative relation (CLAIM-546), so recovery when rare is not by itself harmony" (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a).

So CLAIM-tmpyakra presupposes the individuation that QUESTION-tmpn9t5a says is missing. Its own defeater, "A case where the function cannot be reproduced apart from its present occupant", is the question's first unsettled item seen from the other side. That presupposition is now its own question: "How is a capability identified apart from the organizations that now hold it, so that conserving the capability can be told from conserving its occupant?" (GD3: QUESTION-tmpw2rz2, Open, the assistant's). It records a candidate criterion, "individuate a capability by the demand it meets and the routes that realize it, carrying CLAIM-916's step 4 to an ecosystem", and two difficulties: "a route may be held only by one firm, as tacit knowledge is, which collapses the capability into its holder; and the demand individuates the function only if the specification is given, which is QUESTION-485's problem." The model that would decide it is stated as a lead: heterogeneous producers, a shared infrastructure bottleneck, an externality and declared public demands, comparing a uniform conduct constraint with a regime that also changes infrastructure access and "measuring the compatibility complex, realized violations and the recovery of rare capabilities" (LEAD-tmpy0jkr, Open, stage idea). It needs, in its own words, "a declared bearer of recovery, a firm or a capability". The lead is not built.

The tacit-knowledge difficulty has an industrial reading on the record. Cohen and Levinthal argue that a firm's ability to use outside knowledge "rests on its prior related knowledge, built largely as a byproduct of its own R&D and not quickly bought", and that "a firm that stops investing in a fast-moving field may be locked out of it even after major advances" (THEORY-1518, Active as a report of the authors' account; the thesis itself is THEORY-1543, Proposed). The report's own caveat is that "It does not show that any firm that stopped R&D was locked out, or how long recovery takes." A panel of OECD manufacturing industries finds that R&D "raises the rate at which frontier technology is absorbed as well as the rate of own innovation" (THEORY-1523, Active). Where a capability is of this kind, built by the holder's own work, separating it from its holder is hardest.

Reading CLAIM-tmpyakra beside QUESTION-tmpn9t5a gives it a test: conserving a capability, rather than its occupant, would mean that the capability has positive growth when rare once demand for it returns and support is withdrawn, whoever comes to hold it, and a regime that keeps the incumbent alive by continuing subsidy would fail that test even if the capability survives. This reading is not new. The question's note of 2026-11-23 states it: the motivating draft "restates this question's contrast of mechanism against compensation, and answers part of it: conserve a capability and its conditions of reproduction, not its occupant (CLAIM-tmpyakra)" (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a). JD3 is therefore a restatement of QUESTION-tmpn9t5a and its note, with CLAIM-556's regeneration level, not a join. This paper adds only that the answer is conditional on the individuation QUESTION-tmpw2rz2 asks for.

The record holds several results on what "without continuing support" requires, none cited by the motivating draft.

- **When support can be withdrawn.** "Transient support can be withdrawn safely only if it started a process that keeps itself going on a flux that will stay available", carried the party past any threshold, outlasted the threat it stood in for, and left the work to the party; "inputs the party cannot produce must stay supplied as a standing part of the arrangement", and "persistence after withdrawal is needed to tell which, though slow reversion can mimic it" (CLAIM-600, Active, interpretive, the assistant's). Two demonstrated results stand under it. Where capacity is learned by doing the work and decays with disuse, "what decides dependence is the order in which supply and capacity are used, not the amount of supply" (CLAIM-602, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's). And where capacity is needed to grow capacity, "support that does the work has a deadline after which withdrawal no longer restores capacity, and a slow taper of such support is worse than an abrupt cut" (CLAIM-605, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's). The line's account integrates these, with the claim on dependence below (SYNTHESIS-032, Proposed). For a regulator, a subsidy that keeps a rare capability's holder operating by doing its work would, on these results, lower the capability it keeps and could pass the point after which withdrawing the subsidy no longer lets it recover.
- **Readings from aid and ecology.** "Predictable aid weakens the recipient's own upkeep, as in the Samaritan's dilemma" (THEORY-1480, Active as a report of Ostrom and colleagues' account); handed-over aid activities continued "where the receiving programme had dedicated resources, functioning infrastructure and a legal commitment and the project's linkage to it was operating before exit" (THEORY-1496, Active, an executive summary with no comparison group); and restoring a degraded ecosystem's pressure-free conditions "can fail because the degraded phase leaves a reinforcing legacy of its own" (THEORY-1468, Active as a report; the thesis is THEORY-1542, Proposed). The last bears on "when demand for it returns": the return of the conditions a capability needs need not bring the capability back.
- **Interruption is not extinction.** "The cessation of an organizational perspective's enactment need not imply the loss of the dispositions, capacities or reproductive relationships from which it can be reinstated", and "temporary unavailability is not extinction, though prolonged interruption of reproduction can end a tradition" (CLAIM-421, Active, interpretive). A capability that no firm now exercises may still be held where it can be reinstated, which a regulator's census of active producers would miss.
- **Complements.** "Where successful response requires several complementary capacities, the value of preserving one depends on whether the others remain deployable" (CLAIM-587, Active, demonstrated, ChatGPT's); a constructed case has two capacities each improving while the chance that both hold falls (CASE-237, Proposed). Conserving one capability apart from its complements may conserve nothing usable.

Two results on recovery when rare bear on the occupant question directly. In a network model of a core and a periphery, the core "is a regime the dynamics regenerate, not an asymmetry entrenched in its first holders": deleted, it is refilled from the periphery, and "What recovers is a role filled by interchangeable units, not a specialist with a niche" (CLAIM-875, Proposed, empirical, the assistant's). And in a population of identical operators, "with no spare operator a rare specialist made rare recovers", while "with two spare operators a removed specialist is replaced and a suppressed one is shut out, whether or not it kept its competence" (CASE-341, Proposed). Both separate the recovery of a function from the recovery of its holder, in models that are not industries: where substitutes stand ready, the function survives the loss of its holder, and the holder, once suppressed, may not come back.

Two Proposed entries bear on the dependent-supplier case of the motivating draft. A capability lost because another reliably supplies it is "dependency or division of labour" according to the supply, not the loss: one-way supply set from outside leaves a dependence "that withdrawal punishes", reciprocal loss leaves interdependence (CLAIM-603, Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's). That is a criterion for which losses of capability a regulator should treat as harm, on the condition that the capability can be named. And where an outside party maintains a capability, the record holds two readings of whether the maintainer is part of what persists: "a maintainer that restores an individual from the organization it finds in it is a channel of transmission, never part of what is transmitted" (CLAIM-983, Proposed, interpretive), or "an outside maintainer's organization is part of what is transmitted just insofar as it holds organization the repertoire depends on that the individual does not carry and that a competent unrelated replacement, working from the individual, would not supply" (CLAIM-986, Proposed, interpretive). They are alternatives, and the choice between them is the owner's. A regulator that supplies or maintains a capability on an industry's behalf is, on the second reading, part of the capability, which bears on what withdrawing it does.

### 12.5 Norms that stabilize

The motivating draft cites QUESTION-371 for the idea that regulation can stabilize coexistence "by altering the dynamics through which competing groups persist". QUESTION-371 (Open) asks something narrower: whether "a moral norm" can work "among cooperating groups as a stabilizing mechanism does in coexistence theory, limiting a dominant group more than it limits its rivals so that a group reduced to rarity can increase again". It distinguishes an equalizing norm ("a cap, a transfer"), a stabilizing one ("limits on dominance, protections for minorities, rules against exclusion") and a loyalty norm, which is neither. Its note of 2026-11-22 says the industrial counterpart, "does a rule that alters access to essential infrastructure stabilize, where subsidies to entrants only equalize?", is a sibling filed as QUESTION-tmpn9t5a, and "does not answer it". QUESTION-371 is also explicit about what it does not ask: "Not whether such norms are justified" (citing CLAIM-075).

The paper therefore cites QUESTION-371 as the moral-norm form of §12.4's question, not as support. The motivating draft's sharpest sentence here, "Compensating participants for permanent exclusion is not equivalent to removing the mechanisms that make exclusion unavoidable", is the equalizing–stabilizing distinction of both questions, and both are open. What the record holds Active is the negative part: excluding a constituent relieves no frustration of the original demands (CLAIM-554). CLAIM-875 and CASE-341 (§12.4) are the record's nearest models of a stabilizing mechanism at work, and neither is a norm or a regulation.

### 12.6 Two homonyms: self-regulation and biological regulation are not public regulation

One entry near this section uses the word "regulatory" in another sense. CLAIM-916 (Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's) gives a rule for the constraint set of "A regulatory capacity": the set holds "each constraint the capacity's own constraints depend on for their production", so that "once the agent and the capacity are named only the agent's boundary is left to choose". Its "regulatory capacity" is an agent's capacity to regulate itself, the object of viability theory's constraint set (THEORY-134, Active), not a public regulator's authority over an industry. CLAIM-tmpyakra's defeater names CLAIM-916 as an entry that might already state it. On this paper's reading it does not. Its fourth step, a union over "substitutable routes", gives one capacity several realizers, which is close to a capability with several possible occupants, but it presupposes the capacity named ("C is fixed by the claim under test") and so presupposes the individuation that §12.4 lacks; QUESTION-tmpw2rz2 takes up that step as a candidate criterion. Its open choice of boundary is filed as two readings, narrow (CLAIM-917, Proposed) and wide, in which "a capacity supplied from outside is held by the agent and is precarious with its supply" (CLAIM-915, Proposed). The wide reading is the formal shape of the dependent supplier's position, and this paper only notes the parallel.

A reading uses the word in a third sense. Bich and colleagues "define biological regulation as second-order control by a subsystem the organism produces but which runs at its own rate and stoichiometry, is triggered by perturbations and switches the constitutive regime between viable states" (THEORY-2221, Proposed). It is not a public regulator either, but its structure, a controller produced by the system it controls, running at its own rate and switching the system between viable regimes, is close to §§7 and 13's picture of a regulator inside the order it governs. The paper notes it as a parallel and rests nothing on it.

### 12.7 What §12 establishes

- **Active.** Coexistence is not harmony, exclusion is not relief, and viability does not fix what ought to persist (CLAIM-546, CLAIM-554, CLAIM-075). Joint satisfiability does not certify that a demand deserves to be met (CLAIM-725). Support that does a party's work can lower what it keeps and has a deadline after which withdrawal does not restore it (CLAIM-602, CLAIM-605), and safe withdrawal has stated conditions (CLAIM-600).
- **Proposed.** What harmony is (TERM-tmppvlfi, with an unmet condition), what kind of unity it yields (CLAIM-tmp37le7, with TERM-tmpm0s7g and CLAIM-tmp31b3v), and what to conserve (CLAIM-tmpyakra, which presupposes the missing individuation).
- **Open.** Whether regulation can stabilize a rare capability's recovery rather than equalize or hold it in place (QUESTION-tmpn9t5a, QUESTION-371), how a capability is identified apart from its holders (QUESTION-tmpw2rz2), how accommodation is measured (LEAD-tmppp23a), and where public demands get their justification (§13.1).

The positive target the motivating draft proposes, "the recoverability of differentiated capabilities", is therefore a well-posed research target, not a result.

## 13. The governance of governance

### 13.1 Who specifies the demands

The motivating draft asks: "Who specifies the demands? Who determines which capacities are worth maintaining? Who decides whether a new form of industrial organization constitutes legitimate adaptation or unacceptable deviation?" It answers that "These questions cannot be answered by control theory alone." That matches the record exactly, and it should be read as the statement of an open question, not a result. The record holds six open questions on it:

- "From where does a relationship, a capability or a restriction derive its legitimacy, and from where its adequacy?" (QUESTION-485, Open, the owner's, U31 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse").
- "When is answerability required of an authority or governing capability: under what conditions is justification owed, to whom, and why?" (QUESTION-480, Open, the owner's, U36 of the same exchange).
- "Under what conditions is the allocation of authority to evaluate and constrain an actor sufficiently independent of that actor's capacity to influence the allocation?" (QUESTION-482, Open, ChatGPT's wording).
- "Who has standing to authorize changes to an arrangement's governing structure, by which procedures, and which protections lie beyond any ordinary delegation or revision of it?" (QUESTION-481, Open, ChatGPT's wording, from the same exchange). For a regulator, this is who may rewrite the demand specification, and what no rewriting may touch.
- Whether "relationally indexed normative judgments" can "be objective about the standards and perspectives themselves" (QUESTION-129, Open). CLAIM-746 quotes the exchange it came from: "I don't think compatibility and reachability can derive the moral authority of a norm from nothing."
- When an agent's own processes revise its will-organization, "what makes the result the continuation of that agent's will rather than a replacement authored by something else" (QUESTION-364, Open). For a regulator that rewrites its own mandate, this is the question whether the result is still the institution that was authorized.

The owner's thesis says the same of itself: "It does not say who sets the demand specification, or what makes it legitimate" (CLAIM-tmpz67ew, Proposed, the owner's). The assistant's note of 2026-11-23 on QUESTION-485 now records the gap as that question applied to the specification: "A demand specification is a restriction in this question's sense: it fixes which histories of an industrial ecosystem count as acceptable. Who legitimately sets it, and from where it derives its legitimacy, is this question applied to the specification" (GD1: the note on QUESTION-485, Open; the note is the assistant's). The pieces the record does hold are negative: viability does not settle what ought to persist (CLAIM-075), a demand's satisfaction condition does not carry its warrant (CLAIM-725), and a standing distinction among four judgments of an authority, "the warrant of the obligations it is to serve, its jurisdiction over the matter, its arrangement's capacity to realize those obligations together, and its capacity to reach compliant arrangements and correct failures by permitted means", of which "neither of the first two follows from any of the others" (CLAIM-746, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's as corrected by the filer).

The readings the record holds on institutional levels give the question a structure, though not an answer. Schlager and Ostrom separate operational rights from collective-choice rights, "the latter being authority to define future operational rights", with "a constitutional level" that "devises the collective-choice rules" (THEORY-1957, Proposed); the IAD framework distinguishes "nested operational, collective-choice and constitutional levels, in which collective-choice rules fix who may change operating rules and how" (THEORY-1809, Proposed); and durable self-governance of shared resources is marked by "user-made and user-monitored rules" and "nested layers of governance" (THEORY-149, Active as a report of Ostrom's account; "The principles are correlates of survival, not tested causes"). On this structure, a demand specification is a collective-choice rule and its setter holds a collective-choice right; who may hold it is the constitutional question QUESTION-481 asks. A contract reading bears on the specification's gaps: "In an incomplete contract the party holding residual rights of control decides the contingencies the contract did not cover", so a relationship "fair in every specified term" can leave one party's terms at the other's discretion, "exercised, and seen, only when an uncovered contingency arises" (CLAIM-728, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). A harm the specification does not name is such a contingency.

The analysis beside this draft adds one thing to the question of who specifies, within its model (§9 describes the model). When a harm arises that the specification does not name, an arrangement that never enters it into its specification "scores zero violations on D0 and on its own specification, while the harm runs for all 120 periods", and "Only the true demand set ranks the two correctly" (analysis/README.md, item 7). That is CLAIM-361's point that excising a demand "has changed the comparison and has not been satisfied" (CLAIM-361, Active, interpretive), measured: an arrangement that scores itself against the specification it controls cannot see what it has excised. The record already holds the mechanism nearby, uncited by the motivating draft. "For a fixed arrangement, expanding the recognized demand set cannot improve conjunctive compliance and cannot decrease the count of recognized violations; where governance penalizes newly recognized violations without rewarding their discovery, an organization may acquire an incentive not to improve its identification of its obligations" (CLAIM-662, Active, demonstrated, the owner's and ChatGPT's wording). Its simulation found that an institution "penalized for every recognized violation and unrewarded for discovery" learns "to make no inquiry in every setting while its internal record shows almost no violations, against half its budget spent on inquiry when an independent evaluator scores it" (CASE-tmp8mk6w, Active, filed and promoted on 2026-11-23). The analysis's arm differs from both in that the choice to excise is an input, not learned. Reading it, with CLAIM-662, as an instance of CLAIM-727's last clause, below, that where authority is to be constrained by independently justified claims, "its own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has", was the first draft's step (join JD4: the model's excision result read as CLAIM-727's self-certification limit, through CLAIM-361). The account of correcting a regulator lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails as a claim, an instance": CLAIM-727's clause is a possibility ("cannot always establish"), and a model in which an arrangement scores itself clean on the specification it controls while the harm runs "is an instance of it, not a further sentence". Its content belongs on the record as a CASE that illustrates CLAIM-727 and CLAIM-361, as CASE-tmp8mk6w does for hidden requirements, and which reference set scores the unnamed demand stays QUESTION-tmphovmg (SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, Proposed). So the paper offers the arm as an instance of CLAIM-727, not as a further claim. The same run shows that under the peak budget rule a review that needs capacity "can never start, so the demand is excised by default, not by choice". The result is not yet on the record: GD7 stays open, and the study and this arm are to be filed as CASEs, in a later pull request.

### 13.2 Capacity to act is no evidence of capacity to be corrected

The motivating draft rests its §13 on one entry, correctly cited: "An arrangement of institutions can keep, or increase, its capacity for effective action while its capacity to be effectively corrected by others degrades, because the two are maintained by different relations, so the first is no evidence of the second" (CLAIM-462, Active, interpretive, A's candidate from the exchange of 2026-10-26). Its source separated four capacities that "are not different degrees of one quantity": effective action, restraint, correction and reconstruction. The relations that hold the capacity to be corrected are ones the arrangement's action does not need: "a court's orders reaching an enforcer who will act on them, information reaching a reviewer". "The institutions are formally continuous. The organization through which they exercised mutual constraint is not necessarily continuous." The entry's cases (CASE-176, CASE-175 and CASE-170, all Active) are real institutional episodes, and it says of them: "The cases show the pattern, not its frequency."

Four Active entries give the mechanism.

- **Within one organization.** "Maintaining a regime of effective action and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes are not the same achievement" (CLAIM-099, Active, interpretive, the main result of the owner's essay), and "A successful corrective act, an executable procedure and a maintained organizational capacity are three different things" (CLAIM-422, Active, interpretive). CLAIM-462 rests on both.
- **Action that moves its own correction.** "An intervention can change what it would take to correct it: by altering the distribution of control, access, resources or enforcement before it is reviewed, it can make a specified corrective outcome costlier or unreachable while the standards that govern that outcome stay the same" (CLAIM-464, Active, interpretive). In §§6–9's language, an intervention can move the corrective configuration out of reach at the reviewer's budget without any rule changing.
- **Authority over authority.** "A constraint may govern conduct without governing the conditions under which it remains authoritative" (CLAIM-086, Active, interpretive, from the owner's essay).
- **An arrangement that erodes its own governors.** "Where the capacities a collective needs to govern an arrangement are learned by exercise and lost with disuse, and the arrangement does the work through which they are exercised, the arrangement can erode the capacity to govern it, so that whether a collective can correct an arrangement is not independent of the arrangement" (CLAIM-673, Active, interpretive). It applies CLAIM-605's deadline (§12.4) to governing capacities. For a regulator that takes over the work through which an industry's own oversight or an affected party's expertise is exercised, it says the regulator can erode the capacity that would correct it.

Two Proposed constructions separate the mechanisms. In one, two organizations "with the same rules, appeal rights, decision rate and initial corrective capacity" differ only in whether their decisions use up corrective capacity, and with the rule revised after T days "the first can still correct its accumulated errors until T = 100, the second only until T = 20"; it separates throughput, specification lag and "endogenous foreclosure (the decisions themselves reducing capacity)" (CASE-280, Proposed, stipulated). In the other, two arrangements differ "only in whether the executive's signal gates the execution of corrective orders", and the observable traces part "only on an order against the executive that it withholds, and first at execution" (CASE-380, Proposed). The second shows why the first-order record of a regulator's decisions need not reveal whether its correction is gated.

For a regulator these say that the motivating draft's worry, "Its revision procedures can become ineffective even while formal rules remain unchanged", has an Active form, and that a regulator's demonstrated ability to change its own rules is no evidence that others can correct it. The second-order form, that an institution can be "reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons for revising that mechanism", is Proposed (CLAIM-719, Proposed, interpretive). The record's term for the capacity it describes is *normative governability*: "its maintained, role-appropriate capacity to recognize and respond to justified normative considerations, including considerations that require revising its own governing mechanisms" (TERM-128, Proposed, ChatGPT's wording). The record has no case of second-order unresponsiveness. The one candidate examined, CASE-289, turned out on reading the court's opinion to be a first-order failure (SYNTHESIS-104, SYNTHESIS-038, both Proposed accounts); the mechanism the record files for it is that "A categorical administrative gate can make evidence that a governing standard requires to count unable to change a decision" (CLAIM-745, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's, on the owner's example). The assistant's note of 2026-11-23 on CLAIM-719 records the absence: "No case on the record shows an institution responsive at the first order and unresponsive at the second", and the manuscript's model "offers only an analogue: under the peak budget rule a review that needs capacity never starts, so a new demand is excised by default, which is a failure to revise for want of means, not of responsiveness to reasons" (GD6: the note on CLAIM-719, Proposed; the note is the assistant's).

### 13.3 The structure of challenge

The motivating draft says the governing arrangement "must also preserve appropriate pathways through which affected parties and independent institutions can challenge its decisions". Two Active entries say what such a pathway is made of.

- **Control of the challenge needs its own warrant.** "Authority over a decision does not carry authority over the conditions on which it may be challenged: control of the cost, timing, evidence, forum or remedy of a challenge needs its own justification, and where an actor's authority is meant to be constrained by independently justified claims, its own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has" (CLAIM-727, Active, interpretive, ChatGPT's).
- **Three entitlements, each failing without the next.** For a party affected by a decision, explanation, contestation and interruption "are separate entitlements, and each without the next fails in its own way: explanation without contestation is transparency without accountability, contestation without interruption a right that arrives too late, and interruption without reasons an arbitrary veto" (CLAIM-740, Active, interpretive, ChatGPT's).

CLAIM-740's middle clause is the timing point of §§6–7 stated for challenge: a contest that cannot suspend execution arrives after the intervention has moved the corrective outcome out of reach (CLAIM-464). The record files the Proposed extension of this, that "A protected objection is effective only if six achievements hold together" (CLAIM-732, Proposed, interpretive), and the term for the maintained capability, *protected contestability*, "robust to a declared class of opposition; not a guarantee of success, a veto or costless adjudication" (TERM-130, Proposed).

Four Proposed entries say how a challenge pathway is weakened without any formal change. "The cost of imposing a decision and the cost of questioning, suspending or reversing it are separate", so an institution can lower the first while the second stays or rises, and "friction moves from the exercise of power to its contestation" (CLAIM-731, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). "An organization can be disposed to answer a credible challenge by reducing the capacity for future challenge, classifying the challenge as the problem and restricting the challenger, so that the later absence of challenges reads as success" (CLAIM-619, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). An opposition can be "independent as an actor" while "its capacity to change the arrangement it opposes depends on another party's control of its resources, standing, corrective authority or time", and "that controlled incapacity, and not incapacity or failure as such, is what makes a contained opposition a mark of domination" (CLAIM-736, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). And the readings on voice: exit and voice are both "recuperation mechanisms", and an organization's managers "may favour whichever response least threatens their hold on power" (THEORY-152, Proposed; Hirschman); voice divides into "affective voice, which seeks to persuade those in power, and effective voice, which binds through transparent processes" (THEORY-1918, Proposed; Frey and Schneider). A regulator's challenge pathway is the effective kind only if a challenge can bind it.

### 13.4 Effective answerability: which sense

The motivating draft concludes that the response architecture "must include not only industrial control and institutional revision, but effective answerability", and contrasts a "technically adaptable regulatory institution" that is "politically unanswerable" with a "formally contestable institution" that cannot "implement corrections within meaningful deadlines". The record uses "answerability" in two senses and warns against reading them as one.

- **The normative relation, by dimension.** *Answerability* is "the normative relation in which the actor owes the party some form of answer, an account, reasons, responsiveness, an effective opportunity to contest, or judgment and redress", and it has four dimensions: normative ("Does the actor owe reasons, consideration, or correction to this party?"), institutional, effective ("Can the party's protected claims actually become consequential under foreseeable conditions?") and maintained. "These dimensions come apart. A defendant can owe a claimant a remedy even when the claimant cannot afford to pursue it." The bare term is the normative dimension; "the others are said with their qualifier" (TERM-126, Proposed, ChatGPT's wording). It is directed: "the party owed, the recipient of reasons, the forum that judges and the party that remedies can differ" (CLAIM-739, Proposed, interpretive). And it is bounded: "Influence over others does not by itself ground a duty to answer to them", since answerability "is owed where the justified structure of a relationship requires it, not wherever an action has effects" (CLAIM-749, Active, interpretive, a counter, ChatGPT's). A regulator's influence on an industry does not settle to whom it owes an answer.
- **Reachability plus correctability.** CLAIM-746's fourth judgment, which its source labelled "dynamic answerability", is an authority's "capacity to reach compliant arrangements and correct failures by permitted means". CLAIM-746 warns: "'Answerability' in A74's label means reachability plus correctability, not the directed normative relation of TERM-126 and CLAIM-739. The record should not read the two as one." TERM-126 says the same from its side: A74's sense is "the effective and maintained dimensions without the normative one".

**This paper means TERM-126's effective and maintained answerability**: that the claims of parties to whom the regulator owes an answer can actually become consequential, and stay so, under foreseeable conditions. It presupposes the normative dimension (who is owed what), which §13.1 leaves open. The record relates it to domination: a party is dominated, relative to "a declared standard of answerability", by "an agent's or role's capacity, used or not, to set or change the terms on which the party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate, where the capacity is not answerable to the party" (TERM-124, Proposed, the assistant's). The motivating draft's other half, a regulator able to "implement corrections within meaningful deadlines", is correctability in TERM-085's sense, or CLAIM-746's dynamic judgment, and this paper calls it correctability, not answerability. The draft's two failures are then the two coming apart: correctable and not answerable, answerable and not correctable.

Keeping the two apart as separate requirements is not this paper's step. TERM-126 and CLAIM-746 already separate them, and CLAIM-523's note of 2026-11-23 records that the motivating draft states its achievements, "including a formally contestable institution that cannot correct in time, without citing it". The record also holds the separating case for "answerable and not correctable": "When an institution produces challengeable decisions faster than its corrective process can resolve them, the formal right to challenge stays with everyone while its preventive effect cannot: if valid challenges arrive at rate λ and are resolved at rate μ < λ, at most a share μ/λ can ever be resolved in time, whatever the order of review" (CLAIM-705, Proposed, demonstrated, ChatGPT's wording, the derivation the filer's). Its companion says which orders reach the bound: "under insufficient review capacity, scheduling distributes preventive rights" (CLAIM-704, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's). Against an opponent who can act on the means of correction, correctability becomes "a robust quantity, the chance that a corrective policy reaches its objective against every strategy in a declared class of opposition" (CLAIM-724, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). That the two come apart is therefore a restatement of TERM-126, CLAIM-746 and CLAIM-705 (JD5). That a regulator is *required* to have both is not stated, and the account of correcting a regulator lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails": the requirement "adds a normative premise no component states: who is owed an answer", which §13.1 leaves open (GD1) and the record holds open in QUESTION-480 (SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, Proposed). The paper states the two as separate, and the requirement only as conditional on that premise. It also connects §12: the correctability conjunct of harmony (TERM-tmppvlfi) is closer to effective answerability, a constituent's claims becoming consequential, than to TERM-085's correctability, a reading the same account finds a partial fit (JD1).

### 13.5 Three achievements, and a regulator's own failures

The paper's §10 diagnoses why an ecosystem's response fails. The same diagnosis applies to the regulator. For a normatively relevant discrepancy to change what an organization does, "three distinct achievements are needed and any can fail while the others hold: legibility, by which the discrepancy becomes recognizable; volitional efficacy, by which a corresponding demand can move action; and correctability, by which a fitting revision can be carried out within deadline and resources; none establishes that the demand or the revision is justified" (CLAIM-523, Active, interpretive, the owner's synthesis). The entry adds two cautions that bear on §13.1: "A nonempty intersection does not show the agent will choose such a history", and "An empty intersection does not show the agent is beyond criticism", since its earlier actions may have destroyed the capacities required. It does not say "Who has the authority to decide that a change is warranted", which it hands to QUESTION-364.

A regulator can therefore fail to be corrected in three ways that need different remedies: criticism of it never becomes legible to it, becomes legible and is given no authority, or is given authority and cannot be executed in time. The record states the consequence for those who depend on a controller: "Where a dependent party's claims on an arrangement's controllers fail of legibility, volitional efficacy or correctability in the controlling organization, the dependent lacks effective means of influence and challenge, and where they fail so at every party able to constrain the controllers it lacks effective protection" (CLAIM-686, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's). The motivating draft names all three failures ("Its standards can be incomplete. Its indicators can become detached from their underlying purposes. Its authority can be captured.") without separating them. The record also holds that capture can work through the evaluative framework a regulator adopts rather than through its decisions (THEORY-859, Proposed), and that an organization "can be governed from outside without any of its decisions being made for it, through the evidence its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh evidence" (CLAIM-404, Active, interpretive, on the owner's cases). The second is the record's account of the motivating draft's point that "The regulator is not an omniscient observer standing outside the ecosystem". It sits uneasily with the owner's thesis, whose gloss reads the ecosystem as governed "from outside by a regulator who is not one of the constituents whose demands are specified" (CLAIM-tmpz67ew). This paper follows the motivating draft and CLAIM-404: the regulator is a participant, and its evidence and criteria are channels through which it is governed.

### 13.6 The owner's correction-coupling principle

Since the motivating draft was written, the owner has stated a principle that gives §13 a mechanism where the draft has an aspiration ("institutions capable of being changed by evidence, legitimate challenge, and changing public demands"):

> "A system's ability to correct itself depends not simply on having critics or feedback, but on how evidence, evaluation, and authority are coupled—and what the system is prevented from rewriting or rejecting." (CLAIM-tmp4h9rc, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's, 2026-11-23.)

The owner gave four constructions as its instances. Three are Active and demonstrated, each in a model, and one is Proposed. The three Active ones were filed by the assistant on 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-23 and promoted on 2026-11-23 (§14.7).

- **Protected evidence.** "In a simulated sensor network whose correspondences drift, revision improves prediction on positions the network did not choose only when it answers to beacons whose position and channel the revision can neither rewrite nor refuse; revision by the sensors' disagreement alone restores consistency and not accuracy" (CLAIM-tmpvqmwc, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's; CASE-tmp33vf4, Active).
- **Scoring apart from agreement.** In a population on the coupled meaning–influence model, "grants indexed by role with the critic role scored by observed relevance leave neither belief factions nor capture, while scalar grants fail one way or the other"; "indexing by role without a separate score buys nothing" (CLAIM-tmp5wozo, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's).
- **An identifiability limit.** "Where no process holds evidence on a block of tasks that does not come from the proposals themselves, a governance rule that treats its members alike cannot tell honest dissent from an attack that pushes the same block as hard" (CLAIM-tmpxoxys, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's).
- **Capture through a return channel.** The loop between an evaluator and the party it evaluates "can capture the evaluator's reading" even when "the evaluator's conferral reads no interpretation at all"; "informational independence does not prevent it while a resource channel runs, and what does is a return channel too weak to close the loop" (CLAIM-tmpz8q70, Proposed, demonstrated).

The assistant's reading, in the entry, is that the evidence half is already held, "What keeps a judgement–observation loop from confirming itself is evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests" (CLAIM-342, Active, interpretive), and that what the principle adds is two further conditions: "authority's coupling" (the return channel; grants indexed by role and scored apart from agreement) and "protection" (what the revision can neither rewrite nor refuse). The principle's negative half, that critics or feedback do not suffice, follows from the four components, each of which exhibits them with correction failing. Its positive half is a prediction that has not been tested: "The claim, read strictly, predicts separate requirements. In one model that hosts all four, removing any one alone should defeat correction while the others hold." "If one condition is found to entail the others, the claim reduces to that one" (CLAIM-tmp4h9rc). The model is proposed as a lead: one population with four switches (evidence from outside the proposals, its protection from revision and refusal, a return channel with a strength, scoring by relevance or by agreement), scored "on ground truth the population never sees" (LEAD-tmpdzbyn, Open, stage idea, the assistant's). A finding from another domain has the same shape as the principle's "not simply on having critics": "Where an intervention acts in a system's constraint structure can predict whether it achieves a target better than how much intervention there is, and adding an intervention to a successful set can make it fail" (CLAIM-562, Active, empirical, in simulations of folding sheets). It is a parallel, not support.

Two cautions on how far the principle reaches here. First, its components are a sensor network and populations of agents on a meaning–influence model, not regulators. Carrying them to a public regulator's relation to industry, courts, auditors and affected parties is not stated on the record, and the record's account of the exchange declines a nearby statement, a label: "'An ecology of regulatory perspectives' (A33 §7). Its components are CLAIM-342 and CLAIM-403. Beyond them it is a label" (SYNTHESIS-tmp4zamw, Proposed). The account of the governance leads' models also lists among what is not stated "That the owner's conditions are separate requirements", which "LEAD-tmpdzbyn would test" (SYNTHESIS-111, Proposed). Neither decline reaches this paper's carriage: SYNTHESIS-tmp4zamw's note of 2026-11-23 says its decline "tested a label against two entries, and it was made before the owner's correction-coupling principle existed", so "the decline does not reach JD6". The carriage is now mapped where the principle is, in SYNTHESIS-111 at version 3, under "Not stated on the record", with the verdict "fails, a carry": the four instances are a sensor network and populations on the meaning–influence model, "the record holds no regulatory or industrial case", and the readings "are relabellings and state no inference" (SYNTHESIS-111, Proposed). This paper does not state the carriage as held (JD6, not stated, SYNTHESIS-111). What follows is an illustration of what the principle would mean for a regulator, not a step of the argument: a regulator's protected evidence would be evidence about the industry it cannot rewrite or refuse (independent measurement, mandatory disclosure); its authority's coupling would be how standing and resources flow back from the regulated to the regulator (the return channel of capture); and its scoring would be whether objections gain weight by their relevance or by agreement with the regulator's current view. Second, whether the conditions are separate requirements is open, as SYNTHESIS-111 says.

### 13.7 A protected anchor that is wrong: the regress measured once

The motivating draft ends §13 on a paradox: a regulator "must maintain enough identity to preserve legitimate commitments and institutional responsibility", but "must not stabilize its own interpretive and authority structures so thoroughly that criticism cannot become consequential". The record holds a result that measures one form of it, in one model.

The assistant ran the sensor network of CLAIM-tmpvqmwc with a protected beacon that is itself miscalibrated. "What a system is prevented from rewriting or rejecting bounds what it can correct as well as enabling correction" (CLAIM-tmpw11e6, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's; a counter to CLAIM-tmp4h9rc). Over 40 seeds, with the beacon correct the network's late error is 0.044; with the beacon miscalibrated by |δ| = 0.361 it is "late error 0.360 (0.328 to 0.393), radius 0.114": the network converges on the beacon's error and is as internally consistent as with a correct beacon. A rarer protected survey of the beacon, used only by a slower revision of its calibration, brings the error to 0.122 when it comes every 100 steps and 0.293 every 500. "An unrewritable, unrefusable anchor imports its own error whole, and nothing inside the system can see it, since consistency is unaffected." The reason nothing inside can see it is held Active: the miscalibration "is unidentifiable from inside the sensor network by a symmetry", since a world with a miscalibrated beacon and a shifted world with a correct one "give every report identically", so "correcting it needs evidence that breaks the symmetry from outside" (CLAIM-tmpklggb, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's).

The claim reads its own result as a regress: "This is CLAIM-342 again, one level up. The regress stops at whatever is not itself revised" (CLAIM-tmpw11e6). That the motivating draft's reflexive problem has this form is therefore CLAIM-tmpw11e6's statement, and JD7 is a restatement of it, not a join. Carrying the regress to a regulator is not stated, and the account of correcting a regulator lists that carriage under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, a carry" (SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, Proposed). What protects correction (evidence it cannot rewrite, commitments it cannot drop) also fixes what that correction answers to. If the protected anchor is wrong, the system converges on its error while staying consistent, and only a further, slower protected channel, at that channel's rate, corrects it. The owner's principle says protection enables correction; the assistant's counter says it also bounds correction. The paper's reflexive conclusion needs both. Applying it to a regulator, whose anchors are its mandate, its statutory standards and its measurement regime, is an illustration under §13.6's unstated carriage (JD6), not a step.

The record holds Proposed entries on the regulatory side of the same paradox. "Insulating a regulator from future political revision fixes in place the interests of whoever designs the insulated regime": it "relieves the underinvestment that electoral pressure on prices causes when the designers serve consumers, and entrenches capture, removing the electoral pressure that would correct it, when they serve the regulated industry" (THEORY-858, Proposed; Schram and Ule). Its evidence is the laboratory study reported in THEORY-856 (Active), in which "capture was assigned and independence was a fixed price". That is CLAIM-tmpw11e6's anchor with an interested placer. The owner's own claim on protective regimes gives the mechanism by which protection can outlive its reason: "A protective regime, in a person, an institution or a system of states, becomes self-maintaining when the interventions it authorizes shift the evidence used to judge its necessity", and "it is a failure only where the regime has become less responsive to evidence that it is no longer needed" (CLAIM-445, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The readings on path dependence add that political institutions "are built to be hard to change" (THEORY-885, Proposed; Pierson), extending North's account of path-dependent institutional frameworks (THEORY-429, Proposed). On the other side, a prediction: institutions "able to recognize, authorize and carry out revisions of their own governing standards avoid persistent normatively significant failures better than institutions that only monitor outcomes against fixed standards, where the inherited standards are inadequate to the problems met, and not on routine work" (CLAIM-685, Proposed, empirical and untested, ChatGPT's). A matched comparison of public standards bodies with and without a standing revision mechanism finds "recognized inadequacies corrected within days to a few years where an effective revision means exists and left standing for years to centuries where it does not"; it is "small-N, chosen not sampled, illustrative not powered" (CASE-377, Proposed).

Three cautions keep this in proportion.

1. **It is a counter to the owner's claim, and Proposed.** Its note of 2026-11-23: "Promoting this claim would make its concession due on CLAIM-tmp4h9rc, the owner's own claim (ADR-059), and writing it in is the owner's." This paper does not write that concession. It reports the counter as the assistant's and the principle as the owner's, unrevised.
2. **It is one model.** The claim's title says the error falls "only through a further, rarer protected channel", and its note concedes that this "also reaches beyond the one model, as its body concedes". It "does not show that every protected anchor must be revisable". LEAD-tmpdzbyn would test it in a second model.
3. **Who places the anchor is untested.** The case says it cannot show "Anything about who places the beacons. Here the world draws them" (CASE-tmp33vf4). For a regulator, who places the anchors is §13.1's question of who specifies the demands. The measured regress says what happens once an anchor is placed; it says nothing about whether it was placed rightly. The model that would test it is now a lead: an anchor "placed by a party with interests in what it reads", measuring "how far correction answers to the anchor's placement rather than to the world", with "a placer with no interest" as its negative control (GD8: LEAD-tmphisy8, Open, stage idea, the assistant's). THEORY-858's laboratory capture is the nearest reading.

### 13.8 What §13 establishes

- **Active.** An arrangement's capacity to act is no evidence of its capacity to be corrected by others (CLAIM-462, CLAIM-099, CLAIM-422), an intervention can make its own correction costlier or unreachable under unchanged standards (CLAIM-464, CLAIM-086), and an arrangement that does the work through which its governors' capacities are exercised can erode them (CLAIM-673). Control of a challenge needs its own warrant, and explanation, contestation and interruption are separate entitlements (CLAIM-727, CLAIM-740). Influence alone does not ground a duty to answer (CLAIM-749). Legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability can each fail alone (CLAIM-523), and a dependent whose claims fail so lacks effective influence (CLAIM-686). For a fixed arrangement, recognizing more demands cannot improve compliance, and a regime that penalizes discovery teaches an institution not to inquire (CLAIM-662, CASE-tmp8mk6w).
