# CLAIM-tmpqhxsz: Strong unitary executive theory, as its defenders state it, rests on the constitutional vesting of the executive power in one officer and on an accountability argument, that unity makes responsibility traceable to an officer the electorate can judge, not on a claim that effective unity requires hierarchy; and the correctives of the executive that matter most, Congress, the courts and elections, lie outside the executive it unifies, so the common-mode charge holds only for correctives inside the executive or dependent on its resources, case by case

## The gap

A38's table states the theory's assumption as "Effective unity of executive
action requires hierarchical unity of governing authority", and answers it
with "The first four do not logically entail the fifth" (CLAIM-tmp1wlu2).
The theory's defenders do not argue from that assumption, and the exchange
does not meet the argument they do make.

## The defenders' statements

From the assistant's knowledge; unverified until read (LEAD-tmpdy0f8),
except where checked.

- **Text.** The case of Calabresi and Prakash ("The President's Power to
  Execute the Laws", 1994) and of Calabresi and Yoo (The Unitary Executive,
  2008) is textual and historical: Article II vests "the executive Power" in
  "a President", and the Take Care Clause makes the President answerable for
  the laws' execution. Its force does not depend on any claim about what
  effective unity requires.
- **Accountability.** The functional rationale is Hamilton's, in Federalist
  No. 70 (LIT-tmp4cqb2), which A38 cites for "energy, unity and
  responsibility": a plural executive "tends to conceal faults and destroy
  responsibility" (checked against the Avalon Project text). The claim is
  not that unity of authority is entailed by unity of accountability, but
  that for an elected executive, accountability to the electorate requires
  that someone the electorate can judge directs and can remove those who
  exercise executive power. Officials insulated from the President are, on
  this argument, insulated from voters. A38's "do not logically entail"
  answers an entailment no defender asserts.
- **A40 §3.5 concedes the converse** ("Independence does not establish
  normative governability") but does not connect it to this rationale,
  which is the theory's main argument.

## Where the correctives are

The Constitution's correctives of a President are mostly outside the
executive: impeachment, appropriations, oversight, the Senate's consent,
judicial review and elections. A38 §2 itself says the theory "is not
necessarily a claim that presidential power overrides Congress, the courts
or the law". The common-mode model (A38 §2) needs the corrective processes
to share a failure cause with what they correct.

That holds, and the critique has force, for correctives the strong theory
does put under presidential direction and removal: inspectors general,
independent regulatory commissions, and prosecutors investigating the
executive. It holds partly for external correctives, which depend on
evidence the executive holds and on enforcement it performs: CLAIM-404's
informational route. Each such dependence has to be shown, not assumed;
A40 §3.4's test (LEAD-tmpvo9fs) is the way to show it.

## The mark

A38's statement of the theory's assumption is a partial strawman: it
mislocates the theory's warrant, from text and accountability to a
functional claim about unity. Its description of strong and weak versions,
and of the theory's concern with "the structure of authority within the
executive", is accurate. ARG-tmpz2gmo holds the narrowed inference.
