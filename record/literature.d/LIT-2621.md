# LIT-2621: Amnesic Probing: Behavioral Explanation with Amnesic Counterfactuals

Elazar, Y., Ravfogel, S., Jacovi, A. & Goldberg, Y. (2021). Amnesic Probing: Behavioral Explanation with Amnesic Counterfactuals. *Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics* 9: 160–175. doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00359. arXiv:2006.00995.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1961](../notes.d/NOTE-1961.md).*

- **Probing and use come apart** (§4): "the probe accuracy does not
  correlate with task importance as measured by our method (Spearman
  correlation of 8.5, with a p-value of 0.871)".
- **Removed information comes back downstream** (§7.1): for coarse POS,
  unmasked, "the information is highly recoverable in subsequent layers
  when applying the amnesic operation on the first seven layers"; the
  drop is 5.72–12.69 points there and 16.57–46.39 in the second half. The
  masked setting shows "an opposite trend".
- **Only linear information is removed** (§9), and "the classifiers can
  rely on arbitrary features that happen to correlate".

## Standing in the record

Filed from A's turn on concept erasure, which says that high probing
accuracy did not reliably predict whether removing the probed property
would affect word prediction, that removed information could become
decodable again downstream, and that for unmasked coarse POS early-layer
removal left more recoverable than later-layer removal (A's words
paraphrased here; the exchange was not rechecked by the filer). The
reader found all three right; the correlation rests on six properties, so
"did not reliably predict" is weaker evidence than it sounds. It applies
INLP ([LIT-2619](LIT-2619.md)) and is among the methods Kumar, Tan & Sharma
([LIT-2653](LIT-2653.md)) criticize. [THEORY-1888](../theory.d/THEORY-1888.md) carries its central account.
