# THEORY-tmpx81wa: Asserting is undertaking a commitment that entitles others to reassert it and carries a responsibility to show entitlement if challenged; every interlocutor keeps score of everyone's commitments and entitlements, so the score is doubly perspectival, and a speech act's significance is the change it makes to the scores, fixed by its content's inferential role

## Source

Brandom (1994), [LIT-794](../literature.d/LIT-794.md) (*Making It Explicit*, Harvard University Press),
skimmed in [NOTE-622](../notes.d/NOTE-622.md) from the author's own copy: the Preface (pp. xi–xxv)
and chapter 3, §I (pp. 141–157) and §III.3 to §IV.5 (pp. 172–193). The
rest of the 741 pages was not read. **This account rests on a skim.**

## What was actually shown

The parts read give definitions and a model, not theorems.

**Assertion (§III.3).** Asserting undertakes a commitment, authorizes
further commitments (one's own inferential consequences and the
audience's reassertions), and takes on a conditional responsibility to
vindicate entitlement if appropriately challenged. Entitlement is
inherited by justification, intrapersonally and across contents, and by
deferral, interpersonally within one content ("the buck is passed to A").
"Asserting is the fundamental speech act."

**Default and challenge (§III.4–5).** Many claims are entitled until
appropriately challenged, which stops the regress. A challenge is itself
an assertion of an incompatible claim, assessed the same way. An
unentitled claim loses its authority to entitle anyone to its
consequences; no external sanction is needed.

**Scorekeeping (§IV.1–4).** Adapting Lewis's "Scorekeeping in a language
game", the score is the commitments and entitlements an interlocutor
attributes to each participant. It is kept for each interlocutor by each
interlocutor, so what C is committed to according to A can differ from
what C is committed to according to B. When B asserts p, scorekeeper A
adds p to B's commitments, closes them under commitment-preserving
inference, removes entitlements p's incompatibilities preclude, adds
entitlements by entitlement-preserving inference, and assesses B's
entitlement to p. The change follows "according to a uniform pattern"
(Dummett) from the content's inferential role: what it follows from, what
follows from it, and what it rules out.

**Force (§IV.5).** Deferral, disavowal, query and challenge attach
different force to the same contents.

**What could have come out otherwise.** As a philosophical model it is
offered as sufficient, and in its main structure necessary, to confer
propositional content. The parts read state that claim; the arguments for
it are in chapters not read.

## What this does not say

- **That the model is argued here.** Chapters 1–2, which argue for the
  normative approach, chapter 4 (practical commitments) and chapter 8 (de
  re ascription and communication across perspectives) were not read.
- **That it describes actual practice.** By the author's account it is
  "not our actual practice but an artificial idealization of it", limited
  to semantic inference, with Gricean implicature left to an outer shell.
- **That there is one score.** Scores are per scorekeeper, so the model
  gives no single objective conversational state unless a perspective is
  chosen. Neither the book, as read, nor [CLAIM-014](../claims.d/CLAIM-014.md) says how to choose it.
- **That non-assertive acts are covered.** Orders and promises are
  parasitic on assertion. Whether practical commitments reach
  obligations and relationships is in chapter 4.
- **That two renderings with the same content and force can differ in
  significance.** On this model they cannot, since the change is fixed by
  content and force. [NOTE-622](../notes.d/NOTE-622.md) marks that as a tension with [CLAIM-054](../claims.d/CLAIM-054.md),
  unless what that claim names lies outside the doxastic score.
