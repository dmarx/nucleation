# NOTE-tmppyrwx: Colman et al. — Explaining strategic coordination: Cognitive hierarchy theory, strong Stackelberg reasoning, and team reasoning

## Contribution

Experiments discriminating four explanations of coordination in
common-interest games.

## Key results

1. **Team reasoning.** "Team-reasoning players are assumed to ask, What do
   we want, and what should I do to play my part in achieving it?" (p. 9).
2. **Its belief condition.** "A player has no reason to adopt the
   team-reasoning mode in the absence of a belief that the other player(s)
   will do likewise" (p. 9).
3. **Cheap.** "Team reasoning can be accomplished even more easily,
   especially in larger games, by scanning the payoff matrix for the cell
   with the greatest payoff sum, and then checking that it is unique." (p.
   20).
4. **Several modes.** "Most players considered two or more of these
   reasoning processes before choosing their strategies." (p. 1).

## Assumptions

Adult players; modes identified by games where theories diverge.

## Concepts

Cognitive hierarchy; strong Stackelberg reasoning; team reasoning.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-1630**, a parallel: team reasoning is cheap and occurs, but is
  a minority mode in these games.
- **The owner's view**, background: what team reasoning needs is a
  first-order belief about the partner's mode of reasoning, not nested
  beliefs (the reader's inference from the definitions).

## Limitations

Adults; manuscript pages; supplementary material unread.
