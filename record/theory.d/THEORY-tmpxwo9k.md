# THEORY-tmpxwo9k: Cayley reads Illich as holding that tools, institutions included, become counterproductive past a second watershed and tend toward radical monopoly, the dominance of one kind of product that renders alternatives inaccessible or unimaginable, so that the test is whether one can still find an alternative or decently do without

## The account

Cayley reads Illich as holding that "A radical monopoly exists where all alternatives have been rendered either inaccessible or unimaginable", with the test "whether one can still find an alternative or decently do without the monopolized commodity". The car environment is the standard case; burial, where a de facto monopoly was followed by legislation making it compulsory, is another.

## Evidence

The reading in NOTE-tmpc4m2u, results 1–5. Corroborates CLAIM-tmpbypt7's measure and mechanisms.

## What it does not say

That radical monopolies arise only without law: Illich's burial case ends in law.
