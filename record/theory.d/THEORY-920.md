# THEORY-920: As Mancinelli and Bach's systematic review reports it, rating it B, in three laboratory studies of healthy humans a signalled, costless avoidance response offered for a stimulus never paired with the aversive outcome was used, and withdrawing it raised expectancy of the outcome for that stimulus, though Xia et al. (2019) found no change in skin conductance or startle; every study signalled the response's availability, which may itself have read as a danger cue

## Source

Mancinelli & Bach, [LIT-1477](../literature.d/LIT-1477.md), read in full in [NOTE-1134](../notes.d/NOTE-1134.md) from the
PsyArXiv preprint: pp. 19–20 and 26–27, and Fig. 4 (p. 53).

## What was actually shown

The review reports that "signalling the availability of a costless AR for
a previously and currently never punished CS (i.e., the CS-) has been
observed to elicit this AR, and to increase threat expectation once this
AR option is removed" (Cameron et al. 2022; Engelhard et al. 2015; Xia et
al. 2019), "although CR (SCR and FPS) were not affected (Xia et al., 2019)"
(pp. 19–20). It rates the phenomenon B.

The authors argue that this "must invoke an inference mechanism that
deduces CS–US contingencies without direct experience: after all, the
agent never sees the CS- being followed by punishment" (p. 26). A
model-free actor-critic cannot produce it. Their first candidate: people
"might counterfactually infer danger for the safe CS, through the
artificial belief that if they had not avoided, when they had the chance,
they would have been punished" (p. 26). Their second: latent-cause
inference, in which the withdrawal of avoidance marks a new context.

What could have come out otherwise: threat expectancy for a stimulus that
had never been followed by harm could have stayed at its floor whatever
was done about it.

## What this does not say

- **That people infer danger from their own avoidance.** The review gives
  that as one explanation. The signal that avoidance is available "might
  be understood by participants as a signal for US delivery" (p. 19), and
  every study used one. Signalling is "unusual in real-world or clinical
  avoidance settings" (p. 26).
- **That the effect reaches fear responses.** In Xia et al. (2019),
  skin conductance and startle did not change; the review does not say
  whether the other two studies measured them.
- **Anything about anxiety disorders.** Healthy participants only.

## Note of 2026-10-30: the dropped clause

The old title said the withdrawal of avoidance raised expectancy "though
not their skin conductance or startle in the one study that measured
them". That clause is not kept as a Proposed reading (ADR-047, kind 3).
It was imprecise: the review reports only that "CR (SCR and FPS) were not
affected (Xia et al., 2019)" (pp. 19–20) and does not say whether the
other two studies measured them, so "the one study that measured them"
read more into the review than it says. The rest of the old title, the
three studies' finding in the record's voice, is kept as a Proposed
reading that extends this one.
