# THEORY-1895: Di Volo and Férézou show that in anaesthetised mouse barrel cortex two colliding whisker-evoked waves sum sub-linearly, about 30% below the linear sum at peak, as their heterogeneous excitatory–inhibitory mean-field model predicted from the lower gain of populations already activated

## Source

Di Volo & Férézou (2021), [LIT-2654](../literature.d/LIT-2654.md); read in [NOTE-1976](../notes.d/NOTE-1976.md).

## The result

A single-whisker deflection evokes a wave that spreads at about 0.1 mm/ms
with direction-dependent intensity. A bistable E/I mean-field medium, in
which a local input "can potentially trigger a chain of jumps to high
activity states that spreads in space", reproduces it once its excitatory
coupling map is inferred from each animal's data. The inferred model
predicted that two waves would collide sub-linearly. The experiment then
found that "the activity evoked by the two stimuli presented sequentially
is lower than the linear summation of the responses to the two stimuli
presented separately", with "a mean peak at around -30%" in all six mice.
In the model the cause is a sub-linear population gain, which needs
conductance-based synapses and a higher inhibitory gain. Linear summation
could have been found; it was not.

## What it does not show

- The effect in awake animals, at other delays or for other whisker pairs.
- That the gain mechanism is the cause in the animal. That is the model's
  attribution, untested by manipulation.
- Spirals, phase singularities or annihilation.
- That no cortical regime superposes linearly. It bounds the generality
  of linear-interference accounts such as [LIT-2633](../literature.d/LIT-2633.md)'s; it does not
  refute them in their own regime.
