# LIT-tmpxkxq0: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: Model, Processes and Outcomes

Steven C. Hayes, Jason B. Luoma, Frank W. Bond, Akihiko Masuda and Jason
Lillis (2006), *Behaviour Research and Therapy* 44(1):1–25 —
DOI-10.1016/j.brat.2005.06.006

## Key takeaways

- **The model.** The main source of psychopathology is "the way that
  language and cognition interacts with direct contingencies to produce an
  inability to persist or change in the service of long term valued ends".
  Cognitive fusion, behaviour governed by verbal networks rather than by
  the contingencies, supports experiential avoidance. Together they shrink
  contact with the present, harden a conceptualized self, and push values
  aside. The result is psychological inflexibility.
- **The treatment.** Psychological flexibility is "the ability to contact
  the present moment more fully as a conscious human being, and to change
  or persist in behavior when doing so serves valued ends". It is built
  through six processes: acceptance, defusion, being present, self as
  context, values and committed action. ACT is defined by these processes,
  "not a specific technology".
- **The evidence, as the authors weigh it.** Correlational, component,
  mediational and outcome studies are "broadly supportive". There are not
  enough well-controlled trials to call ACT more effective than other
  active treatments. Where ACT and cognitive therapy were compared, they
  appear to change different processes.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the read statement of the
ACT model, the most-cited account of psychological flexibility and the one
the `psychopathology-and-treatment` blurb points to. No anthology topic
holds it, and it carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.

It carries two earlier works further, and the `extends` relations name
them:

- **Experiential avoidance ([LIT-tmpsjur3](LIT-tmpsjur3.md)).** The review defines experiential
  avoidance with the 1996 paper's words and makes it one of the six
  inflexibility processes, now fed by cognitive fusion. Without that
  construct the model has no account of why people narrow their lives.
- **Relational Frame Theory ([LIT-tmpn4p2x](LIT-tmpn4p2x.md)).** The review calls ACT "the
  applied extension" of the RFT programme and derives its four
  implications for psychopathology from the book (p. 6): normal verbal
  processes underlie suffering; context controls the impact of cognitive
  networks; networks are historical and are not eliminated; and direct
  attempts to change them elaborate them. The read statement of RFT is
  [LIT-tmp6n7fc](LIT-tmp6n7fc.md).

Later papers build on it. Kashdan and Rottenberg ([LIT-tmpt6rok](LIT-tmpt6rok.md)) place ACT's
flexibility among other literatures on flexibility and cite this review's
meta-analysis. Macri and Rogge ([LIT-tmp9golj](LIT-tmp9golj.md)), unread, meta-analyse its
six-process model as a set of treatment mechanisms. Hayes and Hofmann's
process-based therapy (2019), filed in parallel by another agent, widens
the same process focus beyond ACT.

**Which version.** Only an early author manuscript could be read lawfully.
Its model and argument match the published abstract. Its numbers are not
the published ones and should not be quoted from this record without the
