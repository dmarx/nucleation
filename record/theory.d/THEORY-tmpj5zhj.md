# THEORY-tmpj5zhj: Deng and colleagues show that a soft composite whose magnetic particles are held in a meltable polymer can have its magnetization pattern rewritten by local laser melting under a programming field and fixed again on cooling, so one film can be given many different shape responses to the same actuation field

## Source

Deng, Sattari, Xie, Liao, Yan and Lin (2020), LIT-tmp6fzgs; read in
NOTE-tmp5sqfp. A neighbour of Yeow, Yang and Ren (2024), LIT-tmpxnx47, which
was not read.

## What was actually shown

- **Writing** (abstract; Results, p. 2): with the polymer "temporarily
  melted by transient laser heating, the orientation of the magnetic
  particles can be re-aligned upon change of a programming magnetic field",
  and on cooling "PCL is quickly solidified, thus fixing the programmed
  magnetic orientations".
- **Rewriting** (Discussion, p. 9): "the programmed magnetic anisotropies
  can be reprogrammed to different ones", shown on the same films, with
  shapes held over 100 actuation cycles with alternating reprogramming.
- **Many shapes, one field** (Results): S and zigzag strips, origami-like
  shapes and kirigami robots with different gaits, all under a 150 mT
  actuation field. The rewritten film could have kept memory of its old
  pattern; by the reported shapes it did not.

## What this does not say

- That the rewriting works in place, tetherlessly, inside a device: it
  needs an external laser and programming field.
- Anything about actuator–structure interfaces, the other half of Yeow et
  al.'s idea.
- How many rewrites the film survives beyond the 100-cycle test.
- Anything about resources, budgets or the cost of reconfiguring.
