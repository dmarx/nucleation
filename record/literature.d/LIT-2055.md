# LIT-2055: Explicit kinematic equations for degree-4 rigid origami vertices, Euclidean and non-Euclidean

Foschi, R., Hull, T. C. & Ku, J. S. (2022). Explicit kinematic equations for degree-4 rigid origami vertices, Euclidean and non-Euclidean. *Physical Review E* 106(5): 055001. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.106.055001. arXiv:2206.12691.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1606](../notes.d/NOTE-1606.md). Sections are the arXiv version's.*

- **Closure** (§II): a necessary condition for a vertex to fold rigidly is
  that the product of the crease rotations R(e_i, ρ_i) be the identity,
  cited to belcastro and Hull (2002).
- **Flat-foldable degree 4** (§II, Theorem 1, cited to Origametry and Tachi
  and Hull): "adjacent folding angles have a linear relationship when
  parameterized by the tangent half-angle".
- **Two modes** (Corollary 1): "Degree-4 rigid-foldable vertices have
  exactly two folding modes".
- **Curvature splits them** (§III, Example 1): "in both the elliptic and
  hyperbolic cases the configuration spaces are disconnected", because "it
  is impossible to unfold the vertex so that all the creases have folding
  angles of zero at the same time" (with folding angles inside (−π, π);
  see [NOTE-1606](../notes.d/NOTE-1606.md)).
- **Bistability proved** (§V A): "the rigid origami configuration space of
  the square twist pouch determined by sector angle θ is discrete".

## Standing in the record

Registered as a stand-in for Hull's chapter "Rigid Foldings"
([LIT-2039](LIT-2039.md)), which a paywall kept from being read. It restates, as its
Theorem 1 cited to Origametry, the chapter's flat-foldable degree-4
fold-angle equations, linear in the tangents of half the folding angles,
and goes beyond them to all degree-4 vertices. It does not stand in for
the chapter's reciprocal diagrams, its zero-area theorem or its
infinitesimal and second-order rigidity. It also states belcastro and
Hull's single-vertex closure condition ([LIT-2067](LIT-2067.md)), but not its global
form. The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures does not cite it;
its exact bistable pouch bears on A's point that bistability is "a physical implementation of a form of memory", as a
kinematic bistability, not a dynamical one. Filed from it: [THEORY-1381](../theory.d/THEORY-1381.md), and its wider clause [THEORY-tmp0i15x](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp0i15x.md).
