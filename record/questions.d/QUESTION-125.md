# QUESTION-125: When a candidate block gains or loses the gap that isolates it as a parameter varies, does the change in its identifiable boundary coincide with a bifurcation of its dynamics, and what does each way of their coming apart show?

## Why it is a question

A6 §2: "Suppose the interaction structure varies with a parameter θ:
A = A(θ). A collective may become sharply distinguishable when an eigengap
opens, and cease to be robustly identifiable when that gap closes. This is
not automatically a dynamical bifurcation. It is a **structural or
identifiability transition**." It asked when the two coincide, and:
"Their divergence may be just as revealing as their coincidence. A
coalition might undergo a major change in behavior while preserving its
internal organization. Conversely, a structural boundary could change
while observed collective behavior remains almost identical."

The two are different events in one spectrum. [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)'s boundary is a
gap in the interaction matrix, strong couplings inside the block and weak
ones across its edge, clearing the Occam factor (δ/ε > τ), and the form
"lasts as long as that block persists". [CLAIM-164](../claims.d/CLAIM-164.md) says what the gap makes
definite: the block's subspace, and a single direction only when its own
gap is wide. A bifurcation is an eigenvalue crossing the stability
boundary, as in [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md), where the leading eigenvalue of the
meaning–authority map crosses +1 at βγ = (1 − α)(1 − δ). A gap can open
far from any crossing, and a crossing can happen inside a block whose gap
does not move.

The record already holds one dissociation. [THEORY-265](../theory.d/THEORY-265.md), as [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md) reads
it: in a modular coordination game "the block verdict is the same before
and after entrainment. What changes is timing". That is a change of
dynamical regime with the boundary unchanged. The other direction, a
boundary that forms or dissolves with behaviour unchanged, has no case.

[QUESTION-079](QUESTION-079.md) is the neighbouring question for an agent's regimes: discrete
switches between metastable states or continuous reorganization, and what
then counts as a transition. If reorganization is continuous, a boundary
may change with no bifurcation at all.

## What would count as an answer

A parametrized family A(θ) with three regions, built or found: one where
the block's gap closes at the parameter value where a regime loses
stability, one where the regime changes with the gap intact, and one where
the gap closes with the regime and its observable behaviour intact. For
each, a statement of which of regime identity, organizational
individuality and observable function changed. A6's caution applies to
any answer: "attractor-basin membership is not numerical identity.
Different individuals can occupy the same attractor, and one individual
can cross between attractors."

## Note of 2026-11-07: candidates for two of the three regions, from "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"

From A14 §2 and A20 §4 of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (2026-11-06 to 07; https://chatgpt.com/share/6aee7a9b-f13c-83e9-a865-6a29c2c3ff7e), filed as [CLAIM-626](../claims.d/CLAIM-626.md):

- **The gap closes where the regime loses stability.** Two detuned phase oscillators: the restoring rate √(4K² − Δω²) of the locked relative phase vanishes at the locking saddle-node |Δω| = 2K. A horizon-relative boundary (λ⊥H ≫ 1) is lost earlier, while the locked state still exists.
- **The regime goes with the gap intact.** [CLAIM-521](../claims.d/CLAIM-521.md): the maintenance fails at its fold while the coupling's gap stays near its full value.

The third region, a gap closing with the regime and its behaviour intact, still has no case. The filer adds: the locking example's early loss of the horizon-relative boundary comes near it, but there the gap is shrinking toward the bifurcation, not closing apart from it. The question stays open.

## Note of 2026-11-19: the exchange "Topological protection explained"

Two readings filed from this exchange bear here, and both already carry bears_on to this question. Neither is a gap in a block, so both bear on the question's last part: which of regime and observable function changed.

- **[THEORY-tmpvtafk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpvtafk.md)** (Iqbal and colleagues, read). In networks with equivariant middle layers, trainability at great depth turns on with the symmetry-breaking transition at σ_W = 1. In generic networks the same order parameter turns on and brings no gain. So one dynamical transition coincides with a functional change in one family and comes apart from it in another.
- **[THEORY-tmp8my3p](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp8my3p.md)** (Gili and colleagues, read). A balanced partition changes between rest and task. There is no parametrized family and no dynamics, and behaviour changes too, so it fills none of the three regions.

A12 §5 proposed measuring an order parameter, a defect density or persistence statistic and a task-relevant information measure beside the loss as noise varies in Iqbal and Welling's model, to see which transition explains the change in performance. The noise level it reports (about 0.0046) is unverified: the paper, [LIT-tmp01nqp](../literature.d/LIT-tmp01nqp.md), is Unfetched.

The question stays open. No status change.
