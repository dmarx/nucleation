# LIT-679: A Random Matrix Analysis of Random Fourier Features: Beyond the Gaussian Kernel, a Precise Phase Transition, and the Corresponding Double Descent

Zhenyu Liao, Romain Couillet and Michael W. Mahoney (2020), *NeurIPS 2020*; *J. Stat. Mech.* (2021) — [ARXIV-2006.05013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05013)

## Key takeaways

- **The Gaussian kernel is the wrong limit.** For fixed n and p the RFF
  Gram matrix converges entrywise to the Gaussian kernel as N → ∞ (Eqs.
  1–2), but with n, p, N comparable it does not converge in spectral norm.
  So kernel-based predictions of training error miss badly when N/n < 1
  (Figure 4).
- **A deterministic equivalent replaces it** (Theorem 1). E_W[Q] ≈
  Q̄ = ((N/n)(Kcos/(1+δcos) + Ksin/(1+δsin)) + λIn)⁻¹, where
  [Kcos]ij = e^{−(‖xi‖²+‖xj‖²)/2} cosh(xiᵀxj), Ksin uses sinh, and
  (δcos, δsin) solve δσ = (1/n) tr(KσQ̄). As N/n → ∞ the δ go to 0 and Q̄
  goes to (n/N)K⁻¹, the kernel answer (Remark 2).
- **Exact training and test MSE** (Theorems 2 and 3), each a first-order
  term plus a second-order correction through a 2×2 matrix Ω. Test data
  must be concentrated random vectors (Assumption 2), which covers Lipschitz
  maps of Gaussians and so GAN images, and they need not be independent of
  the training data.
- **Two phases and a singular boundary.** As λ → 0, for 2N < n the δ scale
  as 1/λ; for 2N > n they stay O(1) (Section 4.1, Figures 5–6, 8). Training
  MSE is continuous at 2N = n, but det(Ω⁻¹) scales as λ, so the test MSE
  diverges there whenever the test set is not close to the training set.
  That is the double-descent peak (Remark 9, Figure 7). If the test data are
  within σ² ≲ λ of the training data, the test error stays equal to the
  training error (Section 4.2, Figure 10).
- **Over-parameterize and regularize.** For fixed λ > 0 the minimum test
  error is always at 2N > n, and the global optimum is a heavily
  over-parameterized model with a non-vanishing λ (λopt ≈ 0.2 in Figure 7,
  which shows no peak).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 with the owner's batch ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)), as one of its two
exact accounts of double descent. It is the record's first random-matrix
paper.

**It extends Martin and Mahoney ([LIT-672](LIT-672.md)).** That paper argued
qualitatively that, in the limit where data and capacity grow together,
learning has phases with sharp transitions. This paper works in the same
proportional limit, cites the statistical mechanics of learning for it, and
derives such a transition exactly for one model. Remark 9 says double
descent is "a natural consequence of the phase transition between two
qualitatively different phases of learning", citing Martin and Mahoney.

**Beside Dereziński, Liang and Mahoney ([LIT-683](LIT-683.md)).** That paper derives
exact non-asymptotic double descent for minimum-norm linear regression by
changing the design distribution. Dereziński et al. thank Liao for pointing
out the connection to random-matrix resolvents. In both papers an effective
regularization is fixed by a self-consistent trace equation: δσ = (1/n)
tr(KσQ̄) here, n = tr(Σ(Σ + λnI)⁻¹) there. Neither paper compares itself
with the other, so no relation is declared.

**Beside the kernel-regime holdings.** The N/n → ∞ limit of this paper is
kernel regression with the Gaussian kernel (Remark 7). The record's
NTK-regime readings work in that infinite-feature limit. Bietti and Bach
([LIT-608](LIT-608.md)) give the spherical-harmonic eigenstructure of ReLU kernels,
Basri et al. ([LIT-612](LIT-612.md)) and Cao et al. ([LIT-624](LIT-624.md)) the eigenspace-by-eigenspace
fitting rates ([THEORY-086](../theory.d/THEORY-086.md)). This paper is the correction those analyses do
not have when N is comparable to n.

**Anthology.** The double-descent curve itself is held there as Belkin et
al. ([ANTH-LIT-297](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-297.md)), which this paper cites as the empirical signature. It
is ML theory, with a practical conclusion about choosing N and λ, so
`anthology-candidate`.
