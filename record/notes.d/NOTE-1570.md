# NOTE-1570: Damavandi, Hagh, Santangelo and Manning — Energetic rigidity I

## Contribution

A single classification of when the geometric rigidity tests that come
from mathematics (first-order rigidity, which is Maxwell–Calladine
counting, and second-order rigidity) imply the energetic rigidity that
physicists measure, organized by the number of states of self-stress and
the spectrum of the prestress matrix. It argues that second-order rigidity
is a better proxy than the shear modulus, and proposes reconfiguration by
toggling a structure between floppy and rigid.

## Key insight

The existence of a state of self-stress is geometry; the load it carries
is energy. Counting can be trusted only where no self-stress exists, and
physical systems are driven precisely to the non-generic configurations
where self-stresses appear.

## Assumptions

- **Hookean energy**, quadratic in generalized strains f_α: E = ½Σ f_α².
  Other energy functionals are future work.
- **At an energy minimum**, at zero temperature.
- **Global motions only**: rattlers and danglers are excluded.
- **Some implications hold only "for specific choices"** of the rest
  values, that is of the prestress (the dashed arrows of Fig. 2).

## Key results

- **Thesis** (abstract): energetic rigidity "is a more useful notion of
  rigidity in practice than two more commonly used rigidity tests".
- **Counting** (abstract): "We find that constraint counting robustly
  predicts energetic rigidity only when the system has no states of self
  stress."
- **Where self-stress comes from** (Introduction): the Maxwell–Calladine
  theorem "shows that one should also subtract the number of states of self
  stress, equilibrium states of the system that can carry a load, because
  they arise from redundant constraints."
- **Why counting usually works, and when it does not** (Introduction):
  "Maxwell constraint counting works because these non-generic
  configurations are ostensibly rare", but "In many physical systems of
  interest, however, the dynamics or boundary conditions drive the system
  towards specific, non-generic states".
- **Two rigidities** (Sec. I B): "Energetic rigidity is not necessarily
  equivalent to structural rigidity when the system is prestressed (E >
  0), though the two are the same when E = 0".
- **Existence versus load** (Sec. I C): "The existence of states of self
  stress only depends on the geometry of the system and does not imply
  that the system has to be prestressed." Under external tension a system
  "will naturally evolve to a nongeneric configuration having at least one
  self stress."
- **The classification** (Sec. II; App. A), with H = RᵀR + P:
  - Case 1, N_s = 0: first-order rigidity implies energetic rigidity.
  - Case 2A, P = 0: second-order rigidity implies energetic rigidity at
    quartic order, even with shear modulus G = 0.
  - Case 2B, P positive semidefinite: second-order rigidity implies
    energetic rigidity; with a single self-stress, G > 0.
  - Case 2C, P with negative eigenvalues: "In this case, we have been
    unable to derive analytic results for whether first-order or
    second-order rigidity implies energetic rigidity."
- **First-order rigid, yet not energetically rigid** (Sec. II): "Even a
  system that is first-order rigid may not be energetically rigid under
  some conditions", as in jammed packings where scaling the contact forces
  drives the lowest nontrivial Hessian eigenvalue to zero.
- **Not the shear modulus** (Sec. I A): "Importantly, defining rigidity
  based on G is not equivalent to energetic rigidity."
- **Selection** (Sec. II): a mathematician needs only some combination of
  self-stresses, but "that particular self stress may not be the linear
  combination of self stresses that the system chooses as its prestress
  based on external forces [31]."
- **Design** (Sec. III): "apply a global perturbation that makes the system
  floppy, (2) rearrange its components at no energy cost, and (3) apply a
  reverse global perturbation to make it rigid again." And: "Unlike
  structural rigidity, energetic rigidity is not defined solely by the
  geometry – predictions also depend on the energy functional."

## Concepts

*As in Table I.*

- **Energetically rigid**: "any nontrivial global motion increases the
  energy".
- **Structurally rigid**: no nontrivial global motion preserves the
  constraints f_α.
- **First-order** and **second-order rigid**: no nontrivial global motion
  preserves f_α to first, or to second, order.
- **Prestress stable** (after Connelly and Whiteley): some self-stress σ
  makes Σσ_α∂∂F_α positive definite on the nontrivial first-order flexes.
- **Prestressed**: f_α ≠ 0 at the minimum.
- **Prestress matrix** P = Σ f_α ∂∂f_α, the Hessian's second term.
- **Floppy mode**: a nontrivial linear zero mode.
- **Isostatic**: "An energetically rigid system with no states of self
  stress".

## Connections

It takes its rigidity hierarchy and the notion of prestress stability from
Connelly and Whiteley ([LIT-2080](../literature.d/LIT-2080.md), read in part in [NOTE-1588](NOTE-1588.md)), and
asks which of its rungs reach the energetic notion. Rocks and Mehta
([LIT-2112](../literature.d/LIT-2112.md)) work at linear order in the same Hookean setting. Part II,
cited as [39], applies the theory to spring networks under tension and to
vertex models; not read.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures** (A, cited as M3).
  - A says the paper "explicitly argues for energy-based rigidity as a
    complement to geometric rigidity criteria": right, though the paper's
    own claim is stronger, "a more useful notion of rigidity in practice".
  - Missing from A: the case classification, which is the paper's result;
    the selection problem, that the prestress a system adopts may not be
    the stabilizing one, which matters for any organizational use, since a
    stabilizing configuration existing is not the system occupying it; and
    the floppy–rigid reconfiguration route, which bears on A's Part 4
    about transitions and reconfiguration slack.
  - **On A's Maxwell–Calladine caveat**: supports its stability half, since
    a first-order rigid system can fail to be energetically rigid under
    prestress. It adds what A lacks: the count is taken at a configuration,
    and dynamics select the non-generic configurations where rank drops and
    self-stresses appear.
  - **On redundant constraints and self-stress.** The synthesis attributes
    to Rocks and Mehta ([LIT-2112](../literature.d/LIT-2112.md)) the point that redundant constraints
    can create states of self-stress; that sentence is A's paraphrase and
    not in their paper. This paper is the one that says it: states of self
    stress "arise from redundant constraints". It also qualifies it: a
    self-stress need not carry load. Its existence is geometric, and
    whether the system is prestressed along it depends on rest lengths and
    external forcing. Connelly and Whiteley ([LIT-2080](../literature.d/LIT-2080.md), Remark 6.2.1)
    give a stronger instance.
- **[CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)** (stability does not entail viability). Qualifies, as an
  analogy only. The paper separates constraints preserved (structural)
  from energy raised (energetic) and shows each can hold without the other
  under prestress, a two-notion gap of the claim's logical shape; but the
  claim is about dynamical viability, not local rigidity.
- **[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md) and [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)** (frustration as joint unrealizability,
  ordinal before quantitative). Qualifies. The existence of a self-stress
  is a geometric, structural fact; the prestress carried is energetic and
  selected by forcing. That separation of existence from load sits close
  to the record's line between ordinal frustration and its quantity, and
  supports A's statement that structural and mechanical frustration are
  not interchangeable.
- **[CLAIM-345](../claims.d/CLAIM-345.md)** (equal controllability rank does not fix the cost of
  control). Supports, by analogy only: equal first-order counts do not fix
  energetic stiffness; G can be zero in an energetically rigid system
  (Case 2A) and positive in others.
- **[CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)** (regime, organization and capacity to transform persist or
  fail separately). Unrelated: the floppy–rigid toggle is a mechanical
  route, not a claim about separable persistence.
- **[CLAIM-365](../claims.d/CLAIM-365.md)**. Unrelated.
- Filed: [THEORY-1419](../theory.d/THEORY-1419.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Case 2C** has no analytic result; the authors say more restrictive
  conditions are needed case by case.
- **A missing example**: they "have not been able to identify an example" of
  a second-order rigid system with several self-stresses and G = 0.
- **Hookean energies only.**
- **Applications** are in paper II, not read here.
- **Slips in the text**: "Whitely" for Whiteley throughout; the
  Maxwell–Calladine index theorem is equated with first-order rigidity in
  Sec. III, loosely conflating a count with a property; one sentence in
  Sec. I B, on systems rigid only at third order or beyond, reads garbled
  and is not relied on here.

## Open questions

- When the prestress matrix has negative eigenvalues, what decides
  energetic rigidity?
- Which prestress does a forced system select among its self-stresses, and
  when is it the stabilizing one?
