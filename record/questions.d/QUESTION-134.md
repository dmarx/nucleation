# QUESTION-134: When influence decides access to learning opportunities, does the resulting feedback make a marginalized specialist lose its competence, so that it cannot recover influence even when its former niche becomes relevant again?

## Why it is a question

Suppose influence and learning opportunities are coupled:

  influence → opportunities to demonstrate competence → learned competence
  → influence.

A specialist that is temporarily unpopular gets fewer observations and
less training. Its measured competence declines, and the decline
justifies further suppression. In the end it may become genuinely
incapable of the role it was specialized for. That is different from
underuse. The organization has changed the specialist's future competence
by denying it the opportunities it needed to keep that competence.

[CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)'s note of 2026-10-18 names the loop at the level of estimates.
[CLAIM-356](../claims.d/CLAIM-356.md) distinguishes passive, selective and regulated retention of a
learned capability. A representation can stay stored in the weights
without being protected against interference from later training. A
specialist can stay nominally present without being able to reassert
influence. [CLAIM-362](../claims.d/CLAIM-362.md) asks that a process keep access to consequential
roles. This question asks what makes that access real: recovery requires
that the competence survive the period of disuse.

The niche is partly endogenous. Operators' influence changes which
evidence is later encountered and used to evaluate them ([CLAIM-297](../claims.d/CLAIM-297.md)). A
system evaluated by probes that also supply its training signal can
become excellent at the probes without keeping the intended capacity
([CLAIM-266](../claims.d/CLAIM-266.md)).

## What would answer it

An experiment separating three things:

- **loss of influence**: the specialist's weight falls;
- **loss of competence**: its performance in its niche, measured on
  held-out evaluation it was not selected on, falls;
- **loss of access**: the training and evaluation opportunities it
  receives fall.

It needs two distributions: one that generates the system's adaptive
selection pressures, and another that independently checks what
capabilities it retains. The decisive result would be a specialist whose
competence on the independent distribution is lost through deprivation
and does not return when its contexts recur, against a control whose
opportunities are decoupled from its influence.

## What would not answer it

A specialist that loses influence and keeps its competence. That is
under-use, which [QUESTION-132](QUESTION-132.md)'s recovery exponent already covers.
