# NOTE-tmp8zw3r: Łukasik et al. — Multiple origins of interdependent endosymbiotic complexes in a genus of cicadas

## Contribution

It turns two reports of a strange event, *Hodgkinia* splitting in one
*Tettigades* species ([LIT-1563](../literature.d/LIT-1563.md)) and in *Magicicada* ([LIT-1612](../literature.d/LIT-1612.md)), into a
pattern within one genus: repeated, independent, of variable depth, and
apparently irreversible.

## Key results

- **Repeated splitting** (Abstract). "a single ancestral Hodgkinia
  lineage has split at least six independent times in Tettigades over
  the last 4 million years, resulting in complexes of between two and
  six distinct Hodgkinia lineages per host."
- **Uneven parts** (Abstract). Genomes in a complex "differ
  dramatically in relative abundance, genome size, organization, and
  gene content". Each lineage "retains a small set of core genes
  involved in genetic information processing", and the gene loss
  "suggests that extensive sharing of gene products among symbiont cells
  must occur".
- **The whole keeps the old job** (Abstract). Complexes "encode nearly
  complete sets of genes present on the ancestral single lineage and
  presumably perform the same functions as symbionts that have not
  undergone splitting".
- **Cells, not only genomes** (Results, microscopy). Probes for the
  rRNA variant of one genome "stain only a subset of cells", and probes
  for different genomes give "nonoverlapping signal" in *T. chilensis*,
  extending the earlier result for *T. undata* and *M. tredecim*. No
  ultrastructural difference among *Hodgkinia* cells across species.
- **Depth tracks time** (Discussion). Lineage number "positively
  correlates with the time since the first split": >3 My and 5 to 7
  lineages in two clades, about 1 My and 2 to 3 in the *undata* clade.
  "This suggests that the initiation of splitting begets more splitting
  and that symbiotic complexity cannot be ratcheted back once started."
  Splitting is "not necessarily clocklike".
- **Nonadaptive, and costly** (Discussion). "We have previously
  speculated that the process of Hodgkinia lineage splitting is
  nonadaptive for the cicada host and perhaps deleterious in some
  cases." More cell types likely raise the cost of maintenance and
  transmission; compartmentalized pathways "may result in the
  Hodgkinia population becoming less efficient in making nutrients";
  "each click of the ratchet-like process of splitting makes it
  increasingly difficult for the cicada to control its endosymbiont
  population."

## Assumptions

- Lineage counts come from assembled genomes and marker sequences; the
  number of independent splits ("at least six") is a lower bound from
  the gene phylogenies.
- The time scale comes from host phylogeny calibrated by the authors;
  the deep relationships among *Tettigades* species are "not
  well-supported in any dataset".
- "Presumably perform the same functions" is inferred from gene content,
  not measured as nutrient output.

## Concepts

- **Lineage splitting** — one symbiont lineage giving rise to several
  co-resident lineages, each losing different genes, so that they
  become interdependent.
- **Symbiotic complex** — the set of *Hodgkinia* lineages in one host.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-177](../cases.d/CASE-177.md)** takes three facts from this work. Two hold: "at least six
  independent times in *Tettigades* over four million years" and
  "together the complexes encode nearly the ancestral gene set". The
  third, "two to six lineages per host", is the abstract's figure; the
  discussion gives "5 to 7 Hodgkinia lineages" for the two oldest
  clades, which may count lineages across a clade's populations rather
  than per host. The case's note says so and leaves it unverified until
  Figure 3 or the datasets are read.
- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)** (evidence, through [CASE-177](../cases.d/CASE-177.md)): the paper's own reading
  is the case's "More parts, more dependence, no new function" and
  "constituent change that costs the whole", stated by the authors as
  speculation. It adds two things the case lacks: splitting appears
  irreversible ("cannot be ratcheted back"), and it is said to reduce
  host control (C) with each step.
- **[THEORY-1106](../theory.d/THEORY-1106.md)** (contrast, background): the transmission paper's
  claim of host compensation builds on the cost named here.

## Limitations

- Nonadaptiveness is the authors' interpretation, not tested; they ask
  why splitting happens only in some species and do not settle it.
- No measure of nutrient provision or host fitness.
- The SI (datasets of lineage counts per sample) was not read.
