summary: >-
  Gerardi & Maestri (2020). A firm and a worker who privately knows a
  persistent low or high cost meet for potentially infinitely many
  periods; the firm can commit only to one-period menus, and the worker
  can quit for good. Once the firm learns the type it holds the worker to
  his reservation wage, so revealing a low cost forfeits all future rent:
  the ratchet effect. Impatient parties still allow the type to be learned
  for any prior; with patience, separation with both types employed is
  impossible, and as patience tends to one the outcome is unique: a
  pooling contract forever, with nothing learned, below a prior threshold,
  and firing the high-cost worker at once above it, both inefficient.
  Allowing rehiring yields a folk theorem near the commitment payoff.

# LIT-tmpbit9m: Dynamic contracting with limited commitment and the ratchet effect

Gerardi, D. & Maestri, L. (2020). Dynamic contracting with limited commitment and the ratchet effect. *Theoretical Economics* 15(2): 583–623. doi:10.3982/TE2449.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpoec9n](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpoec9n.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **The ratchet.** "The ratchet effect is in place since the firm has the
  incentive to change the terms of trade and offer more demanding contracts
  when it learns that the worker is highly productive" (abstract, p. 583).
- **Patience stops learning or ends the relationship.** "If the prior
  probability of the worker being productive is low, the firm offers a
  pooling contract and no information is ever revealed. In contrast, if
  this prior probability is high, the firm fires the unproductive worker at
  the beginning of the relationship" (p. 583); "In both cases, the limiting
  equilibrium allocation is inefficient" (p. 584).
- **Impatience lets the firm learn.** "when the discount factor is not too
  high, the firm is able to extract the worker’s private information
  independently of the value of the prior" (p. 584).
- **A remedy.** "rehiring is another possible remedy to the ratchet effect"
  (p. 586).

## Standing in the record

Registered at the owner's request as a stand-in for Freixas, Guesnerie and
Tirole (1985), [LIT-tmpfzy5q](LIT-tmpfzy5q.md), which could not be fetched. It stands in for
the phenomenon and the setting, a principal unable to commit who revises
terms on revealed performance, not for that paper's results: it cites it
only as one of the two-period papers that "pioneered" the subject. It adds
what the pasted bibliography on emergency readiness lacks: in a long
relationship the ratchet can stop learning altogether or make the principal
cut the relationship, rather than only distort output, and resumable
relationships undo it. Its setting is a labour relationship, not planning
or budgeting, so carrying it to organizational budget ratchets is an
analogy. Filed from it: [THEORY-tmp6sg3n](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp6sg3n.md).
