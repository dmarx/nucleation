# CLAIM-946: Restoring a deviation to the shared structure does not tell a population whose members evaluate one another's interpretations from one whose members copy one another's answers: members who copy answers about a shared world, most of them agreeing, restore a minority's deviation rather than average it, so restoration cannot be the condition that distinguishes a shared perspective from coordinated feedback in general

## The claim

[TERM-148](../terms.d/TERM-148.md)'s third condition asks that a population restore a
minority's deviation to its shared structure, and not average it into
the rest. Its benchmark was unconditional averaging of members' states,
under which a deviant share f moves the rest by f of the deviation, a
dilution ratio of f/(1 − f).

In [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md) members interact through answers, not states: each
moves its prototype toward the stimulus under the label its partner
gave. A deviant member's answers differ from the majority's only near
the boundaries of the deviant category. So the majority's answers pull
the deviant back, and the deviant's answers pass very little to the
majority. Unconditional conformity restored the deviation with a
dilution of 0.024 [0.004, 0.045], against the threshold of 0.125 stated
in advance, and stayed near that in every setting tried after the
verdict. Enforcement by a referee (0.031) and checking against the world
(−0.030) also passed. The condition does not separate them.

So restoration is what coordinated feedback on answers does anyway, and
cannot be what tells a perspective from it. This meets the failure rule
the term was filed with, and bears on [CLAIM-417](CLAIM-417.md)'s defeater, that
"organizational perspective" cannot be told from coordinated feedback in
general, for this reading. [TERM-149](../terms.d/TERM-149.md), the rival reading, asks
instead that uptake follow a standard of evidence; the claim says
nothing against it.

## What it does not say

- Not that A8's "disagreement has consequences" is wrong, only that
  restoration is not a test of it.
- Not about populations that average states directly, or in which a
  deviant majority meets a minority.
