# LIT-tmpwwll5: Motivational trade-offs and potential pain experience in hermit crabs

Mirjam Appel & Robert W. Elwood (2009), *Applied Animal Behaviour
Science* 119(1–2):120–124 — DOI-10.1016/j.applanim.2009.03.013

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read beyond the abstract.*

- **Graded cost.** Crabs in preferred or unpreferred shell species were
  tested "subjecting their abdomens to electric shocks of gradually
  increasing intensity."
- **The threshold moves with the resource.** "The first observable
  response was not affected by shell species but those in preferred
  shells evacuated at a higher shock level than those in poor quality
  shells."
- **The authors' reading.** "Thus, they seem to trade-off the requirement
  to retain a high quality shell with that of avoidance of the noxious
  stimulus." They take the findings as consistent with pain rather than
  a nociceptive reflex.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the test CLAIM-tmp83ge2 proposes. Do the data
look continuous or thresholded? Each crab's act is all-or-none: it stays
until some shock and then leaves. But a continuous indifference curve,
probed by raising the cost at a fixed resource, also gives an
all-or-none switch, at the point where the curve is crossed. What tells
the regimes apart is whether that switch point moves with the resource.
Here it does: a better shell raises the shock at which the crab leaves.
That is the compensatory pattern, and it is evidence against a level
threshold at the shock levels tested. With two shell qualities, though,
it is two points on a curve. ALARM's override (THEORY-090) predicts that
the switch point stops rising once the resource is good enough, a
ceiling at c*. Showing whether that happens needs a range of resource
qualities and shocks up to injury, which the design, as the abstract
describes it, does not have. The unaffected "first observable response"
could be a fixed reflex beneath the trade-off. That would be the two-layer picture
CLAIM-tmp83ge2's first defeater names: a currency and an override as
separate mechanisms in one animal.
