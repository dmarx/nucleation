# CLAIM-tmpon3g9: The use-level counterpart of Saussure's value is an utterance's contrast with the alternatives its speaker could have used in context; values do not cross between languages, which is why fidelity is graded transport through an anchored correspondence rather than identity of value

## The claim

This is the record's reply of 2026-10-10 to [CLAIM-tmpwevyr](CLAIM-tmpwevyr.md). It is a
proposal, not a decisive refutation: it grants the objection's premises,
denies that its conclusion follows, and offers a restatement of the
structuralist premise whose test is its defeat condition.

**The premises, granted.** Saussure's value is fixed inside one language
state ([THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md): "The account concerns the relations inside a language
state"). French *mouton* "may share a signification with English *sheep* but
not its value, because English also has *mutton* for the meat". A
translation moves a text into another language state, where every value is
fixed afresh. And value is a fact of *langue*, while [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s
observables, "stance, footing, alignment, expected uptake", are facts of
utterances in use. The objection's third point, that nothing in the line
argues that a relational account of the language system carries over to
utterances, is conceded outright. [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md)'s last paragraph, which says the
manuscript's observables track value, overreaches.

**The conclusion, denied.** From "values do not cross" the objection infers
that the premise "argues against transport". What it argues against is
*identity* of value across languages, and the line does not claim that.
[CLAIM-106](CLAIM-106.md) makes fidelity "a graded, potentially asymmetric property of
transport, not an equivalence relation", with equivalence only as its
zero-distortion limit. *Mouton* and *sheep* are the paradigm of why: a
rendering of *mouton* by *sheep* keeps the signification and changes the
value, so it is faithful in some respects and not in others, which is a
graded verdict, not a yes or no. And transport between two systems whose
relations are internal to each needs a correspondence fixed from outside
both, which [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s own qualification from relative representations
already says: "for transport between systems they work only once something
ties the anchor in one to the anchor in the other". So "values do not
cross" is the reason fidelity is graded transport through an anchored
correspondence. It is not a reason against transport.

**The restatement at the level of use.** The objection's own suggested
remedy was "a restatement of [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md) at the level of use, where stance and
footing live, with a reading that supports a relational account there". The
use-level counterpart of value is an utterance's contrast with the
alternatives its speaker could have used in that context. That is the
premise of the rational-speech-act model ([THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md): "the interpretation
of a fixed form depends on what else the speaker could have said"), and it
is what Bergen, Goodman and Levy found in people ([LIT-809](../literature.d/LIT-809.md)): a novel
symbol's interpretation is fixed by its contrast with the alternative the
speaker could have used. Read so, the premise is relational where the
observables are, and Saussure is precedent for the form of the idea, not
its source. [CLAIM-tmp9negs](CLAIM-tmp9negs.md) shows how such contrasts enter a fidelity
criterion: each language's listener computes them with its own
alternatives, and the criterion compares the results.

## What it answers

[CLAIM-tmpwevyr](CLAIM-tmpwevyr.md), on [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md) and [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md). The premises are granted, the
third point is conceded, and the conclusion is answered by the distinction
between identity of value and graded transport. The objection stays open,
because whether use-level contrast is the structuralist premise restated or
a replacement for it is arguable, and this claim does not settle that.

## What it does not say

- It does not say Saussure's value *is* utterance contrast. Value is a fact
  of the language system; contrast with contextual alternatives is a fact of
  use. The claim says the second plays, for the line's observables, the role
  the first plays for terms.
- It does not say the correspondence is solved. Which correspondence of
  contexts and alternatives is admissible, fixed independently of the
  fidelity it is used to measure, is [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md).
- It does not answer [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s other qualification: in [LIT-809](../literature.d/LIT-809.md)'s
  experiment the contrast works only because one alternative is tied to an
  object by resemblance, so use-level contrast comes with reference, not
  instead of it.
