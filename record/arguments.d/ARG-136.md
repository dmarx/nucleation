# ARG-136: The branching example A gave in reply shows the owner's bulk-and-tail pattern when it is read as a separation of scales, and not when it is read as a gap in support

## The inference

1. A7 §2, after the cutoff table: "So the system exhibits exactly the
   qualitative behavior you're suggesting: **a substantial concentration
   of probability at small sizes, alongside an increasingly extended but
   sparsely populated tail**."
2. A7 §4: "In a conventional continuous critical transition, there isn't
   usually a genuine gap between the bulk and the tail."
3. In the cascade ([CASE-303](../cases.d/CASE-303.md)) the median stays at 2 while s_c runs
   from 187 to about 2 × 10⁶ as R goes from 0.9 to 0.999, and the law
   is strictly decreasing at every size.
4. So the example bears out the owner's sentence read as two scales
   pulling apart ([CLAIM-829](../claims.d/CLAIM-829.md)), and tells against it read as a gap
   ([CLAIM-845](../claims.d/CLAIM-845.md)). The percolation and epidemic runs ([CASE-305](../cases.d/CASE-305.md))
   find the gap only above threshold.

A's two sentences are not in conflict. They answer different readings of
"separated", and A does not say so.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** For
  reading (b), a cascade whose median grew in step with the cutoff would
  falsify it. The Borel law rules that out: the median is fixed by P(S = 1)
  and P(S = 2), which barely change near R = 1. For the denial of reading
  (a), a near-side model with a gap would falsify it. None of the three
  checked has one.
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** Partly. A bulk
  with a long power-law tail and no transition is also produced by a
  mixture of exponential rates ([CLAIM-769](../claims.d/CLAIM-769.md)). What separates reading (b)
  from that rival is the movement of the cutoff with a control parameter,
  which a fixed mixture lacks. A single snapshot of a distribution cannot
  separate them.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** The "bulk scale" is
  measured by the median and the 90th percentile, and the "tail scale" by
  s_c. The 90th percentile does drift (21, 54, 62), slowly and with a
  bound, while s_c grows a hundredfold per decade of 1 − R. The
  conclusion needs only the ratio to diverge, which it does.

Proposed: the example supports reading (b), and supports the denial of
reading (a) below a continuous transition, for the mean-field cascade and
2D percolation.
