# CLAIM-002: A text's identity across retellings is a family of relations, namely identity of the artifact, continuity of transmission and similarity of communicative function, which is why a distant retelling can be recognizably descended from an original it no longer resembles

## The claim

A50 §7: "His many translations aren't alternative perfect realizations of a
single invariant structure. They occupy different regions of a multidimensional
space of partial preservation ... not only can different translations preserve
different structures, but **the act of translation changes the conditions for
subsequent translation**. That may be the most consequential point. The object
being transported is itself undergoing reconstruction, and each reconstruction
becomes the source for the next."

A52 §13.5: "Develop the philosophical idea that a text's identity across
realizations may be characterized by a structured family of preservation and
distortion relations. Distinguish identity of an artifact, continuity of
transmission, and similarity of communicative function. Consider how these
different notions jointly explain why a distant retelling may remain
recognizably descended from an original despite substantial semantic or
pragmatic change."

It is an answer to [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md) on its second horn, with a reason the horn needed:
descent is not similarity.

## Where it went

The manuscript's conclusion asks "which differences and relationships can be
recognized across a specified family of transformations", which keeps the
family and loses the three relations and the point about descent. Nothing argued
against it.

Proposal v5 restated it once more (A82 §9.4, under U30): "Propose that
continuity across transformations can be characterized by a family of directed
preservation relations. Distinguish historical descent, pragmatic similarity,
and exact equivalence. Revisit Hofstadter's analogical conception of identity."
C6 dropped it; its two uses of "descent" are gradient descent.
