# LIT-379: Anger makes fake news viral online

Chuai & Zhao (2020), arXiv:2004.10399 (v1 22 April 2020, v3 27 August 2020;
cs.SI).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp382zf](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp382zf.md).*

- **Data.** 22,479 fake posts tagged by Weibo's fact-checking committee
  after wide spread, and 10,000 true posts from verified accounts,
  2011–2016. Emotion is the share of a post's emotional terms in a
  hand-labelled five-emotion lexicon of 6,155 terms (87.1% coverage).
- **Anger up, joy down.** Mean anger share is 0.165 in fake and 0.112 in
  true news, joy 0.443 against 0.609; the gaps are larger between highly
  retweeted fake and lowly retweeted true posts, and hold under two
  classifiers and a rank measure. Disgust, sadness and fear are
  inconsistent.
- **Retweets.** In linear models of retweets within 48 hours with
  controls, anger share has coefficient 23.96 and joy −29.56 among fake
  posts (R² 0.084), 22.28 and −35.98 among all posts (R² 0.134). The
  paper's "5.8 more retweets … in fake news" is the all-news figure;
  within fake news it is about 5.4.
- **Elsewhere.** On unlabelled English COVID-19 and election tweets
  anger predicts reaching 10 retweets (logit 0.215); the English
  fake/true gaps are small (0.123 against 0.110) or rest on 478 tweets.
- **Survey.** 1,291 responses to 15 posts; anger-dominated posts (two
  of them) drew slightly more anxiety-management and
  information-sharing motive than joy-dominated ones, at p ≈ 0.02–0.04.
- **Intervention.** Flagging posts above 20% anger would catch 46% of
  highly retweeted fake news and 22% of highly retweeted true news, on
  the assumption that a flagged post is never retweeted.
- **Design.** Observational throughout; "causally" in the text means
  "with controls in a regression".
