# LIT-tmpiu2vx: An Emerging Dilemma for Reciprocal Causation

Caleb Hazelwood (2023), *Philosophy of Science 91(3):595–614 (July 2024)* — DOI-10.1017/psa.2023.124

## Key takeaways

- If natural selection is an emergent, population-level causal process, it is composed of (not caused by) individual-level processes like niche construction, so it cannot be both cause and effect of niche construction; denying that selection is emergent or causal fares no better — EES advocates must drop reciprocal causation involving selection.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

In debates over the extended evolutionary synthesis, "reciprocal causation" is the view that adaptive evolution runs both ways, with organisms and environments acting on each other through niche construction and natural selection. Hazelwood argues this is incompatible with treating natural selection as a metaphysically emergent causal process. That emergent character creates a dilemma neither horn of which saves reciprocal causation, so EES proponents should give up the claim that selection takes part in cycles of reciprocal causation.
