# NOTE-tmpwl20t: Choifer — Interpretational Complexities in Developmental Research and a Piagetian Reading of the False-Belief Task

## Contribution

A philosophical defence and reinterpretation of Piaget's egocentrism
against its later refutations.

## Key results

1. **The proposal.** "My new interpretation of the test suggests two
   different ways of perspective-taking of which one is egocentric in
   Piaget’s sense." (p. 923).
2. **The misreading.** "This conclusion displays a crucial
   misunderstanding of Piagetian thought." (p. 930); "according to Piaget,
   childish egocentrism cannot increase but only decrease with age" (p.
   931).
3. **Early switching.** Non-subject-related perspective-taking "is already
   developed in the second year of life and is manifested in the child’s
   ability to switch between different vantage points"; "This switching
   proceeds egocentrically, i.e., without connection to any specific
   subject." (p. 936).
4. **Against early false belief.** "the implicit false-belief paradigms
   cannot secure the status of the other person as other" (p. 947).

## Assumptions

False belief is the criterion of self–other differentiation; belief is
meta-representational.

## Concepts

Childish versus adult egocentrism; decentration; subject-related versus
non-subject-related perspective-taking.

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-837](../literature.d/LIT-837.md)** (Piaget), support for a charitable reading of his concept.
- **The owner's question**, a qualification from the other side: on this
  reading a three-year-old's "we" would be undifferentiated rather than
  recursive, which also avoids second-order theory of mind. The paper says
  nothing of sharing or fairness.
- **Tomasello's 2016 reply on joint intentionality** (on the base branch),
  a contrast: applying "sharing" to infants is the observer's ascription.

## Limitations

No new data; the criterion that passing false belief marks differentiation
is stipulated; it does not engage the colour-filter data.
