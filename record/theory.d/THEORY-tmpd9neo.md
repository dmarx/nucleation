# THEORY-tmpd9neo: Müller proposes that social interaction is self-organization in multilayer networks of neural, physiological and behavioral signals, in which circular causation across levels gives rise to dynamical and structural order parameters that in turn constrain the interacting individuals, and predicts that coordination emerges as coupling across layers increases, is most stable within an intermediate range of coupling, and shows bidirectional influence between order parameters and local dynamics

## Source

Müller (2026), "Self-organization and circular causation as synergetic
foundations of social interaction: a multilayer network perspective",
*Frontiers in Network Physiology* 6: 1776969, LIT-2252, skimmed in
NOTE-tmpnfczs.

## The account

Individuals in interaction form coupled dynamical systems whose layers
(neural, autonomic, behavioral) are nodes and edges of a multilayer
network, with within- and cross-frequency coupling. When circular
causation passes a threshold, the dyad or group converges on shared order
parameters, dynamical (synchrony, phase relations) or structural (stable
topologies, hyper-brain modules), which constrain the members. Six
predictions follow: emergence with coupling, nonlinear transitions,
multilayer amplification, metastability, instability under excessive
coupling, and bidirectional influence across scales.

## Bearing

On CLAIM-526, its sixth prediction would test whether social order
parameters have dynamics of their own, but only by intervention, not by
bidirectional dependence (CLAIM-334). On CLAIM-327, its order parameters
constrain only if read and acted on. Notes only.
