# LIT-160: Units and Levels of Selection

Elisabeth Lloyd (2005), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/ENTRIES/selection-units/>

## Key takeaways

- The "units of selection" debate bundles four separable research questions — interactor, replicator/reproducer, manifestor-of-adaptation and beneficiary — pursued by two multilevel schools (Wrightian evolutionary-change and Fisherian adaptationist/kin-selection) with different models, so many apparent disagreements are answers to different questions.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Evolution by natural selection is biology's crowning theory, yet which entities are selected is contested. Lloyd clarifies the debates by distinguishing four research questions, often confused, and two schools of multilevel genetics. She then reviews particular debates — group selection, species selection, genic selectionism, genic pluralism, transitions in individuality, holobionts — showing which questions, alone or combined, drive each.
