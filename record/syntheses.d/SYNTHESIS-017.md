# SYNTHESIS-017: Records secure traceability, not correctness

*A synthesis of records-of-inquiry. Version 4 takes the exchange "Alignment consequences brainstorm" (ChatGPT, 2026-11-26) into account, under ADR-151; the exchange's map of turns is in its curation entry. Its part on controlled forgetting crosses into record-as-apparatus, where most of those entries also sit. It adds nothing. Its standing is its components'.*

## The question

What can a record of a developing argument secure about that argument,
and what does it not secure? And since keeping everything has a cost,
what may such a record cool, condense or forget without losing what it
is kept for?

## The argument

**A record secures traceable change, not preservation.** A developing
argument drops things legitimately, so a record of it cannot aim at
keeping everything; what it can secure is change that is announced and
located (CLAIM-189), and a manuscript is then a view checked against that
record rather than the argument's only copy (CLAIM-178). The record's
grammar limits even that: a move that fits none of its schemes is forced
or lost until noticed (CLAIM-181), and CLAIM-178 takes this in as its
limit, that the record shows what was dropped only of what it held. The
interlocutor's reading gives traceability its object, the accountability
structure of what was asserted, why, how it was challenged and what
justified its revision (CLAIM-250).

**Neither conclusions nor support topology fix revision, and sufficiency
is relative.** The exchange of 2026-10-14 fixed by construction what such
a record is and is not. Two histories can agree on every current
conclusion and call for different revisions (CLAIM-294, CASE-093), and two
records with the same support graph can call for different revisions when
one support fails (CLAIM-293, CASE-089). A state sufficient for a family
of decisions under a model makes the rest of the history worthless for
those decisions, and stops being sufficient when a source is discredited,
the model is revised or the decisions change (CLAIM-300, CASE-082).
Sufficiency, accountability and correctness are three properties, each
able to hold while the other two fail (CLAIM-299, with CASE-082, CASE-084
and CASE-093), and CLAIM-299 draws the consequence for CLAIM-189:
traceability is not sufficiency either. What kept justifications can add
is therefore not information for decisions the current state already
suffices for, but a wider family of decisions and decisions a bounded
agent could not otherwise afford (CLAIM-295, resting on CLAIM-294 and
CLAIM-300). Tracing what a defeated premise carried, which propagates
obligations to review, and reopening an alternative whose defeater is
defeated are the operations this supports (CASE-086, CASE-090).

**Keeping also costs retrieval.** The owner's point, prompted by the
record's size, is that what the record keeps is all equally present:
retired and superseded content, and redundant content that later work
developed on, still weigh on retrieval and discovery (CLAIM-tmpu1fef). The
assistant's count shows where the weight lies: retired entries are under
2 % of the record and Proposed ones a quarter, and dated notes are about
a quarter of the bytes of claims, so the owner's later clauses, not the
first, carry most of it; and the record already keeps one cold store, git
history, for versions though not for entries. The design question that
opens is what a governed record should cool, condense or erase, and how
what it keeps within easy reach can be governed apart from what it
endorses (QUESTION-tmpz8xkx, the owner's). A's answer begins by
separating dimensions: identity from standing, as the record already
does, and both from availability and from fidelity, so that retiring is
an epistemic operation, cooling an allocation of attention and condensing
a change of representation (CLAIM-tmp5zt7f). Availability is logical, not
storage (TERM-tmp96b3g), which lets the record say that an entry is in
force and out of the way; consolidation keeps the same judgement through a
different representation, where supersession replaces the judgement
(TERM-tmp3t5jq). How long an entry has survived is one more dimension, and
A's generational hypothesis, that most new entries are short-lived, is
empirical and untested (CLAIM-tmp48l62).

**The cost of a tier is partly the corpus's.** A extends the record's
pricing of filing (CLAIM-303, in SYNTHESIS-015) to the pricing of a tier:
maintenance, retrieval noise, expected reactivation, expected loss while
unavailable, and transition (CLAIM-tmpg2wk1). The assistant's argument
narrows it: four of those terms are the entry's own, but retrieval noise
is a property of the corpus (CLAIM-314), so it must be taken over the hot
set (ARG-tmpedexz). The net effect is A's second hypothesis: past some
point, growth of what is accessible raises the cost of realizing an
answer by more than holding the answer saved (CLAIM-tmpdid0k, adding the
net effect to CLAIM-314's recognition cost, and the limit QUESTION-095
asks about). The record's generated views add a cost of their own: an
entry repeated across indexes, tag pages, chains and syntheses is met many
times by an unrestricted search, a median of 25 generated pages per
claim, which inflates its prominence without adding evidence
(CLAIM-tmp6toz6, the retrieval-side form of CLAIM-301's danger).

**What may be cooled is fixed by roots and uses, not by standing.** What
is live is relative to declared roots, and roots are not all Active
claims, which would confuse endorsement with relevance; A names five
kinds of liveness, referential, operational, corrective, developmental and
normative (CLAIM-tmpi0o8t). An entry's retention requirement can be
computed as a join over its uses (CLAIM-tmpby6hz), but not when it is
kept as one of two independently sufficient grounds: that requirement is
disjunctive and has no least element, so the join holds only for
conjunctive requirements (CLAIM-tmpwlw8t, the assistant's, demonstrated).
This is CLAIM-293's point carried from revision to retention, that the
topology of support does not fix what must be kept, and the record's
grammar already marks the difference where support is filed as separate
arguments (CLAIM-293, note of 2026-11-26). Cooling must leave
dependencies visible, so that archival does not become a way of silencing
what is inconvenient (CLAIM-tmpdkwbk, citing luria's rule that a
suppression must not become a silence); the record already reports what
rests on a retired entry (CLAIM-664). And retention by observed use
confirms itself, since what is requested depends on what is visible, so a
record that forgets only by its own usage can optimize away the conditions
of its development; A's remedy is an exploration budget for dormant
material (CLAIM-tmp6tc1j).

**What may be condensed is fixed by the corrections it must survive.** A
condensation can keep every present consequence and lose the consequences
of a later correction. The assistant's result says exactly when it does
not: a condensation is safe for a declared, deterministic family of
corrections and queries exactly when it merges no two records some
sequence of those corrections tells apart, the Myhill–Nerode construction
applied to records (CLAIM-tmpnf6q4, demonstrated). It generalizes
CLAIM-294, which is the case where the condensation is to the current
conclusions (CLAIM-294, note of 2026-11-26), and answers the least-state
question for that case at zero tolerance (QUESTION-105, note of
2026-11-26). Condensing may also do more than save: on the owner's
hypothesis that collapsed equivalences become new distinctions
(CLAIM-762), consolidation may make arguments into units of further work
(CLAIM-tmp8gkmi, supposed on CLAIM-762), and condensation that preserves
capability may widen the inquiry frontier, CLAIM-761's stored work applied
to condensing (CLAIM-tmpyk9r5).

## Where it stands

The formal core is firm: CLAIM-293, CLAIM-294, CLAIM-299 and CLAIM-300 are
demonstrated and in force on A's constructions, and the exchange's two
results on condensing and retention (CLAIM-tmpnf6q4, CLAIM-tmpwlw8t) are
demonstrated and Proposed. The normative reading of what the record is
for (CLAIM-189, CLAIM-250, CLAIM-295) is Proposed, and CASE-086 and
CASE-090 illustrate rather than test. The whole design of controlled
forgetting is proposed and unmeasured: the owner's claim and question,
A's dimensions, tiers, prices, roots and hypotheses. The tests are leads:
retention policies compared on a frozen snapshot (LEAD-tmpeqy94), a
replay of forgetting policies over the record's history (LEAD-tmpe579v),
a trial of logical cooling on one line (LEAD-tmpolbqp), mark-and-review
collection from declared roots (LEAD-tmp79qro), a dry-run retention
diagnostic (LEAD-tmpvevob), and compaction through a synthesis
(LEAD-tmpd85zk).

What stays open: the least state a family of revisions needs beyond
declared deterministic corrections, with added facts, nonzero tolerance or
its information-theoretic form (QUESTION-105, refined by QUESTION-188 and
QUESTION-225); which mechanism a structured record's advantage runs
through (QUESTION-108, with CASE-091 as the design); how to judge
sufficiency when inquiry changes its own decision family (QUESTION-112);
what a manuscript's choice of claims must preserve (QUESTION-258); how a
record that automates incorporation keeps it from outrunning evaluation
(QUESTION-471); and how the record should triage its open questions
(QUESTION-496, with CLAIM-763 and CLAIM-764 as candidates). QUESTION-229,
on delayed Lotka–Volterra communities, is here by its line field alone;
no component cites it. QUESTION-288 and QUESTION-289 are Answered, by
CLAIM-294 and CLAIM-300.

## Not stated on the record

- **Keeping the accountability structure as worth its cost.** CLAIM-250
  with CLAIM-295's decision family; "worth" turns on cost, which CLAIM-295
  disclaims and QUESTION-098 holds. The exchange's tier prices
  (CLAIM-tmpg2wk1) would supply a cost; no entry sets them against
  CLAIM-295's values.
- **A's retention values and CLAIM-295.** A's reconstruction, dependency
  and developmental option values (CLAIM-tmpg2wk1) resemble CLAIM-295's
  wider family of decisions; no entry says they are the same.
- **Legitimate dropping as relative to a changing decision family.**
  CLAIM-189's dissolution and loss read through CLAIM-300's relativity and
  QUESTION-112; it needs a bridging premise no component holds.
- **Which of CLAIM-295's values the record's own structure delivers.** The
  computational clause has only one example, and a state built for the
  tasks could carry the same index.
- **Whether CLAIM-299's three properties are independent beyond the three
  separations shown.**
- **CLAIM-178's manuscript test.** No draft written against the record
  has been checked beside one written straight from an exchange.
- **Self-confirming retention and entrenchment.** CLAIM-tmp6tc1j's
  self-confirming allocation of attention resembles CLAIM-184's
  entrenching anchors on the retrieval side; no entry joins them.
- **Condensing alternative support.** CLAIM-tmpnf6q4's criterion and
  CLAIM-tmpwlw8t's disjunctive requirement are stated separately; no entry
  says what a correction-safe condensation of two independently sufficient
  grounds keeps.

## What it does not say

That the record's traceability is shown to be worth keeping; that a
dependency or justification graph suffices for revision (CLAIM-293), or
that a counterexample to conclusion-only memory is evidence for one; that
CLAIM-294 is a case for provenance, since its body rules that reading
out; that an accountable record is correct (CASE-084); or that any
forgetting policy would help the record. None has been tried.
