# LIT-1138: The Social Identity Buffer: How Cognitive, Affective, and Evaluative Ties Sustain Fan Commitment and Loyalty Amidst Club Controversies

Čater, T., Čater, B. & Ivašković, I. (2026). The Social Identity Buffer: How
Cognitive, Affective, and Evaluative Ties Sustain Fan Commitment and Loyalty
Amidst Club Controversies. *Sport Marketing Quarterly*, online first, 1–20.
doi:10.1177/10616934261433626. Open access, CC BY. The issue pagination,
35(3):263–282, is from OpenAlex and is not in the copy read; page
references in the record are to the online-first pages 1–20.

## Key takeaways

- All three identity dimensions predict a smaller imagined commitment
  decline (cognitive β −0.22 and −0.27), and commitment change predicts
  loyalty change (β 0.63–0.73); commitment mediates evaluative identity,
  and "No significant mediation effects were found for cognitive identity"
  (p. 9).
- The authors read the moderation as "stronger fan opposition to a club's
  decision reduces the protective effect of all identity dimensions on
  commitment change" (p. 11); the simple slopes as coded (p. 10) run the
  other way, and the paper does not reconcile them.
- Strong identifiers oppose the decision less (Table 2), so in this sample
  allegiance and judgment of the decision are not held apart.
