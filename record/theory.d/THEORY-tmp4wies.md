# THEORY-tmp4wies: In a soap film under a micrometre thick, Patsyk, Sivan, Segev and Bandres observe light branching as it propagates, because smooth thickness variations act as a weak correlated potential for linear guided waves; the scintillation index peaks near a distance proportional to lc v0^−2/3, branch intensities are heavier-tailed than Rayleigh, and a branch keeps its width for at least ten Rayleigh lengths

Patsyk, Sivan, Segev & Bandres (2020), LIT-tmpv09vj; read in NOTE-tmpg83d3.

## The result

A laser coupled into a free-standing soap membrane is guided as in a slab
waveguide. The effective index follows the film's thickness, so its smooth
thickness variations, reconstructed from the white-light interference
colours, act as a weak (v0 ≈ 1–5%), correlated (lc ≈ 90–350 µm) disordered
potential for the two-dimensional, linear wave equation. The light forms
filaments that keep dividing. Over 10–20 realizations, averaged across the
transverse coordinate, the scintillation index rises sharply near a
distance proportional to lc v0^−2/3, the first-branch scaling of earlier
theory (Fig. 3). Over 100 realizations in one film, the cumulative
distribution of branch intensities has a heavier tail than the Rayleigh
fit for uncorrelated disorder (Fig. 4a–b). One branch keeps its width for
at least ten Rayleigh lengths (Fig. 4e). Each of these could have come out
otherwise: diffuse speckle with Rayleigh statistics, a first-branch
distance unrelated to lc v0^−2/3, or a branch that spread by diffraction.

## What it does not show

- That the scaling exponent is −2/3: it is shown as agreement of computed
  lc v0^−2/3 marks with scintillation peaks across a few films, not as a
  fit, and the law itself is taken from earlier theory.
- That arrested diffraction is universal: one branch is shown, and the
  universality is offered, not tested.
- The anisotropic and rotational law, which is the later contribution of
  Chang et al. (2026), LIT-tmpn3i8n.
- Anything about learning, memory or computation. The effect is linear
  wave transport; that a correlated medium steering a wave into channels
  models how a network routes information is A's analogy, not the paper's.
