# NOTE-112: Poltorak — Wave-function realism and causal closure

## Contribution

Poltorak argues that "every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause" hides two parameters: the domain of closure, and whether the completeness it asserts is causal, dynamical or explanatory. Both become unstable once "physical" is indexed to rival quantum ontologies. Wave-function realism breaks closure restricted to ordinary spacetime but can keep closure relative to fundamental physics, so there is no trans-domain breach. The upshot is a dilemma for causal arguments for physicalism. A spacetime-restricted closure principle is too narrow for serious quantum ontologies, while a theory-relative one needs a non-question-begging criterion of physicality and a dynamical-to-causal bridge. The paper also argues that this gives no support to interactionist dualism, and that Hilbert space, PBR, the Born rule and Bohmian guidance cannot deliver the stronger conclusion.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (pp. 4–7): closure principles are indexed to a domain D and a completeness relation. The "criterion problem" for the physical becomes a structural requirement rather than an objection that can be deferred.
- §3 (pp. 7–9): quantum-state realism "without ontological shortcuts". PBR establishes ψ-onticity within the ontological-models framework, not a concrete causal field or a separate Hilbert-space realm.
- §4 (pp. 9–12): the Scope Argument, premises P1–P6. Wave-function realism is incompatible with spacetime-restricted C(D) and compatible with fundamental-physical C(D). §4.2 explains why this is not a causal breach.
- §5 (pp. 12–14): tests the distinction across primitive ontology and Bohmian mechanics, spacetime-state, multi-field, Everettian, nomological, dispositional and structural readings.
- §6 (pp. 14–16): nonspatiotemporality does not entail nonphysicality. Fundamental stochasticity leaves no "mental causal slot", which is discussed against Wigner, Stapp and Chalmers & McQueen (2022).
- §§7–9 (pp. 16–19): replies to six objections, then limitations (no defence of wave-function realism, nonrelativistic only, no theory of causation), and the conclusion that the pressure falls on how physicalism is formulated.
