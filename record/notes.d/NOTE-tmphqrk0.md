# NOTE-tmphqrk0: Muralidharan and Sundararaman: teacher performance pay against school inputs in Andhra Pradesh

## Contribution

The paper's subject is teacher performance pay, and its design adds what
LEAD-044 asks for: in the same randomized study (AP RESt), two other sets
of 100 schools received inputs worth about the same, so incentives and
inputs are compared at one budget in one school system.

## Key results

- **Design (section 3.3).** Five cells of 100 schools: control, group
  bonus, individual bonus, extra contract teacher, block grant. "2 out of
  the 10 project schools in each mandal were randomly allocated to each of
  5 cells", and "No school received more than one treatment".
- **Equal cost by design (sections 3.4, 7).** The bonus slope of Rs 500
  per percentage point of gain "was set so that the expected incentive
  payment per school would be approximately equal to the additional
  spending in the input treatments". In the event: "The average annual
  spending on each of the input treatments was Rs. 10,000/school, while
  the group and individual incentives programs cost roughly Rs.
  6,000/school and Rs.10,000/school respectively."
- **Effects (section 5.3, Table 9).** Incentive schools, pooled: 0.15 SD
  after one year, 0.22 SD after two. Incentives minus inputs, pooled: 0.06
  SD after one year (p = 0.091), 0.13 SD after two (p = 0.002).
- **Per arm (section 7).** "The individual incentive program spent the
  same amount per school as the input programs but produced gains in test
  scores that were three times larger than those in the input schools
  (0.27 SD vs. 0.09 SD)." The group bonus: 0.16 SD for Rs 6,000, "almost
  identical" in cost effectiveness to the individual.
- **Channel (sections 1, 6).** No difference in teacher or student
  attendance, and none in classroom processes by direct observation. By
  teachers' own unprompted reports, incentive schools gave more homework,
  extra classes, practice tests and attention to weaker children. The
  authors: "the main mechanism for the impact of the incentive program was
  not increased teacher attendance, but greater (and more effective)
  teaching effort conditional on being present."
- **Testing costs (section 7).** About Rs 5,000 per school per round; "The
  incentive program would be more cost effective than the input programs
  even after adding these costs".

## Assumptions

- Cost is the yearly flow of spending; infrastructure was left out because
  its "returns would have to be evaluated over the depreciation life cycle".
- Test scores are the outcome; the paper argues the gains are real
  learning from conceptual items and non-incentive subjects (section 5.5,
  skimmed).

## Concepts

- **Inputs** against **incentives**: resources given unconditionally
  against pay conditional on measured outcomes.
- **Group** against **individual** bonus: school-average gain against the
  gain of a teacher's own students.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-562** (placement predicts better than count). An
  organizational instance at equal budget: the arm acting on the effort of
  teachers already present did three times as much as arms adding a
  teacher or materials. Whether the arms differ in place or only in
  instrument is the question of the two readings this run files; see
  CASE-tmpjfl6r.
- **QUESTION-380**: evidence that where an equal budget enters a
  production structure can matter more than its size; the paper gives no
  predictor computed from the structure.
- **The second half of CLAIM-562** (adding can hurt): not tested; no
  school had two treatments.

## Limitations

- The two input arms are pooled in this paper.
- Place and conditionality are confounded: the effort-side arms are the
  only conditional ones.
- The channel rests on self-reports; direct observation found no
  difference.
- Two years, one state, low baseline learning.
