# SYNTHESIS-105: Learning changes what can still be learned

*A synthesis of a part of the learned-structure line; the line's synthesis, SYNTHESIS-030, is built over it and its sibling parts, and SYNTHESIS-096, the latent-capability part, shares CLAIM-697 and CLAIM-695 with it. Version 4 takes into account the lead run of 2026-11-23 on a scalar optimum of learning and the exchange "Alignment consequences brainstorm" (ChatGPT, 2026-11-26), under ADR-151. It adds nothing. Its standing is its components'.*

## The question

What does learning do to what a learner can still learn, and, since it
does something, what would make a learning process optimal when "some of
the most valuable consequences of learning are changes in what the
learner can later learn, ask and afford, which cannot be specified in
advance" (QUESTION-495, the owner's)?

## The argument

**Learning is judged by what it changes later, at a cost.** Learning,
of a declared system over a declared horizon, is an experience-dependent
change in retained organization that alters what the system can later
do, at what cost and how reliably, judged against the counterfactual in
which the experience did not occur, and kept apart from successful
learning, relaxation, selection and a fixed controller (TERM-131). The
argument for that definition is that it explicates what the record's
claims about learning need, while the definition by improved performance
explicates successful learning, which the term keeps apart (ARG-119,
from the two-step acquisition of CASE-295).

**What a learner can do now, come to do and ask move separately.** The
record holds the parts. A learned system's success is
resource-conditional, and coverage is not delivery (CLAIM-697): this is
the operational frontier. A form's costs to construct, to keep and to
move into it do not fix one another (CLAIM-569). Two forms of support
that give a party the same capabilities now can leave it different ones
later (CLAIM-675, demonstrated for support). A stabilized specification
is stored inquiry that makes later questions cheaper to ask (CLAIM-475),
with TERM-092's inquiry frontier. CLAIM-754 carries CLAIM-675's
divergence from support to a learner's own training: what it can come to
do within an acquisition budget, given a declared interface for what it
may learn from, is a frontier of its own, and the three move separately,
so no context-free scalar ranks learners. What it adds is admitted as a
discriminating prediction: checkpoints of one run with equal present
frontiers will differ in what they cost to teach held-out task families,
which CASE-294 would measure and has not.

The evidence on record is a solvable case read two ways. Scaling a
pretrained diagonal linear network's first layer by c and its readout by
1/c keeps its function and what readout-only training reaches, and
changes what full fine-tuning learns, with the better c reversing between
two main tasks (CLAIM-1043, run in CASE-407). Read as two learners with
one present function, it shows the developmental frontier varying apart
(CLAIM-1013, which corroborates CLAIM-754 through ARG-223, the assistant's
lean). Read as one learner under two procedures, since the rescaling is
exactly a change of step sizes (CLAIM-1011), it shows only that the
developmental frontier is indexed by the procedure admitted (CLAIM-1089,
the objection). On either reading the frontier depends on the interface,
and under the readout-only interface the pair does not come apart at all
(CLAIM-754, its Admission as revised at this review). Which reading
holds is the owner's call.

A's proposal from the exchange of 2026-11-26 puts two more frontiers
beside the three: what an agent can become capable of valuing, and what
can gain legitimate authority over its organization, neither assumed to
move with the others (CLAIM-tmppuz0e). How the first would be measured is
open; the separating test is LEAD-tmp18reb's.

**Training can raise present competence and lower what remains
learnable.** The owner's generalization of overfitting is
overconsolidation: relative to a declared family of later acquisitions
outside the training task distribution, continued stabilization can keep
improving the present objective while raising the budget, with held
capabilities kept, to acquire that family (CLAIM-758, the owner's idea in
A's wording). Its signature is CLAIM-754's: the operational frontier
contains its predecessor while the developmental one does not. It is a
failure only relative to that family and horizon, as a corrective
capacity's value is conditional (CLAIM-520). Reinforcement learning on
verifiable rewards that sharpens without expanding is its RL case
(CLAIM-695, the owner's idea). Whether relief has a deadline depends on
how the capacity to acquire is lost: if it decays with disuse, under
CLAIM-605's model of capacity learned by doing, practice restores it
before a threshold and not after; if training uses it up, only an
intervention that renews it does, at any point; the evidence read so far
fits the second (CLAIM-757, the assistant's, accepted by the owner).
CASE-294 is the test of both, with CLAIM-757's interventions among its
arms.

**So an acquisition is valued by what it does to later ones.** A step
that loses on the task it serves can belong to the best policy when it
lowers the cost of a later acquisition: in CASE-295, P alone nets −2,
Q alone −1, and P then Q +6 (CLAIM-756, A's, resting on CLAIM-303's
reuse). Ranking each step by its own return acquires nothing.

**A learning process has an optimum under four conditions, and two of
them are what learning that changes the learner puts at risk.** The
lead run of 2026-11-23 answers the formal half of QUESTION-495
(LEAD-011). An optimal learning policy is a well-defined scalar optimum
when there is a declared law for the tasks met given what has been
learned, one valuation for every policy compared, a finite value, and
attainment (CLAIM-1197). That learning changes which tasks the learner
will meet is not among the failures: a declared dependence makes what has
been learned part of the state of a decision process, and backward
induction gives the optimum. Three stipulated learners show that the
last three conditions are not implied by the others (ARG-227):

- When each period of learning multiplies later earnings by more than
  discounting shrinks them, investing one period more always beats
  stopping and never stopping earns nothing, so no policy is optimal; a
  bounded version has a finite supremum no policy reaches, and a finite
  horizon restores an optimum, which can lie late (CLAIM-1130, which
  stipulates exploiting as consolidation, CLAIM-758's pattern). For
  QUESTION-495's narrower question it answers the crudest case:
  investing in learnability is better than present competence exactly
  when γg > 1, and then always.
- When what is learned changes what is valued, the valuations of the
  selves the policies would produce fix no scalar optimum: judged by each
  produced self, the ranking turns on rescalings that change no self's
  preferences, and self-ratification can return two policies or none;
  judged by the present self, an optimum exists and counts nothing only a
  later valuation would see (CLAIM-1036). The change of valuation at
  issue need not be a reweighting: it can be a new evaluative
  distinction, a difference that was unavailable or inconsequential to
  the agent coming to direct its evaluation, which is substantive only
  against the agent's own consequential distinctions before and after
  (CLAIM-tmpk8knm, A's, with the assistant's test).
- Several criteria with no declared weights leave only a frontier, and
  CLAIM-754's three frontiers are such a case (CLAIM-1197's list of
  failures).

These qualify CLAIM-754's last clause, that a scalar ranks learning
processes once a task distribution, costs, horizon and evaluative
preferences are declared: a fixed distribution is more than is needed,
and the list is less than enough, since finiteness, attainment and one
valuation must be added (CLAIM-754's note of 2026-11-23). The thesis
that the frontiers move separately is untouched. Whether the
qualification is an objection is the owner's call.

## Where it stands

The premises the frontiers rest on are firm: CLAIM-569 and CLAIM-675 are
Active demonstrations, CLAIM-475 Active. The optimality results are
demonstrated constructions, checked in scratch/studies/lead-011/, and
Proposed. The frontiers, overconsolidation and the two frontiers A added
are interpretive and Proposed, and nothing that would test them has been
run: CASE-294 is a design. Whose each step is matters here: the
generalization of overfitting and QUESTION-495 are the owner's, the
frontiers A's, the optimality constructions the assistant's.

QUESTION-493, what it is for a learning system to know, and
QUESTION-494, which partition a costed memory keeps under feedback, are
the owner's and A's questions from the same exchange and stay in this
part as open. Their answers filed by the lead run of 2026-11-23 are not
integrated here: on knowing, CLAIM-1028, CLAIM-1086, CLAIM-1092,
CLAIM-1100, CLAIM-1109, CLAIM-1142 and CLAIM-1059 (which rests on
CLAIM-754); on memory, CLAIM-1116, CLAIM-1123, CLAIM-1124 and CLAIM-1189.
Each group answers its own question and touches this part's argument
only through CLAIM-697 and CLAIM-754, so each is a part of the line of
its own, with no synthesis yet. QUESTION-497, which of identifiability
and localization fixes a learner's boundary, is open.

Outside the scope, the same complements thread runs to stored work
(CLAIM-761, SYNTHESIS-106), to collapsed equivalences that become new
distinctions (CLAIM-762, SYNTHESIS-108), to the ecology of richness that
rests on CLAIM-754 (CLAIM-1038 and CLAIM-1106, SYNTHESIS-122), and to
the governance of what a system can still become, where CLAIM-tmppuz0e's
constitutional frontier is TERM-tmpmdrmj's and CLAIM-1036's dilemma is
carried to closing a possibility (CLAIM-tmp3ao8j, SYNTHESIS-tmp4kh41).

## Not stated on the record

- **The axiological frontier and the missing optimum.** That a policy
  that moves CLAIM-tmppuz0e's axiological frontier changes the learner's
  valuation in CLAIM-1036's sense, so that no optimum over such policies
  is fixed without a declared evaluator. CLAIM-tmpk8knm says what moving
  that frontier is and that CLAIM-1036's change of valuation can be of
  that kind; joining them is substitution, valid and trivial. Not filed.
- **Overconsolidation under a changing valuation.** CLAIM-758 is a
  failure only relative to a declared family and horizon. No entry says
  whether it is defined when what the learner values in that family
  changes with the learning, CLAIM-1036's case.
- **Relief's deadline as a horizon.** No entry says whether CLAIM-757's
  threshold, past which practice no longer restores the capacity to
  acquire, is a horizon of the kind that restores an optimum in
  CLAIM-1130.

## What it does not say

That real learners face unbounded returns to learning, that the
frontiers are all there are, that overconsolidation has been observed
with held capabilities kept, or that any declared evaluator of a
self-changing learner is the right one.
