# NOTE-105: Dorato — Events and the Ontology of Quantum Mechanics

## Contribution

Dorato argues that an ontology of events is precise, flexible and general enough to serve all three main primitive-ontology formulations of quantum mechanics, and also antirealist views of the wave function. Because these formulations put their basic entities in four-dimensional spacetime, events can connect the quantum picture with the manifest image. If some realism about the wave function is also needed, it must be realism about some kind of power. The second part raises difficulties for the proposal (Esfeld et al.) that in Bohmian mechanics this power is held holistically by all the particles in the universe.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1–2 (pp. 1–4): events are central to the ontology of ordinary language and common sense, so they are the best bridge between the manifest and physical images. He cites Auyang, Dieks and Haag for a similar conclusion from QFT (fn. 1).
- §3 (pp. 5–9): events fit GRWf (flashes), GRWm (matter density) and Bohmian mechanics. §3.1 compares Esfeld et al.'s (2015) neo-Cartesian, structuralist matter points (substances individuated by metrical relations). The two views would coincide only if events also lacked intrinsic powers.
- §4 (pp. 9–13): sets out the wave-function-realist options (Maudlin's primitive state, Goldstein–Zanghì nomological, Monton/Esfeld property of all particles, Albert's 3N-space, Wallace's many worlds) against two antirealist brands. One takes measurement outcomes or interactions as basic (Bohr, Rovelli); the other takes events because particle ontology fails (Haag, Pashby). Events serve both.
- §5 (pp. 13–15): on Esfeld's holistic dispositionalism, it is unclear how a global power relates to local substances that have no intrinsic powers. Moving from the universal to the effective wave function forces a change of ontology, which Dorato avoids by attributing intrinsic dispositions to events. The relation between events and powers is left open for future work.
