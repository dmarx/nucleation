# LIT-tmprh2zk: Evaluating emergency medical service performance measures

McLay, L. A. & Mayorga, M. E. (2010). Evaluating emergency medical service performance measures. *Health Care Management Science* 13(2): 124–136. doi:10.1007/s10729-009-9115-x.

## Key takeaways

*From the abstract on PubMed only; unverified until read.*

- **The question.** Whether locating ambulances to maximize coverage
  within a response-time threshold also maximizes patient survival.
- **The finding claimed.** In Hanover County, seven- and eight-minute
  thresholds "simultaneously maximize patient survival"; nine- and
  ten-minute thresholds "result in more equitable patient outcomes, with
  improved patient survival rates in rural regions".

## Standing in the record

Registered from the pasted bibliography on emergency readiness, where A
glosses it as showing that "the operational metric is not necessarily the
underlying organizational objective". That fits the paper's motivation,
as the abstract states it, better than its findings: the headline result
is that two thresholds coincide with survival maximization, and the
divergence reported is between total survival and equity. Whether a
metric tracks the objective here depends on the threshold chosen.

The paper was not read. Its neighbour, Erkut, Ingolfsson and Erdoğan
(2008), [LIT-tmprfb8b](LIT-tmprfb8b.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmp241k1](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp241k1.md) (account filed as
[THEORY-tmpagbfa](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpagbfa.md)), stands in for the shared idea, that a response-time
standard is a proxy for survival and that locating for the proxy can
differ from locating for survivors, but not for this paper's method or
Hanover County results. The two partly pull against each other: Erkut et
al. say standard coverage models "should not be used for EMS vehicle
location", while this abstract finds a threshold whose optimum is the
survival optimum; Erkut et al.'s own comparison (Table 6) also has
coverage reaching the best-known survival value once response-time
randomness or busy probabilities are modelled. Nothing the record holds
rests on this paper.
