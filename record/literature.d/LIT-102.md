# LIT-102: Pregeometry, Formal Language and Constructivist Foundations of Physics

Xerxes D. Arsiwalla, Hatem Elshatlawy, Dean Rickles (2023), *arXiv preprint (physics.hist-ph); later a chapter in Quantum Gravity and Computation (Routledge, 2025), DOI 10.4324/9781003546139-10* — [ARXIV-2311.03973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03973)

## Key takeaways

- The authors propose that Wheeler's "pregeometry" is best modelled as "structureless structure" — the syntactic types and compositional rules of a formal language such as homotopy type theory — adopting a constructivist coherentism in which space and geometry are built from relations rather than a material substrate.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The paper asks how to formalise the "structure of structures" that the foundations of physics would need, and sketches a metaphysics of pregeometric structure on which notions of quantum geometry could rest. It revisits Wheeler's pregeometry, Leibniz's monadology and modern topos-theoretic approaches, and argues that formal language — particularly homotopy type theory — supplies the building blocks. On this constructivist view, spaces and algebras are type-theoretic constructions, which could let distinct geometries be classified in one framework and address how spatiality arises in computational models such as the Wolfram model.
