# NOTE-1698: Fodor — Radiation of oscillons

## Contribution

A long review of oscillons and oscillatons, centred on the one thing that
separates them from true solitons: their slow radiation. It sets out
where exact breathers exist (only sine-Gordon in 1+1), gives a stability
criterion, explains why 3+1 dimensional oscillons end suddenly and lower
dimensional ones never do, reproduces the Gaussian-data results of Gleiser
and of Copeland, Gleiser & Müller with an independent code, and develops
methods (quasibreathers, spectral numerics, Borel summation) for the
radiation rate, which is exponentially small, and nonzero wherever its
leading coefficient is.

## Key insight

A structure that looks permanent can be told from one that is permanent
by its outflow, not its persistence. Every oscillon but one leaks; what
the leak does to the structure depends on where it carries it. If loss
carries the amplitude toward a stability limit, as in three dimensions,
the end is sudden; if loss slows as the amplitude falls, as in one and two
dimensions, the structure fades forever and never ends.

## Assumptions

- A real scalar field with an analytic potential, on a fixed flat
  background for oscillons (Ch. 2) or self-gravitating for oscillatons
  (Ch. 3, mostly not read).
- Units differ from Copeland, Gleiser & Müller: the potential is rescaled
  with a minimum at φ = 0 and mass √2; C is the Gaussian amplitude and r0
  its radius (§2.6.4).
- Small-amplitude results expand in ε, with ω² = 1 − ε²; the large-amplitude
  ones are spectral numerics.
- Evidence: numerical simulation and asymptotic analysis; no experiment.
- The radiation analysis is spherically symmetric and asymptotic beyond all orders, done
  formally: leading order in ε with an ε ln ε correction; large amplitudes only by spectral
  numerics (§§2.9–2.10).

## Key results

- **One exact family (§2.3).** "for analytic potentials the family of sine-Gordon breathers is the
  only truly periodic localized one-parameter family of solutions which tends to the vacuum solution
  for the small value of the parameter"; for d > 1 "there is no potential for which a family of
  exactly periodically oscillating localized breather solutions can exist". Isolated solutions are
  ruled out only numerically: "Numerical investigations also show that the existence of isolated
  breathers is unlikely, even for large amplitudes". Ch. 1 and §2.7 state the general form: "For a
  real scalar field, apart from the 1 + 1 dimensional sine-Gordon breather, there is no exactly
  time-periodic oscillating finite energy spatially localized configuration."
- **Why (§2.7.1).** Each radiating Fourier mode must meet three conditions: "Three conditions for each
  mode for coupled second order equations is generally too much. This argument shows that genuine
  breather solutions can only exist in some very exceptional cases, such as for the 1 + 1
  dimensional sine-Gordon potential, when the system is integrable." For 3D φ4 quasibreathers "there
  is no such ω where the amplitudes tend to zero" (§2.7.2).
- **Integrability and identity (Ch. 1).** In sine-Gordon, kink and antikink "go through each other
  without any change in their velocity. This is an exceptional property of the integrable
  sine-Gordon theory." In φ4, "oscillons can easily form by the collision of a kink and an antikink,
  after radiating out the unnecessary energy".
- **Generic formation (Ch. 1).** "they are not exceptional states, they evolve from quite general
  initial data", including "randomly chosen not spherically symmetric initial data".
- **Which oscillons exist (§§2.8.1–2.8.2).** Small-amplitude oscillons need λ = 5/6 g2² − 3/4 g3 > 0,
  and "for d ≥ 4 spatial dimensions a family of small amplitude oscillons cannot exist".
- **Energy and stability by dimension (§§2.8.4–2.8.6, §2.7.3).** The core energy goes as ε^(2−d):
  "For dimensions d > 2 the energy grows without bound when ε approaches zero", so in 3D it has a
  minimum. Stability follows the energy: "oscillons with frequencies ω < ωc are stable, while they
  are unstable in case of ω > ωc" (ωc at the minimum, Fig. 2.19). For 3D φ4, "oscillons become
  stable only above a relatively large amplitude value" (0 < ε < εc = 0.254 unstable). In 2D "small
  and moderate amplitude oscillons turn out to be stable. If an oscillon develops from some initial
  data, its lifetime is infinite, it never decays suddenly", though "It is likely that both for d = 1
  and d = 2 dimensions there exists some amplitude where the energy becomes maximal, and above which
  oscillons become unstable, but the value of this limit is currently unknown."
- **The tail measures the loss (§2.7.3).** For a fine-tuned state the oscillon's and quasibreather's
  modes differ by less than 10⁻⁵ in the core; for Gaussian-data oscillons with shape-modes "the
  actual radiation amplitude can be twice as large as the one calculated by the quasibreather
  analogy".
- **The radiation law (§§2.8.1, 2.9).** The tail is "exp(−1/ε) order small"; at leading order its
  amplitude is a coefficient νk times an exponential in −P/ε, P the distance of the nearest complex
  singularity of the leading profile (π/2 in d = 1, smaller in d = 2, 3), with an ε ln ε correction
  for d > 1. "It can be shown relatively easily, that the radiation is exponentially small in terms
  of the parameter ε" (Ch. 4).
- **Checks (§§2.9.4–2.9.5).** For the symmetric sextic potential, ν3 by numerical integration
  (−0.90974966) and by Borel summation (−0.9097496) agree: "the high degree agreement of the results
  obtained by the two independent calculations gives a strong support for the correctness of the
  applied methods". For φ4 in d = 1, ν2 = 8.388660 · 10⁻³, against Segur and Kruskal's
  "(9.0 ± 2.0) · 10−3" in the review's notation.
- **The coefficient can vanish (§2.9.4, §2.9.7).** "The first place where ν3 = 0 is at g5 = 0.26495,
  which is quite close to the value g5 = 3/10 belonging to the sine-Gordon potential, but does not
  agree with it." Then "the order which is linear in ε will determine the radiation"; the
  coefficient derived for that order carries a factor d − 1 and is evaluated only for sine-Gordon in
  d = 2, 3. For truncations of sine-Gordon ν3 falls toward zero with the order kept (Table 2.2),
  "consistent with the fact that in case of the 1 + 1 dimensional sine-Gordon breather there is no
  radiation loss at all".
- **Numerics against theory (§2.10).** "In case of one and two spatial dimensions, stable oscillon
  states oscillate for infinitely long time, without ever decaying"; in 3D an oscillon whose energy
  falls below the minimum "gets into the unstable domain and suddenly decays". For large 1D φ4
  oscillons "the radiation is one or two magnitude larger than the theoretically predicted value. The
  obvious reason for this is that our analytic results are valid only to leading order, for small
  enough ε values"; "by the decrease of the parameter ε the numerical data gets closer to the
  theoretical value".
- **Lifetimes (Ch. 1, Ch. 4, §2.6.4).** 3+1 oscillons "suddenly decay after a few thousand
  oscillations, at most" (Ch. 1); "several thousands of oscillation periods may happen before they
  move into the unstable domain and suddenly decay" (Ch. 4). Reproduced case: for C = 2,
  r0 = 2.70716, "approximately 1240 similar oscillations", decay from "around t = 5800", "during
  about 25 oscillations".
- **Fine-tuning (§2.6.5).** Near a resonance the lifetime grows as γ ln|r0 − r0*| + δ; doubling the
  digits "could double the observed lifetime". The peak states "even if very slightly, but
  necessarily radiate in a detectable way" (Ch. 1).
- **Near-protection (§2.4).** Non-relativistic oscillons "have an approximately conserved adiabatic
  charge" (I-balls).

## Concepts

- **Oscillon / oscillaton**: a long-lived oscillating localized state of a
  real scalar, without or with self-gravity.
- **Breather**: an exactly time-periodic localized solution; for a real
  scalar, only sine-Gordon in 1+1.
- **Quasibreather**: the exactly periodic state kept by an incoming wave
  equal to the oscillon's loss; its tail measures the radiation.
- **Shape-mode**: a slow modulation of the oscillon's envelope, which can
  store and shed energy.
- **Stability by dE/dA**: stable where total energy rises with central
  amplitude.

## Connections

- **Copeland, Gleiser & Müller (1995), [NOTE-1699](NOTE-1699.md).** This carries their
  sudden end below a minimum amplitude, now as a general criterion for 3+1
  dimensions, and reproduces their kind of Gaussian result with an
  independent code; it notes that their Fig. 7 already hinted at the
  lifetime peaks later mapped by Honda and Choptuik. It does not take up their upper bound on the initial radius, nor
  their virialization argument, in the parts read.
- **Gleiser & Krackow (2019), unresolved.** "Resonant Configurations in
  Scalar Field Theories: Can (Some) Oscillons Live Forever?",
  arXiv:1906.04070, Phys. Rev. D 100, 116005. Only its abstract,
  introduction and conclusions were read, as a pointer, and it is not
  registered. Its numerical results "offer support for the conjecture that,
  in both dimensions, resonant oscillons may be infinitely long-lived",
  while it grants that the peak itself would "involve infinite
  fine-tuning". This review, which does not cite it in the text searched,
  holds that the peak states necessarily radiate. Which is right was not
  settled by anything read here.
- **Tong (2005), [NOTE-1694](NOTE-1694.md).** The topological protection oscillons lack;
  the φ4 kink-antikink collision above is where the two meet, charge
  annihilating into a non-topological remnant. In sine-Gordon the kink-antikink
  pair does not annihilate: it passes through unchanged (Ch. 1).
- **The other works of this batch.** Grimshaw et al. (2018): a soliton
  decaying under damping, against the radiative leak here. Herr et al.
  (2014) and Grelu & Akhmediev (2012): structures held by a supply.
