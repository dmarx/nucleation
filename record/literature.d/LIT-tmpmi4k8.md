# LIT-tmpmi4k8: Impaired action-safety learning and excessive relief during avoidance in patients with anxiety disorders

De Kleine, R. A., Hutschemaekers, M. H. M., Hendriks, G. J., Kampman, M., Papalini, S., Van Minnen, A. & Vervliet, B. (2023). Impaired action-safety learning and excessive relief during avoidance in patients with anxiety disorders. *Journal of Anxiety Disorders* 96: 102698. doi:10.1016/j.janxdis.2023.102698.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp5cisg](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5cisg.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **Avoidance of the safety cue, not of danger cues.** OCD (p = .004)
  and PTSD (p = .028) patients pressed the avoidance button more than
  controls to the safety cue; "No significant differences between groups
  were found in reaction to the CS+ ’s" (p. 5).
- **Avoiding without trusting it.** After effective avoidance, OCD and
  PTSD patients still expected the aversive picture more than controls
  (p. 6).
- **More relief, but not only from avoiding.** PDA and PTSD patients
  reported more relief at omissions, controlling for rated aversiveness
  (p. 7). Relief to the safety cue was already higher before avoidance
  was available (p. 5).
- **The mechanism is proposed.** Relief "reinforces the avoidance action
  further, thereby leading to higher levels of avoidance, but apparently
  not by strengthening the action → safety association (which is weakened
  in the patients)" (p. 7). A habit route is called "untested".
- **Limits the authors state.** Cross-sectional, so not causal; no cost
  to avoiding; aversiveness a rival explanation (pp. 7–8).

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it at turn U15 for patients avoiding even during safety cues, with higher threat expectancy and more relief. The abstract matches, and its groups sum to the exchange's 139 patients (33 + 40 + 66).

**Corrected 2026-10-24, from the reading.** The description is right in
outline, with two corrections. The safety-cue avoidance and the higher
expectancy were found in the OCD and PTSD groups, and the higher relief
in the PDA and PTSD groups, not in all patients. And the relief
difference was present before avoidance was possible, so it is not shown
to come from avoiding. For [CLAIM-tmp8z857](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8z857.md) the study weakly supports the
clause that relief reinforces the defence without being evidence of
danger, through the authors' interpretation. It complicates the
identifiability clause: patients avoided without confidence that
avoiding works, which is a different gap from learning that avoidance
works and nothing about the world without it. Filed from it:
[THEORY-tmpfj7ek](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfj7ek.md).
