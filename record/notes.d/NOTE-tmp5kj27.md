  microbiota lost diversity (Chao1, Shannon; PERMANOVA R² = 0.77). In
  offspring at postnatal day 14 (12 pups per group), the authors report
  lower maternal–offspring microbial transmission fidelity, reduced body
  weight, crypt and muscularis thickness, fragmented Claudin-1, reduced
  myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus area without change in neuronal density,

  somatosensory and motor cortex (not medial entorhinal cortex or CA1), and
  lower interneuron density in motor cortex only. Correlations between

# NOTE-tmp5kj27: Nakhal et al. (2026) — Maternal gut dysbiosis is associated with altered enteric and cortical inhibitory circuit development

## Contribution

A descriptive anatomical and microbiome study linking a gestational antibiotic perturbation of the maternal microbiota to co-occurring structural differences in offspring gut and cortical inhibitory circuits.

## Key results

1. Abstract, Discussion: "These findings should be interpreted as descriptive associations and do not establish mechanistic gut–brain interactions."
2. Abstract, Discussion: "the present model does not isolate microbiota­specific mechanisms from potential antibiotic-induced maternal physiological changes" (pdftotext joins "microbiota-specific" as "microbiotaspecific"; check the hyphen).
3. §3.3: "In control dyads, majority of taxa (86%) remained conserved between dams and pups"; dysbiotic dyads: "76% of taxa significantly altered and only 24% retained across generations."
4. Abstract, Results: "reduced dendritic length and branching of GAD67-positive interneurons in the somatosensory and motor cortices, while interneuron morphology in the medial entorhinal cortex and hippocampus was preserved."
5. §4.5: "we cannot definitively distinguish microbiota-mediated effects from secondary maternal physiological consequences of antibiotic exposure." (PDF ligature "eects" in extraction.)
6. §4.6: "Whether these early structural alterations persist into adulthood or translate into functional behavioral phenotypes remains to be determined."

## Assumptions

Vancomycin-induced dysbiosis stands for "maternal dysbiosis"; PND14 is a representative developmental window; pups are the unit of analysis (Fig. 5: "Data were obtained from 12 Ctrl-Off and 12 Dys-Off mice", unpaired t-tests), with no litter term reported.

## Concepts

maternal dysbiosis; vertical microbial transmission fidelity; enteric nervous system (myenteric/Auerbach's plexus); GAD67+ inhibitory interneurons; excitation–inhibition balance; region-specific cortical vulnerability.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** Accurate and appropriately hedged ("associated with", "in mice"). It should add that the perturbation is an antibiotic, which the authors say cannot be separated from microbiota effects, and that no behaviour or function was measured.

- **[CLAIM-166](../claims.d/CLAIM-166.md)** (a lower-level collective such as the gut microbiome can at times drive behaviour and experience; causal in rodents, modest in humans): *leaves open*. The work concerns development of the offspring's tissues under the *mother's* microbiota, not a person's own microbiome driving its present behaviour or experience; it measures structure only (result 6), and its causal reading is confounded by the antibiotic (results 2, 5). It adds at most a further rodent route by which microbial communities may shape neural substrates, which is consistent with the claim's "routes exist" but not evidence for "drive". [CLAIM-166](../claims.d/CLAIM-166.md)'s note of 2026-11-12 (branch) already lists it among unread gut-brain works with "No status change"; the reading supports keeping that.

## Limitations

Small sample (6 dams per group) with pups analysed as independent units, so litter effects may inflate significance; a single antibiotic model; one time point (PND14); structure only, no electrophysiology or behaviour; no microbial rescue, cross-fostering or germ-free control (the authors list these as needed).
