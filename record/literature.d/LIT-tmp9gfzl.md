# LIT-tmp9gfzl: An Organizational Account of Biological Functions

Matteo Mossio, Cristian Saborido and Alvaro Moreno (2009), *The British
Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 60(4):813–841.

The brief's citation (BJPS 60(4), 2009) is correct.

## Key takeaways

- **The definition.** A trait type T has a function iff (C1) T contributes
  to the maintenance of the organization O of S, (C2) T is produced and
  maintained under constraints exerted by O, and (C3) S is organizationally
  differentiated.
- **Closure is not enough.** Self-maintaining dissipative structures, a
  candle flame and Bénard cells among them, realise organizational closure.
  They lack functions because their components "converge" on a single
  pattern; there is nothing to say one part is for.
- **One account, two questions.** Because the trait's existence depends on
  its current effects through closure, a functional attribution answers both
  "why is T there?" and "what is T for?", which etiological and
  dispositional theories split between them.
- **Fine distinctions.** Functional versus useful (oxygen is useful, not
  functional: it fails C2), indispensable versus merely functional (heart
  versus eye), primary versus secondary (the nose warms air; it also holds
  glasses), and dysfunction relative to a regime of self-maintenance.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the biological-autonomy part
of the agency batch. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice.

It matters to the record mainly for one sentence-level fact. [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md) reports
that closure-of-constraints accounts face the candle-flame problem, and
[LIT-526](LIT-526.md) rests its own line on the flame lacking a Markov blanket. Here the
organizational school grants the flame closure in the paper's own example
and moves the line to organizational differentiation. Montévil & Mossio
([LIT-tmpksq6n](LIT-tmpksq6n.md)) later replace this broad closure with closure of constraints
and keep the thesis that constraints subject to closure are functions; they
cite this paper for it. Two of the authors' book, *Biological Autonomy*
([LIT-536](LIT-536.md), Deferred), has a chapter on teleology, normativity and
functionality where the mature version should be.

Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde ([LIT-tmp2zh8b](LIT-tmp2zh8b.md)) cite it as the account of how
an organization generates its own norms, which their definition of agency
requires.
