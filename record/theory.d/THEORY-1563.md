# THEORY-1563: Thorstad argues that on simple models of existential risk higher risk does not raise, and in most variants lowers, the value of reducing it, so that high risk this century is compatible with astronomical value for mitigation only if risk falls soon and stays very low, and that three arguments offered for such a time of perils, from wisdom, an existential risk Kuznets curve and space settlement, are weak, leaving aside the argument from advanced AI

## The account

On Ord's Simple Model, constant value v and constant risk r per century,
cutting this century's risk by a fraction f is worth exactly fv, whatever r
is: "Halving existential risk from 20% to 10% has the same value as halving
it from 2% to 1%" (§2).

If risk stays high, the future a rescue saves is short. Absolute cuts are
worth fv/r; linear growth gives fv/r and quadratic fv(2 − r)/r²; cutting
risk in every century gives (f/(1 − f))(v/r) (§3). In each, higher risk
lowers the value of mitigation.

Astronomical value returns only on a time of perils with a short perilous
period and very low post-peril risk (§3.4). The arguments for one, from
wisdom, an existential risk Kuznets curve and space settlement, do not
establish it (§§4–6). Pessimism then weakens a popular argument for
axiological strong longtermism and can replace pure time preference in
discounting (§7).

## Evidence

- Table 1, a 10% cut: at r = 0.2, 0.5v (linear) and 4.5v (quadratic); at r =
  0.0002, 500v and 5 × 10^6 v.
- Table 2, r = 20%, f = 10%: two perilous centuries and post-peril risk
  0.01% give 160v; fifty perilous centuries give 0.1v.

The proofs are in Appendices A–B. The text of §3.4 gives the 160v case as "a
low 0.1% level of post-peril risk", where Table 2 and the formula give 0.01%
([NOTE-1308](../notes.d/NOTE-1308.md), Limitations).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md) names this work under one of its open lines: "Thorstad
([LIT-1688](../literature.d/LIT-1688.md)) on whether high existential risk lowers the value of reducing
it." Its note of 2026-10-29 records that "Thorstad shows that high
existential risk lowers the value of reducing it unless risk soon falls and
stays low". The reading bears on whether the impersonal ground can guide
action.

## Note of 2026-11-11: narrowed and promoted

The title said that the arguments offered for a time of perils are weak.
Thorstad examines three, from wisdom, an existential risk Kuznets curve
and space settlement (§§4–6), and sets aside the argument that advanced
AI might end the perils (fn. 7), which NOTE-1308 calls the strongest.
The title now names the three and the one left aside.
