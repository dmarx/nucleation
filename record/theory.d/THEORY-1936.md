# THEORY-1936: Police treat Black crime reporters as suspects

## Source

Long (2021), International Review of Victimology 27(3): 344-362 ([LIT-2667](../literature.d/LIT-2667.md)), a partial stand-in for Christie (1986),
[LIT-2716](../literature.d/LIT-2716.md); read in [NOTE-2026](../notes.d/NOTE-2026.md) (draft).

## What was actually shown

Accounts from twenty interviews in which participants reporting crime were questioned as suspects or arrested,
interpreted through Christie's ideal victim, defined as the one who "most readily are given" "the complete and
legitimate status of being a victim", and his ideal offender. "Most victims are ‘non ideal’ (Christie, 1986)".

## What this does not say

- Anything on prevalence or causation: it is a qualitative study with no comparison group.
- Christie's own argument beyond the definitions it quotes.
- That victims who are also wrongdoers are discounted in general; it shows instances of the victim-offender dichotomy
  in policing.
