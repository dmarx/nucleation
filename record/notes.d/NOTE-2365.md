# NOTE-2365: Wimsatt & Schank — Generative entrenchment, modularity and evolvability (2004)

## Contribution

It states generative entrenchment (GE) on a graph, and shows by
simulation that differential selection, which GE produces, has a
non-linear and whole-organism effect: under truncation selection the
fates of functionally unrelated elements are coupled through the
organism's distance from the fitness threshold. Modular change is
possible but rare, needing relaxed selection.

## Key insight

"We define the generative entrenchment (GE) of a node as the magnitude
of this downstream dependency" (MS p. 2): count how many other nodes
can be reached from a node by following the arrows of a directed causal
graph of "the organism plus relevant aspects of its environment".
Elements with more downstream should be more conservative, since more
can go wrong when they change.

## Assumptions

- The phenotype is a generative structure: few elements interacting
  with each other "and with inputs from the environment" (MS p. 2).
- The graph's nodes are "parts, processes, or events" and its arrows
  "consequences of the presence or operation of nodes on other nodes";
  "Environmental inputs, maintained conditions and periodic or other
  patterns of change ... can also have significant generative roles"
  (MS p. 2).
- "Downstream consequences considered should be limited to
  characteristic effects, common and systematic enough that they could
  be consequences of selection" (MS p. 3).
- Simulations: haploid asexual networks of 100 to 248 connections,
  mutation at connection ends, fitness decrements summed with an
  "exposure" above 1 and fitness 0 at a total decrement of 1 or more
  (a truncation threshold), populations of 10 or 100.

## Key results

- **GE is reachability** (MS pp. 2-3): "This is left deliberately
  vague—it could be operationalized in various ways"; "The simplest
  such measure is the sum of downstream nodes" (footnote 8). Nothing
  in the definition requires the graph to be acyclic. Among the generic
  properties of such networks they list "the number of closed cycles in
  the network, and the mean cycle length" (MS p. 5).
- **GE reaches beyond the organism** (MS p. 3): it spans "the
  “extended phenotype” or the “recurrently assembled” resources"; in
  army ants, "the rules of individual interactions ... become
  entrenched" in a colony-level cycle.
- **What lowers GE** (MS p. 4): "Modularity, duplication, and
  functional redundancy should each decrease entrenchment".
- **GE accumulates** (MS p. 4): "Darwinian processes should almost
  inevitably give rise to generative structures"; and footnote 5:
  "pruning unentrenched branches from a tree still leaves a tree".
- **Simulations** (MS pp. 6-14): losses fall "almost entirely among
  connections with lower GE"; with fitness classes and truncation
  selection, trajectories bifurcate, the strongest classes reversing to
  near fixation while the weakest are lost; deviations in one class are
  compensated by the others. Fitness decrements were then "assigned to
  connections, rather than calculated from their generative
  entrenchment" (MS p. 7), so the later results are about differential
  selection, whatever its source.
- **Whole-organism selection** (MS p. 18): "Selection is always
  selection of whole organisms"; "the existence of truncation selection
  itself creates a maximally global form of epistasis".
- **Modularity** (MS pp. 16, 21): modular change requires non-lethal
  losses, small populations and very good conditions; "modular changes
  may happen, but rarely".

## Concepts

- **Generative entrenchment**: as above, a property of a node relative
  to a graph that may include the environment.
- **Exposure**: the maximum sum of fitness decrements an individual can
  carry (MS p. 7).
- **Last straw principle**: near the viability threshold, losing almost
  any component, however decoupled, can be fatal (MS p. 18).

## Connections

It stands in for [LIT-1926](../literature.d/LIT-1926.md) (Wimsatt 2007) for the definition of GE only:
the book's chapter on GE is not this paper, and its essays on
robustness, reduction, heuristics and aggregativity are not restated
here. It answers Kauffman (1985, 1993) on whether selection can keep
network structure. [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md) quotes GE from the book in a form that
matches this paper's: perturbing an entrenched core "catastrophic",
changes in later interactions "tolerable".

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-531](../claims.d/CLAIM-531.md)** (the question [LIT-1926](../literature.d/LIT-1926.md)'s standing put): the source's
  definition is downstream reachability on a directed graph. It does
  not require the core to be maintained by what depends on it, so the
  claim's second defeater, that entrenchment as its source defines it
  entails closure, is not met. Nor does the definition exclude it: the
  graphs have closed cycles, and every node on a cycle reaches every
  other. A core node at the root of a tree is as entrenched as one on a
  cycle of the same reach. This is what [CLAIM-531](../claims.d/CLAIM-531.md) says: entrenchment and
  closure come apart, and an entrenched, closed organization has a cycle
  in its core. The definition also counts "maintained conditions" of the
  environment as nodes, so an entrenched core can be supplied from
  outside, which is the claim's outside supply. Corroborates.
- **[CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md)** (consistent, mechanism-level): structures that later
  ones depend on become conservative; the bicoid example adds
  irreversibility "through loss of once useful but now redundant control
  linkages". A biological instance of entrenchment as lock-in; an
  analogue for the record's anchors, no edge.
- **[CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)** (background): GE is persistence explained by
  dependence, the converse direction of the claim's persistence making
  anchors.

## Limitations

- The simulations mostly assign selection classes rather than compute
  GE, so they show the effects of differential selection, not of
  entrenchment as such.
- GE is deliberately unoperationalized; how cycles enter a measure of
  "downstream" (every node of a cycle downstream of every other) is not
  discussed.
- Small populations and high mutation rates were used to make rare
  events visible; the authors argue the conclusions generalize but do
  not show it.

## Open questions

What does GE measure on a graph that is one strongly connected cycle,
where every node reaches every node? The paper leaves it open, and it is
the case closure needs.
