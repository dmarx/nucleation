# CLAIM-tmpq1p1x: A critique of produced wants cannot rest on how they were formed, by whatever process, without appeal to the worth of their objects or to their effects

## The claim

[CLAIM-689](CLAIM-689.md), as narrowed, holds that a want's social origin as such cannot
ground a critique of it, since every preference is socially formed. This
claim extends that to every manner of formation: a critique of the wants
themselves, as distinct from a finding about whose they are, needs the
worth of their objects or their effects.

## What holds it back

Its rival is in the record. Self-determination theory ([THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)) holds
that a regulation taken in under controlling conditions is introjected
rather than integrated, so how a value was acquired shapes how
autonomously the person acts on it. That discriminates among socially
formed wants by their formation, with no appeal to worth. The claim stands
only if such accounts speak to whose a want is (the authenticity line,
[QUESTION-057](../questions.d/QUESTION-057.md)) and not to what it is worth; no argument here shows that yet.

## Note of 2026-11-18: what holds it back

Filed Proposed with the narrowing of [CLAIM-689](CLAIM-689.md). [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)'s account of
introjection is unanswered: the claim needs an argument that formation
under control bears on autonomy and not on a want's worth.
