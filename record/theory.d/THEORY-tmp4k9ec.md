# THEORY-tmp4k9ec: Pain is a functional state of a whole organism, possessing a certain functional organisation as a probabilistic automaton, rather than a brain state, and this is the more plausible empirical hypothesis because one pain predicate applies to physically different organisms; the hypothesis keeps swarms out only by a stipulated condition that no pain-feeler has parts that separately possess the organisation

## Source

Putnam (1967), *Psychological Predicates*, [LIT-396](../literature.d/LIT-396.md). The original (*Art,
Mind, and Religion*, pp. 37–48) is closed. [NOTE-335](../notes.d/NOTE-335.md) is a Skimmed reading of
a course excerpt of the 1975 reprint ("The Nature of Mental States"),
enough to quote the hypothesis and condition (3) verbatim and not enough to
say what the elided text contains. The account is filed on that reading
because it is the founding statement of a view that the record's full
readings of Block ([LIT-407](../literature.d/LIT-407.md)), Chalmers ([LIT-404](../literature.d/LIT-404.md)) and Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md))
all take as their starting point, and they quote it in the same terms.

## What was actually shown

**The hypothesis, verbatim from the excerpt.**

> (1) All organisms capable of feeling pain are Probabilistic Automata.
> (2) Every organism capable of feeling pain possesses at least one
> Description of a certain kind (i.e., being capable of feeling pain is
> possessing an appropriate kind of Functional Organization).
> (3) No organism capable of feeling pain possesses a decomposition into
> parts which separately possess Descriptions of the kind referred to in (2).
> (4) For every Description of the kind referred to in (2), there exists a
> subset of the sensory inputs such that an organism with that Description
> is in pain when and only when some of its sensory inputs are in that
> subset.

A Description is any true statement that a system has states related to
one another and to inputs and outputs by a given Machine Table; states are
specified "only 'implicitly'" by its transition probabilities. The
organisation must include something like a "preference function", an
"inductive logic" and "pain sensors".

**The argument against the brain-state view.** The brain-state theorist
must find one physical-chemical state shared by every possible pain-feeler,
mammal, octopus and extraterrestrial. One psychological predicate
applicable to a mammal and an octopus with different physical correlates
collapses that view, and "it seems to me overwhelmingly probable that we
can do this". The functional hypothesis needs only a shared organisation,
and behaviour is evidence for that. It is offered as an empirical
hypothesis, a "schema", accepted for its fruitful predictions and
questions.

**Condition (3).** "The purpose of condition (3) is to rule out such
'organisms' (if they can count as such) as swarms of bees as single
pain-feelers." No other motive appears in the passage read.

**What could have come out otherwise.** As a hypothesis it could fail: a
shared physical state across pain-feelers could be found. The paper reports
no such search; its support is plausibility.

## What this does not say

- **That functionalism is incompatible with dualism.** Putnam says it is
  not: a body plus a soul can be a probabilistic automaton.
- **That clause (1) constrains anything.** It "is, in fact, empty, since
  everything is a Probabilistic Automaton under some Description". That
  liberalism is what condition (3) patches.
- **That condition (3) is principled.** It is stipulated, with one motive.
  Block shows it ad hoc ([LIT-407](../literature.d/LIT-407.md), [NOTE-366](../notes.d/NOTE-366.md)), and [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) sets out why
  no anti-nesting principle yet has independent support.
- **That condition (3) concerns whether the parts feel pain.** It concerns
  whether they separately possess the pain-capable organisation.
  Schwitzgebel's "parts which are separately capable of feeling pain" is a
  paraphrase ([NOTE-335](../notes.d/NOTE-335.md), Corrections).
- **What the 1967 printing says.** Whether it words the hypothesis and
  condition (3) as the reprint does is unknown here.

## Connections

- **The dispute it began.** [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) holds that a mind of mindless parts
  and a mind of minded parts are one functionalist move run in two
  directions; condition (3) is the first of the anti-nesting principles it
  examines.
- **Implementation.** Chalmers ([THEORY-tmppylq2](THEORY-tmppylq2.md)) shows that clause (1)'s
  emptiness survives a counterfactual definition of implementation for
  finite-state automata, and that a richer formalism is needed if
  "possessing a Description" is to constrain anything.
- **Experience.** [THEORY-095](THEORY-095.md) holds that phenomenal character is not fixed
  by long-arm functional role, and leaves short-arm functional accounts
  open. Putnam's organisation is defined over sensory inputs and motor
  outputs, so it is on the open side. Block's China brain ([LIT-407](../literature.d/LIT-407.md)) is the
  case pressed against functionalism about experience as such.
- **The causal-powers alternative.** Searle ([THEORY-tmpted1p](THEORY-tmpted1p.md)) rejects the
  functional route for intentionality.
