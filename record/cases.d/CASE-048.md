# CASE-048: The same film joke, seen as a child and again as an adult

## The case

A family film or TV show carries a line addressed over the children's heads:
a double entendre the child hears as silliness and the adult hears as a
joke about something else. A frequently cited instance is the line in
*Shrek* (2001) wondering whether Lord Farquaad, with his towering castle, is
"compensating for something". The child laughs at the delivery. Twenty
years later, the same person rewatches the same scene and gets the other
joke.

Held fixed: the artefact (the same frames and soundtrack), and the person as
one continuing individual. Varied: the reader's state, meaning their common
ground, knowledge and adaptation.

## What it can show

Both readings of the storage claim meet here:

- **For [CLAIM-160](../claims.d/CLAIM-160.md).** The writers designed for two audiences at once.
  The intended meaning was fixed at the write, and the adult layer was
  meant to be missed by children. What changed over twenty years is what
  the reader can reconstruct: fidelity across states of adaptation of one
  interpreter, which [CLAIM-074](../claims.d/CLAIM-074.md) already keeps apart.
- **For [CLAIM-171](../claims.d/CLAIM-171.md).** For the person, the scene became a different
  joke, and nothing they did recovered a meaning that was waiting. It
  became one.

The case also ties this line to the self lines. The same receiver at two
ages is one person with two common grounds. The child's reading is the
child's layer, not a failed adult reading. That bears on [QUESTION-023](../questions.d/QUESTION-023.md)'s
overhearer, who misses insider meaning by design.

**Two readings that do not meet.** The child's reading and the adult's
are sections over two contexts, twenty years apart, that do not overlap.
By [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) they glue, and the person holds no contradiction in
having laughed at different jokes at different ages. They meet only when
the adult remembers the child's reading while having the adult's. Memory
makes the overlap here, as self-prediction does for interests in
[CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md). That overlap is where the experience the case trades on
happens: the moment of seeing what the line meant all along.

It is stipulated. The *Shrek* line is named as an instance and not
analysed. Whether real viewers report the shift this way is not documented
here.

## Note of 2026-10-12: two senses of "cover", left to the owner

Version 2 says the child's and the adult's readings "are sections over two
contexts, twenty years apart, that do not overlap", and so glue by
[CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md). It does not state the coverage, which [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) says decides
whether anything glues. PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121)'s [CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md) uses an operational
sense: a context is a question readers answer determinately. In that sense
the child and the adult may be asked one question, and their difference is
then distortion on a shared context, or a change of cover made by the
reader's state with the artefact fixed. [CASE-tmpa82q4](CASE-tmpa82q4.md), where the speaker
"intended both readings", says the case "shows that uptake moves with the
audience, not that the audience's role constitutes the act". Which sense
of cover this case uses, and whether a designed dual address can tell
[CLAIM-160](../claims.d/CLAIM-160.md) from [CLAIM-171](../claims.d/CLAIM-171.md), is left to the owner.
