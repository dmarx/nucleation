# THEORY-tmpo67z2: Gardner argues that Sen's contrast between transcendental and comparative theories of justice is overdrawn, since ranking injustices needs a standard of justice from which departures are measured, and that Sen's preference for securing capabilities over achieved functionings as the object of public policy is asserted, not argued

## The account

Gardner argues that Sen's contrast between "transcendental institutionalism" and realization-focused comparison is overdrawn: to rank injustices one needs a standard from which departures are measured, which returns Sen to Rawls. And Sen's preference for capability over achievement as the object of public policy ("The central issue ... is the freedom to choose how to live", IoJ p. 238) is asserted: "But where is the argument?"

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpsll57](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpsll57.md), results 1–5. It agrees with Valentini ([THEORY-434](THEORY-434.md)). Its bearing on [CLAIM-tmp6wuql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6wuql.md) is argued in [ARG-tmpar7tl](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpar7tl.md).

## What it does not say

Nothing on democracy, human rights or the impartial spectator, which Gardner says he leaves aside.
