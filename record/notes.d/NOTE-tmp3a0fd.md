# NOTE-tmp3a0fd: Fricker — Evolving Concepts of Epistemic Injustice

## Contribution

A retrospective by the concepts' author. It does not define them anew. It
says what they are for, draws a boundary she wants kept strict
(non-deliberateness), names the pair "discriminatory epistemic
injustice" to leave room for distributive kinds, and accepts one
extension, structural testimonial injustice. It also says the concepts
were built against Marxist standpoint theory and Foucauldian reductionism.

## Key insight

Both concepts pick out wrongs done to someone as a knower *without anyone
meaning to*. Testimonial injustice is a misjudgement, so it needs a hearer
who really does see the speaker as lesser. Hermeneutical injustice needs
no culprit at all, because its cause is a marginalisation in who gets to
shape shared meanings. Deliberate discrediting, gaslighting and feigned
incomprehension are real and related, but they are other wrongs.

## Assumptions

- **Meaning is use.** "There is no point trying to put precise boundaries
  on the concept in advance" (p. 1). The strictness argued for is a
  recommendation about usefulness, not a definition.
- **The generic definition stands.** Someone "wronged in their capacity as
  an epistemic subject" (p. 1), as in the book (Fricker 2007).
- **Interpersonal first.** Theorising starts "from marginalized lives" and
  from the transactional level, and builds up to the structural (pp.
  7–10).

## Key results

- **Discriminatory epistemic injustice (p. 1).** Both kinds are
  "fundamentally a form of (direct or indirect) discrimination".
  - Testimonial: "The cause of testimonial injustice is a prejudice
    through which the speaker is misjudged and perceived as epistemically
    lesser (a direct discrimination)."
  - Hermeneutical: "The cause of a hermeneutical injustice is a background
    inequality of hermeneutical opportunity—specifically, hermeneutical
    marginalisation in relation to some area of social experience",
    which puts them "at an unfair disadvantage in comprehending and/or
    getting others to comprehend an experience of that kind (a somewhat
    indirect discrimination)".
  - Distributive epistemic injustice, after Coady: "receiving less than
    their fair share of an epistemic good, such as education". It fits the
    generic definition too. The distinction is not "deep and/or
    exclusive" (n. 1).
- **Strictness about intention (pp. 1–3).** "In testimonial injustice the
  absence of deliberate, conscious manipulation is definitive, at least in
  my conception." Ripley "does not epistemically misjudge Marge at all",
  so what he does ("wicked puppetry—a third-personal intentional
  gaslighting") is not testimonial injustice. Greenleaf, whose prejudice
  Ripley works on, does Marge "a grave testimonial injustice".
- **Hermeneutical injustice is not prevented by good faith (pp. 3–4).**
  The hearer "might be trying in earnest to understand". "The cause of the
  injustice is structural", so it "will tend to persist regardless of
  individual efforts". Virtuous listening "will somewhat erode
  hermeneutical marginalisation". Eradication needs "sufficient social
  equality in general", since marginalisation "is a product of social
  powerlessness (and is a form of it)".
- **Non-deliberate is not non-culpable (p. 4).** Prejudiced thinking is
  "almost always culpable in some degree—it's just also very ordinary".
  "If … a hearer really does straightforwardly dissemble, pretending not
  to understand … then that is a closely related but distinct injustice."
- **Structural testimonial injustice (pp. 8–9).** Anderson's case: a list
  of expert witnesses "that no one has refreshed for a good while", so
  that "(through no one's fault, let us add)" the same witnesses are
  drawn on, and those who would have been listed "are effectively
  silenced". This is "pre-emptive testimonial injustice (Fricker 2007,
  ch. 6)": "Their views are not sought, and this is owing to a structural
  prejudice (the un-refreshed list)." Such injustice "would have to be
  pre-emptive". Once anyone speaks, it is "transactional as well as
  structural". "The extension of the idea of testimonial injustice to
  structural forms is very helpful."
- **Applications (pp. 11–13).** In healthcare, the testimonial injustice
  of patients' expertise not being recognised (Carel and Kidd), and the
  over-generalised diagnosis of delusion (Kyratsous and Sanati). In
  hermeneutical terms, ill people's non-expert terms "are not
  sufficiently shared across social space".

## Concepts

- **Discriminatory epistemic injustice.** Her new label for testimonial
  and hermeneutical injustice together.
- **Distributive epistemic injustice.** An unfair share of an epistemic
  good.
- **Hermeneutical marginalisation.** Unequal hermeneutical opportunity in
  "some area of social experience". It is a product and a form of social
  powerlessness.
- **Pre-emptive testimonial injustice.** A speaker's word not sought at
  all, because of prejudice. In the book it is ch. 6.
- **Transactional and structural** (Anderson). An injustice in an
  exchange between parties, or one built into an institution's
  arrangements.

## Connections

Fricker cites her own 2007 book throughout, and her 2016 paper on Mills's
"white ignorance", which she distinguishes from hermeneutical injustice.
She follows Anderson (2012) on structural injustice and Coady (2010) on
distributive injustice, and accepts Medina, Pohlhaus, Dotson and Mason on
how hearers sustain these injustices. Her two earlier papers, read here
in [NOTE-tmpieo7h](NOTE-tmpieo7h.md) ([LIT-tmpo9j0c](../literature.d/LIT-tmpo9j0c.md), 2003) and [NOTE-tmpgy7n9](NOTE-tmpgy7n9.md) ([LIT-tmp71qsd](../literature.d/LIT-tmp71qsd.md),
2006), are the first statements of the two concepts.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmplo3ff](../theory.d/THEORY-tmplo3ff.md) is confirmed on the generic wrong and the two causes,
  and two things are added.** This chapter says "a prejudice", not
  "identity prejudice", like the précis. So the précis's wording is the
  author's settled usage in 2017, though the book's own wording is still
  unread. It adds the non-deliberateness condition on testimonial
  injustice, which the précis implies ("prejudice on the hearer's part")
  but does not state. It also gives the cause of hermeneutical injustice
  as "hermeneutical marginalisation in relation to some area of social
  experience", which is less tied to a social group than the précis's
  "relative powerlessness of a social group". [NOTE-tmpgy7n9](NOTE-tmpgy7n9.md) makes the
  difference explicit.
- **[QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md), part 1.** Anderson's un-refreshed list is close in form
  to the question's case: a standing arrangement decides whose word is
  sought, through no one's fault. On Fricker's terms, a filing practice
  that never solicits some contributors' moves could be structural,
  pre-emptive testimonial injustice. That holds only if the arrangement
  embodies a *prejudice*, a misjudgement of those contributors as
  epistemically lesser. A filing grammar that leaves out a kind of move
  regardless of who makes it is not such a prejudice. [THEORY-tmpa9fyk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpa9fyk.md)
  states the condition.
- **[QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md), part 2.** "Not deliberate" cuts both ways. The record's
  grammar was chosen deliberately, but leaving out a contributor's moves
  was not. On this chapter that does not take it out of hermeneutical
  injustice, whose cause is structural and survives good faith.
- **Nothing here is ML practice.**

## Limitations

- A short handbook chapter. Its strictness about intention is advocacy
  for a usage, and it concedes the concepts' use has "already evolved".
- It restates the definitions rather than arguing for them. The arguments
  are in the book and in the two earlier papers.
- The structural case is Anderson's, imagined. Fricker adds "through no
  one's fault" to it herself.

## Open questions

- Whether a structural testimonial injustice needs a prejudice located in
  someone's judgement, or only in the arrangement. Here "the un-refreshed
  list" itself is called the prejudice, which stretches "misjudgement".
- Where Ripley's "intentional gaslighting" belongs, if not in either
  concept. She leaves it unnamed.
