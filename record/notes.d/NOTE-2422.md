# NOTE-2422: Kolmogorov — Zur Deutung der intuitionistischen Logik

## Contribution

The paper has two halves, which it says can be read from two standpoints
(p. 58). §1 needs no intuitionist epistemology: "Neben der theoretischen
Logik, welche die Beweisschemata der theoretischen Wahrheiten
systematisiert, kann man die Schemata der Lösungen von Aufgaben [...]
systematisieren" [beside theoretical logic, which systematizes the schemes
of proof of theoretical truths, one can systematize the schemes of
solution of problems]. That gives a new "Aufgabenrechnung" [calculus of
problems], and "Nach der Form fällt die Aufgabenrechnung mit der
Brouwerschen, von Herrn Heyting neuerdings formalisierten, intuitionistischen
Logik zusammen" [in form, the calculus of problems coincides with
Brouwer's intuitionistic logic, recently formalized by Heyting] (p. 58). §2
accepts the intuitionist assumptions and argues that intuitionistic logic
should be replaced by the calculus of problems, "denn ihre Objekte sind in
Wirklichkeit keine theoretischen Aussagen, sondern vielmehr Aufgaben"
[for its objects are in reality not theoretical statements but problems]
(p. 58).

## Key results

- **A problem is given by examples, not defined** (p. 59): "Wir definieren
  nicht, was eine Aufgabe ist, sondern erklären dies durch einige
  Beispiele." The examples: find four integers with xⁿ + yⁿ = zⁿ, n > 2;
  prove Fermat's theorem false; draw a circle through three given points;
  given one root of a quadratic, find the other; supposing π = n/m, find a
  like expression for e. Footnote 4: the basic concepts of propositional
  logic, statement and proof, "befinden sich nicht in besserer Lage" [are
  in no better position].
- **What a problem fixes includes its means** (p. 59, n. 2): "Um ganz
  präzis zu sein, sollte man natürlich bei der Formulierung dieser Aufgabe
  die erlaubten Konstruktionsmittel angeben" [to be quite precise, one
  should of course state the permitted means of construction when
  formulating this problem].
- **Conditional and contentless problems** (p. 59). Problems 4 and 5 are
  "konventionelle" [conditional] problems; in the fifth the supposition is
  impossible, so the problem is "inhaltslos" [without content]. "Der
  Beweis, daß eine Aufgabe inhaltslos ist, wird weiter immer als ihre
  Lösung betrachtet" [the proof that a problem is contentless will always
  be counted as its solution].
- **Two problems are different where two statements are not** (p. 59,
  n. 3): finding the four numbers differs from proving Fermat false, though
  as statements the two are classically equivalent.
- **The connectives** (pp. 59–60). a ∧ b: solve both; a ∨ b: solve at
  least one; a ⊃ b: "vorausgesetzt, daß die Lösung von a gegeben ist, b
  lösen" [given a solution of a, solve b], the same as reducing the
  solution of b to that of a; ¬a: given a solution of a, obtain a
  contradiction. Footnote 5: ¬a is not "prove a unsolvable"; that a is
  unsolvable implies ¬a and not conversely. (x)a(x) is the problem of
  giving a general method for solving a(x) for each value of x, in finitely
  many steps known before x is chosen (p. 60). ⊢p(a, b, c, …) is the
  problem of giving a general method for p for every choice of problems.
- **Solving is subjective; mathematical solutions are generally valid**
  (p. 61): "Daß ich eine Aufgabe gelöst habe, ist eine rein subjektive
  Tatsache, welche an sich noch kein allgemeines Interesse hat" [that I
  have solved a problem is a purely subjective fact, of no general interest
  in itself]. Logical and mathematical problems have the property of "der
  Allgemeingültigkeit ihrer Lösungen" [the general validity of their
  solutions]: a solution can be presented intelligibly to all, and it is
  necessary that it be recognized as correct, a necessity of "einigermaßen
  idealen Charakter" [a somewhat ideal character] that presumes able
  hearers.
- **Problems have no truth** (p. 61, n. 8): all this holds of proofs of
  theoretical statements too, but "Es ist jedoch wesentlich, daß jede
  bewiesene Aussage *richtig* ist; für die Aufgaben hat man keinen dieser
  Richtigkeit entsprechenden Begriff" [it is essential, though, that every
  proved statement is correct; for problems one has no concept
  corresponding to this correctness].
- **The calculus** (pp. 61–63). Postulated as solved: group A, eleven
  formulas of Heyting's (2.1–4.11), and group B, three rules (from ⊢p ∧ q,
  ⊢p; from ⊢p and ⊢p ⊃ q, ⊢q; substitution). Their consequences coincide
  with Heyting's first paper's axioms and rules, "folglich können wir alle
  Formeln dieser Abhandlung als Aufgaben deuten" (p. 63); the coincidence
  is asserted, the computation omitted.
- **Excluded middle as a problem** (p. 63): ⊢a ∨ ¬a asks "eine allgemeine
  Methode anzugeben, um für jede Aufgabe a entweder eine Lösung zu finden,
  oder aus der Gegebenheit von dieser Lösung einen Widerspruch zu
  folgern!" [to give a general method that for every problem a either finds
  a solution or derives a contradiction from its being given]. A reader
  who does not think himself omniscient will not put it on his list of
  solved problems. ¬¬(a ∨ ¬a) (4.8) is solvable; ¬¬a ⊃ a cannot be, since
  with 4.8 it gives a ∨ ¬a. A formula false in classical logic is not
  solvable: from it the calculus derives the plainly contradictory ⊢¬a
  (p. 64, citing Glivenko).
- **§2: negation and existence** (pp. 64–65). The intuitionist principle,
  after Weyl: every statement not without content "soll auf eine oder
  mehrere ganz bestimmte, für unsere Erfahrung zugängliche Sachverhalte
  hinweisen" (p. 64). The negation of "every element of the infinite set K
  has A" fails it; Brouwer's redefinition makes it the existence statement
  that a chain of inferences leads to contradiction, and Brouwer's
  existence statements are false before the construction and true only
  after. Kolmogorov's resolution (p. 65): there is a problem, "in der Menge
  K ein Element mit der Eigenschaft A zu finden", which "hat wirklich einen
  bestimmten, von dem Zustand unserer Kenntnisse unabhängigen Sinn" [really
  has a determinate sense independent of the state of our knowledge]; once
  solved, one has the empirical statement "Jetzt ist unsere Aufgabe
  gelöst". So Brouwer's existence statement splits into "das objektive
  Element (die Aufgabe) und das subjektive (ihre Lösung)", and nothing is
  left over to call an existence statement proper.
- **The main result** (p. 65): "Für eine allgemeine Aussage ist es im
  allgemeinen sinnlos, ihre Negation als bestimmte Aussage zu betrachten."
  Then excluded middle holds for every statement whose negation has sense,
  and the object of intuitionistic logic, as a logic of statements,
  vanishes; in the domain of problems its formulas get a new sense.
- **Against Heyting** (p. 65, n. 17, added in proof): the interpretation is
  close to Heyting's in *Erkenntnis* 2 (1931), "bei Heyting fehlt
  allerdings eine klare Unterscheidung zwischen Aussagen und Aufgaben"
  [Heyting, however, lacks a clear distinction between statements and
  problems].

## Assumptions

- That "problem" and "solution" are understood without misunderstanding
  in concrete mathematics (p. 59), as "statement" and "proof" are.
- That mathematical solutions are generally valid: a presented solution
  must be recognized by any competent hearer (p. 61).
- §2 only: Weyl's principle that a statement with content points to
  determinate states of affairs accessible to experience.

## Concepts

- **Aufgabe** (problem): something to be done (find, prove, construct),
  solved or not, with no truth value; may carry a supposition
  (conditional) and its permitted means.
- **Lösung** (solution): its doing; that it was done is a subjective fact,
  but for mathematics its presentation is generally valid.
- **inhaltslose Aufgabe** (problem without content): one whose
  supposition is impossible; proving that counts as solving it.
- **Aufgabenrechnung** (calculus of problems), **elementare
  Aufgabenfunktionen** (problem functions built with ∧, ∨, ⊃, ¬, all four
  independent, unlike the classical ones, p. 60, n. 7).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-949](../claims.d/CLAIM-949.md), its correspondence of a question with a problem.** The
  claim says "Kolmogorov read a proposition as a problem and its proof as
  a solution". The paper keeps statements and problems apart and faults
  Heyting for not doing so (n. 17); proofs and solutions run parallel, but
  only a proved statement is correct (n. 8). What he reads as problems are
  the formulas of intuitionistic logic, and in §2 the intuitionist's
  negations and existence statements. The identification of propositions
  with problems is the later BHK and propositions-as-types gloss, not this
  paper's. The correspondence itself, a question in [CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md)'s sense as a
  problem, fits in what it asserts and is narrower in two respects and
  wider in one; the dated note on [CLAIM-949](../claims.d/CLAIM-949.md) gives the verdict, and
  [ARG-245](../arguments.d/ARG-245.md) argues it.
- **[CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md)**: a parallel and a contrast. A problem, like the record's
  question, demands rather than asserts and fixes what counts as solving
  it; but its sense is fixed "von dem Zustand unserer Kenntnisse
  unabhängig", so the calculus has no counterpart of the claim's last
  clause, that an inquiry can revise what an answer could be.
- **[TERM-095](../terms.d/TERM-095.md)**: footnote 2 puts the permitted means of construction in the
  problem's formulation, where the term puts the admissible interventions
  𝓘 in the inquiry beside the answer space.
- **[TERM-154](../terms.d/TERM-154.md)** (witness): the existence statement as a problem of
  finding an element plus its solution (p. 65) is the witness's sense.
- **[TERM-153](../terms.d/TERM-153.md)** (certificate): a parallel. The general validity of a
  mathematical solution (p. 61), presentable so that any competent hearer
  must recognize it, is what a certificate is for; Kolmogorov makes it a
  property of the domain, not a record kept.
- **[TERM-152](../terms.d/TERM-152.md)** (specification): a problem with many solutions is a
  partial specification; a contentless problem is one no realization
  passes.

## Limitations

- "Problem" is never defined, by design (p. 59), so the paper cannot settle
  what a problem is beyond its examples and its connectives.
- The coincidence with Heyting's calculus is asserted and the derivations
  omitted ("Wir verzichten hier auf die weitere Ausführung", p. 63).
- The account of solution is informal: what a general method is, and when
  one is given, are left to the reader's understanding (p. 60 and n. 6).
- Domain: logical and mathematical problems, whose solutions are generally
  valid (p. 61). Problems whose solutions cannot be presented so that all
  must accept them are outside it.
- §2's own question is left open (p. 65, n. 16): "Welche logischen Gesetze
  gelten für Aussagen, deren Negation keinen Sinn hat?"
