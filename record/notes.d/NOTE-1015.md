# NOTE-1015: Schüler and Wiskott — Slow feature analysis as variational inference

## Contribution

It puts SFA's slowness loss inside a variational objective with a
nonlinear generative model, where earlier probabilistic SFA (as the
authors describe it) needed linear emissions. With a random-walk prior and
an instantaneous Gaussian encoder, the KL term of the ELBO is the
unordered slowness loss, and the reconstruction term takes over from
SFA's unit-variance and decorrelation constraints. The paper does not
claim equivalence with SFA and does not solve for any optimum.

## Key insight

Slowness is the price of the encoder disagreeing with a random-walk prior.
If the prior says z_t ≈ z_{t−1} with unit noise and the posterior
approximation is N(g(x_t), I) at each step separately, then the KL cost
of each step is half the squared jump of the encoder's mean. In VAE terms
the roles swap: the KL "regularizer" is SFA's objective, and the
reconstruction "objective" is what stops the features collapsing to
constants. What keeps them from collapsing is the fixed encoder noise: a
feature shrunk below the noise is lost to reconstruction.

## Assumptions

- Time series x_{1:T}, x_t ∈ ℝⁿ; latents z_{1:T}, z_t ∈ ℝ^d.
- **Prior**: Markov, p(z_t | z_{t−1}) = N(z_{t−1}, I) (Eq. 13). No decay
  (λ_i = 1 in the paper's contrast with Turner and Sahani), so no
  stationary distribution, and the latent dimensions are independent and
  equally slow. p(z_1) is not specified.
- **Forward model**: p_θ(x_{1:T} | z_{1:T}) = Π_t p_θ(x_t | z_t) (Eq. 14),
  differentiable in θ, otherwise free.
- **Variational family**: instantaneous, q(z_{1:T} | x_{1:T}) =
  Π_t q(z_t | x_t), with q(z_t | x_t) = N(g_φ(x_t), I), unit covariance
  fixed (Eqs. 19–20). This is narrower than the posterior's structure
  (Eq. 15) and than mean-field or causal alternatives (Eqs. 17–18), which
  the authors name and set aside.
- **Linear case**: q(z | x) = N(Wᵀx + b, I), p(x | z) = N(Vz + o, I)
  (Eqs. 43–44), and "for large T" the lagged second moment equals the
  unlagged one.
- The ordering of SFA's features is discarded from the start (Eq. 2).

## Key results

- **The ELBO (Eq. 40).** L = Σ_t E_{z_t~q} log p_θ(x_t | z_t)
  − ½Σ_t ‖g_φ(x_{t−1}) − g_φ(x_t)‖². Re-derived: the exact KL is
  ½Σ_{t≥2}‖Δg‖² + (T − 1)d/2, plus a t = 1 boundary term the paper does
  not write ([THEORY-775](../theory.d/THEORY-775.md), Step 1). *Holds when:* the assumptions
  above, for any g and any forward model.
- **Diagonal covariances (§6).** For any diagonal Σ_p and Σ_q only the
  Σ_p⁻¹-norm changes, which reweights the features' contributions. True
  of the mean term; with Σ_q ≠ Σ_p the trace and log-determinant terms
  also enter, as constants in g.
- **Linear objective (Eq. 41).** L = c − (T/2)tr(VᵀV) − ½Σ‖x_t − x̃_t‖²
  − ½Σ‖Wᵀx_{t−1} − Wᵀx_t‖², with x̃_t = VWᵀx_t + Vb + o. The paper prints
  + before the reconstruction sum; Appendix Eq. 54 has the correct −.
- **Necessary condition (Eq. 42, Eq. 92).** WᵀĊW = VᵀV and
  o = x̄ − VWᵀx̄ − Vb, with Ċ the second moment of the step differences.
  Re-derived ([THEORY-775](../theory.d/THEORY-775.md), Step 3). Necessary only.
- **The optimum, which the paper does not give** (the record's,
  [THEORY-775](../theory.d/THEORY-775.md), Steps 4–6). On whitened input the maximizers are
  W = [e_1 … e_k] diag(s_i*) Rᵀ, e_i the bottom eigenvectors of Ċ (SFA's
  slow features), s_i* = (μ_i^{−1/2} − 1)^{1/2} for μ_i < 1 and 0
  otherwise, R any orthogonal matrix. μ_i < 1 is lag-one autocorrelation
  above ½. On raw input a feature of variance c is kept iff
  c > 2(1 − ρ), so the solution depends on the input's scale.

## Method

Not an algorithm with experiments. The paper derives an objective and
proposes training g_φ and p_θ jointly by stochastic variational inference
with sampling from q (§7). It reports no implementation and no
experiments.

## Concepts

- **Unordered slowness objective**: ⟨‖g(x_{t+1}) − g(x_t)‖²⟩_t for a
  vector g, the sum of SFA's per-feature losses without its sequential
  order (Eq. 2).
- **PSFA**: the authors' name for Turner and Sahani's probabilistic SFA,
  a linear Gaussian state-space model with linear emissions.
- **Instantaneous factorization**: q(z_t | x_t) only, mimicking SFA's
  instantaneous feature map (Eq. 19, Fig. 4c).
- **DVAE**: dynamical variational autoencoder (Girin et al. 2021), the
  family this model belongs to.

## Connections

It follows Turner and Sahani (2007) and the extensions it lists (Omori
2013 with observation noise; Fan et al. 2018 with t-distributed noise;
Tsujimoto and Omori 2020 with switching dynamics; Puli and Huang 2024
with nonlinearities) and places SFA among dynamical VAEs (Girin et al.
2021). Bengio and Bergstra (2009) are cited for SFA with the constraints
as soft penalties; Schüler et al. (2019) for SFA trained by gradient
through differentiable whitening. The related-work paragraph cites
"Kingma & Ba, 2014" (the Adam paper) for variational autoencoders where
Kingma and Welling (2014) is meant.

## Bearing on the record

- **What the paper says about Turner and Sahani, exactly.** §2: "Almost
  all subsequent work in this direction ... is based on an initial
  treatment of probabilistic SFA (later called PSFA) (Turner & Sahani,
  2007) in which SFA was derived from a linear Gaussian state space model
  ... with linear emissions. Linear SFA was recovered as the
  maximum-likelihood estimate of the emission model, with exact
  equivalence only in the limit of zero observation noise and annealing
  of the internal dynamics towards white noise", with the footnote
  "Although the latter mainly for scale correction." §4: Turner and
  Sahani "assumed the next step to be centered on a linear transformation
  Λz_{t−1} using a diagonal Λ with ordered diagonal elements λ_i". That
  is all. It is a summary by a later author and is not a reading of
  [LIT-1287](../literature.d/LIT-1287.md), which stays Unavailable.

- **The record's own check of that kind of model** (not the paper's, and
  not Turner and Sahani's text). Take x_t = A z_t with A square and
  invertible (no observation noise) and independent latents
  z_{i,t} = λ_i z_{i,t−1} + (1 − λ_i²)^{1/2} η_{i,t}, λ_i ∈ (0, 1)
  distinct. With W = A⁻¹ (rows w_i) and edge terms ignored, the
  log-likelihood per step is log|det W| − Σ_i [a_i w_iᵀCw_i +
  b_i w_iᵀĊw_i], a_i = (1 − λ_i)/(2(1 + λ_i)), b_i = λ_i/(2(1 − λ_i²)),
  using ⟨(y_t − λy_{t−1})²⟩ = (1 − λ)²⟨y²⟩ + λ⟨(Δy)²⟩. Stationarity gives
  2w_jᵀ(a_iC + b_iĊ)w_i = δ_ij. For i ≠ j this and its swap are two
  linear equations in w_iᵀCw_j and w_iᵀĊw_j whose determinant vanishes
  only if b_i/a_i = b_j/a_j; b/a = λ/(1 − λ)² is strictly increasing on
  (0, 1), so both cross terms vanish, and the ML rows are generalized
  eigenvectors of (Ċ, C), SFA's pencil ([THEORY-746](../theory.d/THEORY-746.md)), with no limit
  taken. The pairing that maximizes the likelihood sends the largest λ to
  the smallest eigenvalue, since log(1 + κμ) is supermodular. The output
  variances are 1/(2(a_i + b_iμ_i)), which is 1 when the data fit the
  model and tends to 1 as λ_i → 0 for any data. That matches the paper's
  footnote that the white-noise limit is for scale. Checked: with
  λ = (0.9, 0.7, 0.3), a random 3 × 3 mixing and 10⁵ steps, the
  ML rows found by BFGS had C-cosines with the SFA eigenvectors of 1 to
  10⁻⁵, in the predicted order.

- **[THEORY-733](../theory.d/THEORY-733.md), [THEORY-257](../theory.d/THEORY-257.md): slowness and closure, the record's
  derivation.** For a stationary Gaussian VAR(1) x_t = Ax_{t−1} + ε_t with
  covariance C, the lag covariance is AC and Ċ = 2C − AC − CAᵀ. If the
  process is time-reversible (AC = CAᵀ), Ċ = 2C(I − Aᵀ), so Ċw = μCw iff
  Aᵀw = (1 − μ/2)w: every SFA direction is a left eigenvector of A, any
  span of them is Aᵀ-invariant, and so it is exactly dynamically
  independent by [THEORY-733](../theory.d/THEORY-733.md). The reversible Gaussian VAR(1)s are exactly
  the noise-free square mixtures of independent AR(1) latents above, with λ_i allowed in (−1, 1) (put
  A = C^{1/2}SC^{−1/2} with S symmetric and diagonalize S). Off
  reversibility it fails: for A = [[0.8, 0.5, 0], [0, 0.5, 0.4],
  [0.1, 0, 0.3]] the slowest SFA direction w had
  ‖wᵀA − (wᵀAw)wᵀ‖ = 0.26 and |cos| 0.88 with the nearest left
  eigenvector, against 0.0015 and 1.000 in a reversible example. So in
  the linear Gaussian case, slowness selects closed coarse-grainings
  exactly when the process is reversible; otherwise the slowest feature is
  generally not closed and carries memory of the rest. Neither paper
  states this. It deserves a demonstrated CLAIM, with this NOTE as its
  ground.

- **[QUESTION-217](../questions.d/QUESTION-217.md).** [NOTE-971](NOTE-971.md) asked under which generative assumptions
  slow features are coarse, predictively sufficient or quickly learned.
  - *Generative assumptions*: two are now on the record, the paper's
    (random walk, any emission, instantaneous Gaussian encoder) and the
    noise-free linear AR(1) one checked above. In both, slowness is
    placed in the prior.
  - *Coarse*: in the linear whitened variational case the optimal spans
    nest by slowness as the latent dimension grows, so a slowness order
    is a refinement order of spans there, up to a cut at ρ = ½; within a
    span there is no order ([THEORY-775](../theory.d/THEORY-775.md)). This is the commuting case
    again, as in [THEORY-746](../theory.d/THEORY-746.md).
  - *Predictively sufficient*: the paper does not address it, and its
    encoder sees only x_t. In the linear reversible case the slow
    features are closed, which is self-sufficiency, not sufficiency for
    predicting x.
  - *Quickly learned*: not addressed.
- **[QUESTION-222](../questions.d/QUESTION-222.md).** By the reversibility result, a slow linear feature of
  an irreversible Gaussian VAR(1) is generally not Markov on its own:
  slowness does not buy freedom from memory. The paper's suggestion to
  condition q on the past (§8) is the variational counterpart of a
  Markovian augmentation, and it is not developed.
- **[QUESTION-218](../questions.d/QUESTION-218.md).** Nothing directly.
- **[CLAIM-331](../claims.d/CLAIM-331.md).** Both probabilistic readings write slowness into the
  prior: Turner and Sahani by an ordered Λ (as reported), this paper by
  a random walk. A probabilistic SFA cannot count as evidence that higher
  levels are slower. It is consistent with [THEORY-746](../theory.d/THEORY-746.md)'s conclusion that
  in SFA "higher is slower" is imposed by the objective.
- **[CLAIM-327](../claims.d/CLAIM-327.md).** Nothing on endogeneity.
- **Anthology.** No instruction for machine-learning practice; §8's
  research directions are suggestions, not findings. The
  anthology-candidate flag is for the representation-learning content.

## Limitations

- **No experiments, no optimum.** The paper derives an objective and
  argues about it. Whether the variational objective learns anything
  SFA-like with a nonlinear g is not shown.
- **Slips.** Eq. 35's spurious −d and the dropped constant; the sign in
  Eq. 41; "equivalent" for a necessary condition in §7; the unwritten
  t = 1 term and unstated p(z_1); the Adam citation for VAEs.
- **The decorrelation argument fails in the linear case.** The optimal
  features are an arbitrary rotation of scaled slow features, so they
  are correlated unless R = I. The reconstruction term secures a
  non-redundant span, not decorrelated outputs.
- **A threshold SFA lacks.** On whitened input, features with lag-one
  autocorrelation ρ ≤ ½ get zero weight, whatever k is. On raw input the
  threshold is c > 2(1 − ρ), which depends on scale.
- **The model is improper as a stationary process.** A random walk with
  unit steps has no stationary distribution, which the authors note.
  How this interacts with stationary data is not discussed.
- **Turner and Sahani are summarized, not reproduced.** The record's
  check above is of a noise-free model of that kind, not of their paper.

## Open questions

- What does the variational objective learn with a nonlinear encoder, and
  does the ρ > ½ cut have a nonlinear counterpart? A solution for a
  polynomial or kernel encoder on a known generative process would
  answer it.
- With q conditioned on the past (Eq. 18), does the optimum become a
  predictive state, sufficient for the future of x? A linear Gaussian
  computation, comparing it with the Kalman filter's state, would answer
  it and bear on [QUESTION-222](../questions.d/QUESTION-222.md).
- With observation noise, how do the ML directions of the linear AR(1)
  model depart from SFA's pencil? Turner and Sahani's paper is the place
  to look once a copy is reached.
