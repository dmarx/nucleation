# NOTE-162: French — Unitary inequivalence and structural realism

## Contribution

The existence of unitarily inequivalent representations in QFT has been offered, notably by Howard, as a serious problem for structural realism. Ladyman's founding example relied on the Stone–von Neumann equivalence of matrix and wave mechanics, and that equivalence fails for systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom. French sets out two responses: adopt conventional QFT, or take the relevant structure to span the space of inequivalent representations. He draws on spontaneous symmetry breaking and on superselection and quantum statistics, and suggests how the two options might be combined.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction: particle interpretations of QFT (Fraser; Haag's theorem; Bain's asymptotic particles) and field interpretations (Baker) are both in trouble, which pushes the realist toward structure. Quasi-set non-individuals are mentioned as an object-oriented alternative.
- "The Generation of Inequivalent Representations": algebraic QFT is the natural home for structuralism. Howard's challenge is that inequivalence is "of necessity" structural, not just ontological underdetermination.
- "Option 1: Adopt conventional QFT": Wallace's defence of Lagrangian QFT with cut-offs, in which the Standard Model is formulated, set against the AQFT view that such a theory is not well defined. This leads into underdetermination between AQFT and conventional QFT (Fraser vs Wallace).
- "Option 2: the Swiss Army Knife approach": Case 1 is spontaneous symmetry breaking, where the symmetry is preserved across all vacuum representations ("symmetry spreading"). Case 2 is superselection sectors and statistics, with permutation invariance as a superselection rule, paraparticles, and DHR analysis. The result is "trans-world structuralism", combined with Ruetsche's "representational realism".
- Conclusion: the realist can take conventional QFT's structure as approximately true. Alternatively, following Ruetsche's dissolution of the problem, she can adopt a modal conception of structure. Morrison's scepticism about a realist reading of SSB is flagged as the cost.

## Open questions

- A leading OSR proponent's response to a physics-based objection, and a test case for whether structure survives beyond the finite-dimensional QM on which OSR was built.
