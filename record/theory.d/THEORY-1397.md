# THEORY-1397: Coecke, Fritz and Spekkens propose that a resource theory is a symmetric monoidal category of resources and free transformations, that partitioning a process theory into free and costly processes generates such theories of states and of processes, and that the questions usually asked of a resource theory depend only on its preordered commutative monoid of convertibility, whose combination law can vary independently of its order

## Source

Coecke, Fritz & Spekkens (2014; 2016), [LIT-2032](../literature.d/LIT-2032.md); read in [NOTE-1591](../notes.d/NOTE-1591.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The definition** (Def. 2.1), a proposal rather than a theorem: morphisms
  are transformations that "can be implemented without any cost".
- **Generation** (Theorems 3.4, 3.12; Definition 3.20): a partitioned
  process theory yields theories of states, of parallel-combinable and of
  universally-combinable processes. Theorem 3.12's proof (Appendix A) was
  skimmed, and Lemmas 3.11 and 3.19 are informal sketches in the paper.
- **Decategorification** (Theorem 4.2): forgetting which free morphism
  converts gives a preordered commutative monoid; "the monoid structure of
  a resource theory can vary independently of the preorder structure" (§1).
- **Sum against maximum** (Examples 4.3–4.4): food combines by addition,
  proficiency by supremum; catalysis is excluded in non-interacting
  quantity-like theories (Theorem 4.13).

## What this does not say

- That transformations have costs, or how costs of successive steps
  compose: the morphisms are free, and quantitative conversion is the open
  problem of §6.
- That maximum and sum compose costs along a process: in the paper they are
  combinations of resources in parallel.
- Anything about time, rates, replenishment or a free set that changes.
- That the information it forgets, which transformation achieves a
  conversion, is unimportant for other
  questions; it is set aside for convertibility only.
