# ARG-142: Sloppy-model theory, whose account assumes a regular Fisher matrix, shows by analogy that the behaviour of sloppy models carries over to overparameterized neural networks

## The inference

A19 builds the sloppy-model picture on two cases the record holds: the
hopping model, whose Fisher eigenvalues separate by a factor of order t
when the density is seen after t steps ([THEORY-774](../theory.d/THEORY-774.md)), and the critical
Ising model under decimation, where two stiff directions survive and the
irrelevant ones fall by up to six decades ([THEORY-777](../theory.d/THEORY-777.md)). From them,
[CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md): parameter and behavioural complexity come apart when
predictions depend on a few stiff combinations. A19 §10 then carries this
to networks: "A neural network may contain billions of parameters, but
only some combinations may significantly affect particular outputs or
tasks. Identifying those combinations is related to sensitivity analysis,
effective dimensionality, and model compression. The analogy isn't
exact—neural networks introduce additional issues of training,
overparameterization, and generalization—but the conceptual connection is
strong."

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are they alike, and is it the respect the conclusion
  needs?** Both have many parameters and outputs sensitive to few
  combinations of them. The conclusion needs more: that the network's
  behavioural complexity is measured as a sloppy model's is, by the
  number of Fisher eigenvalues above the resolution the data give, with
  the rest spread over decades below. That is the respect in which the
  record's two cases are sloppy.
- **What relevant difference is there?** Regularity. A sloppy model, as
  [CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md) states it, has a Fisher matrix of full rank whose
  eigenvalues are small but nonzero. An overparameterized network is
  singular: its Fisher matrix loses rank on whole sets of parameters
  (permutations, dead units, merged units, rescalings), and at such points
  the decisive structure is at higher order than the Fisher matrix sees.
  [CLAIM-813](../claims.d/CLAIM-813.md): "at a singular point the Fisher spectrum does not give
  the learning coefficient", and the record's measure of a singular
  model's effective complexity is that coefficient, which falls below half
  the parameter count where the optimal set is degenerate ([THEORY-087](../theory.d/THEORY-087.md),
  [THEORY-1253](../theory.d/THEORY-1253.md)). A model can be singular with no spread of eigenvalues at
  all (Y ∼ N(ab, 1)).
- **What transfers, and what does not?** Transfers: that a network's
  outputs can be sensitive to few combinations of its parameters, and
  that fitting all parameters is not needed to predict well. Does not
  transfer: the account of why, through a hierarchy of nonzero Fisher
  eigenvalues, and the count of behavioural complexity it gives. Where
  sloppiness and singularity do meet, at a fold edge and at finite sample
  size ([CLAIM-812](../claims.d/CLAIM-812.md), [ARG-138](ARG-138.md)), the network's sloppy directions end
  on strata, and the stratum, not the spread, decides the count.

## Verdict

The analogy does not carry [CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md) to overparameterized networks
in the strong form A19 §10 calls "strong". Its weak form, that few
combinations matter, holds of networks but is not the sloppy-model claim
and does not need it. Rejected as an inference to [CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md) for
networks; [CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md) itself, for regular models, is untouched.

## Note of 2026-11-21: Transtrum, Machta & Sethna (2011) and Transtrum et al. (2015), read

A dated note; the status is not changed. Read in [NOTE-2247](../notes.d/NOTE-2247.md) and
[NOTE-2242](../notes.d/NOTE-2242.md). Two bearings:

- **The premise is the originators' own.** The 2011 paper's count (Eq. 16):
  "if M < 2N there will exist nontrivial singular points of the metric at
  non-extreme values of the parameters", and it equates network metric
  singularities with model boundaries, citing Amari, Park and Ozeki
  ([LIT-2960](../literature.d/LIT-2960.md)). An overparameterized network has M < 2N, so on their
  own terms its metric is singular at finite parameters, as this
  argument's "relevant difference" says.
- **But the originators did press the analogy, on small cases.** The 2011
  paper's 4-4-2 tanh network has geodesic widths tracking its singular
  values (Fig. 6b), and the 2015 review reports "a clear hyperribbon
  structure" for an autoencoder's code, unpublished. The A19 §10 analogy
  therefore has a source in the programme, which this argument did not
  record. Neither case is an overparameterized network in its weights:
  [ARG-150](ARG-150.md) weighs them and finds they do not carry [CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md)
  there. The verdict here stands on that.

## Note of 2026-11-21: Amari, Park & Ozeki (2006), read

Supports the relevant difference this argument rests on. The Fisher
matrix of a multilayer perceptron "degenerates, and its inverse G⁻¹(θ)
does not exist in the set C : vw = 0", and such singular points "are not
isolated but form a continuum" at finite parameters (NOTE-tmptlwkf,
THEORY-tmpbobud); the paper's point is that regular-model theory fails
there ("The Cramér-Rao theorem is no longer valid at the critical set C").
It qualifies the earlier dated note's report that the 2011 paper equates
network metric singularities with boundaries, citing this source: the
source speaks of critical sets and cone apices, not boundaries
(ARG-tmpewh6z).
