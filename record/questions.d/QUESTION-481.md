# QUESTION-481: Who has standing to authorize changes to an arrangement's governing structure, by which procedures, and which protections lie beyond any ordinary delegation or revision of it?

## Why it is a question

A85 §8 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), on the
Declaration's and Virginia's right to reform government, separates three
capacities: to act under a governance structure, to correct departures
from its standards, and

> the capacity to transform the governance structure when the structure itself becomes inadequate or illegitimate.

and limits the third at once: "A majority's legitimate authority to reform
government cannot by itself justify extinguishing the underlying rights of
a minority." A90 XII names the problem:

> We therefore need a theory of **bounded constituent authority**: which actors or communities have standing to authorize changes to governance, which procedures are appropriate, and which protections remain beyond ordinary delegation or revision.

"This issue remains substantively unresolved. But its formal location is
now clear. It concerns the allocation of legitimate powers and immunities
over the transformation of governing structures."

The Virginia text bears on the first part. Its §3, as the National
Archives transcribes it, gives the right to "a majority of the community",
not to the community as a whole as A85 §1 says; so the historical source
already answers "who" with a majority, and the limit A states is a limit on
that majority.

## What the record holds

- **[CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)** (Active): an operative regime, the governing organization
  among regimes, and the capacity to transform that organization can
  persist or fail separately. This is A's third capacity, without its
  allocation.
- **[CLAIM-685](../claims.d/CLAIM-685.md)** (Proposed): institutions able to recognize, authorize and
  carry out revisions of their own governing standards do better where
  the inherited standards are inadequate. It is about the benefit of the
  capacity, not who holds it.
- **[CLAIM-410](../claims.d/CLAIM-410.md)** (Active): removing a constraint changes a profile of who
  holds governing positions, revision among them. A90 XII cites it:
  "where the governing powers move, whose claims constrain them, and who
  can legitimately revise their allocation".
- **[QUESTION-041](QUESTION-041.md)** (Open): which channels from members keep a whole's
  course correctable, and whether losing them is losing the capacity to
  transform. It asks about channels, not about limits on what they may
  do.

The limit half is where [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md) bears: if no delegation can confer
authority to extinguish certain standing, then no reconstitution by those
who delegated can either. [QUESTION-486](QUESTION-486.md) asks whether rights of that
kind can be had at all.

## What would count as an answer

- **A standing rule**: who may authorize a change of governing structure
  (all members, a majority, those affected, those who constituted it),
  with a reason that does not presuppose the structure being changed.
- **A procedure condition**: what makes a reconstitution authorized rather
  than a seizure, stated so that the two can be told apart by their
  histories and not only their outcomes.
- **A boundary**: which protections no reconstitution may remove, and an
  argument for the boundary that does not reduce to "whatever the current
  constitution entrenches".
- **A construction** in which an arrangement acts and corrects well while
  no one subject to it can change its governing rules (A90 XII's
  examples: a platform whose appeal rules can be revised arbitrarily),
  matched with one that differs only in that capacity.
