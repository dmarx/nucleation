# CLAIM-tmp0talu: Not every difference between translations is a difference of frame: some change the act being framed

## The claim

A18, by analogy with passive and active transformations: in a passive one "An
analogous communicative act is expressed and recognized under different
linguistic and social conventions"; in an active one "The translator actually
changes the communicative act—for example, converting affectionate teasing into
moral condemnation". "The party always gets its cut" is roughly passive, "The
high life eats your paycheck" roughly active ([CASE-tmpcc78z](../cases.d/CASE-tmpcc78z.md)). "**This distinction
matters because not every difference between translations can be explained as
a difference of frame. Some differences are changes to what is being
framed.**"

As an objection it limits the owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmp1bwsn](CLAIM-tmp1bwsn.md)): frame cannot be the main
thing if some variation is not variation of frame.

## Where it went

A24 gave up the fixed act U that a passive transformation holds fixed ([TERM-tmpuhia8](../terms.d/TERM-tmpuhia8.md)),
and the distinction went with it. The manuscript §4 separates "(i) exact
preservation, (ii) representation-changing equivariance, (iii) approximate
preservation, and (iv) structural reorganization", but has no way to tell (ii)
from (iv) in a given pair. Restated without an underlying act, the criterion
would be: an active transformation changes the decision-relevant observables
(stance, force) beyond what the change of target context requires.
