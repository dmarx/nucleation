# LIT-210: Philosophy of Microbiology

Maureen A. O'Malley, Emily C. Parke (2020), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/microbiology/>

## Key takeaways

- Philosophy of biology's animal-centred habits distort its conclusions: taking microbes seriously changes debates over species concepts, the tree of life, major transitions, model systems, and — through microbiome research — biological individuality and causal explanation.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry asks why philosophers should pay attention to microbes, given philosophy's focus on humans and philosophy of biology's historical focus on animals. It argues in four steps: microbes do most of the planet's biological work and underlie every major transition; microbiology gave rise to fields such as modern medicine, molecular biology and genomics; microbes serve as material models of general phenomena; and microbiome research bears on human nature, individuality and causality.
