# ARG-tmp51yxc: The persistence of maternal microchimeric cells, and the dependence of offspring tolerance on one marked subset of them, are evidence that the separation of mother and offspring is incomplete at the level of cells, and not by themselves evidence that individuality is graded

## The inference

1. [THEORY-tmp4rc81](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4rc81.md): microchimeric cells pass both ways, persist for decades
   and colonize almost any tissue.
2. [THEORY-tmp6t2l2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp6t2l2.md): in mice, one marked subset of maternal cells sustains
   the offspring's tolerance of maternal antigens, and depleting it removes
   the tolerance while the rest persist.
3. So after birth each organism holds living cells of the other's lineage,
   and some of them do work in its regulation.
4. [CLAIM-tmpn0ytb](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpn0ytb.md) concludes that the separation gestation begins is not
   completed at birth at the level of cells, which bears on the view that
   individuality is graded or processual.

Steps 1–3 support the first half of the conclusion. The second half,
gradedness, needs a premise the readings do not supply: that incorporation
of another's cells makes the host less of a separate individual, not just a
heterogeneous one.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** For
  the premise, no: two organisms whose separation was complete at birth
  would hold none of each other's living cells. For gradedness, yes. A
  sharply bounded organism can have parts of foreign origin, as a
  transplant recipient does. That is A11 §8's rival, and A11 §3 states its
  version: "An organism may become dynamically independent of another
  organism while retaining components acquired through their earlier
  coupling."
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** Not between
  gradedness and heterogeneity. What could: whether the host's
  self-maintenance depends on the foreign cells (A11 §7's third test,
  whether "the host can maintain its relevant organization independently
  of the continuing presence of those cells"), and whether the dependence
  runs back to the source organism. Depletion that removes a function
  shows dependence for that function; it is a step toward the first
  test, not the second.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** Partly. The tolerance
  result is a mouse result reported by a press post. The paper
  ([LIT-tmpxdjn3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxdjn3.md)) is Unfetched, and only its abstract was seen. The human
  brain result ([LIT-tmp3n00g](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3n00g.md)) is a transcriptomic identity in surgical
  epilepsy tissue, Unfetched, and identity is not function. Both readings
  call the field's definitions unsettled, and what counts as mixture
  depends on whether cell-free DNA and transient cells are counted.

## Verdict

Proposed. The inference holds for the premise and not, as it stands, for
the thesis. The claim says "bears on", which is the strength the evidence
supports. Reading the Immunity paper and the brain preprint is the next
step either way.
