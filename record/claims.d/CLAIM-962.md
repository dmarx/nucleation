# CLAIM-962: Reading the determinable questions as the operational domain, the operational cover is a genuine down-closed sub-cover of the scenario with inherited restriction maps, and determinacy selecting that sub-site is the sheaf-theoretic link the objection asked for, so a change of determinate-answerability is a change of operational domain and "cover" is used correctly

## The claim

This is the second of two readings [CASE-368](../cases.d/CASE-368.md) supports; it is the
owner's choice between them. It takes the modelling decision that the
operational scenario's domain is the determinable questions themselves: a
receiver population's "live" measurements are the ones it answers
determinately, D = D_θ(e) ([TERM-155](../terms.d/TERM-155.md)).

On that reading, [CASE-368](../cases.d/CASE-368.md) shows (fact 2) that the induced family
{C ∩ D : C ∈ M} is a genuine cover of D, down-closed and carrying the event
presheaf's restriction maps: a sub-site of the scenario. So the operational
cover **is** a family of contexts with restriction maps, the lead's first
horn, and this is the sheaf-theoretic relationship [CLAIM-251](CLAIM-251.md) said was not
yet shown — determinacy selects a down-closed sub-site. This claim therefore
objects to [CLAIM-251](CLAIM-251.md)'s conclusion that the operational notion is "not by
itself a change of cover": once the determinable questions are the domain, a
change in which questions are determinate is a change of that domain, and a
cover is a family of subsets of the domain, so it is a change of cover in
[CLAIM-251](CLAIM-251.md)'s own corrected sense (its note of 2026-10-19: "Changing the
measurement domain changes the cover too"). It corroborates [CLAIM-201](CLAIM-201.md)'s
usage: a rendering that makes a source-determinate question indeterminate for
target readers removes it from the operational domain, a genuine change of
cover.

The link is sheaf-theoretic (an induced sub-site), not a functor of e in any
canonical sense: the selection is still thresholded and non-injective
([CASE-368](../cases.d/CASE-368.md), fact 4). This reading answers [CLAIM-251](CLAIM-251.md) by taking its
"or a sheaf-theoretic relationship" branch rather than its "functorial" one.

## What it does not say

- It does not claim a functor from empirical models to covers; the sheaf
  relationship is the induced-sub-site construction, and θ is external data.
- It does not say the operational and sheaf covers are the same object; they
  are related as a scenario and its determinacy-induced sub-site.
- It does not make a change of cover into contextuality ([CLAIM-201](CLAIM-201.md): it
  "changed which questions can be asked, not shown that the answers cannot be
  glued").
- It does not decide whether to keep "cover" in the operational entries or
  adopt [TERM-155](../terms.d/TERM-155.md)'s name throughout; that, and the choice between this
  reading and [CLAIM-961](CLAIM-961.md), are the owner's.

The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the
owner's call.
