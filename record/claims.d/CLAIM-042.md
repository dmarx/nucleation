# CLAIM-042: Levels organize observables, not objects: a communicative object is identified by the intersection of constraint regions picked out by observables at several levels

## The claim

U28: "of course, this isn't a strict hierarchy. it's more like a hierarchy of
levels at which observables exist, and the 'objects' are intersections of
regions of observables." A75: "That's a more fundamental formulation ... You're
proposing something closer to an **ontology induced by overlapping regions of
observability**, where levels organize the observables rather than the objects
themselves." And: "*teasing* is not simply an object at the pragmatic level. It
is recognizable through a particular conjunction of linguistic, intentional,
evaluative, social, and sequential observables. That is, the category itself
may be a cross-level intersection."

Manuscript §1–2: "These observations are not ordered into one strict
ontological ladder"; "An outcome v defines a constraint region R(o,v)={s:o(s)=v}.
An identified communicative object may be represented by an intersection of
such regions."

## What it does not say

That the intersection lives in one shared space: A78 generalized it to local
sections ([CLAIM-038](CLAIM-038.md)), and the manuscript keeps both. Nor that similarity is
overlap: A75 rejected μ(X_u ∩ X_v)/μ(X_u ∪ X_v) in the same reply because
"source and target regions may inhabit different observational spaces", which
needs a correspondence of observables (the manuscript's τ, §6).
