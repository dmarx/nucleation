# LIT-tmpsf0sf: On Social Facts

Margaret Gilbert (1989), *Routledge, London and New York (x + 521 pp.; ISBN 0-415-02444-7)*; reissued by Princeton University Press, 1992 (paperback ISBN 9780691020808) — DOI-10.1515/9780691214627 (the Princeton edition)

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, under the topic
`social-ontology` that [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added, as Gilbert's founding book.

`Deferred` because no lawful full text was reachable, so it is not read
and no NOTE is filed. What follows is only what the publishers and the
chapter metadata say.

**What the publisher says.** Princeton's catalogue page frames the book
around the question whether social groups are real independently of
individuals' thoughts and beliefs. It says Gilbert analyses shared
action, social convention, group belief and the concept of a group, and
proposes that core social phenomena are "plural subject" phenomena. It
names Durkheim, Simmel, Weber and David Lewis among the thinkers
discussed.

**Contents** (chapter titles and pages from Crossref, Princeton edition):

- I Introduction: everyday concepts and social reality (1–21)
- II 'Social action' and the subject matter of social science (22–57)
- III Action, meaning, and the social (58–145)
- IV Social groups: a Simmelian view (146–236)
- V After Durkheim: concerning collective belief (237–314)
- VI Social convention (315–407)
- VII On social facts (408–445)

Chapter V, on collective belief, is why `epistemology` is tagged.
Chapter VI is Gilbert's treatment of convention, which takes up David
Lewis; Lewis's *Convention* is being filed in the record the same day
([LIT-tmp9s7wn](LIT-tmp9s7wn.md)). Chapter II's question, what social science is about, is
why `social-science` is tagged.

**Neighbours.** The short version of the plural-subject account is
"Walking Together" ([LIT-tmpe6ufg](LIT-tmpe6ufg.md)). The record's only reading of Gilbert so
far is second-hand, through Tomasello's commentaries ([LIT-125](LIT-125.md), [NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md)).

No instruction for machine-learning practice is visible in what was
seen, so nothing here points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.**

- Whether the book already uses "joint commitment", or only "plural
  subject", and how the two are related here.
- The argument of chapter VI against Lewis's account of convention as a
  regularity sustained by preferences and expectations.
- What chapter V says a group's belief is, and whether a group can
  believe what no member believes.
