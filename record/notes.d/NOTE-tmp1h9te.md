# NOTE-tmp1h9te: Moen (2019) — The Unabomber's Ethics

## Contribution

What Moen calls the first careful philosophical criticism of these ideas ("never before been subject to careful philosophical criticism", Abstract), separating the empirical worries he accepts from the evaluative premises he rejects.

## Key results

1. §2, Moen's paraphrase of the argument about cars: cities develop so that it is "difficult to be a pedestrian", which prompts more car use.
2. §3: "Technological progress is indeed an arms-race" in which restraint is out-competed.
3. §3: control faces "both an epistemic problem and a coordination problem".
4. §3: Kaczynski "assumes an ethical theory according to which the benefits of technology have little or no real value".

## Assumptions

Moen counts technology's benefits and harms even-handedly and takes its empirical uncontrollability largely as given.

## Concepts

Evaluative asymmetry; power process; surrogate activity; arms race; the epistemic and coordination problems of control.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmpbypt7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md)**: corroborates two of its mechanisms, in a source independent of the exchange: infrastructure (the car city, result 1) and competition (result 2, which Moen endorses). Moen treats Kaczynski's refusal to count options the system leaves open as an unsupported premise, which favours the claim's measure, what remains open, over its rival.
- **[CLAIM-tmpsmg1c](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpsmg1c.md)**: supports its premise. The epistemic problem of control is the uncertainty with later learning the claim's reason needs; Moen states no burden rule.
- **The exchange** (A1 §3, A2) argued against Kaczynski's assumption that dependence destroys autonomy; Moen's diagnosis says the same from outside.
- **Sensitivity.** The paper treats the work as an object of criticism; nothing of its programme is filed.

## Limitations

Moen's reconstruction is about two pages and draws on a later book of Kaczynski's, not this one.
