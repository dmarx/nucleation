# CLAIM-1293: Contracts can form between processes

## The claim

The owner's words, at U14, after A15 had asked how demands, claims,
standing, reasons, principles, duties, violations and harms fit together:
"a concept that might help us analyze these phenomena is the notion of
service level agreements. contracts can form between processes. contracts
can even operationalize standing through e.g. prioritization structure in
the contract that governs resolution of disputes like races."

Three parts:

- **A concept for analysis.** The service-level agreement, an agreement
  between a service and those that depend on it about what it will
  deliver and what follows when it does not, is offered as a tool for
  analysing the normative vocabulary A15 had laid out.
- **Contracts form between processes.** The parties need not be persons:
  processes inside one organization can come to stand in such relations.
- **Contracts operationalize standing.** Standing, in A15 an entitlement
  to consideration or intervention, can be given an operational form: a
  contract's prioritization structure decides whose claim prevails when
  processes contend, as in a race.

## Where the record stands

- No entry held service-level agreements, contracts between processes or
  priority structures before this filing. The nearest are [TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md)
  (precedence, jurisdiction and suspension, which A16 §2 says a contract
  can set all at once), [TERM-101](../terms.d/TERM-101.md) (a grant runs through a maintained
  relation of granting, delegation or eligibility), [CLAIM-277](CLAIM-277.md) (durable
  authority as the regularity of grants), [CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md) (claimed precedence
  and granted authority are different quantities) and [CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md)
  (residual control in an incomplete contract, between persons).
- Standing is held as moral standing in [TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md) and as directed
  answerability in [CLAIM-739](CLAIM-739.md). The owner's proposal gives it an operational
  sense that presupposes neither moral consideration nor agency.
- "Contract" here is not the contractualist's. [QUESTION-483](../questions.d/QUESTION-483.md) and
  [LEAD-033](../leads.d/LEAD-033.md) ask whether reasonable rejection explains the record's
  allocations of normative power; that is a test of justification among
  persons. The owner's contracts are maintained relations among processes
  that settle contention, and the second part of the proposal
  ([CLAIM-1267](CLAIM-1267.md)) keeps them apart from moral duty.

## What A16 made of it

A16 calls the service-level agreement "a missing intermediary between
functional organization and normative organization" and maps the
engineering vocabulary (indicator, objective, agreement, priority class,
admission control, error budget, escalation, renegotiation) onto the
record's. It narrows the example: priority inversion "is not technically
identical to a race condition", and "operational standing is not reducible
to priority. A low-priority process could possess a protected guarantee of
eventual service, an entitlement to escalate a missed deadline, or
immunity from arbitrary cancellation." Its development is filed as
[TERM-174](../terms.d/TERM-174.md), [CASE-427](../cases.d/CASE-427.md), [CLAIM-1239](CLAIM-1239.md) and [CLAIM-1270](CLAIM-1270.md).

## What it does not say

- It does not say that processes bound by such contracts have moral
  standing or owe one another anything morally; the second part of the
  proposal says the opposite ([CLAIM-1267](CLAIM-1267.md)).
- It does not say that every priority rule is a contract, or that a
  contract's priority structure exhausts the standing it secures.
