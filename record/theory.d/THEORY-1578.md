# THEORY-1578: Stich argues that Sober and Wilson's evolutionary arguments from parental care give no evidence for psychological altruism, the strongest of them failing if hedonism rests on sticky sub-doxastic beliefs, which would make it as reliable as an ultimate desire for the child's welfare, and holds that evolutionary and psychological altruism are logically independent

## The account

Whether a desire for someone else's good is ultimate or instrumental is
a question about where it sits in practical reasoning, not about its
object. A parent who wants to help her child as an end is an altruist; one
who wants it because she believes she will feel bad otherwise is a
hedonist; they act alike. Sober and Wilson argue selection would favour
the first for reliability. Stich grants that hedonism depends on beliefs
linking helping to one's feelings, and that beliefs can be undermined,
but if those links are sticky sub-doxastic states hedonism is as
reliable. Evolutionary and psychological altruism are independent, so
kin selection is silent about motive.

## Evidence

Argument with counterexamples from other emotion systems; no model of
costs ([NOTE-1282](../notes.d/NOTE-1282.md)).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) cites Hamilton's rule as "an evolutionary analogue of a
self that extends to kin". Stich's independence of evolutionary and
psychological altruism means kin selection bears on the question only by
analogy. His division also tells the question's two readings apart: a
desire for another's good instrumental to the agent's own, or an
ultimate desire whose object is counted as part of the self.
