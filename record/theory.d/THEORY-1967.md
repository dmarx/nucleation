# THEORY-1967: Scanlon holds that contractualism explains the Hohfeldian structure of moral rights, claims, liberties and powers including a promisee's power to release the promisor, by the reasons people have to insist on principles with that structure, without taking rights as basic, so that it matters little which parts of the structure are called rights

Scanlon, "Reply to Leif Wenar" (2013), [LIT-2746](../literature.d/LIT-2746.md).

## The account

"Contractualism seeks to explain not only first-order claim rights but also
powers, as well as the prior liberties" (p. 403). Both parties "have reason
to insist on a principle that includes the condition "unless B consents to
X's not being done," rather than a principle that would make promisory
obligations unalterable once they are undertaken" (p. 403). "I don't think
that we need to appeal to the idea of a right to explain why our moral
situation has this form, and don't think it matters very much exactly which
parts of this structure we denominate as rights" (pp. 403–404).

## What it does not show

- That powers once justified structure later justification: that is
  [CLAIM-735](../claims.d/CLAIM-735.md)'s addition.
- Institutional powers, or authority over obligations beyond the promise
  case, which Wenar argues contractualism needs.
