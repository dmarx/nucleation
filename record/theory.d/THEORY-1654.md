# THEORY-1654: Microchimeric cells pass in both directions between mother and offspring, are rare, can persist for decades and colonize almost any tissue, the brain among them, while their function, the mechanism of their persistence and what counts as microchimerism remain open

## The account

Pregnancy exchanges cells in both directions, and breastfeeding and
transplantation add more. The cells are rare, around one host cell in a
million, persist for decades, and have been found to differentiate into
almost any cell type in almost any tissue. Some are tolerated through
regulatory T cells that their presence sustains, and one maternal subset in
mice is needed for tolerance while the rest persist without it. Whether
the cells have a function, how they escape rejection, and whether the term
covers transient material or only engrafted, active cells are the field's
open questions.

## Evidence

The review's synthesis of its cited studies and its 29 experts' questions
([NOTE-1768](../notes.d/NOTE-1768.md)). No new data.

## What it does not say

That microchimerism makes mother and offspring partly one organism, or that
it grades individuality. The review speaks of "a complex interaction
between two genetically distinct individuals".
