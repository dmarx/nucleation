**Why the AISTATS paper.** The 2016 AISTATS paper was read in place of UAI
2015. It holds the two-sided "fundamental causal coarsening theorem",
which partitions effects as well as causes, as Hoel's coarse-grainings
do. UAI 2015's theorem covers only the causes of a fixed target.

## What the readings settle

- **QUESTION-204's second half has its test.** Causal feature learning puts
  two micro states in one macro state exactly when manipulating to either
  gives the same distribution of effects. It needs no intervention
  distribution. THEORY-tmpyfcov derives the link to effective information,
  checked numerically on 245,394 cases.
  - **The derivation.** Under one full-support distribution used at both
    levels, a coarse-graining keeps all the micro EI exactly when it merges
    only causes with identical rows and only effects with proportional
    columns. Those are causal feature learning's partitions and their
    refinements, whatever the distribution.
  - **Where the two agree and part.** On which coarse-grainings lose
    nothing, the causal criterion and the test agree. Hoel's
    uniform-per-level EI does not: Eberhardt & Lee's (13) and (14) merge
    causes with different rows.
  - **What stays open.** Whether a graded criterion can rank lossy
    coarse-grainings without bringing a distribution back.
- **The test cannot show emergence.** Its verdict is the coarsest
  partition with unambiguous manipulations, and the micro never loses. So
  it can confirm that a macro is proportionate, as on Hoel's (11), but
  never that causation emerges there. This adds to the finding of
  THEORY-tmphfpe7 against THEORY-787. Whether THEORY-787 is Rejected is
  still the owner's call.
- **Cernat 2011 is not a test of CLAIM-430's defeater.** This rests on the
  abstract and the method text quoted in search results; the paper was not
  read. All three arms read a friendship story. What varies is whether the
  Roma character's friend is from the reader's own group, not whether the
  participant takes part. The defeater still has no within-study test in
  the record.

## Errors found

- **Chalupka et al. 2016, Theorems 5 and 12:** the effect partition by
  equal columns is called minimal sufficient. It is not, because effects
  with proportional columns merge with no loss. Rows (0.2, 0.4, 0.4) and
  (0.1, 0.2, 0.7) are a counter-example, checked here. The overview's
  normalization is the correct criterion, but it applies it to clusters
  only.
- **The overview:**
  - eqs. (5)–(6) drop Bayes's 1/P(h);
  - eq. (1) has a label slip, and Lemma 8 an index slip.
- **Lemmer & Wagner's reference for Cernat** carries Ertmer et al.'s venue
  (Internet and Higher Education 14:251–261). This settles which of the two
  references NOTE-tmpgbgvt found wrong.
