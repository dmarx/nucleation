# LIT-tmpafhxm: Organizational Accounts of Malfunction: The Dual-Order Approach and the Normative Field Alternative

Barandiaran, X. E. (2025). Organizational Accounts of Malfunction: The Dual-Order Approach and the Normative Field Alternative. *Biological Theory* 21(1), 51–69. DOI 10.1007/s13752-025-00500-z

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-tmpliz3l](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpliz3l.md)).

- **The dual-order account is too narrow.** Malfunction there needs a
  regulated switch between regimes that a first-order trait fails to
  follow. So a stiffening artery at rest, or a stress response that never
  triggers, cannot malfunction.
- **Its two assumptions are false of physiology.** First-order norms are
  not all-or-nothing (bradycardia, hypoxia), and regulation is mostly
  continuous modulation, not switching.
- **The normative field.** The viability space has viable, precarious and
  terminal regions. The field is "the minimum constant parametric change,
  for each point of the precarious region, necessary to avoid the
  irreversible disintegration of the system".
- **Three failures.** Subfunction: right direction, too slow. Malfunction:
  wrong or no direction. Nonfunction: nothing done where something must be.
  Regulatory traits fall under the same standard.
- **Compensation costs viability**, so it does not hide a dysfunction, and
  some illness is a shrinking of the viability space rather than a failure
  to contribute. Shown in a one-variable toy model; no field is computed for
  a real organism.

## Why this is here

A11 asked what makes a trusted policy's success good for the organization
and not just good at keeping its own authority. That is [CLAIM-tmpf1063](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpf1063.md)'s
open edge and [QUESTION-tmpiyoc5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpiyoc5.md). [THEORY-tmp9epie](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp9epie.md) records the account and
answers the question in part. An authority structure is a regulation, and
regulations can malfunction against the organization's own field. A
shrinking of the viability space is harm on this account, which is where
protective lock-in ([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)) would register. But the standard is the
whole's viability. An authority that keeps the organization as viable as
before is not dysfunctional, however it came to rule, and note 23 leaves
open which norms prevail when a regulatory level becomes autonomous.

The filing said the work criticizes Mossio, Saborido and Moreno 2009
([LIT-570](LIT-570.md)). It cites that paper as part of the standard approach, but the
definitions it criticizes are quoted from Moreno and Mossio's 2015 book
([LIT-536](LIT-536.md), unavailable) and Saborido 2011. The filing's "viability theory"
is Ashby's viability space with bifurcation analysis; Aubin is not cited.
