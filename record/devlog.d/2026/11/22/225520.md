**Going right, so far.** The exchange "Explain First Contact RL" runs to
seventeen owner turns and about 240 KB of visible text, and ChatGPT read the
record from main before answering: every one of the 71 codes it cited
exists, and their titles match what it cited them for. So the filing is
mostly a map, and the work is in the parts the record does not hold.

The lead read the whole transcript first, then split it by stretch of turns
into five section workers (First Contact and the scroll interface;
dispositions and the self; Luria, flow, team and singularity; ecology,
measures and the representation hypotheses; regulation), forks that share
the lead's reading. Each got the codes its stretch cites, the ledger items
the lead had listed, and the rule from [ADR-139](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-139.md): list every file, append
dated notes at the end of a held file and retry on a failed edit, commit
nothing. Temporary codes are random, so parallel `luria new` calls cannot
collide.

**A choice worth checking at the retro.** One thesis, about what a self is,
recurs across four of the five stretches (A13, A15, A21 to A22, A24, A26,
A29), each version revising the last. Giving it to any one worker would
have made that worker wait on the codes the others file. Instead each
worker files its own versions as ChatGPT's claims, and the lead audits the
whole lineage under step 9b once all five have reported. Whether that left
the lineage's account thinner than a single owner would have made it is the
question for the retrospective.

**Going wrong, and caught by CI.** Twenty-two of the entries the section
workers drafted had a body heading shorter than the title, or a heading
missing its code prefix. Each worker had run the narrow reference scan,
which does not check headings, and so each reported its files clean.
docs-check failed the pull request with one violation per file. The fix
was mechanical: each heading rewritten from the title, then checked with
`luria.lint.check_title`, the check docs-check itself runs. The step was
missing from the read-and-file skill, which now states it. A brief for
drafters should carry it too.
