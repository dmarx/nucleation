### Summary
A constructivist grounded-theory study of 12 self-selected Polish BDSM practitioners (6 F, 6 M by designated gender, one genderfluid; aged 20-49), interviewed once each without a fixed schedule. Coding yielded categories for gender, sexual orientation, "Identification with the BDSM role" and "What does BDSM mean to me?" (11 subcategories: a game; path to satisfaction; bond with partner; accepting one's carnality; psychological needs; overcoming limits; diversity; testing preferences; exploration; curiosity; earning money). From these the authors propose "the initial model of the process of defining one's own sexuality" (p. 121): confrontation with one's needs, then belonging to the BDSM community, ending in identification with BDSM or in a BDSM role, the two joined as a continuum; with consequences (gains and costs) alongside.

### Contribution
A small, descriptive qualitative study that gives a named distinction, identification versus role, and asserts a continuum between them. Its value for the record is the participants' own words separating a scene-bounded role from a role that extends into everyday life and self-description, plus material on concealment, coming out and stigma. It is not a test of the model.

### Key results (page, verbatim)
- Source of the distinction (p. 122, citing Ortmann & Sprott): identity "relatively permanent in comparison with the role which may become fluctuant, related to the context, specific situation and/or activity".
- The authors' terms, introduced during analysis (p. 128): "identification (as a process of developing an identity) and a role (assumed in a given situation)".
- Switch (p. 122): one "who finds satisfaction in both domination and submission, and who likes to change roles according to the changing situation". M3 is a switch (Table 1); no excerpt from him on switching.
- Role as identity (p. 130): participants "often considered their role to be their identity, nature or something acquired through sexual experiences"; "the role is part of their identity rather than a label describing a set of behaviours in a specific context". M1: "I am a master, and only a master." F3: "I think that it is the same sort of orientation."
- The continuum, first as an assumption (p. 130): "it might be assumed that their understanding of BDSM practices would be on a continuum – from just assuming a role in the relationship to identification, present also when the role is not assumed." Then: "This assumption was confirmed in the responses of the participants", some describing practices "as something external and a different kind of experience rather than an area related to their identity".
- Role as game (p. 131): M1 "We're playing a game, I assume a role, and the other person assumes another one." M2 "For me it's a game." F6 (submissive): "You need to earn it".
- Model (pp. 138, 140): "a multidirectional process which can lead to identifying with a role in BDSM, also in a non-sexual context"; starts with "the experience of confrontation with one's needs"; "the next stage is joining the BDSM community". p. 140: "other participants understood the role as a sexual scene which they enter in the course of a session. In the model discussed, we presented it as a continuum. Identification with sexual practices is not a condition necessary to end this process."
- Everyday-life extension and temporariness (p. 141): identification means BDSM "also has reference to everyday life, for instance, in the form of continuing relationships of dependence or perceiving oneself in the context of those practices"; taking up a role is "entering a certain role only in the course of the session limited in time"; "it is rather a continuum between them".
- Starting point (p. 141): "The starting point of shaping one's identification with BDSM practices was gender identity and sexual orientation."
- Summary (p. 144): "a certain stage-like nature of the process"; "Identification may lead to BDSM becoming one's unique lifestyle".
- Stigma and attribution: men at meetings assume F3 "is submissive because you are a female" and treat her so "till they are slapped in the face" (p. 129); "internalized kinkphobia" (p. 139); F3: being dominant "would be perceived worse in my social group than admitting, for example, that I am a lesbian" (p. 141). Concealment or coming out (pp. 139, 141, 143).
- Diagram 1 (p. 137): boxes Confrontation with the need -> Belonging to the BDSM community -> Identification / BDSM Role (dashed two-way arrow between them); Intragroup relations; Consequences. Gender identity and orientation are not drawn.

### Method and assumptions
- Sample: 12, self-selected via "thematic forums and websites" (p. 123); gender balanced by design; all males heterosexual, four females heteroflexible, two heterosexual (Table 1). Unpaid, anonymous, ethics approval (no number).
- Interviews: unstructured, single, in person, at participant-chosen locations (public or home), "from 15 minutes up to 1.5 hours" (p. 123); 8 topics listed (p. 126).
- Coding: word-by-word, line, in vivo, focused coding, graphic models per interview; two researchers "performed individually and then the data were joined and compared with each other" (p. 127). No agreement statistic, no member check, no audit trail, no account of translation from Polish (English in vivo terms kept).
- Saturation asserted: "Saturation is reached when the information is doubled" (p. 127); no evidence of when it was reached.
- Positionality: only general: "the researcher's own experiences, beliefs, values and opinions are not without significance" (p. 123). Nothing on the authors' own relation to the community. Terms used "descriptively" with "no reference to a specific theoretical concept" (p. 123).
- Internal inconsistency: p. 129 "Three out of four examined females" were heteroflexible; Table 1 shows four of six. Limitations (p. 144) say "no individuals identifying as non-heteronormative volunteered", though one participant is genderfluid and four heteroflexible.

### Concepts (authors' words)
- Identification: "a process of developing an identity" (p. 128); extends to "everyday life" (p. 141).
- Role: "assumed in a given situation" (p. 128); "only in the course of the session limited in time" (p. 141).
- Identity: from Ortmann & Sprott, "relatively permanent" (p. 122); otherwise undefined.

### Verdict on the abstract
Accurate as a report of what the authors say. Stages, the gender/orientation starting point, the two modes of self-definition and the everyday-life extension are all in the text. But the abstract's "showed stages" overstates: the model is "proposed" from retrospective single interviews; no stage sequence is traced per participant. "Can be seen as a continuum" is the authors' hedged framing; the paper places no participant at an intermediate point.

### Bearing on the record
- QUESTION-260 (constitutive vs influencing): supplies cases and a candidate marker, does not answer. The paper's identification is a role that operates "present also when the role is not assumed" and in "everyday life", against a role entered and left with the scene: a person-level analogue of constitutive vs worked-through. The continuum does not object to the dichotomy in any argued way; at most it suggests constitutiveness has degrees of scope (scene, relationship, everyday life, self-definition). Two cautions. The data are self-descriptions (TERM-072 sense 2), not evidence about practical organization (sense 1). And the same participants talk both ways (M1 "only a master" p. 130 and "a game" p. 131; F3 "part of me" p. 130 and an addition to "vanilla sex" p. 134), which reads better as two dimensions (self-ascription; bounds of enactment) than as one continuum. That is a point for the question: "constitutive" may need to be stated per dimension.
- QUESTION-257 / CLAIM-421 (kinds of non-enactment): supplies cases, does not distinguish them. Between scenes, a scene-bounded role is held but not engaged by arrangement: activation, not loss of conditions; the scene is itself the condition, set up deliberately. Participants "currently looking for a partner" (F3, M2, M3, G4; Table 1) are closer to loss of the conditions for enactment while identification persists. The community stage ("intragroup relations") is where social reproduction would sit. The paper itself does not frame any of this as cessation.
- TERM-075: supplies cases for each sense, by report. Held: men assuming F3 submissive (p. 129); the submissive man as "not a real man" (p. 128). Imagined attribution and imagined audience: F3's "my social group" (p. 141). Appropriated: "internalized kinkphobia" (p. 139), an appropriated negative attribution; and community role-labels taken up for self-presentation (Fetlife's 65 role terms, p. 122). Concealment shaped by expected judgement without appropriation is the "2 and 3 without 4" pattern (CASE-142).
- TERM-072: the paper's "role" and "identity" are both represented perspectives (sense 2). Only a parallel.
- TERM-045: the paper's "identity" spans dispositional ("a part of my personality", p. 130), persisting ("relatively permanent"), narrative and presented senses. Cite it as "self (sense open)".
- TERM-099: parallel only. "Testing and discovering one's preferences" and "overcoming one's limits" look like formation, but participants frame them as discovery ("we are what we are", p. 130), and the paper cannot tell formation from an always-held disposition (the term's Installation exclusion and A52's caution).
- TERM-166: no bearing (no dyad or group treated as a unit).
- CLAIM-454: parallel. Role labels (Yost & Hunter's 11, Fetlife's 65; p. 122) are a feature allocation; the same label ("submissive") is realized as identity in one person and as a game in another (F6, p. 131).
- CLAIM-543: parallel. Scene roles are characters entered and left. F6's "You need to earn it" shows the dyad's roles conditioning one another's availability, not exchangeable components. No interventional data.
- QUESTION-043: a case, no measure. F3 re-reads childhood games as early dominance (p. 138): narrative incorporation (THEORY-060), but no coherence γ.
- Held entries the list misses: CLAIM-421 "Ceased enactment need not lose a perspective" (Active), the kinds QUESTION-257 asks about; CLAIM-196 "Roles in disjoint contexts cannot conflict" (Proposed), with M6's "double life" and concealment vs coming out as audience segregation and collapse; CLAIM-415 "Organized by a judgement no collective holds" (Proposed), with F3's feared "social group"; CASE-141 "The label that confirms itself" (Active), stigma as a parallel; THEORY-1603 "People seek positive identity via comparison" (Active), social identity and low-status group strategies, against concealment and community belonging; THEORY-1114 "Metacontrast sets salient self-categories" (Proposed); TERM-118 "disposition" (Proposed), sense 3 standing repertoire, for the role between scenes; CLAIM-417 "Organizational perspectives explain the self" (Proposed); THEORY-120 "Free will is identification with one's motives" (Proposed), a homonym: the paper's "identification" is not Frankfurt's. No held entry on BDSM, coming out or minority stress (the "kink" hits are physics).

### Limitations and gaps
- What the continuum is a continuum of is never said. "Identification" is a process and "role" a situational state, so the poles are not of one kind. Candidate axes (temporal scope, contexts reached, self-ascription) are not separated.
- The continuum was "assumed" from sample diversity before the data "confirmed" it (p. 130), and the confirmation shows two kinds of answers, not intermediate cases. No participant is placed on it.
- The stages are proposed, not shown: single retrospective interviews, no per-participant sequence; "multidirectional" (p. 138) sits uneasily with "stage-like" (p. 144). The "starting point" (gender identity and orientation) is not in the diagram and is not argued from the data beyond participants introducing themselves that way (p. 128).
- Small self-selected online sample, no non-heterosexual males, single short interviews (some 15 min), varied settings, no validity checks beyond two-coder comparison, no positionality. The count error on p. 129 and the "non-heteronormative" statement on p. 144 lower confidence in the reporting.
- Clinical framing (non-pathologizing) is well motivated but outside the data.

### Candidate THEORY
Sentence: Niebudek and Iniewicz find, in grounded-theory interviews with 12 Polish BDSM practitioners, that practitioners define their practice either by identification with a BDSM role, which extends beyond sessions into everyday life and self-description, or by taking up a role only for a time-limited scene, and propose that these lie on a continuum rather than forming an either-or.
promote_when: An independent study of a different and larger sample classifies practitioners' self-definitions by stated criteria and finds intermediate positions between scene-limited role-taking and identification extending into everyday life, not two separate groups.
