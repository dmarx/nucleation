# NOTE-033: "Broadview Six" grand jury transcript, 9 Oct 2025

## Contribution

This is the transcript of the first federal grand jury session (Special June 2024 Grand Jury, N.D. Ill.) at which prosecutors sought to indict six protesters over a 26 Sept 2025 confrontation with an ICE supervisor's vehicle outside the Broadview, Illinois, processing facility. The charges sought were felony conspiracy (18 U.S.C. §372) and misdemeanour counts under §111. An agent narrated protest videos, and the prosecutor then explained the law and answered jurors' questions. Press coverage reports that this panel declined to indict (a "no bill") and that an indictment was returned only at a third presentation, on 23 Oct 2025. The case later collapsed, and Judge April Perry ordered the transcripts released on 9 June 2026.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Transcript pp. 3–4 (Doc. 219 at 3–4): Mecklenburg's opening. She had asked to wait for "the Thursday grand jury" because of mutual trust, and said "I don't charge people unless I'm absolutely sure". She instructed jurors to set politics aside. CNI reports that the judge had earlier described this as "putting her personal credibility and trustworthiness on the line", that is, vouching.
- Transcript pp. 5–8: The prosecutor's preview of the facts. A supervisor inched his vehicle through a crowd that surrounded it and banged on it, and "Pig" was scratched into it. The defendants were identified from social-media posts. She offered her own view ("This is definitely not testimony") that his calm prevented a tragedy.
- Transcript pp. 53–62: Jurors ask why the driver did not stop or call for backup, whether the scratches predated the incident (the car sat five days before it was photographed), and whether the truck driver behind him was interviewed. The prosecutor several times labels her answers "argument", not testimony.
- Transcript pp. 63–72: Explanation of §372 conspiracy ("A conspiracy is just an agreement ... You can agree by action") and of §111 simple assault. Jurors ask whether bystanders who filmed would be liable, whether the officer was yet "on duty", and whether a driver may push through people standing in front of a car.
- Transcript pp. 73–77: The prosecutor closes by asking the jurors to sign the indictment (Count 1 under §372; Counts 2–7 under §111). A juror says "I personally feel the complete opposite way". The transcript ends with the reporter's certificate. The vote is not in the transcript.
