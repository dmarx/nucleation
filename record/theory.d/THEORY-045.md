# THEORY-045: Felt affect is the experience of the body's regulatory state, valenced by what that state means for staying alive and present throughout waking experience, not only in emotion; whether what is felt is a prediction of that state or a map of it is unsettled

## Source

- Barrett (2017), [LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), as read in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md): "What is a brain for?",
  "How does a brain perform allostasis?" and the Glossary.
- Damasio & Carvalho (2013), [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md), as read in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md): the abstract,
  Box 1, the section on insular and brainstem lesions, and the concluding
  remarks.

## What was actually shown

Neither paper reports an experiment that tests this claim. Barrett gives a
synthesis of cited work, and Damasio and Carvalho give an opinion piece
argued from lesion cases. The account is the record's reading of where two
independent lines agree.

**What they agree on.**

- **What is felt is the body's regulatory state.** Barrett holds that
  interoception "is at the core of the brain's internal model", and that
  "interoceptive sensations are usually experienced as lower dimensional
  feelings of affect" ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), claim C1 in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)). Damasio and Carvalho
  open with "Feelings are mental experiences of body states" ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md),
  claim C1 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)), and say that a feeling is what an interoceptive
  map is like when it is experienced.
- **The valence comes from the body's state.** In Damasio and Carvalho,
  body states "are necessarily valenced", and feelings portray "the
  advantageous or disadvantageous nature of a physiologic situation".
  Exteroceptive senses borrow their valence from the body. In Barrett,
  every concept "represent[s] changes in affect" because every prediction
  serves allostasis.
- **Affect is not specific to emotion.** Barrett calls valence and arousal
  "basic features of consciousness … that, importantly, are not unique to
  instances of emotion". Damasio and Carvalho's list of feelings runs from
  hunger, thirst and colon distension to compassion. Both extend affect
  beyond humans. Barrett says a fly "may feel affect", and Damasio and
  Carvalho argue from the conserved brainstem that other species very
  likely feel (C6 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)).
- **Affect is not the emotion.** Both papers separate the felt state from
  what an emotion is. For Damasio and Carvalho, emotions are action
  programmes that cause feelings and can run without them (C2). For
  Barrett, an emotion is a categorization of affect and of exteroceptive
  sensation by an emotion concept. This account is about the felt layer
  only, and so it takes no side in the rivalry over what an emotion is
  ([THEORY-057](THEORY-057.md) against [THEORY-055](THEORY-055.md)). Frijda's action readiness
  ([LIT-540](../literature.d/LIT-540.md)) is compatible with it as well.

**What they disagree on.**

- **Prediction or map.** For Barrett the felt state is a prediction. It is
  allostatic, it is issued before the sensory input arrives, and it is
  corrected by prediction error (C1–C3 in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)). Damasio and Carvalho
  never use prediction except to say that feelings help anticipate
  "future adverse or favourable conditions". Their feeling is a
  topographic map of a body state that has already changed. The somatic
  marker paper ([LIT-384](../literature.d/LIT-384.md), [NOTE-332](../notes.d/NOTE-332.md)) sits between the two. Its "as if body
  loop" has somatosensory cortex take on a body-state pattern directly,
  with the body bypassed, and Damasio believes adults now use it more
  than the body loop. A state generated inside the brain and felt as the
  body is close to what Barrett calls a prediction. The paper asserts
  this, and does not show it (C6 in [NOTE-332](../notes.d/NOTE-332.md)).
- **Where it is generated.** Barrett's visceromotor predictions start in
  agranular limbic cortex, in the anterior cingulate and ventral anterior
  insula (C3 in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)). Damasio and Carvalho put the generation of
  feeling in the upper brainstem, and hold that the insula is not
  necessary (C4–C5 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)). They rest this on patient B, whose insulae
  were destroyed by herpes simplex encephalitis and who still feels. Barrett
  cites a patient with the same aetiology ("Roger", Feinstein et al. 2010)
  who still has emotions, and explains the case by degeneracy. Neither
  paper says whether the two are the same person. Either way, each account
  absorbs the case, so it does not decide between them.

## The consciousness holdings

- **Seth ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md), [NOTE-169](../notes.d/NOTE-169.md)).** Seth grounds consciousness in
  interoceptive predictive processing that serves allostasis (C4), the
  "beast machine". Barrett cites him for the base layer. His claim is
  about consciousness, and the reading holds no statement from him about
  affect as such. So he is a neighbour, not a source. He shares
  Barrett's predictive version, and his claim is the larger one: that
  being alive is the ground of experience in general.
- **Affect as the ground of consciousness.** Damasio and Carvalho call the
  "experiential aspect of homeostatic neural mappings … the lowest level of
  the mind and consciousness" and end on "the advent of feelings was
  simultaneously the advent of the mind" (C10, an assertion). Damasio's
  "Feelings Are the Source of Consciousness" ([LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md)) is filed unread.
  This account does not claim that consciousness is grounded in feeling.
  It claims only that, where affect is felt, what is felt is the body's
  regulatory state.
- **[THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md) (AI consciousness cannot currently be settled).** This
  account does not move that deadlock, but it locates it for affect. On
  both readings a system with no body to regulate has no affect in this
  sense, which is the condition Roberts sets for affective states
  ([LIT-207](../literature.d/LIT-207.md), [NOTE-157](../notes.d/NOTE-157.md)). The open question is a system that predicts and
  regulates a body, simulated or built. Barrett's version is stated as
  computation over predictions, and a computational functionalist would
  credit such a system with affect. Damasio and Carvalho tie feeling to
  living tissue, down to a speculation about unmyelinated interoceptive
  fibres (C7 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)), and Seth requires life. That is Birch's Janus
  problem in miniature: the same architecture counts as evidence for one
  side and against the other ([LIT-111](../literature.d/LIT-111.md)). Man and Damasio's design proposal for
  homeostatic "feeling machines" ([LIT-386](../literature.d/LIT-386.md)) is filed unread.
- **Friston ([LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md), [NOTE-421](../notes.d/NOTE-421.md)).** Barrett's "active inference" is the
  neural predictive-coding scheme, not [LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md)'s Markov-blanket theorem.
  Her need for an internal model rests on the good-regulator theorem
  instead (footnote 5). Nothing here depends on the claim that every
  blanketed ergodic system "appears" to infer. Seth's route to life
  passes through a variational–thermodynamic equivalence that [LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md) does
  not supply. [LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md) is not evidence for this account.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say that affect is an emotion, or that emotions are body
  states.** The body-state view of emotion is Damasio's ([LIT-384](../literature.d/LIT-384.md)), and
  Barrett rejects it for emotions while keeping it for affect.
- **It does not say that prediction is how affect is generated.** That is
  Barrett's version. The record holds a read account without it, so the
  prediction claim is the open half.
- **The record holds a dissent.** Minsky ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md), [NOTE-348](../notes.d/NOTE-348.md)) holds that
  "feelings" are attempts by parts of the mind "to describe large-scale
  aspects of mental conditions". Their objects are brain cascades, the
  body is "one more set of resources", and they need reflective
  self-models. If he is right, what is felt is the brain's state and not
  the body's, and this account is false. He argues by example and offers
  no data, which is why it stays Proposed and does not count as refuted.
- **"Is the experience of" is not an identity claim about phenomenal
  character.** The account says what felt affect is about and what varies
  with it. It does not say why it is felt at all.
