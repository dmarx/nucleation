# LEAD-204: From the record's exchange history, trace ideas restated across sessions before they were coded, to tell which kind of drift they show (saturating at the collaborators' framings, linear, or undirected), whether ideas further from those framings are lost sooner, and whether drift accrues per restatement or per unit of time

## Material

The exchanges the nucleating-argument line already draws on ([CLAIM-177](../claims.d/CLAIM-177.md)'s
instances, [CASE-183](../cases.d/CASE-183.md)'s counts), and the curation entries that quote the
owner's turns. For each idea that was restated across sessions before it
got a code: its first statement, each restatement in order, the session
times, and where it was re-derived as though new or redefined without
notice.

## Three questions

1. **Shape of directed drift ([CLAIM-191](../claims.d/CLAIM-191.md)).** Plot displacement from the first
   statement against the number of restatements. Levelling off at a
   framing is contraction ([CLAIM-773](../claims.d/CLAIM-773.md)'s saturating kind); continued
   growth is bias; no consistent direction is diffusion.
2. **Distance and loss ([CLAIM-776](../claims.d/CLAIM-776.md)).** Raters blind to the hypothesis
   judge each idea's distance from the collaborators' default framings at
   its first statement. Compare restatements before loss across that
   distance.
3. **Steps or time ([CLAIM-782](../claims.d/CLAIM-782.md)).** Compare ideas restated equally often
   over different elapsed times, and ideas of equal age restated different
   numbers of times.

## Open before it can be specified

The displacement measure, and the raters. Both inherit [ARG-028](../arguments.d/ARG-028.md)'s question,
whether a measure built in the record's grammar can certify drift, and
[LEAD-201](LEAD-201.md)'s, who judges against a reference the record did not supply.
