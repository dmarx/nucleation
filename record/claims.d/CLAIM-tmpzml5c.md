# CLAIM-tmpzml5c: Search is a paperclip problem on both sides of the ranking

## The claim

The owner's U4 ([CLAIM-tmpe432a](CLAIM-tmpe432a.md)) moves the paperclip problem off AI. It is
"which optimization objectives are permitted to influence significant levers
of behavioural influence". Search has two objectives with such levers, and
the three stages of [CLAIM-tmpv3frk](CLAIM-tmpv3frk.md) run on both.

| Stage | Publishers, optimizing for rank | The engine, optimizing its ranking |
|---|---|---|
| Objective displacement | rank displaces the quality it was meant to reward | engagement and ad revenue displace relevance to the user |
| Jurisdictional expansion | the ranking governs what is written, not only what is found ([CLAIM-tmpcrves](CLAIM-tmpcrves.md)) | the ranking governs which businesses are seen at all, and the engine's own properties enter its results |
| Constitutional capture | — | dominance removes the users' power to compare and leave, the mechanism that would discipline the ranking |

[CLAIM-tmpevykb](CLAIM-tmpevykb.md) says it is jurisdiction, not the objective, that sets what an
optimizer can harm. A ranking criterion that would be harmless as one
engine among many becomes pathological when it decides what most of the world
sees. The remedy in United States v. Google ([THEORY-480](../theory.d/THEORY-480.md)) is aimed at the
third stage. Sharing search data with rivals restores a way to compare and
leave, without changing the criterion.

## What it does not say

That the engine intends this. On [CLAIM-1287](CLAIM-1287.md), a narrow optimizer comes to
value weakening its constraints with no change of character.
