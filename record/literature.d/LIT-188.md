# LIT-188: Scaffolding individuality: coordination, cooperation, collaboration and community

James Griesemer, Ayelet Shavit (2023), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 378, 20210398 (theme issue "Human socio-cultural evolution in light of evolutionary transitions")* — DOI-10.1098/rstb.2021.0398

## Key takeaways

- Evolutionary transitions in individuality can be described without circularity as an idealized developmental sequence of interaction types: coordination in "collections", cooperation in "conexuses", collaboration in "communities". On this view individuality is a special case of collectivity, and new levels of individuality and new inheritance systems evolve in tandem.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Evolutionary transitions, in which entities join a new reproducing collective and lose the capacity to reproduce independently, are hard to describe without circularity, because the notions used (units of selection, individuality, multilevel inheritance) are themselves products of the transition. The authors describe transitions through the kinds of interaction among community members that such transitions require. Reproducing systems hybridize with environmental components through eco-devo scaffolding and form communities of diverse members with heterogeneous interactions, and these communities can become individuals with their own inheritance systems. They argue for treating individuality as a special case of collectivity. They propose an idealized sequence (coordination, then cooperation, then collaboration, the "3Cs") that scaffolds new collective individuals, and use hominid evolution and learning to stress the role of development in both new levels of individuality and new inheritance systems.

[NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md) is the close reading of 2026-09-26, and it placed the work: **Proposed** — a short programmatic sketch, and the authors call it one. It is worth knowing as the compact statement of Griesemer's scaffolding, reproducer and generative-entrenchment view applied to human social transitions. Its headline claims (non-circularity; individuality as a special case of collectivity) appear only in the abstract. Two things would settle it: the model of community "reliability" and endogenization the authors promise in §2 ("We hope to explore such models in future work"), or an application of the 3Cs to a worked case, such as the companion Neolithic paper, that separates collection, conexus and community by measurable criteria.

**Priority for a deeper reading: low — t=25 s. By the authors' own account it is a rough conceptual sketch, and the skim captures its structure.**

What a deeper reading should check:

- A process-first account of biological individuality and of the part–whole relation among reproducers. Relevant to nucleation notes on identity and individuality, alongside [LIT-167](LIT-167.md) (Baedke) and the niche papers [LIT-169](LIT-169.md) and [LIT-127](LIT-127.md).
- The claim that it is non-circular deserves scrutiny: do "coordination / cooperation / collaboration" avoid presupposing the units whose emergence they are meant to explain?
- The move from biological transitions to human cultural and academic communities (the example of discipline formation in §1) is analogical. A deeper reading should check how much it carries.
