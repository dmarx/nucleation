# CLAIM-tmps8ou2: A norm's original justification and its baseline support come apart: the justification can vanish while the norm keeps support from replacement rationales and from the interests it serves, so a norm that outlasts its justification need not have outlasted its support, and a bistable norm ends by a push past its threshold rather than by fading

## What the earlier note said, and what was wrong with it

CLAIM-tmpjg5gx's gap note said that in A22's model "the loop lets a norm
outlast a weakening of its baseline support by a margin set by λ, not its
disappearance". The assistant's summary of it to the owner put it as: "a
norm outlasts a weakened reason for it but not a vanished one".

That ran two things together:

- **The baseline b.** In x' = σ(b + λg) − x, b is the tendency to comply
  without maintenance feedback, everything that supports or opposes
  compliance apart from the reliance loop.
- **The norm's justification.** That is one contributor to b among
  others: replacement rationales, the interests the norm serves,
  sanctions. When the justification goes, b falls by its share, not to
  the collapse point.

## The model, checked

The recomputation is in CLAIM-tmpjg5gx's dated note of 2026-11-24, the
correction of its gap note. In short: at A22's settings an established
norm keeps compliance above 93% while its unaided baseline compliance
falls from 27% to about 1.3%. So the model supports A22's sentence that
"norms can persist even after their original justification disappears",
in the sense in which a justification is one share of the baseline. What
it does not allow is persistence at any baseline whatever.

## The owner's case, read through the model

CASE-tmp3euq0 fits, and shows two further features the model predicts:

- **Justification and baseline came apart.** The chattel-slavery theories
  went with abolition. Other supports took their place: new rationales and
  the interests the norm served.
- **A discontinuous end.** In a bistable system, a norm in the high state
  is not ended by small reductions in support. It ends when support is
  pushed past the threshold, or when the system is pushed into the other
  basin. Overturned precedent and statute are interventions of that size.
- **Hysteresis after the push.** Resistance and de facto segregation after
  1954–1965 are what a system shows when its baseline is moved but its
  state lags.

The case does not show that the reliance loop is what maintained the norm.
Sanctions and violence are rival maintainers, and ARG-tmpqkfwb says what the
case supports.
