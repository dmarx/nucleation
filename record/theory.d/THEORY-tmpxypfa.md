# THEORY-tmpxypfa: Bich, Mossio, Ruiz-Mirazo and Moreno (2016) define biological regulation as second-order control by a subsystem the organism produces but which runs at its own rate and stoichiometry, is triggered by perturbations and switches the constitutive regime between viable states; feedback, network robustness and homeostasis belong instead to the constitutive regime

## The account

Bich, Mossio, Ruiz-Mirazo and Moreno (2016) define biological regulation as
second-order control by a subsystem the organism produces but which runs at
its own rate and stoichiometry, is triggered by perturbations and switches
the constitutive regime between viable states. Feedback, network robustness
and homeostasis belong instead to the constitutive regime, the closure.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpefons](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpefons.md); its key results are quoted there with
locations. Its cases are prokaryotic (chemotaxis, the lac operon), and
"dynamically decoupled" has no measure of how distinct the scales must be.

## Bearing

On [THEORY-072](THEORY-072.md): differentiation at distinct time scales, on this account, is
a condition organisms meet beyond closure, not part of it.
