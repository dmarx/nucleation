# THEORY-1573: Klein and colleagues report that in seventeen labs and 1,550 participants writing about one's own death did not raise preference for a pro-US over an anti-US essay author (g = 0.07, 95% CI −0.03 to 0.17), whether or not the protocol was designed with the original authors

## The account

The effect did not appear even where everything the theory's authors
asked for was done: a harsher essay, a delay, a relaxed and anonymous
setting, and, under the strictest rule, only white, US-born participants
strongly identified as American. The authors conclude: "With these
protocols, in the context of these labs and time in history, we find
little support for this key finding of TMT" (Discussion).

## Evidence

- Set 1: g = 0.07, 95% CI [−0.03, 0.17], N = 1,550; Sets 2 and 3: g =
  0.09, both non-significant; Q(16) non-significant throughout.
- Protocol as moderator: b = 0.01 to 0.07, none significant;
  author-advised labs alone g = 0.08 to 0.19, none significant.
- The harsher essay shifted preferences as intended (68% against 48%
  preferring the pro-US author), so the failure is not put down to the
  worldview not being held.
