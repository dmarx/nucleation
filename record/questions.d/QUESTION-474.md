# QUESTION-474: When automation changes a consequential action from proceeding only on affirmative approval to proceeding unless someone vetoes it, with the reviewer, information, authority and capacity held fixed, what changes in the capacity to prevent harm, in who bears the burden of preventing it, and in whether nonintervention may be read as authorization?

## Why it is open

A23 §8 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c) sets an
approval rule beside a veto rule. Under the first, the institution must
obtain authorization before acting, and its throughput is bounded by the
reviewer's unless approval turns perfunctory. Under the second, it acts
automatically and the reviewer must notice and stop what should not happen,
in time: "A professional's vigilance becomes a resource that the
institution relies upon." The formal right to object can be the same in
both; the burden of the decisive act has changed sides.

The record holds the parts and not the question:

- [CLAIM-549](../claims.d/CLAIM-549.md) and [CLAIM-553](../claims.d/CLAIM-553.md): quiet is not satisfaction, and acceptance,
  activation and intervention thresholds differ, so silence may be any of
  several things.
- [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md): legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability.
- [CLAIM-705](../claims.d/CLAIM-705.md) and [CLAIM-704](../claims.d/CLAIM-704.md): what happens when cases arrive faster
  than review.
- [CLAIM-706](../claims.d/CLAIM-706.md) and [TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md): an approval step and a veto window are
  both gates, with different impedance; a rubber-stamp approval (the HKMA
  paper, [THEORY-1931](../theory.d/THEORY-1931.md)) is a gate without an opportunity.
- [CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md): the reviewer's vigilance as a commitment the institution
  relies on.

## What would count as an answer

A matched construction holding the reviewer, information, authority and
capacity fixed and changing only the default, showing what changes in
preventive capacity (which harms are stopped in time), in operational burden
(who must act, and how often), and in the defensibility of treating
nonintervention as endorsement; and a criterion for when a veto default is
legitimate (for instance, low stakes, reversible effects and a real
opportunity to object, as A23 §8 suggests).

The continuation of the same exchange filed A33 §6 and §10 B as a proposed
study, [CASE-290](../cases.d/CASE-290.md): five measures that tell, when complaints fall,
whether members accept the conditions or have stopped being able to
object. It is distinct from this question, which varies the default and
not the channel, and bears on its last part, whether nonintervention may
be read as authorization.
