# THEORY-411: In Sen's capability approach the weighting of functionings is left to public reasoning, and evaluation can still guide action without agreement on weights: where every weighting in an agreed range, or every view by its own argument, ranks one option above another, that ranking holds, so the approach is deliberately incomplete and yields a partial ordering

## Source

- Wells, IEP "Sen's Capability Approach" (2012), [LIT-284](../literature.d/LIT-284.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-257](../notes.d/NOTE-257.md). §3b is the source of the mechanism; §1b(4) and §5b of its
  rationale.
- Sen (1979), "Equality of What?", [LIT-289](../literature.d/LIT-289.md), read first-hand in [NOTE-258](../notes.d/NOTE-258.md), for
  the partial ordering in Sen's own words (p. 219).

## What was actually shown

**Two stages ([LIT-284](../literature.d/LIT-284.md), §§3b–3c).** Valuation decides which functionings
matter and how much. It is "a social choice exercise requiring both public
reasoning and democratic procedures of decision-making", because a
philosophically fixed list "denies the relevance of the values people may
come to have and the role of democracy" and "the legitimate source of
decisions … must be the people concerned" (Sen 2004b, as the IEP reports
it). Evaluation then asks what capability people actually have, taking the
values as given.

**Partial agreement (§3b).** Reasonable disagreement makes a complete
ranking of functionings unlikely. Action-guiding agreement is still
possible by *intersection*, where different views reach the same judgement
by different arguments, and by *ranges of weights*. The IEP's example: with
weights on literacy relative to health of ½, ⅓, ¼ and ⅕ across four views,
all four rank one unit of literacy plus two of health above two of literacy
plus one of health. A set of basic capabilities (education, health,
nutrition and shelter at minimal levels) can be agreed relatively easily,
for assessing poverty.

**Incompleteness by design (§§1b, 5b).** On the IEP's reading, the gaps
follow from Sen's concern that evaluation should not "take a short-cut by
excluding all sorts of information from consideration in advance". To the
charge of under-theorisation, Sen replies that the approach "is not a
theory of justice but rather an approach to the evaluation of effective
freedom".

**In Sen's own text ([LIT-289](../literature.d/LIT-289.md), p. 219).** The 1979 lecture already concedes
an indexing problem for capability bundles, and answers it with "a partial
ordering" from "broad uniformity of personal preferences", which "must be
supplemented by certain established conventions of relative importance".
Weights are "culture-dependent".

What could have come out otherwise: the admissible weights could have
disagreed on every comparison that matters. The example is constructed so
that they do not. Whether real disagreements leave enough agreed
comparisons is not examined.

## What this does not say

- **Which Sen text states the mechanism.** The IEP attributes intersection,
  ranges of weights and basic capabilities to Sen without a citation. Their
  source is unverified ([NOTE-257](../notes.d/NOTE-257.md), open questions). The 1979 partial ordering
  is the nearest first-hand statement, and it says "conventions", not public
  reasoning.
- **That Sen has a selection procedure.** The SEP entry ([LIT-283](../literature.d/LIT-283.md)) says Sen
  "has never explained in detail how such a selection could and should be
  done", and finds three incompatible readings of "reason to value". The IEP
  gives Sen more of a method than the SEP allows.
- **That partial orderings settle hard cases.** A range agreed on literacy
  and health is not a range agreed on liberty and security. How much a
  partial ordering decides depends on how wide the disagreement is.
- **That the approach is a theory of justice.** On Sen's reply it is an
  evaluative framework. It names no list and no distributive rule.
- **That Sen is a "comprehensive consequentialist".** The IEP asserts it
  without citation, and the SEP reports it as contested.

## Connections

- [THEORY-025](THEORY-025.md) is the negative half: utility and resource metrics cannot
  register claims that arise from differences in conversion. This is the
  structure of what Sen puts in their place.
- Nussbaum ([LIT-288](../literature.d/LIT-288.md)) wants a list of central capabilities with thresholds.
  On this account a list is what Sen declines, for democratic reasons.
  The IEP's §7 sets out how the two differ.
- Valentini's defence of categorical principles ([THEORY-434](THEORY-434.md)) concerns
  Sen's method in *The Idea of Justice*; the partial-ordering idea is what
  that book's comparative approach would rest on.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

The intersection and ranges-of-weights devices the title gives Sen are
attributed to him only by the IEP entry ([LIT-284](../literature.d/LIT-284.md)), without a citation; the
Sen text read ([LIT-289](../literature.d/LIT-289.md)) says "conventions of relative importance", not
public reasoning. It waits on a first-hand reading of a Sen text that
states the devices, and on the applied case its promote_when names; a
narrowing to the IEP's reading would free neither [CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md) nor [CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md),
whose other readings ([THEORY-025](THEORY-025.md), [THEORY-054](THEORY-054.md)) are Proposed.
