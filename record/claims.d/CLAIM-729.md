# CLAIM-729: The duties to comply with a justified rule, to support a justified institution and to help establish a needed one are distinct and none follows from another, and the third is bounded by whether the needed arrangement can be reached from the present one by permissible, resource-bounded transitions and by whose part it is to contribute

## The claim

A102 §5 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b). A reports
that Scanlon, in *Contractualism and Intuitionism*, "recognizes an important
difference between violating an established, justified convention and
failing to help establish a needed convention where none exists", and that
"The latter may itself be morally problematic." A then separates three
requirements: a duty to comply with a justified rule, a duty to support a
justified institution, and a duty to help establish a needed institution.

> These duties do not follow from one another automatically.

A's three illustrations, one for each gap: "A person might have a duty to
comply with an existing conservation rule without being obligated to fund
every possible institutional reform." "A community might have strong
reasons to establish a monitoring system without any particular member
being obligated to supply all the resources." "And an institution may be
obligated to maintain a protective capability because it has undertaken
responsibilities that depend on that capability."

A states the bound on the third duty in the record's reachability terms. With 𝒢 the space of
arrangements, G₀ the present one and G* one that would secure the claims,
the reform problem needs both G* ∈ 𝒢 and G* ∈ ℛ_N(G₀; b, H), arrangements
"reachable through permissible, resource-bounded transformations". "The
first establishes that a suitable arrangement is possible in the model. The
second asks whether the community can actually construct it from its
present condition through justified means." And even then "we must
determine whose responsibilities include contributing to the
transformation."

The example is a community drawing on a shared aquifer with no governing
institution: a principle of sustainable use may be justified while who may
establish restrictions, who must take part, who bears the costs and what is
owed meanwhile are all open.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-560](CLAIM-560.md)** (Active): compatibility and reachability vary
  independently with the budget. The bound on the third duty rests on it:
  an arrangement that would make the demands compatible need not be
  reachable, so a duty to establish it cannot be read off its
  compatibility.
- **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)** (Proposed) holds a duty to maintain feasible means of
  answerability. That is A's third illustration, a duty to maintain a
  capability, and it is grounded in an undertaken responsibility, not in
  the rule. The derivation A offers for it is [ARG-114](../arguments.d/ARG-114.md).
- **[CASE-282](../cases.d/CASE-282.md)**, filed from the part before, is the aquifer with
  numbers: two households, a sustainable limit, and a recycling technology
  that makes all three demands compatible and that neither household can
  finance. It illustrates the gap this claim's bound names.
- **[QUESTION-481](../questions.d/QUESTION-481.md)** asks who has standing to change an arrangement's
  governing structure. The third duty is one source of an answer, and the
  claim leaves the question open.

## What it does not say

- Not that the third duty exists in any given case. A: the latter "may
  itself be morally problematic"; the claim is that it is distinct and
  bounded, not that it holds.
- Not how the contributions are divided. A leaves "whose responsibilities
  include contributing" open, and so does the claim.
- Not what Scanlon says beyond A's report. The paper is not read on the
  record; A's report is taken as A's.
