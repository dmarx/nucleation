# ARG-tmpbyj2f: Grice's factivity test, applied to the steam alarm, places the false alarm's error in a non-factive content

## The inference

1. [THEORY-444](../theory.d/THEORY-444.md) ([NOTE-735](../notes.d/NOTE-735.md), pp. 377–378): natural meaning is factive, "x
   means that p entails p", and no one means anything by it.
2. [CASE-tmpv6i22](../cases.d/CASE-tmpv6i22.md): steam sets the detector off, and the alarm sounds with
   no fire.
3. "The alarm means the detector crossed its threshold" entails its
   content, which is true. That is natural meaning, and it is not in
   error.
4. "The alarm means fire" does not entail a fire. It fails the test, so
   it is not natural meaning.
5. The alarm is a false alarm only under 4. So its error lies in a
   non-factive content: question (a) of [CLAIM-tmpqboxc](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpqboxc.md).

## Critical questions

- **Does the rule apply to this case?** Steps 3 to 5 apply Grice's tests
  as he states them. They are tests for any "x means", and nothing in
  them is restricted to utterances.
- **Is the rule correctly understood?** The factivity test is
  uncontroversial and is all this ARG uses.
- **Does the conclusion go beyond the rule?** Not now. Version 1 went on
  to the source of the norm. The review showed that this step does not
  follow ([ARG-tmp27nss](ARG-tmp27nss.md)), so the conclusion stops at where the error lies.

## What it does not show

Whose norm the non-factive content answers to. For the designed alarm
that is the designer's by the case's stipulation. Elsewhere it may come
from selection history or the system's own maintenance ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md);
[CLAIM-tmph9egz](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmph9egz.md)).
