# LIT-572: Narrative Identity

Dan P. McAdams and Kate C. McLean (2013), "Narrative Identity", *Current
Directions in Psychological Science* 22(3):233–238 — DOI-10.1177/0963721413475622

**On the citation.** The brief's details are right: authors, title, journal,
volume 22, issue 3, 2013.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the abstract on Crossref, not a reading.*

- The abstract's definition: narrative identity is "a person's internalized
  and evolving life story, integrating the reconstructed past and imagined
  future to provide life with some degree of unity and purpose".
- The abstract reports that research on adaptation shows "narrators who find
  redemptive meanings in suffering and adversity, and who construct life
  stories that feature themes of personal agency and exploration, tend to
  enjoy higher levels of mental health, well-being, and maturity".
- On development, it says that research has tracked narrative identity "from
  its origins in conversations between parents and their young children to
  the articulation of sophisticated meaning-making strategies" in
  adolescence and emerging adulthood.
- It names two tasks for the field: to "disentangle causal relations between
  features of life stories and positive psychological adaptation", and to
  explore "the role of broad cultural contexts".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the canonical short review of
narrative identity in personality psychology. It is the source most often
cited for the definition. `Deferred` because it was not read. McAdams's 2019
essay ([LIT-595](LIT-595.md)) is the read substitute. It extends this article: it
quotes the definition and cites the article for the claim that narrative
identity gives a life "unity and purpose". It covers the same ground,
adaptation and development, and adds evolution and culture.

The abstract's first open task, the causal direction between story and
well-being, is the gap the record would need closed before it could promote
[THEORY-060](../theory.d/THEORY-060.md), which holds that life stories shape well-being. See the bearing section of
[NOTE-470](../notes.d/NOTE-470.md).

No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.
