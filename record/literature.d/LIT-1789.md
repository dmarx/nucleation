summary: >-
  Eyerman (2001), chapter 1 read. Cultural trauma is not a wound to individuals but "a dramatic loss of identity and meaning, a tear in the social fabric" of a group, which need not be felt or experienced by all its members. Slavery became the trauma of African Americans "in retrospect": after the failure of Reconstruction, a generation that had not been enslaved made its memory the "primal scene" of a new collective identity, against a white memory of slavery as benign. Membership was set by a shared fate, not shared experience or descent. Carrier groups, chiefly intellectuals and social movements, articulate the trauma; each social generation reworks the memory; it moved from group memory to national memory only with the civil rights movement.
