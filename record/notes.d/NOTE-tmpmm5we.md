# NOTE-tmpmm5we: Mussmann et al. — translocated greater prairie chickens and the perpetuation of conservation reliance

## Contribution

A twenty-year follow-up of a population that was supplied with
individuals, read genetically, which finds that the supply did not leave
a self-sustaining population behind, and an argument, drawn as a
conceptual model (figs 4–5), that reliance on management has two sources,
the extrinsic threat and the population's intrinsic limits, both of which
must be relieved for reliance to end.

## Key insight

Supplying what a population lacks (birds, alleles) while the cause of the
lack (no connected prairie) remains leaves a population that needs more
supply: translocations "may avoid the costs of restoring connectivity but
also invoke long-term intensive management (i.e. the perpetuation of
conservation reliance)" (§4.4).

## Assumptions

- **Baseline.** 32 pre-translocation incidental mortalities gathered over
  19 years serve as the genetic baseline; the authors call it "equivocal"
  (§4.4).
- **Counts from feathers.** Shed-feather genotypes, 2010–2013, give
  mark–recapture estimates of census and effective size.
- **Permanent habitat loss.** Prairie lost to agriculture is treated as
  not coming back on management timescales.

## Key results

1. **Definition and number, second-hand (§1.1).** "84% are now recognized
   as ‘conservation-reliant’ [3], meaning that direct and ongoing
   management will still be required even if recovery is achieved", of
   species listed under the US Endangered Species Act; [3] is Goble et al.
   (2012).
2. **Decline (§1.4).** Illinois birds fell "from millions
   (mid-nineteenth century), to 2000 (1962), then 46 (1998)"; after the
   translocations "only 73 recorded in 2011", with "additional
   translocations initiated but subsequently suspended".
3. **Small and inbred (abstract).** "Low migration rates (less than 1%)";
   "4 year average N = 79"; "average Ne = 13.1; 4 year Ne/N = 0.166";
   "12 significantly different family groups, with relatedness r = 0.31 >
   half-sib r = 0.25".
4. **No genetic rescue signal (abstract).** Average heterozygosity "did
   not differ among contemporary, pre- and post-translocated populations,
   whereas allelic diversity did".
5. **Supply perpetuates reliance (§4.4).** "Translocations may avoid the
   costs of restoring connectivity but also invoke long-term intensive
   management (i.e. the perpetuation of conservation reliance)".
6. **Not durable (§4.4).** "success either ambiguous or transitory,
   neither of which is sustainable".
7. **Two kinds of management (§4.1).** "‘threat management’ (i.e.
   extrinsic to the species) and ‘population management’ (i.e. intrinsic
   to it) [3]", the split again cited to Goble et al. (2012).
8. **Threshold, both sides (§4.4, fig. 5).** "Two factors establish the
   baseline for conservation reliance, and both must be alleviated in
   tandem".
9. **Intrinsic fixes alone (§5).** Small-population approaches alone
   "have a low probability of success and, as a consequence, promote
   rather than alleviate conservation reliance".
10. **Translocated birds do worse (§4.4), cited not measured.** "survival
    for translocated individuals is much lower, even when acclimation is
    properly done [66]".
11. **Can reliance end (§4.5).** "Self-sustaining populations of GRPC in
    Illinois may be an elusive endpoint".
12. **State-listed species (§5).** "just as conservation-reliant as are
    those federally listed [76]"; and, citing Scott et al. (2010) [77],
    "the opportunity for potential management of state-listed species
    that can preclude the federal listing process".

## Concepts

- **Conservation reliance**: as here, that "direct and ongoing management
  will still be required even if recovery is achieved" (after Goble et
  al. 2012).
- **Threat (extrinsic) and population (intrinsic) management.**
- **Reliance threshold**: the level, in the authors' conceptual figure,
  below which a population would no longer need management.
- **Demographic and genetic rescue**; the declining- and
  small-population paradigms; the extinction vortex.

## Limitations

- One species in two counties, four years of genotypes; a baseline of 32
  birds over 19 years.
- The definition, the 84 % and the threat/population split are
  second-hand, cited to Goble et al. (2012). Figs 4–5 are conceptual
  diagrams, not fitted models.
- The lower survival of translocated birds is cited, not measured here.
- Part of the argument about reliance is policy (listing, umbrella
  species) and is not tested.
