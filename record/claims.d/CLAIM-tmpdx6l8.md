# CLAIM-tmpdx6l8: Constraint and contraction narrow an entry through different terms of the drift bound: contraction is the later steps' sensitivity, pulling each restatement toward the transmitters' own fixed point and forgetting the start, while constraint bounds what each step can inject and supplies the reference transport departure is measured from; the record relies on contraction for form, by making its schema the convention restatements converge on, and on constraint for content

## The claim

The owner, at U2, after the account of an entry's narrowing
(CLAIM-tmpvm90n): "relate that account of stabilization to the record's
account of contraction".

Both mechanisms narrow what can vary. They differ in what they narrow
toward: contraction forgets the source, and constraint anchors to it.

**Different terms of the bound.** CLAIM-056: with local discrepancy ε_i and
later sensitivity L_j, departure from a reference transport after n steps is
at most (∏ L_j)·e₀ + Σ ε_i ∏_(j>i) L_j, contractive, neutral or expansive as
the L are below, at or above one. Contraction is a fact about L: how strongly
each restatement pulls toward the restater's own fixed point. Constraints on
an entry act on the other terms. A coded file that is read and not
reconstructed, a closed vocabulary and required relations limit how far one
session can move the entry (ε), and the code gives the chain a reference to
be measured from at all. Constraint does not make the transmitter less
contractive; it shrinks what each step can inject.

**Contraction is wanted for form.** TERM-027 reads a chain's attractor as "a
shared decoding convention rather than a preserved historical object", and
CLAIM-068 proposes that chains are attracted toward conventional regions.
The record's schema is a decoding convention installed on purpose: field
names, the closed vocabularies, role, warrant and status, the prose
conventions the curation norms keep re-imposing. CLAIM-181 holds that the
record fixes the grammar an argument is expressed in. This claim adds the
mechanism by which that grammar is kept: every session's output contracts
onto it, and lint makes the contraction fast. What the record does not want
to contract is the content, and that is what codes and file text hold.

THEORY-489 is both cases at once. Chains of learners forget their starting
function and converge on their shared bias. For content that is the failure
CLAIM-191 describes, restatements drifting toward the collaborators' framings.
For form, where the learners have been given the convention, it is the
intended result.

So the owner's "increasingly narrow inductive prior towards desired outcomes"
(U1) is two narrowings: form converging on a designed attractor, and content
pinned against the transmitters' default one.

## What it does not say

- Not that contraction of form is harmless. CLAIM-181's own worry, a move
  that fits no scheme forced into one or lost, is what contraction onto a
  convention does to content that the convention cannot express.
- Not that the bound can be estimated for the record's sessions. CLAIM-208
  objects that its constants cannot be estimated from one restatement per
  step; this claim uses the bound to name the terms, not to predict.
- Not that the record cannot amplify. CLAIM-059 says amplification needs an
  enlarged state; memoryless sessions alone do not have one, and the record
  supplies one. Whether the record also enables an expansive regime is left
  open (curation entry of this filing).
