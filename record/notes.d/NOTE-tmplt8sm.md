# NOTE-tmplt8sm: CLINIC v. Rubio — the 75-country immigrant-visa policy vacated

## Contribution

A judicial finding that an administrative directive made individualized
eligibility determinations unable to change the outcome for a class defined
by nationality, and that the directive was unlawful on three statutory
grounds. ECF No. 83 is an Opinion and Order on cross-motions for partial
summary judgment, granted in part and denied in part, with a Rule 54(b)
partial judgment and a declaratory judgment.

## Key results

- **The directive** (pp. 1–2): "Consular officers were ordered to refuse
  immigrant visas to nationals of the 75 designated countries, regardless
  of whether the consular officer had, following an individualized
  assessment, determined that the applicant was not likely to become a
  public charge and that the applicant was otherwise eligible for a visa."
  It "has the effect of categorically banning the issuance of immigrant
  visas to nationals of nearly 40% of the world's countries."
- **The Cable**, as the court quotes it (p. 10): "If an officer refuses an
  applicant under 4(a) and then the applicant provides additional evidence
  that demonstrates he or she overcomes the public charge refusal, the
  consular officer should write detailed case notes . . . and refuse the
  applicant 221(g)."
- **Holding** (p. 2): "The Court holds that the Policy is contrary to law
  and was issued in excess of Secretary Rubio's statutory authority."
- **Not the public-charge provision** (p. 32): "While the Court agrees that
  the Policy is contrary to law, as discussed infra, it is not because the
  Policy contravenes Section 1182(a)(4)."
- **Nationality** (p. 34): "the Policy requires consular officers to
  discriminatorily refuse visas solely on the basis of the applicant's
  nationality."
- **Outcome fixed in advance** (p. 42): "whatever determinations they make
  as to eligibility for the applicants subject to the Policy are ultimately
  irrelevant. The outcome is predetermined. The visa will be refused." The
  Policy "displaces consular officers from the role Congress assigned
  them". The Government's argument that officers still enter the refusals
  is "an exercise in Orwellian logic" (p. 40).
- **Notice-and-comment** (p. 47): "The Policy thus does not qualify as a
  legislative rule"; judgment for the Government on that count.
- **Remedy** (pp. 54–58): the defects are "comprehensive, fundamental, and
  severe"; the vacatur "does not extend to denial of immigrant visas on any
  ground other than the Policy"; on remand the court "does not direct any
  particular outcome or process so long as any refusal is not based upon
  the Policy."

## Assumptions

APA review on the administrative record; consular nonreviewability held not
to bar a challenge to the Policy as such; the INA's text read as
unambiguous. A district-court ruling; its appeal and enforcement history
was not checked.

## Concepts

INA § 221(g) refusal; the public-charge ground (§ 1182(a)(4)); the bar on
nationality discrimination (§ 1152(a)(1)(A)); consular authority
(§ 1104(a)); 22 C.F.R. § 40.6; APA § 706(2); legislative and non-legislative
rules; vacatur and remand.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-745](../claims.d/CLAIM-745.md).** A documented specimen of the claim. The Cable directs
  refusal even after the applicant's evidence "overcomes the public charge
  refusal", and the court finds eligibility determinations "ultimately
  irrelevant". The court found the individualized assessment not displaced
  (p. 32): the evidence was still assessed and could not change the
  decision, which is the claim's "unable to change a decision", not "not
  considered". The "Orwellian logic" passage supports the claim's point
  that compliance does not establish authority. The opinion says nothing of
  moral justification, which the claim keeps separate.
- **[CASE-289](../cases.d/CASE-289.md).** The opinion confirms the case's dates, judge,
  holdings and remedy, and the nationality-discrimination holding the case
  gives only as reported. It adds that the court rejected the public-charge
  argument and ruled for the Government on notice-and-comment, and it shows
  that the Cable ordered refusal, not only a pause.
- **[CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md).** The court's rejection of officer-entered refusals as
  preserved discretion bears on it.
- The exchange's A108 is faithful on the remedy and omits the nationality
  and § 1201(g) holdings and the rejected public-charge argument.
