# NOTE-tmpz7wab: Bridging emotion theory and neurobiology through dynamic systems modeling

## Contribution

A proposal to replace linear appraisal-then-emotion models with a
dynamic-systems (DS) account. It is stated first psychologically, then
neurally, and joined in a single model. On this account "coherent
appraisals are not antecedents of emotions, but emerging outcomes of
interactions among constituent systems underlying appraisal and emotion"
(p. 193).

## Key insight

The unit is the emotion–appraisal amalgam, the "emotional
interpretation", and it is selected, not computed. Many potential
configurations compete, and feedback lets one take over the system and
hold it.

## Assumptions

- That cognition is self-organization in a complex dynamic system:
  "cognitive systems reconfigure themselves by achieving states of
  higher order through the synchronization of their constituents"
  (p. 173).
- A componential view of emotion: "(1) arousal, (2) action tendencies,
  (3) attentional orientation, and (4) affective feeling" (p. 178), with
  appraisal components of perception, evaluation, attention, memory and
  planning.
- That psychological wholes can be mapped onto neural loops whose
  coupling and synchrony can in principle be measured.

## Key results

Argument and model, with neural evidence reviewed:

- **Eight DS principles** (§3.2): emergent order, positive feedback,
  negative feedback and entrainment, circular causality, increasing
  complexity, multistability, phase transitions, interacting timescales.
- **Three phases** (§3.3): trigger (perturbation, then nucleation),
  self-amplification ("Positive feedback thus “grows” appraisal–emotion
  processes that capture the psychological system", p. 176), and
  self-stabilization (negative feedback, constraint satisfaction, a
  superordinate appraisal that may "enslave" its constituents, p. 177).
- **Competition and selection**: "the consolidation of a particular
  “solution” from competing ensembles is rapid and nonlinear" (p. 177).
  Vertical integration gives an EI "psychological momentum or
  imperturbability" and "selects viable EIs" (p. 192).
- **Alternation**: "the most realistic portrait of a self-stabilizing EI
  includes several well-worn interpretations that fluctuate
  intermittently, as familiar images and emotions drive the system
  between available attractors" (p. 177). Mr. Smart may oscillate
  between "angry empowerment versus shameful submissiveness" (p. 192).
- **Thresholds set by state**: "Baseline states not only determine
  thresholds for what is meaningful" but constrain what emerges
  (p. 191).
- **Timescales**: stable EIs drive synaptic change, so learning is
  long-term order produced by enduring EIs (pp. 177, 192–193).
- **Measure**: phase synchrony, "a fixed temporal relation between the
  oscillations of independent regions" (p. 185). The prediction is that
  "the degree of phase synchrony in the theta range will correlate with
  measures of emotional relevance and subjective emotional feelings"
  (p. 186).

## Concepts

- **emotional interpretation (EI)** — an emotion–appraisal amalgam that
  emerges and stabilizes as a whole.
- **self-amplification / self-stabilization** — the positive-feedback
  growth phase and the negative-feedback coherence phase of an EI.
- **circular causality** — after Haken: a higher-order form constrains
  the coupling of its constituents, which in turn sustain it.
- **vertical integration** — coupling across levels of the neuraxis,
  which gives an EI momentum and restricts which EIs are viable.

## Connections

Opposes sequential appraisal models (Scherer, Lazarus, Smith and Kirby),
and agrees with Frijda and Zeelenberg that appraisal can be an outcome.
Draws on Haken's synergetics and on Kelso, Thelen and Smith, and Freeman.
Cites Carver and Scheier (1990, [LIT-tmp63om1](../literature.d/LIT-tmp63om1.md)), the account the record's sign clause
takes ([TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md)), among the few emotion theories with negative
feedback. Sander and Scherer's commentary (pp. 216–217) objects that the
account cannot say when an episode begins or ends, and offers Scherer's
criterion, "a certain degree of synchronization of emotion components
driven by specific appraisal outcomes" (p. 216). Lewis replies that
"Scherer's model and mine share an emphasis on synchronization in emotion
episodes" (p. 225). Scherer's model is read in [LIT-tmpfjacn](../literature.d/LIT-tmpfjacn.md).

## Bearing on the record

**Does Lewis's attractor account already contain "an ordering coming to
dominate"?** It contains the *dynamics* of ascendancy almost exactly, and
not the *ordering*.

- **The dynamics.** The record's term reads ascendancy through Frijda's
  three marks ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)). Lewis's account has all three. Priority: an
  EI must "take over" the psychological system (p. 176), and its
  constituents "lose their independence and become embedded in a larger
  regime" (p. 174). Persistence: vertical integration gives it "momentum
  or imperturbability" (p. 192). Resumption: an attractor is a state the
  system returns to after small perturbations, and the "well-worn
  interpretations that fluctuate intermittently" are returned to
  (p. 177). It also has what [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) needs and Frijda's marks do
  not supply. One wins "from competing ensembles" (p. 177), "only one of
  these converges at a time" (p. 175), and losing is real: Lewis's
  oscillating Mr. Smart is the record's split or alternating ascendancy,
  its reading of ambivalence. And it has no regulator above the
  competitors, which is [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)'s point.
- **What it lacks.** What prevails in Lewis is an *interpretation*, a
  configuration of appraisal and emotion constituents. It is not a
  ranking of options, and so it is not the kind of thing that can be a
  currency or fail to be one. Nothing in the account ranks the system's
  own states by their bearing on its maintenance. Valence enters only as
  something the orbitofrontal cortex "attends to" in anticipated events
  (p. 179). There is no sign clause and no account of intensity beyond
  the degree of synchrony. So the proposal's two distinctive moves are
  not in Lewis. One is that what prevails is an ordering, which makes
  [CLAIM-tmp85p0g](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp85p0g.md)'s question of currency askable. The other is that the
  ordering is of a particular kind, which makes the regime valence.
  Lewis supplies a dynamical mechanism by which such an ordering could
  come to prevail, and a precedent the record should cite rather than
  rediscover.

**On the regimes of [CLAIM-tmp83ge2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp83ge2.md).** Lewis's dynamics are threshold-rich
by design. Effects "may be exponential, subject to threshold effects"
(§3.1), transitions are abrupt, and baseline states "determine
thresholds for what is meaningful" (p. 191). If the earliest valence
were an EI-like attractor, the prediction would be ALARM's
discontinuity, with a threshold that moves with the animal's state
rather than staying fixed. That is a third case beside the claim's
fixed level threshold and its continuous curve: a state-dependent,
possibly hysteretic, switch. Like Scherer's hysteresis
([NOTE-tmpsx1uk](NOTE-tmpsx1uk.md)), it means the trade-off test must control or
record the animal's prior state.

**On measurement.** Lewis's phase synchrony is an operational measure,
EEG or LFP phase locking, but of coherence among regions, not of which
ranking governs action. For [QUESTION-tmpsqwu5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpsqwu5.md) it is a candidate marker
of ascendancy strength, and needs pairing with a behavioural record of
which conflicts are won.

**On [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md).** In Lewis's account, the authority a configuration is
granted is not a quantity separate from the precedence it claims. It is
the degree to which constituents become entrained to it. If that reading
holds, the claimed–granted gap is the transient before stabilization,
and chronic conflict is oscillation between attractors.

## Limitations

The model is a framework, not a fitted dynamical system: no equations,
no estimated parameters. Sander and Scherer's charge that it gives no
operational boundaries for an episode is fair for the psychological
model. Lewis answers it with the phase model, not with a measure. Only
part of the neural review was read here.

## Open questions

Whether an EI's selection among "competing ensembles" can be described
as one ranking winning over others, which is the bridge the record needs.
And whether theta-band synchrony tracks which of two conflicting
tendencies governs behaviour, or only how much is going on.
