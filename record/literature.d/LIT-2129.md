# LIT-2129: Modelling DNA origami self-assembly at the domain level

Dannenberg, F., Dunn, K. E., Bath, J., Kwiatkowska, M., Turberfield, A. J. & Ouldridge, T. E. (2015). Modelling DNA origami self-assembly at the domain level. *The Journal of Chemical Physics* 143(16): 165102. doi:10.1063/1.4933426. arXiv:1509.03066.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1645](../notes.d/NOTE-1645.md). Sections are the arXiv v2's.*

- **Cooperativity makes hysteresis.** "As partially-formed regions are
  relatively disfavoured, cooperativity tends to exaggerate hysteresis
  (the system must pass through more substantial free-energy barriers to
  assemble or melt, slowing down kinetics)" (Sec. III B); at loop exponent γ = 2.5 and stacking
  strength 0 to 3 the gap Tm − Ta grows from 0.4 to 2.5 °C.
- **Asymmetric barriers.** "unbinding transition rates grow rapidly with
  temperature, whereas binding rates are essentially constant" (Sec. II B):
  cooling from 0.1 to 10 °C per minute lowers Ta by 5.3 °C and raises Tm by
  about 1.3 °C; fewer staples lower Ta and leave melting largely unchanged.
- **Cooperativity is local.** Removing a staple's neighbours lowers its
  incorporation temperature and suppresses its hysteresis; removing
  distant staples does almost nothing.
- **Not crystal nucleation.** The analogy "with crystal nucleation is not
  so obvious" (Sec. I), since "Staples bind to the scaffold only, not to
  other staples".
