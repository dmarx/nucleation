# QUESTION-330: How does anxiety change exploration across tasks and facets: does it reduce foraging exploration once stress and effort cost are controlled, does somatic anxiety reduce checking for threat through aversion to detecting it rather than a lower value placed on the information, and, in the same participants across more than one bandit task, does directed exploration rise with trait or cognitive anxiety and fall with state or somatic anxiety?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-907](../theory.d/THEORY-907.md) and [THEORY-917](../theory.d/THEORY-917.md) hold what the review reports, mostly from
sub-clinical samples, single tasks and single anxiety measures, with
stress and anxiety treated together and none of the main findings
replicated.

## What would answer it

The former promote_when of each, unchanged.

**[THEORY-907](../theory.d/THEORY-907.md).** For the foraging pattern: a replicated study with
clinical as well as sub-clinical anxiety, in which less leaving of
depleting patches is shown to come from anxiety rather than stress or
effort cost, with the environment's reward rate known. For the checking
result: a replication of the predator-foraging finding, in which lower
checking with somatic anxiety is shown, by modelling or manipulation, to
come from aversion to detecting the threat rather than from lower
valuation of the information. It is refuted if, with effort and stress
controlled, anxiety does not reduce foraging exploration, or if anxious
people check as much as others once the cost of detection is equated.
Further correlational studies with a single anxiety measure cannot
settle the mechanism.

**[THEORY-917](../theory.d/THEORY-917.md).** A study in which the same participants do more than one
exploration task (a Horizon Task and a restless or two-armed bandit at
least), with state, trait, somatic and cognitive anxiety measured by the
same instruments, and preferably including clinical anxiety, showing
directed exploration rising with trait or cognitive anxiety and falling
with state or somatic anxiety in each task, and random exploration
unrelated; replicated in a second sample. It is refuted if, in such a
study, the facets do not differ in direction, or if the directions
reverse between tasks. Further single studies with one task and one
measure cannot settle it.
