The owner asked how the record stood on remix culture. The answer was
that it held nothing on the subject: no Lessig, nothing on sampling,
appropriation art, fair use or Creative Commons. The nearest works were
Jenkins's *Convergence Culture* ([LIT-1121](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1121.md)) and Jenkins, Ford and Green's
*Spreadable Media* ([LIT-1143](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1143.md)), both registered unread from the sports
fandom exchange (workbench entry of 2026-10-16). The nearest case was the
Juggernaut redub ([CASE-100](../../record/cases.d/CASE-100.md)). The owner then asked for Lessig's *Remix* and
the two Jenkins books to be filed.

**Remix: filed and read, [LIT-tmpreyek](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpreyek.md) and [NOTE-tmptp3k0](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmptp3k0.md).** Lessig released the
book under CC BY-NC, and Bloomsbury Academic published that edition. The
copy read is the publisher's PDF as redistributed under the licence on
the Internet Archive. It has the Penguin Press pagination. The book was
read in full in three parts by three sub-agents, each told to read every
line of its part, and the NOTE was composed from their reports. Every
quotation in the LIT and NOTE was then checked against the extracted text
by script. Four that did not match were corrected. A note number was
also checked and corrected: Patterson is ch. 9 n. 10, not n. 9.

- **Tags.** `society-and-governance` first, since the book is about law
  and policy, then `social-science`. No new topic was needed. The closest
  words, `social-ontology` and `myth-and-folklore`, would be wrong.
- **Not in the anthology.** A grep of its record/ (clone of 2026-10-20,
  commit d1573d2) for "Lessig", "Convergence Culture" and "Spreadable
  Media" found nothing. The book carries no instruction for
  machine-learning practice, so it has no `anthology-candidate` flag.
- **What it bears on.** Its argument that remix needs the original,
  because the meaning is carried by the reference, is the sharpest
  contact with the record's argument. It says that for remix,
  [CLAIM-002](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-002.md)'s "identity of the artifact" is the relation that does the
  work, the reverse of translation. The NOTE raises, without filing, a
  question: can recognizability of the source artifact be a preservation
  target in its own right? For [CASE-100](../../record/cases.d/CASE-100.md) and [CLAIM-332](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-332.md) the book supplies
  vocabulary (sharing economy, hybrid, RW remix) and no answer, because it
  never asks whether a population acts as a population.

**Jenkins: searched, not reached, both now Unavailable ([ADR-041](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-041.md)).**
Neither book has a lawful open copy that this client could find.

- Crossref gives no licence or full-text link for either NYU Press
  Scholarship Online DOI.
- The host, De Gruyter Brill, answered with an empty bot-check page.
- The Internet Archive holds the books only as controlled lending.
- An Internet Archive upload of the Spanish translation of *Convergence
  Culture* carries a CC BY-ND label. It is not the publisher's, and it was
  not used.
- OpenAlex refused the query because the shared daily budget was spent,
  and the DOAB API refused this client. So the search is not complete,
  and the status notes say so.

Both stay Unavailable until a copy is supplied. *Convergence Culture* is
the one to get first: *Remix* takes its Potter Wars account and its split
between "prohibitionist" and "collaborationist" companies from it.

**Not done.** No QUESTION or CLAIM was filed from the reading. The
question about recognizability is in the NOTE's open questions, for the
owner to take up or leave.
