# THEORY-tmpkd4kn: Gaboriaud and colleagues find, in a preregistered mouse-tracking experiment, that intent, outcome and the agent's causal role each make wrongness and blame judgments harsher, but that the two judgment types differ little: only causality weighed more in blame, and by a very small amount

## Source

LIT-tmp8xz2k, read in NOTE-tmpe8t2g (Europe PMC full text, PMC12372680).

## What was actually shown

In 145 participants judging 64 scenarios, intent had a large effect, and harmful outcome and the agent's own causation had moderate effects, on both judgment types. Of the predicted differences between wrongness and blame, only one held: "the causality effect was stronger (+ 0.16) in the case of blame judgments (b = 0.98) compared to wrongness judgments (b = 0.82)". The authors: "this study does not reveal clear differential weight for intent and outcome inputs in blame versus wrongness judgments", and the distinction "must not be overestimated".

## What this does not say

- Anything about responsibility for patterns, for conditions others act under, or for prevention: none was manipulated.
- That the three-input model is confirmed: the authors call the support partial.
- Anything beyond student vignette judgments in a within-participant design.
