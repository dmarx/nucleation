The owner offered their microbiome as an example of drive from a lower
level ("my microbiome can impact things like my affect and level of hunger").
The record held the holobiont line for corals and several interoception
theories, but no human gut–brain literature. Two open-access reviews were
read, one per half of the example.

## What entered

- **Han et al. (2021), on appetite** ([LIT-903](../../record/literature.d/LIT-903.md), [NOTE-706](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-706.md),
  doi:10.1186/s40168-021-01093-y, PMC8293578, CC BY). The routes from
  microbial metabolites and protein mimics to host appetite. Mostly rodent,
  by the authors' own statement.
- **Forssten et al. (2022), on mood** ([LIT-915](../../record/literature.d/LIT-915.md), [NOTE-703](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-703.md),
  doi:10.3390/nu14030568, PMC8840472). The mouse-to-human gap. Written by
  employees of a probiotics manufacturer. It documents its own failed
  translations.

The canonical review, Cryan et al. (2019), "The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis"
(*Physiological Reviews* 99: 1877–2013, doi:10.1152/physrev.00018.2018),
was not open in Europe PMC. At about 130 pages it was set aside, not
registered.

## How they were read

Each was read in full by a reader working for the assistant, from the Europe
PMC full-text XML converted to text. The reader drafted a digest with
verbatim quotations. The seventeen quotations used in the entries were
checked against the extracted texts by script, and all matched.

## Selection

Both were chosen from Europe PMC by query (open-access reviews; appetite or
mood; ranked by citations). The appetite review was the most cited. The mood
review was chosen over more-cited ones because its subject is the
translation from animal to human evidence, which is the weak point of the
owner's example. Its industry authorship is recorded in its NOTE.

## Boundary

Neither is held in the anthology. A grep of its record for "microbio" and
"gut-brain" found nothing relevant. Neither carries an instruction for
machine-learning practice.
