# LIT-tmpa221k: Moral Foundations Theory: The Pragmatic Validity of Moral Pluralism

Jesse Graham, Jonathan Haidt, Sena Koleva, Matt Motyl, Ravi Iyer, Sean P.
Wojcik, Peter H. Ditto (2013), *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*
47, 55–130 — DOI-10.1016/B978-0-12-407236-7.00002-4

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 in the morality-as-cooperation batch ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)), as the
main statement of the theory morality-as-cooperation defines itself
against. It extends Haidt and Joseph's founding essay, [LIT-tmpbptbc](LIT-tmpbptbc.md), from
four modules to five foundations and adds criteria for adding more, and
the MFQ-2 paper, [LIT-tmpaywst](LIT-tmpaywst.md), extends it in turn by splitting Fairness.
No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

**The comparison with morality-as-cooperation.** [LIT-047](LIT-047.md) (read in
[NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md), claim C7) argues that MAC is broader and more precise than MFT,
because MFT has no foundation for kin altruism, reciprocal altruism, hawkish
displays or property, and includes Care and Sanctity, which are tied to no
type of cooperation. Checked against this chapter, [NOTE-tmp05ouq](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp05ouq.md) finds:

- **Kin and reciprocity: the omission claim fails.** Table 2.4 gives Care's
  evolutionary model as kin selection (Hamilton 1964) and attachment, and
  Fairness's as reciprocal altruism (Trivers 1971). The text calls the
  Fairness foundation "largely just an elaboration of the psychology
  described by Trivers" (p. 114). The narrower charge in [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)'s
  footnote 1 holds better: MFT folds reciprocity and fairness into one
  foundation, and the chapter itself reports that its fairness measures
  "centered on equality rather than equity" (p. 106).
- **Property: named, not omitted.** Ownership/theft is one of three
  candidates the chapter calls "good candidates for foundationhood" (p.
  104). It is not one of the five and has no measure.
- **Bravery: omitted.** No foundation or candidate covers it.
- **Sanctity: the real disagreement.** The chapter grounds Sanctity in
  disgust and the behavioural immune system, not cooperation, as [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)
  says. It also reports Sanctity as the strongest predictor of attitudes
  on most culture-war issues (p. 88), so it is evidence that purity is
  moralized, the point Bloom's commentary pressed. By MFT's own criteria
  it is the weakest foundation: no evidence of it in nonhuman primates or
  infants (Table 2.4).
- **"Ad hoc".** The chapter answers the charge that its list is
  arbitrary with five explicit criteria, and says it "engaged in no post
  hoc evolutionary theorizing" (p. 113). The criteria test candidates;
  they do not generate them, as game theory does for MAC.

So on this text, "more precise" survives in one sense: MAC derives its
list from a model and MFT does not. "Broader" does not survive. Each list
has items the other lacks: MFT has Sanctity, care beyond kin, and Liberty
as a candidate; MAC has bravery and a property domain.

The chapter also treats two other rivals in this batch. It classes
Baumard, André and Sperber's mutualism ([LIT-tmpof37t](LIT-tmpof37t.md)) and Gray's dyadic
harm (the line of [LIT-tmp0aaqg](LIT-tmp0aaqg.md)) as monisms that cannot explain disgust's
effects on judgment. It treats Rai and Fiske's relationship regulation
([LIT-tmpiuf8w](LIT-tmpiuf8w.md)) as compatible with MFT.

[NOTE-tmp05ouq](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp05ouq.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the place to read MFT as a theory rather than a
questionnaire. Its empirical half is a review of the authors' and others'
MFQ studies, largely of Western internet samples, which the chapter itself
says are probably WEIRD (p. 92).
