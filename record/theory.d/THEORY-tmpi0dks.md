# THEORY-tmpi0dks: Ashford and Mulgan report Scanlon's contractualism as holding an act wrong when it would be disallowed by any set of principles that no one could reasonably reject as a basis for informed, unforced general agreement, judged only by individuals' reasons for objecting to a principle and its alternatives, without aggregating lesser complaints against a weightier one, and covering only what we owe to one another

Ashford & Mulgan, "Contractualism" (SEP, 2018 revision), [LIT-tmp3y2ok](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3y2ok.md).

## The report

The formula, quoting Scanlon (1998, p. 153): "An act is wrong if its
performance under the circumstances would be disallowed by any set of
principles for the general regulation of behaviour that no one could
reasonably reject as a basis for informed, unforced, general agreement."
Only "various individuals' reasons for objecting to that principle and
alternatives to it" count (p. 229), and contractualism "does not allow a
number of lesser complaints to outweigh one person's weightier complaint".
It "covers only the realm of what we owe to one another".

## What it does not show

- Scanlon's own text: this is a report, with quotations.
- Scanlon's view after 2025, when he gave up the bilateral reading the
  entry describes ([LIT-tmpgoyge](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgoyge.md)).
- Anything about rights or normative powers beyond substantive
  responsibility.
