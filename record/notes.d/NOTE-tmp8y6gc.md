# NOTE-tmp8y6gc: Abuhamdeh — Investigating the flow experience

## Contribution

A survey of how flow is measured, and an argument about what it is.
Across 42 PsycINFO studies from July 2014 to July 2019, flow was
operationalized in 24 distinct ways (Table 1). Three inconsistencies are
traced to their sources: continuous against discrete, enjoyable or not,
and the state against its conditions. The paper's proposal is to measure
flow only as Csikszentmihalyi conceived it and to use task involvement and
intrinsic motivation for everything below it.

## Key insight

Much of what is called flow is absorption. Absorption, as task
involvement, is a purely cognitive matter of attention and is not
enjoyable in itself; flow, as Csikszentmihalyi defined it, is absorption
that is also highly enjoyable and rare. Whether "flow" is positive
therefore depends first on which of the two a study measured.

## Assumptions

- **Csikszentmihalyi's conception is the only valid one** (Summary and
  Conclusion): "flow" is a proper noun with his conceptualization
  "pre-installed", so it is "the default conceptualization, and this is
  true regardless of its merits".
- **Appraisal theory** (Issue 2, Source #1): appraisals are usually
  automatic, need little attention, and become more automatic with
  practice, so emotion can arise during absorbed, skilled activity.
- **Emotion does not need self-awareness**, only its recognition and
  labelling does (Issue 2, Source #2), citing infants, non-human mammals
  and children without a cortex.

## Key results

- **The review (Table 1; Appendix).** 111 PsycINFO hits under the heading
  "Flow (consciousness state)", 70 with an operationalization, 42 after
  exclusions (trait flow, collective flow, unclear or multiple
  operationalizations, validation studies, non-English). 24 distinct
  operationalizations; most continuous; 17 of 42 without enjoyment or
  autotelic experience; most common scales mix experience items with
  condition items.
- **Issue 1: discrete or continuous.** Csikszentmihalyi described flow as
  rare and optimal (1975 preface, quoted: "On the rare occasions that it
  happens, we feel a sense of exhilaration, a deep sense of enjoyment that
  is long cherished"). Continuous scales were adopted for statistical power
  and turn "low flow" into "a contradiction in terms". They also lose
  discriminant validity against task involvement.
- **Issue 2: is flow enjoyable?** Csikszentmihalyi "conceptualized flow as
  an enjoyable experience" and has kept to it (1975 preface, quoted: "the
  experience itself is so enjoyable they will do it even at great cost, for
  the sheer sake of doing it"). Three sources of the view that it is not:
  - **Seligman** (Authentic Happiness, Flourish), quoted: "it is the absence
    of emotion, of any kind of consciousness, that is at the heart of
    flow", and "the concentrated attention that flow requires uses up all
    the cognitive and emotional resources that make up thought and
    feeling." Abuhamdeh answers from appraisal theory: appraisals are
    automatic and need not use attention.
  - **Absence of self-awareness** read as absence of emotion (Kyriazos et
    al. 2018). Abuhamdeh: "self-awareness is not a precondition for the
    experience of emotions, only the recognition and labeling of them."
  - **Csikszentmihalyi's own word "pleasure".** He quotes Flow (1990):
    "None of these [flow] experiences may be particularly pleasurable at
    the time they are taking place, but afterward we think back on them and
    say, 'That really was fun' and wish they would happen again." He
    answers that Csikszentmihalyi reserves "pleasure" for experiences that
    satisfy biological needs, "restorative homeostatic experiences", and
    that an artist working all night without food or rest had no
    "pleasure" in that sense, "But this should not be misinterpreted as
    implying that the artist did not enjoy him/herself."
- **Issue 3: state against conditions.** Clear goals, immediate feedback
  and challenge–skill balance are conditions, not the state; scales that
  average them in conflate cause and effect. The 10-item Core Flow Scale
  is the exception and "may be the best option".
- **Where to draw the boundary (Question 1).** No sharp threshold is likely;
  the quadrant model labels a quarter or more of daily experience as flow;
  self-identified flow episodes (the Flow Questionnaire) come closest to the
  concept but were used by one study in 42. Taxometric methods are
  suggested.
- **Below flow (Question 2).** Task involvement, "the degree to which an
  individual concentrates on and becomes absorbed in an activity", is "a
  purely cognitive phenomenon representing the degree of attentional
  involvement in an activity; it is not inherently enjoyable and motivating
  in concept, though it often predicts both." Intrinsic motivation, measured
  by enjoyment and interest, captures flow's emotional and motivational
  side.

## Concepts

- **Flow** — on Csikszentmihalyi's conception, a rare, discrete, "optimal"
  state of consciousness that is highly enjoyable and autotelic.
- **Autotelic** — intrinsically rewarding; the paper treats it as
  synonymous with intrinsic motivation.
- **Pleasure against enjoyment** (Csikszentmihalyi, as reported) —
  pleasure is the satisfaction of biological needs, "restorative
  homeostatic experiences"; enjoyment is the reward of absorbed,
  challenging activity, which may conflict with those needs.
- **Task involvement** — degree of concentration on and absorption in an
  activity; cognitive, continuous, not inherently enjoyable.
- **Conditions of flow** — clear goals, immediate feedback, balance of
  challenge and skill (Figure 1, from Nakamura and Csikszentmihalyi 2002).

## Connections

It rests on Csikszentmihalyi (1975, 1990) and on Nakamura and
Csikszentmihalyi (2002, [LIT-914](../literature.d/LIT-914.md) in the record, unread), from which its
Figure 1 is taken. Its foil is Seligman (2002, 2011). It draws on appraisal
theory (Moors, Scherer, Lazarus) and on the task-involvement literature
(Harackiewicz; Elliot). Swann et al. (2018) is cited for a Kuhnian
diagnosis of the field.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-441](../claims.d/CLAIM-441.md): partly.** The objection waits on whether flow is reported
  as positive during the episode, and names exit 3, that "people in flow
  are absorbed rather than happy, and that the enjoyment is reported
  afterwards". This reading confirms that the literature is divided on
  exactly that point: Seligman says flow is emotionless, and
  Csikszentmihalyi (1990), as quoted, says flow experiences may not be
  "particularly pleasurable at the time" and are called "fun" afterwards.
  Abuhamdeh argues that flow is enjoyed during the episode, but by
  definition and by appraisal theory, not by data, and the scales he
  reviews are filled in after the activity. So exit 3 is neither shown
  nor excluded, and the objection's defeater ("flow is not reported as
  positive during the episode, only after it") is not met. Two things the
  reading adds. First, flow is enjoyable by Csikszentmihalyi's definition,
  so "flow is positive" is partly analytic: an absorbed episode that is
  not enjoyed is, on this view, task involvement, which is "not inherently
  enjoyable". Second, as Abuhamdeh reports it, Csikszentmihalyi separates
  pleasure, the restoration of homeostatic needs, from enjoyment, which can
  run against them. That bears on exit 1: flow's positivity is placed on
  the enjoyment side and not on the side of an ordering of the
  organism's states by their bearing on its maintenance. That is half of
  exit 1; it does not show that the enjoyment is intrinsic pleasantness.
- **[TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md): partly.** Its nonvacuity condition makes valence undefined
  where nothing is contested, and flow is the case pressed against it.
  The reading does not decide whether flow is positive in the episode.
  It does supply a distinction the record can use: absorption alone (task
  involvement, as in [CASE-051](../cases.d/CASE-051.md)'s absorbed writer) is not inherently
  enjoyable, so undefined valence there is no anomaly; and if flow's
  enjoyment is not homeostatic "pleasure", it takes flow's positivity off
  the maintenance ordering, half of exit 1, without showing it is
  intrinsic pleasantness; exit 1 in full would leave the definition
  standing. Nothing here bears on exit 4.
- **[CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md): partly, by way of [CLAIM-441](../claims.d/CLAIM-441.md).** The same: the flow case
  against its first consequence is narrowed, not answered.
- **[CLAIM-162](../claims.d/CLAIM-162.md) and [CLAIM-433](../claims.d/CLAIM-433.md).** [CLAIM-162](../claims.d/CLAIM-162.md) reads flow as nothing overridden;
  this paper does not discuss conflict among motives. [CLAIM-433](../claims.d/CLAIM-433.md)'s
  restriction to goal-conduciveness valence is the distinction exit 1
  relies on, and the pleasure/enjoyment split is evidence for it within
  flow research, at second hand.
- **[LIT-914](../literature.d/LIT-914.md).** It stays unread. This paper uses it only for Figure 1, so
  it is not a substitute for reading it.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **No data on experience.** The review counts operationalizations; the
  claims about how flow feels rest on quotations and argument.
- **Second-hand quotations.** Csikszentmihalyi and Seligman are quoted, not
  read here; the "pleasure" passage is interpreted by Abuhamdeh.
- **Definition by stipulation.** That Csikszentmihalyi's conception is the
  only valid one "regardless of its merits" makes the enjoyment of flow
  partly a matter of naming.
- **The review is not exhaustive** (Appendix): five years, one database,
  one subject heading.

## Open questions

- Is flow positive while it happens? Experience sampling during absorbed,
  skilled activity, with valence probed at the moment and again later,
  would settle exit 3; the reading names no such study.
- Does enjoyment in flow dissociate from goal-conduciveness, as the
  pleasure/enjoyment split suggests? That would decide between exits 1
  and 4.
