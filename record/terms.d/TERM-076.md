# TERM-076: goal-conduciveness valence, organizational: a regime in which the negotiation among a system's perspectives has, approximately, a potential, an ordering of the system's own states by their bearing on its maintenance along which the moves that win its conflicts are improvements, persisting and restored after interruption; signed by whether the system runs ahead of or behind a reference course along that potential, and as intense as the negotiation's decisions follow it rather than any rival ordering

## Definition

In [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)'s notation, let an organization 𝒪 face, at time t, the
attainable options X_t. Each of its perspectives i ranks X_t by a
preorder ≿ᵢ and holds an authority that the organization's endorsement
dynamics set ([CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md), [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md)). The organization's **appraisal** is
the negotiation N_t that takes the profile of rankings and authorities
to a collective relation R_t on X_t.

- **A common currency at t**, in the owner's sense, is ordinal
  extendibility: the negotiation has an ordinal potential on a window
  around t, so some complete ordering agrees with every win
  ([TERM-077](TERM-077.md)). It requires only that a compatible extension exist, not an
  observed shared evaluation of every option; how options never
  contested compare is open across the class. It is not ordinal
  comparability of every pair, not a scalar currency, a real-valued
  representation, and not a cardinal trade-off structure with fixed
  exchange rates; on continua the last two come apart from the first
  ([CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md)), and this term uses only the first.
- **Potential.** Read the perspectives as players, each with its own
  ranking. A ranking ≿_v is a *potential* of the negotiation when every
  move that wins one of its conflicts is an improvement along ≿_v: the
  perspectives' rankings differ, but what prevails among them climbs one
  shared ordering. This is Monderer and Shapley's ordinal potential ([LIT-1462](../literature.d/LIT-1462.md)),
  carried from a game's unilateral deviations to a negotiation's
  conflicts. Exact potentials are rare, so the term says *approximately*,
  and the approximation is ordinal: the least fraction of the window's
  wins whose reversal leaves them acyclic ([TERM-077](TERM-077.md)). The Hodge
  decomposition (Candogan, Menache, Ozdaglar and Parrilo for games, [LIT-1442](../literature.d/LIT-1442.md); Jiang,
  Lim, Yao and Ye's HodgeRank for pairwise comparisons, [LIT-1439](../literature.d/LIT-1439.md);
  [THEORY-894](../theory.d/THEORY-894.md)) is not the test of existence: its gradient is a
  least-squares fit to margins and can reverse a comparison the wins
  settle ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), part 4). It is kept as a measure of cardinal
  inconsistency, for records whose margins are intensities. Frijda's
  three marks of control precedence ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)), priority,
  persistence and resumption, are how a potential shows itself in time;
  an ordinal potential that has them is a persistent potential
  ([TERM-078](TERM-078.md)), and this term is a persistent potential of a
  particular kind.
- **Existence and kind are separate.** That the negotiation has a
  potential at all is its common currency; on a finite set of outcomes it
  is exactly that no chain of winning moves cycles ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md),
  part 1; Monderer and Shapley, [THEORY-896](../theory.d/THEORY-896.md)). Which ordering the
  potential is, and whether it ranks the system's own states by their
  bearing on its maintenance, is a further fact. The potential is nobody's
  ranking until it is shown to be one.
- **Nonvacuity.** A window with no contested pairs has an acyclic,
  empty relation of wins, so every ordering is trivially its potential.
  Valence is *undefined* there, not zero or neutral: the term applies only
  over a non-empty set of contested pairs on which ≿_v and some rival
  ordering disagree. It is the thesis's first consequence (CLAIM-440), now a
  condition of the definition, stated for contest: what it needs is
  contested pairs in the record of wins, not demands that are jointly
  infeasible (TERM-060). The two come apart both ways (CLAIM-449).
- **The kind.** ≿_v ranks the system's own states by their bearing on
  its maintenance: its essential variables in Ashby's sense ([THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)),
  the graded norm of Di Paolo's adaptivity ([THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)), what [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)
  calls a regulatory state's meaning "for staying alive".
- **Identification.** A record of wins fixes a class of potentials, not
  one: increasing transforms aside, orderings that differ only on pairs
  never contested fit it equally ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), part 7). So the claim
  that the potential is an ordering of this kind, or is a particular
  perspective's ordering, is identified only by intervention: devaluing
  an outcome or perturbing the system and contesting the pairs on which
  the candidate orderings differ ([QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md)). Until then the
  term is reported as holding of the class.
- **Goal-conduciveness valence** is a regime in which a ranking of that kind
  is a persistent potential of the negotiation over a non-empty set of
  contested pairs.
  - Its **sign** is whether the system runs ahead of or behind a
    reference course along ≿_v: ahead is positive, behind negative, on
    course neutral. This is Carver and Scheier's account, whose neutral
    point is a reference rate of progress, not rest ([LIT-1441](../literature.d/LIT-1441.md), read
    through [LIT-1449](../literature.d/LIT-1449.md) and [NOTE-1102](../notes.d/NOTE-1102.md)): a system standing still
    where it expected to advance is behind. Comparing positions on an
    ordering needs no measure, so the clause stays ordinal. Where the
    class of potentials 𝒫_t the record admits is not a singleton, the
    sign is set-valued: V_t = {sgn_P(x_t, r_t) : P ∈ 𝒫_t}, for the
    system's state x_t and reference state r_t. The system has a
    determinate sign, positive, neutral or negative, only when V_t is a
    singleton, that is, when every admissible potential ranks x_t and
    r_t alike; otherwise the sign is partially identified, and narrowing
    it needs an intervention that contests the pair. With the two
    orderings of [CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), part 7, one puts a state ahead of its
    reference and the other behind it (part 10).
  - Its **intensity** is the potential's strength: the share of the
    window's contested wins that agree with ≿_v, on the pairs where ≿_v
    and a rival ordering disagree. It is ordinal, counting wins without
    weighing them; a weighted version that counts margins is cardinal and
    is stated as such where margins are intensities. Coherence, a low
    least reversal fraction, is a precondition and not the measure: a
    stable compromise is as coherent as one perspective deciding
    everything ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), part 3). Where ≿_v is fixed in advance,
    as the maintenance ordering or a perspective's ranking, its agreement
    can be scored on the contests at hand. Where it is selected from the
    record, as a best-fitting ordering, it is scored on contests held out
    from the selection: on wins drawn at random, a best-fitting ordering
    agrees with 0.87 of the contests it was fitted to and 0.50 of fresh
    ones (part 11). Like the sign, intensity is reported across the class
    where the class is not a singleton, as the range of agreements its
    members attain.
  - **Ambivalence** is conflict that no ordering absorbs, read in the
    sequence of windows and not in a netted record: circular triads or a
    positive least reversal fraction within a window, and reversals of
    the same pair's outcome across windows, against a null model that
    keeps the record's graph of contested pairs. A pair won alternately by
    each side is ambivalent although its net flow is zero
    ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), part 5). The Hodge cyclic share's baseline of
    (n − 2)/3n holds only for a complete tournament with every pair
    contested once (part 2); on a sparse graph the baseline depends on the
    graph. No ordering absorbing the conflicts is what Sen's theorem allows
    ([THEORY-027](../theory.d/THEORY-027.md)) and Tversky's cycles show ([CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md), part 3b).
    **Neutrality** is a persistent potential with the system on its
    course.

## Revision of 2026-10-23: goal conduciveness, not hedonic

Drafted as "hedonic valence", the owner's word. Revised before merge, at
the owner's direction, after Berridge's liking–wanting dissociation
showed that the regime this term defines tracks 'wanting', not 'liking'
([THEORY-893](../theory.d/THEORY-893.md), [CLAIM-438](../claims.d/CLAIM-438.md)). On the component process model's
levels of valence ([LIT-1458](../literature.d/LIT-1458.md), [CLAIM-433](../claims.d/CLAIM-433.md)) the ordering defined
here is goal conduciveness, need satisfaction and goal attainment, and
intrinsic pleasantness, 'liking', is a separate appraisal with its own
ordering. Hedonic valence in that sense is not what this term defines.

## Revision of 2026-10-23: the review of PR [#327](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/327)

A review of the pull request that filed this term found six faults,
and all six were conceded before merge:

1. It defined a common currency as a complete, transitive relation,
   which [CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md) shows is not a currency on continua. The
   definition now separates ordinal extendibility (first named
   comparability), ordinal comparability, scalar currency and
   cardinal trade-off structure, uses only the first, and weakens it from
   complete and transitive to acyclic.
2. Existence alone made a conflict-free system vacuously eligible. A
   nonvacuity condition now makes valence undefined there.
3. It measured approximation and intensity with the Hodge gradient,
   which can reverse an ordinal comparison. Both are now ordinal; Hodge
   measures only cardinal inconsistency.
4. Its ambivalence measure read a netted record, which erases
   alternation, and applied a complete-tournament baseline everywhere. It
   now reads windows in sequence against a graph-preserving null model.
5. [QUESTION-266](../questions.d/QUESTION-266.md) predicted that intrinsic pleasantness and goal
   conduciveness coincide, which this term does not imply. The question
   now makes their covariation conditional.
6. It named the potential as one ordering, which a record cannot fix.
   It now reports the class and identifies a member only by intervention.

The review also proposed the hierarchy that the three terms now
carry: ordinal coherence ([TERM-077](TERM-077.md)), its persistence over time
([TERM-078](TERM-078.md)), goal-conduciveness valence (this term), and felt valence
as a candidate only ([CLAIM-439](../claims.d/CLAIM-439.md)). The review called the second level
"dynamical ascendancy"; it is named for the potential instead, keeping
the rename below. The synthetic cases behind each fix are in
scratch/formal/valence.py.

**A second review**, of the revised pull request, found three more
faults, also conceded before merge. Acyclicity establishes ordinal
extendibility, not comparability, and the first level was renamed
([TERM-077](TERM-077.md)). An approximate persistent potential is no Lyapunov
function, and the second level now separates exact from approximate
([TERM-078](TERM-078.md)). And where the class of potentials is not a singleton,
sign and intensity are set-valued, and an ordering selected from the
record is scored on held-out contests (above).

## What it is not

- **Not "dominance", and not "ascendancy".** The notion was drafted as
  "dominance" and renamed on 2026-10-23, because the word is taken: in
  affective science "dominance" is the third axis of Mehrabian and
  Russell's pleasure–arousal–dominance model, the felt sense of being in
  control, which Fontaine and colleagues' potency–control dimension
  continues ([LIT-1437](../literature.d/LIT-1437.md)) and which the component process model's own
  authors call "dominance" ([LIT-1458](../literature.d/LIT-1458.md), p. 4); in decision theory a
  dominated option is one worse on every count. It was renamed
  "ascendancy" the same day, and renamed again, at the owner's direction,
  to "potential", because the game-theoretic notion says exactly what was
  meant and brings its theorems with it: an ordering prevails when the
  negotiation has it as a potential, and how far it prevails is the
  share of contested wins that follow it. "Precedence" was not used,
  because [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md) uses it for the priority a motive claims, as against
  the authority it is granted. "Potential" in physics and in free-energy
  accounts is compatible: a function the dynamics descend or climb.
- **Not intrinsic pleasantness.** 'Liking', the hedonic impact of a
  stimulus, is a separate appraisal (see the revision above). Whether it
  can be the potential of some ordering is open ([CLAIM-433](../claims.d/CLAIM-433.md)).
- **Not a felt state.** The term is functional. Whether the regime is
  felt is [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md), and whether it is identical with felt valence is
  Veit's thesis ([THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)), not this term's.
- **Not frustration** ([TERM-060](TERM-060.md)). Frustration is a relation among
  demands that no admissible transformation meets jointly. Valence on
  this term presupposes contest, not frustration: rankings can contest
  while every demand is satisfiable, and demands can be jointly
  infeasible before any contest is recorded (CLAIM-449). Where no pair is
  contested, no ranking has anything to prevail over, and the nonvacuity
  condition leaves valence undefined whether or not the demands are
  frustrated.
- **Not any potential goal.** A ranking of world states, however
  potential, is not valence until it ranks the system's own states by
  their bearing on its maintenance. Whether a firm's potential metric is a
  ranking of that kind is a question about the firm ([QUESTION-267](../questions.d/QUESTION-267.md)).
- **Not a potential game in the strict sense.** A potential game has
  players changing their own coordinates of a joint profile; a
  negotiation over one indivisible choice has no such coordinates. The
  finite result needs only the relation of winning moves, so it applies to
  either; for contested indivisible choices it is read on the options
  themselves, with HodgeRank ([THEORY-894](../theory.d/THEORY-894.md)) as the cardinal measure
  where margins are intensities. The reading also needs a dynamics, one
  winning move at a time, under which the potential is climbed; it does
  not hold for simultaneous moves.
- **Not a scale.** Neither sign nor the ordering needs a measure. Where
  the potential ranking is continuous it has a numerical representation;
  where it is a graded lexicographic override it has none
  ([CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

CLAIM-441's flow case is unanswered. On CLAIM-162's reading of flow nothing
is contested, so this definition leaves flow's valence undefined while flow
is reported as positive. Which of CLAIM-441's four exits holds turns on
whether flow is positive during the episode, and the flow literature
(LIT-914) is unread. Its fourth exit would change this definition's
nonvacuity condition. CLAIM-440's consequence 1 still says frustration and
wants the same correction when its cluster is reviewed.
