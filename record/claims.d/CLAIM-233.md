# CLAIM-233: The central theses' defeat conditions pit them only against embedding, propositional or surface similarity, and two rivals the record holds, functional equivalence as Nida states it and a proposition with its illocutionary force, also predict that judged pragmatic observables beat text similarity

## The objection

Every thesis-level defeat condition in the line has the same form. Human
judgements of fidelity, or of shared kind, are predicted by a text-similarity
score as well as by the theory's measures.

- [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md): "predicted as well by propositional or embedding similarity as
  by measured preservation of pragmatic observables (stance, footing,
  alignment, expected uptake)".
- [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md): "predicted as well by topic-factor, embedding or surface
  similarity as by independently measured relational profiles".
- [CLAIM-005](CLAIM-005.md): "Embedding or propositional similarity between source and
  rendering predicts held-out human communicative-fidelity judgements as well
  as the context-indexed distribution distance does."
- [CLAIM-078](CLAIM-078.md): "Structural and decision-theoretic measures add no prediction of
  communicative-identity changes beyond text similarity".

No rival account the record holds says that fidelity judgements follow
embedding or surface similarity. [CLAIM-023](CLAIM-023.md) says the contribution is "not that
translation is pragmatic or that speech acts change contexts, but a specified,
testable account of which communicative distinctions a translation
preserves". It names Gutt (LIT-784, unread), and it records that the
translation-studies works the exchange recommended (Nida, House, Reiss and
Vermeer, Nord) are absent from the manuscript and from the record. One is
held through quotation: Nida's principle, as CASE-056 records it, is that "the
relationship between receptor and message should be substantially the same as
that which existed between the original receptors and the message". That puts
fidelity in the effect on the receiver, not in the text, so a
functional-equivalence account predicts that ratings of stance and footing
beat embedding similarity on renderings built to dissociate the two. A pass is
what it predicts, and the test cannot tell the thesis from the position
[CLAIM-023](CLAIM-023.md) disclaims as not new. Whether Gutt's interpretive resemblance
predicts the same is CLAIM-tmphcevw's question, which waits on a reading of Gutt.

The same holds for the rival [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) itself names. The manuscript's
Abstract, as [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md) quotes it, sets the account against "a privileged
global semantic object". A global object enriched with force, a proposition
together with an illocutionary type, is not embedding similarity, so it
passes the test too.

The record does state a test that would discriminate. [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md)'s "Where
it stands" says what would settle its question: "a latent act variable that
adds nothing once the relational observables are measured". That test is in
no defeat condition.

The line's distinctive machinery has defeat conditions of its own: global
compatibility ([CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md)), the Blackwell order ([CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md)), intertwining
([CLAIM-117](CLAIM-117.md)) and the composition bounds ([CLAIM-056](CLAIM-056.md)). The thesis-level defeat
conditions depend on none of them. If all four failed, [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) and
[CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) would still survive their own defeat conditions, provided judged
pragmatic observables beat text similarity.

The weakness was seen once before, for an earlier thesis. [CLAIM-083](CLAIM-083.md)
(Superseded) says the manuscript's Case I criterion "is weighed against
embedding similarity, not against static pragmatic similarity". [CASE-005](../cases.d/CASE-005.md)'s
Experiment 2 had the stronger comparison, choosing translations "by maximum
semantic similarity, by maximum static pragmatic similarity and by maximum
trajectory similarity", and [CASE-005](../cases.d/CASE-005.md) records that "the three-way selection
design is gone by A52". Neither entry says it of the central theses'
defeat conditions.

Granted, because each step is read off the texts of the defeat conditions
and of [CLAIM-023](CLAIM-023.md).

## What it does not say

- It does not say the theses are false, or that a functionalist rival is
  right.
- It does not say that beating embedding similarity is worthless. It is the
  right first test, and failing it would defeat the theses.
- It does not say how to repair the defeat conditions; that is the owner's.
  The obvious repair adds a rival that discriminates to each `defeated_if`:
  static pragmatic similarity without the transport structure, human-rated
  propositional sameness, or [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md)'s latent act variable.

## Note of 2026-10-28: split under [ADR-061](../decisions.d/ADR-061.md)

The old title ended in a verdict on support: "surviving them would not
tell the thesis from the functional and relevance-theoretic accounts".
That verdict is now [ARG-026](../arguments.d/ARG-026.md), from the central defeat test
([CASE-184](../cases.d/CASE-184.md)) to [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md), [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md), [CLAIM-005](CLAIM-005.md) and [CLAIM-078](CLAIM-078.md), form
evidence, Rejected, as its answer to "does it tell the hypothesis apart
from its rivals?". This claim keeps what the rival accounts predict,
which is about the subject matter, and undercuts that argument. [CLAIM-232](CLAIM-232.md)
no longer rests on it; the two complement each other. The text above is
unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-30: narrowed to the rivals the record holds

The nineteenth status review narrowed the title (ADR-057). Version 2 said
"every rival account the record names (functional equivalence, Gutt's
interpretive resemblance, speech-act pragmatics)" predicts a pass. Gutt is
named and unread (LIT-784, Unavailable), so what his account predicts moves to
CLAIM-tmphcevw, Proposed. Functional equivalence stays, held through Nida's
principle as CASE-056 quotes it, and speech-act pragmatics stays as the
proposition-with-force rival above. The objection to ARG-026 needs one rival
that passes the test, and two remain.
