# LIT-1361: Phenomenal structure: What is it and what is it for?

Isaac, A. M. C. (2025). Phenomenal structure: What is it and what is it for? *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 6. doi:10.33735/phimisci.2025.11773.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpn1hle.*

- **A stance, not a thesis.** "structuralism as developed here is not a
  theoretical assertation or result, but rather a stance" (§1), judged by
  the trajectory of its models.
- **Three layers.** Quality spaces (geometry), constancies (invariance
  under transformations, "group-theoretic") and forces of organization,
  valence and affordance (vector fields). The link of each to its
  mathematics is "conceptual", not empirical (§2).
- **Geometry is not enough.** "The geometrical structure of phenomenal
  experience by itself is not enough to generate meaning" (§3). Constancy
  turns flux into "a stable representation of an objective world", after
  Cassirer, and the varying and invariant levels of colour experience are
  present together.
- **Internal transformations.** Perceived shape in depth matches no
  linear projection, and apparent rotation needs no physical rotation, so
  constancies are invariants of transformations within experience
  (THEORY-tmpt62hk).
- **Isomorphism as a regulative ideal.** Second-order, "functional"
  isomorphisms among experience, world and brain, loosely meant; the
  failures of CIELAB, DKL and loudness models drive de-idealization (§4).
- **Convergence as evidence.** Models from different behavioural routes
  that agree are taken to target experience, the "least common
  denominator"; convergence is "never perfect" (§5,
  THEORY-tmpeq5py).
- **Closing puzzle.** Averaging over subjects makes the target a
  "standard observer" (§6).

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange cited this paper as a structuralist programme built around geometrical, group-theoretic and dynamical structure. Its group-theoretic structure, which separates subject from object, is one candidate for what a perspective adds to an interpretation.
