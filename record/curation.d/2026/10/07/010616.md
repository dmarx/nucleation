The owner's essay "The Organization of Will" cites four works the record
did not hold. They entered because the essay cites them, and because the
record of the essay's argument, being built alongside it, needs something
to point at when a step of the argument leans on one of them. That is the
whole reason. None of them was read, and none was filed for its merit.

- Fraenkel, *The Dual State* (1941), [LIT-805](../../record/literature.d/LIT-805.md): the normative and the
  prerogative state, and authority over whether the rules apply.
- Ashby, *Design for a Brain* (1952; the essay cites the 1960 edition),
  [LIT-787](../../record/literature.d/LIT-787.md): ultrastability, regulation against alteration of the
  parameters of regulation.
- Canguilhem, *On the Normal and the Pathological* (1943; English 1978,
  reissued by Zone Books in 1989), [LIT-817](../../record/literature.d/LIT-817.md): health as a capacity to
  change norms. The essay uses it interpretively.
- Craver, *Explaining the Brain* (2007), [LIT-832](../../record/literature.d/LIT-832.md): constitutive against
  causal relevance, and mechanistic explanation.

## How deeply

Registered only. Each entry is `Deferred` and says in its body that it was
written from secondary description: Crossref's records and chapter lists,
the publisher's abstract (Fraenkel), library catalogues (Canguilhem) and
reviews (Craver). No NOTE or THEORY was filed.

## Choices worth knowing

- **Dates.** `published:` is the first appearance, as in [LIT-738](../../record/literature.d/LIT-738.md): 1941
  for Fraenkel (the English edition is the first publication), 1952 for
  Ashby, 1943 for Canguilhem's thesis. None of these has a known day, so
  each is the first of January. Craver's is Crossref's 2007-06-07.
- **DOIs are of later editions** where the first has none: Oxford's 2017
  reissue of Fraenkel, Springer's digitization of Ashby's 1960 edition,
  and Reidel's 1978 translation of Canguilhem. Canguilhem's title follows
  that DOI (*On the Normal and the Pathological*), not Zone's.
- **Tags.** Ashby has no cybernetics word in the vocabulary; he is filed
  under complex-systems, as Beer's Viable System Model works are, with
  behavioral-integration for the feedback-control reading of adaptation.
  Canguilhem is filed under philosophy-of-biology, since his question is
  what biological norms are; psychopathology-and-treatment was not used,
  because the book is about disease in general, not mental disorder.

## Boundary

None is held in the Anthology of the SOTA, and no anthology topic could
hold them: they are political and legal theory, cybernetics, philosophy of
medicine and philosophy of neuroscience. A grep of the anthology's record
for the authors, titles and DOIs found no entry.
