# NOTE-tmpwskha: Mashour, Roelfsema, Changeux & Dehaene — Conscious Processing and the Global Neuronal Workspace Hypothesis

## Contribution

A twenty-year retrospective on the global neuronal workspace (GNW) by two of
its originators (Changeux and Dehaene, 1998) and two collaborators. It adds no
new data. What it adds is a consolidated statement of the theory's tenets
("late ignition, metastable sustained activity, long-distance cortical
projections, top-down mobilization", Conclusions), a mapping of signal
detection theory onto ignition, an extension of the theory from content to
level of consciousness (anaesthesia, disorders of consciousness, sleep), a
proposal relating it to attention and working memory, and a comparison table
against IIT, higher-order and recurrent processing theories.

## Key insight

The difference between seeing and not seeing a stimulus is not a difference in
early sensory processing but a late, non-linear event. A stimulus that pushes
enough activity into a densely interconnected core of long-range neurons trips
a threshold, after which reciprocal excitation sustains and amplifies it and
makes it available to every specialised processor at once. Below the
threshold the same stimulus leaves a fading wave. That threshold is the
decision criterion of signal detection theory, the ignition is what the P3 and
late prefrontal firing measure, and anything that degrades the core's long
connections (anaesthetics, lesions, sleep) raises the threshold or abolishes
ignition.

## Assumptions

- **The explanandum is access.** "Unless otherwise specified, the term
  'consciousness' in this review will be replaced by conscious access"
  (Introduction), following Dehaene and Naccache's claim that global
  availability "is what we subjectively experience as a conscious state".
  Access can occur without overt report.
- **A two-space architecture.** Specialised modular processors, plus GNW
  neurons with long-range reciprocal axons that can "selectively mobilize or
  suppress" processors (Central Tenets). Large layer II/III pyramidal cells
  are central; layer V contributes.
- **Ignition is exclusive.** A subset of workspace neurons codes the current
  content and the rest are inhibited, so one content at a time occupies the
  workspace.
- **Prefrontal cortex is a hub, not a seat.** The theory is "not a
  localizationist theory"; the prefrontal cortex matters because of the
  density of broadcasting neurons, and the involvement of primary sensory
  areas is "task and stimulus dependent" (The Role of Recurrent Activity).
- **Spontaneous activity.** Simulations show stochastic ignitions at rest,
  which the authors match to the fluctuating functional connectivity of the
  awake brain.

## Key results

- **Simulations (Simulations of the Global Workspace).** Spiking networks with
  AMPA feedforward and NMDA feedback produce either a self-amplifying cascade
  ("ignition", with gamma synchrony) or a decaying wave for the same input
  (Figure 2A). Joglekar et al. (2018), built independently on macaque tracer
  connectivity, also produce late, sudden ignition if feedforward input is
  balanced by local inhibition and is strong enough to reach prefrontal
  cortex.
- **Late divergence (Empirical Evidence).** Seen and unseen trials diverge
  around 200–300 ms, generalising across vision, audition and touch (Noel et
  al. 2018; Sanchez et al. 2019). The first ~200 ms can be preserved on unseen
  trials. A retro-cue up to 900 ms after a stimulus can make it seen.
- **The P3 controversy, conceded.** A visual awareness negativity (~260 ms)
  remains when stimuli are task-irrelevant but reported seen, without a P3
  (Pitts et al.). Whether such stimuli are "truly seen as opposed to being
  merely potentially visible but unattended" is called unresolved.
- **Prefrontal ignition and SDT (van Vugt et al. 2018; Figure 2B–C).** In
  monkeys reporting weak stimuli, misses can drive V1 and V4 strongly; hits
  and false alarms show late sustained prefrontal firing, misses only a
  decaying transient. The SDT criterion is the ignition threshold;
  sensitivity is propagation efficiency; pre-stimulus firing predicts false
  alarms.
- **Causal evidence for top-down amplification (Recurrent Activity).** In
  mice, optogenetically silencing feedback from secondary motor to
  somatosensory cortex attenuates late somatosensory amplification and
  prevents perception (Manita et al. 2015). NMDA receptors dominate feedback
  (Self et al. 2012).
- **Working memory (Conscious Representations and Working Memory).** The
  attended working-memory item is conscious and uses the GNW; items outside
  the focus are held by weaker local firing or "activity-silent" synaptic
  changes and are unconscious. Prediction: only active (conscious) states can
  be transformed, e.g. rotated. Trübutschek et al. (2019) are cited in
  support.
- **Anaesthesia (General Anesthesia; Figure 5).** Propofol, sevoflurane and
  ketamine all disconnect prefrontal from posterior parietal cortex in human
  fMRI, preferentially affect frontal feedback, and collapse the diversity of
  functional connectivity across GNW nodes onto the anatomical scaffold in
  monkeys. Long-latency evoked potentials go first. Central thalamic
  stimulation reverses anaesthesia in monkeys; cholinergic stimulation of
  medial prefrontal (not parietal) cortex restores wakefulness in
  sevoflurane-anaesthetised rats.
- **Disorders of consciousness and sleep.** Weighted symbolic mutual
  information indexes level of consciousness in 181 EEG recordings (Sitt et
  al. 2014). Stage 1–2 sleep preserves the first ~200 ms and loses ignition
  and the P3 (Strauss et al. 2015). REM dreaming involves frontal gamma, and
  lucid dreaming more.
- **Comparison (Table 1).** GNW: ignition of a brain-scale network, PFC a
  hub contributing to ignition, measured by late (~300 ms) global ignition.
  IIT: posterior hot zone, PFC "not essential for experience itself".
  HOT: second-order representation, anterior PFC essential. RPT: feedback in
  sensory pathways, PFC not essential. "It is difficult to adjudicate
  experimentally among these four related theories."

## Concepts

- **conscious access**: information, unlike unconscious information,
  accessible to many processors (working memory, report, motor behaviour);
  the review's explanandum.
- **ignition**: "the sudden, coherent, and exclusive activation of a subset of
  workspace neurons coding for the current conscious content, with the
  remainder of the workspace neurons being inhibited".
- **GNW neurons**: widely distributed excitatory neurons with long-range
  reciprocal axons, able to receive from and broadcast to any processor.
- **level vs content**: overall state (awake, asleep, comatose) versus what is
  experienced; the review applies the GNW to both.
- **activity-silent working memory**: items held in short-term synaptic
  changes rather than firing; on this account unconscious.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmpwyfwm](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwyfwm.md).** The Vishne et al. result it states (prefrontal content
  only at onset, a stable ventral temporal code for the duration) was the
  GNW's registered prediction, so it does not count against the GNW. On this
  review's account sustained prefrontal activity accompanies maintenance for
  a later response or manipulation; a passively viewed image needs neither.
  The review supports the theory's "frontoparietal content confined to
  onset" clause as expected by the GNW, not as a challenge to it.
- **[THEORY-tmprpyvx](../theory.d/THEORY-tmprpyvx.md).** The review places the workspace in long-range,
  reciprocally connected hubs and makes no claim about lamination: its
  emphasis on layer II/III and V pyramidal cells is about mammalian cortex.
  A nuclear avian pallium with an NCL that ignites on "seen" reports is
  what a GNW reading of birds would predict. The review offers no evidence
  on birds.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md).** It states the workspace as a computational theory and
  ends by hoping it can be implemented in a device, which is the
  functionalist premise that theory says indicator approaches presuppose.
- **Filed as [THEORY-tmpiqxi6](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpiqxi6.md).** Conscious access is global availability
  produced by threshold ignition in a long-range hub network. It rivals

## Limitations

- **A proponents' review.** Evidence against the theory is acknowledged
  (VAN, the anaesthetised brain's dynamics, correlational lesion data) but
  not weighed systematically.
- **Access, by stipulation.** The review does not argue that access is all
  there is to consciousness; it replaces the term. A reader who holds that
  phenomenality can outrun access will find no argument against that here,
  only the claim that it is untested.
- **The prefrontal evidence is mostly correlational**, as the authors say;
  the causal results are in rodent somatosensation and in anaesthesia
  reversal, which bears on level rather than content.
- **No-report confounds.** The monkey rivalry data are presented as free of
  report and working-memory confounds; the review does not consider
  post-perceptual cognition without report.

## Open questions

- Does the P3 or the earlier VAN track awareness? A no-post-perceptual-
  cognition paradigm with intracranial prefrontal recording would decide
  between the review's reading and Block's.
- Is ignition necessary for experience, or only for maintenance and report?
  The COGITATE adversarial collaboration, which the review announces, was
  designed to test this against IIT.
- Self-consciousness: the review proposes error awareness (a late Pe
  ignition after a non-conscious ERN) as a "minor extension" of the GNW; it
  gives no account of how meta-representations are built.
