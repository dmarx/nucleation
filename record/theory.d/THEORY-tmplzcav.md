# THEORY-tmplzcav: Adult Tsimane'-Spanish bilinguals use Tsimane' colour terms in narrower, less overlapping extensions than Tsimane' monolinguals, and split the shared green-blue term pair into green and blue, without borrowing Spanish terms

## Source

Saima Malik-Moraleda, Kyle Mahowald, Bevil R. Conway and Edward Gibson
(2023), [LIT-tmpyky5z](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyky5z.md), *Psychological Science* 34(12), 1350–1362; read in
[NOTE-tmpkl6ez](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpkl6ez.md) from the PubMed Central copy, supplement unread.

## What was actually shown

Naming, selection and focal-choice tasks on 84 Munsell chips, with 71
Tsimane' monolinguals, 30 Spanish monolinguals and 30 Tsimane'-Spanish
bilinguals tested in both languages. Average surprisal falls from
Tsimane' monolinguals (5.16) to bilinguals in Tsimane' (4.42), bilinguals
in Spanish (4.02) and Spanish monolinguals (3.70), each step significant
by permutation test. The bilinguals' Tsimane' has two more majority terms,
shandyes (green) and yụshñus (blue), which monolinguals select for the
same chips; each of the six modal terms covers fewer chips for bilinguals,
and all but yụshñus and jäinäs differ in distribution between the
Tsimane'-speaking groups; neighbouring pairs overlap less (green-blue KL
0.10 against 1.18; yellow-brown 0.45 against 1.39; red-purple 1.52 against
4.58). Purple did not differ between any groups. No Spanish colour term
appears in the bilinguals' Tsimane', and Spanish's light-blue term has no
Tsimane' counterpart for them. The result could have come out otherwise:
the bilinguals' Tsimane' could have matched the monolinguals', or taken in
Spanish words.

## What this does not say

- **Not that learning Spanish caused it.** The bilinguals lived in San
  Borja, more industrialised than the monolinguals' communities, and the
  authors allow that "it might be the interactions in the culture".
- **Not that a term was added to Tsimane'.** The words are the same; the
  contrasts drawn with them multiply.
- **Not that the boundaries are Spanish's.** Bilinguals' Tsimane' falls
  between the two monolingual groups on every pair reported.
- **Not about perception.** The authors say the study concerns naming.
- **Not a test of value by contrast.** No terms outside the colour field
  were measured, so a general sharpening of naming is not excluded; see
  [ARG-tmpcpvnb](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpcpvnb.md) for what it gives [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md).
