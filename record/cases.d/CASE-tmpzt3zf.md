# CASE-tmpzt3zf: Carol and Dan: the partner who strikes more often is the one who is dominated

## The case

A3 §1, "Relationship B: One person controls the other's life":

> Carol and Dan also have physical fights. Perhaps Carol has even struck
> Dan more times than Dan has struck Carol.
>
> But Dan controls their finances, monitors Carol's messages, threatens to
> destroy her reputation if she leaves, isolates her from friends, and
> punishes her whenever she challenges him.
>
> Carol sometimes lashes out violently. Yet she lives in continual fear of
> what Dan will do next, while Dan can generally behave however he likes.

A3 §5 states it as "an extreme illustration" (its Person A is Carol's
position, its Person B Dan's):

| Question | Person A | Person B |
|---|---|---|
| Number of times they struck their partner | 4 | 2 |
| Threatened to kill their partner | No | Yes |
| Controls their partner's finances | No | Yes |
| Prevents their partner seeing friends | No | Yes |
| Afraid of their partner | Yes | No |

"If you only counted strikes, A would appear more violent. If you examined
the full pattern, B might emerge as the primary coercive aggressor."

A3 §1 sets beside it Alice and Bob, who fight destructively, both
violently, with neither controlling the other's money, friends or
movements: violence without domination.

## What it can show

That a tally of harmful acts and the allocation of power over the
relationship can point in opposite directions, so that answering one does
not answer the other ([CLAIM-tmpkivlz](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpkivlz.md)). Carol's strikes keep their
verdict: A3 §4, "Experiencing abuse does not give someone unlimited moral
permission to hurt their partner." What Dan holds is a capacity, to set
the terms on which Carol can spend, see friends, disagree and leave, which
is domination in [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)'s sense whether or not he uses it on a given
day.

## What it cannot show

Being fictional, nothing about how often relationships take this shape,
which is the subject of Michael P. Johnson's typology as A relays it, with
A's own caveat that a 2023 review found "mixed support for established
typologies". Nor how domination is to be established in a real case: A3
§6, "human motives aren't always clear" and "power isn't
one-dimensional". It names no real couple; the record files no case of the
dispute the exchange began with.
