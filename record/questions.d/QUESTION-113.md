# QUESTION-113: When a system names a category, which criterion is it meeting: that the data supports the distinction, that a deadline forces it, or that it survives refinement and compression, and do the three ever pick the same categories?

## Why it is a question

**Three criteria.**

1. **Evidence** ([CLAIM-154](../claims.d/CLAIM-154.md)). Keep a distinction when d'² exceeds the
   Occam cost. It is about the data and grows with copies.
2. **Retrieval** ([CLAIM-307](../claims.d/CLAIM-307.md)). Build a distinction when finding an item in time
   requires it. It is about tasks and deadlines and falls with copies.
3. **Persistence.** The owner's LEGO monograph, Part III, worked example
   "Archetype Persistence via Rank, Blowup, Compression". Its
   co-occurrence matrix over four parts (wing, engine, window, door) is
   M = ½·11ᵀ + 0.3·uuᵀ + 0.1·ssᵀ. Here u = (1, 1, −1, −1) separates
   spaceship from castle and s = (1, −1, 0, 0) splits wings from engines.
   Its spectrum is {2, 1.2, 0.2, 0}, checked here. Pruning modes below
   0.25 drops the wing–engine split and keeps the spaceship–castle axis,
   which "persists" through refinement and compression. [MOM-CLM-138](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-138.md)
   rejected the nominal compatibilism built on this. Calling archetypes
   "structurally forced" is the realist commitment under another name.
   It kept the observation: "Some descriptors *are* far more stable than
   others under refinement ... and that stability is measurable."
   [CLAIM-164](../claims.d/CLAIM-164.md) says what such a mode picks out: the gap picks the object.

**How they might relate.** Persistence looks like evidence summed over
scales: a mode with a wide gap is one the data supports at every
resolution tried. If so, criteria 1 and 3 are one criterion and only
retrieval is separate. But persistence is relative to the refinements
tried and the threshold chosen (0.25 in the example), which evidence is
not. And a persistent category can still fail retrieval, if its members
are similar to one another and the one wanted is rare.

## The owner's objection, in the source

The owner pressed the monograph's drafting on this point twice, in its
Part III ("Eigenmodes, Archetypes, Platonics, Stable Cores"). The first
objection is not quoted there, but the draft's reply concedes it: "acts
like an object" risks triviality, since any valid descriptor persists and
is shareable. The second is quoted: "our definition of "object like"
needs to apply to objects as well." A
criterion for when a description earns a name has to separate
descriptions from one another and to apply to particulars too. Evidence
and retrieval (criteria 1 and 2) already apply to both: a particular brick
is worth a bin of its own for the same reasons a kind is. Whether
persistence does is part of this question. [CASE-097](../cases.d/CASE-097.md) shows that, stated
for eigenvectors, persistence fails even in the monograph's own example.

## What would answer it

A derivation, or a counterexample, relating a mode's persistence across a
family of refinements to its Bayes factor at each. And for retrieval, a
case where a category persistent in the data is split for speed, or one
retrieval forces is pruned by both other criteria.
