# THEORY-2026: Pinwheel density is π per hypercolumn

## The account

The orientation preference map is the argument of a complex field z(x); pinwheels are its zeros, of charge ±1/2. A Swift-Hohenberg model of z with a nonlocal interaction of range σ has, when that interaction dominates, stable aperiodic solutions built from n ≈ 2πσ/Λ plane waves on the critical circle. For large n the field is Gaussian and the expected number of zeros per Λ² is π; the variance across realizations goes to zero. Numerically, intermediate n gives densities between 2.9 and 3.2. In rival models pinwheels annihilate over time or crystallize, so a stable density near π is a signature of long-range-dominated self-organization.

## What it does not show

- Any measurement: whether cortex has density near π is the 2010 paper's question, unread here.
- That pinwheels do anything: they are treated as countable features of the pattern.
- Behaviour far from threshold.
