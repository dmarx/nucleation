# THEORY-1335: In Juarrero's constraint account, context-independent constraints take conditions away from equiprobability and context-dependent ones away from independence (after Gatlin); enabling constraints couple separate processes into a coherent dynamic whose constitutive constraints then act top down as second-order governing constraints that confine its components to a subregion of state space; a constraint is defined as what raises or lowers barriers to energy flow without transferring kinetic energy, carries no time-scale condition, and the governing regime is held not to be other than the components

## Source

Juarrero (2023), [LIT-1995](../literature.d/LIT-1995.md), chapters 2, 3 and 6, pp. 21–86; read in
[NOTE-1532](../notes.d/NOTE-1532.md) from the MIT Press open-access PDF.

## What was actually shown

What is shown is the account's content, and the reading checked each
element against the text.

**The definition.** "Constraints are entities, processes, events, relations,
or conditions that raise or lower barriers to energy flow without directly
transferring kinetic energy" (p. 40). Nothing in it is relative to a time
scale.

**The two kinds.** Context-independent constraints "take or keep conditions
away from equiprobability"; context-dependent constraints "take conditions
away from independence (Gatlin 1972)", so that processes become "mutually
conditional on one another and on the history and context in which they
formed", "measured with conditional probability" (p. 67).

**The bottom-up step.** Enabling constraints (credited to Pattee 1973,
Salthe 1985 and Juarrero 1999) "irreversibly link and couple previously
separate ... entities at the same scale as the constraints" and "drive
parts-to-whole phase transitions to emergent coordination patterns" (p. 71).

**The top-down step.** The constitutive constraints of the result "do double
duty as governing constraints" (p. 79), "second-order context-dependent
constraints (Juarrero 1999)" (p. 81), acting "often as negative feedback
loops" (p. 79) to "restrict the behavior of the system and of its
components or stages to a subregion of the system's overall possibility
space" (p. 80). This is offered as top-down causation that, because it
transfers no energy, avoids overdetermination and respects closure (pp. 25,
46).

**The identity condition.** Governing constraints "are not spatiotemporally
other than the components and constraints from which they were formed and
which they control" (p. 81; again pp. 84, 95).

The account could have been stated otherwise: with a time-scale condition
on constraints, as Montévil and Mossio's has ([LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)), or with the
governing regime as a distinct entity. It is not.

## What this does not say

- **That constraints transfer no energy.** That is asserted with examples
  (the timing of a swing's kick, p. 46; vasculature, p. 82), not argued, and
  a governing constraint such as a vessel is maintained by dissipating
  processes.
- **That the account is true or explanatory.** The author calls the book
  "speculative metaphysics" (p. 32).
- **That "context-dependent constraint" is [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s constraint.** It is a
  conditional dependence; [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s is conserved at the time scale of the
  process it acts on. The book adopts [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s closure only in chapter 7
  (pp. 93–94).
- **That governing constraints give an agent control in the libertarian
  sense.** The book accepts "indetermination and uncertainty" as the price
  of creativity (p. 235) and gives no account of control over them; Froese
  ([NOTE-1521](../notes.d/NOTE-1521.md)) cites the 1999 book among accounts that do not explain
  whose efficacy a top-down constraint is.
