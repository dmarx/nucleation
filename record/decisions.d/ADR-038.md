# ADR-038: A concept invoked as the record defines it is cited by its TERM code where it is used; a word used in another's sense is left unlinked

## Context

[ADR-032](ADR-032.md) made TERM the record's concept construction. A changed definition
becomes a new version or a superseding TERM, and claims point at the
definitions they are stated in through `uses:`. On 2026-10-11 the concept
pass found the day's load-bearing words ("self", "agent", "combined form")
used across some twenty claims with almost no TERM links. The sweep that
followed left the older essay claims' text alone, because their "agent" is
the paper's term of art. The owner answered with the rule this decision
records.

## Decision

- **Cite at the point of use.** When an entry's body invokes a concept as a
  TERM defines it, the bare TERM code follows its first use in that body.
  `luria link --fix` spells the target.
- **Leave other senses unlinked.** A word used in another sense stays bare:
  a reading's term, the will paper's supposed "agent" ([CLAIM-080](../claims.d/CLAIM-080.md)), or an
  ordinary-language use. The absence of a code is then information, not an
  omission. Where the difference matters, the TERM's "what it is not"
  section says how the other sense relates. [TERM-tmp1913d](../terms.d/TERM-tmp1913d.md) does this for
  "agent".
- **`uses:` is narrower than citation.** A claim lists in `uses:` only the
  TERMs it is *stated in*. An inline citation that discusses a term, as its
  history or as a contrast, is a citation and not a use. The sweep's check
  found four such cases and kept them out of `uses:` ([CLAIM-004](../claims.d/CLAIM-004.md),
  [CLAIM-055](../claims.d/CLAIM-055.md), [CLAIM-139](../claims.d/CLAIM-139.md), [CLAIM-142](../claims.d/CLAIM-142.md)).
- **Quotations are never altered.** A code for a term that occurs inside a
  quotation goes outside it, or not at all.
- **Terms with several versions are linked by hand.** "Fidelity" (eight
  Active versions), "frame" (five) and "communicative object" (two) need a
  judgement about which version a passage means. They are linked when an
  entry is written or revised, not by sweep.
- **Adding a citation is not a revision.** An inline TERM code changes no
  wording, like the targets `luria link --fix` writes, so it takes no
  version bump. Changing the wording to state a sense does take one, as in
  [CLAIM-tmpmf12z](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpmf12z.md) and [CLAIM-tmptsdgt](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptsdgt.md).

## Alternatives

- **`uses:` only.** The relation exists, but a reader of the prose cannot
  see which occurrence of a word is the defined one.
- **Link every occurrence.** That is noise. The first use per entry fixes
  the sense for the rest.
- **Rewrite older claims into the new vocabulary.** That changes what they
  said. Citing where they already invoke a defined concept does not.

## Consequences

- **The first sweep** (2026-10-11) used a script that matches distinctive
  TERM phrases outside quotations, headings, comments and tables. It
  proposed 26 sites. One was a false positive, the *line* name
  "will-organization", and was excluded. The other 25 were read and
  linked. Thirteen `uses:` entries followed, after four proposed ones were
  refused on reading.
- **The norm is prose until it is a mechanism** ([DP-3](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-3)). The check belongs in
  luria as a lint report, "a TERM's title phrase used in an entry without
  its code", with an acknowledgement directive for deliberate other-sense
  uses. That is a candidate upstream issue, not filed. Until then a sweep
  is re-run by hand when TERMs are added.
