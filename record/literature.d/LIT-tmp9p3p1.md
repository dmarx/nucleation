# LIT-tmp9p3p1: The Dual State: A Contribution to the Theory of Dictatorship

Ernst Fraenkel (1941), *The Dual State: A Contribution to the Theory of
Dictatorship*, trans. E. A. Shils, New York: Oxford University Press;
reissued by Oxford University Press in 2017 with an introduction by Jens
Meierhenrich — DOI-10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198716204.001.0001

## Key takeaways

*Registered from secondary description, not read.* What follows is what
the publisher's abstract for the 2017 reissue and the chapter metadata in
Crossref say.

- **Two states in one.** The abstract states the thesis: in National
  Socialist Germany a *normative state*, "which protected the legal order
  as expressed in legislation, decisions of the courts, and decisions of
  administrative bodies", coexisted with a *prerogative state*, "governed
  by the ruling party, and unrestrained by legal guarantees".
- **Read through the courts.** The relation and conflict between the two
  is analysed through German court decisions and the development of
  judicial practice. Chapter titles in the reissue include "The Prerogative
  State", "The Limits of the Prerogative State" and "The Normative State".
- **Three parts.** The legal order; the effect of the parallel structures
  on politics and society; and the relation between the dual state and
  German capitalism, asking whether the dual state came out of a crisis of
  capitalism.
- **Written from inside.** The abstract calls it the only comprehensive
  analysis of National Socialism written from within Hitler's Germany.

## Standing in the record
