# NOTE-tmp3za6r: Atari et al. 2023, the MFQ-2

## Contribution

- A theoretical revision of MFT: Fairness is split into Equality and
  Proportionality, taken from Rai and Fiske's relationship regulation (pp.
  6–8).
- A new 36-item instrument developed from the start in several cultures,
  with a published item list and scoring key (Appendix).
- Evidence that the higher-order structure of the foundations varies by
  population (Study 2 networks), and that the MFQ-2 predicts related
  constructs better than the MFQ-1 (Study 3).

## Key insight

The five-foundation model was measured almost entirely in English on
WEIRD internet samples, and the MFQ-1 is not invariant across cultures.
Building the instrument in many populations at once, with Fairness split,
yields scales that compare across nations for five of six foundations, and
shows that the familiar two-cluster structure of moral values is a WEIRD
pattern, not a universal one.

## Assumptions

- **Six foundations**, defined in Table 2 (pp. 11–12). Care: avoiding
  emotional and physical damage to another individual. Equality: equal
  treatment and equal outcome. Proportionality: reward in proportion to
  merit or contribution. Loyalty: cooperating with ingroups and competing
  with outgroups. Authority: deference to legitimate authorities and
  defence of traditions. Purity: avoiding bodily and spiritual
  contamination and degradation.
- **Proportionality = equity.** The paper uses "proportionality" for equity
  because "equity" now means equality of outcomes in some usage (fn. 3).
- **Samples.** Online panels (CloudResearch, Qualtrics), stratified by age,
  gender and, where possible, ideology; educated enough to complete an
  online survey (p. 65).
- **WEIRDness** is Muthukrishna et al.'s cultural distance from the U.S.

## Key results

- **Development (Studies 1a–1c, N = 3,360; U.S., India, Iran, Ecuador,
  China).** 116 items cut to 90, 71 and then 50; Iran's sample stopped at
  100 of a planned 200 because sanctions prevented paying participants (fn.
  4).
- **Structure (Study 2, N = 3,902, 19 nations, about 205 each, May 2021).**
  ESEM on 36 items: CFI .979, RMSEA .024. MGCFA across nations: CFI .896,
  SRMR .070, taken as configural invariance. ω ranges .73–.95; Purity is
  lowest (.73 in Japan).
- **Invariance (Table 6, p. 30).** Alignment non-invariance of intercepts:
  Care 5.3%, Equality 21.9%, Proportionality 11.4%, Loyalty 24.6%,
  Authority 16.7%, Purity 39.5%. Only Purity exceeds the 25% threshold;
  the "temple" item is the most non-invariant.
- **Equality and Proportionality.** Mean r = .21 (SD .13) across nations,
  from .47 (UAE) to −.04 (Belgium); more orthogonal in WEIRDer nations.
- **Cultural distance (p. 32).** Distance from the U.S. correlates with
  all six; most strongly with Purity (r = .43), then Loyalty (.25).
- **Gender.** Women higher on Equality and Purity; men higher on
  Proportionality, Loyalty and Authority; no significant Care difference.
- **Religion.** Purity's association with religiosity is the largest (B =
  .75); cross-religion variation exceeds cross-nation variation.
- **Ideology.** Care and Equality go with the left; Proportionality,
  Loyalty, Authority and Purity with the right.
- **Networks (pp. 38–41).** In the U.S., Care–Fairness and the binding
  three form separate clusters. Across the 19 nations, exploratory graph
  analysis finds one dimension in most and two in three (Ireland, New
  Zealand, Peru). Authority is the most central node in 14 of 19.
- **Validity (Study 3, N = 1,410).** 17 of 18 preregistered correlations
  with criterion scales are supported (the miss: Authority with left-wing
  authoritarianism). Mean adjusted R² over 30 outcomes: MFQ-2 .37, MFQ-1
  .30; adding the MFQ-2 to the MFQ-1 adds 13.7% on average.

## Concepts

- **MFQ-2** — 36 statements rated 1 (does not describe me at all) to 5
  (describes me extremely well); the MFQ-1's "Relevance" format is dropped
  (p. 13).
- **nomological network** — the pattern of relations among foundations and
  with other constructs, estimated here as a psychometric network.
- **alignment method** — multi-group factor analysis that estimates group
  means while allowing a few non-invariant parameters; 25% non-invariance
  is the accepted ceiling.
- **WEIRD cultural distance** — Muthukrishna et al.'s distance of a
  population from the U.S. on cultural values.

## Connections

- **MFT.** Extends Graham et al. 2013 ([LIT-tmpa221k](../literature.d/LIT-tmpa221k.md)), reversing its claim
  that there is no Equality foundation (p. 114 of that chapter).
- **Relationship regulation.** The Equality–Proportionality split and
  their definitions are Rai and Fiske's ([LIT-tmpiuf8w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpiuf8w.md)), cited throughout
  pp. 6–8 and 60.
- **Morality-as-cooperation.**
  - [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) ([NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md)) is named on p. 64 as a rival typology that "left out
    Purity".
  - [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s footnote 1 said the MFQ has no reciprocity items. The MFQ-2
    defines Equality as including "balanced reciprocity" (p. 60), but none
    of its six Equality items is about reciprocating; the nearest is a
    Proportionality item about cheaters being punished.
  - The MAC-Q, filed in this batch as [LIT-tmp0moou](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0moou.md), found MFQ-1 purity
    items rated as relevant as loyalty and authority items in UK samples.
    The MFQ-2 adds that Purity endorsement is higher in less-WEIRD
    populations.
- **Monism.** Baumard et al. ([LIT-tmpof37t](../literature.d/LIT-tmpof37t.md), "mutualistic fairness") and
  Gray and Schein ([LIT-tmp0aaqg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0aaqg.md), "all morality is harm") are named as
  monist theories put forward in WEIRD populations by WEIRD researchers
  (p. 55).
- **Purity's definition.** The paper drops the MFQ-1 item that mentioned
  God because it proxied religiosity, and reports that Purity still forms
  one factor across sexual, linguistic and "natural medicine" content (p.
  58). [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md) ([NOTE-132](NOTE-132.md)) argues the puritanical part of purity is
  cooperative; the MFQ-2 does not separate that part from contamination.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md), C7.** This paper's six do not contain MAC's seven, nor the
  reverse. MAC has bravery and property, absent here; the MFQ-2 has Purity,
  Care for any sufferer, and Equality as an outcome preference, absent
  from MAC. The paper's charge that MAC omitted Purity is the substantive
  disagreement, and it is the one MAC's own questionnaire concedes (as
  reported in [LIT-tmp0moou](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0moou.md)).
- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md)'s open question.** The MFQ-2 measures endorsement of purity,
  not moralization coded from ethnography. It shows purity is endorsed
  more outside WEIRD populations, but its Purity means are the ones the
  authors say not to compare across groups without caution.

## Limitations

- Online, literate, mostly urban samples; no small-scale societies (p.
  65).
- About 200 per nation; 19 nations for country-level correlations, with
  spatial dependence handled by Conley standard errors but not formally
  tested (p. 65).
- Purity fails intercept invariance, so its cross-national comparisons are
  the least secure, and they carry the paper's headline about WEIRDness.
- Study 3 is 82% U.S., so the MFQ-2's predictive advantage is shown mainly
  in one country.
- Candidate foundations (liberty, honor, honesty, ownership, efficiency)
  remain unmeasured (p. 64).

## Open questions

- Does Purity's association with cultural distance survive a Purity scale
  that is invariant?
- Would a reciprocity scale (returning favours, punishing defectors) load
  with Equality, with Proportionality, or on its own? That would settle
  whether MFT's Fairness conflated reciprocity with distribution, as [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)
  claims.
- Do bravery and property, MAC's domains with no MFT counterpart, form
  factors when written into a cross-cultural pool built the same way?

## Corrections

- none to a seeded skim (there was no seed)
- **Cross-loading items.** The paper says "some Purity items" load below
  .30 with cross-loadings (p. 24). By Table 4, four items load more
  strongly on Authority than on their own foundation: "the human body
  should be treated like a temple" (Purity .21, Authority .37); "the
  strength of a sports team comes from the loyalty of its members" (Loyalty
  .10, Authority .36); "proud when a person in their community wins in an
  international competition" (Loyalty .27, Authority .28); "happy when
  people are recognized on their merits" (Proportionality .27, Authority
  .40). The problem is not only Purity's.
- **Dimension count.** EGA is said to reveal "one dimension in 16 samples
  and two dimensions in four samples (Ireland, New Zealand, and Peru)" (p.
  40). That is 20 for 19 nations, and three are named.
- **Direction label.** "Cultural distance from the U.S. (higher
  WEIRDness)" (p. 32) labels distance as WEIRDness; greater distance from
  the U.S. is lower WEIRDness, which is how the results are then read.
  These three may be fixed in the version of record.
