# CLAIM-1103: The Syrian three-generation study separates the germline route from one route the other designs leave open, the care of a parent who lived through the trauma, because the germline-exposed grandchildren's mothers were exposed only in utero, so their methylation difference is evidence for a route that does not pass through an exposed parent's experience

## The claim

In the paternal designs (Costa et al.; Yehuda et al.'s paternal PTSD) the
exposed parent raises the child, so his altered care is a route. In
Mulligan et al.'s 1980 group the grandchildren's mothers had no
experience of the violence as persons: they were fetuses. A care route
running from a parent's memory, symptoms or trauma-shaped parenting is
absent between mother and grandchild. The grandchildren still differ from
controls at 14 sites, at genome-wide significance in two models. On this
reading the study is a partial separation, the first for human trauma:
not from every route, but from the one the paternal designs never remove.

## Why it objects to [CLAIM-1017](CLAIM-1017.md)

[CLAIM-1017](CLAIM-1017.md)'s table puts this design in the row that removes none of
the routes. This claim says it removes one parenting route that no other
design found removes. That is the route most of the clinical tradition
names: the exposed parent's care, silence and style ([NOTE-1307](../notes.d/NOTE-1307.md)).

## What would tell against it

- If the mothers' prenatal exposure itself altered their care, then
  parenting by a prenatally affected mother is a route, and nothing is
  separated. The study measured no maternal behaviour.
- If the grandmothers' care of the grandchildren, or the 1980 community's
  memory of Hama, carries the effect. The study measured neither.
- If the 14 sites do not replicate.

## Standing

This is the assistant's statement of a reading the evidence admits, set
beside the one it leans to. The owner chooses between them.
