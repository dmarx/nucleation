# NOTE-1996: Cerrato, Sahlgren and Heilmann (2026) — Taking the Whys Seriously: Limitations of Counterfactual Explanations in Justification and Recourse

## Contribution

A measurement of how far a counterfactual explanation depends on choices the deploying organization made before it was generated, and an argument that, as justification or as recourse, such explanations hide those choices and with them the question whether the decider should have acted otherwise.

## Key results

1. Abstract: naive use of CEs for justification and recourse can lead to "ignoring contestable choices made throughout the machine learning (ML) pipeline"; decisions and their counterfactuals are "artifacts of an organization's materialized design and governance choices".
2. Abstract: CEs "preclude consideration of whether the decision-maker ought to have acted differently."
3. RQ1, Fig. 2: "Three of the four interventions move both methods clearly" outside the band set by the method's validity threshold τ; salary caps are "viable answers to a 'why' question"; validation strategy "is the only case in which the upstream intervention does" not dominate τ.
4. RQ2, Fig. 3: in three of four experiments, DiCE-kdtree's Value Agreement falls from 0.39 under τ to 0.18–0.26 under upstream interventions, NICE's from 1.00 to 0.61–0.87.
5. Discussion: "we do not report this result intending to demonstrate a limitation of CEs per se"; the target is the "fixed model assumption".
6. Justification: CE use "obfuscates agents' responsibilities by precluding consideration" that the provider or deployer should revise their choices; CEs can be "used selectively to shirk responsibility and public accountability".
7. Recourse: under equally defensible upstream choices there may be "no uniquely identifiable action the decision subject should" have taken; treating CEs as advice "risks encouraging affected subjects to comply with potentially problematic policies".
8. Conclusions: "Recourse is relational and involves multiple parties"; a relational view does not "place onto the subjects the burden of simply adapting to institutional discretion".

## Assumptions

A stylized screening task built on American Community Survey data, with earnings standing in for recruiter judgement; two retrieval-based CE methods; random forests; the threshold τ as the yardstick of a significant effect. Recourse is taken, after Venkatasubramanian and Alfano, as a modally robust good.

## Concepts

Counterfactual explanation; algorithmic recourse; fixed model assumption; upstream intervention on design parameters; explanation sensitivity; Rashomon effect; individualistic against relational recourse; the decision-maker as "benevolent paternalist".

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: supports its split of control and burden by a different mechanism. Control over the conditions of a decision (its criteria) stays with the deployer while the task of securing a better outcome is handed to the subject, and the explanation's frame makes the deployer's part invisible (results 6–8). It says nothing of compensation that leaves no trace or of credit and blame.
- **[CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)**: a CE supplies legibility of the subject relative to a fixed model but not of the organization's choices, so the discrepancy that could move the organization, an unjustified criterion, is not made recognizable.
- **[CLAIM-686](../claims.d/CLAIM-686.md)**: the authors' "it may be impossible for subjects to reverse their decisions through contesting such choices absent appropriate transparency" is an instance of the legibility failure the claim names.
- **[THEORY-1841](../theory.d/THEORY-1841.md)**: the pipeline's design parameters are a concrete case of Feenberg's contingent technical code.
- **The exchange**, A19 §7 and reading list: accurate. "Shift responsibility" is A's word; the paper's is that the individualistic framing places the burden on the subject and forecloses the decider's.

## Limitations

One stylized task; two retrieval-based methods; random forests only; the τ band is the authors' yardstick; the Conclusions describe "a single CE method and metric", which does not match the body's two methods and three agreement scores; unrefereed preprint; figures not inspected.

## From the reading of 2026-11-17

*Quotations here are carried from the reading of 2026-11-17, whose filer rechecked them against its copy on 2026-11-18; they were not rechecked at the reconciliation.*

- **Central finding** (abstract): "an organization's choices on
  measurement models for feature and labels, business requirements, model
  validation, and the metric of model success have as much or more impact
  on the generated counterfactuals as the specifics of the generating
  method".
- **Why only retrieval-based methods**: optimization-based methods were
  excluded because seed-to-seed variance swamped the effects.
- **Order of justification** (Implications for Recourse): "upstream choices
  must be relatively settled, normatively speaking, prior to evaluating
  CEs for recourse"; and "AR is an inherently relational practice involving
  both decision subjects and decision-makers as parties whose behavior may
  require revision".
- **[CLAIM-tmpohafo](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpohafo.md)** (explanation, contestation and interruption are
  separate entitlements): corroborates its first split. An explanation can
  be given while the choices that would have to be contested stay hidden;
  the explanation points the subject at the wrong target. Nothing on
  suspending execution.
- **[CLAIM-tmpcpijj](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpcpijj.md)** (authority over a decision does not carry authority
  over the conditions of challenge): related, not filed as a relation; the
  deployer fixes what an explanation-based challenge can see.
- **[CLAIM-410](../claims.d/CLAIM-410.md)**: weakly related; the decisive discretion sits in upstream
  choices, consistent with authority moving into design ([LIT-2661](../literature.d/LIT-2661.md)).
- **The LIT's first framing**, "the distinction between recourse and
  contestability", is the record's: the paper argues that recourse properly
  understood is relational and includes contesting the decider's choices.
- **Limitation**: the text says "four" interventions and lists five (τ
  being the baseline), and one passage refers to a "$40k baseline" where
  the stated default cap is $60k; the figures should be checked before
  anything rests on the numbers.
