# THEORY-1494: Evans and Popova's count by sign and significance of 44 estimates from 19 studies of conditional and unconditional cash transfers in low- and middle-income countries finds that transfers almost never raise spending on alcohol and tobacco: 82% of the estimates are negative, only two are positive and significant, both non-experimental, none of 17 randomized estimates is positive and significant, and the pattern does not differ between conditional and unconditional programs, across regions or with duration of receipt

## Source

Evans & Popova (2017), [LIT-2150](../literature.d/LIT-2150.md); read as World Bank Policy Research
Working Paper 6886 (2014) in [NOTE-1649](../notes.d/NOTE-1649.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The count** (p. 8; Tables 5–6): of 44 estimates, 12 negative and
  significant, 24 negative and insignificant, 6 positive and
  insignificant, 2 positive and significant: "82% of estimates are
  negative, and just 5% of estimates are significant and positive".
- **The positives** (p. 8): both from non-randomized estimates, "not
  consistent across estimates within the same study", and tiny: "one is
  less than a penny, whereas the other is 21 cents".
- **Randomized only** (p. 8): of 17 estimates from 8 trials, "zero that
  are positive and significant".
- **Heterogeneity** (pp. 9–10, 14): conditional and unconditional programs
  alike, "so this does not seem to be a function of conditions"; no
  regional difference; no relation to duration of receipt, which ran from
  6 months to 5.5 years.
- Each of these could have come out the other way: the search included
  any study reporting such an impact.

## What this does not say

- That transfers lower such spending: most negative estimates are
  insignificant, and nothing is pooled.
- A bounded effect: there is no pooled estimate or interval, and the
  estimates are not independent.
- That the 2017 journal version reports the same: it was not read.
- That self-reports are unbiased: the social-desirability argument is
  argued, and its own text figures (24% and 9% on p. 11) do not match
  Tables 5–6.
- Anything about work or capability, or about states and projects: it
  removes a household-level stereotype the record's "slack effort"
  ([CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)) might borrow, and tests none of its state-level forms.
