summary: >-
  Scheffer et al. (2009), Nature 461. A review of generic early-warning
  signals for critical transitions. Near a fold bifurcation the dominant
  eigenvalue goes to zero, so recovery from perturbations slows; under
  ongoing perturbation this shows as rising lag-1 autocorrelation and
  variance. Skewness and flickering are further signals. Spatial systems
  add rising cross-correlation and pattern sequences. Climate, ecosystem,
  epilepsy, asthma and finance cases are reviewed. The same signals,
  weaker, precede non-catastrophic thresholds; the timing of a shift stays
  hard to predict; cyclic and chaotic systems are "less well studied".

# LIT-tmp88vcm: Early-warning signals for critical transitions

Scheffer, M., Bascompte, J., Brock, W. A., Brovkin, V., Carpenter, S. R., Dakos, V., et al. (2009). Early-warning signals for critical transitions. *Nature, 461(7260), 53-59*. doi:10.1038/nature08227.

## Key takeaways

Read on 2026-11-03 from the thesis-chapter version ([NOTE-tmpyug9h](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyug9h.md),
Skimmed).

- Critical slowing down and its three signals, with the caution that they
  mark proximity to a broad class of thresholds ([THEORY-tmp7q08l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7q08l.md)).
- No early-warning theory yet for chaotic systems or basin-boundary
  collisions (p. 24 of the chapter).

## Standing in the record

Critical slowing down as an early warning. The record holds Scheffer et al. 2001 and Scheffer and Carpenter 2003 ([LIT-720](LIT-720.md), [LIT-726](LIT-726.md)); this is the review A16 and A21 cite.
