# CASE-tmp6idy5: Communities forming subcommunities on Reddit, the owner's example of learned abstractions becoming operative structures that change later behaviour: specializations nested in broad communities on the owner's map, college-football team communities organized by inside jokes and rivalries, and joke communities that grew and lapsed; with a proposed longitudinal study of whether a community makes its descendants cheaper to form

## The specimen

At U37 the owner answered ChatGPT's proposal to test "whether learned
abstractions become operative structures that change subsequent
behavior" with a real case: "a sociological demonstration of this could
be community->subcommunity evolution on reddit".

- **On the owner's map** ([THEORY-tmp5zxkk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5zxkk.md), [NOTE-tmpbivmm](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpbivmm.md)), a small
  subreddit nested in a large one shows as a directed edge into it, read
  as a specialization forked from the larger community; the map shows a
  large American sports cluster and microcommunities embedded in larger
  ones.
- **Joke communities.** The map's post names communities "that have
  sprung up around April fools jokes" (/r/TheButton, /r/Periwinkle,
  /r/Orangered), sprawling and then inactive.
- **Team communities** (the owner's report, not on the map): college
  football and "associated per-team micro communities (many of which are
  motivated by stuff like inside jokes and rivalries)".

## What it is a case of

A distinction that arises in interaction (a team, a rivalry, a joke)
becomes something people can name, join, maintain and govern, which then
organizes later activity, including further differentiation. ChatGPT
(A82) put the contrast with [CASE-296](CASE-296.md): "A genre cluster is initially
something an observer identifies. A subreddit is also something
participants can join, maintain, govern, and reproduce." It proposed the
sequence shared practice, recognizable distinction, named community,
independent organization, further differentiation, and three classes of
formation: specializations of broad interests, identities anchored
outside Reddit (teams), and identities arising inside it (jokes), the
last being "potentially the cleanest example because the identity need
not be imported from an established institution". It illustrates
[CLAIM-762](../claims.d/CLAIM-762.md)'s reification, socially.

## The proposed study (A82)

- **Data.** A sequence of monthly graphs rather than the map's flattened
  maximum, which "removes much of the temporal ordering"; the unit is a
  candidate new subreddit observed around its formation.
- **Outcomes.** Formation (how much parent activity precedes creation),
  individuation (persistent distinctive participation and practices),
  complementarity (does the new group expand, redistribute or displace
  the parent's engagement), and recursive productivity (does it
  facilitate further related communities).
- **Developmental productivity.** For a community C and a family D of
  possible descendants, whether C changes "the hazard or cost of forming
  viable members of D, relative to matched contexts lacking that parent
  organization": a social counterpart of the developmental frontier
  ([CLAIM-754](../claims.d/CLAIM-754.md)), and of inherited organization as stored work ([CLAIM-761](../claims.d/CLAIM-761.md)).
- **Cautions.** Controls for popularity, platform growth, external events
  and selection; and two senses of parent kept apart, "the source of
  early participants without being the organizational cause of the new
  group", or a continuing home community rather than a split.
- **Prior work**, read on 2026-11-19, and carrying less than A82 said:
  - Hessel, Tan and Lee (THEORY-tmps2gur) measure three of these
    outcomes on affix-named pairs: founders by the early-participant
    fraction, displacement by the activity ratio, complementarity by the
    exploration effect. Users who try a spinoff are, in most kinds of
    pair, more likely to raise their activity in the original over 30
    days, negative for some kinds, with causation disclaimed; that is
    short-term individual activity, not the absence of fragmentation A82
    read into it. About half of related pairs share few founders, which
    bears out the caution on the two senses of parent.
  - Krohn and Weninger (THEORY-tmpj5upc) find that links to subreddits
    that did not yet exist preceded a sixth of later creations, most
    such links never followed by one. They report correlation, not the
    natural experiment A82 described, and measure nothing institutional:
    where members come from and whether created communities last are
    their open questions.

## What it does not show

Nothing is measured. The map is built for discovery: its direction is a
reading, its months are flattened, and it cannot say which community came
first or whether a parent caused a child. The two works were read on 2026-11-19 and are
correlational.
