# NOTE-tmpa8ntc: Морфология сказки

## Contribution

Folktale study before Propp classified tales by category (Miller, Wundt)
or by plot type (Aarne's index; Volkov's fifteen plots) and described them
by motifs (Veselovsky) or by Bédier's constant "elements". Propp argues
that each of these cuts across the material, because the parts of tales
move freely between tales. He proposes a new unit, the function of a
character, and shows on Afanasyev's tales 50–151 that these units are
few, ordered, and common to all wondertales. After the book, a tale can be
described as a sequence of symbols, two tales compared by their schemes,
and the class of wondertales defined by structure rather than content.

## Key insight

Ask what the characters do, not who they are. Baba Yaga, Morozko, a bear
and a wood-spirit all test the stepdaughter and reward her: one function,
four performers. Functions are "extremely few, and characters extremely
many", which explains at once the tale's dazzling variety and its
monotony.

## Assumptions

- **The material.** Afanasyev's collection, nos. 50–151 by his own
  numbering, taken as given rather than chosen; justified by the claim
  that functions repeat so much that a hundred tales exhaust them, with
  a larger "control" material looked at but not shown.
- **The class.** "Wondertales" are provisionally Aarne types 300–749. By
  ch. IX the class is redefined by the result: "a tale built on the
  regular alternation of the functions given, in various forms, with some
  absent from each tale and others repeated". Tales outside the scheme
  are by that definition not wondertales.
- **Function identity is fixed by consequence.** Identical acts are
  different functions if they lead to different things (a test followed by
  a magical agent is the donor's test; followed by marriage, a difficult
  task; followed by a quest, a dispatch). The analyst decides by what
  follows.
- **Authenticity.** Pure structure is "proper only to the peasantry, and
  a peasantry little touched by civilisation"; Grimm's tales show it less
  purely; deviations in humorous tales count as decomposition.

## Key results

- **Thesis I.** Functions of characters are the constant, stable elements
  of the tale, independent of who performs them and how.
- **Thesis II.** The number of functions known to the wondertale is
  limited: 31, after an initial situation (α).
- **Thesis III.** The sequence of functions is always the same. Ch. IX
  qualifies it: inverted sequences occur (D–G–Z before A; departure
  before learning of the misfortune); struggle can follow pursuit;
  recognition and exposure, wedding and punishment, can swap;
  transfiguration is the least fixed. These are called oscillations, "not
  a new compositional system".
- **Thesis IV.** All wondertales are of one type. Every function can stand
  in one tale with every other, except struggle–victory (B–П) and
  difficult task–solution (З–Р), which occur in 41 and 33 of the 100
  tales and together in one move only three times; in two-move tales the
  struggle move comes first and the task move second, and the
  two-move tale is "the basic type of all tales".
- **Spheres of action.** Seven: villain (villainy, fight, pursuit); donor
  (preparation and provision of the magical agent); helper (spatial
  transference, liquidation, rescue, solution of tasks, transfiguration);
  princess and her father (tasks, branding, exposure, recognition,
  punishment, wedding); dispatcher; hero (departure, reaction to the
  donor, wedding); false hero. Hence "the tale knows seven characters".
- **Helpers.** Universal (only the horse, in this material), partial and
  specific (one function: the ball of thread guides, the sword kills); "the
  magical agent is nothing but a particular form of the magical helper".
- **Linked variants.** Some paired functions bind their subtypes
  absolutely (a fight in the open field with victory in the open field);
  some partly (abduction with several kinds of recovery); the rest combine
  freely, and "it is by replacing one subtype by another of the same
  element that the metamorphosis of tales proceeds".
- **Generation.** "One can create new plots artificially in unlimited
  number": take any A, one of the possible B, then C↑, any D, then G, and
  so on, omit or treble elements, and assign functions to characters;
  "the schemes come alive, they become tales" (answering Shklovsky's "by
  laws not yet known").
- **The teller's freedom.** Not free in the order of functions, in
  linked subtypes, sometimes in the attributes a function requires, and
  in what the initial situation must include; free in which functions to
  omit, how each is realised, the naming and attributes of characters,
  and language.
- **Plot and variant.** Predicates give the composition and subjects and
  objects the plot; distinguishing plot from variant is impossible
  ("from qualitative the question becomes quantitative"), so the whole
  stock of wondertales is "a chain of variants".

## Method

Compare tales across plots; isolate the constant actions by abstracting
from performer and manner; name each by a noun of action with a symbol and
subtypes; fix each by its consequence, which resolves assimilations
(identical acts serving as different functions) and double meanings (one
act serving two functions at once); then transcribe each tale as a string
of symbols, separate its moves (from each villainy or lack to its
resolution), and stack the strings to read off the order and the
alternatives. Non-function elements are catalogued separately:
connectives (how one character learns what another did), trebling devices,
motivations ("the most unstable" elements, probably late), forms of entry
of characters, and attributes.

## Concepts

- **функция (function)**: an act of a character defined by its
  significance for the course of the action.
- **ход (move)**: a development from villainy (A) or lack (a) through
  intermediate functions to a wedding or another resolution; each new
  villainy or lack opens a new move.
- **круг действий (sphere of action)**: a group of functions belonging
  logically to one role.
- **ассимиляция (assimilation)**: one function realised in the form
  proper to another.
- **начальная ситуация (initial situation)**: the opening enumeration of
  family or hero; a morphological element, not a function.
- **недостача (lack)**: the morphological equivalent of villainy,
  "comparable to zero, which in a row of figures has a definite value".
- **детонация ("detonation")**: a mismatch between a move's opening and
  its resolution (Ivan sets out for a horse and returns with a princess),
  material for the study of transformations.

## Connections

Goethe's morphology frames the book (the epigraphs on metamorphosis and
the Urpflanze). The critics engaged are Veselovsky (motif and plot), Bédier
(constants and variables), Aarne (the type index), Volkov, Wundt and
Shklovsky. The book defers two things to later work: the transformations
of tales (named here as a separate study) and the historical roots of the
wondertale. Doja, Capocchi and Santucci ([LIT-tmp9axrl](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9axrl.md)) list Propp's
morphology among the narrative formalisations computational work has
attempted. Lévi-Strauss's structural analysis of myth ([LIT-775](../literature.d/LIT-775.md), unread)
is the usual counterpoint; the Italian edition (1966 by Dundes's dating in the 1968
English edition, [NOTE-tmpxboen](NOTE-tmpxboen.md); this note first gave 1969) carried his
intervention and Propp's reply (per Open Library), not read here.

## Bearing on the record

- Produces **[THEORY-tmpdu50e](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpdu50e.md)**: the functional composition of the
  wondertale, with what it does not establish.
- **Transformation and transmission.** Propp's account of what a teller
  can and cannot change is a claim about which features of a tale survive
  retelling: the order of functions and linked subtypes are constrained;
  performers, attributes, motivations and wording are free, and
  "everything that enters the tale from outside submits to its norms" (a
  devil becomes villain, helper or donor). That is a structural thesis
  about the corpus, not a measurement of retellings; the record's
  transmission-chain readings ([LIT-769](../literature.d/LIT-769.md), [LIT-771](../literature.d/LIT-771.md), [LIT-tmpj7bvq](../literature.d/LIT-tmpj7bvq.md)) are the
  kind of evidence that could test it.
- **Saussure.** Propp's constant-and-variable analysis is independent of
  Saussure ([LIT-774](../literature.d/LIT-774.md)), whom the OCR text never names; his model is Goethe's
  morphology, not linguistics.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- One collection, Russian, nineteenth-century, edited by Afanasyev; a
  hundred tales, from a starting point fixed by Afanasyev's numbering.
- The coding is Propp's alone; nothing in the book tests whether another
  analyst would assign the same functions, and assignment by consequence
  gives the analyst latitude.
- The universality of the order is stated with exceptions that are then
  explained away (inversions, oscillations, humorous decomposition).
- The one-type thesis is helped by defining the class as the tales that
  fit the scheme.
- The study of attributes and of transformations, which the preface says
  the full version contained, was cut; claims resting on them (the basic
  form, the myth hypothesis) are asserted without the evidence.

## Open questions

- Does an independent coding, by several analysts with agreement
  measured, of wondertales outside Propp's hundred reproduce the 31
  functions and their order?
- Do the B–П and З–Р branches and their order hold in other traditions,
  or only in the Russian?
- If tales are generated from the scheme as Propp proposes, do listeners
  accept them as tales of the kind, and does retelling move non-conforming
  tales toward the scheme?
