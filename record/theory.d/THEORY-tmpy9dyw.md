# THEORY-tmpy9dyw: Constitution is best inferred, and only abductively, from the failure to break a coupling: a set of parts constitutes a phenomenon when every cause of the phenomenon is also a cause of some member of the set, no smaller set does as much, and no expansion of the variables studied turns up a cause of the phenomenon that spares them all, so constitutive relevance is fixed by the set a part belongs to and no finite series of experiments settles it

## Source

Baumgartner and Casini (2017), LIT-tmps6sc3, §§4–5, pp. 12–18
(NOTE-tmp5hk5o); the criterion it develops is Baumgartner and Gebharter's
(FH) and (CR), LIT-tmpkhdpi, pp. 20–22 (NOTE-tmpeljd8).

## What was actually shown

**The definition (B&C p. 15).** Φ₁(X₁) is constitutively relevant to Ψ(S)
iff some variable set V contains Ψ(S) and a set Φ(X) = {Φ₁(X₁),…,Φₙ(Xₙ)}
such that: (1) Parthood: every complex instance of Φ is part of an
instance of Ψ; (2.i) every cause of Ψ in V is a common cause of Ψ and at
least one Φᵢ; (2.ii) no proper subset of Φ satisfies (2.i); (3) No
De-Coupling: the coupling "cannot be broken (invalidated) by expanding
V."

**Why redundancy-freedom.** Without (2.ii), a sterile effect of a
constituent (the car's air conditioning, run by the engine) or a detached
part damaged by the same cause (the ashtray in a crash) would join the set,
since every cause of the phenomenon reaches them too, through a
constituent. Dropping them leaves the coupling intact, so (2.ii) excludes
them (B&C p. 13).

**Why abductive.** Data from any finite V are reproduced by a pure
common-cause model (THEORY-tmp3pqnm). The two models differ in what they
predict when V is expanded. The common-cause model gives no reason why a
surgical cause of Ψ should never appear; the constitutive model makes it
impossible. When repeated expansions fail to break the coupling, the
constitutive model is preferred "because it exceeds the latter in
explanatory power" (B&C pp. 14–15). B&G give the same argument for (FH)
(pp. 20–21).

**Logical form (B&C p. 16).** (3) is a negative existential, so NDC
constitution can only be "inductively corroborated", like causal
irrelevance. A surgical cause of Ψ falsifies one candidate set
conclusively, but not the constitutive relevance of its members, who may
belong to another set (pp. 17–18).

**Extrinsic, not relativized (pp. 16–17).** Whether Φᵢ is a constituent
depends on whether it belongs to some constituting set, not on a chosen V;
across expansions the relation holds throughout or not at all.

## What this does not say

- **How to test it.** "For reasons of space, we must postpone a
  methodological operationalization of NDC to another occasion" (p. 17);
  how much expansion suffices has "no universal rule" (footnote 10).
- **That practice proceeds this way.** "We have done none of that sort for
  NDC here" (p. 19).
- **That the constituting set is unique**, or what follows if Ψ has two.
- **Where the boundary is.** Condition (1) takes parthood, a thin
  spatiotemporal containment (footnote 1), as given.
- **Anything about constitution by relations in general.** The relation
  is a part's behaviour realizing a whole's behaviour, not relations
  fixing what a relatum is.
