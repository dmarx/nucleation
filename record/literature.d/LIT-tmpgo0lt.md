# LIT-tmpgo0lt: Fiduciary Duties and Moral Blackmail

Simon Keller (2016; issue 2018). Fiduciary Duties and Moral Blackmail. *Journal of Applied Philosophy* 35(3): 481–495. DOI 10.1111/japp.12234. Read from the author's preprint (pages 1–30).

## Key takeaways

- **Moral blackmail** (p. 2): "To commit moral blackmail is to perform a
  wrongful act that forces someone to do something by making her
  alternatives morally unacceptable." It is wider than McConnell's notion:
  no evil need be threatened, and the act wanted need not be wrong.
- **A wrong can create a genuine obligation** (p. 21): "you can have a
  genuine moral obligation because you have been treated wrongly". The
  victim's complaint, "it is morally wrong that I have this moral
  obligation" (p. 26), is coherent.
- **Fiduciaries are exposed** (pp. 22–25): their roles become special
  moral relationships whose obligations of partiality others can trigger.
  "It really is more difficult to go on strike if you are a nurse or a
  teacher than if you are a policy analyst or a software developer."
- **Distribution** (pp. 26–27): those exposed can reasonably expect
  better pay or conditions, and "the bad things can become instruments of
  systemic injustice."
