# CLAIM-tmpex9rg: The sensitivity that formal chaos gives a will's dynamics is not control over them: in Kane's self-forming actions, chaotic amplification keeps the outcome open, but it magnifies the agent's efforts, uncontrolled fluctuations and outside inputs alike, so what would make the outcome the agent's is how its own control couples to the amplifying directions, relative to noise and to other parties' inputs and with what feedback, which sensitivity does not supply; and under weak noise what amplification touches lasts only where it reaches the slow modes

## The claim

Kane ([LIT-tmpnqv86](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnqv86.md), ch. 8, as the standard reading has it; not yet read here)
places indeterminacy in conflicted choice, amplified by chaotic neural
dynamics. Bishop's SEP entry ([LIT-tmp9hfqf](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9hfqf.md)) states the gap the owner points
at: incompatibilists "such Robert Kane (1996)" still have "to demonstrate
agents can effectively harness such indeterminism".

The owner's connection puts that gap in control terms:

- **Sensitivity is not control.** A positive Lyapunov exponent is a property
  of the dynamics. It amplifies whatever enters along the unstable
  directions: the agent's effort, noise, another party's input.
  [CLAIM-tmpptgk8](CLAIM-tmpptgk8.md) shows that it lowers the cost of reaching an outcome and,
  without feedback, lowers the agent's share of it against noise. What
  would make the outcome the agent's is a property of the agent's channel:
  its reach ([CLAIM-354](CLAIM-354.md), [CLAIM-345](CLAIM-345.md)), its allocation against other channels,
  and its feedback.
- **Openness is not control either.** Deterministic chaos has a unique
  future; it gives practical unpredictability, not Kane's metaphysical
  openness (A22 §2). And metaphysical openness, if present, gives no
  control by itself. That is the luck objection ([THEORY-328](../theory.d/THEORY-328.md), [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md)),
  stated as the absence of a channel.
- **Slow modes decide what lasts.** Under weak noise, chaos makes generic
  influences short-lived: operator growth spreads them and they decay,
  while only what is near-conserved stays slow ([LIT-tmp8mp1c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8mp1c.md),
  [THEORY-tmpggrkf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpggrkf.md)). By analogy, an amplified effort shapes a will
  durably only where it reaches the slow variables, which is the owner's
  "slow modes" and [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)'s scale separation.
- **A rival emphasis.** Jennings ([LIT-tmpasemk](../literature.d/LIT-tmpasemk.md), [NOTE-tmpby7i2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpby7i2.md)), whom Kane
  read in draft, reverses the classic problem: "the task now is to explain
  how we manage to find stability in a stochastic one" (p. 3), and
  "constraint, in a noisy environment, is what gives us power over
  ourselves" (p. 33). On her account what makes the outcome the agent's is a
  stable constraint, not amplification. That fits this claim's point that
  sensitivity supplies no control, and [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)'s constraints, though her
  constraint is a boundary, not a feedback channel.
- **Reflexive control is the test case** (U19). The leverage that makes
  self-formation cheap makes outside steering cheap. Comparing the agent's
  channel with an outsider's for the same transition, by their Gramians, is
  one measurable part of whose the outcome is (A20 §4; [QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md)'s note
  of 2026-11-02).

## What it does not say

- **That Kane is wrong.** Kane's reply, that the indeterminacy is a
  hindrance internal to the effort that overcomes it, would make the
  separation of inputs ill-posed. It is the claim's defeater, unread here.
- **That responsibility follows from a good channel.** Guidance control
  without regulative control ([THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md)) is the nearest held account of
  what a channel could ground. Ultimate responsibility is set aside
  ([CLAIM-022](CLAIM-022.md)).
- **That the slow-mode point is shown for brains or wills.** It is shown
  for weakly dissipative quantum many-body systems and carried over by
  analogy.

## Note of 2026-11-03: the readings

- **The SEP quotation is verbatim** (§6.4; [NOTE-tmpyhyrz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyhyrz.md)). Bishop adds that
  amplification "may be severely constrained", by damping and by negative
  local Lyapunov exponents at arrival (§4), and without citation by neural
  redundancy ([THEORY-tmpnjaik](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpnjaik.md)). That supports the slow-modes clause.
- **A libertarian concedes the core.** Froese ([NOTE-tmpn7fkx](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpn7fkx.md)): amplified
  irruptions "cannot directly control … cannot force the formation of the
  behavior that is to come" (§5.2.3). His remedy is attunement, but in the model
  he cites the resets are arbitrary, so nothing tells the agent's input from
  noise ([THEORY-tmped3yc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmped3yc.md)).
- **No chaos in the irruption model.** Sánchez-Cañizares 2026 ([NOTE-tmpbmoj1](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpbmoj1.md))
  has a one-dimensional gradient flow and does not cite Kane; the mind's only
  channel is to the landscape's parameters, so it sets which outcomes exist, not
  which is reached ([THEORY-tmp1jrus](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp1jrus.md)).
- **Kane is still unread.** The book is Unavailable and the 1999 and 2009 papers
  unreached, so "as the standard reading has it" stands.

## Note of 2026-11-03 (later): Kane, read

Kane's "Libertarianism" (2009) was read at the owner's direction ([LIT-tmpmi6rp](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmi6rp.md),
[NOTE-tmpku07s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpku07s.md)), so the note above saying Kane is unread is out of date for
that paper; the 1996 book is still Unavailable.

- **The attribution checks.** The chaos is Kane's: conflict is reflected in the
  brain "by movement away from thermodynamic equilibrium—in short, a kind of
  'stirring up of chaos' in the brain that makes it sensitive to
  micro-indeterminacies at the neuronal level" (p. 39; [THEORY-tmpjn453](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpjn453.md)).
- **The defeater is his reply, and it does not defeat the claim.** Kane makes the
  indeterminacy "a hindrance or obstacle to the attainment of the goal" inside
  goal-directed effort (p. 42), which is what this claim's defeater names. But
  he concedes that indeterminism lessens control: "diminished control in such
  circumstances does not entail diminished responsibility" (p. 43;
  [THEORY-tmpjkav4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpjkav4.md)). So his reply is about responsibility, and grants this
  claim's point about control.
- **His escape is to make the noise the agent's own.** In a self-forming action
  the noise hindering each choice "is coming from her own will" (p. 40), so
  there is no outside noise for the agent's share to be measured against. That
  holds only if the indeterminacy is all endogenous to the conflict, which Kane
  assumes ([NOTE-tmpku07s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpku07s.md)).

Juarrero 2023 ([NOTE-tmpv0g6b](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpv0g6b.md)), registered as a stand-in for the 1999 book, does
not discuss Kane, chaos, sensitivity or free will. Its control is confinement by
governing constraints through negative feedback (pp. 79–80, 101) and analog
control under Ashby's law (p. 168), which fits this claim and Jennings's
emphasis without arguing for either. It is prior art for the constraint half of
this claim only.
