# LIT-591: Internal Family Systems Therapy

Richard C. Schwartz and Martha Sweezy (2020), *Internal Family Systems
Therapy*, 2nd ed., New York: The Guilford Press (304 pp.; e-book 12 August
2019, hardcover 23 September 2019, © 2020) — <https://www.guilford.com/books/Internal-Family-Systems-Therapy/Schwartz-Sweezy/9781462541461>

**On the citation.** The brief's details are right: Schwartz & Sweezy, 2nd
ed., 2020, a book. The first edition (1995) was Schwartz's alone.

## Key takeaways

*From chapter 1, the only part read, and the publisher's contents.*

- **Two paradigms.** IFS is "a synthesis of two paradigms: the plural mind
  ... and systems thinking". Intrapsychic processes form a system, treated
  with the concepts of structural, strategic and Bowenian family therapy.
- **Parts are not pathology.** Extreme parts are "talented and resourceful
  but constrained by the traumatic events that generated extreme emotions
  and beliefs (burdens)". Protectors keep exiled, vulnerable parts away.
  Internalization and introjection are "burdens that can be released rather
  than ... qualities of a part".
- **The Self.** When interfering parts "step back", clients report "that's
  who I really am, that's my self". This is "an entity that is described and
  approached in many different ways in spiritual traditions". It "did not
  need to develop over time", and leads the inner system with "clarity,
  calm, confidence, courage, and compassion". The goal of therapy is "to help
  clients become Self-led".
- **Contents** (Guilford): Part I, overview (origins, individuals as
  systems, the Self, burdens, IFS and the body, the therapist's role); Part
  II, practice with individuals (seven chapters, from "Setting the Table" to
  "Unburdening Exiles"); Part III, families, couples and larger systems;
  Part IV, research and "The Laws of Inner Physics".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the canonical text of a
multiplicity-of-self psychotherapy. It is `Deferred` because only chapter 1
was read. The record's practice, as with [LIT-396](LIT-396.md), is a Deferred LIT with a
Skimmed NOTE when a book is read only in a publisher's excerpt. The
substitute the owner allows, a shorter lawful work by the same author, is the
IFS Institute's "Evolution of the Internal Family Systems Model"
([LIT-579](LIT-579.md)), read in full. Chapter 1 retells its central case, with the
client renamed from "Diane" to "Cora".

Tags. `psychopathology-and-treatment` comes first: IFS is a psychotherapy
with a theory of what symptoms are, namely protective parts in extreme roles.
`self` is for its central claim that there is a Self distinct from the parts.
`behavioral-integration` is because IFS models a person as many
semi-autonomous subsystems ("polarizations", "coalitions of extreme parts
vied for power") that act as one under good leadership. That is the
blurb's "how an organism's many control systems come to act as one", in
clinical rather than computational terms.

Where it bears on the record:

- **ACT's self as context** ([THEORY-044](../theory.d/THEORY-044.md), [THEORY-051](../theory.d/THEORY-051.md)). Both IFS and ACT
  hold that a person can step back from thoughts and feelings to a
  non-judging perspective, and that doing so is therapeutic. They differ on
  what that perspective is. ACT's self as context is a learned verbal
  perspective "not the content". IFS's Self is innate, has qualities
  (compassion, curiosity, calm), and leads. The NOTE on chapter 1 sets out
  the comparison.
- **Ainslie's interests** ([LIT-586](LIT-586.md)). Picoeconomics models a person as
  successive "interests" bargaining over time. IFS models simultaneous parts
  in protective roles. Both are multiplicity models of one agent. IFS has no
  account of time and Ainslie has no Self.
- **Metzinger** ([LIT-568](LIT-568.md)). On the self-model theory, IFS's Self is a
  candidate for the transparent partition of the self-model, and its "parts"
  for opaque, attended contents. That is an interpretation. Neither text
  makes it.

No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.
