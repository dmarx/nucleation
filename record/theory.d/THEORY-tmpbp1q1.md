# THEORY-tmpbp1q1: Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy propose that communication be treated as transduction, the continuity of an action through processes that it transforms, rather than as exchange between parties, with meaning the contribution an action makes, in retrospect, to a joint individuation and coexisting meanings the mark of concurrent individuations, and they assert that the problem of scaling agency from individual to collective then vanishes

## Source

Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy (2024), [LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), pp. 1355–1358 and
1363–1365; read in full in [NOTE-1484](../notes.d/NOTE-1484.md).

## The proposal

- **Communication is transduction.** It is "the continuity of action
  through beings who are in a perpetual state of becoming", and "any
  communication also implies a transformation" (pp. 1356, 1364).
- **Meaning is retrospective.** "action contributes to informing a system
  that, retrospectively, gives that action its meaning" (p. 1357).
- **Organizations are not consensus.** "Coexisting meanings of the same
  action indicate that several individuations are ongoing" (p. 1358),
  against Weick.
- **Scaling up dissolves.** The problem of scaling from individual to
  collective agency "vanishes the moment communication is viewed
  eventfully" (p. 1364). This is asserted, with the MSF meeting as
  illustration.

## What this does not say

- **That the proposal is right.** Whether the treatment changes any
  analysis's verdict is [THEORY-1278](THEORY-1278.md)'s question.
- **That information-theoretic communication is wrong.** The paper sets
  the transmission view aside; it does not engage it.
- **How to bound the notion.** With pistons communicating movement,
  "communication" covers every causal continuity.
