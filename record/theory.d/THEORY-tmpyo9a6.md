# THEORY-tmpyo9a6: For empirically equivalent dual theories read naively, adding the common core as a candidate world does not break the underdetermination, and neither do super-empirical virtues or an overarching theory: each route to a single actual world needs a further metaphysical principle, such as the identity of indiscernibles

## Source

Le Bihan & Read (2018), [LIT-113](../literature.d/LIT-113.md) (*Philosophy Compass* 13(12)), read in full
in [NOTE-152](../notes.d/NOTE-152.md) from the accepted manuscript. Page numbers are the manuscript's
printed numbers.

## What was actually shown

**The setting.** A duality is a map between the solution spaces of two
theories under which related models agree on their empirical substructures.
The map may be many-one (fn. 4, p. 3). Each model is read naively: the world
it represents is isomorphic to it (fn. 8, p. 5). Antirealism and
structuralism, including OSR, are set aside (§4).

**The taxonomy (§3, Fig. 1, pp. 7–9).** Which worlds count as candidates for
actuality gives six cases: all duals with or without the common-core world
(underdetermination, cases 1 and 2), one dual with or without it
(discrimination, 3 and 4), and the common core alone or nothing (nihilism, 5
and 6). The common-core approach is cases 1, 3 and 5, so it overlaps with
discrimination in case 3.

**The common core does not help on its own (§5).** Moving from case 2 to case
1 by adding the common-core world "has made the situation worse" (p. 12):
it adds one more empirically adequate candidate. Super-empirical virtues and
Occam's razor are "first and foremost" practical and remove no candidate.
The identity of indiscernibles would take case 1 to case 5, "but why should
we commit ourselves to such a metaphysical principle?" (p. 13).

**Nor does an overarching theory (§8.1, pp. 17–19).** If the duals' solutions
simply are solutions of a deeper theory, it adds no distinct ontology and
the underdetermination remains. If it does add one, a further principle is
still needed, and the identity of indiscernibles need not supply it, since
the deeper theory need not have less structure.

**Discrimination can be motivated case by case (§6, pp. 13–15).** For
AdS/CFT, preferring the CFT side is defensible because only it is fully
constructed. Where one dual carries gauge redundancy, the identity of
indiscernibles applied within that theory supports the other.

**What could have come out otherwise.** One of the familiar routes could have
selected a world without an extra premise. On the paper's analysis none
does.

## What this does not say

- **That dual theories are physically equivalent, or that they are not.** The
  verdict is conditional: given naive internal interpretation, a single world
  needs an extra principle.
- **That it holds for the stronger notion of duality.** De Haro & Butterfield
  ([LIT-199](../literature.d/LIT-199.md)) define duality by isomorphism of a bare theory. Their account of
  internal interpretation argues a route to case 5. This paper leaves that
  route open and does not engage it ([NOTE-152](../notes.d/NOTE-152.md), Connections).
- **That structural realism dissolves the problem.** Structuralism is set
  aside, not weighed (§4, fn. 18). The common-core approach is distinguished
  from both versions of structuralism (fn. 19).
- **That pluralism is a live option.** On pluralism, all dual structures are
  co-instantiated in one world. It is judged "prima facie consistent", with an
  overdetermination problem unanswered and an "ontological problem" named but
  not discussed (pp. 19–21).
- **That discrimination for AdS/CFT survives a full construction of the bulk
  side.** The argument depends on the bulk being known only perturbatively.

## Connections

- Belot's treatment of gauge reduction as removing physically otiose
  variables ([LIT-122](../literature.d/LIT-122.md); [THEORY-tmp58gih](THEORY-tmp58gih.md)) is the intra-theoretic move this paper
  borrows in §6 and contrasts with the inter-theoretic use in §5.
- De Haro & Butterfield ([LIT-199](../literature.d/LIT-199.md)) give the common-core construction the
  paper assumes (fn. 13).
- THEORY-tmpyus6z files their verdict for the stronger notion: in the cases
  they examine, inequivalent duals are not empirically equivalent, and a
  cautious realist commits to the common core. That is not a rival to this
  account, which is conditional on empirical equivalence and naive reading.
