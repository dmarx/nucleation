# NOTE-tmpd9gwh: Belot — Time and change in mechanics

## Contribution

The chapter examines how classical (non-quantum) physical theories represent time and change. Its main aim is to clarify the nature and scope of the "problem of time" — the knot of technical and interpretive difficulties rooted in general covariance that obstruct the quantisation of general relativity. It approaches GR by first working through better-understood cases, from Newtonian particle mechanics through field theories, gauge theories and time-dependent systems, each preparing a feature of GR responsible for the problem.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 Introduction (pp. 133–137): distinguishes claims about time as a feature of spacetime (solutions) from time as encoded in the symmetries of the laws; the chapter focuses on the latter, noting that in GR the laws have an infinite-dimensional symmetry group while generic solutions have none.
- §§2–4 (Hamiltonian/Lagrangian mechanics, symplectic and presymplectic geometry, Lagrangian field theory): builds the apparatus — spaces of initial data vs spaces of solutions, and symmetries/conserved quantities via Noether.
- §5 (Time and change in well-behaved field theories): time is represented by a flow on the symplectic state space and changeable quantities by functions on it; the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian pictures agree.
- §6 (Complications — singular dynamics, gauge freedom, time-dependent systems): shows where the well-behaved picture breaks, preparing the GR case.
- §7.2 The Problem of Time (pp. ~209–211): defines it as the failure of the flow-based representation of time and change for GR as a theory of the whole universe; explicitly disavows the "change is an illusion" moral, recommending the reduced space of solutions, not of initial data, as the setting.
- §7.3 Finding Time in GR (pp. ~211–221): develops "geometric times" (e.g. CMC) as surrogate slicings; argues the content of GR is exhausted by the set of all its Hamiltonianisations, notes distinct choices are not expected to give unitarily equivalent quantisations, and closes that a solution likely comes "from some other direction entirely".

## Open questions

- The standard technical-philosophical reference on the problem of time; a strong anchor for anything on time, change, and gauge/symmetry interpretation in physics.
- Check the argument that the content of GR is "exhausted by the set of all Hamiltonianizations" and how it relates to Earman's and Maudlin's opposing readings of change in GR.
- Its distinction between symmetries of laws and symmetries of solutions connects to Wigner's geometric vs dynamical invariance ([LIT-tmpp3x7k](../literature.d/LIT-tmpp3x7k.md)).
