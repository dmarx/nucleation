# LIT-656: Loss Surface Simplexes for Mode Connecting Volumes and Fast Ensembling

Gregory W. Benton, Wesley J. Maddox, Sanae Lotfi and Andrew Gordon Wilson
(2021), *Proceedings of the 38th International Conference on Machine
Learning*, PMLR 139 — [ARXIV-2102.13042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.13042)

## Key takeaways

- **Modes are joined by volumes, not only curves.** SPRO trains each new
  connecting vertex θ_j to minimize the mean loss over H = 5 points sampled
  uniformly from the current simplicial complex, minus λ_j log V(K), with the
  volume from Cayley–Menger determinants (Eq. 1). Two modes and three
  connectors give a complex in which every point of the connectors' simplex
  defines a low-loss path between the modes (Fig. 3). Complexes join 4 modes
  through 3 connectors (VGG-16, CIFAR-100) and 7 modes through 9 connectors
  in 12 simplexes (VGG-16, CIFAR-10; Fig. 4).
- **The low-loss region is at least 10-dimensional.** Adding connectors
  between two VGG-16 modes on CIFAR-10, the complex keeps volume (every one of
  25 sampled models above 98% train accuracy) until the eleventh connector,
  when it collapses from about 10⁵ to about 10⁻⁴ (§4.3, Fig. 5).
- **A curve is already a family.** Treating Garipov et al.'s curve objective as
  a likelihood and fitting a SWAG posterior over the bend gives a distribution
  of connecting curves, all of low loss (§4.1, Fig. 2).
- **Simplexes make cheap ensembles.** From one trained network, ESPRO adds
  vertices one at a time (about 10 epochs each on CIFAR-10 and 20 on CIFAR-100,
  against 200 and 300 for a full run) and ensembles 25 samples from the
  resulting simplexes. A VGG-16 deep ensemble of 3 has about 6.2% error on
  CIFAR-10; the same 3 networks extended to 2-simplexes have about 5.7%
  (§5.4). It holds for ResNet-56 (with BatchNorm statistics recomputed per
  sample) and for vision transformers on CIFAR-100. With temperature scaling
  it beats deep ensembles, MultiSWA and MultiSWAG under CIFAR-10 corruptions
  (§6.2).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the batch [ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md) opened `loss-landscapes` for; "star domains and
connecting simplexes" is one of the topic's named subjects. It **extends**
Garipov et al. ([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)) directly: SPRO is their curve-finding procedure
generalized from one bend to many connecting points, and from a curve to the
simplexes the points span. Its first experiment puts a posterior over their
curve. It credits Garipov et al. and Draxler et al. ([LIT-653](LIT-653.md)) jointly
with discovering low-loss curves.

Git Re-Basin ([LIT-661](LIT-661.md)) reads this paper as having shown that "SGD
solutions form a connected volume of low loss", and Entezari et al.
([LIT-652](LIT-652.md)) as conjecturing that the volume is convex modulo permutation.
That pairing frames the question the star-domain readings in this batch
([LIT-659](LIT-659.md), [LIT-682](LIT-682.md)) ask: whether the volume is convex after
alignment, or star-shaped around some centre.

The ensembling half is an instruction for practice that the anthology could
hold, hence `anthology-candidate`. The anthology holds none of its baselines
(deep ensembles, MultiSWAG) by arXiv id.
