# LIT-tmp8vfsi: Deciding Advantageously Before Knowing the Advantageous Strategy

Antoine Bechara, Hanna Damasio, Daniel Tranel, Antonio R. Damasio (1997), *Science 275(5304), 1293–1295* — DOI-10.1126/science.275.5304.1293

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for "some Antonio Damasio". No
anthology topic holds it: it is an experiment on human decision making and
awareness, with lesion patients. It carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

[NOTE-tmphij0e](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphij0e.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-01, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the experiment the somatic marker hypothesis is cited for,
and Damasio's 1996 statement of the hypothesis ([LIT-tmpa9mef](LIT-tmpa9mef.md)) previews it in
one sentence. It is short and worth reading in full, notes included, because
the notes say less than the abstract:

- **Robust:** all ten controls and none of the six patients developed
  anticipatory skin-conductance responses (Fisher's exact test, one-sided
  P < 0.001); and the three patients who reached the "conceptual" period,
  and could say which decks were good and why, still chose the bad decks.
  That knowledge without the bodily signal did not guide choice is the
  paper's strongest finding.
- **Weaker than stated:** that controls chose advantageously, and responded
  to the risky decks, before they knew. In the pre-hunch period the
  controls' preference for the good decks was not significant (note 9); the
  SCR effect reported there is a group-by-period interaction, and no
  bad-versus-good deck difference in that period is reported (note 8).
  Controls chose the good decks significantly in the hunch period, when they
  already said they liked some decks and guessed which were risky.
  "Before knowing the advantageous strategy" therefore holds for the
  articulated strategy, and less clearly for any knowledge at all.

The awareness measure is two open questions asked every ten cards from card
20 on. Later work contested the paper on exactly that point, with more
sensitive questioning (Maia and McClelland 2004, DOI-10.1073/pnas.0406666101,
not held here and not read for this filing); a reader should take that
debate with the paper.

In this record the experiment bears on the question of what is conscious in
a decision, which the record's consciousness reading treats mostly for
machines ([LIT-056](LIT-056.md), [LIT-191](LIT-191.md)). It is a case where a bodily signal and a verbal
report separate in one person: controls had the signal before the report,
and patients had the report without the signal. Damasio's later papers on
feeling and consciousness are being filed in this record at the same time;
whether they rest anything on this dissociation is for their readings to
say.
