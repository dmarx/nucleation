summary: >-
  Keller, Muller, Sejnowski and Welling (2024), ICLR 2024, arXiv
  2309.08045. A recurrent network whose recurrence is a ring convolution
  initialised as a one-step shift (the Wave-RNN) carries travelling waves
  in its hidden state, and against a matched wave-free network (the
  identity-initialised RNN) it learns faster and keeps inputs far longer:
  more than five orders of magnitude lower loss on the copy task, the
  adding task solved at length 1000, and much more input information
  retained with the recurrence frozen. The authors read the wave field as
  a register in which propagation stops later inputs overwriting earlier
  ones; randomly initialised local recurrences learn waves in training.
  Here because A15 of the exchange "Topological protection explained"
