# QUESTION-121: Within a single mind, what plays the part of the operator model's independent scoring: what evidence reaches a judgement–observation loop, or what scores it, without passing through the interpretation it tests, and does any of it survive the loss of all action?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md) shows that, in the linearized operator model, scoring operators
independently of the dominant interpretation removes capture.
[CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) generalizes this. A judgement–observation loop is kept
honest by evidence whose arrival or scoring does not run through the
interpretation it tests. In an organization of people, an outside auditor
or a scoring rule fixed in advance can supply that. In one mind there is no
outside. Unless something inside does it, [CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md)'s objection
stands, and the anchor is action after all.

## Two things the locked-in case runs together

- **Evidence the loop did not select.** A paralysed person still sees
  what passes before their eyes and hears what is said to them. Hearing
  survives in the completely locked-in state, since Chaudhary et al.'s
  patient was trained on auditory feedback ([LIT-1200](../literature.d/LIT-1200.md)). The world acts
  on the person even when they cannot act on it.
- **Contingency between intention and outcome.** This is what Kübler and
  Birbaumer say is lost, and what they say extinguishes goal-directed
  thinking ([LIT-1199](../literature.d/LIT-1199.md)). The split is theirs: patients "may loose the
  perception of the contingency", while "passive sensory information
  processing is intact in CLIS even at the most complex semantic
  processing levels" ([NOTE-948](../notes.d/NOTE-948.md)).

[CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) needs only the first. [CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md)'s empirical form
concerns the second. So the extinction hypothesis, even if true, would not
show that the loop lacks an anchor. It would show that something other
than an anchor is lost with action.

## Candidates

1. **Unselected input.** Events in the world that reach the senses
   whether or not the loop asked for them, together with exogenous
   attention capture. What is unselected is their arrival. How they are
   read still runs through the interpretation ([THEORY-593](../theory.d/THEORY-593.md)), and a
   policy can attenuate them ([THEORY-285](../theory.d/THEORY-285.md), [NOTE-766](../notes.d/NOTE-766.md)).
2. **The body.** Visceral and other bodily states the interpretation does
   not set, mapped in the brain ([QUESTION-059](QUESTION-059.md); Damasio, [THEORY-500](../theory.d/THEORY-500.md)).
   Against: on constructed-emotion accounts, interoception is itself
   predicted ([NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)), so its scoring runs through the model.
3. **Commitment before evidence.** A prediction or judgement made, and
   held, before the evidence it is scored against arrives. The order in
   time does the work the auditor does in Regime B: what is scored was
   fixed before the interpretation it might have bent towards. Damasio's
   anticipatory skin-conductance responses, which come before the choice
   ([THEORY-603](../theory.d/THEORY-603.md)), have this shape. Against: an earlier answer's error
   carries into a later one when the two share a hypothesis space
   ([THEORY-339](../theory.d/THEORY-339.md)), so commitment can also propagate error.
4. **Processing signals.** The felt ease or difficulty of processing,
   inferred from precision rather than content ([THEORY-662](../theory.d/THEORY-662.md)). It
   scores how the interpretation is going, not what it says. Against: its
   cause is attributed to "the most salient external source", which
   brings the interpretation back in.
5. **Other people.** Testimony, correction and reports. QBism treats
   another's answer as a measurement outcome entering one's own experience,
   but one the agent elicits: "When Alice elicits an answer from Bob, she
   treats this as she treats any other quantum measurement" ([NOTE-955](../notes.d/NOTE-955.md)).
   On that reading it is action again. For a locked-in person, reports that
   arrive unasked stay open inbound, and those are candidate 1, socially
   structured. Against: whom to believe can still be selected.

## What would count as an answer

- **Which candidate works.** A loop that does not lock in, with action held
  fixed, and a demonstration that removing a given candidate makes it lock
  in.
- **For candidate 3, answered in the linearization by [QUESTION-239](QUESTION-239.md) and [CLAIM-340](../claims.d/CLAIM-340.md):**
  commitment is not equivalent to Regime B. The coupling has the sign of
  κ′ − κ̄, where κ′ is how far the interpretation bends the reading of the
  evidence and κ̄ how far it bends the committed predictions. The original
  question was: a formal result in the style of [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md): in the
  operator model, is scoring against predictions committed before the
  evidence equivalent to Regime B (γ ≈ 0)?
- **The empirical counterpart, read.** Feldman and Lynch ([NOTE-957](../notes.d/NOTE-957.md))
  find that measurement effects shrink with expertise, direct experience
  and involvement. The reason is that the person already holds formed,
  rehearsed judgements, which form where the environment mediates "strong,
  frequent, positive or negative outcomes". That favours candidate 3
  (commitment before evidence) over candidate 1, and it locates the source
  of the commitments in experience with outcomes, which leans toward
  [CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md). Morwitz et al.'s report that mere measurement weakens
  with prior experience ([LIT-1189](../literature.d/LIT-1189.md)) is known only from its abstract.
