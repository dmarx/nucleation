# QUESTION-tmpq93du: When does cue-dependent retrieval make bounded reasoning better, and when does it produce anchoring, path dependence or entrenchment?

## Why it is a question

A20 §5: "A bounded interpreter cannot consider all potentially relevant
information on every decision. It needs a way to prioritize retrieval.
Anchors, contexts, and initial hypotheses can provide that
prioritization. But prioritization makes judgment path-dependent." And
"An anchor makes information easier to reach. A bias can emerge when what
is easier to reach is mistaken for what deserves greater weight."

The selective-accessibility account of anchoring (Strack and Mussweiler,
[LIT-tmpkn9x0](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkn9x0.md) and [LIT-tmpzxnap](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzxnap.md), unread) is the established mechanism, as A20 said: "we
wouldn't be discovering retrieval-mediated anchoring from scratch." In
the record, [CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) says anchors entrench. This question asks when
the cost [CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) names is paid, and when the benefit [CLAIM-182](../claims.d/CLAIM-182.md) names is.

An answer separates retrieval, evaluation and judgement under a varied
frame, and says for which tasks and cue policies selection helps.
[CLAIM-tmp23r9z](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp23r9z.md) keeps the answer from being "always bias".
