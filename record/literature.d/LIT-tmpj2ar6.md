# LIT-tmpj2ar6: Constitutive relevance & mutual manipulability revisited

Craver, C. F., Glennan, S., & Povich, M. (2021). Constitutive relevance & mutual manipulability revisited. *Synthese*, 199, 8807–8828 — DOI-10.1007/s11229-021-03183-8

## Key takeaways

*Not read.* From the published abstract, read on the Springer page on
2026-11-01.

- **The definition.** "Entities or activities are constitutively relevant
  to a phenomenon when they are parts of the mechanism responsible for that
  phenomenon."
- **The criticism.** The critics argue that applying Woodward's account of
  intervention to constitutive relevance "conflates causation and
  constitution, thus rendering the account incoherent". The authors reply
  that the criticism comes from "failing to distinguish the semantic,
  epistemic, and metaphysical aspects of the problem".
- **The amendment.** MM becomes "a refined epistemic criterion, the
  'matched interlevel experiments' (MIE) account". This is "grounded in
  the plausible metaphysical thesis that constitutive relevance is causal
  betweenness".

## Standing in the record

This is the text that would settle whether [CLAIM-tmpg57ci](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpg57ci.md) is right that
the manipulability conditions take the mechanism's boundary as given.
"Causal betweenness" is the part to check. If a constituent is whatever
lies causally between a phenomenon's input and its output, then an
outside controller that closes the production loop lies between them
too. On that reading, betweenness alone does not put the controller
outside the system any more than mutual manipulability does. That is
the question for [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md) and [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md). Until a copy is read it is
only a question.
