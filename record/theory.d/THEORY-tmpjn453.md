# THEORY-tmpjn453: On Kane's account, self-forming actions occur under motivational conflict, when tension moves the relevant brain regions away from thermodynamic equilibrium, "a kind of stirring up of chaos" that makes them sensitive to micro-indeterminacy at the neuronal level, so that the choice is undetermined

## Source

Kane (2009), [LIT-tmpmi6rp](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmi6rp.md), pp. 39–41; read in [NOTE-tmpku07s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpku07s.md).

## What was actually shown

Nothing empirical. The paper states: "There is tension and uncertainty in our
minds about what to do at such times, I suggest, that is reflected in
appropriate regions of our brains by movement away from thermodynamic
equilibrium—in short, a kind of 'stirring up of chaos' in the brain that makes
it sensitive to micro-indeterminacies at the neuronal level" (p. 39), and adds
two crossing recurrent networks carrying the competing motives (p. 40).

## What this does not say

- **That such sensitivity gives control.** Kane concedes it lessens control
  (p. 43; [THEORY-tmpjkav4](THEORY-tmpjkav4.md)).
- **That the indeterminacy is all endogenous.** The account needs it to be
  ([NOTE-tmpku07s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpku07s.md), Bearing).
