# NOTE-tmp12jp1: boyd (2008), Taken Out of Context: collapsed contexts

## Contribution

An ethnography of how American teenagers use social network sites,
organised by the properties of networked publics and three dynamics they
produce. For this record the contribution is the second dynamic, collapsed
contexts, and the evidence that self-presentations which differ by
audience are trouble only when the audiences meet.

## Key insight

A person keeps different presentations for different audiences, and the
difference is not a defect while the audiences are kept apart. What
networked media change is not the presentations but the boundaries: they
bring the audiences together, and the presentations then collide.

## What it says

- **The dynamic** (p. 34): "Collapsed contexts: the lack of spatial,
  social, and temporal boundaries makes it difficult to maintain distinct
  social contexts."
- **The paradigm case**, after Meyrowitz (1985: 43) (pp. 36–37). Stokely
  Carmichael used "very different rhetorical styles when speaking to
  blacks and whites". Broadcast forced a choice: "There was no neutral
  speaking style". He "was able to maintain distinct styles as long as he
  was able to segment social groups, he ran into trouble when electronic
  media collapsed those social groups".
- **Collisions before networked media** (p. 38). Weddings, "in which
  context collisions are common, are frequently scripted to make everyone
  comfortable". Meeting one's boss while out with friends is awkward, "but
  since both parties are typically aware of the collision, it can often be
  easy to make quick adjustments". Online, "the performer is unaware of
  audiences from different contexts, magnifying the awkwardness and making
  adjustments impossible."
- **Distinct contexts are not deception** (p. 37). The view that "only
  those with something to hide need privacy" misses "the ways in which
  context helps people properly locate their performances". Bilingual
  speakers choose a language by context.
- **Teens and their audiences** (pp. 145–146). "Negotiating multiple
  audiences creates context collisions and teens feel more pressure when
  they are forced to contend simultaneously with different audiences,
  such as peers and parents." Their tactics are structural (privacy
  settings, false details) and social.
- **A ranking forced across contexts** (p. 225). Friends "from a sports
  team might be different from friends in geometry class. All of those
  situational distinctions are erased in the Top Friends feature. As a
  result, friends from different contexts are forced into a single spot
  for comparison."

## Assumptions

- Goffman's dramaturgy, impression management and the presentation of
  self (1959) are "at the core" of its approach to identity (p. 125).
- The analysis of collapsed contexts is Meyrowitz's account of broadcast
  media, extended to everyday people.

## Limits

- An ethnography of US teenagers in 2004–2008. Its claims about collapse
  are documented with interviews and examples, not measured.
- The Carmichael case is secondhand, through Meyrowitz, who was not read.
