# THEORY-577: Our judgments that consciousness poses a hard problem are behaviour, so explaining them is an easy problem that can be solved without mentioning consciousness; and a theory that names a mechanism as the basis of consciousness owes an account of how that mechanism produces those judgments, which IIT and biological theories struggle to give, since a functional duplicate with zero integrated information, or in silicon, would make the same reports

## Source

Chalmers (2018), "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness", *Journal of
Consciousness Studies* 25(9–10), 6–61, [LIT-425](../literature.d/LIT-425.md), read in full in [NOTE-354](../notes.d/NOTE-354.md)
(body and footnotes; the reference list sampled). Page numbers are the
journal's.

## What was actually shown

**The problem (intro, §1).** Explain problem intuitions, the dispositions
to judge that there is a gap between physical processes and
consciousness: explanatory, metaphysical, knowledge and modal intuitions
at the centre, with distribution intuitions ("robots are not conscious")
among the neighbours. Problem reports are behaviour, so "at least if we
accept that all human behaviour can be explained in physical and
functional terms", explaining them is an easy problem in the 1995 sense
([LIT-406](../literature.d/LIT-406.md)). The explanation must be topic-neutral: it may not mention
consciousness.

**Candidates (§2).** Thirteen proposals, each tested against "the problem
of belief": a mechanism that would equally make beliefs seem puzzling
explains nothing distinctive, since beliefs are also introspected, opaque
and immediately known. Introspective models are necessary and not
sufficient; the summary picture (p. 34) is opaque introspective concepts
that attribute primitive relations to primitive qualities, with a sense of
acquaintance.

**The challenge (§3).** "If a theory says that mechanism M is the basis of
consciousness, then it needs to explain how mechanism M plays a central
role in bringing about judgments about consciousness."
- *IIT:* "for every system with high integrated information there will be
  a computationally isomorphic simulated system with zero integrated
  information" that reports alike, so high Φ "plays no essential role in
  explaining phenomenal intuitions". Not a "knockdown".
- *Biological theories* (Block 2009, [LIT-632](../literature.d/LIT-632.md)): non-conscious silicon
  duplicates would share the intuitions.
- *Global workspace and first-order representationalism* can begin to
  answer, since only globally available information is reported, but leave
  the problem reports themselves unexplained. *Analytic functionalism*
  fails outright, because it makes the hard problem as unpuzzling as the
  easy ones.

**A caution about machines (fn 26).** A simple theorem-prover agent
(Muehlhauser and Shlegeris) "mirrors at least some aspects of our
phenomenal reports, while being so simple that most people would deny that
it is conscious". If that continues, "we should probably be cautious"
about tests that take puzzled phenomenal reports as evidence.

**What could have come out otherwise.** IIT or a biological theory could
have had a way to make its basis central to report. The paper finds a
prima facie problem, and notes that "various ways" of responding exist
that it does not pursue.

## What this does not say

- **That IIT or biological theories are false.** The challenge is a
  constraint, and the IIT case assumes that the zero-Φ simulation *judges*
  anything, on a functional reading of intuition. A theorist who denies
  that the simulation has judgments resists it ([NOTE-354](../notes.d/NOTE-354.md), Limitations).
- **That illusionism is right.** If the reports can be fully explained
  without consciousness, either consciousness is an illusion or its link to
  our talk looks like luck. The paper blocks the debunking argument by
  separating descriptive or modal independence from causal and
  constitutive independence (§5), concedes that "it is hard to avoid a
  sense of coincidence entirely", argues that only strong illusionism
  dissolves the hard problem (§6), and rejects strong illusionism because
  "people sometimes feel pain" (§7). The dialectic, it grants, does not
  settle it ([THEORY-633](THEORY-633.md)).
- **That the meta-problem has been solved.** The favoured picture is an
  explanandum sketch, and the only computational test reported is one
  model by others.
- **That problem intuitions are universal.** That they are widely shared is
  called "an empirically defeasible assumption", and the evidence cited is
  thin (§1.1, fn 8).

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md).** This is a constraint on what report evidence can
  establish: a basis that does not explain the reports cannot be confirmed
  by them, and fn 26 extends [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md)'s mimicry point to puzzled
  phenomenal reports, the behaviour most often proposed as hard to fake.
  It applies to any indicator list taken from a theory whose basis does not
  explain report ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md)); that application is [NOTE-354](../notes.d/NOTE-354.md)'s.
- **IIT.** An independent objection to Φ as the basis of consciousness,
  beside its ill-definedness for real systems ([THEORY-523](THEORY-523.md)). [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)
  already uses it as a ground against IIT that does not come from IIT's
  verdict on groups.
- **Organisational invariance** ([THEORY-507](THEORY-507.md)) has the same shape, a
  functional duplicate making the same judgments, and draws the stronger
  conclusion that the duplicate is conscious. Here only "no essential role"
  is drawn.
- **The exchange it continues.** Dennett's "Facing Backwards" ([LIT-397](../literature.d/LIT-397.md))
  said only a conviction is left once the functions are subtracted; this
  paper takes that conviction as its explanandum and asks realist and
  illusionist alike to explain it ([THEORY-694](THEORY-694.md), [THEORY-633](THEORY-633.md)).
