# NOTE-tmpejwjy: Gallo, Lynch and Tomy — Out of Site, Out of Mind? The Role of the Government-Appointed Corporate Monitor

## Contribution

A difference-in-differences study of 193 deferred and non-prosecution
agreements (N/DPAs) between the Justice Department and listed firms,
2001–2019, about a third requiring an on-site government-appointed
monitor. It compares monitor firms with non-monitor N/DPA firms before the
agreement, during its term ("probation") and after it, on counts of
penalised violations from Good Jobs First's Violation Tracker across 47
agencies, and on a text measure of ethics-and-compliance norms from
earnings calls.

## Key results

- **During, not after.** "N/DPA firms with Corporate Monitors commit
  18%–25% fewer violations relative to N/DPA firms that are not required to
  appoint Corporate Monitors, but only while the Monitor is on site"; "violations
  increase to pre-N/DPA levels following the Monitors' departure" (p. 5).
  In Table 5, Treat × During Probation is −0.267 (t = −2.61) in the full
  sample, −0.284 and −0.313 in the matched samples; Treat × Post Probation
  is −0.091, −0.145 and −0.022, none significant (recomputed as percent
  changes in scratch/studies/lead-101/results.json: about −23% to −27%
  during, −2% to −14% and not distinguishable from zero after).
- **No pre-trend.** "We do not find significant differences in violations
  between Monitor and non-Monitor firms prior to the N/DPA" (p. 5).
- **Norms follow the monitor.** Monitor firms "only experience an
  improvement in their ethics and compliance during the time the Monitor is
  on site—there is no long-term increase in our measures" (p. 6); a
  mediation analysis finds "only a significant indirect effect of the
  Monitor on violations through the Monitor's impact on ethics and
  compliance norms" (p. 6).
- **What persists.** "Our results in table 5, panel A–panel C, show a
  persistent effect of Board Changes and Accounting Mandate on reduced
  future violations" (Board Changes × Post Probation −0.168, t = −1.71, full
  sample; −0.280, t = −2.30, matched); but the monitor does not act through
  them: the triple interaction with Post Probation is insignificant.
- **Except for some monitors.** Monitors with prior monitoring experience
  or longer tenure "are associated with improvements in firms' commitment
  to ethics and compliance norms that persist beyond the tenure of the
  Monitor".
- **Not lighter scrutiny.** EPA inspections do not fall during monitorships,
  which the authors take against the reading that regulators simply look
  away from monitored firms.

## Assumptions

Parallel trends between monitor and non-monitor N/DPA firms, supported by
the absence of a pre-trend and by matching on agreement terms, governance
and the agreements' text. Violations are counted as penalised enforcement
outcomes attributed to the parent. The norms measure assumes that what
managers say unscripted on earnings calls tracks group behaviour norms,
"real or purported" (p. 6).

## Concepts

Corporate monitor; N/DPA; probation as the agreement's term; corporate
culture as shared values (hard to change) and group behaviour norms (easier
to change), after Kotter and Heskett.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-432, part 5 (evidence, partial).** Its first half, that a
  corporation changed only by sanction reverts when the sanction lifts, is
  what this study finds of the change a monitor makes: it lasts while the
  monitor is there. Both arms are sanctioned, so the contrast is between
  more and less sanction, not between sanction and redesign. Filed as
  CLAIM-tmpm9a3z.
- **CLAIM-431 (no separation).** Reversion after a sanction lifts is also
  what the prudence reading predicts; the study cannot tell the two apart.
  The outcome is penalised violations, so it measures how often harm became
  a sanction, not whether harm was weighed; reputation and benefit are not
  held fixed.
- **The board-change result** admits two readings, filed as alternatives
  (CLAIM-tmpuvgxw, CLAIM-tmpioexk).
- **QUESTION-265 (background).** Evidence on what in a corporation would
  outlast a threat.

## Limitations

- Listed firms only. One outcome family (penalised violations), which is
  itself the product of enforcement.
- "Post Probation" is the end of the agreement, which the authors equate
  with the monitor's departure ("Typically, the Monitor is on site at the
  firm during the probation period", p. 32); a monitorship that ended
  earlier or later is not separated.
- An insignificant post-period coefficient is not shown to be zero: the
  point estimates stay negative.
- The norms measure counts ethics words in managers' speech; the authors
  call the commitment it tracks "real or purported".
