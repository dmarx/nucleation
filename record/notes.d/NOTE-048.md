# NOTE-048: Law Dork on DHS v. D.V.D. (South Sudan removals order)

## Contribution

The post reports and criticises a Supreme Court order in the third-country-removal litigation. Eight noncitizens had been held at a US base in Djibouti after the district court ruled that their removal violated its injunction. The Court's 3 July order, granting the government's motion to clarify, allowed the removal to go ahead. Geidner quotes Kagan's concurrence (she disagreed with the earlier stay but could not see how a district court can compel compliance with an order the Supreme Court has stayed) and the Sotomayor–Jackson dissent. He sets the order alongside other events of the day: passage of the budget bill, and letters from Attorney General Pam Bondi about the TikTok law.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Holding as characterised: the district court's remedy order could not be enforced because the Supreme Court had stayed the underlying 18 April injunction on 23 June.
- Dissent quotes: the men would be turned over to South Sudanese authorities "without regard for the likelihood that they will face torture or death". The majority "creates new law on civil contempt". "Other litigants must follow the rules, but the administration has the Supreme Court on speed dial."
- Kagan's concurrence, quoted: she "continue[s] to believe" the stay was wrong but concurs.
- Author's commentary: the order further cuts back district-court authority after the prior week's ruling ending universal injunctions. He reads the Bondi TikTok letters (released under FOIA) as a sign that "the constitutional government we have operated under ... is no longer in effect". That framing is the author's own.
