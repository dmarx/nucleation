# NOTE-1645: Dannenberg et al. — Modelling DNA origami self-assembly at the domain level

## Contribution

By the authors' account the first domain-level model of origami folding
that is at once thermodynamically self-consistent, explicitly kinetic and
free of an assumed pathway, set against Arbona et al.'s single pathway with
no timescale (Sec. I). It identifies two sources of cooperativity between
staples that bind only the scaffold, loops shortened by already-bound
staples and coaxial stacking, and one anti-cooperative effect (duplex
inside a loop stiffens it); and it explains why hysteresis is asymmetric.

## Key insight

Binding and unbinding answer to temperature differently: a duplex forms at
a nearly constant rate, set by an entropic barrier, and opens at a rate
that rises steeply with temperature, set by an enthalpic one. So a system
cooled quickly or short of staples lags far below its equilibrium
transition on the way in, and barely lags above it on the way out.
Cooperativity raises the barriers and widens the gap.

## Assumptions

- **One isolated scaffold** in a constant free staple concentration
  (the experiment depletes staples "by a factor of two"; the model
  ignores it).
- **Only fully complementary domains hybridize**; no misbinding, no
  scaffold secondary structure, no explicit geometry (stacking applied
  regardless of bends).
- **Loops** as freely-jointed chains, valid over many Kuhn lengths; the
  loop exponent γ and stacking strength n are "not fitted or optimized"
  but chosen to match the scale of hysteresis (γ = 2.5, n = 2).
- **Global model** restricted to planar designs with each staple's side of
  the scaffold fixed from the design; results restricted to staples of at
  most two domains.
- **Binding rate** fixed at 10^6 /M/s, state-independent; temperature
  piecewise constant over 1 s.

## Key results

- **State space (Sec. II A).** A two-domain staple is in one of five
  states, a single-domain one in two, so "the size of the state space is"
  2^j × 5^l.
- **Barrier character (Sec. II B).** "unbinding transition rates grow
  rapidly with temperature, whereas binding rates are essentially
  constant"; "the barrier to duplex opening is primarily enthalpic, whereas
  the barrier to duplex formation is primarily entropic." Unbinding rises
  by about an order of magnitude per 2 °C (Sec. III D).
- **Global against local (Sec. II C 4, Eq. 15).** In the global model
  "changes in free energy are independent of pathway"; "The local model is
  therefore not thermodynamically self-consistent." The two give close
  numbers (Table I) because loop cost is logarithmic in length.
- **Seam cost (Sec. II C 5).** Binding a second seam staple is "less
  favourable than a contiguous 32-base-pair domain within our model",
  "even with coaxial stacking strength n = 2."
- **Baseline has no hysteresis (Sec. III A, Figs. 8a, 9).** With γ = 1.5,
  n = 0: "The model shows very little hysteresis: annealing and melting
  curves nearly overlap", where experiment shows "significant hysteresis".
  Annealing width (20 to 80% bound) about 9 °C, but one staple goes 20 to
  80% over about 2.5 °C: the overall width "represents the range of
  incorporation temperatures for individual staples".
- **Cooperativity and hysteresis (Sec. III B, Table I).** At γ = 2.5,
  n = 0, 1, 2, 3 gives hysteresis Tm − Ta of 0.4, 1.0, 1.8, 2.5 °C and
  annealing width 8.6, 6.5, 5.1, 4.2 °C. "As partially-formed regions are
  relatively disfavoured, cooperativity tends to exaggerate hysteresis (the
  system must pass through more substantial free-energy barriers to
  assemble or melt, slowing down kinetics)."
- **Uniform stabilization (Table II).** Halving or doubling the loop
  prefactor C moves Ta from 65.3 to 68.2 °C while hysteresis stays at 1.9
  to 1.6 °C and width at 5.0 to 5.2 °C.
- **Loop cooperativity removed (App. E, Table IV).** At γ = 2.5, n = 2,
  hysteresis falls to 0.8 °C (1.7 with loops, local model); at n = 0 it is
  0.2 °C for every γ.
- **Staple concentration (Sec. III D, Fig. 10a).** At 2 against 20 nM, a
  substantial drop in Ta while melting is largely unchanged: "reduced
  concentrations slow the rate of hybridization but not melting."
- **Ramp rate (Sec. III D, Figs. 10b-c).** From 0.1 to 10 °C per minute,
  Tm rises about 1.3 °C and Ta falls 5.3 °C, because "annealing rates do
  not notably increase with decreasing temperature, and so the system
  responds relatively slowly to being lowered rapidly below its
  equilibrium melting temperature." The authors question Sobczak et al.'s
  conclusion that "folding rather than unfolding was not in equilibrium".
- **Local cooperativity (Sec. III D, Fig. 11).** Removing staple X's
  neighbours lowers its incorporation temperature significantly; removing
  equivalent distant staples has very little effect; "hysteresis exhibited
  by staple X is strongly suppressed if its neighbouring staples are
  removed".
- **A forbidden move (App. A 5).** For a three-domain staple, the direct
  transition 101 → 111 "is impossible, as the central staple domain and
  the scaffold cannot twist round each other"; the model would need
  modifying to forbid it.
- **Misbinding (Sec. IV).** Off-target interactions "would have to be
  remarkably strong to cause significant effects as in the critical
  temperature range", where even a complementary domain binds
  transiently.
- **Characterization (Sec. II E).** Only SYBR Green fluorescence, "a proxy
  for the degree of hybridisation", 80 to 40 °C at 1 °C per minute,
  compared as −dI/dT with the simulated −dP/dT.

## Concepts

- **Domain-level abstraction**: each staple domain bound or unbound;
  half-bound and fully bound staples.
- **Incorporation temperature**: the highest temperature at which a
  staple's full-binding probability exceeds 50% on annealing.
- **Ta, Tm, hysteresis, width**: annealing and melting temperatures, their
  gap Tm − Ta, and the 20-to-80% annealing width ΔTa.
- **Global against local shape free energy**: loop cost over all faces of
  a planar embedding (a state function) against the smallest loop closed
  (path-dependent).
- **Local cooperativity**: a staple's binding helped by its bound
  neighbours, not by distant ones.

## Connections

Its local model is the one Dunn et al. (2015), [LIT-2135](../literature.d/LIT-2135.md), use to
predict fold distributions; this paper builds the self-consistent global
version and shows the two agree closely. It reinterprets the hysteresis
reported by Sobczak et al. (2012) as kinetic. Schneider, Möritz and Dietz
(2019), [LIT-2122](../literature.d/LIT-2122.md), offer an isothermal, domain-resolved dataset for
calibrating models of this kind and find, as this paper's loop term
implies, that loop entropy shapes the order of folding. Like the held
simulation of DeLuca et al. ([LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md), [NOTE-1561](NOTE-1561.md), [THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)) it has no
misbinding; unlike it, it finds local cooperativity and hysteresis rather
than topological traps, and it has no configuration beyond the bonding
state.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md) (feasible, reachable, reliably reachable).** Leaves open:
  the model reports occupancy, not yield, and cannot misfold.
- **[CLAIM-583](../claims.d/CLAIM-583.md) (same complex, different reachability).** Qualifies weakly:
  the same routing and target with different staple sequences (Fig. 8a
  against 8b) or neighbours (Fig. 11) change the temperatures and order of
  incorporation; reachability itself is not lost.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md) (threshold to reach against requirement to hold).**
  Supports in a kinetic, rate-dependent sense: reaching (annealing) lags
  further below equilibrium than leaving (melting) lags above it, and
  fewer staples lower Ta but not Tm. The resource is undercooling and
  time, and the model has an equilibrium state function, so this is
  hysteresis from barriers, not from a peak-cost path in the claim's sense.
- **[CLAIM-581](../claims.d/CLAIM-581.md) (peak-cost ultrametric).** Leaves open; contrast: the global
  model's free energy is a state function, the local model's is not, and
  neither is a peak-cost metric.
- **[CLAIM-591](../claims.d/CLAIM-591.md) (directed costs).** Supports at the edge: the 101 → 111 move
  is forbidden though the states differ by one domain, and binding and
  unbinding rates depend on temperature in opposite ways.
- **[CLAIM-569](../claims.d/CLAIM-569.md) (construct, maintain, transition).** Supports: a uniform
  stabilization moves where the structure holds (Ta up 2.9 °C over a
  fourfold change in C) while hysteresis, the cost of crossing, barely
  moves; concentration moves construction and not dissolution.
- **[CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md) (assemble, persist, regenerate).** Nothing new: assembly and
  thermal persistence only.
- **[CLAIM-557](../claims.d/CLAIM-557.md), [CLAIM-574](../claims.d/CLAIM-574.md), [CLAIM-570](../claims.d/CLAIM-570.md), [CLAIM-592](../claims.d/CLAIM-592.md) (observing against
  acting).** Weak: bulk fluorescence reads only total hybridization and
  cannot tell which staples or which partial structure; the model's
  per-staple incorporation resolves what the experiment cannot. No probe
  costs. [CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md) and [QUESTION-374](../questions.d/QUESTION-374.md): no bearing.
- **[CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) (anchors entrench).** Qualifies: bound staples lower the
  loop cost for neighbours, but the effect is local and reversible at
  these temperatures, and no wrong structure can entrench in the model.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md) (scale separation makes constraints).** Mild support: a
  domain equilibrates within a narrow window (about 2.5 °C for one staple)
  while the slower ramp sets the order, and bound staples pin loops for
  later binding.
- **[QUESTION-380](../questions.d/QUESTION-380.md) (when a small compatible nucleus helps).** Bears: the
  authors find the analogy "with crystal nucleation is not so obvious",
  since staples bind only the scaffold; cooperativity arises through loops
  and stacking, and is local.
- **[CLAIM-568](../claims.d/CLAIM-568.md) (four ways a transition fails).** Heat here is the
  actuation that overcomes a barrier; the forbidden 101 → 111 move is the

- **[CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md) (restoring conditions need not restore capacity).** A
  kinetic, rate-dependent instance of hysteresis, not a bistable one.
- **[THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md) (abrupt change is not evidence of alternative stable
  states).** A caution borne out: this hysteresis shrinks with slower
  ramps; it is slow return, not alternative stable states.
- **[CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md) (the nucleation threshold of an argument).** The authors'
  doubt about crystal-like nucleation in origami bears on using origami as
  its physical analogue.

## Limitations

- **Simulation of one small tile** (166 domains) against one experiment,
  fluorescence only; parameters chosen by eye.
- **No misfolding, off-target binding, secondary structure or geometry**;
  the global model is planar only; staples of at most two domains.
- **Staple depletion** in experiment ignored by the model.
- **Hysteresis is kinetic**, ramp- and concentration-dependent, in a model
  with a unique equilibrium at each temperature: the paper claims no
  bistability or kinetic traps.
- **No yield, AFM or gel validation.**

## Open questions

- Does the asymmetry survive staple depletion and misbinding? A model with
  both, checked against a ramp-rate series, would tell.
- How much of measured hysteresis is loop cooperativity and how much
  stacking? Varying stacking by sequence design at fixed loops would
  separate them.
