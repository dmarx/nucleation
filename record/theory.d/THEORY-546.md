# THEORY-546: A person persists by psychological continuity, not by sameness of substance or of the human animal; Locke's sameness of consciousness, which he leaves undefined, meets the charges of circularity and intransitivity only when recast as overlapping chains of causally dependent psychological states, with a clause against branching

## Source

Locke (1694), "Of Identity and Diversity", *An Essay Concerning Human
Understanding* II.xxvii, [LIT-464](../literature.d/LIT-464.md), read in the sixth-edition text in
[NOTE-385](../notes.d/NOTE-385.md). The objections and repairs: Olson (2002; rev. 2023), the SEP
entry "Personal Identity", [LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md), read in [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md); and Parfit (1971),
[LIT-447](../literature.d/LIT-447.md), read in [NOTE-378](../notes.d/NOTE-378.md).

## What was actually shown

**Identity is relative to the kind (Locke §§3–8).** A mass of atoms is no
longer the same mass if one atom is added or taken away; an oak, a horse
and a man are the same while they share "the same continued life" in
"constantly fleeting particles of matter" (§6). "It being one thing to be
the same substance, another the same man, and a third the same person"
(§7).

**The criterion (§9).** A person is "a thinking intelligent being, that has
reason and reflection, and can consider itself as itself, the same
thinking thing, in different times and places". Its identity reaches "as
far as this consciousness can be extended backwards to any past action or
thought".

**Man and person come apart (§§10–15, 23).** Different substances joined by
one consciousness make one person (§10). The prince's soul, "carrying with
it the consciousness of the prince's past life", in the cobbler's body is
"the same person with the prince" and "the same cobbler to every one
besides himself" (§15). Two "incommunicable consciousnesses" acting one
body by turns are two persons (§23). "Person" is "a forensic term,
appropriating actions and their merit" (§26).

**What Locke leaves open.** "The same consciousness" is never defined.
Locke grants it is "not the same individual act" (§13), and his gloss, "a
present representation of a past action", would include false memory,
which he sees and refers to "the goodness of God" (§13). He does not
consider that consciousness reaching from A to B and from B to C need not
reach from A to C ([NOTE-385](../notes.d/NOTE-385.md), Limitations).

**The repairs.** The survey sets out three objections to a memory
criterion and their answers ([LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md) §4). It fails transitivity (the
student, the lawyer and the old woman), answered by chains of overlapping
connections. It is circular, since genuine memory presupposes identity,
answered by quasi-memory: Parfit's q-memory is memory without the
condition that the experience was one's own, which "vindicates the 'memory
criterion' of personal identity against the charge of circularity"
([LIT-447](../literature.d/LIT-447.md), p. 16). It fails dreamless sleep, answered by causal dependence
(Shoemaker): beliefs "have caused themselves to continue existing". Fission
then forces either multiple occupancy or a non-branching clause (§5), which
Parfit supplies: X and Y are the same person if psychologically continuous
and no contemporary person is continuous with either ([LIT-447](../literature.d/LIT-447.md), p. 13), as a
sufficient condition only. In the survey, psychological views are the
majority position.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The objections could have shown
that no psychological relation can be transitive, non-circular and robust
through gaps. Each has an answer the survey reports, and the open case
that remains is brain-state transfer.

## What this does not say

- **That Locke made identity a matter of memory alone.** Velleman reads him
  so ([LIT-292](../literature.d/LIT-292.md), [NOTE-268](../notes.d/NOTE-268.md)). The chapter's word is "consciousness", of
  "thoughts and actions" and of concern for one's happiness (§§17, 26),
  though every case it uses is one of remembering.
- **That Locke answered the objections.** He did not anticipate circularity
  or intransitivity; the repairs are two and three centuries later.
- **What we are.** A persistence criterion does not say what kind of thing
  persists by it. Olson's too-many-thinkers objection is that if we persist
  psychologically we are not organisms, yet the organism thinks too, so
  there are two thinkers in your chair and you cannot know which you are
  ([LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md) §6). The survey reports the replies and calls them "hard to
  defend". It is written by the leading animalist, and [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md) calls it a
  survey with a side.
- **Whether brain-state transfer counts.** Left open (Shoemaker yes, Unger
  no), and it is the case on which copying and re-instantiation turn.
- **That identity is what matters.** That is a further question
  ([THEORY-447](THEORY-447.md)), and the non-branching clause is where Parfit concludes
  it is not.
- **Where the self's boundary is.** Locke's little finger, part of me while
  consciousness reaches it (§§11, 17), is a claim about the self's extent,
  asserted "as every one who reflects will perceive".

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-043](../questions.d/QUESTION-043.md)** asks what coherence measure is conserved along a
  person's trajectory when the person persists. This account constrains the
  answer: what persists is not any content, since memories fail
  transitivity and are lost, but an overlapping chain of causal dependence
  among psychological states. That is a relation along the trajectory, not
  a conserved quantity, and it can branch, which is why a clause against
  branching has to be added.
- **Hume** ([THEORY-681](THEORY-681.md)) turns Locke's analogy between the identity of
  plants and animals and that of persons against him: both are fictions.
  His Appendix says "most philosophers seem inclin'd to think, that
  personal identity arises from consciousness", which is this chapter's
  thesis.
- **Williams** ([THEORY-531](THEORY-531.md)). The prince and the cobbler is the
  third-person telling, with its verdict; the first-person series reverses
  it.
- **The question map** ([THEORY-417](THEORY-417.md)). This is an answer to the
  persistence question, not to the characterization question that narrative
  accounts take up.
- **Floridi** ([THEORY-650](THEORY-650.md)) credits Locke with separating kinds of
  sameness question. Locke's own diagnosis, that the difficulty "rather
  rises from the names ill-used" (§28), is half of Floridi's deflation.
