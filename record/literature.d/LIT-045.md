# LIT-045: Structural Realism

James Ladyman (2007), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (ed. Edward N. Zalta et al.); substantive revision 2023-05-18* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/structural-realism/>

## Key takeaways

- The entry presents structural realism (Worrall 1989) as the attempt to keep both the no-miracles argument and the pessimistic meta-induction: what survives theory change, and what we are entitled to believe, is a theory's mathematical or relational structure. It then separates an epistemic version (we can know only structure) from an ontic version (structure is all there is). The ontic version is motivated by quantum individuality, gauge symmetry and spacetime physics, and faces the "relations without relata" objection.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry calls structural realism, by the lights of many realists and antirealists, the most defensible form of scientific realism, and surveys the field. It covers the realism debate the view responds to, the epistemic (ESR) and ontic (OSR) forms and their variants, their connections to case studies in physics and to metaphysics, logic and philosophy of mathematics, the main objections, and related structuralisms in other areas.
