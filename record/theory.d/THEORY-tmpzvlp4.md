# THEORY-tmpzvlp4: On Zelnik, Meron and Bel, in a soil-water–biomass model fitted to Namibian fairy-circle grassland the rainfall range where uniform grass and a periodic gap pattern are both stable contains a subrange of many stable hybrid mosaics, and brief droughts or wet spells that leave it add or remove gaps one domain at a time, which the authors identify with fairy-circle birth and death; repeated pulses carry the system gradually between the two states, a shift the same pulses do not cause from a uniform or strictly periodic start

## Source

Zelnik, Meron & Bel, [LIT-tmpijst5](../literature.d/LIT-tmpijst5.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmppk4i4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmppk4i4.md) from the
arXiv manuscript.

## What was actually shown

- **Continuation** (Fig. 1) gives the snaking branch of hybrid states
  inside the bistable range. With each dimensionless parameter moved by
  ±10%, the hybrid states persist (Appendix B).
- **Simulation** started from the 2004 satellite images reproduces one
  observed birth and one death under a one-year shift in rainfall (Fig.
  2).
- **Periodic pulses** move a hybrid start stepwise and leave uniform or
  periodic starts unchanged (Figs. 4 and 10). Either result could have
  come out otherwise: the pulses might have moved every start, or none.

## What this does not say

- That real fairy circles are hybrid states. The authors call their
  evidence compelling but say it "does not rule out alternative
  hypotheses".
- That real regime shifts in fairy-circle grassland have been gradual.
  This is not observed.
- Anything about hollow shrub rings, or about transitions between two
  plant types.
