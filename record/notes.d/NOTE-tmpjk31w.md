# NOTE-tmpjk31w: Crawford (2006) — Shop Class as Soulcraft (essay)

## Contribution

A case for the manual trades, and an account of deskilling as the relocation of judgment.

## Key results

1. Opening: a decline in tool use betokens a mode of life "more passive and more dependent".
2. "the degradation of work is ultimately a cognitive matter."
3. On Ford: he "destroyed the possibility of an alternative way of working".
4. Office work is next: "draining it of its cognitive elements".
5. "What ordinary people once made, they buy".

## Assumptions

Judgment is formed by experience; its relocation is a loss to the worker.

## Concepts

Craftsmanship; degradation of work; separation of planning from execution.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmp6wuql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6wuql.md)**: corroborates its premise at the level of occupations: an arrangement that does the cognitive work leaves those it serves less capable (results 2, 4). Historical argument, not a test of individual decay with disuse.
- **[CLAIM-tmpbypt7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md)**: corroborates its competitive mechanism: Ford's method made the alternative impossible (result 3).
- **[CLAIM-670](../claims.d/CLAIM-670.md)**: result 5 illustrates dependence produced by provision.
- **[CASE-tmpf71w4](../cases.d/CASE-tmpf71w4.md)**: the essay's examples of designs that leave the hard step to the learner motivate the study; they are not evidence.

## Limitations

An essay; the book's later chapters are not covered.
