# LIT-tmpazqjv: Patterns All the Way Up: Prolegomena to a Future Naturalized Metaphysics

James Ladyman (2026), *In T. Millhouse, S. Petersen & D. Ross (eds.), Dennett's Real Patterns in Science and Nature, MIT Press (2026), pp. 59–76* — DOI-10.7551/mitpress/15550.003.0006

## Key takeaways

- Ladyman restates the Ladyman–Ross metaphysics as fifteen theses drawn from scientific practice. The existence criterion is "to be is to be a real pattern", a pattern that "indispensibly figures in projectible generalisations" (p. 2). Realism is realism about natural modal structure, which is "absolute" in formal mode but "domain and scale-dependent" in material mode (thesis 4). There is no single hierarchy of levels and no assumed fundamental level (theses 10–11, fn. 9). Ontology is effective and scale-relative (thesis 12). Scientific real patterns are lossy compressions, unlike the Game of Life's; the ideal gas laws use 3 degrees of freedom for ~10²³ (thesis 14). He quotes ETMG's Principle of Naturalistic Closure verbatim and calls it "problematic" because it refers to fundamental physics (p. 8).

## Standing in the record

Filed at the owner's request on 2026-09-26, to put Ladyman and Ross's ontic structural realism and rainforest realism on the record. Their book *Every Thing Must Go* (OUP 2007) could not be obtained from a legitimate source, nor could the two founding papers (Ladyman 1998, Ross 2000); this work is one of four freely available statements from which the views are pieced together. It was filed `Deferred`, unread. [NOTE-tmpkt67x](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpkt67x.md) is the close reading of 2026-09-26, and it placed the work: **Active** — Worth reading as the most recent and most compact statement of rainforest realism by one of its two authors, and the one that explicitly revises *Every Thing Must Go*. It weakens the PNC, denies that "relational structure is fundamental" was meant as fundamentalism, and recasts real patterns modally and statistically as well as information-theoretically. Its fifteen theses are asserted and illustrated, not argued. It presupposes the book's definitions, which it does not restate.
