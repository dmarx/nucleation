# LIT-tmpybpjq: The Rubber Hand Illusion: Feeling of Ownership and Proprioceptive Drift Do Not Go Hand in Hand

Rohde, M., Di Luca, M. & Ernst, M. O. (2011). The rubber hand illusion: feeling of ownership and proprioceptive drift do not go hand in hand. *PLoS ONE* 6(6), e21659. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0021659.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpzkabf](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzkabf.md).*

- **Drift without ownership.** Felt finger position drifted toward the
  rubber hand as far when the hand was only seen (6.47 cm) as under
  synchronous stroking (about 6 cm), and the two drifts correlated
  within participants (r = 0.49). Participants did not report owning
  the hand in the vision-only condition, though that report is
  anecdotal.
- **Asynchrony, not synchrony, makes the difference.** Continuous
  asynchronous stroking for 120 s held drift at 0.98 cm against 5.9 cm
  synchronous. Interrupted every 10 s for measurement, asynchronous
  stroking gave as much drift as synchronous, while the questionnaire
  in Experiment 1 still showed ownership only for synchrony.
- **The proposed mechanism.** Seeing a hand near one's own pulls felt
  position toward it by default; prolonged asynchrony is evidence
  against the "unity assumption" that vision and proprioception belong
  together, and halts that integration. Ownership comes from a
  different, visuotactile process.
- **Drift is not a proxy.** "Conclusions about feelings of ownership
  cannot be drawn from measuring proprioceptive drift alone", and the
  same caution applies to physiological and neural correlates.
- **Limits.** Groups of ten; a visual probe of finger position, which
  the authors do not claim generalises to reaching; and in Experiment 2,
  where the critical vision-only condition was run, ownership was not
  measured by questionnaire.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request, among experimental works
building on the rubber hand illusion. It answers, in part, an open
question the record already asked twice: [NOTE-tmpt0l4z](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpt0l4z.md) (Botvinick & Cohen
1998, [LIT-tmpu9yys](LIT-tmpu9yys.md)) asks whether drift measures ownership or only
visuo-proprioceptive recalibration, and [THEORY-tmpef8lf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpef8lf.md) says it "does
not say what the drift measures". This study's answer is that drift,
measured this way, tracks seeing the hand and is suppressed by
prolonged asynchrony, not produced by the illusion. Read in full:
**Active**.

It meets the second refutation condition of [THEORY-tmpef8lf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpef8lf.md) for the drift
half of that THEORY, in one laboratory with small groups, and leaves the
questionnaire half standing; it has no suggestion control. It bears on
the drift clauses in the `promote_when` of [THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md) and
[THEORY-tmpom3z2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpom3z2.md), which count drift as a measure of ownership, and by
analogy on the drift measure of self-location in Lenggenhager et al.
2007 ([LIT-tmpwaa0j](LIT-tmpwaa0j.md)), which [THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md) leans on. It is a worked case
for [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md): a behavioural measure that correlated with report
turned out to dissociate from it. The NOTE sets this out.

Read with it: Abdulkarim & Ehrsson 2016 ([LIT-tmp7kozi](LIT-tmp7kozi.md)), which manipulates
felt hand position directly and finds ownership unchanged, the
converse dissociation. Filed in parallel by other readers: Longo et
al. 2008, Kalckert & Ehrsson 2012, and the work on demand
characteristics and suggestion (Lush 2020; Lush et al. 2020; Ehrsson et
al. 2022; Lush & Seth 2022; Roseboom & Lush 2022). It carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice.

One of the sources of [THEORY-tmpfufvf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfufvf.md) (filed 2026-10-20), which restates the rubber hand synchrony effect as a difference in reports, separates drift from those reports, and leaves open whether expectation produces the difference.
