# NOTE-1563: Kaelbling, Littman and Cassandra — Planning and acting in partially observable stochastic domains

## Contribution

By the authors' account it has two aims: "The first is to recapitulate
work from the operations-research literature [36,42,56,59,64] and to
describe its connection to closely related work in AI. The second is to
describe a novel algorithmic approach for solving POMDPs exactly" (§1,
p. 101). Reference [56] is Smallwood and Sondik. The novel approach is the
witness algorithm, whose internals were not read here.

## Key insight

An agent that cannot see the state should act on its belief about the
state, and nothing else: the belief is all of the past it needs. Once
control is over beliefs, gathering information is just another action
whose value the model computes, so "there is no distinction drawn between
actions taken to change the state of the world and actions taken to gain
information" (§1, p. 100).

## Assumptions

- Finite sets of states, actions and observations; the model ⟨S, A, T, R,
  Ω, O⟩ is known and correct (§3.1).
- Finite-horizon undiscounted or infinite-horizon discounted reward; the
  authors call discounting a convenient approximation (§6.5).
- States are enumerated, not factored, which the authors call "A weakness
  of the" methods they describe (§1, p. 100).

## Key results

- **Sufficient statistic** (§3.2, p. 106): belief states "comprise a
  sufficient statistic for the past history and initial belief state of
  the agent", citing [1,56]. "Because the belief state is a sufficient
  statistic, the optimal policy is the solution of a continuous space"
  belief MDP (§3.4, p. 108).
- **PWLC value** (§4.1, p. 110): the value of each t-step policy tree is
  linear in the belief, and the optimal value is their maximum: "So, Vt is
  piecewise-linear and convex". Projected onto belief space it yields "a
  partition into polyhedral regions", each with one optimal policy tree
  (p. 111).
- **Value of information** (§4.1, p. 111): moving to a low-entropy belief
  at a cost "is only worthwhile when the value of the information (the
  difference in value between the two states) exceeds the cost of gaining
  the information".
- **Earlier exact algorithms** (§4.4, p. 114): "Cheng [10] and Smallwood
  and Sondik [56] also try to avoid generating all of Vt+ by constructing
  Vt directly. However, their algorithms still have worst-case running
  times exponential in at least one of the problem parameters". The first
  exact finite-horizon algorithm "was described by Sondik [56,58]" (§4.5,
  p. 117).
- **Infinite horizon** (§4.6, p. 118): "This is not necessarily true for
  the infinite-horizon discounted value function"; it stays convex but may
  have infinitely many facets.
- **The tiger problem** (§5.2, p. 120): with a hidden tiger behind one of
  two doors and a listening action costing 1 that is not fully accurate, the
  optimal two-step policy "never chooses to act, only to listen".
- **Discounted** (§4.1, p. 109): the t-step policy-tree values carry the
  discount γ. The original's finite-horizon setting, possibly undiscounted,
  is not restated.
- **Scale** (§7, p. 127): even the witness algorithm "becomes impractical
  for problems of modest size (|S| > 15 and |Ω| > 15)".

## Concepts

- **Belief state**: a probability distribution over S (§3.3).
- **State estimator** SE(b, a, o): the Bayes update of the belief after
  action a and observation o (§3.2–3.3).
- **Policy tree**: a depth-t conditional plan, an action at the root and a
  subtree for each observation (§4.1).
- **Useful policy tree and parsimonious representation**: a tree that is
  optimal somewhere in belief space, and the set of only those (§4.2).

## Connections

  Sondik and one other work (p. 106);

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-153](../cases.d/CASE-153.md)** (the self-protecting sentry): supports. Its hidden binary
  world and costly inspection that reveals the state with 90% accuracy is
  the tiger problem's structure, and this paper is the standard formal
  reference for it.
- **[CLAIM-520](../claims.d/CLAIM-520.md)** (the value of keeping a corrective capacity is conditional
  on cost and use): qualifies. In a POMDP an audit is an information action
  whose value the model computes from cost and consequence, in the sense
  of the value-of-information passage (p. 111), so the claim's conditional
  value falls out of a standard model rather than needing a special one.
- **[TERM-055](../terms.d/TERM-055.md)** (operational availability, given the cue it actually has,
  before the deadline): qualifies. Its "given the cue it actually has"
  matches the belief state and its "before the deadline" a finite horizon, but a POMDP value is an
  expected reward under a belief, not a probability of timely deployment.
  The term could cite the framework; the two are not the same quantity.
- **A's characterization of Smallwood and Sondik.** That it is a
  foundational finite-horizon POMDP treatment agrees with how this paper
  credits it. That it gives "the formal apparatus for provisional dispatch
  followed by reassessment and cancellation" is A's application: nothing in
  this paper mentions dispatch, and the original's applications are unread.
  The apparatus fits the case, since a provisional dispatch that also
  gathers information is a natural POMDP action, but A omits that exact
  solution is exponential in the worst case: the frame describes the
  decision, it does not supply a tractable dispatch method at scale.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- The authors': enumerated state spaces (§1); exact solution intractable in
  general (§4.4); discounting is an approximation of convenience (§6.5).
- This reading's: §2, the witness algorithm's internals, §5.3–5.4 and most
  of §6 were not read, and the paper's account of Smallwood and Sondik has
  not been checked against the original.

## Open questions

Whether Smallwood and Sondik's own paper states the piecewise-linear
convexity result as this paper derives it, and whether it treats any
application, can only be settled by reading [LIT-2038](../literature.d/LIT-2038.md).
