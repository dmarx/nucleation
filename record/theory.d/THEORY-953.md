# THEORY-953: On Isaac's structuralist account, the strongest evidence that a quality-space model targets phenomenal experience, and not only behaviour or neural activity, is convergence between models of the same space derived through different behavioural procedures, since experience is what those procedures share; the convergence observed is coarse, with discrepancies that often exceed each method's own uncertainty, and the models average over subjects, so what they converge on is a standard observer

## Source

Isaac (2025), *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 6, [LIT-1361](../literature.d/LIT-1361.md), read in full
in [NOTE-1163](../notes.d/NOTE-1163.md) from the publisher's open-access PDF. §5 ("Making
progress on consciousness") with footnote 15, footnote 2 of §2.1, and the
closing puzzle of §6.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested in the paper. It answers the question "how can we be
sure it studies phenomenal experience at all, rather than merely patterns
of behavior and their neural substrate?" in three parts, the third its
"own considered view".

- **Relations are behaviourally manifest.** "phenomenal relations can be
  empirically investigated without independent access to phenomenal
  relata". Whether your rose looks like mine cannot be settled, but
  whether it looks closer to the poppy or to the carnation can, through
  discrimination and matching, and the patterns can be compared.
- **Introspection keeps the target.** Phenomena found by observation
  (afterimages, the Purkinje shift) guide models at both levels, and
  phenomenology is "front-loaded" into design. But intent "is also only
  part of the answer", because models such as CIELAB were built with no
  phenomenological motive.
- **Convergence.** CIELAB is derived from colour matching, "purely
  behaviorally realized judgments of indifference"; OSA-UCS from
  "explicitly reflective and consciously assessed judgments of sameness of
  distance". Their metrics agree coarsely, with "some degree of
  distortion" (Oleari 2001, cited). "To the extent that they do exhibit
  the same structure, however, the fact that they are derived from
  different kinds of behavior, detouring in different ways through
  conscious phenomenal experience, constitutes evidence that they target
  experience itself." The same is proposed for odour (descriptor atlases
  against perfumers' practice) and timbre (similarity judgements against
  descriptors): experience "is the least common denominator".

The paper states the counter-evidence itself. "observed convergence is
never perfect", and "in many cases, the discrepancies between different
methods ... are greater than the levels of uncertainty each method asserts
individually" (also footnote 2). Some take this as evidence against a
well-defined metric (MacLeod and Willen 1995 for visual space; Decock 2006
for colour; Barwich 2019 for smell). Isaac's reply is that convergence is
"a regulative ideal": the structuralist is committed to explaining and
correcting the discrepancies. And in §6 the models "rely constitutively on
averaging over subjects", so "the target is a kind of ideal, a 'standard
observer'", which he leaves as a puzzle.

What could have failed: convergence could have been absent, and for visual
space and odour the critics say it is.

## What this does not say

- **Not that convergence excludes the rival explanations.** Two procedures
  can agree because they share the stimulus's physical geometry, or a
  common decision and response process, without sharing an experience.
  The "least common denominator" argument names experience as the common
  factor and does not rule these out. That is the gap the promote_when
  asks to close.
- **Not that convergence has been shown.** It is coarse for colour, the
  best case, and disputed elsewhere.
- **Not a measure.** The paper gives no degree of agreement that would
  count as enough, and no account of which discrepancies are corrigible.
- **Not about individuals.** Averaged models target a standard observer;
  whether any individual's experience is reached is left open.
- **Not about whether a state is conscious**, only about whether a model
  of character targets experience. Blindsight and masking are raised as
  the worry, and the reply is the three parts above, not a test for
  consciousness.
