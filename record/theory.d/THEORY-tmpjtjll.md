# THEORY-tmpjtjll: When mental control returns after mind-wandering, the self-model represents the return as the person's own act whether or not the person's control system caused it, because it must bridge to earlier control to keep the autobiographical self-model continuous

## Source

Metzinger (2015), [LIT-tmp0w298](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0w298.md), pp. 289–292, read in [NOTE-tmprpztl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmprpztl.md);
Metzinger (2013), [LIT-tmp23d7b](../literature.d/LIT-tmp23d7b.md), pp. 4–5 and 11, read in [NOTE-tmp97akw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp97akw.md);
Metzinger (2017), [LIT-tmppbt0s](../literature.d/LIT-tmppbt0s.md), pp. 6 and 20–21, read in [NOTE-tmpk5ghu](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpk5ghu.md).

## What was actually shown

**The hypothesis.** "the re-emergence of an EAM, really triggered by
unconscious events, may necessarily involve a confabulatory element ('I
generated this insight myself!') in order to ensure the coherence of the
autobiographical self-model over time. In order to be able to conceive of
myself as an autonomous mental agent again, I simply must have been a
mental agent in the preceding conscious moment too, because I had the
ability, the potential, all the time. The transition must have been
self-caused, because a 'representational bridge' has to be built to
earlier instances of M-autonomy" (2015, p. 291). "If this is true, an
illusory phenomenology of self-causation will be a necessary
neurocomputational fiction in the construction of any new EAM" (pp.
291–292).

**The epistemic point.** "the phenomenology of M-autonomy does not justify
the claim that the functional property of M-autonomy was actually present"
(2015, p. 290). A meditator's "I have just realized that I was daydreaming
and redirected my attention" "may be false" as a description of what did
the work: it "may sometimes not be a personal-level event at all, but a
shift in the subpersonal self-model that is then misdescribed" (2013, p. 4;
2015, p. 289).

**The evidence offered.** Schooler et al. (2011, Box 1), after Wegner
(2002): "regaining meta-awareness may be accompanied by an illusion of
control" (2013, p. 11; 2015, p. 291). Neither was read here.

**The generalization.** In 2017 the sense of origination in any agency is
"the activation of an internal self-model trying to explain away the
surprise involved in suddenly achieving autonomous self-control", and the
adult's model of self-causation "could therefore be an enculturated post-hoc
confabulation", installed by caregivers who ascribe veto and origination to
children before autobiographical memory begins (pp. 6, 20–21).

**What could have failed.** The argument from coherence could have allowed
the self-model to represent an outside cause; it does not, because it
cannot "consciously simulate myself as having unconsciously known about my
ability" (2015, p. 291). That is an argument about what the model can
represent, not an observation.

## What this does not say

- **That every return is confabulated.** "at least in many such cases"
  (2015, p. 291); which cases is not said.
- **That the person never ends an episode.** On the 2015 reading the
  ability is kept throughout ([THEORY-tmptbvmq](THEORY-tmptbvmq.md)); the claim is about the
  attribution of a particular return.
- **That the confabulation is harmful.** It is "functionally adequate", a
  condition of a coherent self-model over time.
- **That experience is unreal**, or anything about phenomenal
  consciousness. The misdescription is of a cause.
