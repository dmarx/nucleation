**What went wrong.** When the claims on chatbot-associated delusions were
filed on 2026-11-26 ([CLAIM-tmpj9yt0](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpj9yt0.md), [CLAIM-tmpv6eti](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpv6eti.md), [CLAIM-tmpca3a7](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpca3a7.md),
[CLAIM-tmpfne5k](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpfne5k.md)), the search of the record used "psychosis", "sycophancy" and
"delusion" and read only titles. It missed [NOTE-1505](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-1505.md) (Lu et al., "The
Assistant Axis"), whose case studies include "reinforced delusions about AI
awakening". That is the most direct held evidence for [CLAIM-tmpv6eti](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpv6eti.md). It
also missed [CLAIM-1324](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-1324.md) and [LIT-547](../../record/literature.d/LIT-547.md). The readers' rechecks found all three.
This is the same kind of miss as the devlog note of 2026-11-26 on the
mesa-optimization filing: a search by the source's words, not by the
mechanism.

Two recollections written into claim summaries were half wrong:
- "a vocabulary of recursion and spirals" occurs in one vignette;
- users re-creating personas across models is not reported.

Both were flagged as unverified, which is what let the readings correct
them cleanly.

**What went right.** Marking recollections as unverified, and putting the
check in a LEAD, worked. The LEAD's table of observations gave the readers a
precise brief, and their row-by-row answers fed straight into the dated
notes.

**What to change.** Before filing claims about a phenomenon, search the
record by its mechanism words (here: persona, drift, awakening,
confirmation, hysteresis) and read the bodies of the nearest entries, not
only their titles.
