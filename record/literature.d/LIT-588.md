# LIT-588: A theoretical model of phase transitions in human hand movements

Hermann Haken, J. A. Scott Kelso and Heinz Bunz (1985), *Biological
Cybernetics* 51(5):347–356.

Read in full on 2026-10-30 (NOTE-tmp0jngz), from the publisher's scan on
Kelso's lab page.

## Key takeaways

- **The phenomena modelled (§2, p. 348).** Four features of Kelso's
  experiments (1981a, 1984), summarized and not shown: only two stable
  phase relations; an abrupt switch from anti-phase to in-phase at a
  critical cycling frequency; only in-phase beyond it; and, when frequency
  is lowered again, no return to anti-phase. The transition frequency
  divided by each subject's preferred frequency was constant across
  subjects (p. 347).
- **The order-parameter equation (§2, pp. 349–351).** φ̇ = −∂V/∂φ with
  V = −a cos φ − b cos 2φ, chosen as the simplest periodic, symmetric
  potential. Anti-phase is lost when |b| < |a|/4; b is tied to movement
  amplitude, which falls as frequency rises.
- **The derivation (§3, pp. 351–353).** Two self-sustained oscillators
  (a Van der Pol variant whose amplitude falls as 1/ω) with a coupling
  that must be nonlinear and must involve a time delay or time
  derivatives; Eq. (3.26) recovers the φ equation, with a critical
  amplitude. Noise is needed for a transition to start (Eqs. 3.28–3.29).
- **What it does not report.** Critical slowing and critical
  fluctuations are named as phenomena "that will also be analyzed" in
  future studies (p. 355). They were reported the next year, in Kelso,
  Scholz and Schöner (LIT-tmp8grr0).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the coordination-dynamics
part of the agency batch, as the canonical citation for the HKB model:
Raja et al. (LIT-598) cite it for coupled oscillators measured by relative

*Dynamic Patterns* (LIT-578) is the book-length treatment. The first-hand
data on fluctuations and on intentional switching are in two Physics
Letters A papers filed the same day (LIT-tmp8grr0 and LIT-tmpupvec). It
bears on THEORY-069. No instruction for machine-learning practice.
