# SYNTHESIS-045: Resource amount does not fix assembly success

*A synthesis of a part of the realizability line: the part filed from the DNA origami readings of 2026-11-05, A's four syntheses on DNA origami pasted by the owner the same day, the assistant's constructions on them, and the studies the lead runs of 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-23 executed for its questions. The line's synthesis is built over it and its sibling parts; SYNTHESIS-042, reaching under resources, holds CLAIM-562 too. Version 3 is written under ADR-151. It adds nothing. Its standing is its components'.*

## The question

When does establishing a small, internally compatible subsystem make a
coherent global organization more likely, and can its effect be predicted
from the constraint architecture (QUESTION-380, A's)? Behind it is the
part's wider question: if not the amount of resource, what fixes whether
an assembly reaches its target?

## The argument

**The amount of resource does not fix success.** Where the resource that
pays for crossing barriers also sets the costs, as heat does in DNA
origami, the target can be reached only in a window of levels, and a
schedule can reach what no constant level reaches; budgeted reachability
with fixed costs is the special case (CLAIM-594, constructed in
CASE-216). Whether more of every resource helps turns, at each branch
point, on whether the competing steps consume further resource, as excess
staples block a fold (CLAIM-598, computed in CASE-217). At equal cost,
where the resource is placed matters, and at equal integrated exposure,
when (CASE-215).

**A productive nucleus is defined by what its establishment does
later.** A productive nucleus is a partial organization whose
establishment, against a declared target, context, horizon and
comparator, causally improves forming the target, distinct from a
structural seed and a kinetic nucleus, and a high committor does not by
itself show it (TERM-117, A's). Two consequences are demonstrated.
Persistence is not productivity: two intermediates of equal residence
time can have different committors, since a state's exit rate cancels
from its committor, though for success by a deadline the independence
fails (CLAIM-595); stabilizing a nucleus past an optimum can make it
easier to form and lower what forms (CASE-218). And yields cannot tell
a nucleus that speeds a later step from one that only gives a head start
(CLAIM-596), which bears on the route QUESTION-397 asks about.

**Adding to a working set can make the target less likely.** In
self-folding sheets, where an intervention acts predicts success better
than how much intervention there is, and an added actuator can stop a
fold that succeeded (CLAIM-562, from Stern, Pinson and Murugan). Because
establishing more relations can lower the chance of the target, two
productive nuclei can combine into a trap, and the productive sets are
not closed upward (CLAIM-599, resting on CLAIM-562). Two executed models
now show it without the incompatibility being built in. Running
CASE-219's design and its variant (CASE-374), each of two alternative seams alone is a
productive nucleus and both together a trap, and the committor ranks a
one-bond nucleus above a four-bond misfold, a separation a count of
formed bonds cannot register (CLAIM-969). In a kinetic constraint-growth
model of another kind (CASE-389), adding a seed already in its target
state, with every set still compatible with the target, can halve the
chance of exact assembly, and island coverage ranks seed sets better
than their number, so both of the sheets' findings reappear out of
sample (CLAIM-1085).

**No predictor computed from the architecture alone has transferred.**
Island coverage of the folding-island kind is monotone in the seed set,
so it cannot flag a superset that lowers the chance (CLAIM-562's note of
2026-11-07). The race reading of folding islands, pre-registered as its
non-monotone replacement, fails the registered test in CASE-389: the
seeds that hurt there have large islands, and the errors come from the
order in which components reach the binding threshold (CLAIM-1205). A
post hoc count of the states each component could still take when it
first reaches that threshold meets the criterion on fresh architectures
of the same system, with weak flags (CLAIM-1204); it is a surrogate for
scoring by the committor, computed without solving the dynamics
(CLAIM-1204, on CLAIM-969). What this shows is filed both ways. On the
filer's lean, whether a seed set helps is decided by what is forced
when each component first binds, so a predictor must take the order and
threshold of growth among its conditions (CLAIM-1090). Against it, the
tested system has no coherent alternative branch for a small-island
seed's territory to fall into, which is how the sheets misfold, so the
race reading's home case is untested (CLAIM-1078). Choosing is the
owner's call.

**In organizations, the evidence bears on where, not on more being
worse.** Open evaluations coded under rules fixed before coding found one
case of equal budgets at different places (CASE-406): in Andhra
Pradesh's schools, arms of equal yearly cost differed threefold. Read as
placement, where a budget enters can matter more than its size
(CLAIM-1160, the filer's lean); read otherwise, place is confounded with
a conditional instrument, and the case is an example, not a test
(CLAIM-1107). None of the coded cases has a larger set of interventions
doing worse than a smaller one, and the one nested comparison went the
other way, so the organizational form of CLAIM-562's second half is not
shown (CLAIM-1063). None gives a predictor computed from the
organization's structure.

## Where it stands

The constructions are firm: CLAIM-594, CLAIM-595, CLAIM-596, CLAIM-598
and CLAIM-599 are Active demonstrations. The executed studies, CASE-374
and CASE-389, are the assistant's models, and what they ground is
Proposed. QUESTION-380 stays open: no predictor has been tested across
systems and passed, and the question's home case, a system with coherent
alternative branches, has no study (LEAD-183 was carried out on another
kind). QUESTION-397 asks by which route a partial organization helps;
CASE-269 (first line distributed-agency) and CASE-308 (first line
transient-support) are designs for it, the second run. QUESTION-400 asks
whether reallocating a fixed stock selects which form results; CLAIM-969
bears on it. Five questions ask whether the origami readings the part
rests on reproduce: QUESTION-402, QUESTION-410, refined by QUESTION-445,
QUESTION-419 and QUESTION-428. CASE-219 and its variant CASE-220 are A's designs,
run in a model of the assistant's in CASE-374. Outside the line, CLAIM-1218
proposes that a meme can be a nucleation site (SYNTHESIS-125).

## Not stated on the record

- **Productive sets and budgets.** That a set productive at one supply
  can be unproductive at a larger one, so productivity is monotone
  neither in the set (CLAIM-599) nor in the resource (CLAIM-598). It
  follows at once from CLAIM-598's falling yield; trivial, not filed.
- **Exposure against stock.** CASE-215 computes its timing at equal
  integrated exposure and says equal expenditure "needs the stock model
  below, and is not shown here".
- **Large islands that hurt.** No entry says whether CASE-389's harmful
  seeds, which force much and hurt through the order of binding, are the
  kinetic form of QUESTION-380's protected defect of 2026-11-15, or of
  CLAIM-595's persistence without productivity.
- **The surrogate against the committor in one system.** CLAIM-1204 calls
  its count a surrogate for committor scoring, but the count was tested
  in CASE-389 and the committor computed in CASE-374. No entry says
  whether the count ranks CASE-374's seams and their trap as the
  committor does.

## What it does not say

That DNA origami folds by the toys' mechanisms or at their numbers; that
organizations have constraint architectures of these kinds, which each
claim says needs a model of its own; that adding an intervention ever
makes an organization fail; or anything with standing of its own.
