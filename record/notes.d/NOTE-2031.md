# NOTE-2031: Rubel, Castro and Pham (2021) — Agency Laundering and Information Technologies

## Contribution

A five-condition definition of agency laundering (§7.2) that turns on an accountability view of responsibility and separates laundering from honest delegation by its fifth condition, the failure to account. The wrong is not that values are built into systems: the concern is "not merely that values are instantiated within such systems" (p. 138).

## Key results

1. p. 143, the definition's third and fifth conditions: "a ascribes (implicitly or explicitly) morally relevant qualities to b’s actions"; "(5) in virtue of (4), a fails to adequately account for events within X".
2. p. 143, the defining feature: the chair "are forestalling others’ ability to demand an account for the chair’s actions within" a domain of their legitimate concern.
3. p. 144, n. 21: "Note that agency laundering does not require that one infringe one’s substantive role responsibilities".
4. pp. 151–152, Uber's surge prompts launder and its goal reminders do not: "Surge periods are not a natural feature about the world that" Uber measures, and "Uber’s claim that it is simply the market’s doing is an inadequate account".
5. p. 155, the Wisconsin Supreme Court's Loomis rule keeps the trial court's account: "Loomis addresses condition (5), and it is not a case of agency laundering."
6. p. 157: invoking the "idea of a responsibility gap is a mechanism by which people may launder their agency."
7. pp. 158–160: "any process or socio-technical system can be a mechanism for laundering"; humans in the loop "can be a means of laundering just as well as automation itself."
8. p. 161, left open: "can a person who uses a tool to make decisions launder their agency" without intending to.

## Assumptions

Responsibility as accountability: being open to demands for an account of one's reasons and intentions (§7.1.2, after Strawson, Oshana, Smith). Moral responsibility as the conjunction of role, causal and capacity responsibility (§7.1.1). Only justifiable roles confer role responsibility (n. 8). A collective can be responsible; if it cannot, its members launder (§7.3).

## Concepts

Agency laundering; role, causal and capacity responsibility; forestalling demands for an account; Chair and Democratic Chair; preemptive laundering; bias laundering (Cegłowski) and masking (Barocas and Selbst), both subsumed; humans in the loop (Wagner); moral crumple zone (Elish, cited, [LIT-2665](../literature.d/LIT-2665.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)**: an independent statement of its structure for an agent's own part. Laundering is a wrong distinct from any substantive violation (n. 21), and its content is a failure of answerability: others cannot demand the account they are owed. [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md) concerns keeping violations knowable and contestable; the chapter, not obscuring one's own causal role.
- **[QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md)**: answers a version for a firm. Facebook's role responsibility "is not reducible to any particular individual developer"; if collective responsibility does reduce, the members launder. Whether laundering can be inadvertent is left open (§7.8).
- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: touches its blame-placement half only, and conditionally. A human in the loop with "too little time to respond" could be a means by which "the moral responsibility of the larger entity" goes unaccounted for, though systems like Wagner's and Elish's could "launder agency (though not necessarily)." Nothing on credit for invisible compensation.
- **The exchange** (A19 §1, A26 §V) presents laundering as human value choices appearing as impersonal technical outputs. That loses condition (5): an algorithmic output its deployer still fully accounts for is not laundering (Loomis), and the mechanism need not be technological.

## Limitations

Conceptual analysis; the cases are taken from journalism and court records. Facebook's role responsibility is argued for, not established. Intent, degree and scale are left open.

## From the reading of 2026-11-17

*Quotations here are carried from the reading of 2026-11-17, whose filer rechecked them against its copy on 2026-11-18; they were not rechecked at the reconciliation.*

The second reading used the open-access PDF of the whole book and read
chapter 7 with the acknowledgements and the contents; §§7.3, 7.5 and 7.6
quickly. It adds these.

- **The 2019 article**: the book's acknowledgements say the chapter
  reproduces it "in large part". Whether the discussion of Elish and humans
  in the loop (§7.7.2) is in the article is not known.
- **The concept** (p. 137): "obfuscating one's moral responsibility by
  enlisting a technology or process to take some action and letting it
  forestall others from demanding an account for bad outcomes that
  result."
- **The definition in full** (p. 143): "(1) a is morally responsible with
  respect to some domain X, and (2) a ensures that b (some process, person,
  or entity) has some causal responsibility with respect to X, and (3) a
  ascribes (implicitly or explicitly) morally relevant qualities to b's
  actions (e.g., relevance, neutrality, reliability), and (4) in virtue of
  (2) and (3), a obscures the scope of their causal responsibility with
  respect to X, and (5) in virtue of (4), a fails to adequately account for
  events within X for which they are morally responsible."
- **The wrong** (p. 144): "the laundering part of agency laundering cuts
  straight to the heart of what responsibility is by undermining the
  ability of others to ask the agent to provide an account."
- **Uber's goal reminders and queuing** fail condition (4); "it is the
  decision to deploy the system that requires an account" (p. 157); the
  mechanism "need not be technological at all" (p. 160).
- **Elish, inverted** (p. 159): "In our conception, humans' causal
  responsibility could obscure the causal responsibility of a technical
  system."
- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**, its upstream half: an agent responsible in a domain can,
  through a process, obscure its causal role and escape the demand for an
  account, and a human in the loop can be the laundering medium. It also
  qualifies the claim: laundering needs a misleading ascription and a
  failed account, and whether it can be inadvertent is left open, while the
  claim says "Neither concept requires a conscious mastermind". The first
  reading judged the bearing conditional (above); the relation is written
  for the upstream half only.
- **[CLAIM-472](../claims.d/CLAIM-472.md)** (choosing an apparatus is open to evaluation): Democratic
  Chair and the COMPAS discussion put the account owed in the decision to
  deploy. Corroborates.
- **[TERM-tmp8pnnr](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8pnnr.md)**: the chapter's role, causal and capacity responsibility
  is a neighbouring taxonomy of senses, with no constitutive sense.
