# QUESTION-068: Would settling whose a will-organization is settle the agent's accountability for what it does, or does accountability need conditions, such as normative competence and avoidability, that ownership does not supply?

## The question

[QUESTION-011](QUESTION-011.md) asks what makes a will-organization the agent's own. This question asks what an answer to it would buy. If ownership were settled, would accountability follow? Or does accountability need further conditions, such as being able to recognise the right reasons or having been able to act otherwise, which ownership does not give? Filed Deferred, as its line is.

## Why the record needs it

[CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md) disclaims ownership: explaining practical efficacy does not settle whose the will is. [CLAIM-016](../claims.d/CLAIM-016.md) tried to supply ownership and was deferred. Both assume that ownership is the missing piece that would make a will-organization something an agent answers for. If accountability comes apart from ownership, two things follow. Reopening the authenticity line would not by itself deliver responsibility. And the disclaimer in [CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md) would leave less open than it seems to.

## What the record holds

- [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md): identification is not enough. JoJo "meets the structural conditions" and is excused for lacking normative competence. Limit: second-hand, and the primary texts are unread.
- [THEORY-147](../theory.d/THEORY-147.md): attributability and accountability are "independent kinds" of responsibility. Accountability needs only that the agent acted as she ought not, was in a position to know it, and that it was up to her. Limit: the positive view is Wolf's "hunch".
- [THEORY-118](../theory.d/THEORY-118.md): blame needs the ability "to have acted on the right reasons", and praise does not. So any avoidability condition applies to blame alone. Limit: second-hand, and it gives no answer to Frankfurt cases of wrongdoing.
- [THEORY-119](../theory.d/THEORY-119.md): responsibility needs no alternative possibilities when "what made the action unavoidable played no part in bringing it about". Limit: the record has read the dilemma defence and the replies only in survey.

## What would answer it

A case in which an agent's will-organization is plainly her own and she is still not accountable, or the converse, whose verdict survives the dispute between [THEORY-118](../theory.d/THEORY-118.md) and [THEORY-119](../theory.d/THEORY-119.md). Or an argument that ownership already contains the competence and control conditions. A further survey of Frankfurt cases would not answer it.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Zeiser, read

Zeiser (2024, [LIT-2666](../literature.d/LIT-2666.md), [NOTE-2007](../notes.d/NOTE-2007.md)) holds that "who is to blame?", "who made the decision?" and "who is in a position justify this decision?" can have different answers, and gives ordinary cases: "Sometimes, for example, the right person to blame didn’t actually make the decision", and a user relying on a black-box tool may lack answerability and still deserve blame for negligence. In decision support, a human can be the accountable decision-maker of a decision whose values are not hers (the attributability gap), and he treats "responsibility without attributability" as a coherent aim where blame and liability are what matter. This is evidence for the question's second horn, that accountability does not follow from ownership. It leaves open the question's real target, whether ownership of a will-organization (not of a single decision's values) would supply the competence and control conditions.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Watson's fairness and standing conditions, through King, and Strawson's exemptions, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmptax6l THEORY-tmpjhphq LIT-2718 — new or Unavailable; the readings filed on 2026-11-18 and the unread paper one quotes -->

King 2013, read ([NOTE-tmplo4r7](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplo4r7.md), [THEORY-tmptax6l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmptax6l.md)), quoting Watson 1996 ([LIT-2718](../literature.d/LIT-2718.md), unread); Strawson 1962, read ([NOTE-tmpnfib1](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpnfib1.md), [THEORY-tmpjhphq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpjhphq.md)). Both THEORYs now carry `bears_on` this question.

The question asks whether accountability needs conditions, "such as normative competence and avoidability, that ownership does not supply". Two readings say yes, in different ways.

- **Avoidability, in Watson's words as King quotes them**: "[i]t is unfair to impose sanctions upon people unless they had a reasonable opportunity to avoid incurring them" (237). King adds a condition on the one who holds accountable: "if I lack the standing to hold you to a legitimate demand, then you cannot be accountable to me" (p. 8). And the faces come apart: "one can have something attributable to them that one is not accountable to others for. Watson makes this plain" (p. 13).
- **Competence, in Strawson's exemptions**: pleas such as "He's only a child" or "His mind has been systematically perverted" "invite us to suspend our ordinary reactive attitudes towards the agent" (p. 193), whoever's act it was.

Neither settles the question for will-organizations; both are evidence that settling whose a will is does not settle accountability for persons. The question stays Deferred with its line.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Zeiser read: for AI-supported decisions, accountability can be had without attributability
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmpzdtc8 — new; the reading filed on 2026-11-18 -->

Zeiser 2024, read ([NOTE-tmpnpm7l](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpnpm7l.md), [THEORY-tmpzdtc8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpzdtc8.md), bears_on). For AI decision support, settling whose a decision is does not settle accountability, and accountability can be had without attributability: "the right person to blame didn't actually make the decision, such as when a manager takes the blame for her employee"; and, where only "structures for blame and liability" are wanted, "we could still aim for responsibility without attributability, for example through 'blank check responsibility'" (Conclusion). He still holds that attributability gives "a more robust basis for accountability". The domain is individual decisions with tools, not will-organizations.
