# QUESTION-315: Does industrial selection, industry funding of researchers whose methods already favour its product, produce industry-favourable consensus in real research fields through those researchers' productivity and their training of newcomers, rather than through funded researchers changing their methods or results, and does an independent funder that discounts industry-funded work counteract it?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-146](../theory.d/THEORY-146.md) holds what Holman and Bruner's bandit model shows: honest
Bayesian researchers who differ in method, whose influence and training of
newcomers follow productivity, drift toward a worse treatment when
industry funds those whose methods already favour it, though funding
changes no one's beliefs. The paper's evidence beyond the model is one
historical case, the antiarrhythmic drug disaster, read from a secondary
history. Whether real research fields reach industry-favourable consensus
by this route, or mainly by funded researchers changing their methods or
results, is open. It is one candidate answer to [QUESTION-047](QUESTION-047.md)'s question of
which community-level mechanisms set what a community of sound inquirers
comes to believe.

## What would count as an answer

Evidence from real research fields rather than models. Studies that trace
methods through funding and training lineages, showing that researchers'
methods were the same before and after industry funding, that funded
researchers trained more of the next generation, and that a field's share
of industry-favourable methods grew by that route. Also comparisons of
funders whose merit assessments do or do not count industry-funded work.
The account is in trouble if industry-favourable consensus in such fields
arises mainly through funded individuals changing their methods or
results. More simulations of the same model family, and further case
histories read through the model, cannot settle it.
