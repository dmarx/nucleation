# THEORY-tmp8fvy7: Being reproduced does not entail reproducing: genes of multicellular organisms, like hearts, are reproducees with little reproductive autonomy and differ from hearts in reproductive causal control, and material overlap between parent and offspring is required of reproducers, which are whole life cycles, but not of reproducees

## Source

Bourrat (2025), [LIT-157](../literature.d/LIT-157.md) (*Synthese* 205:62), read in full in [NOTE-100](../notes.d/NOTE-100.md).

## What was actually shown

**Reproducee (§2, pp. 4–7).** B is a reproducee of A iff B comes into
existence after A, A is a cause of B's existence, and B is of A's relevant
class. The three conditions rule out co-occurrence, mere recurrence and mere
production. Choosing the relevant class is pragmatic; there is "no
mind-independent way" to fix it (p. 6).

**Reproducer (§3, pp. 8–10).** A reproducee with reproductive autonomy: "the
capacity to reproduce within a range of (relevant) background conditions"
(p. 8). Autonomy is graded and comparable only across similar backgrounds.
With non-null development every life-cycle stage is "at best a mere
reproducee", so whole life cycles are the reproducers (p. 10).

**Darwinian individual (§5, pp. 13–17).** A reproducer with high reproductive
causal control: heredity that survives as the parent's description is made
finer. Control is distinct from fidelity. A reproducer with 50% colour
fidelity whose blue offspring are always azure scores higher than one with
100% colour fidelity and none for shade (p. 16).

**Genes (§6, pp. 17–20).** Genes and hearts are both mere reproducees on
autonomy. They differ in control: a nucleotide switch in a gene reappears in
offspring genes, and changes to a heart do not (p. 19). Relative to a
cellular background on timescales shorter than cell division, an autonomous
retrotransposon is a reproducer and a regular gene is not (p. 18).

**Material overlap (§4, pp. 10–13).** Any continuous tracking of matter
through a reproducer's life cycle meets hybrid objects holding both future
parental and future offspring parts, so overlap is necessary for reproducers.
A parent virion causes an offspring virion with no overlap, so it is not
necessary for reproducees. Griesemer and Godfrey-Smith partly talk past each
other (p. 12).

**What could have come out otherwise.** A definition could have failed to sort
a standard case. The cases were chosen as examples, and the measures that
would test the placements were not computed.

## What this does not say

- **That autonomy and control have been measured.** Autonomy is to be measured
  by conditional mutual information between parent and offspring given the
  environment, and control by interventional mutual information at finer
  grains (Griffiths et al.'s INF). Neither is computed. The two autonomy
  recipes (pp. 8 and 10) are different quantities, and the first can be
  negative ([NOTE-100](../notes.d/NOTE-100.md), C5). The control recipe has no normalisation, so raw
  mutual information favours finer grains (C13).
- **That the autonomy proxy fits the definition.** The definition is a range
  of backgrounds. The proxy measures resemblance given the environment, which
  is closer to heredity.
- **That overlap is necessary in general.** The hybrid-object premise is
  asserted for all reproduction and shown only for retroviruses, via
  Griesemer (C8). "Material overlap" is never defined.
- **That the gene verdict is categorical.** It holds relative to a chosen
  background and timescale, and the paper still states categorical
  conclusions (C16).
- **That control evolves in transitions in individuality.** The seeded skim had
  it backwards: scaffolded collectives get high control "for free" from their
  cells, and what must evolve is autonomy (p. 21; [NOTE-100](../notes.d/NOTE-100.md), corrections).

## Connections

- Lloyd's account of units of selection ([LIT-160](../literature.d/LIT-160.md); [THEORY-tmp1x94l](THEORY-tmp1x94l.md)) champions
  Griesemer's reproducer, which this paper builds on, and objects to
  "Darwinian individual" as a fusion of roles. Lloyd's entry predates this
  paper and is critical of Bourrat's earlier programme.
- [LIT-168](../literature.d/LIT-168.md) (SEP, Biological Individuals) is seeded as treating genes as
  Darwinian individuals. This paper denies it, "let alone Darwinian
  individuals" (p. 21).
- The two formal weaknesses, nothing computed and conditioning on the
  environment in place of intervention, run through Bourrat's earlier [LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md)
  and [LIT-049](../literature.d/LIT-049.md) as well. This is the one of the three whose main contribution
  does not depend on its measures.
