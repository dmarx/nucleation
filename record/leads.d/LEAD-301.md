# LEAD-301: Read the research on the felt quality of insight: the fluency account, the Eureka heuristic and its failures, false insights, and the illumination and verification of mathematical discovery

## What to read

1. **The fluency account.** Topolinski and Reber (2010), "Gaining insight into the 'Aha' experience", Current Directions in Psychological Science 19(6):402-405, doi 10.1177/0963721410388803.
   - Four marks of the aha: suddenness, ease, positive affect, the feeling of being right.
   - All four explained by a sudden jump in processing fluency.
   - A rival view puts the cause in overcoming an impasse.
2. **The Eureka heuristic.**
   - Laukkonen, Ingledew, Grimmer, Schooler and Tangen (2021), "Getting a grip on insight: real-time and embodied Aha experiences predict correct solutions". The intensity of the feeling predicts correctness.
   - Laukkonen et al. (2023), "Insight and the selection of ideas", a review.
3. **Its failures.**
   - Laukkonen, Kaveladze, Tangen and Schooler (2020), "The dark side of Eureka: artificially induced Aha moments make facts feel true", Cognition 196. Includes worldview statements.
   - The same group (2022), "Irrelevant insights make worldviews ring true", Scientific Reports.
   - Grimmer et al.: warnings reduce but do not remove false Aha moments.
4. **False insights.** Danek and Wiley (2017), "What about false insights?", Frontiers in Psychology 7:2077, PMC5247466.
   - 70 participants and 37 magic tricks.
   - Components of the feeling: pleasure, suddenness, certainty.
   - Strong ahas go "clearly, but not exclusively" with correct solutions.
5. **Mathematical discovery** (from memory).
   - Poincaré, "Mathematical creation" (in *Science and Method*, 1908).
   - Wallas, *The Art of Thought* (1926): preparation, incubation, illumination, verification.
   - Hadamard, *The Psychology of Invention in the Mathematical Field* (1945).
6. **Related** (from memory). Burton, *On Being Certain* (2008), on the feeling of knowing as a sensation independent of reasoning; Metcalfe and Wiebe (1987) on "warmth" ratings, which rise suddenly before insight solutions and gradually before others.
