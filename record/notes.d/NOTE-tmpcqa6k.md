# NOTE-tmpcqa6k: Cuny and Poelman (2022) — Evolution of koinobiont parasitoid host regulation and consequences for indirect plant defence

## Contribution

Koinobiont parasitoids let the host keep feeding while their larvae develop inside it, and regulate the host's growth: usually arresting it (smaller hosts eating less), sometimes prolonging it (larger hosts eating more), and some species switch with conditions. The regulation is done by injected and secreted factors, and it changes the plant's responses to the parasitized herbivore, so it reaches the wider community.

## Key results

- Mechanisms: "Host regulation is often a concerted process directed by the parasitoid larva itself and factors that parasitoids inject along with the eggs into the host, such as endogenous viruses, venom, and teratocytes."
- PDVs: "The association with viruses arose in two separate lineages of parasitoids belonging to the Braconidae and Ichneumonidae families".
- Teratocytes "play an important role in the arrestment of the host growth by the production of proteins and miRNAs that interfere with host hormones (e.g. juvenile hormones or ecdysteroids)".
- Direction is plastic: "some parasitoids exert plasticity in the direction of their host regulation and are able to arrest or prolong their host development according to environmental conditions".
- Table 1: parasitized hosts "may yield hosts that are up to 97% smaller than unparasitized hosts".

## Bearing on the record

**How far it stands in.** Restates the effector toolkit (venom, PDVs, teratocytes, larval secretions) and its action on host development and feeding. Does not cover immune suppression in detail, metabolism, behavioural manipulation, or molecular/omics findings since 2022, which are Huang et al.'s stated focus.

- **CLAIM-tmpu69tn**: **supports** that one organism's injected factors (including domesticated viruses) govern another's development and feeding through the host's own endocrine machinery; the regulation serves the parasitoid.
- **CLAIM-tmpoxhqq** (found on recheck): **supports by extension**: control by selected adaptations through the host's own machinery (its hormones), with plasticity in direction that is what adaptation, not pathology, predicts.

## Limitations

Focus on koinobionts and plant-mediated effects; skimmed; not a molecular review.
