The owner asked how the record stands on random projections. A search of
`record/` found three uses and no reading of the theory behind them. The
uses were random Fourier features ([LIT-679](../../record/literature.d/LIT-679.md), [THEORY-106](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-106.md)), the
compressed-sensing bound in Toy Models of Superposition ([NOTE-278](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-278.md)), and
the thresholded random projections of sparse distributed memory and the
mushroom body ([NOTE-242](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-242.md), [NOTE-670](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-670.md)). The search covered random projection,
Johnson–Lindenstrauss, the restricted isometry property, sketching,
locality-sensitive hashing, Achlioptas and fly hashing. No LIT, THEORY,
QUESTION or CLAIM had any of them as its subject. The owner asked for the
anchor works to be filed and for a question to tie the threads together.

## What entered

All seven were registered Deferred and unread. Metadata was checked against
Crossref, and against arXiv where there is an arXiv id.

- **Johnson & Lindenstrauss (1984)** ([LIT-tmp7skui](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp7skui.md), doi:10.1090/conm/026/737400).
  The lemma itself.
- **Dasgupta & Gupta (2003)** ([LIT-tmpjy3gf](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpjy3gf.md), doi:10.1002/rsa.10073). The
  elementary proof most later work quotes.
- **Achlioptas (2003)** ([LIT-tmppzjx6](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmppzjx6.md), doi:10.1016/S0022-0000(03)00025-4).
  Shows that signed, zero-mean matrices with entries ±1, or √3·{+1, 0, −1}
  with two thirds zeros, are enough. Filed as the ensemble the fly and SDM
  use; the reading showed it is not (corrected after the reading).
- **Larsen & Nelson (2017)** ([LIT-tmp8s1wj](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp8s1wj.md), arXiv:1609.02094). Shows the
  bound is tight, so ε⁻² log n is a threshold.
- **Baraniuk, Davenport, DeVore & Wakin (2008)** ([LIT-tmpcfdvm](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpcfdvm.md),
  doi:10.1007/s00365-007-9003-x). Derives RIP from JL concentration. Filed
  as the bridge to the superposition bound; the reading showed it bridges
  sufficiency only (corrected after the reading).
- **Donoho & Tanner (2009)** ([LIT-tmpwlsvo](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpwlsvo.md), arXiv:0906.2530). The phase
  transition that [NOTE-278](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-278.md) asks about.
- **Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha (2017)** ([LIT-tmpokc0j](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpokc0j.md),
  doi:10.1126/science.aam9868). The fly olfactory circuit read as
  locality-sensitive hashing.

The question is [QUESTION-tmpa0dq9](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-tmpa0dq9.md), on the `formal-apparatus` line, because
it asks about a general result that other lines borrow.

## Boundary

None of the seven is held in the anthology. A grep of its `record/` (clone
at d1573d2) for the titles, identifiers, Johnson–Lindenstrauss, random
projection, restricted isometry, Achlioptas and mushroom body found the lemma
used only inside Linformer ([ANTH-LIT-529](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-529.md)) and random projections only inside
TracIn ([ANTH-LIT-400](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-400.md)). Six of the works are mathematics, and the seventh is
a neural circuit evaluated as a retrieval algorithm. No anthology topic
holds any of them, so none carries `anthology-candidate`.

Rahimi and Recht's random features paper is held in neither record and was
not filed. It is ML practice and belongs to the anthology.

## Depth

None of the seven was read, and every key takeaway is the result as it is
standardly stated. The reading that would move the question first is
Baraniuk et al. If its proof is as short as its title says, the
superposition bound in [NOTE-278](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-278.md) can be rewritten in the record's own terms.

## Reading, the same day

The owner then asked for the seven to be read. Six were read in full, each
from a lawful copy named in its LIT's history: [NOTE-tmpmv42v](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpmv42v.md) (a course-hosted
scan of the published article), [NOTE-tmpve6z2](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpve6z2.md) and [NOTE-tmppltl7](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmppltl7.md) (author-posted
copies), [NOTE-tmp4oub5](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmp4oub5.md) (a course-hosted 2002 journal draft, not the JCSS
text, whose Elsevier copy returned 403), and [NOTE-tmp4mti8](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmp4mti8.md) and [NOTE-tmpw0lqu](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpw0lqu.md)
(arXiv). The fly paper is `Unavailable`. Its bioRxiv preprint is open but
returned 429 to this client throughout, so a browser fetch would clear it.

The readings corrected the filing in several places, and each LIT's
history says where. The ones that matter for the question are these. The
fly's matrix is not an Achlioptas ensemble. Baraniuk et al. explain
sufficiency of k log(N/k), not necessity. Donoho–Tanner universality is
asymptotic. The JL lower bound does not bound superposition capacity.
[QUESTION-tmpa0dq9](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-tmpa0dq9.md) is at version 2 with these corrections.
