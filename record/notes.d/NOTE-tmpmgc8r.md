# NOTE-tmpmgc8r: How the structure of language is acquired

## Contribution

[LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md) showed, for classification, that deep networks learn a random
hierarchical grammar from a number of examples polynomial in the input
size, and tied that number to when patch–label statistics rise above
sampling noise. This paper does the same for self-supervision, where the
statistics are between tokens of the sequence. It computes how token
correlations fall with the depth of the tokens' common ancestor, shows that
a finite sample turns this into an effective context window t*(P), predicts
a sequence of loss steps at P_ℓ ≈ v m^(2ℓ−1), and checks them in
transformers and CNNs. It then conjectures that the link between sample
size, correlation range and usable context holds for real text, and tests
that on two character-level corpora.

## Key insight

In a tree-generated sequence, how strongly two tokens depend on each other
is set by how far up the tree their common ancestor sits, and it falls by a
factor of about m per level, because each generation below the ancestor
dilutes it. A sample of P sequences measures dependences only down to a
noise floor of (v²P)^(−1/2), so it sees only the near, shallow part of the
tree. What it sees is enough to group tokens: tuples generated by the same
hidden symbol are interchangeable for everything outside it, so they have
identical correlations with the target, and grouping them reconstructs the
hidden symbol. Each level reconstructed lets the next, longer-range and
weaker, correlation be used. More data means a longer usable context and a
deeper representation, and the loss falls in steps.

## Assumptions

- **Grammar.** The RHM of [LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md) with the class replaced by an unlabelled
  root drawn uniformly from v symbols: L levels, vocabulary v at every
  level, branching s, m production rules per symbol, chosen uniformly among
  unambiguous rule sets (m ≤ v^(s−1)); every rule equiprobable; a datum is
  the d = s^L leaves. Fixed tree geometry, uniform rule probabilities and
  unambiguity are acknowledged not to hold for language (§6).
- **Task.** The last token is masked and predicted from the other d − 1
  (§2), a simplification of both masked and autoregressive language
  modelling; P training sequences drawn from the data distribution.
- **The analytic regime.** The correlation plateaus (Eq. 8, App. D) are
  computed as variances over draws of the grammar, standing in for
  variances over the vocabulary, asymptotically in v and m, with the
  hidden symbols' marginal probabilities replaced by 1/v. The noise floor
  (App. E) is a central-limit estimate in the same limit.
- **Networks.** Transformers on RHM data are stacks of multi-head attention
  only (no residuals, normalisation or MLPs), trained with Adam; CNNs have
  filter size and stride s, depth log d / log s, ReLU, maximal-update
  parametrisation, SGD. Batch size is the smallest that converges to
  training loss 10⁻³. On real text, BERT-like encoders of 3 and 6 layers on
  character-level blocks of t + 1 tokens.

## Key results

- **Eq. 7–8.** For the masked token and a token whose lowest common
  ancestor with it is at level ℓ, C̃(t) = C̃^(ℓ) for t = s^(ℓ−1), …, s^ℓ − 1,
  with C̃^(ℓ) ≈ ((1 − m/v^(s−1))/(v³ m^(2ℓ−1)))^(1/2), and so
  C̃(t) ~ t^(−β), β = ln m/ln s. The mean of each covariance over grammars
  is zero (Eq. 40); its sign is random. *Holds:* asymptotically in v and m;
  checked against empirical correlations at L = 3, s = 2, v = 32, m = 8
  (Fig. 1).
- **App. E, Eq. 9.** The empirical C̃ from P samples saturates at
  (v²P)^(−1/2); correlations are resolved out to t* = s^(ℓ*) − 1 with
  C̃^(ℓ*) > (v²P)^(−1/2) > C̃^(ℓ*+1); t*(P) ~ P^(1/(2β)).
- **Eq. 10–11.** Conditioning on the last s^ℓ − 1 tokens gives a loss
  bounded by L̄_ℓ = log N̄_ℓ, with N̄_ℓ the mean number of values of the
  last token compatible with that context, solved by recursion; for large
  v and m, L̄_ℓ → log(1/(1 − f)) + v f^ℓ with f = m/v^(s−1).
- **§4.1.** An s^ℓ-gram counter needs a sample exponential in t_ℓ = s^ℓ − 1;
  a learner given the hidden variables needs one growing as m^ℓ, a power
  of t_ℓ.
- **Eq. 12, App. F.** Correlations of the target with a whole s-tuple, and
  the noise on them, both shrink by √m, so the thresholds are unchanged:
  P_ℓ = v m^(2ℓ−1)/(1 − m/v^(s−1)); P_1 ≈ vm, P_2 ≈ vm³. Grouping tuples by
  their correlations reconstructs the level-1 symbols, which correlate more
  strongly with the target than their children do; iterating gives the
  sequence. An argument, not a theorem about trained networks.
- **Eq. 13.** Putting ℓ ≈ log P/(2 log m) into Eq. 11 gives an early
  logarithmic decay and then a power law P^(−α) with
  α = |log f|/(2 log m). The paper prints α = log f/(2 log m), which is
  negative since f < 1; the sign is a slip. The power law is the envelope
  of the steps, which is the paper's claim to unify emergence and scaling.
- **Fig. 2.** Depth-3 attention-only transformers, L = 3, s = 2, v = 32,
  m = 8 and 11: loss steps near P_ℓ, plateaus near L̄_ℓ, "good qualitative
  agreement". With the context cut to t = 1, 3, 7 the curve follows only
  the first ℓ steps.
- **Fig. 3, Eq. 14.** Depth-4 CNNs, L = 4, s = 2, v = 16, m = 4: the ratio
  r_ℓ/s_ℓ of a layer's cosine similarity under resetting the production
  rule below a level-ℓ symbol to that under replacing the symbol falls from
  about 1 to about 0 near P_ℓ, for layers above ℓ only.
- **Footnote 3.** A linear function of a one-hot s-patch cannot be constant
  on each set of synonymic tuples in general: v(m − 1) constraints on vs
  weights, satisfiable only if m ≤ s + 1. So a layer becomes invariant to
  level-ℓ synonyms only above layer ℓ, after a nonlinearity.
- **App. G.** Depth-3 CNNs, L = 3, s = 3, v = 11, m from 8 to 23: the first
  two steps collapse on P_1 and P_2 = vm³ (Figs. 7, 8). The third step in
  CNNs comes earlier than P_3 (Fig. 6); the authors' explanation is weight
  sharing, which would make later steps scale as m^(ℓ+1) rather than
  m^(2ℓ−1), and they could not test it systematically. P_1 grows as t + 1
  (Fig. 9), which the analysis does not capture.
- **Figs. 4, 5, Eq. 16.** tiny-Shakespeare (3-layer transformer) and
  WikiText-103 (6-layer), characters as tokens: C̃(t) ~ t^(−β) with
  β ≈ 1.4 and 1.55; empirical curves collapse under C·P^(1/2) against
  t/P^(1/z), z = 2β ≈ 2.8 and 3.1; the losses for t = 1 to 15 collapse as
  L(P, t) = t^(−αz) f(P/t^z) with the same z and α ≈ 0.1 and 0.095.

## Concepts

- **Correlation function C̃(t)** — the root-mean-square over the v² token
  pairs of the covariance between position d − t and the masked position d
  (Eq. 6). A measure of how strongly two positions depend, not of which
  tokens carry the dependence.
- **Effective context window t*(P)** — the largest distance at which the
  correlation exceeds the sampling noise of P samples.
- **Sample complexity P_ℓ** — the P at which correlations with tokens out
  to s^ℓ − 1 emerge from noise (Eq. 12).
- **S_{ℓ,i} and R_{ℓ,i}** — replace the i-th level-ℓ symbol with another,
  or keep it and redraw the production rule below it. A representation
  that encodes the symbol alone is invariant to R but not to S.
- **Hidden variables** — the nonterminal symbols of the grammar; here
  "synonyms" ([LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md)'s word) are the m tuples one symbol rewrites to.

## Connections

It extends [LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md) ([NOTE-677](NOTE-677.md)) from feature–label to feature–feature
correlations, and inherits its debt to Mossel's hierarchical models and to
Malach and Shalev-Shwartz's clustering argument. It sits beside the
synthetic-grammar probes of transformers (Allen-Zhu and Li; Zhao, Panigrahi,
Ge and Arora, who show masked language modelling is optimised by
inside–outside-like algorithms on PCFG data), and cites Lin and Tegmark and
DeGiuli's random language model for power-law correlations in grammars.
Its empirical anchor is Saphra and Lopez's finding that LSTMs learn short
dependencies before long ones. It reads the scaling laws of Kaplan et al.
as an envelope of emergent steps, against kernel-limit accounts (Spigler,
Geiger and Wyart; Bahri et al.; Bordelon et al.). The same group's
Sclocchi et al. (diffusion) and Tomasini and Wyart (sparse RHM) are cited
and not held.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md).** Of the RHM papers, this is the nearer to the question,
  because the hierarchy is read off a co-occurrence matrix of observable
  tokens rather than from token–label counts. In the large-m limit, where
  the marginals tend to 1/v, the paper's covariance is v⁻² times the
  relative deviation from independence, P(µ, ν)/(P(µ)P(ν)) − 1, that
  [LIT-855](../literature.d/LIT-855.md)'s word2vec factorises ([THEORY-182](../theory.d/THEORY-182.md)), but positional (one position
  pair at a time) rather than word–context. It still does not answer the
  question: it builds no embedding, computes no spectrum and looks for no
  direction, and C̃ is a norm over the vocabulary. What it does show is how
  an implication hierarchy appears in such a matrix, and three points
  carry over (the inferences are this record's, not the paper's):
  1. *Ancestry shows as equality of rows.* Tuples with a common parent are
     conditionally independent of everything outside it given the parent,
     so their co-occurrence with any outside token is identical. A
     co-occurrence embedding of tuples would put synonyms at one point:
     the hierarchy appears as a partition, nested level by level, rather
     than as one direction per ancestor.
  2. *Depth shows as strength, and a finite corpus truncates it.* Entries
     carried by a level-ℓ ancestor are smaller by about m per level, and
     only those above (v²P)^(−1/2) are measured. A derivation for
     [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md) on such data would have to say which levels a corpus of
     a given size can show at all, not only whether they are linear.
  3. *Membership is not linear in the input code.* Footnote 3: a linear
     function of the one-hot patch cannot be constant on each synonym
     class unless m ≤ s + 1. In this model the parent of a patch is not a
     linear feature of it; it becomes one only after a nonlinear layer.
  Single tokens, unlike tuples, belong to the rules of several parents,
  so a token's own co-occurrence row mixes ancestors; the clean equality
  is for tuples. And the RHM's covariances have zero mean and random sign
  over grammars, so nothing in them is additive across levels, which is the
  property [THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md) needs.
- **[THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md) ([LIT-863](../literature.d/LIT-863.md)).** Contrast only. [LIT-863](../literature.d/LIT-863.md) names "the random
  hierarchy model of Cagnetta et al." as where hierarchical attributes go
  next; this paper supplies the co-occurrence statistics of that model but
  no attributes in [LIT-863](../literature.d/LIT-863.md)'s sense, and its structure is random, not
  multiplicative.
- **[THEORY-182](../theory.d/THEORY-182.md) ([LIT-855](../literature.d/LIT-855.md)).** Both describe learning from co-occurrence in
  stages, ordered differently. In [THEORY-182](../theory.d/THEORY-182.md), with the population
  statistics given, gradient flow learns eigenvectors one at a time in
  order of eigenvalue (stages in training time). Here the stages are in
  sample size: a correlation becomes usable when it rises above the
  sampling noise, and longer-range ones are weaker. They are not in
  conflict, and a finite-sample version of [THEORY-182](../theory.d/THEORY-182.md) would combine them.
- **[THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md) ([LIT-860](../literature.d/LIT-860.md)).** [THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md)'s symmetry is that co-occurrence
  depends only on separation, which makes the matrix Toeplitz or
  circulant and its eigenvectors Fourier modes. In the RHM it depends on
  the depth of the common ancestor, an ultrametric of the tree, not on
  separation: two adjacent tokens on either side of a top-level split are
  as weakly correlated as the farthest pair. If the [THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md) argument
  were run on a tree-dependent kernel, the eigenvectors would be tree
  wavelets, as in [THEORY-186](../theory.d/THEORY-186.md) ([LIT-862](../literature.d/LIT-862.md)). The paper does not consider this.
  Its real-text measurement treats correlation as a function of distance
  alone, which is [THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md)'s premise, at character level.
- **[THEORY-195](../theory.d/THEORY-195.md) ([LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md)).** The same mechanism in a second setting, with
  the same caveat: in the RHM, the correlation threshold and the counting
  of the grammar give the same expression. The real-text test goes some
  way towards what [THEORY-195](../theory.d/THEORY-195.md)'s `promote_when` asks: β is measured from
  the correlations, then used to rescale the losses, with no grammar to
  count. But it tests the effective context window, not the reconstruction
  of hidden variables, and the early third step in CNNs shows the
  threshold is not all that sets P.
- **[THEORY-186](../theory.d/THEORY-186.md) ([LIT-862](../literature.d/LIT-862.md)).** Another stagewise account of learning a
  hierarchy. As with [LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md), the stages here are in sample size and go
  shallow to deep. In [THEORY-186](../theory.d/THEORY-186.md) they are in time and go coarse to fine,
  i.e. root first. Here the levels nearest the leaves come first. These
  two orders are opposite, and they should not be cited as one account.
- **[CLAIM-046](../claims.d/CLAIM-046.md).** Weakly: the paper cites work showing that when data fit
  both a PCFG and a non-hierarchical model, recurrent networks and
  transformers do not consistently prefer the hierarchy, an instance of
  identical observations from different hidden-state models. It does not
  test this itself.
- **[CLAIM-119](../claims.d/CLAIM-119.md) and [LIT-267](../literature.d/LIT-267.md).** No bearing beyond [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md)'s.
- **New account.** [THEORY-tmpyaqz7](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyaqz7.md), Proposed: correlation range, sample
  size and representable depth.
- **Practice.** The paper gives no instruction. Its subject, what the
  statistics of language are and how they shape data-scaling curves, is
  what the anthology's `signal-structure` and `training-optimization`
  topics hold, hence the flag on the LIT.

## Limitations

- **No proof for trained networks.** The step from measurable correlations
  to networks that reconstruct the hidden variables is an argument; the
  authors say a proof needs a theory of deep networks' dynamics on
  hierarchical data (§6).
- **Agreement is qualitative in places.** Transformer steps are compared
  with P_ℓ by eye; the CNN collapses cover the first two steps only; the
  third CNN step comes earlier than predicted, and P_1 ∝ (t + 1) is
  unexplained.
- **Small synthetic ranges.** L ≤ 4, s = 2 or 3, P up to a few million; one
  setting each for the representation probes.
- **The language test is narrow.** Character-level tokens, two small
  corpora, 3- and 6-layer models, contexts up to 15 characters. It tests
  the conjecture about context and sample size, which needs only
  correlations that decay, not a hierarchy. Power-law correlations in text
  do not show that text is tree-generated.
- **Uniform, unambiguous, fixed-shape grammar.** The steps in the
  correlation function, and hence in the loss, come from these
  assumptions, which real text does not satisfy (§6); context-sensitive
  structure is out of scope.
- **Two printing slips.** The exponent α in Eq. 13 has the wrong sign as
  printed, and §3 says Fig. 1 uses L = 4 where the caption says L = 3.

## Open questions

- A proof that gradient descent on a deep network learns the masked token
  of RHM data from about P_ℓ examples, or one with weight sharing at
  m^(ℓ+1).
- With non-uniform (Zipfian) production probabilities, whether the steps
  smooth into the power law the conjecture assumes, and what sets β.
- The spectrum of a word–context co-occurrence matrix generated by the
  RHM, or by a grammar whose symbols carry attributes: whether its leading
  eigenvectors are tree wavelets of the hidden hierarchy, and whether any
  ancestor has a linear direction. That is the form in which this model
  would answer [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md).
- On real text at word level, whether the context at which the loss
  saturates still scales as P^(1/(2β)) with β from word correlations.
