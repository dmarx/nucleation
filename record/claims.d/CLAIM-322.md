# CLAIM-322: Agreement under a probe family is evidence that two objects share structure only to the degree that the family was chosen independently of the comparison and is evaluated on cases it was not chosen on, so a family selected to make two objects look alike shows only that it can be selected

## The claim

This is [CLAIM-264](CLAIM-264.md)'s evidential half, stated on its own. [CLAIM-264](CLAIM-264.md): "Agreement
under a probe family is evidence of shared structure only to the degree
that the family was chosen independently of the comparison and is
evaluated on cases it was not chosen on. A family selected to make two
objects look alike shows that it can be selected."

It rests on [CLAIM-264](CLAIM-264.md)'s exact half. Two objects can agree on every probe
in a restricted family without being isomorphic, and matching each probe's
marginal leaves the joint across probes unconstrained. So agreement under a
family is not identity, and what it shows depends on how the family came
to be. A family chosen for the comparison at hand can be chosen to agree; a
family fixed independently and tested on cases it was not chosen on can
fail to, and its agreement is evidence for that reason.

The rule is [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s remedy applied to probes, as [CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md) first drew
it for communicative realizations.

## What it does not say

- It does not say which probes to choose, or when a family is rich enough
  for a task ([QUESTION-031](../questions.d/QUESTION-031.md)).
- It does not say agreement under a chosen family is no evidence at all,
  only that it is weak evidence that the objects share structure.
- It does not say that held-out probes settle identity. The exact half
  still holds of them: unless the family is rich enough, agreement under it
  is an equivalence coarser than identity.
