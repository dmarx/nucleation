# THEORY-1919: No test settles how coercion affects blame

## Source

[LIT-2673](../literature.d/LIT-2673.md), Scott Anderson's SEP entry "Coercion" (2006, substantive
revision 2023), read in [NOTE-2014](../notes.d/NOTE-2014.md) (Skimmed: §§2.1.3, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and most
of 3.3 read in full; the history and the threats-and-baselines sections
skipped).

## What was actually shown

§3.2 surveys the accounts and concludes that "There is no single, widely
agreed upon theory that explains when an agent is or is not responsible for
something that happens in the world". What limits responsibility for one
act "might fail to limit one’s responsibility for killing someone". Excuse
is mainly for "a total breakdown in her will", so "most cases of diminished
responsibility under coercion will appeal to justification rather than
excuse". Of the moralized tests, "it is unclear that any of them is robust
enough to handle more than a fraction of the cases". The psychological test
fails because "the magnitude of the inducement by itself seems not to
suffice". The entry adds that coercion is "a strategic and dynamic
interaction between two agents", so the responsibility rule shapes what
coercers attempt. It also reports a defence for a target of coercive
control whose "decisional authority" the coercer "usurped" (Galoob and
Sheley 2021).

The corroboration of [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md) is partial and second-hand. The entry
assesses the coerced party's responsibility on its own terms while treating
the coercer's structuring of the situation as what changes it. It never
treats the coerced party's share as subtracted from a fixed total. The
bearing on [QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md) is the strategic point: whose agency a wrong runs
through is partly fixed by the standards that assign responsibility, and
those standards change what the influencing party does.

## What this does not say

- That the coercer answers for what the coerced party does. §3.2 is about
  the coercee alone.
- Anything about manipulation, which the entry rules out by stipulation.
- Which test is right. The entry reports partial tests and endorses none.
- That coercive control excuses. Galoob and Sheley's proposal is a legal
  defence reported in one paragraph.
