# NOTE-432: Explanatory Models for Psychiatric Illness

## Contribution

This paper takes the explanatory pluralism of Kendler's 2005 paper
([LIT-552](../literature.d/LIT-552.md), cited as reference 1 for its opening premise) and gives it
a method. It imports the mechanistic philosophy of science of Bechtel,
Craver and Wimsatt into psychiatry: explanation is the decomposition and
reassembly of multilevel mechanisms. It then uses Marr's levels to settle
the relation between biological and psychological explanation as
implementation rather than replacement. No new empirical result is
claimed.

## Key insight

Two different integration problems are at stake, and the paper keeps them
apart. The first is part–whole: a disorder's causes sit at different
levels (molecules, neurons, persons, societies) of one mechanism, and
these levels interact non-additively and in loops. The answer is to
decompose where the system is locally decomposable and then stitch the
pieces together. The second is perspectival: brain and mind are two
descriptions of the same process. The answer is that the psychological
description states the function, and the biology is how the function is
implemented. On that view a gene-to-protein finding has not explained
schizophrenia until someone can say how the protein abnormality produces
delusions and hallucinations. The paper puts this as the resident's
question: "how does this abnormality in protein Y lead to the
characteristic symptoms?"

## Assumptions

- **Mechanistic explanation is the right model** for the biological and
  social sciences relevant to psychiatry. Bechtel is quoted: "the
  decomposition required by mechanistic explanation is reductionist, but
  the recognition that parts and operations must be organized into an
  appropriate whole provides for a robust sense of a higher level of
  organization".
- **Levels are part–whole relations** ("molecules make up a membrane,
  neurons make up a circuit … individuals make up a society").
- **The brain is functionally "a machine designed by evolution"**, so
  reverse-engineering applies, and function can only be stated in
  psychological terms ("Neurochemical terms will not work").
- **Marr's scheme generalises.** It was designed for information-processing
  systems such as vision. The paper grants that "many psychiatric problems
  are not currently amenable to an information-processing perspective" but
  holds that "Marr's underlying logic is nonetheless valid for psychiatry
  across the board".

## Key results

These are arguments, with illustrative evidence cited.

- **Against law-based explanation.** A physics-derived law model, with
  "all real causal processes … understood from one perspective and one set
  of laws", "has been counterproductive" in mental health and has fed both
  hard reductionism and hard emergentism. The home-heating parable sets the
  three views side by side: the reductionist dismantles the thermostat, the
  emergentist models the temperature curve, and the mechanist models the
  whole house.
- **Decomposability criteria** (after Bechtel and Richardson, and Wimsatt). An "easily
  decomposable" system shows aggregativity (parts combine additively),
  "intersubstitutable" parts with context-independent functions, and no
  causal loops. Loops can be intra-level or inter-level. The most important
  is "recursive, or top-down, causality", in which the system's output
  feeds back on its own components.
- **Alcohol dependence fails all three.** Risk factors sit at four levels
  (biological/genetic, psychological, social, cultural/economic). Genetic
  effects vary with religion, marital status and social environment, so
  additivity and intersubstitution fail. There are causal loops: genes
  shape the response to ethanol, which shapes expectancies and consumption;
  tolerance produces a positive feedback loop; temperament selects risky
  peers, the "brain has feet"; and "reverse" cultural transmission makes
  offspring of heavy drinkers abstain more often.
- **Other disorders.** Sensitivity to adversity is context-dependent.
  Neuroticism generates the stressors that raise depression risk. A fearful
  child's avoidance prevents habituation. In panic patients, the illusion
  of control over CO₂ reduced panic symptoms (Sanderson, Rapee and Barlow
  1989).
- **Nondecomposability is not defeat.** Science has handled nondecomposable
  systems before (the citric acid cycle) by first finding "subareas of
  local decomposability". "The naive emergentism that opines that the
  system is so complex and interrelated that we cannot possibly study any
  part in isolation is just plain wrong", and the reductionist belief that
  the parts "simply fit together" is "no less wrong". With Wimsatt, more
  detail should reveal more emergence: etiological pathways will be
  "deeply recursive", a "causal thicket", and reassembly involves Craver's
  "explanatory oscillation" across levels.
- **Implementation, not replacement.** In replacement, the higher-level
  construct becomes redundant, as temperature is to mean kinetic energy.
  In implementation, the lower-level theory supplies "the mechanistic
  details of how the functions proposed by the more abstract theory
  actually get accomplished". Following Gold and Stoljar on Kandel's
  Aplysia work, Hatfield on vision and Bechtel on memory, neuroscience has
  progressed by implementing processes psychologists first described.
  Conclusion: "biology will implement but not replace psychology", and the
  two "will coevolve".
- **Concessions.** Hard reductive and emergent perspectives can still be
  useful. Therapies can come from gene findings without any account of
  mechanism, and CBT "emerged from psychological constructs that contained
  no biology". Such perspectives give only part of the picture.

## Concepts

- **mechanistic explanation**: the explication of causal mechanisms and
  of "how those mechanisms are actually instantiated in the world".
- **hard reductionism / hard emergentism**: the views that psychiatric
  illness is best explained solely in molecular-neuroscience terms, or
  solely in mental or social terms that "cannot be deduced from biology".
- **level**: a part–whole (component–system) relation within one
  mechanism, distinct from a perspective on the same process.
- **easily decomposable system**: aggregative, with intersubstitutable,
  context-independent parts and no causal loops.
- **top-down (recursive) causality**: the output of the whole system feeds
  back on its own components.
- **local decomposability**: a subsystem simple enough to study in
  isolation.
- **explanatory oscillation** (Craver): moving back and forth between
  levels while reassembling a mechanism.
- **implementation vs replacement**: a lower-level theory either fills in
  how a higher-level function is done, or makes the higher-level construct
  redundant.

## Connections

- **Kendler 2005 ([LIT-552](../literature.d/LIT-552.md)), unread; relation declared as `extends`.**
  This paper cites it as reference 1 for its opening premise that
  etiological models "need to be pluralistic or multilevel". Its two
  sections develop what the 2005 abstract names but this record cannot
  read. The 2005 paper's "patchy reductionism" and "piecemeal integration"
  become local decomposition and reassembly. Its mind–brain propositions
  become the Marr-based implementation thesis. The 2005 propositions
  against substance dualism and epiphenomenalism are not argued here.
- **Engel ([LIT-548](../literature.d/LIT-548.md)), unread.** Cited (reference 3, with Adolf Meyer) as
  an "earlier integrationist account". Kendler offers the mechanistic
  approach as its "conceptually rigorous descendant", which implies that
  Engel's model lacked a method of integration. The paper does not say
  this outright.
- **Borsboom ([LIT-547](../literature.d/LIT-547.md)).** Both reject a single common cause and both
  put causal loops at the centre. Kendler's loops cross levels (gene →
  sensitivity → expectancy → environment). Borsboom's run among symptoms,
  and Principle 2 pushes genes and environments into the external field or
  into the constitution of a symptom. Borsboom cites Kendler's later
  papers for the absence of common causes.
- **Hofmann and Hayes ([LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md)).** Both reject eliminative
  reductionism. Both cite the illusion-of-control panic study as a
  psychological, top-down effect. Kendler's "biology implements
  psychology" is compatible with their demand that processes be stated in
  modifiable psychological terms.
- **Salkovskis, Sighvatsson and Sigurdsson ([LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md)).** The fearful
  child whose avoidance "prevent[s] the habituation of the initial fear
  response" is a maintenance loop of exactly the kind their Theory A is
  built on, with avoidance protecting a threat meaning.
- **Mental causation ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md)).** Pernu asks whether interventionist
  evidence supports nonreductive mental causation. Kendler's
  implementation relation offers a non-eliminative alternative to that
  framing: the psychological level is not reduced away, because it states
  what the mechanism does.

## Bearing on the record

- **It supports the convergence proposed in the batch report** (disorder is
  maintained by self-reinforcing loops, not a single latent cause). Kendler
  adds that the loops cross levels, including gene–environment and
  top-down loops. That would widen the candidate THEORY from "loops among
  symptoms" to "loops across levels".
- **Candidate THEORY, Proposed:** "In explaining psychiatric disorders,
  biological findings implement psychological functions rather than
  replacing them." Source: this paper. Promote it only with further
  sources and with cases from psychiatry, not vision or memory.
- **No ML instruction.** Marr's levels are well known in machine learning,
  but here they are used for psychiatric explanation. Nothing here belongs
  in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Illustrative, not systematic.** The alcohol-dependence sketch is "much
  simplified", and the loop examples are selected cases.
- **Marr's generalisation is asserted.** The paper concedes that many
  disorders are not yet amenable to an information-processing account,
  then holds that the logic applies "across the board".
- **No account of how reassembly is done** beyond the analogies to cell
  biology and memory; "stitching" is named, not specified.
- **The implementation thesis is argued from neuroscience successes in
  perception, learning and memory**, not from any psychiatric case.

## Open questions

- Are there psychiatric constructs that biology will replace rather than
  implement, as kinetic energy did temperature? The paper names none and
  rules none out.
- Do cross-level loops of Kendler's kind fit inside a symptom-network model
  ([LIT-547](../literature.d/LIT-547.md)), or do they need nodes that are not symptoms?
- What would show that a reassembled multilevel model of a disorder is
  complete?
