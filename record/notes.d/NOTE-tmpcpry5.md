# NOTE-tmpcpry5: Intentional Systems

## Contribution

The paper names a predictive strategy that everyone already used, the
Intentional stance, and separates it from two others, the design and
physical stances. It then puts the strategy to three uses. It gives a
criterion for having beliefs that needs no answer about consciousness or
composition. It gives an accounting rule, the loan of intelligence, for
finding the hidden homunculi in a theory of the mind. And it locates the
normativity of belief in the preconditions for ascribing it.

## Key insight

To treat something as a believer is to bet that it is well designed for
its ends and then to compute what a rational agent in its position would
do. The bet can be good without settling what the thing is made of or
whether it feels anything. But every time a theory uses that kind of
description for a part of the mind, it borrows intelligence it has not yet
explained, and the debt is repaid only when the part's "reader" is
analysed into something that needs no Intentional description.

## Assumptions

- **Intentionality is linguistic** (fn. 1, p. 87). An idiom is Intentional
  if co-designative substitution fails to preserve truth or its objects
  resist ordinary quantification. Dennett calls this "apparently slapdash"
  and relies on consensus about cases.
- **Optimal design** is the anchor. Rationality "so far means nothing more
  than optimal design relative to a goal or optimally weighted hierarchy of
  goals" and constraints (p. 89).
- **Natural selection warrants the assumption** for organisms. With it, one
  can ascribe beliefs and desires to aliens from their needs and
  environment, "conceptually innocent anthropomorphizing" (pp. 92–93).
- **Rationality includes logic.** An Intentional system must be supposed
  to follow modus ponens or believe its corresponding conditional; there is
  a trade-off between rules and truths (pp. 94–95, fn. 5 on Carroll's
  tortoise).

## Key results

The paper argues; it proves nothing and reports no data.

- **The three stances** (pp. 87–90). Design-stance predictions assume no
  malfunction; Intentional predictions additionally assume optimal design,
  so they fail on "design weaknesses and lapses". For the best chess
  programs only the Intentional stance is practical.
- **Observer-relativity and pragmatism** (pp. 87, 91). Being an Intentional
  system is relative to "the strategies of someone who is trying to explain
  and predict its behavior"; whether one calls what is ascribed "beliefs or
  belief-analogues or information complexes or Intentional whatnots makes
  no difference" to the calculation; one may "switch stances at will".
- **Possession of information is already Intentional** (pp. 90–91). It is
  "epistemic possession", not storage: the sheik who owns the Britannica
  but reads no English shows the difference.
- **Common sense is Intentional** for people and animals (pp. 93–94).
  Consistently failed predictions push us to a design stance, as with the
  insane.
- **Imperfection degrades prediction** (pp. 95–96). As logical truths are
  banished from a creature's beliefs, Intentional prediction becomes
  "cumbersome and undecidable" and ends in the design stance. Moving to
  design-stance explanation is "the proper direction for theory builders",
  since stopping at the Intentional level leaves intelligence unexplained.
- **Loans of intelligence** (p. 96). Endowing a state with content posits
  a reader; the loan must be repaid by "finding and analyzing away these
  readers or comprehenders". Intentionality is the "medium of exchange":
  "wherever a theory relies on a formulation bearing the logical marks of
  Intentionality, there a little man is concealed."
- **Behaviourism masks Intentional prediction** (pp. 97–99). The
  mathematician in a Skinner box presses "35" before training; the mouse
  trained to take four steps would, on Skinner's laws, jab at air if the
  bar moved; a man trained to pick the longer display would be immune to
  Müller-Lyer. The box leaves room for one motion and no false belief.
- **The research programme** (pp. 99–100). "Intentional theory is vacuous as
  psychology because it presupposes and does not explain rationality", yet
  "our most promising tactic is to take out intelligence-loans … and then
  look for mechanisms" that pay them back. AI does exactly this. Game theory
  and economics are Intentional but normative, and economics succeeds
  "because individual men are in general good approximations of the optimal
  operator in the marketplace".
- **Unmetaphysical** (p. 100). The concept abstracts from "composition,
  constitution, consciousness, morality, or divinity"; it is "much easier"
  to decide whether a machine is an Intentional system than whether it
  thinks or is conscious. "Are there mental treasures that cannot be
  purchased with Intentional coin?"
- **Belief's two norms** (pp. 101–104). (1) Normally, if x believes p, p is
  true; (2) normally, if x avows p, x believes p. Hence "a false belief
  system is a conceptual impossibility" (p. 101), and a capacity for false
  communication would be "just an emission proclivity". The norms break the
  belief–desire circle "in the long run and in general" (p. 103).
- **Incorrigibility** (pp. 103–106). When avowals are irrational, leaning on
  rationality overrides the speaker and leaning on avowal grants him
  irrational beliefs; "there is no coherent Intentional description of such
  an impasse". Neither phenomenology nor neurophysiology breaks the tie,
  since both presuppose a rationality standard. Fixing a tolerated error
  rate would be an "Official Tolerance Rule", confusing "the norm they
  follow with what gets by in their world" (p. 106).

## Concepts

- **Intentional system**: a system whose behaviour can be (at least
  sometimes) explained and predicted by ascribing beliefs and desires (p. 87),
  relative to someone's strategy.
- **Design stance**: prediction from functional design assuming no
  malfunction, "irrespective of the physical constitution" (p. 88).
- **Physical stance**: prediction from physical state and laws of nature;
  the only stance that predicts malfunction (p. 88).
- **Intentional stance**: prediction assuming optimal design, ascribing the
  information and goals the system ought to have (p. 90).
- **Loan of intelligence**: the debt a theory incurs by endowing a state
  with content, which posits an unexplained reader (p. 96).
- **Epistemic possession**: having information in the sense belief
  requires, not mere storage (p. 90).

## Connections

It cites Brentano for Intentionality, Quine (*Word and Object* §13) on
translating logical connectives, Carroll's tortoise, Skinner and Quine as
the behaviourists, Hintikka's epistemic logic (fn. 7, fn. 12), Phillips
Griffiths and Mayo on belief's normativity (fn. 9), and Dennett's own
*Content and Consciousness* (1969) and "Mechanism and Responsibility"
(fn. 2, "forthcoming"). It is reprinted as chapter 1 of *Brainstorms*
(1978).

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md).** The bridge account says the downward and upward
  readings agree on agency and divide on experience. This paper supplies the
  agency side with a criterion explicitly blind to consciousness (C2), which
  is why agency and experience can come apart there at all. It does not
  apply the stance to groups: economics is Intentional because individuals
  approximate optimal agents, not because a market is one (p. 100). The
  loan rule (C4) is the constraint on the downward direction: Minsky's
  agents ([LIT-tmp4jv7c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4jv7c.md)) explain a mind only if their content is repaid in
  parts that need none. "Artificial Intelligence as Philosophy and as
  Psychology" ([LIT-tmp13bcy](../literature.d/LIT-tmp13bcy.md)), unread here, is where Dennett says how.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) (AI consciousness).** C2 is a reason to keep belief
  ascriptions to AI systems out of the evidence for their consciousness. The
  paper itself makes the separation, at the definition.
- **[NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md) and [LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md).** Schwitzgebel's §6 answers a pragmatic objection
  Dennett sent him in correspondence. This 1971 paper is pragmatic about
  belief and silent on ascribing consciousness, so it is not the source of
  that objection. True Believers' requirement that a believer be "reliably
  and voluminously" predictable is the nearer ancestor.
- **[LIT-206](../literature.d/LIT-206.md) (Goldstein & Lederman).** Their "objective" interpretationism is
  not relativised to an interpreter; this paper's is (C1). The relativity is
  what True Believers ([LIT-tmp45885](../literature.d/LIT-tmp45885.md)) later withdraws.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- The criterion is stated, not defended against over-generation. The paper
  never asks whether a lectern or a thermostat qualifies; True Believers
  does.
- "Rationality" is optimal design relative to goals the predictor ascribes,
  so the criterion's content depends on goal ascriptions it does not
  constrain.
- The evolutionary arguments for belief's norms (pp. 101–102) are
  assertions about what selection would favour, with no evidence offered.
- Its picture of 1971 chess programs as inaccessible to design-stance
  prediction is asserted.

## Open questions

- Can a loan of intelligence always be repaid by decomposition, or only
  sometimes? The paper states the debt; Brainstorms ch. 7 claims the
  repayment schedule.
- If the stance is pragmatic and observer-relative, is there any fact about
  whether a system "really" believes? The paper says the question is
  "misplaced" (p. 91); True Believers answers it differently.

## Corrections

- The paper is the first *printed* statement of the stance. Its fn. 2
  points to "Mechanism and Responsibility", then forthcoming, and the 2017
  *Brainstorms* publisher's description calls that essay the first
  articulation. "Introduces" in the earlier draft of the LIT was too strong
  and has been changed.
