# ARG-tmpx1cys: That preserving an arrangement's structure does not preserve its capacities, applied to conservatism, shows that conservatism conflates persistence with value and counts only the dangers of reform

## The inference

Premise ([CLAIM-tmpa8tdg](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa8tdg.md)): keeping an arrangement's structure does not keep
what it realizes, persistence does not show justified value, and the status
quo has costs. Unstated premise: conservatism holds the contrary.
Conclusion ([CLAIM-tmplc0vv](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmplc0vv.md)): conservatism is deficient there.

## Critical questions

- **Does the target satisfy the rule's condition?** Not as the tradition's
  theorists state it. A38 §3 concedes that Burke's conservatism "is not
  simply a doctrine of maintaining every existing arrangement", and Burke's
  own text pairs "conservation and correction" ([LIT-tmpus6zs](../literature.d/LIT-tmpus6zs.md), checked). The
  exchange names no theorist who holds the conflation.
- **Is the theory's own warrant the one attacked?** No. Its warrant is a
  presumption under uncertainty and irreversibility, built on premises A38
  §3 grants (incompletely legible dependencies, reconfiguration beyond
  slack). [CLAIM-tmp8mpgs](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8mpgs.md) shows such a presumption can be cost-minimizing
  even when the reform wins in expectation, so "the status quo is not a
  zero-cost baseline" does not by itself tell against it.
- **What survives?** That the cost of waiting falls on those the
  arrangement burdens (A40 §4.4), and that the presumption must be for the
  arrangement's goods, not its structure (A40 §4.3). Both are held in
  [CLAIM-tmpa8tdg](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa8tdg.md) and [CLAIM-tmpdzcj6](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdzcj6.md).
- **What would revive it?** A conservative position shown from its texts to
  treat endurance as establishing value, or popular conservatism shown to;
  [LEAD-tmpdy0f8](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpdy0f8.md) would look.
