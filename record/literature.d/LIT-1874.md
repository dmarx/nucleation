# LIT-1874: Corporate Agency and Possible Futures

Mulgan, T. (2019). Corporate Agency and Possible Futures. *Journal of
Business Ethics*, 154(4), 901–916. doi:10.1007/s10551-018-3887-1.
Published online 3 May 2018. Open access, CC BY 4.0.

## Key takeaways

- Four positions on corporate agency, of which "I can find no mainstream
  theorist who defends extreme collectivism" (p. 904).
- A moralised condition: "The most demanding moral criterion would require
  reliably appropriate response" (p. 905). A group with no mechanism for
  it "is irredeemably psychopathic" (p. 910).
- "The only temporally robust account is moralised extreme collectivism"
  (p. 914), an argument from what future philosophers will find credible.

## Why this is here

[CLAIM-271](../claims.d/CLAIM-271.md), [THEORY-131](../theory.d/THEORY-131.md) and [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) each record that Velasquez's
objection, that a corporation is not an agent and so not morally
responsible ([LIT-1429](LIT-1429.md), [LIT-1432](LIT-1432.md)), is registered and unread. Both of
Velasquez's papers are Unavailable. This paper was found on 2026-10-30 as
one that sets out Velasquez's position among the accounts of corporate
agency it weighs.

Read in full on 2026-11-22 ([NOTE-2306](../notes.d/NOTE-2306.md); [THEORY-2216](../theory.d/THEORY-2216.md)). It **stands
in for Velasquez only in part**: it reports what he holds and which
premises he relies on (biological embodiment, Searle's account of
consciousness, a realist method), by citation and paraphrase, and not his
argument, so his objection is still not answered in the record. Its
citation of "Velasquez (2003), p. 38" does not match its own reference
list or [LIT-1432](LIT-1432.md)'s pagination. It also bears on [QUESTION-265](../questions.d/QUESTION-265.md), through its
psychopathic corporate groups.
