# LIT-tmp13bcy: Artificial Intelligence as Philosophy and as Psychology

Daniel C. Dennett (1978), "Artificial Intelligence as Philosophy and as
Psychology", ch. 7 of *Brainstorms: Philosophical Essays on Mind and
Psychology*, Montgomery, VT: Bradford Books, 1978 (Library of Congress
78013723). Also in M. Ringle (ed.), *Philosophical Perspectives in
Artificial Intelligence*, Humanities Press and Harvester Press, 1979. Reprinted in the MIT Press edition of
*Brainstorms* (1981; DOI 10.7551/mitpress/1664.003.0011) and its
40th-anniversary edition (2017, pp. 119–138).

## Key takeaways

*Registered from a contemporary summary, not a reading. Everything below is
what Stich (1980) reports, with his page references to the 1978 printing.
Dennett's reply begins: "I have no quarrel with Stich's summary of my
views."*

- **The worry.** Stich calls the essay "a travel guide for philosophers"
  to AI, which also states and answers what Dennett labels **Hume's
  Problem**: a representation is useless unless someone reads it, so a
  psychology of internal representations seems to need a homunculus to
  interpret them, and so to be circular or regressive.
- **The answer.** Homunculi are harmless if they are stupider than the
  whole. "If one can get a team or committee of relatively ignorant,
  narrow-minded, blind homunculi to produce the intelligent behavior of the
  whole, this is progress" (p. 123). Each can be explained by "teams of
  still stupider ones" (Stich's paraphrase), and "eventually this nesting
  of boxes within boxes lands you with homunculi so stupid ... that they
  can be, as one says, 'replaced by a machine'" (p. 124). The ellipsis is
  Stich's.
- **AI as the method.** AI's bottom-level homunculi are and-gates and
  or-gates, and AI theorists organise "armies of such idiots" (p. 124, as
  quoted by Stich) to produce intelligent behaviour. No homunculus may
  "duplicate entire the talents they are rung in to explain" (p. 123).
  "Homunculus talk is ubiquitous in AI" (p. 123).

**The brief's "army of idiots".** The phrase Stich quotes is "armies of
such idiots", and he quotes it as a fragment. I could not read the sentence
it comes from, so it is not quoted in full here.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request for Dennett, as the essay in
which the debt that "Intentional Systems" ([LIT-tmpt1t9z](LIT-tmpt1t9z.md), read in
[NOTE-tmpcpry5](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpcpry5.md)) names in 1971, the "loan of intelligence" that every
content-ascribing theory takes out, is said to be repayable.

**How it relates to Minsky and Block**, on Stich's summary:

- **Minsky** ([LIT-tmp4jv7c](LIT-tmp4jv7c.md), the 1977 memo, read in [NOTE-tmp6hixb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6hixb.md); *The
  Society of Mind*, [LIT-tmpqepho](LIT-tmpqepho.md), unread). Both describe a mind as an
  organisation of agents none of which has the whole's competence. They
  differ in direction and in what the agents are for. Dennett's homunculi
  are an *explanatory* device: boxes in a top-down flow chart, each
  borrowing intelligence, posited and then discharged by decomposition
  until nothing borrowed remains. Minsky's agents are a *design*: simple
  from the start, built upward, and their interactions are the mind. So
  Dennett's argument is the licence for Minsky's picture rather than a
  version of it: it says why a mind of agents is not a regress, as long as
  the agents are stupider than what they compose. Whether either text cites
  the other is not known here.
- **Block** ([LIT-tmpc6np5](LIT-tmpc6np5.md), read in [NOTE-tmpxt8ou](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxt8ou.md)). Block's homunculi-heads
  run the other way. His homunculi are whole people, the citizens of China,
  each doing one table entry, so they are *smarter* than the role they
  fill. Block's note 19 replaces each one with a mindless gate and says his
  doubt about qualia survives. That replacement is the bottom rung of
  Dennett's discharge, reached in one step. So discharge does not answer
  Block: it shows that homunculi cost a theory of *intelligence* nothing,
  which Block concedes (he grants a homunculi-head beliefs); Block's doubt
  is about qualia, which discharge does not address. The two essays appeared
  in the same year, 1978.

For [THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md) this is the downward direction's warrant. A society of
mindless agents explains a mind exactly when it discharges its homunculi,
and Schwitzgebel's upward case ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md)) is the one where the parts are as
smart as, or smarter than, the whole, the case discharge was never meant
to license. Selfridge's demons ([LIT-tmpol46q](LIT-tmpol46q.md)) sit on Dennett's side of that line, and
so, as [THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md) describes it, does Hofstadter's ant colony in GEB
([LIT-tmpsm6ia](LIT-tmpsm6ia.md), unread).

A reading should check, against the essay itself: the full "armies of such
idiots" sentence; whether the essay cites Minsky, Selfridge or Newell and
Simon for the top-down method; and whether it says anything about
consciousness or only about intelligence. The Mind's I ([LIT-tmpg4bap](LIT-tmpg4bap.md)) and
Searle's Chinese room ([LIT-tmp543s0](LIT-tmp543s0.md), whose systems reply is a
homunculus question) are the nearest filed neighbours.

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice. It is a
philosopher's account of 1970s AI method, and the anthology does not hold
it.
