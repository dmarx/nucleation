# NOTE-490: Block — The Mind as the Software of the Brain

## Contribution

It sets out the computer model as a set of distinct claims, about
intelligence, about meaning-respecting processing, and about
intentionality, and shows they stand or fall separately. Its new pieces,
relative to Block's earlier papers, are the clean intelligence/intentionality
split (future- versus past-oriented), the counterfactual answer to "is a wall a
computer?", and the "Reductionist Cruncher" against Stich.

## Key insight

The software metaphor is three metaphors. "Functional analysis" explains
intelligence by mechanical decomposition. The "syntactic engine" explains
how a symbol-shuffler can respect meaning, by an isomorphism. Neither
explains intentionality, which needs a theory of where symbols get their
meaning, and a swamp-brain shows intelligence without it.

## Assumptions

- **Two kinds of definition**: of the word versus of the thing, as with
  water and H₂O; intelligence is a functional kind, like a gene or a
  mousetrap (§11.1.2).
- **Primitive processors** are where cognitive science hands over to a
  "realization" science, and the boundary is as fuzzy as chemistry's with
  physics (§11.1.4).
- **The orthodoxy**: Fodor's language of thought, with meaning from
  functional role, including perception and action (§11.3).
- **A protoscientific concept of belief**: causally active, explicitly
  represented belief, distinct from the ordinary concept (§11.3.1).

## Key results

- **Turing test (§11.1.1).** Practical and theoretical uses fail; as
  conceptual clarification it lacks a specification of the judge, and making
  the judge good at thinking about thinking turns the test into "A machine
  thinks if our best thinkers ... think it thinks", which is false. The
  Aunt Bubbles machine refutes supervenience of the mental on the behavioural
  (n. 1, crediting Block 1978, 1981 and White 1982, and Jackson's name
  "Blockhead"). Turing's test focuses on performance, not competence.
- **Functional analysis (§§11.1.3–11.1.5).** Multiplication decomposes into
  adding, decrementing and testing for zero; an adder into AND and XOR gates.
  Computational equivalence of non-primitive devices is not input–output
  equivalence: two multipliers with identical behaviour, one converting to
  binary, are not equivalent.
- **Intelligence vs intentionality (§11.2).** Intentionality without
  intelligence (thermostats on McCarthy's view, tree rings, words on a page,
  with only derived intentionality); intelligence without intentionality
  (the swamp-brain).
- **Syntactic and semantic functions (§11.2.1).** The symbol function maps
  numeral pairs to numerals; the semantic function maps numbers to numbers;
  their isomorphism explains how the adder adds. Symbols are found by first
  mapping rational relations, then identifying states by function.
- **Is a wall a computer? (§11.2.2).** Searle's isomorphism is "cheap" only
  if it need cover the actual run; it must cover what the machine "could have
  performed". Whether something is a computer is "in part up to us", but
  "not totally".
- **Dennett's objections to the language of thought (§11.3.1).** Infinite
  beliefs: protoscientific belief is explicit, so most people did not believe
  trees do not light up like fireflies until they read the sentence. Queen out
  early: no explicit rule, so no belief in the protoscientific sense, and
  predicting from it can mislead. Sister in Cleveland: belief is a holistic
  computational relation, not mere storage.
- **Systematicity (§11.4).** It is right for conscious thought, where external
  symbols can themselves be the combinatorial medium (arithmetic), but its
  application to unconscious processing is "more tenuous"; an imagery system
  might explain animals' red/green symmetry.
- **Reductionist Cruncher (§11.5).** The argument that syntax undermines
  content would let physicochemistry undermine syntax and quantum theory
  undermine statistical mechanics; "nature has real joints at many levels".
  Whether a vocabulary marks a genuine level is empirical.
- **Chinese room (§11.6).** The systems reply's negative half: from "Bill does
  not understand Chinese" one cannot infer the system does not. Its positive
  half: two systems can share a body, as in multiple personality. The
  nine-to-five extension shows which system implements which. Block doubts
  the Chinese system is phenomenally conscious and suggests Searle's real
  argument is that intentionality requires consciousness (Searle 1992).

## Concepts

- **Primitive processor** — a component whose operation is explained by a
  realization science, not cognitive science (§11.1.4).
- **Syntactic engine driving a semantic engine** — a device whose symbol
  transitions are isomorphic to rational relations among their meanings
  (§11.2.1).
- **Original vs derived intentionality** — content a system's states have
  for it, versus content inherited from such systems (§11.2).
- **Protoscientific belief** — a causally active, explicitly represented
  belief (§11.3.1).
- **Reductionist Cruncher** — the observation that any argument from a lower
  level's greater generality and grain to a higher level's dispensability
  applies again one level down (§11.5).

  equivalence of non-primitive devices is fixed by their internal organisation,
  not input–output behaviour.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- A survey: Stich's argument is "a simplified version", and the reply to
  Searle is the systems reply without CRS's theory of meaning.
- The intelligence/intentionality split rests on the swamp-brain intuition,
  which Block says "arguably" lacks intentional states.
- The consciousness doubt is stated, not argued.

## Open questions

- If consciousness depends on implementation, which features of
  implementation, and how would that be tested? The chapter leaves it at a
  doubt.
