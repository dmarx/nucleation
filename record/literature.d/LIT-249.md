# LIT-249: Provable Guarantees for Self-Supervised Deep Learning with Spectral Contrastive Loss

Jeff Z. HaoChen, Colin Wei, Adrien Gaidon, Tengyu Ma (2021), *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 34 (NeurIPS 2021, oral); first appeared as arXiv preprint* — [ARXIV-2106.04156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04156)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpbkfrz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpbkfrz.md).*

- **The loss is a factorization.** The population augmentation graph has
  edge weights w_xx′ = E_x̄[A(x|x̄)A(x′|x̄)]. Writing each row of F as
  w_x^(1/2) f(x) turns ‖D^(−1/2)AD^(−1/2) − FFᵀ‖²_F into
  L(f) = −2E[f(x)ᵀf(x⁺)] + E[(f(x)ᵀf(x⁻))²] plus a constant (Lemma 3.2).
- **The minimizers are eigenvectors.** By Eckart–Young, F̂ = F*·diag(√γ)·R,
  with F* the top-k eigenvectors of the normalized adjacency and R
  orthonormal. A positive row scaling and an invertible right factor leave
  linear-probe predictions unchanged (Lemma 3.1).
- **The guarantee.** If augmentations predict the label with error at most
  α, the linear probe on the population minimizer has error
  Õ(α/ρ²_⌊k/2⌋) (Theorem 3.8). Here ρ_i is the sparsest i-partition
  conductance of the graph. The proof is a Rayleigh-quotient bound on
  class indicators (Theorem B.3) and a higher-order Cheeger inequality.
- **Finite samples.** The empirical loss is unbiased, and Rademacher
  bounds carry the guarantee to finite pretraining data (Theorems
  4.1–4.3), assuming the hypothesis class contains a population
  minimizer.
- **Infinite spaces, conditionally.** Appendix F assumes a uniform bound
  w(u,v)/w(u) ≤ B·w(v), derives that the normalized kernel is
  Hilbert–Schmidt, and proves that an orthonormal eigenbasis exists. The
  extension of the main theorems is asserted with the proof omitted.
- **Experiments.** Linear-probe accuracy matches or beats reproduced
  SimCLR and SimSiam on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, and is 66.97% on
  ImageNet at 100 epochs. The trained encoder projects outputs to a sphere,
  which the theory does not.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 while pursuing, at the owner's request, the connection
*Riesz, reproducing kernels and spectral representation learning* (see the
curation entry of that date). Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmpbkfrz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpbkfrz.md).

It is held as the founding statement that a contrastive objective is a
spectral decomposition of an operator built from the positive-pair
distribution. The other spectral readings of self-supervised learning
build on it or restate it: Johnson, El Hanchi and Maddison ([LIT-258](LIT-258.md)),
Tan et al. ([LIT-227](LIT-227.md)), Balestriero and LeCun ([LIT-228](LIT-228.md)) and [LIT-235](LIT-235.md).

- **[THEORY-007](../theory.d/THEORY-007.md)** cites Lemmas 3.1–3.2 and states them correctly.
- **[THEORY-001](../theory.d/THEORY-001.md)** cites Lemma 3.2 in support. The paper's minimizer is
  rank-k; it does not name K⁺.
- **[THEORY-005](../theory.d/THEORY-005.md)** describes Assumption F.1 as Hilbert–Schmidt, the
  finite-χ² condition. The NOTE records that it is a stronger,
  uniform-bound assumption.
- **[THEORY-009](../theory.d/THEORY-009.md)** is borne out: the eigenbasis comes from the symmetry of w,
  used throughout though never named, and Riesz does not appear.
- **[THEORY-tmpakup2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpakup2.md)** is filed from it: the linear-probe guarantee of
  Thm 3.8, at the population optimum of the spectral contrastive loss.

Lemma 3.1 is the formal version of "a linear probe only sees a
representation up to GL(k)", with a positive per-point scaling added. That
bears on concept-direction geometry (cf. [ANTH-LIT-606](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-606.md)).

Tagged `anthology-candidate` ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)). The paper proposes a training loss
and an encoder design, which is machine-learning practice. It is kept here
by the owner's decision of 2026-09-26 that new work stays in nucleation
until a transfer is judged appropriate ([ADR-010](../decisions.d/ADR-010.md)). What this record reads
it for is the operator statement, not the practice.
