# NOTE-tmpv5axa: Martinengo et al. — Spaced digital education for health professionals

## Contribution

A meta-analytic estimate of the spacing effect in digital education for
health professionals, kept apart by modality (spaced online education,
such as email modules and question apps, against spaced digital
simulation on high-fidelity or VR simulators) and by outcome level
(knowledge, skills, behaviour). It also documents that the included
studies give no rationale for their spacing intervals.

## Key insight

Spreading digital learning over repeated sessions helps knowledge a
little and practice somewhat, measured just after training; for
simulated skills the evidence is two small studies.

## Assumptions

- **Spacing** is two or more repetitions of the content on different
  days; intervals of less than a day are excluded.
- **Effect** is measured at immediate post-intervention: "We used the
  immediate postintervention results in the analyses" (Methods).
- **Design**: RCTs, cluster RCTs and quasi-RCTs, searched from January
  1990 to February 2023; groups combined in multi-arm studies.

## Key results

- **Knowledge (Abstract).** "Spaced online education was superior to
  massed online education for postintervention knowledge (n=9, 39%;
  standardized mean difference [SMD] 0.32, 95% CI 0.13-0.51, I2=66%,
  moderate certainty of evidence)."
- **Behaviour change (Abstract).** Spaced online education beat massed
  online education and no intervention, SMD 0.67 (0.43–0.91), moderate
  certainty.
- **Simulation skills, as the abstract gives it.** "Spaced digital
  simulation was superior to massed simulation for postintervention
  surgical skills (n=2, 9%; SMD 1.15, 95% CI 0.34-1.96, I2=74%, low
  certainty of evidence)."
- **Simulation skills, as the Results give it.** "The pooled estimate of
  2 (67%) studies [76,79] showed that spaced digital simulation is
  superior to massed simulation (SMD 1.24, 95% CI 0.84-1.64, I2=74%) in
  improving learners’ skills." The two disagree, and study [79] concerns
  nursing students managing a patient with chronic lymphocytic leukemia,
  not a surgical skill.
- **Time and errors (Results, Skills).** "The pooled estimates for the
  time to complete a task (3, 60%, studies) and the number of errors (2,
  40%, studies) showed a nonsignificant difference between intervention
  and control groups."
- **Knowledge retention (Results).** "The pooled estimate showed a small
  difference in knowledge retention favoring spaced online education over
  controls (SMD 0.38, 95% CI 0.10-0.65, I2=0%)."
- **Intervals (Discussion).** "none of the studies provided a rationale
  for this differing length".
- **Bias (Abstract).** "Most studies had an unclear or a high risk of
  bias (n=19, 83%)."

## Concepts

- **Spaced education:** "an educational technique that promotes
  long-term knowledge retention via multiple repeated learning sessions
  separated by prespecified time intervals".
- **Spaced digital simulation:** "studies using high-fidelity simulators,
  including virtual reality (VR)–based instruments, to provide skills
  training and assessment that was repeated over spaced intervals."
- **Outcome levels:** mapped onto Miller's levels of clinical competence
  (knows, knows how, shows how, does).

## Connections

**Against Patocka et al., [LIT-tmps018e](../literature.d/LIT-tmps018e.md), known only from its abstract:**

- *Restates:* the comparison of spaced with massed simulation training
  for health professionals; skills outcomes for surgical tasks;
  heterogeneity of designs and outcomes; the absence of patient outcomes
  for simulation (behaviour change for simulation rests on one study
  here); the lack of a rationale for spacing intervals.
- *Does not restate:* Patocka's retention advantage for spaced training,
  particularly on time to complete procedures, since this review uses
  immediate post-training results only and its pooled time outcome is
  non-significant; Patocka's overall verdict that spaced training was
  "generally as effective" as massed, where this review's two-study pool
  favours spacing.
- *Scope differs:* this review is restricted to digital or high-fidelity
  simulation and to randomized and quasi-randomized designs, but includes
  spaced online education; Patocka covers all simulation and prospective
  comparative designs, without online education.

## Limitations

The authors':

- no spacing-specific search terms (compensated by citation searching);
- heterogeneous outcomes and instruments;
- mostly unclear or high risk of bias;
- too few studies for subgroup or publication-bias analyses.

The reader's:

- the abstract and body disagree on the headline simulation SMD (1.15
  against 1.24);
- a nursing case-management study is pooled as "surgical skills";
- title screening for the 2023 update was by one reviewer with
  machine-learning assistance (ASReview, with a stopping rule);
- the Results' risk-of-bias counts do not obviously sum to the abstract's
  19.

## Open questions

- Does spacing in simulation training improve retention over months, not
  only performance just after training? A trial with delayed retention
  tests would tell; Patocka's review, if read, may hold such studies.
