# THEORY-tmpw20ca: Under active inference, the contents of consciousness are posterior beliefs about the hidden states of world, body or brain, and a posterior is conscious when it has the capacity to inform discrete, counterfactual policy selection, at a timescale slow enough to abstract from sensory flux and with enough precision to tell alternatives apart; on this account no conscious content changes without a change in the posterior, and a stimulus given precision by an attention or saccade policy should reach awareness sooner than a retinally matched one

## Source

Whyte et al. (2026), LIT-tmpyamns, read in full in NOTE-tmpjgegs from
arXiv:2410.06633v2: section 5 (pp. 24–31), with the models of section 4 and
Appendices 1–2. Page numbers are the manuscript's. The modelling studies it
reviews were not read.

## What was actually shown

The paper is a review with a theoretical conclusion, and three small
simulations of its own.

- **The core, by inspection of the models.** Every reviewed model minimises
  variational and expected free energy, so "all behaviour, both conscious
  and unconscious, must, in some minimal sense, be aimed at minimising these
  quantities" (p. 25). Each model maps conscious content to the posterior
  over states: "The most minimal such link implicit in all the models
  reviewed above is between the contents of consciousness and the inferred
  hidden state" (p. 25). Each model reads out awareness where a discrete
  level drives report.
- **The criterion.** "the conscious/unconscious distinction is tied to the
  mechanism(s) of conscious access, and that it occurs at the
  discrete-continuous interface" (p. 26). It is dispositional: "it is the
  capacity (in a counterfactual sense) for such a posterior belief to inform
  policy selection processes at the right timescale and with sufficient
  precision that makes it conscious" (p. 27). The authors hold it "a
  necessary and sufficient condition for content to become conscious in
  humans (and non-human animals with similar neuronal architectures)"
  (p. 27).
- **What could have come out otherwise.** The reviewed no-report model
  lost its prefrontal and P3b-like responses without report while the
  second-level posterior stayed reportable (p. 18); it might have lost
  both. The paper's re-run rivalry model fits Levelt's second and fourth
  propositions (Appendix 1); it might not have. Two predictions are named
  as close to the core (pp. 29–30): reward should trade off against
  epistemic value in rivalry (Appendix 2), and "To See is to Look", so a
  precision-weighted stimulus "will enter the contents of consciousness
  sooner than a retinally-matched stimulus that is not the target of a
  saccade" (p. 30). Neither is tested in the paper.

## What this does not say

- **It is not a criterion for experience in general.** Its condition is
  the mechanism of access, read off models that had to report. The authors
  "remain agnostic as to whether this condition is both necessary and
  sufficient in systems vastly different to ourselves" (p. 27), and say the
  link between content and posterior "is consistent with non-conscious
  creatures exhibiting behaviour governed by active inference" (p. 26). It
  does not meet CLAIM-tmp1lvwf's defeater, which asks for a condition
  independent of report.
- **The third part is not derived.** The first two parts are "in some sense
  logically entailed" by the framework; the third is "an inference to the
  best explanation" (p. 27).
- **The content link is assumed in every model, not tested.** "Assuming a
  correspondence between the posterior over states and the content of
  awareness" (p. 14) is the step each model of content takes.
- **The empirical confirmations are second-hand.** One, of the no-report
  model's first prediction, is cited without a reference in v2 (p. 18).
- **It is not identity.** The paper speaks of correspondence and link. It
  takes no side between constitution and production (CLAIM-tmpdga80).
- **The precision prediction is not yet a test of experience.** A stimulus
  that reaches report sooner is a fact about access. Whether that is a fact
  about experience is QUESTION-048's question, and THEORY-094's point that
  post-perceptual cognition survives without report applies.
