  symmetric real scalar field in 3+1 dimensions with a symmetric or
  asymmetric double-well potential. Subcritical bubbles started at rest with
  Gaussian radius between about 2.4 and 4.5 m^-1 shed part of their energy

# LIT-tmpfxol8: Oscillons: Resonant configurations during bubble collapse

Copeland, E. J., Gleiser, M. & Müller, H.-R. (1995). Oscillons: Resonant configurations during bubble collapse. *Physical Review D* 52(4): 1920–1933. doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.52.1920. ARXIV-hep-ph/9503217.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpyp0bm, of arXiv v2. Section numbers are the paper's.*

- **Long-lived, not stable.** Oscillons are solutions which, "although
  unstable, are extremely long-lived" (Abstract), and "instability is a
  relative concept, which only makes sense in context" (§I): a lifetime
  means something only against the time-scales of the system it sits in.
- **Neither topology nor charge.** Defects evade Derrick's theorem because
  "Topological conservation laws guarantee the stability of these
  configurations", nontopological solitons through "the conserved global
  charge Q" (§I). An oscillon, a single real scalar, has neither.
- **A window, and a common remnant.** Bubbles inside a window of initial
  radius "settle into a period of long-lived stability" (§III); "after
  shedding a sufficient fraction of their initial energy, the subcritical
  bubbles enter the oscillon stage" at a plateau energy "regardless of their
  initial radius". Below the plateau energy, or with the core field short of
  the inflection point, none forms; these are "sufficient conditions".
- **The lower bound is explained, the upper is not.** "only for R0 > Rmin ≃
  2.42 are oscillons possible" (§IV.A), in agreement with the simulations;
  "why above a maximum initial bubble radius no oscillons are possible" is
  left as work "in progress" (§IV).
- **A plateau, then an abrupt end.** While it lasts "energy is slowly being
  radiated away" (§IV.B), until the core amplitude falls to a minimum
  "below which the oscillon slips into the linear regime and quickly
  decays" (§IV.A).
- **No lifetime formula.** "we were unable to obtain an analytical
  expression for the lifetime" (§IV.C); the best virialized live longest.
  Lifetimes are read off the energy plateau in the figures, "accurate to
  within 5%", with no stated rule for where the plateau starts and ends.
- **Radial only.** "we only investigated stability to radial perturbations"
  (§VII).

## Standing in the record

Filed for the owner's suggestion that soliton formation bears on the
record's questions of individuation and persistence. It is the record's
first reading on oscillons, and the third route to persistence that the
other works of this batch lack: Herr et al. (2014) and Grelu & Akhmediev
(2012) give structures held by a supply, Tong (2005) one held by an
invariant, Grimshaw et al. (2018) a soliton decaying under damping. Here
the structure is held by its own nonlinearity in a closed, conservative
system and leaks. It bears most on CLAIM-600, whose point that persistence
after withdrawal can be slow reversion it supports in a sharp form: a
plateau with practically no outflow for 10^3–10^4 m^-1, hundreds of
times the linear lifetime, then a sudden end. It qualifies CLAIM-604, since a formation threshold and
an abrupt collapse occur here with no flux and no upkeep at all; it
contrasts with the exponential decay after withdrawal in CLAIM-597; and it
gives TERM-088 a case of dynamical individuation with a finite life and no
outside cause of its end, a third kind beside THEORY-072's flame and
organism. THEORY-136's "slow return excluded" names the confusion it
illustrates. The later literature on decay is Fodor (2019), LIT-tmpbn6ad;
topological protection is in Tong (2005), LIT-tmptstez. Filed from it:
THEORY-tmplxt7t. No instruction for machine-learning practice.
