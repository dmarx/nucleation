# LIT-tmpbptbc: Intuitive ethics: how innately prepared intuitions generate culturally variable virtues

Jonathan Haidt, Craig Joseph (2004), *Dædalus* 133(4), Fall 2004, 55–66 —
DOI-10.1162/0011526042365555

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 in the batch the owner asked for, which fills out the
record's coverage of morality-as-cooperation with its own programme, its
rivals and its evolutionary sources ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)). This is the first of the
rivals. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

It is the text the record's main morality-as-cooperation paper argues
against. Curry, Mullins and Whitehouse's sixty-society test, [LIT-047](LIT-047.md), read
in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md), quotes it twice: for purity's original triggers, "people with
diseases, parasites [and] waste products" (Table 1, p. 59), and for purity
as an "odd corner" of morality (p. 60). Both quotations are accurate.
The second is used for more than it says. The essay calls purity odd "at
least for modern Westerners, who tend to think of morality as strictly
concerned with how we treat other people". It does not say purity is not
about how people treat each other, and it goes on to show purity
intuitions being used socially, for instance to teach racism (p. 63).

The essay also does not fit the later charge, made in [LIT-047](LIT-047.md), that the
foundations omit kin altruism and reciprocal altruism. Its suffering
module is grounded in a mother's response to her own child in distress
(pp. 58–60), and its reciprocity module in Trivers' reciprocal altruism
(fn. 9). [NOTE-tmpach38](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpach38.md) sets out the comparison.

The essay builds on Haidt's social intuitionist model, [LIT-tmpux6l9](LIT-tmpux6l9.md), which
it cites for the claim that moral judgment is mostly intuitive. Graham and
colleagues' 2013 chapter, [LIT-tmpa221k](LIT-tmpa221k.md), is the theory's later full
statement, and it extends this essay to five foundations with explicit
criteria. The purity foundation proposed here is what Fitouchi, André and
Baumard's moral disciplining theory ([LIT-101](LIT-101.md)) sets out to replace with a
cooperative account of puritanical norms.

[NOTE-tmpach38](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpach38.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is short, it is the source the later debate quotes, and it
states the nativist, intuitionist, pluralist position more plainly than the
long statements do. It is an essay: the four modules are proposed from a
reading of five books, and the evidence it mentions is cited, not shown.
