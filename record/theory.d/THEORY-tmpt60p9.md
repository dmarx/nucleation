# THEORY-tmpt60p9: Stark holds that coercive control, a strategic course of conduct combining violence, intimidation, isolation and control to dominate a partner and deprive her of rights and liberties, is the typical form of partner abuse, and that responses focused on discrete assaults miss it

Stark (2015), [LIT-tmpnw255](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnw255.md).

## The account

Abuse is typically a course of conduct (p. 4), its violence "repeated,
frequent or routine but low-level", combined with intimidation, isolation
and control; tactics that "taken alone, may not rise to the level of
abuse" make the pattern "in combination" (p. 5). Its harm is to "autonomy,
dignity and liberty" as well as safety (p. 6). So "Interventions focused on
discrete assaults (rather than course of conduct) miss the significance of
abuse in the vast majority of cases" (p. 3).

## What it does not show

- **Its figures.** Prevalence and arrest statistics are given without
  sources.
- **Anything about mutual violence.** The account has a perpetrator and a
  victim; it does not say how to judge a dominated party's own acts.
- **The book's argument.** It is a summary for legislators.
