# THEORY-tmp5hurd: Omidvar, Bestler, Zahedi Fard, Zilberberg and Serra-Garcia show, in a chain of buckled polymer beams under a standing and a cyclic compression, that bits stored as buckling domains are moved one unit cell per load cycle, the domain wall advancing when a boundary mode localized at it, softened by the opposite domain's pressure, becomes unstable, a ratchet they distinguish from Chern-number pumping; logic gates built from such racetracks are shown in simulation only

## The account

Main beams under strong static compression are bistable and store a bit. Coupling beams under weak compression link them. A cyclic compression that alternates sign every two sites gives a four-site pump pattern. When it runs, a single soliton (a domain of 1s between 0s) shifts by one unit cell per cycle, and exchanging the static compressions reverses the direction. The modelled cause is not band-topological pumping. On the experimental path the excitation bands close and the Chern number is ill-defined. A boundary mode appears at the wall from the bulk polarization of the excitations, and the opposite domain's pressure softens it until it goes unstable, so the wall hops two sites. The mode's softening to zero frequency just before each hop was measured and imaged. Branched racetracks with negative couplings give NAND, NOT and a half adder in tight-binding simulation.

## What it does not show

- Chern-number (index-protected) transport: the authors rule it out on their path.
- Persistence without the standing compression: the bits depend on it.
- Built logic circuits: these are simulated.
- That the text read (arXiv v1) matches the published PRL.
