# THEORY-441: In spatially compact vacuum general relativity the Hamiltonian vanishes, so time is represented by no flow on a symplectic space and change by no functions on a space of states; this problem of time does not come from the 3+1 split, is absent from asymptotically flat general relativity, and does not show change to be illusory

## Source

Belot, "The Representation of Time and Change in Mechanics", in Butterfield &
Earman (eds.), *Philosophy of Physics* (Elsevier 2007), pp. 133–227; [LIT-122](../literature.d/LIT-122.md),
read in full in [NOTE-118](../notes.d/NOTE-118.md).

## What was actually shown

**The well-behaved case (§5).** Given global existence, uniqueness and a
dynamical time-translation group, the space of solutions S and the space of
initial data I are symplectic. A slicing gives a one-parameter family of
symplectic isomorphisms T_Σt : S → I that intertwine time translation on S
with time evolution on I. That family lets initial data represent recurring
states, and lets functions on S represent changeable quantities.

**Damage that can be repaired (§6).** Singular dynamics (the planar Kepler
problem's solution space is non-Hausdorff), gauge freedom (presymplectic
spaces, isomorphic after reduction "when all goes well") and time-dependence
each break part of the picture, and each has a repair.

**General relativity (§7.1–7.2).** For vacuum GR with compact Cauchy surfaces
the Hamiltonian obtained by the usual rule is h ≡ 0. Dynamical trajectories
stay inside gauge orbits and are constant on the reduced space of initial
data (p. 202). There is no ℝ-action implementing time on S, on the reduced
S′ or on the reduced I′. Hence: "time is not represented in general relativity
by a flow on a symplectic space and change is not represented by functions on
a space of instantaneous or global states" (p. 209).

**What it is not caused by (p. 210).** The problem is derived in the covariant
Lagrangian picture, so it is not an artefact of the 3+1 decomposition. In
asymptotically flat GR the Poincaré group at infinity supplies a non-zero
Hamiltonian and change is represented in the familiar way (pp. 204–206).
Lack of a preferred slicing, slicing freedom and diffeomorphism invariance are
therefore not sufficient. The source is geometry that is fully dynamical, with
no background structure "at spatial infinity or elsewhere".

**Not an illusion (pp. 209–210).** The reduced space of solutions contains
worlds with change (Big Bang solutions) and without it (Einstein static). So
the formalism fails to represent change; it does not show there is none.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The problem could have disappeared in
the covariant picture, or appeared equally in asymptotically flat GR. It did
neither.

## What this does not say

- **That geometric time solves it.** Constant-mean-curvature time gives a
  time-dependent Hamiltonian for some spatial topologies (Example 54). Belot
  offers §7.3 "by way of further clarification of the problem of time, rather
  than as a suggested resolution" (p. 196).
- **That every generally covariant theory escapes or suffers it.** That an
  artificially covariant theory on a fixed background escapes is hedged ("I
  think", p. 210), with a residual worry about the conformal class.
- **That the reduced spaces are canonically isomorphic.** That is "presumed"
  (p. 202), citing Fischer & Moncrief.
- **That GR's content is the set of all its Hamiltonianizations.** That is a
  stated view, "entirely in the spirit" of GR (p. 220), not an argued result.
- **Anything settled about quantum gravity.** Distinct geometric times are not
  expected to give unitarily equivalent quantizations; the hopes are "long
  shots" (pp. 220–221).
- **That the result covers all of GR.** It is for vacuum, zero cosmological
  constant, globally hyperbolic, spatially compact solutions. The problem
  "only arises in those versions of general relativity most appropriate to the
  cosmological setting" (fn. 7, p. 137).
