# CLAIM-091: A translation can reach pragmatic equivalence by compensating for changed social coordinates with changed linguistic ones, so lexical infidelity can be required for pragmatic fidelity

## The claim

A24, with context C = (u, s, a, k, g) ([TERM-031](../terms.d/TERM-031.md)): translation changes the words
while trying to keep the speaker's relation to the audience,
(u, s, a, k, g) → (u′, s, a, k′, g′); reattribution keeps the words and changes
the footing, (u, s, a, k, g) → (u, s′, a, k, g) ([CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)). "**A translation
may achieve pragmatic equivalence by compensating for changes in the social
coordinates of an utterance through changes in its linguistic coordinates.** In
other words, lexical infidelity can be required for pragmatic fidelity."

A18 had the observation without the mechanism: "fidelity is sometimes achieved
*through* lexical deviation, not despite it."

## Where it went

The manuscript states the consequence (Abstract: an expression "can change
almost every word while preserving the interpersonal relation enacted by the
utterance") and its Case III manipulates social alignment with objects held
fixed, but the hypothesis that wording changes *because* the social coordinates
changed, and in proportion to them, is not stated. Nothing in owner turns
U1–U13 argued against it.

## What it does not say

That any lexical change is licensed. The claim is that some are required, and
which ones is set by the change of context.
