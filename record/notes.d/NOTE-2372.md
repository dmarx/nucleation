# NOTE-2372: Caprioglio and Berthouze 2024, read for whether a spectral gap between levels decides when a module acts as one oscillator, and when a reduction of the network needs a rate separation

## Contribution

A minimal model in which hierarchical modularity, without heterogeneous
couplings, delays or hubs, produces chimeras and metastability, and an
account of which states follow which part of the Laplacian spectrum. The
reading is [THEORY-2291](../theory.d/THEORY-2291.md).

## Key results

1. **The hypothesis, supported in the model** (abstract): "the brain's
   hierarchically modular mesoscale structure alone can give rise to
   robust metastable dynamics and (metastable) chimera states in the
   presence of phase frustration."
2. **Two pathways** (abstract): "In the upper layers, we show how the
   symmetry-breaking states depend on the slow eigenmodes of the system.
   In the lowest layer instead, metastable dynamics can be achieved as the
   separation of timescales between layers reaches a critical threshold."
3. **The module as a unit, by a gap** (§3.4, p. 14): the modules'
   metastability falls sharply once the second spectral gap exceeds about
   2; "when the timescale separation between hierarchical layers 1 and 2
   is large enough and modules are well defined, modules of oscillators
   remain completely synchronized, such that each subset effectively
   behaves as a single macro-oscillator." Below it, "the slow modes of the
   system [...] affect the synchronization dynamics within modules,
   resulting in random (de)synchronization patterns that persist".
4. **A reduction that needs the gap** (§3.4, pp. 13–14; §4, p. 16):
   keeping only the slow Laplacian modes and aggregating modules gives a
   weighted network like the two-population model, whose chimeras are
   known from an Ott–Antonsen analysis (their ref. 49). The match holds
   for low mean degree; "a good agreement with the results in [49] was
   only possible for low values of k and the presence of a large second
   spectral gap. This result suggests that, when the gap between fast and
   slow modes is small, the contribution from the fast modes should not
   be discarded."
5. **Not derived** (§4, pp. 16–17): "the precise mechanism that leads to
   metastable chimera states remains unclear"; "we cannot exclude the
   possibility that the behaviours we observed are the result of finite
   size effects"; and an Ott–Antonsen analysis of the nested model "is not
   immediate". They suggest a synergetic reading, the modules'
   fluctuations as those of "enslaved" fine-grained order parameters.
6. **Robustness** (§3.5): metastability in the upper layers is lost once
   about 10% of edges are rewired; with frequency spread δω < 2 (k = 51.2)
   or δω < 10 (k = 21) the results are unchanged.

## Assumptions

Identical natural frequencies (ω = 1); phase lag only between modules,
β = π/2 − α = 0.1; K = 50/k; Euler steps of 0.001; 100 network seeds. The
Laplacian is that of the linearized model without phase lag, used as a
guide to the frustrated dynamics, not derived for it.

## Concepts

Chimera (coexisting coherent and incoherent groups of identical
oscillators); metastability index (variance in time of a local order
parameter); spectral gaps of the graph Laplacian as gaps between the
characteristic times of layers; the Laplacian renormalization group,
which discards modes faster than a chosen time.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md)** (corroborates). Key result 3 is the claim's first half,
  with the condition of its version 5: the module becomes a unit, a
  constraint on its members' phases, when the rate gap between levels is
  large against the spread within; when the gap is small the slower modes
  break it up. Simulation, one model family.
- **The verdict on Ott and Antonsen** ([CLAIM-1058](../claims.d/CLAIM-1058.md), [CLAIM-1088](../claims.d/CLAIM-1088.md)).
  Key result 4: away from all-to-all coupling, the authors' reduction of a
  network to an Ott–Antonsen-solvable model held only with a large gap
  between fast and slow modes. Support for [CLAIM-1058](../claims.d/CLAIM-1058.md)'s point that the
  separation-free closure is particular to its class; it does not touch
  the reading of "more persistent".
- **[CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)** (corroborates, weakly). A module acts as one oscillator
  while its spectral gap is large, the gap-isolated block as the unit;
  the paper's threshold is numerical, for one network family.
- **[QUESTION-391](../questions.d/QUESTION-391.md)** (bears on). The coarse-grained description fails as the
  gap closes, because the fast modes come back into the slow dynamics
  (key result 4).
- **[CLAIM-481](../claims.d/CLAIM-481.md)** (note only). The gaps here are made by the network's
  modular structure under Laplacian-like coupling, as in the claim's
  Laplacian flow.

## Limitations

- Numerical only, N = 512, with a finite-size effect left open by the
  authors; the gap threshold near 2 is for one size and one H.
- The Laplacian spectrum describes the unfrustrated linearized model; how
  far it governs the frustrated dynamics is supported by the matches
  reported, not shown.
- The mechanism of the metastable chimeras is not given.
