# ADR-058: A THEORY can corroborate a CLAIM: evidence the record links to its own claim without the claim resting on it is corroborated_by, not grounds

## Context

[ADR-044](ADR-044.md) added `corroborates` for the record's own claims: one claim gives
independent evidence for another "without that claim depending on it", so
retiring it costs the other support and not its footing. The relation runs
from CLAIM to CLAIM. Readings, which are THEORYs, could reach a claim only
through `grounds`, the claim's evidence from outside the record, which
the claim stands on.

The status review of 2026-10-27 ([ADR-057](ADR-057.md)) listed 38 edges where an Active
CLAIM or ARG grounds on, or rests on, a Proposed entry. Three readers
reviewed 27 of the dependents. In most of them the Proposed reading was
cited as follows:

- **As later evidence.** [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)'s note of 2026-10-12, "evidence now
  linked", added [THEORY-142](../theory.d/THEORY-142.md)'s stylized simulation.
- **As an instance.** [CLAIM-164](../claims.d/CLAIM-164.md)'s note says of [THEORY-078](../theory.d/THEORY-078.md) and [THEORY-084](../theory.d/THEORY-084.md):
  "This claim does not need them: its core is Davis–Kahan."
- **For an attribution.** [CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md) says: "the finite case of what Monderer
  and Shapley call ... ([THEORY-896](../theory.d/THEORY-896.md)), proved here because their paper is
  unread."

In each case the claim did not need the reading. Several of the claims'
own directives said "nothing here rests on it". `grounds` said otherwise.

## Decision

- **`corroborated_by` on a CLAIM may name a THEORY,** and a THEORY has
  `corroborates`, its converse. It means what [ADR-044](ADR-044.md)'s relation means:
  independent evidence, and no dependence.
- **`grounds` keeps its meaning,** a reading the claim stands on. A claim
  whose argument uses a reading's contested thesis keeps it in `grounds`.
  If the reading is Proposed and the claim Active, that is an inversion,
  and a status review decides it.
- **Attribution and quotation take no edge.** Where a reading is cited
  only to say who said something, or to quote a definition, the body's
  citation is the link. A definition the claim depends on is better carried
  by a TERM, or by an Active THEORY split off to state it, as [THEORY-tmp5sao6](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5sao6.md)
  does for Blackwell's definitions.

## Alternatives considered

- **Drop the edges.** This removes the inversion and also the record of
  which evidence was found for the claim. That record is what
  "evidence now linked" notes were made to keep.
- **A general `cites` relation.** A body citation already records that a
  claim cites a document, and the rendered page links it. A field would be
  a second copy ([DP-3](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-3)).
- **Let `complements` take a THEORY.** `complements` relates two claims
  that sit beside each other in an argument, not evidence for one of them.

## Consequences

- An Active claim corroborated by a Proposed reading is not an inversion,
  so the worklist's count falls to what is really unresolved.
- Rejecting a THEORY that corroborates a claim leaves the claim's status
  alone, and a reviewer may still want to look at the claim. The THEORY's
  page lists what it corroborates.
- The review that adopted this decision moved the edges it found. Other
  `grounds` edges added as evidence remain, and a status review that meets
  one moves it.
