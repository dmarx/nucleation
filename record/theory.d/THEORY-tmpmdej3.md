# THEORY-tmpmdej3: Early public-sector pilots of generative-AI work tools, on Choroszewicz's ethnography, position their users as moral crumple zones: responsibility for oversight, verification and interpretation is allocated to users beyond their authority, control and capacity to influence the tools, through mechanisms that codify oversight as a user burden and insulate the tools and their developers

Choroszewicz (2026), [LIT-2689](../literature.d/LIT-2689.md), read in [NOTE-tmpgwh6b](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpgwh6b.md).

## The finding

Two Finnish public organizations, three LLM-based tools, two and a half
years of digital ethnography. Five mechanisms: assumed human oversight and
liability; immature tools presented as "sparring buddies"; trial and error
offloaded to users; limited transparency of development; and persuasive
fallibility with a burden of vigilance. Users were placed "to act as
testers, AI outputs' quality verifiers and interpreters" despite the tools'
"technical immaturity and opacity" (p. 9). "Codifying responsibility as a
user burden insulates technologies and their developers from
accountability" (p. 10).

## What it does not show

- **Blame after a harmful outcome**: no such incident is reported.
- **Generality**: early pilots in one country, by the author's own lens,
  which she says may oversimplify.
