# CLAIM-727: Authority over a decision does not carry authority over the conditions on which it may be challenged: control of the cost, timing, evidence, forum or remedy of a challenge needs its own justification, and where an actor's authority is meant to be constrained by independently justified claims, its own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has

## The claim

A83 §3E of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): suppose
A may decide, B may challenge, and A can also "change the cost,
availability, timing, evidentiary standards, or enforcement of B's
challenge. A's first-order authority over the decision and its
second-order authority over the challenge are distinct. Even if the first
is justified, the second requires separate consideration."

> **An actor's authority over a substantive decision does not automatically confer authority to determine the conditions under which affected parties may challenge that decision.**

"Such authority might be legitimately delegated, for example to
administer ordinary procedures. But its allocation and exercise need
justification." The concern: "a conflict between the purpose of
accountability and the capacity of the accountable actor to disable it."

A83 §7 adds the epistemic half. With J₀ the justification of a decision,
J₁ of the authority to make it and J₂ of the procedure by which both are
evaluated, "If A controls all three levels, then apparent justification
may be self-confirming." This does not start a regress: the test is
"whether the institutional arrangement provides appropriately independent
standards, evidence, representation, and channels of review". Hence:

> **Where an actor's authority is supposed to be constrained by independently justified claims, the actor's own judgment that it has satisfied those claims cannot always suffice to establish that it has done so.**

"That is a defeasible design principle, not a claim that every private
decision requires external adjudication."

Two applications from the same exchange belong here. A85 §9, on the Ninth
Amendment: "nor does the institution's authority to administer a
catalogue of rights necessarily confer unrestricted authority to
determine that catalogue's moral completeness". And A81 §14's proposed
question, "Under what circumstances may an actor legitimately determine
the costs, evidentiary requirements, authority allocations, or remedial
pathways through which its own conduct is evaluated?", to which this claim
gives the form of an answer: not by virtue of its authority over the
conduct.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)** (Proposed): the second-order duty to maintain feasible
  means for violations to become knowable, contestable and correctable.
  That duty presupposes that someone may set the terms of contest; this
  claim says the decider is not that someone by default.
- **[CLAIM-619](CLAIM-619.md)** (Proposed): an organization disposed to answer a challenge
  by reducing the capacity for future challenge. That is one way of
  exercising the second authority without justification.
- **[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)** (Proposed): domination includes the capacity to set the
  terms on which a party can contest, not answerable to it. This claim
  says what makes such a capacity need an answer: it is separate from
  the authority it would protect.
- **[CLAIM-359](CLAIM-359.md)** and **[CLAIM-282](CLAIM-282.md)** (Active): authority can amplify itself
  through recognition loops even where self-granting is forbidden. A83 §7
  cites them for why preventing explicit self-authorization "may be
  insufficient". How independent the evaluator must be is
  [QUESTION-482](../questions.d/QUESTION-482.md).
- **[QUESTION-479](../questions.d/QUESTION-479.md)** asks whether a party under a justified constraint
  may set or amplify the cost of making it effective, the owner's siege
  ([CLAIM-748](CLAIM-748.md)) put as a question. This claim does not answer it; it
  says the answer cannot come from the party's authority over the
  constrained conduct. [CLAIM-724](CLAIM-724.md) gives the measure.
- **[CLAIM-739](CLAIM-739.md)** lists "control over recourse" among the grounds of
  answerability. This is what that ground generates.
- Bears on [QUESTION-480](../questions.d/QUESTION-480.md): filed from A83, A's answer to the owner's U36,
  it says one condition under which justification is owed, control over
  the conditions on which a decision may be challenged, and that the
  decider's own authority cannot discharge it.

## What it does not say

- Not that the decider may never administer its own procedures. A83 §3E
  allows it by justified delegation.
- Not that every decision needs an external reviewer. A83 §7 ties the
  principle to authority "supposed to be constrained by independently
  justified claims", and A83 §9 Case A keeps private discretion outside it.
- Not that a claimant's power to demand review is unlimited. A83 closes
  on "protection against domination through an unrestricted power to
  demand answers".

## Note of 2026-11-17: Schlager and Ostrom, and Locke, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1957 NOTE-2077 — new readings and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed or Skimmed -->

Schlager & Ostrom 1992, read ([NOTE-2062](../notes.d/NOTE-2062.md), [THEORY-1957](../theory.d/THEORY-1957.md)): corroboration of the claim's structural premise, which the THEORY carries. Authority over one level does not carry authority over the next: "The five property rights are independent of one another" (p. 252); "It is possible ... to have management rights without exclusion rights" (p. 252); and holding collective-choice rights is "the difference between exercising a right and participating in the definition of future rights to be exercised" (p. 251), with a constitutional level above that, at which "Constitutional-choice actions entail devising collective-choice rules" (n. 2). What the paper does not address is challenge: its upper levels devise operational rules; they are not the cost, timing, forum or remedy of contesting a decision. It adds an observation the claim may use: "Since authorized users do not design the rules they are expected to follow, they are less likely to agree to the necessity and legitimacy of the rules" (p. 257). Locke's Second Treatise, skimmed ([NOTE-2077](../notes.d/NOTE-2077.md)), gives a classical form of the claim's refusal of self-certification: to "who shall be judge whether his trustee or deputy acts well", "The people shall be judge" (§240), not the trustee. No status changes.
