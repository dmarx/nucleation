# NOTE-tmpnfib1: Strawson — Freedom and Resentment

## Contribution

The reactive-attitudes account of moral responsibility. Holding people
responsible is constituted by participant attitudes (resentment,
gratitude, indignation, guilt) that rest on and express a demand for
goodwill or regard, not by a judgment that some metaphysical condition
holds. That reframes the dispute between "optimists" and "pessimists"
about determinism.

## Key results

- **The demand under the attitudes** (§5, p. 200): "The personal reactive
  attitudes rest on, and reflect, an expectation of, and demand for, the
  manifestation of a certain degree of goodwill or regard on the part of
  other human beings towards ourselves".
- **Generalized** (§5, p. 200): the vicarious analogue rests on "the demand
  for the manifestation of a reasonable degree of goodwill or regard, on
  the part of others, not simply towards oneself, but towards all those on
  whose behalf moral indignation may be felt".
- **Excusing pleas** (§4, pp. 192–193): pleas such as "He was pushed" and
  "They left him no alternative" "do not invite us to see the agent as
  other than a fully responsible agent. They invite us to see the injury
  as one for which he was not fully, or at all, responsible."
- **Exempting pleas** (§4, p. 193): "He's only a child", "His mind has been
  systematically perverted", which "invite us to suspend our ordinary
  reactive attitudes towards the agent".
- **The objective attitude** (§4, p. 194): to see another "as an object of
  social policy; as a subject for what, in a wide range of sense, might be
  called treatment".
- **Blame keeps membership** (§6, p. 207): the attitudes "tend to promote
  an at least partial and temporary withdrawal of goodwill", which "is,
  rather, the consequence of continuing to view him as a member of the
  moral community; only as one who has offended against its demands." "The
  making of the demand is the proneness to such attitudes."
- **The framework** (§5, pp. 198–199): this commitment "is part of the
  general framework of human life"; (§6, p. 208) "As a whole, it neither
  calls for, nor permits, an external 'rational' justification."
- **Abysses** (§6, pp. 205–206): if the objective attitude were adopted to
  all offenders, the recommendation could be addressed "Only to the
  powerful, the authorities. So abysses seem to open."
- **Verdict on optimism** (§6, p. 210): "Our practices do not merely exploit
  our natures, they express them."

## Assumptions

That interpersonal life is constituted by the reactive attitudes; that no
sense of "determined" is the one operative in our actual excusing and
exempting practices; that the framework is given with human society.
Strawson allows that his descriptions may reflect "local and temporary
features of our own culture" (p. 210).

## Concepts

- **Reactive attitudes**: personal, vicarious (moral) and self-reactive.
- **Participant and objective attitudes.**
- **Two kinds of plea**: those that leave the agent a full participant and
  remove or reduce responsibility for the injury, and those that suspend
  the attitudes toward the agent (later literature's excuses and
  exemptions).
- **The demand for goodwill or regard.**

## Bearing on the record

- **The exchange's characterization** (A7: "intimately connected to these
  expectations of goodwill and respect"): faithful, with one word off.
  Strawson's word is "regard", not "respect"; and the link is closer than
  connection: "The making of the demand is the proneness to such
  attitudes."
- **[CLAIM-tmpa2l21](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2l21.md)** (second-order wrongdoing): qualifies it. Strawson's
  first kind of plea is coercion: "He was pushed", "They left him no
  alternative". It leaves the agent "a fully responsible agent" while the
  injury may be one for which he was "not fully, or at all, responsible".
  That fits the claim's "does not take over their culpability" only in
  part: on Strawson's wording coercion can remove responsibility for the
  act altogether, not only lessen it. And his second kind includes "His
  mind has been systematically perverted", an exemption, so conditioning
  carried far enough takes the agent out of the reactive attitudes, which
  the claim's "conditioned" case does not mark. Strawson says nothing about
  the wrong of whoever did the pushing or perverting, the claim's core.
- **[QUESTION-068](../questions.d/QUESTION-068.md)** (Deferred): Strawson's exemptions are conditions on
  holding responsible that ownership of a will does not supply.
- **What blame is**: blame as "partial and temporary withdrawal of
  goodwill" within continued membership. Scanlon's *Moral Dimensions*
  ([LIT-tmp2dnmn](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2dnmn.md)), which A sets beside it, is unread.

## Limitations

Strawson calls his argument "nothing more than a schema". Read from an OCR
scan of the first printing; not compared with the later reprints.
