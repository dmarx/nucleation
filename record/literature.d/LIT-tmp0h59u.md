## Key takeaways

*From the abstract only.*

- **A reply aimed at one conclusion.** Nichols & Bruno ([LIT-1269](LIT-1269.md),
  [NOTE-996](../notes.d/NOTE-996.md)) concluded that the folk judge psychological continuity
  necessary for personal identity. This paper disputes that claim.
- **Three concepts, not one.** The authors argue that in "hypothetical
  cases of transformations" the folk "rely on different concepts of
  'person', 'identity', and 'individual'", and that "identity can be
  ascribed even when post-transformation individuals are no longer
  categorized as persons".
- **New data against necessity.** They report evidence "suggesting that
  the folk do not consider psychological continuity to be necessary for
  an individual to be categorized as a person or for ascribing identity
  over time and through transformations".
- **A shared measure, and a doubt about it.** Like Nichols & Bruno, they
  assume "folk judgments about identity can be read off the use of proper
  names". They also doubt that current designs can "adequately
  distinguish between qualitative and numerical identity judgments".
- **A sceptical conclusion.** They take these points as "a good reason to
  be skeptical about the prospects of using folk intuitions in
  philosophical theorizing about personal identity".

What a reading would test:

- **The person/identity distinction ([THEORY-417](../theory.d/THEORY-417.md), [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md), [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md),
  [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)).** The abstract's central claim is the folk counterpart of
  distinctions the record already keeps. [THEORY-417](../theory.d/THEORY-417.md) separates the
  personhood question (what a person is) from the persistence question
  (what it takes for one to persist). The paper says the folk separate
  them too: they can judge that the same individual persists while
  denying it is still a person. [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md) keeps `individuation` apart from
  `personhood`, and the paper's third concept, "individual", sits there.
  [NOTE-996](../notes.d/NOTE-996.md) found that Nichols & Bruno use "self" and "person"
  interchangeably and run [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)'s senses together; this reply, as
  abstracted, makes the parallel objection from the folk side. The
  paper's doubt about qualitative against numerical identity is the
  distinction [THEORY-417](../theory.d/THEORY-417.md) draws between characterization and persistence.
  [NOTE-996](../notes.d/NOTE-996.md) raised the same worry about Nichols & Bruno's abstract probe.
  If the full text bears this out, it is convergent evidence that ordinary
  usage marks these distinctions. It is not evidence that the
  distinctions are correct, which [THEORY-417](../theory.d/THEORY-417.md) holds on conceptual grounds.
- **[THEORY-531](../theory.d/THEORY-531.md)'s version 2 addition** (ordinary verdicts do not follow
  the person of the telling). The abstract says nothing about first- and
  third-person versions, so it is not known whether this paper retests
  that point. Nothing here supports or weakens the addition until the
  paper is read.
- **[THEORY-546](../theory.d/THEORY-546.md)** (psychological continuity). The paper reports that the
  folk do not treat psychological continuity as necessary for identity.
  Like the Nichols & Bruno result it disputes, that is a finding about a
  folk concept, not about what persistence is. [THEORY-546](../theory.d/THEORY-546.md)'s
  promote_when already says imagined cases "cannot settle it".
- **What folk judgement can establish ([QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md), [LIT-451](LIT-451.md)).**
  The authors' own conclusion is the record's caution: a survey shows how
  a population applies a concept, and getting from that to the fact needs
  a further premise that the concept is reliable about it. [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)
  keeps the parallel distinction for reports of experience; the parallel
  is mine. A folk split between "person" and "identity" would also fit
  Hume's verdict ([LIT-451](LIT-451.md)) that the hard cases are "grammatical rather
  than philosophical"; that pairing is mine too.
