# NOTE-tmpfw3f7: Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy — Agency without agents

## Contribution

A "strong process" theory of agency for organization studies, in answer to
Hernes's "How can there be agency without agents?" (epigraph, p. 1349).
Agency is not "the capacity to act" of an actor (p. 1350) but "the
affective force, expressed as a communicative event, that governs the
transition from one process of individuation to another" (abstract). It
works by sharing "excess action" (p. 1351).

## Key insight

The definition takes Massumi's definition of affect: "a power to affect
and be affected [that] governs a transition, where a body passes from one
state of capacitation to a diminished or augmented state of capacitation"
(Massumi 2015, quoted p. 1353). The paper keeps only the augmenting
direction for agency: "we define agency as the affective force that
governs the transition from a lesser to an augmented ability to act" (p.
1354). The diminishing direction becomes "lack of agency", which "may
induce a process of disindividuation" (p. 1351).

## Assumptions

- **Process ontology.** There are "no already-constituted human beings
  prior to communication/becoming" (p. 1357).
- **Simondon's metastable systems.** Every being is metastable, with
  regions organized at different energy densities, and individuation
  resolves the "disparities" between them through communication (p.
  1355).
- **Equivalence.** Communication, affect, agency and becoming are
  equivalent, which the paper finds pointed to by Whitehead, Bateson and
  Massumi (p. 1354).

## Key results

The paper's results are conceptual.

1. **Excess action.** Agency occurs when one individuation process takes up
   "the excess action of another individuation process (the feelings you
   cannot deal with, unchanneled energy, skills in search of an outlet,
   etc.)". Sharing that burden "may achieve greater unity and lead to a
   greater capacity to act for each" (p. 1351).
2. **Transindividuation and disindividuation.** Processes that share
   action may start a collective process, transindividuation, said to be
   "essential to organizational becoming" (abstract). When the sharing
   fails, the result is disindividuation, "the experience of needing to
   reorganize in order to deal with a preindividual element that resists
   incorporation" (p. 1356).
3. **Communication as transduction.** Communication is "the continuity of
   action through beings who are in a perpetual state of becoming" (p.
   1364), and every communication is a transformation (p. 1356). Meaning
   is retrospective: "action contributes to informing a system that,
   retrospectively, gives that action its meaning" (p. 1357).
4. **Scaling up dissolves.** The problem of scaling from individual to
   collective agency "is only relevant ... when communication is regarded
   as an interaction between already-existing agents, operating at
   different levels, and vanishes the moment communication is viewed
   eventfully" (p. 1364).
5. **Perspectivism.** "Agency is a matter of perspective or perspectivism
   ... Who or what has agency is a premature question" (p. 1357).
   Describing action from a human starting point is "an 'agential cut'"
   (Barad), an analytical choice.

## Claims

- **Method** (pp. 1357–1358). Study "dividuals", not individuals. "Start in
  the middle". Do not rely on participants' understandings, since
  "Research that is grounded in people's understandings is unable to
  attune to the processual nature of the world". Organizational becoming
  is not the reduction of equivocality (against Weick): "Coexisting
  meanings of the same action indicate that several individuations are
  ongoing" (p. 1358).
- **Responsibility** (pp. 1366–1367). Responsibility is reframed as a duty
  toward "lesser existences" whose individuation is precarious, after
  Lapoujade and Jonas. The authors answer the charge that agency without
  agents is apolitical: "process philosophy reveals its political and
  critical potential" (p. 1366).

## Method

Reanalysis of a published excerpt of Cooren's (2018) ethnography of
Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) in the Democratic Republic of the Congo,
2005 (pp. 1359–1363). Two local health officials ask Robert, an MSF
representative, for MSF's surgeon to train Congolese physicians. Robert
hesitates and says "MSF we are not a university". He then works his way
to accepting an informal version: "at first sight, I don't have major
objections" (line 75).
- **What is new.** The authors rewatched the video to add nonverbal
  detail, and they extend the transcript past where Cooren stopped (lines
  68–76).
- **The reading.** The request is excess action of the health
  department's transindividuation. Robert's pauses and averted gaze are
  disindividuation. His monologue reorganizes MSF so that both can
  continue. Robert is "not an agent, but a vector of becoming" (p. 1364).

## Concepts

- **Individuation.** The processual emergence of physical, technical,
  psychological and collective entities.
- **The preindividual.** The source of an individuation's energy.
- **Excess action, or affective exaggeration.** Action a process cannot
  incorporate, which pushes it to reach beyond itself.
- **Transduction.** Communication as transformation.
- **Transindividuation.** Collective individuation.
- **Disindividuation.** Interrupted individuation.
- **Field of relation.** A being in becoming; some fields are dense and
  hard to reorganize, others loose and easy.
- **Inflection.** "the pure Event", after Deleuze (p. 1361).

## Connections

- **Hernes.** The paper extends Hernes's process account of agency by
  specifying the communication he leaves untheorized (p. 1353).
- **Cooren.** It positions itself against Cooren's ventriloquism and the
  communicative constitution of organizations (CCO) tradition, which "still
  tend[s] to entify agents" (p. 1364).
- **Barad.** It borrows the agential cut, held in the record as [LIT-1653](../literature.d/LIT-1653.md),
  Unavailable, and read in [THEORY-1083](../theory.d/THEORY-1083.md) and [THEORY-1071](../theory.d/THEORY-1071.md).
- **Simondon.** The source is *Individuation in Light of Notions of Form
  and Information*, which the record holds as [LIT-1648](../literature.d/LIT-1648.md), Unavailable.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) is the record's form of "agency without agents".** It holds
  that a distributed agent's response can be real while the single owner a
  self-description attributes it to is a representational construction.
  The paper reaches a stronger conclusion: there are no agents prior to
  communication at all. It does so without [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)'s distinction between
  the binding, which is real, and its attribution, which is constructed.
- **[CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md)** holds that perspectival, agential and institutional
  boundaries need not coincide. The paper's "agency is a matter of
  perspective" is a version of it. But the paper does not say what makes
  one cut better than another. [CLAIM-405](../claims.d/CLAIM-405.md) says individuation is relative to
  a criterion without being arbitrary, which the paper's perspectivism
  leaves open.
- **[CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md)** is closest to the reanalysis. An interaction can make
  available a way of relating that takes part before the reinterpretation
  it later seems to express. Robert discovering that MSF already teaches
  is that pattern.
- **[CLAIM-515](../claims.d/CLAIM-515.md)'s four questions, which the paper does not separate.**
  - "Augmented ability to act" is a change in capability, [CLAIM-515](../claims.d/CLAIM-515.md)'s
    exercisable.
  - Its source in "excess action" is a mechanism, [CLAIM-515](../claims.d/CLAIM-515.md)'s realized.
  - Its continuation through transindividuation is maintenance.

  The paper treats all three as one event.
- **[CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md).** The responsibility section, a duty to processes whose
  individuation is precarious, presupposes a standard for which processes
  should continue. [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md) holds that legibility, efficacy and
  correctability do not supply one. The paper's agency is defined by
  augmentation, so it builds a valence into the definition.

## Limitations

- **No independent criterion.** Nothing identifies "excess action",
  "augmented ability to act" or "disindividuation" apart from the
  analyst's reading of the episode. In the reanalysis they are inferred
  from pauses, gaze and hesitations, and the analysis leans on
  supposition: "we may suppose that he knows his refusal will disrupt the
  individuation of the physicians-in-becoming" (p. 1362); "Assuming that on
  line 14, Robert is about to express a refusal" (p. 1362). No observation
  is described that would count against the account.
- **The reanalysis attributes inner states to individuals.** It cites
  Robert's knowledge, surprise and discomfort, which is the entitative
  description the paper rejects. The authors concede that the reanalysis
  "still bears traces of entitative thinking" (p. 1364).
- **The equivalence risks vacuity.** If a piston "communicates movement"
  (p. 1364) and every being-in-becoming is a field of relation, then
  "communication" and "agency" apply to everything that changes. This is
  the broad horn of the dilemma the record filed against its own normative
  thesis ([CLAIM-512](../claims.d/CLAIM-512.md)). The organizational description has to add discrimination
  to earn its place.
- **Valence is defined in.** Agency is the transition to augmented ability
  only; diminution is its "lack". So whether an event shows agency depends
  on a prior judgment of which ability matters and whose. The paper does
  not supply that judgment.
- **Perspectivism and realism pull against each other.** "Agency is a
  matter of perspective" (p. 1357) sits uneasily with agency as
  "coextensive with the very process of becoming" (p. 1354). The paper
  does not reconcile the two.

## Open questions

- Whether "excess action" can be operationalized: for instance, as demand
  on a process that exceeds its capacity, measured independently of how
  the encounter resolves.
- Whether transindividuation predicts anything that interaction analysis
  of the same episode does not.
- What methods would let researchers "individuate into" what they study
  (p. 1366). The authors leave this open.
