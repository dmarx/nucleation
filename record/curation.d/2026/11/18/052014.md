The owner's expectation does not hold. Seven of 28 members carry the
line, and ADR-tmpived4 says why: the manuscript's question and its last
sections sit in the line, while its body relays claims the record held
under other lines. The line holds eight claims the manuscript does not
use. All of them come from turns after A76: CLAIM-717, CLAIM-725,
CLAIM-732, CLAIM-735, CLAIM-739, CLAIM-741, CLAIM-747 and CLAIM-749.

Two judgements could go the other way:

- **CLAIM-736** is counted. SYNTHESIS-100 says A49 dropped A37's
  controlled opposition "apart from Arnstein". But §14.5's "A dominating
  organization may hold elections, maintain grievance processes, permit
  criticism ... while ensuring that these arrangements rarely constrain
  those in power" states the claim's proposition without its frame.
- **CLAIM-720** is counted for §4, which states the limit in the
  self-binding case. §4 does not mention inalienability.

## Passages held by no entry, left

- **§1's three arguments, and §16's freedom as "the legitimate governance
  of capability".** These are the thesis as a whole. SYNTHESIS-100's
  audit filed no claim from it; its parts are the members above.
- **§3.1's constitutive capabilities** ("powers over the conditions of
  agency require a distinctive form of justification"). This is A's
  classification, offered as a suggestion ("This does not establish ...
  It does suggest"). No separating case or derivation admits it, and
  CLAIM-708 and CLAIM-727 hold its uses.
- **§4's general proposition** is held only in parts: CLAIM-720 for its
  proviso, and CASE-128 for the contrast. That a self-imposed
  restriction "may constitute an exercise of autonomy" is the record's
  earlier reading of Schelling and Ainslie, not a claim.
- **§10's four capacities** (action, restraint, correction,
  reconstruction). This is A's list. Only "a legal reversal may restore
  an official status without restoring a lost livelihood" goes beyond
  CLAIM-464, and it is an observation, not argued.
- **§14.5's counterfactual test** ("What happens when a participant
  raises a justified objection that conflicts with the preferences of
  the actor controlling the arrangement?"). It is CLAIM-718's last step
  applied, and is not filed apart.
- **§15's three classes of matched cases.** They are a research plan
  under CLAIM-718. The record's proposed studies CASE-280, CASE-285 and
  CASE-290 are of the third class, and none was built from §15.
- **A53 §8's central argument and its formula**: "Labor theory tells us
  who pays ... Contract theory ... who governs the terms ... Auction
  theory ... how the relationship induces participants to compete". This
  is a heading for CLAIM-728 and CLAIM-744, and it states no proposition
  of its own. Its second claim is CLAIM-703 and CLAIM-713, as above.
- **A55's first candidate principle**, special protection for a
  contractual restriction that preserves another's authority. It is the
  consent-to-liability case of CLAIM-734 stated as a policy, and it is
  left as a recommendation.
- **A57 §8's table of five questions.** It is a recap, and its fifth
  question is CLAIM-716's.
- **A59's "wrongful unilateral imposition"** as a parent definition, and
  its bullying pattern. It is A's family name over CLAIM-723's mechanism.
  CLAIM-723 records the owner's bullying proposal and its narrowing to
  one mechanism; the parent is left as a name with no separating case.
- **A71 §12's two projects**, a theory of standing against a theory of
  organizational adequacy. This is a plan for the manuscript. CLAIM-746
  holds the distinction it rests on.
- **A76's closing question** about "organizational freedom". It restates
  QUESTION-478 and QUESTION-485 and is not filed apart.
- **The references and provenance table.** The citation errors are
  corrected in the curation entry of 2026-11-17. The works A names
  outside the record (Keller, Elish, Arnstein, Bourgeois) are handled
  there: Elish is registered, and Bourgeois is named only in passing.
