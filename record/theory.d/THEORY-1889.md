# THEORY-1889: Bound states in continuum need zero coupling

## Source

Sadreev (2021), [LIT-2648](../literature.d/LIT-2648.md); skimmed in [NOTE-1983](../notes.d/NOTE-1983.md). The neighbour of
Hsu et al. (2016), [LIT-2625](../literature.d/LIT-2625.md), unread.

## The result

A BIC "lies inside the continuum and coexists with extended waves, but it
remains perfectly confined without any radiation". Decoupling from all
channels "is a necessary and sufficient condition for a resonance state to
be the BIC". The review names four routes: "orthogonality of the eigenmodes
to the propagating modes", "full destructive interference of outgoing
resonant modes", resonators that each "serve as an ideal mirror", and
accidental decoupling under smooth deformation. In the two-level model
(Eq. 5) one state is "decoupled from the continuum owing to exact
destructive interference of leaking eigenmodes" while its partner "acquires
maximal coupling with the continuum". At that point "maximal transmittance
coalesces with the maximal reflectance (collapse of Fano resonance)".

## What it does not show

- Robustness. A true BIC needs exact parameters or symmetry; practical ones
  are "quasi-BICs", and a "rigorous theorem forbids BICs in finite
  dielectric structure".
- Photonic-crystal BICs as momentum-space polarization vortices, Hsu et
  al.'s main subject, which the review skips.
- That a BIC is invisible from outside. That follows from W entering the
  scattering matrix on both sides (Eq. 4), and it is the reader's reading,
  not a sentence of the review.
