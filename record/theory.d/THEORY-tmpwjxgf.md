# THEORY-tmpwjxgf: Bencherki and Iliadis propose that persons and organizations are not successive stages of individuation, with the collective built up from individuals, but coexisting informational levels that one action can reorder at once, each preserving the characteristics of its organized components, so that scaling up from individual to organizational action is neither a sequence of steps nor a synthesis

## Source

Bencherki & Iliadis (2021), [LIT-1922](../literature.d/LIT-1922.md), proposition 2 and manuscript pp.
14–17, read in full in [NOTE-1483](../notes.d/NOTE-1483.md) from the authors' manuscript. Bencherki
(2017), [LIT-1901](../literature.d/LIT-1901.md), p. 780, read in [NOTE-1482](../notes.d/NOTE-1482.md), says the same.

## The proposal

After Simondon, the scaling-up problem in the communicative constitution
of organization "is not a matter of sequential physical steps (individual
people first and then a collective) nor is it a synthesis". Levels of
informational abstraction coexist, "in the same way that the DNA of a
single cell exists and is as real as the human individual to whom it
belongs" (ms. pp. 14–15). One action can reorder several levels at once:
a synaptic chemical deficit changes a person's behaviour and an
organization's human-resources work (ms. p. 17). Proposition 2: "An
employee is not prior to the organization, nor is the organization an
abstraction of its employees; they each exist fully and simultaneously."

Bencherki (2017): "I am not, as a human being, anterior to society; at
any moment, I am contemporaneous to each of my cells and to the whole
universe" (p. 780).

## What this does not say

- **That the proposal is right.** Whether the coexisting-levels
  description discriminates from a scaling-up one is [THEORY-1277](THEORY-1277.md)'s
  question.
- **That there is no aggregation.** Whether some organizational outcomes
  are better explained as aggregates is not addressed.
- **What fixes a level.** Domains are distinguished "for practical
  purposes" by their internal ordering.
- **That coexistence is observed.** The cell-to-organization example is a
  thought experiment.
