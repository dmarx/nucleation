# LIT-tmp543s0: Minds, brains, and programs

John R. Searle (1980), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3(3), September 1980,
pp. 417–424 (target article; with open peer commentary and the author's
response, pp. 417–457) — DOI-10.1017/S0140525X00005756. Read from the
unedited penultimate draft BBS circulated for commentary, posted by Stevan
Harnad, BBS's founding editor
(https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~harnad/Temp/.searle.pdf). Reprinted as
selection 22 of *The Mind's I* ([LIT-tmpg4bap](LIT-tmpg4bap.md)).

## Key takeaways

- **Target.** "Strong AI": the appropriately programmed computer "really is
  a mind", literally understands, and its program explains human
  understanding. Weak AI, the computer as a tool for studying the mind, is
  not attacked.
- **The room.** Searle, knowing no Chinese, follows English rules to match
  Chinese symbols (a "script", a "story", "questions") and hands back
  "answers" indistinguishable from a native speaker's. He understands
  nothing; so the program is not sufficient for understanding, and nothing
  shows it is even necessary or contributory.
- **The systems reply** (the whole room understands) is answered by
  internalisation: let the man memorise the rules and do the work in his
  head. "There isn't anything at all to the system that he does not
  encompass", so the system does not understand either. A subsystem that
  understands Chinese would also make stomachs and livers "understanding
  subsystems", and if thermostats have beliefs (McCarthy), "the mind is
  everywhere".
- **The robot, brain-simulator and combination replies** fail the same
  way: put the man inside the robot, or have him work water pipes that
  simulate a Chinese speaker's synapses. A robot that behaves like us earns
  an ascription of intentionality only until we learn it runs a formal
  program.
- **What could think.** "Only a machine could think, and indeed only very
  special kinds of machines, namely brains and machines that had the same
  causal powers as brains." Whether another chemistry could do it is "an
  empirical question". Programs have "only a syntax but no semantics".
- **Diagnosis.** Strong AI survives on an ambiguity in "information
  processing", a residual behaviourism (the Turing test is "unashamedly
  behavioristic and operationalistic") and a residual dualism: it only
  makes sense if "where the mind is concerned, the brain doesn't matter".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 for *The Mind's I* ([LIT-tmpg4bap](LIT-tmpg4bap.md)), in the batch the
owner asked for on Daniel Dennett. Until now the record held the Chinese
room only as others report it: [NOTE-176](../notes.d/NOTE-176.md) records that Šekrst's paper
([LIT-212](LIT-212.md)) offers it as its only argument that current models lack
understanding; Schwitzgebel's book ([LIT-191](LIT-191.md), read in [NOTE-104](../notes.d/NOTE-104.md)) gives it a
chapter; Seth ([LIT-135](LIT-135.md), [NOTE-169](../notes.d/NOTE-169.md)) distinguishes his biological naturalism
from Searle's. This paper is where the argument lives, and it is narrower
than those uses: it denies that a program *suffices* for understanding,
not that a machine could understand.

**Bearing on [THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md) (the society-of-mind bridge).** The systems
reply is the downward move [THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md) describes, a competence
ascribed to a whole whose part (the man) lacks it, and Searle's answer is
the most famous refusal of it. His internalisation reply turns the
decomposition into a containment ("the system is just a part of him") and
then reads the man's ignorance off as the system's. That is an inference
from part to whole, the inference Block ([LIT-tmpc6np5](LIT-tmpc6np5.md)) and Schwitzgebel
([LIT-159](LIT-159.md)) do not accept in the other direction. Searle's ground is not an
anti-nesting principle and not an architectural criterion, the two routes
[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md) finds. It is a third: a claim about which *causal powers*
produce intentionality, which a formal organisation cannot carry because
it can be "carved off" from them. The account should name it as such. It
also bears on the nation case: by Searle's reasoning a nation's
organisation, as organisation, could no more understand than the water
pipes, whatever its citizens understand.

**Bearing on [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md).** The paper is one of the two positions that
account says cannot settle the AI question by evidence. Searle's own
answer to the combination reply concedes that a robot behaving like us
would rationally be credited with intentionality "as long as we knew
nothing more about it". What overturns the ascription is learning that it
runs a formal program. So for Searle the evidence that matters is
architectural, about how the behaviour is produced, which is the level
[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) calls "Janus-faced" after Birch ([LIT-111](LIT-111.md)).

**Neighbours.** Turing's paper ([LIT-tmp265e0](LIT-tmp265e0.md)) is the test Searle calls
behaviourist. Block's China brain ([LIT-tmpc6np5](LIT-tmpc6np5.md)), which the issue's
commentators cite, is the sibling thought experiment: Block doubts the qualia
of a functional duplicate, Searle the intentionality of a formal one.
Chalmers's reply in his paper on implementation, being filed now by other
readers, finds two minds in the room. The issue's combined reference list tags each
entry with the initials of whoever cites it, and carries entries tagged DD
and DRH: Dennett and Hofstadter wrote commentaries on this article in the
same issue (unread here), and who reprinted it a year later with
Reflections of their own (unread). Dennett's other replies to Searle are
being filed separately.

**Boundary.** It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice, and
the anthology does not hold it.
