The owner asked on 2026-11-19 to "read those additional works you
registered late": Wang and Slotine (LIT-tmpwo4ry), Chen, Kunin, Yamamura
and Ganguli (LIT-tmp3cyf2) and Poole, Lahiri, Raghu, Sohl-Dickstein and

rechecked every quotation by script against the downloaded texts.

## Copies

- **Chen and colleagues:** arXiv 2306.04251v3 (NeurIPS 2023).
- **Poole and colleagues:** arXiv 1606.05340v2 with its supplement.

## What was filed

- A NOTE (Read) and a Proposed THEORY for each: NOTE-tmp9eyoy and
  THEORY-tmprqrdn; NOTE-tmpsybfr and THEORY-tmp4kekv; NOTE-tmpa7swy and
  THEORY-tmpitdat. Each LIT Active at version 2.
- Relations: THEORY-tmprqrdn corroborates CLAIM-481 and bears on
  QUESTION-063 and QUESTION-147; THEORY-tmp4kekv bears on QUESTION-206 and
  QUESTION-080; THEORY-tmpitdat corroborates CLAIM-360 and bears on
  QUESTION-281.
- **A correction.** THEORY-tmprpscg (the reading of Lohmiller and
  Slotine) said partial contraction keeps the distinctions transverse to
  the manifold. It erases them and keeps those along it. Corrected in
  place, version 2.
- **A citation that overreaches.** Velarde and colleagues cite Chen and
  colleagues for "Any parameter set that is invariant under GD becomes a
  stable attractor under stochastic GD for sufficiently large learning
  rates". The paper gives a sufficient condition for sets invariant under
  every minibatch, for a continuous model, and shows collapse stopping
  above a critical learning rate in a toy. The note on THEORY-tmpz825j
  says so. No ARG: the overreach is in a reading's account of its source,
  not in a support relation the record argues.
- **Dated notes on fifty-two entries**, each saying which kind of bearing
  it is (step 8): sixteen from Wang and Slotine, twenty-five from Chen and
  colleagues, eleven from Poole and colleagues. Among them: CLAIM-481's
  open point on selective contraction now has its source;
  CLAIM-tmpw8xkt's objection that contraction leaves a single class is
  answered by partial contraction, which keeps a continuum, not classes;
  Chen and colleagues' collapse is a candidate mechanism, by analogy, for
  overconsolidation (CLAIM-tmp8ljvp) and plasticity loss
  (THEORY-tmpgjb4y), with a measure for CASE-tmphsr99; and Poole and
  colleagues never speak of an edge of chaos, an emphasis that is
  Schoenholz and colleagues' (THEORY-tmpqtb3q, THEORY-tmpvtafk).
