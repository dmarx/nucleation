# CLAIM-tmpe5u7i: An arrangement that does not fund what it requires can be carried by its members' moral commitments to those it serves: the commitment that keeps them from withdrawing turns the arrangement's need of them into its hold on them, the shortfall falls hardest on the most conscientious, and those who control the arrangement can come to rely on the subsidy without intending to exploit anyone

## The claim

A13 §4 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):
"Consider a hospital that chronically understaffs its emergency
department. The hospital's management knows that nurses will not willingly
abandon patients in urgent need." "No one needs to issue an explicit
threat." "The party that should bear the cost of satisfying an obligation
arranges for another party to bear it instead. And the more conscientious
the second party is, the more effectively the arrangement can function."

A15 §3 gives the arithmetic. Care requires 100 units; management pays for
85 and relies on conscientious staff for the other 15, "an *implicit moral
subsidy*". Its hold-up reading: a nurse has invested in the care relationship,
and "The nurse can't simply stop caring about patients at the end of a
contractual shift", so the employer can truthfully say "*We aren't forcing you to
stay. You're free to leave when your shift ends.*" A15 §3 also names the
loop by which effort hides the shortfall from those who set resources,
which is filed with its budget rule as [CLAIM-tmp0ogo4](CLAIM-tmp0ogo4.md).

**Who bears it.** A13 §10: "the people least willing to compromise their
moral commitments are made to absorb the greatest burdens. Over time, the
institution may reward people who are more comfortable offloading those
burdens." A15 §4 separates "**Moral commitment:**" from "**Moral
capacity:**" and "**Moral burden:**": the commitment need not wane while
the capacity to act on it is drawn down ("the exploiter can draw on the
target's capacity until that capacity is damaged, without necessarily
diminishing the target's sense of obligation").

**Why the need gives no hold.** [CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md): what sorts a dependence is who
controls exit, renegotiation and supply, so that "with the part able to
withdraw or renegotiate, the whole's need of it becomes the part's hold on
its terms". A commitment to patients takes withdrawal away from the nurse
without anyone else taking it. The arrangement's dependence on her is as
great as ever, and the leverage runs the other way. That inference is the
filer's statement of what A's case shows against [CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md); A30 §4 makes
the same connection and asks it as a question ([QUESTION-tmpbzq36](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpbzq36.md)).

## How it sits with the record

- **[QUESTION-461](../questions.d/QUESTION-461.md)** asks when an organization's dependence on subordinated
  participants raises their authority and when it reproduces arrangements
  that keep their contributions from becoming effective claims. This is a
  candidate mechanism for the second branch.
- **[CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)**'s non-legible harm and **[CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md)**'s arrangement that
  produces the dependence it exploits are its neighbours; here the
  dependence is the members' own commitment, which the arrangement did not
  produce but uses.
- **[TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md)** makes exploitation relative to a declared standard. A15 §8's
  four dimensions (consent, burden, control, exit) are A's candidate
  standard for this case.
- [CLAIM-tmp76x3e](CLAIM-tmp76x3e.md) is the deliberate, interpersonal form; this is the
  institutional form that needs no one's intent. [CLAIM-tmpb47ul](CLAIM-tmpb47ul.md) carries the
  same members on to blame. [CASE-tmpm0rpn](../cases.d/CASE-tmpm0rpn.md) (three hospitals with the same
  output and budget) is a construction from the same exchange.

## What it does not say

- Not that every understaffed service runs this way. A15 §3: "This is a
  theoretical mechanism, not a claim that every understaffed hospital
  deliberately operates this way. Institutions can also under-resource care
  because of genuinely unavoidable constraints."
- Not that relying on conscientiousness is wrong as such. A15 §8: "Every
  functioning society relies on people doing things for reasons other than
  material self-interest."
- Not that the remedy lies with the members. A13 §4: "Telling nurses to stop
  caring about patients is not a satisfactory response to exploitative
  staffing practices."

## Note of 2026-11-17: Keller read: the moral subsidy sought deliberately, in caring roles; Robillard and Strawser not reached

Keller 2016, read ([NOTE-tmp0loqu](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0loqu.md), [THEORY-tmpg9i9l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpg9i9l.md), corroborates). His law-firm case is the subsidy sought on purpose: "I can increase your workload without having to increase your pay and without losing clients for the firm" (p. 24). He ties it to caring roles: "It really is more difficult to go on strike if you are a nurse or a teacher than if you are a policy analyst or a software developer" (p. 25); such exposure makes it "more difficult, morally, to keep control over her time and energy, and to bargain for better conditions at work" (p. 26); and "the bad things can become instruments of systemic injustice" (p. 27). That supports the claim's hold and its burden on the conscientious. It supports only the deliberate form: all Keller's cases are intentional, where the claim allows reliance "without intending to exploit anyone".

The summary says A13 §4 "draws on Robillard and Strawser's 'moral exploitation' as A relays it". That stays unverified: Outsourcing Duty ([LIT-tmpewphn](../literature.d/LIT-tmpewphn.md)) is Unavailable and Robillard's dissertation ([LIT-tmp5mwf4](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5mwf4.md)) Unfetched. The publisher's description of the book ("wrongfully exploited by being made to shoulder an excessive amount of moral weight") and the dissertation's abstract ("exploited by being made to shoulder additional moral burdens") are consistent with A's relay, no more.
