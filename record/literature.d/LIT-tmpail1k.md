# LIT-tmpail1k: The Duality of Content

Jesse J. Prinz (2000), *Philosophical Studies, 100(1), 1–34* — DOI-10.1023/A:1018612122389

## Key takeaways

- Concepts have two distinct wide contents — a "nominal" content fixed by the observable features used to categorize, and a "real" (referential) content fixed by incipient causes plus nomological covariation — echoing Locke's nominal vs real essences. No abstract was reachable, so this is reconstructed from secondary sources. Prinz argues that concepts carry two kinds of content, each wide rather than narrow. Nominal content is tied to the observable, appearance-level properties by which things are categorized. Real or referential content is the underlying kind or essence those properties depend on. On the secondary account, real content is fixed by a concept's incipient cause (what first caused it to form) together with nomological covariation between the concept and its referent, a hybrid of Dretske's informational theory and Fodor's asymmetric dependence. The distinction is presented as a descendant of Locke's nominal and real essences and as an alternative to the standard wide/narrow split. Prinz later calls nominal content "cognitive content" (Furnishing the Mind, 2002).
