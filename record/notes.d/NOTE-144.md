# NOTE-144: Consent and the formation of preferences

## Contribution

Under ideal conditions, consent turns something impermissible into something permissible, but coerced consent does not. Pettigrew argues that consent also fails when the preferences behind it were formed in certain ways under pressure from unjust social forces. He catalogues mechanisms of preference formation, sorts them into those that can ground effective consent and those that cannot, and explains the division. He ends with a puzzle: the relevant mechanisms are usually opaque to the person consenting and to the person receiving consent.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction (pp. 1–2): consent switches two moral facts — the recipient's permission, and third parties' permission to intervene; coercion, poor options, lack of information and so on are defeaters, and there are defeaters of defeaters. The paper concerns one defeater, how preferences were formed, including reflectively endorsed preferences that are nonetheless not "authentically mine".
- "Two Attempts" (pp. 3–4): the perfectionist content-based account fails in both directions (Woolf's Angel in the House: permissible content, ineffective consent). The historical account (Walker 1995; Enoch 2020: preferences shaped by unjust conditions against the agent) is examined next.
- "On the Formation of Preferences" (pp. 4–7) introduces seven mechanisms: Sanctions, Beliefs, Association, Choice, Imitation, Amor Fati and Attention, drawing on empirical work on preference change (e.g. Bardi et al. 2014).
- "The Effectiveness of Consent" (pp. 8–9): Sanctions imposed by unjust forces, and Beliefs formed in a manipulated evidential environment, diminish effectiveness; Attention does not, even when the contact was forced — which handles Enoch's kidnapped pianist.
- Conclusion (p. 10): moral facts are sensitive to formation mechanisms that are often opaque, so uncertainty about whether consent is effective is "ubiquitous and quotidian".

## Open questions

- A mechanism-sensitive account of when shaped preferences still count bears directly on consent to, or preferences induced by, recommender systems and persuasive AI; check the full treatment of Association, Choice, Imitation and Amor Fati, which the skim did not reach in detail.
- The closing puzzle of moral uncertainty is unresolved; check whether other work of Pettigrew's (e.g. on choosing for changing selves) addresses it.
