# CASE-tmpziral: Same marginal, different updates: two joint distributions over a hypothesis and a condition that agree on the hypothesis and disagree on how to revise it

## The case

A6's first version: two groups both put P(H) = 0.9, one from a direct
experiment, one from a simulation that relies on an untested calibration
C. Then C is invalidated. A8 made it a construction. H, C ∈ {0, 1},
P(C = 1) = 0.5.

| | history A | history B |
|---|---|---|
| P(H = 1 \| C = 1) | 1.0 | 0.8 |
| P(H = 1 \| C = 0) | 0.8 | 1.0 |
| P(H = 1) | 0.9 | 0.9 |

On learning C = 1: 1.0 in A, 0.8 in B.

## What it can show

That a memory keeping P(H) cannot make both updates. Its facts are its
definition.

## What it cannot show

A6: "That an accountability graph is the best way to preserve the
distinction, or that this occurs when the agent instead retains a
complete sufficient posterior". Keeping the joint over (H, C) is enough.
[CASE-tmp390tl](CASE-tmp390tl.md) is the matched case where the state suffices; A6: "Together, they
force us to articulate what is meant by current belief state before
making claims about its limitations."
