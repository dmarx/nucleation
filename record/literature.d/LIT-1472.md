# LIT-1472: Intransitivity of preferences

Amos Tversky (1969), *Psychological Review* 76(1):31–48 —
DOI-10.1037/h0026750

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* The model is given as a later source states it.

- **The lexicographic semiorder.** In Manzini and Mariotti's statement of
  it, "a preference is generated by the sequential application of
  numerical criteria, by declaring an alternative x better than an
  alternative y if the first criterion that distinguishes between x and y
  ranks x higher than y by an amount exceeding a fixed threshold". With a
  zero threshold it is the ordinary lexicographic order; with a positive
  one it is Luce's semiorder ([LIT-1460](LIT-1460.md)) placed in front of a
  second criterion.
- **Why it cycles.** Take options x₁, …, xₙ in which each step lowers the
  first criterion by less than the threshold and raises the second. Each
  adjacent pair is then decided by the second criterion, so x₂ beats x₁,
  x₃ beats x₂, and so on. Across the whole chain the first-criterion
  difference passes the threshold, so x₁ beats xₙ. That is a cycle. The
  construction is the record's, from the definition; the paper's own
  experiments are reported (by search summaries, not read) to use risky
  gambles built in this way, small steps in probability against steps in
  payoff.
- **Contested.** Birnbaum and Gutierrez (2007) and Regenwetter, Dana and
  Davis-Stober (2011) are reported to find little reliable individual
  intransitivity on retest. Neither was read.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the correction to [CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md) and
[CLAIM-435](../claims.d/CLAIM-435.md). Does Tversky's lexicographic semiorder match the
"threshold override"? Only in one of two readings, and the claims should
keep them apart.

- **A level threshold** is part 3a's construction. Above a cost c* the
  alarm ranks by cost alone; below it a continuous trade-off decides.
  That is a weak order and has a (discontinuous) representation, as
  part 3a shows. It is not a semiorder.
- **A discrimination threshold** is Tversky's construction. The alarm
  decides between two options when their costs differ by more than ε,
  and otherwise the resource decides. That is a lexicographic semiorder:
  its indifference is intransitive, its strict preference can cycle, and
  it is not a weak order. Where it cycles it has no numerical
  representation even on a finite set: neither a utility (x ≻ y when
  u(x) > u(y)) nor Luce's threshold kind (x ≻ y when u(x) > u(y) + δ),
  since either form makes strict preference acyclic. It is not even a
  semiorder then, a semiorder's strict part being acyclic.

ALARM's "100% weight" ([THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md)) can be read either way. The two
readings predict different things in the trade-off experiment
[CLAIM-435](../claims.d/CLAIM-435.md) proposes. A level threshold predicts a discontinuity at c*
that does not move with the resource. A discrimination threshold
predicts no fixed discontinuity, but cycles among options whose costs
step by less than ε while their resources step up. So the test should
include a Tversky-style chain as well as the indifference curve. That
needs a within-subject design with repeated pairwise choices, and the
contested replication record warns how hard such cycles are to
establish.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
