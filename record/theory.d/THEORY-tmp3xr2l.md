# THEORY-tmp3xr2l: Miller, as Shaw-Young reports his 2007 statement, holds that remedial responsibility for a bad situation is assigned through several distinct connections to it, moral responsibility, outcome responsibility, causal responsibility, benefit, capacity and community, so that it need not fall on whoever caused the situation or was at fault

Shaw-Young (2008), [LIT-tmpqdygg](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqdygg.md), reporting Miller (2007).

## The account

Remedial responsibility is assigned through "moral responsibility, outcome
responsibility, causal responsibility, benefit, capacity, and community"
(thesis p. 20, Miller pp. 100–104). Capacity can suffice: the lone walker
on the river bank must rescue the child (p. 29). Under coercion, if the
coercer "disappears from the scene", the duty to care for the injured
falls on the one made to inflict the injury (p. 25).

## What it does not show

- **The 2001 article's list.** Only the 2007 statement is reported.
- **How the connections are weighed** when several agents are connected.
- **Blame.** The theory assigns repair, not culpability.
