# LIT-682: Exploring Neural Network Landscapes: Star-Shaped and Geodesic Connectivity

Zhanran Lin, Puheng Li and Lei Wu (2024), arXiv preprint — [ARXIV-2404.06391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06391)

## Key takeaways

- **The minimum manifold of a two-layer ReLU teacher–student model is
  explicit.** With an orthonormal teacher of M neurons, inputs uniform on
  the sphere and m ≥ M student neurons, every global minimum has student
  neurons that are zero or a positive multiple of one teacher direction,
  with the weights on each teacher direction summing to one (Theorem 6).
  Two minima are linearly connected exactly when every student neuron is
  zero in one of them or points the same way in both (Lemma 7).
- **Two pieces suffice, given width.** Two minima drawn uniformly from the
  manifold are 2-piece linearly connected with probability at least
  1 − M((M² − 1)/M²)^{m − 2M} (Theorem 8). Any two are 4-piece linearly
  connected once m ≥ 2M − 1 (Theorem 9).
- **Star-shaped connectivity.** For k minima, a common centre linearly
  connected to all of them exists with probability at least
  1 − M((M^k − 1)/M^k)^{m − kM} (Theorem 10). With m ≥ kM, a centre
  2-piece-connected to all k always exists (Theorem 11). For deep linear
  networks of depth L and width m > 1 + r(L − 1), r minima almost surely
  share a linearly connected centre (Theorem 18).
- **Geodesic connectivity comes from sparsity.** For minima drawn
  uniformly, the normalised geodesic distance is O(√M) whatever the width
  (Theorem 12). For minima drawn with a fraction r of neurons switched off,
  as SGD tends to find, it is at most 1 + c₂/(r√m), which tends to 1 as the
  width grows (Theorem 14).
- **In practice, with no permutations.** Minimising the expected loss along
  the lines from a candidate centre to each of five trained models finds a
  centre whose fold-line barriers are near zero (loss barrier 3.1e-05 to
  1.0e-02 against 1.25 to 16.91 on the direct lines; Table 1). The estimated
  normalised geodesic distances are 1.001 to 1.051 (Table 2).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 with the owner's mode-connectivity batch ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). It
is the batch's theoretical source for star-shaped connectivity, and it
reaches the result with no permutation step at all. That sets it apart from
Sonthalia et al. ([LIT-659](LIT-659.md)), who arrive at a star domain *modulo*
permutation as a relaxation of the convexity conjecture. The two were
concurrent. Sonthalia et al. cite this paper as obtaining star models
"linearly connected with a finite number of solutions to larger nets like
the VGG16", and differ from it by adding permutations.

It extends Garipov et al. ([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)). The paper says its investigation
is "inspired by" Garipov's observation that connecting paths can be
piecewise linear with as few as two pieces, and its theorems explain when
that two-piece chain exists. It also tightens Kuditipudi et al.
([LIT-671](LIT-671.md)), who proved paths of at most ten linear segments under
dropout or noise stability. This paper gets two to four segments, but only
in its teacher–student and linear settings, which are much narrower than
Kuditipudi's.

Its "geodesic" is the shortest path inside the minimum manifold under the
Euclidean metric on parameters. That is a different object from the
Fisher–Rao geodesic of Tan et al. ([LIT-662](LIT-662.md)).

No instruction for practice. It is flagged for the anthology as part of the
landscape line the anthology holds through Entezari et al. and Git
Re-Basin.
