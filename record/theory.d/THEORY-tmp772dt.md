# THEORY-tmp772dt: The content of a mental representation has two factors, a narrow conceptual role inside the head and a referential factor relating it to the world, and a long-arm role theory that gets Twin Earth truth conditions right reduces to the same two

## Source

- Block (1986), *Advertisement for a Semantics for Psychology*,
  [LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmposp1c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmposp1c.md): pp. 619–630 (narrow
  meaning, conceptual role, the social dimension), 635–639 (one factor or
  two) and 643–644 (meaning and reference). Page numbers are the
  journal's.
- Block (1987), *Functional Role and Truth Conditions*, [LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md),
  read in full in [NOTE-tmp5ellt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5ellt.md): pp. 167–178 (Twin Earth, private and
  public use, the convergence), n. 31 and the Appendix (pp. 179–181).

## What was actually shown

**Two factors.** Block (1986), [LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), states the theory: meaning has
"a conceptual role component that is entirely 'in the head' (this is
narrow meaning) and an external component that has to do with the
relations between the representations in the head ... and the referents
and/or truth conditions of these representations in the world"
(pp. 627–628). Conceptual role is "total causal role, abstractly
described", limited to the relations that mediate inference, decision
"and the like". Narrow meaning is motivated by explanation: Teen and her
Twin Earth duplicate share a thought about "Michael Jackson", and two
people who think "I am in danger" leap aside for the same reason; what
they share explains what they do (pp. 619–625).

**Role fixes the kind of reference, not the referent.** Which theory of
reference is true of names, causal rather than descriptive, is a fact
about how names function in our thinking, so conceptual role
"determines the nature of the referential factor" without determining
reference; the same role maps to H₂O here and XYZ on Twin Earth
([LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), pp. 643–644). [LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md) repeats it: "The functional
role factor determines the nature of the referential-truth-conditional
factor (without determining reference or truth conditions themselves)"
(p. 170).

**Long-arm roles reduce to two factors.** Harman's one-factor theory
extends roles into the world. To handle a visitor to Twin Earth, the
elm–beech case and borrowed reference to Aristotle, it must build in
normal context, experts' roles and causal chains running into the past.
"If you take Harman's long-arm conceptual roles and 'chop off' the portion
of these roles outside the skin, you are left with my short-arm conceptual
roles. If the outside-the-body part that is chopped off amounts to some
familiar sort of theory of reference, then the difference between
Harman's one-factor theory and two-factor theory is merely verbal"
([LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), p. 637). Block (1987), [LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md), makes this a
general convergence. A private-use theory must add a referential factor to
separate aluminum from twin aluminum, since the defining terms share
extensions on both planets (pp. 168–169). A public-use theory, use "in
the sense in which it could be recorded by a movie camera", is identical
on both planets, and becomes adequate only by importing a dominant causal
source, borrowed reference and experts (pp. 174–178). The result is "two
distinct (though not independent) sub-theories": one "at least partly
inferential" and inside the head, the other "very like a traditional
theory of reference". "The whole thing is equivalent to the two-factor
theory we arrived at starting with private use" (p. 178).

**Social meaning.** For Burge's arthritis, "two-factor theory can try to
capture such phenomena in the referential factor", the causal chain
mediated by people who know more; "alternatively, two-factor theory may
have to expand to three-factor theory, allowing a distinct social factor
to meaning" ([LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), p. 630). The 1987 appendix gives the first
treatment: what is needed is "functional role in a language", cashed out
through "the structure of deference", not an average of speakers, and
individual roles still matter ([LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md), pp. 179–181).

What could have come out otherwise: a long-arm theory could have fixed
reference with resources that are not a theory of reference in disguise,
or the internal remainder could have differed between the twins. In
Block's cases it does neither.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say narrow content is explanatory.** "Is narrow content
  an explanatory notion, either in scientific psychology or everyday
  life? I haven't tried to answer these questions" ([LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md), p. 178).
  The 1986 paper argues it is, from shared behaviour across Twin Earth;
  the 1987 paper only makes room for it, and lists the objections without
  answering them.
- **It does not say one bookkeeping is wrong.** Which to call the theory,
  one factor or two, is a matter of purpose: "The short-arm roles are
  better for psychological explanation, long-arm roles are better for
  thinking about translation and communication" ([LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md), n. 31).
  The claim is that they are equivalent, not that the long-arm form is
  false.
- **It gives no identity conditions for roles.** The "crucial question",
  what counts as same and different role, is posed and left; the 1986
  paper calls itself an advertisement, and Block says he is "far from a
  true believer" ([NOTE-tmposp1c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmposp1c.md), Limitations).
- **The social factor is not built.** Whether it is part of the
  referential factor or a third factor is left open in both papers.
- **It is not functionalism about experience.** Block holds this account
  for intentional content while denying functionalism for qualitative
  states ([LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), p. 635), which is the split [THEORY-tmpdtf2t](THEORY-tmpdtf2t.md),
  filed alongside, relies on.

## How it bears on the record

**The reply [NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md) needs to give Toon.** Drayson ([LIT-112](../literature.d/LIT-112.md), read in
[NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md)) answers Toon's metasemantic premise, that intentionality belongs
first to public, socially normed representations and that inner
representations are "locked away" from convention, by saying that a
functional role semantics can let mental representations take content
from social and communicative use, and she cites "Block 1987", which is
[LIT-tmp1az05](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1az05.md). [NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md) records that this is "named, not developed". This
account is the developed form, and it gives the reply a sharper shape
than Drayson's sentence. On Block's theory the content of an inner
representation is two-factored. Its internal factor, conceptual role, is
in the head and is shared by twins in different communities. Its
referential factor, or a third, social factor, is where use in a
community, borrowed reference and deference to experts enter. So the
inner representation is not locked away from convention: one of its two
factors is constituted by it. That is the reply. It is also a
qualification of Drayson's wording. On Block's own conclusion the social
dependence sits in the referential or social factor, not in the
representation's internal role, so a defender of RTM who wants the inner
role itself socially fixed gets no support from Block. Block's 1995
chapter ([LIT-tmp5vkka](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5vkka.md), n. 4) adds the case in point: the reference of
'Aristotle' in the language of thought derives from the public word.

**The record's other holdings on intentionality.** No theory in the
record states an account of mental content or metasemantics, so no
relation is declared. Among the works:

- Teleosemantics ([LIT-149](../literature.d/LIT-149.md), Martínez) is in the family whose teleological
  "wheel" Block argues cannot be naturalised, since selection need not aim
  mechanisms at truth ([LIT-tmpsiqov](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsiqov.md), pp. 659–660). That bears on it as an
  argument, not as a rival theory filed here.
- Interpretationism about language models ([LIT-206](../literature.d/LIT-206.md)) maximises
  truth or rationality by charity; Block rejects truth-maximising charity
  and derives a coherence-based charity from conceptual roles (p. 645).
- Drayson's realism about beliefs as whole-person functional states
  ([NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md), C2) does not need representations at all; this account is
  about the content of representations if there are any. They are
  compatible, and Block's own commitment to representations is the
  minimal, empirical one (pp. 641–642).

**Inverted Earth.** Block's note 26 to [LIT-tmp8qzkt](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8qzkt.md) uses this account's
equivalence: long-arm roles are short-arm roles plus a referential
component. [THEORY-tmpdtf2t](THEORY-tmpdtf2t.md) asks the functionalist to supply, for
qualitative content, the short-arm factor that this account supplies for
intentional content.
