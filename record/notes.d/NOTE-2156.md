# NOTE-2156: Bengio, Louradour, Collobert and Weston — Curriculum learning

## Key results

1. **Shapes (§5).** "the best generalization is obtained by doing a 2-stage curriculum where the first half of the total allowed training time (of 256 epochs) is spent on the easier examples rather than on the target examples." Unordered training on the union of both sets gave a "final test error still significantly worse than with the curriculum". Training on the easier shapes alone "yielded poor results".
2. **Language model (§6).** "We chose as a curriculum strategy to grow the vocabulary size"; the curriculum "crosses the error of the no-curriculum strategy after about 1 billion updates", ending at log-rank 2.78 against 2.83.
3. **Perceptron (§4.2).** "We train the Perceptron with 200 examples (i.e., 200 Perceptron updates) and measure generalization error at the end": error at a fixed budget.
4. **Mechanism.** "curriculum learning can be seen as a particular form of continuation method" (Abstract); "the curriculum strategies appear on the surface to operate like a regularizer" (§1).
5. **Limits (§7).** Krueger and Dayan found "worse results were obtained with fixed neural resources"; "In the experiments we performed, that pace was fixed arbitrarily."

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-756](../claims.d/CLAIM-756.md) and [CASE-295](../cases.d/CASE-295.md).** The shape experiment is a real instance of [CASE-295](../cases.d/CASE-295.md)'s pattern: an earlier acquisition that does poorly alone ("poor results") belongs to the best policy within a fixed budget, and the union control separates order from data. Measured as final error, not a lowered later cost.
- **[CLAIM-596](../claims.d/CLAIM-596.md).** The paper mostly reports end yields, which that claim says cannot tell a generative nucleus from a head start.

## Limitations

Hyperparameters tuned for the baseline; figure values approximate; no cost-to-threshold measure.
