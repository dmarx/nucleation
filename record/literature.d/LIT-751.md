# LIT-751: The Problem of Intransigently Biased Agents

Bennett Holman & Justin P. Bruner (2015), *Philosophy of Science*
82(5):956–968 — DOI-10.1086/683344

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read; only the abstract was seen, on the authors'
institutional portals.* By the abstract, it extends Zollman's
network-epistemology model with one epistemically impure agent among
honest truth-seekers, and concludes that an intransigently biased agent
keeps the community from ever converging on the truth. It explores two
solutions, one a procedure for endogenous network formation in which
agents choose whom to trust, and applies the result to medical research.
The abstract does not say what the other solution is.

The authors' 2017 paper ([LIT-744](LIT-744.md)) describes it as having used
Zollman's model "to investigate the medical research community", and
cites it among works suggesting that industry-funded research be given
less weight. The 2017 model differs: no agent there is intransigent or
swayed by its funding, and the community's bias comes from which methods
are funded.
