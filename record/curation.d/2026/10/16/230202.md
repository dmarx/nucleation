**Registered from the reading.** Powers, Clark & McFarland (1960), Part I,
[LIT-tmpgo33i](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpgo33i.md), doi:10.2466/pms.1960.11.1.71. It is in the same reprint
volume and gives perceptual control theory's "imagination connection". Only

2026-10-16, commit d1573d2).

**What the readings corrected:**
- **Monti et al.** [CASE-tmpxgcq2](../../record/cases.d/CASE-tmpxgcq2.md) had run two patients together. The patient
  who answered yes/no questions kept inconsistent motor responses. The two
  who stayed unresponsive at the bedside were not asked questions.
- **Pattee** does not put measurement first; for him control of
  construction came first. Corrected in [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md).
- **QBism** separates judgement (state assignment) from measurement
  (action), and so belongs among the action–perception rivals. Corrected
  in [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) and [QUESTION-tmpr5tck](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-tmpr5tck.md).
- **Slovic.** The wording [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) quoted was the manuscript
  abstract's. The published text is quoted now. His evidence bears on the
  synchronic reading, not the diachronic one, and he and Morwitz were
  removed from the claim's grounds.
- **Powers's 1973 paper** has no imagination mode. The 1960 paper has one.
  Corrected in [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md).
