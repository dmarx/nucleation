# THEORY-1632: Brewer argues that close relationships and group memberships are two distinct forms of social identification by which the individual self is extended to include others, the relational self taking in particular others and the collective self a symbolic group, and that they meet distinct needs rather than one need for belonging

## The account

"The relational and collective levels of self ... represent two
different forms of social identification, i.e., processes by which the
individual self is extended to include others as integral to the
self-concept" (p. 3). The relational self takes in particular others who
are "not interchangeable" (p. 5); the collective self a symbolic group
"independent of personal relationships within the group" (p. 3). Humans
need both, "belonging with and belonging to" (p. 24). Each level is
regulated by opposed needs, with transfer between them. Relational
collectivists bound their in-groups tightly; individualists identify
more with large, inclusive groups.

## Evidence

Correlational measurement studies, gender differences and
cross-cultural findings interpreted after the fact; no test of
substitution ([NOTE-1290](../notes.d/NOTE-1290.md)).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) asks how far "a self can extend to include others and
groups". Brewer states that premise in her own words and splits it:
the relational and the collective extension are different selves, so
an answer has to say which larger self it means. Her criteria of a
self's level include self-description, apart from caring, and
importance ratings, which are caring.
