# CLAIM-958: Closing-nucleus loop is autocatalysis

## The claim

In [CASE-365](../cases.d/CASE-365.md), the teacher does one thing: form nuclei. The nucleus
does one thing: make teachers. Both draw on one material, and nothing else
in the model is constrained by either.

**The dynamics are autocatalytic.** Eliminate T and the dynamics are those
of a product, N, that enhances the rate of its own production through an
intermediate. That is [QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md)'s definition of autocatalysis.

**Reading it as closure costs a distinction.** Calling it closure under
[TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md) would make every two-member catalytic cycle closed. The
question keeps autocatalysis and closure apart. Once they merge, its
closure row adds nothing to its autocatalysis row.

**A sign in the time scales.** At the base point k_T = d, so each
constraint is produced at the scale at which it is conserved. That is the
case [CLAIM-395](CLAIM-395.md)'s reading (B) sets aside.

## What it does not say

- **Not that [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s definition is unmet.** It may be met to the
  letter, as [CLAIM-956](CLAIM-956.md) argues.
- **What it does say.** The letter is not the sense in which [QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md)
  asks for closure.
- **What would meet the question on this reading.** A loop in which at
  least one constraint acts on a process other than the production of its
  partner: for example, a nucleus that also does work, with the work
  feeding back into maintenance. That would carry the question further.
