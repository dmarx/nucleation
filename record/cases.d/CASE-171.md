# CASE-171: Mealybugs, Tremblaya and Moranella: a bacterium inside a bacterium inside an insect, amino-acid pathways patched together from three genomes and genes transferred from other bacteria, and an inner symbiont replaced several times while the transferred genes stayed

## The case

The mealybug *Planococcus citri* has two bacterial symbionts nested one
inside the other. The gammaproteobacterium *Moranella endobia* lives in the
cytoplasm of the betaproteobacterium *Tremblaya princeps*.

- **A metabolic patchwork ([LIT-1565](../literature.d/LIT-1565.md)).** The two symbionts' nutrient genes
  barely overlap. Several essential amino-acid pathways need interspersed
  gene products from *Tremblaya*, *Moranella* and possibly the insect.
- **Genes from elsewhere ([LIT-1569](../literature.d/LIT-1569.md)).** *Tremblaya*'s 139-kb genome likely
  degenerated after it acquired *Moranella*. At least 22 expressed genes
  transferred to the insect's genome from several other bacteria fill
  symbiont gaps, and none of them come from *Tremblaya*. The system depends
  on genes from at least six lineages. The authors call this a path to
  intimacy distinct from the organelles'.
- **Turnover ([LIT-1572](../literature.d/LIT-1572.md)).** Across five mealybug species, *Tremblaya* goes
  back to one infection. The gammaproteobacteria inside it come from
  several replacements by related lineages. The insect's transferred genes
  stayed stable through the turnover.

## What it can show

- **Gene loss without transfer to a controlling genome.** Reduction here
  was compensated by genes from third parties. It was not compensated by
  moving the reduced symbiont's genes to the host ([QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md), G).
- **Organization that outlasts its constituents.** The arrangement
  persisted while one of its members was replaced. This bears on whether a
  partnership is one individual or a succession, and on [THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md)'s test
  of whether complementation alone gives closure.
