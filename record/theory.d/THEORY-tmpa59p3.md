# THEORY-tmpa59p3: Feenberg argues that technology is neither neutral nor determined, that its design embodies a contingent technical code reflecting the values of dominant groups, that technological development is a field of social struggle with many feasible outcomes, and that an economic code that rewards deskilling could be replaced by one that values skill

## The account

Feenberg argues that "Technological development is a scene of social struggle" over "the variety of possible technologies and paths of progress among which we must choose"; that "The idea that industrial technology is irredeemable is essentially determinist"; and that the capitalist economic code "rewards deskilling".

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpgnw4a](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpgnw4a.md). Corroborates [CLAIM-tmp6wuql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6wuql.md)'s premise.

## What it does not say

Evidence that institutions able to revise their codes fail less ([CLAIM-tmpu2cwc](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpu2cwc.md) asks for it).

## Note of 2026-11-13: beside Winner

Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" (THEORY-tmpvlt2n), read on 2026-11-13 as a stand-in for Mumford, is the older statement of this account's first part: a design or arrangement that settles an issue in a community in favour of some interests, as Moses's overpasses did, is a technical code at work. The two do not rival. Winner adds that the latitude of choice "vanishes for all practical purposes once the initial commitments are made", which limits the contingency this account relies on to the time before commitment; Feenberg's contestable codes are most contestable then. No relation written; neither builds on the other in the record's sense.
