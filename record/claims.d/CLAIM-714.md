# CLAIM-714: A present incapacity to recognize a violation does not show that the incapacitated party is not responsible for the incapacity: where it dismantled the capability in breach of a duty, the incapacity is a consequence of its earlier wrong and does not excuse it, the same failure of responsiveness differs in significance with its history, and where no one is culpable for the incapacity the duty to repair still stands

## The claim

A110 §7 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), "The
self-induced incapacity problem". At t₀ institution A can evaluate whether
its activity violates claim r. It adopts a policy that destroys the
monitoring or expertise needed. At t₁ it cannot detect the violation and
says it lacks the information to act. A separates CannotDetect_{t₁}(A, r)
from NotResponsibleForIncapacity(A, r): "The first does not imply the
second." If A violated a justified duty at t₀ by dismantling the
capability, "then its present incapacity may be one consequence of its
earlier wrong."

> **The incapacity that mitigates blame for an immediate decision can itself be the product of an earlier blameworthy decision.**

"Where different actors made those decisions, culpability may attach
upstream rather than to the frontline agent. Where the incapacity developed
through collective routines without one individually culpable author, the
organizational duty to repair may still persist."

## Four histories, one failure (A108 §5, folded in)

A108 §5 raised it first, as a tension with Wolf: for Wolf an individual's
inability to recognize reasons can mitigate responsibility, but "It would
be strange to conclude that the resulting institution is therefore *less
accountable* simply because it has become less capable of recognizing its
obligations." A gives four histories behind the same observed failure:
unavoidable incapacity, negligent deterioration, strategic insulation, and
recognized but disregarded reasons. "The observed lack of responsiveness
might be identical in these cases. Its normative significance is not."

> **An institution should not necessarily be excused by a deficiency in normative responsiveness when the deficiency results from a breach of its own responsibilities.**

A110 §7 restates three of the histories as Cases A to C (unavoidable
limitation, negligent deterioration, deliberate insulation) and concludes
that "**reasons-responsiveness cannot be evaluated adequately from present
behavior alone when responsibility for the mechanism itself is at issue**".

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md)** (the owner's) is what it rests on: responsibility for
  an act and for the incapacity through which it was done can diverge.
  This claim is the case where both lie with one actor at two times.
- **[QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md)** asks whether the origin of a mechanism bears on whose an
  organization's counter-normativity is. This claim adds a clear instance
  on the formation side: the organization that removed its own capability
  is both installer and maintainer, and its earlier act is not excused by
  its later state. It does not answer the question, which is about
  mechanisms installed by another.
- **[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)** (Active): an intervention can make its own correction
  costlier or unreachable. Dismantling the capability to detect a
  violation is such an intervention, made before review.
- **[CLAIM-721](CLAIM-721.md)**, filed from the part before: a restriction that
  produces the incapacity later cited to justify it. Same structure, with
  the incapacity produced in another party.
- **[CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)** and **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)** (Proposed) hold the reproduction of
  non-legibility as a wrong distinct from the harm, and **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)**'s
  grades (A60 §8) run from unavoidable limitation to strategic
  self-insulation. This claim gives those grades their use: they decide
  whether the incapacity excuses.
- **[THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md)** (Active): Fischer and Ravizza make responsibility
  historical through the ownership of the mechanism. A110 §7 notes the
  parallel and that the institutional case needs further distinctions
  "among designers, officeholders, maintainers, and those holding authority
  to intervene". **[CASE-291](../cases.d/CASE-291.md)** is the matched test.

## What it does not say

- Not that every incapacity is self-induced. A110 §7's Case A is "Possible
  outcome: wrongful or harmful action without a corresponding culpable
  failure to maintain the missing capability."
- Not that the duty that was breached is established here. Whether
  dismantling the capability breached a duty needs the maintenance duty of
  [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md), argued in [ARG-114](../arguments.d/ARG-114.md).
- Not that an institution is a moral person. A108 §5: "We cannot simply
  anthropomorphize an institution and treat it as a morally impaired
  individual."
