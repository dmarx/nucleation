# LIT-077: What is quantum in quantum randomness?

Philippe Grangier, Alexia Auffèves (2018), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 376(2123):20170322 (online 2018-05-28), discussion-meeting issue "Foundations of quantum mechanics and their impact on contemporary society"; arXiv preprint first* — [ARXIV-1804.04807](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04807)

## Key takeaways

- Within the Contexts-Systems-Modalities ontology, quantum randomness is ontological, not epistemic. It comes from the mismatch between an elementary system's discrete (quantized) exclusive modalities and the continuously variable state of its classical context, and adding that continuity condition yields Born-rule, unistochastic probabilities.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Quantum randomness is often called ontological and classical randomness epistemological, but what quantization and discreteness contribute to the difference has seldom been addressed. The authors answer within their "contextual objectivity" ontology (CSM), where quantum randomness arises from contextuality together with quantization. They argue this changes what quantum theory is for. In particular, it undercuts reductionist programmes that try to derive the classical world from many quantum systems. A second part treats quantum physics and thermodynamics as two theories of randomness and traces their mutual influence. It ends with technological uses of quantum randomness in quantum thermodynamics.

What a deeper reading should check:

- It is the Grangier–Auffèves CSM programme in brief. Its reliance on unistochastic transition matrices is a direct point of contact with Barandes' stochastic-quantum correspondence (c15), and a joint reading could compare the two routes to Born-rule probabilities.
- The derivation of quantum theory from (i)–(iii) is only cited, not reproduced. A deeper reading needs refs [14, 15] (Auffèves & Grangier, Sci. Rep. 2017, and related papers).
- The anti-reductionist claim, that the classical world cannot emerge from many quantum systems, is strong and should be checked against decoherence-based accounts.
