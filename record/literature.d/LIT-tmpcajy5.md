summary: >-
  Syropoulos, Kraft-Todd, Mah, Markowitz & Young (2023). Thirteen studies (six main, seven supplementary; N = 161,633) on felt responsibility to future generations (RFG). In Eurobarometer data from 34 European countries (N = 109,513), a majority in 33 agreed "We need to reform to benefit future generations even if that means making some sacrifices now"; agreement barely related to ideology, age or gender (meta-r .03 to .07) and weakly to subjective status (.13). In US samples RFG was endorsed more than responsibility to reduce one's own contribution to climate change (RCC), correlated with conservatism half as strongly (meta-r −.21 against −.45), and predicted self-reported proenvironmental behaviour, concern and policy support. In a preregistered experiment (N = 3,175), five brief interventions (a letter to a person born today, legacy reflection, reflection on past generations' sacrifices, a park video, a 385-word excerpt of *What We Owe the Future*) each raised RFG and proenvironmental intentions by small amounts (d = .14 to .54); only the letter raised donations. Reflection on past sacrifices raised RFG but not RCC. All outcomes are self-reports or a small donation. **status_note**: worth reading as the largest study of felt responsibility to future generations, its spread and its political neutrality, and as the record's only experiment comparing legacy, reciprocity, a particular future person and an impersonal longtermist text as routes to it; read in place of Syropoulos and Markowitz 2021.
