# LIT-tmpldiuy: What Is Enactivism?

Ward, D. (2026). What Is Enactivism? *Adaptive Behavior* 34(4–5): 253–271. doi:10.1177/10597123261450094.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpl4pbc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl4pbc.md). The manuscript has no page numbers; references are to sections.*

- **Two axes.** Where cognition's processes are (skull, or
  brain–body–world) is one question. What the meaning of mental states
  "fundamentally depends on", information or organization distinctive of
  living things, is another, and the two are orthogonal (§3).
- **Most embodied views are informationalist.** In embodied predictive
  processing, bodily and worldly factors "are important only insofar as
  they help implement the hierarchical pattern of information flow upon
  which cognition ultimately depends" (§4). Cybernetic loops could run in
  "autonomous drones, or suitably constituted and embedded LLMs", so they
  are characteristic of life, not distinctive of it. Biological
  naturalism about consciousness is usually informationalist too (fn. 6).
- **Enactivism is organicist.** Its core is sense-making by
  self-maintaining living systems (§5). Ward grants that the argument for
  life–mind continuity is "complex and controversial".
- **Primacy and reciprocity.** Lived experience is the primary
  explanandum and evidence, but it is reshaped by the vocabularies,
  scientific ones included, through which we understand it (§§1, 6).
  Meditators' reports are not "privileged insights into primal
  experiential truths" (§6).
- **Not a verdict.** "The lesson is not that informationalism is wrong
  ... it is that it is partial" (§7). Whether enactivists are right is
  "not something I aim to establish here" (§6).

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange cited this paper for life–mind continuity and the primacy of lived experience, and drew from it the enactive challenge to the thesis: that an organization can be held without current input does not show it could arise without an embodied history. Bears on [CLAIM-tmpjw8ev](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjw8ev.md).

The reading corrects the exchange's description. The paper does give
life–mind continuity and the primacy of experience, as the exchange said.
It does not give the challenge the exchange drew from it, that an
organization held without current input need not be one that could arise
without an embodied history. The paper does not discuss brains in vats,
development, current input or grounding. That challenge is the exchange's
own inference from the enactive view. The objection the paper does
support is different: on its map, [CLAIM-tmpjw8ev](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjw8ev.md) and [CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md) are
informationalist, and an enactivist would hold that what makes an
organization meaningful, and so experiential, is its inherence in a
living, self-maintaining system. See [NOTE-tmpl4pbc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl4pbc.md), Bearing on the
record.
