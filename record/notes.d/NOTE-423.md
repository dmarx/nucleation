# NOTE-423: Nonequilibrium thermodynamics and maximum entropy production in the Earth system

## Contribution

A synthesis of the maximum-entropy-production (MEP) programme for Earth
science. Its four parts:

- an introduction to nonequilibrium entropy budgets through box models;
- an order-of-magnitude entropy budget of the whole Earth;
- a survey of MEP applications to turbulent, hydrological and biotic
  processes;
- an argument that MEP gives the Gaia hypothesis a thermodynamic basis,
  with negative feedbacks at an MEP state and life driving the planet
  toward higher entropy production.

## Key insight

Wherever a flux depletes the gradient that drives it, entropy production
(flux × force) is zero at both extremes: no flux, or a flux so strong it
leaves no gradient. In between there is a maximum. If a system's internal
configuration is free to vary, MEP says it sits at that maximum. The
freedom has to come from somewhere, for example turbulent eddies,
vegetation or soil heterogeneity. The thermodynamic picture is then about
which boundary conditions are "flexible".

## Assumptions

- **MEP's own condition.** "If there are sufficient degrees of freedom
  within the system, it will adopt a steady state at which σ is
  maximized" (p. 655). "Sufficient" is not defined.
- **MEP is taken as given** for the implications section. That section is
  explicitly "under the presumption that MEP is justified sufficiently
  well" (p. 670).
- **Isothermal or near-uniform temperature** where dissipation is
  identified with entropy production times T, since 1/T "usually does not
  vary by much" (p. 655).
- **The box models** use linear conductive fluxes, F_ab = k(T_a − T_b),
  with k reinterpreted as an eddy diffusivity when MEP is demonstrated
  (case 3, p. 658).
- **Radiative entropy** is computed without the 4/3 factor, judged "of no
  relevance to the energy exchanges on Earth" (p. 661).

## Key results

- **MEP vs Prigogine** (pp. 655–656, 673). "Prigogine's principle states that
  the steady state is one of minimum entropy production compared to
  transient states". Once a steady state is assumed it "makes no further
  predictions", in particular among several possible steady states. MEP
  "acts as a selection principle of the most probable out of many possible
  steady states". The text illustrates the two with Fig. 1 (relaxation)
  and Fig. 2 (selection among steady states). Applying Prigogine's
  principle to steady-state configurations, as in geomorphology, is called
  "possibly for the wrong reasons".
- **Box models** (Figs. 1–2).
  - An isolated two-box system relaxes to equilibrium with σ → 0 and
    S → S_max.
  - A forced system reaches a steady state with S < S_max. It moves away
    from equilibrium when the forcing time scale is shorter than the
    mixing time scale.
  - With alternative heat-loss pathways, σ_ab peaks at an intermediate
    conductivity. In that model the MEP state is also the state of minimum
    entropy S, furthest from equilibrium. The author warns this is "clearly
    no definite proof that this is necessarily always so" (p. 659).
- **Earth's entropy budget** (Fig. 3, §"Entropy production by Earth system
  processes"), in mW m⁻² K⁻¹:
  - absorption of sunlight: 555 at the surface and 258 in the atmosphere;
  - scattering: about 26;
  - terrestrial radiative exchange: about 26;
  - the hydrologic cycle: 23;
  - the carbon cycle: about 6, 70% of it at photosynthesis;
  - ground heat diffusion: about 1.
  - Turbulent processes are small in the budget. Their point is that they
    enhance radiative entropy export.
- **Surveyed MEP applications.**
  - Atmosphere: Paltridge's climate model and its successors; Lorenz et
    al. on Mars and Titan; GCM sensitivity studies in which entropy
    production saturates at a maximum.
  - Ocean: Shimokawa and Ozawa, where the MEP state is the most stable of
    several steady states.
  - Mantle and earthquakes.
  - Land evapotranspiration (Wang et al.).
  - Sediment decomposition (Meysman and Bruers).
  - Each is reported, not reproduced.
- **Implications, given MEP.**
  - An MEP state answers perturbations with negative feedback. A change in
    boundary conditions first gives positive feedback until the new
    optimum is reached (Fig. 7).
  - Planetary MEP mediated by life can give Gaia-like homeostasis (Kleidon
    2004a).
  - The Earth may have evolved stepwise toward higher entropy production.
  - MEP "provides the basis to unite" Odum's maximum power principle,
    Bejan's constructal law and self-organized criticality.

## Concepts

- **Net entropy exchange (NEE)**: the divergence of entropy fluxes across
  the boundary, with export positive. In steady state σ = NEE (p. 655).
- **Thermodynamic equilibrium (TE)** as reference state for each cycle:
  - for the hydrologic cycle, a saturated atmosphere;
  - for the carbon cycle, CO₂ in equilibrium with the hot interior;
  - for motion, rest (Table 1).
- **"Batteries"**: processes that generate the free energy driving a
  cycle away from TE, such as atmospheric circulation for water, and
  photosynthesis and the geologic interior for carbon (Fig. 4).

## Bearing on the record

- **Is MEP established? No, on this text's own account.** "At this point,
  no one would argue that MEP is well established and that applications
  are without their limitations" (p. 673). The record should treat MEP as a
  proposed principle with a body of successful model fits and a contested
  derivation. Dewar 2003 and Grinstein and Linsker together are the
  derivation and the dispute. This review is the applications.
- **The objection to Prigogine, as this literature states it.** The minimum
  principle is not wrong but answers another question: it says nothing
  about which steady state is selected. So it cannot do the work Prigogine's
  programme and its geomorphological users asked of it. The review does not
  state the conditions under which Prigogine's theorem holds. Whether that
  objection agrees with Landauer's 1975 objection ([LIT-532](../literature.d/LIT-532.md)), that no
  entropy-based quantity can characterise the steady state at all, cannot
  be checked: Landauer 1975 could not be read.
- **Misreporting of the critics.** The review says Grinstein and Linsker
  "point out some technical flaws in the derivation of the linkage of MEP
  and SOC". Their Comment ([NOTE-422](NOTE-422.md)) makes that point second. Its
  first and main point is that Dewar's 2005 derivation of MaxEP fails for
  nonlinear constitutive relations, i.e. far from equilibrium, where the
  review applies MEP. The review understates the critique.
- **Life as dissipation.** The second half is where the record's
  dissipative-structure and life literatures meet. See the LIT's Standing
  for [LIT-192](../literature.d/LIT-192.md) and [LIT-211](../literature.d/LIT-211.md). The review offers no criterion separating a
  living dissipator from an abiotic one, and its biotic MEP claims are model
  sensitivity studies.
- **No ML instruction.** The anthology does not hold it.

## Limitations

- **Advocacy.** The author is the principal proponent, and much of the
  surveyed evidence is his own model work.
- **MEP is presumed** in the implications section, by the author's
  statement.
- **"Sufficient degrees of freedom"** is the condition MEP needs. It is
  never made operational, so a failure of MEP can always be read as too few
  degrees of freedom.
- **The Prigogine comparison** is by example. The review never states the
  conditions under which the minimum principle is a theorem.
- **Box-model results** such as MEP coinciding with minimum S are flagged
  by the author as not general.

## Open questions

- A derivation of MEP that holds far from equilibrium. The author calls
  for "a more solid foundation" (p. 673).
- What makes a system's boundary conditions flexible enough for MEP? The
  author speculates that this sets limits on emergent complexity (p. 659).
- Can planetary MEP be tested against Earth history, for example stepwise
  increases in entropy production at the oxidation events?
