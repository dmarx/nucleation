# TERM-tmpjdel0: apparatus, in the operational sense: a realized organization that selectively couples differences in one system to distinguishable outcomes in another; by function it may differentiate, retain, enforce and revise, and the four come apart

## Definition

An **apparatus**, in the operational sense, is a realized organization that
selectively couples some differences in one system to distinguishable outcomes
in another (A19 §11, A23 §X). It may serve any of four functions (A23 §X):

| function | what it does | example |
|---|---|---|
| differentiation | makes some physical differences produce distinct outcomes | a sensor, a chemical receptor |
| retention | keeps an outcome across later change | a memory, an inscription, a molecular state |
| enforcement | makes a kept distinction affect what happens next | a feedback controller, an executable validator |
| revision | changes the criteria or operations by which distinctions are made | an adaptive sensor, a scientific practice, a governed ontology |

The four come apart. A record can be made and never used, and a fixed
controller enforces without revising. Revision is not part of every apparatus,
but it is where A23 placed "the beginning of a theory of reflexive inquiry".

An interior that has been composed away also counts. Its effective boundary
relation constrains what interacts with the composite, and A22 called it
apparatus for that reason (CLAIM-tmpose3y).

## The distinction it draws

From the agential sense of TERM-tmp0est4. A19 §5 named three readings of an
apparatus: informational (it makes correlations), operational (it implements and
stabilizes distinctions) and constitutive (it takes part in fixing the
boundaries of the phenomenon). This term is the second reading, with the first
included. Barad's apparatus is the third, and A23 §VII said dynamical
stabilization "does not derive Barad's constitutive thesis". A claim that uses
"apparatus" should say which sense it means. A claim made in this sense commits
to nothing about constitution.

From an observer. No consciousness is presupposed (A23 §X). "Calling the
stronger forms *inquiry* remains a substantive terminological choice."

From soundness. A23 §X: "**A self-modifying apparatus is not necessarily an
epistemically self-correcting apparatus.**" Revision is a function the
apparatus has. Whether its revisions improve anything is a separate question
(QUESTION-tmpubsi9).

## Why it is proposed

The record already uses the word in two places. CLAIM-tmpl228b calls luria an
apparatus in Barad's sense. TERM-tmp0est4 defines "cut" by the apparatus that
enacts it. The exchange's later turns needed a weaker sense that could be
applied without arguing the constitutive thesis first. Luria fits it on all
four functions:
- its schemes differentiate entries;
- its files retain them;
- its lint enforces the distinctions;
- its decisions revise them (CLAIM-419; CASE-tmp0zvle measured how).

What would settle it: adoption if a claim or note needs the operational sense
and can say so by citing this term. Rejection if every use the record makes of
"apparatus" turns out to be Barad's.
