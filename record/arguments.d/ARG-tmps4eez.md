# ARG-tmps4eez: Millikan's biosemantics, read beside the SEP survey, independently corroborates that a false alarm's error cannot lie in its natural meaning, and qualifies the separation of where the error lies from what fixes the norm

## The inference

1. [THEORY-tmpfugkf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfugkf.md) ([NOTE-tmpqexrv](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqexrv.md), p. 9): actually representing, as
   picturing and carrying natural information, "is not a matter
   determined by a history of selection"; "attributions of truth and
   falsity ... make sense only by reference to function"; and "'true'
   versus 'false' ... do not apply to natural signs".
2. [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md) (a): the steam alarm's natural meaning, that the detector
   crossed its threshold, is factive and true; the false alarm's error
   lies in a non-factive content, "fire".
3. Step 1 reaches step 2's conclusion by another route: Millikan's
   natural signs, like Grice's natural meaning ([THEORY-444](../theory.d/THEORY-444.md)), cannot be
   false, so whatever is false in a false alarm is a content of another
   kind. That is corroboration, since [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md) rests on Grice ([ARG-082](ARG-082.md))
   and not on this reading.
4. The qualification. On the same account, a sign has a content that can
   be false only "by reference to function" (step 1), and the survey adds
   that explaining "how content is determined and how the possibility of
   error are accommodated are not separate tasks" ([THEORY-tmpuzicj](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuzicj.md);
   [NOTE-tmp60j0q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp60j0q.md), §3). So whether a signal has a content that can be
   false at all, question (a), is answered only once something fixes the
   content, question (b). Grice's test says where a false alarm's error
   would lie; it does not by itself show that a signal with no source of
   norm has a content that can be false.
5. For [CASE-243](../cases.d/CASE-243.md) this costs nothing: the alarm is stipulated to be
   designed to warn of fire, so (b) is given and (a) follows. For a
   non-designed system, which is where [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md) leaves (b) open, (a) is
   open with it.

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** Step 1's quotations are
  from the chapter's account of what representing is (p. 9), not from a
  passing remark, and the chapter's opening (pp. 2-3) says the same in
  general terms: teleology exists to explain "how falseness in
  representations is possible". The survey's "not separate tasks" is the
  authors' own view, stated against a reading of Millikan 2004 on which
  function explains only error.
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** Partly. Millikan's
  account covers "intentional signs generally", animal signals and road
  signs included (p. 2), but the chapter is about mental representation
  and says nothing about artefacts. So the corroboration of (a) carries
  to the alarm as a sign; nothing here says what fixes a designed
  alarm's content, and step 5 relies on [CASE-243](../cases.d/CASE-243.md)'s stipulation, not on
  Millikan.
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?**
  [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md) does not cite this reading; this ARG is why the reading is
  written as `corroborates`, not `grounds`. It is not an `objects_to`:
  the qualification narrows how far (a) and (b) are independent, and
  [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md)'s text already says (b) is open for non-designed systems.

## Verdict

Proposed. The corroboration is of question (a) only. The qualification
is filed here, not as a revision of [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md), whose text is the owner's
to change.
