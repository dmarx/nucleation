# LIT-244: Natural Kinds (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

Alexander Bird, Emma Tobin (2008), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (ed. Edward N. Zalta)* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-kinds/>

## Key takeaways

- Whether scientific kinds are natural breaks into four questions (are the classifications natural, are they into kinds, what the kinds are, and do they have essences). The entry maps the positions from conventionalism to essentialist strong realism, notes a recent turn toward pluralist and epistemology-first views and even eliminativism, and questions whether Kripke–Putnam semantics can deliver essences.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Sciences sort their objects into kinds, and to call a kind natural is to say it follows the structure of the world rather than human interests. Scientific realism holds that successful sciences uncover such kinds, and that this justifies inductive inference. The entry covers the metaphysics of natural kinds: naturalism vs conventionalism, realism, essentialism and eliminativism. It then looks at how kinds work in biology, chemistry, psychology and the social sciences, at the epistemology of kinds, and at the semantics of natural-kind terms from descriptivism to direct reference and externalism.
