**The owner's prompts**, verbatim. First: "file the luria lint report
upstream. I'm wondering if there's a way we can generalize this to be
general purpose config declaration. it's possible it might even already be
a supported feature and the change we need is just in the project
luria.toml rather than somethign upstream. maybe." Then, mid-answer: "with
the "uses:" relation, isn't this a "pushdown" from the field to the
prose?" Then: "go ahead with all four, explain: cited".

**What was found.** The owner was right that half the check already
existed. Luria's `explain:` key on a reference field is pushdown: with
`explain: cited`, every code in the field must be cited in the body. Its
converse is pushup: `[[uses::TERM-…]]` written in prose is expanded by
`luria link --fix` into a citation and added to the field. Neither is a
new lint check, and nucleation needed one line in `luria.yaml`. The half
that is not there is phrase detection, a TERM's title used in an entry
that neither cites nor lists it. No field exists to declare that against,
so it stays upstream as dmarx/luria#370, narrowed by a comment there.

**What firing it found.** The report gave 70 rows, against the 60 my own
count had predicted. My count was a grep for codes in the body. The
report counts what luria treats as a citation, and the extra ten were not
traced one by one. Each row was read. Most took a
`[[uses::…]]` after the first eligible use, outside quotations, headings,
italic titles and formulas. Rows whose term is named only inside a quoted
source, or applied without being named, or which point at a TERM since
superseded, took a `ref::uses-file:` line saying which. Superseded TERMs
are kept in `uses:`, because the claim was stated in the definition
current when it was written. The fix was not mechanical at the edges.
Three citations first landed inside formulas or split a phrase, and two
were moved twice before reading cleanly.
