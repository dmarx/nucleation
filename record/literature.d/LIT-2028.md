# LIT-2028: Do Expiring Budgets Lead to Wasteful Year-End Spending? Evidence from Federal Procurement

Liebman, J. B. & Mahoney, N. (2017). Do Expiring Budgets Lead to Wasteful Year-End Spending? Evidence from Federal Procurement. *American Economic Review* 107(11): 3510–3549. doi:10.1257/aer.20131296. (NBER Working Paper 19481, September 2013, revised January 2018.)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1553](../notes.d/NOTE-1553.md). Page numbers are the NBER file's PDF pages.*

- **The spike.** "8.7 percent of spending occurs in the last week of the
  year" (§1, p. 4), against about 1.9 percent in a uniform week, in all
  major agencies.
- **The quality drop.** "Projects that originate in the last week of the
  fiscal year have 2.2 to 5.6 times higher odds of having a lower quality
  score." (§1, p. 5), the effect carried by the agency CIO's rating.
- **Waste defined.** Spending whose marginal return to the agency is below
  the social cost of funds (Definition 1, p. 9); the buffer held against
  uncertain needs is what expiry turns into such spending.
- **Commitment.** "If Congress cannot commit to a budget rule, the agency
  will never roll over any budget authority, and allowing rollover will not
  produce an efficiency gain." (Proposition 3, p. 30).
- **Rollover and grace periods.** DOJ's rollover authority "reduces the
  volume of year-end spending by 9.5 percentage points"; in the
  calibration "a one-month grace period achieves 41 percent of the welfare
  gains from full rollover" and a four-month one 90 percent (§5.4, p. 35).

## Standing in the record

The first work in the record on budgeting, rollover or year-end spending.
The pasted bibliography on emergency readiness files it under budgeting,
ratchets and incentives, and its description of the paper is accurate but
leaves out what matters most to that bibliography's theme: the model's
buffer is held precisely for uncertain, possibly urgent needs, and an
expiring budget converts the unused buffer into low-value spending. The
paper mentions the ratchet effect and its model does not exhibit one
(§5.2). Siemroth's later model, [LIT-2103](LIT-2103.md), recasts it as moral hazard
with costly auditing. Filed from it: [THEORY-1421](../theory.d/THEORY-1421.md). No instruction for
machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
