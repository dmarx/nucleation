# LIT-tmphayso: Spatially distributed computation in cortical circuits

Gepshtein, S., Pawar, A. S., Kwon, S., Savel'ev, S. & Albright, T. D. (2022). Spatially distributed computation in cortical circuits. *Science Advances* 8(16): eabl5865. doi:10.1126/sciadv.abl5865.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpqb8qd](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqb8qd.md).*

- **A standing wave, not a travelling one.** The stationary response "is a
  distributed spatial pattern of activation ... it is a standing spatially
  damped wave". Its period "is an intrinsic property of the circuit".
- **Interference sets the preference.** Inputs a half period apart "cancel
  one another". Outside the stimulated region, "The SF of the response in
  zone 2 is independent of the stimulus SF."
- **A new prediction, confirmed in MT.** "In both monkeys, the preferred TF
  follows a nonmonotonic function of stimulus SF".
- **Linear only at low contrast.** At higher contrast the interaction
  "additionally depends on the amplitudes of the neural wave".

## Standing in the record

A's characterization holds: repeated excitatory–inhibitory motifs, and
interference of distributed neural waves to explain context-dependent
responses. A reader should keep it apart from propagating waves
([LIT-tmp0aok6](LIT-tmp0aok6.md)). The evidence is a 1-D model, two observers and one earlier
MT data set. The closing suggestion of wave interference for AI is
speculation with no result behind it. No held entry bears on it.
