summary: >-
  Treisman and Gelade (1980), Cognitive Psychology 12(1). In visual search for a
  coloured letter, a target that differs from all distractors by one feature (a
  colour, blue, or a shape, S, among brown Ts and green Xs) was found in a time
  almost independent of display size (positive slopes 3.8 ms/item for colour, 2.5
  for shape, display sizes 1–30), while a target defined by a conjunction (green
  T among brown Ts and green Xs) gave linear slopes of 28.7 ms/item when present
  and 67.1 when absent, the roughly 1:2 ratio of serial self-terminating search;
  13 sessions of practice did not make the conjunction pop out. Making the conjunction's
  colours and shapes harder to tell apart nearly tripled the slope but kept it linear,
  and a single-feature target hard to discriminate (an ellipse of intermediate size)
  was slow but not item-by-item serial. Even in feature search, target-absent trials
  were slow and grew with display size (25.1 ms/item). The displays were at most
  30–36 well-spaced items on a card, with unlimited viewing; the paper argues that
  conjunction structure, not similarity, makes search serial, and does not model
  similarity.

# LIT-tmp9g55n: A feature-integration theory of attention

Treisman, A. M. and Gelade, G. (1980). A feature-integration theory of attention. *Cognitive Psychology* 12(1), 97–136. DOI 10.1016/0010-0285(80)90005-5

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-tmpe7gfj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpe7gfj.md)). A target that alone has one feature, a colour or a distinctive shape part, is found in time almost independent of display size (3.1 ms/item on average, up to 30 items). A target identified only by a combination of features its neighbours carry separately is found by a serial self-terminating scan (28.7 ms/item when present, 67.1 when absent), and practice did not change this. In this paper serial search comes from conjunctions, not from similarity. Similarity on one dimension made search slow but not item-by-item. Confirming absence cost a scan even when a present target popped out (25.1 against 3.1 ms/item).

## Why this is here

The owner's LEGO parable ([CASE-tmpd5exw](../cases.d/CASE-tmpd5exw.md)): "if it's a single black brick in a box
of white bricks: ok, it's buried but its' still detectable." [CLAIM-tmpjmtw0](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjmtw0.md) uses
the feature/conjunction distinction to say what an arrangement buys:
sorting on one attribute leaves the wanted brick to be told from its
binmates by one feature only.
