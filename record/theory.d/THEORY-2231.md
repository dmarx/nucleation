# THEORY-2231: Hodgkinia split six times in Tettigades

## Source

Łukasik et al. (2018), *PNAS* 115(2): E226–E235 (online 26 December
2017), [LIT-1586](../literature.d/LIT-1586.md), read in [NOTE-2321](../notes.d/NOTE-2321.md).

## What was actually shown

Nineteen populations from about 1,000 specimens, seven clades. Host and
*Sulcia* phylogenies agree on the clades; *Hodgkinia* genomes were
assembled per sample, and gene phylogenies place the splits. FISH on
rRNA variants shows the lineages in non-overlapping cells. The count of
independent splits could have been one, with descendants sharing it;
the gene phylogenies say six or more.

## What this does not say

- That splitting is nonadaptive or harmful to the host: the authors'
  speculation.
- That the complexes do what the single lineage did: inferred from gene
  content ("presumably").
- The lineage count per host in every clade: the discussion's "5 to 7"
  for the oldest clades sits beside the abstract's "two and six".
