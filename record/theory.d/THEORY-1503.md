# THEORY-1503: Davis and Weinstein find, testing unique against multiple equilibria by threshold regression on the Allied bombing of Japanese cities, that city population, the city share of aggregate manufacturing and city-industry output pooled over eight manufacturing industries tend to return after the shock to their prior shares: the Schwarz criterion prefers a single equilibrium for manufacturing and for city-industry output; for population it slightly prefers two equilibria, but that fit, like the best two-equilibrium fit for industries, fails the requirement that larger negative shocks lead to lower equilibria; and pooled city-industry shares were still recovering in 1969, which the authors suggest may be because the hardest-hit places take longer, though without Hiroshima the persistence is indistinguishable from zero

## Source

Davis & Weinstein (2004; 2008), [LIT-2201](../literature.d/LIT-2201.md); read in
NOTE-1690 from NBER w10252. The journal version was not read. Page
numbers are the working paper's.

## What was actually shown

- **The test (§II, pp. 7–8).** With one equilibrium, shock-period and
  recovery-period growth lie on a slope −1 line through the origin; with
  several, on parallel slope −1 lines with ordered intercepts beyond
  thresholds. Fitted by threshold regression with one to four equilibria,
  chosen by the Schwarz criterion, subject to the intercept-ordering
  criterion (p. 33).
- **Population (pp. 32–33).** Two equilibria slightly preferred by the
  Schwarz criterion (77.1 against 75.6), but every multiple-equilibria fit
  fails the ordering criterion, the low intercept having the wrong sign; "Following enormous shocks there is no
  evidence that cities unravel further or fail to return to their former
  size."
- **Manufacturing (p. 34).** "the Schwarz criterion clearly favors the
  model of one equilibrium." The two-equilibrium fit passes the ordering
  criterion, but its intercepts "are not statistically distinct".
- **Industries (pp. 37–39).** A unique equilibrium preferred; the
  two-equilibrium fit's threshold at −0.67 log units fails the ordering
  criterion; ρ about 0.34, indistinguishable from zero without Hiroshima;
  the authors offer, as "one possible reason", that "places hit extremely
  hard take longer to recover".

What could have come out otherwise: a preferred model with ordered
intercepts below a threshold would have shown the hardest-hit cities or
industries settling at a lower level.

## What this does not say

- **That no threshold exists.** Thresholds beyond the shocks, varying by
  city, or depending on neighbouring cities are assumed away or untested
  ([CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) is narrowed, not refuted).
- **That every industry recovers.** Single-industry tests lack power
  (p. 39).
- **That support cannot make a place.** "Yet it is a different question
  whether directed greenfield investment can permanently alter the course
  of regional development" (p. 43); the TVA ([THEORY-1461](THEORY-1461.md)) was such
  investment.
- **Why places recover.** Fundamentals, surviving land claims, labour and
  infrastructure acting as a focal point are offered, not separated.
