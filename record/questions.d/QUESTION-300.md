# QUESTION-300: Is any motivation aimed at another's good and at the good of no self, however far a self can extend to include others and groups?

## Why it is a question

The owner's position is [CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md): there is no selflessness.
People with collectivist attitudes have a larger self-identity, and
collectivism is a self-interest that respects the good of a group
identity. Three bodies of work make the position more than a slogan.

- **Inclusive selves.** Self-categorization theory treats social identity
  as a self-categorization at a more inclusive level, no less a self than
  personal identity ([THEORY-1114](../theory.d/THEORY-1114.md), from Turner et al. 1994). Related
  accounts:
  - self-expansion treats close others as included in the self (Aron et
    al., [LIT-1706](../literature.d/LIT-1706.md));
  - identity fusion treats the personal and group self as merged
    (Swann et al., [LIT-1735](../literature.d/LIT-1735.md));
  - cultural psychology finds selves construed interdependently (Markus
    and Kitayama, [LIT-1703](../literature.d/LIT-1703.md)).
- **Helping traced to oneness.** Cialdini et al. ([LIT-1728](../literature.d/LIT-1728.md)) found that
  helping tracked perceived self–other "oneness", not empathy.
- **Evolution.** Hamilton's rule ([THEORY-655](../theory.d/THEORY-655.md)) has organisms valuing a
  relative's fitness by relatedness: an evolutionary analogue of a self
  that extends to kin.

Two things keep it open:

- **Empirical.** Batson et al. ([LIT-1700](../literature.d/LIT-1700.md)) report that empathy-induced
  helping survives control for self–other merging ([CLAIM-494](../claims.d/CLAIM-494.md)).
- **Conceptual.** If "self" means whatever one cares about, the thesis
  cannot be false ([CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)).

Two further positions cut across the dichotomy:

- **Team reasoning** (Sugden, [LIT-1782](../literature.d/LIT-1782.md)) makes "what is good for us" a
  mode of reasoning that is neither self-interest nor altruism.
- **Johnston** ([LIT-1759](../literature.d/LIT-1759.md)) runs the thesis in reverse: if the self is
  as large as what one identifies with, the good person's self is
  humanity.

The question also touches the phenomenology of the "we" the owner
mentioned:

- Zahavi ([LIT-1758](../literature.d/LIT-1758.md)) on how one comes to see oneself as one of us;
- León ([THEORY-1115](../theory.d/THEORY-1115.md)) on a "we" built by individualization, not
  depersonalization;
- Parfit's question of what matters in survival ([THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md)).

## What would count as an answer

- **A criterion of the self's extent that can come apart from caring.**
  Candidates: perceived overlap (Aron's inclusion-of-other-in-self scale),
  fusion, salient self-categorization, or relatedness in Hamilton's sense.
- **For a yes:** evidence that concern for another's welfare motivates
  action when that criterion shows no extension of the self to the other.
- **For a no:** evidence that every such motivation disappears when the
  extension is controlled.
