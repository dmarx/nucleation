# LIT-1596: Genome sequence of the endocellular bacterial symbiont of aphids Buchnera sp. APS

Shigenobu, S., Watanabe, H., Hattori, M., Sakaki, Y. & Ishikawa, H. (2000). Genome sequence of the endocellular bacterial symbiont of aphids *Buchnera* sp. APS. *Nature* 407(6800): 81–86. doi:10.1038/35024074.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1236](../notes.d/NOTE-1236.md).*

- **A small genome by loss.** 640,681 bp, 583 ORFs, 26.3% G+C. The gene
  set is a subset of the ancestor shared with *E. coli* and
  *H. influenzae*, with no duplications of its own.
- **Complementary and interlocked.** Genes for the amino acids the aphid
  cannot make are kept; those for the ones it can are almost all gone,
  cysteine excepted. Host-made glutamate and aspartate are precursors of
  the symbiont's products ([THEORY-1103](../theory.d/THEORY-1103.md)).
- **Self-maintenance lost.** No phospholipid synthesis except cls, almost
  no LPS genes, few transporters, no recA or uvrABC, and no
  transcriptional regulators except dnaA, though the genes they
  regulated are kept.
- **Organelle-like, by inference.** The authors propose that the host
  supplies phospholipid or the enzymes to make it, "like mitochondria
  do". Nothing is tested.
- **What it does not show.** That the host cannot reproduce without
  *Buchnera* (cited, not shown), or how the genome shrank: drift is not
  mentioned.

## Why this is here

A1 proposed a CASE for aphid–*Buchnera*, the obligate insect–bacterial association, and the record held *Buchnera* only through [NOTE-114](../notes.d/NOTE-114.md). This is the founding genome paper, registered as the primary source for [CASE-173](../cases.d/CASE-173.md). The paper's opening sentence says the mutualism "is so obligate that neither can reproduce independently", citing earlier work. That is mutual dependence in the sense of [LIT-1570](LIT-1570.md), asserted here rather than shown: the genome shows the symbiont's side only.
