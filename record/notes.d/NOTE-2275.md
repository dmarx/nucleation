# NOTE-2275: Martínez-Sánchez, Arranz & Lozano-Durán — Decomposing causality into its synergistic, unique, and redundant components

## Contribution

A decomposition of the information about a target's future, in the style
of partial information decomposition, with 2^N terms for N sources (SURD
"produces only 512 terms" for nine variables, against a Williams–Beer
lattice of "over 10^23 terms", SI §S1.2; 2⁹ = 512), all nonnegative and
adding up exactly, with a bounded leak term. Benchmarked against
conditional Granger causality, conditional transfer entropy, convergent
cross-mapping and PCMCI, then applied to two turbulent flows.

## Key insight

What the paper calls causality is predictive information, "forward-in-time
propagation of information", split by how each source shares it; and what
the observed set cannot account for is measured, not assumed away.

## Assumptions

- Causality as information: "causal relationships emerge as a result of
  transitional probabilities between states".
- "The method also assumes that the time signals are stationary".
- The leak "alleviates the need for the assumption of causal sufficiency".
- Probabilities by binning (50–100 bins per variable) or transport maps;
  results depend on the partition (SI §S4).
- Success on the benchmarks means results "consistent with the functional
  dependencies of the system, rather than on the concrete value of the
  causal strength".
- The redundancy definition is chosen for "consistency with the
  properties presented ... along with the ease of interpretability", not
  defended axiomatically (SI §S1.2).

## Key results

- **Decomposition** (p. 3, eq. 1): H(Q⁺ⱼ) is the sum of redundant, unique
  and synergistic terms plus ΔI_leak→j, which "is the causality from
  unobserved variables, referred to as the causality leak".
- **Invariance** (SI §S1.4): the components "are invariant under
  invertible transformations of Q".
- **Energy cascade** (pp. 8–9, Fig. 6): the variables are "the time
  signals of the volume-averaged energy transfer" at filter widths 163η,
  81η, 42η and 21η of a 1024³ simulation of forced isotropic turbulence.
  "The unique causalities ... vividly capture the forward energy cascade
  of causality from large to smaller scales"; "no unique causality is
  observed from smaller to larger scales, and any causality from the
  backward cascade arises solely through redundant relationships"; read as
  "no new information is conveyed from the smaller scales to the larger
  scales", which "justifies the success of subgrid-scale modeling".
- **Leaks** (p. 9): "approximately 47% of the causality" for Σ2 is carried
  by variables outside the four; Σ1 has the "smallest leak of 14%", its
  future "mostly determined by its own past".
- **Rivals** (p. 9): CGC and CCM "do not support the hypothesis of a
  forward energy cascade"; CTE and PCMCI agree in direction, weakly.
- **Boundary layer** (p. 10): results "distinctly support the prevalence of
  top-down" interactions, while the leak "is 99% for both" signals.
- **Lags** (p. 15): each target's lag "was determined as the time required
  for maximum" cross unique causality.

## Concepts

- **Redundant, unique and synergistic causality**; **causality leak**.
- **Specific mutual information**, per state of the target, sorted and
  differenced to define the components.
- **Interscale energy flux** Σ̃ = τᵢⱼS̄ᵢⱼ, a physical transfer, whose time
  series are SURD's variables.

## Connections

The rival decomposition is Williams and Beer's ([LIT-1172](../literature.d/LIT-1172.md)), which the paper
names as its main foil. Its redundancy, taken per target state from the
sorted singleton specific informations, has the shape of Williams and
Beer's I_min; that is this filing's reading of SI step 3, not the paper's
statement. It is in the family of predictive measures relative to an
information set held in [SYNTHESIS-073](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-073.md), [TERM-057](../terms.d/TERM-057.md) and [TERM-058](../terms.d/TERM-058.md). Other rival
decompositions the record holds, [LIT-1162](../literature.d/LIT-1162.md) and [LIT-042](../literature.d/LIT-042.md), are background.

## Limitations

- The cascade result rests on four volume-averaged scalars from one
  simulation; local backscatter that cancels in the average cannot show.
- Each lag is chosen to maximize cross unique causality, a selection that
  favours finding cross-scale unique terms.
- Leaks of 47% (Σ2) and 99% (boundary layer); the authors' own discussion
  says a link "would still be deemed insignificant" at a 99% leak.
- Observational and stationary: no interventions.
- One redundancy function among many, not defended axiomatically; results
  depend on the estimator.
