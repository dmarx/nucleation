# LIT-tmpreyek: Remix: Making Art and Commerce Thrive in the Hybrid Economy

Lessig, L. (2008). *Remix: Making Art and Commerce Thrive in the Hybrid Economy*. New York: The Penguin Press; London: Bloomsbury Academic (CC BY-NC).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmptp3k0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptp3k0.md).*

- **Two cultures, by degree.** Read/Only culture is consumed; Read/Write
  culture is remade by those who consume it. The terms are borrowed from
  file permissions (p. 28). The twentieth century was the first in which
  popular culture was professionalized, and digital tools revive RW. Lessig
  says the difference is one of weight, not kind (pp. 28–29).
- **Remix needs the original.** Remix is collage. Its meaning comes from
  the reference, which only the original can carry, so a re-creation
  cannot replace the quoted material (pp. 74–76). Only the technique and the ease of sharing are new (p. 82).
- **The copy trigger.** Copyright regulates copies, and every digital use
  makes one. So the law reaches every ordinary use, and RW culture is
  "presumptively illegal" (pp. 98–103). The 1909 move from "publishing"
  to "copies" made that possible.
- **Three economies.** In a commercial economy money is a normal term of
  exchange. In a sharing economy it is the one inappropriate term, and
  adding it can crowd sharing out. A hybrid joins the two and lasts only
  while the two stay distinct (chs. 6–7).
- **Five reforms, and one of norms.** Free amateur remix; register after
  an initial term; simplify; stop regulating the copy as such; decriminalize
  file sharing by a levy or a blanket licence (ch. 9). Creative Commons is
  presented as the voluntary version (ch. 10).
