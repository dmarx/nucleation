# THEORY-tmp1vsf5: Zhang and colleagues (2026) report that when coding tasks are split among specialist LLM agents, task-level test pass rates can be high while the cross-agent dependencies stay unresolved (32 of 190 outcomes passed at least 80% of tests while resolving fewer than half their dependencies, none the reverse), that stronger coding models do not reliably collaborate better, and that no coordination protocol improves dependency resolution for every model. 2's 32-of-190 sentence and the abstract's and §5.4's statements against the text

## The account

Zhang and colleagues (2026) report that when coding tasks are split among
specialist LLM agents, task-level test pass rates can be high while the
cross-agent dependencies stay unresolved (32 of 190 outcomes passed at least
80% of tests while resolving fewer than half their dependencies, none the
reverse), that stronger coding models do not reliably collaborate better, and
that no coordination protocol improves dependency resolution for every model.

2's 32-of-190 sentence and the
abstract's and §5.4's statements against the text.
