  is bistable with an energy landscape fixed at fabrication. Springs added
  along the axis, about it, or along the mountain creases prestress the
  cell ("geometrical frustration", global or crease-level), move its first
  stable state to a nonzero base energy, tune its energy barrier
  continuously and can switch it from bistable to monostable. In a stack of
  three cells with 8 stable states and 56 ordered transitions, 16 need
  intermediate states and 8 more are blocked by the ordering of the cells'
  barriers, leaving 32 feasible; prestressing reorders the barriers in
  place and makes the 8 blocked transitions feasible. Also shown:
  frustration-dependent history effects under twist, a frustrated
  curved-crease tube, and a shape-morphing truss.

# LIT-tmprldni: Origami frustration and its influence on energy landscapes of origami assemblies

Zang, S., Zhao, T., Misseroni, D. & Paulino, G. H. (2025). Origami frustration and its influence on energy landscapes of origami assemblies. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 122(36): e2426790122. doi:10.1073/pnas.2426790122.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmp7wdij. Sections are the paper's.*

- **A fixed landscape, made tunable from outside.** "standard Kresling
  origami cells exhibit nontunable intrinsic energy landscapes determined
  by their geometry and material properties, limiting their adaptability
  after fabrication." Prestressing springs fix that: "we induce either
  global or localized (i.e., crease level) frustration, which allows
  changing the energy barrier (cell or assembly)." (Abstract.)
- **Bistable to monostable.** "by controlling the level of prestress in the
  negative mode, we can switch the instability behavior from bistable to
  monostable" (Results, Parametric study).
- **Eight of 56 paths opened.** "only thirty-two out of fifty-six folding
  paths are feasible" in the standard stack; the frustrated stack "enables
  the eight previously infeasible folding paths of Fig. 4C to be achieved
  as feasible folding paths in Fig. 5B." The 16 that need intermediate
  states are not among them (Experimental Study on Assemblies).
- **In place, not by redesign.** Changing the cells instead "inevitably
  introduces new infeasible folding paths and does not support in-situ
  reconfiguration."

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures cites it as O6, for
adjustable prestress in Kresling assemblies that changed energy barriers
and folding sequences and made previously inaccessible paths available.
That is right. A's "localized or global deformation modes" misplaces the
words: in the paper "localized or global" says where the prestress sits.
And "previously inaccessible" means eight of 56 transitions, those blocked
by the ordering of the barriers. The paper's "frustration" is stored
elastic energy from prestress applied by springs and handles, the
energetic sense that TERM-060 sets apart from its own. So it bears out A's
point that mechanical and structural frustration are "not interchangeable".
