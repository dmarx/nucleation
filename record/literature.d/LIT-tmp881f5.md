# LIT-tmp881f5: International Handbook of Multigenerational Legacies of Trauma

Danieli, Y. (Ed.) (1998). *International Handbook of Multigenerational Legacies of Trauma*. New York: Plenum Press.

Not read. The summary is drawn from the abstract or from what the work is
standardly cited for. A reading should check it first.

## Why this is here

The clinical tradition that named the phenomenon, before the epigenetic turn. For [QUESTION-tmp8bttd](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp8bttd.md).

## Read in its place

The work cannot be read here, so the record holds it at second hand.

Danieli, Norris and Engdahl 2016 (NOTE-tmptxas9) operationalize the
handbook's framework, TCMI, which Danieli set out in its introduction (pp.
1–17). The paper restates its core: identity as many spheres continuous in
time, trauma as a rupture producing "fixity", the conspiracy of silence,
continuity against rupture, and survivors' victim, numb and fighter
styles. It tests them on 422 survivors' children. It cites three of the
handbook's chapters (Auerhahn and Laub, Felsen, Solomon). At second hand,
Engdahl's ISTSS review of the handbook (Engdahl is a co-author here, so
not independent) lists its populations and reports a chapter by Suomi and
Levine naming three routes in animals: observational learning, parental
behaviour and prenatal effects. Missing still: the handbook's own text;
Danieli's introduction in her words; any chapter's data; the non-Holocaust
populations; and the chapter on animal routes. The review does not list
Japanese-American internees, whom the LIT's summary names.
