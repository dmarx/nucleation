On an aggregative, undiscounted view, each second's delay in settling the Virgo Supercluster loses the potential for about 10^29 human lives if minds can run on computers, or 10^13 if they must be biological. Yet the same arithmetic makes safety, not speed, the total utilitarian's priority: a one-percentage-point cut in existential risk is worth a delay of over 10 million years, so "Maximize expected aggregate utility!" becomes "Minimize existential risk!". On a person-affecting view, which counts only people who exist or will exist, extinction is bad for what it does to people now alive. Then, given a non-negligible chance that some of them live to share the cosmic benefits through life extension, existential safety is very important but must be balanced against speed.
