# QUESTION-tmpkqvxv: How does evidence of what an arrangement can feasibly realize bear on which principles governing it can reasonably be rejected: can expanding what it can realize change which principles are justifiable, and do mistaken beliefs about feasibility distort that judgment?

## Why it is a question

A100 §8 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b) splits
two problems. Justificatory compatibility asks which principles survive
Scanlon's test of reasonable rejection, which A calls "a schematic
reconstruction, not an algorithm supplied by Scanlon". Resource-bounded
compatibility asks which demands derived from a chosen principle P can be
met together:

    K_P(G; b) = { S ⊆ D(P) : ∃ h ∈ H_feas(G; b) ∩ H_P ∩ ⋂_{d_i ∈ S} A_i }.

A then denies that the order runs one way:

> Information about feasibility can change which governing principles people have sufficient reasons to accept or reject.

"Suppose one principle requires an expensive institution that cannot
realistically be sustained. A proposed alternative may protect the same
important interests at much lower cost. That matters to reasonable
rejection. Likewise, demonstrating that a rights-protecting reform is
genuinely feasible can weaken objections based on its supposed
impossibility." And: "The existence of a feasible institutional
arrangement does not prove its legitimacy." A100 §13 states the research
question: "Do beliefs about institutional feasibility distort assessments
of what burdens people can reasonably be asked to bear? Can constructive
capability expansion change which principles become justifiable?"

The record's apparatus takes the standard as input. [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md),
[CLAIM-586](../claims.d/CLAIM-586.md) and [CLAIM-560](../claims.d/CLAIM-560.md) say what can be realized and reached under fixed
demands; [CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md) insists the demands stay fixed while relief is judged.
None says whether, or how, what is realizable should change which demands
are fixed in the first place. [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) blocks one direction (viability
does not establish what ought to persist); this question asks about the
other.

## A case it can be put to

The aquifer, [CASE-tmp9vd9x](../cases.d/CASE-tmp9vd9x.md): without recycling the two households' basic
requirements and the sustainable yield cannot all be met; with it they
can. A100 §10 compares three principles for the technology (unrestricted
exclusion, compulsory uncompensated access, protected access with
compensation) and notes that if no arrangement is feasible, "that fact may
call for revisiting the principles governing how unavoidable scarcity is
shared". Whether the recycling changes which principle could reasonably
be rejected, and not only which demands can be met, is this question in
miniature.

## What would count as an answer

- **A dependence shown.** An argument that the reasons available for
  rejecting a principle include the costs of realizing it, so that a
  change in what can be realized changes which principles survive; with a
  case in which the same parties, reasons and interests yield different
  verdicts at two levels of capability.
- **An independence shown.** An argument that feasibility bears only on
  which implementation of a justified principle is required (an
  "ought implies can" filter on demands), never on the principle, so the
  feedback A proposes runs only from principle to demand.
- **A distortion test.** A way to tell a rejection based on a mistaken
  belief about feasibility from one based on correct belief, and what
  the record's reachability results ([CLAIM-560](../claims.d/CLAIM-560.md)) would contribute to
  correcting it.

It sits beside [QUESTION-tmpk7u3e](QUESTION-tmpk7u3e.md), which asks whether the record's account
answers contractualism or only supplements it.
