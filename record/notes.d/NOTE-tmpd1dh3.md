# NOTE-tmpd1dh3: Empirical Approaches to Altruism

## Contribution

A reference survey of the empirical case for psychological altruism. It
fixes the target with the standard account: altruism is an ultimate
desire for another's well-being, and egoism says all ultimate desires are
self-interested. It sets aside the biological and social-science senses
of the word. It then takes Batson's empathy–altruism programme as the
best empirical work, and reports what it has and has not ruled out. For
the record, its value is §5.3. That section states the self-other merging
challenge, quotes the measures both 1997 papers used, and argues that
none of them measures what the challenge needs.

## Key insight

The relevant identity is numerical, not qualitative. Saving for one's own
old age is egoistic because old Sophia *is* young Sophia, not because she
resembles her (§5.3). So a merging test matters for egoism only if it
shows that A takes herself to be numerically identical with B. Perceived
similarity and overlap in trait ratings measure likeness. "It is far from
clear what the other two tests [IOS and use of 'we'] are measuring"
(§5.3). The authors conclude that the merging literature gives no
evidence that people lose the distinction between themselves and others.
Its remaining challenge is causal: whether empathy or "merging" drives
the helping.

## Assumptions

- **The standard account** (§2). Ultimate desires are those not produced
  by practical reasoning from other desires. Egoism and altruism are
  claims about them. Some desires are neither: revenge, duty, preserving
  art.
- **Numerical identity is the self's extent that matters** (§5.3). The
  authors give no argument that an *extended self-concept* short of
  identity could make a desire self-interested. They do not discuss it.
- **Batson's conception of empathy**: "an other-oriented emotional
  reaction to seeing someone suffer" (Batson 1991: 58, quoted in §5),
  distinct from self-oriented personal distress.

## Key results

- **Egoism–altruism and evolution** (§3). Biological altruism lowers the
  actor's fitness. It neither entails nor is entailed by psychological
  altruism (Sober and Wilson 1998). Piccinini and Schulz (2019) add a
  second axis, how a desire was produced. An ultimate desire for another's
  good that was acquired by reward learning is altruistic only
  "non-classically".
- **Social punishment** (§5.1; Fultz et al. 1986, Study 2). Lonely "Janet";
  empathy by perspective-taking instructions; anonymity versus scrutiny.
  Mean time offered, as the table prints it: high scrutiny 0.67 (low
  empathy) and 1.71 (high empathy); low scrutiny 1.29 and 2.44. High
  empathy raised helping under both, against the
  social-evaluation hypothesis.
- **Aversive-arousal reduction** (§5.2; Batson, Duncan et al. 1981,
  Experiment 1). Taking "Elaine's" shocks. Proportion helping: easy
  escape 0.18 (low empathy) and 0.91 (high empathy); difficult escape 0.64
  and 0.82. Empathy was manipulated by similarity of values. The same
  pattern held across six experiments, and broke only when the shocks were
  "clearly painful". Stocks et al. (2009) manipulated the ease of
  *psychological* escape and found empathy still predicted helping.
- **Self-other merging** (§5.3).
  - The challenge, after Aron and Lerner: in closeness, perspective-taking
    or empathy, the self–other boundary may vanish. Batson concedes that
    if it does, "so does the distinction between altruism and egoism"
    (2011: 148, quoted).
  - Cialdini et al.'s oneness index (1997: 484, quoted): the IOS scale of
    seven increasingly overlapping circle pairs, plus a 7-point item on
    how far one would use "we" of the relationship.
  - Batson, Sager et al.'s three measures: the IOS scale; perceived
    similarity, "How similar to you do you think the person …is?" (1 =
    somewhat, 9 = extremely; 1997: 500, quoted); and the mean absolute
    difference between self- and other-ratings on personal attributes
    (1997: 498, quoted).
  - The verdict: none of the four tests shows perceived numerical
    identity. Someone who believed he existed in two bodies would be
    delusional (May 2011b: 32, as cited).
  - The causal dispute: Cialdini et al. report three studies in which
    empathy plays no causal role and "merging", "a label for whatever the
    IOS scale and the use-of-*we* test measure", drives helping. Batson et
    al. "pointed out a number of methodological problems", and "when they
    conducted a pair of experiments that avoided these methodological
    problems, the role of empathy in producing helping behavior was
    clearly evident". The entry names neither the problems nor the
    numbers.
  - Robustness: the studies are small. McAuliffe et al. (2018), N = 680
    online, preregistered, were "unambiguously supportive" (2018: 504, as
    quoted) of the empathy–helping link. Note 16 adds that their evidence
    suggests perspective-taking instructions may not raise empathy;
    "remain objective" instructions lower it.
- **What is still open** (§§5.4, 6 [printed as 7], 8).
  - A divine punishment hypothesis: secrecy does not hide a choice from an
    omniscient God, so the Janet studies cannot exclude it.
  - Principlism, an ultimate desire to uphold a moral principle, which
    Batson grants is untested.
  - Ultimate desires to comply with any culturally acquired norm.
  - Habitual or Pavlovian helping, not driven by ultimate desires at all
    (Gęsiarz and Crockett 2015).
- **Development** (§7). Eighteen-month-olds help without reward, sometimes
  at a cost, and rewards can crowd it out (Warneken and Tomasello). These
  studies control egoistic confounds far less than Batson's.

## Concepts

- **standard account**: altruism is an ultimate desire for another's
  well-being; egoism, that every ultimate desire is self-interested.
- **ultimate desire**: a desire not produced or sustained by practical
  reasoning from other desires.
- **numerical versus qualitative identity**: being the same thing, versus
  being alike.
- **self-other merging**: the hypothesis that in closeness or empathy the
  conceptual boundary between self and other disappears.
- **principlism**: motivation with the ultimate goal of upholding a moral
  principle (Batson 2011: 220, quoted).
- **classical versus non-classical altruism** (Piccinini and Schulz): an
  ultimate desire for another's good from an altruistic or neutral
  mechanism, versus from an evolutionarily selfish one such as reward
  learning.

## Bearing on the record

- **Batson's objection (CLAIM-tmpybc4k).** The entry is the record's
  first independent report of how the 1997 exchange came out. It sides
  with Batson. But it gives the result in one sentence, with no design or
  numbers, so it does not let the record check the claim. One
  discrepancy needs checking: LIT-tmp7qbeb's summary and CLAIM-tmpybc4k
  say "four experiments", and the entry says Batson et al. "conducted a
  pair of experiments". The claim's wording should not repeat "four" until
  the paper is read.
- **The owner's thesis (CLAIM-tmpjylq8).** §5.3 bears on it at two
  points. These are inferences; the entry does not discuss the thesis.
  - It rules out the strongest version of the thesis. If "a self that
    includes the other" means that one takes oneself to *be* the other,
    nobody normal does, and the measures do not show it.
  - It leaves the thesis's own version unexamined. The thesis says the
    *self-identification* extends, not that the helper is numerically one
    with the helped. The entry's test of relevance, numerical identity,
    assumes that only one's own numerically identical person can be the
    object of a self-interested desire. The thesis denies exactly that, so
    the entry does not refute it. It does show that the thesis must say
    why an extended self-concept counts as "self" for the purpose of
    interest, since Sophia's case gets its force from identity.
- **The triviality objection (CLAIM-tmprudek).** The entry supplies one
  criterion of the self's extent that plainly comes apart from caring,
  numerical identity. It is too strong for the thesis to use. It calls the
  IOS scale and the "we" item, the criteria the thesis and its objection
  both name, unclear in content. So it sharpens the objection: the record
  holds no validated criterion of extension between identity and caring.
- **What happened when Batson controlled for merging.** From this entry:
  three merging measures, including Cialdini's IOS scale; correction of
  unnamed methodological problems; empathy's role "clearly evident".
  Nothing more. The question stays open until LIT-tmp7qbeb is read or a
  fuller secondary account is found. Miyazono and Inarimori point to
  Batson 2018, chapter 10, for the methodological problems.
- **A dated note to CLAIM-tmpybc4k is proposed:**

## Limitations

- **A survey.** It reports results second-hand and selectively. For the
  1997 exchange it gives measures, not findings.
- **No pagination.** Citations are to sections.
- **The numerical-identity argument rests on one example** and does not
  consider graded or extended self-concepts.
- **The main page and the notes were saved separately.** The scratchpad
  copy lacked the notes.
- **Typos and slips.**
  - "Cialdini et al. (1977)" for 1997 (§5.3).
  - "Aron et al. 1980: 242" for what is probably 1991 (§5.3).
  - The section after §5.4 is headed "7. Beyond Egoism vs. Altruism"
    though the contents list it as 6, so there are two §7s.
  - §3 refers twice to "table 1" for Piccinini and Schulz's framework. No
    such table is in the entry; its Table 1 is the Fultz predictions.
  - "the well-being of the that person" (§2); "reassuring in this respect
    to" and "that that emerged" (§5.3).
  - In-text "Sripada & Stich 2006" against a bibliography entry of 2007.
    Note 21 cites "Stich & Donaldson 2019" against a bibliography entry of
    2018. Note 3 cites "Mill 1861 [2001" against 1861 [2011].
  - Note 21 discusses "the Demjanuk case", which the main text no longer
    mentions. It is a remnant of an earlier version.
  - In Table 3 the low-scrutiny cells exceed the high-scrutiny ones in
    both empathy conditions. Neither hypothesis predicts that, so the row
    labels may be swapped. The table is reported here as printed; the
    empathy effect holds either way.
  - "PhilPapers.ord" under Other Internet Resources.

## Open questions

- What were the methodological problems Batson et al. found, and how did
  their two experiments control merging: statistically, by manipulation,
  or both? Batson 2018, chapter 10, may say.
- Is there a criterion of self-extension between numerical identity and
  caring that bears on whether a desire is self-interested? Neither this
  entry nor May (as reported) considers one.
