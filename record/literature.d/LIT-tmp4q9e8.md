# LIT-tmp4q9e8: From gut microbiota to host appetite: gut microbiota-derived metabolites as key regulators

Hui Han et al. (2021), *Microbiome* 9: 162 — [doi:10.1186/s40168-021-01093-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-021-01093-y)

## Key takeaways

*Read in full; see [NOTE-tmplvb3o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplvb3o.md).*

- **Mechanisms are many and mostly rodent.** SCFAs, bile acids, indoles and
  bacterial proteins act on gut hormones (GLP-1, PYY, ghrelin, leptin) and
  on the hypothalamus.
- **Bacteria make look-alikes of host appetite peptides.** "ClpB, the best
  studied bacterial protein, can act as a mimetic of alpha-melanocyte-
  stimulating hormone (α-MSH) to result in similar anorexigenic effects".
- **The human evidence is thin, by the authors' own statement.** "To date,
  our understanding of the gut microbiota roles in modulating appetite is
  mainly based on in vitro studies and rodent models."
