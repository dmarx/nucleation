# TERM-101: grant, between an organization's processes: a conferral that runs through a maintained relation of granting, delegation, eligibility or support, so that the effect on another's future influence is mediated by a relation the organization keeps; withdrawing it is revocation

## Definition

From A14 §4, quoted in [TERM-053](TERM-053.md)'s note of 2026-11-04: "A positive
influence derivative alone would establish facilitation, but not
necessarily endorsement." "We should require some evidence that the causal
effect is mediated by a maintained relation of granting, delegation,
eligibility, or support before interpreting it as endorsement." What A14
called endorsement is here a grant.

i grants j influence in region x when i confers influence on j there
([TERM-103](TERM-103.md)) and the effect runs through a relation R_ij that the
organization maintains: a delegation, an eligibility rule, a standing
support. Revoking R_ij is negative grant, revocation. The relation, not the
effect alone, is what makes it a grant, so a grant can be identified by
intervening on the relation and seeing the conferral go.

## What it is not

- **Not every conferral.** Suppressing j's rivals confers influence on j
  and grants j nothing.
- **Not the criterion for granting.** [CLAIM-552](../claims.d/CLAIM-552.md) holds the criterion,
  a perspective's tolerance for another's contribution, and separates
  permissibility, the grant and its effect. Meeting the criterion is not
  granting, and a grant need not raise j's influence if the rule does not
  carry it ([QUESTION-270](../questions.d/QUESTION-270.md)'s denied uptake; [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md)'s precedence claimed
  against authority granted).
- **Not endorsement in Frankfurt's sense** ([TERM-102](TERM-102.md)), though a
  reflective attitude may be what maintains the relation.
