# NOTE-1969: Seguin et al. — Connectome communication and the spread of direct electrical stimulation

## Contribution

A large causal test of connectome communication models against measured,
millisecond-scale signal spread, as opposed to correlational BOLD.

## Key insight

Stimulation spreads as if by diffusion along the connectome, not along
shortest paths. Much of it is still explained by plain distance.

## Assumptions

CCEPs from epilepsy patients stand for physiological signalling. One
normative connectome (1,000 HCP subjects) for all patients.
Intra-hemispheric pairs only. A response counts at z > 5 within 800 ms.

## Key results

- **Data** (Results): "550 participants, 29,055 stimulations and 2.77
  million pairs of recording electrodes".
- **Diffusion wins** (Results): "search information consistently ranked as
  the most explanatory network measure of response probability and
  amplitude"; between unconnected regions "search information and
  communicability markedly outperformed alternative network measures and
  ED".
- **Indirect paths count even where a direct one exists**: the top measures
  "surpassed the explanatory power of SC weights even for associations
  computed exclusively for pairs of anatomically connected regions".
- **Prediction**: "Euclidean distance (40.2% average contribution), search
  information (32.5%) and FC (24.6%) explained R2 = 54% of out-of-sample
  variance in response probability"; R² = 31% for amplitude.

## Concepts

Structural connectome; routing–diffusion spectrum; search information;
communicability; CCEP; F-TRACT.

## Bearing on the record

No held entry. The measured pairwise responses include indirect paths.
They are the macroscale empirical counterpart of Randi & Leifer's
connected response functions ([LIT-2615](../literature.d/LIT-2615.md)). A's counts are exact; the
venue is Neuron, not Nature Neuroscience.

## Limitations

Preprint, not the version of record. Supplementary figures, including the
null-network controls, not seen.
