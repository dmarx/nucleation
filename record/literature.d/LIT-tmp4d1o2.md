  essay asking whether large aid sustained over decades weakens the
  recipient state's institutions, as resource rents do. It reviews four
  channels: macroeconomic and fiscal effects, including the soft budget
  constraint; donor practice, whose fragmentation and parallel structures
  draw staff and attention from the civil service; aid substituting for
  taxation, where the literature is negative on balance but "not a
  conclusive result", with grants and not loans associated with lower
  revenue; and accountability turned toward donors rather than citizens.
  Aid was meant as a temporary subsidy for revenue collection and
  institution building; in some three dozen countries it has stayed at a
  tenth of GNI or more for two decades. Following Kaldor, a state moves
  from aid dependency to self-sufficiency by learning to tax. They commit
  to no threshold, relaying Berg's 5 % and Clemens and Radelet's 15–45 %
  of GDP.

# LIT-tmp4d1o2: An Aid-Institutions Paradox? A Review Essay on Aid Dependency and State Building in Sub-Saharan Africa

Moss, T., Pettersson, G. & van de Walle, N. (2006). An Aid-Institutions Paradox? A Review Essay on Aid Dependency and State Building in Sub-Saharan Africa. Center for Global Development Working Paper 74, January 2006. https://www.cgdev.org/sites/default/files/5646_file_WP_74.pdf. Also on SSRN, doi:10.2139/ssrn.860826.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpd45i4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpd45i4.md). Page numbers are the paper's.*

- **Meant to be temporary.** Aid "is supposed to provide temporary
  financial assistance in order to encourage certain long-term behaviors:
  revenue collection, investment in physical and human capital, and the
  establishment of the institutions of a developmental state" (p. 5).
- **Not tapering.** "a core set of roughly three dozen countries that have
  received a tenth of GNI or more in aid for at least the last two
  decades" (p. 3), against a Marshall Plan that "lasted only a few years
  and never exceeded 3 percent of GDP".
- **The route out.** For Kaldor, "the key determination of whether a state
  moves from aid dependency to economic self-sufficiency is the degree to
  which the state learns how to tax" (p. 10).
- **The evidence.** "Generally, thus, the literature finds a negative
  relationship between aid and revenue collection, but this is not a
  conclusive result." (p. 13) Grants lower revenue and loans raise it
  (Gupta et al. 2004).
- **Moral hazard.** "the flow of revenues to the state is not affected by
  government efficiency, so there will be a tendency for governments to
  underinvest in developmental capacity." (p. 15)
