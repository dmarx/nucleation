# QUESTION-tmpepdzg: Does categorical compositional semantics constrain transport between context-indexed empirical models, so that how a whole utterance's observables are carried is fixed by how its parts' are, and does the constraint reach past propositional content?

## Why it is a question

Two clusters in the record treat meaning categorically and do not meet.

- **Composition.** DisCoCat ([LIT-273](../literature.d/LIT-273.md), read in [NOTE-245](../notes.d/NOTE-245.md)) composes word
  meanings along a pregroup parse into a sentence meaning. The Frobenius
  version ([LIT-272](../literature.d/LIT-272.md), [NOTE-246](../notes.d/NOTE-246.md)) gives relational words copy maps. Both
  are maps from a grammatical structure to vector spaces. Neither says
  anything about context of use or about carrying a meaning into another
  medium.
- **Transport.** The manuscript's transport carries a context-indexed
  empirical model from one observational scenario to another
  ([CLAIM-125](../claims.d/CLAIM-125.md)). It treats an utterance's observables as given, not as built
  from the observables of its parts.

The review that raised this put it as: "different constructions, but their
categorical treatment of composition may help formalize the relationship
between compositional linguistic structure and observational transport."

There are two ways the comparison could turn out, and they differ.

1. **Composition constrains transport.** A transport that respects the
   grammatical composition would be fixed on whole utterances by what it does
   on words and on the composition maps. A rendering that breaks the
   composition (a paraphrase that reparses) would then be visible as a
   failure of that respect.
2. **Composition stops at the proposition.** What DisCoCat composes is
   propositional and distributional meaning. The manuscript argues that
   preserving the proposition is not sufficient to preserve the
   communicative act ([CLAIM-061](../claims.d/CLAIM-061.md), with [CLAIM-053](../claims.d/CLAIM-053.md)'s correction). If
   footing and illocutionary force are not compositional in DisCoCat's
   sense, the constraint covers only the part the manuscript says is not
   enough.

## What would count as an answer

- **A construction.** Transport stated as a map between compositional
  models that commutes with the composition, together with a statement of
  which empirical-model properties it then preserves. The properties that
  matter are global compatibility and the noncontextual fraction
  ([THEORY-174](../theory.d/THEORY-174.md)).
- **A separation.** A pragmatic observable the manuscript uses, such as
  footing or force, shown to be non-compositional over a parse: two
  utterances with the same parts and the same composition that differ on it.
  That would confine the comparison to propositional content.

Either settles the question. A further demonstration that DisCoCat and
sheaves are both categorical does not.

## What the record holds toward an answer

- **The meeting point already exists.** Abramsky and Sadrzadeh's Semantic
  Unification ([LIT-841](../literature.d/LIT-841.md), [NOTE-644](../notes.d/NOTE-644.md)) builds a presheaf of discourse
  representations and glues sentence meanings over a cover of anaphoric
  resolutions. It is the record's earliest bridge between the
  Abramsky–Brandenburger framework and semantics. Its obstruction to gluing
  is inconsistency of literals, not contextuality, and it has no transport.
- **Contextuality in phrase meaning.** Wang et al. ([LIT-842](../literature.d/LIT-842.md), [NOTE-645](../notes.d/NOTE-645.md))
  test noun–verb phrases, which are compositional units, for contextuality.
  Their corpus estimates are almost always signalling. So the data on which a
  compositional constraint would be checked are the data [CLAIM-125](../claims.d/CLAIM-125.md)'s
  second open problem is about.
