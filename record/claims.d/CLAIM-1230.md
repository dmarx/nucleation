# CLAIM-1230: Virality and generativity come apart

## The claim

A2 §6: "This suggests a distinction between **memetic virality** and
**memetic generativity**. A viral meme produces copies; a generative meme
produces structures. The most consequential memes may not be those that
reproduce most frequently, but those around which the greatest amount of
information can coherently organize." And: "If memes function as
nucleation sites, their significance need not be proportional to their
informational complexity."

A4 §6 widens Dawkins's three properties (replicative fidelity, fecundity,
longevity; [TERM-172](../terms.d/TERM-172.md) sense 1) by six more: nucleation capacity,
generativity, structural reach, entrenchment, organizational
dispensability and corrigibility, and says "These properties should not
be treated as a hierarchy." Its examples: "A highly transmissible meme may
organize very little. A rarely transmitted meme may induce profound
organization. A meme may also successfully nucleate an organization that
subsequently eliminates it."

## Gaps

- **No measure of generativity is given,** so as stated the claim could be
  made true by definition. A4 §9 points toward one, "the organizational
  changes following its introduction, rather than merely counting
  references to it"; the defeater above requires it be fixed beforehand.
- **The non-hierarchy is asserted, not shown.** A4 supports it by
  [CLAIM-474](CLAIM-474.md), whose partial order is of a different set of achievements;
  [ARG-248](../arguments.d/ARG-248.md). Whether the nine properties are independent is an
  empirical matter for [LEAD-260](../leads.d/LEAD-260.md).
- **The replicator-side baseline** is a model of popularity under
  competition for attention, such as the one A2 cites for criticality
  ([LIT-3207](../literature.d/LIT-3207.md), unread): it predicts how often memes are copied, which is
  the side this claim holds does not fix the other.
