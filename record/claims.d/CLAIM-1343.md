# CLAIM-1343: Reliance on restraint makes departures costly

## The claim

A36 §3: "The legally permissible behavior of an institution can become
substantially broader than the behavior other institutions ordinarily
expect it to exhibit. Those other institutions can then develop their own
capacities around the expectation of restraint." §5 joins this to the
Chubby case: cultural and professional norms "constitute stored
regulatory work", and "The resulting risk is not simply that someone
might violate a norm. It is that the encompassing organization may have
ceased maintaining adequate alternatives to the norm's successful
operation."

## Not every departure is a wrong

A36 §5 sorts departures, citing Tushnet's constitutional hardball
([LIT-3236](../literature.d/LIT-3236.md), unread):

- "An action may be legally prohibited."
- "An action may be legally permitted but violate an established
  convention."
- "An action may challenge an unjustified convention."
- "An action may reveal a contingency for which no settled convention
  exists."
- "An action may establish a new operative understanding of institutional
  authority."

"Departing from precedent is not intrinsically wrongful. Some conventions
deserve to be transformed." The claim is about the vulnerability, which
holds whichever of these a departure is; whether the departure was
justified is a separate question, which A36 puts as "whether the
transition preserves or undermines justified relationships of authority,
standing and interdependence".

## The gap

A36 gives no instance in which institutions are shown to have given up an
alternative to another's restraint. The one case it works through, the
two-term tradition ([CASE-425](../cases.d/CASE-425.md)), shows a convention breaking and being
made law, not an erosion of fallbacks before the break. What the claim
needs, and the SRE case has, is the dependents' lost alternative. A
study of that is one of the six cases of [LEAD-273](../leads.d/LEAD-273.md). The section is the
assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
