# LIT-tmp8wfl8: Nonequilibrium thermodynamics and maximum entropy production in the Earth system

Axel Kleidon (2009), *Naturwissenschaften 96(6), 653–677* (review, open access) — DOI-10.1007/s00114-009-0509-x

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 with the critics of Prigogine's extremum principles and
the maximum-entropy-production literature, at the owner's request to fill
out the record's coverage of dissipative structures. The brief named
Kleidon and Lorenz's 2005 edited book, *Non-equilibrium Thermodynamics and
the Production of Entropy*, or this review if the book was unreadable. The
book has no lawful open text, so this review is filed instead, and the book
is not.

[NOTE-tmpre14j](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpre14j.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the record's statement of the MEP programme as its
advocates practise it, and of the distinction this literature draws
against Prigogine:

- Prigogine's minimum principle concerns the approach to a steady state;
- MEP chooses which of several possible steady states is realised.

It is also candid about MEP's footing. It calls the principle "proposed",
says its foundation in Dewar's work ([LIT-tmpbf3wy](LIT-tmpbf3wy.md)) is "still work in
progress", and lists the criticisms, Grinstein and Linsker ([LIT-tmpflluj](LIT-tmpflluj.md))
among them. Its account of that Comment omits the Comment's main point,
that the far-from-equilibrium derivation fails. It defers to Martyushev and
Seleznev's review ([LIT-tmp9n5sf](LIT-tmp9n5sf.md)) for MEP's physical foundation; the record
could not read that review.

Its second half is the bridge to the life-as-dissipation literature, which
another reading on 2026-10-02 is filing. It argues that biotic activity
raises planetary entropy production by enhancing the absorption of sunlight.
On that view, homeostasis of the kind the Gaia hypothesis postulates would
follow from MEP at the planetary scale. That puts living systems on the
dissipative-structure side of the demarcation dispute Nahas and Sachs
describe ([LIT-192](LIT-192.md), [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)), and treats life as the most effective of
Earth's dissipative processes, not as a different kind of system. The
"Dissipative Self-Organizing Systems" cluster of definitions of life in
[LIT-211](LIT-211.md) is this view's home among definitions. The review gives no
criterion separating a living dissipative system from a convection cell,
and does not try to.
