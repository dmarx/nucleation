# NOTE-tmpupv4l: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure — Rules 11, 26 and 37

## Contribution

The positive-law guard in US federal civil procedure against filings and
discovery used to harass, delay or raise an opponent's costs. Proportionality
makes the parties' resources and the importance of the issues part of the
scope of discovery.

## Key results

- **Rule 11(b)(1)**: a paper must not be "presented for any improper
  purpose, such as to harass, cause unnecessary delay, or needlessly
  increase the cost of litigation".
- **Rule 11(c)(4)**: "A sanction imposed under this rule must be limited to
  what suffices to deter repetition of the conduct or comparable conduct
  by others similarly situated." Rule 11 sanctions are discretionary
  ("may"), after notice, with a 21-day safe harbor for motions.
- **Rule 11(d)**: "This rule does not apply to disclosures and discovery
  requests, responses, objections, and motions under Rules 26 through 37."
- **Rule 26(b)(1)**: discovery "proportional to the needs of the case,
  considering the importance of the issues at stake in the action, the
  amount in controversy, the parties' relative access to relevant
  information, the parties' resources, the importance of the discovery in
  resolving the issues, and whether the burden or expense of the proposed
  discovery outweighs its likely benefit."
- **Rule 26(g)**: a discovery request is certified "not interposed for any
  improper purpose, such as to harass, cause unnecessary delay, or
  needlessly increase the cost of litigation" and "neither unreasonable
  nor unduly burdensome or expensive"; for a violation the court "must
  impose an appropriate sanction".
- **2015 Committee Note**, quoting 1983: the standards apply "in an
  even-handed manner that will prevent use of discovery to wage a war of
  attrition or as a device to coerce a party, whether financially weak or
  affluent." Resources cut both ways: "consideration of the parties'
  resources does not foreclose discovery requests addressed to an
  impecunious party, nor justify unlimited discovery requests addressed to
  a wealthy party." The burden "lies heavier on the party who has more
  information, and properly so."
- **Rule 37(b)(2)(A)(v)–(vi)**: sanctions include "dismissing the action or
  proceeding in whole or in part" and "rendering a default judgment against
  the disobedient party".

## Assumptions

Federal civil actions only. The guard works through judicial discretion
and through motions by the opposing party, which cost money themselves.
Rule 11 is discretionary; Rule 26(g) and Rule 37(a)(5) are mandatory.

## Concepts

Improper purpose; certification by signature; safe harbor; sanction limited
to deterrence; proportionality; the parties' resources; protective order;
motion to compel; expense-shifting; default as a sanction.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-748](../claims.d/CLAIM-748.md)** (litigation as siege). The law names the claim's
  tactics as improper purposes, "harass, cause unnecessary delay, or
  needlessly increase the cost of litigation", and the Committee Note names
  "a war of attrition": the mechanism is recognized as possible and as
  wrongful. The guard is partial: Rule 11 reaches papers only, delay by
  means within the rules is reached only where a paper's purpose is
  improper, and enforcement needs a motion and the court's discretion. Its
  effect in practice is not shown by the texts.
- **[QUESTION-479](../questions.d/QUESTION-479.md).** A partial answer in positive law: in US federal
  civil litigation a party may not raise the other's costs through papers
  or discovery for an improper purpose, and "whether financially weak or
  affluent" makes the limit bind both sides.
- **[CASE-287](../cases.d/CASE-287.md).** Proportionality's resource factor corresponds to the
  exchange's remedy discussion; the rules give no numbers.
- **[CLAIM-687](../claims.d/CLAIM-687.md)** and **[CLAIM-619](../claims.d/CLAIM-619.md)**: the symmetric limit and an
  organization's raising the cost of challenge.
- The exchange's A64 is faithful. Its "out of proportion to the parties'
  resources" (the LIT's summary) is approximate: resources are one of six
  factors, and cut both ways.

## Limitations

Rule texts and one Committee Note. No case law on how often sanctions are
imposed, which is needed before judging the guard's effect. US federal
civil procedure only.
