# THEORY-tmpk1fow: In team-sport fandom, perceived rivalry intensity lowers fans' sense of being respected by the rival's fans but raises their sense of in-group distinctiveness and cohesion, and these gains run through the perception that the rivalry is reciprocated, not through disidentification with the rival

## Source

- Berendt & Uhrich (2016), [LIT-1147](../literature.d/LIT-1147.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmpp2j50](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpp2j50.md) from
  the authors' accepted manuscript.

## What the account says

A rival is an identity threat and an identity resource at once. Fans who
see their rivalry as more intense believe the rival's fans respect them
less. They also see their own fan community as more distinct and more
cohesive, and as better regarded by fans of other clubs. The resource
side depends on the belief that the rival returns the antagonism, that
one's own club is "the rival for the rival". It does not depend on how
strongly fans separate themselves from the rival.

## What was actually shown

One cross-sectional survey, December 2014, eight clubs, N = 571 after
excluding the 164 who named no archrival. The total effects are as
above. The direct paths from intensity to the three positive outcomes
are null (−.08, .04, .01), and the indirect effects through reciprocity
are significant. The pattern could have come out otherwise, with
disidentification carrying the effect or with no positive effects at
all.

## What this does not say

- **Not that rivalry causes these outcomes.** The design is
  correlational; the arrows are the model's.
- **Not that rivalries are good on balance.** The negative effect on
  regard from the rival's fans is the largest single effect.
- **Not anything about fans without a rival**, who were excluded.
- **Not that perceived reciprocity is accurate.** Whether the rival's
  fans in fact return the antagonism was not measured.

## Bearing

- **[CASE-098](../cases.d/CASE-098.md).** It ties fans' identity to a relational perception, the
  mutuality of the rivalry, which is the two-sidedness the case's
  institutionalized rivalry requires. Still individual-level evidence.
- **[THEORY-tmpo85ke](THEORY-tmpo85ke.md).** That fans' regard for their group differs by which
  out-group judges it is what SCT's context-dependence predicts; the
  study does not test SCT.
