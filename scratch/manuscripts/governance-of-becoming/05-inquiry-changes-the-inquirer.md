# Article 5. Inquiry Changes the Inquirer

## Costed epistemic frontiers, stored specification, and adaptive readiness

**Abstract.** What an organization can find out depends on more than what it can answer once someone hands it a question. This article sets out what the Nucleation record holds about that dependence, and with what standing. It starts from three frontiers that are said to move separately: what a learner can do now, what it can come to learn, and what questions it can formulate. That separation is a Proposed claim. The article then sets out the owner's thesis, which the record holds as Active, that inquiry is a form of specification that can revise what an answer could be. It also gives the owner's second thesis, likewise Active, that formulating a question is itself a costed realization, so that the questions an agent can ask depend on what it has already realized. The clause on stored specification, that a stabilized specification makes some later questions cheaper and others harder, is a consequence ChatGPT drew from that thesis (A17 and A18), not the owner's own. The formal centre is a demonstrated result, Active since the status pass of 2026-11-24. At one budget, the questions an agent can formulate and the answers it can reach once a question is supplied do not fix which question–answer pairs it can reach together. Their families over all budgets do fix them, under a declared rule for composing the two costs, except where the way a question is formulated changes what answering it costs. On this ground the article reads authorization and legibility as costs and conditions on the same budget. The bounds, deadlines and coupling results around that centre were rerun by a second session on 2026-11-25 and reproduce, but stay Proposed, because the chain they rest on bottoms out in a Proposed premise, CLAIM-1114. The article reads the record's executed studies of suppressed inquiry and matched-output readiness as models of governance that changes whether and how much an organization goes on to inquire. And it reads overconsolidation as a Proposed generalization of overfitting. The record has read the machine-learning literature on it, and that reading qualifies the claim; nothing has been measured. The title thesis, that inquiry changes the inquirer, is held in some senses and not in others. Where it would mean that inquiry is learning, an independent reader found the article's candidate join (J5.1) trivial once the learning system is named. What is left is a definitional question, now a note on TERM-131.

## 5.1 Three frontiers that move separately

ChatGPT's claim, filed from A71 §6 of "Topological protection explained", is that a learner's acquired organization "fixes three resource-indexed frontiers that move separately". These are the operational frontier ("what it can reliably do now within an operating budget"), the developmental frontier ("what it can come to do reliably within a further acquisition budget, given a declared interface for what it may learn from") and the inquiry frontier ("what it can formulate and pursue"). So "no scalar measure of competence or plasticity ranks learners or learning processes independently of context, though one can once a task distribution, resource costs, horizon and evaluative preferences are declared" (CLAIM-754, Proposed, interpretive). Eighteen entries name it. Its defeater asks for "A showing that one frontier fixes another: that within a declared class of learners and tasks, equal operational frontiers imply equal developmental frontiers, or that the inquiry frontier is a function of the operational one" (CLAIM-754).

Two of its parts are held separately, and a third held result bears on them.

- **The operational frontier is resource-conditional.** This is the owner's idea, in A's formalization: a disposition "is resource-conditional", repeated sampling raises a frozen model's chance that some attempt succeeds "from p to 1 − (1 − p)^N with no change to its parameters", and "coverage is not delivery, which needs a selector that recognizes success" (CLAIM-697, Proposed, demonstrated). The account that holds this beside its empirical side is "What a learned system retains is held relative to its readers, cues, interventions and budgets" (SYNTHESIS-096, Proposed), one of A51's "Existing backbone" entries for this article. Its empirical side is that reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards "can make a disposition the base model already had cheaper to realize … without enlarging what the base reaches at large budgets", which is "an empirical question per run" (CLAIM-695, Proposed, empirical).
- **Equal present capability can leave unequal later capability.** This is demonstrated, but for support, not for a learner's own training: "Two forms of support that give a party the same capabilities now can leave it different capabilities later … support that does the work shrinks what the party can reach unaided while support that covers only its shortfall does not" (CLAIM-675, Active, demonstrated).
- **What sustains a capability is not what acquires it.** A51's other backbone entry here: "a capability can be cheap to keep and costly to acquire, so having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it" (CLAIM-569, Active, demonstrated). It is the held separation of maintenance from acquisition cost that the developmental frontier presupposes (§5.8.2).

The definition of learning the three frontiers presuppose is ChatGPT's: "an experience-dependent change in the system's retained organization that alters what it can later do, at what cost and with what reliability, judged against a counterfactual in which the experience did not occur" (TERM-131, Proposed). The account that maps this line calls itself "learning as an experience-dependent change in a costed repertoire; three frontiers, operational, developmental and inquiry, that move separately" (SYNTHESIS-105, Proposed). Its central claim, CLAIM-754, is Proposed and interpretive. So A51's opening move, "Begin with the separation of the three frontiers", begins on Proposed ground. What is Active under it is the inquiry frontier (TERM-092) and CLAIM-675's result for support.

## 5.2 Inquiry as specification, and stored specification

### 5.2.1 The owner's two theses

At U15 of the exchange of 2026-10-27 the owner wrote that "inquiry is a form of specification." The record holds it as: "a question fixes which differences would count as an answer, an inquiry adds the operations that make those differences accessible, and what it finds can stabilize into a specification that constrains later inquiry; so inquiry stands to its findings as a specification to its realizations, with one difference, that an inquiry can revise what an answer could be" (CLAIM-470, Active, interpretive). The claim "does not say that inquiry makes what it investigates" (CLAIM-470, summary). The object it is stated in is the owner's requested term: inquiry as "(Σ, A, 𝓘, 𝓞, 𝓚, 𝓑)", a language, an answer space, interventions, observations, criteria and resources, and "it is reflexive when any of these becomes what it is pursuing" (TERM-095, Active).

At U17 of the same exchange the owner added: "resource constraints impact specification in exactly the same way as they impact realization. I can't interrogate a question that takes me more time than I have to even ask it." The record holds it as: "Specifying is itself a resource-bounded realization: formulating a question takes time, concepts and computation as realizing a configuration takes energy and matter, so the questions an agent can ask are a product of what it has already realized; a stabilized specification is past inquiry stored so that later questions become cheaper to ask, and it can make other questions harder" (CLAIM-475, Active, interpretive). The thesis is the owner's. The clause on stored specification is a consequence ChatGPT drew at A17 and A18 ("A17 and A18 drew a consequence … A stable specification amortizes the work that made it", CLAIM-475, summary). Its object is the inquiry frontier: "the questions it can formulate, making them determinate enough to pursue, with the concepts, records, tools, time and computation it actually has; distinct from the questions its language can express, from those it can pursue to a stated standard, and from those that have answers" (TERM-092, Active).

The relationships among these achievements "form a partial order, not a nested sequence" (CLAIM-474, Active, interpretive). No step below assumes that formulating, pursuing and answering come in one order.

### 5.2.2 What stored specification does

CLAIM-475's last clause is two-sided, and the record keeps both sides.

- **It cheapens.** The owner's thesis on stored work generalizes it: "A stabilized constraint can be stored work: by replacing later work with cheaper operations it changes which activities are realizable within a fixed per-task budget, so amortization becomes capability expansion without any added degrees of freedom or resources" (CLAIM-761, Active, interpretive; Article 3, §3.8.1). ChatGPT's governance reading is that "slow organizational modes arise partly as ways of amortizing inquiry and coordination costs" (CLAIM-1060, Proposed, interpretive). Its step from amortization to slow modes is ARG-238 (Proposed), "which CLAIM-755 narrows to the economic sense of slow" (CLAIM-1060, summary). The account of stored work keeps a limit on this side: "a fall in one party's cost may be work transferred, not saved" (SYNTHESIS-106, Proposed). And storing is not free. "Filing an argument moves work across time", and it is "worth its cost only when expected reuse covers it" (CLAIM-303, Active, interpretive). A counter adds a cost of using what is stored: "certifying that stored answers cover a new question is NP-complete even for conjunctive queries", "so each filing raises the cost of later reuse" (CLAIM-314, Proposed, interpretive, a counter). So "it cheapens" is net of a recognition cost that the record holds only as Proposed.
- **It hardens.** A dated note on CLAIM-475 (A18 §8 of "Memes, Contracts") reads a contract as "a materialized answer to a historically recurring question" that "can make unfamiliar questions more expensive". Its bearing is "an instance … The claim is unchanged." A finer category also changes what is noticed: "refining a category makes misfits in the refined region more noticeable rather than fewer" (CLAIM-794, Proposed, empirical). That claim is about a record's grammar, and its test is a recode of CASE-300. Three held entries are instances of hardening. A sufficient state "can stop being sufficient when a source is discredited, the model is revised or the decisions change" (CLAIM-300, Active, demonstrated). Question drift is "a provisional answer becoming part of the question an inquiry is pursuing" (TERM-056, Active). And "Anchors entrench" (CLAIM-184, Proposed, interpretive, a counter).

The account of this line puts it in one sentence: "stored specification cheapens some questions and hardens others; and what makes reconfiguration self-governed is an open question" (SYNTHESIS-114, Proposed). The open question is QUESTION-388 (Open): "What distinguishes a programmable system from one that governs its own reconfiguration?"

### 5.2.3 In what sense inquiry changes the inquirer

The title can be read three ways, and they stand differently.

1. **Inquiry changes which questions the inquirer can later ask, and at what cost.** That is CLAIM-475 (Active): the askable questions "are a product of what it has already realized", and stored specification moves their costs. In this sense the title restates an Active entry. A18 §18's "Inquiry as both product and mechanism of reflexive individuation" was declined for that reason: "no claim passes admission; it generalizes held claims and is an instance of CLAIM-475" (SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr, Proposed).
2. **Inquiry can take its own language, answer space, criteria or resources as its object.** That is TERM-095's definition of reflexive inquiry, with CLAIM-470's "an inquiry can revise what an answer could be". It is a definition and a thesis, both Active, and the title adds nothing to them.
3. **Inquiry is a way the inquirer learns.** On this reading, the change that stored inquiry makes is a learning in TERM-131's sense, an experience-dependent change in retained organization that alters what the inquirer can later do and at what cost. **No entry states this.** The account of the learning-and-inquiry line lists among the links it does not state "That learning and inquiry are one process, or that inquiry is a kind of learning … SYNTHESIS-116's self-formation and TERM-131's learning are not tied by any entry to CLAIM-470's specification" (SYNTHESIS-118, Proposed, "Not stated on the record"). The reading here is narrower than the one declined. It does not say that inquiry is a kind of learning. It says that an inquiry whose finding stabilizes produces a change that meets TERM-131's definition. The decline did not test that narrower form.

The draft put this third reading to ADR-075's test as its own join (**J5.1**), and it **failed**. The independent reader's verdict has two parts (REVIEW-2026-11-25, Part B).

- *Whether an inquiry is an "experience".* TERM-095 settles most of this. An inquiry includes interventions and observations (𝓘, 𝓞), and those are experience on any reading of TERM-131. What remains is formulating a question with no observation at all.
- *Which system learned.* TERM-131 requires a claim to say "which system and horizon it means". If the stored specification is inside the declared inquirer, the join follows at once, and it is sense 1 (CLAIM-475) relabelled as learning: trivial. If it is outside, in a shared record or notation, then the record's holders changed, not the inquirer. So the join is trivial once the system is fixed, and has a free parameter while it is left open. No narrower sentence passes.

The residue is a definitional question for TERM-131 (Proposed, with a version 3 waiting on the owner): does formulating a question, with no observation, count as an experience that can change the learner? It is now a dated note on TERM-131 (note of 2026-11-25), in the assistant's voice. The article uses the title only in senses 1 and 2. The record's nearest held statement in sense 3 concerns answering, not inquiry, and is Proposed (§5.4).

### 5.3.2 The result

CLAIM-1190 is ChatGPT's thesis from A58, on the owner's U30. It says that inquiry "can fail at three sites, forming queries, producing answers and their joint realizability, and the third is not reducible to the first two" (CLAIM-1190, Active, demonstrated). A58 gave two reasons. Its §1 reason was a shared deadline: an organization "might be able to formulate a question within ten hours and, if given the question for free, produce an answer within ten hours", and it cannot thereby do both within the same ten hours. Its §2 reason was formal: two relations, 𝓕_1 and 𝓕_2, that share their projections onto questions and onto answers. That second reason "holds of any relation and its projections. It does not reach a query frontier and an answer frontier indexed by question, which A58 itself defines" (CLAIM-1190, "The two reasons"). What carries the claim is the first reason, as the assistant worked it out:

> "At one budget, the questions an agent can formulate and the answers it can attain once a question is supplied do not fix which question–answer pairs it can attain together, but their budget-indexed families do when the costs of formulating and answering compose by a declared rule; what is left over is the rule, and where the way a question is formulated changes what answering it costs, not even the least costs fix it" (CLAIM-1115, Active, demonstrated; the assistant's).

The four constructions, as the kept script reproduces them:

1. **One budget does not fix the pairs.** Two systems both cost 10 to answer. One costs 10 to formulate and the other 2. At B = 12 both have the same askable question and the same conditional answer. Under sequential spending the first can reach no pair (20 > 12) and the second reaches (q, a) (12 ≤ 12).
2. **The families over budgets do fix them, under a declared rule.** From the frontiers at every budget the least costs can be recovered, and with the additive rule the joint set follows. Checked over B = 0…39, the recovered set equals the true one at every budget.
3. **The rule matters.** Formulating and answering each cost 6, and the horizon is 10. Run in sequence, 12 > 10, so the pair is out of reach. Run in parallel, 6 ≤ 10, so it is in reach.
4. **Formulation can change the cost of answering.** One way of formulating q costs 2 and leaves an answer costing 9. Another costs 6 and leaves 3. The least costs sum to 5, but the true joint cost is 9. At B = 5 and B = 8 the least costs say the pair is reachable when it is not.

The script also has a negative control, two routes whose cost of answering does not depend on the route of formulation. There the least costs give the joint set at every budget.

**A51's abstract overstates this.** It says that the existing results "establish that the feasibility of question formation and the conditional feasibility of answering do not determine their joint realizability under a shared resource budget". That holds at one budget. Over the budget-indexed families, under a declared composition rule, they do determine it, except in construction 4's case. The record's account states the qualifier: "At one budget they do not fix it; over all budgets they do under a declared composition rule, except where how a question is posed changes what answering it costs" (SYNTHESIS-122, Proposed, step 13). CLAIM-1190's defeater states it too: the claim is "Not defeated … by reconstructing the joint pairs from the frontiers at every budget under a declared rule (CLAIM-1115's construction 2), which uses more than the one budget." The joint relation is a site of its own because the coupling (the rule, the schedule, and what formulating does to answering) is in neither frontier (CLAIM-1115, "What follows"). Its irreducibility is relative to one budget. It is not irreducibility outright.

CLAIM-1190's title, read without its defeater, says more than this: that the pairs are "not determined by the questions it can ask and the answers it can attain taken separately". Its history concedes that it asserts no more than construction 1 shows. The title and the scope are inconsistent, and the article cites the scope. Whether the record should note this on CLAIM-1190 or narrow its title is the owner's call (G5.8). The status pass that promoted it already put its demonstrated promotion to the owner's eye (curation entry of 2026-11-24 23:11, "For the owner").

### 5.3.3 Bounds, depth and deadlines

## 5.4 Same answers, different development

ChatGPT's claim, as revised to concede an objection, is that "Two organizations that give the same answers can differ in what answering does to them, so a response kernel that includes the organization's state after answering, observed through its later responses rather than compared as a state, separates what an answer benchmark treats as equivalent, such as one that learns from disagreement and one that suppresses it" (CLAIM-1156, Proposed, interpretive). This is the record's nearest statement that answering changes an organization. It is Proposed, and it does not use TERM-131. For inquiry in general the record holds a demonstrated form: "Two inquiry histories can agree on every current conclusion and still call for different revisions of it, so a current marginal belief is not a sufficient state for updating on evidence about what it depended on" (CLAIM-294, Active, demonstrated). It concerns histories of belief, not organizations' answering, and is cited beside CLAIM-1156 and CLAIM-1095, not as either.

The objection it concedes is the assistant's. A kernel that includes the post-state "is not an observational quantity: compared directly, it separates any two organizations whose post-states differ, even when no test could tell them apart". So the comparison "should observe the post-state only through its later responses, as a bisimulation pseudometric does" (CLAIM-1095, Active, demonstrated, a counter; grounds THEORY-247). The construction gives these distances:

| pair | answers only | post-state compared directly | bisimulation |
|---|---|---|---|
| s, t: identical, distinct states | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| u, v: same answers now; u learns, v suppresses | 0 | 1 | 5.4 |

Only the bisimulation metric gets both pairs right. This is a stipulated two-query construction. It shows what a measure must do, not that any organization learns or suppresses.

## 5.6 Reach indexed by actor and by authority

This section takes over §3.7 of A49's Article 3, "The Cost of Becoming", under A51's plan; that file is superseded (`superseded/03-cost-of-becoming.md`), and A51's Article 3 keeps only its bar on exit (Article 3, §3.9.3). ### 5.6.1 Four predicates on a transition, kept in three places

### 5.6.2 An obligation bars a transition and does not price it

ChatGPT's claim (A32 §13) is that "A genuine obligation enters a cost model of exit as a condition on which transitions are permissible, not as a cost: withdrawal can be feasible and affordable yet impermissible until alternatives are arranged or the duty is transferred, so a party who controls the circumstances that keep the restriction in place controls the other's exit without raising its price" (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6, Proposed, interpretive). Read beside §5.5.4, the record has a cost reading of authorization and a bar reading of obligation, and it has not stated how the two compose on one transition.

### 5.6.3 Together and alone

The mechanism is held first in prose. "A capability lost because another reliably supplies it is the Black Queen's loss", and whether it is dependency or division of labour "is decided by the supply, not the loss" (CLAIM-603, Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's, on the owner's remark of 2026-11-05 that "assured help makes recipients lose skill" sounds "closely related to niche differentiation and specialization phenomena"). Resting on it, an arrangement "can produce the dependence through which it exploits": by reliably supplying what a party then ceases to supply for itself, "it creates a reliance on the provider that the party did not have before" (CLAIM-670, Proposed, interpretive; A's, A9 §2 of "About Plantation Paradise", filed at the owner's review of #488).

Three results bear on what one party can reach unaided, and all three are Active and demonstrated. A fourth, Proposed, follows them.

- CLAIM-675: support that does the work "shrinks what the party can reach unaided while support that covers only its shortfall does not".
- CLAIM-602: where capacity is learned by doing and decays with disuse, "what decides dependence is the order in which supply and capacity are used, not the amount of supply". Its model is dC/dt = λP(1 − C) − μC.
- CLAIM-605: the threshold version of CLAIM-602. Where growth also needs capacity already present, dC/dt = λPC(1 − C) − μC has an unstable threshold below which capacity does not regrow, so "support that does the work has a deadline after which withdrawal no longer restores capacity, and a slow taper of such support is worse than an abrupt cut" (CLAIM-605, Active, demonstrated; promoted at a delegated status review on 2026-11-06, its script rerun by the twenty-fifth status review). It is "the capability trap that CLAIM-602 named as a limit", Azam et al.'s trap, and LEAD-171's note of 2026-11-22 records it as held.

A fourth result, executed, rests on CLAIM-605 and applies its dynamics to a restriction. In capacity dynamics learned by doing, "making reinstatement a threshold on capacity turns a restriction justified by a lack of competence into a self-perpetuating trap", with "a recoverability deadline" (CLAIM-950, Proposed, demonstrated; the assistant's, carrying out LEAD-104; run as CASE-364, Proposed, executed study). The reading behind "support that does the work" is Ostrom and colleagues' Samaritan's dilemma: "predictable aid weakens the recipient's own upkeep" (THEORY-1480, Active).

All four are about a scalar capacity, not a set of reachable states over TERM-114. The trap the article's floor holds is CLAIM-605's, Active and demonstrated; CLAIM-950 is its Proposed application to restriction. A note on the lead of 2026-11-24 reads the first two as "step 3's construction for solo reach under supply, with its negative control (shortfall support), already demonstrated" (LEAD-tmpeo8wq).

In values, ChatGPT's A24 §4 model has specialization raise joint production while it lowers one party's independent capacity. At full specialization with no protection of B's fallback, "the split is 14.75 to 11.25 in A's favour; with full protection, 12.25 to 13.75" (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1, Proposed, interpretive). The bargaining step is the assistant's and was checked. Under the equal-weight Nash solution, u_B = (V − o_A + o_B)/2, so B's fallback moves the split "whether or not B ever takes the outside option". ChatGPT's caption limits the model: "Maintaining fallback capacity is not priced in this model" (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1). That this model already has the lead's shape in values was a join of the first draft of A49's Article 3 (**old J3.10**, superseded file 03, §3.7.4): accurate, but it fails as a claim, and it is held as a dated note on LEAD-tmpeo8wq, the second of 2026-11-24. ## 5.8 Overconsolidation and adaptive readiness

### 5.8.1 The owner's generalization

When A48 §8 named "the possibility of learning oneself into a trap", the owner answered at U22 of "Topological protection explained": "sounds like you've landed on a generalization of overfitting that I think captures the more recent notion of "over cooking" a learning process." The record holds it in ChatGPT's wording. Relative to a declared family of later acquisitions outside the training task distribution, "continued stabilization of a learner can keep improving its present objective while raising the budget it needs to acquire that family to a declared reliability with its held capabilities kept above a declared floor … it is a failure only relative to that family and horizon" (CLAIM-758, Proposed, interpretive). Twelve entries name it. The wording quoted is version 2, revised "after the readings" of 2026-11-19, and two of its clauses were forced by them (CLAIM-758, "Version 2, after the readings"; its note of 2026-11-19).

- *The later family lies outside the training task distribution.* Yin and colleagues (THEORY-2056, Proposed) and Rajendran, Irpan and Jang (THEORY-2053, Proposed) show meta-learners whose training loss improves while adaptation to new tasks from the same distribution is lost. Yin and colleagues call it "a subtle form of task overfitting", and task-level overfitting is one of the failures the claim's defeater keeps it apart from. Version 2 excludes it by declaring the later family outside the training distribution.
- *Acquisition is priced with held capabilities kept.* Springer and colleagues (THEORY-2006, Proposed) are "the claim's prime case, and they qualify it". Past an inflection point a longer-pretrained model ends worse after fine-tuning, mainly because fine-tuning degrades more of what it held. Version 2 counts collateral forgetting as part of the cost of acquiring.
- *Neighbours, not the signature.* Han and colleagues (THEORY-2015, Proposed) compare weight-decay regimes and do not follow a time course. Dohare and colleagues (THEORY-2027, Proposed) show plasticity loss with the present objective falling, not rising. Raghu and colleagues (THEORY-2025, Proposed) find that "feature reuse is the dominant factor" in fast adaptation, and track neither quantity over training.

The note of 2026-11-19 concludes: "No reading shows the full signature: present competence rising, a declared later acquisition costing more, held capabilities kept." The account says the same: "That any learner is overconsolidated: nothing has been measured" (SYNTHESIS-105, "What it does not say"). A neighbouring claim, also Proposed, asks whether relief has a deadline. It "depends on how the capacity to acquire is lost", and "the evidence read so far fits" the case where training uses it up, which renewed practice does not restore (CLAIM-757, Proposed, interpretive).

### 5.8.4 The matched-organization study, and what it must separate

ChatGPT's A63 §9.4 proposes organizations "matched in initial expertise, technical resources and performance on familiar inquiries but differing in how inquiry is authorized and perspectives maintained", with "a difference in success on the novel ones" as the discriminating result (CASE-395, Proposed, proposed-study). **It has not been run.** Since 2026-11-25 it is held as LEAD-tmp8ogv0 (Open, specified), "in simulation first, with an arm on slack that holds authorization fixed" (G5.1). Its own text gives the obstacle: the matched-performance model "already shows the predicted pattern from a different mechanism, which the design must separate". In CASE-412 and CASE-397 the pattern is "produced by budget rules and reserves, not by authorization or perspective maintenance" (CASE-395). So a matched-on-familiar, different-on-novel result would not, by itself, discriminate authorization from budget.

*Expectation, before any result (mine, written when no lead held the study; LEAD-tmp8ogv0 now carries the slack arm).* If the study ran as A63 designs it, the familiar–novel gap should track slack (a held reserve) at least as strongly as it tracks the authorization regime. *Falsifier:* with slack held fixed, as CASE-395's note proposes ("an organization that keeps a reserve and one that does not, with authorization held fixed"), the gap persists across authorization regimes. Only that arm would bear on CLAIM-1016 and CLAIM-1177 rather than on CASE-412's mechanism. The slack arm is not my invention. Its held content is that reserve and redesign "change different components of readiness" and, under a budget that follows peak use, "are complementary requirements rather than independently sufficient alternatives" (CLAIM-1093, Active, interpretive; promoted in the status pass of 2026-11-24, which flagged its interpretive promotion for the owner's review, curation entry of 2026-11-24 23:11, "For the owner").
