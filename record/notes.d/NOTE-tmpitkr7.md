# NOTE-tmpitkr7: Wardrope (2019) — Does clinical ethics need a Land Ethic?

## Contribution

An exegesis of the Land Ethic in three components (community concept, ethical sequence, ecological conscience) and an argument for it in clinical ethics.

## Key results

1. Abstract: treating "communities (both human and biotic) as valuable in themselves rather than just through the aggregate welfare of their individual participants".
2. The community concept: "In neither of these accounts is there space for irreducible community-level value". Individual organisms "are defined in terms of the larger structure and constitutively dependent upon it" (after Callicott).
3. What is a Land Ethic?: "the ‘ethical sequence’ that situates the value of such community as extending, not replacing, values assigned to individuals".
4. The ethical sequence: "taking the community concept seriously need not blind us to the needs of the individual"; the Land Ethic asks "respect for his [sic] fellow members, and also respect for the community as such." (quoting Leopold 1949, p. 204).

## Assumptions

Leopold argues by cultivating moral perception, not by derivation. Value can attach to communities irreducibly. The accretion view of moral progress is used prescriptively; Wardrope calls it descriptively naïve.

## Concepts

Land Ethic; community concept; ethical sequence; ecological conscience; environmental fascism; holism and individualism.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-tmp9bbpk**: the biotic community is closer to TERM-074's sense 1, a whole that is not an agent and whose members need not be persons. It answers A12's holism sub-question one way (the whole's value is added to the members', not substituted). But the bearer of the obligation is the members, as moral agents, not the whole: respect is owed *by members* to fellow members and to the community, and nothing is said to be owed *by the community* to its parts. Filed as `bears_on`, with a dated note.
- **CLAIM-075**: a contrast. The Land Ethic makes a community's "integrity, stability, and beauty" right-making, an ethic in which a whole's persistence is a value in itself, though Wardrope denies it derives an ought from viability. No relation written.
- **TERM-083**: communities bear irreducible value; whether that is standing in the term's sense the paper does not say.
- **As a stand-in for LIT-tmpmtbj6**: substantial for A12's point, none for the 2020 paper's health-justice argument.

## Limitations

About half read. The reading of Leopold is the author's. The setting is clinical ethics, not wholes and parts in general.
