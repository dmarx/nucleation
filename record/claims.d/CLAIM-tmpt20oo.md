# CLAIM-tmpt20oo: Translation is a context-conditioned transport process that preserves selected structures of communicative meaning to varying degrees while introducing distortions that may accumulate, attenuate or amplify under composition

## The claim

A50 §7: "I'd replace the strong claim *Translation preserves the structure of
communicative operations* with something that accommodates both preservation
and transformation: **Translation is a context-conditioned transport process
that preserves selected structures of communicative meaning to varying degrees
while introducing systematic distortions that may accumulate, attenuate, or
amplify under composition.**" A52, outline v4, *What Survives Translation?
Communicative Frames, Dynamical Transport, and the Evolution of Meaning*:
"Translation is a context-conditioned process of communicative reconstruction.
Each act of reconstruction transports aspects of an utterance's semantic,
pragmatic, and interactional structure into a new realization while introducing
distortions shaped by the interpreter, communicative frame, and transmission
environment."

A52 §1.1 put the question it answers: "*What must survive a transformation for
the resulting utterance to remain recognizably a translation of the original?*",
the nearest ancestor of the title question ([QUESTION-tmp3lk3n](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp3lk3n.md)).

## Why it was replaced

The manuscript keeps every part of it in §6 and §8 and moves the thesis to what
transport preserves: observables and their relations ([CLAIM-tmpww3q0](CLAIM-tmpww3q0.md)). That move is
later than this chunk.
