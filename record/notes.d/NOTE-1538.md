# NOTE-1538: West, Fisher, Gardner and Kiers — Major evolutionary transitions in individuality

## Contribution

A two-condition definition of a transition in individuality, and a table
that scores near-misses by which condition they fail. Six research
questions follow, with two more on maintenance and breakdown. It also
claims to unify the transitions: how groups form explains where
transitions have and have not happened, against Maynard Smith and
Szathmáry's "mixture of explanations" ([LIT-2001](../literature.d/LIT-2001.md), unread).

## Key results

1. **Definition** ("Defining Major Transitions"). "First, entities that
   were capable of independent replication before the transition can
   replicate only as part of a larger unit after it, termed mutual
   dependence ... Second, there is a relative lack of within-group conflict
   such that the larger unit can be thought of as a fitness-maximizing
   individual". The genetic code, sex and language are excluded.
2. **Two steps** ("Steps"; Fig. 1). "(i) the formation of a cooperative
   group and (ii) the transformation of the cooperative group into a more
   cohesive and integrated entity". The second "typically involves"
   division of labour, dependence through specialization and
   communication. The steps "may not have clear borders, and can move
   gradually from one to the next".
3. **Order depends on the kind of transition** ("Steps"). Within a
   species, division of labour comes after groups form. "with transitions
   between members of different species, individuals can be specialized to
   perform different tasks before the formation of cooperative groups".
4. **The two conditions come apart** (Table 1). Social aphids have
   negligible conflict and no mutual dependence. *Melipona* bees have
   mutual dependence and conflict. Slime molds, biofilms, cooperative
   vertebrates, humans, legumes with rhizobia, squid with *Vibrio* and gut
   microbiota have neither. Fig. 5: "Negligible conflict is not sufficient
   for a major transition".
5. **Cooperation needs a benefit and a return.** It needs "some ecological
   or efficiency benefit" and "some mechanism that leads to the benefits of
   cooperation being directed back to the cooperator and/or their
   relatives". Between species, that mechanism is partner-fidelity feedback
   and host sanctions.
6. **How groups form decides conflict.** "The major transition to obligate
   multicellularity has taken place only in species where daughter cells
   stick together after division and so group formation is clonal".
   Aggregation "has led only to facultative multicellularity". Between
   species, vertically transmitted clonal symbionts "could transmit to more
   individuals only by increasing the reproductive success of their host".
7. **Where mutual dependence comes from.** "In most cases, mutual
   dependence seems to arise as a result of extreme division of labor."
   Repression of competition and genetic drift are other routes, with the
   host taking over roles its symbionts lose. "There is an almost complete
   lack of work addressing why certain traits are lost".
8. **Not Hamilton's rule alone** ("The World Made Simpler"). "a lower
   relatedness (r) cannot be made up for just with a greater ecological
   benefit to cooperation (B/C)". Kin discrimination within groups "played
   a limited role", and the repression of competition did too, except for
   policing in haplodiploids.
9. **A mosaic** ("Pragmatism"). Negligible conflict "can be
   trait-dependent, even within an organism", so "we should expect blurry
   edges with a mosaic of features". Mutual dependence is judged relative
   to a defined social group, and can be reversed.
10. **Open.** Group transformation is much less understood than group
    formation. The added questions are how new conflicts are suppressed
    after a transition, and "What conditions favor the breakdown of major
    transitions?"

## Assumptions

- Inclusive fitness is what organisms maximize.
- Every transition is "the same problem": "How are the potentially selfish
  interests of individuals overcome to form mutually dependent cooperative
  groups?" This collective-action framing is assumed, not argued.
- Conflict is analysed among helpers, not between helpers and parents.

## Concepts

- **mutual dependence** (also "interdependence", "contingent
  irreversibility"), relative to a defined group;
- **negligible within-group conflict**, so little that its consequences
  for the group "can be effectively ignored";
- **cooperation**: a trait that benefits another and "has evolved at least
  partially because of that benefit".

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-1351](../theory.d/THEORY-1351.md).

- **The synthesis's §2** lists cooperation, division of labour,
  communication, mutual dependence and reduced conflict as what
  transitions need. That matches the abstract, with two corrections.
  Conflict must be *negligible*, not reduced: lower r "cannot be made up
  for". And only two of the five are defining conditions, mutual dependence
  and negligible conflict. Division of labour and communication are what
  the second step "typically involves". The two-step account is accurate,
  but its steps have no clear borders, and between species specialization
  can come first.
- **[CLAIM-474](../claims.d/CLAIM-474.md)** (a partial order, not a sequence). No conflict, and an
  instance. Table 1 separates the paper's two defining conditions in both
  directions: aphids have one, *Melipona* the other. The authors soften
  their own steps. [CLAIM-474](../claims.d/CLAIM-474.md)'s note of 2026-11-04 records this.
- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md).** The paper gives an order of conditions: the conditions
  of group formation first, which align interests, and mutual dependence
  later, usually through extreme division of labour, or through drift with
  the host taking over lost roles. Its criterion for a completed transition
  is mutual dependence together with negligible conflict, and Table 1 shows
  that neither suffices alone. [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)'s note of 2026-11-04 records
  this.
- **[THEORY-141](../theory.d/THEORY-141.md)** (Okasha). It agrees that conflict must be overcome and
  that a transition is more than altruism. It disagrees on mechanism:
  policing and kin discrimination played a limited role within species,
  and group formation did the work. It assumes the "same problem" framing
  that Okasha cautions against. [THEORY-141](../theory.d/THEORY-141.md)'s note of 2026-11-04 records
  this.
- **[THEORY-1048](../theory.d/THEORY-1048.md)** (the Black Queen). Not cited. The paper's drift route,
  with the host taking over lost roles, is compatible with it.
- **[CLAIM-546](../claims.d/CLAIM-546.md).** Weak support. Cooperation needs a mechanism that
  returns its benefits to the cooperator, and coexistence supplies none.

## Limitations

- A review whose claims of unification rest on the cited literature.
- Inclusive-fitness reasoning is used heuristically. The authors say
  explicit theory is still missing for conflict between species.
- The ideas are called "tentative".
