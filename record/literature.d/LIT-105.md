# LIT-105: On the Evolution, Science, and Metaphysics of Consciousness

Walter Veit (2025), *Adaptive Behavior 34(1):53–71 (online 2025-12-27); preprint on PhilSci-Archive (#27585)* — DOI-10.1177/10597123251413204

## Key takeaways

- Veit defends and extends the evolutionary, bottom-up account of consciousness from his book A Philosophy for the Science of Animal Consciousness against four commentaries, distinguishing his naturalism from autopoietic enactivism, answering the charge of residual Cartesianism, and defending consciousness as an evaluation-first adaptation.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The article is Veit's reply to four critical commentaries on the evolutionary account of consciousness developed in his 2023 book. He clarifies what his evolutionary bottom-up approach commits him to, in response to de Weerd, and distinguishes his naturalistic framework from autopoiesis and enactivism, in response to Sachs. He answers Frankish's charge of residual Cartesian commitments and Suzuki's prediction-based alternative to his evaluation-first view, including Suzuki's criticisms on disunity and his doubt that consciousness is an adaptation. He closes by addressing Chincarini on how the pathological complexity thesis bears on animal welfare science.
