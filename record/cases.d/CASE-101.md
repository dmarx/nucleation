# CASE-101: Three simulated populations with the same turnover and the same aggregate statistics: one whose members respond alike to a common input, one that transmits its norms to its replacements, and one held together by an institution that selects and trains its members

## The case

A12 §7 stated the three arms:

- **A, common input.** "Individuals have little interaction, but all
  respond to the same repeating external events. The aggregate is highly
  predictable because the environment is predictable."
- **B, reproducing organization.** "Individuals inherit norms from
  previous members, modify them through interaction, and transmit them
  onward."
- **C, institutionally maintained.** Individuals turn over and stay
  coordinated "because an enduring external institution selects, trains,
  and governs them."

The arms are built so that observable aggregate statistics and turnover
rates match. A11 §9 had a third arm of its own instead of C: an original
population that splits in two after a relocation ([CASE-105](CASE-105.md)).

## What it would show

Where the criteria disagree. A12: a common-input population "might
display high observational persistence but little endogenous historical
organization"; an institutionally maintained one "might exhibit a highly
effective macrostate while its boundary must include the external
governing institution"; a reproducing one "might show strong genealogical
continuity while lacking exact dynamical independence". The criteria to
compare are Krakauer et al.'s A*, A and nC ([LIT-1137](../literature.d/LIT-1137.md), unread), dynamical

[CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)'s priced block.
