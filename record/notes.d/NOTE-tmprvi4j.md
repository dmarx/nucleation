# NOTE-tmprvi4j: Hance et al., wavefunctions as knowledge and reality

## Contribution

In quantum foundations, "ontic" (about what exists) and "epistemic" (about knowledge) have been identified with Harrigan and Spekkens' formal definitions within the ontological models framework, and those definitions are mutually exclusive. The authors argue that nothing in the informal notions prevents a wavefunction from representing reality and knowledge at once. They propose that the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem, and much else in the debate, be reconsidered in that light.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §II gives examples of terms that are both ontic and epistemic. Thermodynamic entropy depends on what can be distinguished (Wallace's "control theory" reading), and a proper mixture represents both the system and our ignorance of which eigenstate it is in. It also criticises Leifer's and Friederich's exclusive definitions.
- §III restates the ontological-models framework: response functions A(S|λ), distributions p(λ|ψ), recovery of the Born rule (Eq. 1), and the ψ-complete / ψ-supplemented taxonomy. ψ-ontic means disjoint supports (Eq. 5).
- §IV: overlap is sufficient for an epistemic reading but not necessary. The authors give a weaker sufficient condition (Eq. 6): response functions not equal to the Born probability throughout Λψ. ψ-dependent models (Schlosshauer–Fine) are exhibited as both ontic and epistemic, and Hardy's "deduce ψ from λ" argument is rejected via the fingers-and-numbers analogy.
- §V: PBR, Colbeck–Renner, Hardy, Patra et al. and Ruebeck et al. rule out overlap only. They "do not rule out all epistemic models", and the formal terms should not be conflated with the informal ideas. A footnote cites Hermens against the Colbeck–Renner–Leegwater theorem.
