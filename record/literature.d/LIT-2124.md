# LIT-2124: Cooperativity, entropy, and effective concentration in DNA origami self-replication

Ni, H., Zhou, F., Zhu, G., Bershadsky, L., Ai, S., Wang, K., Sha, R., Seeman, N. C. & Chaikin, P. M. (2026). Cooperativity, entropy, and effective concentration in DNA origami self-replication. *Science Advances* 12(21): eaed9517. doi:10.1126/sciadv.aed9517.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1630](../notes.d/NOTE-1630.md).*

- **Cooperativity as effective concentration.** "the translational
  entropy penalty is paid only once when the first several bonds form";
  bound, the effective concentration rises "around a million fold
  increase resulting in a shift in melting temperature of ~50°C".
- **A predictive model.** It "predicts melting temperatures to ~2°C where
  there is a 50°C shift arising from cooperativity" (abstract).
- **Replication, imperfect.** "about 1.9 replication rate for dimers
  reducing to 1.6 for tetramers", where "the rate is 2 for a full yield and
  1 for no growth".
- **A size limit in practice.** "We found that we could barely separate
  tetramers before the DNA origami cross-tiles started unraveling."
- **Everything is supplied.** Monomer tiles, the thermal program, UV, and
  "The sample is then split to repeat the cycle".
