# LIT-177: The Relational Interpretation of Quantum Physics

Carlo Rovelli (2021), *arXiv preprint (chapter for the Oxford Handbook of the History of Interpretations of Quantum Physics, per arXiv comments)* — [ARXIV-2109.09170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09170)

## Key takeaways

- RQM dissolves the measurement problem by treating quantum theory as a theory of sparse facts that are relative to interacting systems, with transition amplitudes yielding probabilities only when their arguments are facts relative to the same system, and decoherence making some facts approximately stable.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Rovelli presents the relational interpretation as a solution to the measurement problem built on an ontology of sparse, relative events ("facts") rather than quantum states. Facts occur in interactions between any two physical systems and are relative to those systems. The technical core is that quantum transition amplitudes give physical probabilities only when their arguments are facts relative to one and the same system. Where decoherence suppresses interference, the relativity of facts can be ignored and facts behave as approximately stable.
