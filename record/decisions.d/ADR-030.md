# ADR-030: design-rationale joins the topics

## Context

The owner is considering extending this record with argument-development
schemes: questions, claims and objections, in the manner of IBIS. Systems
of that kind have been built since 1970, and the literature on them is
mostly a literature of why they went unused. The owner asked for that
literature to be held here, as the cautionary evidence for the extension:

- Kunz and Rittel's 1970 working paper, which introduced IBIS;
- gIBIS (Conklin and Begeman 1988) and QOC (MacLean et al. 1991), the two
  best-known notations and tools;
- Conklin and Burgess Yakemovic (1991), a field trial where capture was
  sustained;
- Grudin (1988, 1994) on why groupware fails, and in particular the
  disparity between who does the work and who gets the benefit;
- Buckingham Shum and Hammond (1994) and Lee (1997) on what design
  rationale costs and when it repays the cost;
- Shipman and Marshall (1999) on why users will not formalize.

No topic held them. The nearest words each say something else:

- `society-and-governance` is law, politics and institutions;
- `epistemology` is knowledge and justification, not tools for recording
  reasons;
- `cognition` is the mind as information processing, and covers the
  overhead argument only from the side of the person paying it.

The anthology's `human-ai-interaction` is "how people work with a model
through an interface". These works predate the models and are about
people recording their reasons for each other, so no anthology topic can
hold them either.

## Decision

Add **`design-rationale`**: recording the reasoning behind a design or a
decision as it is made, and why systems for doing so are or are not used.
That includes:

- argumentation notations and tools: IBIS, gIBIS, QOC and their successors;
- design space analysis and argumentation-based rationale;
- the cost and benefit of capture, and who bears each;
- premature formalization and the cognitive overhead of explicit structure;
- the groupware adoption problems that the design-rationale literature
  cites as its own.

All nine works in the batch take `design-rationale` as their only tag.
Grudin's two papers are about groupware in general, not rationale capture.
They are filed under this word because the rationale literature takes
their cost-and-benefit argument as its central explanation, and that is
the question the owner is asking of them.

## Alternatives considered

- **`society-and-governance`, `epistemology` or `cognition`.** Each is the
  nearest wrong word for some of the batch and the wrong word for the rest.
- **`human-computer-interaction`, or `cscw`.** Broader, and it would hold
  Grudin and Shipman and Marshall without the stretch noted above. But the
  owner's question is rationale capture, and a word for all of HCI would
  invite filing under it whatever an interface paper happens to be about.
  Widen the word if the holdings outgrow it.
- **Separate words for argumentation tools and for their failure modes.**
  With nine works, one word is enough. The failure-mode literature is
  about the tools, and is not read apart from them.

## Consequences

- A later argument-development scheme in this record, if the owner adopts
  one, has its prior art and its known failure modes under one tag page.
- Work on argumentation as logic or rhetoric, as opposed to its capture in
  a system, still goes under `logic` or `philosophy-of-language`.
