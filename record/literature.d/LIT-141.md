# LIT-141: Emergence: from physics to biology, sociology, and computer science

Ross H. McKenzie (2025), *arXiv preprint* — [ARXIV-2508.08548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08548)

## Key takeaways

- Taking novelty (the whole has properties the parts lack) as the single defining mark of emergence, McKenzie argues reality is stratified into levels with their own ontology and epistemology, and that the key move in understanding emergence is finding a mesoscale at which new, weakly interacting entities appear, studied via effective theories and toy models.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

McKenzie defines an emergent property by novelty: a many-part system has properties its parts lack. He reviews other putative marks — universality, order, complexity, unpredictability, irreducibility, diversity, self-organisation, discontinuities and singularities — noting it is not established whether any is necessary or sufficient for novelty. Through examples spanning physics, biology, the social sciences and computing, he frames emergence via scales and a stratified reality, stresses the mesoscale and modularity, and presents effective theories and toy models (the Ising model above all) as the core methods.
