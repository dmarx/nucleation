# NOTE-2047: Pettit — On the people's terms, publisher previews

## Contribution

As far as the openings show: legitimacy defined as popular control,
analysed into three properties of the people's influence, each paired with
an institutional means.

## Key results

- **Justice and legitimacy** ("Political legitimacy", p. 130): "It is one
  thing to argue that the social order imposed by a state is just, it is
  quite another to argue that the political imposition of that order is
  legitimate."
- **Control** ("Democratic control", p. 239): "people will control the
  state just insofar as they have an individualized, unconditioned and
  efficacious influence that pushes it in a direction that they find
  acceptable", with "unconditioned independence by the resistive character
  of the citizenry" and "efficacy by the insulation of the channels of
  popular influence against the distorting effects of electoral pressures
  and private lobbies."
- **The tradition** (Conclusion, p. 293): "The main ideas in the republican
  tradition are: freedom as non-domination, the mixed constitution and the
  contestatory citizenry." "The republican theory of legitimacy gives the
  state a democratic job specification, requiring it to operate under
  equally shared, popular control."

## Assumptions

Freedom as non-domination is the core value; legitimacy is distinct from
justice.

## Concepts

Freedom as non-domination; the mixed constitution; the contestatory
citizenry; popular control; individualized, unconditioned and efficacious
influence; the resistive citizenry.

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-130](../terms.d/TERM-130.md).** "Contestatory citizenry" and "unconditioned"
  influence belong to the same family as the term's protected
  contestability; the openings do not define "unconditioned".
- **[QUESTION-482](../questions.d/QUESTION-482.md).** Insulating popular influence from "electoral
  pressures and private lobbies" is a requirement of independence, stated
  for the people's influence over the state.
- **[CLAIM-724](../claims.d/CLAIM-724.md).** Nothing read on classes of opposition.
- The exchange's A83 is partly verified: control must be "unconditioned"
  and "efficacious" against domination; its gloss "not dependent on
  government officials' willingness to cooperate" is not in the text read.

## Limitations

Only the opening paragraphs of three parts. The meaning of
"unconditioned", the model of contestation and the institutional argument
are unread.
