# NOTE-tmphkehf: Umpleby (2016) — Vladimir Lefebvre's Theory of Two Systems of Ethical Cognition

## Contribution

A short, appreciative account of Lefebvre's two ethical systems by a collaborator, with no mathematics and no tests. It is read as a thin stand-in for Lefebvre (1980), LIT-tmpor36r, and Lefebvre (1982), LIT-tmp37qct.

## Key results

1. p. 65: two systems come from one model, "Depending on whether the combination rule for the exponents is addition or multiplication".
2. p. 65: "In the first ethical system, the end does not justify the means. In the second ethical system, the end does justify the means."
3. p. 66: the first "is dominant in the West". There "a saint is willing to compromise"; in the second, "a saint is willing to confront".
4. p. 67: people are "imprinted" with one system early. Umpleby says, without sources, that the theory was used by both governments as the USSR collapsed.

## Assumptions

Cultural ethical differences are deep, binary and imprinted, and anecdotes show them.

## Concepts

Reflexion; the three-level model; first and second ethical systems; saint, hero, philistine and dissembler; compromise and confrontation.

## Bearing on the record

- **The exchange** (A11 §5): confirms the two systems and their cultural reading. A11's table puts the rejection of compromise in "system I". In Umpleby that is the stance of the second, Soviet system, while the first, Western system makes the compromiser the saint. A's table should not be filed as Lefebvre's until the primary is read.
- **CLAIM-404**: confirms only Lefebvre's place in the reflexive-control tradition.

## Limitations

Three pages, anecdotal and uncritical, by a collaborator. No substitute for the model.
