# THEORY-1437: Cumberworth and Reinhardt review simulation results indicating that, in DNA origami, a staple's later domains bind without a second translational entropy penalty once its first domain is bound, while each loop closed with the scaffold adds a penalty that depends on the order of binding, so that in the small designs simulated staples tend to bind fully once started and assembly needs no nucleation barrier, unlike DNA bricks

## Source

Cumberworth and Reinhardt (2025), [LIT-2121](../literature.d/LIT-2121.md); read in part in
[NOTE-1626](../notes.d/NOTE-1626.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Later domains** (§2.1): they bind "within the same overall structure,
  and there is no analogous loss of translational degrees of freedom upon
  bonding", but each closes a loop whose entropic cost "depends on the
  order in which the staple domains bond to the scaffold".
- **Full binding once started** (§§2.1, 2.3): "once a staple bonds with
  its first domain, it will typically bond fully before additional staples
  bond", in the small designs simulated.
- **No barrier for origami** (§2.3): free-energy projections "mostly
  downhill"; for bricks a barrier "is essential for the target structure to
  form in high yields" (§2.2).
- **Could have come out otherwise**: the simulations could have shown
  staples binding partially and interleaving, or origami needing a barrier
  like bricks.

## What this does not say

- That it holds for large or 3D origami: the authors cite evidence that
  full binding fails in larger 3D lattice designs.
- That it is measured: it is a review of simulation.
- That origami cannot have a barrier: one can be designed in by stacking,
  a long loop-closing staple or toehold exchange.
- That blocking never matters: it is judged rare at typical
  concentrations, which leaves low concentrations open.
