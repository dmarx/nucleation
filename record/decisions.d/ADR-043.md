# ADR-043: A warrant field on CLAIM, saying how a claim is supported (demonstrated, empirical, interpretive) apart from whether the record accepts it and in whose voice, piloted before it is required

## Context

At A8 of the exchange of 2026-10-14 the assistant sorted what the exchange's
cases had shown: "The key distinction is between what we've demonstrated by
construction, what we've made plausible through examples, and what still
requires empirical evidence." The owner, at U9: "that's a useful
distinction, do we have anything like this already segregating CLAIMs? if
not, we should consider adding it."

What a CLAIM carries now:

- `status`, the record's credence, in THEORY's words.
- `role`, its voice: thesis, counter, granted, disclaimed, supposed
  ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)).
- `grounds` and `rests_on`, what it is supported by, without saying what
  kind of support that is.
- `line`, with `formal-apparatus` as a second line for general results
  ([ADR-040](ADR-040.md)). That marks some formal claims, by subject matter rather than
  by warrant: a formal-apparatus claim can be Proposed and conjectural.

CASE has `standing` (how much is fact) and `kind` ([ADR-042](ADR-042.md)), and ARG has
`form`. Nothing on the claim says how the claim itself is supported. Three
Active claims can be a theorem, a finding reported in five studies, and a
reading the record finds persuasive, and they look the same.

The exchange produced its own examples. [CLAIM-294](../claims.d/CLAIM-294.md) and [CLAIM-302](../claims.d/CLAIM-302.md) are
Active by construction. [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md) is Proposed and interpretive.
[CLAIM-297](../claims.d/CLAIM-297.md) is Proposed and would become a demonstrated existence result if
its model were analysed.

## Decision

- **An optional `warrant` field on CLAIM**, from a closed vocabulary. A9's
  five terms, narrowed:
  - `demonstrated`: follows from stated premises, definitions or a
    construction. It licenses the conclusion under its assumptions.
  - `empirical`: supported by observation or experiment, scoped to the
    evidence.
  - `interpretive`: supported by conceptual argument, analogy or
    synthesis, settled neither way.
  - A9's `stipulated` is dropped, as A9 suspected it should be: "it
    describes how a proposition is being used, more than how it is
    justified. The existing `role: supposed` already covers much of
    that."
  - A9's `unestablished` is held open for the pilot. It may be
    `interpretive` with status Proposed, in which case it is a second copy
    of status.
- **Single-valued, attached to the claim as stated.** A9: "Split claims
  whose formal and empirical scopes are materially different rather than
  adding a multi-valued field that silently conflates them." That is
  [ADR-040](ADR-040.md)'s practice for formal results, applied to warrant.
- **Never inherited.** A9: "`warrant: demonstrated` must not propagate
  automatically along `supports` or `rests_on` edges. A demonstrated
  premise can support a conjectural empirical conclusion." A lint could
  later report a claim marked `demonstrated` that rests on one that is not.
- **Orthogonal to status.** `Proposed` with `demonstrated` is coherent: the
  proof holds and its interpretation is disputed.
- **Piloted, not required.** Classify the claims filed from the 2026-10-14
  exchange and a heterogeneous sample of older ones (A9 named [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md),
  [CLAIM-199](../claims.d/CLAIM-199.md) and [CLAIM-250](../claims.d/CLAIM-250.md)), see whether the terms divide them without
  strain, then decide whether to require the field. A18: "test the
  classification on a handful of heterogeneous claims before changing the
  schema."

## Alternatives considered

- **Nothing new: let `formal-apparatus` and prose carry it.** The line
  marks subject matter, not warrant, and prose is what [DP-3](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-3) says gets
  followed probabilistically.
- **Warrant on ARG and grounds instead of the claim.** A9's own objection:
  one proposition may be proved in one setting and supported empirically
  in another. The record already holds ARG form for the inference. The
  field is about the claim as stated, and the remedy for mixed warrant is
  a split.
- **A confidence scale.** That is `status`. A9: "This is deliberately not
  another confidence scale."

## Consequences

- If adopted: a vocabulary `claim-warrants` in luria.yaml, an optional
  field on CLAIM, the template documenting it, and the pilot's
  classifications.
- The warrant of a claim is the record's judgement and can be wrong in the
  same way status can. It is versioned like status.
- The risk A18 named: warrant becomes "another label applied to a compound
  proposition whose mathematical and empirical parts should have been
  split instead." The pilot should count splits it forces.

What the pilot found:

- **The three terms divided the claims, with one split.** [CLAIM-298](../claims.d/CLAIM-298.md) was
  compound, as proposed: its psychological form is now [CLAIM-315](../claims.d/CLAIM-315.md)
  (empirical), on which [CLAIM-298](../claims.d/CLAIM-298.md) (interpretive) rests. No other claim
  forced a split.
- **`unestablished` is dropped.** Every claim it might have marked was
  interpretive and Proposed, so it would have been a second copy of
  status.
- **Conditions belong in the claim, not the warrant.** [CLAIM-302](../claims.d/CLAIM-302.md) and
  [CLAIM-304](../claims.d/CLAIM-304.md) are demonstrated, conditional on pseudorandom generators; the
  condition is in their titles, and the warrant is plain `demonstrated`.
- **Citing a theorem does not make a claim demonstrated.** [CLAIM-199](../claims.d/CLAIM-199.md) rests
  on Blackwell's theorem but concludes a reconciliation of two standards,
  so it is interpretive. The same holds for [CLAIM-314](../claims.d/CLAIM-314.md), which applies an
  NP-completeness result for conjunctive queries to filed arguments in
  prose: the result is demonstrated, its application is not. Neither
  forced a split, because the theorem is cited from a THEORY rather than
  claimed.
- **Demonstrated within a model is still demonstrated.** [CLAIM-307](../claims.d/CLAIM-307.md),
  [CLAIM-309](../claims.d/CLAIM-309.md) and [CLAIM-310](../claims.d/CLAIM-310.md) follow from stated models. Whether the models fit
  people or bricks is what their "What it does not say" sections are for.
- **Orthogonal to status, as intended.** Proposed and demonstrated occurs
  ([CLAIM-308](../claims.d/CLAIM-308.md), [CLAIM-313](../claims.d/CLAIM-313.md): the arithmetic holds and the reading is open), as
  does Active and interpretive ([CLAIM-312](../claims.d/CLAIM-312.md)).

**Requiring the field.** The owner, on the first version of this PR, which
left it optional: "There's no point adding this if we don't use it", and
"'failing' is a forcing function to make you fill in the field. this is a
feature, not a bug." The field is required on every CLAIM, whatever its
status, and every claim in the record was classified in the same
contribution. The lint A9 suggested, a demonstrated claim resting on one
that is not, is not built: that pattern is legitimate when the premise is
stated as a condition, so it would be a report to read, not a violation.

## Classification of the whole record

With the field required, the remaining 289 claims were classified the same
day by eight readers working from this ADR, each reading every claim in
its batch in full, and the results were applied without exception. With
the pilot, every CLAIM in the record now carries a warrant: 87
demonstrated, 12 empirical, 216 interpretive.

Rules the full pass settled, beside the pilot's:

- **An empirical hypothesis with no observation under it is
  interpretive.** Readers disagreed here ([CLAIM-105](../claims.d/CLAIM-105.md), [CLAIM-220](../claims.d/CLAIM-220.md),
  [CLAIM-263](../claims.d/CLAIM-263.md) and [CLAIM-274](../claims.d/CLAIM-274.md) were first marked empirical); they were set to
  interpretive, following the pilot's finding that `unestablished` would
  only repeat status. `empirical` means some observation the record holds
  supports the claim as stated, even if only a census of the record itself
  or a documented case.
- **A claim checked against the record's own wording and definitions is
  demonstrated** when the check is mechanical ([CLAIM-200](../claims.d/CLAIM-200.md), [CLAIM-210](../claims.d/CLAIM-210.md),
  [CLAIM-211](../claims.d/CLAIM-211.md)), and interpretive when it turns on how a thesis is read.
- **A supposition (role `supposed`) is interpretive.** None of the three
  terms describes a premise taken for the argument's sake; `interpretive`
  is the least wrong, and the role already says what kind of thing it is.
- **Superseded and Rejected claims carry the warrant of the claim as it
  was stated.**
