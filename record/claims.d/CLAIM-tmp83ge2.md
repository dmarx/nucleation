# CLAIM-tmp83ge2: Veit's common currency and ALARM's override are two regimes of dominance by one ordering, compensatory and lexicographic, so their rivalry over what the first felt valence did becomes the question whether the earliest dominant orderings are continuous: in motivational trade-off experiments, a continuous indifference curve between a noxious cost and a resource for Veit, a step at a threshold beyond which no resource buys the cost for ALARM

## The claim

THEORY-101 and THEORY-090 are declared rivals on what the first
consciousness did. For Veit it weighs options in a common currency. For
ALARM it gives an extreme signal "100% weight" through behavioural
programmes that, its authors say, do "not involve a choice". THEORY-101's
body names the condition under which the rivalry would dissolve: "if
ALARM's '100% weight' were recast as a weight in a common currency rather
than an override of it."

On the owner's reframing (CLAIM-tmpz8lli) both are dominance by an
ordering of the animal's own states.
- **Veit's currency is compensatory dominance.** The dominant ordering
  trades off against the others in proportion: a larger resource buys a
  larger cost. As an ordering of cost–resource pairs it is continuous,
  and by Debreu's theorem it has a continuous numerical representation
  (CLAIM-tmp85p0g, part 3).
- **ALARM's override is lexicographic dominance.** Above threshold, the
  alarm dimension decides, and other concerns decide only its ties. As
  an ordering of cost–resource pairs it is lexicographic, and on a
  continuum it has no numerical representation at all (CLAIM-tmp85p0g,
  part 2). ALARM's "no choice" is the limiting case in which the
  dominant ordering leaves a single option at the top.

So the rivalry does not dissolve as THEORY-101 imagined, into one
currency with an extreme weight: a lexicographic order is not an extreme
weight in any currency. It is recast. The two accounts agree that the
earliest valence is dominance by an ordering of the animal's own states,
and disagree on whether that ordering is continuous.

## The test

THEORY-101's promote_when already names the experiment: "an animal
accepting a noxious cost for a resource, in proportion to both". Fit the
animal's choices between cost–resource pairs.
- **Veit predicts** a continuous indifference curve: for every cost there
  is a resource that buys it, and the price rises smoothly.
- **ALARM predicts** a step: below a threshold of cost, trade-offs (or
  none, if there is no choice at all); above it, no resource buys the
  cost.

The prediction is about the earliest affective systems, so the
comparison is across lineages, as THEORY-101's promote_when says. Both
regimes can occur in one animal at different intensities (a currency
below the threshold, an override above), and on this reframing that is
expected rather than a contradiction: it is one ordering whose dominance
becomes lexicographic past a point.

## What it does not say

- That either account is right about the origin of consciousness, or
  that either regime is felt. The recasting is functional.
- That the mathematical contrast settles the empirical one. A finite set
  of tested pairs can always be fitted by a currency (CLAIM-tmp85p0g,
  part 1), so the test needs enough pairs to tell a steep curve from a
  step, and a design that shows the step does not move with the size of
  the resource.
