summary: >-
  Chen, Zhu and Zheng (2026), Auditing the Product, Not the Recursion: The
  Evaluation Evidence Behind Recursive Self-Improvement Claims. A
  meta-research census of 114 eligible arXiv studies (frozen October 2026),
  90 of which call their own system recursive self-improvement (RSI), coded
  from full text against a codebook that separates a *product* claim (the
  final system is better) from four *process* claims: accumulation across
  rounds, repeatability of the loop, improver gain, and compounding.
  Evaluation of the product is common; evidence for the process is rare.
  Three propositions explain why product evidence cannot stand in for
  process evidence: selection among noisy scores inflates development gains;
  an audit grader that shares the development judge's bias inherits it; and
  when the improver changes with the system state, the improver's effect is
  not identified from the trajectory. The paper ends with a claim-indexed
  reporting standard. Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
