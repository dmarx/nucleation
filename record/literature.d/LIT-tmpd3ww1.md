# LIT-tmpd3ww1: Back to Bentham? Explorations of Experienced Utility

Daniel Kahneman, Peter P. Wakker & Rakesh Sarin (1997), *Quarterly Journal
of Economics* 112(2):375–405 — DOI-10.1162/003355397555235

## Key takeaways

- **Two utilities.** "“Decision utility” is the weight of an outcome in a
  decision. “Experienced utility” is hedonic quality, as in Bentham’s
  usage" (p. 375). An amnesic patient who is shocked by one of two toasters
  is indifferent between them. His decision utilities are equal and his
  experienced utilities are not (p. 376).
- **Instant utility is the measured primitive.** It is "a measure of
  hedonic and affective experience, which can be derived from immediate
  reports of current subjective experience or from physiological indices"
  (p. 376). It is bipolar with a "natural zero point" at neutrality
  (p. 380).
- **Remembered utility follows Peak-End.** In film clips, colonoscopies,
  cold water and loud sounds, global evaluations track the average of peak
  and end, and duration has "little or no independent effect" (p. 381).
- **Choices follow memory.** Most cold-water subjects chose to repeat the
  Long trial, with 60 s at 14°C plus 30 s of slightly less painful water,
  over the Short one: 17 of 21 among those whose pain fell, 69% overall
  (pp. 386–387). The decision was dominated, and "the statement that
  decisions maximize utility is not a tautology; it can be proved false"
  (p. 388).
- **Total utility is an integral.** Three axioms make total utility the
  integral of a nondecreasing transform of instant utility: neutral
  concatenation, monotonicity in instant utility, and monotonicity in total
  utility, which gives separability. Instant utility need only be ordinal
  apart from its zero (Theorem 1, pp. 390–391).
