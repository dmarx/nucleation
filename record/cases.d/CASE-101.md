# CASE-101: Three simulated populations with the same turnover and the same aggregate statistics: one whose members respond alike to a common input, one that transmits its norms to its replacements, and one held together by an institution that selects and trains its members

## The case

A12 §7 stated the three arms:

- **A, common input.** "Individuals have little interaction, but all
  respond to the same repeating external events. The aggregate is highly
  predictable because the environment is predictable."
- **B, reproducing organization.** "Individuals inherit norms from
  previous members, modify them through interaction, and transmit them
  onward."
- **C, institutionally maintained.** Individuals turn over and stay
  coordinated "because an enduring external institution selects, trains,
  and governs them."

The arms are built so that observable aggregate statistics and turnover
rates match. A11 §9 had a third arm of its own instead of C: an original
population that splits in two after a relocation ([CASE-105](CASE-105.md)).

## What it would show

Where the criteria disagree. A12: a common-input population "might
display high observational persistence but little endogenous historical
organization"; an institutionally maintained one "might exhibit a highly
effective macrostate while its boundary must include the external
governing institution"; a reproducing one "might show strong genealogical
continuity while lacking exact dynamical independence". The criteria to
compare are Krakauer et al.'s A*, A and nC ([LIT-1137](../literature.d/LIT-1137.md), unread), dynamical
independence ([THEORY-257](../theory.d/THEORY-257.md)), effective information ([THEORY-222](../theory.d/THEORY-222.md)) and
[CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)'s priced block.

The record already holds the answer for arm A on one criterion, which the
exchange did not cite: Bourrat ([LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md), [NOTE-027](../notes.d/NOTE-027.md)) argues that an
environment that fully determines S makes A* positive for "a
reconstructed entity", and that only an interventional Â scores it zero.
So the study should add Â, with the caution [NOTE-027](../notes.d/NOTE-027.md) records: Bourrat's
claim that Â equals Krakauer's A is false in general. Arm C is
[CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s made-versus-imposed boundary in population form.

A negative result is informative too. A11 §9: if the measures cannot tell
the arms apart, "temporal information propagation alone is insufficient
to recover the kind of historical social individuality that fandom seems
to exhibit." Fictional by construction; nothing is run.

## Note of 2026-10-23: a precedent for arm B

A5 of the exchange of 2026-10-23, "Framework Overview Request" cited Jacobs and Campbell (1961, [LIT-1423](../literature.d/LIT-1423.md), Unavailable)
for this design's transmission arm: a norm surviving the replacement of
all who set it. A5: CASE-101's contribution "should concern
*discriminating mechanisms of persistence*, not demonstrating
intergenerational transmission itself." [CASE-148](CASE-148.md), perspective
transplantation, is the extension A4 proposed.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Krakauer et al. read, and the split into S and E

[LIT-1137](../literature.d/LIT-1137.md) is now read ([NOTE-1400](../notes.d/NOTE-1400.md)), so "unread" above no longer holds.
The reading confirms that arm A, common input, is where A* fails, as
Bourrat argued and [THEORY-424](../theory.d/THEORY-424.md) holds. It adds a condition on the design.
A conditions on the environment, so it scores the common-input arm near
zero only if the common input is among the environment's variables. All
three of Krakauer et al.'s measures are relative to the split between
system and environment, so the study should state which variables it
puts in E for each arm. The proposal is otherwise unchanged.
