# QUESTION-279: What would certify that no remedy is available to an institutional arrangement, rather than that its present procedures failed to find, authorize or carry one out before the deadline?

## Why it is a question

The owner, at U10, quoting A9's "correctability frontier": "reminds me of
the 'if I can't find it, it isn't there' stuff. let's poke out the
resource-boundedness here."

A10 split "No remedy is available" into four propositions, which "do not
form a simple universal hierarchy": existence (some feasible sequence of
actions would achieve the corrective objective), knowability (enough
information exists somewhere to identify it), findability (the arrangement
can discover it with the search it has) and deployability (it can
authorize, coordinate and execute it before the deadline). And three
statements that can differ in truth value in one situation:

- "We have not found a lawful means of returning this person": a report of
  search failure.
- "No lawful means of returning this person exists": an exclusion of
  alternatives.
- "We cannot find and execute a lawful means before the deadline": a
  resource- and time-relative capability claim.

The record has the structure for items. [CLAIM-312](../claims.d/CLAIM-312.md): a search that fails
by the deadline establishes operational unavailability outright, and
absence only with a certificate that the unsearched remainder cannot hold
the item, from exhaustion, an invariant of the arrangement, or a
closed-world default. [CLAIM-311](../claims.d/CLAIM-311.md) is why exhaustion is not required: a
sorted index certifies non-membership. [TERM-055](../terms.d/TERM-055.md) is the availability, and
[TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md) carries it to corrective objectives.

What makes it open for remedies:

- **The search space is not a collection.** A remedy is a plan across
  institutions, and its space includes coalitions ([CLAIM-457](../claims.d/CLAIM-457.md)) and legal
  constructions not yet tried. What would play the sorted index's part, an
  invariant that rules out the unsearched plans, is unclear.
- **The searcher may be the party.** A10: "one actor may influence the
  search environment faced by the actor responsible for reviewing or
  correcting its behavior." An executive's statement that it cannot comply
  is "evidence about the government's stated position", and a certificate
  issued by the party whose conduct is challenged is not independent
  ([CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)'s requirement, for a judgement–observation loop).
- **The budget is endogenous.** A10: "for institutions, the budget is itself
  partly an outcome of political and organizational decisions", so an
  office that no longer exists can make "correction is impossible"
  operationally true. Two questions then: "What can the system correct
  now?" and "How did it acquire, preserve, or lose that capacity?"
- **A real absence exists too.** After the East Wing's demolition
  ([CASE-170](../cases.d/CASE-170.md)) the original fabric cannot be kept by any plan; at CECOT
  ([CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md)) a return proved possible after it was said not to be. A10: "A
  useful theory must distinguish a remedy that truly no longer exists from
  one that exists but cannot be discovered or activated."

An answer would say what evidence licenses each of A10's three
statements, which of them a court is entitled to accept from a party, and
what an arrangement must maintain beforehand so that a true "no remedy"
can be certified quickly and a false one exposed. A10's design: two or
three institutions with the same information, authority, resources and
actions, differing only in how the information is arranged (pre-indexed
plans and escalation paths, unstructured search, or complementary
information without a channel), alike in routine operation and parting
under an urgent correction; A10: "The project should not take the
divergence alone as evidence for its ontology."

## Note of 2026-10-29: two courts' answers to a party's account of itself

Two readings of this date give partial answers, of one shape.

- **The Supreme Court in *Abrego Garcia***, [LIT-1606](../literature.d/LIT-1606.md) ([NOTE-1376](../notes.d/NOTE-1376.md)), did
  not accept the government's position as settling what could be done. It
  left in place the requirement to facilitate release and asked the
  government to "share what it can concerning the steps it has taken and
  the prospect of further steps": a court asking for the evidence behind a
  claim of inability before accepting it.
- **The Grand Chamber in *El-Masri***, [LIT-1619](../literature.d/LIT-1619.md) ([NOTE-1391](../notes.d/NOTE-1391.md)), has a
  burden-shifting rule (§§ 152–153, 165–167): where events lie within a
  State's exclusive knowledge, it must give "a satisfactory and convincing
  explanation", and a bare denial against concordant evidence fails. It
  is a rule for facts, not for claims of inability, but it is the same
  shape: the account of the party whose conduct is challenged is not
  accepted on its say-so where it alone holds the evidence.

Neither says what would certify that no remedy exists. The question is
unchanged and stays Open.
