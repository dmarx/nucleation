# LEAD-198: Test whether the joint commitment supplies the motive and the conspicuous split the content of children's equal division, by crossing commitment with the availability of a salient or known split

## Design

**At age five and over** (tacit salience available). Collaborating pairs
either make a joint commitment or do not; the division they face either
has a salient split or not. Visibly unequal contributions make a
proportional split salient instead of an equal one.

| | Salient split | No salient split |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment | Settle quickly on the salient split, equal or proportional | Effort to settle; protest about the process |
| No commitment | Settle on the salient split only where outcomes are interdependent | Nothing to settle; keep more |

**At age three** (no tacit salience, no recursion). Commitment against
none, crossed with a division whose equal answer the child has already
stated as the norm against a novel division with no stated norm.

| | Equal norm stated | Novel division, no norm |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment | Equal split | No settled split; appeal to the partner or an adult |
| No commitment | Keep more, as with windfalls | Keep more |

**Measure the motive** as well as the split: protest, justification and
leave-taking, as [THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)'s evidence does. Killen and Smetana report
that no study has examined why three-year-olds share equally after joint
work.

## First step

Search the developmental literature for studies that cross commitment with
collaboration ([THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)'s own promote_when) or with salience, before
designing anything.
