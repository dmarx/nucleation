# CLAIM-1396: An apparent objective can have four different sources

## The claim

A11 §12 (https://chatgpt.com/share/6b07d7ae-9434-83ea-888b-9b20796f291c):

| Type of optimization | Source of apparent objective |
|---|---|
| Explicit | A specified objective directly governs decisions. |
| Institutional | Authority relations organize decisions around a prevailing criterion. |
| Selectional | Differential reproduction favours organizations exhibiting particular behaviour. |
| Descriptive | An observer fits an objective to the behaviour of an organization. |

"A corporation may behave as though it maximizes shareholder value because
of its particular governance regime. That does not establish that
shareholder value exists as a primitive organizational motivation." So
"the paperclip maximizer metaphor might itself be guilty of reification:
treating an objective inferred from organized behaviour as the entity
responsible for producing that behaviour."

## What it rests on

[CLAIM-482](CLAIM-482.md) (Active): an organization's revealed preference exists exactly
when its winning moves never cycle, is fitted to one regime of authority,
and does not predict what an intervention on authority does. That makes
the descriptive objective a fit, and the institutional one its source in
one regime. [CLAIM-1359](CLAIM-1359.md) (A6's) holds the selectional route: organizations
can converge on power with no instrumental reasoning, because those that
fail to keep what their continuation needs become less prevalent.

It qualifies the owner's U4 ([CLAIM-1398](CLAIM-1398.md)) without contradicting it:
"We're already paperclip maximizing" is true of what corporations do on
any of the four readings, and the four differ in what would stop it.

## Note of 2026-11-26: what separates the four (the assistant)

A gap: A says the four "should not be conflated" and gives no way to tell
them apart. Each answers a different intervention, holding the others
fixed:

- **Change the specification** (rewrite the target, the bonus formula):
  behaviour that follows it was explicit.
- **Change the authority** (who decides, who can veto, with the
  specification fixed): behaviour that follows it was institutional, and
  [CLAIM-482](CLAIM-482.md) says the fitted objective will fail to predict the change.
- **Change the selective environment** (what lets organizations survive
  and grow, with every organization's specification and authority fixed):
  a selectional objective moves the population's composition, not any one
  organization's conduct, so it shows in entry, exit and growth rates, not
  in decisions.
- **None of these**: a descriptive objective has no mechanism of its own,
  and so predicts under none of them. It is what remains when the other
  three are absent, which is the sense in which treating it as a cause is
  a reification.

The four can coexist, as A says; the interventions say how much of an
apparent objective each carries. The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
