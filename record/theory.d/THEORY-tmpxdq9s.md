# THEORY-tmpxdq9s: How far fear falls during an extinction or exposure session, or across sessions, does not predict how much fear is expressed at follow-up, in animals, human laboratory samples and fearful human samples

## Source

Craske, Treanor, Conway, Zbozinek & Vervliet, LIT-tmpz7j0g, read in full
in NOTE-tmpqrvri from the NIH author manuscript (PMC4114726): the
sections "Inhibitory Learning vs Habituation and Behavioral Testing
Approaches to Exposure" and "(1) Expectancy Violation".

## What was actually shown

The paper reports two findings from the literature. First, "the amount by
which fear has reduced at completion of extinction is not predictive of
the amount of fear expressed at the follow-up extinction retest in either
animals or human laboratory samples". Second, "the amount by which fear
reduces by the end of an exposure trial or series of exposure trials is
not predictive of the fear level expressed at follow-up assessment in
fearful human samples". It adds that sustained arousal during extinction
goes with less fear at retest, and that in several of the authors'
studies "failure to habituate throughout exposure therapy was not
associated with poorer outcomes".

What could have come out otherwise: within-session habituation could
have predicted outcome, as habituation models (Foa & Kozak 1986) hold it
must.

## What this does not say

- **That fear reduction is harmful.** It is said not to predict, not to
  hinder.
- **That the finding holds in diagnosed clinical samples generally.** The
  cited exposure studies are of fearful, mostly analog, samples
  (acrophobia, contamination fear, public speaking).
- **What does predict outcome.** The paper points to expectancy
  violation and to variability of fear. Both are argued and cited, not
  established here.

## Connections

- **CLAIM-tmp8z857.** The authors' gloss, "divergence in response
  systems", is a cited instance of the claim's dissociation between
  anxious affect and what has been learned (CLAIM-283, CLAIM-349). It is
  not a measure of defensive authority.
