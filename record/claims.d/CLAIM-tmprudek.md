# CLAIM-tmprudek: If the self is defined to include whatever an agent cares about, the claim that all motivation serves the self is true by definition and rules nothing out; the thesis has content only with a criterion of the self's extent that can come apart from what the agent cares about

## The claim

Psychological egoism has long been charged with equivocation. Read one
way, that every motive is the agent's own, it is trivially true. Read the
other way, that every motive aims at the agent's own good, it needs
argument. The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmpjylq8](CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)) faces the same risk at a
new joint, the extent of the self. If the self is whatever the agent
identifies with, and identifying with something means caring about it,
then "all action serves an extended self" says only "all action serves
what the agent cares about".

The thesis avoids this only by tying the self's extent to a criterion
that can diverge from caring, and predicting that motivation follows the
criterion. Candidates:

- perceived self–other overlap (Aron's scale);
- identity fusion;
- salient self-categorization;
- relatedness.

## What it does not say

- **Not that the thesis is false.** It says what the thesis must add to be
  testable.
- **Not that every criterion of the self is circular.** Self-categorization
  theory's salience by fit, for instance, is fixed by the comparative
  context, not by what the agent values ([THEORY-tmpo85ke](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo85ke.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-29: candidate criteria, tested

Works read in place of unavailable papers test the candidate criteria this
objection lists.

- **Self-definition and self-investment come apart** (Leach et al.,
  [NOTE-tmp8tnhr](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8tnhr.md)). Identification splits into self-definition (seeing
  oneself as like the average member) and self-investment (satisfaction,
  solidarity, centrality). They correlate .61 to .77, but faced with their
  group's misdeeds, self-definition predicted guilt and investment
  predicted defending the group. Self-stereotyping is a criterion of the
  self's extent that separates from caring.
- **The overlap circles are weaker than supposed.** In Leach et al. the
  self–group circles correlated more with satisfaction and solidarity than
  with self-stereotyping, against the authors' prediction. Branand, Mashek
  and Aron ([NOTE-tmpd7oz0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpd7oz0.md)) report that the IOS scale is validated as
  subjective closeness. Their cognitive signs of inclusion are better
  candidates, because caring alone does not predict them: slower
  self-judgements where a partner differs, and self–partner confusions in
  memory.
- **Implicitly, partly separable** (Pinter and Greenwald, [NOTE-tmpyj4e9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyj4e9.md)). An
  IAT of self–group association and one of liking correlated .48 to .66,
  and their explicit versions .71.
- **Salience moves without caring** (Smith and Mackie, [NOTE-tmpfj8cp](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfj8cp.md);
  Spears and Postmes, [NOTE-tmpeg80o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeg80o.md)). Emotions and influence shift with
  whichever category is made salient by instruction or context. That fixes
  the self's level independently of what one values. But the highly
  identified reappraise their group's misdeeds to avoid guilt, so caring
  feeds back.
- **Inside social identity theory** (Scheepers and Ellemers,
  [NOTE-tmpwzzpw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwzzpw.md)). Tajfel defined social identity to include "the value or
  emotional significance attached to that membership", so the theory's
  social self already contains caring, though its measures separate the
  two. The chapter reports, without a source, that where they are
  separated, commitment predicts behaviour better. If so, motivation
  follows caring rather than the self's extent.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the criterion in time

- **Future-self overlap predicted where caring did not** (Hershfield,
  [NOTE-tmp70rn1](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp70rn1.md)). Similarity to one's self in ten years, on overlapping
  circles, predicted patient choice, and directly reported liking and
  caring did not. But the caring reports were near ceiling, so the
  separation is suggested, not shown.
- **The objection reappears in the theory of welfare** (Luper, SEP,
  [NOTE-tmpl7p19](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl7p19.md)). If posthumous harm is the thwarting of desires, and any
  satisfied desire counts as one's good, legacy-seeking is self-interest
  by definition. The same triviality, one level down.
