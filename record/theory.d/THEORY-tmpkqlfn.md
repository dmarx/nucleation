# THEORY-tmpkqlfn: Zhang, Li, Zheng, Genin and Chen show that a perforated elastomer whose cells become bistable under compression breaks into domains of opposite rotation separated by ordered, reproducible walls, which a strong-coupling continuum limit describes as the static kink–antikink lattice of the φ⁴ equation

## Source

Zhang, Li, Zheng, Genin & Chen (2019), LIT-tmpzqudr; read in
NOTE-tmpwjex5.

## The result

Above a critical strain, "Spontaneous symmetry breaking took place": cells
rotate inward or outward, and "highly localized topological domain walls
emerged to accommodate the incompatible deformation", "ordered and robust
(as confirmed by 6 tests for each case)". A rod-and-spring model reduces
each cell to a double-well potential. With strong coupling, the continuum
limit is the λφ⁴ equation. Its periodic solution, a kink–antikink lattice
with "Q=0", matches experiment and simulation. Walls appear only above
about 7 cells, and further pairs are added in steps of the theoretical
period. Three other architectures that meet the two design conditions also
show alternating kinks. Disordered, stochastic localization was the
alternative outcome.

## What it does not show

- Kane–Lubensky topological polarization or zero modes. "Topological" is
  the kink's charge, and the paper's "topological mechanical
  polarizations" has no winding-number calculation behind it.
- Protection of single kinks: with Q = 0 each kink is paired with an
  antikink, and the walls are held by lattice pinning.
- Persistence without load. Below the critical strain the cells are
  monostable and the walls are gone.
- That a wall is softer than the bulk, or any dynamics; the solitons are
  static.
