# CLAIM-tmps5hv6: Anticipated objections are better met by precise definitions, constructions and results than by hedges

## The claim

U19: "rather than providing our readers with a map with which to attack our
work, let's try to address anticipated criticisms directly and generally
withhold hedging. we shouldn't need to concern ourselves with hedging if our
formalizations are sufficiently precise." A49: "That doesn't mean concealing
limitations or presenting conjectures as theorems. It means making the scope of
every claim sufficiently precise that many of the objections no longer apply
... **define rather than defend; demonstrate rather than disclaim.**"

Its best instance is [CLAIM-tmplxhnv](CLAIM-tmplxhnv.md): the collapse objection answered by a definition.
Its model sentence became the manuscript's §9 style ("We distinguish
noncommutativity of stochastic state transitions from incompatibility of quantum
observables and contextuality of empirical measurement systems").

## What it removed

Between outlines v3 and v4: the Limitations section with its failure conditions
(A48 §15.5), the H1–H4 hierarchy, the limits of the ICL analogy, the novelty
section ([CLAIM-tmp7cc3w](CLAIM-tmp7cc3w.md)), and the refrains' status as "exploratory stimuli". A49 said
the failure conditions belonged in the methods "as **predefined decision
rules**"; A52 did not put them there. Two substantive points went in the same
sweep without being hedges ([CLAIM-tmpo49t2](CLAIM-tmpo49t2.md)). Whether the rule holds is the question its
defeat condition asks: some limits are facts about the evidence, not about the
formalization.
