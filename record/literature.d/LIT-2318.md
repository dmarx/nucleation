summary: >-
  Bao et al. (2026), Contemporary AI lacks the imagination to diverge or
  negate in science. Authors of 121,640 recent preprints (bioRxiv, medRxiv,
  ChemRxiv, PsyArXiv, EdArXiv, SocArXiv; arXiv excluded as dominating
  pretraining corpora) were asked to rate hypotheses that 26 LLMs generated
  from the puzzle and context extracted from their own papers, with leakage
  of the authors' hypotheses filtered out. 6,749 scientists returned 25,139
  rating sets on novelty, feasibility, probability and adoption
  favourability. Findings: non-reasoning LLMs cluster in a narrow "hivemind"
  while reasoning models spread wider; every model proposes null hypotheses
  less often than humans; scientists favour ideas resembling their own and
  weight probability over novelty, with senior social scientists the most
  resistant; LLM ideas rate worst in social science; LLM judges, novelty
  metrics and top reward models agree with experts near chance, while a
  Qwen3-14B reward model trained on the ratings beats a human peer-review
  agreement baseline. A 39-million-paper OpenAlex analysis finds the share
  of abstracts with no null claims rose after ChatGPT's release, and an
  agent-based simulation argues an interior optimum for AI adoption. Read
  for the exchange "luria experiment design".
