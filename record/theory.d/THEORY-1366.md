# THEORY-1366: Freidlin and Wentzell hold that for a dynamical system under small random perturbations, the behaviour over long times, exit from a domain of attraction and which stable set the stationary distribution concentrates on, is decided by the logarithmic asymptotics of the probabilities of rare events on finite intervals, given by the infimum of an action functional over the relevant paths

## Source

Freidlin and Wentzell, *Random Perturbations of Dynamical Systems*, 3rd
ed. (2012), [LIT-2035](../literature.d/LIT-2035.md); read in [NOTE-1562](../notes.d/NOTE-1562.md) from the front matter
only (prefaces and Introduction, pp. i–xxviii). Stand-in for the exit
problem: Berglund (2011), [LIT-2037](../literature.d/LIT-2037.md).

## What was actually shown

What was read is an outline, not the theorems.

- **The programme (p. xix).** Ranking which unlikely events on a finite
  interval are more and less improbable "serves as a key to the question
  of what the behavior, with probability close to 1, of the process" will
  be on an infinite interval.
- **The functional (p. xx).** P{ρ(Xᵋ, ϕ) < δ} ≈ exp{−ε⁻²S(ϕ)}; as ε → 0
  "only the summand with smallest S(ϕ) becomes essential.", so the constant
  in the asymptotics of a rare event is the infimum of S over its paths.
- **Exit (p. xx).** With nearly equal small exit probabilities on
  successive intervals and weak dependence between distant ones, the
  first exit time "has an approximately exponential distribution". The
  exit-time theorem itself, lim 2ε log E τ = inf over the boundary of the
  quasi-potential, is restated in [LIT-2037](../literature.d/LIT-2037.md) (its Theorem 2.2).
- **Several stable sets (p. xxi).** If passage from K₁ to K₂ costs V₁₂ and
  back costs V₂₁ with V₁₂ < V₂₁, "then it becomes plausible that for small
  ε the process spends most of the time in the neighborhood of" K₂; the
  V's are infima of S over connecting paths. Not restated in the stand-in.

## What this does not say

- That these are the book's theorems as stated: only the outline was read.
- Anything about sharp asymptotics or prefactors. The book gives "rough
  results on large deviations" only (p. xxiv).
- Anything about random vector fields, which the book excludes (p. ix), or
  about noise that is not small.
- Anything about organizations, readiness or capabilities kept for rare
  use. That rare events govern a diffusion's long-run behaviour is not, by
  itself, an argument about how to value a rarely used capability.
