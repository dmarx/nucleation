# CLAIM-710: Removing friction can remove interruptibility

## The claim

The owner, U11 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c,
2026-11-15):

> we can view automation as the effort to reduce procedural friction, but
> paradoxically procedural friction exposes (or perhaps even characterizes)
> opportunity for intervention.

Two readings, both the owner's:

- **Exposes.** Friction (a handoff, a wait, a review, an approval that
  someone must give) is where a decision in progress becomes visible to
  someone other than whoever is making it, while it can still be changed.
  Removing the friction removes the visibility at that point.
- **Characterizes.** An opportunity to intervene is a place in a process
  where it can be stopped or redirected before its consequences settle,
  and that is what a piece of procedural friction is. On this reading,
  friction-free and interruptible are in tension by definition, not only
  as it happens.

The paradox is that the property automation is built to remove is one of
the properties through which others can act on the process. A21 §1's
caseworker, applicant and supervisor each had such a step: "the procedure
doesn't just implement a decision. **It provides opportunities for the
decision to become responsive to objections that couldn't have been fully
specified beforehand.**" A21 §5: "**Automation can preserve the formal
right to object while eliminating the practical opportunity to prevent
the harm that makes objection necessary.**" A21 §2 says which friction is
meant: meaningful intervention needs visibility, deliberation,
contestability and control, and "a delay alone guarantees none of them";
"**Politically and morally valuable friction is not delay itself, but the
preservation of meaningful points at which an unfolding process remains
open to intervention.**"

## Where it sits in the record

- [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) (Active) names what an objection needs in
  order to change what an organization does: legibility, volitional
  efficacy and correctability. This claim is about where in a process the
  occasion for an objection lies. A30 §2 of the exchange made the
  connection: "procedural friction is one possible institutional mechanism
  for maintaining the three achievements across time", and "not every
  interruption provides all three". The point of intervention this claim
  locates is the occasion; [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) says what the occasion
  must carry to work.
- [CLAIM-462](CLAIM-462.md) (Active): an arrangement's capacity for
  effective action can grow while its capacity to be corrected degrades.
  Removing friction raises the first. This claim names one way the second
  falls with it.
- [CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md) (Active), the owner's from 2026-10-26: an
  intervention can make its own correction costlier before review. With
  friction removed there is less time between action and the point after
  which that happens.
- [CASE-003](../cases.d/CASE-003.md), Northstar-A and Northstar-B, is the
  record's controlled contrast for one kind of friction: an authorized
  objection that suspends deployment, against one that does not.
- [CLAIM-410](CLAIM-410.md) (Active, the owner's): removing a constraint redistributes
  governing positions rather than reducing governance. A21 §2 says the same
  of friction: "**What looks like a reduction in friction from the
  organization's perspective may actually be a transfer of discretion from
  one group of people to another.**"
- [CASE-250](../cases.d/CASE-250.md)'s "interface friction" is a cost put in a user's way to
  steer them; it is extractive friction in A21's sense, not this.

## Attribution

A21: "I'd sharpen your observation slightly. Friction doesn't merely
*expose* opportunities for intervention. In many institutional settings,
it actually **constitutes the opportunity for intervention**". The owner's
parenthesis, "(or perhaps even characterizes)", had already made that
move, and A21 §2 then credits it to "your phrasing". The stronger form is
the owner's. A26 §VI, the synthesis the owner asked for at U14, credits
it: "Your observation about procedural friction gave the framework a more
general political dimension." A26 tells the rest of the framework as
joint ("we called"), though its coinages are A's.

## A's developments, mapped, not adopted

The exchange's A21 and A23 (ChatGPT's) build on U11. They are A's voice;
adopting any is the owner's call.

- A21 strengthens "exposes" to "constitutes": friction "actually
  **constitutes the opportunity for intervention**, because it creates the
  temporal, informational, and organizational space in which someone can
  notice a problem, exercise judgment, contest a decision, or refuse to
  participate". That is the owner's
  "characterizes", which the owner hedged.
- A21 §3: friction is distributed, not only removed. "What looks like a
  reduction in friction from the organization's perspective may actually
  be a transfer of discretion from one group of people to another." A
  benefits system that decides at once and appeals that take weeks have
  low execution friction and high contestation friction. A21 §9's
  "strongest proposition": "The distribution of friction partly
  constitutes the distribution of political power."
- A21 §4: "extractive friction" (it costs those subject to a process time
  and effort and gives them no influence) against "deliberative friction"
  (it lets reasons, exceptions and objections in). The claim is about the
  second.
- A26 §VI: "countervailing friction", "procedural mechanisms that
  constrain consequential exercises of institutional authority while
  minimizing unnecessary burdens on those subject to it".

The continuation's filing of the same exchange (share
https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b) filed four of
these as A's claims, each Proposed and in A's voice, with a proposed study:
execution against contestation friction ([CLAIM-731](CLAIM-731.md)); explanation,
contestation and interruption as three entitlements ([CLAIM-740](CLAIM-740.md));
cognitive against political friction ([CLAIM-715](CLAIM-715.md)); a slowly revised
rule over a fast process ([CLAIM-730](CLAIM-730.md)); and the timing study
[CASE-285](../cases.d/CASE-285.md). This claim stays the owner's and does not rest on them.

## What it does not say

- **That friction is good.** The owner's claim is about where intervention
  opportunities are, not that more friction is better. Extractive friction
  offers none. A's own caution, A26 §XI: "friction isn't inherently
  valuable." A23 §4: "**The aim is not more friction or less friction in
  general. It's a more just distribution of friction.**"
- **That only friction can supply them.** A33 §8 and A35 §7 argue that
  slowly set standing constraints with fast enforcement can keep a process
  interruptible without delaying it. That is filed as an objection to the
  "characterizes" reading,

## Note of 2026-11-16: Hong Kong Monetary Authority, read

A regulator's paper supports the "exposes" reading in two ways. First, its
answer to agents that outrun oversight includes restoring delay: velocity
limits commensurate "with the demonstrated effectiveness of oversight" (§4.3,
p. 19). Second, "The last opportunity for human intervention may occur"
several steps before harm is visible (p. 18). Against an overly strong
"characterizes" reading, it also shows friction without opportunity: an
approval step that remains in place but is perfunctory under volume (§4.4,
p. 20). Relation: none.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Natali, read

Natali, Naiseh, Cabitza and Frischmann ([LIT-2687](../literature.d/LIT-2687.md)) were registered against this claim from A21 §2 of the exchange. Their poster is about a different friction: "deliberate inefﬁciencies" placed "at critical points" of one user's interaction with an AI, against automation bias. That is cognitive friction, as A23 §7 itself classes it, not the procedural friction of handoffs, reviews and approvals between parties that this claim is about. It gives the claim no support and reports no evidence. It does show a case the claim's gloss of "exposes" (visible "to someone other than whoever is making it") does not settle. If the executor is the AI pipeline, a pause before its output shows a human the machine's proposal before it becomes the decision, which is exposure in the owner's sense. If the executor is the human user, the friction exposes the decision to no one but its maker, and what it opens is the executor's own correction. Whether such friction counts depends on who executes the process. The claim may want to say so.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Alfrink, read

Alfrink, Keller, Kortuem and Doorn (read as a stand-in for Harid, [LIT-2676](../literature.d/LIT-2676.md), which could not be fetched) synthesize the contestable-AI literature. Two of their findings bear here. One feature they gather is a second system that every automated decision is run through: "If disagreement between both systems occurs, decision can be flagged for human review." That is a designed point at which a decision in progress is shown to someone other than its maker before it takes effect, the claim's "exposes" reading built in. And for subjects: "Requests for human intervention are necessarily post-hoc, since they happen in response to discrete decisions." Absent such a designed point, the subject's opportunity to intervene comes after the decision. The paper supports the claim's concern as a design consensus, not as a finding: its sources are mostly "not based on empirically validated interventions".

## Note of 2026-11-16: Herd and Moynihan, read

Herd and Moynihan's 2023 review ([LIT-2699](../literature.d/LIT-2699.md), [NOTE-2021](../notes.d/NOTE-2021.md)), read as a stand-in for their 2025 JEP article
([LIT-2660](../literature.d/LIT-2660.md)), concerns friction borne by claimants: learning and compliance steps that "substantially
restrict access to basic rights, benefits, and services" (p. 96). Removing it by automation raised access:
"By 2016, 34 states used automatic" Medicaid reenrollment (p. 103). This is the extractive friction the
claim already says it is not about, and it does not bear on the claim's thesis. It sharpens two things the
claim's "What it does not say" could add: automation that removes claimant-side friction can leave the
poorest outside the automated path (the 2021 CTC by automatic deposit missed non-filers, p. 109), so friction
is moved, not removed; and the discretion a human step carries cuts both ways (case "workers tend to use
discretion in ways that are more likely to penalize Black" beneficiaries, p. 105). Relation: bears_on.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Frey and Schneider, read

The paper bears on A23 §5's development of this claim (friction as infrastructure for voice), not on the
owner's wording. Effective-voice mechanisms are steps a platform's decision must pass (a vote, a binding
petition threshold, a removal process): friction in [TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md)'s sense. Platforms, built for low
friction, keep exit and expression cheap and leave out these steps: "The absence of effective voice has
gone largely unnoticed." Relation: bears_on.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Citron, read

Citron's "Technological Due Process" ([LIT-2719](../literature.d/LIT-2719.md), [NOTE-2003](../notes.d/NOTE-2003.md)) documents, in US benefit and watch-list
systems, both ways automation can remove a point at which a decision can still be interrupted. Removing
the human step: Florida's mandatory online application offered no category for relative caregivers, and
"Eligibility workers were unable to overrule this decision" (p. 1264). Keeping the step but emptying it:
in mixed systems "Automation bias effectively turns a computer" program's suggestion into a final
decision, and "The distinction between mixed and fully automated systems has the" potential to disappear
(p. 1272); hearing officers' trust in computers makes "the guarantee of an impartial reviewer" illusory
(pp. 1283–84). The first supports the "exposes" reading. The second is a formal friction step that offers
no effective opportunity, which the claim already says it does not cover. It leaves the "characterizes"
reading open, and leans against it: Citron's remedies (audit trails, testing suites before launch and on
every change, open code, stakeholder review boards, p. 1305–12) restore intervention by upstream
constraint rather than by delay, which is [CLAIM-711](CLAIM-711.md)'s line. Relation: corroborates (the "exposes"
reading).

## Note of 2026-11-16: Bovens and Zouridis, read

Bovens and Zouridis ([LIT-2661](../literature.d/LIT-2661.md), [NOTE-2030](../notes.d/NOTE-2030.md), read in the authors' manuscript) describe the removal of the
very steps this claim locates intervention in. The street-level agency was disciplined by steps a decision
had to pass before it took effect: "The obligation to obtain the signature of their superior is another
means to check the power of" low-level servants, ex ante legal review, and in traffic cases a judge (§2.2,
§3.2). The system-level agency decides routine cases without human interference; "Only if the citizen
should" lodge a complaint or objection "will a specialized official enter into the picture" (§4), after the
decision. This supports the "exposes" reading with two documented cases. It leaves "characterizes" open and
leans against it: the authors' remedies (§6.2 "hardship clauses", a charter guaranteeing human assessment
on request; §6.1 informatization review; §6.3 published decision trees) restore intervention through a step
the citizen can trigger and through review of the system, not through delay in every case. Also relevant:
the transformation itself happened without an occasion for objection: "an explicit debate on the
desirability of these transformations never did ensue" (§7). Relation: corroborates.

## Note of 2026-11-16: de Boer and Raaphorst, read

de Boer and Raaphorst ([LIT-2674](../literature.d/LIT-2674.md), [NOTE-1997](../notes.d/NOTE-1997.md)) study a partially automated inspection system whose design
is the mirror image of the claim's case: INSPECT generated the verdict and inspectors "could deviate from
this verdict but only after getting permission from their supervisors" (p. 49). Automation here did not
remove a gate in front of the decision; it put one in front of the human's override. The study measures
self-reported enforcement style and perceived discretion, not points of intervention, so it neither
supports nor tests the claim. It does show that where a process is automated, which side of the friction
the human stands on is a design choice. Relation: bears_on (no corroboration).

## Note of 2026-11-16: Elish, read

The handoff problem (p. 50: last-minute takeover "is something humans do not do well") is a
formal point of intervention that humans cannot reliably use. It bears on what an "opportunity to
intervene" consists in, beside the friction question: an opportunity that exists on paper is not
yet effective. Relation: none.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Bovens and Zouridis, read

## Note of 2026-11-22: [CASE-285](../cases.d/CASE-285.md) run by simulation
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CASE-316 CLAIM-879 — new; the executed study and claim of LEAD-068, filed 2026-11-22 -->
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-711 — Proposed; cited in the note of 2026-11-22 on LEAD-068's simulation -->

[CASE-316](../cases.d/CASE-316.md) carries out [CASE-285](../cases.d/CASE-285.md) in a queueing model. The model has no handoffs or approvals; its "friction" is the time before an action becomes irreversible, and taking that time away, with the overseer unchanged, removed intervention: 0.994 of objectionable actions stopped at the slow speed, 0.396 at ten times it. That is [LEAD-068](../leads.d/LEAD-068.md)'s "what would count" for the "exposes" reading, in stipulated form. Precommitted limits that hold what they catch and delay nothing else won back their coverage with little delay. Two readings of that, which the record leaves to the owner ([TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md)):

- *if friction is impedance*, the limits are a stipulated process in which interruptibility is kept with no delay to the actions that pass, the kind this claim's defeated_if names against "characterizes"; being stipulated, it shows the arrangement is coherent, which [CLAIM-711](CLAIM-711.md) already argues, not that one exists;
- *if friction includes a gate the executor cannot pass alone*, the limits are friction relocated, [CLAIM-711](CLAIM-711.md)'s own defeater, and the model supports both readings alike.

[CLAIM-879](CLAIM-879.md) states the result. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Frischmann and Benesch, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-2267 — new of 2026-11-22, Proposed; the account filed from the reading for LEAD-152 -->

Frischmann and Benesch, "Friction-In-Design Regulation" ([LIT-3136](../literature.d/LIT-3136.md), [NOTE-2359](../notes.d/NOTE-2359.md), [THEORY-2267](../theory.d/THEORY-2267.md)), registered and read for [LEAD-152](../leads.d/LEAD-152.md). They state the owner's "exposes" in a sentence and without evidence: "Interconnection often should be seamful, as that friction provides opportunity/space for governance" (p. 387). Their friction is mostly time for one person to "stop and think" (p. 388), the cognitive friction this claim is not about. What bears is a distinction they report from Frischmann and Ohm's "Governance Seams" (p. 405 n. 89): a speed bump makes a driver bear a cost and does "not involve a seam or enable governance"; a checkpoint or a CAPTCHA stops passage until a condition is shown, and does. That is the claim's own caveat (friction that obstructs without offering a point of intervention) with a name in the literature, and it leans against the "characterizes" reading taken of friction as such: only the gate kind ([TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md)) is a place where someone can act. It also leaves open something the claim does not settle: every gate they describe lets the party that set it check the person passing (sobriety, humanity, identity). Whether a gate exposes a decision to intervention, and whose intervention it exposes it to, are separate questions; de Boer and Raaphorst's inspectors (note of 2026-11-16) showed the same from the other side. Relation: none (no evidence). No status changes.
