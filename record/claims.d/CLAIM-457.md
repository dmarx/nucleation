# CLAIM-457: That no single institution can bring about a remedy does not imply that no combination of them can: the institutions able together to realize a corrective outcome need include none able alone, so a true statement of unilateral inability is not a statement that the outcome is unattainable

## The claim

The owner, at U7: "the DOJ ignoring court orders not to deport people to
CECOT, then claiming they didn't have the authority to bring them back,
while CECOT claimed they didn't have the authority to send them back".

A7: "No single institution has unilateral control" does not imply "No
institution, or combination of institutions, can remedy the situation."
A8 gave the object. For a set V of institutions and a corrective outcome
J, let

K_J = { S ⊆ V : S has a feasible coordinated intervention realizing J }.

Take V = {A, B}, with A able to request release and admit, B able to
release and transfer, and J requiring a release by B and an admission by
A. Then {A} ∉ K_J and {B} ∉ K_J, while {A, B} ∈ K_J. Each institution's
statement of its own inability is true, and J is attainable. That is the
whole demonstration; nothing in it depends on the facts of CECOT, which
only show the pattern occurring ([CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md)): the return of 6 June 2025 and
the transfer of July 2025 were coordinated acts of both governments.

A8 and A9 refined K_J into four families over the same coalitions:
physically capable, legally authorized, willing, and compellable. They
need not coincide, so a coalition can be able and authorized and
unwilling, or willing and not compellable by any court.

## What it does not say

- **Joint responsibility.** A8: "joint ability to produce an outcome does
  not itself imply joint legal responsibility for producing or correcting
  it. We would need a separate normative mapping." Thompson's prospective
  institutional responsibility ([LIT-1599](../literature.d/LIT-1599.md)) is the reading that would
  supply one.
- **A joint agent.** A9: "the existence of a feasible coalition does not
  establish that coalition members share a collective intention, nor does
  it settle their responsibilities" ([CLAIM-418](CLAIM-418.md), [TERM-080](../terms.d/TERM-080.md)).
- **That any particular statement of inability was false.** It says only
  what such a statement does not establish. Which coalitions were in fact
  able, authorized and willing at CECOT is an empirical and legal
  question.
- **Anything about K_J's other structure.** K_J is upward closed when
  adding a member cannot block a plan, which need not hold: a further
  institution can veto. A8 compared it with [CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md)'s compatibility
  complex and noted the difference: "A coalition sufficient for an outcome
  is not the same kind of object as a subset of mutually compatible
  demands."

## Note of 2026-10-29: the order presupposes it, and Fiss on corrective duty

Two readings of this date bear on the claim's edges, not on its logic.

- **The order in *Abrego Garcia***, [LIT-1606](../literature.d/LIT-1606.md) ([NOTE-1376](../notes.d/NOTE-1376.md)), behind
  [CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md), says nothing about joint ability. It presupposes that the
  United States can take steps toward a release that El Salvador would
  carry out.
- **Fiss**, [LIT-1600](../literature.d/LIT-1600.md) ([NOTE-1364](../notes.d/NOTE-1364.md)), is a precedent for the separate
  normative mapping "What it does not say" asks for. In his structural
  suit the wrongdoer disappears: "the costs and burdens of reformation are
  placed on the organization, not because it has 'done wrong' … but
  because reform is needed". Corrective duty is assigned to an addressee
  without a finding of individual fault. It is one way joint ability could
  be mapped to duty; it is not part of this claim.

The claim is unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the remand order reads "facilitate" as one member's share

The district court's order on remand, [LIT-1841](../literature.d/LIT-1841.md) ([NOTE-1417](../notes.d/NOTE-1417.md)), says
what "facilitate" required of one member of a two-state arrangement: its
own share, the steps "available to them" toward a release that El Salvador
would carry out, by the means its past practice shows (requests,
embassies, travel documents, transport). It orders nothing of El Salvador.
That is this claim's construction: A can be ordered to do A's part of a
plan that needs B, without anyone ordering the outcome. Like the Supreme
Court's order, it presupposes and does not decide that the two together
can realize the outcome. The claim is unchanged.
