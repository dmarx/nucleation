## 4. Inquiry as specification: the reflexive turn

### 4.1 Inquiry is a form of specification

Learning, in §3's sense, forms capacities within a system's existing way of posing its problems. Inquiry can change that way. The record's account is the owner's: "inquiry is a form of specification" (CLAIM-470, Active, interpretive).

> A question fixes which differences would count as an answer, an inquiry adds the operations that make those differences accessible, and what it finds can stabilize into a specification that constrains later inquiry; so inquiry stands to its findings as a specification to its realizations, with one difference, that an inquiry can revise what an answer could be.

TERM-095 (Active) writes an inquiry as a tuple with six components:

- **a language** in which the question is posed;
- **an answer space** that says which differences would count as an answer;
- **interventions** that make those differences accessible;
- **observations** that report them;
- **criteria** for judging candidate answers;
- **resources** it runs on.

**An inquiry is reflexive when any of these becomes what it is pursuing.**

The tuple generalizes the effective-description regime of §2.2. CLAIM-470 adds that the regime is itself a product of inquiry and can become its object. It thereby distinguishes two ways an inquiry can change its own regime:

- **Refinement** adds distinctions within an existing language and answer space. Counterexample-guided abstraction refinement (LIT-1652) is the formal case: an abstraction is split exactly where a spurious counterexample shows it too coarse.
- **Revision** changes what counts as an answer, a criterion, or a unit of inquiry.

CLAIM-470's distinctive clause concerns revision. CLAIM-470 itself names the study that would test whether revision does work that refinement cannot (§19.4).

Two disanalogies with CEGAR, recorded in CLAIM-419's notes, mark what makes reflexive inquiry hard outside formal verification:

- **CEGAR's misfits are certified.** A spurious counterexample can be checked against the concrete model, so every wrong merge that matters eventually surfaces. Most inquirers have no concrete model to check their categories against. A move their categories forced silently leaves no trace (§2.4's third principle).
- **CEGAR only splits.** Inquiry can also add, merge, re-carve and withdraw (§2.3's second principle).

**On provenance.** The tuple, A16's reading of luria as separating "working within an inquiry regime and investigating the regime itself", and the CEGAR analogy came from ChatGPT's turns in the owner's exchange of 27 October, which the owner adopted (TERM-095, CLAIM-470). Several formal results in §§2–3 were likewise proposed or derived by assistants and checked in the record. The theory is a product of the very kind of human–AI inquiry Part III studies.

**Engineering as inquiry (added 2026-11-21).** The same relation runs the other way in engineering (CLAIM-tmphigw4, Proposed): it starts from a specification and seeks a realization, and it becomes inquiry in the reflexive sense when building revises the specification. It differs from inquiry into a given object in that its interventions make the object its observations judge. A second reading takes building as a method of inquiry, the record itself being one (CLAIM-tmpm9ep1, Proposed). The record's own case: its staging branch was specified, reviewed and checked, and its first use found a requirement nobody had stated (CASE-tmpi16kb, Active).

### 4.2 Specifying costs, and stored specifications move the frontier

CLAIM-475 (Active, interpretive) adds the resource bound. "Specifying is itself a resource-bounded realization." Formulating a question takes time, concepts and computation, as realizing a configuration takes energy and matter. In the owner's words: "I can't interrogate a question that takes me more time than I have to even ask it."

The questions an agent can ask are therefore a product of what it has already realized: its **inquiry frontier**, "the questions it can formulate, making them determinate enough to pursue, with the concepts, records, tools, time and computation it actually has" (TERM-092, Active). The frontier is distinct from the questions its language can express, from those it can pursue to a stated standard, and from those that have answers.

A stabilized specification is past inquiry stored so that later questions become cheaper to ask (CLAIM-475), the inquiry form of §3.6's stored work. The same storage entrenches. Familiar distinctions are cheap and new ones costly, so "an apparatus may expand its capacity for inquiry while simultaneously entrenching a particular mode of individuation" (A17, quoted in CLAIM-475). Of a research record in particular, the same exchange warned:

> If revising a schema is vastly more expensive than adding another record under the existing schema, inquiry may remain trapped in a locally convenient representation. … The cost of changing a distinction influences how long the distinction persists.

Progress, on this account, can be measured in questions: as expansion of the set of questions that can meaningfully be investigated under fixed resources (CLAIM-475).

**Stored specifications as accrued predicates (added 2026-11-21).** The record gives this section's stored specifications a model (CLAIM-tmpj4fzn, CLAIM-tmp8v75o, both Proposed). Every predicate filed on an entry, a field's value, a premise, a quoted passage, is a test its later versions must pass, and the revisions still open to it are the intersection of what passes, which only shrinks as predicates accrue. Two kinds differ in what they do. A *descriptor*, one the entry already passes when it is added, moves nothing and holds the entry where it is; an *imposition*, one it fails, moves it, to a convention or back to its source. Descriptors never conflict, the entry being their witness; only impositions can leave no admissible version. In the control-theoretic sense the owner fixed (TERM-tmp0g123, Proposed), accruing predicates *stabilizes* an entry, damping perturbations in the dimensions they constrain, and a soft predicate damps by 1/(1 + λ) per restatement where a hard one damps wholly. That is the mechanism of this section's cheapening and its entrenchment at once.

### 4.3 What a language cannot say

A system's distinctions are drawn in some vocabulary. The record's **relational vocabulary** of a class of descriptions is "the arities and kinds of relation it can state … and so which constraints are expressible in it at all" (TERM-091, Active). It lets one say that a constraint is *inexpressible* in a vocabulary, which is different from its being false or unknown.

Inexpressibility can arise from composition, not only from omission. Eliminating an equilibrated interior from a chain of springs leaves the boundary with "a coupling between x₁ and x₃ that neither part has"; if the interior relaxes slowly, it leaves a memory kernel, a constraint of a new kind (CLAIM-480, Active, demonstrated). A vocabulary adequate to a system's parts may therefore fail for their composite. The record keeps open whether such vocabulary is forced by the composite or by the class of descriptions in use (QUESTION-283, Open).

### 4.4 Inquiry as a distinct achievement

The record's broadest structural claim places inquiry among other achievements of organized systems (CLAIM-474, Active, interpretive). From interconnection to individuation, the relationships are "distinct achievements that form a partial order, not a nested sequence: none implies the next". It names six:

- **interconnection** fixes what joint behaviour is possible;
- **compatibility** fixes which local descriptions admit a coherent whole;
- **dynamics** fixes which organizations persist;
- **individuation** fixes which have sufficiently robust identity and boundaries;
- **organizational autonomy** fixes which help maintain their own constitutive constraints;
- **inquiry** fixes which can measure and revise their own models and apparatuses under resource bounds.

Inquiry, on this account, can occur without autonomy. A system can revise its models without maintaining itself, and an autonomous system need not inquire.

### 4.5 Programmable, or self-governing?

When does a system revise its regime *itself*? The record has a question, a claim and a caution:

- **The question.** "What distinguishes a programmable system from one that governs its own reconfiguration?" (QUESTION-388, Open). The record's readings show that internal feedback is not enough: a mechanical sheet that rewrites its own crease stiffnesses does so on a supervisor's verdict.
- **The claim.** What endogenous governance adds over a behaviourally equivalent system with an external scheduler is not that it allocates authority among its own processes, "but that it can revise its own governing standards when they break down" (CLAIM-485, Active, interpretive).
- **The caution.** The capacity to revise one's standards is not the capacity to revise them well (CLAIM-424, Active, interpretive).

An operative regime, the governing organization among regimes, and the capacity to transform that organization "can persist or fail separately" (CLAIM-025, Active, interpretive). An organization can become more competent inside a regime while becoming less capable of changing the organization that keeps the regime in place.

A further distinction applies to improvement over time. A collective can get better at producing results, or better at improving how it produces them. The test of the second is whether a later improver does better than an earlier one from the same starting state at the same budget, *without losing the capacity to detect failures in its own apparatus* (QUESTION-457, Open).

**The eighth principle follows.** Reflexive inquiry, revising one's own language, answer space, criteria or resources, is a distinct achievement from learning within them. It is self-governed only where the criteria of revision are the system's own and remain revisable. Its products are stored specifications that cheapen some questions and entrench against others.

## 5. Memory, transmission and identity

### 5.1 Iterated transmission converges on the transmitters

Any inquiry that runs across time must pass its results forward, through people, sessions, documents or generations. The record holds a general account of what happens to content passed along a chain.

- **Convergence is not preservation.** "Retaining information about where a Markov chain started and staying close to the starting distribution are different properties: a chain can forget its initial state while its distribution stays far from where it began" (CLAIM-270, Active, demonstrated). Equally, "transmission is not degradation" (CLAIM-081, Active, interpretive).
- **The endpoint reflects the transmitters.** A stable endpoint of cultural transmission may be "a shared decoding convention rather than a preserved historical object" (TERM-027, Active, quoting A110). Serial transmission is reconstructive and filtered by the transmitters' inductive biases (CLAIM-090, Proposed, empirical). ARG-035 (Proposed) qualifies the support. Griffiths and Kalish's theorem and Kirby, Cornish and Smith's chains "show different things", and convergence to the transmitters' prior is proved only for sampling learners.
- **Small errors need not stay small.** The same local error can be damped, accumulated or amplified by later reconstructions, and local similarity can coexist with large global drift (CLAIM-056, Active, interpretive). The event to detect may be a *phase change*, the first generation at which a different configuration dominates, which per-step error does not show (CLAIM-114, Proposed).

### 5.2 Arguments drift in identity

The record's own founding observation is an instance. "In the record's exchanges with collaborators who keep no memory, the argument repeatedly loses identity: concepts are redefined silently, objections and readings come back as though new, and the thesis changes without anyone arguing for the change" (CLAIM-177, Active, empirical, across six exchanges). In the documented case, a manuscript drafted straight from an argument's matured state dropped 24 of its items, none on request, and changed the thesis with no turn arguing the move (CLAIM-324, Active, empirical).

The record reads restatement across memoryless sessions as iterated transport "with only a weak channel back to the source". Without anchors, restatements drift toward the collaborators' own framings rather than at random (CLAIM-191, Proposed, interpretive), as §5.1 predicts where its conditions hold.

Two refinements matter:

- **Maturing drops things legitimately** (TERM-052). An absent item is either dissolution or loss.
- **An inquiry can drift in its question** as well as its concepts (TERM-056, Active).

### 5.3 What anchors

**Anchoring in general (added 2026-11-21).** An earlier version of this section asked only how a record holds an entry steady across sessions. That is one case of a wider behaviour. The record files "anchoring" in three places it had not joined:

- **Transmission:** a restatement held to its source (CLAIM-191, Proposed; CLAIM-772, Proposed).
- **Correspondence:** a map between two systems that has content only if it is "anchored to independently measured distinctions" (CLAIM-011, Active, demonstrated).
- **Judgment:** a value that pulls a later estimate toward itself "without adding evidence about the target" (CLAIM-315, Proposed), which is anchoring as a bias.

CLAIM-tmp1xigl (Proposed, interpretive) holds that these are one mechanism, a reconstruction pulled toward a fixed reference. They differ in what the reference is tied to, and that tie sets what the anchor secures:

| The reference is tied to | By | It secures | It fails as |
|---|---|---|---|
| the target | a channel independent of the inquirer: a measurement, an intervention, a specimen, an independent reader | content: the inquiry's states say something about the target, and a mismatch with it registers | a channel that only looked independent (CLAIM-011's uninformative correspondence) |
| an earlier state of the inquiry | storage and an address: a text, a code, a protocol | continuity: the inquiry stays the same inquiry, and its change is traceable | entrenchment where the earlier state was wrong (CLAIM-184); staleness where it was revised |
| nothing | salience | coordination at most | bias: the pull without the evidence |

The account does not make the join a review of the record declined, that independent evidence, anchored correspondence and competent uptake are one requirement (SYNTHESIS-066). Those fall in one row, and what counts as independent stays that row's free parameter. Its content of its own is the join across rows. The pull that biases an estimate and the pull that keeps a restatement faithful are the same pull, and CLAIM-011 and CLAIM-315 are the separating case: the same pull, with opposite effects on fidelity to the target.

Three consequences matter for the theory.

1. **An anchor is good or bad by its tie, not by its fixity.** Every anchor is a fixed point that reconstructions are pulled toward. Whether the pull brings an inquiry closer to its target depends on whether the target set that point. "Stability is not fidelity" splits in two. Fidelity to the source is what an anchor of the second kind secures. Fidelity to the target is what only the first kind can secure.
2. **An inquiry needs anchors in order to learn.** A discrepancy registers only against something the inquirer cannot reinterpret at will. Without a fixed reference, a conflicting observation can be absorbed by moving the reference instead (P5's discrepancy, P3's independence). Kuhn, as one reading has him, makes firm commitment to a tradition what "lets research recognize where the tradition fails" (THEORY-2096, Proposed): the commitment holds the expectation still, and the world, through an independent channel, supplies the mismatch. An anchor is what an obstruction is an obstruction *to*.
3. **An anchor of the second kind must be selectively permeable.** It should be fixed against the inquirer's own reconstructions (summarizing, rereading, reasoning from memory) and open to the channel that ties it to the target (new evidence, a failed prediction, an independent reader). Too open to the first, and it drifts. Too closed to the second, and it entrenches. When it is revised through the second, everything derived from it has to follow.

The mechanisms the record holds for keeping a chain to its origin, and the record's own design, fall under this account.

**The mechanisms.** The digests behind this paper find four. The grouping is ours; the record states each separately.

- **Exact conservation.** Under Markov reconstruction, an observable conserved exactly at every step and not constant on the start's support "keeps information about the start at every generation", while a merely bounded conservation defect does not prevent forgetting (CLAIM-635, Active, demonstrated).
- **Independent evidence.** A judgment–observation loop is kept from confirming itself by "evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests" (CLAIM-342, Active, interpretive).
- **Anchored correspondence.** A correspondence must be anchored to independently measured distinctions and evaluated on held-out tasks (CLAIM-011, CLAIM-266, Active).
- **Address.** "Storage is not anchoring, addressability is: a structure that is kept but cannot be pointed at part by part does not stop re-derivation, and the same content becomes an anchor once its parts get addresses" (CLAIM-182, Proposed, empirical).

Independent evidence and anchored correspondence are ties of the first kind. Exact conservation and address are anchors of the second kind, which hold an inquiry to its own earlier states.

The predecessor manuscript built on the last of these. An argument's nucleation threshold is whatever makes a structure persist, and more complex structure can grow only on anchors (CLAIM-185, Proposed). What counts as the same claim persisting is fixed by an amendable decision, not discovered (CLAIM-195, Active). Recurrence can trigger anchoring: coding a thing the second time it turns up catches drift the first time it could occur (CLAIM-176, Proposed).

An earlier draft of this paper read a stable code as CLAIM-635's exactly conserved observable, and offered that as the formal reason an address anchors. As stated, the reading does not hold, for three reasons:

- one argument restated is a point mass, which CLAIM-635 excludes;
- a conserved code keeps which item a chain started from, not its content;
- anchoring by address works by consulting stored content, which takes the chain outside CLAIM-635's Markov hypothesis.

The record has since filed a narrower form that survives these reasons (2026-11-21, both Proposed). CLAIM-772 holds that forgetting a source and anchoring to it are one contraction, told apart by what each restatement step conserves exactly. A chain that contracts everywhere except on what it copies without error ends keeping exactly the start's value on that conserved part. The record copies content exactly, by code and verbatim reading, and leaves form to contract onto its schema. ARG-124 states the application. The conservation results carry over if a session's restatement of an entry is a Markov step and reading a coded entry verbatim copies its text exactly, which "holds for the text and not for its interpretation".

What anchors, then, is not the code but the exact copying of the text that the code makes possible. The weak point is interpretation, which no verbatim read conserves.

Two objections to CLAIM-772, filed the same day and restated in general terms at the owner's ruling (both Proposed), refine this. Each holds for any inquiry; the record is the instance that raised it.

- **An inquiry that keeps its sources is a star around them, not a chain.** A laboratory rereads its notebook, a court the precedent, a scholar the edition; in this record each session reads the stored entry rather than the last restatement of it (CLAIM-tmppkqzl). Friedkin and Johnsen's re-injection of a fixed start (THEORY-1161), which CLAIM-772 had set aside as a second route, is conservation on the state enlarged by the source, so the route set aside is the record's main one. On that structure an interpretation does not random-walk away from the text. It settles on an offset toward the reader's prior. That offset is bounded, is not removed by reading again, and is the same for every reader who shares the prior, so those readers' agreement cannot reveal it. Only a reader whose prior differs can. The chains the record does have are its derived texts: summaries, titles quoted from memory, accounts, manuscripts. This paper's fact-checks and its admission audit (§7.4) found errors in exactly those, and found them with independent readers.
- **Exact copying keeps staleness too.** In any inquiry whose sources are revised, an exact copy is anchored to the version it was taken from: a copied protocol keeps a step the published one dropped, a quoted result survives the original's correction. Where the source is revised, the copy keeps what the revision removed (CLAIM-tmp0kqq4, empirical). RETRO-004's summary is the case (§14.5). Against a revisable source, what anchors a derived text is a tie to the current version: regeneration from the source, or a recorded version. Exactness does not.

In the terms of the general account, the reader's offset is anchoring of the third kind at work inside an anchor of the second kind. The stale summary is an anchor of the second kind that did not follow a revision.

**When an anchor is set (added 2026-11-21).** A descriptor holds the value an entry has when it is added, not the value it was filed with (CLAIM-tmp8v75o, part 5, Proposed): drift before the pin is kept, not undone. So when an anchor is set decides what it preserves, the origin if at filing and a drifted state if later, a formal reason for the record's habits of coding and quoting at filing.

### 5.4 Anchoring has costs

- **Entrenchment.** "Anchors entrench: a structure that persists early can lock in its framing, so that later structure grows on it and alternatives become hard to reach" (CLAIM-184, Proposed).
- **Who pays.** When an agent with no memory files, capture and benefit fall to the same party, but "the disparity does not disappear. It moves": the owner now pays to read and correct (CLAIM-179, Proposed).
- **When it pays.** Filing moves work across time and pays only when reuse covers it (CLAIM-303, Active). Where reuse cannot be forecast, a break-even rule is within a factor of two of the hindsight optimum (CLAIM-308, Active, demonstrated).
- **Where two justifications part.** The cost rule and the drift-catching second-occurrence rule part when filing is dear. Every reconstruction paid for in between is unanchored drift that the competitive guarantee does not price (CLAIM-630, Proposed, demonstrated).
- **Drift before coding, as a queue.** Suppose ideas arrive at rate λ, one first-come coder codes them at rate κ > λ, and each is restated once per unit of time while it waits. Then an idea's expected drift before it is coded is σ²/(κ − λ) in mean square for features copied with error, and grows without bound as arrival approaches the coding rate. Coding at a fixed occurrence caps it at that many restatements' worth (CLAIM-777, Proposed, demonstrated). This is CLAIM-186's "arrival against anchoring" made exact.
- **Slowing is not anchoring, unless drift accrues by time.** Whether slowing an exchange, without anchoring anything, reduces drift as much as anchoring does turns on whether drift accrues per restatement or per unit of time. If per restatement, only anchoring bounds it (CLAIM-782, Proposed).
- **The most original ideas are the most fragile.** The account predicts that the ideas lost soonest are those furthest from the collaborators' own framings. Contraction toward those framings carries a distant idea past the critical distance in a few restatements, and never carries a near one past it (CLAIM-776, Proposed). If so, the second-occurrence rule protects least what most needs protecting, since the most distant ideas may not survive to a second occurrence.

### 5.5 What a record of inquiry can secure

If maturing drops things legitimately, a record cannot aim at preservation. What it can secure, the record proposes, is "traceability, change that is announced and located" (CLAIM-189, Proposed, filed as a counter to an earlier claim of the record's own). That is defined over the argument's accountability structure: what was asserted, why, how it was challenged, and what justified its revision (CLAIM-250, Proposed).

Demonstrated results bound what such a record is:

- **Conclusions do not fix revisions** (CLAIM-294, Active).
- **Support topology does not fix revisions**, because a graph records "that support exists, not how much" (CLAIM-293, Active).
- **Sufficiency is relative.** A sufficient state stops being sufficient "when a source is discredited, the model is revised or the decisions change" (CLAIM-300, Active).
- **Sufficiency, accountability and correctness each come apart from the other two** (CLAIM-299, Active).

CASE-084 (Active) is the vivid case: "the perfectly accountable false theory", a community whose record traces every commitment and whose vocabulary leaves out the right explanation. What a kept record of justifications can add is a wider family of decisions, and decisions a bounded agent could not otherwise afford (CLAIM-295, Proposed). It decides nothing on its own: retiring an entry reports what rests on it and leaves each dependant to a judgment (CLAIM-664, Active, demonstrated).

### 5.6 Identity across change

If an inquiry's content can change, what makes it the same inquiry?

- **Causal transmission, not resemblance.** A collective that replaces all its members persists, when it does, "by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by resemblance" (CLAIM-329, Proposed). More generally, an organized individual's identity over time is carried by the transmission of the relations that keep its repertoire realizable, not by any invariant structure (CLAIM-615, Proposed).
- **Succession can be plural.** When bundled continuities separate, several successors can each genuinely continue a collective in a different respect (CLAIM-328, Active).
- **Interruption is not loss.** A perspective's enactment can stop without the loss of what could reinstate it. An interruption may be of activation, of enactment or of reproduction (CLAIM-421, Active).
- **Participants' judgments help make the succession.** At a change of steward, their judgments of which successor continues a project are causally productive of the succession (CLAIM-617, Proposed).

**The ninth principle follows.** Without anchors, content passed through memoryless transmitters can drift toward the transmitters' own biases (proved for sampling learners), and a stable result is not evidence of a preserved one. An anchor secures what it is tied to. Tied to the target by an independent channel, it gives an inquiry content and lets a discrepancy register. Tied to the inquiry's own earlier states, by exact copying of what is addressed, it gives continuity, not correctness. Tied to nothing, it is bias. What a record can aim to secure, on the record's Proposed account, is traceable change, not fidelity. An inquiry stays the same inquiry by transmitting what keeps it going, not by staying the same.

## 6. Collective inquiry

### 6.1 A whole's direction is not its members'

Many inquiring systems are collectives. "An organized whole's operative intention is not the expectation of its members' intentions; it is produced by systemic mechanisms that can evolve apart from what the members intend" (CLAIM-030, Active). Three cautions follow:

- **The whole's success does not establish its members'**, and the converse holds too (CLAIM-060, Active).
- **A description adequate for the whole can merge distinctions its members need** (CLAIM-348).
- **A perspective is not thereby an agent.** Possessing or enacting an organizational perspective does not establish an independent agent, and perspectival, agential and institutional boundaries need not coincide (CLAIM-418, Active).

### 6.2 Correction is a chain of separable achievements

The record's most consistent pattern about collectives is a series of non-implications. Each link can fail while the others hold:

- **Having, knowing and deploying.** What a system has, what it knows it has, and what it can deploy within a task's deadline are different (CLAIM-296, Active).
- **Access, authority and action.** An organization's access to a signal, the signal's authority over action, and action on it are separable (CLAIM-349, Active).
- **The ladder of uptake.** A contribution's admission to an organization's inquiry, its recognition in apt terms, its credibility, its uptake into judgments, and its contributor's part in revising the standards of uptake are separable. An organization "can widen access while every channel by which a less authoritative contribution could change the criteria of authority stays closed" (CLAIM-403, Active).
- **Three achievements for a discrepancy to change action.** Legibility, by which the discrepancy becomes recognizable; volitional efficacy, by which a demand can move action; and correctability, by which a fitting revision is carried out (CLAIM-523, Active).
- **Six for an objection.** A protected objection is effective only if it becomes formulable, legible, taken up by an authorized channel, sustainable by the objector, corrected and maintained. Its chance of success is no greater than that of its weakest stage (CLAIM-732, Proposed).

### 6.3 Self-confirming evaluation

A collective that scores its own inquiry risks confirming itself:

- **Two routes to lock-in.** Lock-in from selectively admitted evidence and lock-in from self-scoring "need different remedies". Correcting for how evidence was selected "is not correcting how it is scored" (CLAIM-466, Active, demonstrated).
- **Outside influence without outside decisions.** An organization can be governed from outside "through the evidence its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh evidence" (CLAIM-404, Active).
- **Competition over the test.** Competing perspectives "compete over the tests that measure them as well as under them", and a perspective that gains influence over the test can change what later success shows (CLAIM-416, Active).
- **Feedback between meaning and authority.** In the record's model of coupled meaning and influence, only the feedback loop between them can destabilize a symmetric state, and the direction that grows mixes meaning with authority (CLAIM-330, Active, demonstrated).

The remedy common to all of these is CLAIM-342's: evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests.

### 6.4 Correctability is a maintained state

Collectives can lose their capacity to be corrected while gaining in other respects:

- **Effective action and correctability by others are maintained by different relations,** so one can grow while the other degrades (CLAIM-462, Active). Maintaining a regime of effective action and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes "are not the same achievement" (CLAIM-099, Active).
- **An intervention can change what it would take to correct it** (CLAIM-464, Active).
- **A capacity lost past a threshold may not return** when the original conditions return; it needs better conditions or rebuilding (CLAIM-463, Active, demonstrated).
- **First-order responsiveness is no evidence of second-order responsiveness.** An institution can be reliably responsive to reasons *within* its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons for revising the mechanism (CLAIM-719, Proposed).
- **Absent challenge can be manufactured.** An organization can answer a credible challenge by reducing the capacity for future challenge, so that the later absence of challenge reads as success (CLAIM-619, Proposed).
- **Safeguards can close the channels self-governance needs.** Safeguards against capture can also restrict the disagreement and revision self-governance needs (CLAIM-359, Active).

The record's documented case is CASE-138 (Active): Challenger to Columbia. NASA corrected its launch decisions and safety organization after 1986, and the Columbia board found in 2003 that related failure mechanisms had recurred, while the lessons-learned system "was little used in between". The record's own gloss: "A correction recorded is not a capacity maintained."

The record also holds a Proposed empirical prediction about when self-revision pays (CLAIM-685, Proposed, empirical). Institutions able to recognize, authorize and carry out revisions of their own governing standards avoid persistent failures better than institutions that only monitor outcomes against fixed standards, *where the inherited standards are inadequate to the problems met*. They do not, on routine work.

One more caution concerns what records do for a collective. In a simulated organization, keeping an incident team's records through a handoff cut the company's error in estimating its own state "about eightfold while barely changing what it does" (CASE-166, Proposed; reported, not re-run). A record may improve what a collective knows more than what it decides.

**The tenth principle follows.** For a contribution to change what a collective does, and then how it judges contributions, a chain of separable achievements must hold: admission, recognition, credibility, uptake, and revision of the standards of uptake. Each link can fail while the others hold. A collective can learn at the lower links, changing its procedures on a contribution, while its standards of uptake stay fixed. For objections, the record proposes that the weakest stage bounds the whole (CLAIM-732, Proposed). Its capacity to be corrected is a state that must be maintained, can erode while performance improves, and is not shown by the absence of challenge. Its evaluation of itself confirms itself unless some evidence arrives, or is scored, independently of what it tests.
