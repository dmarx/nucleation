# NOTE-1564: Li & Choi — Rigidity control of general origami structures by facet planarity

## Contribution

- A method of controlling rigidity through facet planarity that works for
  origami with arbitrary polygonal facets, generalizing earlier work on the
  Miura-ori (Chen & Mahadevan 2019; Li & Choi 2025).
- A proof that n − 3 constraints are needed for an n-gon, and that the
  triangulation used to encode no-shear does not bias the DoF count.
- The triangular-facet ratio identified as the structural variable that
  governs the transition, with a five-parameter empirical formula for the
  critical density.

## Key insight

Equal numbers of planarity constraints remove very different numbers of
degrees of freedom depending on which facets carry them: a triangle is
planar already, so constraining it removes nothing. How fast a pattern
rigidifies is therefore set by its mix of facet shapes and by how facets
are chosen, and a choice among more candidates sharpens the transition, as
in explosive percolation.

## Assumptions

- **Infinitesimal rigidity** at one configuration: each pattern was taken
  "at a folded configuration with a folding percentage of 25%", generated
  with Origami Simulator (other percentages in the SI, not read).
- **Numerical rank** by QR with an adaptive tolerance; since "all origami
  structures considered in our work are foldable, we further restrict the
  computed DOF values to lie within the feasible range [1, dinitial]."
- **Random selection** under the two rules, 100 runs for each
  k ∈ {1, 2, 4, …, 32}.
- **A phenomenological fit**: the tanh model's parameters are fitted for
  each pattern and resolution.

## Key results

- **Abstract.** "the rigidity of a wide range of origami structures can be
  controlled by enforcing or relaxing the planarity condition of selected
  facets"; the aim is to "identify key origami structural variables that
  govern the critical rigidity percolation transition."
- **Theorem (Methods).** "The number of sub-planarity constraints required
  to control the planarity of an n-sided polygonal facet is exactly n − 3."
- **Triangles (Results).** "imposing planarity on triangular facets does
  not reduce the global DOF".
- **Power of choices (Results).** "the standard deviation across
  simulations decreases as k increases, indicating that the power of
  choices has a strong effect, leading to a narrower transition width in
  rigidity percolation."
- **An asymmetry between the rules (Results).** "actively favoring a
  specific facet type is more difficult than simply avoiding it."
- **Discussion.** "in all origami structures, the rigidity control is
  highly relevant to the presence of triangular facets."
- **The critical density.** ρ* is the least ρ at which at least half the
  runs reach minimum DoF, fitted by a tanh model in (−1)^r log k with a
  linear term in the triangular-facet ratio t; a hypergeometric model
  bounds the chance that a step leaves the DoF unchanged.

## Concepts

- **Planarity constraint density**: ρ = facets selected / total facets.
- **Critical transition density ρ\***: the least ρ with P(minimum-DOF
  structure) ≥ 1/2.
- **Most / Least Efficient rule**: of k sampled candidates, pick the facet
  whose planarity gives the minimum (or maximum) resulting DoF.
- **Normalized DOF**: d̃ = (d − 1)/(d_initial − 1).
- **Triangular-facet ratio t**: the initial fraction of facets that are
  triangles.

## Connections

It is a direct extension of Chen, Choi and Mahadevan (2020), [LIT-2064](../literature.d/LIT-2064.md)
(its ref. 39), from kirigami links to origami facets, and of Chen and
Mahadevan's Miura-ori rigidity control (PNAS 2019). It cites Scheffer's
early-warning papers as motivation for critical transitions but does not
use those methods.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures.** It cites this as
  O8, with the quoted phrase "critical densities where rigidity changes".
  That phrase is A's paraphrase, not in the paper. The substance is right
  but thin. A misses that the transition depends on the selection rule and
  on the candidate-pool size k, with larger k narrowing it; that the
  triangular-facet ratio is the governing structural variable; that the
  predictive formula is fitted, not derived; and that everything is
  infinitesimal rigidity at one configuration. The paper's "rigidity
  percolation" is the density at which half the runs of a fixed-size
  pattern reach minimum DoF, not a threshold in the thermodynamic limit.
- **[CLAIM-345](../claims.d/CLAIM-345.md) (equal controllability rank does not fix the cost of
  control).** Related, not support: equal numbers of constraints remove
  very different numbers of DoF depending on where they go. That is
  constraint efficiency, not control cost.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md) (scale separation makes constraints).** Qualifies, weakly.
  Planarity is idealized as a hard constraint switched on or off.
  Physically it is a ratio of bending to folding stiffness, and the paper's
  toggle is the idealized end of the energy-scale picture that Silverberg
  et al. (2015), [LIT-2100](../literature.d/LIT-2100.md), make explicit.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md) and [CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md).** Unrelated in detail, beyond the generic
  point that DoF counts and their placement differ.
- The record has no entry on rigidity percolation or constraint
  redundancy as such. Filed: [THEORY-1377](../theory.d/THEORY-1377.md). No instruction for
  machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The authors'.** Rank detection is subject to numerical error
  (Supplementary Note 2, not read); the tanh model is empirical; open work
  includes a stochastic-process theory for E[ρ*], crease design, curved
  folds and 3D assemblies.
- **One configuration.** The DoF is infinitesimal at a single folding
  percentage, so finite foldability and second-order effects are not
  addressed.
- **Illustration, not experiment.** The paper models in Fig. 3 are
  illustrative.
- **Extrapolation.** Predictions for high-resolution patterns rest on fits
  made at lower resolution.
