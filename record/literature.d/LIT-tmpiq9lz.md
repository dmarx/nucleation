  discontinuity in response to a driver, non-recovery after a

# LIT-tmpiq9lz: Direct experimental evidence for alternative stable states: a review

Schröder, A., Persson, L. & De Roos, A. M. (2005). Direct experimental evidence for alternative stable states: a review. *Oikos* 110(1): 3–19. doi:10.1111/j.0030-1299.2005.13962.x.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmph4p4q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmph4p4q.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **Direct tests are few and lab-biased.** "13 experiments have directly
  demonstrated the existence of alternative stable states while 8 showed
  the absence of ASS in other cases. 14 experiments did not fulfil the
  requirements of a conclusive test, mostly because they applied a too
  short time scale" (p. 3). "Ten out of 12 conclusive laboratory
  experiments demonstrated bistability, while this was only true for three
  out of nine field experiments" (p. 7).
- **Removing the pressure usually restored the state.** Of tests of
  non-recovery, "Fifteen such tests were found, of which three
  demonstrated ASS, five their absence and seven were inappropriate tests"
  (p. 9); the appropriate ones were all grazer exclusions in terrestrial
  systems.
- **The stability criterion.** A state is stable only "if it stays
  quantitatively and qualitatively unchanged despite that all its
  initially resident individuals have replaced themselves" (p. 6).
- **Abruptness and feedback are not enough**: indirect evidence does "not
  rule out alternative, often more parsimonious, explanations like an
  abrupt, but still continuous, response to driving parameters" (p. 4),
  and "the net effect of all positive and negative feedbacks may still be
  negative" (p. 5).

## Standing in the record

Active, as the direct-evidence companion to [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md) and [LIT-726](LIT-726.md): it
counts how rarely manipulation has shown alternative stable states, and
how rarely removing a pressure has failed to restore a system. Any claim
in the record that a degraded or a supported state is held by
alternative states, rather than recovering slowly, should be judged by
its non-recovery test over a full turnover. Filed from it:
[THEORY-tmpm618c](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpm618c.md).

It stands in for Suding, Gross & Houseman (2004), [LIT-tmpisunw](LIT-tmpisunw.md), only as
the bar that paper's "Recent experimental work" must meet; it is not about
restoration, does not cite or restate Suding et al., and carries none of
their cases. The counts above are from the body text and its Table 2.
Appendix 1, the per-experiment table, extracted garbled; any figure taken
from it, such as the reading that whole-lake biomanipulations drifted back
to turbid, is unverified until checked against a clean copy. No
instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
