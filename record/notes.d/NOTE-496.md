# NOTE-496: Two neural correlates of consciousness

## Contribution

The paper turns the phenomenal/access distinction of Block (1995) into a
claim about the brain: there are at least two NCCs, and a framework with
two makes more sense of the data than one with a single NCC. It also
proposes a method, signal-detection theory, for getting at experience that
is not accessed.

## Key insight

Access is a competition with one winner. A content that loses does not
thereby lose the recurrent sensory activity that, in the clear cases, is
the basis of experience. So the competition for access gives a mechanism
by which experience can occur without access, and signal detection gives a
way to see it: hold the sensory signal fixed and move the decision
criterion, and report changes while the sensory correlate does not.

## Assumptions

- **Koch's definition of the NCC**, "the minimal set of neuronal events and
  mechanisms jointly sufficient for a specific conscious percept", read as
  allowing more than one NCC per percept.
- **Core versus total NCC** (Shoemaker; Chalmers): the core distinguishes one
  experience from another; the rest of the total NCC is enabling background.
- **The global workspace model** of access (Baars; Dehaene and Changeux).
- **The SDT model of report**: a report reflects both the strength of the
  sensory signal and a criterion that rewards, penalties and catch-trial
  rates shift.

## Key results

- **MT/V5 as a core phenomenal NCC for seen motion.** Seven lines of
  evidence: activation during motion perception; microstimulation biasing
  judgements by column direction; akinetopsia after bilateral damage;
  activation by the motion aftereffect and its disruption by TMS;
  activation by implied motion in stills; moving phosphenes from TMS to
  MT/V5.
- **Recurrence.** With TMS to MT/V5 and V1 overlapping in visual space, a V1
  pulse 5–45 ms after the MT/V5 pulse made subjects report mostly
  stationary phosphenes (Pascual-Leone & Walsh), suggesting a feedback loop
  is needed.
- **Winner-take-all access.** In Dehaene et al.'s attentional-blink variant,
  at the 260 ms lag subjects almost never used intermediate visibility
  ratings.
- **The exclusion paradigm.** Visser and Merikle's reward manipulation
  changed exclusion but not inclusion performance. On Snodgrass's SDT
  reading, a lowered criterion lets weak conscious perception of "reason"
  block "-son" while subjects think they saw no word. So some 50 ms stimuli
  are weakly conscious without being broadcast.
- **Neural SDT (Supèr et al. 2001).** V1 figure–ground modulation tracks
  detection for salient (16-pixel) targets and few catch trials. Reducing
  the target to 4 pixels with 20% catch trials, or raising catch trials to
  50% at 16 pixels, makes modulation the same whether or not the monkey
  saccades. Restoring 0% catch trials at 4 pixels restores the coupling.
  The modulation vanishes under anaesthesia and is probably recurrent.
- **Box 1.** Nakamura and Mishkin removed frontal, parietal and superior
  temporal areas in one hemisphere of monkeys; with limbic areas intact the
  animals made exploratory eye and head movements. Block suggests access
  areas may be removable with experience left intact.

## Concepts

- **phenomenal NCC**: the minimal neural basis of the content of an
  experience.
- **access NCC**: the neural basis of a content's being broadcast in the
  global workspace.
- **core vs total NCC**: the part that distinguishes one experience from
  another, versus the whole sufficient condition.
- **criterion (SDT)**: the threshold a subject implicitly sets for reporting
  a stimulus, separate from the strength of the signal.

## Limitations

- **Every empirical claim is second-hand** and summarised in a few lines;
  the paper is an Opinion piece.
- **The phenomenal NCC is identified from reported cases**, the circle Block
  (2007) names "the methodological puzzle". This paper does not yet face it.
- **Exclusion results** support "weakly conscious" stimuli only on one SDT
  interpretation; Visser and Merikle read their own result as attentional.

## Open questions

- Box 2: does the fusiform face area in an extinction patient show
  recurrent activation, and figure modulation, when he says he sees no
  face? If so, that would be a phenomenal NCC without report.
- Why recurrent activation of MT/V5 should be the basis of seen motion
  rather than another experience or none: Block calls this a form of the
  hard problem.
