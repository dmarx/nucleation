# QUESTION-297: When the effects of trauma reach descendants who did not live through it, by which route do they travel: germline epigenetics, the womb, parenting, or a group's narrative and identity; and on the identity route, does an ancestor's injury become part of the descendant's self?

## Why it is a question

The legacy line ([QUESTION-301](QUESTION-301.md), [QUESTION-299](QUESTION-299.md)) asks how the living
reach forward to people after them. Intergenerational trauma is the past
reaching forward into the living. Four routes are proposed, and they make
different claims about what is inherited.

- **The germline.** In mice, a father's conditioned fear of an odour shows
  in his offspring and grand-offspring, with methylation changes in sperm
  (Dias and Ressler, [LIT-1746](../literature.d/LIT-1746.md); reviewed by Bohacek and Mansuy,
  [LIT-1738](../literature.d/LIT-1738.md)). In humans the evidence is:
  - epidemiological: Överkalix (Pembrey et al., [LIT-1764](../literature.d/LIT-1764.md)) and Civil
    War prisoners' sons (Costa et al., [LIT-1696](../literature.d/LIT-1696.md));
  - molecular, in small samples: FKBP5 methylation in Holocaust survivors'
    offspring (Yehuda et al., [LIT-1692](../literature.d/LIT-1692.md)).

  Critics hold that germline reprogramming erases most marks in mammals,
  and that human studies cannot exclude genetic, uterine and cultural
  routes (Heard and Martienssen, [LIT-1745](../literature.d/LIT-1745.md); Horsthemke, [LIT-1762](../literature.d/LIT-1762.md);
  Bird, [LIT-1708](../literature.d/LIT-1708.md)).
- **The womb.** Prenatal famine leaves lasting methylation marks (the
  Dutch Hunger Winter, Heijmans et al., [LIT-1751](../literature.d/LIT-1751.md)). The exposure is the
  person's own, so this is intergenerational, not inheritance through
  untouched generations.
- **Parenting and silence.** The clinical tradition (Danieli, [LIT-1701](../literature.d/LIT-1701.md);
  Yehuda and Lehrner, [LIT-1715](../literature.d/LIT-1715.md)) traces transmission through care and a
  "conspiracy of silence". Yet meta-analyses find no general
  second-generation harm in non-clinical samples of Holocaust survivors'
  children (van IJzendoorn et al., [LIT-1718](../literature.d/LIT-1718.md)), and none in the third
  generation (Sagi-Schwartz et al., [LIT-1770](../literature.d/LIT-1770.md)).
- **Narrative and identity.** A group's shared memory of an ancestral
  injury becomes a marker of its identity: Volkan's "chosen trauma"
  ([LIT-1727](../literature.d/LIT-1727.md)), and the sociology of cultural trauma, in which carrier
  groups make an event an injury to the collective (Alexander et al.,
  [LIT-1679](../literature.d/LIT-1679.md)). Lehrner and Yehuda ([LIT-1793](../literature.d/LIT-1793.md)) join this to the
  biology.

The record already holds two neighbours:
- **Cultural persistence:** attitudes formed under slavery persist
  locally by cultural transmission ([THEORY-889](../theory.d/THEORY-889.md), from [LIT-1388](../literature.d/LIT-1388.md)).
- **Inherited non-genetic state in bacteria:** E. coli cells inherit a
  transient state from their mothers ([THEORY-179](../theory.d/THEORY-179.md)).

matters.

## How it meets the owner's thesis

On the identity route, descendants feel as their own an injury done to
people they never knew. That is the thesis's extended self ([CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md))
running backward in time. Read that way, a descendant's grief or anger
over an ancestral injury is self-regarding: the injured self includes the
ancestors. It is also the reverse of Pitcher's posthumous harm
([LIT-1740](../literature.d/LIT-1740.md)), where the dead are harmed by what comes after them. Here
the living are harmed by what came before them. On the biological routes
no self is needed: what passes is a physiological state, not an
identification.

## What would count as an answer

- **On the routes:** a human study that separates germline transmission
  from uterine, parenting and cultural routes. For example, children of
  trauma-exposed fathers conceived after the trauma and raised apart from
  them, or adoptees. Or evidence that no such separation is achievable.
- **On the identity route:** whether descendants' distress over an
  ancestral trauma follows their identification with the group, measured
  apart from their knowledge of the events and from family transmission.
  If it does, the injury is carried as part of a social self, and
  [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)'s test applies to the past as well as the future.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the germline route, read

Five works were read: the mouse study, the Civil War study and the three
sceptical reviews. Together they narrow the germline route.

- **What counts as inheritance** (Heard and Martienssen, [NOTE-1254](../notes.d/NOTE-1254.md)).
  An effect is transgenerational only in a generation never exposed: the
  grandchildren of a father exposed before conception, the
  great-grandchildren of a pregnant mother. Survivors' children are an
  intergenerational case. Two rounds of reprogramming make transmission
  past them unlikely in mammals. What reaches grandchildren travels, on
  present evidence, by the womb, by care, by habit or by story.
- **What the best animal case carries** (Dias and Ressler, [NOTE-1281](../notes.d/NOTE-1281.md)).
  Offspring were more sensitive to the odour their father was shocked
  with, with more neurons for its receptor, through IVF and
  cross-fostering. The authors call what passes "sensitivity", not fear.
  The proposed sperm mark is modest, in groups as small as four. A tuning
  of perception needs no representation of the ancestor.
- **What the best human case carries** (Costa et al., [NOTE-1255](../notes.d/NOTE-1255.md)). Sons
  of the worst-held prisoners of war had a higher older-age mortality
  hazard (about 1.11); daughters did not. The authors favour an
  epigenetic route only by elimination and have no data on parenting.
  One untested line touches the identity route: return "to a grateful
  nation" may have given captivity a meaning that protected sons. On that
  reading a group's story buffers transmitted harm as well as carrying it.
- **The human test cannot yet be run** (Horsthemke, [NOTE-1265](../notes.d/NOTE-1265.md)). A human
  study can exclude genetic inheritance but not ecological or cultural
  inheritance. The causal step "can hardly be done in humans". Adoptee and
  paternal-only designs weaken the confounds without removing them. The
  question's first test is, for now, the second answer it allows: no clean
  separation is achievable.
- **The named human cases are weak** (Bird, [NOTE-1264](../notes.d/NOTE-1264.md)). Överkalix ignored
  cultural transmission within families and risks multiple testing; the
  FKBP5 study rests on 32 people. Bird cites a larger Swedish cohort
  (Vågerö et al. 2018) only for failing to replicate the
  paternal-grandfather effects on diabetes and heart disease. That paper's
  title reports such an effect on all-cause and cancer mortality, so his
  account is one-sided.

Two cautions about this consensus. The three sceptics are not
independent. Heard reviewed Bird's article, Bird commented on Heard's
manuscript, and Bird and Horsthemke have written together. They are one
school, not three judgements.
And their chief confound, families passing on habits, is itself one of
the question's routes. The social routes, parenting and identity among
them, gain share by default, not by evidence; nothing read yet chooses
between them.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the other routes, read at second hand

Seven works were read in place of the closed ones. They correct two of
the question's labels and shift weight among the routes.

- **Two labels are wrong.** Överkalix is a male-line nutritional case,
  not a germline trauma case (Pembrey, Saffery and Bygren, [NOTE-1320](../notes.d/NOTE-1320.md)).
  - The exposure was a grandparent's food supply in mid-childhood, and
    good supply, not poor, went with higher grandchild mortality.
  - The authors leave the mechanism open, grant that culture matters and
    that replication is needed, and measured no family culture.

  The FKBP5 study is intergenerational, not a germline test (Youssef et
  al., [NOTE-1310](../notes.d/NOTE-1310.md)). It had 32 survivors and 22 offspring against 8 and 9
  controls. Methylation was higher in survivors and lower in offspring:
  the child does not carry the parent's mark.
- **The proponents concede the disputed steps** (Jawaid and Mansuy,
  [NOTE-1296](../notes.d/NOTE-1296.md)). In mice, sperm RNA from traumatized fathers reproduces
  their symptoms in offspring. The authors say it is "not clear" how
  effects pass beyond the first generation, and that germline dependence
  "is not established in humans". Both sides now agree on the
  intergenerational effect in mice and on the gaps.
- **The womb route is real and narrow** (Heijmans et al., [NOTE-1248](../notes.d/NOTE-1248.md)).
  Conception during the Hunger Winter went with slightly lower IGF2
  methylation six decades later (0.488 against 0.515); exposure late in
  pregnancy did not. It is one gene, in blood, with no outcome measured,
  from famine, not psychological trauma.
- **The parenting route finds nothing in the general population**
  (Fridman, [NOTE-1291](../notes.d/NOTE-1291.md)). The meta-analyses' authors, through their
  student, restate:
  - no second-generation traumatization in non-clinical, well-designed
    samples (32 samples, 4,418 offspring);
  - none in the third generation (13 samples, N = 1,012).

  Her own registry sample of survivors' daughters showed no psychological
  or cortisol difference. The comparison families shared the survivors'
  national memory, so the design tests family routes with the identity
  route held fixed.
- **The identity route passes by category, not descent** (Eyerman,
  [NOTE-1246](../notes.d/NOTE-1246.md); Volkan, [NOTE-1250](../notes.d/NOTE-1250.md)).
  - Cultural trauma is a group's loss of identity and meaning, which
    "need not necessarily be felt by everyone". Slavery became the trauma
    of black Americans whether or not their ancestors were enslaved.
  - Volkan's chosen trauma is an image, possibly mythologized, called up
    by a present threat. But his mechanism is parents depositing it in
    children, so his identity route does not separate from parenting.
- **A group's story buffers as well as carries.** Fridman's survivors
  found protection in building a state. Costa et al.'s "grateful nation"
  ([NOTE-1255](../notes.d/NOTE-1255.md)) is the same suggestion.
- **The nearest thing to the identity test** (Hirst, Cyr and Merck,
  [NOTE-1305](../notes.d/NOTE-1305.md)). Of children nine or younger at 9/11, 89% could report a
  parent's memory of where they were, and stronger identification as
  American went with knowing it better. It falls short of the test: it
  measures knowledge, not distress, the channel is the family, and the
  direction is unknown.

Still unread: Lehrner and Yehuda's paper joining the biology to cultural
trauma ([LIT-1793](../literature.d/LIT-1793.md)). Danieli's handbook ([LIT-1701](../literature.d/LIT-1701.md)) is read at second
hand in the next note.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Danieli's framework, read at second hand

Danieli, Norris and Engdahl ([NOTE-1307](../notes.d/NOTE-1307.md)), read in place of the handbook,
test its framework on 422 adult children of survivors.

- **The parenting route runs through the parent's style.** Wartime
  internment reached the child only through the parent's victim style
  (stuck in the loss, overprotective, volatile), with small indirect
  effects (.05 to .06). The post-war family weighed more than the war.
- **Silence is not the main channel.** Numb style, which includes the
  family's "conspiracy of silence", had an effect only through mothers
  and dropped out of the final model. With Fridman's thesis, that is two
  works that do not put silence first.
- **No comparison group.** The paper locates the route among survivors'
  children without showing harm against Fridman's null ([NOTE-1291](../notes.d/NOTE-1291.md)).
  The two results are compatible.
- **The ancestor and the injury come apart.** Children who counted their
  murdered grandparents as their own reported fewer impacts, and this
  item had the largest total effect (.21). Meanwhile "obsession with the
  Holocaust" is scored as part of the harm. Taking in the ancestor went
  with less harm; taking in the injury is part of the harm. The question's
  phrase "an ancestor's injury becomes part of the descendant's self" covers
  both, and they should be kept apart.
- **A group's story as buffer, again.** Settling in Israel went with
  milder impacts (total effect −.16). Reading that as the nation's story
  protecting the family is an inference, the third time the record has
  met it (Costa et al., Fridman).
