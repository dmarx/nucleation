# CLAIM-115: What survives translation is the set of communicative distinctions and relations a receiving system can still reconstruct and act upon, not the wording or the proposition

## The claim

The manuscript argues that an utterance's communicative identity is better
described by a system of relationally organized observables (register, stance,
footing, presupposition, expected reply) than by a global semantic object.
Preservation is then directed and task-relative: what a translation keeps is
whatever distinctions the receiver can still recover and use. The opening
example — three renderings of a bawdy refrain that share a situation model but
differ in footing and illocutionary force — is offered to show that preserving
the proposition is neither necessary nor sufficient.

## What it does not say

It does not say propositional content is irrelevant, only that it is one
observable among several. It does not say the observables are fixed once
for every study: the manuscript makes their choice explicit rather than
deciding it, and a study that tests the thesis fixes its own, with their
weights, before its data, binding the thesis to that choice (CLAIM-238). The
margin and power are the record's, not §10's.
Its defeat condition is empirical (Case I–III, §10), and none of those studies
has been run.

## Note of 2026-10-18: the normative generalization, and two citation faults

A21 §8 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 generalized this claim from meaning to
norms: "**The successful transport of a norm or commitment requires
preserving its relevant relational and practical role, not merely its
verbal or representational content.**" And: "Preserve information ≠
preserve meaning ≠ preserve normative effect." Its example is a statement
that "might function as a promise in one institutional setting but become
merely a description in another". That is this claim's thesis, what a
receiving system can still reconstruct and act upon, with the act's
normative effect as the observable. A21 did not cite this claim. The
coarse-graining form of the same move is [CLAIM-350](CLAIM-350.md) and
[CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md), and its gauge form is [QUESTION-130](../questions.d/QUESTION-130.md).

Two faults in A21 and A22's citations of the line, recorded so that they
are not repeated:

- **[CLAIM-036](CLAIM-036.md) was cited as live.** A21: "meaning depends on differential and
  relational structure, which translation may selectively preserve or
  transform"; A22: "relational meaning and context-indexed interpretation".
  [CLAIM-036](CLAIM-036.md) is Superseded ([CLAIM-036](CLAIM-036.md), then [CLAIM-108](CLAIM-108.md), then [CLAIM-017](CLAIM-017.md), then
  this claim), and its contrast with transport "was overstated and conceded
  at U37". This claim is the live one. A22 wrote, of its own list, "All
  existing statuses and qualifications should be preserved".
- **[CLAIM-014](CLAIM-014.md) was misdescribed.** A21: "communicative meaning can include
  changes to commitments, obligations, and permissions"; A22: "communicative
  acts as transformations of normative relations". [CLAIM-014](CLAIM-014.md) holds that
  Krifka's performative updates fix which changes in conversational
  standing count, and that Blackwell comparison tests whether a rendering
  preserves the information needed to recover them. A21's description fits
  [THEORY-162](../theory.d/THEORY-162.md) or [THEORY-665](../theory.d/THEORY-665.md). [CLAIM-014](CLAIM-014.md)'s real content is the more useful one
  for A21 §8: it is the record's existing test of whether a rendering
  preserves normative effect.

The status is unchanged.
