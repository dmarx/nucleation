# QUESTION-tmp3n3we: Can a sequence of restatements keep an argument's history recoverable in principle while making it infeasible to reconstruct?

## Why it is a question

A14 §2: "Preserving the existence of a reconstruction is different from
preserving the feasibility of that reconstruction." Even if every step of
A₀ → A₁ → … → Aₙ preserves enough to reconstruct the previous one, "the
cost of reconstructing the original justification can grow as the
transformations accumulate." It separated logical, computational and
epistemic recoverability.

A14 offered it as "an additional interpretation of the drift described in
[CLAIM-191](../claims.d/CLAIM-191.md)", and said "Whether it explains the observed exchanges is
still open." The record's own case, the will exchange of [ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md), is
where it would be checked: were the drifted concepts recoverable from the
transcript, and at what cost?

An answer either exhibits growth of reconstruction cost along a chain of
individually invertible restatements, or shows the drift the record
documents was loss and not cost.
