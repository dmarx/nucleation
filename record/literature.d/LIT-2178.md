# LIT-2178: Gift-giving and reciprocity in global society: Introducing Marcel Mauss in international studies

Heins, V. M., Unrau, C. & Avram, K. (2018). Gift-giving and reciprocity in global society: Introducing Marcel Mauss in international studies. *Journal of International Political Theory* 14(2): 126–144. doi:10.1177/1755088218757807.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1679](../notes.d/NOTE-1679.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **Unguaranteed but obliging.** A gift carries no "(legal or contractual)
  guarantee of reciprocation", yet gifts "seem to demand that they are not
  only accepted but also returned in one form or another"; "The failure
  to return a gift changes its very character." (p. 127).
- **Relation, not redistribution.** Gift exchange between groups is "not
  so much the redistribution of resources but rather the creation and
  maintenance of relationships", and "sometimes also about establishing
  hierarchy and exclusive prestige" (p. 128).
- **Potlatch and kula.** The potlatch is "total services of an agonistic
  type"; the kula is "circular, not antagonistic" and "creates a permanent
  connection" (p. 128).
- **The unreciprocated receiver.** Quoting Mauss: "The unreciprocated gift
  still makes the person who has accepted it inferior" (p. 131).
- **The Marshall Plan.** On Dillon, "the French felt the obligation to
  repay gifts but did not have the possibility to fulfill their obligation
  and thereby to become accepted as equals" (p. 131); the Plan, "instead of
  being sufficiently kula-like and based on circular reciprocity", took on
  features of a potlatch (p. 132).
