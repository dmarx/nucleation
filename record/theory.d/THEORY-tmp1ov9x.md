# THEORY-tmp1ov9x: Zidda and colleagues found that, freely viewing pictures in which a cue specific to their own trauma was set in a neutral background, 19 unmedicated people with PTSD made their first fixation on the background later than 18 trauma-exposed controls without PTSD, though they reached the cue as fast, and that latency to the background, with the earliest visual potential, predicted recognition of rearranged cue–background pairs the next day

## Source

Zidda and colleagues (2025), *Scientific Reports* 15: 16362, [LIT-tmpmkmjq](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmkmjq.md),
read in full from the Europe PMC open-access text in [NOTE-tmpoybvl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpoybvl.md).

## What was actually shown

Pictures were built by cutting a trauma-related object out of its scene
and placing it in a neutral background; each participant saw thirty, of
their own trauma type, for 6 s each. In time to first fixation there was
a stimulus-type by group interaction (F(1,35) = 5.121, p = .030): the PTSD
group reached the background later than controls (p = .018) and the cue
no later (p = .511). The PTSD group was also slower to its first fixation
on either region (p = .046). The next day it did worse on recombined
pairs than on intact ones, relative to controls (p = .032), and latency to
the background added about a fifth of the variance in that memory to the
earliest visual potential.

What could have come out otherwise: the groups could have differed on the
cue as well as the background (the authors predicted "shorter fixation
latencies to the cue" in PTSD, and found none), or not at all. With trauma-unrelated cues they did not
differ.

## What this does not say

- **Not that people with PTSD sample context less.** Only the first look
  was timed. Dwell, fixation counts and coverage of the background are not
  reported, so the authors' "processed the context more extensively" for
  controls is not shown by the gaze measure.
- **Not that they withhold disambiguating evidence.** The background was
  neutral and told the viewer nothing about whether the cue was dangerous.
  The behaviour [THEORY-285](THEORY-285.md) models is attenuation of exactly the
  observations that would correct a harmful-context prior.
- **Not anything about recall.** No recall was cued, and there was no
  condition without the trauma cue to set against it; the cue itself is
  the reminder.
- **Not persistence.** Duration of PTSD is not reported, and the design
  is cross-sectional.
