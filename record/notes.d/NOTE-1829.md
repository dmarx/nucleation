# NOTE-1829: Anonymous — TRACE-OU-Bench (Hugging Face dataset)

## Contribution

A benchmark for deciding whether a proposed change to an evolving ontology should be applied, and how it should be corrected, where each decision changes the state later decisions are judged against.

## Key results

- Design: "a sequential benchmark for ontology-update decisions under evolving ontology states".
- No leakage across time: "Future ontology state is never inserted into earlier proposal records."
- Label meanings: MODIFY means "useful schema idea, but at least one structured field must change"; REJECT means "unwarranted, redundant, or structurally incorrect change"; INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE means the source and ontology "do not support a grounded directional decision".
- Base rates shift across splits. Train: 97 ACCEPT, 153 MODIFY, 96 REJECT, 4 INSUFFICIENT. Test: 67, 2, 81, 0. The generator proposed NO_UPDATE for 38.6% of Train and 96.7% of Test.
- Annotation: "One primary human annotator followed a written ontology-governance protocol." Intra-annotator agreement comes from 10% blinded repeats: κ 0.957, 0.844 and 1.000 on the three splits.
- Evaluation protocol: fit on Train, use Calibration only for selection or calibration, evaluate on Test. Random reshuffling "is not the canonical protocol".

## Assumptions

A single annotator's adjudication is the ground truth, with intra-rater reliability only and no inter-rater agreement. The generator's proposals define what can be judged: one generator, so recall of needed updates is measured only through the prefilter audit. Downstream safety is scored as a label (downstream_safe), not by running the ontology.

## Concepts

Ontology update proposal; active ontology snapshot (O0→O1→O2); adjudication labels; component correctness; prefilter audit; source-text-free release with hash-verified reconstruction.

## Bearing on the record

The exchange says it "evaluates ontology-update proposals against the active ontology snapshot, with accept/modify/reject decisions". It adds that its card "reports a single primary annotator, placeholder publication metadata and no repository-wide license", and recommends treating it "as a possible supplementary dataset, not a settled primary benchmark". **Accurate on every point.** The exchange omits the fourth label (INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE) and the sequential O0→O2 construction, which is the feature that matters most for the luria questions.

- **[CASE-253](../cases.d/CASE-253.md)** (governed schema adaptation): **supports a design element and supplies a ready instrument**. The case's table of failure kinds includes "missing evidence … change nothing", and its rule is that tasks must include "cases where the correct action is no schema change". TRACE-OU-Bench builds both in: INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, REJECT as "unwarranted, redundant", and many NO_UPDATE proposals. Its sequential snapshots match the case's demand that a repair be checked by "later operations on the repaired record". The difference: TRACE-OU scores a decision about a proposal. The case asks an agent to recognize the misfit, and to propose and migrate the change itself.
- **[QUESTION-458](../questions.d/QUESTION-458.md)**: **leaves open**. The benchmark's protocol is a governance protocol (accept, modify, reject under written rules), but it records no rationale and no migration of existing entries. It is a further point in the comparison class for "performance-gated" against "governed" evolution, nearer the governed end than EvoOntology.
- **[CLAIM-667](../claims.d/CLAIM-667.md)**: no direct bearing. There is no fixed-against-modifiable arm.

## Limitations

The reader did not inspect the data, so the label counts and κ values are as the card reports them. VALIDATION.md was not read. The paper is unpublished, its authors unknown.
