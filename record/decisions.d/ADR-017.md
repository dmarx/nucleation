# ADR-017: social-ontology joins the topics

## Context

The owner asked how the record covers social ontology. It covered the
philosophy of mind of groups, a strand built up around [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md):
Schwitzgebel's conscious United States, Kammerer's reply, List on group
agents, and List & Pettit and Huebner unread. It held almost nothing on the
prior question of what a group, an institution or a social fact is.
Searle appeared ten times, all for the Chinese Room. Gilbert, Bratman,
Tuomela, Lewis's *Convention*, Guala, Epstein, Haslanger and Hacking were
absent.

None of the existing words could hold the subject:

- `society-and-governance` is law, politics, policy and institutions as
  practised;
- `social-science` is empirical and theoretical work on people and
  societies;
- `metaphysics`, `agency` and `mereology` each hold one face of it.

The group works carried all of these at once, which is how a missing word
shows. The owner asked for the literature to be filed and the topic added.

## Decision

Add `social-ontology` to `topics`, in the `philosophy` group, after
`phenomenology`. Its blurb: "what social facts, groups, institutions and
social kinds are, and what makes them exist — collective intentionality,
joint commitment, status functions, convention, social construction, and
whether groups can be agents or minds".

The last clause is deliberate. Whether a group can be an agent or a mind is
asked by social ontologists and philosophers of mind alike, and the
record's group-mind works belong on this page as well as on
`consciousness`.

**Retagged.** No tag was removed.

- **First tag:** [LIT-391](../literature.d/LIT-391.md) (List & Pettit, *Group Agency*), whose thesis that
  group agents exist over and above their members is a social-ontological
  claim.
- **Third tag, after their subject:** [LIT-401](../literature.d/LIT-401.md) (List, "What is it like to be
  a group agent?") and [LIT-426](../literature.d/LIT-426.md) (Huebner, *Macrocognition*).
- **Last tag:** [LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md) (Schwitzgebel, the United States), [LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md)
  (Tomasello, obligation as pressure from a joint agent "we") and [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md)
  (Griesemer & Shavit, collections, conexuses and communities).

## Alternatives considered

- **Widen `society-and-governance`.** It is the record's word for courts,
  doctrine and policy, read for what they did. A question about what makes
  a court exist at all is a different subject, and a reader browsing either
  would be badly served by the other.
- **Widen `social-science`.** The subject is argued by philosophers about
  the objects social scientists study. Economists' institutions (North,
  Guala) are on the boundary, and they carry both words.
- **A narrower word, `collective-intentionality` or `group-agency`.** Each
  names one programme. Social construction and social kinds (Hacking,
  Haslanger, Ásta) and Epstein's grounding framework are not about
  intentions or agency, and would need a third word.

## Consequences

The filings that follow in the same contribution lead with the new word.
[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s anti-nesting question now has a literature to draw on: the
grounding-and-anchoring distinction (Epstein), rules in equilibrium
(Hindriks & Guala), and whether group agents are "over and above" their
members (List & Pettit).
