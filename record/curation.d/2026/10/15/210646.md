The owner asked whether the warrant splits had downstream effects on the
record's argument. A check of every relation into the changed claims found
none that broke an argument. Two findings were acted on here, at the
owner's request.

**Citations of moved content.** Bodies were not rewritten when claims were
split, so quotations stayed accurate, but some passages cited a claim for
content it no longer holds as its own. A reader went through every
citation of the split claims in live text and found fifteen that cite the
moved part; nine more were found in reading notes, a THEORY and LIT
entries. Where a passage quotes the original, the original's code stays and
the new home is added; where it only refers to the content, it now names
the new claim. Dated text (curation, workbench, history notes, decisions,
and notes that list claims by their titles at a date) was left as it was.

**A suspected missing premise, cleared.** [CLAIM-190](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-190.md) says it needed
"probe-relative equivalence and the point that training probes cannot
certify". That second point was first read as [CLAIM-264](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-264.md)'s evidential rule,
now split off, but it is [CLAIM-266](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-266.md), which [CLAIM-190](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-190.md) already rests on. No
change was needed.

**Left for the owner.** Whether [CLAIM-123](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-123.md) and [CLAIM-264](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-264.md), now pure
derivations, should be promoted to Active; and twelve demonstrated claims
from the classification pass that rest on claims that are not demonstrated,
each of which should either state its premise as a condition or be marked
interpretive.
