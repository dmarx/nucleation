# NOTE-1327: Yu et al. — AvAtar active OT alignment

## Contribution

It defines active learning for alignment by optimal transport (Definition
3.1, p. 3) and gives a query score built from OT itself. Supervision
enters the transport problem by shrinking the costs of known pairs,
C̃ = (1 − βH) ⊙ C (eq. 3, p. 3). The score of a candidate pair is how fast
a utility of the transport plan would change if that pair were
supervised, ∇_{H_ij} f (Definition 4.1, p. 4). The score of a source
object is its pair scores weighted by its row of the current plan
(Definition 4.4, eq. 8, p. 5). The derivative through the entropic OT
solution is computed by the adjoint-state method from one (n + m) linear
system (Lemma 4.2), solved by conjugate gradients (Lemma 4.3), in time
linear in n + m when the plan is sparse (Theorem 4.5).

## Key insight

The question the oracle answers is fixed: "Given an object xs from the
source set X , which target object yt ∈ Y is the correct alignment of xs
?" (p. 3). The only choice is which xs to ask about. AvAtar ranks them by
a first-order prediction of how much an answer would sharpen the global
alignment. Since accuracy on unlabeled objects is unknown, "we quantify
the quality of alignment results by effective utility functions" (p. 3):
the plan's squared norm, its entropy, or its graph consistency. The
method thus values a question by its expected effect on the coherence of
the current answer, not on its correctness, and is scored on correctness.

## Assumptions

- **Entropic OT with a fixed cost.** Lemma 4.2 differentiates the
  optimality conditions of min ⟨C̃, T⟩ − ε Ent(T) over Π(µ, ν) (eqs. 2,
  11–13, p. 14). It holds when T is that problem's exact solution and C̃
  does not depend on T.
- **Supervision as cost shrinkage**, C̃ = (1 − βH) ⊙ C, with β = 1 by
  default (Table 9, p. 22), so a labeled pair's cost becomes zero.
- **A continuous relaxation of the label.** The score is a derivative at
  the current H, a first-order estimate of a change of size β in one
  entry.
- **An oracle that answers correctly**, one source object per query, all
  queries of equal cost (p. 3).
- **A proxy utility f** chosen per task (Table 2, p. 4).
- **Experimental protocol (B.3, p. 19):** "randomly selects 20%
  ground-truth alignment as prior supervision for each run", then "all
  baselines are given the same total query budget of 20% ground-truth",
  in 10 query rounds; 5 runs averaged; the aligners' default
  hyperparameters.

## Key results

- **Lemma 4.2 (p. 4; proof pp. 14–16):** ∇_C̃ f = (1/ε) T ⊙ (y_α1ᵀ +
  1y_βᵀ − ∇_T f), where (y_α, y_β) solves [[diag µ, T], [Tᵀ, diag ν]] y =
  [(T ⊙ ∇_T f)1, (T ⊙ ∇_T f)ᵀ1] (eqs. 5–6). I checked it by finite
  differences (history).
- **Singularity and solvability (A.2–A.3, pp. 16–17):** A is singular,
  with (1, −1) in its null space; it is positive semidefinite since
  zᵀAz = Σ T_ij(s_i + t_j)²; b is orthogonal to (1, −1), so CG converges
  (Lemma 4.3). The gradient is unaffected by which solution is taken, since
  adding c(1, −1) to y leaves y_α1ᵀ + 1y_βᵀ unchanged. The appendix says
  the null space is exactly span(1, −1) (eq. 41); that holds when the
  support of T is connected, as it is for a dense entropic plan, but not
  for the thresholded sparse plan used for speed. The conclusion survives,
  since b is orthogonal to each component's vector.
- **Pair and object scores (eqs. 7–8, p. 5):** I(p_ij) = −βC_ij(∇_C̃ f)_ij
  and I(p_i) = Σ_j T_ij I(p_ij), the weights justified as "Ti,j encodes the
  posterior alignment probability" (p. 5).
- **Complexity and convergence (Theorems 4.5–4.6, pp. 5–6, 17–18):**
  O((k/n_b)K(n + m)) time with K CG iterations, by sparsity of T; CG on the
  singular system converges linearly at the usual rate in the ratio of
  extreme nonzero eigenvalues (Hayami 2018).
- **Network alignment (Table 3, p. 7):** four datasets, two aligners
  (PARROT, JOENA), seven baseline strategies. The best AvAtar variant is
  best in all 16 cells. Its margin over the best baseline is 0.003 to 0.028
  MRR ("up to 2.8% in MRR", p. 6), over Random 0.027 to 0.158.
- **Cross-domain alignment (Tables 4–5, p. 7):** image–text retrieval on
  CIFAR-10-C and ImageNet-C and grounding on COCO and Flickr30K, with GOT-W
  and GOT-FGW; AvAtar best in every cell, by under 0.025 Recall@1 over the
  best baseline.
- **Posterior weighting matters (Table 7, p. 9):** with uniform weights
  instead of T, MRR on Douban falls from 0.837 to 0.669 (AvAtar-L2), below
  Random's 0.751 in Table 3 at the same round; on Phone-Email from 0.629 to
  0.522, below Random's 0.568.
- **Efficiency (Fig. 2, p. 7; Table 6, p. 8; Fig. 10, p. 21):** up to 25×
  faster than TopMatchings; sparse CG up to 8.6× faster than dense with the
  same MRR; linear scaling on ER graphs to 10⁴ nodes.
- **Against a non-OT pipeline (Table 8, p. 21):** PARROT + AvAtar beats
  FINAL + Attent by 0.38 to 0.49 MRR, which compares aligners more than
  query strategies.

## Method

Algorithm 1 (p. 5): remove already-labeled source objects from the pool;
solve the alignment; repeat until the budget k is spent: compute I(p_i)
for all pool objects, take the top n_b, ask the oracle their matches, set
H_ij = 1 for the answers, re-solve the alignment.

## Concepts

- **Active OT-based alignment** — given objects X, Y, an OT aligner, prior
  supervision H, budget k and an oracle, choose k source objects whose
  labels "maximally improves the alignment performance for the unlabeled
  objects in X" (Definition 3.1, p. 3).
- **Utility function** — a scalar of the plan standing in for alignment
  quality: ‖T‖², the plan's entropy, or a graph-consistency term
  tr(TᵀM₁T) + tr(TM₂Tᵀ) with Laplacians M₁, M₂ (Table 2, p. 4).
- **Pairwise query impact** — ∇_{H_ij} f (Definition 4.1).
- **Posterior object query impact** — the T-weighted average of a source
  object's pairwise impacts (Definition 4.4).

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-092](../terms.d/TERM-092.md) and [CLAIM-475](../claims.d/CLAIM-475.md): a clean instance of choosing within a fixed
  vocabulary, and only that.** Be exact about what is chosen. The
  candidate is a source object; the oracle supplies its match; the answer
  enters as one cost entry set to zero. The question form, the two object
  sets, the cost function, the aligner and the utility are all fixed in
  advance, and every query costs one unit of a budget of 20% of the true
  pairs, spent in ten batches after a random 20% (p. 19). What is
  evaluated is MRR or Recall@1 of the resulting alignment. So AvAtar
  allocates a budget over questions already askable, by a first-order
  estimate of each answer's effect on a proxy. Nothing in it extends the
  vocabulary in which questions become askable: it cannot propose a new
  object, a new relation to ask about, or a new form of answer, which is
  the half of [TERM-092](../terms.d/TERM-092.md) that A7 kept apart. It also says something about
  [CLAIM-475](../claims.d/CLAIM-475.md)'s costs: the paper does measure the cost of choosing (query
  time, Fig. 2, "time required to select query candidates", p. 19), but
  charges the budget only for answers. The cost of formulating questions
  is zero here, because the question set is given, so the paper bears on
  neither of the claim's defeaters.
- **[CLAIM-496](../claims.d/CLAIM-496.md): a working use of coherence as a proxy for fidelity,
  and its limit.** The utilities reward questions whose answers would make
  the plan more nearly a permutation or more consistent with the graphs,
  coherence measures, because accuracy on unlabeled objects is unknown
  (p. 3). It works on these benchmarks, where the truth is near one-to-one
  (Fig. 5). Table 7 shows how much rests on the current plan: averaging
  impacts uniformly instead of by T does worse than random queries. The
  record's claim predicts the failure case: where coherence and fidelity
  come apart, a coherence-driven chooser would ask the questions that
  sharpen a wrong alignment. The paper tests no such case.
- **[CASE-185](../cases.d/CASE-185.md):** if its correspondence arm fits stochastic kernels or
  GW with anchors, AvAtar's score is a principled way to choose which
  items to have rated or anchored next under a rating budget. It assumes
  an oracle that knows the true correspondence, which a translation study
  may not have.
- **For the anthology, not here:** the paper's practical advice, which
  query strategy to use for OT-based alignment, is an instruction for
  machine-learning practice. The LIT carries anthology-candidate.

## Limitations

- **No variances.** Tables 3–5 average 5 runs and report no spread; the
  margins over the best baseline, often under one point, cannot be judged.
- **Lemma 4.2 assumes fixed-cost entropic OT.** PARROT, JOENA and GOT-FGW
  are not that; the Gromov term of FGW makes the cost depend on T. How the
  gradient is applied to them is not stated, and Appendix D lists GW as a
  future extension (p. 22).
- **First-order scores for a finite change.** With β = 1 a label sets a
  cost to zero; the derivative at H = 0 predicts that only locally.
- **Whether labeled objects are excluded from the reported MRR** is not
  stated: B.3 averages over "the size of source object set" (p. 19), while
  Definition 3.1 targets unlabeled objects.
- **Sign slip in Table 2:** the entropy utility is written ‖T ⊙ log T‖₁,
  which is the plan's entropy, but the gradient given, log T + 1, is that
  of Σ T log T, the negative entropy. The text's intent, to favour more
  deterministic plans (p. 4), fits the gradient, not the formula.
- **Scope.** Point cloud registration is named in the abstract and not
  tested; the authors expect less benefit there (p. 21).

## Open questions

- Does the T-weighted score still help when the initial alignment is badly
  wrong, as with fewer prior labels than 20%?
- How does the first-order score compare with the actual effect of a label,
  computed by re-solving, on small problems?
- What would an active method look like that may also propose new anchor
  types or new relations to measure, so that the vocabulary itself is
  chosen?
