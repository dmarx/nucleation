# LIT-1077: Memory as a Computational Resource

Dasgupta, I. and Gershman, S. J. (2021). Memory as a computational resource. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 25(3), 240–251. DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2020.12.008

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-865](../notes.d/NOTE-865.md)). A short review arguing that memory is used to avoid redundant computation: results are stored and reused (memoization), either whole ("top-down") or as solutions to subproblems ("bottom-up"), and amortized inference and deep Q-networks are "distributed" memo-tables that generalize to new inputs. It illustrates this in mental arithmetic, mental imagery, probabilistic inference and planning, mostly with response times that flatten with practice and with biases that a cache explains (reuse of earlier inferences, choices based on stale cached values, reuse of policy fragments). It has equations for its examples but no cost model: the space–time trade-off is named, and when caching pays is listed as an outstanding question. Memory is internal throughout; retrieval cost is mentioned once, and offloading or external stores not at all.

## Why this is here

Turned up by the citation search on [QUESTION-095](../questions.d/QUESTION-095.md) (amortized retrieval as amortized computation) and registered at the owner's request. It states that retrieval is computation, for internal memory, and by the search cites no offloading work.

The reading ([NOTE-865](../notes.d/NOTE-865.md)) corrects this. The paper does not say retrieval is computation; it says memory replaces computation and treats retrieval as nearly free. What it adds is staleness in the strict sense: cached values "not yet updated" after the world changes.
