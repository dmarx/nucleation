# CLAIM-182: Storage is not anchoring, addressability is: a structure that is kept but cannot be pointed at part by part does not stop re-derivation, and the same content becomes an anchor once its parts get addresses

## The claim

Both exchanges that this record reconstructed were stored the whole time,
as shared transcripts (workbench 2026/10/07/011440 and 2026/10/09/025539
give the links). The participants could have scrolled back. They
re-derived instead. The permissiveness objection came back six or more
times as though new ([ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md)). The passive/active distinction came back
after the manuscript dropped it ([CLAIM-004](CLAIM-004.md)). The relativity analogy came
back after it was rejected ([ARG-004](../arguments.d/ARG-004.md)). Storage did not stop any of it.

What changed things was giving the content addresses. The reconstructions
labelled every turn, U for the owner and A for the assistant, and filed the
recurring moves under codes. From then on a claim could cite "A125" or
[CLAIM-017](CLAIM-017.md), and a later entry could rest on it, object to it or supersede
it. The transcript's content did not change. Its parts became addressable.

The same holds for a manuscript draft ([CLAIM-178](CLAIM-178.md)). A draft persists,
but its claims cannot be pointed at one by one. Nothing rests on them, so
when a draft drops one, no reference breaks and nothing reports the loss.

So for anchoring ([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)), the trigger that matters is the one
that gives a thing an address. Persistence is necessary but not enough.

## What it does not say

- It does not say storage is useless. Without the transcripts nothing
  could have been reconstructed. Storage is what makes addressing possible
  later.
- It does not say an address is enough. A code that nothing points at
  anchors nothing. Stable identity and discoverability may matter too
  ([QUESTION-038](../questions.d/QUESTION-038.md)).
- The comparison is not controlled. Re-derivation inside a live exchange
  and citation after reconstruction happened at different times, for
  different reasons.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

The comparison is not separated from who was reading. The re-derivations
happened inside live exchanges with collaborators who kept no memory, and
the citations came after reconstruction, from readers with the record open.
No case counts re-derivation of the same stored content before and after its
parts got addresses, which [CASE-183](../cases.d/CASE-183.md)'s inbound references per code could
begin to supply.

## Note of 2026-11-15: keyed retrieval from superposed storage, an analogy

<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1876 THEORY-1882 CASE-271 — Proposed; the readings and the case of 2026-11-15, cited as analogies, nothing here rests on them -->

CLAIM-182's distinction between keeping content and being able to address it has exact computational and physical models. Three readings of 2026-11-15 give them: Plate's holographic reduced representations ([LIT-2644](../literature.d/LIT-2644.md), [THEORY-1876](../theory.d/THEORY-1876.md)), holographic embeddings ([LIT-2624](../literature.d/LIT-2624.md)) and angle-multiplexed volume holography ([LIT-2618](../literature.d/LIT-2618.md), [THEORY-1882](../theory.d/THEORY-1882.md)). A phase-coded memory is another. In each, many contents are kept superposed in one medium, with nothing spatially separate, and each is still recovered part by part through a key: a cue vector, a relation vector or a reference angle, retrieving by correlation with cross-talk from the rest. The key's selectivity comes from the reader or the medium (dimension, or thickness through Bragg selectivity), not from where the content sits. An address there is a relation between a cue and the stored pattern, not a location. That would sharpen "What it does not say": an address need not be a location.

[CASE-271](../cases.d/CASE-271.md) shows three further things:
- the same association can be retained but unaddressed under one cue and addressed under another;
- readdressing, suppression and deletion look the same at a single query;
- a designed extra query restores what a suppressed readout hid.

HRR adds a qualification. Keyed readout of a superposed store is noisy, and it becomes exact only through a second, content-addressed clean-up memory.

These are models of addressability in a memory. They are not evidence about records or archives, the claim's own domain, and none bears on whether codes suppress re-derivation. No status change.

that date), and almost every code is cited; that tells an anchor from a kept
structure only among things already coded, and does not count re-derivation
of the same stored content before and after its parts got addresses, which
is the comparison the claim needs. The readings of [LIT-2618](../literature.d/LIT-2618.md), [LIT-2624](../literature.d/LIT-2624.md) and
[LIT-2644](../literature.d/LIT-2644.md) (the note of 2026-11-15) are models of keyed retrieval, not
evidence about records. The before-and-after count is still not made.
