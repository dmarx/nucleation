# CLAIM-tmpkx7ct: Execution impedance, independent constraint and intervention opportunity vary independently: an institution can cut the time and cost of execution while reducing, preserving or expanding others' opportunities to intervene, and can keep a step the executor cannot pass alone that opens no opportunity at all, so the three must be assessed separately, and whether the owner's procedural friction is impedance or constraint is left open

## The claim

Three properties of a step in a process ([TERM-tmp91lqy](../terms.d/TERM-tmp91lqy.md)):

- **execution impedance**: whether it adds time or cost to execution;
- **independent constraint**: whether the executor can pass it alone;
- **intervention opportunity**: whether another party can bring an
  objection that changes what the process does.

They vary independently. The readings of 2026-11-16 supply instances:

- **A gate without an opportunity.** The HKMA paper ([THEORY-tmpeq8z1](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpeq8z1.md)):
  "A formal approval step is not, by itself, meaningful human oversight";
  an approval the process waits on, which volume has made perfunctory, is a
  gate and impedance with no real opportunity. A standing limit stops only
  what was foreseen when it was set.
- **A gate without impedance.** The HKMA's agent harness, and Alfrink et
  al.'s decisions that must pass a second system, with disagreements flagged
  for human review ([THEORY-tmp97bfr](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp97bfr.md)): continuation depends on a condition
  the executor does not control, at no cost in time.
- **Impedance used for an opportunity.** The HKMA's velocity limits slow
  execution to what review can handle: impedance added for the sake of an
  opportunity.
- **All three removed together.** Bovens and Zouridis ([THEORY-tmpn8tsl](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpn8tsl.md)): the superior's
  signature and the official's discretion removed by system-level
  bureaucracy were gates, impedance and opportunities at once.

So an institution can lower execution cost while reducing, preserving or
expanding others' opportunities to intervene, and the three must be assessed
separately. This is the claim that survives the open question of whether
"procedural friction" in the owner's claim means impedance or constraint.

## The question left open

If friction is a gate, A's objection removes impedance and not friction,
and the owner's "characterizes" reading survives as nearly a definition
that is false of gates opening no opportunity. If friction is impedance,
the objection stands against "characterizes". In either case the owner's
"exposes" reading keeps its content: automation that removes the steps
which wait on people removes the moments at which a decision in progress
is visible to them. The record does not settle the word.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Hong Kong Monetary Authority, read

The HKMA's architecture is the reply's best case, but it raises the question
who the "executor" is. The limits bind the agent. The deploying institution
sets and enforces them, through its own change control, though with an
independent control owner (§5.3) and with the HKMA's possible "suspension-
related measures" above it (executive summary, p. 7). If the executor is the
agent, the limit is enforced by a mechanism the executor does not control,
and the reply holds. If the executor is the institution, the HKMA case is the
defeater's: a standing limit that keeps the process interruptible while the
executor controls the mechanism. The reply should name its executor.
