## Summary

Martela and Ryan ask whether beneficence, "the sense of having a positive
impact in the lives of other people" (p. 116), meets SDT's second minimum
criterion for a basic need: that its frustration predict ill-being over and
above the frustration of autonomy, competence and relatedness. Study 1
(N = 332, MTurk, US) builds a four-item beneficence-frustration scale
("the feeling that one's behaviors and actions have caused harm to
others", p. 117). Study 2 (N = 444, general life) and Study 3 (N = 426,
"the single unhappiest event" of the last two weeks) enter it after the
three BPNSFS frustration scales. It correlates with every ill-being
indicator, but adds no unique variance to any, and the three needs fully
mediate it. The authors propose a new category, the "basic wellness
enhancer": satisfaction raises well-being, frustration has no unique
cost. Beneficence "might be better considered" one (p. 130), stated as
tentative and "premature".

## Contribution

- A beneficence-frustration scale (4 items, α .84–.93), separable by
  factor analysis from beneficence satisfaction and from the three need
  frustrations.
- The first test of the frustration criterion for a candidate need.
- The conceptual distinction need / wellness enhancer, set beside SDT's
  older growth-need / deficit-need distinction (pp. 118, 130–131): a
  deficit need (safety) protects against ill-being without enhancing
  growth; a wellness enhancer does the reverse.

## Key results

- **Criteria (pp. 117–118).** Two that a candidate "must minimally meet"
  (Ryan & Deci 2017, p. 81): satisfaction uniquely enhances well-being;
  frustration uniquely harms it. Further: explanatory (mediates), content-
  specifying ("a predictor of wellness rather than an indicator"), growth
  not deficit, universal, plus an evolutionary story, no moderation by
  valuing, and gravitation toward need-satisfying settings.
- **Satisfaction criterion already met** in earlier work (pp. 116,
  118): Martela & Ryan 2016b, three studies incl. a 10-day diary;
  "beneficence as a candidate need has passed one crucial test".
- **Study 1 (pp. 121–122).** Two-factor EFA, satisfaction vs frustration
  (r = −.462), frustration items "reverse-scored"; two satisfaction items
  cross-load > .50. Frustration correlates −.71 with self-esteem, which
  the authors call "surprisingly high". Both satisfaction and frustration
  predict vitality, meaning, life satisfaction, self-esteem; only
  frustration predicts negative affect, only satisfaction positive affect.
- **Study 2 (pp. 123–125, Tables 1–3).** Beneficence frustration
  correlates .63/.81/.72 with autonomy/competence/relatedness frustration;
  the .81 with competence is "noteworthy given that the items ... don't
  have much content overlap". Step 3 ΔR² ≤ .003 for six of seven outcomes;
  the exception, meaning in life (β = .20, p = .003), runs the wrong way
  and is read as "a suppression effect likely due to multicollinearity"
  (pp. 125–126). Full mediation by the three needs.
- **Footnote 2 (p. 124).** Without competence frustration as a control,
  beneficence frustration does predict positive affect, self-acceptance,
  negative affect, depression, anxiety (Study 2) and situational
  depression and anxiety (Study 3): "it is especially competence
  frustration that accounts for the insignificant relations".
- **Study 3 (pp. 126–128, Tables 4–5).** Same null for negative affect,
  situational depression, situational anxiety (ΔR² ≤ .002); full mediation.
  Positive affect and situational meaning correlate positively with
  frustration in the unhappy-event frame, which the authors explain post
  hoc (sad events are meaningful).
- **Conclusion (p. 129).** "in no case was it independently connected to
  any well-being or ill-being indicator when controlling for the
  contribution of the three basic need frustrations".
- **Three explanations offered (p. 129):** design fault (judged unlikely;
  the "I fear" items checked and dropped without change); self-deception
  about harming others making self-reports unreliable; or the effects run
  through the loss of the three needs (forced harm → autonomy; accidental
  harm → competence; damaged ties → relatedness).
- **Wellness enhancer (p. 130).** "universal conditions for improving
  human thriving, the satisfaction of which should lead to more optimal
  development ... But their frustration in life might not directly make
  that life miserable." Other candidates named: nature, morality, novelty.
  "it is premature to make any conclusions of whether beneficence should
  be seen as a wellness enhancer".

## Assumptions

- The BPNSFS frustration scales validly measure the three needs'
  frustration, and frustration is "an active thwarting ... rather than
  mere lack of satisfaction" (p. 117).
- Incremental variance in cross-sectional self-report regressions is the
  test of the frustration criterion; the established needs get priority in
  the regression order (entered at Step 2).
- Beneficence frustration is operationalised as having harmed others, not
  as being prevented from helping (the alternative is named, p. 129).

## Verdict on the source's characterization

The record's characterizations are right in substance. NOTE-440's "its
frustration 'failed to predict incremental variance above the frustration
of autonomy, competence, and relatedness', so it is classed a 'well-being
enhancer'" matches the finding, with two corrections: the paper's term is
"basic wellness enhancer", and the classing is offered as a "tentative"
proposal the authors call "premature" (pp. 130–131), not a verdict. The
LIT summary's "beneficence's frustration adds no variance" is accurate for
Studies 2–3. The 2026-11-03 search-result abstract (LIT-1983 history v2)
is confirmed by the paper. NOTE-1502's "test beneficence by the
frustration criterion" is right.

## Limitations (including gaps in the argument)

- Cross-sectional, self-report, MTurk, US only; the authors say so (p. 130).
- **Entry order asserted, not argued.** Beneficence is entered last
  (Step 3), so shared variance is credited to the three needs by design.
  Full mediation with beneficence as X and the needs as M presumes the
  causal direction (harm → need frustration → ill-being) that a
  cross-sectional design cannot show; the reverse (need frustration →
  feeling one harms others) is not considered.
- **Collinearity unaddressed as a rival reading.** Footnote 2 shows the
  result depends on controlling competence frustration (r = .81). The
  authors read this as competence explaining beneficence; it could equally
  be that the two scales share a negatively keyed self-evaluation factor
  (cf. the −.71 with self-esteem). No latent or bifactor model is tried.
- **Asymmetry in standards.** Earlier satisfaction findings are accepted
  as meeting criterion 1, while the frustration null is offered as
  possibly the measure's fault (self-deception), so the test cannot clearly
  disconfirm the authors' fallback either way.
- **Wellness enhancer is defined by the null.** The new category is
  introduced from one failed test, and its membership criterion (no unique
  frustration effect) is the result being explained.
- Appendix 1 items were not seen in this copy.

## Candidate THEORY

"Martela and Ryan (2019) find that frustration of beneficence, measured as
feeling one has harmed others, correlates with ill-being but adds no
variance to it once autonomy, competence and relatedness frustration are
controlled, and on that ground propose, tentatively, that beneficence is a
basic wellness enhancer, whose satisfaction raises well-being but whose
frustration has no unique cost, rather than a basic psychological need."

promote_when: Evidence from a design that does not rest on entry order
among collinear self-reports: a longitudinal or experimental study in
which thwarted beneficence (manipulated, or measured by others' reports)
fails to predict later ill-being once the three needs' frustration is
modelled, ideally with a latent model separating beneficence from
competence frustration; or a replication outside US online samples (see
LIT-1980). Refuted if beneficence frustration predicts ill-being uniquely
under such a design, or if the null disappears when competence
frustration is modelled latently or with balanced keying.
