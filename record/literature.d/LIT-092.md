# LIT-092: DOJ Changes Course

Joyce Vance (2026), *Civil Discourse with Joyce Vance (Substack newsletter)* — <https://joycevance.substack.com/p/doj-changes-course>

## Key takeaways

- Vance reports that, a day after moving to voluntarily dismiss its appeals in the consolidated law-firm executive-order cases in the D.C. Circuit, the Justice Department filed a "Motion To Withdraw Motion To Voluntarily Dismiss Appeals". She argues that the reversal shows the Department serving the President's wishes rather than the law, at a cost to its credibility with courts.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

This is a follow-up to the author's previous night's post. The Associate Attorney General filed a motion withdrawing the government's motion to dismiss its appeals of lower-court rulings that struck down executive orders targeting four law firms. The firms opposed. Vance explains the procedural posture (plaintiffs/appellees against government appellants, cases consolidated in the D.C. Circuit, opening brief due that Friday) and predicts that the government will lose. She then connects the episode to broader criticism of DOJ's credibility: the presumption of regularity, a contempt threat in Minnesota habeas cases, the handling of the Epstein files, and Senator Tillis's call for DHS Secretary Noem to resign.
