# CLAIM-076: Cross-modal chains expose reconstruction because media differ in expressive affordances, and each interpreter, such as a captioner, may add motives and relations that were not present

## The claim

A110 §21: "Each stage operates through a different observational system. Language
expresses temporality, irony, and implied motives in particular ways. Images
express spatial arrangements, posture, facial expression, and visible
interactions. The captioner may infer motives or speaker relations that were not
explicitly present. This makes cross-modal telephone games particularly useful
for studying how relationships are reconstructed rather than directly copied." Its
illustrative pathway, "a hypothetical chain ... not a reported experimental
result": friends laughing → a diner image → a caption about a friend's poor
spending → an adviser's caution.

## Where it went

Manuscript §10 Case III runs the chain ([CASE-009](../cases.d/CASE-009.md)) without saying why media matter.
C7 Appendix D: "For cross-modal chains, do not let captioners see the source text
or the intended communicative condition unless testing a specified
side-information intervention." That is the control the affordance argument
implies, kept without its argument. The improving direction is [CLAIM-087](CLAIM-087.md).
