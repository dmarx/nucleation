# THEORY-536: A group agent's attitudes are real and non-redundant: each group belief needs members' role-based acceptance, by assent in the group's name or by acting on it in an executive role, but that acceptance does not explain or reduce the group belief, because which members' acceptance counts, and that it plays the role of belief, is fixed only from the group-level view

## Source

Christian List and Philip Pettit (2012), "Episteme Symposium on *Group
Agency*: Replies to Gaus, Cariani, Sylvan, and Briggs", *Episteme*
9(3):293–309, [LIT-730](../literature.d/LIT-730.md), read in full in [NOTE-582](../notes.d/NOTE-582.md) from the authors'
typescript. Page numbers are the typescript's. *Group Agency* ([LIT-391](../literature.d/LIT-391.md)) is
unread; its pages ("GA") are quoted from the replies. The four
commentaries were not read.

## What was actually shown

**The reply to Sylvan (pp. 10–14).**

- Role-based acceptance is members treating a proposition as true for
  group purposes, by assenting in the group's name or acting on it in an
  executive role (p. 12). It is necessary for every group belief.
- It does not explain the group belief: "it is the group belief that
  explains the role-based member acceptance, not the role-based member
  acceptance that explains the group belief" (p. 13). Knowing which
  members' acceptance counts, and that the structure lets the putative
  belief play the role of belief, already requires the group-level view.
- No "plausible, accessible recipe" renders group agents redundant
  (p. 14). Non-redundancy is a matter of degree, and the theory lies toward
  the non-redundant end (citing GA 77–78).
- Executive members must act on the group's propositions but "need not
  personally assent to it" (p. 12). Workable group agents "allow any
  proposition, however long endorsed, to be rejected on grounds of
  inconsistency" (pp. 11–12).

**What the agential view adds.** It lets us "see higher-level
regularities" and respond "in abstraction from much of what happens at the
lower level" (GA 6, quoted p. 10).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Sylvan's challenge was that a
recipe from member acceptance makes the group posit dispensable. The reply
could have conceded a recipe for some class of group. It concedes only
that redundancy comes in degrees.

## What this does not say

- **That a recipe has been shown impossible.** The claim is that no
  plausible, accessible one exists. That is argued against one commentator
  in a reply ([NOTE-582](../notes.d/NOTE-582.md), C3 moderate), not proved.
- **That any group agent exists.** The book's argument omits the empirical
  premise that collections form and act on robustly rational attitudes,
  which it "mostly take[s] for granted" (GA 75, quoted p. 14; C6 weak).
- **That joint intention is analysed.** An acceptable account of it is
  presupposed, not supplied (p. 13).
- **That group attitudes are felt.** The authors are agnostic about hedonic
  group states (p. 16). Functionalism is assumed throughout (p. 5).
- **That the group's attitudes fail to supervene on members.** [THEORY-127](THEORY-127.md)
  says group judgments supervene on members' whole sets of judgments.
  Non-redundancy is a claim about explanation and accessibility, compatible
  with that supervenience.
- **That the premise-based procedure is the model.** It is "merely a
  particularly simple – but also rather inflexible – instance" (p. 1).
