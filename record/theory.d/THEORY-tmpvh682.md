# THEORY-tmpvh682: Seeing a human-shaped body from the position of one's own eyes while it is touched or moves in time with one's own body makes people report that body as their own and respond to threats against it with more skin conductance than after out-of-time stimulation, even when the body is another person's and their own body is in view; that a first-person view is necessary, and that the illusion moves self-location, were not tested

## Source

Petkova & Ehrsson (2008), "If I were you: perceptual illusion of body
swapping", *PLoS ONE* 3(12):e3832, [LIT-tmp4qdyy](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4qdyy.md), read in full in
[NOTE-tmpb1ult](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpb1ult.md) from the publisher's PDF, with Figures 2, 5 and 7 viewed.

## What was actually shown

**Report.** With HMDs fed from cameras at a mannequin's eyes, two minutes
of synchronous stroking of its abdomen and the participant's unseen one
moved agreement with "It felt like the mannequin's body was my body" from
about −0.55 to about +1.3 on a −3 to +3 scale (n = 32).

**Physiology.** A knife apparently cutting the mannequin drew more skin
conductance after synchronous stroking than after asynchronous stroking or
than a spoon (n = 10); the synchronous–asynchronous difference recurred
when the hands rather than the abdomen were stroked (n = 13) and was
absent for a body-sized box (n = 12).

**The swap.** With cameras on the experimenter's head and hands squeezed
in time, a knife over the experimenter's wrist drew more response than one
over the participant's own visible wrist; after out-of-time squeezing it
did not. Hand × Condition F(1, 19) = 17.08 (n = 20).

What could have come out otherwise: SCR could have tracked the knife and
the scene and not the synchrony; in Experiment 2 the knife–spoon
difference vanished after asynchronous stroking, and in Experiment 5 the
two hands did not differ after asynchronous squeezing.

## What this does not say

- **That a first-person view is necessary.** No third-person condition
  was run. Later work cited by Kilteni et al. ([LIT-tmpt4bij](../literature.d/LIT-tmpt4bij.md)) reports that
  third-person views abolish the full-body illusion; that is their
  evidence, not this paper's.
- **That self-location or a "centre of awareness" moved.** Ownership was
  asked about and threat responses measured; where participants felt
  themselves to be was not.
- **That SCR measures ownership.** The link is cited from earlier work.
  SCR differences show the conditions are treated differently, and
  participants could tell the conditions apart.
- **That only human-shaped bodies can be owned.** The box drew less
  response under both stroking conditions, and no interaction was tested.
- **That the questionnaire effect is free of expectancy.** Three of four
  control statements also rose under synchronous stroking.
- **Anything about identity.** The "swap" is of a perceived body, not of
  memory, character or person (compare [LIT-462](../literature.d/LIT-462.md)).
