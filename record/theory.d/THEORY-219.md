# THEORY-219: Agency in the sense that bears on responsibility needs a standpoint that settles and carries forward revised evaluative standards when its inherited ones break down, and planning, autonomy, memory and coordination do not supply one

## Source

Kahl (2026), [LIT-1033](../literature.d/LIT-1033.md), read in [NOTE-723](../notes.d/NOTE-723.md): §3 (the presupposition
argument), §4 (continuity pressure), §5 (the test and its cases, with the
group-agency reply in §5.3 and the borderline cases in §5.5).

## What was actually shown

An argument, with cases. Nothing is measured.

**Two senses of agency.** Operational autonomy is task execution with
little prompting, and Kahl accepts the word "agentic" for it. Attributive
agency is agency "in the sense relevant to responsibility, authority, and
governance". The paper is about the move from the first to the second.

**The presupposition argument.** Decision theory, planning theory and
hierarchical endorsement all take a unified evaluator as given. Dung's
capability dimensions measure "what a system can do", not "what is at
stake for the system in doing it". So none of them can supply the
evaluator, and using them as sufficient for agency "assumes what it ought
to explain".

**The condition.** Continuity pressure "begins where an inherited
evaluative frame no longer settles how future operation should proceed."
Authorship attaches when the system settles a revised frame and later
assessment proceeds through it. It is absent "where revision is
externally imposed, where rollback avoids carrying forward the
consequences of revision, or where the system merely selects among
revision paths already ranked by a fixed higher-order criterion."

**The cases.** Tool-using agents fail by resetability, reinforcement
learners by an "antecedent admissibility structure" over every update,
multi-agent workflows because "unity of action is not unity of
authorship", and institutional systems because their standards are
"authored elsewhere". A skill-library agent and a continually learning
clinical system come closer and still fail. The paper says what would
count: a system that treats failure "as evidence that the governing
evaluative frame no longer settles what should count as success", settles
a revision under breakdown, and carries it forward.

**Group agents.** A workflow may be a group agent in List and Pettit's
sense. What separates it from a court is "whether revision of that rule
under breakdown is internal or external to the candidate group agent", not
whether the rule was designed.

## What this does not say

- **It is a necessary condition, not a theory of agency.** Kahl says so.
  A system meeting it is not thereby responsible.
- **It is not minimal agency.** [THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md)'s conditions make a bacterium
  an agent. This account is about who can bear responsibility and
  authority, and it drops the organisational grounding: continuity here is
  "not numerical identity, personhood, or selfhood". The two answer
  different questions. Both deny that optimising an externally fixed
  function makes an agent, and they reach that by different routes.
- **It does not say current systems cannot meet it in principle.** The
  negative verdicts are about typical deployments, and the paper declines
  to rule artificial agency out.
- **It does not make "settled under breakdown" precise.** Every revision
  has some prior structure. How much antecedent ranking disqualifies a
  revision is not said, and the paper gives no positive case.
- **It is not an instruction for building systems.** The governance
  sections recommend tracing responsibility to the people who set
  standards. That is advice to institutions about where accountability
  lies, and it is not part of this account. Nothing here says how to
  build a system that would pass.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-092](../questions.d/QUESTION-092.md) (endogenous governance).** A partial answer, and
  not the one the question expected. Internal allocation of authority
  among components does not count: a workflow's "voting rules, ranking
  functions, confidence thresholds" coordinate execution and "do not
  generate authorship of the governing frame". What counts is whether the
  system revises its own integration rule and standards under breakdown.
  So the scheduler's location is beside the point, and the location of
  revision is the point. Two behaviourally matched systems differ on this
  account when only one can revise its own rule, which is the
  question's "stronger sense", located in revision of standards rather
  than in allocation of authority.
- **[CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md) (fixed objectives cannot show self-constitution).**
  Supported from a different direction. Kahl's argument is about
  responsibility, not self-constitution, and cites no enactive work.
  It reaches the same verdict on systems whose objectives are fixed from
  outside, and on self-modification bounded by "an antecedent
  meta-objective". It is stricter about the claim's second answer: a
  modeller's objectives that the population later revises would count
  only if the revision is not ranked by a rule the modeller wrote.
- **[CASE-081](../cases.d/CASE-081.md) (learned self-maintenance).** Kahl's account settles
  the case's question of origin against present organisation in favour
  of present organisation, as the group-agency reply shows. The second
  stage counts only if the system settles revised standards under
  breakdown. Running on a learned policy after the reward stops is not
  that.
- **[CLAIM-285](../claims.d/CLAIM-285.md) and its aggregation rule.** An endorsement scheme
  whose aggregation rule no operator controls is, on this account, an
  orchestration regime, unless the population can revise the rule under
  breakdown.
- **[CLAIM-116](../claims.d/CLAIM-116.md).** Consistent with it. Adaptation, meta-learning and
  self-modification are regulation within a frame; on this account none
  establishes an author of the frame.
- **[CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md).** Continuity pressure resembles the capacity to transform
  the governing organization. The paper does not show the two coincide,
  and nothing here rests on it.
- **Benthall and Salge ([LIT-1031](../literature.d/LIT-1031.md), not read)** argue from enactive
  constitutive autonomy to a condition on legal responsibility. The two
  papers reach neighbouring verdicts on current systems by unrelated
  criteria.
