# THEORY-520: When coordination between two autonomous agents and the continuation of their encounter shape each other, the interaction gains an autonomy of its own, a precarious self-sustaining organization that lasts only while both agents stay autonomous; coordinating movement then coordinates sense-making, so some social understanding, such as recognising a partner, is a property of the interaction's dynamics and not of either individual

## Source

De Jaegher and Di Paolo (2007), [LIT-571](../literature.d/LIT-571.md) (*Phenomenology and the Cognitive
Sciences* 6: 485–507), read in full in [NOTE-456](../notes.d/NOTE-456.md). [NOTE-456](../notes.d/NOTE-456.md) declined a THEORY
"because the source does not carry a claim beyond the definition itself;
the evidence is illustrative". This document is filed Proposed under that
caution: it states the account the definition commits to, and the one
reported result that bears on it.

## What was actually shown

**Coordination is cheap (pp. 490–491).** Coordination is "the non-accidental
correlation between the behaviours of two or more systems that are in
sustained coupling" (p. 490). Pendulum clocks do it; pairs swinging
pendulums phase-lock when they can see each other (Schmidt and O'Brien).
Coordination can be absolute or relative, with phase attraction (Kelso).

**The double influence (pp. 492–494).** In a social encounter the pattern of
coordination affects whether the encounter continues, and the encounter
affects the likelihood of coordination. That loop makes the encounter "an
emergent and autonomous process", "typically a fleeting one".

**The condition (pp. 493–495).** The interaction is social only while the
interactors remain autonomous. Body heat shared at a bus stop is coupling
without regulation, so not social. Coercion that destroys an interactor's
autonomy is, "at that point", no longer social.

**Participatory sense-making (p. 497, Fig. 2).** Because movements are the
tools of sense-making, coordinating movement coordinates sense-making. It
runs from one partner's sense-making being modulated (a video-link delay
changes the meaning of a pause) through orientation (pointing) to meaning
made jointly (charades).

**Perceptual crossing (pp. 501–502), reported.** In Auvray et al.'s minimal
virtual-tape experiment, partners recognise each other because both search
for each other; mutual scanning is the only globally stable configuration.
"The sense-making that solves the task only happens at the level of the
collective dynamics."

**What could have come out otherwise.** In perceptual crossing, recognition
could have been explained by each participant's detection of a moving
object. The reported result is that it depends on both partners searching.

## What this does not say

- **That the interaction is an agent.** It has its own precarious closure,
  but the paper does not call it an agent or a subject. It is dyadic and
  fleeting.
- **That a minutes-long encounter has operational closure in the strict
  sense.** How a fleeting relational pattern has processes that "would run
  down" in isolation is not spelled out ([NOTE-456](../notes.d/NOTE-456.md), Limitations).
- **That individual mechanisms explain nothing.** The case against
  mirror-neuron explanations is an argument (C5, weak).
- **That social difficulty is relational.** The remark on autism is one
  sentence, citing De Jaegher 2006.
- **Anything about groups.** Groups larger than two are set aside.

  additional agent. This account gives the closest criterion the record
  holds short of agency: coordination becomes an autonomous organization

  while the members stay autonomous. It constrains the question; it does
  not answer it, since autonomy here is not agency.
