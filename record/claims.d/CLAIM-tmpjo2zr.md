# CLAIM-tmpjo2zr: An organization can be governed from outside without any of its decisions being made for it, through the evidence its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh evidence; the two routes are distinct and each is possible without the other, so informational and evaluative independence do not imply each other; and where the organization's role is to evaluate another that affects its evidence or resources, the interpretation–authority loop can close between the two institutions, so that formal independence of the evaluator's procedure does not make its evaluation independent

## The claim

The owner, at U20 of the exchange of 2026-10-20: "maybe cases like
reflexive control and regulatory capture could be informative here". A20
§1: both are ways "one organization can acquire influence over another's
governance without formally becoming its governor". Reflexive control
acts on "The target's informational premises, framing, or perceived
alternatives" and leaves "Its decision procedure"; capture acts on "The
regulator's priorities, evidentiary practices, authority allocation, or
decisions" and leaves "The formal regulatory institution". A20 §9: "**Control
over an organization need not consist in issuing its decisions. It can
consist in shaping the conditions under which the organization determines
its own decisions—and in establishing feedback processes through which
that influence becomes self-sustaining.**"

Three parts.

**Two routes.** A26 §4 separated them with two constructions, both
checked here ([CASE-tmp3uarw](../cases.d/CASE-tmp3uarw.md)).

- *Evidence.* For a fixed objective U, a*(e) = argmax_a E[U(a, ω) | e]
  moves with the evidence e supplied, "even if the regulator reasons
  correctly on the evidence it receives." [THEORY-404](../theory.d/THEORY-404.md) (Active, read with the
  proofs) gives the strong form: a sender who commits to a signal moves a
  fully informed Bayesian receiver, who knows the design. A20 §4: "A
  regulator might update its beliefs correctly on every signal it
  receives, yet its decisions can still be systematically influenced by
  who determines which signals are generated and disclosed." [THEORY-146](../theory.d/THEORY-146.md)
  gives a second form, influence through which producers of evidence
  prosper: industry funding biases a community "without corrupting any
  individual".

  The readings of 2026-10-21 fix the reach of this route and qualify
  "reasons correctly". For Bayesian procedures the reach is exact: an
  omniscient designer of information can attain any obedient joint law of
  states and decisions, and no more (Bergemann and Morris, THEORY-tmpetatd).
  Information design holds payoffs fixed, so it cannot reach the second
  route, as the claim's independence of routes says. Two weaker receivers
  are governed too. Weatherall, O'Connor and Bruner's audience is misled by
  genuine, honestly produced results, selected; it applies Bayes to what it
  receives but does not model the selection, which the authors name as a
  remedy (THEORY-tmpnq01l). So "reasons correctly" there means correctly
  on each item, not on the sample; THEORY-404's receiver, who knows the
  design, is the sophisticated form. And Besley and Prat's voters, fully
  rational, are governed by the removal of their evidence: a captured
  press stays silent, they stay at their prior, and they re-elect, neither
  deceived nor informed (THEORY-tmp6l5uw). The OECD report states the route
  as policy: undue influence "can even be achieved without directly
  involving (and without the knowledge of) public decision-makers, by
  manipulating information" (NOTE-tmpk4llg). At the scale of a public,
  agenda-setting is the route's illustration: what reaches the agenda, not
  what is concluded, and in McCombs and Shaw only as an aggregate
  correlation (THEORY-tmpbonn4).
- *Criteria.* With U_λ(a) = v₁(a) + λv₂(a) and λ = 0, "Even perfect
  information about both properties does not ensure the regulator will
  consider the second. The limitation is not missing evidence. It is the
  adopted evaluative framework."

  Short, Hill and Brown measure one case (THEORY-tmp1kuko): the public
  weighs layoffs, quality and lobbying far above the price effects that
  antitrust's consumer-welfare standard counts, and lawyers diverge from
  the public on replacing it far more than partisans do. Their note 4
  states the split in their own words: ideologues act "not by leveraging an
  informational asymmetry per se, but by developing ideological
  perspectives that redefine the 'public interest'" (NOTE-tmpxvj22;
  THEORY-tmpw39mh, Proposed, is the general form).

A26: "**Informational independence:** An institution retains access to
evidence not wholly determined by the interests it evaluates.
**Evaluative independence:** It retains meaningful authority to examine and
revise the criteria by which evidence is judged relevant to its purposes.
Neither guarantees the other." And: "An organization can have excellent
evidence and still omit a relevant value from its objective. It can have
appropriate stated objectives but systematically receive selected
evidence. It can have relevant evidence and objectives but lack a process
through which its evaluation rules can be challenged."

The record holds each half without the split. [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) is the
informational one: what keeps a loop from confirming itself is "evidence
whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it
tests". [CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md) is the evaluative one: adequacy "is fixed by the
evaluative target, and the target cannot be justified by the description
that preserves it." Between them sits the reading of evidence, [CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md)'s
κ′: how far the prevailing interpretation bends what the evidence is taken
to show, the place A22 §4 put legibility. "**An evaluator can be formally
independent yet epistemically dependent on categories, measurements, or
evidence channels governed by what it is evaluating.**"

**An evaluator in the loop.** The owner, at U21: "'interpretive advantage'
at the top of that feedback loop seems like it has implications for news
media capture as a political strategy". A21 §3 put the evaluator inside
the loop it is meant to check, with the loop running between institutions:
"Public interpretation → Political and regulatory decisions → Distribution
of resources and authority → Conditions of future media production →
Public interpretation." "If the evaluator becomes dependent on the
evaluated actor, an important corrective relationship may weaken." In
[CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md)'s terms the evaluated party's standing is a and the evaluator's
reading is m. Where the party affects what the evaluator reads (β) and the
evaluator's reading affects the party's standing (γ), the loop that
[CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md) shows can destabilize one organization runs through two. A20
§5, from [CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md): "**A formally independent review procedure does not
guarantee independent evaluation when the evidentiary standard itself is
controlled by the interests being evaluated.**"

**What breaks it.** A21's criterion: "whether the processes conferring
epistemic authority can themselves be corrected by evidence independent of
the authority they confer". That is [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s anchor, stated for public
knowledge. An outside evaluator can also correct the loop: "An independent
investigative report, for instance, may introduce evidence that changes
which public interpretations remain credible" (A21 §6). In [CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md)'s
terms that is the corrective regime, κ′ < κ̄ and γ < 0, which does more
than [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md)'s γ = 0, and which fails only by oscillation once
|βγ| ≥ 1 − αδ. A21 used only γ = 0.

Schram and Ule's experiment shows a correction removed by design rather
than by the loop (THEORY-tmpqzjvn). Their capture is assigned, the
policymaker's payoff equal to the operator's, and their independence is
the first policymaker fixing the price for all periods. With capture,
independence raised prices (P6 from 1.78 to 3.19): independence from
voters, installed by a captured designer, cut the only correction left.
That bears on the last clause, formal independence that does not make an
evaluation independent, by a route the claim does not name; it is payoff
alignment, not either of the two routes. THEORY-tmpvj1mm (Proposed) is the
authors' general conclusion.

## Where it comes from

The owner's ideas at U20 and U21, restated at A20 §§3–5 and §9, A21 §§3–6,
A22 §4, A23 §8 ("**An organization can retain formal authority over its
decisions while becoming increasingly dependent on other actors for the
categories, observations, and evaluative standards through which those
decisions are made.**"), A25 §8 ("the **evidentiary and evaluative regime
itself**") and A26 §4. A27 §6 applied the evaluative route to
consultation: an institution "can satisfy a nominal requirement to consult
affected agents while denying them effective participation in defining the
decision's relevant questions or criteria." [CLAIM-tmpyxupb](CLAIM-tmpyxupb.md) is why the
claim is neutral on the number of rules. A20 §6: "regulatory capture and
deregulation are not opposites", and "The defining feature is not the
number of rules."

It bears on [QUESTION-100](../questions.d/QUESTION-100.md) (an institutional form of its capture branch),
[QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md) (manipulation by an organized whole, "as ... a network of
state channels does") and [QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md) (manipulation with nothing in the
present structure showing it). [CASE-108](../cases.d/CASE-108.md)'s variant that "compromises the
processes that evaluate the others' competence" is the same design for
learning systems, proposed and not run. [CLAIM-359](CLAIM-359.md)'s audits "independent of
the audited" are the safeguard. Longino's uptake and public standards
([THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)) are the evaluative route's positive side: on that reading
criticism confers objectivity only where it has venues, uptake, public
standards and tempered equality of intellectual authority.

## What it does not say

- **That any institution is captured.** It names possible mechanisms. A20:
  "This is an application of the formal model, not an established
  empirical account of any particular regulatory agency." A21: "That is
  not a claim that any particular media organization or political movement
  has been captured." A26: "We can establish those distinctions now without
  making contested claims about any specific contemporary political actor
  or policy." The works the replies cited for real cases were read on
  2026-10-21, except Stigler (LIT-tmphei2g) and Entman (LIT-tmpq9j8p),
  which are Unavailable. Di Tella and Franceschelli give an association
  within newspaper and presidency, not a showing of covert intent; A21's
  effect size is the published abstract's, and the working paper read
  gives 0.31 front pages per standard deviation (THEORY-tmplatcg). Szeidl
  and Szucs give two-way favors at five events, with private advertisers
  as control (THEORY-tmpkd2bw). They ground the claim's first two parts
  as instances, not its general form.
- **That the readings support the loop between institutions.** Every
  economic work read is static or one-shot per episode (Besley and Prat's
  bargain, Di Tella and Franceschelli's association, Szeidl and Szucs's
  events, Schram and Ule's capture assigned by design rather than produced
by the evaluation), and
  Weatherall, O'Connor and Bruner's audience feeds nothing back to the
  scientists. In Hungary, when the 2015 fallout cut the reward, content
  favors stopped after a lag (THEORY-tmpkd2bw): dependence that did not
  sustain itself once the resource link was cut. The third part stays
  interpretive.
- **That the routes never produce each other.** The nearest thing in the
  record to the defeat condition is Szeidl and Szucs's endogenous-ownership
  counterfactual: targeted advertising raises an outlet's value to a more
  pro-government owner, so control of resources can, through a change of
  ownership, change the owner's ideal point and with it the criteria
  (THEORY-tmplydnj, Proposed; NOTE-tmpefpic). It does not defeat the
  claim: it runs from resources through ownership to criteria, not from
  evidence to criteria, and it is a model counterfactual.
- **That influence over evidence is wrongful.** [THEORY-404](../theory.d/THEORY-404.md)'s paper "does not
  analyse manipulation". A20: "Bayesian persuasion proves that information
  design can influence decisions; it does not establish that doing so is
  deceptive, illegitimate, or harmful." Nor that selection is capture. A21:
  "ordinary editorial selection is unavoidable". Nor that concentration is
  capture. A25 §8: "We should avoid using *capture* as a synonym for
  *concentration*." What capture would need is [TERM-tmpe6jtq](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe6jtq.md)'s standard,
  set apart from the process judged.
- **That the favoured interpretation is wrong.** A21: "A particular
  interpretation can prevail because it is better supported." Whether
  entrenched authority is capture or merited trust is [QUESTION-087](../questions.d/QUESTION-087.md), and
  [CLAIM-277](CLAIM-277.md) gives no criterion.
- **That the loop between institutions is a result.** [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md) and
  [CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md) are linearizations of one organization's model. A20 called
  [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md) "the corresponding symmetry-breaking theorem" for regulators;
  it is a local instability condition for [CASE-092](../cases.d/CASE-092.md)'s unbuilt model, and the
  two-institution version has not been derived. The claim is interpretive.
- **That an evaluator's dependence on others is a failure of its autonomy.**
  A21 §6: "Such external dependence is not necessarily a failure of
  autonomy. It may be one of the organizational conditions that makes
  meaningful accountability possible."
