# CLAIM-145: Trope analysis of the kind A155 proposes is Propp's and Lévi-Strauss's transformational analysis, whom the manuscript should cite as precursors

## The claim

Two halves.

**Prior art.** Propp found that in the wondertales he analysed "the constant
elements are 31 functions of characters", and that the tales "differ in who
performs the functions and how" ([THEORY-167](../theory.d/THEORY-167.md), from [LIT-828](../literature.d/LIT-828.md); [LIT-830](../literature.d/LIT-830.md) is the
English translation). That is A155's "recasting": the same relational
pattern with different characters or setting. Propp also names inversions
and swaps. [CLAIM-019](CLAIM-019.md) holds Lévi-Strauss's transformational analysis, "as it
has been modelled computationally" ([LIT-804](../literature.d/LIT-804.md), [LIT-807](../literature.d/LIT-807.md)), as direct prior
art for structural transport. A155's table of transformations (recasting,
inversion, subversion, deconstruction, parody) is its descendant. A155
names neither. No reply from U44 on cites Propp, and none connects
Lévi-Strauss to the tropes, though A102 had named Propp's *Morphology* among
the sources and A178 §3.1 keeps Lévi-Strauss as motivation for the
relational account.

**Circularity.** [THEORY-167](../theory.d/THEORY-167.md)'s `promote_when` carries the warning that
applies here: further analyses of single tales that fit the scheme cannot
settle it, since the scheme was built to fit such tales. TV Tropes is a
community version of the same situation. Editors write a trope's
description from its examples, and then add examples that fit the
description. A measure that predicts those labels predicts editorial
agreement. So agreement with the catalogue cannot by itself validate
[CLAIM-147](CLAIM-147.md)'s structural account. This is [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s point carried to
labels: a criterion fitted on the data it is checked against certifies
nothing.

## Partly answered

The exchange saw the circularity half. A155 §5 asks for "a small,
independently validated set of tropes" and evaluation "on held-out trope
families". A178's Study B: "Control for circularity by selecting trope
definitions and structural anchors independently of the models being
evaluated", and its Ch12.9 is "Controlling circularity and label leakage".
That is [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s remedy, and it meets the second half if it is carried
out. A218's Study 5 drops the control. The prior-art half is not answered
anywhere in the exchange.

## What it does not say

- It does not say TV Tropes is useless as data. It gives candidates for
  testing, not validation.
- It does not say a structural account of tropes is false.
- It says the manuscript would have to cite Propp and Lévi-Strauss as
  precursors, and that labels written to fit their examples cannot be the
  test. It is [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) applied to one corpus.

## Note of 2026-10-28: split under [ADR-061](../decisions.d/ADR-061.md)

The claim had "Two halves". The circularity half is a verdict on support:
a measure that predicts trope labels predicts editorial agreement, so the
labels cannot validate [CLAIM-147](CLAIM-147.md). It is now [ARG-014](../arguments.d/ARG-014.md), from [CASE-038](../cases.d/CASE-038.md) to
[CLAIM-147](CLAIM-147.md), Rejected, with [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s point and A178's control in its
body. This claim keeps the prior-art half, which says [CLAIM-147](CLAIM-147.md)'s account
is not new, not that it is false. So it no longer objects to [CLAIM-147](CLAIM-147.md),
and it does not undercut the argument.
