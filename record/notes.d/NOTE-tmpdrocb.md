# NOTE-tmpdrocb: Jia and others 2026 — Neuron–tumor crosstalk in cancer: molecular mechanisms and translational advances

## Contribution

An organizing frame, "tumor neuron hijack", for the nervous system's part in cancer, with a three-way crosstalk of nerves, immune cells and tumour cells at its centre, and a map of therapeutic and translational directions.

## Key results

- The frame: "We define this pathological rewiring and neural circuit subversion as tumor neuron hijack, a sophisticated malignant strategy analogous to immune escape."
- Tumours are active recruiters: "Tumors do not passively receive neural innervation; instead, they actively construct a neural microenvironment that promotes tumor growth."
- Two routes: brain tumours embed in pre-existing networks through AMPA-receptor synapses and metabolic coupling, while "peripheral solid tumors lack an intrinsic neuronal network and must recruit nerves de novo from the surrounding peripheral nervous system (PNS)."
- Sympathetic signalling is presented as the main organizer of immune evasion, through a neural–MDSC–T cell suppression axis; in prostate cancer models β-blockers or ADRB2 antagonists reduce MDSC accumulation and improve checkpoint blockade (preclinical, cited).
- Translation is early: the authors list the lack of non-invasive biomarkers of tumour innervation, β-blocker dosing that diverges from cardiovascular use, and the scarcity of fresh tissue with intact nerves; propranolol with pembrolizumab has entered clinical testing (NCT03384836).

## Assumptions

- That mechanisms shown in mouse models and co-culture carry over to patients; the review says most insights are preclinical.
- "Hijack" and "strategy" are the authors' metaphors for selected cell behaviour; the review does not argue for agency.

## Concepts

- **Tumor neuron hijack**: the authors' term for tumours recruiting, engaging and reprogramming nerves to their own benefit, by analogy with immune escape.
- **Perineural invasion**: cancer cells migrating along nerve sheaths.
