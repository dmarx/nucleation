# Part C. The model, diagnosis and lock-in

## 9. A model of matched performance and divergent readiness

Sections 6 and 8 said that two organizations can look the same in ordinary operation and differ in what they can still become. This section makes that claim concrete in a small model and runs it. The motivating draft (ChatGPT's article) set out three regimes and stated their outcomes in a table, as "analytical outcomes under stipulated assumptions". Here the table is replaced by an executed study, in `analysis/` beside this draft. It has a model, a sweep, 21 tests with a negative control, and the outputs of a run made on 2026-11-23. Every number below comes from `analysis/results.md` or `analysis/results.json` from that run. `analysis/README.md` records the run (2026-11-23, with the code as committed in 28a3708c1); the two output files themselves do not yet carry the date or the commit, and are to be stamped with it. The tests were rerun on 2026-11-23 and pass.

**What the model is.** It is an executed illustration of claims the record already holds as demonstrated, chiefly CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-tmpg8yl2. It does not test them. Two kinds of result in it are not evidence for anything. The matched present output holds by construction (§9.3). And the checks that CLAIM-577's and CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's conditions hold in the model are checks on the model, which add no warrant to claims that are already demonstrated (§9.4). What is new in it is exactly the outputs that depend on the modeller's choices, and §9.9 names the choices each depends on: III-a doing worse than I (§9.5), the break-even pL* and how it moves with the deadline and with p (§9.6), the step function (§9.7), and the generality of a reserve against a redesign (§9.4 and §9.8; join JC5, now CLAIM-tmpv0azx).

Three limits come first. The model is the drafting assistant's (Claude's), built on the record's entries and on an earlier simulator. All of its rates and costs are invented. And it is a model, so what it shows is what follows from its assumptions, not what happens in any industry (§9.10).

### 9.1 Where the model comes from

**The mechanism it runs is held.** The motivating draft's first regime is the record's CLAIM-577 (Active, demonstrated), which the record labels as the assistant's own proposition: "A budget set each period to the peak resource spent the period before never rises, so the configurations an organization can reach shrink monotonically, and any transition whose threshold exceeds the cost of holding the current configuration is lost after one period spent holding it, unless the budget is raised from outside by at least the difference". The threshold of a transition is CLAIM-571's (Active, demonstrated): "the least, over admissible transition paths, of the peak requirement along the path, and it is infinite when no admissible path joins the two". The claim's own scope matters for everything below: it holds "For a fixed architecture and one resource that must be held at each moment".

**Earlier simulators ran parts of it.** LEAD-080 (Open) asked for a readiness simulator to be run under the peak rule and under carry-over. CASE-315 (Proposed) is that run. It modelled a heavy rescue squad, holding at 0.7 and 1.3 with the squad out, over 300 runs of ten years. Its title gives the result: the squad "is lost for good in every run within months where it is sent only on confirmation, in a fifth of runs within nine years where it is sent provisionally to each of six reports a month, and never where it drills each month or the unspent budget rolls over". Its caveat: "The model is the assistant's and its rates are invented." From that run the record took CLAIM-878 (Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's): under the peak rule "a capability stays funded exactly as long as every period contains a mobilization at its cost, whether a use, a drill, a false alarm or a cancelled dispatch". A second loop of the same family has also been run. QUESTION-386 (Open; A's proposal, in the exchange on emergency readiness) asks "Under what funding and incentive rules does allocating resources according to observed activity cause an organization to lose capabilities whose value is concentrated in rarely encountered circumstances?", and LEAD-036 (Open) asked for that loop to be modelled "against a control that decouples maintenance funding from observed activity". CASE-343 (Proposed, the assistant's model) ran it with competence that decays and is rebuilt by funded maintenance. Its title: "a budget that follows observed activity loses a capability used once in two and a half years or more rarely in every run or nearly, one used four times a year in three or four runs in ten, and none when maintenance is funded apart from activity". The present model has no competence and no decay. It loses its transitions by the budget rule alone, so it isolates CLAIM-577's mechanism from CASE-343's.

CASE-315 and CASE-343 are Active executed studies, promoted at the owner's word of 2026-11-24 ("promote the ten"; ADR-057) after a session other than the filer's reran each from a copy and reproduced it. In the study, at the same expected funding, "the coupled rule lost the capability within thirty years in every run at one occasion in two and a half years and in 39 % at four a year, and the decoupled rule never did" (CLAIM-921, summary). The two mechanisms are distinct, and this paper keeps them so. The motivating draft of the series that takes this paper as its Article 2 says it in one line: one "concerns loss of affordable transition pathways; the other concerns loss of the underlying capability" (A51 §3, ChatGPT's, of CASE-412 and CASE-343). CLAIM-577 loses a route and leaves the capability to hold untouched; CLAIM-921 loses the competence itself. A capability lost the second way is regained at its construction cost, not its upkeep. That is CLAIM-569's capability "cheap to keep and costly to acquire" (§11.1), and the claim says so of itself: "That it is costly to rebuild is one reason to keep it; whether the reason is good depends on what it is for, the question of telling a warranted reserve from waste" (CLAIM-569, "What it does not say", pointing to QUESTION-395, §9.6). The Part I draft's join J3.8, which said this, is a restatement of CLAIM-569 (SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr, Part I joins section). The account of the readiness line declines a broader form, "The three requirements of a form, each lost by its own mechanism", as "a conjunction" (SYNTHESIS-031, Proposed), and this paper does not state it. A reading gives the same shape for knowledge: because absorptive capacity accumulates, "a firm that stops investing in a fast-moving field may be locked out of it even after major advances" (THEORY-1518, Active; Cohen and Levinthal, who "test only the static part"; §11.3).

**The record already had an instance of redesign.** The motivating draft's third regime, a cheaper staging path, has a precedent the draft does not cite. CASE-198 (Proposed) is "An architecture-mediated transition: moving between two configurations by first switching to a staging architecture under which the path is cheaper, moving, then switching back, lowers the peak resource need from 1.3 to 1.0". In its numbers the reconfiguration slack falls "from 0.6 to 0.3", not to zero, so the staged route still needs a reserve. CLAIM-922 (Proposed, demonstrated) generalizes this: "a deadline shorter than every route through the staging architecture takes that remedy away". The present study shows the same thing in its own numbers (§9.5).

**The disputed step.** ChatGPT's draft set reserve and redesign side by side as "different means". The record files that as ChatGPT's CLAIM-tmpf71gk (Proposed, interpretive): "Maintaining a reserve and redesigning a transition are distinct routes to readiness: one keeps enough capacity to cross an existing transition barrier, the other lowers the barrier itself". The assistant objected in CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 (Proposed, demonstrated, counter): "Under a budget set each period to the last period's peak use, a redesigned transition is lost like the original unless its threshold is at most the cost of holding the present configuration, so redesign lowers the reserve a transition needs by the fall in its threshold and replaces the reserve only where it leaves no reconfiguration slack". The draft's table gives the redesigned regime an "accessible transition capacity" of 2. CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 points out that this capacity "is a budget held at 2: a reserve of 2 − r(A) = 1", one unit above the holding cost that the text never states. It concludes: "Otherwise reserve and redesign are complements, not alternatives." CLAIM-tmpf71gk's own Standing section concedes the point: "the two are distinct in what they change but not alternatives". Both entries are Proposed, and choosing between them is the owner's call.

**The lead this study answers.** The draft's §9.6 proposed a computational study and gave no design for it. The study reuses CASE-315's budget accounting, its drill, its partial-rollover and CASE-200 rules, and its way of seeding runs, without changing that study's code. Putting CLAIM-571's paths, CLAIM-577's rule, CASE-200's carry-over, CLAIM-878's drill and CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's reserve into one model is this study's own work. The record keeps them as separate entries (join JC1: one model from five entries). ### 9.2 The model

**Configurations and paths.**

- Two production configurations, A and B, each cost r = 1 a period to hold.
- The original path runs A → M → B over two edges, with r(M) = 3, as in the draft.
- The redesigned architecture adds a staged path A → S1 → S2 → B over three edges, with r(S) = θ′. The reference value is θ′ = 2.
- Each edge is completed with chance q = 0.5 a period. While it is being crossed it occupies the larger r of its two ends.
- A path's threshold is its peak, and an architecture's threshold is the least over its paths (CLAIM-571).
- The graph is symmetric, so the same paths lead back.

The record's worked instance of a threshold above both ends is CASE-233 (Active): "on configurations [0, 1] with a holding requirement r(q) = 0.7 + 2.4q(1 − q), both ends cost 0.7, every path between them passes 1.3 at q = 1/2". ARG-077 (Active) draws the lesson: "the threshold for reaching a configuration need not equal its requirement". The model's A → M → B is the same shape in round numbers. What a transition needs beyond holding has a record name. TERM-106 (Active) defines reconfiguration slack as "the temporary resources a system needs to move between two configurations it can each hold, beyond what holding either one requires, while keeping specified commitments". In the model the original route's slack is 3 − 1 = 2 and the staged route's is θ′ − 1. A reserve below is resource held to meet that slack.

Choosing a slower staged route, three edges to the original's two, is a stated assumption that the study does not vary (§9.9).

**Budget rules.** B is the period's capacity ceiling, its budget, and s is the period's peak use. A regime's reserve is its budget less the holding cost of 1.

- *Peak*: B′ = s (CLAIM-577).
- *Floor*: B′ = max(s, F), a budget of at least F, that is a held reserve of F − 1. In the control arms the commitment can lapse, with a hazard per period, and the rule then becomes the peak rule.
- *Carry*: B′ = min(3, B̄ + ρ(B − s)) with B̄ = 1.5 and ρ = 1. This is CASE-200's rule (Proposed), with a cap added. CASE-200's title states that rule's effect: "rolling over the 0.3 left unspent reaches it, while a rule that cuts or expires unspent funds forecloses it".
- *Drill*: under the peak rule, when nothing else is under way, the staging configuration is occupied at θ′ for part of the period, so that period's peak use is θ′. This is CLAIM-878's mobilization, done on purpose, and the "third mechanism" that CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 names under "What it does not say": "a drill each period at the transition's peak, keeps the route funded by the rule itself".

**Contingency, outside money, deadline.**

- Each period, with chance p, demand switches to the other configuration.
- The organization takes the fastest path it can afford.
- If no path is affordable, outside money arrives after a geometric wait, with chance g = 0.1 a period. It raises the budget to the least threshold, which is the minimum CLAIM-577's step 4 says is needed ("Only outside money restores it"). Who supplies such money is QUESTION-373's subject (Open, the assistant's): "Who supplies the resource that carries a system across a transition's bottleneck, the environment, a designer, or the system itself from reserves it accumulates while operating". The model makes it exogenous and does not answer the question.
- A transition, once started, is funded to the end.
- A response later than the deadline τ costs L.
- A reserve costs c per unit of budget above holding, per period. The upfront cost of a redesign is not charged; it is reported as a break-even.
- Output is 1 in any period with no contingency pending, and 0 otherwise.

**Readiness.** Readiness is TERM-115's (Active), "the probability that an organization operating under a specified policy can deliver an effective response to a specified contingency within its deadline". It is measured under an imposed contingency, do(c), after 50 quiet periods. TERM-115 says why: "The do(c) is the point: this is readiness for a specified situation, imposed, not success conditional on the situations that happened to be observed." As in §6, R(t) is the chance of having reached B within t periods.

**Regimes and controls.**

| Regime | Path | Budget rule | Budget (reserve above holding) | What it stands for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | original | peak | falls to 1 (0) | the draft's utilization-matched regime (CLAIM-577) |
| II | original | floor at 3 | 3 (2) | the draft's reserve regime |
| III-a | redesigned | peak | falls to 1 (0) | the draft's redesign, read as having no reserve |
| III-b | redesigned | floor at θ′ | θ′ = 2 (1) | the draft's redesign with the reserve CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 says it needs |
| IV | redesigned | peak, with a drill each period at θ′ | θ′ = 2 (1), held by the drill | CLAIM-878's mobilization as policy |
| V | original | carry | up to 3 (up to 2) | CASE-200's carry-over |

The controls are II and III-b with a reserve that lapses (hazard 0.005 a period), and IV drilling only every second period. The last is the negative control.

**Expectations, with what would falsify each.** The expectations are fixed before the results, and are taken from the motivating draft's stipulations (its §9 and table) and from the conditions of the entries the model implements. Each is graded in the section named.

- **E1. Present output is the same in every regime** (the draft's table: "Present output | Same | Same | Same"). *Falsified if* any regime's output before the first contingency differs from another's. This holds by construction in the model, so its grade counts as no evidence (§9.3).
- **E2. Regime I loses the route to B after one period spent holding** (the draft's §9.1: "the alternative configuration B is no longer reachable because the intermediate configuration requires 3"; CLAIM-577). *Falsified if*, under the peak rule and with no outside money, a route whose threshold exceeds the holding cost stays affordable after one holding period. Graded in §9.4. The draft's table calls the route "Unaffordable"; CLAIM-577's "unless" says outside money can restore it, and the model includes such money.
- **E3. Regime II keeps the route affordable, and its reserve "has an opportunity cost. Maintaining it is not automatically optimal"** (the draft's §9.2). *Falsified if* II's readiness is no higher than I's at some deadline, or if II is the cheapest regime at every deadline. Graded in §§9.5 and 9.6.
- **E4. The redesigned regime reaches B with an "Accessible transition capacity" of 2 and only an "Upfront redesign cost"** (the draft's table). Under the peak rule CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 predicts the opposite. *Falsified if* the staged route, with threshold θ′ > 1, is lost after one holding period under the peak rule unless a reserve of θ′ − 1 is held. Graded in §9.4.
- **E5. Reserve and redesign are alternative means to readiness** (the draft's "different means"; CLAIM-tmpf71gk). *Falsified if* redesign without a reserve gives no readiness that the original route without a reserve lacks, so that redesign needs the reserve to help at all. Graded in §§9.4 and 9.5.
- **E6. "we should not simply maximize reserve capacity"** (the draft's §9.4). *Falsified if* readiness rises with every increment of reserve and the largest reserve is the cheapest regime at every deadline. Graded in §§9.5 and 9.7.
- **E7. A rigid institution "might continue satisfying every demand it recognizes while failing to revise its categories"** (the draft's §9.6), and CLAIM-361's separation is needed to see it. *Falsified if* an organization that never admits a new demand shows violations against the original specification or against its own. Graded in §9.8. The scorecards make this hold by construction too: a demand never entered cannot be violated on a list that omits it.
- **E8. Under peak accounting, a drill each period holds the budget at its cost** (CLAIM-878's "A drill each period holds the budget"). *Falsified if* IV's budget falls below θ′ while it drills every period. Graded in §9.4.
- **E9, the negative control. Drilling only every second period loses the staged route after the first undrilled period** (CLAIM-878, step 1). *Falsified if* IV drilling every second period keeps the route without outside money. Graded in §9.4.

The study reports more than these expectations grade. Results marked † below were not stated as expectations before the run; they were found after the fact.

**The run.** Long runs use 8 seeds of 4,000 periods each. Imposed contingencies use 2,000 replications per regime. All regimes run on one seed see the same contingencies and, where their paths agree, the same step times.

### 9.3 Present output is matched

Before the first contingency, every regime produces 1.0000 per period, and with no contingency at all every regime produces 1 in every period (tested). This equivalence is built in, not found: in the model, output does not depend on the budget except through how quickly a contingency is met. So E1 holds by construction, and its holding counts as no evidence. That is exactly the situation the draft's argument needs. Ordinary output cannot tell the regimes apart. CLAIM-577's "What it adds" says the same: "The loss leaves no trace in output." The record already has a constructed case of the kind for a single capability: CASE-210 (Proposed; A's proposal, in the exchange on emergency readiness), "Two departments perform no extrications during ordinary operation, but only one can satisfy a deadline-constrained extrication demand under intervention".

Once contingencies arrive, the regimes come apart. That divergence also follows from CLAIM-577, which the model implements; the model shows its size under the model's numbers, not that it occurs. At the reference values (p = 0.02, θ′ = 2):

| Regime | output overall | capacity above holding | mean response (periods) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 0.7868 | 0.171 | 12.76 |
| II | 0.9183 | 2.000 | 4.13 |
| III-a | 0.7578 | 0.117 | 14.83 |
| III-b | 0.8820 | 1.000 | 6.11 |
| IV | 0.8820 | 1.000 | 6.11 |
| V | 0.9158 | 1.941 | 4.25 |
| II with lapse | 0.7924 | 0.241 | 12.47 |
| III-b with lapse | 0.7668 | 0.149 | 14.53 |
| IV, drill every second period | 0.7623 | 0.124 | 14.72 |

This is the draft's central sentence, "Current-performance equivalence does not imply adaptive-capacity equivalence", run as a model. The record already holds it in general form. CLAIM-079 (Active, demonstrated; from the owner's essay): "Stability does not entail viability: a system can occupy a stable regime while losing access to trajectories that keep satisfying its constraints". CLAIM-414 (Active, demonstrated) shows that matched ordinary trajectories can conceal differences that "a specified intervention exposes".

### 9.4 The exact conditions hold, and what the study adds to the dispute

**CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 hold exactly in the model.** The tests check four things:

- under the peak rule the budget never rises;
- one holding period forecloses every route whose threshold exceeds 1;
- a redesigned route is kept under the peak rule exactly when θ′ ≤ 1, tested for θ′ ∈ {1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3};
- a floor F keeps a route exactly when F is at least its threshold, tested for F from 1 to 3 in steps of 0.25.

These tests confirm that the model implements the two entries' conditions, and that their consequences follow there. They are a check on the model. They add no warrant to a claim that is already demonstrated. On them, E2 holds, with the qualification that outside money restores I's route after a wait (R_I(20) = 0.845), as CLAIM-577's "unless" allows. E4 is falsified as the draft worded it: the redesigned route is not kept by redesign alone.

In the draft's own numbers, its redesigned regime, whose table row gives an "Accessible transition capacity" of 2, is III-b, which holds a budget of 2, a reserve of 1. Without that reserve the regime is III-a. Its route is lost after one holding period, as the original route is under I, and readiness falls to R(6) = 0.147 against III-b's 0.647. On this point the run sides with CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 against CLAIM-tmpf71gk as worded: within the model, redesign and reserve work together; neither substitutes for the other. E5 is falsified in the model. One reading of CLAIM-tmpf71gk survives, the one its Standing section already concedes: the two change different things, the barrier and the capacity to cross it. The study takes no side on that reading.

**The drill is a reserve by another name, under peak accounting.** Regime IV holds no reserve by commitment, yet its budget sits at θ′, because every period's drill sets that period's peak use. E8 holds. Its capacity cost equals III-b's (1.000 against 1.000), and its readiness curve is identical to III-b's at every deadline (the dominance test reports them "equal"). This follows from CLAIM-878: what the peak rule funds is whatever was mobilized, whatever the purpose. CLAIM-878's own words: "A drill each period holds the budget". So the "third mechanism" of CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's last clause is not a third route to readiness in cost or in effect. It is a way of holding the same reserve. That correction is already on the record: CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's) says that "The 'third mechanism' of 'What it does not say', a drill each period at the transition's peak, is CLAIM-878's, already held". This paragraph restates CLAIM-878 (join JC2, a restatement of CLAIM-878 and of the note of 2026-11-23 on CLAIM-tmpg8yl2; SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00 says so: "JC2 is stated by CLAIM-878 and the note on CLAIM-tmpg8yl2").

The drill and the reserve differ in what can break them. This too is on the record, in CLAIM-878's note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's, filed for this manuscript, on a claim that is Proposed), which records the equal cost in this model and "two differences".

- *A reserve lapses when the commitment ends.* In the note's words, "The drill needs no commitment from whoever sets the budget, since the peak rule funds it, so a lapsing reserve leaves it intact". Under a lapse hazard of 0.005 a period, II's R(6) falls from 0.894 to 0.752 and III-b's from 0.647 to 0.535.
- *A drill fails when a period is skipped.* Drilling every second period loses the route after the first undrilled period: R(6) = 0.147, the same as III-a. This is the negative control (E9), and it fails as predicted. The novel-demand arm (§9.8) shows a second way the drill breaks: a review that crowds out the drill takes the route with it.

So a reserve depends on a commitment, and a drill depends on a schedule being kept. CLAIM-878 had said the same of its squad: "Monthly drills kept it in every run under either policy". The drill in this model only holds the budget. The record gives a drill a second role that the model leaves out: CLAIM-558 (Active, interpretive) holds that "a capability kept for rare events is maintained by practice outside operation, not by operation", because a procedural capability "decays with the time since its last use, and practice in the interval slows the decay". The model has no competence to decay, so it counts only the drill's effect on the budget.

**A reserve is general within its budget; a redesign is specific to its transition.†** In the draft's numbers redesign halves the reserve, from 2 to 1. CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 says so: "Not that redesign is worthless: it halves the reserve in A48's numbers." But the lower threshold belongs to one path, between A and B. II's budget of 3, a reserve of 2 above holding, is capacity: it can be spent on any transition whose threshold is at most 3. III-b's budget of θ′ = 2, a reserve of θ′ − 1 = 1, covers only routes whose threshold is at most θ′. When a demand arrives that the specification did not name (§9.8), II can meet it from its reserve, and III-b and IV cannot (join JC5: a redesign is specific to one transition and a reserve is not, from CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 and the novel-demand arm). The join passed the record's admission test by derivation, with a discriminating prediction, and is now filed as a claim in the assistant's voice, for one capacity usable anywhere: "a reserve held at budget B keeps every transition whose threshold is at most B, while a redesign lowers only the thresholds whose least-peak path can use the redesigned route", so a redesign that lets the reserve fall "loses every other transition whose threshold lies above θ′ and whose least-peak path does not use the redesign" (CLAIM-tmpv0azx, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's; integrated by SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00, Proposed). The join holds only in a stated range. It rests on the novel route's threshold, r(N) = 2.5, which the modeller chose between III-b's budget of 2 and II's of 3. At r(N) ≤ 2, III-b's and IV's budgets would cover the novel route too, and the two regimes would not differ on it. At r(N) > 3, neither budget would. So the finding is that a reserve serves any transition whose threshold is at most its budget, not that a reserve serves any transition. The account lists the general form under "Not stated on the record": "A reserve serves any transition" is "false where reserves have a location (CLAIM-579) or there are several resources (CLAIM-585)", and CLAIM-tmpv0azx "states only the one-resource form" (SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00). It also rests on the model's single resource, in which capacity held for one transition is fungible across all of them. CLAIM-579 (Active, interpretive) says what a reserve is where that fails: "A reserve is described by where in a configuration space it can be applied, not only by how much there is: an organization can hold ample slack and still be pinned, when none of it can be brought to bear at the bottleneck of the transition it needs" (§9.9).

Section 6 used a note the assistant added to TERM-115 on 2026-11-23, which the record does not file as a claim. It says two organizations "are ranked the same way at every deadline exactly when one curve lies at or above the other at every time (first-order dominance); where the curves cross, which is more ready is fixed only by the deadline". The study classifies every pair of regimes this way, for t from 0 to 40 with a tolerance of 0.02:

- II and V are equal, and they dominate every other regime. By readiness, II (with V) ranks first at every deadline from 2 to 20. Which regime is most ready does not depend on the deadline.
- II dominates III-b and IV, which are equal to each other.
- III-a is dominated by every other regime, including I.†
- I and III-b cross between t = 2 and t = 3.† At t = 2, I with outside money (0.025) is ahead of III-b (0), because the staged route cannot finish in fewer than three periods. From t = 3 on, III-b is ahead. This pair, and I against IV, are the only pairs whose order depends on the deadline.

So E3's first half holds: II's readiness is above I's at every deadline. The crossing is CLAIM-922's deadline effect (Proposed, demonstrated) seen in this model: the budget the staged route saves is paid for in time.

The worst result for redesign is III-a, below even I.† On these assumptions, redesign without a reserve is worse than no redesign at all, which goes further against E5 than §9.4 did. The reason is that the minimum outside money of CLAIM-577's step 4 buys the cheapest route, and with redesign the cheapest route is the slower staged one. This is on the record as CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's): "So 'Not that redesign is worthless' holds with a reserve; without one, redesign can do worse than none". The note says what it rests on: "two of the modeller's choices that the record does not hold: outside money buys the cheapest route, not the fastest, and the staged route is longer. If it bought the fastest, III-a would match I." With each edge crossed with chance 0.5 a period, the note computes, three edges take 6 periods on average against the original's 4, "a difference of 2 against the 2.07 observed".

**Ranking by long-run cost.†** Cost is c × reserve + L × late rate, at p = 0.02, L = 50 and c = 0.1. The readiness ranking does not change between t = 3 and t = 12, but the cost ranking changes with the deadline:

| τ | cheapest | order |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | I | I < III-a < II < V < III-b < IV |
| 3 | II | II < V < I < III-a < III-b < IV |
| 4 | II | II < V < I < III-b < IV < III-a |
| 6, 8 | II | II < V < III-b < IV < I < III-a |
| 12 | III-b, IV | III-b < IV < V < II < I < III-a |
| 20 | III-b, IV | III-b < IV < I < V < III-a < II |

At τ = 2 the reserve is cheapest nowhere, though II is in time far more often than I under an imposed contingency (R_II(2) = 0.237 against R_I(2) = 0.025). In the long runs most contingencies are late at that deadline in every regime, and the lateness the reserve saves is worth less than the reserve costs: II's cost is 0.975 a period against I's 0.820. So the cheapest regime at τ = 2 is the one that holds nothing, because a reserve's cost exceeds the lateness it saves there, not because no regime is in time. At τ = 12 and τ = 20, III-b and IV cost 0.081 and 0.100 a period less than II, before any redesign cost. Those numbers are the largest per-period amortized redesign cost at which redesign pays. II is not the cheapest regime at every deadline, so E3's second half holds, and with it E6's: the reserve is not "automatically optimal". So the draft's open question, whether reserve or redesign is better, has no answer independent of the deadline when it is asked of cost; asked of readiness alone, the answer in this model is the reserve, at every deadline. This fits the record's no-optimum objection, CLAIM-tmphjm0q (Proposed, interpretive, counter; §14): "There is no optimal regulation until the demand specification, the instruments, the horizon and the trade-offs among demands are declared".

### 9.6 When a reserve pays, and what that does not settle

The draft warns that "we should not simply maximize reserve capacity" and gives no criterion. QUESTION-395 (Open) asks: "Beyond what records kept of each capacity bound, what observations, interventions, or models can distinguish a warranted reserve of specialized capability from wasteful redundancy when ordinary operation seldom exercises it, and how is that evidence weighed against the capability's cost?" The proposition its "wasteful redundancy" refers to is filed bare as CLAIM-584 (Proposed, disclaimed): "Maintaining a specialized capability that is seldom or never exercised for its specialized function is wasteful". The record neither holds nor rejects it. A dated note on QUESTION-395 (2026-11-23) names the parameter the draft leaves free: "The missing parameter is the contingency's probability times its loss against the reserve's cost per period". The study computes the break-even.

A reserve (II against I) pays when the contingency's probability times its loss exceeds a threshold:

  p·L > pL* ≈ c·Δcap / ΔR(τ),

where Δcap ≈ 1.83 is the extra capacity the reserve holds and ΔR(τ) is the gain in readiness at the deadline. In the study, pL* is the value at which c times the extra capacity equals L times the reduction in late contingencies per period. Its form is the one QUESTION-395's note names, so this is a restatement of that note (join JC3, a restatement of QUESTION-395's notes of 2026-11-23; SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00: "JC3 is stated by QUESTION-395's notes, with its identification half open"). The record has a threshold of the same kind elsewhere: CLAIM-561 (Active, demonstrated) finds that provisional dispatch pays when p > (c0 + g)/(H + c0 − c1), a probability against a ratio of costs to losses, and its title draws the moral for cancellations: "a cancellation rate alone therefore cannot identify waste". CLAIM-559 (Active, demonstrated) gives its sequential form: "provisional activation and subsequent cancellation of a capability can both be optimal under one unchanged objective". The values below are on the record as QUESTION-395's second note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's; "Partial, the weighing half only").

| p | τ = 3 | τ = 6 | τ = 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.005 | 0.579 | 0.363 | 0.625 |
| 0.01 | 0.630 | 0.394 | 0.571 |
| 0.02 | 0.701 | 0.401 | 0.565 |
| 0.05 | 1.15 | 0.502 | 0.689 |

The table is at c = 0.1. pL* is linear in c: at τ = 6 and p = 0.02 it is 0.200 at c = 0.05 and 0.802 at c = 0.2. The set of p·L at which the reserve is preferred is closed upward (tested). Three features matter, and all three were found after the fact.†

- **pL* is not a function of p·L and c alone.** It is U-shaped in the deadline. It is lowest where the deadline falls between the reserve's transit time and the wait for outside money. There the reserve makes the most difference. At a short deadline even the reserve's own route is seldom finished in time (R_II(3) = 0.492), so a reserve buys little lateness; at a long one outside money arrives anyway.
- **It also moves with p, through the rule itself.** Under the peak rule, a transition just made leaves the budget at the transition's peak for one period. So frequent contingencies keep I readier in operation than it is under do(c). This is CLAIM-878's mechanism: a use is a mobilization. As a result, pL* rises with p at τ = 3 and τ = 6. At τ = 12 it does not move monotonically (0.625, 0.571, 0.565, 0.689).
- **The formula is an approximation.** The formula with imposed readiness gives 0.27 to 0.31 at τ = 6, where the long runs give 0.36 to 0.50, and 0.41 to 0.48 at τ = 3, where the long runs give 0.58 to 1.15. The difference is the gap between readiness under do(c) and readiness observed in operation.

**What this does and does not do for QUESTION-395.** It gives a decision rule *if readiness is known*. To use it, a decision-maker needs p, L, c, the deadline, and the paths' thresholds and transit times. Within the model it says when CLAIM-584's proposition holds of the reserve (p·L below pL*) and when it fails. It does not answer the question's identification problem, which is which observations tell a warranted reserve from waste. QUESTION-395's second note says so: "So the weighing against cost has a model answer, and the identification half of this question, which observations tell a warranted reserve from waste, stays open." The study sharpens that problem instead. Under the peak rule, readiness observed in operation and readiness under an imposed contingency differ, and the difference depends on how often the capability is used. CLAIM-517 (Active, demonstrated) holds the general point: "fidelity measured in ordinary operation certifies nothing about viability". CLAIM-632 (Active, demonstrated) holds the bound for records kept per capacity: they "bound a reserve's readiness for a contingency between the sum of their probabilities less one less their number and the weakest of them, and identify it only when the capacities' dependence is known", leaving the band between "to joint evidence such as a deadline-bound exercise". The model has one capacity, so CLAIM-632's band does not arise in it. QUESTION-395 stays Open.

**Two neighbours of the question** (added 2026-11-25 from the Part I draft of governance-of-becoming, old §§3.5 and 3.7.4; standings as of 2026-11-25). The record's term for what the reserve is holds the question open by definition: organizational slack is "resources an organization holds beyond what its current output requires, some useful and some wasteful, which the output alone does not tell apart" (TERM-107, Proposed; filed at the owner's request on 2026-11-04, after Bogetoft and Hougaard). In this model the reserve is slack in that sense, and §9.3's matched output is why output cannot tell. And a reserve can be worth keeping for what it does unused: "A fallback capacity can matter without being used: it keeps a party able to refuse, and so changes the terms others offer" (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's, A24 §7 of the exchange "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity", its bargaining step the assistant's), which "is why efficiency measured on use will tend to remove it" (ibid.). The Part I draft read that removal as CLAIM-921's use-coupled loss (its join J3.7), and the join failed: it adds nothing to CLAIM-tmp2jhc1's own sentence, a reserve never exercised is CLAIM-921's limiting case with no occasions, and CLAIM-921 excludes models in which use outside commitment counts as activity (SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr, Part I joins section). This paper does not assert it. CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 bears on QUESTION-395 by naming one use a peak or break-even accounting like §9.6's does not price.

### 9.7 Reserve size is a step function

The study sweeps reserves from 0 to 2 in steps of 0.25, for each architecture. Readiness does not grow smoothly with the reserve.† Any reserve strictly between two path thresholds gives exactly the readiness of the next lower threshold: in the original architecture, every reserve from 0 to 1.75 gives R(6) = 0.269, and a reserve of 2 gives 0.907. In the redesigned architecture, R(6) is 0.151 from 0 to 0.75, 0.664 from 1 to 1.75, and 0.907 at 2. Under common random numbers these values are identical, not merely close. So the only reserves worth considering are the path thresholds less the holding cost: 0, θ′ − 1 and 2. A reserve between them is paid for and buys nothing. E6 holds here as well: readiness does not rise with every increment of reserve.

This is the converse of CLAIM-679 (Active, demonstrated), ChatGPT's sentence in the record: cutting the resource held "removes exactly the transitions whose threshold lies between the old holding and the new". Adding resource that reaches no new threshold reaches no new transition. The converse is on the record as CLAIM-679's note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's): "Where readiness depends on the budget only through that set (more budget does not cross a path faster), readiness is a step function of the reserve B − r(q), with steps only at θ_j − r(q). A reserve strictly between two thresholds buys exactly what the lower one buys, and costs more." The note keeps its scope: "It need not hold with several resources, where the sufficient budgets form an upward-closed set with a Pareto frontier (CLAIM-585), or with cumulative costs (CLAIM-646)" (§9.10).

### 9.8 A demand the specification does not name

The draft's §9.6 asks the study to separate two things: an organization getting better at meeting its original demands, and an organization changing which demands it is judged by. It cites CLAIM-361 (Active, interpretive) for the distinction: "a demand excised or suppressed has changed the comparison and has not been satisfied, an endogenous revision of what the organization maintains is a transformation of it and not relief". CLAIM-361 makes that decidable against "A fixed reference set D₀ of the original demands".

**The arm.** After 50 quiet periods, holding A begins to cause a harm the specification does not name. A configuration C, also held at 1, is free of the harm. It is reached over A → N → C with r(N) = 2.5, a threshold the modeller chose between III-b's budget of 2 and II's of 3 (§9.4). The organization follows one of two policies.

- *Excise*: never enter the new demand into the specification.
- *Revise*: first review it for 3 periods. Each review period occupies 0.5 above holding, and is granted only if the budget allows. Once revised, the new demand is met like any other contingency.

Each run is scored three ways: against the original specification D₀, against the organization's own current specification, and against the true demands. The horizon is 120 periods and there are 400 replications.

**What it finds.**

- **Excision scores perfectly on two of the three scorecards.** It has zero violations against D₀ and against its own specification, while the harm runs for all 120 periods. Revision shows violations on both, including the output lost while moving to C: 4.1 periods under II and 13.3 under III-b, both with outside money. Only the true demand set ranks the two correctly. E7 holds, and by construction of the scorecards. This is CLAIM-361's warning, met in a model: a score kept on the original or on the organization's own demands rewards the organization that never admits the new one. The record holds the incentive this sets up, outside the model. CLAIM-662 (Active, demonstrated; the owner's statement, on ChatGPT's example): "For a fixed arrangement, expanding the recognized demand set cannot improve conjunctive compliance and cannot decrease the count of recognized violations; where governance penalizes newly recognized violations without rewarding their discovery, an organization may acquire an incentive not to improve its identification of its obligations". CASE-tmp8mk6w (Active, the assistant's simulation) runs it: an institution "penalized for every recognized violation and unrewarded for discovery ... learns to make no inquiry in every setting while its internal record shows almost no violations, against half its budget spent on inquiry when an independent evaluator scores it". Here excision is an input; there it is learned.
- **Under the peak rule, excision happens by default.†** When the review needs capacity (0.5 above holding), it can never start under I or III-a: the review is blocked in all 120 periods and the harm runs throughout. The organization has not chosen to excise. Its budget rule has left it unable to revise.
- **A reserve covers the new route; a redesign does not.†** II's budget of 3 (a reserve of 2) covers C's threshold of 2.5, and the harm lasts about 6.1 periods. III-b's and IV's budget of 2 (a reserve of 1) does not cover it. They depend on outside money, and the harm lasts about 15.3 periods. With no outside money (g = 0), III-b and IV revise their specification and never reach C, so the harm runs for all 120 periods. IV is worse: with no outside money, the review uses the capacity the drill used, so the drill cannot run, the budget falls, and the staged route is lost too (its budget ends at 1, against III-b's 2). These results hold for r(N) = 2.5; at r(N) ≤ 2 III-b and IV would reach C from their own budgets (§9.4; join JC5, CLAIM-tmpv0azx).

**The model's limit here.** The choice between excising and revising is an input to the model. Nothing in the model makes it. What the model shows is how each choice scores, and which budgets let a revision be acted on.

**A reading the record declines, and a question now filed.** CLAIM-361 concerns a demand that was in D₀ and was removed. The study's new harm was never in D₀. Reading the arm's never entering a new demand as excision carries CLAIM-361 past its own case (join JC4: CLAIM-361 applied to a demand outside D₀; declined by SYNTHESIS-049). The account of the distributed-agency line, SYNTHESIS-049 (Proposed), declines a close link under "Not stated on the record": "**Entrenchment as relief.** That authority entrenched by self-confirmation relieves frustration by removing demands from contention", "a free parameter, whether a demand no process presses is removed or only quiet". So this paper does not state JC4 as held. It uses the arm only to show how the scorecards rank the two policies. The arm also exposes a step the record did not hold. Scoring against "the true demands" needs a reference set that is neither D₀ nor the organization's own, and CLAIM-361 says it "does not say how to choose D₀, H or B". That step is now filed as QUESTION-tmphovmg (Open, the assistant's): "Against which reference set is an organization scored for a demand that was never in its specification, when neither the original specification nor its own current one names it?" Its candidates each move the question "to who holds the set and with what right, which is QUESTION-485's". Who holds that third reference set, and how it is revised, is the subject of §13.

### 9.9 What depends on the modeller's choices

Five choices change the results, and each is stated in `analysis/README.md` or in the model:

- **The staged route is slower**, three edges to two. If it were as fast as the original, III-b would match II at every deadline with half the reserve, and the crossing in §9.5 would disappear.
- **Outside money buys the cheapest route**, not the fastest. If it bought the fastest, III-a would match I, and redesign without a reserve would stop being worse than no redesign (CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's note of 2026-11-23).
- **The review uses transition capacity.** That is why the drill and the review compete in §9.8.
- **The carry rule is capped at 3.** Without the cap, V's budget would grow without bound in quiet periods.
- **The novel route's threshold is r(N) = 2.5**, between III-b's budget of θ′ = 2 and II's of 3. That choice is what makes II reach C and III-b and IV not (§9.8). At r(N) ≤ 2, III-b's budget would cover it, and JC5's contrast would not appear; at r(N) > 3, II's would not either. JC5 depends on it, and on the single fungible resource (§9.4), which is the scope CLAIM-tmpv0azx states.

One more choice is built into the policy. The organization takes the fastest path it can afford, so its choice reads its budget. With these paths, more budget never makes it slower, and readiness rises with the reserve. CLAIM-654 (Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's) shows that this need not hold in general: "a process whose choices depend on the remaining budget can reach a target at a small budget and miss it at a larger one, though the admissible-path reachability of the same architecture grows with the budget". The step function of §9.7 assumes, as CLAIM-679's note says, that "more budget does not cross a path faster".

Other parts of the analysis do not depend on the five choices: CLAIM-577's and CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's conditions (§9.4), the step function (§9.7) within its scope, the equivalence of drill and reserve under peak accounting, and the way excision scores (§9.8). Each follows from the budget rule and the thresholds.

### 9.10 What the model shows and what it does not

**What it shows, within the model.** Items 1 to 3 illustrate what the record already holds; items 4 to 7 are the model's own outputs and depend on the choices in §9.9.

1. Ordinary output can be matched exactly, by construction, while readiness differs by a factor of three or more at deadlines from two to six periods (about 9.5 at t = 2, 6.1 at t = 3, 4.6 at t = 4): R(6) is 0.894 for II and 0.267 for I. At longer deadlines the ratio shrinks, as outside money reaches I (at t = 8, 0.961 against 0.395, about 2.4; at t = 12, about 1.6).
2. CLAIM-577's and CLAIM-tmpg8yl2's conditions hold exactly, so the draft's redesigned regime needs a reserve (E4 falsified as worded; E5 falsified in the model).
3. Under peak accounting, a drill holds a reserve without a commitment. It costs what the reserve costs, and it fails when the schedule is broken rather than when the commitment is withdrawn (CLAIM-878 and its note of 2026-11-23).
4. By readiness, II (and V, equal to it) is the most ready regime at every deadline. What depends on the deadline is the order of I against III-b and IV, which cross between t = 2 and t = 3, and the ranking by long-run cost: I is cheapest at τ = 2, II from τ = 3 to 8, III-b and IV at τ = 12 and 20. On these assumptions, redesign without a reserve is worse than no redesign.
5. A reserve pays above a break-even pL* that depends on the deadline and on how often the capability is used, not on p·L and c alone.
6. Readiness is a step function of the reserve, for one resource and finitely many paths.
7. Scorecards kept against the original specification, or against the organization's own, reward excision. A budget held at the peak of its use can leave revision unfundable. A reserve covers a demand the specification did not name when, and only when, the new route's threshold is within its budget.

**What it does not show:**

- **Anything about real industries or real budget rules.** The account of the readiness line, SYNTHESIS-031 (Proposed), says it does not claim "That the readiness argument applies to any real organization; its constructions and models say their organizational readings are analogies." The account of reaching and paying, SYNTHESIS-024 (Proposed), says it does not claim "That organizations have configuration spaces with such costs", and quotes CLAIM-579: "No organizational case in the record shows slack present in total and absent at the bottleneck." CLAIM-577 says real rules are "rarely pure spend-matching". CLAIM-921 (Active, demonstrated, as of 2026-11-25; Proposed when this section was written) shows that a rule with a base budget under its use-following part can lose a capability too, through decaying competence, whenever the base alone sustains competence below the level at which the capability is committed (§9.1); this model does not represent competence. A budget rule with people in it is also beyond the model. CLAIM-700 (Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's in the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse") holds that where members' compensation covers a shortfall and "the organization budgets to what it observes, the compensation and the quiet it buys are read as adequacy, resources are cut toward what the present configuration needs, and the reconfigurations that would remove the shortfall pass out of reach". Its source calls it "A possible reinforcing loop, not a universal prediction about institutions".
- **Anything about cumulative or multiple resources.** The model has one capacity resource. CLAIM-646 (Proposed, demonstrated): "Peak-capacity thresholds do not fix cumulative transition costs". CLAIM-585 (Active, demonstrated): with several resources the sufficient budgets form a set that "need not be convex, so no threshold on a weighted total of the resources describes it". CLAIM-587 (Active, demonstrated): "Where successful response requires several complementary capacities, the value of preserving one depends on whether the others remain deployable, so individual resource utilization need not track the organization's response capability". CASE-237 (Proposed) is its instance: "Two complementary capacities whose success probabilities each rise from 0.8 to 0.85 while the probability that both hold falls from 0.8 to 0.7". CLAIM-878 itself says its drill accounting fails for "budgets in money or hours", which count how long a capability is out. The draft's §9.5 calls for several contingencies with different deadlines, a "multidimensional temporal response profile", and none of that is run here. The extension is filed, with its design fixed, as LEAD-tmppzp9z (Open, stage specified, the assistant's): "Extend the readiness simulator to two capacity resources, a cumulative cost of transition and several contingencies with different deadlines, and test whether readiness stays a step function of the reserve, whether a drill still holds the budget as a reserve does, and how the ranking of reserve against redesign changes". Its checks include CLAIM-878's limit: "If a drill still keeps the money budget at the transition's cost per period at no more than a reserve's cost, that limit is wrong in this model."
- **Whether a redesign creates a capability or only cheapens one.** QUESTION-392 (Open) asks it. CLAIM-922 answers it in another model, according to whether the home configuration space is connected. Here the space is connected, so the redesign only cheapens.
- **QUESTION-395's identification problem** (§9.6).

The study is on the record as CASE-tmpo8bsc (Proposed, an executed study, filed 2026-11-23), with its novel-demand arm as CASE-tmp95f8e (Proposed); both cite this directory's analysis as the single copy of the data. Several are filed as dated notes, cited above, on the entries they bear on: CLAIM-878 (the drill), CLAIM-tmpg8yl2 (III-a below I), QUESTION-395 (the break-even) and CLAIM-679 (the step function).
