# LIT-2038: The Optimal Control of Partially Observable Markov Processes over a Finite Horizon

Smallwood, R. D. & Sondik, E. J. (1973). The Optimal Control of Partially Observable Markov Processes over a Finite Horizon. *Operations Research* 21(5): 1071–1088. doi:10.1287/opre.21.5.1071.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. What follows is what a later work says of it, unverified against
the original.*

- **The belief state.** Kaelbling, Littman and Cassandra (1998),
  [LIT-2056](LIT-2056.md), cite it, with one other work, for the belief state being a
  sufficient statistic for the past history (§3.2, p. 106).
- **An exact algorithm, exponential in the worst case.** The same authors
  say Smallwood and Sondik construct the value function directly, and that
  such algorithms "still have worst-case running times exponential in at
  least one of the problem parameters" (p. 114); they credit Sondik with
  the first exact finite-horizon algorithm (p. 117).

## Standing in the record

Registered from the pasted bibliography on emergency readiness, and not
read: no lawful copy was found. A characterizes it as a foundational
finite-horizon POMDP treatment, which agrees with how the neighbour credits
it, and as the formal apparatus for provisional dispatch followed by
reassessment and cancellation, which is A's application and not a claim
made for the paper.

**The neighbour.** Kaelbling, Littman and Cassandra (1998), [LIT-2056](LIT-2056.md),
read in [NOTE-1563](../notes.d/NOTE-1563.md), stands in for it. It restates, in the same
framework, the belief state as a sufficient statistic, the
piecewise-linear convex finite-horizon value function (which it derives
itself rather than attributing to this paper alone), and the existence and
exponential worst case of Smallwood and Sondik's algorithm. It does not
give the original's algorithm in detail, its proofs, its notation or
reward conventions, or any example or application of its own. The account
filed from the neighbour, [THEORY-1395](../theory.d/THEORY-1395.md), names this paper as the
original and asks for it to be read before promotion.
