# NOTE-1615: Stern et al. — Supervised training of a creased sheet by crease-stiffness plasticity

## Contribution

A protocol by which a mechanical material's own plastic changes, gated by
a correct-or-incorrect signal, reshape its multistable energy landscape so
that it generalizes a force-to-fold mapping to inputs it never saw, and a
statistical account of why heterogeneous stiffness prunes attractors. The
abstract states the setup: "During training, we fold the sheet using
different training forces and assume a learning rule that changes
stiffness of creases in response to their folding strain."

## Key insight

The sheet's untrained attractor structure is already a rich, random
classifier of force patterns. Training does not add structure; it removes
it, merging attractors so that those that survive line up with the
desired classes. Learning here is selective destruction of folded states,
and overtraining is the same process carried too far.

## Assumptions

- **A self-folding origami energy model** with torsional-spring creases,
  from the authors' earlier work; quasi-static folding from flat, the sheet
  returned to flat before each example.
- **Plastic creases.** Stiffness changes with folding strain at a rate α,
  with nonlinearity r = 2 (Eq. 1); "Our learning framework requires
  materials that can plastically stiffen or soften when strained
  repeatedly".
- **An external supervisor** compares each fold with a reference state for
  its class, built from the untrained sheet's averaged response (SI Note 2,
  not read).
- **For the counting argument (Eq. 2)**, folding angles are taken as random
  and stiffnesses as random with spread Δk; the scaling of β with sheet
  size is found numerically.

## Key results

- **The rule (Results).** "if the response is the desired one, as
  determined by a ‘supervisor’, folded creases are allowed to soften in
  proportion to their folding strain. If the response is incorrect, creases
  stiffen instead."
- **Generalization (Results, Fig. 3).** "High test accuracy is observed
  (Fig. 3c,d) (∼ 80% of the test examples classified correctly)"; the
  abstract says the process "reshapes non-linearities inherent in folding a
  sheet so as to show the correct response for previously unseen test
  forces."
- **The mechanism (Results).** "the learning process merges attractors of
  the untrained sheet such that the surviving attractors recapitulate
  features of the desired force-fold mapping." With structures above an
  energy E_F inaccessible, the number accessible goes as
  2^N[1 − erf((αk̄ − E_F)/(βΔk))] (Eq. 2): "Hence the number of surviving
  folded structures should decrease fast with ∆k", as observed (Fig. 4d).
- **Overtraining (Results).** "The failure mode of over-training is
  typically that all forces fold the sheet into a single folded structure,
  resulting in no classification."
- **Sheet size as capacity.** Larger sheets gain more from more training
  data (Fig. 5), the familiar pattern of model complexity against data.
- **Iris (Complex classification).** Trained on 10 examples of I.
  versicolor and I. virginica, the sheet "was tested on 80 unseen examples
  of these species; the trained sheet was able to identify the species of
  91% of previously unseen specimens correctly."
- **Feasibility (Experimental considerations).** A rule that only softens
  gives "similar accuracy as earlier" (Fig. 7a); the spread of stiffness in
  well-trained sheets "does not exceed 30% of the mean stiffness value for
  a medium sized (28 crease) sheet", within the range of shape-memory
  polymers (Fig. 7b).

## Concepts

- **Attractor** of folded structure m: the set of force patterns that lead
  to it, "defined as the ‘attractor’ of folded structure m in the space of
  force patterns".
- **Training and test accuracy**: the fractions of training and of unseen
  patterns that fold the sheet into the correct structure.
- **Epoch**: "one pass through the entire training set".
- **Memory versus learning** (Discussion): "A robust memory implies a
  trained model that shows the correct response for all of the training
  examples (i.e., low training error), even at the expense of overfitting
  such training data. In contrast, in learning, we seek a model that
  generalizes and responds correctly to unseen examples".

## Connections

It rests on Stern, Pinson and Murugan (2017) for the exponential number of
folding pathways of self-folding sheets and for the energy model, and
draws on memory formation in disordered matter and on directed aging of
elastic networks. Rocks et al. (2017), [LIT-2060](../literature.d/LIT-2060.md), is the designed
counterpart: there an external algorithm prunes a spring network to a
chosen response; here the material changes itself under a supervisor's
signal.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures.** It gives the
  published title and PMC id and the gloss "plasticity and nonlinear material response can train mechanical systems"; that gloss is A's, not the paper's words, and is thin. Plasticity
  alone does not train the sheet: an external supervisor's verdict gates
  the sign of the local rule. The central result, generalization to unseen
  inputs, which is what separates it from memory formation, goes
  unmentioned, as does that it is simulation. A gives no authors. The
  synthesis's progression from fixed geometry through reconfigurable and trainable to self-maintaining is consistent with it: the sheet is trainable, and the
  supervisor is outside it, so it does not reach self-maintaining.
- **[THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)** (Ashby: adaptation by trial and error needs a second
  feedback, through essential variables, that changes the primary
  feedback's parameters). Qualifies: the sheet has the two-level form, fast
  folding and slow stiffness change driven by an outcome signal, but the
  signal comes from an external supervisor, not from essential variables
  of the sheet's own. A contrast case: parameter adaptation without
  endogenous essential variables.
- **[CLAIM-443](../claims.d/CLAIM-443.md)** (a learned prior over which processes govern, its bias
  trained by reward-modulated Hebbian updates). Analogy in form: a
  supervisor-gated local update in a physical substrate. The claim is
  about governance among processes, which the paper does not touch.
- **[CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)** (an operative regime, the organization selecting among
  regimes, and the capacity to transform it can persist or fail
  separately). Qualifies loosely: folded structure, crease stiffnesses and
  training rule are separable here, and overtraining destroys the
  selecting organization's use while the sheet still folds. The paper does
  not frame it so.
- [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md), [CLAIM-365](../claims.d/CLAIM-365.md), [CLAIM-495](../claims.d/CLAIM-495.md), [CLAIM-538](../claims.d/CLAIM-538.md), [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md) and [THEORY-171](../theory.d/THEORY-171.md),

  [THEORY-171](../theory.d/THEORY-171.md) is narrower than the synthesis's placing it among "the local-to-global questions": it concerns causal contextuality.
- Filed: [THEORY-1411](../theory.d/THEORY-1411.md). It is a physics model that borrows supervised
  learning's vocabulary; no instruction for machine-learning practice and
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- The authors': it needs materials that stiffen or soften plastically
  under repeated strain, and the sign-switching rule needs conditions
  (temperature, pH) that reverse the effect; the stiffness range is
  limited; overtraining.
- Computation only, on a simplified energy model; no sheet was trained.
- The supervisor is external and needs a reference state per class.
- Small tasks: two classes, and Iris with a handful of examples; the
  higher-dimensional tasks are in the unread Supplementary Notes.
- A reading of the preprint; the retitled PNAS text may differ.

## Open questions

- Can a physical sheet be trained this way? A fabricated sheet of a
  shape-memory polymer trained by the softening-only rule would tell.
- Could the correct-or-incorrect signal come from the sheet's own state,
  so that no external supervisor is needed?
