# THEORY-tmpq12rp: US law bars any part of an NTSB report of its determinations on an accident, probable cause included, from evidence or use in a civil action for damages arising from it, and NTSB regulation says its investigations are not conducted to determine anyone's rights, liabilities or blame: the Board's causal findings are fenced off from liability

49 U.S.C. § 1154(b), [LIT-tmphhd10](../literature.d/LIT-tmphhd10.md), read in [NOTE-tmp45cjg](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp45cjg.md).

## The account

"No part of a report of the Board, related to an accident or an
investigation of an accident, may be admitted into evidence or used in a
civil action for damages resulting from a matter mentioned in the report"
(§ 1154(b)). The bar is on the "Board accident report", "containing the
Board's determinations, including the probable cause"; "there is no
statutory bar to" admitting factual accident reports (49 CFR 835.2).
Investigations "are not conducted for the purpose of determining the
rights, liabilities, or blame of any person or entity" (49 CFR 831.4(c)).
The stated purpose is to keep the Board "free of the entanglement of such
suits" (49 CFR 835.3(a)).

## Bearing

- **[CLAIM-tmpx4kjt](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpx4kjt.md)**: grounds. The claim's condition, an investigator
  that traces cause without assigning blame or liability, is this
  arrangement in law.
- **[QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md)**: bears on. It is the legal form of the question's
  first answer: causal tracing institutionally separated from blame.

## What it does not show

- That the separation changes what investigations find.
- Anything about criminal proceedings, which the texts read do not
  mention.
