Part of the THEORY backfill audit. The natural-sciences topic group held
seventeen LITs with a `Read` or `Skimmed` NOTE and no THEORY citing them as
`source`. Each was re-read from its NOTE and LIT to decide whether the
reading supports one or more THEORY documents. The owner's hypothesis is
that a work read closely enough to carry a NOTE usually supports at least
one THEORY.

Ten of the seventeen became a THEORY's source. The seven that did not are
the group's reviews, a dated forecast, an unrefereed single-study preprint,
and two crystallization papers whose readings route their content to the
record's own CLAIMs instead. So the hypothesis held for ten of seventeen
here, weaker than for the moral-psychology batch, and the misses are
explained by what kind of work each is, not by shallow reading.

## Choices made

- **Status.** `Active` only where the record believes the account as
  stated: a proved result, a described model with its scope, or a
  mechanism supported by a confirming test. `Proposed` where the reading
  marks the account argued, untested, one-system or (for the Eulalia model)
  a plausibility argument. Each Proposed THEORY names a kind of result that
  would settle it and says what cannot.
- **Topic tags.** The group's primary tag `natural-sciences` leads every
  THEORY, with the secondary tags the readings carry (`mathematics`,
  `game-theory`, `complex-systems`, `ecology`, `philosophy-of-biology`,
  `probabilistic-modeling`, `emotion-and-affect`, `moral-psychology`).
  Following [ADR-018](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-018.md), the cooperation sources read as biology are not
  tagged `moral-psychology` except Trivers, whose human section is itself an
  account of moral emotion.
- **No `answers` edges.** None of the ten answers a QUESTION of the
  record's own closely enough to warrant the link; the overlaps are
  topical, not answering.
- **Relations between the new THEORYs.** `extends` edges where one account
  builds on another's mechanism: Hamilton Part II and Nowak extend Hamilton
  Part I, and Nowak extends Axelrod & Hamilton. No `rivals` edges: these
  accounts of cooperation are complementary, not competing.
