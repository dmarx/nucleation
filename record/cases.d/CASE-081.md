# CASE-081: Self-maintenance learned for a designer's reward: a system that maintains itself because it was trained to, and later revises the policy and keeps maintaining itself without further instruction

## The case

A10 §6.1: "One particularly important boundary case is a system that learns
to maintain itself only because an external designer rewards that behavior.
Does learned self-maintenance become genuinely endogenous once the system
can revise the policy and maintain the associated governance without
continued external instruction? That question may expose a distinction
between the historical origins of agency and its current constitutive
organization."

The case has two stages. At first, maintenance serves an objective the
designer fixed. Later, the system revises how it maintains itself, and the
reward is gone.

## What it can show

At the first stage it is [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md)'s target: the norms are the
designer's. Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde's C5 ([THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md), [NOTE-458](../notes.d/NOTE-458.md))
says systems optimizing an externally fixed function are not models of
agency. The second stage is where the objection could be answered, if what
the system now maintains is set by its own organization. A10 leaves open
which stage counts. The question is whether origin or present organization
settles it.

## What it cannot show

It does not show that the second stage happens. A system that keeps
maintaining itself after the reward stops may only be running the learned
policy, its norms still the designer's, inherited. Telling the two apart is
[QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md) again.
