summary: >-
  Pembrey, Saffery and Bygren (2014). A position paper by an 18-member network, two of whose authors led the Överkalix studies. It uses "transgenerational" for any outcome in offspring of an exposure in a parent before conception or in an earlier ancestor. In Överkalix cohorts of about 100 people each, a paternal grandfather's good food supply in mid-childhood went with higher mortality in his grandsons (rate ratio 1.67) and poor supply with lower (0.65); for paternal grandmothers and granddaughters, 2.13 and 0.72. In ALSPAC, sons of fathers who began smoking before 11 carried more fat from age 13. The authors answer the charge of multiple testing with prior hypotheses and coherence across studies, grant that replication is needed, and hold that a sensitive period in the grandparent's mid-childhood, not adolescence, makes cultural or genetic inheritance alone unlikely. They stay agnostic on mechanism and ask cohorts to collect ancestral exposure data.
