# CASE-132: One complaint, two taxonomies: a report of an unsafe practice is correctly classified both by equipment and by workers' obligations, the intake procedure needs one primary class, and the class chosen decides whether the report reaches a body that can change working conditions

## The case

The owner, at U18, quoting A17 ("The deeper question is whether some
apparent conceptual disputes are really disputes about the allocation of
limited representational resources"): "sounds like another good
opportunity to try to come up with some illustrative CASEs".

A18's CASE O: an organization receives an account of an unsafe working
practice. "Its operational taxonomy classifies the event by the equipment
involved. Its labor-relations taxonomy classifies it by the obligations
imposed on workers. Both classifications accurately describe the same
event. But the intake procedure requires one *primary* classification,
which determines which department receives the report first." Classified
operationally, it goes to equipment maintenance; classified as a
workplace-rights issue, to "a process empowered to reconsider working
conditions".

A19's variant removes the easy answer: the report is indexed under both
and searchable under both, and only one classification lets it initiate
review.

## What it shows

- **Compatible descriptions, competing uptake.** [CLAIM-310](../claims.d/CLAIM-310.md) shows that
  sorting schemes over determinate objects never conflict as
  descriptions; what they compete for is arrangement. Here the
  arrangement is a route to authority. A18: "When does selecting a
  classification cease to be a neutral organizational convenience and
  become an allocation of practical authority?"
- **Recognition without uptake.** [CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md) separates a contribution's
  admission, its recognition in apt terms, and its uptake into judgements.
  A19's variant makes the report admitted and recognized under both
  classes and taken up under one.

## What it does not show

That the dispute is only about resources. A classification can also be
wrong, and two taxonomies can disagree about what the event is. The case
holds both descriptions true to isolate the routing.
