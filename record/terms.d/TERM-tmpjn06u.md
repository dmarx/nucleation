# TERM-tmpjn06u: hyperarticle: a living, versioned body of a work's claims, evidence, methods, objections and revisions, each addressable and attributed and linked by typed relations, of which a conventional article is one view; not a paper with more hyperlinks, and not a document that is simply kept editable

## The definition

A5: "A hyperarticle is a living, navigable scholarly work in which claims,
evidence, arguments, methods, data, objections, and revisions are
interconnected rather than flattened into a single linear narrative. The
conventional article remains available as a coherent reading experience,
but it becomes one possible view of a richer underlying knowledge
structure." And what it is not: "A hyperarticle is not simply a paper with
more hyperlinks, nor a perpetually updated Google Doc." A8 puts the
difference in the relations: "There's a meaningful difference between
saying two documents are related and saying that one experiment supports a
particular hypothesis, another contradicts it".

Its parts, each already in the record's grammar:

- **addressable parts**: codes ([CLAIM-182](../claims.d/CLAIM-182.md): addressability, not storage,
  is what anchors);
- **typed relations**: `rests_on`, `grounds`, `objects_to` and the rest
  ([CLAIM-479](../claims.d/CLAIM-479.md));
- **versions**: `version` and `history` on each entry, and git;
- **attribution**: the voice each entry is filed in;
- **views**: generated pages, and manuscripts drawn by [ADR-143](../decisions.d/ADR-143.md).

A11 restates it in the owner's terms: "A hyperarticle is a view with
memory over claims, evidence, arguments, and revisions", a feed in the
sense of [TERM-tmpx49xx](TERM-tmpx49xx.md).

## What would settle it

Adoption is the owner's. The term is used by the owner's thesis
[CLAIM-tmpjf44i](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjf44i.md), and the exchange's later turns (U21, U24 to U28) build
on it.
