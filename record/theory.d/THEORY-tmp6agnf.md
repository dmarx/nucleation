# THEORY-tmp6agnf: Smit, Helmich, Bringmann, Oldehinkel, Wichers and Snippe report that in 37 formerly depressed people tapering antidepressants, rising autocorrelation and variance in momentary affect preceded recurrence in about a third of those who relapsed and appeared in about a sixth of those who did not, so that critical slowing down is detectable within persons before some transitions into depression, with low sensitivity

## Source

Smit, Helmich, Bringmann, Oldehinkel, Wichers & Snippe (2025),
LIT-tmpuur53, read from the authors' 2022 preprint in NOTE-tmpptg2p.

## The account

People in remission who were stopping antidepressants rated their affect
five times a day for four months. In each person, the trend in lag-1
autocorrelation and variance of negative affect over a moving 28-day
window was tested before any transition. Across 17 preregistered
settings, such a rise preceded recurrence in 32.9% of those who relapsed
and appeared in 16.2% of those who did not; the group difference in the
trend was significant for variance (d = 0.60) and not for
autocorrelation (d = 0.45). Where present, the rise began five to eight
weeks before the transition on average. The authors take this as the
first systematic within-person evidence of critical slowing down before
recurrence, too insensitive for clinical use, and add that its absence in
most relapses "may indicate that in the majority of patients, the
recurrence of depressive symptoms is not a critical transition". It
replicates across people the single-patient findings of Wichers et al.
2016 (LIT-tmpdpj4x) and 2020.

## What it does not show

Hysteresis: the design has no driver that moves back, so it cannot show
the depressed state persisting after its trigger has returned. Nor does
it show what holds the state once entered, or that a maintaining link
does.
