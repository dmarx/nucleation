# THEORY-tmpo2jzk: Phenomenal binding of the parts of an experience can be modelled as agreement, on overlaps, of measured similarity structures assigned to the parts by a presheaf, and the broad quale is fixed by its parts exactly when that presheaf is a sheaf; a sheaf failure may mark disunity, as proposed for binocular rivalry, or context dependence within one unified experience, as in the Ebbinghaus illusion, and the framework does not yet say which

## Source

Lee-Youngzie, Tsuchiya, Robinson, Dietz & Monti (2026), *Entropy* 28(6),
615, [LIT-tmphb8p5](../literature.d/LIT-tmphb8p5.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmpfadjy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfadjy.md) from the PMC deposit of
the version of record: §4.1 (the framework), §4.1.5–4.1.6 (the two readings
of failure), §5.2 and Appendix A. Locations are sections, since the copy
read has no page numbers.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was measured. The paper builds a framework and reads examples
through it:

- **The framework.** The parts of an experience, ordered by subsumption,
  form a finite topology. A presheaf Q sends each part to the
  dissimilarity matrix of its qualia, measured by similarity judgements,
  and each inclusion to a restriction map that, by Definition 1, never
  decreases dissimilarity. For the visual field the parts are the two
  hemifields, their overlap at the vertical meridian, and the whole.
- **Binding as gluing.** "Conditions for phenomenal binding are formalized
  as the compatibility condition for the gluing of local sections" (§5.2).
  When Q is a sheaf, the broad quale is "wholly determined by, and
  decomposable into" the narrow ones (§4.1.5).
- **Failure, read two ways.** For binocular rivalry, "violation of the
  sheaf condition may be diagnostic of phenomenal disunity" (§4.1.5). For
  narrow qualia measured in isolation, a failure shows "interactions
  between parts that are irreducible to a predictable combination of
  narrower qualia considered in isolation" (§4.1.6). That is the Ebbinghaus
  case, an ordinary unified experience.
- **A test.** A consistency radius on measured matrices quantifies
  incompatibility on overlaps. A small radius means "we fail to reject the
  hypothesis that the sheaf structure formalizes the structure of
  experience" (§5.1).

What could have come out otherwise is all in the future: the consistency
radius could be no different in rivalry than in fused viewing.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say that the unity of an experience is a global
  section.** The paper assumes unity is always present ("their conjoint
  phenomenology always possesses the phenomenal quality of unity", §1),
  and it counts a broad quale not fixed by its parts as unified (§5.2).
  What it formalizes as gluing is binding of narrow into broad qualia.
  Against [QUESTION-tmpzje42](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpzje42.md)'s first answer, this is a case in which a sheaf
  failure is not disunity.
- **Its global section is data, not an experience.** The term is reserved
  for "the coherent global data that results from a sheaf" (Definition
  A14). That a gluing of similarity data tracks a unity in the experience
  is the structural approach's premise, assumed and not tested.
- **The rivalry reading does not follow from its definitions.** Gluing
  applies to sections that "agree throughout their intersection"
  (Definition A15). Rivalry is sections that disagree on the overlap, so
  the sheaf condition is not violated. Only the tribar example, a cycle of
  three with pairwise agreement, is a gluing obstruction, which is
  [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s shape.
- **The gluing condition, as defined, is thin.** Definition 1's
  restriction maps are functions on trials with an inequality on
  dissimilarities. Read so, the left and right restrictions of a trial are
  the same trial and always match. The test with content is the empirical
  consistency radius, which needs a definition of how a measured row
  restricts that the paper does not give ([NOTE-tmpfadjy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfadjy.md), Key results).
- **The worked topology is malformed**, though repairable. Definition A7's
  example lists down-sets of a diamond order as the up-sets of a chain,
  and omits the whole space (checked by script, [NOTE-tmpfadjy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfadjy.md)).
- **It does not reach beyond two regions of one modality at one moment**
  (§5.3.1), and it does not touch functional binding, which "can occur
  without conscious experience" (§1).
