**What happened.** The owner asked whether the record had an account of "the notion of separability raised in the conversation" (the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws"). The assistant found three senses in the exchange: separation of scales or levels, separation of classes, and spectral separation. It mapped each to held entries and proposed a term and a note. Then it asked: "Which sense did you mean? If it was sense 1 in the critical case, the note on SYNTHESIS-054 is the substantive part." The owner's go-ahead ("dialectic on") adopted that lean. So the term, which covers all three, was filed, but the substantive note was filed for sense 1 only.

**How it was found.** The owner: "one of the conventions of the dialectic regime is supposed to be that when you encounter ambiguity in how a phrase might be interpreted, you file all of the available options (within reason). err on the side of adding richness to the record." CLAUDE.md says so: "When a turn admits several valid readings that would be filed differently, file each, marked as alternatives, and say which is which; the owner chooses."

**The fix.** Notes for senses 2 and 3 as well: on SYNTHESIS-109 (what can be told apart, with the spectral threshold as a budgeted telling-apart) and on SYNTHESIS-083 (a readout separates what the spectrum does not show). The term was also added to the two accounts of the exchange.

**What to keep.** The rule was applied in the same session's filing, where U5 and U23 each got two readings filed as alternatives. It was not applied to a question about the record, as if a question were not a turn. The question was a turn, and its ambiguity changed what would be filed. A question that ends "which did you mean?" while the regime is on is the sign. File each reading, then say which the assistant leans to. Note too how the go-ahead worked: because it adopts a stated lean (ADR-127), a lean stated in place of filing the alternatives narrows the filing to that lean.
