# NOTE-tmpkxx6g: Sax (2026) — The Double Darkness of Dark Patterns

## Contribution

A conceptual framework ("double darkness") separating and then relinking the descriptive and normative dimensions of dark patterns, aimed at legal/policy precision under EU law.

## Key results

- §2.1: dark-patterns definitions each carry "some criteria to determine which user interface tricks... have the required dark patterns-flavour" and "a wrong-making feature", and "these two elements are not always clearly distinguished."
- §3: "A clear and precise overview of the ways in which dark patterns are dark in its first sense... does not automatically give one a clear and precise overview of the ways in which dark patterns are dark in the second sense"; the first sense "does not, by itself, explain the second sense".
- §3: autonomy's core, after Christman (2004), is "competence in reflection and decision making and (on some views) authenticity of values, desires".
- §3: "all normative influence-types can indeed be understood as forms of influence that are antithetical to autonomy."
- §3: Sax distinguishes manipulation (a wrong-making influence) from nudging (interest-serving), criticizing Luguri & Strahilevitz for using them interchangeably.
- §6: option sketch for the DFA to abandon the per-decision autonomy frame for an interests-based "hostile design" fairness standard (citing Timms 2025; Albert et al. 2026), where "hostile design choices as such should be considered unfair, irrespective of whether one can... establish an impairment/distortion".

## Assumptions

EU legal frame (UCPD, DSA, AI Act, prospective DFA) as the context that makes influence-type precision matter. Autonomy as the master value these instruments protect. A (procedural) competence+authenticity core of autonomy, explicitly borrowed, not defended.

## Concepts

Dark 1 / Dark 2; normative influence-types; conceptual drift; entwinement of descriptive and normative; operationalizing autonomy via influence-types; Timms's hostile-design/hostile-patterns alternative; covert steering (manipulation).

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** A's summary ("work on dark patterns conflates what an interface does with why, if at all, that influence is wrongful") is accurate and is almost exactly Sax's Dark 1 / Dark 2 distinction.

- **LIT-1002** (Susser, Roessler & Nissenbaum; manipulation as covert influence; its account is cited in LIT-1002's note of 2026-11-12 as the "what/why" contrast at A23 §5): Sax qualifies and extends it. Sax cites Susser et al. (2019) for the authenticity/authorship point and for his own definition of manipulation ("sneakily steer... behind one's back"). He separates the descriptive "what an interface does" from the normative "why it is wrongful", which is precisely the distinction LIT-1002's note attributes to this work. Supports LIT-1002's framing and adds that the wrong-making feature must be specified per influence-type, not assumed.

- "dark pattern": only LIT-1002 and NOTE-745 held. "hostile design", "Digital Services Act", "Unfair Commercial Practices", "nudging": nothing held under those exact terms. LIT-1465 (taxonomy of addictive design features) is a Dark-1-style neighbour in the record. 
- Not a duplicate (DOI absent from record/).

## Limitations

Conceptual/legal, EU-specific; no empirical content. The Dark 2 autonomy proposal is offered as "modest, tentative", not defended against the hostile-design alternative. VoR not read.
