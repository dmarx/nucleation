# THEORY-tmpqy5fx: Preschool children's voluntary delay of gratification is lengthened by overt or cognitive distraction from the rewards and shortened by attention or ideation that keeps the rewards salient, so effective delay depends on suppressing attention to the rewards rather than dwelling on them

Mischel, Ebbesen & Zeiss (1972), LIT-tmpappcx.

## The experiments

In the self-imposed delay paradigm, children with both rewards in view
and nothing to distract them waited under a minute; with a toy or told to
"think fun" they waited 8.59 and 12.12 minutes (Experiment I). With the
rewards hidden, told to think about them, they waited 0.78 minutes against
roughly 13–14 otherwise (Experiment III). The prediction that ideating
about absent rewards sustains delay could have held; it failed.

## What it does not show

- **That children choose the strategy.** The distraction was supplied by
  the experimenters, and children did not cover the rewards on purpose
  before waiting.
- **That the contingency stops mattering.** Distraction without the reward
  contingency kept no one in the room.
- **Generality.** The authors decline to generalize beyond this paradigm.
