## 2. Dependence produced by successful provision (A51 §§1–2; A49 §§1–3)

- **CLAIM-602** (Active, demonstrated, thesis; the assistant's, at the owner's request of 2026-11-05 to look to aid and development; promoted "at the status review's ruling (the owner delegated these rulings)", 2026-11-06; not a member). "outside supply that does the work first lowers the capacity kept, and the longer it does all of it the longer recovery takes once it stops, while supply that covers only what the organization cannot yet do leaves its learning untouched, so what decides dependence is the order in which supply and capacity are used, not the amount of supply". In the model, capacity is "0.769 without aid, 0.625 at A = 0.5, 0.25 at 0.9 and none at full substitution". ## 3. Control over the terms, and domination (A51 "culminating argument"; A49 §§2–3, abstract)

- **CLAIM-690** (Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's, "accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13"; rests on CLAIM-428; member). "the same magnitude of dependence can run either way, and what sorts it is who controls the terms on which it is met, exit, renegotiation and the conditions of supply". Its ground is **CASE-264** (Proposed, thought experiment, fictional; the assistant's), through ARG-110 (Proposed). Two workshops have S = 10, the same dependence, integration and membership, and differ only in control. Under symmetric Nash, P's share is 4; when R sets the terms, P gets 1. "Change in P's share per unit of the whole's need" is 1/2 against 0. R can choose P's payoff "from 1 to 8, a width of 7". "What it does not show": "That the numbers are the world's"; "That B is exploitation".
- **CLAIM-682** (Proposed, interpretive; A's, A13 §II of "Analyze technological civilization trap", narrowed at v2; member). A dependence is "presumptively dominating by those who control it when three conditions coincide": an important sphere, "consequential discretion", and the dependent lacks "effective means of protection, influence, challenge or justified withdrawal". It is "not for every unfreedom, and impersonal constraint that no one holds is outside it". Its note of 2026-11-24 (A42 §4) gives legibility as a route by which the conditions come to hold.
- **CLAIM-686** (Active, demonstrated; the assistant's; rests on CLAIM-523; member). The third condition's failures of normative efficacy entail lack of influence and challenge, and "justified withdrawal lies outside those three achievements, so the domination threshold's third condition is met by failures of normative efficacy only together with a failure of exit". Its summary: CLAIM-523 states the three "as needed, not as sufficient", and "the converse does not follow".
- **CLAIM-687** (Active, interpretive; A's; not a member). Accountability "should rise with how indispensable the arrangement has become". It concedes CLAIM-749.
- **CLAIM-tmp2jhc1**, the fallback that changes terms unused, is in `cost-and-possibility.md` §3f. Its Nash step is the assistant's, and A24's caption says "Maintaining fallback capacity is not priced in this model".
- **CLAIM-728** (Proposed, interpretive; A's residual-control reading of **the owner's connection at U24 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"** ("labor theory, contract theory, and auction theory are feeling relevant"); not a member). "a relationship fair in every specified term can still leave one party's terms at the other's discretion". **ARG-210 is Rejected**: Grossman and Hart and Hart's lecture "tell against clause 3", locating the effect "*ex ante*, through anticipation". The note of 2026-11-22 leaves **two alternatives for the owner**: A, widen the timing, or B, keep it and narrow the scope. Ambiguity as leverage (CLAIM-tmpro73c) is in `contracts-and-standing.md` §4.
- **The cost of contesting.** CLAIM-731 (`cost-and-possibility.md` §3c) separates execution friction from contestation friction, developing **the owner's CLAIM-710** (U11, "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"). **CLAIM-748** (Proposed, demonstrated; **the owner's, U28 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"**, "One party 'outlasting' the other in the courts can almost be interpreted as a form of siege warfare"; not a member): "an actor can make a legally recognized corrective objective unreachable ... by raising what pursuing the objective costs ... above what that party can sustain". Its ground CASE-287 (Proposed, construction, fictional; A's, rechecked) gives the claimant 60 at four months and "nothing if the defendant can stretch it to seven". Halving the runway "cuts its chance of relief from 86 to 63 per cent". **CLAIM-716** (Active; A's, at the owner's U25 there, which named the case): "the breaching party gains leverage over the resolution of its own breach"; it was rechecked at promotion ("B accepts 25 against 20 ..."). **CLAIM-879** (Proposed, demonstrated; the assistant's; grounds CASE-316, Proposed, executed study): with the same oversight, at ten times the speed the share stopped in time "fell from 0.994 to 0.396".

## 4. Conscientiousness, moral commitment and constrained exit (A51 "subsequent sections"; A49 §§4, 6)
