# NOTE-tmp8sct4: Religion and Science (SEP)

## Contribution

The entry introduces the interdisciplinary field of science and religion and the questions of compatibility and mutual influence it studies. It outlines the history and scope of both terms and the main taxonomies of their interaction, then surveys how science has related to five traditions — Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism and Judaism. It closes with contemporary topics where they meet, focusing on divine action and creation and on human origins.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1.2 stresses that "science" and "religion" are recent, context-dependent terms (e.g. Aquinas's religio meant piety).
- §1.3 presents Barbour's (2000) four-way taxonomy — conflict, independence, dialogue, integration — as the most influential, while reporting historians' objections (Cantor and Kenny 2001) and that the conflict thesis did not in fact originate with Draper and White.
- §1.4 covers the scientific study of religion from early-modern natural histories (Fontenelle 1724) onward.
- §2 argues that encounters with non-Christian traditions (e.g. Hindu conceptions of God) differ from the Christian case.
- §3 covers divine action and creation ex nihilo, and human origins in the Abrahamic traditions; the close recommends "science-engaged theology" (Perry and Ritchie 2018).
