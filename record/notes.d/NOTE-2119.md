# NOTE-2119: Springer and others 2025 — Overtrained Language Models Are Harder to Fine-Tune

## Contribution

Springer, Goyal, Wen, Kumar, Yue, Malladi, Neubig and Raghunathan name "catastrophic overtraining": past an inflection point in the pre-training token budget, the model after post-training gets worse even though the base model keeps improving. They show it on public intermediate checkpoints (OLMo-1B, with OLMo-2-7B and LLM360-Amber in the appendix) under instruction tuning and multimodal tuning, reproduce it in a controlled family of small OLMo models pre-trained from scratch to 4B-128B tokens (each annealed to zero, removing the single-run schedule confound), and give a deep-linear-network account. Their mechanism is "progressive sensitivity": for a modification of fixed size (Gaussian noise or fine-tuning at a fixed learning rate), the loss of pre-training performance grows with pre-training length. The phenomenon they measure is mainly forgetting caused by the modification, not slower acquisition of the new task.

## Key results

- Base models keep improving while post-trained ones get worse (§2.2, p. 3): "Extended pre-training always improves base models." and "a base model pre-trained on 3T tokens shows up to 3% lower response rate (AlpacaEval score) than one pre-trained on just 2.3T tokens".
- The mechanism is forgetting that grows with pre-training (§1, p. 2): "for modifications of equal magnitude, models that have undergone longer pre-training exhibit greater forgetting".
- Progressive sensitivity under fine-tuning (§3.4.1, p. 6): "For a fixed learning rate, the change in perplexity increases monotonically with the number of pre-training tokens."
- With the learning rate tuned for the new task, the new task itself sometimes gets worse, and the old one more often (Fig. 6 caption, p. 7): "ID perplexity degrades with extensive overtraining (RTE, TREC); C4 perplexity degrades in GSM8k, Starcoder-Python, MR, and RTE."
- Protecting the old capability costs the new one (§3.4.2, p. 7): choosing the smallest learning rate "would delay the inflection point. However, this would also result in a lower ID performance."
- Theory (§4, Theorem 4.7, p. 10): "Catastrophic overtraining is inevitable without regularization." and "Regularization can delay the degradation of pretraining performance at the cost of downstream performance."
- Scale (§3.1, p. 4): "catastrophic overtraining is not observed on OLMo-7B models for pre-training token budgets up to 3T tokens".
- The single-run confound, named and removed in the controlled study (§3.2, p. 4): "each pre-training budget corresponded to a different final learning rate due to the annealing schedule".

## Assumptions

- That C4 perplexity after fine-tuning stands for the pre-trained knowledge kept, and the fine-tuning task's held-out perplexity for what was acquired.
- That a fixed learning rate makes modifications of comparable size across checkpoints. The authors say "we do not provide a formal justification".
- In the theory, a two-layer linear network that learns the singular values of a linear map one by one (Saxe et al.), with the fine-tuning task sharing singular vectors and differing in spectrum ("(α, r)-misaligned").
- That the controlled models (15M-90M, cosine schedule annealed to zero) behave like large models with released checkpoints.

## Concepts

- Catastrophic overtraining: post-training performance falling as pre-training is extended. The inflection point is the token budget where it begins.
- Progressive sensitivity: at a fixed size of parameter change, the damage to pre-training loss grows with pre-training length.
- The trade at fine-tuning time: the optimal learning rate's trend with pre-training length decides whether degradation shows up on the new task, the old one, both or neither (Fig. 7's three scenarios).

## Bearing on the record

- [CLAIM-758](../claims.d/CLAIM-758.md): qualifies. This is the claim's prime case, and it shows present competence rising (the base model) while what training leaves after a later acquisition falls. But the authors' mechanism is forgetting caused by the acquisition ("greater forgetting"), which the claim's Not-forgetting bullet sets aside. It is neither spontaneous forgetting nor a higher budget to acquire the new task. It is a third thing: the price of an acquisition, paid in held capability, rises with training. Only some results show the new task itself acquired worse (RTE, TREC at a tuned learning rate, and AlpacaEval on OLMo-1B). The claim's defeated_if first clause is not met, but the case supports the claim only if the cost of acquiring includes collateral loss of held capabilities. The claim should say so or cite this case more narrowly.
- [CLAIM-757](../claims.d/CLAIM-757.md): leaves open, with one caution. No intervention restores plasticity late: the only mitigations are fine-tuning-time choices (a smaller learning rate, regularization toward the pre-trained weights), which trade the new task against the old. The paper names replay, LP-FT and WiSE-FT as untested. Its inflection point is a point in training time after which outcomes worsen, and it is explained by forgetting. That is the kind of explanation the claim's last defeater names.
- [CASE-294](../cases.d/CASE-294.md): qualifies the design. The paper measures the final performance after a tuned fine-tune, not the least budget to reach a threshold (A_s), so it does not supply the case's primary estimand. It does support the case's two-axis scoring (the new task gained and the old lost), since its effects show up mostly on the old axis. It also names the single-run annealing confound that the case already concedes for Pythia, and removes it by annealing each budget to zero.
- [THEORY-1353](../theory.d/THEORY-1353.md): qualifies. The paper offers a rival mechanism for training making later change costly: features learned incrementally make a fixed-size update destroy more, as opposed to Dong's depletion of uncommitted heads. The two need not conflict, but here more pre-training raises sensitivity, not scarcity of sites.
- [CLAIM-675](../claims.d/CLAIM-675.md): supports in the training domain. More training gives a better model now and a worse one after a later step, so the two cannot be ranked by present capability.
- [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md): supports its point that trading one demand for another is not relief. Lowering the learning rate protects the old capability by giving up some of the new (§3.4.2; Theorem 4.7), which in [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)'s terms changes which demand is met rather than enlarging what can be met jointly.

## Limitations

- Real-model evidence is three model families with one run each. The 3T-against-2.3T contrast on OLMo-1B is a few points. At 7B the effect is not seen up to 3T tokens.
- The controlled study uses models of 90M parameters or fewer and mostly perplexity, not accuracy.
- Acquisition cost in budget terms (steps or examples to reach a level) is not measured.
- The theory is a linear model with a shared-singular-vector task. The proofs were not checked in this reading.
- Sensitivity is shown to correlate with sharpness only in an appendix that was not read.
