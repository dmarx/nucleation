# THEORY-tmp5tsat: In Sheldon, Elliot, Kim and Kasser's three studies of most satisfying events, self-esteem was as salient as autonomy, competence and relatedness in every sample and the largest unique predictor of event affect balance among ten candidate needs in two of the three US samples (β = .28, .38); the authors, SDT researchers, concluded that there may be four fundamental needs and judged it imprudent to treat self-esteem as an outcome

## Source

Sheldon, Elliot, Kim and Kasser (2001), LIT-tmpd3l9z, pp. 328–337; read in
NOTE-tmp3xmvo.

## What was actually shown

**Salience.** Self-esteem was first or tied first in mean salience in the
three US samples (4.08, 3.65, 3.97) and second in South Korea (3.23, after
relatedness 3.65) (Tables 2, 4, 6).

**Unique prediction of affect balance, all ten candidates entered**:
- Study 1 (last month, N = 315): self-esteem .28, autonomy .17, competence
  .12, relatedness .12, money–luxury −.17.
- Study 2 US (last week, N = 152): autonomy .29, competence .25,
  relatedness .21; self-esteem not significant.
- Study 2 Korea (N = 200): autonomy .26, self-esteem .23, security .25,
  relatedness .20, money–luxury −.22; competence .10, ns.
- Study 3 (last semester, N = 233): self-esteem .38, autonomy .17,
  relatedness .12 (marginal), competence .00, money–luxury −.14.

Self-esteem could have been salient but redundant with the three SDT needs
once they were controlled; in Studies 1 and 3 it was the largest unique
predictor.

**The authors' reading** (p. 336): SDT "does not have a way to account for
these results, except perhaps to consider self-esteem as a well-being
outcome rather than a predictor", which "might be imprudent"; "there may be
four fundamental psychological needs, not three: autonomy, competence,
relatedness, and self-esteem."

## What this does not say

- **That self-esteem is a basic need.** Both criteria are self-reports
  about one recalled event, and the self-esteem items ("Quite satisfied with
  who I am") are close to the affect they predict.
- **That SDT's later reply is wrong.** The data cannot distinguish a need
  from an outcome of the other needs; the authors say so.
- **That competence is unimportant.** It carried the unique variance in
  unsatisfying events (Study 3), which the authors read as a deficiency
  role.
- **Anything beyond students in two countries.**
