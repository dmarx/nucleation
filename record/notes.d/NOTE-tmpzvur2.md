# NOTE-tmpzvur2: Reisinger and Cederroth (2026) — FDA Approval of the Congenital OTOF Gene Therapy Otarmeni Reshapes the Future of Hearing Loss Treatment

## Contribution

Comments on the FDA's approval (announced 23 April 2026) of Regeneron's Otarmeni, the first gene therapy for a genetic hearing loss (DFNB9, OTOF), and on convergent results from other programmes. Because the therapy is gene-specific and time-sensitive, infants with bilateral hearing loss need genetic diagnosis; clinical sequences should change so that children with profound loss and intact otoacoustic emissions get a genetic diagnosis before a cochlear implant. Outcomes are incomplete: a quarter of the CHORD trial children reached normal pure-tone sensitivity; others average 40–50 dB HL, possibly from incomplete transduction and unrestorable synapse loss; speech-in-noise, listening effort and fatigue need assessment. It calls for equitable access (Regeneron's free US provision; uncertain in Europe), a European rare-disorders network and registry with core outcomes, and attention to the Deaf community.

## Key results

1. "the joint indication of a gene-specific and time-sensitive therapy makes genetic diagnosis of infants with bilateral hearing loss a necessity."
2. "children diagnosed with profound hearing loss but intact otoacoustic emissions should first receive a genetic diagnosis before receiving a cochlear implant."
3. "One-fourth of the CHORD children reached normal hearing sensitivity on pure-tone audiometry; however, normal pure-tone sensitivity does not necessarily equate to fully normal hearing in complex listening environments."
4. "from an ethics point of view, it will be critical to progress in the foreground of the Deaf community, being attentive to their perspectives and expectations, and support families that choose or decline gene therapy."
5. Competing interests: "E.R. is co-inventor on a patent application for dual-AAV vectors to restore hearing."

## Assumptions

Hearing restoration as the clinical goal; reported trial results (Regeneron, NEJM; Shu's team, Nature) as given; equity framed as access to diagnosis and therapy.

## Concepts

DFNB9 / otoferlin; dual-AAV gene therapy; time-sensitive window; genetic diagnosis before cochlear implantation; pure-tone vs speech-in-noise outcomes; synaptopathy; access and equity; core outcome measures; Deaf community perspectives.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-tmp2lvhi**: slight. The editorial treats the decision as the family's ("families that choose or decline gene therapy") and makes the clinical pathway time-pressed (diagnose before implanting), which narrows the window in which parents decide for an infant; it names the Deaf community as a party whose perspective should be in the foreground, which bears on whether the child's "welfare" is a neutral ground. No argument about legitimacy of surrogate authority.

## Limitations

An editorial; claims about outcomes are reported from others' trials; one ethics sentence; first author has a declared patent interest.
