# CLAIM-184: Anchors entrench: a structure that persists early can lock in its framing, so that later structure grows on it and alternatives become hard to reach, and retirement by status is the record's remedy only if superseded anchors stop attracting new structure

## The claim

If persistent structures are what more complex structure grows on
([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)), the first structures to persist get built on first. An
early framing, given a code, gathers claims that rest on it, objections to
it and terms defined against it. Replacing it later means re-anchoring all
of that.

The physics read for this line has the extreme case. Dunitz & Bernstein
([LIT-917](../literature.d/LIT-917.md), unread) are cited for crystal forms that could no longer be
made once a more stable form had appeared, because seeds of the new form
were everywhere. Ostwald ([LIT-920](../literature.d/LIT-920.md), [NOTE-709](../notes.d/NOTE-709.md)) shows the mechanism
at the bench: once a room has been used for alum, a supersaturated drop
cannot be kept free of seeds, and a single stray seed revives a sterilized
batch (pp. 291, 324). Luria names the same danger on the record side. It is
named for a mnemonist whose total recall crowded out meaning ([LU-ADR-011](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-011.md)).

The record's remedy is retirement by status. A Superseded claim stays,
with its successor named, so that what pointed at it still resolves, and a
reader is sent on. That works only if new structure stops growing on the
retired anchor.

## What would answer it

Two findings would. The first: Superseded entries draw almost no new
citations after retirement, other than citations of them as history, so
supersession does turn growth off. The second: a framing replaced
late in an argument costs no more to replace than one replaced early.

The check can be run on this record now. Count the citations of each
Superseded CLAIM and TERM made after its retirement date, and separate
those acknowledged as history (`inactive-ok`) from the rest.

## Note of 2026-10-11 (later): constancy against resilience

[THEORY-132](../theory.d/THEORY-132.md) (Holling) is added as a ground. Managing a system for constancy
can erode its resilience, its capacity to absorb change and persist within
its domain. That is a stronger form of this counter. A record that enforces
identity strictly maximizes constancy, and may reduce the argument's
capacity to reframe. [CLAIM-195](CLAIM-195.md) draws the same point from the
constraint-set side.

## Note of 2026-10-12: the bar for lock-in

The will-organization line's [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) (protective lock-in) sets the bar
this counter has to clear: a regime that "generates the conditions that
justify its own continuation and reduces the capacity to leave it". That
needs a loop, and [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) excludes inertia and habit. Path dependence
alone, Ostwald's ubiquitous seeds, does not qualify. [THEORY-178](../theory.d/THEORY-178.md) adds that,
in the one regulatory network studied, a lasting state needs a positive
circuit. [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md) says what would show one: hysteresis or a lasting shift
after a temporary push. This counter has not shown a loop. A candidate
would be that forcing moves into the grammar miscodes them, the miscodes
produce correction entries, and those entries feed more filing. That is
unshown.

## Note of 2026-10-31: the dense field, in organization studies


Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), pp. 1356–1357) state this
counter's worry as a property of fields. Each being-in-becoming is a
"field of relation, more or less densely assembling the preindividual
action it has integrated thus far". Dense fields, "crystals or well-oiled
bureaucracies", "cannot be reorganized easily when they meet other
fields". Loose ones, "water or a project team", take up what they meet.
The paper offers no measure of density and no account of how a field
becomes dense, so it adds a name for lock-in and not a mechanism. It does
place lock-in where this claim does: in what has accumulated, not in any
single anchor. The crystal that is the paper's dense field is also the
record's seed ([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md), note of this date). No status, title or warrant
changes.

## Note of 2026-11-01: lock-in as generative entrenchment
<!-- inactive-ok-block: LIT-188 LIT-1926 — Deferred, unread; Proposed: read in full; cited from the re-reading of LIT-188, not leaned on -->

The mechanism this claim names has a standard source the record now
holds as a registration: Wimsatt's generative entrenchment
([LIT-1926](../literature.d/LIT-1926.md), unread), as Griesemer and Shavit quote it ([LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md), §2;
[NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md)). An entrenched element is one "that later-evolved interactions
will depend on for their existence and character, so that perturbations
of it will tend to be catastrophic to the whole system while changes in
the later interactions tend to lead to tolerable system changes". That is
this claim's "replacing it later means re-anchoring all of that", with
the asymmetry stated. The paper also calls the outcome "highly
contingent, even if contingently irreversible" (§2): nothing makes a
given anchor entrench, but once it has, the change is unlikely to be
undone. The record's remedy, retirement by status, works against that
asymmetry only if superseded anchors stop gaining dependents, which is
this claim's own test. Same structure, not shown same mechanism.

No status, title or warrant changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the constraint-architecture readings

Two readings from the pasted synthesis on constraint architectures support the claim by physical analogy. In DeLuca et al.'s simulated DNA origami ([NOTE-1561](../notes.d/NOTE-1561.md)), early coalescence that sets the scaffold wrongly locks the structure out of the folded state: one run, the best folded at 1 s, is second worst by 3.6 s. In Stern et al. ([NOTE-1571](../notes.d/NOTE-1571.md)) folding islands are local seeds that fix the global outcome, and with no backtracking at zero temperature a badly placed seed locks in a distractor branch. Both are physical lock-in, not the entrenchment of a framing, and neither bears on retirement by status. Neither is thermodynamic nucleation. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the DNA origami readings

Read on the owner's request to connect the record's realizability inquiry to DNA origami; the works were chosen by the assistant.

**Sobczak, "Investigations of the DNA origami folding process".** Sobczak's dissertation (2018), [LIT-2138](../literature.d/LIT-2138.md), read in part in [NOTE-1644](../notes.d/NOTE-1644.md), supports the claim by
physical analogy: in a short-domain design, cooling slowly through temperatures "low enough for some
random match binding to occur but too high for the correct domains to form" leaves a sample that
"does not show any folding whatsoever", while shock-freezing through them lets it fold. Early
spurious bindings entrench. It is a mechanism of molecules, not of framings.

**Majikes et al., "Revealing thermodynamics of DNA origami folding via affine transformations".** Majikes et al. (2020), [LIT-2117](../literature.d/LIT-2117.md), infer, untested, that a staple's longest domain binds first and that the short folds it enables "further reduce the loop entropy for nearby staples and the system assembles progressively". That is early structure lowering the cost of later nearby growth, and weak support for the claim's first half. The paper shows neither lock-in nor blocked alternatives, and the authors say confirming the order "will require additional testing in systems with multiple folds".

**three DNA origami folding readings (Dunn et al. 2015; Dannenberg et al. 2015; Schneider, Möritz and Dietz 2019).** Dunn and colleagues ([NOTE-1643](../notes.d/NOTE-1643.md)) support it in a physical system: "the early formation of
long-range connections can be very effective in forcing particular folds", and strong early
seams trap folding. Dannenberg and colleagues ([NOTE-1645](../notes.d/NOTE-1645.md)) qualify it: bound staples help
their neighbours bind, but the effect is local and reversible at folding temperatures, and nothing
wrong can entrench in their model. Schneider, Möritz and Dietz ([NOTE-1641](../notes.d/NOTE-1641.md)) qualify it again:
early domains are needed for the fast path and their loss reroutes folding, but no wrong early
anchor locks in a misfold. Which designs entrench and which merely accelerate is left open. No
status changes.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the further DNA origami readings

The works cited in A's two syntheses on DNA origami, pasted by the owner on 2026-11-05, read in this pull request.

**Lee Tin Wah et al., "Observing and controlling the folding pathway of DNA origami".** Lee Tin Wah et al. (2016), [LIT-2137](../literature.d/LIT-2137.md), qualify the claim. Early nuclei
at well-defined positions do set which way the rectangle folds, supporting
that early structure frames later growth; but by the authors' equilibrium
account the outcome is not locked in (a quench to 57 °C still yields
well-formed rectangles, and the finished rectangle is the same at 40 °C),
which cuts against entrenchment as inevitability.

**Majikes, Hasni, Haridas, Robertson, Pintar, Zwolak and Liddle, "Complex cooperativity in DNA origami revealed via design-dependent defectivity".** Majikes et al. (2026), [LIT-2127](../literature.d/LIT-2127.md), give weak, indirect support. A design with many short early folds, and so many nucleation points but few routes to the long folds, was more defective. The authors read this as "additional kinetic traps for the topologically necessary long folds". This is local entrenchment of early structure. It is not lock-in of a framing, and no pathway was observed.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-06: what holds it back

The count under "What would answer it" was run on 2026-11-06 by scratch/formal/claim184_185_counts.py, which has a control. It found 50 Superseded CLAIMs and TERMs. Of the citations of them in other entries, 897 are dated on or before retirement and 71 after it, in 27 codes. Of those 71, 54 carry an inactive-ok acknowledgement and 17 do not. All 17 unacknowledged ones read as history: a successor naming what it revises, chains of supersession, history notes recording a move, inline "Superseded" glosses, and one converse edge to the retired claim's own premise. A sample of the acknowledged ones reads the same way. So on this record superseded anchors stop attracting new structure. That answers the clause "retirement by status is the record's remedy only if superseded anchors stop attracting new structure" in favour of retirement by status. Three caveats. Citations are dated without git history: body text at the latest dated note above it, or else at the entry's filing date, so a later edit to older text is missed. 46 of the 50 retirement dates are the successor's filing date, used as a proxy. And an acknowledgement is not proof of a history use, which is why the unacknowledged ones were each read. What still holds the claim: its first half, that an early anchor locks in its framing so that alternatives become hard to reach, has no result here, and the loop [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) requires (note of 2026-10-12) is still not shown.
