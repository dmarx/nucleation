# CLAIM-1345: Distributed pathologies map onto organizations

## The claim

A16 §5, organizational analogues:

| Pathology | A16's analogue |
|---|---|
| Race condition | "Outcome depends on inadequately governed ordering of competing activities." |
| Deadlock | "Processes mutually prevent one another from satisfying their commitments." |
| Livelock | "Processes remain active and responsive but make no relevant progress." |
| Starvation | "A process persistently fails to receive resources needed to satisfy its demands." |
| Priority inversion | "Dependency relationships defeat the prevailing allocation of precedence." |
| Thrashing | "Repeated switching consumes resources needed for substantive activity." |
| Cascading failure | "Local breaches propagate through interdependent processes." |
| Stale state | "Activity is governed by representations no longer adequate to circumstances." |
| Split brain | "Distinct governance regimes exercise incompatible authority over shared state." |
| Backpressure failure | "Upstream activity exceeds downstream capacity to accommodate it." |
| Retry storm | "Individually corrective responses collectively intensify the original failure." |
| Silent failure | "Violations occur without becoming legible to corrective processes." |

A18 §6, the same for inquiry, among them deadlock ("Evidence requires
permission; permission requires evidence"), starvation ("Some classes of
questions never receive investigative resources"), race ("An irreversible
response occurs before relevant evaluation completes"), thrashing
("Continual changes of question or acceptance criteria prevent sustained
investigation"), silent failure ("A consequential discrepancy never
becomes an actionable question") and retry storm ("Repeated demands for
justification consume the resources required to produce justification").

The point of the correspondence is the remedy: "adding computational
resources may not resolve epistemic deadlock. Increasing the priority of
an inquiry may not resolve priority inversion. Producing more information
may not resolve incompatible evaluation criteria. Different organizational
failures demand different interventions." A16 names tests: [CLAIM-451](CLAIM-451.md)'s
account of dissonance "could be investigated using systems exhibiting
livelock or costly governance oscillation", frustration through deadlock
and resource-allocation models, agnotology through "failures of
observability, breach detection and error propagation".

## Where the record stands

Several rows are held under other names: epistemic deadlock is
[CLAIM-1140](CLAIM-1140.md)'s circle between evidence and authorization; priority
inversion is [CASE-427](../cases.d/CASE-427.md); silent failure is the undetected route of
[CLAIM-1003](CLAIM-1003.md); [CLAIM-556](CLAIM-556.md) already orders repairs by level, each with its
own. What the claim adds is the catalogue as a source of independently
specified mechanisms, and the warning that the remedies do not transfer
across rows.

## What it does not say

A18: "They need not imply that every corresponding psychological or
institutional phenomenon is literally identical to its computational
analogue." The claim is that the patterns are candidates to be tested, not
that an organization's failure is a deadlock because it resembles one.
