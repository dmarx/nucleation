# THEORY-tmp31kx6: Hancock and colleagues define metastability as a property of a dynamical system, not of data: an attractor landscape with regions that attract in some directions and repel in others, near which trajectories dwell and escape without noise, as against multistability, where noise drives switches between stable states; they find symmetry breaking a common enabler across its routes, from the ghost of a fixed point to cycles of saddles and chaotic itinerancy, and propose that dwell times, exponential for noise-driven multistability, may tell the two apart

## Source

Hancock and colleagues (2024), "Metastability demystified — the
foundational past, the pragmatic present and the promising future",
*Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 26(2): 82–100, [LIT-2266](../literature.d/LIT-2266.md), skimmed from the
authors' accepted manuscript in [NOTE-tmp3om59](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp3om59.md).

## The account

Monostability, bistability and multistability have attractors that trap
trajectories; switching among them needs noise. Metastability is
"unstable attraction": saddles, or the memory of a fixed point after a
saddle-node, slow the system, which then leaves without noise. Routes:
the ghost of a fixed point in Kelso's bimanual model, cycles of saddles
from delays or heterogeneous coupling, winnerless competition under
asymmetric stimuli, chimeras in phase-lagged community models, chaotic
itinerancy among Milnor attractors; symmetry breaking enables each. The
empirical signatures (such as the variance of the Kuramoto order
parameter) are heuristics, not definitions. Dwell times may separate the
cases: exponential for noise-driven multistability under standard
assumptions, gamma-like in metastable models.

## Bearing

[QUESTION-281](../questions.d/QUESTION-281.md): two of its transitions, escape over a barrier by noise and
