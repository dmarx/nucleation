# LIT-tmpjtbws: Why Replication Is Overrated

Uljana Feest (2019), *Philosophy of Science, 86(5), 895–905 (December 2019)* — DOI-10.1086/705451

## Key takeaways

- All experimentation and replication rests on individuation judgments (what counts as the same effect, manipulation, measure), and much of experimental psychology consists in probing those judgments — so direct replication has a place but is less central than the replication-crisis debate assumes.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Debates about psychology's replication crisis assume that direct replication is valuable, mostly because it exposes questionable statistical practices. Feest looks more broadly at what replication is in psychological experiments. She argues that every experiment and replication involves individuation judgments, and that experimental psychology often turns on testing whether those judgments are adequate. Conceptual and material questions are therefore ubiquitous in research, and replication, though it has its place, is less central to psychology than is often supposed.
