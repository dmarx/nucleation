## Alternatives, for the owner

- **A (the run's lean), [CLAIM-1017](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-1017.md).** The Syrian "germline" group is
  separated from none of the other routes. Its mothers were exposed in
  utero and raised by the exposed grandmothers, in the exposed community.
- **B, [CLAIM-1103](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-1103.md) and [ARG-229](../../record/arguments.d/ARG-229.md).** It is a partial separation, from
  care by a parent who lived through the violence, which no other design
  removes.

They differ on whether the middle generation's prenatal exposure and
upbringing count as routes that reach the grandchild. B is the reading
the paper's authors invite; A is the run's reading of what its design
supports.

## Offered to the owner, not filed

From reading across the five works, the run suggests a design that would
meet [CLAIM-644](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-644.md)'s `defeated_if` and [CLAIM-1017](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-1017.md)'s. Take the children of
one sperm donor whose donations span his exposure to a trauma, conceived
from donations made before and after it, carried and raised in unrelated,
unexposed families. That would hold the gamete's DNA fixed in expectation,
remove the womb and rearing, and vary only the exposure. It is a
hypothesis about feasibility, not a found study. It would cost a search of
donor-registry and military sperm-banking literature. It could go in as a
LEAD at stage `idea` if the owner accepts it.
