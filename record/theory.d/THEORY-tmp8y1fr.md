# THEORY-tmp8y1fr: Implementation intentions affect performance only when the goal intention they serve is strong and activated, so the cue-triggered response is gated by the goal and does not fire on the cue alone: a narrative conclusion from a handful of studies in 2006, matched in 2024 by large plan effects under high motivation (d = .79, k = 8) and none for physical activity when intentions were weak

## Source

Gollwitzer & Sheeran, LIT-tmpt9ye6 (NOTE-tmp61nj5), pp. 103–104; Sheeran,
Listrom & Gollwitzer, LIT-tmpks817 (NOTE-tmpauwdm), Table 2 and p. 177.

## What was actually shown

- **2006, narratively (p. 103).** Studies in which goal intentions were
  measured (Koestner et al. 2002b; Orbell et al. 1997; Sheeran et al.
  2005c) found strong plan effects only with strong goal intentions;
  studies that primed or deactivated the goal (Bayer et al. 2004; Sheeran
  et al. 2005c, Study 2; Seehausen et al. 1994, cited through Gollwitzer
  1996) found plans affected performance only with the goal active. "Thus,
  action control by implementation intentions does not involve a
  mechanistic elicitation of action in the presence of environmental cues
  but rather respects the presence versus absence of activated strong goal
  intentions" (p. 103).
- **2024, across tests.** Plan effects were larger when the goal intention
  was strong, activated or self-concordant than when it was not (d = .79,
  k = 8; Table 2). Among 31 physical-activity tests split at the median of
  reported intention, planning helped with strong intentions (d = .31) and
  "had no effect when intentions were weak" (p. 177).

What could have come out otherwise: plans could have produced the
response on the cue whatever the state of the goal, which is what a cached
cue-to-action link with no gate would do.

## What this does not say

- **That plans adapt when the planned response is wrong.** The 2006
  authors say of a plan that fails or harms: "We do not know yet what
  happens" (p. 104). Gating by the goal is not revision of the mapping.
- **That the gate survives repetition.** Repeated acting on a plan
  produced reported habit (Orbell & Verplanken 2005, cited p. 108); a habit
  may fire without the goal. Not tested here.
- **That the evidence is pooled.** The 2006 conclusion is narrative; the
  2024 moderator is between tests, k = 8.

## Connections

- **CLAIM-tmp0qupq.** The claim contrasts a cached cue-to-action policy
  with a learned relation by which one process recruits another that can
  choose. This finding is a third arrangement between them: a cached
  mapping licensed by a higher-level state, the goal. It is closer to the
  claim's prior over actions, since the goal decides whether, not what.
  The claim's test, changing which action is appropriate, is the case the
  authors left open.
- **CLAIM-349.** The goal keeps authority over whether the planned
  response is allowed to act; the plan's own authority is conditional.
