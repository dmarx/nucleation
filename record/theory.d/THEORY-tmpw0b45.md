# THEORY-tmpw0b45: Araki, Berejikian, Ford and Blouin find hatchery salmonids generally less fit in the wild than wild fish, worst for nonlocal segregated stocks (lifetime relative fitness 0.02–0.37), and show by a quantitative-genetic model that domestication selection on a single trait can explain a decline of more than 30% per hatchery generation only when selection is strong in both captivity and the wild and heritability is high, while arguing it plausible when selection acts on several traits across the life cycle

## Source

Araki, Berejikian, Ford and Blouin (2008), LIT-tmpzkw55; read in
NOTE-tmpkfeb0, from the Europe PMC full text of PMC3352433.

## What was actually shown

- **The review** (Table 1). Fourteen studies of hatchery against wild
  fish breeding in the same habitat. RF "generally lower than one".
  Nonlocal segregated steelhead: "Lifetime RF = 0.02–0.37". Local stocks
  "generally perform better than nonlocal stocks, but often worse than wild
  fish" (Abstract). Two old local stocks matched wild fish where the
  'wild' fish were largely hatchery-descended.
- **The Hood River contrast** (Table 1; Integrated broodstocks of local
  origin). Two hatchery generations against one, both spawned, reared and
  released alike: "(m) 0.55 (f) 0.55", read as genetic and cumulative.
- **The model** (Selection – single-trait model; Figs 2–3). Truncation
  selection in the hatchery, Gaussian stabilizing selection in the wild,
  the breeder's equation: "all three conditions are required", and "low
  levels of truncation (LT ≤ 0.5) cannot explain the >30% fitness decline,
  and neither can ω2 > 5 or h2 ≤ 0.2". Hatchery mortality of "only 5–15%"
  is too little for viability selection in the hatchery alone. This could
  have come out the other way: a model in which modest single-trait
  selection sufficed.
- **The ranking of mechanisms** (Mechanisms of fitness decline). Mutation
  accumulation too slow, inbreeding would need to be "unrealistically
  high", domestication selection "The last and most likely explanation".
  Argued from rates in the literature, not measured.
- **Several traits.** Argued plausible from candidate traits (growth,
  aggression, predator avoidance, mate selection), not computed.

## What this does not say

- That domestication selection was shown to cause the Hood River decline:
  "no studies have empirically examined the mechanism"; Christie et al.
  (2012) test it.
- That the per-generation loss is 30% or 40%: the paper's own wording
  varies, and the Science paper (LIT-tmpcmwya) was not read.
- That local wild broodstock prevents the loss: it is "mitigated to some
  extent" with "little evidence" it can be avoided.
- That supplementation fails to raise numbers: some programmes raised the
  number of adults spawning in the wild; what is undocumented is a rise in
  wild productivity.
- Anything outside salmonids, and mostly outside steelhead.
