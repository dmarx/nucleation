# THEORY-tmpjqe6l: Campbell and colleagues report that in every periodical cicada species Hodgkinia exists as a complex of at least twenty circular molecules, with an order of magnitude more DNA than the ancestral genome and most of its genes, little synteny between species and gene dosages that differ between species groups, so that the splitting took a different course in each

## Source

Campbell et al. (2017), *Current Biology* 27: 3568–3575, [LIT-1602](../literature.d/LIT-1602.md), read
in [NOTE-tmpzfvzz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzfvzz.md).

## What was actually shown

Metagenomes from six *Magicicada* species not sequenced before and two
Australian relatives, with *M. tredecim* from earlier work. At least 20
closed circles per *Magicicada* species, 1.20 to 1.68 Mb per complex,
135 to 145 of 186 ancestral functional genes, little synteny among 256
circles except between the closest species, and relative gene
abundances grouped by species group. A convergent dosage pattern across
species would have pointed to host direction or a benefit; none was
found.

## What this does not say

- That circles are cells: the authors say they do not know for most.
- That the host plays no role: they think it likely "heavily involved"
  in sharing products between cells.
- That the complexes lost function: the combined gene set seems to keep
  the nutritional contribution.
