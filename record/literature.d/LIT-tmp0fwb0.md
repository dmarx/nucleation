# LIT-tmp0fwb0: Towards a Theory of Collective Emotions

Christian von Scheve and Sven Ismer (2013), *Emotion Review* 5(4):406–413 — DOI-10.1177/1754073913484170

## Key takeaways

- **Collective emotion is convergence, not a group's state.** The authors
  define it as synchronous convergence in affective responding across
  individuals towards one event or object. The minimal case needs no
  awareness of one another: drivers in one traffic jam, with the same goal
  and the same beliefs, are angry together.
- **Two processes amplify the baseline and change its quality.** One is mutual
  awareness, through contagion in co-presence or through mediated sharing.
  The other is membership in a collective, through shared appraisals, social
  identity, collective intentions, norms, collective memory and group-based
  sentiments.
- **I-mode and We-mode.** Shared private concerns give I-mode collective
  emotion. Appraisals grounded in collectively intentional states, held as a
  member, give We-mode collective emotion, which the authors expect to differ
  in quality. Contagion in a crowd can create the identity that makes the
  We-mode possible. They offer this as a hypothesis, illustrated by protests.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-04 at the owner's request, with four works on emotion in
groups and organisations, to connect the record's emotion holdings to its
social-ontology holdings. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice.

In this batch it is the theoretical frame the other works fit into.

- **Barsade ([LIT-tmpojrfm](LIT-tmpojrfm.md))** is a causal test of the face-to-face
  mechanism. She manipulated a confederate's mood in work groups and
  measured contagion and its effect on cooperation and conflict. Barsade's
  Study 2 measures group contagion as convergence, the ICC of members' mood
  rising from .00 to .24. That is this paper's definition, operationalised a
  decade earlier. The two are compatible, not rival. This paper treats
  contagion as one mechanism among several, and Barsade tests only that one.
  This paper does not cite it. It cites Barsade & Gibson (1998) and Kelly &
  Barsade (2001) instead.
- **Sanchez-Burks & Huy ([LIT-tmp9q3ir](LIT-tmp9q3ir.md)), unread**, by their abstract, give
  the perceiver's side. Emotional aperture is the ability to recognise the
  *composition* of diverse emotions in a collective. That presupposes what
  this paper's definition allows: a collective's emotion can be a
  distribution, partly converged, rather than one shared state.

On the record's emotion theories. The paper presupposes appraisal-caused
emotion, extended socially (Lazarus & Smith 1988, Parkinson, Manstead &
Fischer). That is the tradition of Lazarus ([LIT-550](LIT-550.md)) and of Frijda
([LIT-540](LIT-540.md)). Frijda's 1994 account of sentiments as appraisal dispositions is
what the paper uses for group-based sentiments. Barrett ([LIT-542](LIT-542.md)) denies that
appraisals cause emotions. On her view convergence would come from shared
emotion concepts. That lies closer to this paper's "culture and shared
knowledge" strand, and the paper does not consider it.

On social ontology. The paper uses the collective-intentionality literature
to separate aggregate from non-aggregate collective intention, citing
Gilbert's "Walking Together" ([LIT-477](LIT-477.md)), Bratman's "Shared Intention"
(1993; the record holds his 1992 "Shared Cooperative Activity", [LIT-471](LIT-471.md)),
Tuomela and Tomasello. It builds bottom-up "in the tradition of methodological
individualism" by choice, and does not argue that groups cannot be subjects
of emotion. It cites Huebner's "Genuinely collective emotions"
([LIT-tmpdb036](LIT-tmpdb036.md)), which argues they can, as one of the accounts relating
collective intention to collective emotion, and does not answer it. The
two are not declared rivals: the convergence definition does not exclude a
genuinely collective emotion held by a group. It leaves such states
outside its scope as a matter of method, and the We-mode collective
emotion is where the two would meet. Huebner's later book ([LIT-426](LIT-426.md)) is the
record's other holding. No relation is declared to any of these. The paper
uses them and does not extend, test or answer any of them.
