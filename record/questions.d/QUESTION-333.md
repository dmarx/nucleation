# QUESTION-333: Does how far fear falls within or across exposure or extinction sessions predict fear at follow-up once initial fear is controlled, in the studies Craske et al. 2014 cite or a later meta-analysis, and in well-powered clinical samples?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-921](../theory.d/THEORY-921.md) holds what Craske et al. ([LIT-1500](../literature.d/LIT-1500.md), read in [NOTE-1137](../notes.d/NOTE-1137.md)) report:
that fear reduction during extinction or exposure does not predict fear at
follow-up, in animals, human laboratory samples and fearful human samples.
The review reports no data of its own, its reference list was not read
here, and the cited exposure studies are of fearful, mostly analog,
samples (acrophobia, contamination fear, public speaking). Habituation
models hold that within-session fear reduction must predict outcome.

## What would answer it

[THEORY-921](../theory.d/THEORY-921.md)'s former promote_when, unchanged.

A reading of the studies the paper cites (Baker et al. 2010; Culver et
al. 2012; Kircanski et al. 2012; Prenoveau et al. 2013; Plendl &
Wolfgang 2010; Rescorla 2006), or of a later meta-analysis, finding
that within- and between-session fear reduction during exposure or
extinction does not predict fear at follow-up. It is refuted by a
well-powered clinical sample in which fear reduction during exposure
reliably predicts follow-up outcome after initial fear is controlled.

## Note of 2026-11-04: Gershman, Blei & Niv, "Context, learning, and extinction", read

Gershman, Blei & Niv (2010), [LIT-tmpdnjkj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdnjkj.md), read in [NOTE-tmpn30ni](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpn30ni.md), give a mechanism for a negative answer. In their latent-cause model, extinction trials are assigned to a new latent cause. The fall in responding during extinction is then learning about that new cause, and the conditioning cause's statistics are untouched; renewal follows when test trials look like they came from the old cause. On that account, within- or across-session decline need not predict fear at follow-up. The paper models no follow-up fear and no exposure therapy, so extending it to this question is the record's inference, not the authors'. It leaves open whether decline indexes anything about the old cause at all. No status changes.
