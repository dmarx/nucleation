# ADR-067: Works become documents, a manuscript's prose is anchored to the argument paragraph by paragraph, and a ledger audits it

## Context

[ADR-032](ADR-032.md) gave the record a `works` vocabulary: a claim says
which manuscript it is marshalled into, and membership is independent of
credence. By 2026-11-01 ten works had been registered. Everything else
about them was prose in a YAML blurb: when each was drafted, that four
essays were "later folded into" The Organization of Perspective, that
one was only an idea. Nothing could be Superseded, dated or nested. No
manuscript's prose was in the record. Claims pointed into it from their
summaries ("Section 6.2", "the paper's central thesis").

The record's own argument says what that costs. CLAIM-178 says a manuscript
should be a view over the argument and not its only copy. CLAIM-324 counted
the What Survives Translation? draft dropping 24 items and moving its thesis
with no turn arguing the move. CLAIM-189 and CLAIM-250 say that what can
be secured is traceability, not fidelity.

The exchange "Explain Nucleation Website" (2026-11-01) produced a
philosophy article, The Individual and Its Conditions, and asked for it to
be chunked into record objects assembled as a view. A first cut added
OUTLINE and SECTION schemes: sections of prose, each with a loose list of
entries it drew on. The owner was "not in love with" it. Read against the
other works, it had four faults:

- it inverted the record's convention, sections pointing at claims where
  claims point at manuscripts;
- a section was a chunked copy, with nothing tying a sentence to the claim
  it asserts, so it could still overstate or drop a claim unseen;
- it left works as a vocabulary, so the new outline restated what the
  vocabulary blurb held;
- it borrowed the credence vocabulary to mean "draft".

Of the options weighed with the owner, they chose three together: works as
documents, anchored prose, and an audit.

## Decision

**A. WORK is a scheme** (`record/works.d`), replacing the `works`
vocabulary.

- `status` is the credence vocabulary, read as *whether the work is
  live*: Active, or Superseded with `superseded_by` naming the work it was
  folded into or replaced by. `stage` is how far it has got: idea,
  outlined, drafted, submitted, published.
- `works:` on CLAIM, ARG, CASE and QUESTION is now a reference to WORK, with
  `members` as its converse. The facet pages that the vocabulary generated
  under `docs/claims/works/` go. The WORK page and the ledger take their
  place.
- A WORK's body holds what the blurb held, its abstract where it has one,
  and anything the editors keep beside it. Its `sections` field orders
  its SECTIONs, if its prose is held here.
- The migration: WORK-001 to WORK-010, numbered in the order the works were
  registered. WORK-006 to WORK-009, the four essays, are Superseded by
  WORK-005. Their membership on claims stays as history, so those claims
  cite a Superseded work. That is the warning the record wants: a
  reference to a retired document, made knowingly.

**C. A manuscript's prose is anchored.** A SECTION holds one section's
prose under "## Manuscript text". Only that part is transcluded. Each
paragraph ends with directives saying what it does with the argument:

- `<!-- ref::asserts: CLAIM-… -->` when the paragraph stakes the work on
  the claim or argument;
- `<!-- ref::discusses: … -->` when it uses an entry without staking the
  work on it: a case, a term, a question, a contrast, a limit.

`luria link --fix` pushes the directives up into the section's `asserts`
and `discusses` fields. `explain: cited` reports a code held there that no
paragraph cites. So frontmatter and prose cannot drift apart silently,
and the paragraph is the unit a reader can trace. A paragraph that claims
something the record holds no entry for is left unanchored and is named in
the section's "Revision notes". Those notes are candidates for filing.
SECTION `status` is Active for the current text, Superseded when replaced.

**E. A ledger, and patterns.** Each manuscript has two views, each a
two-line template over the macros in `record/views.d/manuscript.md.j2`:

- **the reading copy**, assembled from the WORK and its sections;
- **the ledger**, which shows for each section what it asserts and
  discusses with each entry's status and warrant, flags an entry revised
  after the section was, lists what the work stakes that the record does
  not hold Active, and lists members that no section uses.

Four patterns warn on the same drift across every work with sections:
`unplaced_in_work`, `asserted_outside_work`, `asserts_rejected` and
`asserts_superseded`. They are warnings. Each was fired on a sabotaged
copy before this landed (DP-4). None goes into `fail_on` until it has
been seen to hold at zero on the default branch.

## Alternatives

- **OUTLINE and SECTION beside the vocabulary**, the first cut. Rejected
  for the four faults above.
- **Assemble the manuscript from claims alone**, one claim per step and
  connective prose in the template. Truest to CLAIM-178, but a claim's
  title is not prose a reader can follow, and the connective text would
  still live outside the record.
- **Keep manuscripts out of the record** and formalize the "Section 6.2"
  pointers as a field on claims. This was the least machinery, and the
  fallback if prose in the record turns out not to earn its keep. It
  catches no drift, because there is no prose to compare against.
- **Anchors as visible bare codes in the prose.** They would link in the
  reading copy and clutter it. The directives are invisible when rendered,
  and the ledger shows the same facts better.

## Consequences

- A new manuscript needs a WORK, its SECTIONs and two `views:` entries,
  each pointing at a two-line template. A work whose prose lives elsewhere
  needs only the WORK.
- Moving a section is an edit to the WORK's `sections`. Revising one is an
  edit to the SECTION, and a revised paragraph should revise its
  directives too.
- The reading copy carries the directives as HTML comments. They are
  invisible on GitHub, but an export for submission must strip them.
- Staleness is by date: the ledger compares an entry's latest dated
  history with the section's. An entry revised without a history note
  is not flagged.
- The schemes are local. The anthology has nothing like them, and a work
  moving between the records does not carry them.
