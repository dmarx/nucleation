# CLAIM-890: Set beside the Northstar model, control theory's regulation and executive control give the control-only models, which miss the difference, so against them the will-organization variables are implemented and not replaced; planning theory, holding the plan fixed as the case does, gives no difference at all; institutional theory already writes the relations as rules-in-use, so for an organization the difference is one an existing theory gives, and what is left of the explanatory-gain claim is that one description serves across realizers, which a model of one realizer cannot test

## The claim

[CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md) names four fields that "already study every mechanism the
account names". [CLAIM-159](CLAIM-159.md) answered executive control and Ainslie's account
of resolve. [CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md)'s note of 2026-11-18 says "planning theory, control
theory's regulation and institutional theory are still not set beside the
account". [CASE-325](../cases.d/CASE-325.md)
gives a test each can be put to. What would each field's own model of the
construction predict for the combinations of changes in authority never
seen together?

- **Control theory's regulation gives the control-only models.**
  - In Ashby, a second feedback changes the primary feedback's parameters
    whenever an essential variable is outside its limits ([THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)).
    That is metacontrol. Its trigger is error in the regulated variable,
    and it is the same under Northstar-A and Northstar-B.
  - In Powers's hierarchy, two higher systems that demand contradictory
    settings of a shared lower system leave it at a "virtual
    reference-level" ([NOTE-959](../notes.d/NOTE-959.md), pp. 39–42). Which one prevails is a matter
    of gains, not of who holds a position.
  - To tell A from B, either theory must make the arrangement change a
    gain or a reference. That is [CASE-325](../cases.d/CASE-325.md)'s gates, and they miss the
    unseen combinations even with unlimited data.
  - The expected value of control ([THEORY-1110](../theory.d/THEORY-1110.md)) is a softmax choice of
    control by value less cost, which is gate C2.
  - So on [QUESTION-056](../questions.d/QUESTION-056.md)'s criterion these fields do not replace the
    account. A model in their variables that matches it has to add a
    variable for who holds which position and what follows from holding
    it. That variable is the relation: the mechanism then implements the
    will-organization, as [THEORY-048](../theory.d/THEORY-048.md) says lower-level findings do.
- **Planning theory gives no difference.**
  - [CASE-003](../cases.d/CASE-003.md) holds the plan fixed: "The concern, the information and the
    formal priority are the same in both."
  - Bratman's planning agent is governed by cross-temporal links among its
    intentions ([THEORY-076](../theory.d/THEORY-076.md), [NOTE-464](../notes.d/NOTE-464.md)). Those links are the same in A and B.
  - A model built from the plan alone is the blind controller C0, which
    loses on every seed of the governed world.
  - This holds for the planning theory the record has read. Bratman's
    account of shared agency, in which participants' sub-plans mesh
    ([LIT-488](../literature.d/LIT-488.md)), could treat who may halt deployment as a sub-plan. It is
    Unavailable, and that verdict waits on it.
- **Institutional theory already writes the relations.**
  - Crawford and Ostrom's grammar codes a statement by attribute, deontic,
    aim, condition and or else ([THEORY-1787](../theory.d/THEORY-1787.md)). "If an incident is classified
    as severe, deployment must stop" ([CASE-002](../cases.d/CASE-002.md)) is such a statement, and the
    override is its condition.
  - Who classifies, and whether a specialist's objection suspends, are
    authority and position rules in Polski and Ostrom's sense
    ([THEORY-1809](../theory.d/THEORY-1809.md)).
  - The interventions of [CASE-325](../cases.d/CASE-325.md) change operating rules, which in
    the Institutional Analysis and Development framework is the
    collective-choice level's business.
  - An institutional analysis of Northstar therefore has the four
    relations as its variables. Put as a model, it is the will-organization
    model, and it composes the same way.
  - For an organization, then, the difference [CLAIM-889](CLAIM-889.md) prices is one
    an existing theory gives.

So [CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md) fails on two fronts in this case and holds on the third.
What is left of [CLAIM-487](CLAIM-487.md) is its own wording: that one description
"across a person, a team and an institution" explains what each field
describing its own realizer does not. In this case the account's
variables are the institutional ones. The gain, if there is one, would be
that the same variables serve where no institution is, as in the reluctant
writer ([CASE-013](../cases.d/CASE-013.md)). That needs a second realizer modelled with the same
variables, and nothing here does it.

## What it does not say

- **That institutional theory explains persons.** The claim is that it
  gives the difference for an organization, not elsewhere.
- **That the account is a relabelling.** That would follow only if no
  second realizer shares the variables. The claim leaves that open.
- **That the lead's own criterion is met as written.** [LEAD-089](../leads.d/LEAD-089.md) says a
  difference "one an existing theory already gives, defeats [CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md)".
  On this reading such a difference supports [CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md) on that front and
  bears against [CLAIM-487](CLAIM-487.md)'s uniqueness. The lead's wording looks
  inverted, and this claim reads it that way.
- **That the readings were re-read for this.** Each is used as the record
  holds it. Polski and Ostrom's chapter was only skimmed, and [THEORY-1809](../theory.d/THEORY-1809.md),
  [THEORY-1787](../theory.d/THEORY-1787.md) and [THEORY-076](../theory.d/THEORY-076.md) are Proposed readings of what the authors
  propose.
