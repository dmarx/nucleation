# QUESTION-tmpcs4qg: Are teasing and reproach individuated by the speaker's communicative intention, or partly by a frame the parties sustain together and by their standing?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) says which act an utterance performs is partly constituted by its
relations to participant roles, norms and expected uptake. For acts with
standing conditions, such as a judge's reprimand, the record has an account
it has read: Searle's status functions and deontic powers ([LIT-482](../literature.d/LIT-482.md)).
Teasing is the line's central case, and for it the record has no account at
all.

[CLAIM-tmpc25sn](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpc25sn.md) names the standard rival. On the intention-based account of
illocution (Grice's speaker meaning, 1957; Strawson's "Intention and
Convention in Speech Acts", 1964; Bach and Harnish's *Linguistic
Communication and Speech Acts*, 1979), an act is individuated by the
speaker's communicative intention, an intention to be recognized as having
a certain attitude or aim. On it the friend teasing and the parent
reproaching differ in what each intends the hearer to recognize, and their
relations to the audience are evidence of the act, not part of it. The
record's own model for [CLAIM-111](../claims.d/CLAIM-111.md) is of this family: [THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md)'s listener
interprets an utterance "by inverting a model of a speaker".

The tradition has replies, and the record holds none of them read.

- **Keying.** Goffman's *Frame Analysis* (1974) treats play as a keying of
  a frame, a transformation of serious activity that the participants
  recognize and sustain together. If teasing is keyed play, whether an
  utterance teases depends on whether the frame is jointly held, not only on
  what the speaker intends. The book is not in the record; *Forms of Talk*
  ([LIT-780](../literature.d/LIT-780.md)) is held unread.
- **Uptake.** Austin held that an illocution is not performed unless uptake
  is secured ([LIT-785](../literature.d/LIT-785.md), held unread). If so, the hearer's recognition is a
  condition of the act, not only evidence of it.

[CLAIM-tmpcq79k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpcq79k.md) adds the translation form of the question: a translation
changes the audience and the norms, so whether teasing survives depends on
whether the target culture's norms define the same roles.

## What would count as an answer

A reading of the intention-based line and of its rivals, and a documented
or elicited case in which the speaker's intention is held fixed and the act
verdicts of informed participants change with the parties' standing, with
whether the frame is jointly held, or with uptake. Such a case would show
that teasing is partly constituted by relations, and [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) would cover
it. A body of cases in which the verdicts follow the speaker's intention
whatever the standing and uptake would show the reverse, and [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md)
would hold only for acts with standing conditions.
