# LIT-597: Self-Constitution: Agency, Identity, and Integrity

Christine M. Korsgaard (2009), *Self-Constitution: Agency, Identity, and
Integrity*, Oxford: Oxford University Press — DOI-10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199552795.001.0001

**On the citation.** The brief's details are right: Korsgaard, 2009, a book.
The record's [LIT-468](LIT-468.md), "The Constitution of Selves", is not Korsgaard's. It is
Schechtman's 1996 narrative account of personal identity, filed Deferred.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the contents and from what a read chapter says of it, not
a reading.*

- Chapters, with Crossref's pages: 1 Agency and Identity (1–26); 2 The
  Metaphysics of Normativity (27–44); 3 Formal and Substantive Principles of
  Reason (45–58); 4 Practical Reason and the Unity of the Will (59–80); 5
  Autonomy and Efficacy (81–108); 6 Expulsion from the Garden: The
  Transition to Humanity (109–132); 7 The Constitutional Model (133–158); 8
  Defective Action (159–176); 9 Integrity and Interaction (177–206); 10 How
  to be a Person (207–214).
- As "The Normative Constitution of Agency" ([LIT-587](LIT-587.md)) reports the book:
  we "constitute our own agency or activity by adopting certain laws to
  govern our actions" (§§1.4, 4.3, 5.1). Chapter 6 says that the
  self-consciousness of rational animals turns their attitudes into "a heap"
  that must be unified. §7.2.11 gives the Spartan example: an attack counts
  as an act of Sparta only if it is ordered by Sparta's rulers.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the canonical statement of
self-constitution. It is `Deferred` because it was not read.
[LIT-587](LIT-587.md) (read in full) is the read substitute, and **extends** it in the
LIT scheme's sense. The chapter restates the book's argument and cites its
sections for each step. The owner's brief asked whether the record's [LIT-468](LIT-468.md)
was this book. It is not: [LIT-468](LIT-468.md) is Schechtman's narrative view, the other
"constitution" view of the self. Read together they give two accounts in
which persons make themselves: by narrative (Schechtman) and by
law-governed choice (Korsgaard).

`personhood` is carried because the book's last chapter is "How to be a
Person", and because, as the Tanner Lectures ([LIT-564](LIT-564.md)) already argue,
integrity in the sense of being one is what makes someone "a person at all".
`ethics` is carried because the book's thesis is that good action is action
that constitutes its agent.

No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.
