# THEORY-tmpbzmql: In explaining psychiatric disorder, biological findings implement psychological functions rather than replacing them

## Source

- Kendler (2008), [LIT-539](../literature.d/LIT-539.md), read in full in [NOTE-432](../notes.d/NOTE-432.md) from the NIH author
  manuscript. Its section on "The Nesting of Biological and Psychological
  Explanatory Perspectives" is the whole argument for this account.

Kendler 2005 ([LIT-552](../literature.d/LIT-552.md)) is the paper the 2008 one cites for its premise
that etiological models must be pluralistic. Its abstract asserts mind–brain
causation in both directions and argues against dualism and
epiphenomenalism. It is filed unread, so nothing here rests on it.

## What was actually shown

Kendler ([LIT-539](../literature.d/LIT-539.md)) distinguishes two relations a lower-level theory can bear
to a higher-level one ([NOTE-432](../notes.d/NOTE-432.md), C6). In **replacement** the higher-level
construct becomes redundant, as temperature is once it is mean kinetic
energy. In **implementation** the lower-level theory supplies "the
mechanistic details of how the functions proposed by the more abstract
theory actually get accomplished". Marr's levels give the order: function
is stated first, and it can only be stated in psychological terms
("Neurochemical terms will not work"). A gene-to-protein finding has not
explained schizophrenia until someone can say how the abnormal protein
produces delusions and hallucinations.

The evidence offered is historical. Following Gold and Stoljar on Kandel's
Aplysia work, Hatfield on vision and Bechtel on memory, neuroscience has
advanced by implementing processes psychologists described first. Kendler
concludes that biology and psychology "will coevolve". He also concedes
that hard-reductive and hard-emergent work can still be useful: therapies
have come from gene findings without a mechanism, and CBT "emerged from
psychological constructs that contained no biology".

What could have come out otherwise is the historical record: the cited
cases could have been ones in which the psychological construct was
dropped. None of the cited cases is psychiatric, and the record has read
none of them. That is the reason for Proposed. Kendler himself grants that
Marr's scheme was built for information-processing systems and that "many
psychiatric problems are not currently amenable" to that perspective, then
holds that its logic is "valid for psychiatry across the board". That step
is asserted.

## How it bears on mental causation, levels and emergence

- **It is a thesis about explanation, not about extra causes.** Pernu
  ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md), [NOTE-091](../notes.d/NOTE-091.md)) argues that the neural evidence for mental causation
  supports it only by identifying the mental with the neural: what is
  multiply realised in brain–computer interfaces is a coarse neural
  variable, not the intention. Kendler's implementation relation does not
  need the psychological level to be a distinct cause, or to be multiply
  realised. It needs only that the function be stated psychologically and
  that the biology be how it is done. That fits Pernu's deflationary
  option, in which mental causes are physical causes, as well as a
  nonreductive one. What it denies is that the psychological description
  drops out of the explanation, which Pernu's identity reading does not
  claim either: "physicalism is not microphysicalism". [NOTE-432](../notes.d/NOTE-432.md) makes the
  same point, that implementation is a non-eliminative alternative to the
  framing Pernu argues within. So the two sources are compatible, and the
  record holds no argument that makes psychiatric explanation choose
  between them.
- **Levels are part–whole, and that limits "top-down" talk.** Kendler
  treats levels as part–whole relations within one mechanism, and lists
  "recursive, or top-down, causality" among the features that defeat
  decomposition. Hazelwood ([LIT-156](../literature.d/LIT-156.md), [NOTE-137](../notes.d/NOTE-137.md)) argues from Craver and
  Bechtel and from Woodward's independent-fixability condition that a
  whole and its own parts cannot stand in a causal relation. Kendler's
  examples pass that test, because they run through time and through other
  people: tolerance raising later drinking, offspring of heavy drinkers
  abstaining. They are diachronic loops between distinct variables, not a
  whole acting on its parts at an instant. That reading is the record's,
  not Kendler's.
- **It is neither hard reduction nor hard emergence.** Kendler rejects both
  by name ([NOTE-432](../notes.d/NOTE-432.md), C4). The emergence the record holds from physics
  (Rizi, [LIT-150](../literature.d/LIT-150.md); McKenzie, [LIT-141](../literature.d/LIT-141.md)) is weak emergence by coarse-graining,
  which is close to what Kendler's local decomposition and reassembly
  assumes. Neither physics paper discusses minds.
- **It is functionalism about psychiatric explanation.** Stating a function
  first and its realiser second is the functionalist premise that
  [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) runs in two directions for minds and groups. Kendler applies
  it to explanation only, and says nothing about consciousness.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say biology is unimportant.** Implementation is a demand
  that biological findings be connected to function, not a claim that they
  are secondary.
- **It does not say the psychological level causes the biological one.**
  Kendler's "top-down" loops are cross-level in his sense, but they need no
  causation by a whole of its own parts (above).
- **It does not cover the 2005 paper's claims** on first-person experience,
  dualism and epiphenomenalism. [LIT-539](../literature.d/LIT-539.md) does not argue them.
- **It does not settle what a psychological function is for psychiatry.**
  Functional contextualism, the philosophy behind acceptance and commitment
  therapy ([LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md), [NOTE-431](../notes.d/NOTE-431.md)), limits causal analysis to manipulable
  context and says thoughts do not cause actions "except as regulated by
  context". On that view the explanatory level is a person–context
  relation, not an internal function, so it would not accept Kendler's
  Marr-style ordering as stated. The two have not been compared in print in
  anything the record holds.
