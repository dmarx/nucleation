# QUESTION-526: What standard makes the loss of a pathway to alternatives justified by the benefits of consolidation, so that destructive lock-in can be told from productive specialization?

## What makes it open

[TERM-164](../terms.d/TERM-164.md) separates destructive lock-in from productive specialization
by whether "the benefits of consolidation" justify the pathways lost. Its
Limits say the clause has no standard. Without one the test classifies
nothing: every loss of a pathway can be called productive or destructive
at will.

Two partial candidates:

- **A burden that rises with irreversibility** ([CLAIM-684](../claims.d/CLAIM-684.md)): the burden of
  justifying a narrowing rises with its size and irreversibility. Its
  reason runs out "where nothing will be learned"; it says who bears the
  burden only in part, and who judges not at all.
- **The break-even** of the manuscript's §9.6, reversed: consolidation that
  prices a pathway out is justified when keeping the pathway costs more
  than the expected late losses it prevents. It needs the readiness it
  compares to be known ([QUESTION-395](QUESTION-395.md)) and the trade-offs among demands
  declared first ([CLAIM-1108](../claims.d/CLAIM-1108.md)).

Neither says whether "justified" also needs justification owed to, and
answerable to, those whose alternatives are lost ([QUESTION-480](QUESTION-480.md)).

## What would count as an answer

A standard that classifies a stated loss of a pathway, says who judges it,
and either is cost-benefit with its trade-offs declared or says what it
adds to cost-benefit.

## Note of 2026-11-26: the exchange "Alignment consequences brainstorm" (ChatGPT, U2–A4, U3–A5)

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-1364 — filed from the exchange of 2026-11-26, Open or Proposed -->

A4 §16 quotes this question and says "The record acknowledges that its
existing criteria are insufficient", which is accurate ([TERM-164](../terms.d/TERM-164.md)'s
clause "has no standard"). It adds one requirement for an answer: it
"would need to account for the opportunities forgone by *not* adopting a
technology. The status quo is itself a trajectory that creates
dependencies, incurs costs, forecloses possibilities, and sometimes causes
irreversible harm." It cites [CLAIM-684](../claims.d/CLAIM-684.md) as recognizing that objection,
which it does: its own text raises that a burden rising with
irreversibility "systematically preserves a worse status quo".

A5 closes by making this question "not merely a question about AI or
technology, but a foundational question about social development itself".
That widens its scope; it adds no candidate standard.

The owner's U2 ([QUESTION-551](QUESTION-551.md)) asks a case of this question for
capabilities, and its partial answer ([CLAIM-1364](../claims.d/CLAIM-1364.md)) gives one mark a
standard here would have to use: whether the supply that replaces a lost
pathway is reciprocal and whose terms govern it. A bearing; the question
stays open.
