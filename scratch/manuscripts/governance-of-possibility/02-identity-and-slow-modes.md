# Part B. Identity, filtering and slow modes

Sections 1–2 treated regulation as acting on what an industrial ecosystem can become: which sets of demands some attainable history can meet, and which of those an organization can reach. This part asks what must stay the same for that to make sense. Something has to persist through the reorganizations that governance brings about, or there is nothing whose possibilities are being governed. Something also has to stay open to change, or the possibilities cannot be used.

As in the rest of the paper, each step cites the entry of the Nucleation record that holds it, with its standing on 2026-11-23: **Active** (the record accepts it) or **Proposed** (filed and argued, not adopted), and **demonstrated** (shown by construction or proof), **empirical** (observed) or **interpretive** (argued). Voices are kept apart. "The owner" is the record's owner; "ChatGPT" is the other assistant in the exchange "Explain First Contact RL", from which the motivating draft of this paper came, and is "A" in the record's quotations (A38, A40 and so on are its turns); "the assistant" is Claude, which filed the exchange. An Active entry is the record's whatever its origin. A Proposed entry is told in the voice of whoever put it forward. Where this paper joins entries that the record keeps apart, it says so and marks the join (JB1, JB2, …). Where an entry already states what the draft marked as a join, the text says so and cites the entry, and the join is a restatement. Where an account of the record has declined the join, the text says so and does not state it as held. A join that no entry states and no account had declined is now filed in an account of the record (SYNTHESIS-123 for this part's), which lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict of the record's admission test (ADR-075, step 5); the text cites the account and gives the verdict, and where the join passed, cites the claim filed for it. Where the first draft needed a step that the record lacked, it marked a gap (GB1, GB2, …). Each such gap is now filed in the record as a Proposed entry in the assistant's voice, and is cited where it falls; GB5 is listed as an open item for the owner. None is settled in this paper's prose.

Two limits apply to this whole part. First, none of the cases on which it rests is a real firm, profession, regulator or industrial ecosystem. They are finite constructions (CASE-116, CASE-189), formal derivations, and models (§9). The industrial examples are illustrations. Second, carrying results about dynamical rates into institutions is itself an analogy, and the record marks one such carriage as *supposed* (CLAIM-730, §7.2). This part says where it makes that carriage.

**What this part rests on.** Its firmest ground is four Active, demonstrated results, each a short derivation: the filter's trade between tracking and noise, with its per-frequency optimum (CLAIM-1152, §4); the separation of a mode's persistence from its cost of redirection (CLAIM-1192, §5.2); the deadline floor on how fast a governing reference must be revisable (CLAIM-1010, §7.3); and the sufficient condition on retuning a governance parameter (CLAIM-1051, §7.3). With them stand CLAIM-626's three meanings of a slow mode (Active, demonstrated; §5.1), CLAIM-1084's temporal complex (Active, demonstrated; §6.3), and two Proposed derivations filed for this paper in the assistant's voice: the speed–noise product of a first-order reference (CLAIM-1137, §4) and the conditions of the tangent–transverse argument (CLAIM-1079, §5.3). The Proposed theses about identity and adaptability, ChatGPT's (CLAIM-1135, CLAIM-1104, CLAIM-1070, CLAIM-1066) and the owner's (CLAIM-1144), are read here as interpretations of those results, not as further results. One fact belongs beside these standings. CLAIM-1152, CLAIM-1192, CLAIM-1010, CLAIM-1051, CLAIM-1084, CLAIM-626 and CLAIM-1040 each record that they were "Promoted to Active at the status review's ruling (the owner delegated these rulings)" on 2026-11-23, the day this paper was drafted. The ruling was an assistant's, under the owner's standing delegation, and in several cases the assistant had also supplied the step that was checked (§14.7).

## 3. Why identity stabilization is necessary

### 3.0 What has to persist, and in which sense

The motivating draft opens this section by saying that "the possibility of reorganization presupposes an organization capable of reorganizing". The record gives that thought a sharper form than the draft does, and the form comes from how relief is judged.

The record's primitive for an organization's difficulty is a conflict among the demands it maintains (CLAIM-347, Active, interpretive; §2). Whether a change relieves that conflict, it holds, must be judged "for an identified organization against a fixed reference set of its original demands, under an explicit correspondence between earlier and later demands". Two consequences follow: "a demand excised or suppressed has changed the comparison and has not been satisfied", and "an endogenous revision of what the organization maintains is a transformation of it and not relief" (CLAIM-361, Active, interpretive). So identity is needed before any reorganization can be called an improvement. Without a criterion for which organization persists, an observer cannot tell three outcomes apart: an ecosystem that met its demands, one that dropped them, and one that became a different ecosystem with different demands. That identity is a precondition of assessing governance is CLAIM-361's own requirement, in its first clause: relief "must be judged for an identified organization". The draft marked this as a join (JB1); it is a restatement of CLAIM-361. The record's account of frustration leaves open a question near it, "Whether QUESTION-371's stabilizing norm would relieve frustration in CLAIM-554's sense or only change who persists" (SYNTHESIS-051, Proposed).

The next question is what kind of persistence is meant. The record refuses a single answer. "Persistence" of an organization means one of five things, "and a claim should say which" (TERM-071, Active):

- **state:** a collective variable changes slowly;
- **dynamical:** characteristic responses continue;
- **historical:** "The organization that produces the collective is causally transmitted across successive populations of members";
- **viable:** trajectories that keep satisfying a constraint set remain available;
- **reinstatable:** the capacity to reinstate an organization after inactivity.

The senses dissociate. TERM-071 points to constructions that have one without the others (CASE-116, §3.2).

The sense this paper needs is the historical one. The record states its criterion: "A collective that replaces all its members persists, when it does, by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by resemblance: an organization of the same kind that arises independently is not its continuation, and a lapse in its coherence need not end it" (CLAIM-329, Active, interpretive). Three further entries fix what that criterion does and does not cover.

- **Product and producer come apart.** A pattern can persist while every constituent is replaced "and some constituents work against it", "so the persistence of a product and the persistence of the organization producing it are separate facts" (CLAIM-280, Active, interpretive). A regulated output, such as a supply of a product or a level of emissions, can stay steady while the organization that produces it is replaced underneath it, and the reverse.
- **Reinstatement is not identity.** "What persists when a self persists through inactivity is the capacity to reinstate an organization, not its continuous running, and continuity of that kind can branch and merge, so it is not an identity relation" (CLAIM-287, Active, interpretive). A mothballed plant, a dormant standard or an archived procedure can be reinstated more than once, by more than one successor.
- **Self-governance is a further achievement.** "A self, in the self-governing sense, is a historically continuous organization of processes that takes part in governing the conditions of its own persistence, differentiation and transformation, not merely one that persists" (CLAIM-284, Active, interpretive).

The record's senses of individuation keep the last point apart from the first (TERM-088, Active). An organization can be individuated *dynamically*, its organization persisting under specified perturbations with robust boundaries, while "sustained wholly from outside, like a convection roll fed by an imposed gradient. That is individuation without autonomy." *Organizational autonomy* is the further achievement of contributing to the maintenance of its own constitutive constraints. "The senses are not stages, and not degrees of existence." An industrial cluster kept together only by a subsidy would be, on this reading, dynamically individuated without autonomy. That example is this paper's, not the record's.

Two further Active entries sharpen "not by resemblance". "Isomorphism is not persistence, in either direction: two separately made mechanisms with the same costed transformation structure are isomorphic and still two, and one mechanism whose hinge is replaced can persist while its costed structure becomes non-isomorphic to the earlier one" (CLAIM-593, Active, demonstrated). And succession can be plural: "When the continuities that ordinarily come bundled in a collective (legal franchise, civic role, interpersonal transmission, narrative and place) are separated, several later collectives can each be a genuine successor of the earlier one in a different respect, without any of them, or the earlier collective, having branched in numerical identity" (CLAIM-328, Active, interpretive; it rests on one documented case, a baseball club). A sector whose licences, workforce and plant pass to different successors could have several successors, each in a respect. That reading is this paper's illustration.

Two Proposed claims, both A's in the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition", go further. One holds that what persists is "the particular organization that keeps its repertoire realizable, not the repertoire it keeps, nor any state, mode or boundary; that organization may lie inside the individual or outside it" (CLAIM-611, Proposed, interpretive). The other holds that participants' "judgements of which successor continues it are causally productive of that succession" (CLAIM-617, Proposed, interpretive). The first is a step of a thesis the record files from the same exchange, much of it the owner's and its integration A's: an organized individual's identity over time "is carried by the causal transmission of the relations that keep its repertoire realizable, not by its constituents or by any structure invariant across its realizations" (CLAIM-615, Proposed, interpretive). Read for industry, the first would allow that what keeps an industrial organization's repertoire realizable lies partly outside it, in a regulator among other places, and the second that a market's or a regulator's verdict on succession can help make it. Both readings are this paper's, and both claims are untested.

So, read on the record's Active ground, the identity that governance must stabilize is the historical continuity of an organization of relations, transmitted causally through turnover of members, technologies and rules. It is not the invariance of a configuration. The motivating draft says much the same, but tells it as "Nucleation's" approach while echoing ChatGPT's own Proposed claims about the self (CLAIM-1044, CLAIM-1175, both Proposed). The audit of the draft found this (curation entry of 2026-11-23). The Active entries above carry the point without them.

*Carrying these results to firms, professions and regulators is not held on the record.* The record's cases are collectives, coalitions, artworks defended in shared online spaces and selves, not industrial institutions. The draft marked the carriage as a join (JB2: CLAIM-329, CLAIM-280, CLAIM-284 and TERM-071 applied to industrial organizations). The record's accounts decline its nearest links. The account of persistence lists as not stated "That a slow variable the members regenerate carries persistence through turnover", and "The 2005 Nationals of CASE-105 are a realistic case against it" (SYNTHESIS-047, Proposed). The account of the exchange behind this paper says of the industrial analogue that "No component states that an industrial ecosystem satisfies TERM-166's integration condition. Left as an analogy" (SYNTHESIS-122, Proposed). So the industrial readings in §3 are illustrations, not steps (JB2, declined by SYNTHESIS-047 and SYNTHESIS-122). One limit travels with the criterion. What must stay causally continuous for a later organization to be the earlier one, "and not a similar one produced anew", is open (QUESTION-118, Open). A regulator that reorganizes a sector can therefore ask whether the sector's organization was transmitted, but the record cannot yet tell it where continuation ends and causal descent begins.

### 3.0.1 The owner's tension, and two readings of "inherent"

The puzzle this paper takes up is the owner's. At U20 of the exchange, the owner named "the inherent tension between identity stabilization and adaptability, and how appropriate response to demands is timescale dependent". The record holds it as the owner's thesis: "There is an inherent tension between stabilizing an organization's identity and keeping it adaptable, and the appropriate response to a demand depends on the timescale of that demand" (CLAIM-1144, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The motivating draft states it in its abstract and conclusion without naming it as the owner's.

The word "inherent" can be read two ways. The readings are the assistant's, filed with the claim:

- **Strong:** "stabilizing identity always costs adaptability".
- **Weak:** "the two always bear on each other and must be managed together".

Each reading has its own defeater on the record. The strong one is defeated by "an organization whose identity-preserving constraints never make any later demanded change slower or costlier". The weak one is defeated by "an organization with an identity in the record's sense (CLAIM-287) whose adaptation never bears on what preserves it". ChatGPT, at A38, rejected the strong reading ("The tension becomes more interesting if we avoid treating identity and adaptability as opposing objectives"). The record's review of 2026-11-23 "leans to the weak one", but which reading the owner means "is unruled" (CLAIM-1144, note of 2026-11-23).

**This paper does not choose between them; the choice is the owner's.** Later sections bear on it in both directions. §5.2 shows that slowness does not by itself make a mode costly to redirect, and §7.1 that a slowly set rule can be applied fast. Both tell against the strong reading, as A38's examples do. §5.1 and §7.2 show stabilizing structures whose slowness hides lost capacity or forecloses redress. Those are cases in which the two do bear on each other. Neither set of results settles which reading is meant.

The assistant has filed a gloss on the tension, as a supposition: that it "has the shape of the paradox of wu-wei, that one cannot try to be effortless", with cultivation and unlearning as the two classical routes to its two halves (CLAIM-1061, Proposed, interpretive, supposed, the assistant's). It is supposed because its sinological framing is the assistant's recollection of a standard summary and its source is unread, and it is a gloss on CLAIM-1135 (§5.3). It does not choose between the readings either.

### 3.1 Fast activity makes slow structure

How does a relation among fast activities become something that constrains them? The record's answer is a claim about rate differences: "Rate differences help make structure and not only mark it: where some degrees of freedom settle faster than others by more than the rates vary among them, the relation they settle into is a constraint on the system over the window between the two rates, so what is a realization at one rate works as a constraint at another; and organization can make new rate differences in turn, as when modes slow near an instability and become order parameters, so hierarchies of rates and constraints can produce one another" (CLAIM-478, Proposed, interpretive). The claim was filed from the owner's question at U12 of an exchange of 2026-10-27, which asked how scale separation "participates in creating structure", and from ChatGPT's answer.

The mathematics is standard, and the claim says so: "Not new as mathematics. Every step is a known result. The claim is the interpretation". For a system with fast variables x and slow variables y, εẋ = f(x, y) and ẏ = g(x, y), if the fast subsystem has an attracting equilibrium x = h(y), the system approaches a slow manifold on which x ≈ h(y). Tikhonov's and Fenichel's theorems give the conditions (CLAIM-478). On the slow timescale the relation x = h(y) behaves as a constraint. That is the record's sense of a constraint, after Montévil and Mossio: a constraint C "acts on a process at a time scale τs(C) at which its relevant aspects are conserved" (TERM-070, Active). CLAIM-478 glosses the definition as "something that acts on a process while its relevant aspects are conserved at that process's time scale".

The claim's present wording differs from the motivating draft's, which writes the mechanism with "0 < ε ≪ 1". The owner asked for the change: "maybe I'm being brash calling it 'scale separation' and a more appropriate -- or at least easier to demonstrate -- assertion would call it 'rate differences'" (CLAIM-478, version 5, 2026-11-20). No asymptotic limit is needed. What carries content is a comparison between levels and within them: "Where the rates within a level vary more than they differ between levels, the rates still make structure, but not that level." How large the between-level ratio must be, set against the spread within a level, is open (QUESTION-500, Open).

Three entries bear on that comparison. The assistant reads it formally: "the ratio of the rate within a level's blocks to the rate between them is the margin by which the block aggregate is a slow manifold and the coarse variable closes" (CLAIM-781, Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's, after a remark of the owner's). The record holds, Active, that rate differences do not come from coupling alone: "Coupling does not by itself separate time scales"; "separation comes where the coupling dissipates relative differences faster than collective ones" (CLAIM-481, Active, demonstrated). On this paper's reading, frequent dealings among firms make no slower convention unless they also damp the firms' differences. And a Proposed claim, A's in the exchange "Topological protection explained", cautions against reading every durable arrangement this way: dynamical and economic separation of time scales "are different and come apart both ways: a slow variable can save no work, and a result reused cheaply need not be an attractor of any dynamics" (CLAIM-755, Proposed, demonstrated).

The relation also runs in reverse. The record holds, from the owner at U8 of an exchange of 2026-10-16, that "higher-level variables are characteristically slower and longer-lived than the activity that produces them, though slowness is a mark of a level and not its definition" (CLAIM-331, Active, interpretive). ChatGPT's form of the loop, quoted there: "fast interactions reproduce slow variables, and slow variables condition subsequent fast interactions." CLAIM-478 adds that the loop can make the rate differences themselves, as when modes slow near an instability.

The motivating draft's industrial readings are illustrations: repeated contracts settling into expectations, repeated practice into a standard, repeated exercises of authority into a convention. No case on the record shows any of them. *Reading an industrial convention as a relation that settled faster than what it now constrains is this paper's carriage of CLAIM-478 into institutions.* That carriage is the one the record marks as supposed for CLAIM-730 (§7.2). Whether a governing rule is a slow variable in CLAIM-478's sense, the draft's gap GB3, is now a lead with its measures fixed: in one institution with a documented history of a rule and of the practice it governs, measure how fast the practice settles after the rule changes against how often the rule is revised (LEAD-222, Open, stage idea, the assistant's). Its defeater is met "if the practice's settling time is not shorter than the rule's revision interval, or if revisions follow practice".

Four limits on the record keep this mechanism from proving too much.

- **Slowness makes a constraint available, not effective.** "Not that slowness makes a constraint effective." ChatGPT: "A rule stored in a file may persist for years without influencing anything." The claim: "Slowness makes a constraint *available*." Whether it is used and maintained are further conditions (CLAIM-478, "What it does not say"; §3.2).
- **Slow structure fails slowly.** Flack, read on the record: an aggregate that summarizes poorly will likely fail, and "failure may just take longer if the time scale separation is very large" (CLAIM-478, note of 2026-10-27). A slow structure's persistence is weak evidence that it is still adequate. §11 takes this up as lock-in.
- **Rate separation can be lost from outside.** Ashwin and colleagues, read on the record: "separation can be lost because the input speeds up, with no mode slowing" (CLAIM-478, same note). A convention that constrained practice well when demands changed slowly can stop constraining it when the demands speed up, with nothing in the convention changed.
- **Slowness is not, by itself, new organization.** Scheffer and colleagues, read on the record: "slowness alone does not show new organization forming" (CLAIM-478, note of 2026-11-03).

The record's one measurement of rates in a real organization is of itself. Its prose is revised "about once in 22 days" and its statuses "once in 128" (CASE-180, Active). That is evidence of rates, not of relaxation, and of nothing industrial.

### 3.2 Slowness alone is not constituency

A slowly changing variable need not be part of an organization. The record states this as a limit of CLAIM-331: "Not that a slow variable is a level." ChatGPT, quoted there: "a constant external label can also have a long timescale". And: "A variable's persistence is not, by itself, evidence of the persistence of the organization that generates it" (CLAIM-331, Active, interpretive). The record's account of resolve draws the same line for a commitment, which must keep "the capacity to revise it; mere slowness or persistence is not resolve" (CLAIM-344, Active, interpretive).

What would make a slow variable a constituent? The record has a Proposed answer: "A slowly changing collective variable, such as a reputation, a rivalry or a standing, is part of a collective's dynamics and not only an observer's average when its members read it, condition what they do on it, and by doing so regenerate it" (CLAIM-327, Proposed, interpretive). Its tests, from ChatGPT at A9:

- "timescale separation (τ_Z ≫ τ_X, not sufficient)";
- predictive usefulness over a matched baseline;
- endogenous maintenance;
- causal uptake under intervention.

The claim's own notes cut its support. Its three stages "are the exchange's synthesis of 2026-10-16 … it is not Flack's", and Flack's paper, once read, is "evidence for the first stage only" (CLAIM-327, notes of 2026-10-27 and 2026-10-29). The motivating draft cites CLAIM-327 as what Nucleation "emphasizes" without saying that it is Proposed.

The record's firmest ground here is a construction with two arms that pull against each other (CASE-116, Active).

- **Arm A, slow without uptake.** A register flips with probability 0.01 a step, so its second eigenvalue is 0.98. The members are resampled each step and never read it. ChatGPT: "Strong timescale separation exists, but constituent agents never read or regenerate Z. It is a persistent statistic". **Slowness is not sufficient** for a variable to be part of the members' organization.
- **Arm B, regenerated without slowness.** Two members read a shared register and act on it with noise, and one member's action writes the next value. The register's second eigenvalue is 0.2, so it forgets on the members' own timescale. Under do(Z_t = 0) the next value is 1 with probability 0.4; under do(Z_t = 1), with probability 0.6. The members' reading makes a difference to the register. **Slowness is not necessary** for endogenous maintenance. This holds within the construction's own limit, kept verbatim: "This demonstrates only *weak* endogenous maintenance. If maintenance is defined to require robust long-term persistence, this counterexample no longer establishes the separation."

A third arm passes a rule through an archive between episodes that never run together. It is transmission without running, CLAIM-287's reinstatable capacity in miniature. The case also states what it cannot show: identity ("causal transmission alone does not settle whether A and B are numerically the *same* collective"), or a theorem independent of its definitions.

For a regulator, the two arms give a test with two sides. A legal standard is not part of an industrial organization because its text persists (arm A). And a fast-moving practice can be constitutive if participants read it and regenerate it (arm B). The test that discriminates is interventional: change the variable and see whether the participants' conduct, and through it the variable's next value, changes. That is CLAIM-327's fourth test. The record holds more generally that ordinary operation does not reveal it. A coarse-graining "exactly informationally closed under a process's ordinary dynamics need not preserve its responses to an intervention on the mechanism" (CLAIM-407, Active, demonstrated). *The reading of a legal standard as CASE-116's register, and the interventional test as the regulator's criterion of constituency, are this paper's illustration*, under the carriage the accounts decline (JB2, declined; §3.0). LEAD-222 (Open) would measure the rates such a reading needs.

## 4. Identity stabilization as temporal filtering

An organization cannot treat every change in what it observes as a reason to change what it is. Some variation is disturbance to be absorbed; some is a changed demand to be met. The record holds a precise form of this, filed from ChatGPT's turns A42 and A44 and checked by the assistant:

"Identity stabilization can be read as temporal filtering: an organizational variable that adapts at rate k to a noisily observed demand tracks variation slower than k and ignores variation faster, its tracking error grows with the demand's frequency as ω²/(k² + ω²) while the noise it passes grows with k as k²/(k² + ω²), so neither the slowest nor the fastest adaptation is optimal in general, and the per-frequency optimum S_d/(S_d + λS_n) passes variation where genuine demand outweighs noise and holds steady where noise outweighs it" (CLAIM-1152, Active, demonstrated; the wording is ChatGPT's).

The model is a governing reference z, for instance a standard's threshold, adjusting at rate k toward a measured demand d observed with noise n:

  ż = k(d + n − z), so H_k(s) = k/(s + k).

The tracking error has squared gain ω²/(k² + ω²) at frequency ω, and the noise reaches z with squared gain k²/(k² + ω²). Raising k lowers the first at every nonzero frequency and raises the second. So a fast-adapting reference follows real change and passes noise, and a slow one ignores noise and lags real change.

The record adds what the motivating draft leaves out: the best response when the operator is free. Weight passed noise by λ against tracking error, and at each frequency the quantity S_d|1 − T|² + λS_n|T|² is least at

  T* = S_d/(S_d + λS_n),

where S_d and S_n are the spectra of demand and noise. This is the non-causal Wiener filter ("Ignoring causality, this is the Wiener filter"), checked by direct minimization at one setting, "0.7273 against the formula's 0.7273" (CLAIM-1152). It says where an organization should let its environment move it: in the bands where genuine demand outweighs noise, and nowhere else. It also says that the question has no answer from the organization's side alone. The optimum depends on S_d, S_n and λ, that is, on what the demand is, what the noise is, and how the two errors are weighed.

Two qualifications from the same claim limit the reading.

- **An organization is more selective than a low-pass filter.** ChatGPT, A44: "it selects which variations are allowed to alter which relationships". A single rate k is one channel. An organization has many relations, each with its own rate, and fast restoration across some can go with slow change along others (§5.3). One further point is this paper's own, and elementary: T* is an arbitrary function of frequency, set by the two spectra, while a single first-order rule k/(s + k) can only approximate it. One adaptation rate per relation is in general not the optimum, even for a stationary demand.
- **Spectra do not describe every demand.** "Fourier spectra cannot fully characterize event-triggered obligations, irreversible harms, or response deadlines". Those need TERM-115's readiness (§6). The motivating draft's §4 omits this limit and §6 then needs it.

The filter also bears on a gap in a result §7.3 uses. The record's bound on retuning a reference, that one revisable at rate v cannot meet a demand at distance 1 by deadline H unless vH ≥ 1 − δ − ε, "says nothing of what a fast-revisable reference costs against transient fluctuations" (CLAIM-1010, Active, demonstrated). CLAIM-1152 prices that cost as the noise a faster reference passes. The first draft found no combined result that says how fast a reference should be revisable given both a deadline and a noise spectrum (its gap GB4). One is now filed, for the first-order reference above:

"For a reference adapting as ż = k(d + n − z), the time to come within δ of a unit step in demand, ln(1/δ)/k, times the stationary variance it takes from white noise of intensity σ², kσ²/2, is σ² ln(1/δ)/2 whatever k is, so a deadline H for the step sets a floor σ² ln(1/δ)/(2H) on the noise the reference passes" (CLAIM-1137, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's).

The derivation is short (CLAIM-1137).

- **The deadline picture.** With no noise and a unit step in d at t = 0, z(t) = 1 − e^{−kt}, so |1 − z| ≤ δ from t_δ = ln(1/δ)/k on.
- **The frequency picture.** With d = 0 and n white of intensity σ², z is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, dz = −kz dt + kσ dW, with stationary variance ∫₀^∞ (k e^{−ku})² σ² du = kσ²/2. In CLAIM-1152's terms it is the noise passed, σ² ∫ k²/(k² + ω²) dω/2π.

So t_δ · Var z = σ² ln(1/δ)/2, independent of k. "Speeding the reference up shortens the time to meet a changed demand and raises the noise it passes in exact proportion; no k improves both." A deadline H for meeting the step to within δ needs k ≥ ln(1/δ)/H, hence Var z ≥ σ² ln(1/δ)/(2H): "the shorter the deadline, the more noise the reference must pass". The claim's own check is numerical: with σ = 0.7 and δ = 0.05, the product is 0.7340 at every k tried, equal to σ² ln(20)/2. The coordinator adds a test of the derivation to `analysis/` (`test_speed_noise.py`).

Its limits are its own. It is "Not the best k: that needs the two errors weighed, as CLAIM-1152's λ does." It says "Nothing for other filters": "a higher-order filter, or one shaped to the spectra (CLAIM-1152's T*), can trade differently." And with noise, "being within δ by H is a probability, as TERM-115's readiness is; the claim gives the time of the mean response only."

CLAIM-1137 ties the frequency picture to the deadline picture for one rule. It does not take in CLAIM-1010, which it calls "a different model, a rate limit rather than a first-order rule". Setting a deadline's speed floor beside the filter's noise cost was the first draft's join JB3 (CLAIM-1152 with CLAIM-1010). For the first-order rule it is now stated by CLAIM-1137, and the account of the timing of response and revision says so: "JB3 is stated by CLAIM-1137, for the first-order rule" (SYNTHESIS-123, Proposed). What is not stated is the carriage of the conserved product to CLAIM-1010's rate-limited reference; the account lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "nothing filed", since carrying it to a rate limit "would need a model of its own" (ibid.). This paper uses the product only for the first-order rule (join JB3: stated by CLAIM-1137; its carriage to a rate limit not stated, SYNTHESIS-123).

### 4.1 The filter classifies its own inputs

The filter model takes the spectra as given. In an institution they are not given: what counts as noise is partly decided by the institution. The record holds this as a second claim from the same turns:

"What an organization's filtering treats as noise is itself governed: if objections to its established categories are classed as high-frequency disturbance, stabilization that succeeds against its current classification of noise can fail against the demands it exists to answer, so governing the distinction between demand and disturbance is a higher-order task, and the capacity to recognize a demand as demand-worthy is a slow capability that must stay open to correction" (CLAIM-1040, Active, interpretive; the wording is ChatGPT's).

ChatGPT's example (A44 §3): a regulatory institution that "treats objections to its established industrial categories as high-frequency disturbances", where "some objections are evidence that the consensus is wrong". Then "successful stabilization against the institution's *current classification* of noise can amount to failure against the underlying public demands." And: "the process deciding what merits a response has itself become an entrenched organizational mode."

What the claim adds to the record is shown by a separating case. Take an institution that admits evidence without bias (CLAIM-466's remedy applied) and has every stage of uptake in place (CLAIM-403 satisfied), but whose filter treats a whole class of signal as transient "because of the rate at which they arrive and not because of their content". The premises hold, and the demands behind the objections are never answered. So "the classification of signal against noise is a variable of the institution's temporal organization, and is not fixed by its admission rules" (CLAIM-1040, its admission under ADR-075). The neighbours it draws on are Active:

- suppressing a demand is not satisfying it (CLAIM-361, Active, interpretive);
- admission, recognition, credibility, uptake and a part in revising the standards of uptake "are separable, each possible without the later ones" (CLAIM-403, Active, interpretive);
- "Lock-in from selectively admitted evidence and lock-in from self-scoring need different remedies" (CLAIM-466, Active, demonstrated);
- an architecture adequate under ordinary operation can lose what an intervention would reveal (CLAIM-407, Active, demonstrated). That is "why the architecture must be tested by interventions, not ordinary operation".

The motivating draft calls this a second order of governance, a step beyond filtering: ordinary filtering asks how to respond to recognized demands, and reflexive governance asks how to revise the criteria by which demands become recognizable. The nearest entry on the record is ChatGPT's from another exchange: "An institution can be reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons for revising that mechanism, and because its own actions change the mechanism, responsiveness at the first order is no evidence of responsiveness at the second; identical decisions can come from different capacities to be governed by reasons" (CLAIM-719, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's). Its own admission limits it: "ρ^(2) is named, not defined: A gives no case family or response set for revisions of M." No entry relates the two claims as a claim. Reading the classification of noise in CLAIM-1040 as the object of CLAIM-719's second-order responsiveness was the first draft's join (join JB4: CLAIM-1040 with CLAIM-719). It is now recorded as a reading and held open in QUESTION-522 (below), and the account of the timing of response and revision lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, a free parameter": CLAIM-719's ρ^(2) is "named, not defined", so reading the classification into that slot gives no measure, and "Any revision of the filter would count" (SYNTHESIS-123, Proposed). This paper therefore uses it as a reading, not a step. The record's account of accountability without blame leaves a neighbouring question open, "whether a blameless review is a mechanism of second-order responsiveness in CLAIM-719's sense" (SYNTHESIS-104, Proposed). The term for the capacity at issue, normative governability, includes "considerations that require revising its own governing mechanisms" (TERM-128, Proposed).

What a regulator would need in order to act on this, the record does not hold. CLAIM-1040 says the classification "must stay open to correction" and does not say how. CLAIM-719's second-order measure is undefined. The draft's gap GB2 is now an open question: "By what mechanism is an organization's classification of signal against noise revised, and what observation would measure its responsiveness to reasons for revising that classification, apart from its responsiveness within it?" (QUESTION-522, Open, the assistant's). It records that join JB4 makes the two gaps one, and names what would count as an observation: "the lag between the first arrival of a class of input later shown to carry demand and its reclassification, against the lag in responding to inputs already classed as demand".

Four entries bear on what such a mechanism would face. One is Active and demonstrated: in a simulated forager, "revising which features count is needed for, and pays only on, a change whose warning sign is a feature it never attended to; a change on a feature it already attends to is met by re-estimating" (CLAIM-1149, Active, demonstrated). Read for an institution, which is this paper's step, revising the classification of noise would pay where a new demand arrives through a class of signal the institution does not attend to, and nowhere else. The other three are Proposed. An organization "can be disposed to answer a credible challenge by reducing the capacity for future challenge, classifying the challenge as the problem and restricting the challenger, so that the later absence of challenges reads as success" (CLAIM-619, Proposed, interpretive, A's in the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"); that is a classification of objections as noise that also removes the signal. "A categorical administrative gate can make evidence that a governing standard requires to count unable to change a decision" (CLAIM-745, Proposed, interpretive, A's in the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"). And institutions able to revise their own governing standards are predicted to avoid persistent failures better than those that only monitor outcomes against fixed standards "where the inherited standards are inadequate to the problems met, and not on routine work, where the added governance costs without benefit" (CLAIM-685, Proposed, empirical, A's in the exchange "Analyze technological civilization trap").
 The model of §9 has an arm in which a harm the specification does not name begins mid-run. It shows that a regime which excises the new demand scores no violations against its own specification while the harm continues, and that under some budget rules a review that needs capacity can never start. That is CLAIM-361's distinction made executable. It shows how each choice scores; it does not make the choice between excising and revising emerge from anything (analysis/README.md, item 7).

## 5. Which modes must be stabilized

If identity is selective, the question is which degrees of freedom to hold. A natural answer is "the slow ones", since slow modes are what an organization keeps. The record's results say that the answer cannot be read off a mode's slowness. A slow mode can mean three different things, and slowness does not fix how hard a mode is to change.

### 5.1 A slow mode has no single organizational meaning

The record holds, stated as the record states it: "Critical slowing has no single organizational meaning: when a collective mode slows, reduction to it improves; when the mode that restores a collective relation slows, as two detuned phase oscillators near the edge of locking, the reduction fails while the locked state still exists; when the maintenance of the coupling slows, the reduction stays good while the organization nears its fold" (CLAIM-626, Active, demonstrated). It went Active at the status review of 2026-11-23. The motivating draft calls it Proposed.

The three arms are about whether a *reduction* holds, that is, whether a description of many units as one collective unit stays good. Each arm is a computation.

- **Collective.** Near a pattern-forming instability a few modes slow and become order parameters, and reduction to them improves. This is CLAIM-478's second half.
- **Transverse.** Two phase oscillators with detuning Δω and coupling K have a relative phase obeying φ̇ = Δω − 2K sin φ. A locked state exists for every |Δω| < 2K, with restoring rate λ⊥ = √(4K² − Δω²), which goes to zero at the edge of locking. "Over a horizon H the one-unit reduction is good while λ⊥H ≫ 1 …, so it is lost before the locked state is."
- **Maintenance.** In a construction where reciprocal production holds a coupling, the maintenance's recovery rate "goes to zero at its saddle-node while the coupling's spectral gap there stays near 0.989" (CLAIM-521, Active, demonstrated; CASE-189, Active, fold at damage ≈ 0.691). The coupled units remain well described as one unit until the support for that coupling gives way. A second construction shows the same arm in a model of coordination: in the HKB model whose coupling c is produced at αc²/(1 + c²) against unit decay, "as α falls toward 2 the capacity's recovery rate at its stable level goes to zero while anti-phase coordination stays stable, its relaxation time near 1" (CASE-304, Proposed).

The conclusion: "So an observed slow mode does not say, by itself, whether organization is forming, a reduction is failing, or a maintained capacity is failing." The claim also says what it does not establish. It does not say "That early-warning signals can tell the three apart", nor "That the three kinds are exhaustive". A note of 2026-11-21 adds two more sources of slow modes, neither of which signals a fold: a conserved quantity spreading, and a soft mode protected by a broken symmetry (CLAIM-626).

The motivating draft recasts the arms in organizational terms: transverse slowing as "weakening effective coordination", maintenance slowing as a relationship continuing "to function over shorter horizons". The record's note on that passage calls it a "Restatement, with drift": it "recasts whether the collective reduction stays good as whether coordination weakens or the relationship continues to function" (CLAIM-626, note of 2026-11-23). The governance reading is ChatGPT's (A40), recorded on the claim as a parallel and not an edge: "a slow mode cannot automatically be interpreted as a stabilizing identity" (CLAIM-626, note of 2026-11-22). This paper keeps the record's form. For governance it means three things. A slowly changing feature of an industrial ecosystem may be an organization consolidating. It may be the restoring relation among its parts weakening, so that treating the ecosystem as one unit is no longer safe. Or it may be the maintenance of a coupling failing, so that everything looks integrated until it collapses. Observing the slowness does not say which. §10 takes up what would.

The classic ecological form of a related separation is on the record as a reading. Stability, "a system's speed of return to equilibrium after disturbance", and resilience, "its capacity to absorb change and persist within its domain of attraction", "are distinct properties that can trade off, so a system can be highly unstable and highly resilient, and managing it for constancy can erode its resilience" (THEORY-132, Active; Holling 1973). On this paper's reading, a sector held steady by management can be losing what would let it absorb a change, and its steadiness would not show the loss.

### 5.2 Persistence, control and reproduction are different timescales

The second reason slowness does not settle what to stabilize is that a mode's rate of relaxation is not its cost of redirection. The record holds this as a demonstrated result:

"A mode's slowness says how it persists, not how hard it is to redirect: for ȧ = −γa + bu the least quadratic effort to move a from 0 to Δ by time H is 2γΔ²/(b²(1 − e^{−2γH})), which falls as γ falls, so a slower mode is cheaper to redirect through the same input, and a mode with b = 0 cannot be redirected through it at any rate; persistence, redirection and the renewal of a mode's supports are three timescales that need not coincide" (CLAIM-1192, Active, demonstrated).

The idea and formula are ChatGPT's (A40). The monotonicity and the wording are the assistant's: A40 had said a smaller γ "can actually make the displacement cheaper", and the assistant's check showed that, for fixed b and H, the least effort strictly increases with γ and tends to Δ²/(b²H) as γ → 0. The formula is the one-mode case of a general result: "Equal controllability rank does not fix the cost of control", and "the least effort is Δxᵀ W_T^† Δx, set by the Gramian and not by its rank" (CLAIM-345, Active, demonstrated). That result carries a caveat a regulator comparing institutions needs: comparing costs across systems "needs inputs in common units" (CLAIM-345).

The size of the effect is easy to see from the formula. With H = 1 and b fixed, the least effort relative to a neutral mode (γ → 0) is about 1.10 at γ = 0.1, 2.31 at γ = 1 and 10.0 at γ = 5 (computed here from CLAIM-1192's closed form). A mode that relaxes fast resists being held at a new value, because it keeps relaxing back while it is pushed. A slow mode stays where it is put.

The claim names three timescales: "τ_relax (natural persistence), τ_control (least effective intervention time) and τ_reproduce (renewal of the mode's supports)". ChatGPT's illustration: "A legal norm may persist for decades, be suspended in an emergency within minutes, and depend on institutional practices that must be reproduced over years." A note adds a fourth timescale, sensing (how soon a deviation becomes recognizable). It also adds a third factor in a mode's weight for governance, besides persistence and controllability: its consequence for the demanded outcomes. "Another mode may be slow but irrelevant to the specified outcome" (CLAIM-1192, note of 2026-11-23, after A42). Beside it the record holds that "permitted directions, actuated directions and transitions achievable at acceptable cost are three objects, the second contained in the first at each instant" (CLAIM-589, Active, demonstrated).

**An apparent conflict, and where entrenchment can sit.** This result seems to sit badly with §11 and with a step of ChatGPT's claim on the governance of temporal response structure, "Slow modes can entrench error" (CLAIM-1070, Proposed, interpretive), and with the record's notion of destructive lock-in (TERM-164, Proposed). If slow modes are cheaper to redirect, how can slowness entrench? No entry reconciles the two. The negative half of a reconciliation is CLAIM-1192's own: a mode's slowness "says how it persists, not how hard it is to redirect", and its supports renew on a third timescale. In an organization, then, entrenchment cannot sit in slowness itself, measured as a low γ under a fixed outside input. That half restates the claim. The positive half, that entrenchment must sit in one of three other places, each held on the record, was the first draft's join (join JB5: CLAIM-1192 and CLAIM-345 with CLAIM-589, CLAIM-626, CLAIM-679 and CLAIM-400, against CLAIM-1070 and TERM-164). Each of the three places is stated by the entry named below. That the three exhaust where entrenchment can sit is not, and the account of the timing of response and revision lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, a recap as a list, and not exhaustive as a disjunction": the record holds other places, CLAIM-1040's classification of noise (§4.1) and CLAIM-423's authority over the faster components (§7.0) (SYNTHESIS-123, Proposed). So the three below are places entrenchment can sit, not the only ones.

- **In the control channel.** The input can be missing or weak. b = 0 means no redirection "at any rate", and a small b makes it costly whatever γ is (CLAIM-1192; CLAIM-345). A permitted direction need not be actuated (CLAIM-589). Entrenchment here is a fact about who can act on the mode and through what. It is not a fact about how fast the mode relaxes. The assistant's reading carries the one-mode result to direction: a change of given size "is cheap along W's strong directions, costly along its weak ones and impossible outside its range", W the controllability Gramian (CLAIM-998, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's).
- **In the supports.** The processes that renew the mode can fail on τ_reproduce while the mode itself still looks firm: CLAIM-626's maintenance arm. The capacity to move can also be spent by holding still. In the record's resource model, at the limit "the organization can still hold where it is and can reach no configuration whose transition carries any reconfiguration slack" (CLAIM-679, Active, demonstrated). Reconfiguration slack is "the temporary resources a system needs to move between two configurations it can each hold, beyond what holding either one requires, while keeping specified commitments" (TERM-106, Active). §11 develops this.
- **In feedback through maintained states.** The open-loop model of CLAIM-1192 does not cover a mode that feeds back on the processes that set it. In the record's meaning–authority model, a maintained state with persistence λ adds terms in 1/(1 − λ) to the couplings, "the slower it is the more it adds", "so a lean in the regime can outlast the advantages it was built from" (CLAIM-400, Active, demonstrated). There, slowness does entrench, but only through the loop: the persistent state amplifies the processes that regenerate it. An outside input then has to overcome the loop, not just the relaxation. The amplification runs through the state's moving interpretation or conferral, not through every change it makes: the record's account of that model declines a link because "CLAIM-400 says a regime that only varies κ′ moves no linear threshold" (SYNTHESIS-086, Proposed). A reading from another domain puts multistability in the same place: in an ensemble of Boolean rules on the regulatory core of E. coli, a transient knockout can leave the network in a different attractor, and "every gene that does so reaches a strongly connected component containing a positive circuit" (THEORY-178, Active).

The record holds a further distinction that bears on the word. Generative entrenchment, in which later interactions depend on a core, "does not by itself give organizational closure": an entrenched core "can sit at the root of an acyclic dependency order", and such an order "persists only while something outside supplies its core" (CLAIM-531, Active, interpretive). Entrenchment of that kind is a fact about what depends on a core, not about the core's rate.

On this reading CLAIM-1070's step, "Slow modes can entrench error", is shorthand that needs qualifying. A slow mode with an open control channel and intact supports is the easiest kind to redirect. What can entrench is a slow mode whose channel is closed or captured, whose supports are failing or spent, or which reinforces its own regeneration, among other places the list does not exhaust. The record holds a prediction of this kind as ChatGPT's (A40): "Among organizations with matched relaxation spectra, those whose slow modes lack actuated, affordable control directions should fail the demands that need those modes moved." If failures track slowness and not controllability, the claim fails (CLAIM-1066, Proposed, interpretive). The draft found no case or lead that tests it (its gap GB6). One is now filed, with two arms: a model arm of linear organizations with the same eigenvalues and different input matrices, which "checks the reading, not the world", and an empirical arm of organizations "matched on how fast a slow feature relaxes after disturbance" and differing in whether any party controls it through a channel with a known cost (LEAD-219, Open, stage idea, the assistant's). The record has an ablation study of the same shape for an agent's slow structure: ablating it "tells resolve from mere persistence only by what the ablation removes and only in conjunction" (CLAIM-982, Proposed, demonstrated; CASE-383, Proposed).

### 5.3 Can strong stabilization enable adaptation?

The motivating draft's §5 makes a further claim: "Strong organizational stabilization can enable extensive adaptation." Its model splits perturbations of an organization, near the set M_θ of configurations that satisfy its defining relations, into a transverse part and a tangential part:

  ẋ⊥ = −K⊥x⊥ + B⊥u,  ẋ∥ = A∥x∥ + B∥u.

Strong restoration across M_θ (large K⊥) keeps the defining relations, and unconstrained motion along M_θ lets the organization reconfigure within them. ChatGPT's example: an interoperability standard lets production shift among participants without renegotiating the interfaces.

This is ChatGPT's, and the record holds it only as Proposed. Its fullest form is "Identity stabilization and adaptability are different achievements of one temporally organized system, not two competing quantities: an organization keeps identity by selectively conserving some relations while allowing or producing change in others, it can restore its organization fast while transforming slowly within it, what it conserves may be a configuration or the generative continuity by which it revises its configurations, and some of its most important identity-bearing structures may be those that keep its other identity-bearing structures revisable" (CLAIM-1135, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's, A44). Its step: "A strongly stabilized organization can be highly adaptable precisely because it does not need to reconstruct its basic coordination after every disturbance." An earlier form is "Identity stabilization and adaptability are not two ends of one scale", with "a constraint's adequacy" depending "on the timescales of the demands it governs and on how fast it can be revised" (CLAIM-1104, Proposed, interpretive, ChatGPT's, A38). Neither is the record's account. Both wait on Proposed premises: CLAIM-1104 rests on CLAIM-478 and on CLAIM-758 (overconsolidation, §11), and CLAIM-1135 rests on CLAIM-1104 and CLAIM-916. CLAIM-1135's admission says that it "answers the owner's U20 on the weak reading of 'inherent'". That is ChatGPT's claim offering an answer on one reading. It is not a ruling on which reading the owner meant (§3.0.1).

The argument also needs two conditions that neither the motivating draft nor ChatGPT's claims state. The first draft marked them as a gap (GB1). They are now filed with a derivation (CLAIM-1079, below).

- **The tangential directions must be actuated.** Freedom along M_θ is useful only if available inputs can drive it: B∥ must reach the directions a demand needs, at acceptable cost. The record holds that this does not come with permission: a permitted direction need not be actuated, nor an actuated one affordable (CLAIM-589). A standard that removes the need to renegotiate interfaces does not, by itself, give any participant the capacity to take up the shifted production.
- **The blocks must be decoupled.** The model's two equations share only the input. If tangential motion fed the transverse dynamics, for instance through a term Cx∥ in the first equation, or because the restoring rate itself varied along M_θ, then large reconfiguration within the organization would push it off its defining relations. Strong restoration would then have to grow with the excursion, and might not keep up. The draft's inference holds for its block-diagonal model. It is not shown for organizations whose interior reconfiguration loads the very relations being stabilized.

A third condition the draft does state: when a demand needs a change of the defining relations themselves, a change of θ rather than of x, stabilization of M_θ is the obstacle, not the enabler. That case belongs to §§7–8.

The filed result: "In the linear tangent–transverse model with a coupling C x∥ in the transverse equation, the deviation from the defining relations is at most ‖C‖X/κ + ‖B⊥‖‖u‖₂/√(2κ), for κ the least restoring rate and X the largest tangential excursion, so strong stabilization leaves tangential adaptation possible only where the demanded move is actuated and affordable and the restoring rate grows with the excursion it must carry" (CLAIM-1079, Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's). Its first term is tight: "keeping the deviation below ε while carrying an excursion X needs κ ≥ ‖C‖X/ε in the worst case: the restoring rate has to grow in proportion to the excursion". Its actuation condition uses CLAIM-589's objects: the move must lie in the range of the Gramian, and its least effort, in CLAIM-345's form, must be within budget; "Conditions 1 and 2 do not depend on K⊥." The check is a simulation of 300 random systems, in which the largest ratio of the deviation to the bound was 0.953. It says nothing where the restoring rate varies along M_θ, nothing where the demand needs θ itself changed, and "Not that any organization has this linear structure." A case where interior reconfiguration loads a standard's interfaces would still be needed to show any of this outside the model.

Two Active results on aggregation make a related point for discrete blocks. The closure defect of a Simon–Ando aggregate, when every block has equal column sums, "is ε·QC(I − VQ)", first order in the coupling between blocks (CLAIM-386, Active, demonstrated), while a block's dynamical dependence is "of second order in the coupling into the block, whatever the coupling out of it" (CLAIM-390, Active, demonstrated). Weak coupling between blocks is sufficient for a coarse variable to close, not necessary: "in a linear Gaussian VAR(1) with uniform coupling among n units, the sum of all units is exactly dynamically independent at every stable coupling strength, while every partition of the units into two or more groups couples as strongly across its edges as within them" (CLAIM-388, Active, demonstrated; from A's two-unit case, made exact by the filer).
