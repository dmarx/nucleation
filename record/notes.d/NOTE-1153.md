# NOTE-1153: Wolpert & Tumer — Optimal reward functions in distributed reinforcement learning

## Contribution

When many reinforcement learners share a world, the usual design gives
each the world utility G as its reward, the "team game", and this "scales
to large problems very poorly", because each member "can have a hard time
discerning the echo of its behavior on the world utility when the system
is large" (p. 2). The authors' Collective Intelligence (COIN) framework
had replaced it with the Wonderful Life Utility, whose free "clamping
parameter" had been set to zero. This report derives, by a series of
approximations it only sketches, a better value, and shows it does better
in one problem.

## Key insight

Two properties are wanted of a member's private utility γ_η and they pull
apart.

- **Factoredness (alignment).** γ_η(C(ζ₀)) ≥ γ_η(C(ζ′₀)) ⇔
  G(C(ζ₀)) ≥ G(C(ζ′₀)) whenever ζ₀ and ζ′₀ differ only in member η's
  initial state (p. 3, image). Ordinal: the member and the world rank the
  member's alternatives alike. A system factored at every point is one
  where γ_η = Φ_η(ζ outside η's effect set, G(ζ)) with ∂Φ_η/∂G > 0 (the
  Theorem, Eq. 1, p. 3; proof in the authors' earlier work, not here). At
  a factored system's private-utility Nash equilibrium, G is at a critical
  point, so "there can be no TOC for a factored system" (p. 3).
- **Learnability (signal over noise).** How much γ_η moves with η's own
  state against how much it moves with everyone else's (Eq. 2, p. 4). The
  team game γ_η = G is factored but poorly learnable when G depends on
  everyone.

The Wonderful Life Utility, WLU_η = G(ζ) − G(CL(ζ)), subtracts the world
utility with η's effect set clamped to a fixed vector. The subtracted term
does not depend on η's effect set, so the WLU is factored by the Theorem,
and the subtraction removes much of the others' noise. "Note the crucial
fact that to evaluate the WLU one does not need to know how to calculate
the system's behavior under counter-factual starting conditions" (p. 3,
image): G is re-evaluated on the actual worldline with η's part replaced.

## Assumptions

- **Deterministic dynamics** ζ = C(ζ₀), covering pseudo-random computer
  systems (p. 3).
- **Private utilities as sums of rewards** R_η(ζ_t) (p. 3).
- **The effect set** of η, the components its initial state affects, is
  approximated in the experiments by η's own actions alone: "to first
  order, η's actions have no effects on the actions of other agents"
  (p. 4, image).
- **The optimal clamp** is derived by minimising the expected inverse
  square of learnability, through "further approximations — too long to
  go through here" (p. 4, image). The answer is a clamp equal to the
  expected effect set, approximated as one average action for all agents,
  with the action distribution guessed uniform.

## Key results

- **The bar problem (pp. 4–5).** N = 60 agents each week choose one of
  seven actions, each attending a fixed set of l of the seven nights,
  l = 1 to 6. G sums φ(x) = x e^(−x/c) over nights, c raised with l to keep
  congestion level. Learners are Boltzmann choosers on exponentially aged
  reward estimates. Averages over 20 runs.
- **Three clamps (p. 5).** To zero (absence), WL⁰: φ(x) − φ(x − 1) on the
  member's night, computable from that night's attendance alone. To all
  ones, WL¹: the change over the other six nights. To the average action
  1/7, WLᵃ.
- **Figure 1a.** WLᵃ "performs well for all problems"; WL¹ poorly for few
  nights and as well as WLᵃ at six; WL⁰ "shows a slight drop in
  performance" as nights increase; G "a much more pronounced drop", poor
  throughout (pp. 5–6).
- **Figure 1b.** At l = 1, WLᵃ is insensitive to the learners' temperature
  until choice is nearly random; WL⁰ needs more exploration and has a
  narrower good range; WL¹ and G are worse (p. 6).

## Concepts

- **World utility G**: the designer's measure over the whole history.
- **Private utility γ_η**: what member η's learner maximises.
- **Team game**: γ_η = G for all η.
- **Factored**: member and world rank the member's alternatives alike,
  others fixed.
- **Effect set**: the components of the history η's initial state can
  affect.
- **Clamping**: replacing η's effect set by a fixed vector in G's
  argument; the Wonderful Life Utility is G minus G so clamped.
- **Learnability**: the ratio of a utility's sensitivity to η's own state
  to its sensitivity to others' (Eq. 2).

## Connections

The source of COMA's counterfactual baseline ([LIT-1517](../literature.d/LIT-1517.md)), which cites
this programme as difference rewards and its expectation version as the
"aristocrat utility". The El Farol problem is Arthur's (1994); the
tragedy of the commons and Braess' paradox are the failures factoredness
is meant to rule out. The ACS paper ([LIT-1515](../literature.d/LIT-1515.md)) restates the programme
"using a novel mathematical framework" and adds the Aristocrat Utility, by
its abstract.

## Bearing on the record

- **The lead's description, "what if this process had not acted", needs
  two corrections.**
  - The WLU is not a causal counterfactual. It re-scores the actual
    history with η's contribution replaced and everyone else's behaviour
    as it was; it needs no model of how others would have responded, and
    the paper says so as its advantage (p. 3). A process's credit under it
    is its marginal contribution against a fixed background, not what the
    organization would have done without it.
  - "Had not acted", the zero clamp, was not the best clamp. The expected
    action did better, and the paper's sketch derives why: the clamp that
    maximises learnability is what the member typically does. COMA later
    made the same move.
- **[CLAIM-446](../claims.d/CLAIM-446.md) (identifiability).** Irrelevant to the trap, relevant
  to its diagnosis. The WLU needs G evaluable at the clamped history,
  which in a congestion game is a formula. An organization that cannot
  evaluate the outcome without its defensive process, because it has never
  run without it, cannot compute this credit either. What the paper offers
  the claim is a vocabulary: the defensive process's private utility can
  be factored (aligned with the organization's) and still poorly
  learnable, and poor learnability, not misalignment, is what the WLU
  fixes.
- **[QUESTION-270](../questions.d/QUESTION-270.md) and [QUESTION-271](../questions.d/QUESTION-271.md), failure 4.** The WLU is
  computed for every member on every step, heeded or not, because every
  member acts and G is known. It is a procedure for full-information
  credit, not for bandit feedback. Nothing here credits a process whose
  alternative is never realised.
- **[CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md) and [CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md) (ordering and currency).** A sharp
  complement. Factoredness is purely ordinal, member by member: it asks
  only that each member's utility rank its own options as the collective
  does, the others fixed, and its characterisation allows any increasing
  transform. It is the same condition QMIX's supplement states for values
  ([NOTE-1154](NOTE-1154.md)). So the record now holds an ordinal alignment condition
  between members' and collective's orderings that needs no common scale
  among the members, and a theorem that it holds exactly when each
  member's utility is a monotone function of the collective's.
  [CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md)'s "common currency" is G here, given by the designer; the
  paper's question is how to hand it to members so that they learn.
- **[CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md) and [CLAIM-416](../claims.d/CLAIM-416.md) (endorsement and tests).** The team game is a
  test that is fair (factored) and uninformative (unlearnable); the WLU is
  a test as fair and more informative. That is a precise instance of
  [CLAIM-416](../claims.d/CLAIM-416.md)'s distinction between a test's fairness and what success under
  it shows.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice is drawn here.

## Limitations

- **A short report.** The optimal-clamp derivation is a sketch, and the
  central theorem's proof is elsewhere.
- **One problem, one learner, sixty agents.** The effect set is
  approximated by the member's own actions, ignoring effects on others.
- **Not the paper the lead named.** The ACS paper adds a framework and the
  Aristocrat Utility; whether it changes the results here is not known.
- **The scan.** The OCR is broken on several pages, and Figure 1's plots
  are too small to read values from; the results are taken from the text.
- **Wording.** The conclusion's "These results confirm our theoretical
  prediction" (p. 6, image) is stronger than one experiment with a sketched
  derivation supports.

## Open questions

- How does the average-action clamp compare with COMA's policy-weighted
  baseline when agents specialise and the action distribution is far from
  uniform? The authors expect the uniform guess to be "more accurate at
  early times" (p. 4).
- What does the WLU credit when members' actions do change each other's
  behaviour, so the effect set is not one's own actions?
