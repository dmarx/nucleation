# CLAIM-tmpxl4mg: A performance tolerance and a normative constraint do different work: an error budget permits a specified rate of service variation and allocates corrective effort by it, while a right determines what conduct is permissible toward each holder and may require remedy for an isolated violation, so a statistical tolerance for failures does not make any failure within it acceptable

## The claim

A36 §20: "Error budgets are valuable in SRE because reliability and
innovation compete for finite resources." "But the analogy has an
essential limitation. A constitutionally protected right is not simply an
availability target. An agency might reasonably establish a service
objective for processing applications within a specified time. That does
not imply a corresponding entitlement to violate a fraction of applicants'
rights." Its table:

| Performance tolerance | Normative constraint |
|---|---|
| Permits specified service variation | Determines what conduct is permissible |
| Can support statistical evaluation | May protect particular individuals |
| Can trigger reprioritization | May require remedy even for isolated violations |
| Can be negotiated within delegated authority | May not be waivable by the institution being evaluated |

"A statistical tolerance can help allocate corrective resources without
establishing that every failure within that tolerance is morally or
legally acceptable." A36 offers this as "an important contribution of the
normative portion of our framework to SRE itself".

## What it adds to the record

[CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md) holds that a description adequate for an institution's
aggregate performance can merge states its members' claims must
distinguish. This claim is the decision-side counterpart: a tolerance set
on the aggregate allocates effort and does not settle what is owed to the
person in the merged state.

## Gap filled: the law prices error too

The table puts "Can support statistical evaluation" on one side only. US
procedural due process does not. Mathews v. Eldridge ([LIT-tmpvluk2](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvluk2.md))
weighs, among three factors, "the risk of an erroneous deprivation of such
interest through the procedures used, and probable value, if any, of
additional procedural safeguards" (syllabus, checked on the LII page on
2026-11-24). How much procedure a right gets is set partly by an accepted
rate of error. So the distinction cannot be that rights admit no
tolerance. What survives is narrower: an error rate can justify how much
procedure to provide, and cannot by itself license an identified wrong
decision or extinguish the claim to have it corrected. The claim is
stated in that narrower form, "does not make any failure within it
acceptable", and the first defeater asks whether even that holds. The
section is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the
owner's call.
