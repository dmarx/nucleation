# LIT-tmpr5hsy: Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason

Michael E. Bratman (1987), *Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason*,
Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press (viii + 200 pp.); reissued Stanford:
CSLI Publications, 1999 — <https://openlibrary.org/works/OL55014W>

**On the citation.** The brief's details are right: author, title, 1987, a
book. It has no DOI. The record's URL is the Open Library work record, the
most stable public catalogue entry found.

## Key takeaways

*Not a reading. What is recorded here is what Bratman's 2018 essay
([LIT-tmp5j4f4](LIT-tmp5j4f4.md)) cites the book for.*

- Intentions are elements of partial, hierarchical plans. They settle
  practical questions, pose problems of means, and filter options. A prior
  intention is not a "ghostly hand" that reaches over time. It works by
  persisting, the 2018 essay citing p. 5.
- Reasonable agents often do not reconsider their prior plans, given the
  costs and risks of reconsideration. The 2018 essay calls this "a general
  theme" of the book.
- The "snowball effect": executing an intention can create new reasons to
  stick with it (p. 82, as cited).
