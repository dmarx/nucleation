# QUESTION-049: When a corporation is said to know or intend something no member does, is the attribution tracking a mind the corporation has, or shaping the corporation to meet it?

## Why it is a question

- [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md): a corporation that forms its judgments through authorized
  avowal and corrects the inconsistency of aggregating its members' views
  "has a mind of its own", and is fit to be held responsible.
- [THEORY-137](../theory.d/THEORY-137.md): attributions of mental states to institutions "work largely
  by shaping the institutions to which they are made rather than by
  describing states they already had", "whether or not institutions really
  have minds".

The two are not strictly incompatible. A regulative attribution could
build a mind that then exists. But they disagree about what the
attribution is evidence of, and so about how [QUESTION-009](QUESTION-009.md) could be
settled by looking at how corporations are treated.

What would count as an answer: a case where corporate "knowledge" or
intention persists, and guides action, without regulative uptake, which
would favour [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md). Or one where it appears only once a norm is
imposed and lapses when the norm is lifted, which would favour [THEORY-137](../theory.d/THEORY-137.md).

## Note of 2026-10-12: a typed partial answer

[THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) now carries an `answered_by` edge, for the tracking side.
