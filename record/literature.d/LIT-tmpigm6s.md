# LIT-tmpigm6s: The varieties of contemplative experience: A mixed-methods study of meditation-related challenges in Western Buddhists

Jared R. Lindahl, Nathan E. Fisher, David J. Cooper, Rochelle K. Rosen, Willoughby B. Britton (2017), *PLOS ONE 12(5), e0176239* — DOI-10.1371/journal.pone.0176239

## Key takeaways

- Among 60 Western Buddhist meditators, purposively sampled for difficulties, plus teacher-experts, meditation-related experiences fall into 59 categories across 7 domains (cognitive, perceptual, affective, somatic, conative, sense of self, social). The same experience can be very positive or very negative, and 73% reported moderate-to-severe impairment in at least one domain, with a median symptom duration of 1–3 years.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Meditation is promoted for health, drawing on Buddhist sources, but those sources also describe many other effects. The Varieties of Contemplative Experience (VCE) study looks at under-reported experiences: challenging, distressing or impairing ones, or ones needing extra support. It combined qualitative interviews with Western practitioners and experts in the Theravāda, Zen and Tibetan traditions with a follow-up survey on causality, impairment and practice variables. Thematic analysis produced a taxonomy of 59 experiences in 7 domains and 26 categories of influencing factors in 4 domains (practitioner, practice, relationships, health behaviours). Interpretation, valence and severity varied widely even when the phenomenology was similar.
