# NOTE-tmpe43ee: Rubel, Castro and Pham — Agency Laundering and Information Technologies, chapter 7

## Contribution

A five-condition definition of agency laundering that separates it from
honest delegation ("Democratic Chair") and from responsibility gaps, and
applies it to private and public algorithmic systems. The mechanism "need
not be technological at all" (p. 160).

## Key results

- **The concept** (p. 137): "obfuscating one's moral responsibility by
  enlisting a technology or process to take some action and letting it
  forestall others from demanding an account for bad outcomes that
  result."
- **Definition** (p. 143): an agent a launders their agency where "(1) a is
  morally responsible with respect to some domain X, and (2) a ensures that
  b (some process, person, or entity) has some causal responsibility with
  respect to X, and (3) a ascribes (implicitly or explicitly) morally
  relevant qualities to b's actions (e.g., relevance, neutrality,
  reliability), and (4) in virtue of (2) and (3), a obscures the scope of
  their causal responsibility with respect to X, and (5) in virtue of (4), a
  fails to adequately account for events within X for which they are
  morally responsible."
- **The wrong** (p. 144): "the laundering part of agency laundering cuts
  straight to the heart of what responsibility is by undermining the
  ability of others to ask the agent to provide an account."
- **Delegation is not laundering** (pp. 144–145): in "Democratic Chair" the
  committee "is not a sham", so condition (5) fails.
- **Uber** (pp. 150–152): surge pricing is laundering: "Surge periods are
  not a natural feature about the world that Uber measures, but a period
  defined and deployed by Uber", and "The market is setting the price" is
  "an inadequate account". Goal reminders and queuing are not: condition
  (4) fails.
- **COMPAS in Loomis** (§7.6): not laundering, since the court requires
  other factors and so addresses condition (5); "it is the decision to
  deploy the system that requires an account" (p. 157).
- **Gaps as a means** (p. 157): "invoking the idea of a responsibility gap
  is a mechanism by which people may launder their agency."
- **Humans in the loop** (pp. 159–160): "having humans in the loop may
  itself obscure causal and moral responsibility"; of Elish's crumple zone,
  "In our conception, humans' causal responsibility could obscure the
  causal responsibility of a technical system"; and "humans in the loop
  can be a means of laundering just as well as automation itself."
- **Left open** (p. 161): "can a person who uses a tool to make decisions
  launder their agency inadvertently? This appears compatible with our
  definition".

## Assumptions

- An accountability view of responsibility (Smith, Oshana): to be
  responsible is to be one of whom an account may be demanded.
- The laundering agent is already morally responsible in the domain.
- Laundering needs a misleading ascription of qualities to the process.

## Concepts

- **Agency laundering**.
- **Role, causal and capacity responsibility**, in a Hart-style structure.
- **Chair** and **Democratic Chair**.
- **Bias laundering** (Cegłowski) and **masking** (Barocas and Selbst).

## Bearing on the record

Filed as [THEORY-tmpwjlal](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwjlal.md).

- **Which of the original's results it restates.** By the authors' account,
  the 2019 article "in large part": the definition, the wrong and the
  cases. Whether the discussion of Elish and humans in the loop (§7.7.2) is
  in the article is not known.
- **[CLAIM-tmpb47ul](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpb47ul.md)**, the upstream half: an agent responsible in a domain
  can, through a process, obscure its causal role and escape the demand
  for an account. The chapter links this to Elish (p. 159): a human in the
  loop can be the laundering medium, which is the claim's downstream half.
  It also qualifies the claim: laundering needs a misleading ascription and
  a failed account, and whether it can be inadvertent is left open, while
  the claim says "Neither concept requires a conscious mastermind".
  Corroborates.
- **[CLAIM-472](../claims.d/CLAIM-472.md)** (choosing an apparatus is open to evaluation): Democratic
  Chair and the COMPAS discussion put the account owed in "the decision to
  deploy the system" (p. 157). Corroborates.

  is a neighbouring taxonomy of senses, with no constitutive sense.
