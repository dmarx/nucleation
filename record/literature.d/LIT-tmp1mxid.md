# LIT-tmp1mxid: Quantum mechanics near closed timelike lines

David Deutsch (1991), *Physical Review D* 44(10), 3197–3217 — DOI-10.1103/PhysRevD.44.3197

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for, and by
how A161 uses it.

- A quantum system that interacts with a copy of itself on a closed
  timelike curve is required to be self-consistent as a density matrix:
  the state on the loop is a fixed point of the map the interaction
  induces on it.
- Such a fixed point always exists, so the grandfather paradox has no
  quantum counterpart in this model. Its classical shadow is the negation
  loop: x = ¬x has no solution in {0,1}, but p ↦ 1 − p has the fixed
  point p = ½. A161 gives exactly this: "A distributional fixed point
  exists even though no deterministic fixed point exists."

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-10-10 to ground [CLAIM-tmpc4chg](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpc4chg.md), which contrasts
causal-loop consistency with sheaf contextuality: passing to distributions
creates a fixed point for the first and gives no global distribution for
the second. See the curation entry of that day.

`Deferred` because it is not read. Two things a reading should check:

- whether the paper states existence for every finite-dimensional system
  and every interaction, as the record assumes from the Brouwer-type
  argument, and what it says about uniqueness, which is the open issue;
- how it relates its condition to the classical consistency condition of
  Friedman et al. 1990 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.42.1915, not held), which
  A161 cites beside it.

Lloyd et al.'s post-selected closed timelike curves (arXiv:1005.2219, not
held) are a rival model that treats the grandfather paradox differently.
The record does not take Deutsch's model to be the physics of closed
timelike curves; it uses only its fixed-point condition, as a contrast.
