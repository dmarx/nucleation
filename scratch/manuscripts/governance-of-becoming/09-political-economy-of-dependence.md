# Article 9. The Political Economy of Dependence

## Cooperation, exploitation, domination, and the governance of contractual costs

**Abstract.** Successful provision can produce dependence. Where a capacity is learned by doing and decays with disuse, supply that does the work lowers the capacity a party keeps. Where growth also needs capacity already present, supply that does all the work has a deadline after which withdrawal no longer restores the capacity. These results are Active and demonstrated, in models with invented parameters. This article sets out what the Nucleation record holds about what happens next, and with what standing. Everything that turns such a dependence into exploitation or domination is Proposed. That includes the thesis that what sorts a dependence is who controls the terms on which it is met, the test for when dependence is presumptively domination by its controllers, and the claims on exploited conscientiousness. The record defines exploitation and domination only relative to a standard declared with each judgment, and it supplies no such standard. One step was missing: a model in which the terms move as the fallback decays. The assistant ran one for this article, and it is a Proposed-grade computation with illustrative parameters. Put into a Nash bargain as the party's outside option, the decaying capacity moves the split against the party while it never exits and no term is rewritten. The fall can be undone by ending the supply up to the capacity deadline, and not after it. Under capacity-first supply, or where the party cannot leave, the decay moves nothing. None of this shows that any arrangement is exploitative or dominating. A51 is right that what remains is comparative evidence and normative argument.

### 9.1 Dependence produced by successful provision

A51 says the mechanism of capability substitution "is not merely a speculative possibility awaiting its first model". That is right for the models.

**Supply that does the work.** "outside supply that does the work first lowers the capacity kept, and the longer it does all of it the longer recovery takes once it stops, while supply that covers only what the organization cannot yet do leaves its learning untouched, so what decides dependence is the order in which supply and capacity are used, not the amount of supply" (CLAIM-602, Active, demonstrated; the assistant's). In its model, dC/dt = λP(1 − C) − μC, capacity settles at "0.625 at A = 0.5 and 0.25 at A = 0.9", against 0.769 alone. After full substitution, demand is met again "only after 3.84, 6.38 and 11.38 units for substitution lasting 10, 20 and 40". "Service is whole throughout, so nothing looks wrong while the supply continues."

**A deadline.** Where growth also needs capacity already present, dC/dt = λPC(1 − C) − μC, "C below 0.276 decays to nothing" and support that does all the work has a deadline: "after ln(0.7/0.276)/0.3 = 3.10 time units capacity has crossed the threshold, and withdrawal no longer restores it" (CLAIM-605, Active, demonstrated; the assistant's; it rests on CLAIM-602 and on CLAIM-604, Active, demonstrated). Tapering is worse the slower it is: from C = 0.45 under support doing 95 % of the work, "any taper longer than 9.13 periods traps the party". "A gentler withdrawal is the riskier one." Capacity-first support "needs no taper, since it withdraws itself, but it cannot lift a party already below the threshold" (CLAIM-605). A restriction that removes the doing reaches the same kind of deadline, 1.98 time units in its model (CLAIM-950, Proposed, demonstrated, on CASE-364, Proposed, executed study).

**Trajectories, not present sets.** "support, or a technology, cannot be ranked by present capability sets and has to be assessed by the capability trajectory it produces" (CLAIM-675, Active, demonstrated; A's, "Analyze technological civilization trap"). It was re-derived at its promotion: "C = 0.156 against 0.7 at t = 5". Its source's limit: the model is "not empirical evidence that a particular technology produces such decline". Article 5 (§5.8.2) cites it for operational gain with developmental cost.

**The cross-domain account, told at its standing.** A51 says "The Black Queen mechanism, endosymbiosis, substitutive assistance and the Samaritan's dilemma already supply a cross-domain account". The record holds the readings and leaves their joining Proposed.

- *Black Queen.* Loss of a leaky, costly function is "favoured by ordinary individual selection in large free-living microbial populations", and it stops "until the remaining producers just supply the community" (THEORY-1048, Active).
- *Samaritan's dilemma.* "predictable aid weakens the recipient's own upkeep" (THEORY-1480, Active, as a report of Ostrom and colleagues' account). The entry says the evidence is "five short illustrative field cases with no counterfactual".
- *Endosymbiosis.* In one model "mutual dependence ... evolves ahead of reproductive cohesion", "but only under the mapping" its authors' appendix qualifies (THEORY-1050, Active). The account of the symbioses is that "which properties each route to dependence brings about, and which make the partnership a new individual, is open" (SYNTHESIS-053, Proposed).
- *The join.* "A capability lost because another reliably supplies it is the Black Queen's loss ... and whether it is dependency or division of labour is decided by the supply, not the loss" (CLAIM-603, Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's, on the owner's remark of 2026-11-05). Two holds stand on it: "The title should be narrowed to the organizational case it concedes", and "Moore's thesis (THEORY-1459) is not held" (note of 2026-11-06). The line's account declines the strong reading: it does not say "That aid recipients are like microbes in mechanism" (SYNTHESIS-032, Proposed, "What it does not say").

So the record holds a demonstrated mechanism in models, readings of it in several domains, and a Proposed claim that joins them. "Already supply" holds for the first. CLAIM-603's three-arm test, by the capability trajectory under withdrawal, is proposed in its note of 2026-11-13 and not run (LEAD-166, Open, costed; LEAD-197, Open, specified, for learners).

### 9.2 Producing a dependency, and encumbering one

A51 asks the article to keep apart "dependency-producing exploitation" and "creating a new encumbrance within a preexisting dependency". The record keeps them apart at the owner's word: the owner's review of #488 asked that the distinction "not be lost" when CLAIM-669 was narrowed (CLAIM-670's summary).

- **Producing it.** "An arrangement can produce the dependence through which it exploits: by reliably supplying what a party then ceases to supply for itself, it creates a reliance on the provider that the party did not have before, and the provider can use that reliance to set terms the party could earlier have refused" (CLAIM-670, Proposed, interpretive; A's sense, A9 §2 of "About Plantation Paradise"). It rests on CLAIM-603. Its own summary says "No reading supports it yet". A second route to produced dependence is the party's own relationship-specific investment, hold-up (note of 2026-11-17). Its sources are not read (Limits).
- **Encumbering it.** "An arrangement can exploit through the access it grants: a needed good offered on terms that leave the recipient with a lasting encumbrance ... that narrows later exit or renegotiation" (CLAIM-669, Proposed, interpretive; A's). Its one reading is Seamster and Charron-Chénier's "predatory inclusion", where "the need predates the access, the encumbrance is debt, and exit is closed by bankruptcy law".

Two further claims bear on the baseline. "A technology can become practically compulsory without any rule requiring it", so "the freedom an arrangement leaves is measured by what remains open to a non-adopter" (CLAIM-677, Proposed, interpretive; A's). And "the present distribution of options is not a neutral baseline, and an actor cannot justify its terms by the narrowness of options it helped narrow without justification" (CLAIM-747, Proposed, interpretive; A's; it rests on CLAIM-670).

CLAIM-670's note of 2026-11-24 gives its conclusion in a bargaining model, A22 §9's. "As B's relationship-specific investment lowers o_B, B's share falls by half of each unit lost". A22 draws the point: "The parties may continue to consent to the terms. But what they can afford to refuse has changed." A22's own caption: "This establishes bargaining vulnerability, not by itself exploitation or injustice." In that model o_B is lowered by hand. §9.4 lets it fall by CLAIM-605's dynamics.

### 9.3 Control over the terms

A51's "culminating argument concerns control over the terms of dependence, rather than dependence or contribution considered in isolation". That is the record's thesis, and it is Proposed.

**The thesis.** "the same magnitude of dependence can run either way, and what sorts it is who controls the terms on which it is met, exit, renegotiation and the conditions of supply, while the magnitude and the integration that produced it set only how much rides on it" (CLAIM-690, Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's). Its ground is a fictional contrast, through ARG-110 (Proposed), whose own summary says "that magnitude never sorts a dependence is not shown".

**The contrast.** CASE-264 (Proposed, thought experiment, fictional; the assistant's) has two workshops with surplus S = 10, the same dependence each way, the same integration (CLAIM-603's route, run the same way in both) and the same membership. In A, P can withdraw and renegotiate, and the symmetric Nash solution gives P "a_P + (S − a_P − a_R)/2 = 0 + (10 − 0 − 2)/2 = 4". In B, R sets P's terms and P cannot leave: "R pays the least that keeps P working, r = 1, and keeps 9". The change in P's share per unit of the whole's need is 1/2 in A and 0 in B, and in B R can put P's payoff anywhere "from 1 to 8, a width of 7". What it does not show: "That the numbers are the world's", and "That B is exploitation".

**Not reciprocity.** CLAIM-603's title frames the aim "as reciprocity". Its note of 2026-11-12 records A6 §2's objection, "reciprocal dependence is neither sufficient for freedom nor sufficient for justice", and answers that "mutual dependence of outcomes is not mutual control of terms". A51 puts control, not reciprocity, at the centre, and so does this article (J9.3).

**A fallback that works unused.** "A fallback capacity can matter without being used: it keeps a party able to refuse, and so changes the terms others offer" (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1, Proposed, interpretive; A24 §7, answering the owner's U19). The bargaining step is the assistant's: "each unit of B's outside option raises B's share by half a unit, and lowers A's by the same, **whether or not B ever takes the outside option**". The claim separates "Operational redundancy" from "Constitutional redundancy", and it holds that "optimizing away every apparently unused capacity can inadvertently optimize away the conditions of non-domination" (quoting A24). Its own limit: "Not that a party with a fallback is undominated", since a fallback secures only exit among CLAIM-690's three.

**Residual control.** "a relationship fair in every specified term can still leave one party's terms at the other's discretion, and that discretion is exercised, and seen, only when an uncovered contingency arises" (CLAIM-728, Proposed, interpretive; A's, A53 §2 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse", on the owner's U24 connection to contract theory). Its argument from the economics, ARG-210, is **Rejected**: the readings "tell against step 3, locating the effect of residual control *ex ante*, through anticipation". CLAIM-728's note of 2026-11-22 leaves two alternatives for the owner. A widens the timing, at the cost that the claim "no longer separates cleanly from an ordinary account of anticipated bargaining power". B keeps the timing and narrows the scope to "the *visible exercise* of residual authority". This article uses CLAIM-728 only as a form control can take, and it does not choose between them.

### 9.4 Terms that move as the fallback decays (the assistant's computation)

**What was missing.** The record holds the decay of a capacity under substitutive supply (CLAIM-605), and it holds the Nash split that moves with an outside option (CASE-264, CLAIM-tmp2jhc1, CLAIM-670's note). In the second, the outside option is set by hand. No entry joins them, so no model has the terms moving as the fallback decays. The digest searched for one by mechanism ("hold-up, bargain, outside option, threat point, Nash, fallback ..."; its §9) and found the joint dynamics "not done". The assistant searched the "Not stated on the record" sections of every account for these terms, CLAIM-605 and CASE-264. None declines the join. One lead names the question. LEAD-tmpeo8wq (Open, idea) keeps open "whether solo reach's shrinkage, not a price, moves the terms the other can offer (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1)" (its first note of 2026-11-24). Article 5 (§5.6) cites that note.

**Status of what follows.** This is the assistant's computation for this article. It has not been put to the record, checked by anyone else, or tested under ADR-075. It is Proposed-grade at most, and every parameter it adds is illustrative. The script is `analysis/fallback_decay_bargaining.py` (run with `python3 -I`; deterministic; it prints its figures and "checks pass"). It was run on 2026-11-25, on this branch at c31c8d6e, with the script uncommitted.

**The construction.**

- *Capacity.* P's capacity C follows CLAIM-605's model with its parameters (λ = 1, μ = 0.3, τ = 1.5). Under full substitution (A = 1), C(t) = 0.7e^(−0.3t). The script recovers CLAIM-605's threshold 0.2764, kept state 0.7 and deadline 3.098.
- *The bargain.* CASE-264's arm A is used: S = 10, R's stand-alone value o_R = 2, and the symmetric Nash split u_P = o_P + (S − o_P − o_R)/2. The script recovers CASE-264's 4 and 6 at o_P = 0, and CLAIM-tmp2jhc1's 8, 9 and 10.
- *The fallback.* P's outside option o_P(t) is what P could secure alone from its capacity at t. It is valued forward: w times the annuitized, discounted value of P's stand-alone output min(τC_s, 1), with C_s following the no-support dynamics from C(t). The added parameters are w = 4 (the value per unit time of meeting the whole demand alone), discount rate r = 0.25 (the baseline, fixed before the run) and a horizon of 80. So P's share starts at 6. A second valuation, current output only (o_P = w·min(τC, 1)), is reported beside it. These are the two readings LEAD-tmpeo8wq's second note of 2026-11-24 leaves open: capacity as the actor's state, or a cost of reaching that "includes rebuilding the capacity".

**Expectation, before the run.** No entry states one. The assistant's, set down before running: P's share falls as C decays, with a step at CLAIM-605's deadline, because a fallback that will recover is worth far more than one that will not. *Falsifier:* no change in the rate of fall at the deadline.

1. **The terms move with no exit and no rewritten term.** P's share falls from 6.000 to 4.199 over six time units, and R's rises by the same. Each unit of fallback lost moves half a unit to R (checked at every step). That is CLAIM-tmp2jhc1's threat-point property, run on a fallback the supply itself erodes.
2. **The fall is steepest at the deadline, and it is a step only for a patient party.** At r = 0.25 the largest fall per 0.05 is in the interval centred at t = 3.075, against a deadline of 3.098. A third of the fall by t = 6 lies within half a unit of the deadline (33 %, between t = 2.6 and 3.6). How step-like the fall is depends on the discount rate. Within half a unit of the deadline lie 85 % of it at r = 0.02, 73 % at 0.05, 56 % at 0.1 and 22 % at 0.5. For r ≤ 0.25 the steepest interval is at 3.075. At r = 0.5 it moves to t = 2.275. The expectation is borne out for the location of the steepest fall at r ≤ 0.25. It fails as a step except for a patient party.
3. **Before the deadline the terms can be restored, and after it they cannot.** Ending the supply at T = 2.0 or 3.0 and waiting 20 units restores P's fallback toward 4 (o_P = 4.000 and 3.916). Ending it at T = 3.5 or 5.0 leaves o_P at 0.041 and 0.003, and P's share near 4, CASE-264's arm A with nothing to fall back on. In this model CLAIM-605's deadline for withdrawal is also a deadline for the terms.
4. **Valuing the fallback by current output misses the deadline.** Once τC < 1, the current-output valuation leaves P less than the forward one before the deadline and more after it. The two cross in the interval (3.05, 3.1), where C passes the threshold. Its own fall is steepest at t = 0.225. Under the first of LEAD-tmpeo8wq's two readings the terms still move, but the deadline does not show in them.

**Controls.**

- *Capacity-first supply (a negative control).* Capacity, the fallback and P's share stay at 0.7, 4 and 6.000 for t in [0, 6].
- *Partial substitution below CLAIM-605's a\* = 0.585 (A = 0.5).* Capacity settles at 0.400, above the threshold, and the share at 5.535, with no cliff. This matches the note of 2026-11-13 on CLAIM-605, that substitution below a* "has no deadline".
- *CLAIM-602's model, which has no threshold (a control that could fail).* The same pipeline gives a smooth fall, from 6.000 to 5.547 over [0, 6], with a largest fall per 0.05 of 0.005 against 0.042. So the concentration at the deadline comes from CLAIM-605's threshold and not from the pipeline.
- *CASE-264's arm B (P cannot leave).* P gets 1 whatever its fallback is worth, so the decay moves nothing. This holds by construction, and it is CLAIM-690's point in the dynamic case: whether the decay matters at all depends on control over exit.
- *Take-it-or-leave-it with exit.* Here R offers P exactly its fallback, so P bears the whole fall, 4.000 to 0.399, twice the Nash fall.

**What it does not show.**

- *That the numbers are the world's.* Every parameter is CLAIM-605's, CASE-264's or invented here (w, r, the horizon).
- *That anyone intends it or gains by prolonging it.* The supply is exogenous. R's cost of supplying, and so whether R would choose to supply past the deadline, is not modelled. That would be the next computation (G9.2).
- *That the fall is exploitation or domination.* No standard is declared, as TERM-123 and TERM-124 require. The model shows "bargaining vulnerability", in A22's phrase, produced by supply.
- *Anything about reach over TERM-114, the authority index or the four predicates* that LEAD-tmpeo8wq asks for. It is a scalar capacity and a scalar split.

**What would go back to the record.** A candidate claim, in the assistant's voice, Proposed and demonstrated in the model. Where a party's fallback is a capacity learned by doing that needs capacity to grow (CLAIM-605), and its terms are bargained against that fallback (CASE-264's arm A), substitutive supply moves the terms against it while it never exits. The movement can be reversed by ending the supply until CLAIM-605's deadline and not after. Under capacity-first supply, or where the party cannot exit, the decay does not move the terms. It would rest on CLAIM-605 and CASE-264, complement CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 and CLAIM-670, and carry dated notes on LEAD-tmpeo8wq and CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 (J9.1; G9.1). The joins test has not been run on it.

### 9.5 The cost of contesting

A49 asks about "Resources for contestation and renegotiation". The record holds constructions for this, done in part.

- **Friction moves.** "The cost of imposing a decision and the cost of questioning, suspending or reversing it are separate, so an institution can lower the first while the second stays the same or rises" (CLAIM-731, Proposed, interpretive; A's, developing the owner's CLAIM-710).
- **Siege.** "an actor can make a legally recognized corrective objective unreachable for another party, with the merits, the law and that party's preferences unchanged, by raising what pursuing the objective costs, in spending or in time, above what that party can sustain until relief arrives" (CLAIM-748, Proposed, demonstrated; **the owner's**, U28 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse": "One party 'outlasting' the other in the courts can almost be interpreted as a form of siege warfare"). Its ground is CASE-287 (Proposed, construction, fictional; A's, every number "rechecked with python3 -I"). A claimant "nets 60 if judgment takes four months and nothing if the defendant can stretch it to seven", and "halving the claimant's runway from eight months to four cuts its chance of relief from 86 to 63 per cent". Whether using it is wrongful is QUESTION-479.
- **Leverage from a breach.** "the breaching party gains leverage over the resolution of its own breach" (CLAIM-716, Active, interpretive; A's, at the owner's U25, which named the case; its numbers rechecked at promotion: "B accepts 25 against 20").
- **Speed.** In a queueing model, "at ten times the speed the share of objectionable actions stopped before irreversibility fell from 0.994 to 0.396" (CLAIM-879, Proposed, demonstrated; the assistant's, on CASE-316, Proposed, executed study).

These are the cost of contesting terms. §9.4 models the cost of leaving them. No model joins the two (G9.3).

### 9.6 Conscientiousness, moral commitment and the price of exit

A51's "subsequent sections examine the exploitation of conscientiousness, moral reliability, induced vulnerability and hidden regulatory subsidies". The record holds each as a Proposed mechanism, built on one Active distinction.

- **Governed through sound values.** "One party can wrongfully bring about circumstances in which another incurs a genuine obligation, so that the second does right in discharging it and is still wronged", and "the obligation so created is no larger than the circumstances support" (CLAIM-703, Active, interpretive; A13's, at the owner's U7, after Keller). Article 6 (§6.2) counts it as the third route of reflexive control.
- **Commitment as a resource.** "A moral commitment is exploitable as a resource: because a committed party reliably acts on it, whoever controls the circumstances that trigger it can capture what its fulfilment yields and leave its costs with that party", "with the burden falling hardest on its most conscientious members" (CLAIM-713, Proposed, interpretive; A15, at the owner's U8; it rests on CLAIM-703). A15's example is a hospital that "funds 85 of the 100 units of care it needs". Its defeater ends: "the claim is a mechanism, not a frequency".
- **The hidden subsidy.** Where conscientious compensation covers a shortfall and the organization budgets to what it observes, "the compensation and the quiet it buys are read as adequacy, resources are cut toward what the present configuration needs" (CLAIM-700, Proposed, interpretive; A33, at the owner's U16). It separates from its premises by a case: under "a statutory nurse-to-patient ratio", "no rule budgets to what is observed, so no cut follows and the loop does not close". Its measurement link, that "The system's failure is attributed to the person who stopped compensating for it", is CLAIM-702 (Proposed, interpretive; A's). Of SYNTHESIS-099's readings, "none corroborates" CLAIM-726, CLAIM-702 or CLAIM-700 (SYNTHESIS-095, Proposed). A22 §11's "Hidden regulatory subsidy" is "A restatement by the same kind of source, not independent evidence" (CLAIM-700, note of 2026-11-24).

**QUESTION-472, A51's "distinctive moral dimension".** It asks: "Can a party's ability to activate another's genuine moral obligations constitute control over the terms of that other's dependence ... and if so, what separates it from ordinary reliance on someone's conscientiousness?" **Price or bar.** The record holds two ways in which the terms of withdrawal can be controlled, and they should not be merged. CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 and §9.4 move terms by a price: the value of the fallback in the disagreement point. CLAIM-tmpdu9v6 moves exit by a bar: a condition on permissibility, by which a party controls the other's exit "without raising its price". A51's "transforming the costs of withdrawal and renegotiation" covers the first. The second is not a cost (J9.4).

### 9.7 Domination, and who holds it

A51: "Domination is characterized through consequential discretion and inadequate means of protection, influence, challenge or justified withdrawal." The record holds this as a presumption test, and only for domination by an arrangement's controllers.

- **The test.** "A dependence becomes presumptively dominating by those who control it when three conditions coincide": an important sphere, "consequential discretion over the dependent", and the dependent "lacks effective means of protection, influence, challenge or justified withdrawal"; "this is a test for domination by an arrangement's controllers, not for every unfreedom, and impersonal constraint that no one holds is outside it" (CLAIM-682, Proposed, interpretive; A's, A13 §II of "Analyze technological civilization trap", narrowed at version 2). The three conditions together raise a presumption. None of them is a definition, and none alone is sufficient.
- **The third condition needs exit to fail.** Failures of legibility, volitional efficacy or correctability for the dependent's claims entail lack of influence and challenge, and "justified withdrawal lies outside those three achievements, so the domination threshold's third condition is met by failures of normative efficacy only together with a failure of exit" (CLAIM-686, Active, demonstrated; the assistant's). It rests on the owner's CLAIM-523 (Active, interpretive), which states the three achievements as needed, and, in CLAIM-686's words, "the converse does not follow". §9.4 bears on the exit half. A fallback that decays past the deadline is one way justified withdrawal comes to fail with every term unchanged.
- **The term.** Domination is "an agent's or role's capacity, used or not, to set or change the terms on which the party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate, where the capacity is not answerable to the party under a standard declared with the judgment" (TERM-124, Proposed; the assistant's). "Its holder must be an agent or role; an arrangement with no controller can constrain but does not dominate in this sense." The term carries a note, "Not adopted: one use departs from the definition", because CLAIM-730 makes a rule the holder.
- **What is owed before the threshold.** "The accountability that those who control an arrangement owe to those who depend on it should rise with how indispensable the arrangement has become to them" (CLAIM-687, Active, interpretive; A's).

**The holder line, and A51's next article.** A51 says this article "Supplies the developmental political economy subsequently applied to enshittification". Two of the record's mechanisms for that may have no holder. CLAIM-677's practical compulsion arises "without any rule requiring it". In CLAIM-tmpjnly0 (Proposed, interpretive; A46 §10) the erosion of the constraints on extraction "can be produced by the optimization itself, with no change in the character of those who run it". Read this way, a case of either may fall outside CLAIM-682's test and TERM-124's sense. CLAIM-682 was narrowed for this reason: Marcuse, Han and Benjamin "describe complete constraint with no one holding discretion, which some accounts call domination" (its summary, ARG-109). The article does not join them (J9.5).

### 9.8 The owner's U17

At U17 of "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity", the owner wrote: "we should analyze how exploitation fits into this picture, since it can expose relied upon expectations that create opposrtunities for asymmetric relationships". The record holds two readings as alternatives, and the choice is the owner's (README open item 4).

- *Exposure as revelation.* "Exploitation can expose, in the sense of reveal, the relied-upon expectations an implicit contract kept tacit" (CLAIM-tmppx64t, Proposed, interpretive; not a member). This is the reading A22 takes.

Downstream of the first, Part I's join J3 passed as CLAIM-tmpu77ve (Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's wording). A51 cites none of these. They bear on the article's opening: dependence produced by cooperation is one kind of relied-upon expectation.

### 9.9 Negative results and non-equivalences

A51 §5 lists "Dependence does not establish domination." For this article the record's negative findings are these:

- Equal dependence can leave the part a hold on its terms or none (CASE-264). Its contrast is fictional, and "B is exploitation" is among what it does not show.
- Reciprocal dependence "is neither sufficient for freedom nor sufficient for justice" (CLAIM-603, note of 2026-11-12, quoting A6 §2).
- A bargaining model "establishes bargaining vulnerability, not by itself exploitation or injustice" (CLAIM-670, note of 2026-11-24, quoting A22).
- CLAIM-523's achievements are needed, not sufficient, and exit lies outside them (CLAIM-686).
- In the computation of §9.4, the decay moves nothing where P cannot leave, or under capacity-first supply. The current-output valuation of a fallback misses the deadline.
- A slow taper of substitutive support is worse than an abrupt cut (CLAIM-605).
- CLAIM-700's loop does not close under a statutory ratio (its separating case).
- CLAIM-728's timing clause is not supported by its readings (ARG-210, Rejected).

In A51's form these give three non-equivalences, each in its model or case. Dependence is not domination. A bargaining disadvantage is not exploitation. Mutual dependence is not mutual control of terms.
