# LIT-tmpt9xwi: Pleasure: the common currency

Michel Cabanac (1992), *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 155(2):173–200 —
DOI-10.1016/s0022-5193(05)80594-6

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* From the PubMed abstract only, checked verbatim
against the PubMed record. As physiologists, "we have no common currency
that allows us to equate the strength of the motivational drive that
accompanies each regulatory need". The organism must nonetheless rank its
needs when they conflict (after McFarland and Sibly 1975). "A theory is
proposed here according to which pleasure is this common currency."
Pleasure is measured "by choice behavior (in the case of animals), or by
the rating of the intensity of pleasure or displeasure (in the case of
humans)", and "The tradeoffs between various motivations would thus be
accomplished by simple maximization of pleasure." The abstract promises a
review and the author's recent experiments. The thermal-discomfort
trade-offs with joint hedonic ratings, and the treatment of alliesthesia,
are not described in it and were not seen.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the record's account of valence as dominance
([CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md)). Its part 2 recasts a common currency as the negotiation
admitting an ordering, and Cabanac is the thesis it recasts, alongside
Veit ([THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)). One point is visible from the abstract alone. For
animals, Cabanac measures pleasure *by choice*. That merges the record's
layer (1), dominance from conflicts won, with layer (2), a hedonic marker
independent of choice. For animals, the paper cannot test whether the two
agree. Only the human joint-rating experiments could, and they are what a
reading should check first.
