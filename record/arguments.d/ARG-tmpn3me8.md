# ARG-tmpn3me8: Energy and Equity supports the compulsion claim's mechanism and measure with the shoe and school cases, the non-adopter barred without any intentional conspiracy, but qualifies "without any rule requiring it": the technology's adoption needs no rule, while the exclusion of those who decline it can be enforced by gatekeepers and by law

## The inference

1. **The mechanism.** "Industry dominates traffic as soon as daily life comes to depend on motorized trips" (p. 14), and the monopoly arises "by virtue of its acquired ability to create and shape the need which it alone can satisfy". That is CLAIM-tmpbypt7's infrastructural mechanism, with Illich's addition, already filed from the excerpt ("Cars create distance"), that adoption enlarges the need.
2. **The measure.** The harm falls on transit, the self-powered alternative, and on the barefoot, whose "transit is in no way restricted by their lack of" shoes until shoes become a condition of access. What is measured is what remains open to the one who declines: the claim's measure.
3. **The qualification.** In the shoe case, "people are compelled to be shod ever since access to schools, jobs, and public services was denied to the barefoot. Teachers or party officials define the lack of shoes as a sign of indifference toward “progress.”" In the school case, "Only when laws were enacted to make schools both compulsory and free did the educator assume the power to deny learning opportunities on the job". No rule required anyone to buy shoes, and "Without any intentional conspiracy" the barefoot were barred; but gatekeepers did the barring, and for schooling a law came first.

So the claim's title is right about adoption and too strong about exclusion. A technology can become compulsory without a rule requiring its use; the shrinking of the non-adopter's options can still be enforced, by officials exercising discretion or by law. That is a second qualification beside the burial case, where a de facto monopoly is codified after the fact.

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** Yes. The cases are Illich's own illustrations of the general law in chapter 6, and the gatekeepers are in the same sentences as "Without any intentional conspiracy".
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** For the mechanism and the measure, yes, though Illich's main case is transport and his measure is equity in life-time and autonomy. For the qualification, the scope is exact: it concerns the phrase "without any rule requiring it".
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** The claim does not rest on Illich; the relation is `corroborates`. Illich supports a claim narrower than the title: compulsion arising without a rule of use, and an exclusion that may be enforced.
- **Is the qualification an objection?** No. The claim says the technology becomes compulsory without a rule requiring it, which Illich confirms; the exclusion being enforced does not contradict that. It shows the claim's mechanisms are not the only route by which options shrink, and that gatekeepers' discretion can enter, where CLAIM-tmpkjv7u's test of domination by controllers begins to apply.

## Verdict

Proposed. Energy and Equity corroborates CLAIM-tmpbypt7's mechanism and measure and qualifies its title: "without any rule requiring it" describes the adoption, not the exclusion. The title is kept, as with the burial case; the dated note on the claim records the qualification. Whether the title should be narrowed to say so is the owner's call.
