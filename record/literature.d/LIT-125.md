# LIT-125: The moral psychology of obligation

Michael Tomasello (2019), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 43, e56 (issue 2020)* — DOI-10.1017/S0140525X19001742

## Key takeaways

- The human sense of obligation — its peremptory force and its tie to agreement-like interactions — develops from forming a shared agent "we" in collaboration, so obligation is a "we > me" self-regulation, a self-conscious motivation to preserve one's cooperative identity.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Tomasello takes from moral philosophy two features of obligation: its demanding, quasi-coercive force and its link to agreement-like interactions such as promises, where breaches draw protest, apology and guilt. Using comparative and developmental evidence, he argues that these features stem from forming a joint agent "we" that both directs and self-regulates collaboration. Children first show a sense of obligation inside, not outside, joint collaborative activity, and later toward in-group but not out-group members linked by collective agency. Placing one's fate in a partner's hands turns instrumental pressure into the pressure "we" put on "me" to meet shared expectations.
