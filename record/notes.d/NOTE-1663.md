# NOTE-1663: Moore — Revenues, state formation and the quality of governance

## Contribution

A name and a scheme. The fiscal (social) contract proposition is stated
outright, and the states of the fiscal-sociology literature are sorted by
where their revenue comes from: demesne, tax and fiscal states "depend
largely on earned income that places considerable organizational,
bureaucratic, and political demands on the state apparatus" (p. 304);
rentier states do not. The mechanism is a side effect of raising revenue:
taxing obliges the state to build a bureaucracy, to be present on the
ground and to bargain, and rents remove all three obligations. Section VI
moves the policy question from a replay of European state building to the
conditions under which more dependence on tax would trigger bargaining.

## Key insight

The source of a state's revenue, not only its size, shapes the state.
Revenue that must be earned from citizens builds and keeps the machinery
that earns it, and accountability comes with the bargain; revenue that
arrives without effort leaves that machinery unbuilt or lets it decay.
Aid, in this scheme, is a rent.

## Assumptions

- **A rational-choice exchange.** The European mechanism trades revenue for
  representation and policy, enforced by mobile capital's exit option and
  by interstate competition (pp. 300–301). Moore concedes that the summary
  appears deterministic (n. 3).
- **Oil and aid as one class.** Both are "rents", though the evidence
  mainly concerns oil.
- **A representative forum exists almost everywhere** (p. 303), so what is
  scarce is collective action by taxpayers.

## Key results

- **The proposition (p. 299).** "relative to other types of states, tax
  states will tend toward accountable, representative government. This I
  will term the fiscal (social) contract proposition."
- **Unearned income (p. 304).** "Rentier states live largely off unearned
  income: the state is resourced with little organizational or political
  effort on the part of the state apparatus".
- **Aid as rent, and its scale (p. 305).** "the dominant type of strategic
  rent in the contemporary world is the many forms of development aid"; it
  is "the largest income source for the governments of most poor
  countries, typically accounting for a half or more of their total
  financial resources".
- **The aid evidence is weak (p. 306).** "the causal connection may be
  partly or wholly reversed: development aid may be concentrated on those
  countries that appear to suffer most from" bad government; Knack (2001)
  gives only some evidence of association with declining institutions.
- **Autonomy (p. 306).** Rents give rulers a guaranteed "source of income
  that makes them independent of their citizens" (Moore's scare quotes
  around "guaranteed"); "Why listen to
  citizens".
- **Capacity kept by use (p. 307, pathology 5).** "active revenue raising
  may be an important means of keeping the state machinery alive and
  active at the grassroots. If the revenue-raising function is permitted
  to decay, weak states leave themselves vulnerable". Quoting Odom (1992):
  "economic assistance tends to become an alternative source of revenue
  for the local regime, allowing it to neglect its domestic tax base".
- **No bureaucracy built (pp. 307–308, pathology 7).** "There is little
  incentive to establish an efficient public bureaucracy. The task of
  raising revenue from the mineral facilities requires few specialists".
- **Oil (p. 308).** Cross-national work "convincingly demonstrates" that oil
  states are less democratic with weaker rule of law (Ross; Wantchekon;
  Esanov et al.), on what Moore calls "partial or indirect measures"; the
  model "does not take into account the absolute size of oil rents".
- **No answer (p. 310).** "I am unable to provide anything like an answer
  to this question about the potential governance dividend from
  taxation." Cutting rents alone would have "no serious chance of
  success".
- **Concessional finance (p. 311).** It "creates incentives for governments
  to reject the negotiation route and to go for what is almost always the
  easier option"; IMF lending can let the Fund "mandate modes and levels
  of taxation, bypassing internal representative and legislative
  processes".
- **Origin against persistence (p. 311).** "In principle, we need to
  distinguish between explaining the origins of collective action and
  explaining its persistence."
- **One case (p. 313, after Easter 2002).** Poland's taxing of labour
  incomes "generated more resistance in the short term, but led to the
  creation of a more reliable revenue stream"; Russia, having bargained
  with a few large enterprises, "reverted to a more bureaucratic and
  coercive strategy".
- **Aid synchronized with bargaining (p. 316, n. 32).** It "could in
  principle (if effectively managed) reinforce those processes. That,
  however, requires intervention capacities considerably greater than
  those normally displayed by aid and development agencies."
- **Cited only (p. 316, n. 30).** "increased taxation does indeed tend to
  precede increases in measured levels of democracy (Ross, 2004)."
  Botswana is "the grand exception" among mineral states (n. 16); of EITI
  and resource funds, "Neither route seriously constrains any government"
  (n. 26).

## Concepts

- **Fiscal sociology**; **demesne, tax and fiscal states**.
- **The fiscal (social) contract**: revenue traded for representation.
- **Earned and unearned income**: revenue that does or does not demand
  organizational and political effort of the state.
- **Natural-resource rents and strategic rents**, aid the dominant modern
  form of the second.
- **The seven pathologies of rentier states**, among them autonomy from
  citizens, non-transparency and an ineffective bureaucracy.
- **The governance dividend**: the hoped-for gain from more reliance on
  tax.
- **Quasi-voluntary compliance; coercive against negotiated taxation;
  collective action by taxpayers**.

## Connections

It draws on Tilly, Brewer and Daunton for European state formation, on the
rentier-state literature (Mahdavy, Beblawi and Luciani, Chaudhry, Karl) and
on Ross, Knack and Wantchekon for the cross-national evidence. Moss,
Pettersson and van de Walle (2006), filed in this batch, take up its aid
half and add the economists' tax-effort literature. The collective action
by taxpayers that Section VI asks for is the side of governance Ostrom's
work holds: [LIT-737](../literature.d/LIT-737.md) and [THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md) on user-made institutions, [LIT-719](../literature.d/LIT-719.md) on
polycentric governance. [LIT-1325](../literature.d/LIT-1325.md), on policy capture, is adjacent to
Moore's point that particularistic deals with large taxpayers undercut
collective bargaining (p. 312).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (supplied maintenance).** Supports it by analogy and goes
  further. A state fed by rent or aid has no incentive to build the
  bureaucracy and bargaining that raising its own revenue requires, and
  the revenue-raising machinery it had decays (pathologies 5 and 7). Supply
  does more than fail to make the organization independent: it lets its
  own production capacity run down. There is no withdrawal evidence, and
  Moore expects cutting rents alone to fail.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) (independence from the founding intervention).** The
  clearest bearing. Independence needs earned income that obliges the
  state to bargain; concessional supply blocks it; supply synchronized
  with internal bargaining could help, but needs more than donors have
  shown. No thresholds.
- **[QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md) (a self-maintaining nucleus).** The tax bargain is a
  candidate cycle: revenue funds the state, the state serves taxpayers,
  and their prosperity and consent supply revenue (p. 300). Moore
  separates origin from persistence and calls persistence path-dependent,
  which fits the question's framing.
- **[CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md) (assembly, persistence, regeneration kept apart).** Supports
  it: origins and persistence of collective action need separate
  explanations, and a rentier state persists without regenerating its own
  capacities.
- **[CLAIM-516](../claims.d/CLAIM-516.md) and [CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md) (capacities with no internal producer;
  decay with disuse).** Qualitative support in the state setting: unused
  revenue raising decays and leaves the ground to rivals. The evidence is
  assertion and one quotation from counterinsurgency literature; nothing
  formal and no rates.
- **[THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md), [CLAIM-273](../claims.d/CLAIM-273.md), [QUESTION-085](../questions.d/QUESTION-085.md) (supplied against self-maintained
  order).** An analogue only: the rentier state is resourced from outside;
  the tax state maintains the conditions of its own revenue.
- **[TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md) (resource types).** The paper separates a windfall flow from
  one that needs capacity to collect: the source of a flow, not only its
  size, shapes the organization. That distinction is not among [TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md)'s
  types.
- **[TERM-117](../terms.d/TERM-117.md) (productive nucleus).** Easter's Poland case is a candidate: a
  harder early route paid off later in reliability. A single cited case.
- **[CLAIM-598](../claims.d/CLAIM-598.md), [CLAIM-520](../claims.d/CLAIM-520.md), [QUESTION-386](../questions.d/QUESTION-386.md), [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md), [THEORY-153](../theory.d/THEORY-153.md).** Loose
  analogues at most: large rents raise the payoff to coups (p. 307: "The
  rewards are potentially very high"); keeping accountability costs rulers
  the need to "take risks by sharing power" (p. 301); IMF revenue targets
  push toward coercive collection; the fiscal contract reads as mutually
  dependent constraints, which Moore does not frame as closure.

## Limitations

- **A review with no data of its own**; it is as good as Ross, Knack,
  Wantchekon and Easter.
- **The aid half is rudimentary**: confounded and possibly reverse-caused.
  Oil carries the evidence.
- **Nothing on exit**: what happens when aid stops, tapers or is handed
  over is not discussed beyond the expectation that cutting rents alone
  fails.
- **No thresholds or dose-response.**
- **The European model leans on war, naval trade and mobile capital**, none
  of which carries over (pp. 302–303, 310).
- **Written in 2004.** The later literature, Moore's own WP280 among it, was
  not read here.
