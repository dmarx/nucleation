# LIT-tmp3z5v4: No specific relationship between hypnotic suggestibility and the rubber hand illusion

Ehrsson, H. H., Fotopoulou, A., Radziun, D., Longo, M. R. & Tsakiris, M. (2022). No specific relationship between hypnotic suggestibility and the rubber hand illusion. *Nature Communications* 13, 564. DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-28177-z. Matters Arising on Lush et al. (2020), *Nat Commun* 11, 4853.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpdtj68](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpdtj68.md).*

- **The synchrony effect does not track hypnotisability.** Across all
  353 participants, the difference in illusion ratings between
  synchronous and asynchronous brushing is unrelated to SWASH score
  (rho = -0.066, p = 0.219, BF01 = 5.685), as Lush et al.'s preregistered
  analysis found in their no-instruction group. For drift the result is
  null and inconclusive (BF01 = 1.657).
- **Even the least suggestible have the illusion.** In each SWASH
  quartile (n = 84 to 92) synchronous ratings exceed asynchronous ones
  and exceed zero (Wilcoxon, all p < 0.001), and the quartiles do not
  differ in the size of the effect (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.405).
- **Explicit suggestion does not move it.** Telling participants the
  synchronous or the asynchronous condition would work better left the
  synchrony effect the same in the three instruction groups.
- **The correlations are not specific.** Hypnotisability explains about
  9% of variance in synchronous ratings, but also 9% in asynchronous ones
  and 12% in an "impossible" visual-hallucination control item; against
  that item, no positive relation remains.
- **Graded asynchrony.** Cited studies (not read here) find the illusion
  fades as asynchrony grows and is gone beyond about 200–300 ms, a
  temporal rule the authors say phenomenological control does not
  explain.
