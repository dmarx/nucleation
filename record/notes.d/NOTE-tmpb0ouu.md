# NOTE-tmpb0ouu: The current status of research on the structure of evaluative space

## Contribution

A review of the evaluative space model (ESM) by two of its authors and two
colleagues. It restates the postulates, answers critics (Russell and
Carroll's bipolarity) with the postulates of oscillation and
calibration, reviews behavioural and neural evidence, and draws clinical
implications.

## Key insight

The output of the affect system can be bipolar while its mechanism is
not. Behaviour "may be constrained to a single bipolar dimension", and
the response surface is many-to-one over a bivariate evaluative plane. So
states that the output cannot tell apart, neutrality and ambivalence, are
different states of the mechanism (§Abstract; Figure 2).

## Assumptions

- Positivity goes with appetitive motivation and negativity with
  aversive. Anger is treated as defensive in its "distal motivation"
  (§The Evaluative Space Model).
- The affect system is the integrated set of mechanisms for telling
  hostile from hospitable stimuli. Reportable feeling is not the target.
- The ESM "is not a theory of what elicits affective states or emotions".

## Key results

- Behavioural and neural evidence for separable positive and negative
  channels: uncorrelated positive and negative affect, distinct striatal
  and orbitofrontal regions, and distinct transmitter systems.
- Coactivation in the field (graduation day, Larsen et al. 2001), in the
  laboratory (disappointing wins, Larsen et al. 2004), and by
  oscillation between opposite images (Norris, Eichenold & Cacioppo
  2009).
- A negativity bias across reflexes, attention, ERPs and fMRI, and a
  positivity offset in mere exposure and person perception.
- Individual differences in both asymmetries are stable and predictive
  (Ito & Cacioppo 2005).

## Claims

## Concepts

- **modes of evaluative activation** — reciprocal, uncoupled
  (independent) and nonreciprocal (coactivation).
- **objective ambivalence** — coactivation of positivity and negativity.
  **Subjective ambivalence** is its experience, including the blur that
  fast oscillation produces.
- **response surface** — the net predisposition toward (+) or away from
  (−) the target, over the evaluative plane. Its isocontours are
  "many-to-one mappings between the affective response surface and the
  underlying evaluative space".
- **negativity bias / positivity offset** — higher gain for negativity,
  higher intercept for positivity.
- **heterarchical organization** — evaluation is represented at every
  level of the neuraxis, and higher levels can override lower.

## Connections

Summarizes Cacioppo & Berntson 1994 (LIT-tmpglbdp)
and Cacioppo, Gardner & Berntson 1997, 1999
(LIT-tmpj2mcy), all unread. Argues against
Russell and Carroll's bipolarity. That is also the shape of Barrett's
core affect (LIT-1353). Cites Gray and Lang's two-system theories, which
it calls reciprocal, Carver & Harmon-Jones on anger, and Kahneman and
Tversky on loss aversion as a negativity bias.

## Bearing on the record

The record's proposal (CLAIM-tmpz8lli, TERM-tmp8gbrt) makes hedonic
valence dominance by one ordering of a system's own states. It reads
ambivalence as "either no ordering at all … or split dominance", and
neutrality as "dominance with no movement". The ESM bears on this in
five ways.

- **The distinction is anticipated.** That ambivalence is not neutrality,
  and that a single bipolar measure collapses them, is the ESM's founding
  point (Figure 2: "neutral to ambivalent … yields the same middling
  expression"). The proposal should cite the ESM for it.
- **The ESM puts the difference in a different place.** For the ESM both
  states are positions in a two-channel *activation* space, low-low and
  high-high. For the proposal they are properties of the *dominance
  structure* among perspectives. The ESM's ambivalence still produces an
  ordering: the net predisposition is a single number, "middling". So
  "no ordering at all" is not the ESM's ambivalence. Split dominance is
  closer. Two channels each strongly active, pulling toward approach and
  toward avoidance, can be read as two rankings each dominating part of
  the system. But the ESM combines them into one output and does not
  describe them as perspectives that win conflicts. The proposal's
  "split dominance" is therefore a reinterpretation of coactivation, not
  a restatement of it. It owes an argument that the two channels are
  perspectives in CLAIM-347's sense.
- **The same-output result is a warning for measurement.** If neutral
  and ambivalent states give the same net predisposition, a record of
  which option is chosen cannot tell them apart. QUESTION-tmpsqwu5's
  "conflict record" must record which *perspective's ranking* each
  decision followed, not only the decision. The ESM supplies marks that
  a choice record lacks. Ambivalence is "highly arousing". At high
  coactivation "energy expenditure is taxing over long periods of time".
  Its eliciting circumstances "tend to be avoided". Each is a candidate
  independent marker for the proposal's defeater ("ambivalent and neutral
  states that a record … cannot tell apart").
- **The asymmetries cut against a symmetric sign clause.** The proposal
  gives sign as the direction of movement along the ordering, with
  nothing to choose between up and down. The ESM's negativity bias
  ("The gain for the negative activation function is higher") and
  positivity offset are asymmetries the proposal does not predict. If
  intensity is dominance strength, the negativity bias says downward
  movement dominates more strongly than equal upward movement. That is
  compatible with the proposal, but must be added to it.
- **Heterarchy bears on CLAIM-tmp83ge2's defeater.** The ESM says
  "different modes of activation" can "be dominant or prevail depending
  on the level of representation". Reflexes are reciprocally constrained
  and rigid, and "higher levels of the neuraxis have the ability to
  override these dominant responses". CLAIM-tmp83ge2 reads Veit's
  currency (THEORY-101) and ALARM's override (THEORY-090) as two regimes
  of one ordering. It is defeated if "an override and a currency" are
  "shown to coexist in one animal as separate mechanisms". The ESM's
  heterarchy, with rigid low-level dispositions and flexible higher-level
  weighing that can override them, looks like such coexistence. It is not
  a demonstration, since the ESM does not show that the low level is
  lexicographic or the high level compensatory. But it is the sort of
  architecture the defeater names, and the record should look at it
  before promoting CLAIM-tmp83ge2.

## Limitations

A review, partly a defence of its authors' model. Many claims are
postulates rather than results: the rarity and cost of coactivation,
calibration, and the clinical predictions. The figures were not seen,
only their captions. The ESM is explicitly not a theory of what elicits
affect, so it is silent on the proposal's "kind" of ordering, by bearing
on maintenance.

## Open questions

Whether coactivation is two dominant rankings or one ranking with high
input on both sides. Whether a positivity offset means the dominant
ordering has a positive default slope at rest, which would make the
proposal's neutral point, no movement, mildly positive.
