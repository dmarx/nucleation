On 2026-10-26 the owner asked "how's the record on neural circuit
theory?". The answer found that the record's circuits line ([QUESTION-039](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-039.md),
[QUESTION-234](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-234.md), [THEORY-802](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-802.md), [THEORY-018](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-018.md), [LIT-968](../../record/literature.d/LIT-968.md), [LIT-961](../../record/literature.d/LIT-961.md), [LIT-323](../../record/literature.d/LIT-323.md) and the
works read that day for the exchange "Experimental Infrastructure Review",
among them Olsson et al., [LIT-tmpe7b5o](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpe7b5o.md), and MIB, [LIT-tmprlq7z](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmprlq7z.md)) leaned on
the core mechanistic-interpretability literature without holding it: the
circuits programme, the transformer framework, the first circuits in
pretrained language models, the tools for finding and testing circuits,
and the dictionary-learning line. The owner said "go ahead and register
and read those". Fourteen works were registered and read the same day,
in four batches.

Each was checked as not held first (literature.d and notes.d searched by
title words, first author, identifier and venue URL). None was; the only
hits were passing mentions (ACDC in [NOTE-106](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-106.md), DAS in [NOTE-809](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-809.md) and
[LIT-961](../../record/literature.d/LIT-961.md), Dunefsky et al. in [NOTE-482](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-482.md)). Per [ADR-048](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-048.md), no anthology check
was made, and no anthology code was known for any of them. All fourteen
carry `anthology-candidate` last ([ADR-005](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-005.md)): they are filed for the
record's questions about what a circuit is and how it is found, and carry
no instruction for machine-learning practice.

**Dates and identifiers.** Under [ADR-002](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-002.md) each takes its first public
appearance. The Transformer Circuits and Distill articles have no arXiv
deposit and take their own pages' dates, and are cited by `url:` (Zoom In
also by its Distill DOI). Causal scrubbing, an Alignment Forum post with
no arXiv or DOI, takes its posting date. The arXiv works take v1. ROME's
v1 was titled "Locating and Editing Factual Knowledge in GPT". The IOI
paper's ICLR 2023 venue was not asserted, the arXiv record naming none.

## What was read of each

Every copy was lawful: arXiv, or the work's own venue (distill.pub,
transformer-circuits.pub, the Alignment Forum's own API). The interactive
figures of the HTML articles were never rendered; their content was
taken from captions, text and inline tables, and each NOTE says so.

- **Zoom In**: the Distill article in full, glossary, footnotes and
  references included. It has no quantitative results.
- **Mathematical Framework**: the current page, which carries a
  follow-up summary of February 2023 and the authors' correction; all
  text sections and appendices. The equations set as images, the videos
  and the problem set were not used.
- **IOI**: arXiv v1, §§1–6 and Appendices A–M.
- **ROME**: arXiv v5, §§1–6 and Appendices A–J; v1 compared only on its
  title, trace numbers, dataset count and edit layer.
- **Causal scrubbing**: the main post in full, with all 22 footnotes. The
  three companion posts (the appendix, and the paren-checker and
  induction results) were not read, so §5's numbers come from the main
  post's summaries of them.
- **ACDC**: arXiv v4 in full, §§1–6 and Appendices B–N.
- **AtP\***: arXiv v1, §§1–6 and Appendices A, B and C.1. The rest of
  Appendix C (edge AtP) and Appendix D were not read. The method
  comparison exists only as charts, read from page renderings, so the
  relative costs taken from them are approximate.
- **DAS**: arXiv v4 in full, §§1–6 and Appendices A–D.
- **Towards Monosemanticity**: the article's text whole (about 165,000
  characters), every section, appendix and all 44 footnotes. The feature
  browsers were not opened.
- **Scaling Monosemanticity**: the article's text whole (about 146,000
  characters), every section, the appendices and all 16 footnotes.
  Numbers that appear only in figures (scaling exponents, the
  automated-interpretability histograms) were not used.
- **Sparse Feature Circuits**: arXiv v3 in full, §§1–8 and Appendices
  A–H; Figure 3 read from a rendering of its page. v1 and v2 were not
  compared.
- **Transcoders**: arXiv v2 in full, Appendices A–H and the references.
- **Circuit Tracing**: every main-text section, and five appendices (CLT
  implementation details; validating the replacement model; nuances of
  steering; unexplained variance and the choice of steering factors; the
  opening of similar features and supernodes). The other appendices were
  not read, and the NOTE's history lists them.
- **Biology**: every section, all ten case studies, Related Work and the
  appendices (Open Questions, Special Tokens, Graph Pruning and
  Visualization); only the Acknowledgments and Author Contributions were
  not read. **It was read in two passes the same day.** The first pass
  covered about half the case studies; the rest were read at the
  coordinator's request so that the NOTE could be Read rather than
  Skimmed, and the NOTE's history says so. [THEORY-tmpzjxhi](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpzjxhi.md) and
  [THEORY-tmpaa9v2](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpaa9v2.md) come from the second pass, and the Proposed
  [THEORY-tmpfralg](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpfralg.md) was revised in place to count its cases for and
  against.

## Errors found in the works

Arithmetic and claims were checked where cheap; the scripts are the
readers' own and are not part of the record.

- **Mathematical Framework.** The authors' own correction (a cyclic
  permutation inside a Frobenius norm) was confirmed numerically:
  ‖AB‖_F ≠ ‖BA‖_F while the eigenvalues agree. "Transformer weights are
  organized into convolutions" should read conv-net weights, and W_QK is
  written with and without its transpose.
- **IOI.** §4.4 calls the naive circuit's faithfulness of 0.1
  "comparable" to the full circuit's 0.46, where lower is better, so by
  this measure the naive circuit is the more faithful. Algorithm 3's
  greedy step maximizes a quantity with no M in it, not the
  incompleteness score §4.2 says it maximizes.
- **ROME.** §2.1's "AIE=8.7% at layer 15" disagrees with Appendix B.1's
  figures (15.0% from 8.47%, an AIE of 6.5%). Figure panels and an
  analysis cited in v5 are left over from v1, and the edit layer is
  given as 18, 17 and 15 in different places.
- **ACDC.** §4.1 says ACDC beats both baselines on tracr-reverse; its own
  Table 2 gives 0.200 against HISP's 0.577 with corrupted activations.
  Appendix H says "8 main heads" and lists seven, and "39 edges" against
  37 elsewhere.
- **AtP\*.** The method's origin is cited as "Nanda, 2022"; the post
  appeared on 2023-02-04, describing work of 2022.
- **DAS.** Table 2's (MoNLI) brute-force row is digit for digit Table 1's,
  almost certainly a copying error, so that comparison is unusable as
  printed. IIA is reported on training data although held-out sets were
  built, C(768, 32) is given as 2e58 where it is 4.2e56, and the
  conclusion that random networks "cannot construct entirely new
  behaviors" is contradicted in part by 0.64 at width 4,096.
- **Towards Monosemanticity.** An index slip in the ablation appendix
  (d_j for d_i). The 79% loss recovered is, by the authors' own word, an
  overestimate.
- **Scaling Monosemanticity.** Two entries of the appendix's table of
  safety-relevant features sit under the wrong categories.
- **Sparse Feature Circuits.** SHIFT is expanded two ways; Gemma Scope's
  width is "8 × d" in §2 (18,432) and 16,384 in Appendix B.2; §4 says the
  Gemma feature ablations damage performance where Table 2 shows balanced
  accuracy rising; and the claim that SHIFT picks features "optimally or
  near-optimally" is not borne out by its own skylines.
- **Transcoders.** "Six of the eight" restricted case studies where Table
  4 lists nine studies, seven restricted; a mis-cited reference for the
  greater-than circuit; and a re-derivation found a per-token LayerNorm
  scale that Eq. 7 omits and Appendix D.4 applies ([THEORY-tmp9ii0y](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp9ii0y.md)).
- **Circuit Tracing.** The Biology summary announces nine case studies
  and lists ten; the cosine agreement one layer after a perturbation is
  "~0.8" in the text and "around 60–80%" in the appendix; and the
  re-derivation of constrained patching found it exact only if the
  normalization denominators are also frozen ([THEORY-tmpbtnf0](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpbtnf0.md)).
- **Biology.** The revenue-table text says the cost rises "by $8,970 from
  $26,880" to $35,820, but 26,880 + 8,970 = 35,850. The poem table's
  "success cases" for the "rabbit" injection include a line ending in
  "hare". The 70% injection figure leaves its denominator unstated, the
  hidden-goal case's ~48% baseline is not explained beside its ~85%, and
  the introduction's "routinely" conflicts with the Limitations' "only
  claims about specific examples".
- **Zoom In and causal scrubbing** have only slips (a glossary typo; a
  misspelled author name in the BibTeX, which AtP\* copies). Zoom In's
  claim that feature visualization "establishes a causal link" overstates
  its argument, and the NOTE records it as a limitation.
