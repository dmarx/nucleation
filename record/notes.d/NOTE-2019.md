# NOTE-2019: Elish (2019) — Moral Crumple Zones: Cautionary Tales in Human-Robot Interaction

## Contribution

A concept for the mismatch between distributed control and concentrated responsibility in
human-machine systems: the human operator "may become simply a component—accidentally or
intentionally—that bears the brunt of the moral and legal responsibilities when the overall
system malfunctions" (Abstract, p. 40), and "the moral crumple zone protects the integrity of the
technological system, at the expense of the nearest human operator" (p. 40).

## Key results

1. p. 41: operators' tendency to become "liability sponges" serves "to fill the gaps in
   accountability that may arise in the context of new and complex systems".
2. p. 44, Three Mile Island: "Without a doubt, actions taken by the plant operators led to the
   accident and exacerbated its severity"; yet, p. 46, "Even as their actions were constrained,
   their culpability was focused on and singled out."
3. p. 49, Air France 447: "While control has been effectively distributed, responsibility has not
   scaled accordingly." "Human error" "generally refers to operator error, not the error of human
   designers or systems architects".
4. p. 49: the automation is credited; the A320-era claim was that it was "smart enough to outsmart
   and save the human every time".
5. p. 50: because the autopilot is certified as software, "the only possible malfunction,
   systemically, is the human pilot, becoming the moral crumple zone"; the last-minute handoff
   "is something humans do not do well".
6. pp. 50-51, scope: "The concept of a moral crumple zone is not a way to explain why these gaps
   occur"; "moral crumple zones are not a property of every complex and automated system"; they
   are "likely to take shape in the immediate aftermath of a highly publicized event or accident"
   (Therac-25 as counter-case).
7. pp. 51-52: "a moral crumple zone is more than just the articulation of a scapegoat"; "The
   technology is maintained as faultless, while the human operator becomes the faulty feature of
   the system."
8. pp. 52-53, Uber Tempe 2018, from the NTSB preliminary report and the press: "Nevertheless, it is
   the safety driver who may now be facing criminal charges for vehicular manslaughter".

## Assumptions

That media coverage and early official statements show how responsibility is attributed; an
American context ("cultural perceptions of blame and responsibility, particularly in an American
context", p. 52); the accident narratives rest on secondary accounts.

## Concepts

Moral crumple zone; liability sponge; human-in-the-loop; handoff problem; deskilling; normal
accident; problem of many hands; certification boundaries.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: corroborates its after-failure half, from a source independent of the
  exchange: blame settles on the operator where the failure is visible, and the system is kept
  faultless (results 2, 3, 7). It adds mechanisms the claim lacks: certification that can only find
  the human at fault, the legal final authority of the operator, media framing. It says nothing of
  the claim's before-failure half or of credit for failures that members' compensation prevented;
  those come from A's joining of Elish with Tronto, and A17 §6's nurse example is A's.
- **[CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md)**: consistent with it. The operator can be at fault (result 2) while those who
  set the conditions drop out of the account.
- **[CLAIM-678](../claims.d/CLAIM-678.md)**: Elish's framing power (certification, official reports, the press) is close to
  the claim's epistemic power, though she does not put it that way.
- **[CLAIM-710](../claims.d/CLAIM-710.md)**: the handoff problem is an intervention point that exists on paper and that
  humans cannot reliably use.
- Related held readings on the handoff and deskilling mechanism: [THEORY-1519](../theory.d/THEORY-1519.md), [THEORY-1847](../theory.d/THEORY-1847.md),
  [CLAIM-675](../claims.d/CLAIM-675.md).
- **The exchange** (A15 §5, A17 §§1, 4) reports Elish accurately. It adds "professional" to her
  "moral and legal". The schematic supervisor and the nurse examples are its own.

## Limitations

Illustrative cases, not a measured pattern; a few headlines and statements per case; written
before the NTSB final report on the Uber crash ([LIT-2664](../literature.d/LIT-2664.md)), which names the operator's
distraction as the probable cause and Uber's safety culture as contributing.
