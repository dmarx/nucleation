# NOTE-tmpyqw1q: Bohn, Kachel and Tomasello — Young children spontaneously recreate core properties of language in a new modality

## Contribution

The first controlled observation of the first steps of a new language-like
system: reference, local convention, drift toward the arbitrary and
compositional structure within one session, where earlier work studied end
products (homesign, Nicaraguan Sign Language) or transmission chains.

## Key results

1. **Overall.** "The gestural code systems that children created ad hoc
   within a 30-min test session exhibited core features of natural
   language and emergent sign languages" (Significance).
2. **Age.** "Six-year-olds spontaneously invented novel referential
   signs." "Three-year-olds, however, imitated model solutions and had to
   be introduced to novel meanings in a piecemeal fashion." (Results).
3. **Convention.** "For both age groups, raters judged gestures from
   the same dyad to be more similar" (Results).
4. **Drift.** "The effect for abstractness ratings was rather small,
   and later gestures were still highly iconic." "Children of both ages
   successfully retained the meaning of a gesture when its form gradually
   drifted from iconic to arbitrary." (Results); "the reduction in
   iconicity over time was largely explained by a decrease in production
   effort" (Discussion).
5. **Grammar.** "Gesture sequences were more frequent in conditions
   with high compared to low relational complexity"; "Compositional
   gesture sequences were almost always understood, which illustrates how
   structure reduced ambiguity." "We found no substantial evidence for
   German word order" (Results).
6. **Interpretation.** "Natural languages can be thought of as local
   conventions that have been lifted to the group level (7)." (Discussion;
   ref. 7 is Origins of Human Communication).
7. **Sample.** "All children (n = 198) came from an ethnically
   homogeneous, midsized German city" (Methods).

## Assumptions

Raters' similarity and abstractness judgments stand in for conventionality
and arbitrariness; a pair's signs in one session model emerging
conventions; training was spoken; one German population; experimenters
prompted iconic gesture when children stalled.

## Concepts

Iconic gesture; reference game; local and group-level convention; drift to
the arbitrary; effort reduction; common ground; compositional sequence;
homesign.

## Bearing on the record

- **LIT-771** and **THEORY-155** (iterated learning), a qualification:
  here structure arises within one pair, from the pressure of
  communicating complex relations, with no transmission between
  generations; the authors predict transmission would add more.
- **LIT-473** (Lewis), a parallel: pairs converge on signs by imitating
  each other, without agreement; only pair-level conventions are shown.
- **THEORY-785** and **THEORY-172** (rational speech act models),
  background: the task is a reference game; no model is fitted.
- **LIT-tmpf6o51**, a test of its account of conventions from iconic
  gesture.

## Limitations

Small arbitrariness effects; pairs only, single sessions; one culture;
adult partners in the grammar studies; measures rest on ratings; the SI
unread.
