# TERM-tmp9b3gk: relational normative realism, as frame-, order-, scale- and perspective-relative normative realism: a normative judgment is indexed to a frame, an organizational or reflective order, a scale, a perspective and a standard, and once these are fixed it is correct or incorrect whatever the judge prefers

## Definition

The owner, at U18: "so maybe something like a frame/order/scale/
perspectival-relative normative realism?" A18: "I'd use **relational
normative realism** as the short name, and your fuller
formulation—*frame-, order-, scale-, and perspective-relative normative
realism*—as its explicit technical characterization."

A normative judgment is written, after A18, J(a; F, O, S, P, N): the action,
state or policy a, judged in

- a **frame** F, "the variables, distinctions, and relevant counterfactual
  comparisons";
- an **order** O, which A18 splits in two: "If you mean first-order versus
  higher-order attitudes, that is different from organizational level. Both
  matter to our account, so I would distinguish **organizational order**
  from **reflective order** rather than silently combine them." The owner
  did not choose. This term keeps both, as separate indices;
- a **scale** S, "the spatial, temporal, or resource resolution of
  evaluation";
- a **perspective** P, "whose interests, commitments, reasons, or standing
  are relevant";
- a **standard** N.

A18 calls the schema "a *schema for analysis*, not yet a theory of normative
truth".

The position is realist in A18's sense when three conditions hold:

1. **"truth conditions independent of the current judgment."** "Once the
   relevant relations and standards are specified, an agent can be mistaken
   about whether an action satisfies them."
2. **"counterfactual discipline."** Verdicts answer "to evidence about what
   would happen under relevant alternatives."
3. **"constraints on admissible perspectives."** "We cannot simply let an
   evaluator choose whichever frame makes a preferred verdict true."

A18: "That third condition is the difficult one. The first two can establish
objective *conditional* normative judgments without yet establishing
objective authority for the conditions themselves." The term is used with
all three. A claim that meets only the first two is indexed judgment, which
is CLAIM-tmpt75xe. Whether the third can be met is QUESTION-tmpq5i0m.

The distinction the term draws is between a judgment's dependence on its
relata and its dependence on the judge. It admits the first and denies the
second.

## What it is not

- **Not relativism.** Relativism, in A17's table, holds that "No
  context-transcending standard can adjudicate between standpoints". The term
  does not say that. A18 names the challenge from the other side, from
  Wong's pluralistic relativism: "why should our position count as *realism*
  rather than a species of constrained relativism?" Until the third
  condition is met the challenge stands, and the position may be
  constrained relativism under another name.
- **Not Frankfurt's view.** NOTE-264 records Frankfurt "Against normative
  realism": "I do not believe that anything is inherently important", and
  the objectivity of normative standards "consists just in the fact that it
  is outside the scope of our voluntary control". That grounds standards in
  what agents cannot help caring about. The term's third condition asks for
  more than that, and Frankfurt denies it can be had.
- **Not frame-invariance.** A18: "Normative objectivity need not mean
  invariance under every change of perspective. It may mean invariance under
  changes that preserve the normatively relevant structure." And: "what makes
  a transformation *normatively admissible* cannot be read off its formal
  structure alone."
- **Not a derivation from physics.** A18's velocity analogy "is
  methodological, not a derivation of moral realism from physics", and the
  gauge reading of the indices is QUESTION-tmpxpu3p, open.
- **Not A21's "multiscale relational normative realism"** as a separate
  term. A21 and A22 use that name for the same position with scale
  stressed. It is this term.
