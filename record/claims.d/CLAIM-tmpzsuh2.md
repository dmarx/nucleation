# CLAIM-tmpzsuh2: A journal can be a feed over editors, its editors being taggers who attach attributable judgements to work wherever it is published, as Bluesky's labellers attach labels to posts they do not host, so that editorial judgement is separated from publication

## The claim

The owner's words: "bsky taggers as journal editors, a journal as a feed
over editors".

A10 spells out the split it makes:

- "Authors produce research."
- "Editors curate research." They tag claims, methods, results or
  articles "with attributable editorial judgments".
- "Journals aggregate editors." A journal is "a named, configurable feed
  over the editorial judgments of a group of curators".

So "a journal isn't a place where manuscripts go to be accepted", and the
same work can be in several journals' feeds, since "They aren't publishing
three different papers. They're offering three different editorial
perspectives on the same research." A10's summary: "a journal becomes a
maintained point of view, not a destination for manuscripts."

A10 reports two precedents, neither checked by the record: Bluesky's
separation of custom feed generators from independent labelling
services, and Harvard's Open Access Tracking Project, "launched in 2009",
which "already uses volunteer taggers to curate scholarly information".

## What it does not say

- Not that a tag is a review. A10: "Tagging something as interesting is
  not the same as establishing that it's correct." [CLAIM-tmpnqzvj](CLAIM-tmpnqzvj.md) is
  that refinement.
- Not that every feed over editors is a journal. [CLAIM-tmptn64c](CLAIM-tmptn64c.md) (the
  assistant's) asks what a feed needs to be one.
- Not who keeps the work available. That is the owner's U6,
  [CLAIM-tmpv0sv7](CLAIM-tmpv0sv7.md).
