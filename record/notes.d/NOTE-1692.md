# NOTE-1692: Herr et al. — Temporal solitons in optical microresonators

## Contribution

Dissipative cavity solitons were known as solutions of the Lugiato-Lefever
equation and had been made, as temporal solitons, only in fiber loops
with a second "writing" laser. This paper reports them in a high-Q
crystalline microresonator, formed spontaneously by a laser sweep, and
identifies them from the steps they leave in the transmitted power. It adds
an existence window in detuning, a formula for the step heights, a bound on
the number of solitons, an account of why the usually unstable
red-detuned regime becomes self-stable, and a tuning method that reaches
the soliton states reliably, "at the push of a button" (SI §VI, p. 15).

## Key insight

A soliton here is a pattern the pump keeps, not a thing that keeps itself.
It rests on two balances at once, gain against loss and Kerr nonlinearity
against dispersion, and on a pump that never stops. What the conditions
fix, they fix entirely: shape and amplitude are set by the resonator and
the pump. What history decides is only how many solitons there are and
where they sit in the cavity.

## Assumptions

- **Model.** Coupled-mode equations equivalent to the Lugiato-Lefever
  equation (LLE) when third- and higher-order dispersion are negligible
  (p. 2); D3 tested and it "does not prevent soliton formation" (SI §I).
  Simulations of 101 and 501 modes neglect mode overlap other than unity,
  "interactions with other mode families, any particularities of the
  resonator geometry and thermal effects" (p. 3), although thermal effects
  are central to the experiment.
- **Analytic soliton.** The SI's soliton "is not an exact solution" (SI
  §II); it borrows the undriven, undamped NLS soliton for the soliton part,
  valid for large detuning.
- **Existence limits.** Taken from an analysis of the damped driven NLS on
  an infinite domain (ref. 30, Barashenkov and Smirnov 1996), said to hold
  with periodic boundaries for a mode number of "typically a few hundred"
  (SI p. 14).
- **One device.** One MgF2 resonator: FSR 35.2 GHz, κ/2π = 450 kHz
  (Q = 4 × 10⁸), D2/2π = 16 kHz, coupled pump 5–30 mW (Methods). The step
  signature was seen at any resonance of the same mode family within
  ±0.5 nm of tuning (p. 4).

## Key results

- **Supply (p. 1; SI §IX, p. 17).** "Their dissipative nature requires
  them to be continuously in contact with an external energy source";
  they "rely on the double balance between parametric gain and resonator
  loss on the one hand and nonlinearity and diffraction or dispersion on
  the other hand". SI: "Due to the cavity loss, these solitons are
  dissipative in their nature and their persistence requires a source of
  energy for replenishment. The latter is provided by continuously and
  coherently driving the cavity."
- **Robustness asserted, not tested (p. 1).** The dissipative nature
  "renders their attractor highly stable and robust against fluctuations",
  cited to refs. 1–2 (Akhmediev & Ankiewicz 2008; Grelu & Akhmediev 2012).
- **Bistability (pp. 1–2).** The Kerr shift makes "two possible solutions
  for the intracavity power can exist for a particular pump laser
  detuning", upper (blue-detuned) and lower (red-detuned) branch; the
  solitons sit on the lower branch "on top of a continuous wave (cw)
  background". In Fig. 3a the orange lines "trace out all possible
  evolutions of the system during the scan" (p. 9): soliton and
  soliton-free states coexist at one detuning.
- **Existence window (SI §§II–III, pp. 13–14; equations from the page
  image).** Solitons need the cw bistability, ζ0 > √3 (the "bistability
  criterion"); the existence condition h > 2γ/π becomes ζ0max = π²f²/8
  (eq. 18), ζ0 = 2(ω0 − ωp)/κ the normalized detuning and f the normalized
  pump. Fig. 3 caption (p. 9): "The green area corresponds to the area
  where solitons exist, the yellow area allows for solitons with a time
  variable envelope; no solitons can exist in the red area." At small
  detuning "the system may undergo chaotic Hopf-bifurcations" (p. 3). The
  window opens only once π²f²/8 > √3 and widens as f² (my arithmetic
  from eq. 18, not the authors').
- **Path dependence (p. 2; SI §VI, p. 15).** Solitons form on "tuning the
  pump laser through the effective zero detuning frequency, into the lower
  branch" (effectively red detuned) "after previously following the upper
  branch"; the ramp runs "in the direction of decreasing optical frequency
  in order to first follow the upper branch before jumping onto the lower
  branch". The state depends on the path, not only on the final detuning.
  The paper does not use the word hysteresis, and no reverse sweep is
  reported.
- **Steps are soliton numbers (pp. 2–3).** The transition to "a single,
  low-noise RF beat note" coincides with the start of the steps, and "the
  first step" "coincides with the zero crossing of the PDH signal", the
  effective zero detuning (p. 2). In simulation "the first step (frame V)
  corresponds to a transition to a state where multiple pulses inside the
  cavity exist. Further steps can be associated with a stepwise reduction
  of the number of pulses" (p. 3); "The intracavity power changes
  discontinuously with the number of solitons" (p. 3). SI eq. 20 (page
  image): mean intracavity power ≃ f²/ζ0² + N ξ(K)(2/π)√(d2ζ0), linear in N.
- **Why the number varies (p. 3).** "the number and height of steps
  fluctuate in repeated numerical scans as a result of random seeding of
  the optical modes (corresponding to vacuum fluctuations)"; "The
  separation between multiple pulses in the resonator is random." The
  solitons "emerge from the modulated intracavity waveform" of the
  preceding comb, which "may explain their spontaneous formation". The
  sentence opens "In agreement with the experiment", but no experimental
  count of N across runs is given. Against this, "The number of solitons
  formed can be controlled by the pump laser detuning" (p. 2), and in
  simulation "Short, simulated pump power drops can be used to induce
  transitions between different soliton states." (Methods, p. 5).
- **Form fixed by the conditions (p. 3, eq. 1).** Ψ(φ) ≃ C1 + C2 Σⱼ
  sech(√(2(ω0 − ωp)/D2)(φ − φj)); "The complex numbers C1 and C2 are fully
  determined by the resonator parameters and the pump conditions". Only N
  and the positions φj are free.
- **Bound on number (SI p. 14, page image).** Two solitons do not interact
  if spaced ≳ (8/B)√(2d2) (ref. 4), giving Nmax < (2π/8)√(ζ0/d2); with
  ζ0 > √3, Nmax ≈ √(κ/D2), "which remarkably coincides with the distance
  between the first generated primary sidebands".
- **Withdrawal and leaving the window (pp. 3–4; SI §VI).** "Once
  generated, the solitons remain stable until the pump laser is switched
  off" (p. 3); "stable for hours until the pump laser is switched off"
  (SI §VI, p. 15). "a hypothetical disturbance in the laser detuning larger
  than the length of the small thermal triangles would terminate the
  soliton operation" (p. 4). Tuning too slowly, the "sudden temperature
  drop" on reaching the lower branch blue-shifts the resonance and causes
  "a loss of the soliton state"; too fast, later heating does the same (SI
  §VI). No decay curve or lifetime after switch-off is measured or
  computed. The field's energy decays at κ, κ/2π = 450 kHz, so on a
  sub-microsecond scale: my inference from the stated κ, not the authors'.
- **Thermal self-stability needs the soliton (p. 4; SI §VII, p. 16).** The
  lower branch "is usually thermally unstable"; with solitons, "the small
  portion overlapping with the soliton inside the resonator is effectively
  blue detuned (upper branch) and responsible for the self-stability", and
  the combined curve's positive slope "is responsible for the effective
  thermal self-stability". This "puts a limit on the maximum length of the
  cavity": fiber loops need active stabilization.
- **Not a laser (SI §IX, p. 17).** "while microresonators are driven by a
  continuous wave pump laser they are not lasers", and "a saturable
  absorber is not required for the stability" of the solitons.
- **Experiment (p. 4).** Single soliton: sech² spectrum, 3 dB bandwidth
  1.6 THz; FROG width 200 fs FWHM; RF beat note signal-to-noise above
  60 dB; pulses spaced by the round trip, 28.4 ps. States of one, two and
  five solitons shown (Fig. 4).

## Concepts

- **Dissipative (cavity) soliton**: a localized pulse that persists by a
  double balance, parametric gain against loss and nonlinearity against
  dispersion, on a cw background, with a supply it cannot do without.
- **Lugiato-Lefever equation**: the damped, driven nonlinear Schrödinger
  equation for a coherently pumped Kerr cavity.
- **Upper and lower branch**: the two cw solutions of the Kerr-bistable
  resonance at one detuning; effectively blue- and red-detuned pumping.
- **Thermal triangle**: the triangular transmission trace of a resonance
  scanned downward, shaped by Kerr and thermal shifts; small triangles,
  one per soliton state, mark the steps.
- **Soliton steps**: discontinuous drops in intracavity power, one per
  change in soliton number.
- **Breather solitons**: solitons with a time-varying envelope, the yellow
  region of Fig. 3a.

## Connections

The paper builds on the LLE (Lugiato and Lefever 1987), Wabnitz's
multi-soliton solution and non-interaction spacing (ref. 4), and
Barashenkov and Smirnov's existence chart for the driven damped NLS (ref.
30). It cites, for the robustness of the dissipative-soliton attractor,
Akhmediev and Ankiewicz (2008) and Grelu and Akhmediev (2012), the latter
read in this batch. It contrasts itself with fiber-loop cavity solitons,
which need a writing pulse and active stabilization. In the record its
nearest held neighbour is Knobloch's review of spatial localization in
dissipative systems, [LIT-1642](../literature.d/LIT-1642.md), registered but not read; [NOTE-414](NOTE-414.md)
(Belousov-Zhabotinsky) is another held reading on a supplied dissipative
pattern.

## Bearing on the record

- **The lead's routes.** (a) Conservative solitons: not tested; the undriven
  NLS soliton is used only as an approximation, and nothing is said about
  collisions. (b) Dissipative solitons: confirmed in the main points, a
  supplied double balance, a pump threshold and bistability; qualified in
  that the range is a window in detuning with an upper bound as well as a
  lower one, and that hysteresis appears only as path-dependent formation.
  (c) Topological solitons: none. Formation from initial data: qualified;
  there is no minimum pulse area here, solitons arise spontaneously from
  noise-seeded modulation and chaos during the sweep, and shedding of
  radiation is not discussed. Slow decay: none; decay after switch-off is
  asserted, set by the cavity loss, and not slow.
- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (supplied upkeep).** Supports the withdrawal half: no pump, no
  soliton. It differs in that the supply here does not replace parts; it
  holds a pattern in a medium whose light turns over at rate κ. The
  claim's e^(−dt) decay after the supply stops is not measured here.
- **[CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) (when support can be withdrawn).** A case where support must
  stay a standing input: persistence after withdrawal is nil, and there
  is no slow reversion to mimic persistence. The sweep that forms the
  solitons can be stopped; the pump cannot.
- **[CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) (self-sustaining upkeep).** Qualifies. A threshold, loss
  outside a window and path-dependent formation are all present, but
  they arise from a nonlinear Kerr response with a supply that does not
  depend on the structure's intact state. State-dependent uptake is then
  one route to the three signatures, not the only one; the claim's scope
  note may allow this.
- **[QUESTION-281](../questions.d/QUESTION-281.md) (transitions by which distinctions come into being).** The
  steps are discrete transitions between states of different soliton
  number, reached by sweeping through the effective zero detuning; creation
  (from modulation instability and chaos) and stabilization (the thermal
  self-locking) are separate here, as the question asks.
- **[CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md) (the unit as a gap-isolated block that lasts while it
  persists).** Its pressure (iii) asks for localized structures that persist
  while their material turns over. Here the intracavity light is replaced
  on the cavity decay time while each soliton keeps its position and
  shape for hours: a case for testing whether the criterion follows a form
  through turnover. The paper gives no spectral or coupling analysis.
- **[TERM-088](../terms.d/TERM-088.md) (senses of individuation).** Supports "dynamical"
  individuation without autonomy: each soliton is countable, locatable and
  persists under perturbation within the window, wholly held by the pump.
  Its form is fixed by the conditions, so individuality lies in number and
  position, not in kind.
- **[THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md) and [QUESTION-085](../questions.d/QUESTION-085.md) (flame or convection cell).** The soliton is
  like a convection cell for its energy, but the thermal self-stability is
  a flame-like feedback of the pattern on its own supply conditions: the
  soliton keeps its own effective detuning stable. One constraint only, not
  closure ([TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)).
- **[THEORY-1273](../theory.d/THEORY-1273.md), [THEORY-1270](../theory.d/THEORY-1270.md), [THEORY-1315](../theory.d/THEORY-1315.md) (metastable maintained states,
  which state is occupied, slowing near a fold).** Bear, untested: the
  paper shows deterministic multistability with path-dependent selection
  of N and calls the attractor robust, but studies neither noise-driven
  escape between soliton numbers nor behaviour near the window's edges.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) and [QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md).** The formation support (the sweep) is
  withdrawable; the upkeep support (the pump) never is. Beyond the thermal
  self-stability the soliton contributes nothing to its own maintenance.
- Filed: [THEORY-1500](../theory.d/THEORY-1500.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- One device; the run-to-run variation in N is reported for simulations
  only, with no experimental statistics.
- Simulations omit the thermal effects that the experiment depends on.
- The existence window is from approximate analytics on an infinite
  domain, checked against simulation for a few hundred modes.
- Path dependence is shown; hysteresis is not named and no reverse sweep
  is reported.
- No lifetime or decay after switch-off is measured; "until the pump laser
  is switched off" is an observation of persistence, not of the end.
- The robustness of the attractor is asserted from citations.
- arXiv v3, not the version of record; equations read from a page image.

## Open questions

- Does a reverse sweep retrace the steps, or does the state hold, as true
  hysteresis would? An up-and-down sweep would tell.
- How is N distributed across experimental runs, and how much does the
  sweep speed or a pump drop control it?
- How fast does a soliton vanish after the pump stops, and does anything
  outlast the cavity decay time?
