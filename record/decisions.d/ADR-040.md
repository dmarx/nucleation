# ADR-040: A formal-apparatus line, held as a second line by the general results that a substantive line borrows, so that setting that line aside does not take them with it

## Context

A line exists "so that a line can be set aside as a whole and what depends
on it can be found" ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)). Setting a line aside is a status change on
its members. The argument page (`docs/argument-lines.md`, chains over
`rests_on` with `line` as the invariant) shows that this no longer works
for pragmatic-transport.

These are the counts from `record/claims.d` on 2026-10-12. Superseded and
Rejected claims are included only in the line totals.

- **pragmatic-transport is most of the argument.** It holds 188 of 262
  claims and 113 of the 153 `rests_on` edges. One component of 99 claims,
  83 of them in pragmatic-transport, fills most of the page. 128 claims
  have no `rests_on` edge at all and do not appear on the page.
- **The other lines rest on it for general results.** Of the 11 `rests_on`
  edges that cross lines, 9 end in pragmatic-transport, and most of those
  end in results that do not mention translation:
  - [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md) (will-organization) and [CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md) (distributed-agency) rest
    on [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md), that sections over disjoint contexts always glue.
  - [CLAIM-189](../claims.d/CLAIM-189.md), 190 and 191 (nucleating-argument) rest on [CLAIM-056](../claims.d/CLAIM-056.md) (error
    propagation), 081 (retention against drift), 142 (observational
    equivalence is relative to probes) and 149 (a training probe cannot
    certify).
  - Only [CLAIM-189](../claims.d/CLAIM-189.md) → [CLAIM-034](../claims.d/CLAIM-034.md) (fidelity is one goal among several) and
    [CLAIM-191](../claims.d/CLAIM-191.md) → [CLAIM-002](../claims.d/CLAIM-002.md) (a text's identity across retellings) depend on
    the account of translation itself. Both edges are genuine: the record
    is a chain of retellings.
- **So setting the line aside takes too much.** Retracting
  pragmatic-transport today would retire [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md), a lemma about sheaves
  that holds whatever the account of translation concludes. With it would
  go one premise each of [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md) and [CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md), and four of the premises
  of nucleating-argument.
- **distributed-agency shows the same pattern, smaller.** Its chain
  [CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md) → [CLAIM-154](../claims.d/CLAIM-154.md) / [CLAIM-164](../claims.d/CLAIM-164.md) is about individuation in general: a
  sufficiency partition relative to a target, the threshold at which a
  unit is worth naming, and what a spectral gap makes definite. It is
  borrowed by the line's own claims about combined forms.

The schema allows a fix that keeps every claim where it was filed. luria
reads a vocabulary field as a list when the scheme declares it
`many: true` (`luria/contract.py`, `values_of`). A list in a field without
it is a lint finding: "holds N values, but the CLAIM scheme declares it one
`lines` value — set `many = true` there if it should hold several". The
chains already treat a scalar as a set of one, and they head a chain with
every value its members hold in common (`luria/invariants.py`, `shared`;
`luria/chains.py`, `_sections`). The restriction is this record's
configuration, not luria's.

## Decision

Approved by the owner on 2026-10-12 ("approved, go ahead with the yaml
change and group A"). Items 1, 2 and the group A values are made in the
same contribution as this decision. The group B splits followed on
2026-10-13, at the owner's request ("go ahead with the group B splits").
Group C was decided the same day, as recommended ("move forward as
recommended").

1. **Allow a claim more than one line.** Add `many: true` to the `line`
   field of the CLAIM scheme in `luria.yaml`. It stays required, and its
   vocabulary stays closed. The other schemes keep one line until a case
   asks otherwise.
2. **Add the line `formal-apparatus`**, blurbed: *results that hold
   whatever any substantive line concludes: gluing and contextuality,
   comparison of experiments and probes, identifiability, conservation,
   and thresholds for naming a unit. Mathematics and method that the other
   lines borrow, held as a second line so that setting a substantive line
   aside does not retire them.*
3. **Set-aside rule for claims with two lines.** Setting a line aside
   retires the members that hold no other line still standing. A claim that
   also holds `formal-apparatus` stays standing. It is retired only on its
   own merits, by refutation or supersession, never with a line.
4. **The test for the second value.** Would the claim keep its truth value
   if every thesis of every substantive line were retracted? And does it
   rest on no claim that would not? Both must hold. The second condition is
   the one that matters in practice: a claim that holds `formal-apparatus`
   while resting on a substantive claim would survive the retraction of its
   own premise.
5. **The claims, sorted by that test** (below). Only group A takes the
   value. Group B is split, and group C is the owner's call.

## Alternatives considered

- **Move the claims instead of adding a value.** This was the assistant's
  first draft. Rejected, at the owner's suggestion:
  - It takes general results out of the chains that use them. The
    pragmatic-transport page would lose the lemmas its arguments run
    through.
  - It forces title changes on the four claims worded for communication.
  - It makes `line` say where a claim ended up rather than what it is
    about.
- **`many: true` with no new line.** For example, [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) would hold
  pragmatic-transport, will-organization and distributed-agency. Rejected:
  - A claim's lines would then record who borrows it, and grow with every
    new borrower.
  - Retraction would depend on how many lines happen to cite a claim, not
    on what the claim is.
- **Leave the lines as they are.** Rejected: the set-aside that [ADR-032](ADR-032.md)
  makes the purpose of a line would retire theorems along with the theses
  that used them.
- **Split pragmatic-transport into several lines.** For example, the
  account of translation, its formal apparatus, and the audit of its
  manuscript ([CLAIM-202](../claims.d/CLAIM-202.md), 211, 216–244 and others). The formal part of that
  is this decision. The audit is left where it is: if the line is set aside,
  its audit should go with it.

## Consequences

- **What a retraction takes.** Retracting pragmatic-transport would
  retire 144 of its 164 standing claims, not all of them. [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md) and [CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md) keep
  their premise. After the group B splits, nucleating-argument loses only
  [CLAIM-034](../claims.d/CLAIM-034.md) and [CLAIM-002](../claims.d/CLAIM-002.md). Those are its real dependence on the account of
  retelling.
- **The argument page will not show fewer crossings.** A step crosses when
  the two claims share no line, and [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md) (will-organization) still
  shares none with [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) (pragmatic-transport, formal-apparatus). The
  crossing report keeps showing every borrowing. What changes is that a
  borrowing from formal-apparatus can no longer be lost to a retraction. If
  that noise proves unwanted, the report could treat a step into
  `formal-apparatus` as a non-crossing. That would be a change to luria and
  is not proposed here.
- **Claims list under both lines.** The claims index groups by `line`
  (`axis: line`), so a claim with two values appears under both headings.
  This is how luria already renders plural fields.
- **Side observation.** [CLAIM-160](../claims.d/CLAIM-160.md) and [CLAIM-171](../claims.d/CLAIM-171.md) (what data written for a
  future reader means) are filed in pragmatic-transport but bear on
  nucleating-argument's subject. With plural lines they could hold both.
  That is not proposed here.
- **Order of work, once approved:**
  1. the `luria.yaml` change (field and line);
  2. the group A values;
  3. the group B splits;
  4. the owner's calls on group C.
