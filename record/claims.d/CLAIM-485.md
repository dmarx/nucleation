# CLAIM-485: In the self-governing sense, what endogenous governance adds over a behaviourally equivalent system with an external scheduler is not that it allocates authority among its own processes but that it can revise its own governing standards when they break down

## The claim

[QUESTION-092](../questions.d/QUESTION-092.md) asks whether an organization that allocates authority among
its own processes can be self-governing in a stronger sense than a
behaviourally equivalent one whose allocation an external scheduler sets.
"Self-governing" is [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)'s sense 5: governing oneself by one's own
standards.

[THEORY-219](../theory.d/THEORY-219.md) (Kahl 2026, [LIT-1033](../literature.d/LIT-1033.md), read in [NOTE-723](../notes.d/NOTE-723.md)) answers a neighbouring question, about agency
in the sense that bears on responsibility. Allocation does not count:
"voting rules, ranking functions, confidence thresholds" coordinate
execution and "do not generate authorship of the governing frame". What
counts is a standpoint that settles revised evaluative standards when the
inherited ones break down, and carries them forward.

The bridge is that sense 5 makes the same cut. To govern oneself by one's
own standards is to be the place where those standards are kept and
changed. A system whose allocation is internal but whose standards are
fixed governs among its processes by standards it did not set and cannot
change. That is [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md)'s second capacity, governing among regimes,
without the third, transforming the governing organization. [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md)
holds that the three can persist or fail separately, so the difference is
real and not a matter of description.

So, to [QUESTION-092](../questions.d/QUESTION-092.md):

- **Endogenous allocation alone:** no stronger sense. Behaviourally
  equivalent, with fixed standards, the two systems are equally
  self-governing in sense 5, which is to say neither is.
- **Endogenous revision of standards:** yes. It is the stronger sense, and
  the scheduler's location matters only insofar as the scheduler is where
  the standards are revised.

**Behavioural equivalence.** Two systems can be matched within a regime and
still differ here. When the regime breaks down, the one that can revise its
standards does something the other cannot. So the difference is
observable, but only outside the regime in which the equivalence was
measured. That is also [CLAIM-288](CLAIM-288.md)'s point about fixed objectives, seen from
the other side.

## What it does not say

- **That allocation is idle.** [CLAIM-284](CLAIM-284.md)'s endogeneity includes it, and
  revision may need it. The claim is that allocation is not what adds the
  stronger sense.
- **That revision is enough for a self.** [CLAIM-284](CLAIM-284.md) also asks for
  historical continuity and self-maintenance. This claim locates only what
  endogeneity contributes to sense 5.
- **That [THEORY-219](../theory.d/THEORY-219.md)'s responsibility-agency is sense 5.** The bridge is
  that both turn on revision of standards, not that the two senses are
  one.
