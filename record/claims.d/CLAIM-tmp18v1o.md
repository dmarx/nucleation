# CLAIM-tmp18v1o: An expectation can be structural rather than represented: a process can be organized so that its working presupposes another's behaviour, and the presupposition can be realized in a capacity it has stopped maintaining, so that it no longer needs, or no longer knows how, to do the thing for itself

## The claim

A20 §2 distinguishes three senses of expectation:

| Sense | A20's example |
|---|---|
| Represented | "A process explicitly predicts that another process will produce an output." |
| Dispositional | "A process prepares for another's output without computing an explicit prediction." |
| Structural | "A process is organized in such a way that its successful operation presupposes another's output." |

The third is the one the owner's U16 needs: an implicit contract between
processes that represent nothing. A20's case: a consumer keeps a buffer
against interruptions; after long dealings with a reliable producer the
buffer goes, "because maintaining it is costly". "Its organization now
presupposes reliable delivery. The assumption is not necessarily
represented anywhere. It has become materially realized through the
absence of a previously maintained capacity."

So the expectation is located in the organization's shape, and one place it
can be is a gap: what the process no longer maintains.

## Why it is more than capability loss

The record holds the loss itself: a capability given up because another
reliably supplies it is the Black Queen's loss (CLAIM-603), and support that
does the work can leave a party unable to recover the capacity once a
deadline passes (CLAIM-605). What the claim adds is a reading of the loss:
as the content of an expectation, with terms. The buffer's size fixes how
long an interruption the consumer still tolerates; its absence fixes that
none is tolerated. A loss read this way says which deviations by the
producer the consumer can absorb and which it cannot, which is what an
implicit contract's tolerances are (A20 §12's T). That is what the
defeater tests: the reading has to predict something the bare loss does
not.

A20 §6's discrete model shows the route, not the contract: with service
held at 1, learned expectation and reliance rise while independent
capacity falls toward 1 − r; when service is withdrawn, functioning drops
to what capacity remains. At A20's default settings (withdrawal at step
40), independent capacity at withdrawal is 0.42 and functioning falls to a
minimum of 0.34 (checked with `python3 -I`, rerunning A20's update rule).
A20 says of it: "It models unilateral dependency formation, not yet the
formation of a reciprocally maintained contract."

## What it does not say

- Not that every structural presupposition is an expectation of a
  particular party. A20 §14's substitution test, whether "reliance concerns
  the function or its particular provider", is what tells the two apart;
  CLAIM-tmp1nubb holds it.
- Not that the process is at fault for the loss. Giving up a costly
  capacity that another supplies is favoured (CLAIM-603).
