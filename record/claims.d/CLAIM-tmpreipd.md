# CLAIM-tmpreipd: Morality is not cooperation, nor only the solutions to its problems: cooperation transforms its participants and creates new dependencies, vulnerabilities and claims, exploitation can be a way of organizing successful cooperation, refusing to cooperate can be right, and moral consideration is not confined to those who cooperate, so morality evaluates the terms on which cooperation is formed, maintained and transformed

## The claim

A23's opening: "morality is concerned not merely with whether agents
cooperate, but with the terms under which their cooperation becomes
organized, sustained and transformed." The argument, by turn and section:

1. **Cooperation makes participants vulnerable to one another** (A23 §1).
   Reliability, trust, specialization, commitment and integration each come
   with a vulnerability: "Behavior becomes predictable and exploitable",
   "Independent alternatives diminish", "Withdrawal becomes increasingly
   consequential". These "are not necessarily defects" but "create a domain
   of relationships requiring normative evaluation".
2. **Exploitation can arise within successful cooperation** (A23 §4).
   Both parties may keep benefiting relative to not cooperating, and keep
   to the norms, while the terms become exploitative under a justified
   standard: "exploitation is not necessarily a failure of cooperation.
   **Exploitation can be a mode of organizing cooperation.**" This is
   stronger than "identifying exploitation with defection or
   free-riding", and is close to the record's CLAIM-669 (exploitation
   "through inclusion that succeeds") and CLAIM-713.
3. **The cooperator–defector division does not track the moral one**
   (A23 §5). Coordinating to harm outsiders is cooperation and potentially
   wrongful; refusing an unjust institution is noncooperation and
   potentially justified. "Cooperation is not necessarily morally good,
   and noncooperation is not necessarily morally bad." CLAIM-546 already
   separates coexistence from cooperation; this separates cooperation from
   moral worth.
4. **Moral consideration is not earned by cooperating** (A24 §10).
   Caregiving is asymmetric and can be required; "Animals, infants,
   incapacitated individuals and other potentially relevant moral patients
   do not need to earn consideration through contributions to collective
   productivity. **Cooperation can generate special moral relationships,
   but moral consideration cannot be restricted to cooperative
   participants.**"
5. **The inverse direction** (A23 §13, A24 §1). Besides "How does
   morality make cooperation possible?", ask "How does cooperation create
   relationships that make morality necessary?": cooperation changes "their
   capabilities, expectations, dependencies, vulnerabilities and
   possibilities for future action" and so creates new reasons, claims and
   responsibilities.

So morality is the evaluation of the terms on which cooperation is formed,
maintained and transformed, with three objects A23 §3 names: "the
formation of relationships", what participants owe "within established
relationships", and "the transformation of relationships", what is
required when one wishes to withdraw or revise.

## Which routes carry it

The routes are not equally strong.

- Steps 3 and 4 separate morality from cooperation outright: a case of
  wrongful cooperation, of justified refusal, or of consideration owed to
  a non-cooperator is a case where the two come apart. Killen's argument
  (THEORY-2110), that independent judgments of fairness let children oppose
  group norms, gives the developmental version of step 3.
- Step 2 separates morality from cooperation *success*, not from
  cooperation's problems: the exploitation it describes is a problem of
  dividing a jointly produced surplus, which morality-as-cooperation counts
  as one of its own (fairness, in THEORY-468's division domain).
- Step 5 does not by itself tell against an account like Curry's. That
  cooperation creates new problems is compatible with morality being the
  solutions to cooperation problems, if the new problems are cooperation
  problems; and A24 runs together where moral dispositions come from with
  what makes moral verdicts right, which A23 §11 itself keeps apart. That
  is ARG-tmpn6xou, Rejected.

The claim therefore stands, if it does, on steps 3 and 4, with step 2 as
an important case and step 5 as a research direction.

## What it does not say

- Not that morality has nothing to do with cooperation. A24 §1:
  "Morality can enable cooperation. Cooperation can generate new normative
  circumstances. Moral inquiry can subsequently transform the conditions of
  cooperation."
- Not which principle settles the terms. A23 §8 and A24 §17 leave the
  grounds of special obligations open between the record's CLAIM-671
  (vulnerability to discretionary power, supposed), CLAIM-959 (associative
  obligation, supposed) and CLAIM-960 (bare relational arrangements add
  none). A24: "The grounds of normative validity, associative obligation
  and moral standing remain open."
- Not that the evolution of moral dispositions shows their validity. A23
  §11 distinguishes "Evolution of normative dispositions", "Stabilization
  of normative organization" and "Normative justification": "The first two
  concern genesis and persistence. The third concerns validity." That gap
  is QUESTION-128's.
