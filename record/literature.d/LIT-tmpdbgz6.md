# LIT-tmpdbgz6: Language Evolution by Iterated Learning With Bayesian Agents

Thomas L. Griffiths and Michael L. Kalish (2007), *Cognitive Science*
31(3):441–480 — DOI-10.1080/15326900701326576

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* From the abstract only: languages are transmitted by
iterated learning, each learner learning from others who learned the same way.
If learners compute a posterior over languages and sample from it, iterated
learning is a form of Gibbs sampling and converges to a distribution over
languages determined entirely by the prior. If they choose the
maximum-a-posteriori language, the outcome depends on both the prior and the
amount of information transmitted, and iterated learning corresponds to a
variant of expectation–maximization. This links constraints on acquisition to
the languages that come to be spoken. [LIT-tmpe93h7](LIT-tmpe93h7.md) is the laboratory
counterpart.
