# NOTE-tmpwwf5q: Transformation transformed

## Contribution

A reflective keynote, not a research article. What it adds is a
distinction: Lévi-Strauss used "transformation" in two incompatible ways,
which Descola names after Goethe and D'Arcy Thompson, and only one of them
fits myth. It also states plainly a consequence often left implicit: in
the analysis of myth the transformation group is not given by the
material but cut by the analyst. And it places Descola's own
ontological fourfold as a structural group of the Goethean kind.

## Key insight

Structural analysis does not describe a thing; it orders a set of variants
so that each is a transformation of the others. Whether the ordering is
discovered or constructed depends on the regime: developing an initial
form generates its variants by a rule, while deforming between given forms
in myth leaves the path to the analyst's knowledge and imagination.

## Assumptions

- That method and doctrine in Lévi-Strauss can be separated.
- That the domain is "superstructures" (kinship, classification, myth,
  food norms, art), sets of phenomena organized systematically.
- That variants of a set have no privileged member: "each variant is a
  variant of the other variants and not of any of them in particular".

## Key results

The lecture's theses, argued from Lévi-Strauss's practice and Descola's
own work:

- **Method.** Elements are characterized by position, not intrinsic
  properties; the analyst brings to light, in a table of permutations,
  "the whole set of possible combinations"; the resulting model "provides
  the syntax of the transformations which allows one to move from one
  variant to another within a class of phenomena".
- **"A structure is not a system."** A structure requires "invariant
  relations which allow moving from one set to another by the means of a
  transformation".
- **Goethean regime** (*Les structures élémentaires*): reciprocity, in its
  minimal form as restricted exchange between two moieties, develops into
  generalized exchange (Australian four- and eight-section systems), its
  combination with bride purchase (the Kachin), and mixtures in China and
  India. Lévi-Strauss, against Radcliffe-Brown, takes as initial the
  structure that develops the principle most widely.
- **Thompsonian regime** (myth): a mythical transformation is the group
  formed by variants of a myth that keep its structure, "including by
  inverting it", and by myths of neighbouring societies that borrow
  mythemes, inverting their motifs or swapping their functions. Unlike
  organisms, whose differences rest on the discontinuities of the genetic
  code, the group of myths is "a virtual continuum upon which the analyst
  performs arbitrary cuts" (citing L'homme nu, 603–4). D'Arcy Thompson
  needs stable forms at both ends; myth analysis has none, and mythical
  transformation introduces discontinuity by contrast, while anatomical
  transformation introduces continuity by deformation.
- **The ontological group.** With interiority and physicality each similar
  or different between a subject and an unspecified other: similar
  interiority, different physicality is animism; different interiority,
  similar physicality is naturalism; shared elements of both within a
  class distinct from other classes is totemism; both distinct but with
  small enough differences for correspondences is analogism.
- **Symmetrization.** Generalizing a local concept (mana, hau), a local
  epistemic stance (Dumont, Strathern) or a native doctrine (Tempels,
  Viveiros de Castro) all fall short; the combinatorial matrix symmetrizes
  better because its totality is never given in advance and is
  reconfigured as elements are added, though it requires worldwide data.

## Concepts

- **transformation**: an ordered passage from one variant to another
  within a group, preserving invariant relations.
- **structure / system**: a structure is defined by invariants across
  sets that allow transformation between them; a system is a set of
  related elements without that.
- **Goethean regime**: transformation as development of an initial
  blueprint (the Urform, the Urpflanze) into its variants.
- **Thompsonian regime**: transformation as continuous deformation in a
  coordinate space between forms already given.
- **symmetrization**: placing the observer's and the observed's cultural
  features on an equal footing.
- **aliud**: an undetermined other, not yet specified by a relation
  (after Descombes).

## Connections

The lecture reads Lévi-Strauss: *Les structures élémentaires de la
parenté*, *Anthropologie structurale* (LIT-775, unread), *Du miel aux
cendres* and *L'homme nu*, with Petitot's genealogy of structuralism's
morphological sources. Its Goethean regime is, by my connection rather than
Descola's, the one Propp's *Morphology* announces in its Goethe epigraphs
(LIT-tmppp40q); Descola does not mention Propp. Its account of meaning by
contrast is Saussure's (LIT-774), which it names as the semiological
dimension of Lévi-Strauss's approach.

## Bearing on the record

- **On Lévi-Straussian transformation as a method.** The lecture supports
  reading structural analysis as a theory of transformations between
  variants, with invariants, rather than as a static inventory of
  oppositions. It also carries a caveat that matters for any use of it as
  a model of how variants are actually related: in myth, by Lévi-Strauss's
  own account, which variants count as transforms of which, and the path
  between them, are the analyst's construction. Descola says this; he
  does not discuss computation or formal modelling.
- **THEORY-159** (value fixed by relations among coexisting terms) is
  Saussure's; Descola's "no human phenomenon has a meaning in itself" is
  the anthropological generalization, asserted, not argued, here.
- No THEORY is filed: the lecture argues a method and reports a reading;
  it establishes no finding of its own.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- A keynote with a personal register; it summarizes rather than shows.
- The Goethe/Thompson distinction is Descola's interpretation; Lévi-Strauss
  credited Thompson, and Goethe only "in passing".
- The ontological fourfold is stated, not defended; the defence is in
  *Beyond Nature and Culture*.
- Nothing is said about how the two regimes would be told apart in a given
  analysis, beyond the presence or absence of an initial form.

## Open questions

- If the transformation group of myths is cut by the analyst, what would
  make one ordering of variants better than another? Descola names no
  criterion.
- Can the Thompsonian regime be made non-arbitrary for myth by fixing
  stable end forms, as D'Arcy Thompson's method requires?
