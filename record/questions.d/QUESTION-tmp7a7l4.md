# QUESTION-tmp7a7l4: Can a collective that keeps its inquiry in a governed record become better at improving its own procedures, a later improver doing better than an earlier one from the same starting state at the same budget, without losing the capacity to detect failures in that apparatus?

## Why it is a question

From U17 of the exchange "luria experiment design"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af35bfc-35f8-83e9-a63d-d594703eabda): "there's
probably also an RSI angle here". A41 separated "improving an agent's
performance" from "improving the system through which agents discover,
evaluate, authorize, and retain improvements", and asked: "Can a collective
of agents improve the shared epistemic and organizational machinery through
which it produces and evaluates its own future improvements?" "The decisive
feature is inheritance."

The record holds what the question probes, without the question:

- QUESTION-388 asks what distinguishes a programmable system from one that
  governs its own reconfiguration; its note of 2026-11-07 records a
  ladder of software self-modification. CLAIM-485 separates allocating
  activity under inherited standards from revising the standards.
- CASE-186 is a proposed study of recursive correctability: whether a
  system that corrects one failure keeps the machinery to correct the
  next. CASE-108 and CASE-144 are proposed studies of governance under a
  compromised or drifting evaluator; QUESTION-271 asks how an organization
  can evaluate its own processes without the evaluation being captured.
- CLAIM-266: probes that supply an optimized system's training signal
  cannot also certify what it preserves.
- TERM-089 separates an apparatus that can revise itself from one whose
  revisions are good; CASE-188 separates warranted persistence from
  entrenchment.

## What separates the senses

A43 sets four achievements apart: a better work product; a validated
self-modification; an improved improver, "the revised improvement procedure
produces better further improvements when tested from the same starting
conditions as the earlier procedure"; and preserved recursive
correctability, recovery after damage to the system's own detection,
verification or revision machinery. "Levels 3 and 4 are where the
distinctive research questions lie. Neither follows automatically from
Levels 1 and 2."

The test for the third is LIT-2273's, as A reports it: "Of 26 studies
claiming an improved improvement procedure, only four compared earlier and
later improvers from identical starting artifacts under matched budgets."
The prior art on self-improving agents, registered unread, is LIT-2319,
LIT-2298, LIT-2290, LIT-2327, LIT-2314, LIT-2310, LIT-2288 and LIT-2278;
LIT-tmp5jugw reports that repairing a targeted failure did not reliably
improve held-out performance.

## What would count as an answer

A study in which a collective improves its procedures over generations, an
earlier and a later improvement procedure are each applied to the same
fresh starting state at the same budget, and the collective's diagnostic
machinery is then damaged (CASE-tmpy1ebu, adapting CASE-186).

- **Yes**: the later improver produces better further improvements on
  held-out tasks, the gain survives replacing every agent with a fresh one
  that inherits only the record, and the collective detects and repairs
  damage to its own checks more often than a matched alternative.
- **No, with a product gain**: task scores rise while the improver does
  not improve, or while correctability falls. A41 names the case:
  "self-degradation disguised as self-improvement", its example: "a team may improve its research throughput by dropping
  expensive independent source verification".
- **A different answer**: A43's "subtler possibility" that the record's
  mechanisms help "precisely because they limit recursive optimization",
  by preventing a team from raising its reported performance by weakening
  its standards.

No answer here settles QUESTION-388: A43 grants that such a study "does
not establish philosophically independent self-governance, because the
experimenter still defines the ultimate comparison". Woods's graceful
extensibility (LIT-2287) and Chang's epistemic iteration (LIT-tmphbhk2),
both unread, are A50's and A51's candidate theories of what the fourth
achievement is.
