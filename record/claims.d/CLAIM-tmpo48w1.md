# CLAIM-tmpo48w1: Two organizations facing the same circumstances with the same acceptable actions can each be minimally adequate while telling apart different and incomparable sets of circumstances, so a difference in the distinctions two perspectives draw is not by itself evidence that either is deficient; which is deficient can be judged only against the policy each commits to

## The claim

A58 §3 of the exchange's second share
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6aeea2bc-d280-83e9-ac1f-f6772adf263c), after
setting out [CLAIM-535](CLAIM-535.md)'s criterion and example: "This is a candidate
explanation for why different agents can develop different perspectives on
the same circumstances without either perspective necessarily being
defective." "Their practices, commitments, and capacities may require
different partitions of the world."

The construction is [CLAIM-535](CLAIM-535.md)'s. W = {1, 2, 3}, A(1) = {a}, A(2) = {a, b},
A(3) = {b}. The partitions {1, 2} | {3} and {1} | {2, 3} both suffice,
neither refines the other, and the trivial partition does not (rechecked).
An organization committed to a in circumstance 2 must tell 2 from 3 and may
merge 1 and 2. One committed to b in 2 must tell 1 from 2 and may merge 2
and 3. Each is blind to the distinction the other draws, and neither is
deficient: each draws as few distinctions as its policy needs.

**What it adds.** [CLAIM-455](CLAIM-455.md), from [CASE-154](../cases.d/CASE-154.md)'s XOR table, orders legibility
regimes only relative to a task: one representation serves one task and
disables another for the same observer. This claim holds the task and the
acceptable sets fixed and varies only the policy. Perspectives, too, are
ordered only relative to the policy each commits to.

**Where it bears (the reader's).** The first filing's comparative designs
compare communities by how they split one category ([CASE-tmphkso5](../cases.d/CASE-tmphkso5.md),
[QUESTION-tmpdalhx](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpdalhx.md)). Two communities may split it differently because they
are committed to different acceptable actions, not because one is less
competent. A design has to fix the policy before it reads a difference in
distinctions as an error.

## What it does not say

- **That the acceptable sets are right.** A: "The theorem doesn't justify
  those sets, and whoever controls that standard can influence which
  distinctions the organization is required to recognize." That is
  [CLAIM-535](CLAIM-535.md)'s "That the acceptable sets are the right ones" and [TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)'s
  caution on the loss.
- **That any two differing perspectives are both adequate.** Only that the
  difference alone does not show which, if either, falls short.
- **That real perspectives are individuated by such partitions.** That is
  the second clause of the defeat condition.
