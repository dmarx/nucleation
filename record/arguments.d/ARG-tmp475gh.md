# ARG-tmp475gh: Interpretive framings change a reader's state as non-commuting measurements do, so the frame participates in determining which meaning becomes actual

## The inference

A21: the reader's interpretation is a state |ψ⟩; the questions "solidarity?"
and "condemnation?" are projectors; if [P_A, P_B] ≠ 0, order matters. With the
same words performing different acts in different frames ([CASE-tmpj5tw0](../cases.d/CASE-tmpj5tw0.md)), A21
moved "from *relativity of descriptions* to *contextuality of properties*" and
concluded [CLAIM-tmpvnofr](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpvnofr.md). It kept the level straight ("the reader's evolving
interpretive state, not ... the poem") and disclaimed physics ("This is not
evidence that the poem or brain is physically quantum").

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are the cases alike?** Order effects and context-dependent
  outcomes.
- **What transfers?** A representational vocabulary for incompatible
  questions. A27: "the first valuable application of quantum mathematics would
  be **representational rather than ontological**."
- **What does not?** The inference from those symptoms to nonclassicality. A24,
  from the record: "Context-sensitive meaning ⟹ Quantum contextuality. That
  implication is unjustified." A27: "Here I would actually moderate something I
  said earlier ... Neither phenomenon is inherently quantum." A30's reset
  matrices ([CASE-tmp11vtm](../cases.d/CASE-tmp11vtm.md)). The undercut is [CLAIM-tmpbwst7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbwst7.md).

A21 itself had flagged the gap ("order effects alone don't establish quantum
contextuality. Classical models with memory or state changes can also produce
them"), but still drew the conclusion.
