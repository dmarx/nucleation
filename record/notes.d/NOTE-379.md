# NOTE-379: Taylor — Consciousness interpreted

## Contribution

Critics from Block (1993, 1994) to Schwitzgebel (2007) have called
Dennett's theory of consciousness elusive or self-contradictory. Taylor
gives one reading on which six of its central claims are consistent. The
new move is to make "Real Patterns" the hub: consciousness is a real
pattern in the drafts, and a user-illusion is a particular kind of real
pattern. With that, the Orwellian/Stalinesque indeterminacy no longer
needs Dennett's "first-person operationalism", and the conflict between
fallibility and authority becomes a difference between literal and
fictional readings of the same report.

## Key insight

A real pattern is a description that trades predictive accuracy for
compression. If consciousness is such a pattern, every question about it
is a question about which description of the brain's drafts best makes
that trade. Questions with no such answer, such as whether the masked
disc was briefly conscious, have no fact behind them, not because the fact
is unverifiable but because both answers describe the pattern equally
well. And a pattern coarse enough to be a user-illusion describes the
brain only metaphorically, so its users are authors of their reports as
much as observers.

## Assumptions

- **Content is taken for granted** (n. 5). Taylor sets Dennett's theory
  of content aside and assumes drafts are content-bearing.
- **Dennett's own description of the masking cases.** The two readings
  are assumed to differ *only* in whether the disc was conscious, with no
  difference in downstream effects (p. 6). Taylor notes this is
  "somewhat simplistic" (n. 8, citing Haase and Fisk 2015). The argument
  for indeterminacy depends on it.
- **Interpretive charity, not truth.** The paper does not argue that the
  view is correct or that it is what Dennett meant (pp. 1–2).
- **Six claims chosen as core** (Box 1). Other claims Dennett has made
  are not reconciled, and his views changed over time (p. 2).

## Key results

- **The real-pattern reading** (pp. 2–4): consciousness is a pattern in
  drafts. Three features of real patterns do the work: the compression
  and accuracy trade-off (Dennett 1991b p. 36), several patterns in one
  system (p. 35), and existence independent of anyone describing it
  (p. 34).
- **Multiple drafts** (Box 2; pp. 4–9). (1) No Cartesian theatre: a
  pattern is distributed, as a Sicilian dragon is not a place on the
  board. (2) It is an open question whether a draft is conscious: being
  part of a pattern depends on relations to other states. (3) No fact
  decides between Orwell and Stalin: the readings are equally good
  compressions with the same predictions. This replaces the argument
  from first-person operationalism, which Taylor sets aside.
- **Global workspace** (pp. 9–10). None of the three claims conflicts
  with GWT. A distributed model of working memory (Christophel et al.
  2017; D'Esposito and Postle 2015) keeps sensory content in sensory
  cortex while it is broadcast, so nothing is "sent" to a theatre.
- **Qualia** (p. 10). Qualia are defined by Dennett's four marks.
  Rejecting them is rejecting one picture, and it is peripheral: CE's
  qualia chapter spends its length on Akins's view of colour, and
  Dennett's 2023 autobiography says he discussed qualia "grudgingly".
- **User-illusion** (pp. 10–13). A user-illusion is a real pattern. It
  gives explanations accepted as complete ("I just looked, and saw
  them") and it is so coarse that its descriptions lapse into metaphor,
  as with the people who call pencils plants (Dennett 2007).
- **Authority** (pp. 13–15). Reports are not immune to error read as
  literal descriptions of the brain (claim v). Read as unwitting
  metaphor they are fictions, over which the subject has an author's
  authority (claim vi). Against Schwitzgebel (2007), it may be more
  charitable to read them as fictions.

## Concepts

- **Draft / content-bearing state** — a brain state that encodes
  information. Dennett's "content fixations", "micro-takings" (with
  Kinsbourne) or "representations" (p. 4). Not ontologically loaded:
  processes and events count (n. 5).
- **Real pattern** — a description of a system more efficient than its
  "bit map", whether or not anyone concocts it (pp. 3–4).
- **User-illusion** — a real pattern so coarse-grained that describing
  the system through it is metaphor (pp. 10–13).
- **First-person operationalism** — Dennett's denial of "consciousness of
  a stimulus in the absence of the subject's belief in that
  consciousness" (CE p. 132, as quoted, p. 7). Set aside by the paper.

  confused; Taylor reads that as a negative thesis only. The two
  readings agree on what is rejected.
- **"Time and the Observer"** ([LIT-431](../literature.d/LIT-431.md), [NOTE-367](NOTE-367.md)). The source of the
  timing cases. Taylor discusses metacontrast only (n. 7 names colour
  phi and the cutaneous rabbit).
- **Frankish 2016** ([LIT-458](../literature.d/LIT-458.md), [NOTE-384](NOTE-384.md)). Not cited. The two
  papers disagree about what is central in Dennett. For Frankish,
  Dennett is illusionism's pre-eminent defender and the denial of
  phenomenal properties is the point. For Taylor, the denial is a
  sideline and the point is a "mild" realism. On Frankish's §1.6 the two
  are compatible: illusionists grant consciousness in an inclusive
  sense, with only quasi-phenomenal properties. Taylor's real pattern
  would be consciousness in that inclusive sense. Taylor does not draw
  this, and he does not discuss Dennett's 2016 adoption of the word
  "illusionism" ([LIT-434](../literature.d/LIT-434.md), unread).
- **Dennett 1980** ([LIT-456](../literature.d/LIT-456.md), [NOTE-371](NOTE-371.md)). Not cited, and the
  closest precedent for the paper's task. Stich charged Dennett with a
  "prima facie contradiction" about pain; Dennett's reply holds that
  there are no pains and that people feel pain, compares pain with
  holes, voices and haircuts, and leaves it there. The CE line on which
  Taylor builds ("as real as haircuts") is that comparison returned.
- **Schwitzgebel 2007** ("No unchallengeable epistemic authority ...")
  is cited for the puzzle about authority and for the charity
  objection. It is not filed.
- **Ladyman and Ross** ([LIT-301](../literature.d/LIT-301.md), unread) and Fallon 2020 are cited as
  readings of real patterns as an intermediate realism.

## Limitations

- **An interpretation, explicitly.** "My goal is not to argue that the
  theory is correct" (p. 1); nor (p. 2) that it is exactly what Dennett meant.
- **The masking argument rests on an idealisation** that the author
  flags (n. 8). If the two readings predict different downstream effects
  (Haase and Fisk 2015), C2 fails for that case.
- **"Which pattern?" is left open.** A system has many real patterns
  (p. 3). The paper does not say which one consciousness is, beyond
  "a user-illusion".
- **Six claims only.** Heterophenomenology, the self, memes and the
  origin of the metaphors are mentioned and set aside (p. 13).

## Open questions

- Is the real pattern that is consciousness the same pattern as the one
  the intentional stance finds, or a different one in the same drafts?
- If authority is an author's authority over unwitting metaphor, what
  makes a sincere report a fiction rather than a false belief? Taylor's
  answer (the coarseness forces metaphor) is stated, not argued.
- Can the compression-equivalence argument be made for a case where the
  readings are known to differ in prediction, rather than stipulated not
  to?
