# CASE-098: A long rivalry between two teams outlives every player, owner and supporter who first carried it: new fans inherit the antagonism, its history and its practices before they know why the game matters

## The case

Two teams have been rivals for generations (A2's example: the Yankees
and the Red Sox). Players, coaches, owners and supporters on both sides
have all been replaced. New supporters still learn that this game matters
more than others, learn the old incidents as the rivalry's history, and
reproduce its chants and its antagonism. A2: "A young fan might learn that
a particular opponent matters *before* understanding why."

What is held fixed: the two franchises and the competitive stakes. What
varies: every person who carries the antagonism.

## What it can show

That a relation between two collectives can be continuous while every
carrier is replaced, and that present antagonism can be transmitted
rather than generated by present conflicts. A2 separated interpersonal
antagonism, collective antagonism (membership disposes one to oppose) and
institutionalized rivalry (an enduring relation that structures both
sides' practices). Only the third is the case's point.

A5 §5 set it beside the dancing couple ([CASE-046](CASE-046.md), [CLAIM-175](../claims.d/CLAIM-175.md)) as a
contrast, not a further instance. The couple coordinates continuously and
may carry a collective variable ([CLAIM-172](../claims.d/CLAIM-172.md)). A rivalry has no obvious
contemporaneous dynamical variable. It is held by archives, scheduled
encounters and transmitted expectations, which is the time-lagged coupling
[CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md) says its block test cannot see ([QUESTION-116](../questions.d/QUESTION-116.md)).

Given its standing, it cannot show that any actual rivalry survived in
this way, that every supporter takes part in it, or that it is a system.
Berendt and Uhrich's survey of Bundesliga supporters ([LIT-1147](../literature.d/LIT-1147.md), unread)
ties rivalry to in-group distinctiveness and collective self-esteem, which
is evidence about identity, not about the rivalry as a unit. A5's
discriminating variation: hold the teams and stakes fixed and interrupt
transmission of the rivalry's history; does the antagonism persist?

## Note of 2026-10-29: Berendt and Uhrich, read

LIT-1147 has now been read (NOTE-tmpp2j50). The sentence above calls it
unread and judges it "evidence about identity, not about the rivalry as a
unit". That still holds, with one addition. In the survey of 571
Bundesliga supporters, rivalry's gains for fans (perceived
distinctiveness, cohesion, regard from other clubs' fans) run through
*perceived reciprocity*, the belief that the rival returns the
antagonism. They do not run through fans' own disidentification from
the rival (THEORY-tmpk1fow).

So what individual fans take from a rivalry depends on how they believe
the other side sees the relation. That is a perceived two-sidedness, the
individual-level shadow of the institutionalized rivalry this case is
about, and still not evidence that the rivalry is a unit.

The paper also reports that 164 of 748 intercepted supporters, mostly at
clubs without a famous rivalry, named no archrival at all. Not every
supporter carries a rivalry.

A5's discriminating variation is untouched: the study does not interrupt
transmission. Talarico and Moore (LIT-1125), on memory of a rivalry game,
is now Unavailable. Qi, Giulianotti and Wiltshire (LIT-1131) is
