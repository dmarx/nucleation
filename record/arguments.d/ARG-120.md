# ARG-120: Two-step acquisition → valued by later effects

## The inference

1. In [CASE-295](../cases.d/CASE-295.md), P alone nets −2 and Q alone nets −1.
2. P then Q nets +6, the best of the four policies.
3. So valuing each acquisition by its own savings rejects both, while the
   best policy includes P, whose return on its own tasks is negative.
4. The difference is P's effect on Q's cost, 10 to 1.

## Critical questions

**Is the case coherent?** Yes. Every quantity is stipulated, nonnegative
and in one unit; the arithmetic was computed. An acquisition that lowers
the cost of another is ordinary: a notation that makes a later proof
cheaper, a skill that is a prerequisite.

**Does it separate the very things the conclusion says come apart?** Yes:
the value of P on its own tasks (−2) against its value in the best policy
(+7 marginal, the difference between +6 and Q alone's −1). It does not
separate them in any learner's actual experience; it is an existence
proof, as the claim needs.
