# ARG-075: Two separately made mechanisms of one design are isomorphic and two, and a mechanism can be stipulated to persist through a change that breaks isomorphism; the first carries over to organized individuals and the second only as consistency, so whether such an individual continues needs a criterion of continuation

## The inference

1. [CLAIM-593](../claims.d/CLAIM-593.md), first direction: two separately made mechanisms with the
   same costed transformation structure "are isomorphic and still two".
   "The count of tokens is not in the structure."
2. [CLAIM-593](../claims.d/CLAIM-593.md), second direction: one mechanism whose hinge is replaced "can
   persist while its costed structure becomes non-isomorphic to the earlier
   one". "The second direction rests on a stipulation", so "can persist"
   means that persistence, so stipulated, "is consistent with the loss of
   isomorphism".
3. Organized individuals, such as software projects, are also tokens
   described by a structure. So [CLAIM-615](../claims.d/CLAIM-615.md)'s "two structurally
   identical copies can be two individuals" and "the structure can change
   under maps that are not isomorphisms while the individual continues"
   are [CLAIM-593](../claims.d/CLAIM-593.md)'s two directions carried to them.

A55 §1 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6aeea2bc-d280-83e9-ac1f-f6772adf263c)
draws the analogy without stating it as one: [CLAIM-593](../claims.d/CLAIM-593.md) "already states the
objection that identical structures need not be the same historical
individual", and the software-fork discussion "supplies new illustrations,
not a fundamentally new theorem".

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are they alike, and is that the respect the conclusion
  needs?** Both are tokens described by a structure. The first direction
  uses only that the count of tokens is not in the structure, which holds
  in any category, so it transfers whole: two forks identical at the fork
  instant are two projects ([CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s note of 2026-11-07).
- **What relevant difference is there?** For mechanisms the second
  direction rests on a stipulation that the hinge swap is a continuation.
  For organized individuals the stipulation is replaced by [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s
  criterion, causal transmission (Proposed), which is itself open. So
  [CLAIM-615](../claims.d/CLAIM-615.md)'s clause on change that is not an isomorphism is shown
  consistent, not shown to occur. A55 §1's "one individual can survive
  changes that destroy structural isomorphism" drops the stipulation.
- **What transfers, and what does not?** What transfers is that "which
  morphisms count as continuations is structure added by assumptions about
  the domain, not read off the category" ([CLAIM-593](../claims.d/CLAIM-593.md)). [CLAIM-615](../claims.d/CLAIM-615.md)'s
  transmission criterion is the assumption it adds for organized
  individuals. What does not transfer is any demonstration that a given
  individual continued: that needs the criterion applied to a case, as
  [CASE-223](../cases.d/CASE-223.md) proposes.

Proposed: the clause on identical copies is supported, and the clause on
non-isomorphic change only as possibility.
