# THEORY-098: The neural basis of perceptual consciousness does not include the prefrontal machinery of cognitive access: it is recurrent activity in sensory cortex

## Source

- Block (2007), [LIT-646](../literature.d/LIT-646.md), read in [NOTE-497](../notes.d/NOTE-497.md): §§13–14 (working
  memory and phenomenology in the brain; neural coalitions and the mesh
  argument). Target article only.
- Block (2005), *Two neural correlates of consciousness*, [LIT-629](../literature.d/LIT-629.md),
  read in [NOTE-496](../notes.d/NOTE-496.md): the phenomenal and access NCCs, the MT/V5 and
  Supèr et al. evidence, Box 1.
- Block (2019), *What Is Wrong with the No-Report Paradigm and How to Fix
  It*, [LIT-637](../literature.d/LIT-637.md), read in [NOTE-495](../notes.d/NOTE-495.md): the no-report evidence on both
  sides, Brascamp et al.'s design, "Anticipating an Objection" and the
  Concluding Remarks.

## The claim, assembled

**Two correlates.** Block (2005), [LIT-629](../literature.d/LIT-629.md), separates a *phenomenal
NCC*, the minimal basis of an experience's content, from an *access NCC*,
the frontoparietal activation that broadcasts a content to reasoning and
report. For one kind of seen motion the phenomenal NCC is recurrent
activity in and around MT/V5. TMS to MT/V5 produces moving phosphenes, and
a V1 pulse delivered within the feedback window after it leaves them
mostly stationary (Pascual-Leone and Walsh), which "suggests that
experiencing moving phosphenes depends not only on activation of MT/V5 but
also on a recurrent feedback loop to V1 and back". Access is a
winner-take-all competition: in the attentional blink, visibility ratings
are almost never intermediate. So a recurrent sensory representation can
lose that competition and stay unaccessed. In Supèr et al.'s monkeys, V1
figure–ground modulation, which vanishes under anaesthesia and is
"probably produced by recurrent processes", averaged the same whether or
not the monkey saccaded to the target once salience was lowered or catch
trials raised. Block takes it as "an indication of, if not directly part
of" a phenomenal NCC, and calls the whole case "a glimmer".

**The mesh argument.** Block (2007), [LIT-646](../literature.d/LIT-646.md), §13, places the
bottleneck of working memory in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex: delay
firing there maintains representations held in posterior areas, and
damage impairs delayed but not simultaneous performance. He places the
core basis of seen motion in a V1–V5–V1 loop (Silvanto et al.). Frontal
lobotomies do not appear to dim basic perceptual content: "Frontal damage
impairs access but it doesn't dim the bulb" (§14). Kouider et al. (2007)
found supraliminal words under distraction activating occipito-temporal
cortex strongly without triggering frontal coalitions. The argument (§14):
"If we assume that the strong but still losing coalitions in the back of
the head are the neural basis of phenomenal states (so long as they
involve recurrent activity), then we have a neural mechanism which
explains why phenomenology has a higher capacity than the global
workspace. If, on the contrary, we assume that the neural basis of
phenomenology includes workspace activation, then we do not have such a
mechanism." Overflow ([THEORY-103](THEORY-103.md)) shows only that the machinery of
experience is partly distinct from that of access; "what the mesh argument
adds is that the machinery of phenomenology does not include the
machinery of cognitive accessibility". Block leaves room for causal
influence in both directions. Top-down amplification may usually be
involved, but it is not constitutive.

**The prefrontal question since.** Block (2019), [LIT-637](../literature.d/LIT-637.md), frames the
dispute as whether certain prefrontal regions (dorsolateral, medial
prefrontal, anterior cingulate, orbitofrontal) are necessary for
perceptual consciousness. The global workspace and higher-order theories
say yes, and recurrent processing and IIT say no. He reports evidence on
both sides from no-report binocular rivalry. Frässle et al. found
prefrontal differences minor once report was removed. Panagiotaropoulos's
group decoded rivalry contents from monkey prefrontal cortex with no
report, in monkeys never trained on a discrimination. Block argues that
neither settles the question, because uninstructed subjects still think
about what they see ([THEORY-094](THEORY-094.md)). In Brascamp et al.'s
inconspicuous rivalry, switches were detected at chance by d′, so nothing
in thought followed them, and prefrontal differences were "altogether
undetectable" by fMRI. Block's verdict is that "the Brascamp et al.
experiment is evidence against prefrontalism, further experiments with
ECog could support prefrontalism", and that the article's "main point
... is to home in on the methodological issue rather than supporting one
side".

## What this does not say

- **It does not say the prefrontal question is settled.** Block (2019),
  [LIT-637](../literature.d/LIT-637.md), says it is not, by no-report designs or by the one
  no-cognition design he offers. That design is a single fMRI null, and
  Block grants that its changes "might require temporal and spatial
  resolution too fine grained for fMRI". The no-report prefrontal decoding
  in monkeys is explained away by the bored-monkey problem, not refuted.
  It too awaits a no-cognition design.
- **It does not say prefrontal cortex plays no causal role.** The claim is
  constitutive: prefrontal access machinery is not part of the basis of
  perceptual experience. Block allows that top-down influence may usually
  be what makes sensory activity strong enough.
- **It does not say recurrence is sufficient for experience in general.**
  The recurrence evidence is for one kind of seen motion (MT/V5) and,
  more weakly, for figure–ground modulation in V1. Block (2005) calls
  different phenomenal NCCs for different contents the expected outcome,
  and leaves "exactly which brain areas are required" as background
  unknown. Block (2019) speaks only of "the back and middle of the head".
- **It does not cover the phenomenology of thought.** Noncognitivists can
  allow that if thought has a phenomenology of its own, it is prefrontal
  ([LIT-637](../literature.d/LIT-637.md)). The claim is about perceptual consciousness.
- **Every experiment is second-hand**, and Block calls the neural details
  of 2007 "wrong, or at least highly incomplete". The argument is an
  inference to the best explanation, and it inherits the contested step
  of [THEORY-103](THEORY-103.md), that the overflow is experienced.

## Why it presupposes the overflow account

This account presupposes [THEORY-103](THEORY-103.md); it does not refine it. The mesh argument takes
overflow as its psychological premise and supplies the neural mechanism
for it, losing recurrent sensory coalitions. If overflow were shown to be
an illusion, the mesh argument would lose its premise, and this account
would rest on the 2005 and 2019 evidence alone.

## The rival account

[THEORY-096](THEORY-096.md) (global availability by ignition, from Mashour et al.,
[LIT-631](../literature.d/LIT-631.md)) puts prefrontal hubs inside the basis of conscious
perception, and treats prefrontal coding of the percept without report
as unconfounded. Block's 2019 paper ([LIT-637](../literature.d/LIT-637.md)) denies exactly that
second step. The rivalry is declared on that file. Inconspicuous rivalry
with intracranial prefrontal recording would bear on both.

## Connections

- **[THEORY-102](THEORY-102.md) (Vishne et al., [LIT-644](../literature.d/LIT-644.md))** is not a source.
  Its data fit this account: for a clearly seen image, content was carried
  in ventral temporal cortex for as long as the image stayed on, while
  prefrontal content was an onset burst that ignored duration. But three
  things keep it from bearing weight here. Every image was seen, so
  experience and access never came apart. The global workspace's own
  pre-registered predictions were met too, as the authors report. And its
  no-report trials still required each image to be judged a non-target,
  which is post-perceptual cognition of the kind [THEORY-094](THEORY-094.md) says
  leaves the onset burst ambiguous. This account does not build on or
  replace that one; it presupposes it, since the argument against the
  no-report prefrontal evidence above takes its conclusion as a premise.
- **[THEORY-100](THEORY-100.md).** In crows, neurons in the nidopallium caudolaterale,
  the functional analogue of prefrontal cortex, followed the reported
  percept. On this account that is an access correlate. It is what the
  account predicts of a region that carries access, and it says nothing
  about the basis of the experience. No relation is declared: the two
  accounts are about different correlates.
- **Block (2009), [LIT-632](../literature.d/LIT-632.md)**, names this the "biological" theory and
  argues that on it broadcasting is "what consciousness does rather than
  what consciousness is".
