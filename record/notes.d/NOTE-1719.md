# NOTE-1719: Gal et al. — Textual Inversion

## Contribution

Personalization by inverting a concept into the text-embedding space of a frozen model: the learned artifact is one input vector, not new weights.

## Key results

- **Frozen model.** §1: "this process leaves the generative model untouched."
- **Amortization, in the authors' words.** §4.2: "we can optimize a single pseudo-word and re-use it for a multitude of new generations. The baseline models, meanwhile, use CLIP for test-time optimization and thus require expensive optimization for every new creation."
- **Up-front cost.** §6: "learning a single concept requires roughly two hours."
- **Reuse within one base.** §4.6: the pseudo-words "can be used in downstream models that build on the same initial LDM model."
- **Identity.** Societal impact: "our model does not currently preserve identity to the extent where this is a concern."

## Assumptions

The text-embedding space of a frozen model is expressive enough; CLIP-based metrics; partially curated samples. The base is Rombach et al.'s 1.4B LDM, not Stable Diffusion.

## Concepts

- **pseudo-word S\*** — a learned token embedding standing for the concept.
- **distortion–editability trade-off** — better reconstruction at the cost of composability in prompts.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-095](../questions.d/QUESTION-095.md): supports, directly.** Pay once for a stored embedding, then reuse it, against baselines that "require expensive optimization for every new creation": the question's trade, stated by the authors. The stored thing is an input that means something only to the text encoder it was learned in.
- **[CASE-235](../cases.d/CASE-235.md):** supports the 2022 row and adds a third shape for the trained route: what is learned can be an input to a frozen model, not weights.
- **[CLAIM-647](../claims.d/CLAIM-647.md):** weakly corroborates the scoping premise. Reuse is claimed only within one base; that a new text encoder would strand the embedding is the filer's inference, not tested.

## Limitations

One base model; CLIP metrics; weak identity preservation by the authors' account; no inference-cost figures beyond the per-concept optimization time.
