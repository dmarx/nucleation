# THEORY-1485: On Schröder, Persson and De Roos, of 35 manipulation experiments testing for alternative stable states, 21 were conclusive over at least one turnover of the longest-lived organisms, 13 of them showing bistability and 8 its absence; positives came mostly from laboratory rather than field systems, and only 3 of 15 tests of non-recovery after a pressure was removed were positive

## Source

Schröder, Persson & De Roos (2005), [LIT-2167](../literature.d/LIT-2167.md); read in [NOTE-1666](../notes.d/NOTE-1666.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Totals** (abstract, p. 3): "13 experiments have directly demonstrated
  the existence of alternative stable states while 8 showed the absence of
  ASS in other cases. 14 experiments did not fulfil the requirements of a
  conclusive test, mostly because they applied a too short time scale."
- **Laboratory against field** (p. 7): "Ten out of 12 conclusive
  laboratory experiments demonstrated bistability, while this was only
  true for three out of nine field experiments (Table 1)."
- **Non-recovery** (p. 9; Table 2): "Fifteen such tests were found, of
  which three demonstrated ASS, five their absence and seven were
  inappropriate tests". All appropriate ones were grazer exclusions in
  terrestrial systems.
- **The criterion** (p. 6): stable only "if it stays quantitatively and
  qualitatively unchanged despite that all its initially resident
  individuals have replaced themselves".

Each experiment could have come out either way, and 8 of the conclusive
21 found no alternative states.

## What this does not say

- That alternative stable states are rare in nature. The review counts
  published tests, which are few and biased toward the laboratory; the
  authors call ASS "just one possibility".
- That a system which recovered after the pressure was removed had no
  alternative state under other conditions.
- Anything about whether restoration or other support can be withdrawn;
  the paper is not about restoration.
- Anything from Appendix 1, which extracted garbled, including the reading
  that biomanipulated lakes drifted back to turbid.
- Anything Suding, Gross & Houseman (2004) showed.
