# NOTE-1297: I Belong, therefore, I Exist: Ingroup Identification, Ingroup Entitativity, and Ingroup Bias

## Contribution

It joins two literatures. Social identity research had studied proximal
causes of group behaviour: identification, entitativity, intergroup
context. Terror management theory had found that reminders of death raise
in-group favouritism, and explained it as defence of a worldview. The
paper asks whether death reminders act on the link between person and
group itself: whether they raise identification with one's group and the
perception that the group is a real entity. It answers yes in one
experiment, and proposes a new function for belonging: a group gives the
self a part that does not die.

## Key insight

"Through identification with social groups, the individuals can project
themselves in space and time, beyond their personal death. They
participate in an entity that is not subject to the mortal fate that
characterizes them as human beings" (p. 137). For that to work, the group
"must be perceived as a 'real' entity" (p. 137). So a death reminder
should raise both identification and perceived entitativity.

## Assumptions

- **Terror management theory.** Awareness of death threatens paralysing
  terror, buffered by faith in a cultural worldview and by self-esteem
  (pp. 136–137). Reminders of death raise the need for the buffer.
- **Social identity theory's definition**: "that part of an individual's
  self-concept which derives from his knowledge of his membership in a
  social group" together with its value and emotional significance
  (Tajfel 1981, quoted p. 136).
- **Entitativity**, after Campbell (1958): "the extent to which a group is
  perceived as having real existence, as being a real entity" (p. 136).
- **Mediators cause bias, not the reverse**, on the strength of earlier
  work and of measuring them first (note 3).

## Key results

- **Design** (pp. 137–138). 48 undergraduates at the University of Padua,
  approached in libraries (95% agreed); 26 women, 22 men, aged 19–33
  (M = 23); all Italian citizens; 24 per condition.
  - Mortality salient: "Please write a short paragraph describing the
    emotions that the thought of your own death arouses in you."
  - Control: the same about "reading a book".
  - Then, in one booklet: entitativity of Italians (a modified scale;
    "Italians have many characteristics in common", "Italians have a sense
    of common fate", "Italy has real existence as a group"); six
    identification items ("I identify with Italians", "Being Italian has
    nothing to do with my identity"); ratings of Italians and Germans on 10
    traits. No delay or distracting task between the manipulation and the
    measures is reported, and no mood measure.
- **Scales** (p. 138). Entitativity 19 of 20 items, α = .89; identification
  α = .90; trait ratings 9 of 10 items, α = .82 for Italians and .68 for
  Germans. Response ranges are not stated.
- **Identification and entitativity** (p. 138). Identification 6.19
  against 5.09, F(1, 46) = 4.86, reported p < .03. Entitativity 5.40
  against 4.77, F(1, 46) = 4.15, p < .05.
- **In-group bias** (p. 138, Table 1). Ratings:

  | | Italians | Germans | bias |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | mortality salient | 6.70 (.75) | 5.69 (.81) | 1.01 (.93) |
  | control | 5.88 (.98) | 5.43 (.91) | 0.45 (.98) |

  Target F(1, 46) = 27.39, p < .001; condition F(1, 46) = 6.63, reported
  p < .01; interaction F(1, 46) = 4.11, p < .05. Bias was present in the
  control group, t(23) = 2.20, p < .05, and larger after the death prompt,
  t(23) = 5.28, p < .0001. The death prompt raised ratings of Italians,
  t = 3.24, p < .01, and not of Germans, t = 1.03, p > .29.
- **Mediation** (pp. 138–139, Figure 1, unstandardized).
  - Entitativity: condition → entitativity .62; entitativity → bias .32
    (.27 with condition); condition → bias .56, falling to .39, not
    significant. Sobel Z = 1.71, p < .08. Path model χ²(1) = 2.05,
    p = .15, R² = .12.
  - Identification: condition → identification 1.09; identification →
    bias .21 (.17); condition → bias .56, falling to .37. Sobel Z = 1.83,
    p < .06. χ²(1) = 1.80, p = .18, R² = .14.
- **Distinct constructs** (p. 139). A two-factor model fitted better than a
  one-factor model (χ²(64) = 108.51 against χ²(65) = 138.86, a difference
  of 30.35 on 1 df; CFI .84 against .73), with the factors correlated .59.
  Both fits are poor by the authors' own description.
- **Shared variance does the mediating** (pp. 139–140). Neither
  identification's residual (after entitativity) nor entitativity's
  residual (after identification) mediated. Their predicted values did.
- **Two readings, not separated** (pp. 140–141).
  - Worldview defence: the group matters because it is "the milieu where
    worldviews are created and maintained"; identification is then an
    epiphenomenon of that need.
  - The group as a buffer in its own right: social identities are
    "noncorporeal and highly symbolic" and "can be perceived as
    transcending the individual mortal fate"; raising entitativity is "an
    attempt to reify the ingroup" (p. 141).

  The authors favour the second for the entitativity result and call it
  "tentative" (p. 141).
- **Self-sacrifice** (p. 141). If the fear of death is the fear of losing
  one's identity, "self-immolation for the ingroup is quite a reasonable
  choice: Through the social extension of the self implemented by group
  belonging, individuals may have the feeling that they are offered
  transcendence."

## Concepts

- **entitativity**: how far a collection is perceived as a real entity,
  with real existence; a degree (Campbell 1958).
- **ingroup identification**: here, the importance of the group to one's
  identity, measured by six verbal items.
- **mortality salience**: an experimental reminder of one's own death.
- **worldview defence**: protecting the cultural belief system that buffers
  the terror of death, by favouring those who share it.
- **proximal and distal causes**: the immediate cognitive and motivational
  causes of group behaviour, against fundamental needs such as the
  management of death anxiety.

## Connections

- **Terror management, measured** (Burke et al., [LIT-1687](../literature.d/LIT-1687.md), unread).
  The meta-analysis reports moderate mortality-salience effects across
  paradigms. The Many Labs 4 replication of the classic mortality-salience
  effect on worldview defence, which failed, may bear on how much weight
  this study can carry; another reader is drafting that note, and the
  record has no code for it yet.
- **Lifton's symbolic immortality ([LIT-1714](../literature.d/LIT-1714.md))**, unread. The paper does
  not cite Lifton, but its "transcendence" through the group, and its
  quotation of Anthony Smith on nationalism's "promise of collective and
  terrestrial immortality" (p. 140), are the same idea.
- **A group entity versus a category of members ([TERM-096](../terms.d/TERM-096.md)).**
  Italians, a nationality, is the term's own example of a category. The
  paper measures entitativity as a degree, which the term sets aside as
  different from its difference in kind. Its scale mixes likeness
  ("many characteristics in common", a category's mark) with common fate
  and "real existence as a group", closer to an entity's.
- **Skorich's group-entity account ([THEORY-1112](../theory.d/THEORY-1112.md)).** That account says
  perceiving a group entity needs cues of shared mental states. Here a
  death reminder, not a cue about the group, moved perceived
  entitativity: the perceiver's need moved it.
- **Identity fusion ([NOTE-1256](NOTE-1256.md)).** Swann et al. explain self-sacrifice
  for the group by self-verification. Castano et al. explain it, more
  speculatively, by transcendence through an extended self. Both make
  sacrifice serve the self as a self-concept, not as a welfare.
- **Self-categorization theory ([THEORY-1114](../theory.d/THEORY-1114.md)).** The authors write
  within social identity theory and do not use depersonalization; their
  closing hypothesis is a shift "from personal to social identities"
  (p. 141).

## Bearing on the record

- **The legacy question ([QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md)).** This is the study the
  question cites for where the temporal and social extensions of the self
  meet. Read, it supports that meeting as the authors' proposal, not as a
  finding: see the thesis below.
- **The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md)).** These are inferences.
  - The paper states the thesis's join of social and temporal extension in
    so many words: "the social extension of the self implemented by group
    belonging" offers transcendence of one's death (p. 141). On it, dying
    for the group is not selfless but "a reasonable choice" for a self that
    fears annihilation. That is the thesis's strongest form, and the record
    now holds it as a published conjecture.
  - The data support a weaker claim. A death reminder raised identification
    and valuing of the in-group. That fits "the self turns to the part that
    does not die", but equally fits "the self defends the worldview the
    group carries". The authors say they cannot tell these apart (p. 141).
    Only the first is the thesis.
  - Nothing measures the self's extent. Identification is six verbal items;
    no overlap measure, no fusion item.
- **The triviality objection ([CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)).** Entitativity is a property
  of the group as perceived, and it separates from identification (r =
  .59). But the two mediate only through what they share, and there is no
  measure of caring about Italians apart from rating them. So the paper
  does not show a criterion of the self's extent coming apart from
  valuing the group.
- **Batson's objection ([CLAIM-494](../claims.d/CLAIM-494.md)).** Not addressed.
- **The term ([TERM-096](../terms.d/TERM-096.md)).** A dated note is proposed:

  > Castano et al. (NOTE-1297) found that a reminder of one's own death
  > raised the perceived entitativity of Italians, a category in this
  > term's sense, in 48 Italian students (5.40 against 4.77). They read it
  > as an attempt "to reify the ingroup" into something that outlasts the
  > self. If so, the difference in kind between a category and a group
  > entity is perceived as a degree, and motivation can move a category
  > toward an entity. Their scale mixes likeness with common fate and
  > "real existence", so it does not say which side of the term moved.

## Limitations

- **One study, 48 participants**, 24 per cell, Italian students recruited
  in libraries. The bias effect rests on one trait list and one out-group.
- **A mortality-salience effect, not a replication-tested one.** No delay
  or distractor before the measures is reported, and mood was not measured
  (note 1 answers the mood objection from the literature).
- **Mediators measured, not manipulated**; the Sobel tests were marginal
  (p < .08, p < .06), and the shared-variance analysis leaves open what
  the common factor is.
- **The proposal is not tested.** Nothing measures transcendence, symbolic
  immortality or how lasting the group is believed to be.
- **Reporting slips.**
  - The definitions are swapped on p. 137: entitativity is glossed as "the
    importance of the group for the identity of the individual" and
    identification as "the perception of the ingroup as a real entity".
  - Figure 1's note reads "*p < .5".
  - The model difference is printed "χ²(30.35)"; it is χ²(1) = 30.35.
  - The between-condition t-tests on ratings are printed t(24); with 48
    participants in two groups the df would be 46.
  - F(1, 46) = 4.86 is printed p < .03 and F(1, 46) = 6.63 p < .01; they
    are p ≈ .033 and p ≈ .013.
- **Typos.**
  - "Univesité" in Yzerbyt's affiliation; "the level identification" (p.
    136); "particular relevant" and "the their cultural worldview"
    (p. 137); "where entered" and "goodness of fit-ness" (p. 139); "must
    be perceive" and "epiphenomenona" (p. 140).
  - In the references: "Pyszcsynzski", "Hogart", "Wheterel", and "n the
    national system.:" in Kelman's title.

## Open questions

- Does the effect replicate, given the doubts Many Labs 4 raises about
  mortality salience?
- Which reading holds? Two groups with the same worldview, one seen as
  lasting and one as about to dissolve, would separate transcendence
  through the group from defence of the worldview.
- Would a death reminder raise self–group overlap or fusion, not only
  verbal identification? That would test the thesis's version directly.
