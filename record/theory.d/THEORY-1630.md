# THEORY-1630: Sugden argues that theories of social preference either fit any choice trivially or cannot represent trust, and that cooperative team reasoning, which intends the common interests of members rather than the collective interest of the team, represents trust and market exchange as joint action for mutual benefit

## The account

"Intending to promote the common interests of team members is not the
same thing as intending to promote the collective interests of the
team" (p. 154). The first is cooperative; the second can ask a member to
lose so that the team gains. Social preferences can fit any behaviour,
since "it is always possible to assume that the relevant player has a
preference for behaving in exactly that way" (p. 148), and building
pro-social motives into payoffs "requires, as a matter of conceptual
necessity, that an individuals' pro-social motivations can always be
represented as payoffs to her as an individual" (p. 145). The Schema of
Cooperative Team Reasoning aims at a mutually beneficial practice, and
a team reasoner need not see her act as a sacrifice, nor as "adopting a
collective goal that transcends her private interests" (pp. 163–164).

## Evidence

A proof for Rabin's model, counterexamples for other models, and a
schema shown to give the intended verdicts in five games ([NOTE-1288](../notes.d/NOTE-1288.md)).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) holds that team reasoning (Sugden) makes "what is good
for us" a mode "that is neither self-interest nor altruism". This paper
is the reading of it the record holds: Bacharach's team reasoner pursues
a collective good close to a larger self's, and Sugden's cooperative
reasoner intends each member's benefit with no larger self in play.
