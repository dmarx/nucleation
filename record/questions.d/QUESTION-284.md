# QUESTION-284: In any domain, when do the constraints on an entity's relations only characterize an entity individuated without them, and when do they partly constitute its identity, its boundary or what it can do; and are a boundary a process detects, one it maintains and one it constitutes three different things?

## Why it is a question

A5 §5 proposed it first: "**When is an individuation merely a partition
imposed by an observational or explanatory regime, and when is the
differentiating process itself constitutive of the individuals it
separates?**" A6 §8, after the owner's U6 removed the domain limit ("the
project's ambitions are extremely abstract and unrestricted"), restated it
over constraints: "**Under what conditions do relational constraints
merely characterize independently existing relata, and under what
conditions do they constitute the identities, boundaries, and
possibilities of the relata themselves?**" A10 §8 named its parts:
"which distinctions are merely descriptive, which are causally
consequential, which are constitutive, and which persist across
transformations?" Those are [TERM-038](../terms.d/TERM-038.md)'s three kinds of relation, drawn
there for a communicative act, here for any entity.

**Three boundaries.** A5 closed on a distinction the record's
individuation claims run together: "**boundaries that are detected,
boundaries that are dynamically maintained, and boundaries that are
constituted through the very processes by which a system becomes
distinguishable**". Its schematic: discovery is 𝒟 → B, a boundary read off
the dynamics; enactment is (𝒟, A) → B_A, a boundary that depends on a
material arrangement A. "These possibilities are not mutually exclusive.
A measuring apparatus could alter a system's dynamics, and a subsequent
analysis could correctly discover the boundaries in the altered system.
The harder question is whether individuation is ontologically dependent
on such arrangements, rather than merely epistemically dependent on
them." [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)'s block criterion detects a boundary; closure of
constraints ([TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)) is a boundary maintained; whether either is
constituted is not asked by either.

## What would count as an answer

For a stated kind of entity, a criterion that tells the three apart and
says what evidence bears on each. A5 proposed the test: on one dynamical
model, compare "a fixed preexisting partition, a partition recovered from
measured dynamics, and an arrangement-dependent partition produced through
coupling to an apparatus", and ask "whether the three accounts make
different counterfactual predictions, and whether any evidence
distinguishes ontological constitution from ordinary causal
modification." If none does, that is itself an answer: [CLAIM-150](../claims.d/CLAIM-150.md)'s point
that the data underdetermine constitution, carried out of communicative
acts.

The record has partial answers. [CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md) says constraints partly
constitute in general, without a criterion for which. [CLAIM-405](../claims.d/CLAIM-405.md) says a
verdict is well defined only once its criterion is fixed, and can then be
false. In the record itself, [CLAIM-476](../claims.d/CLAIM-476.md) claims the constitutive case
for a record's objects. For communicative acts the question is
[QUESTION-030](QUESTION-030.md), which asks which interventions tell constitutive relations
from accompanying ones, and [QUESTION-073](QUESTION-073.md) is its sharpest case.

## Note of 2026-10-31: individuation by internal ordering, without a boundary

<!-- inactive-ok-block: THEORY-tmp4o1dd THEORY-tmpcaccx THEORY-tmp1g3ve — Proposed; the readings filed 2026-10-31, cited as context -->
Bencherki and Iliadis (LIT-tmpwspny, read in NOTE-tmp62e4t) offer an
option this question's three boundaries do not list. They individuate a
domain by its internal ordering and not by any boundary. "What matters,
for Simondon, is not so much an external definition or bracketing of a
domain, as much as an internal ordering whose logic distinguishes
different domains for practical purposes. This should be contrasted ...
with the notion of boundary ... in a Luhmannian perspective that states
that 'to maintain its existence, the organization continuously needs to
reproduce a boundary'" (ms. pp. 20–21). So they reject the maintained
boundary (closure, TERM-070) as what individuates an organization, and
they do not offer a detected one.

- **The record has a formal case of it.** CLAIM-388: a collective
  variable can be exactly closed while no partition of the units is a
  weakly coupled block. A unit is individuated by its dynamics with no
  bounded subsystem to carry it. CLAIM-418 already reads it so. Whether
  "ordered differently from their neighbours" (ms. p. 21) can be stated
  as such a variable is the operational step the readings omit
  (THEORY-tmp4o1dd, "What fixes a level").
- **The readings take the constitutive reading for granted.** Bencherki,
  Brummans and Vézy adopt Barad's agential cut (LIT-tmp3mudg, p. 1357),
  and Bencherki and Iliadis's fourth proposition asks that knowledge of
  an entity "reflect on the way that knowledge itself has individuated"
  (ms. p. 22). Neither offers what this question asks for: a comparison
  that would tell constitution by the inquiry from detection. Their
  illustrations were chosen to fit (NOTE-tmp62e4t, "Limitations"), so
  they could not have told them apart.
- **One sharper point for the question's first half.** Bencherki 2017
  (LIT-tmp1rpyl, p. 782): "a being only possesses its components from a
  particular perspective: their contribution to its individuation
  process; possession is never complete or univocal." That says a
  component is a component of a whole only under the whole's
  individuation. It is a constitutive claim about the part-whole relation
  rather than the boundary. It is stated, not argued.

The question is unchanged and stays Open.
