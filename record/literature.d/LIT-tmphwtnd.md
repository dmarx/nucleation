# LIT-tmphwtnd: Can populations be healthy? Perspectives from Georges Canguilhem and Geoffrey Rose

Élodie Giroux (2021). *History and Philosophy of the Life Sciences* 43(4),
article 111. DOI 10.1007/s40656-021-00463-x, PMC8527978.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmptu1ne.*

- **Canguilhem's health is normativity.** Health is "the possibility of
  transcending the norm, which defines the momentary normal, the
  possibility of tolerating infractions of the habitual norm and
  instituting new norms in new situations" (Canguilhem 1991, pp. 196–197,
  quoted in note 8); disease is "the emergence of a 'new individual norm'"
  (p. 194).
- **It is individual by his lights.** Normativity belongs to the organism as
  an organic totality; a society "is not an individual, because it is not
  an organism endowed with a purpose and a totality that are obtained by a
  specialized system of devices of regulation" (Canguilhem 2012, pp.
  76–77).
- **Biology has loosened the tie.** Units-of-selection debates, species as
  individuals and superorganisms detach individuality from the organism.
- **Epidemiology gives three arguments for population health.** Population
  comparisons reveal associations invisible within a population; integral
  variables such as herd immunity have no individual analogue; and Rose's
  "causes of incidence" differ from "causes of cases".
- **A relative, not a strong, autonomy.** Populations are not to be given
  "an ontological unity as strong as that of the individual organism".
