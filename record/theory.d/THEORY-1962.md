# THEORY-1962: Law guards entitlements by three kinds of rule

Calabresi & Melamed (1972), [LIT-2749](../literature.d/LIT-2749.md).

## The account

The three definitions are on p. 1092: a property rule gives "the seller a
veto if the buyer does not offer enough"; a liability rule lets someone
"destroy the initial entitlement if he is willing to pay an objectively
determined value for it"; an entitlement is inalienable "to the extent
that its transfer is not permitted between a willing buyer and a willing
seller". §V adds that charging a thief only the value taken "would be to
convert all property rule entitlements into liability rule entitlements"
(p. 1125), and "we impose criminal sanctions as a means of deterring
future attempts to convert property rules into liability rules" (p. 1126).

## What it does not show

- That consent, rather than efficiency, is what the conversion harms. The
  paper argues from the approximation in a liability price.
- That inalienability rests on the holder's standing. The paper's reasons
  are instrumental, and its inalienability can be partial and leaves
  compensation in place.
- Anything about bargaining after a breach or the cost of litigation.
