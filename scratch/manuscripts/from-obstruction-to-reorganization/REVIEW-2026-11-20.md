# Review of the draft, 2026-11-20

This review combines four inputs. The first is an independent reviewer who read the draft and the cited entries without the author's reasoning, and spot-checked 21 statements. The other three are searches of the record for points of contact the draft does not use: organisational memory and learning; the formal and classification lines; and the record's process history (decisions, reviews, skills, upstream changes). Every item marked "confirmed" below was re-checked against the entry before it was written here. The draft itself is unchanged.

## Verdict

The descriptive core (§§5–7) is careful, and most of its figures are exact. The paper as framed does not yet hold up, for four reasons:

1. **Its theory is partly built from the system it then tests.**
2. **Its predictions are graded against cases chosen because the outcome had already happened.**
3. **Its distinctive clause (inquiry can *revise*, not only refine) is barely touched by its own cases.**
4. **One of its central factual premises is wrong.** The draft says every grammar change was owner-initiated or owner-ratified, and that no assistant changed the grammar unprompted. The record says otherwise.

Point 4 is also the paper's best opportunity.

## 1. Structural problems, ranked

### 1.1 Attribution is wrong, and the correction is a finding (§§3.2, 8.6, 10)

Confirmed against the decisions:

- **Grammar changes proposed by filing agents, with no owner in the decision record.** ADR-019 (the game-theory topic, "proposed independently by two filing agents") and ADR-022 (the `rivals` relation; "the filing agent ... asked for a relation"). Neither mentions the owner. ADR-058 likewise has no owner word; a status review "drove a config change".
- **Grammar changes proposed by agents and accepted by the owner:** ADR-016, ADR-020, ADR-021, ADR-025, ADR-028.
- **Standing delegation.** The owner gave several blanket permissions, so after early October many changes were ratified in advance rather than one by one:
  - "The owner authorised extending the vocabulary as the record sees fit" (ADR-031, ADR-034);
  - "don't hesitate to extend the vocabulary as needed" (ADR-045);
  - "use your best judgement" (ADR-057);
  - "The owner left the split to the assistant's judgement" (ADR-089).
- **Ratification by merge.** The status review's procedure says "merging it is the assent" (`record/status-review/README.md`). Thirty-seven ADRs are Proposed while their configuration changes are in force.
- **An external assistant changing the schema through the owner.** ChatGPT's reviews, pasted by the owner, revised ADR-075 and ADR-076, adding the `admission` field and removing a status.
- **The reverse case.** ADR-108: the assistant declined the owner's proposed location because it would break a luria journal's contract.

The draft's §8.6 says the case "does not show that the assistants acquired that capacity". The record shows more than that. It shows a *trajectory of delegation*:

1. the owner requests each change;
2. agents propose, and the owner accepts;
3. the owner grants standing permission for a class of changes;
4. assistant-run reviews make changes that a merge ratifies.

That trajectory is evidence for RQ4, and it is far better than the hypothetical delegation §8.7 proposes. **Recommendation:** add an initiator table to the census in §1.2. For each grammar change, record who noticed the need, who proposed the change, who decided, and how it was ratified (explicitly, in advance, or by merge). Rewrite §§3.2, 8.6 and 8.7 around what that table shows.

### 1.2 Hand-picked cases: replace them with a census (§6)

The eight cases are the well-documented ones, so P1 and P2 are tested by selecting on the dependent variable. The record already supplies a sampling frame:

- CASE-178's 50 grammar-changing units;
- the ADR index;
- the process search's list of further grammar changes:
  - relation semantics: ADR-029, ADR-044, ADR-051, ADR-058, ADR-059, ADR-060, ADR-079, ADR-086, ADR-092, ADR-094, ADR-095, ADR-107;
  - field requirements: ADR-040, ADR-067;
  - line rules: ADR-085, ADR-090;
  - more splits and additions: ADR-039, ADR-073, ADR-088, ADR-089, ADR-099, ADR-103;
  - a vocabulary supersession: ADR-070;
  - reversals: ADR-077 and ADR-080 superseded within a day; ADR-047 replaced by the worklist in ADR-057.

CLAIM-419's own canonical amendments include ADR-044, ADR-046 and ADR-049, and none of them is a §6 case. **Recommendation:** code every unit by type, initiator, the evidence of a misfit, and the outcome. Include outcomes of the "noticed and left in prose" kind, such as ADR-032's literature roles, and ADR-050, which was deferred because a lint "would fire 395 times". Present the eight cases as a stratified sample drawn from the census.

### 1.3 Refinement is not revision (§§2.5, 6, 8.8)

CLAIM-470 keeps refining within a fixed answer space (the CEGAR-like regime) apart from revising the answer space. §9.4 uses the same three-way split. Most cases are refinement: §§6.1–6.4 and §7.4 add values, fields or stages. Only §6.6 (withdrawing SYNTHESIS's asserting role) and perhaps §6.7 (redrawing what counts as one inquiry) revise. §8.8's "P2 supported" therefore does not reach the clause that distinguishes inquiry from specification.

The formal search adds support for the distinction. CLAIM-387 (Active) shows that optimal partitions at two budgets need not be nested, so a good revision may merge and re-split rather than refine. **Recommendation:** code each case as refinement or revision in Table 6.0, and regrade P2 honestly.

The CEGAR comparison should also state the disanalogy the record itself stresses. CEGAR's counterexample is certified, whereas a record has no oracle for a misfit (CLAIM-419, note of 27 October). That disanalogy is why P1's selection problem exists.

### 1.4 Circularity: disclose it

- CLAIM-470's body uses CASE-178 as its instance, and the draft then uses CASE-178 as evidence for T2.
- The tuple, the two-loop reading and the CEGAR analogy came from ChatGPT turns (A15, A16) that were reading luria.
- §8.8's paragraph on settling versus entrenchment closely paraphrases SYNTHESIS-012's own analysis.
- All six SYNTHESIS accounts the draft relies on are Proposed.
- T3, the hinge that §1.3 calls "answered by design", rests on CLAIM-189, which is Proposed and filed in the role of a counter.

**Recommendation:** recast T1–T4 as an interpretive framework rather than tested theses, state the provenance in §2, and add a threats-to-validity section (§1.8).

### 1.5 Predictions with no falsifier

- P1 is nearly a truism.
- P3 holds by procedure: ADRs are how the interventions were found in the first place.
- P4's costs are true by definition.
- The "positive side of P4" was added after the fact.
- P6 is a caveat, not a prediction.

**Recommendation:** state a falsifier for each prediction. Then run the one real test of P3 the record already allows. CASE-178 records 10 rewordings of vocabulary terms, 2 removals, and 26 redeclarations of grammar members, and ADR-036 widened a word's sense. Were the earlier tagged entries re-read in each case? If not, that is the "silent change of sense" T3 warns against, and an honest result either way.

### 1.6 Measures true by construction (abstract, §§6.3–6.4, 7.4)

- `kind` and `warrant` are required and lint-enforced, so complete coverage proves nothing. Only the cross-tab distributions are informative.
- The LEAD shift from 96.4% to 69.2% is mostly definitional. Dropping the cost requirement moved exactly the 53 leads that already had a CASE design. That is a migration, not evidence of discrimination.
- The 91% of units adding a vocabulary value that carried a decision record partly measures compliance with ADR-001, which required that a new word be added by decision.

**Recommendation:** lead with the cross-tabs and the coupling of changes within one pull request, and state the policy confound.

### 1.7 Length, order and terminology

- **Redundancy.** CLAIM-181 and CLAIM-419 are explained five times. Costs appear in four places.
- **Cuts worth about 2,000 words:**
  - §2.1's three drift mechanisms;
  - §2.3's rent-or-buy detail;
  - §2.4's bullets;
  - CLAIM-477;
  - QUESTION-072 and QUESTION-366;
  - §7.6.
- **Reordering:**
  - §3.5 reports CASE-178's results before the method;
  - "tier" is used before §5.3 defines it;
  - the grading of the predictions belongs in its own results section after §7, not in the discussion;
  - §3.2 narrates 19 October before 7 October.
- **Undefined terms:** grammar (does it include lint rules and conventions?), line, unit of work, the A/U turn notation, tier.
- **Terminology drift,** awkward in a paper about drift: grammar, schema, configuration, regime and Σ; obstruction, misfit and inadequacy; second-occurrence rule, second-time rule and recurrence trigger. The title's key word, "obstruction", is barely used.
- **Prose:** "the record" acts as an agent ("the record judges", "the record grants"), which blurs the attribution the paper depends on. Codes are dense in the prose. Spelling mixes British and American forms. Unicode symbols appear inside tables.

### 1.8 What a reviewer would demand

- **Threats to validity,** organised by type:
  - construct: counts taken in the system's own grammar, and the decision-record requirement imposed by policy;
  - internal: no counterfactual, and post-hoc signatures;
  - external: one owner, one record;
  - researcher degrees of freedom;
  - author independence: assistants produced the data, the scripts and the paper.
- **Reliability.** No agreement statistic is given for any classification: warrant, kind, Table 6.0, the signatures, or ADR-056's pilot.
- **Cost data.** Owner review time and sessions per migration.
- **Reproducibility.** Commit hashes for each observation point. The local clone is shallow, so §5.4's historical counts could not be re-verified here.
- **Figures.** A timeline of the interventions, and the intervention-cycle diagram.

## 3. Points of contact with the record, by section

### §2: the argument

- **Drift.**
  - CLAIM-081: forgetting an origin is not drifting, and convergence is not preservation.
  - CLAIM-421: a memoryless session interrupts *activation*, not reproduction, so the record keeps the disposition that lets an argument be reinstated.
  - CLAIM-615 and CLAIM-617: identity over time is carried by the transmission of relations, and redrafts are successor claimants. This gives "identity" in drift a theory.
  - CLAIM-090 and SYNTHESIS-067: serial transmission is filtered by the transmitters' biases, which supports CLAIM-191.
- **Anchors.**
  - THEORY-351: Hutchins's speed bugs, where "the cockpit system" remembers.
  - CASE-116 with ARG-121: a formal model of memoryless episodes linked only by an archive. An archive nobody reads is slow and transmits nothing.
- **The objection's source.** Shipman & Marshall, "Formality Considered Harmful" (LIT-756, read), which CLAIM-181 adapts.
- **The workbench session that began the line.** `workbench.d/2026/10/11/075607.md`, where the manuscript's own idea had recurred unnamed. It is an instance of CLAIM-176, and good provenance for §1.
- **Composition creating inexpressibility.** QUESTION-283 with CLAIM-480: eliminating an interior creates a coupling the parts never needed. This formalises TERM-091.

### §3: the mapping

- **Migration fidelity.** CLAIM-666 (Proposed): a migration is faithful when it preserves the accountability structure, not the old graph. ARG-096: preserving the old graph can preserve a false dependency. This gives T3 its criterion. It belongs in the mapping table and in §6.9.
- **The record computes no withdrawals.** CLAIM-664 (Active): retiring an entry reports what rests on it and leaves each dependant to a judgment. Traceability stops short of truth maintenance.
- **A record does not create common knowledge.** CLAIM-668 (Proposed) and TERM-119: an addressable anchor is not shared knowledge among memoryless collaborators.

### §4: related work

- Shipman & Marshall (LIT-756); March (LIT-729, read; also THEORY-142); Argyris's espoused theory (THEORY-145).
- The external contrasts already read in CLAIM-419's note of 12 Nov: OBO, WebProtégé, Git4Voc, and Chong et al.'s schema patching.
- Literatures not yet on the record: qualitative-research codebook evolution (the closest methodological analogue), database schema evolution (PRISM), provenance models (PROV), boundary objects, and LLM agents with persistent memory.
- CLAIM-024 and CLAIM-487: "nothing here is new but a question". The record's own concession to institutional theory.

### §5: baselines the record already holds

- CASE-178 also reports 26 redeclarations of grammar members, 10 rewordings and 2 removals, and halves of 82 vs 24 new terms. These are unused.

### §6: the cases

  - ADR-087 found that 24 of 28 cross-part pairs were mere mentions, so relation type matters.
  - ADR-089 split on question refinement, not coupling.
- **More cases:**
  - CASE-132: one complaint, correctly classified two ways, where the class chosen decides whether it reaches a body able to act. A vivid case for categories routing to actions.
  - CASE-226: two communities shown a conflation, one of which revises while the other reinterprets the evidence.
- **Negative and reversal cases:**
  - ADR-050, deferred at the convention rung because a lint would fire 395 times.
  - ADR-077 and ADR-080, superseded within a day.
  - ADR-047 replaced by ADR-057: 230 of 308 decision files stale, and about 140 pull requests in three days.
- **Incidents where a grammar change did harm:**
  - ADR-085 v2: a sweep added 94 lines to 87 entries, and the owner narrowed it.
  - ADR-090 v2: a view undercounted after ADR-079 stopped storing converses.
  - ADR-065's migration: 95 of 129 questions lacked the tie it was meant to write.
  - ADR-047 v4: narrowing lost the wider claims.

  These test P3, and P4's costs, better than anything in the draft.

### §8: discussion, and what pulls against the theses

- **Records may change knowledge more than action.** CASE-166: keeping the incident team's records through a handoff cut state-estimation error about 8.3×, "while barely changing what it does". If the paper's evaluation scores decisions, anchors may show little effect.
- **A correction recorded is not a capacity maintained.** CASE-138 (Challenger to Columbia), with THEORY-140 and ARG-062. Postmortems can be lessons that nobody reads.
- **Delegation can erode capacity.** CLAIM-602, CLAIM-605 and CLAIM-675 (Active), with CASE-262: support that does the work first lowers the capacity kept, with a deadline after which withdrawal no longer restores it. This bears directly on agent filing and on delegating diagnosis.
- **Memorylessness can help, as turnover does.** THEORY-142 (March's code model): moderate turnover keeps an organisation exploring.
- **The outside support for governance was rejected.** ARG-098 (Rejected): EvoOntology's ablation is not evidence for CLAIM-667. Do not cite it. CLAIM-685 is the institutional form of CLAIM-665 and CLAIM-667.
- **Signatures measured in the grammar are self-confirming.** CLAIM-402 and CLAIM-342: categories institutions act on produce their own confirming regularities. CLAIM-448 and CASE-221: a clean record can be an artefact of what was selected for recording, and a good intervention may at first make the signatures look worse.
- **Low coupling is not necessary for a real unit.** CLAIM-388: a uniformly coupled system can have an exactly closed aggregate.
- **Multi-classification can substitute for splitting.** CLAIM-313 qualifies CLAIM-310. With copies, overlapping partitions coexist.
- **Formal tools for when to split:**
  - CLAIM-350: a coarse-graining preserves a judgment exactly when the judgment is constant on its fibres. This is the lemma behind §8.2.
  - CLAIM-497: the coarsest description whose interventions are unambiguous, a stopping rule against over-splitting.
  - CLAIM-154: reify a unit when its effect exceeds the Occam cost.
  - CLAIM-574 and TERM-069: an equivalence a richer regime breaks "was relative to its regime, not false". A merged category was not an error in its time.
- **The pace risk.** QUESTION-471: how a record that automates incorporation keeps its speed from outrunning its capacity to evaluate. It names Nucleation as a case.
