# THEORY-1333: Bishop's contextual emergence, as stated in his 2024 SEP revision, is an ontological account: stability conditions not given at smaller and faster scales (parameter values, dimensionality, large-scale flows) constrain or enable what is possible and actual at those scales, as convection rolls constrain the motions of fluid molecules, without the brute new causal powers of strong emergence; laws bound possibility and contexts fix actualization

## Source

Bishop, "Chaos", SEP, revision of 11 October 2024, [LIT-1961](../literature.d/LIT-1961.md), §§6.2–6.3;
read in [NOTE-1535](../notes.d/NOTE-1535.md). The entry cites Bishop, Silberstein and Pexton 2022
([LIT-1993](../literature.d/LIT-1993.md)) and Bishop 2024 for the view.

## What was actually shown

A statement of the view with examples, not an argument for it.

- **The definition.** "Contextual emergence emphasizes multi-scale relations
  and the roles of stability conditions not given at smaller length and
  shorter time scales that nevertheless constrain or enable properties,
  entities, and processes at these smaller/shorter scales, making modal
  changes to possibility and actualization at these latter scales" (§6.2).
- **The contrast.** It is one of the "alternative ontological accounts of
  emergence", against strong emergence, "where brute inexplicable bridge
  laws or causal powers come into existence", which "yield a disunified
  world" (§6.2).
- **The examples.** Chaos appears only for classical observables under
  stability conditions such as the nuclear–electron mass disparity (§6.2);
  classical chaos modifies quantum tunnelling, "the opposite direction of
  the supervenience intuition" (§6.3); "emergent large-scale fluid rotation
  constrains possible motions of smaller-scale fluid molecules" (§6.3).
- **Laws.** "Physical laws … may delineate bounds of possibility; they don't
  determine concrete actualization"; "different stability conditions define
  the particulars of a physical context" (§6.3).

## What this does not say

- **That an emergent whole has a causal power its parts lack.** The view is
  framed as constraint and modal change, and causation is treated
  pluralistically (§6.3); "causal power" is set on the strong-emergence
  side (§6.2).
- **That a boundary between phenomenon and context is required.** That is
  Jennings's addition ([THEORY-1314](THEORY-1314.md)); the entry's stability conditions
  include parameter values and dimensionality, which are not boundaries.
- **That the micro description, with the stability conditions written in
  as constraints, fails to reproduce the macro constraint.** That is the
  open question [THEORY-1314](THEORY-1314.md)'s promote_when asks, and the entry does not
  address it.
- **What the 2006 paper says.** [LIT-1987](../literature.d/LIT-1987.md) was not reached; the
  record's gloss of it as about descriptions may be right for 2006.
