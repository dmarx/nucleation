# NOTE-tmp1760r: Nonreductive Physicalism and the Limits of the Exclusion Principle

## Contribution

It turned the exclusion debate from an a priori dispute into a question
about particular causal systems. It gave necessary and sufficient
conditions for upward exclusion (the realizer is the cause) and downward
exclusion (the higher-level property is), within an explicit counterfactual
semantics.

## Key insight

Ask whether the effect depends on how the higher-level property happened
to be realized. If it would still have happened under a different nearby
realization, the realizer makes no difference and the higher-level
property is the cause.

## Assumptions

- Causation as difference-making: F causes G iff F → G and ¬F → ¬G,
  evaluated by Lewis-style spheres with *weak* centring (the innermost
  sphere may hold worlds other than the actual one).
- Deterministic causation, binary properties, no overdetermination clauses.
- Supervenience may be contingent, holding in worlds relevantly like ours.

## Key results

- **Compatibility result** (§IV). (1a) M → B, (1b) ¬M → ¬B, (2a) N → B
  and (2b) ¬N → ¬B hold together iff B holds in all closest M-worlds, fails
  in all closest ¬M-worlds, and fails in all closest ¬N-worlds that are
  M-worlds.
- **Upward exclusion** (§V). This holds iff N is a difference-maker for B
  and either B fails in some closest M-worlds that are ¬N-worlds, or B
  holds in some closest ¬M-worlds outside the smallest ¬N-permitting
  sphere.
- **Downward exclusion** (§V). This holds iff M is a difference-maker for B
  and B holds in some closest ¬N-worlds that are M-worlds: the relation is
  realization-insensitive.

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Kim's exclusion principle is false under difference-making | strong | counterexamples (pigeon, Andersen's monkeys) within the semantics |
| C2 | The revised exclusion principle is contingent, holding in some systems and not others | strong | compatibility result, proved |
| C3 | A higher-level cause excludes its realizer iff the relation is realization-insensitive | strong | theorem, §V and appendix |
| C4 | Special-science causal relations are typically realization-insensitive | weak | an observation about practice, after Woodward |

## Concepts

- **proportionality** — a cause specific enough and no more specific than
  needed for the effect (Yablo).
- **realization-insensitive** — the effect persists in some closest worlds
  where the higher-level property is realized otherwise.
- **downward exclusion** — the higher-level cause excludes its realizer.

## Connections

It answers Kim, builds on Yablo's proportionality and Woodward's
interventionism and his "insensitive" causation, and uses Andersen's
neural-decoding work as its example. Pernu ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md)) argues that example
supports identity rather than autonomy.

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-tmp3fc4z](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp3fc4z.md). It bears on [THEORY-048](../theory.d/THEORY-048.md): if psychological
functions are realization-insensitive causes, biology implements them
without replacing them. That is this paper's downward exclusion. No
relation is declared, since that theory's sources do not use the framework.

For the owner's essay (§5.3), it supplies the missing criterion. Northstar's
organizational property explains the outcome in its own right if the
outcome would survive re-realizing it: different staff in the roles, a
different route through the same authority structure. That is a testable
question, and its answer can go either way.

## Limitations

Binary properties and deterministic causation. Everything turns on which
worlds count as closest, which the authors make context-dependent and
discipline-relative without fixing.

## Open questions

How to settle the similarity ordering empirically for a given system, and
whether the result survives probabilistic causation.
