# CLAIM-tmpwn3wr: If being vulnerable to another's choices grounds special responsibilities in the one who controls them, then a whole that comes to supply what its constituents once supplied for themselves, and so to control conditions they can no longer secure on their own, incurs responsibilities toward them that grow as that integration deepens; what grounds them is the constituents' vulnerability to the whole, not their functional importance to it

## The claim

A12 §1, on Goodin ([LIT-tmpgqxcj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgqxcj.md)): "Goodin's central move is to ground
special responsibilities in **another party's vulnerability to our
actions and choices**". A then turned it on wholes and parts:

> Ordinarily, specialization is discussed as an improvement in collective
> efficiency. Parts lose redundant capabilities because the whole
> supplies those functions more effectively.
>
> But if the whole now controls capacities the parts can no longer
> exercise independently, the dependency may increase the whole's
> responsibilities toward them.
>
> **The whole's responsibilities could therefore grow as its integration
> deepens.**

A12 §10 stated the general form, and its asymmetry: "A part can be
indispensable to a whole without the whole being morally obligated to
preserve that particular part. And a part can be morally entitled to
protection even when its contribution to the whole is negligible. Thus,
**the measure of a constituent's functional importance cannot serve as
the measure of its moral standing**."

## Which sense of "whole"

TERM-074. The integration the claim describes is sense 1: an arrangement
that comes to supply what its parts once supplied, as in CLAIM-603's
aphid and Buchnera or a state and its aid-dependent regions; it need not
be an institution or maintain itself. The supposition, though, is about
responsibility "in the one who controls" the choices, so the claim's
consequent applies only to a whole that can bear responsibility, which in
the cases its sources discuss is an agent, in practice a collective
institution (sense 3), and only toward parts with standing (TERM-083).
For a sense-1 whole that is not an agent, an organism toward its cells,
the claim says nothing; that is the prior part of QUESTION-tmp9bbpk.

## Why it is filed as supposed

The inference from dependence to obligation is the one the record has
refused before. The authenticity line's reciprocal agency ([CLAIM-096](CLAIM-096.md))
was deferred as needing "independent moral argument", and A25 of an
earlier exchange put the refusal as "interdependence ⇏ obligation"
([CLAIM-096](CLAIM-096.md)'s note of 2026-10-18; [QUESTION-007](../questions.d/QUESTION-007.md)). A12 accepts the point:
"Goodin's approach still needs a principle explaining why vulnerability
generates a claim, rather than merely describing a causal relationship.
Otherwise we risk the inference that *Nucleation* already rejects:
deriving normative authority directly from organizational dynamics." So
the claim is conditional. It does not derive obligation from
integration. It says that, given a vulnerability principle, the record's
own account of how integration is produced fixes where the
responsibility grows.

## What it rests on

[CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md): a capability lost because another reliably supplies it is
favoured to the one who loses it, and one-way supply leaves a dependence
that nothing in the dependent's dynamics limits. That is the route by
which a whole comes to control what its parts can no longer secure.
[CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md)'s reciprocal form, each partner supplying what the other gave
up, is where the claim's second defeater would look: a part that gave up
a capability and holds one the whole needs is less exposed to the whole's
choices, though [CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md) warns that mutual dependence can still be
organized as coercive leverage.

## Admission

A12 §7 to §10 is a synthesis turn (curation entry of 2026-11-12), and
this is what it adds. It passes as a discriminating prediction. Given
the supposition, the components leave open whether a whole's
responsibility tracks integration or only vulnerability. The claim says
integration matters through vulnerability. So a whole that takes over a
capacity while its parts keep an exit, or hold something the whole
needs ([CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md)'s reciprocal form), predicts no growth. One-way supply
predicts growth. A case of one-way integration in which the
constituents' exposure to the whole's choices does not rise would go
against it.

## What it does not say

- **That the supposition holds.** Goodin is unread here.
- **What the responsibilities require.** A12 §2 and §4 suggest Kittay
  ([LIT-tmp2vf7t](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2vf7t.md)), keeping supporting relationships just and sustainable,
  and Fineman ([LIT-tmpwqwv3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwqwv3.md)), supplying resources for resilience. Neither
  is read.
- **That every constituent is owed it.** Only parts with standing of
  their own ([TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)) are candidates; A12 §5: "A human constituent can
  possess moral claims that a cell, enzyme, or organizational subprocess
  does not possess in the same sense."

## Note of 2026-11-12: Fineman, read

Fineman's "The Vulnerable Subject and the Responsive State"
(THEORY-tmpk4d9t, read in NOTE-tmpnpbzl) is about one sense-3 whole, the
state, toward persons. It does not support this claim as titled.
Vulnerability on her account is universal and constant ("Vulnerability is
posited as the characteristic that positions us in relation to each other
as human beings and also suggests a relationship of responsibility between
state and individual", p. 255); it is not produced by the whole's taking
over its parts' functions, and the state's responsibility does not wait on
integration. It follows the state's role in constituting the institutions
that confer resilience ("To the extent that these legally constituted
institutions distribute significant social goods, they should be
monitored by the state", p. 272). That supports the governance clause A12
§10 stated, not the growth with integration in the title. The judgement is
ARG-tmpmc9mt, Rejected. Her derivative dependency, "neither inevitable, nor
... universally experienced" (p. 264), is the nearest thing in her account
to integration-made vulnerability. The claim's defeat conditions are not
met: she grounds a special responsibility, in the state's constitutive
role. No status change.
