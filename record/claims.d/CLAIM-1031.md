# CLAIM-1031: Possibility as a query–target game

## The claim

The owner's words, U26: "maybe we could treat this as a game between queries
and targets (e.g. questions and answers)." "This" is the regulation of an
industrial ecosystem as the exchange had developed it through A48, whose
last line proposed formalizing the matched-performance comparison "as a
dynamic game with heterogeneous producers, regulatory authority, and
shocks operating on several timescales." The owner's players are not
producers and a regulator but the two sides of [CLAIM-470](CLAIM-470.md)'s relation: the
query, which fixes what would count as an answer, and the target, which
realizes one or fails to.

The "maybe" is the owner's: the claim is that the game is a workable frame,
not that the organization of possibility is a game in every respect.

## What followed

A50 took it up at length: queries as specifications and answers as
realizations, two frontiers, a robust interrogation game in three forms
([CLAIM-1012](CLAIM-1012.md)), co-adapting standards and responses ([CLAIM-1087](CLAIM-1087.md),
[CLAIM-1053](CLAIM-1053.md)), a capability trap set by routine queries ([CLAIM-1054](CLAIM-1054.md)),
two capacities to maintain ([CLAIM-1003](CLAIM-1003.md)) and a revised thesis for the
manuscript ([CLAIM-1188](CLAIM-1188.md)).

## What it does not say

It does not say who the players' payoffs belong to or what equilibrium the
game has. The assistant's analysis ([CLAIM-1032](CLAIM-1032.md)) shows that A50's
minimax formula is strategic only under stated conditions, and the question
of payoffs and equilibrium in the non-adversarial forms is open
([QUESTION-524](../questions.d/QUESTION-524.md)).
