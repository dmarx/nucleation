# CASE-tmpohiyo: A proposed study of a fall in complaints: with working conditions held fixed, measure separately whether members find the conditions acceptable, see the discrepancy from their standards, are moved to intervene, have a protected channel and can change what the institution does, and compare an organization with timely, protected escalation against one where complaint is costly or ineffective, to tell satisfaction from tolerance

## The case

A33 §6, "A possible empirical test":

> Hold the underlying working conditions fixed and measure separately:
>
> 1. Whether workers regard the conditions as acceptable.
> 2. Whether they recognize discrepancies between actual conditions and
>    professional standards.
> 3. Their motivation to intervene.
> 4. Their access to protected intervention channels.
> 5. Whether intervention changes the institution's behavior.

"A decline in complaints could then be investigated rather than
automatically classified as either institutional success or suppression."

A33 §10 B adds the comparison: "Construct two organizations with identical
underlying demand conflicts. In one, complaints receive timely, protected
escalation. In the other, complaints are costly, ineffective, or
penalized. Hold the employees' substantive evaluations fixed. Measure
whether the organizations differ in recorded conflict, actual correction,
and the visibility of unmet obligations."

**Arms.** The two organizations of §10 B. **Measures.** The five of §6 for
each member, and for each organization recorded complaints, corrections
made and unmet obligations found by an outside audit. **Held fixed.** The
working conditions and the demands they leave unmet.

**In the record's terms.** CLAIM-553 factors observable intervention into
a propensity to mobilize, the chance the discrepancy is legible, and a
channel that carries the intervention. Measure 1 is the acceptance
threshold, 2 legibility, 3 propensity, 4 the channel, and 5 efficacy.
CLAIM-549 shows that raising thresholds enlarges the quiet set and leaves
the satisfied set as it was; the study asks which of the two moved when
complaints fell.

## What it can show

Which of A's three explanations of a silence holds in a given
organization: acceptance (measure 1 met), insufficient activation
(discrepancy seen, motivation low) or failed intervention capacity
(motivation present, channel or efficacy absent). A33 §6 warns that
the last can coexist with rising distress: "Increasingly distressed or
mobilized perspectives", "Declining practical capacity to express or act
on their concerns" and "Decreasing recorded objections". Across the arms,
whether a fall in complaints tracks the channel rather than the conditions,
which is CLAIM-tmp0ogo4's loop observed at its quiet link, and CLAIM-619's
counter-corrective disposition if the costly arm was made so in answer to
complaint.

## What it cannot show

Being stipulated, nothing about any organization until run. Measures 1 to
3 are self-reports and share their biases; measure 5 needs interventions to
happen in order to be observed, and is thinnest where the channel has
failed. Whether a member's acceptance was itself produced by the conditions
is QUESTION-071's problem and is not settled by asking.

A33 §10 A, "Same standards, different slack", is a second study A proposed
in the same section and is not filed here.
