# NOTE-tmp20wap: The owner — What's In A Name? Investigating Bias And Identity In Text-To-Image Models

## Contribution

Hypotheses H1 to H10, among them that a low-bias name's attribute
distribution "approximates the model's global prior" and that identity
strength is "rigidity of distribution of attributes generated by a
given(fixed) prompt" with "flexibility to prompt identity into new
scenarios"; H4 proposes monitoring identity to "evaluate model
fit/grokking (e.g. for LoRA)".

## Key points

1. Method: 107 first names, 128 images each with one portrait prompt, Stable
Diffusion v1.4; identity strength from the mean distance of each image to
the centroid in CLIP space; DINOv2 judged inadequate for this, without
quantitative validation.
2. Distinction: a real, unique person against a "simply self-consistent"
identity the model composes "from biases it learned"; "Identities need not
be veridical to be considered 'strong' for these purposes."
3. Status: results, discussion and insights sections are headings with little
prose; future work asks where the biases live, hypothesizing the CLIP text
encoder.

## Bearing on the record

Supplies the measure for [CASE-tmpoqr8p](../cases.d/CASE-tmpoqr8p.md). Consistency is judged without
reference to the outside world, so the measure scores reliability, not
correctness; a verifier for correctness (a face match against reference
images) is an addition the case makes.

## Limitations

Partly read; no training dynamics; one model; one prompt template.
