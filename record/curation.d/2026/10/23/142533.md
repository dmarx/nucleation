**What the reading corrected, before anything merged.**
- **The sign clause is Carver and Scheier's**, and its neutral point was
  wrong. Their reference is a rate of progress, not rest; the draft
  called a system standing still neutral where they call it behind. The
  claim now reads sign as progress against a reference course.
- **The intensity clause has a known difficulty.** Carver 2003 is reported
  to hold that positive affect lowers a goal's priority. The clause is
  held for negative valence until it is read.
- **The ambivalence–neutrality distinction is the evaluative space
  model's**, and the model's ambivalent and neutral outputs coincide, so
  telling them apart needs a record of which ranking each decision
  followed. The model's heterarchy is named in [CLAIM-tmp83ge2](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp83ge2.md)'s defeater.
- **The Veit–ALARM test was wrong as drafted.** It said a threshold
  override has no currency. It has a discontinuous one; only a graded
  override has none. [CLAIM-tmp85p0g](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp85p0g.md) gained part 3a and [CLAIM-tmp83ge2](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp83ge2.md)
  now separates continuous from discontinuous ascendancy. The
  representation theorem is credited to Eilenberg and Debreu.

**What it changed.**
- **An objection to the owner's thesis**, [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md): ascendancy as
  defined tracks 'wanting', not 'liking' ([THEORY-tmp0bbxk](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp0bbxk.md)). It is the
  thesis's own second defeater. Whether to rename "hedonic" to
  "motivational" valence is left to the owner.
- **The intensity clause is open for both signs.** Hesp et al. make
  negative valence a weakening of the preference ordering's precision.
- **Measurement.** Ascendancy must be read within an episode, since later
  choices follow remembered utility (Kahneman, Wakker and Sarin); a
  self-model of ascendancy cannot be validated by retrospective report;
  Hesp et al.'s lesion comparison tests one without report.
- **Credit.** The dynamics of ascendancy are Lewis's; Scherer's definition
  holds the claimed–granted precedence distinction and his monitoring
  component is a precedent for the felt-as-self-model candidate
  ([CLAIM-tmptd5us](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmptd5us.md)), with the warning that a model may not suffice.
- **The Veit–ALARM test** gained a sweep in both directions, for
  hysteresis, and a summary of the animal data: compensatory at the costs
  tested, and not yet reaching the threshold question.

**What it changed.**
- **"Dominance" renamed "ascendancy"** throughout the line, because the
  word is taken by the pleasure–arousal–dominance model, by Fontaine's
  potency–control dimension (which the CPM's own authors call
  dominance) and by decision theory; quotations are left verbatim.
- **The wanting–liking objection conceded in a principled form**
  ([CLAIM-tmp5lpdu](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp5lpdu.md)): on the CPM's levels of valence the record's ordering
  is goal conduciveness, and pleasantness is a separate appraisal.
  Revising "hedonic" is the owner's decision.
- **Credit.** The competitive form of appraisal is Moors's and EMA's; the
  CPM itself does not describe a negotiation. What stays the record's:
  perspectives as competitors with no regulator above them, a
  negotiation that may yield no ordering, and the kind clause.
- **The felt candidate** gains Grandjean et al. as a partial rival, a
  recruitment hypothesis and a case that would decide between them.
- **Measurement.** Attractor strength (DynAffect) for resumption,
  hysteresis for persistence, devaluation for which ranking prevails;
  the componential papers measure structure across samples, not
  coordination within an episode; a continuity test must fit a model
  that can jump.
