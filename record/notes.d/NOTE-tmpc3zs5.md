# NOTE-tmpc3zs5: Muñoz et al. (2020) — Researcher Perspectives on Ethical Considerations in Adaptive Deep Brain Stimulation Trials

## Contribution

Semi-structured interviews with 23 researchers in aDBS trials, thematic analysis. Eight themes, by frequency: data privacy and security (91%), risks and safety (83%), informed consent (74%), automaticity and device programming (65%), patient autonomy and control over stimulation (57%), patient selection (39%), post-trial access (39%), personality and identity (30%).

## Key results

1. Introduction: aDBS "could exacerbate concerns about felt authenticity of affective states and patient agency due to the fact devices adjust stimulation automatically, which likely occur outside of a patient’s conscious awareness".
2. Results, Automaticity: researchers "were also concerned that patients may be unaware of inappropriate stimulation changes because the changes are occurring automatically".
3. Results, Autonomy and Control: "over half (57%) of respondents raised concerns related to patient autonomy and control over stimulation."
4. Same section, mitigation: concerns are "mitigated substantially by the design of these protocols, where patients do have a controller and at any point can flip themselves out of adaptive stimulation into conventional stimulation" (R_011).
5. Discussion: "for certain patients, providing them with too much control could lead to autonomy being undermined."

## Assumptions

Researchers' views, not patients'; small US-centred sample; reported concerns, not observed harms. It does not offer a conceptual framework of control.

## Concepts

closed-loop / adaptive DBS; automaticity; patient override; clinician vs patient control; felt authenticity; informed consent to moment-to-moment changes.

## Bearing on the record

**How far it stands in.** It restates, empirically, that automatic adjustment can occur without the patient's awareness (result 1–2, Lyu and Chen's "automatic unperceived operation") and that control is shared between patient, device and clinician with disputed allocation (results 4–5). It does not restate their source × perception grid, their claim that system-originated control can be "conflated with patients' internal control", or their dependence/addiction argument.

- **CASE-128** (delegation vs imposed restriction): result 4 (an always-available patient override) and result 5 (too much patient control can undermine autonomy) are the clinical form of the case's harder variant, that "Immediate revocability cannot simply be required in every context". Supports the case's point that the authorization and revisability of the arrangement, not present control, carry the difference.
- **CLAIM-tmpu69tn**: supports the use of aDBS as an "outside, authorized" influence whose operation may be unperceived; it says nothing on alignment beyond safety concerns.
- Recheck: "deep brain stimulation", "aDBS", "closed-loop" on origin/main match no DBS work (hits are unrelated: LIT-1176, LIT-2020, LIT-743). Not held.
