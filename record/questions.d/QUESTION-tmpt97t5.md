# QUESTION-tmpt97t5: Is endogenous governance part of what makes a system self-governing: can an organization that allocates authority among its own processes be self-governing in a stronger sense than a behaviourally equivalent one whose allocation an external scheduler sets?

## Why it is a question

A11 §6: "**Q1. Is endogenous governance constitutive of cognitive
selfhood?** Can an organization with internally generated authority
allocation satisfy a stronger form of self-governance than a behaviorally
equivalent system governed by an external scheduler?" A11 kept it apart from
Q4: one "concerns the constitutive status of endogenous governance, while the
other concerns whether we can empirically discriminate proposed forms of
self-constitution." Q4 is [QUESTION-tmpgjngk](QUESTION-tmpgjngk.md).

In the last filing this question went straight to a thesis, [CLAIM-tmpspyay](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpspyay.md),
without the question being filed. That claim now answers it, and its counter,
[CLAIM-tmpzn7l2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpzn7l2.md), bears on how the answer could be shown.

"Self-governing" is [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)'s sense 5, not the subject sense. The record's
standing disclaimer is [CLAIM-116](../claims.d/CLAIM-116.md): "Regulation, even higher-order regulation,
does not establish a will." Kahl 2026 ([LIT-tmplyjhe](../literature.d/LIT-tmplyjhe.md), unread) is reported to
place agency in keeping one locus of evaluative authority, and that would
make the scheduler's location beside the point. An answer says what endogenous
allocation adds, or argues that behavioural equivalence leaves nothing for it
to add.
