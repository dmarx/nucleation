**Reading after filing, with recollections marked, worked (pull requests
558 to 563).** Over 2026-11-20 and 21 the assistant filed claims relating
the record's rate and contraction results to μP, depth scaling, modular
duality and transformer interpretability, several of them resting on works
it knew only from memory. Each such work was registered Deferred, each
recollection was marked in the claim's text, and each claim's defeater
named the reading that would test it. The owner then asked to read
everything identified as due.

Sixteen readers ran in parallel, one per work, each with the full
read-and-file brief and its own notes branch ([ADR-108](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-108.md)). Fourteen read the
full text within about four minutes; two works were behind bot checks and
were read through neighbours ([ADR-114](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-114.md)). The filer rechecked every quotation
by script against the downloaded texts. All were found, though some only
after allowing for the PDF extraction's interleaved columns, which is worth
knowing: a naive string match reports false misses.

What made it work, worth keeping:

- **The brief asked for a recheck of the record after reading.** Readers
  found held entries the assistant had missed or denied: Freidlin and
  Wentzell and Berglund (the assistant had written that the record held
  neither), Dominé and colleagues on lazy and rich regimes, Geva and
  colleagues on depth.
- **Readers were asked for a verdict on the record's characterization.**
  Three corrections came back: semicontraction keeps nothing by itself (a
  claim had cited it as the general form of keeping a conserved part); the
  bounded hyperbolicity of a log commute-time geometry is trivial for any
  chain (a claim had stated it as content); middle transformer layers
  cannot be repeated (a claim had said they could).
- **Recollections were confirmed as often as corrected.** Depth-μP's
  learned change of order 1/L per block, and the modular norm's per-module
  mass, held. Marking them as recollections cost nothing and let the
  reading confirm or correct them directly.

To revisit: whether the file-then-read order should be the default for
claims that lean on unread works, or whether reading first would have
saved the revisions.
