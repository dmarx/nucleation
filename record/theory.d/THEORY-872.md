# THEORY-872: A self-model compresses the sensory effects of a system's own activity by positing one stable common cause, at fast and slow timescales; there is such a pattern in the signal, so the self is real as a pattern and unreal as a substance, and realists and anti-realists about selves who accept this agree on the facts and differ on the criterion of reality

## Source

Gładziejewski (2025), *Synthese* 206:225, [LIT-1360](../literature.d/LIT-1360.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1056](../notes.d/NOTE-1056.md): §4.1 (pp. 15–17) and footnote 10, on the ground of §2's
equation of predictive processing with compression.

## What was actually shown

An argument, no data:

- **The pattern.** Some sensory regularities are endogenous: touch from
  typing, vision from one's own eye movements, interoceptive hunger.
  "the self is inferred and represented as a common, single cause that
  underlies certain (endogenously generated) regularities in the sensory
  signal" (p. 16). The model "achieves compression by positing a single,
  stable entity as the common cause of many endogenous patterns" (p. 16).
  At fast timescales this is the minimal self; at slow ones, the narrative
  self.
- **No further fact.** Swap two cells between two gliders in the Game of
  Life and the game runs on. Which glider is which has "no further fact
  within the GoL 'world'" to settle it (p. 17). Parfit's argument for
  persons has the same form.
- **The verdict.** Anti-realism about selves-as-substances and realism
  about selves-as-patterns are compatible. "The conflict between those
  positions is apparent, and arises at a meta-level discussion about which
  criteria of reality are more reasonable to use for this particular
  domain" (p. 17).
- **Footnote 10.** The self is partly self-constituted, "a sort of
  self-fulfilling prophecy", because active inference brings behaviour
  into line with the self-model. In some dissociative states the patterns
  may be "better compressed by model that posits two or more narrative
  selves residing within one body".

What could come out otherwise is the compression claim: a single
self-cause might not compress the data better than a model without one.
The paper does not test it.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say the self-model misrepresents.** A posited common cause
  is a lossy compression, and the paper calls the self "as real as any
  real pattern" (p. 17) under deflated criteria. The exchange that filed
  the work described it as treating the self as a real pattern "whose
  representation can exaggerate its unity". The paper does not say that.
- **It is not about experience.** The minimal self "is experienced as" the
  sense of being an embodied agent (p. 16), but nothing is claimed about
  whether or why the pattern is felt.
- **It does not say what makes a self-model a self**, rather than one more
  posited cause among the agent's models.
- **It is not about collectives.** The self here is one organism's model
  of its own sensory effects.
- **The compression is asserted, not computed.** It rests on the cited
  predictive-processing accounts, and on §2's analogy between free energy
  and description length.
