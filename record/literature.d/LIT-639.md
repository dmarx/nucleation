# LIT-639: The Harder Problem of Consciousness

Ned Block (2002), *The Journal of Philosophy* 99(8), 391–425. A longer
version appeared as "Petrus Hispanus Lectures 2003: The Harder Problem of
Consciousness", *Disputatio* 1(15), 4–49 (2003), and the paper is reprinted
in Block's *Consciousness, Function, and Representation* (MIT Press, 2007).

## Key takeaways

- **Two perspectives (§I).** *Deflationism* gives armchair analyses of
  consciousness in terms of function, representation or thought.
  *Phenomenal realism* denies that and holds that consciousness is real, so
  there may be facts about its distribution that are not accessible to us.
  *Naturalism*, as defined here, is the view that it is the default that
  consciousness has a scientific (physical) nature.
- **The hard problem partly dissolved (§§II–III).** Subjective and
  objective are better seen as properties of concepts than of properties.
  Jackson's Mary learns a new concept of an old fact. What remains is that
  we have no idea what objective concept could pick out the same thing as a
  subjective one, like a pre-Einsteinian thinking about mass and energy.
- **Commander Data (§§V–VI).** A merely superficial functional isomorph of
  us, whose physical realizers share nothing with our brains beyond what
  toasters share, and which is not homunculi-headed. Functional likeness is
  a reason to ascribe consciousness, but a defeasible one (C2). We have
  "no conception of a ground of rational belief" either way (C4):
  his consciousness is *meta-inaccessible*.
- **Disjunctivism and superficialism (§§VI–IX).** Naturalism could allow
  Data to be conscious if consciousness were a disjunctive physical
  property or the superficial functional role itself. Theoretical identity
  is accepted by inference to the best explanation (§VII), and neither
  identity would explain anything, so neither could be known.
- **The harder problem (§§IX, XIII).** Physicalism is the default and also
  inaccessible and meta-inaccessible. It is the "subjective default" that
  Data is not conscious, yet the phenomenal realist must hold the question
  open. Block lays out the tension and does not resolve it.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, with six other papers by Block
on consciousness. No anthology topic holds the epistemology of other minds
or of mind–body identity. The paper names no machine-learning system and
carries no instruction for practice.

**It extends "Troubles with Functionalism" ([LIT-407](LIT-407.md), read in [NOTE-366](../notes.d/NOTE-366.md)).**
Commander Data is a superficial functional isomorph in the sense of
Troubles' Functionalism, defined over folk psychology, and the paper places
Psychofunctionalism, which Troubles distinguished, among the phenomenal-
realist options (§IV, n. 13). Its fourth condition on Data rules out a
"homunculi-headed" realization, the case Troubles made famous. It carries
Troubles' problem of liberalism and chauvinism over from metaphysics to
evidence. Block also corrects his earlier framing: Troubles' discussion of
"universal psychology", he says, assimilated the question of exotic
creatures to the explanatory gap, and "I am guilty, too" (§IX).

**It rivals "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" ([LIT-406](LIT-406.md), read in
[NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md)).** Chalmers argues that because reductive explanation leaves
experience unexplained, experience should be taken as fundamental and tied
to the physical by psychophysical laws. Block's §III takes the gap to be
one between concepts, keeps physicalism as the default, and says plainly
that "the standard arguments against physicalism do not work" (§X(B)).
Both accept the explanatory gap; they disagree on whether it shows a gap in
nature. Block cites Chalmers's *The Conscious Mind*, not this paper.

**Bearing on [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md).** That theory holds that current evidence cannot
settle whether an AI system is conscious. This paper argues for a stronger
claim about a narrower case. For a perfect functional duplicate on a
different substrate, we lack not only the evidence but any conception of
what evidence would be. It also explains why both sides of [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)'s
deadlock are stuck. Shared laws would not settle it either: Data's states
will obey some of our laws and not others, and we cannot tell laws of
consciousness from laws of the human realizer (§X(C)). It is the
philosophical source the theory's "dispute over what counts as evidence"
clause lacks.
