# NOTE-tmprgzuf: Responsiveness and Moral Responsibility

## Contribution

It introduced the actual-sequence, mechanism-based reasons-responsiveness
account of moral responsibility. It also named the resulting position
semicompatibilism. "Really just a sketch of a theory" (p. 63), it is the
origin of what became Responsibility and Control ([LIT-684](../literature.d/LIT-684.md)).

## Key insight

A Frankfurt case shows the agent could not have done otherwise, yet he is
responsible. So the condition responsibility needs cannot be about the
agent's alternatives. It must be about the process that actually produced
the act, and whether that kind of process could have responded to a reason
to act differently.

## Assumptions

- The Strawsonian concept: an agent is responsible "insofar as he is
  rationally accessible to certain kinds of attitudes and activities as a
  result of performing the action" (p. 63).
- Mechanisms can be individuated, though "I have left extremely vague the
  crucial notion of "same mechanism"" (n. 18, p. 82).
- Only worlds with the same natural laws count for responsiveness (n. 10).

## Key results

- **Mechanism, not agent.** "An agent is morally responsible for performing
  an action insofar as the mechanism that actually issues in the action is
  reasons-responsive" (p. 66).
- **Strong responsiveness is sufficient, not necessary**: weakness of will
  and culpable wrongdoing defeat it as a necessary condition (pp. 67–68).
- **Weak responsiveness is required**: "there must exist some possible world
  in which there is a sufficient reason to do otherwise, the agent's actual
  mechanism operates, and the agent does otherwise" (p. 68).
- **Temporally intrinsic mechanisms**: a mechanism is relevant only if its
  operating does not entail the act (p. 74).
- **Tracing**: responsibility at T needs a reasons-responsive mechanism at T
  "or some suitable earlier time" (p. 76).
- **Semicompatibilism**: responsibility "is compatible with causal
  determinism, even if causal determinism is incompatible with freedom to do
  otherwise" (p. 78).
- **Against mesh theories**: Frankfurt's and Watson's accounts are "purely
  structural and ahistorical", and a manipulator could induce the mesh;
  "Moral responsibility is a historical phenomenon" (p. 79).

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Responsibility does not require being able to do otherwise | moderate | Frankfurt-type case (p. 66) |
| C2 | Responsiveness belongs to the actual-sequence mechanism | moderate | argued from C1 |
| C3 | Weak reasons-responsiveness is necessary and sufficient for the control condition | weak | argued; note 11 concedes an insane-agent counterexample |
| C4 | Responsibility is compatible with determinism | moderate | follows if C1–C3 hold; "I certainly have not proved" compatibilism (p. 79) |
| C5 | Mesh accounts fail because the mesh can be manipulated | moderate | argued by example (p. 79) |

## Concepts

- **actual-sequence model** — responsibility depends on the properties of
  the mechanism that operates, not on alternative sequences.
- **strong / weak reasons-responsiveness** — doing otherwise in the closest
  worlds with sufficient reason, versus in some such world.
- **receptivity, reactivity** — recognising reasons and acting on them; the
  paper names the ways responsiveness can fail (p. 67).
- **semicompatibilism** — responsibility compatible with determinism even if
  freedom to do otherwise is not.

## Connections

It answers Frankfurt's 1969 argument and argues against the hierarchical
accounts of Frankfurt and Watson. Note 11's counterexample (an agent
weakly responsive only to a bizarre reason) is the problem the later book
([LIT-684](../literature.d/LIT-684.md); précis [LIT-tmpd2o1w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpd2o1w.md)) solves with moderate responsiveness, adding
an ownership condition against manipulation.

## Bearing on the record

It confirms first-hand what [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md) reported from the encyclopedia: the
1987 account was mechanism-based from the start, rested on weak
responsiveness, and had no ownership condition. Its historicism is a claim
against the record's hierarchical account ([THEORY-120](../theory.d/THEORY-120.md)): structure alone does
not settle responsibility. That bears on [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md) (manipulation) as the
encyclopedia reported. In this paper the only historical element is tracing
to earlier mechanisms; the ownership condition that answers manipulation
comes later.

For the owner's essay: it says nothing about groups. Its unsolved problem,
how to individuate "the" mechanism, is exactly the question an
organizational application would have to answer first.

## Limitations

A sketch, by its own account. Weak responsiveness admits the insane-agent
counterexample of note 11. Mechanism individuation is left open (notes
18–19), and responsibility for omissions is not reconciled with
determinism (note 25).

## Open questions

How to individuate mechanisms, and whether a reasons-responsive condition
can avoid both the insane-agent counterexample and manipulation. The later
book is the authors' answer.
