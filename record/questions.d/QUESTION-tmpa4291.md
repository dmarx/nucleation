# QUESTION-tmpa4291: Does Pearl's Causality define intervention as surgery on autonomous equations throughout, in the chapters on identification and the do-calculus, on structural counterfactuals, and on whether do(x) is universal, and what conditions does it put on the surgery there?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-419](../theory.d/THEORY-419.md) takes the do-operator as Pearl defines it in §5.3.3 (pp.
157–158) and the Epilogue (pp. 417, 421) of the second edition of
*Causality*: a surgery that deletes X's own equation, sets X to x and
solves the rest, while observing X = x is ordinary conditioning. Its two
consequences are re-derived. [NOTE-696](../notes.d/NOTE-696.md) read only four excerpts the author
posts, 46 of some 480 pages. Whether the book keeps that definition, and
under what conditions, where the excerpts are silent is not something the
record can say. Until 2026-10-20 this was [THEORY-419](../theory.d/THEORY-419.md)'s promote_when.

## What would answer it

A reading of the chapters that define and use the operator: chapter 3 on
the do-calculus and identification, chapter 7 on structural
counterfactuals, and §§11.4.3–11.4.7 on whether do(x) is universal. It
should confirm, or show otherwise, that surgery on autonomous equations is
the book's definition of intervention throughout, and state the
conditions the book puts on it.

What would not answer it: more citations of the do-calculus, since the
question is what the book says, not whether the calculus is used.
