# NOTE-tmp2eky0: Di Biagio & Rovelli — Relative information, relative facts

## Contribution

The paper gives relational quantum mechanics (RQM) quantitative definitions
of information, relative information, fact and relative fact. They come from
Shannon's theory and Everett's measure, and nothing is added to the quantum
formalism (§§II–IV, pp. 1–4). The new move is the relatum. Facts are relative
to commutative subalgebras of observables, not to whole quantum systems, which
the authors call "a novelty with respect to the RQM literature" (p. 4). Their
own earlier papers, the companion [LIT-tmpqu50n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqu50n.md) among them, are named as
attempts "to define facts relative to entire quantum systems". With these
definitions the paper treats measurement as continuous growth of relative
information (§VII, App. B), shows perspectives merging in a three-system
example (§VI), and re-reads Wigner's friend, EPR and the extended Wigner's
friend scenario (Apps. C–E).

## Key insight

A fact is a probability distribution concentrated on one value, and a
relative fact is a conditional distribution concentrated on one value. That is
a statement about correlations in a joint distribution, and only commuting
variables have one, so what holds a perspective is a set of commuting
variables, whatever system they belong to. Facts are then as continuous and as
graded as information is: "call something a fact when the information is high
enough for the level of accessible resolution or confidence needed" (p. 3).
The joint distribution is always computed from a state relative to some
further system, "generally us" (p. 3), so the relativity iterates. The paper
declines the regress: "exiting relationality is not physics" (§IX, p. 7).

## Assumptions

- **Probabilities for variables with finitely many values.** I_A^max = log N_A
  (eq. 1 and footnote 1, p. 2). Continuous variables enter only through
  characteristic functions χ_[x0,x1] or projector-valued measures (footnote 2,
  p. 3). POVMs are left to ref. [18].
- **The Born rule for commuting variables.** p_ρ(a) = tr Π_a ρ (eq. 11) and
  p_ρ(a, b) = tr Π_a Π_b ρ for [A, B] = 0 (eq. 12), p. 3.
- **A system is a set of distinguished variables**, "represented in the theory
  as distinguished operators on the Hilbert space" (§V, p. 4). A spin-½ is the
  algebra generated by X, Y, Z, and a classical bit is the subalgebra spanned
  by 1 and Z. Which operators are distinguished is assumed, not derived.
- **Every state is relative to a classical subsystem.** "Every quantum state is
  a quantum state of a system relative to some classical (in the sense above)
  system" (p. 4). In every worked example the state is given "relative to us"
  (eq. 13) or to an outside observer O (App. D).
- **Unitary evolution throughout.** No collapse is postulated (p. 4).
- **Ideal measurements in the worked examples.** These use pure ready states
  and perfect correlation. App. B says this idealization is what makes a
  conditional relative fact appear at once (p. 9).

## Key results

### Information measures (§II, pp. 1–2; App. A, p. 8)

- I_A := I_A^max − H_A, with H_A = −Σ p(a) log p(a) (eqs. 1–2). It is zero
  when nothing is known and maximal when p(a) is peaked. Following Everett, but
  he set I_A^max = 0 (footnote 1).
- I_{A:B} := H_A + H_B − H_AB = I_AB − I_A − I_B (eq. 3).
- The conditional information I_{A|b} := I_A^max + Σ_a p(a|b) log p(a|b)
  (eq. 5). The **relative information** is its expectation,
  I_{A|B} := Σ_b p(b) I_{A|b} (eq. 6).
- **Key relation (7):** I_{A|B} = I_A + I_{A:B}. "the mutual information is
  the expectation value of the increase in information about A after looking
  at B" (p. 2). It is the standard identity H(A|B) = H_A − I(A:B) rewritten.
- App. A lists: I_{A:B} is symmetric and I_{A|B} is not; I_{A:B} ≤
  min{log N_A, log N_B}, and at the maximum one of I_{A|B}, I_{B|A} is
  maximal; "I_{A:B} > 0 only when neither A nor B is a fact"; and for
  variables of different systems, I_{A:B} ≤ 𝓘, the quantum mutual
  information, with I_{A:B} ≤ 𝓘/2 for pure entangled states. No proofs are
  given.

### Facts and relative facts (§III, pp. 2–3)

- **Fact:** "the value of a variable A is a fact when the probability
  distribution p(a) is entirely concentrated on one value, namely when the
  information I_A is maximal" (p. 2).
- **Relative fact:** A is a fact relative to B = b when I_{A|b} = I_A^max
  (eq. 9). A is a fact relative to B when that holds for every b with
  p(b) ≠ 0, equivalently I_{A|B} = I_A^max (eq. 10).
- A can be a fact relative to B without being a fact, when
  I_A + I_{A:B} = I_A^max. Example: Robert's location is a fact relative to
  Mary, or to his smartphone, but not to us (pp. 2–3). "nothing here hangs on
  Mary and Robert being self-reflecting humans."
- **"B has information about A"** when I_{A:B} ≠ 0, "in this general and
  nonmental sense" (p. 3). An airport panel and a geological layer are the
  examples.
- **"Every fact is a relative fact"** (p. 3). The argument: information "is
  stored in physical variables and their correlations and can therefore be
  accessed by other systems", so talk of information about a variable is
  "invariably, although implicitly" talk of it relative to some system,
  "generally us".
- **Graded facts.** Information is continuous and "there is simply no aspect
  of the world of which we can be totally certain", so the authors use
  information "as a measure of the definiteness of a fact" and call something a
  fact "when the information is high enough for the level of accessible
  resolution or confidence needed" (p. 3).

### Perspectives belong to commutative subalgebras (§§IV–V, pp. 3–4)

- "only mutually commuting variables may have a joint probability
  distribution, so only these can simultaneously be a fact according to our
  definitions." A collection of commuting observables is a **classical
  subsystem**. "Given a state on an algebra of observables and a commutative
  subalgebra A of a quantum system (a classical subsystem), the collection of
  facts relative to A is the perspective associated to A" (p. 3).
- Defining facts relative to whole quantum systems "leads either to
  ambiguities as to which variables are a fact relative to a system or to
  complementary observables being facts simultaneously" (p. 4, citing
  [22–24]). A quantum system has information about another only through its
  commutative subalgebras, quantified by the quantum mutual information (p. 4).
- **Qubits are not observers**, agreeing with Brukner [24]. A qubit has
  non-commuting variables, so no perspective. One of its variables has a
  perspective but "lacks the resources and structure needed to be called an
  observer" (p. 4). "'perspective' is a wider notion than 'observer'": any
  Copenhagen or QBist observer has a perspective, but not every perspective
  is an observer's (p. 4).
- **The price:** "there are no absolute facts: most statements that are true
  or false are true or false in a perspective and there are no
  perspective-independent facts" (p. 4).

### Perspectives differ, merge, and can be disturbed (§VI, pp. 4–5)

- Relative to us, |ψ0⟩ = |+⟩|a0⟩|b0⟩ (eq. 13). A correlates with Z of the
  qubit S: |ψ1⟩ = (|0⟩|a0⟩ + |1⟩|a1⟩)|b0⟩/√2 (eq. 14). Z is a fact relative to
  A, not to B. Then B correlates with A or Z: |ψ2⟩ = (|0⟩|a0⟩|b0⟩ +
  |1⟩|a1⟩|b1⟩)/√2 (eq. 15). Now I_{Z|A} = I_{Z|B} = I_Z^max and
  I_{A|B} = I_A^max. "The perspectives of A and B have merged."
- If B instead correlates with X of S, the result is eq. (16). X is a fact
  relative to B, A is not a fact relative to B, and "Z is not a fact relative
  to A anymore, even though A did nothing in this last timestep … because the
  interaction between B and S disturbed Z" (p. 5). I checked (16) from (14);
  it is right.
- This is offered as "a solution to RQM's combination problem" [26, 27] and as
  the basis of intersubjectivity. It also diagnoses Frauchiger–Renner: the
  superobservers "change the correlation structure of the variables of the
  friends" (p. 5). Each is a paragraph.

### Measurement is continuous (§VII, pp. 5–6; App. B, pp. 8–9)

- In von Neumann's scheme Σ αₙ|aₙ⟩|b_r⟩ → Σ αₙ|aₙ⟩|bₙ⟩ (eqs. 17–18).
  I_{A:B} goes from 0 to H_A, and I_{A|B} reaches its maximum. "There is no
  special moment in which the information that the apparatus has about the
  system jumps from zero to a finite value" (p. 6). "A discontinuity only comes
  from the definition of 'fact' as something whose probability reaches a
  sufficiently high threshold" (p. 5).
- **The model (B3)–(B7):** Ĥ = iω Σₙ |aₙ⟩⟨aₙ| ⊗ (|bₙ⟩⟨b_r| − |b_r⟩⟨bₙ|) for
  t ∈ [0, π/2ω]. Then |ψ(t)⟩ = cos ωt|ψ_i⟩ + sin ωt|ψ_f⟩ (B5),
  p_t(aₙ, b_k) = |αₙ|²(cos²ωt δ_kr + sin²ωt δ_nk) (B6), p_t(aₙ) = |αₙ|²
  unchanged, and **I_{A:B}(t) = sin²(ωt) H_A** (B7). I rederived (B7) from
  (B6).
- **(B8) has a sign error.** It is printed as I_{A|B}(t) = I_A^max +
  cos²(ωt)H_A (p. 8, checked on the rendered page). By (7) and (B7) it is
  I_A^max − cos²(ωt)H_A, which is I_A at t = 0 and I_A^max at t = T. The
  printed form exceeds I_A^max for t < T. The H_A formula in App. B also lacks
  its minus sign.
- **The paper's own caveat (p. 9).** In this model A is a fact relative to
  B = bₙ for every t > 0, because p_{t>0}(a|bₙ) = δ_{a,aₙ}. That is a
  conditional relative fact appearing at once. The authors call it "an artefact
  of studying a too idealised system, where p_0(bₙ) is exactly vanishing". A
  realistic state is mixed and full rank [56, 57], and then I_{A|bₙ} starts at
  I_A^max − H_A and "smoothly increases to close to maximal over a short time".
  The full-rank case is described, not computed.
- "All the above has actually little to do with quantum theory". A classical
  register shows "the same apparent tension between discrete outcomes and
  continuous information acquisition" (p. 6).

### Classical world, regress, Wigner (§§VIII–IX, pp. 6–7)

- Classicality and intersubjective agreement come from the commutativity of
  the variables that make up "we" and from Zurek's decoherence, einselection
  and quantum Darwinism [33, 34]. Pointer variables become facts relative to
  many environmental variables, and observers reading different fragments
  agree (p. 6). This is cited, not shown.
- "When we say that a variable B has information about another variable A, we
  are saying that this is the case relative to us" (p. 7). "relativity
  iterates [37]". The regress exists "only … if one wishes to exit
  relationality by demanding a truth beyond any context or perspective" (p. 7).
- Questions about what the friend "really" saw are set aside as without
  empirical content, by analogy with absolute motion and absolute simultaneity
  (p. 7). Added ontologies (Bohmian trajectories, "the collection of all
  relative facts" [22]) are "unobservable and are there only to assuage our
  metaphysical unease".
- The conclusion says the reformulation "allows talk of relative facts also
  in-between interactions" (p. 7).

### Appendices C–E (pp. 9–11)

- **EPR (C).** For (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, none of the variables is a fact relative
  to us, but Z₂ is a fact relative to Z₁ and X₂ relative to X₁ (eq. C2). Both
  cannot be facts in one perspective. In GHZ (C3) "the Z variable of any qubit
  is a fact relative to the Z variable of any other qubit, but the X variable is
  not a fact relative to anything". This multipartite structure "is what solves
  the 'preferred basis problem'" [34].
- **Wigner's friend (D).** Relative to Wigner, S–F goes from
  |+⟩_S|ready⟩_F (D1) to (|0⟩|saw Z=0⟩ + |1⟩|saw Z=1⟩)/√2 (D2). Wigner's
  information about Z stays minimal, his information about F falls by log 2,
  and I_{Z|F} goes from minimal to maximal. Wigner's check M = |ψ1⟩⟨ψ1| (D3)
  does not commute with F, yet it yields M = 1 and "Nothing happens to F", and
  Wigner "gains no information about Z or F" by it (p. 10). Relative to an
  outside O, after Wigner asks, H_Z = H_F = H_W = log 2 (D8) with
  I_{Z|F} = I_{Z|W} = I_Z^max and I_{F|W} = I_F^max. "the value of Z relative
  to F is the same as the value of Z relative to W" (p. 10).
- **Extended Wigner's friend (E).** Absoluteness of observed events has two
  parts: Alice's and Bob's statistics are marginals of a distribution over
  Charlie's outcome (E1), and opening the box reveals Charlie's outcome (E2).
  The paper holds (E2) "true according to quantum theory" and (E1) false: "at
  no time will p_QM satisfy (E1)" for x ≠ 1 (E3). "Charlie's outcome is not a
  stable fact with respect to Alice, in the RQM sense of [13]", which is
  [LIT-tmpqu50n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqu50n.md) (p. 11).

## Concepts

- **Information about A**: I_A = I_A^max − H_A, the distance of p(a) from
  uniform, in Everett's sense but shifted to be non-negative.
- **Relative information** I_{A|B}: the expectation over B of the
  information about A conditional on B's value.
- **Fact**: a variable whose distribution is concentrated on one value; in
  practice, one whose information is "high enough" for the resolution or
  confidence needed (p. 3).
- **Relative fact**: A's value is a fact relative to B (= b) when the
  conditional distribution of A is concentrated (eqs. 9, 10).
- **"Has information about"**: B has information about A when I_{A:B} ≠ 0, in
  a "nonmental sense" (p. 3).
- **Classical subsystem**: any collection of commuting observables of a
  quantum system. Classical in the paper's sense, not macroscopic.
- **Perspective**: given a state and a commutative subalgebra, the collection
  of facts relative to that subalgebra (p. 3).
- **Observer**: a special case of a system with a perspective, with "additional
  special properties" such as many records, decoherence and communication
  (p. 4). Not a notion the account needs.
- **Merging of perspectives**: two classical subsystems agree on a variable's
  value and on each other's values (p. 5).
- **Stable fact**: used once (App. E, p. 11) in the sense of ref. [13],
  [LIT-tmpqu50n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqu50n.md). It is not defined here.

## Connections

  replaced "the state" with "the information that a system has about another

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-tmpubsi9](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpubsi9.md): a quantitative criterion for the correlation rung, and
  a measurement rung that cannot be told apart from it.** The paper defines
  "B has information about A" as I_{A:B} ≠ 0, "in this general and nonmental
  sense" (p. 3). A measurement is the same quantity driven to its maximum,
  I_{A|B} = I_A^max (§VII). So A19's first rungs (relational event,
  correlation) get exact criteria that any commuting variables can meet, with
  no observer. But nothing marks off a measurement from any interaction that
  correlates. A19's own definition, "an interaction organized to make
  particular differences consequential", has no counterpart. "Organized",
  "consequential", record, use and revision are not treated, and
  stability is deferred to ref. [13] and Zurek. Facthood is graded, and the
  threshold is set by "the level of accessible resolution or confidence
  needed" (p. 3), which leaves open who or what needs it. The question should
  record that this paper answers the bottom of its ladder exactly, says nothing
  above it, and reintroduces a purpose-relative threshold at the point where
  the record would draw the measurement line. It does carry one thing upward:
  a perspective requires commuting variables (p. 3). A qubit has no
  perspective, and one of its variables has a perspective but is not an
  observer (p. 4). That is a necessary condition on any rung that keeps a fact,
  and a reason the question's apparatus must be "classical" in the paper's
  sense, not in the macroscopic one. A19's "high-priority new prior art" holds
  for the definitions. It does not hold for the measurement–inquiry part of
  the question.
- **[TERM-tmpjdel0](../terms.d/TERM-tmpjdel0.md): differentiation made quantitative, with a cost the term
  does not name.** The term's first function, coupling differences in one
  system to distinguishable outcomes in another, is the paper's relative fact:
  a pointer variable whose values fix the measured variable's, I_{A|B} =
  I_A^max (§VII). Two findings refine it. (a) The coupled outcome variables
  must commute among themselves to hold a perspective at all (p. 3). (b)
  Differentiating one variable can erase relative facts others held. When B
  couples to X, "Z is not a fact relative to A anymore, even though A did
  nothing" (eq. 16, p. 5). A coupling can also yield an outcome while carrying
  no information about the variable of interest. Wigner's M returns 1 and
  "Wigner gains no information about Z or F" (App. D, p. 10). That fits the
  term's "selectively". Retention, enforcement and revision are not touched.
- **[THEORY-621](../theory.d/THEORY-621.md): a correction to one of its stated prices, and worked cases for
  its consistency gap.** The THEORY's title gives as a price that "values are
  not held at all times". On this reformulation relative facts are features of
  the joint distribution at any time, and the conclusion says it "allows talk
  of relative facts also in-between interactions" (p. 7). That price belongs to
  the 2021 chapter's sparse-facts version. A dated note should say the later
  formulation drops it. On consistency, the paper adds worked cases (§VI; App.
  D, D8: "the value of Z relative to F is the same as the value of Z relative to
  W"). Each is computed inside one joint distribution relative to one outside
  system, where agreement follows from probability theory. It does not derive
  the rule that amplitudes give probabilities only between facts relative to
  the same system. App. E restates its content: the interference terms are "a
  symptom of the relationality of facts", and no joint distribution over
  Alice's, Bob's and Charlie's outcomes exists (E3). The THEORY's
  `promote_when` is not met.
- **[THEORY-tmpleurt](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpleurt.md) (Rovelli 1996, consistency shown for one case).** Its
  second half is still only shown in examples. The 2025 paper adds the
  three-system merge and Wigner's friend relative to O. It also adds a
  qualification the 1996 text lacked: perspectives agree only when they
  "couple properly", and coupling to a complementary variable can destroy a
  relative fact held elsewhere (p. 5). That is loss, not contradiction, so it
  neither proves nor refutes the THEORY. Its first half, that a relation
  between views is itself relative to a third system, is restated: "When we say
  that a variable B has information about another variable A, we are saying
  that this is the case relative to us … relativity iterates" (p. 7).
- **[THEORY-tmpduhhr](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpduhhr.md) (Rovelli 1996, systems prior).** The 2025 text keeps the
  relata prior. A system "is more appropriately thought of as consisting of a
  set of physical variables" (p. 4), assumed, as W(S) was. What changes is
  which relatum holds a perspective: a commutative subalgebra, not a system. A
  system as a whole can have none if its variables do not commute. The same
  supports [CLAIM-tmpmand1](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpmand1.md)'s note that RQM is non-eliminative. The subalgebra is
  chosen, not constituted by the relations, so the analogy's "partly fixed by
  the constraints" still finds no support in RQM.
- **[LIT-tmpqu50n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqu50n.md) (the companion, read in parallel).** This paper places its
  predecessor among the attempts to define facts relative to entire quantum
  systems, which "rendered the notion somewhat problematic" (p. 4). It uses
  "stable fact" once, in [13]'s sense (p. 11), and does not restate the stable
  facts account. Whoever reads [LIT-tmpqu50n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqu50n.md) should check whether its
  "relative to a system" survives this paper's correction.
- **[CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) and the self-confirming loop.** This bears on [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) by
  analogy only. The paper says the sole route to another perspective's facts
  is to "interact with either that variable or that system in an appropriate
  way", and that interacting with the wrong variable can remove the fact
  (p. 5). Evidence that reaches a perspective is shaped by how it couples. The
  paper does not take that further.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **The consistency shown is internal to one perspective.** Every agreement
  (§VI; D8) is computed from one joint state relative to one system ("us",
  O). Within one classical joint distribution, if Z is a function of F and Z
  is a function of W, the two agree by probability theory. The question
  critics press is whether descriptions relative to different outside systems
  agree. The paper does not address it beyond (E3) and the dismissal of
  "really" questions.
- **Facthood is a threshold the paper does not fix.** "high enough for the
  level of accessible resolution or confidence needed" (p. 3) brings back a
  purpose or a user at the point where the paper claims "no fundamentally
  special role for observers". The authors do not discuss this.
- **"No special role for observers", precisely.** The paper gives no special
  role to observers, but every state is a state relative to a classical
  subsystem. The role is spread over every commuting set of variables.
- **Which variables are "distinguished" is assumed** (§V). Choosing the
  commutative subalgebra is choosing the perspective, and the paper gives no
  rule for it beyond decoherence (§VIII), and that is cited.
- **Claimed resolutions are paragraphs.** The combination problem,
  Frauchiger–Renner and the preferred-basis problem are each settled in a
  sentence or two (p. 5; App. C).
- **Errors in the text:** (B8)'s sign, and the missing minus sign in App. B's
  H_A (p. 8). Also the GHZ statement that "the X variable is not a fact
  relative to anything" (App. C, p. 9). This is my check, not the paper's: GHZ
  is a +1 eigenstate of X⊗X⊗X, so X₁ is a fact relative to the commuting
  product X₂X₃, a variable of the other two qubits. The statement holds for
  single-qubit variables only.
- **App. A's properties are stated without proof**, including the bound
  I_{A:B} ≤ 𝓘/2 for pure entangled states.
- **v1 was not read**, so I cannot say which of these points the revision
  added.

## Open questions

- Do descriptions relative to two different outside systems, each a
  commutative subalgebra, ever disagree about a third pair's relative facts? A
  general theorem, or a counterexample, would settle the consistency gap
  [THEORY-621](../theory.d/THEORY-621.md) and [THEORY-tmpleurt](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpleurt.md) name ([THEORY-tmphcxoi](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphcxoi.md)).
- What fixes the threshold at which information counts as a fact, and is it
  ever set without a user?
- In a full-rank (realistic) measurement, how fast does the conditional
  I_{A|bₙ} approach maximum compared with I_{A|B}? The paper asserts it is
  smooth (p. 9) and does not compute it.
- Is stability ([13]) needed for a relative fact to serve as a record, or does
  a relative fact at a time suffice? The paper allows facts "in-between
  interactions" and still calls on stable facts for the extended scenario.
