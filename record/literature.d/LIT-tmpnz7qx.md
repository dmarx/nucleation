# LIT-tmpnz7qx: Illusionism Big and Small: Some Options for Explaining Consciousness

Graziano, M. S. A. (2024). Illusionism Big and Small: Some Options for Explaining Consciousness. *eNeuro* 11(10): ENEURO.0210-24.2024. doi:10.1523/ENEURO.0210-24.2024.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpzibsl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzibsl.md).*

- **The chain.** An object (Item 1) is encoded by an automatic model (Item
  2), read by higher cognition (Item 3) and reported (Item 4). The claim "I
  have a subjective experience" needs an Item 2 that encodes it, so the
  question is what its Item 1 is (pp. 2–4).
- **Arrow B.** A theory that explains how neurons produce a feeling must
  also explain how the feeling gets encoded for report. Without that,
  "the conscious feeling that emerges from the brain cannot be the
  consciousness that we say we have" (p. 5). Theories that skip it are
  called "magical mind" theories, IIT and the workspace theory included.
- **Big and small.** Hard illusionism (no Item 1) is judged improbable.
  "Subtle illusionism is necessarily true of all models in the brain and
  must be true of consciousness as well" (p. 7). "Caricature" is preferred
  to "illusion" (p. 2).
- **Retrofits.** Illusionist IIT makes integration the target of a model
  ("consciousness is a distorted representation of integrated
  information", p. 7). Illusionist workspace has the workspace model
  itself (pp. 7–8).
- **Attention schema.** Attention is proposed as Item 1, by causal access,
  resemblance in outline and covariation with report, and the attention
  schema as Item 2 (pp. 8–10).
- **Deep illusionism.** Attention, workspace and integration are aspects
  of "the deep, selective processing of information in the cerebral
  cortex" (p. 11). Asking where consciousness resides is "poorly
  formulated" (p. 11).
- **Its limits.** No new data. The covariation is cited. The step from
  every model's being schematic to the reported essence's being absent is
  assumed. The unification of the three theories is asserted.

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange cited this paper to show that illusionism need not make consciousness wholly fictional. An illusionist layer, the attention schema, can sit on a workspace or integrated-information account, so that real organization is misdescribed by a simplified self-model. The record already holds Frankish's statement of illusionism, [LIT-458](LIT-458.md), and Chalmers's meta-problem, [LIT-425](LIT-425.md). Bears on [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md), [THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md) and [THEORY-577](../theory.d/THEORY-577.md).

**Qualification from the reading.** The description is accurate about the
layer. It needs one qualification. What is not fictional, for Graziano, is
the process modelled, Item 1. The phenomenal experience as reported is
fictional: "The subjective, phenomenal experience that Bob describes is a
distorted construct" (p. 7). His "small" illusionism is therefore not a weak
illusionism in Frankish's sense, which keeps phenomenal properties. It is
Frankish's strong illusionism with a named target. Graziano's "hard"
illusionism, a model of nothing, is further out than Frankish. So the paper
supports [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)'s split between real organization and represented
owner, but extends the illusion to phenomenality, which [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) leaves
open. Arrow B is [THEORY-577](../theory.d/THEORY-577.md)'s constraint, stated independently (the paper
does not cite Chalmers 2018).
