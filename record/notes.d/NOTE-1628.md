# NOTE-1628: Zhou et al. — Mutations in artificial self-replicating tiles

## Contribution

The NYU group's enzyme-free origami replicator, introduced by He et al.
(2017), had been built to copy without error: "the initial efforts were
to make the system as error-free as possible, and in fact the
experiments showed only copies of the seeded patterns" (introduction).
This paper adds a second species, a mutation rate set by mismatched bases,
and growth rates set separately for each species, and shows that a
species arising only by mutation takes over when it grows faster.

## Key insight

In a templated replicator whose conditions are all supplied, selection is
nothing but a difference in growth rate. "Since the growth rate is the
only competitiveness in this artificial self-replication, the mutated CD
dimers with a higher growth rate eventually dominated the system
independent of the initial seed" (Results). The authors' own
characterization: "This is the expected result and a most elementary
example of Darwinian evolution" (Conclusion).

## Assumptions

- **Growth rate is the only fitness**, and it is assigned: "the
  competitiveness in the self-replication growth rate was largely
  determined by the recognition strands design" (Results).
- **A first-order master equation** with constant per-cycle growth and
  mutation rates (SI Eqs. S1–S2, not read) describes the species
  fractions until saturation.
- **Cross-linking only within a species**: AD and CB dimers are excluded by
  non-complementary cross-linking strands, so a CD dimer needs both
  monomers to mismatch.
- **AFM counts are representative**: species are told apart only by the
  designed orientation of the "equal sign", and samples are deposited at
  48 °C "to dehybridize the noncross-linked monomer".

## Key results

- **The replicator, as inherited (introduction).** "We seed and grow one
  dimer species, AB, from A and B monomers that doubles in each cycle"
  (abstract). Without seeds, "any dimers that are formed are sterile and
  do not replicate (19)", ref. 19 being He et al. (2017).
- **The cycle (Results; Fig. 1 caption; Methods).** Tethered on the
  template the monomers' horizontal ends hybridize, UV at 365 nm fixes
  them through CNVK, and "On heating the daughter dimers are released and
  form the parent templates for the next generations." Methods: 48 °C for
  30 min, 22 °C for 1 h, UV for 20 min, about two hours a cycle.
- **Rate set by design and stoichiometry (Results).** "If only half of the
  monomer vertical arms are functionalized with sticky ends, then the
  replication rate is less than a factor of 2 per cycle"; with both,
  ladders give "an exponential growth of up to a factor of ∼10 per cycle".
  "The growth rate is determined both by the thermodynamics of the
  recognition strands and by the relative concentrations of the monomers
  and parent dimers."
- **Mutation set by mismatches (Fig. 2).** FRET melting curves fall with
  each added mismatch; "When the fifth mismatch is introduced, the
  hybridization starts at 23 °C", against cross-linking at 22 °C. With
  three, "we obtain 16% mutated binding", and since both monomers must
  mismatch, about 3.5% of new dimers are CD.
- **Equal growth (Fig. 3).** "the growth rates of the two species AB and CD
  are approximately equal at 3.7 and 3.9 per cycle"; over 15 cycles the
  shares converge, fitted by the master equation; analytically "the two
  species would eventually equalize if they have the same competitiveness,
  the growth rate". 874 dimers counted. Control: "no dimer was formed
  without the seed".
- **Unequal growth (Fig. 4).** AB one-sided, CD ladder: "the growth rate
  increased from 1.6 to 2.5 after 13 replication cycles"; "the mutated CD
  dimers reached the same level as the AB dimers within only five cycles",
  "reaching a plateau after nine cycles". 1,635 dimers counted. The
  abstract puts it as "the CD species can take over the system in
  approximately six generations in an advantageous environment".
- **Parasites near saturation (Results).** The deviation is attributed to
  "accumulation of parasite, sterile, nontemplated dimers which do not
  replicate according to our previous experiments (19)", modelled by "a
  nontemplated binding rate of 0.0007 dimers/monomer cycle" (SI Fig. S8,
  not read).
- **Supplied each cycle (Results; Methods).** "The refresh of monomers
  (A:B:C:D = 1.6:1.6:1.6:1.6 nM) was replenished into the system after
  each self-replication cycle"; "We allow for self-replication by adding
  monomers each cycle proportionate to the parent dimers and their growth
  rate."
- **Error threshold in view (introduction).** "a high mutation rate leads
  to an Eigen catastrophe (22)", so "we have kept the mutation rate low,
  although not yet as low as in living systems".

## Concepts

- **Templated replication**: a dimer holds monomers by vertical sticky
  ends so their horizontal ends, at raised local concentration, hybridize
  and are cross-linked; the daughter is released by heat.
- **One-sided and ladder (litter) designs**: sticky ends on one vertical
  arm give at most 2 per cycle; on both, a ladder of daughters per cycle.
- **Mutation**: a CD dimer templated by an AB parent through mismatched
  recognition ends.
- **Growth rate**: gel dimer fraction after UV over before UV, per cycle.
- **Sterile (parasite) dimers**: dimers formed without a template, which
  do not replicate.
- **Eigen catastrophe**: loss of a species when mutation is too frequent.

## Connections

It restates, as the stand-in for He et al. (2017), [LIT-2132](../literature.d/LIT-2132.md), that
paper's replicator (the cross-tile dimer, CNVK and UV fixation, release
by heat), its doubling per cycle in the one-sided design, and the
sterility of unseeded dimers, which it attributes to He et al. as ref.
19. It does not restate He's selection experiment, protocol or numbers;
the 7,000,000-fold amplification over 24 cycles is known only from Zhang,
Sha and Chaikin (2025). Ni et al. (2026), [LIT-2124](../literature.d/LIT-2124.md), [NOTE-1630](NOTE-1630.md) and
[THEORY-1448](../theory.d/THEORY-1448.md), give the thermodynamics of the same cycle (effective
concentration, melting windows, about 1.9 per cycle for dimers); this
paper gives the population dynamics. Where Shirt-Ediss, Torelli and
colleagues ([NOTE-1627](NOTE-1627.md)) remove off-target binding to raise folding
yield, this paper adds designed off-target binding to make mutants. Like
Wagenbauer, Wachauf and Dietz ([NOTE-1637](NOTE-1637.md)), it reads its outcome from
gels and AFM counts. The folding readings, the DeLuca et al. simulation
([LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md), [NOTE-1561](NOTE-1561.md), [THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)) among them, concern one origami
reaching its shape; this concerns populations of finished origami.

## Limitations

- The SI was not read: the master equations, Tables S3 and S4 of per-cycle
  rates, and Fig. S8's parasite fit are known only from the main text.
- The abstract's growth rate of "two or four per cycle" differs from the
  measured 3.7 and 3.9, and its "approximately six generations" from the
  Results' parity at five and plateau after nine.
- The sample-splitting account is inconsistent: the Results say "one-third
  are used to seed the next generation and two-thirds are used for AFM
  analysis", while the Methods take 10 µL of 30 µL and add 20 µL of fresh
  monomers; the paper does not reconcile them.
- Selection acts only through growth rate under conditions the
  experimenters choose; no species maintains, repairs or builds anything.
- The structural difference between sterile and templated dimers is not
  given.
