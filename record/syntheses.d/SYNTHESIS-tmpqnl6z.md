# SYNTHESIS-tmpqnl6z: When formerly autonomous partners come to depend on one another, which properties each route to dependence brings about, and which make the partnership a new individual, is open: the documented symbioses show dependence, reproductive cohesion, the distribution of functions across genomes and host control coming apart, dependence directional, and integration undone as well as deepened, and the record holds that gene ancestries and stem lengths alone cannot tell a partner from a donor or time its acquisition

part filed from the exchange of 2026-10-27 on reductive evolution and
endosymbiosis, with its cases and the readings of that day and after. The

## The account

- **The question.** QUESTION-277 (the exchange's, of 2026-10-27; Open)
  asks, for each route by which partners lose autonomy, which of five
  dimensions it moves: physiological dependence (D), the distribution of
  essential functions across genomes (G), reproductive cohesion (R), the
  host's regulatory control (C) and the alignment of selection (S); and
  which of them, alone or together, make a new evolutionary individual. The
  exchange's thesis, in its words: "A system can become more
  interdependent without becoming more cooperative, more unified, more
  autonomous, or even more adaptive." The question is the exchange's, and
  "The record takes no position on its answer until the works are read."
- **What its readings found.** QUESTION-277's notes, route by route: the
  Black Queen route "moves D one way and G, and nothing else"; past a
  genome-size threshold "G forces C"; D and R are separate but "their order
  is not settled", since Athreya et al.'s dependence-first result holds
  under their chosen mapping only; transfer and import "is mostly import";
  complementary loss "moves G and the symbiont's D, not C"; host
  compensation "maintains R, perhaps at a cost to C and S"; and
  integration can be "undone in steps". Dependence "stays directional even
  in an organelle".
- **A prediction to test.** QUESTION-353 (the promotion condition moved
  from THEORY-1051; Open) refines QUESTION-277: does the partner with the
  larger, faster-growing population become obligate first more often than
  chance, and does the other's dependence follow births and deaths inside
  the collective?
- **The cases.** Six documented specimens, each Proposed because the works
  that report it are mostly unread:
  - CASE-173, aphids and *Buchnera*: "the case of high mutual dependence
    and high reproductive cohesion reached through reduction";
  - CASE-171, the mealybug's nested symbionts: function "distributed across
    nested boundaries" and "constituent lineages turn over";
  - CASE-177, *Hodgkinia* in cicadas: "dependence and the number of parts
    grow with no new collective function, and the host pays";
  - CASE-174, *Paulinella*: filed as the case where host control emerges
    "by gene transfer and import", which its note of 2026-10-27 corrects:
    the imported proteins are "mostly from the host itself";
  - CASE-167, the nitroplast: "the line between endosymbiont and organelle
    is drawn by stated criteria", R and C high and D asymmetric;
  - CASE-168, *Monocercomonoides*: "the negative control: loss of function
    dissolving an integrated compartment, not deepening dependence on it".
- **What the phylogenomic evidence can show.** Three claims, each the
  record's own judgement split from a reading by the twenty-second status
  review (thesis, interpretive, Active), each bearing on QUESTION-277:
  - CLAIM-506: the chimeric split of the last common ancestor's carbon
    metabolism "does not single out syntrophy";
  - CLAIM-508: "Gene ancestries alone cannot tell a symbiotic partner from a
    source of transferred genes";
  - CLAIM-509: "Stem lengths alone do not establish an order of acquisition
    in eukaryogenesis".
