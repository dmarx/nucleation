# LIT-tmpq41d3: The Paradox of Algorithms and Blame on Public Decision-makers

Ozer, Waggoner, Kennedy (2024). *The Paradox of Algorithms and Blame on Public Decision-makers*. Business and Politics 26(2): 200-. DOI 10.1017/bap.2023.35.

## Key takeaways

*Not read; as the exchange describes it.* Two preregistered survey experiments on blame toward judges who use algorithmic advice. Unverified until read.

## Standing in the record

Read. It marks the limit CLAIM-tmpnfjln draws for itself on automation
(bounds): public blame of a visible official is not shifted onto an
algorithm. It says nothing about blame within organizations.

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read, without the supplement. The ethicists' worry, that following an
algorithm deflects blame and rejecting it magnifies blame, found "no
support" in the experiments. In Study 1 respondents "did blame the
algorithms for the mistake under the agreement" condition, "but this did
not reduce the culpability assigned to the judge"; in Study 2 the effect
of agreeing was mostly mediated by the view that the judge should have used
her own judgment. The authors call the effects small. The exchange's report
(A19 §9) is accurate; it leaves out the direction of the effect.
