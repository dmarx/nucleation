# LIT-681: Optimizing Mode Connectivity for Class Incremental Learning

Haitao Wen, Haoyang Cheng, Heqian Qiu, Lanxiao Wang, Lili Pan and Hongliang Li (2023), *Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning*, PMLR 202:36940–36957 — https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/wen23b.html

## Key takeaways

- **A ridge on the straight line.** Between the expanded previous minimum
  and the new minimum, accuracy on old tasks drops in an interval of the
  linear path for the first increment; for later increments an interval of
  the path does better on all tasks than either end (§3.1, Fig. 2, PODNet on
  CIFAR-100, 10 seeds). How the new classifier is initialised changes the
  depth of the drop: Xavier, Kaiming and Imprint do better than Uniform and
  Normal.
- **A cheap curved path.** OPC writes the path as p_θ(λ) = linear path +
  δ_θ(λ), with δ built layer-wise from cosines and sines at frequencies
  (4i − 3)π/2, and coefficients constrained to sum to zero so the path keeps
  its ends (Eqs. 5–12). Gradient projection keeps the constraint (Eq. 17).
  The loss before a switching point λ* is the old tasks' memory loss and
  after it the new task's (Eq. 4). Sampling points on a cylinder of radius r
  around the path pulls it towards flatter regions (Eqs. 15–16).
- **Gains as a plug-in.** EOPC improves PODNet by 1.21, 1.81 and 2.51 points
  on CIFAR-100 (5, 10 and 25 increments), by 1.39, 2.22 and 3.73 on
  ImageNet-1K, and reduces PODNet's CIFAR-100 forgetting from 19.26, 25.01
  and 28.55 to 7.68, 8.64 and 12.02 (Table 1). Against polygonal-chain,
  Bezier and simplicial-complex paths fitted to the same objective, OPC is
  best at 10 and 25 increments with far fewer learned parameters, and below
  Bezier at 5 (Table 4).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the owner's batch on mode connectivity and model merging, under
`loss-landscapes` ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). It is the class-incremental half of a pair with
Mirzadeh et al. ([LIT-651](LIT-651.md)), which it cites as the pioneer and limits to
task-incremental learning with linear paths. Its own linear-path finding is
consistent with theirs: consecutive continual solutions are not linearly
connected. It differs in what it looks for: not a multitask solution
connected to old ones, but a curved path between consecutive continual
solutions whose middle can stand in for the multitask solution (§4, the
"switching point").

It is measured against Garipov et al. ([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)) and Benton et al.
([LIT-656](LIT-656.md)). App. E fits Garipov et al.'s polygonal chain and Bezier
curve, and Benton et al.'s simplicial complexes, to its own switching-point
objective and reports them in Table 4; its path objective, the expected loss
along the path (its Eq. 2), is the one it takes from Garipov et al. It follows Draxler et al.
([LIT-653](LIT-653.md)) in starting the path search from the straight line, and
Frankle et al. ([LIT-654](LIT-654.md)) for the role of a shared start.

It carries an instruction for practice (add EOPC after each increment),
hence the `anthology-candidate` flag. It has no arXiv version, so the
source is the PMLR URL.
