# NOTE-tmp1p6j3: Frey and Schneider (2020) — Effective Voice

## Contribution

A distinction inside Hirschman's voice, affective against effective, and the thesis that platforms supply
the first and withhold the second.

## Key results

1. Introduction: effective voice is "a form of individual or collective speech that brings about a binding
   effect according to transparent processes"; "If affective voice is the debate, effective voice is the vote".
2. §2: "Platforms thus tame the business consequences of user exit."; "Exit is not a real option for most
   community members."
3. §2: likes and mutes are "affective pressure valves in the guise of effective acts", "providing users the
   ability to alter their own experience but not enact collective change".
4. §2: "Administrators with the power to censor, remove, and ban fellow users do not have to submit to"
   transparency requirements or judicial oversight; "The absence of effective voice has gone largely
   unnoticed."
5. §3: "Make a community’s rules binding on administrators.", possibly "rules that are enforced
   computationally, placing hard limits on behavior"; selection and removal of administrators; binding
   petition thresholds; referenda on governance change.

## Assumptions

Hirschman's frame; peer communities on commercial platforms; argument by example.

## Concepts

Affective and effective voice; entrepreneurial exit; consolidated control; implicit feudalism; lazy
monopolist; polycentricity.

## Bearing on the record

## Limitations

Position paper; examples, not data; platforms, not public administration; preprint.
