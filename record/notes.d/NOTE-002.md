# NOTE-002: Eulalia impact shower

## Contribution

Several lines of evidence point to a surge of large lunar impacts about 800 Ma: the model ages of big craters such as the 93 km Copernicus, and clustering in the ages of Apollo impact glasses. The authors look for a source and point to the Eulalia asteroid family, whose primitive, carbonaceous-chondrite-like parent was disrupted near the J3:1 mean-motion resonance. Their collisional and dynamical simulations send about three-quarters of the fragments into the resonance within about 150 Myr, some directly and some by Yarkovsky drift. From there the fragments reach planet-crossing orbits and raise the bombardment rate on the Moon and the terrestrial planets. The authors speculate that the same shower may connect to biosphere changes on Earth and to a pulse of Martian volcanism.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Background evidence (§2): seven lunar craters with mean ages of 760–820 Ma from Terada et al. (2020), Copernicus among them. Lunar-glass 40Ar/39Ar ages cluster near 800 Ma (Zellner et al. 2009), and meteorite shock-degassing ages point the same way. The background impactor flux at the time is estimated at ≤ half of today's (§2.2, §8).
- Family identification (§3.2): Eulalia members are separated from overlapping families (New Polana, Nysa/Hertha) using SDSS colours and WISE albedos. The age comes from Yarkovsky/YORP drift chronology.
- Results (§4): five candidate solutions give ages of 800–900 Ma. The preferred Solution 5 puts the breakup at T ≈ 865 Ma with the parent at a ≈ 2.48 au (§8). Observational incompleteness corrections are in §5 (Fig. 13).
- Impact flux (§6, Figs. 14–16): remnant-family collisional evolution, collision probabilities, and the numbers and sizes of Eulalia impactors on Venus, Earth, Mars and the Moon.
- Implications (§7) are explicitly labelled "necessarily speculative": Earth impact structures, the Bitter Springs Anomaly, Neoproterozoic Snowball Earth events (§7.1), and Martian volcanism near 800 Ma (§7.2).
- The Eulalia family is also named as a plausible source of Bennu and Ryugu. The inferred composition of the Copernicus impactor is said to be consistent with the returned samples (§3.1, §8).

## Open questions

- The core claim is plausibility, not proof: a source model that *can* account for the crater and glass age cluster. It is worth checking how sensitive the timing and magnitude are across Solutions 1–5, and whether the lunar crater ages (Terada et al. 2020) are robust enough to carry a "spike".
- The Earth and Mars links (§7) are flagged by the authors themselves as speculative prompts for future work, not findings.
