# CLAIM-363: A normative judgment is well-defined only once its relata are fixed (the organization or standpoint it concerns, the standard, the scale and the context), and that dependence does not by itself make it relative: once they are fixed, the judgment can be false

## The claim

The owner, at U17: "how about meaning, judgement, and contextuality? maybe
part of what we've elucidated is that normative judgements necessarily rely
on contexts and relations?" At U18: "so maybe something like a
frame/order/scale/perspectival-relative normative realism?"

A17's statement: "**Normative judgment is contextually constituted and
relationally indexed, without necessarily being arbitrary or merely
subjective.**" And its principle of indexed judgment: "**each kind of
judgment has an associated relation that must be specified for the judgment
to be well-defined**".

The claim has two parts.

1. **Indexing.** "Is this process functioning correctly?" is, in A17's
   words, "incomplete until we specify what organization the process serves
   and what function it is supposed to perform." The relata are those of
   [TERM-059](../terms.d/TERM-059.md): frame, order (organizational and reflective, kept apart
   as A18 asked), scale, perspective and standard.
2. **Not relative.** Once the relata are fixed the verdict is a fact about
   the case. A17: "an action may objectively undermine the viability of a
   particular organization under specified physical conditions. The
   conclusion depends on a relational standard, but the physical
   consequences are not a matter of preference." This is the first of A18's
   three realist conditions, "truth conditions independent of the current
   judgment".

## What the record already held

Each part was in the record for one kind of judgment:

- **Individuation.** A sufficient statistic is sufficient for a target, so
  no partition is universal ([CLAIM-152](CLAIM-152.md)), and "Given a target, the criteria
  decide, and can be wrong." That is both parts for boundaries.
- **Function.** Bickhard's function is relative to the system served, "which
  is why a parasite's gut is functional for the parasite and dysfunctional
  for the host" ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)). A17's tumor and host, and A19's host and
  parasite, are [THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)'s own example.
- **Viability.** It licenses "conditional functional judgment only"
  ([CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md)). The judgment is indexed to an organization, and it does not
  say what ought to persist.
- **Meaning and standing.** A speech act's significance is the change it
  makes to commitments and entitlements, kept score of from each
  perspective ([THEORY-665](../theory.d/THEORY-665.md)); its effect on the context is part of its
  meaning ([THEORY-162](../theory.d/THEORY-162.md)); institutional statuses carry deontic powers in a
  context C ([THEORY-625](../theory.d/THEORY-625.md)). A17's promise is the case: whether it binds
  depends on standing and context, and whether it was kept is not
  subjective.

What the exchange added is the general form: the same two parts for
normative judgment as such.

## What it does not say

A17's table, kept as it was given:

| Claim | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Contextual application** | Different circumstances can warrant different judgments |
| **Relational constitution** | The relevant property exists partly in virtue of relations among entities |
| **Standpoint dependence** | Evaluation may require identifying whose functioning, reasons, or interests matter |
| **Normative relativism** | No context-transcending standard can adjudicate between standpoints |

A17: "The first three do not logically entail the fourth." This claim holds
the first three and does not hold the fourth. It does not deny the fourth
either. Whether some conflicts between standpoints have objectively better
resolutions is A18's realist thesis, which A18 says "is plausible but
requires a much stronger argument" ([QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)). A17 adds that "a
universal principle can itself be relational", so indexing is compatible
with general principles.

Further:

- **It does not say every normative property is relational.** A17's own
  statement is "often". Part (a) of the defeat condition is the test.
- **It does not say context-dependence is contextuality.** A17: "We should
  not derive strong formal contextuality merely from the fact that meaning
  and value are contextual." A failure of global consistency is a further
  question ([THEORY-012](../theory.d/THEORY-012.md), [CLAIM-038](CLAIM-038.md)).
- **It does not say who fixes the relata.** A18: "We cannot simply let an
  evaluator choose whichever frame makes a preferred verdict true." The
  claim's second part needs only that, once chosen, the relata leave the
  verdict to the case. That the choice of relata can itself be wrong is
  the third realist condition, not claimed.
- **It does not ground the standard.** [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) stands: a verdict relative
  to an organization's viability says nothing about whether the
  organization ought to persist.
