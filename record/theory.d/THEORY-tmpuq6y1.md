# THEORY-tmpuq6y1: Skepticism about inner mental representations does not motivate fictionalism about folk psychology, because realism about beliefs as internal, intentional, causal states does not require the representational theory of mind; and a fictionalist who lets pretence yield genuine assertions about the overall state that causes behaviour is committed to that state

## Source

Drayson (2025), *What motivates mental fictionalism?*, [LIT-112](../literature.d/LIT-112.md), read in
full in [NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md) from the author's preprint; page numbers are the
preprint's.

## What was actually shown

**The target.** Toon's case for mental fictionalism has the two steps any
fictionalism needs (§1): a negative claim, that the discourse lacks
truthmakers, and a positive claim, that it is worth keeping anyway.

**Against the negative claim (§2).** Drayson reconstructs it (p. 4): *P1*
on the Cartesian reading, folk psychology is committed to attitudes as
internal, intentional, causal states; *P2* that commitment is a commitment
to the representational theory of mind (RTM); *P3* RTM is false, because
internal mental representation is incoherent; so folk psychology lacks
truthmakers. The argument is valid only if P2 is an entailment, and Toon's
text shows only that Cartesians "tend to subscribe" to RTM.

- *P2 is false (pp. 6–7).* RTM's core is that mental representations are
  individuated apart from attitudes, so believing-that-p and
  desiring-that-p can token the same one. Non-representational
  functionalism (Lewis 1966, Putnam 1967, Loar 1982) individuates attitudes
  as functional wholes with no further content-bearing constituents. Such
  states are internal in Toon's Sellarsian sense (theoretical, not
  spatially inner), intentional at the personal level "even if none of its
  subpersonal realizers" are, and causal as brain states. Making P2 true by
  definition would class these theories as anti-Cartesian and weaken Toon's
  positive argument (p. 7).
- *P3 is unsupported (pp. 7–8).* Toon's case is metasemantic: intentionality
  belongs first to public, socially normed representations. RTM is not
  committed to a reductive, mind-first metasemantics; that is one motive
  for it (Fodor's), and functional role semantics could let inner
  representations take content from social use (Block 1987, [LIT-625](../literature.d/LIT-625.md)).
- Upshot (p. 9): "Even if there were no mental representations, we could
  still coherently claim that there were 'Cartesian' truthmakers."

**Against the positive claim (§3, pp. 9–12).** Toon holds that pretence
about an inner notebook lets us make *genuine assertions* that a person is
in "a certain state", which is what gives fictionalism its causal advantage
over behaviourism and instrumentalism. Drayson's dilemma: if that overall
state is a set of behavioural facts, fictionalism has no causal-explanatory
advantage over anti-Cartesian realism; if it is the state S that causes the
behaviour (Toon p. 52), the assertions commit Toon to S, and "why not
identify state S as Mark's belief?" Realist views on which attitudes are
functional or dispositional states of the whole person with
non-representational causal bases are available (Egan 2025;
Langland-Hassan 2020). So the positive argument "provides Toon himself
with a reason to be a realist".

## What this does not say

- **That mental fictionalism is false or incoherent.** Coherence is assumed
  for the sake of argument (n. 2), and §4 says fictionalism need not wait
  for all realisms to fail. The paper defeats two motivations.
- **That RTM is true.** Drayson shares Toon's skepticism about "an
  architecturally-innate and linguistically-structured" RTM (§4). Her
  point is that realism about beliefs does not stand or fall with it.
- **That whole-person functional states are shown to be causes.** That
  they are causally efficacious "as a state of the brain" is asserted, not
  defended against exclusion arguments ([NOTE-117](../notes.d/NOTE-117.md), Limitations).
- **Which realism is right.** Footnote 20 grants interpretivist views
  (Curry 2025, Williams 2019) the same "overall state" without fictionalist
  semantics. The argument needs only that some realism is available.
- **That the dilemma refutes a fixed position.** Toon "sometimes suggests"
  the behavioural reading (p. 11), so the objection is a dilemma.
- **Anything about artificial systems.** The paper does not discuss them;
  the application to fictionalism about chatbot minds below is the
  record's.

## Connections

- **The metasemantic reply in developed form.** Drayson names Block's
  functional role semantics and does not develop it. [THEORY-089](THEORY-089.md) states
  Block's two-factor account and says how it answers Toon's "locked away"
  premise, with a qualification of Drayson's wording.
- **Fictionalism about chatbots.** Roberts's account ([THEORY-tmp83zag](THEORY-tmp83zag.md)) is a
  fictionalism about AI self-ascriptions motivated by embodiment, not by
  skepticism about representations. Drayson's dilemma carries over to any
  chatbot fictionalist who also wants chatbot talk to track the real causes
  of output; Roberts's own anti-realism leaves belief open, which is where
  a functionalist realism would sit.
- **Interpretationist realism.** Dennett's intentional strategy
  ([THEORY-tmphcjj0](THEORY-tmphcjj0.md)) is an interpretivist realism of the kind footnote 20
  grants, though Drayson does not cite it; Goldstein & Lederman ([LIT-206](../literature.d/LIT-206.md))
  apply such a view to language models.
- **The notebook.** Toon's pretence is a "memory-as-a-notebook game"
  (p. 10). Clark and Chalmers use the same image realistically
  ([THEORY-tmplw3dq](THEORY-tmplw3dq.md)).
