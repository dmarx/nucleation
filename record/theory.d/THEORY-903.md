# THEORY-903: In a 2026 meta-analysis of standalone safety-behaviour manipulations outside exposure, mostly with young non-clinical or subclinical adults and mostly on appearance concerns and social anxiety, reducing safety behaviours lowered symptoms relative to control (d = 0.43, about 0.31 after adjustment for publication bias), and instructed increases raised them (d = 0.65, five studies)

## Source

Goodson, Patel, Zech, Sala & Cougle, [LIT-1491](../literature.d/LIT-1491.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1128](../notes.d/NOTE-1128.md) from the NIH author manuscript (PMC12997458): Results,
Discussion and Table 1. The version of record is closed and was not
read.

## What was actually shown

- **Reduction lowers symptoms.** Against control, d = 0.43 (95% CI
  0.27–0.59; k = 16). Egger's test was significant (p = .012), and
  trim-and-fill gave d = 0.31 (95% CI 0.12–0.51).
- **Dose-response, across studies.** The between-group effect on safety
  behaviour moderated the effect on symptoms (B = 0.23, R² analogue
  0.85). No other planned moderator did.
- **Increase raises symptoms.** In five non-treatment experiments with
  healthy college samples, instructed increases raised symptoms against
  control (d = 0.65, 95% CI 0.46–0.83, I² = 0%). Bias could not be tested.
- **The samples.** 2,048 participants, mean age 24.9, 26.6% male. Most
  studies concerned appearance concerns or social anxiety. One concerned
  PTSD and two checking.

What could have come out otherwise: once exposure is removed, cutting
safety behaviours could have changed nothing, as Meulders et al. (2016)
nearly found for safety-behaviour instructions within exposure.

## What this does not say

- **That safety behaviours maintain anxiety disorders.** Diagnosed
  samples are rare. The authors say only that their results "align" with
  the maintenance theory.
- **How they act.** No included study is reported to measure threat
  estimates, approach, or what was learned once safety behaviours
  stopped. Expectancy effects are possible, since every treatment trial
  gave a rationale.
- **That the process mediates the outcome.** The moderator is between
  studies.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-446](../claims.d/CLAIM-446.md).** It supports the premise that protective behaviour
  does causal work on symptoms. It is silent on the claim's distinctive
  thesis that defensive authority, not belief, is the maintaining link.
- **[THEORY-053](THEORY-053.md).** The moderator is process-outcome covariation of the
  kind that theory asks for, but between studies, not within them.
- **[THEORY-058](THEORY-058.md).** Both directions of the manipulation move symptoms,
  which fits a loop through behaviour without showing that it closes.
