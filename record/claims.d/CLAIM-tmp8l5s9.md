# CLAIM-tmp8l5s9: Norms can persist long after their original justification disappears: in the United States a race-based difference in power outlived the chattel-slavery theories of property and rights that first justified it, and was ended in law only by overturning precedent and by constitutional amendment and statute

## The claim

The owner, 2026-11-24: "persistence of segregationism in the US is an
example of a norm (race based power difference) persisting long after its
original justification (chattel slavery theories of property and human
rights)", and the stronger case is made by "pointing to empirical examples
of precedent being overturned and constitutional amendment".

Two things are claimed:

- **Persistence.** The justification went with abolition; the norm did not.
- **How it ended.** Not by fading as its support weakened, but by
  discontinuous interventions: precedent overturned, amendment and statute.

## Bearing

It objects to the gap note in CLAIM-tmpjg5gx, which said the model lets a
norm outlast a weakening of its reasons "not their disappearance". The
assistant's answer, CLAIM-tmps8ou2, concedes that the note overstated
the narrowing.
