# QUESTION-074: Can the transport structure fitted to single transformations predict what receivers recover under a composition of transformations no data were fitted on, within the conditions under which approximate transports compose?

## The question

The reading: "the transport model should preferably impose constraints that a generic predictive model need not satisfy. For example, if transformations are composable, can the model predict the result of a previously unobserved composition from its constituent transformations? That is a much stronger test than fitting responses to individual source–target pairs. It moves the contribution from richer features toward generalizable structural constraints."

## Why the record needs it

[CLAIM-202](../claims.d/CLAIM-202.md)'s test fits and predicts on held-out renderings of the same transformations. A strong contextual baseline ([CLAIM-205](../claims.d/CLAIM-205.md)) could match it there. A prediction about an unobserved composition is one a generic predictor has no structure to make. This refines [QUESTION-017](QUESTION-017.md), which asks how distortions compose.

The record limits the test. [CLAIM-260](../claims.d/CLAIM-260.md) (Active) holds that approximate transports compose only under a triangle inequality and nonexpansive kernels, and [QUESTION-017](QUESTION-017.md) is answered in part by it. Outside those conditions the formalism predicts no additive bound, so a failed prediction there would not count against it.

## What would answer it

A study that fits the transport structure on renderings under transformations A and B separately, predicts receivers' recovery under A then B, and compares the prediction with the strong contextual baseline fitted on the same data, for transformations shown to meet [CLAIM-260](../claims.d/CLAIM-260.md)'s conditions.
