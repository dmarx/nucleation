# LIT-1549: Causal Scrubbing: a method for rigorously testing interpretability hypotheses

Chan, L., Garriga-Alonso, A., Goldowsky-Dill, N., Greenblatt, R.,
Nitishinskaya, J., Radhakrishnan, A., Shlegeris, B. & Thomas, N. (2022).
Causal scrubbing: a method for rigorously testing interpretability
hypotheses. *AI Alignment Forum*, 3 December 2022.

Read in full on 2026-10-26 ([NOTE-1168](../notes.d/NOTE-1168.md)); the companion posts were not
read. One account was drawn from it: [THEORY-978](../theory.d/THEORY-978.md).

## Why this is here

The owner asked on 2026-10-26 how the record stood on neural circuit
theory; the answer found it held almost none of the core circuits
literature, and the owner said "go ahead and register and read those".
This is the method by which a circuit hypothesis is tested rather than
illustrated, and the source of the resampling-ablation convention that
ACDC ([LIT-1547](LIT-1547.md)) and AtP* ([LIT-1531](LIT-1531.md)) use. The record's circuits
line held induction heads ([LIT-1538](LIT-1538.md)), grokking ([LIT-345](LIT-345.md)) and the
causal-abstraction readings ([LIT-961](LIT-961.md), [LIT-968](LIT-968.md), [LIT-1310](LIT-1310.md)), but no account of
how a stated circuit is checked. It bears on [QUESTION-234](../questions.d/QUESTION-234.md) and [THEORY-802](../theory.d/THEORY-802.md):
its score depends only on the partitions a hypothesis induces, so
basis-related hypotheses can tie by construction.
