# LIT-tmplkm3o: Faultless responsibility: on the nature and allocation of moral responsibility for distributed moral actions

Luciano Floridi (2016), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 374(2083): 20160112 — DOI-10.1098/rsta.2016.0112

## Key takeaways

- Distributed moral actions (DMAs) are morally good or evil actions caused
  by local interactions that are in themselves neither good nor evil, in a
  network of human, artificial and hybrid agents. Full moral responsibility
  for a DMA ("faultless responsibility") should be allocated, by default and
  overridably, to every node causally relevant to it, independently of
  intentionality.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Distributed moral responsibility is unproblematic in concept when it reduces
to a sum of individual human actions that are already morally loaded; it is
then only hard to allocate in practice. In distributed environments, though,
a network of agents, some human, some artificial (a program) and some hybrid
(a team working through a software platform), can cause DMAs, morally loaded
actions that arise from morally neutral local interactions. The paper
analyses the responsibility due to DMAs and argues that full responsibility
goes, by default and overridably, to all the causally relevant nodes,
whatever their intentions. The mechanism adapts three ideas: back
propagation from network theory, strict liability from jurisprudence, and
common knowledge from epistemic logic.

What a deeper reading should check:

- **Responsibility without intention, or agency without it.** The proposal
  allocates responsibility independently of intentionality. A reading should
  say whether that is a claim about agency (the nodes act without
  intending) or only about liability (they answer for what they did not
  intend), since Floridi 2025 ([LIT-tmp6juhh](LIT-tmp6juhh.md)) needs the first.
- **What back propagation is doing.** Whether the borrowing from network
  theory is an algorithm for tracing causal relevance or a metaphor, and
  how "causally relevant" is fixed without one.
- **Common knowledge.** Distributed morality ([LIT-tmpqgb1s](LIT-tmpqgb1s.md)) used distributed
  knowledge; this paper uses common knowledge. A reading should say what
  common knowledge is for here, and whether the change matters.
- **Collective responsibility.** It cites French, May and Olson on
  collective responsibility and collective action, none held here. The
  record's distributed-agency line ([CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md)) and [QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md) ask when a
  network is itself an agent; this paper allocates to the nodes, which may
  be its answer.
