# CASE-142: The label confirmed by resistance: a critic calls someone hostile, the person answers the characterization angrily, and the critic cites the anger as proof, so the judgement is confirmed by the response of someone who rejects it

## The case

A39: "A public critic may describe someone as hostile. The person responds
angrily to the characterization. The critic points to the response as
evidence that the characterization was correct."

Held fixed against [CASE-141](CASE-141.md): the label is false when applied, and
the person never accepts it. What differs is that nothing institutional
has to happen. The person's own response, organized against the judgement,
is the evidence.

## What it shows

- **Rejection does not free.** [CLAIM-415](../claims.d/CLAIM-415.md)'s second step, in a worked case.
  "The imposed identity becomes consequential not because it is accepted
  as true, but because the person organizes a response **against the
  people imposing it**" (A39). The judgement gains authority over the
  person's conduct ([CLAIM-349](../claims.d/CLAIM-349.md)'s second term) without being believed.
- **The song's context.** A39 heard the lyric this way, as "ironic,
  resentful, and defiant" ([LIT-1394](../literature.d/LIT-1394.md)). The couplet's second line, "If I wasn't then why would I say I am", takes the loop one step further:
  the speaker claims the label as his own, so that his defiance and the
  critics' description now say the same thing. That is [TERM-075](../terms.d/TERM-075.md)'s
  appropriated attribution.
- **Resistance can be as governed as compliance.** A39, on adversarial
  identity: "Someone who becomes determined to prove their critics wrong
  may organize considerable parts of their life around the critics'
  judgments." And: "someone who deliberately resists every social judgment
  may be no less governed by the judgments than someone who obeys every
  one." [CLAIM-425](../claims.d/CLAIM-425.md) is the claim this sets up.

## What it does not show

- That every angry answer confirms the label. Whether it does depends on
  how the critic, and any audience, reads it; a different audience could
  read the same answer as justified.
- That the person is wrong to answer. The case is about what the answer
  is taken to show, not whether it is warranted.
