# LIT-tmpak5ia: Catastrophic regime shifts in ecosystems: linking theory to observation

Marten Scheffer & Stephen R. Carpenter (2003), *Trends in Ecology &
Evolution* 18(12):648–656 — DOI-10.1016/j.tree.2003.09.002

## Key takeaways

- **Theory** (Box 2). In a stability landscape, a change in conditions can
  shrink a basin until it disappears at a bifurcation, and the system jumps
  to another basin. To return, conditions must be reversed past a second
  bifurcation: hysteresis. Basin size is resilience, and it can shrink while
  the state looks unchanged, so the system becomes "brittle".
- **Caveats** (§2). Stable states are really dynamic regimes. Shifts have
  multiple causes, and slow erosion of resilience is neglected in favour of
  the obvious trigger. Patterns depend on scale. The control condition must
  be external to the system or much slower than it.
- **Field hints, never conclusive** (§3). Jumps in time series, multimodal
  distributions and dual relationships to a control factor can each also
  come from a stepwise driver or from a threshold that is not a
  bifurcation. Massive colonisation does not count unless the new state
  regenerates itself.
- **Experimental evidence** (§4, Figure 4). Three kinds can come close to
  proof. Different initial states lead to different final states. A
  temporary disturbance leads to a lasting new state. Slow forward and
  backward change in a control factor shows hysteresis. Each has a trap.
  Recovery must not be blocked by isolation. Long return times mimic a new
  state: biomanipulated Lake Zwemlust stayed clear for six years, then
  slipped back. Hysteresis-like loops appear whenever the system is slow
  relative to the driver.
- **Models** (§5). Alternative attractors require positive feedback, but
  "positive feedbacks only lead to alternative attractors if they are
  sufficiently strong", so qualitative reasoning from feedback loops is not
  enough.
- **Coral reefs.** The Caribbean shift to algae is told as nutrient loading
  masked by fish, then by *Diadema*, until a pathogen removed the urchins
  (§1).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, standing in for the unread
Nature paper ([LIT-tmp5f80j](LIT-tmp5f80j.md)). It is the evidential standard for the essay's
"protective lock-in" (§5.4). A regime that persists is not thereby a
self-maintaining alternative state. That needs hysteresis, or a lasting
shift after a temporary disturbance, against the confound of slow return.
Hughes et al. 2007 ([LIT-711](LIT-711.md)) is in the record as a reef case where the
algal state vanished within 30 days of reopening the fish-exclusion cages.
