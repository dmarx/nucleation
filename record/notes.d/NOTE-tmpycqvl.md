# NOTE-tmpycqvl: Arsiwalla et al. — Pregeometry and formal language

## Contribution

The paper asks how to formalise the "structure of structures" that the foundations of physics would need, and sketches a metaphysics of pregeometric structure on which notions of quantum geometry could rest. It revisits Wheeler's pregeometry, Leibniz's monadology and modern topos-theoretic approaches, and argues that formal language — particularly homotopy type theory — supplies the building blocks. On this constructivist view, spaces and algebras are type-theoretic constructions, which could let distinct geometries be classified in one framework and address how spatiality arises in computational models such as the Wolfram model.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (pp. 4–6): sorts foundational work into four classes — interpretation, new mechanisms, new structures, and radical reconceptualisations of the origin of structure and observers — and locates the paper in the fourth.
- §3 (pp. 6–7): foundationalism vs coherentism as answers to the epistemic regress; the authors favour a constructivist coherentism for foundations.
- §4 (pp. 7–9): defines "structureless structures" as the symbolic, relational entities of a formal language, and reads Wheeler's pregeometry as meant to go beyond space, time, matter and law.
- §5 (pp. 9–11): Leibniz's monadology as the first "pre-space" theory, deriving spatial features from non-spatial relations.
- §§6–7 (pp. 11–14): rejects an unexplained physical substrate in favour of an ontology of relations; links the proposal to HoTT/univalent foundations and to computational models of physics.
