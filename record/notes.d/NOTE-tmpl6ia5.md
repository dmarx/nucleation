# NOTE-tmpl6ia5: Vallor (2024) — The Danger Of Superhuman AI Is Not What You Think

## Contribution

A first-hand statement, at the time of The AI Mirror, of its humanistic stance: AI as a mirror of human minds that lacks their substance, and the danger as a reduction of human self-understanding rather than machine takeover.

## Key results

1. Opening: the ideology "fosters the growing devaluation of human agency and autonomy and collapses the distinction between our conscious minds and the mechanical tools we’ve built to mirror them."
2. Second paragraph: "But an AI tool is dark inside."
3. The redefinition, mid-essay: "OpenAI’s AGI bait-and-switch wipes anything that does not count as economically valuable work from the definition of intelligence"; the ideology "slowly corrodes our own self-understanding."
4. The word: "The word “superhuman” means “human, but more so.”"
5. Borgmann, late: "it is precisely when a technology has nearly supplanted a vital domain of human meaning that we are able to feel and mourn what has been taken from us"; AI's "moral and experiential poverty" may prompt "renewed affection for our own capacity to create meaning".
6. Which tasks to hand over: "we asked AI developers to help us with the most meaningless tasks in our lives, the ones that drain our energy for everything else that matters".
7. Closing: "We are in danger of sleepwalking our way into a future where all we do is fail more miserably at being those machines ourselves." And: "I don’t think it is too late".

## Assumptions

Human intelligence is not reducible to task performance. Self-descriptions shape what people become. A Borgmannian view of focal practices, reclaimed when threatened.

## Concepts

- **"Superhuman" AI as ideology**: the redefinition of intelligence as outperforming humans at economically valuable work.
- **Reclamation**: renewed commitment to the humane capacities a technology nearly supplants.

## Connections

A thin-to-partial stand-in for LIT-tmplpkge. It carries the book's stance against "superhuman" AI, AI as a mirror of minds lacking their substance, the danger as devaluation of agency and self-understanding, and reclamation. It does not carry the mirror's backward orientation (trained on past data, reflecting past errors), moral deskilling, practical wisdom and technomoral virtue, or the programme of self-renewal in detail. Adewuyi's report that AI "mirrors past human data" (THEORY-tmpt6mee) is only glancingly supported here ("tools we’ve built to mirror them"). For deskilling, the CyCon paper (LIT-tmpcm4m7) stands in.
