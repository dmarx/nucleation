# THEORY-807: The guppy-type overextension in Hampton's membership data arises because a combined concept is in part a new, emergent concept and not only a logical combination of its parts, so human concept combination runs in two superposed layers, one classical and logical and one quantum and conceptual

## Source

Aerts (2009), [LIT-934](../literature.d/LIT-934.md), read in full in [NOTE-808](../notes.d/NOTE-808.md): the ℝ⁸ model with one
emergent subspace per pair (eqs. 61–62, Tables 3–4) and the two-layer
reading of thought (§§1.6, 3.4).

## What is asserted, and by whom

The thesis is that what carries the guppy effect is emergence: the combined
concept "pet-fish" is partly a new concept, not the meet of "pet" and
"fish", and the data show thought running in a classical logical layer and a
quantum conceptual layer at once. Aerts asserts it ([NOTE-808](../notes.d/NOTE-808.md), C5 moderate,
C6 weak). It is one of the answers [QUESTION-156](../questions.d/QUESTION-156.md) is after, the one that drops
conjunction-as-meet by adding a component outside the lattice.

## What the record holds toward it

- [THEORY-255](THEORY-255.md) (Active): Theorem 9, that taking conjunction and disjunction as meet and join with an order-preserving weight cannot produce over- or underextension, in Aerts's model and, by the record's own check, in any such lattice. That rules out the lattice meet; it does not choose emergence over the other ways out.
- The ℝ⁸ fit to all of Hampton's items ([NOTE-808](../notes.d/NOTE-808.md), C5) is a model with a free parameter per item, so its fit is not a test.

## What would confirm or reject it

A predictive emergent-component model on other data confirms it; an equally
economical model with no emergent component, which places the deviation in a
non-meet operation on the parts or in a non-monotone weight, rejects the
emergence reading as the explanation. [QUESTION-156](../questions.d/QUESTION-156.md) asks which models drop
which assumption; this entry is Aerts's answer.
