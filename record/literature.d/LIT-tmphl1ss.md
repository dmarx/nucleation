# LIT-tmphl1ss: Human Rights

James Nickel (2003), with Adam Etinson from the 2024 revision. "Human Rights". *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 7 February 2003, substantive revision 31 May 2024.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading ([NOTE-tmp8pbdm](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8pbdm.md)).*

- "People have human rights independently of whether such rights are
  present in the practices, morality, or law of their country or culture"
  (§1).
- "It is doubtful that all human rights are inalienable in this sense"
  (§1): an inalienable right is one whose "holder cannot lose it
  temporarily or permanently by bad conduct or by voluntarily giving it up".
- "The best form of existence for human rights would combine robust legal
  existence with the sort of moral existence that comes from being
  supported by strong moral and practical reasons" (§2.1).
- Of the grounds offered, the entry discusses agency/autonomy and dignity
  (§2.2).
