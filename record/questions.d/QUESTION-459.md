# QUESTION-459: Does a shared, typed and governed record help agents who hold different parts of the evidence integrate it and act on it, beyond what a structured communication protocol does, and at which stage does it help: admission, recognition, credibility or uptake?

## Why it is a question

From U12 and U13 of the exchange "luria experiment design"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af35bfc-35f8-83e9-a63d-d594703eabda). The owner
asked whether multi-agent coordination and distributed problem solving were
a better test of luria than single-agent memory, then asked A to develop
its own phrase "a shared epistemic state across collaborators", which
[TERM-119](../terms.d/TERM-119.md) now defines.

The record holds the parts:

- [QUESTION-101](QUESTION-101.md) and [TERM-055](../terms.d/TERM-055.md): whether a resource is available for an action
  can depend on the organization of the whole system, not on what any
  member knows; [CASE-085](../cases.d/CASE-085.md) is the drug that was stocked and could not be
  reached in time.
- [CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md): admission, recognition in apt terms, credibility, uptake and
  participation in governing those can each fail.
- [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md): feasibility, aggregation and epistemic coordination are
  independent obstructions; [NOTE-1381](../notes.d/NOTE-1381.md) reads Halpern and Moses on what
  unreliable channels rule out.
- [CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md) and [CASE-145](../cases.d/CASE-145.md): a shared perspective is not a collective agent.
- [NOTE-055](../notes.d/NOTE-055.md) and [THEORY-402](../theory.d/THEORY-402.md): a collective can hold more resources than any
  member and still lose them in integration.
- [QUESTION-108](QUESTION-108.md): whether a structured record helps by retaining more, by
  easing retrieval, or by making operations cheap.

None asks whether a record like this one changes any of it.

## The baseline it must beat

A39, after searching for prior experiments: "Distributed evidence
integration is already benchmarked." HiddenBench ([LIT-2294](../literature.d/LIT-2294.md), unread)
reports that agents holding distributed information "achieved 30.1%
accuracy, versus 80.7% for single agents given complete information. A
lightweight structured communication protocol improved performance. Luria
therefore needs to compete with that protocol, not just with unrestricted
chat." The paradigm is Stasser and Titus's hidden profile ([LIT-2496](../literature.d/LIT-2496.md));
the meta-analysis of its human studies is held ([LIT-1399](../literature.d/LIT-1399.md)). The shared
structured workspace is not new either: Hearsay-II ([LIT-2512](../literature.d/LIT-2512.md)) and
blackboard control ([LIT-2476](../literature.d/LIT-2476.md)), and an LLM blackboard system
([LIT-2470](../literature.d/LIT-2470.md)). A31 also named CooperBench ([LIT-2325](../literature.d/LIT-2325.md)), AsynCodeBench
([LIT-2277](../literature.d/LIT-2277.md)), MultiAgentBench ([LIT-2280](../literature.d/LIT-2280.md)) and PaperArena ([LIT-2295](../literature.d/LIT-2295.md)) as
external tests. All are registered unread.

## What would count as an answer

A study that holds evidence, models and budget fixed and varies only the
coordination substrate, against the published structured protocol as
well as plain conversation, with each decision-relevant clue traced
through admission, correct interpretation, credit and use ([CASE-252](../cases.d/CASE-252.md)).
Two fairness conditions, A31's: equal total compute measures efficiency,
equal compute per agent the gain or loss from adding agents.

- **Yes, at a stage**: the governed record beats the structured protocol,
  and the trace places the gain at one stage, for instance clues admitted
  and credited but no more often used.
- **No**: the protocol matches the record at matched cost. A31 named this
  outcome among the risks: "A capable coordinator with a shared task list
  and high-quality summaries might perform as well as the Luria-equipped
  team."
- **Not this question**: a failure that is an obstruction [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md)
  places elsewhere, infeasible demands or a broken decision rule, which no
  record can remove. A34: a team "should not be penalized for recognizing
  that its constraints are jointly unsatisfiable".

A team that settles an inconclusive dispute by producing a unified answer
has not succeeded. A32's false-consensus test: a good result can be that
"the team correctly identified that the evidence supports two unresolved
alternatives". [CLAIM-668](../claims.d/CLAIM-668.md) says what such a record can and cannot
represent of its collaborators' states.

## Note of 2026-11-12: the baseline phenomenon and the Secretary arm

Read on 2026-11-12 among the works of the exchange "luria experiment design"; each reading's NOTE and THEORY hold the detail.

**The baseline.** Stasser and Titus (1985, [THEORY-1808](../theory.d/THEORY-1808.md)) first reported
the phenomenon the question's tasks rest on. With the information about
three candidates distributed so that the best one, A, looked best only to
the group as a whole, "only 18% of the 38 groups in the unshared conditions
chose A", against 83% of the 18 groups that shared all of it (p. 1474).
Unshared items mostly did not enter discussion, which places the failure at
admission. The paper never uses the term "hidden profile", which came
later.

**The comparator a record must beat.** HiddenBench (Li, Naito and Shirado,
[THEORY-1747](../theory.d/THEORY-1747.md)) localizes the same failure in agent groups: revealing all
the facts raises accuracy to 92.6% and 98.2% for the two models, so "the
failure lies in surfacing information, not reasoning over it". Its
Secretary arm, a passive summary of what has been disclosed each round,
scores 0.241 and 0.713: "Passive summarization yields more limited and
model-dependent gains". A record that holds only what agents choose to post
is close to that arm. So the record arm must be compared with the Secretary
arm, not only with chat and the structured protocol, and it can help only by
changing what gets surfaced. [CASE-252](../cases.d/CASE-252.md)'s note of 2026-11-12 adds it as the baseline arm.

The other readings:

- **Stage.** CooperBench (Khatua, Zhu et al., [THEORY-1735](../theory.d/THEORY-1735.md)): a free-text
  channel between agents with private states cut conflicts without improving
  integration, and failures were of commitment and expectation; agents
  cooperating did about 30% worse than alone in the abstract's figure, "as
  large as 50%" in the leading models (§4). Its failures sit at uptake.
  Salemi et al. ([THEORY-1744](../theory.d/THEORY-1744.md)): letting holders of partial evidence
  answer a broadcast request improves integration, a result at recognition;
  the shared board it tests carries requests, not structured results. Clark
  and Brennan ([THEORY-1779](../theory.d/THEORY-1779.md)): a record lowers some costs of grounding and
  raises the cost of acknowledgement, which bears on recognition and uptake.
- **Measurement.** Zhang et al. ([THEORY-1729](../theory.d/THEORY-1729.md)): a task-level score hides
  unmet cross-agent dependencies, which only a per-dependency trace shows;
  coordination machinery helped some models and hurt others. DeChurch and
  Mesmer-Magnus ([THEORY-1800](../theory.d/THEORY-1800.md)): if a record helps by making members'
  knowledge converge, only measures of the structure of knowledge detect it;
  agreement ratings do not.
- **Cautions.** Xie et al. ([THEORY-1724](../theory.d/THEORY-1724.md)): agents told to keep structured
  records of findings gained 1.9 to 2.1 points, evidence summaries 10, in a
  single agent with an unenforced format. Zhu et al. ([THEORY-1782](../theory.d/THEORY-1782.md)): more
  agents and longer interaction lowered scores, so team size and compute
  must be matched. Li, Zoumpoulis et al. ([THEORY-1777](../theory.d/THEORY-1777.md)): crowds of one
  model family gain little from aggregation.
- **Not new.** Lesser and Erman's Hearsay-II ([THEORY-1810](../theory.d/THEORY-1810.md)): a shared
  store of typed hypotheses with credibility and support links, written by
  independent contributors, was built and assessed by 1977, as A said; it
  had no distributed evidence and no comparison with a protocol.
- **Fits poorly.** Wang et al. ([LIT-2295](../literature.d/LIT-2295.md), [THEORY-1773](../theory.d/THEORY-1773.md)) has no
  distributed information; at most it offers a transfer task of questions
  needing facts from several papers.

The question is unchanged.
