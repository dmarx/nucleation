# LIT-tmpun6ep: Soliton

Zabusky, N. J. & Porter, M. A. (2010). Soliton. *Scholarpedia* 5(8): 2068. doi:10.4249/scholarpedia.2068. http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Soliton.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpb86f1. Section numbers are the article's.*

- **What holds it** (lead): "A solitary wave is a localized "wave of
  translation" that arises from a balance between nonlinear and dispersive
  effects."
- **What survives collision** (lead): a soliton "emerges from the
  "collision" unchanged except possibly for a phase shift"; "for a
  conservative (non-dissipative) system, a soliton is a solitary wave whose
  amplitude, shape, and velocity are conserved after a collision".
- **Origin** (§1): Zabusky and Kruskal in 1965 "published their numerical
  solutions of the KdV equation (and invented the term "soliton")".
- **Formation from initial data** (§1, Fig. 3): a sine-wave initial
  condition yields "solitons that arise from the initial condition"; Russell
  found that "A sufficiently large initial mass of water can produce two or
  more independent near-solitary waves that separate in time."
- **Relatives are named, not treated** (§3): topological solitons "emerge
  because of topological constraints"; dissipative solitons are "Stable
  localized structures that arise in spatially extended dissipative
  systems"; relatives "do not in general have similar interaction
  properties as solitons".

## Standing in the record

Filed as the read neighbour of Zabusky & Kruskal (1965), LIT-tmpfx0lb, for
the naming, the numerical origin, the elastic collision and the recurrence
picture, in Zabusky's own later words, but not for the letter's numbers; and
of Kivshar & Malomed (1989), LIT-tmpzah9i, only for the definition, which
the article cites the review beside. Filed from it: THEORY-tmp68j1u. It bears
on the record's questions of individuation as a sharp case: an individual
whose identity is the parameter set that survives collision, with no upkeep.
