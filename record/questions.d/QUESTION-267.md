# QUESTION-267: When the ordering that prevails a system's negotiation ranks its states by a proxy for its maintenance rather than by its maintenance, as a firm's potential metric may, is that valence, and is it the form that metric fixation in organizations and addiction in persons share?

## Why it is a question

[TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md)'s restriction, to an ordering of the system's own states by
their bearing on its maintenance, keeps any potential goal from counting as
valence. But a system can rank its own states by a proxy that once
tracked maintenance and has come apart from it.

- **The organizational case.** In Wells Fargo's Community Bank
  ([CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md)), cross-sell was treated as the measure of how the bank was
  doing, and it won the bank's conflicts for a decade against risk, HR
  and complaints. It was not the bank's viability, which the practices it
  rewarded endangered. [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md) reads that case as threat without
  remorse; this question asks what kind of valence, if any, the metric's
  potential was.
- **The personal case.** In addiction a reward signal that once tracked
  the organism's maintenance comes to govern its choices against it.
  The record holds Ainslie on the breakdown of will ([LIT-586](../literature.d/LIT-586.md)), which may
  bear on this.

## What would count as an answer

A criterion that says when an ordering over a system's own states is "by
their bearing on its maintenance" and when it is by a proxy, applicable
to both cases; and a verdict on whether proxy-keyed potential is valence
of a distinct kind, mis-keyed valence, or not valence at all.

## What would not answer it

The observation that the proxy and maintenance diverged, which defines
the case without answering it.

## Note of 2026-10-23: a mechanism, and a coupling

**A mechanism on the personal side.** Incentive sensitization
([THEORY-895](../theory.d/THEORY-895.md), from Berridge and Robinson, [LIT-1461](../literature.d/LIT-1461.md), [NOTE-1109](../notes.d/NOTE-1109.md))
re-keys the strength of 'wanting' to cues, lastingly ("Once induced,
sensitization is very long lasting, and possibly even permanent"), gated
by context ("not always hyper-active, but it is hyper-reactive to drug
cues and contexts"), and cut loose both from pleasure and from the
person's own goals. That is proxy-keyed potential with each of Frijda's
three marks accounted for. It also sharpens this question: the cue-keyed
ordering is no more an ordering of the system's own states than
'liking' is ([CLAIM-433](../claims.d/CLAIM-433.md)'s note of the same day).

**A coupling between a firm and its users.** Flayelle and colleagues'
taxonomy of design features ([LIT-1465](../literature.d/LIT-1465.md), [NOTE-1096](../notes.d/NOTE-1096.md)) describes a
provider-side selection: "design features that increase the frequency of
online behaviour, time-on-device or spending (and therefore revenue) will
be maintained, whereas those that do not will be abandoned" (p. 6).
Read with the record's account, two proxy potentials are coupled: the
firm's engagement metric prevails in its negotiation, and the features it
keeps install cue-keyed potential in its users. The mapping of features
onto the account is the record's, not the authors':
- reinforcement schedules and personalized cues act on strength;
- moving goalposts act on the reference course, which "keep individuals
  engaged for longer than planned" (p. 8), and predict mildly negative
  valence during use;
- near-misses and losses disguised as wins "contribute to the false belief
  that one is progressing" (p. 9), a user-side mis-modelled valence,
  counterpart of [CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md)'s misinformed board;
- autoplay wins without any conflict being posed, which a conflict record
  ([QUESTION-268](QUESTION-268.md)) would miss.

This joins Doctorow's account of platform decay ([NOTE-041](../notes.d/NOTE-041.md)) to [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md)'s
threat without remorse: the harm to users need not be represented
anywhere in the firm. The taxonomy's evidence is mostly cross-sectional or
qualitative, and its authors warn against pathologizing ordinary use, so
it is a description, not grounding. A documented case of a platform whose
own records show features kept for engagement despite internal signs of
harm would test the coupling; candidates exist, but none is read, and
their standing (leak, complaint) would need settling first.
