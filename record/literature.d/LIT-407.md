# LIT-407: Troubles with Functionalism

Ned Block (1978), in C. Wade Savage (ed.), *Perception and Cognition: Issues
in the Foundations of Psychology*, Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of
Science, Volume 9, Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, pp. 261–325.
Open access at the University of Minnesota Digital Conservancy,
https://hdl.handle.net/11299/185298. Reprinted in Ned Block, *Consciousness,
Function, and Representation: Collected Papers, Volume 1*, MIT Press, 2007
(DOI 10.7551/mitpress/2111.003.0006).

The brief's citation (Minnesota Studies 9:261–325, 1978) is correct. One
search result gave pp. 261–326; the scan ends on p. 325 and the Conservancy
record gives 261–325.

## Key takeaways

- The **homunculi-headed robot** (§1.2): a body whose sense and motor
  neurons connect to a cavity of little men, each executing one quadruple
  of a machine table that describes you. Its nomologically likelier variant
  is the **nation of China**: a billion people with two-way radios, the
  machine state shown on satellites, run for an hour. It realises your
  machine table, and there is prima facie doubt that there is anything it
  is like to be it. Block calls this the **Absent Qualia Argument**.
- **Putnam's stipulation** (§1.5) that no pain-feeler "possesses a
  decomposition into parts which separately possess Descriptions" of the
  pain-capable kind is "the ad hoc device of stipulating them away".
  - The all-parts version does not exclude the robot.
  - The no-sentient-proper-parts version excludes pregnant women.
  - The "crucial role" version fails on the elementary-particle people.
    These are tiny beings in spaceships who mimic our particles, so that
    you come to be made of them, with no change to your synapses. "Would
    you be any less capable of feeling pain? … I think not."
- What drives the doubt is not the parts' minds. "What seems important is
  how the mentality of the parts contributes to the functioning of the
  whole" (p. 292). Note 19 goes further: each homunculus is provably
  replaceable by a McCulloch–Pitts "and" neuron, so the argument "does not
  advert at all to the mentality of the homunculi". The doubt rests on the
  simulation being unlike us psychologically and neurally, and on its
  being designed to mimic us (pp. 292–296).
- **Psychofunctionalism** (§2) answers the doubt for beliefs and desires: a
  homunculi-headed system of which every true psychological theory holds
  "could have nonqualitative mental states" (p. 301). Qualia may not be in
  the domain of psychology at all (§2.2).
- **Liberalism and chauvinism** (§3). Inputs and outputs described only as
  inputs and outputs make the theory "wildly liberal". A sheik could rig
  Bolivia's economy to be functionally equivalent to himself, and "the
  economy of Bolivia could not have mental states" is offered as a fixed
  point (p. 315). Inputs and outputs described physically make the theory
  chauvinist.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 for the owner's bridge between Minsky's society of mind
([LIT-429](LIT-429.md)) and Schwitzgebel's conscious United States ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md), read in
[NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md)). Schwitzgebel cites it twice. In §2 it is the counterexample to Putnam's
anti-nesting stipulation. In §7 the China brain is the intuition pump behind
neurochauvinism, "though see Lycan 1981". The Lycan paper is [LIT-419](LIT-419.md). The
book version ([LIT-216](LIT-216.md), read in [NOTE-188](../notes.d/NOTE-188.md)) keeps "Block's tiny people" among its
reductios of anti-nesting.

**Where it sits on the seam.** Block stands on the *composition* side,
with a whole made of minded people, and he also contributes the first
critique of the *anti-nesting principle* itself. Against the claims as
[NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md) numbers them:

- **C3** (a whole of conscious parts can be conscious): Block **contests**
  it for qualia in a Functional simulation, but not on anti-nesting
  grounds. His own n. 19 says the doubt survives replacing every homunculus
  with a mindless gate. On cognition he **concedes** it: a Psychofunctional
  homunculi-head can believe.
- **C4** (Putnam's principle is unjustified): Block **supports** it, and
  supplies its counterexample (§1.5, n. 16).
- **C5** (IIT's exclusion postulate): **neutral**. The paper predates IIT,
  though the elementary-particle case has the same structure as
  Schwitzgebel's anti-exclusion reductios.

His reason for doubting the China system transfers only in part to a
nation. Two of his three grounds are that the system was designed to mimic
a person (§1.6) and that its information processing need not resemble
ours. A nation was designed to mimic nothing, and Schwitzgebel's case does
not need it to resemble a human's processing. That pairing is mine, but it
is the distinction Schwitzgebel draws in fn 20 between the US and "more
artificial cases". The Bolivia "fixed point" is the methodological opposite
of Schwitzgebel's: he treats commonsense bizarreness as weak evidence.

**Correction carried for [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md).** Schwitzgebel's §2, and [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md)'s C4 row
after him, describe Block's counterexample as tiny conscious organisms
playing the roles of your neurons. Block's counterexample is at the
elementary-particle level, a change "only to your microphysics" (p. 292).
For the neuron-level case Block says intuition "seems to founder" (n. 17).
The difference matters for the bridge. At the particle level no technique
of psychology or neurophysiology would reveal any difference in you, so the
case cannot tell whether nesting matters at the level where the parts do
the psychological work.

**Relations not declared.** It argues against Putnam's stipulation
([LIT-396](LIT-396.md)) but does not refute it formally, so no `corrects:` is set. The
group-agency and scale-free-self works filed alongside this one, List and
Pettit's group agents, Huebner's macrocognition and Levin's computational
boundary of a self, all inherit its distinction. Agency or cognition built
from parts is one question; qualia built from parts is another, and Block
answers them differently.

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.

**Later answers to the absent-qualia argument held in this record.**
Chalmers's "Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia" ([LIT-392](LIT-392.md))
argues that the note-17 case, one person per neuron, is conscious if
organisational invariance holds. Dennett's "Quining Qualia" ([LIT-433](LIT-433.md))
denies the property the intuition needs. Neither answers the
machine-table China system directly.
