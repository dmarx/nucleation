# NOTE-tmp3iwtu: Falbo — Hermeneutical Injustice: Distortion and Conceptual Aptness

## Contribution

A distinction between a concept's acquisition (competence, availability in the repertoire) and its
application conditions, and a second species of hermeneutical injustice located in the latter,
caused by the presence, not the absence, of concepts.

## Key results

- Abstract: hermeneutical injustice can result "from the overabundance of distorting and oppressive concepts which function to crowd-out, defeat, or pre-empt the application of a more accurate hermeneutical resource."
- Sec. III: hermeneutical resources "organize members of society and cast them into certain roles and relations with expected behaviors."
- Sec. III: "we also need to consider how newly introduced concepts are operationalized and how they cohere (or fail to cohere) with other extant concepts".
- Sec. IV: "There is thus a need to sharply distinguish between the initial processing or acquisition conditions of a hermeneutical resource, on the one hand, and its proper application conditions, on the other."
- Sec. IV: "A hermeneutical resource can be collectively available yet fail to be applied in socially significant contexts where it matters the most."
- Sec. V: Miller's injustice "didn’t involve a lack of intelligibility, but instead resulted from a failure of conceptual application or conceptual aptness."
- Sec. V: "positive hermeneutical injustice results from the presence of oppressive and distorting concepts that crowd out, defeat, or preempt the application of an available and more accurate concept."
- Sec. V: hermeneutical marginalization extends to "unequal authority and power concerning not just the initial creation and dissemination of conceptual resources, but also their revision, reinforcement, and overall influence and applicability".
- Sec. V: "Overcoming negative forms of hermeneutical injustice—by filling in hermeneutical lacunae—does not entail that positive forms of hermeneutical injustice will be guarded against and rectified as well."
- Sec. VI: "filling in hermeneutical gaps is not enough to ensure hermeneutical justice".
- Note 2: Fricker's own discussion "is sensitive to how oppressive stereotypes—and not just conceptual gaps—can influence hermeneutical injustice, yet she remains focused largely upon lacunae".

## Assumptions

- Conceptual competence does not require correct application in every case (the avocado-as-fruit
  example, sec. IV).
- The Turner case is taken as a case of hermeneutical (not only testimonial) injustice; a reader
  could classify it as identity-prejudicial credibility deflation alone. Falbo addresses the link to
  testimonial injustice in sec. V but does not argue it is not merely testimonial.

## Concepts

Lacuna-centered framework; productive vs. interpretive function of hermeneutical resources;
controlling image (Collins); hermeneutical clash; nested opposition (Balkin); hermeneutical defeat
and preemption; acquisition vs. application conditions; conceptual aptness; positive vs. negative
hermeneutical injustice; failure of operationalization.

## Bearing on the record

A cited it for the point that a shared interpretive failure can come from an existing but
distorting concept, not only from a missing one, applied to a schema that systematically
misclassifies contributions. **Accurate on Falbo's thesis.** The application to a schema is A's
analogy, not Falbo's: her account is about social concepts that carry oppressive power and harm
marginalized knowers; a record's schema misclassifying a contribution is structurally parallel (an
available category preempted by a dominant, ill-fitting one) but lacks the oppression that makes
Falbo's case an injustice.
- QUESTION-072 (does a project memory distribute credibility among contributors by making some
  moves addressable and leaving others as unfiled turns?): **qualifies**, by adding a second
  mechanism. The question is lacuna-shaped (a move without a code is lost); Falbo shows the other
  path: a move can have a scheme available and still be filed under a dominant scheme that
  preempts the apt one, with the credibility loss she describes ("the denial of Miller’s credibility
  and thus diminished her status as a knower"). Leaves the question open; widens what an answer
  must check (misfiling, not only non-filing).
- CLAIM-403 (admission, recognition in apt terms, credibility, uptake and part in revising standards
  of uptake are separable): **supports the recognition-to-credibility rung.** Falbo's positive
  hermeneutical injustice is a case where the contribution is admitted and intelligible, but not
  recognized in apt terms, and loses credibility and uptake as a result; and her note that
  marginalization covers the "revision, reinforcement" of resources matches the claim's last
  stage. It is a case from outside organizations, so it corroborates rather than demonstrates.
- LIT-1004 (Fricker, held, Unavailable): Falbo reports Fricker's lacuna requirement and quotes her
  (2007, 1, 150-51, 162); these are second-hand quotations of Fricker and should not be filed as a
  reading of Fricker.

## Limitations

A philosophical argument from cases (chiefly Turner; also a 2017 case and Manne's inference
patterns); no account of how to tell an apt from a distorting concept in general; the remedy
(social movements) is sketched.
