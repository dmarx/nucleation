# LIT-1871: Why are dreams interesting for philosophers? The example of minimal phenomenal selfhood, plus an agenda for future research

Thomas Metzinger (2013). *Frontiers in Psychology* 4:746. DOI
10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00746, PMC3813926.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1434](../notes.d/NOTE-1434.md).*

- **Transparency, defined.** A transparent representation is "not
  experienced as" a representation; an opaque one is, "for example in
  pseudo-hallucinations or lucid dreams"; there is "a graded spectrum
  between transparency and opacity" (p. 2, note 2).
- **Identification follows transparency.** "Because our conscious body
  model is transparent, that is, because it cannot introspectively be
  recognized as a model, we fully identify with its representational
  content" (p. 3).
- **A narrower MPS.** Since a robust sense of self is reported where body
  representation is reduced to a point, self-identification may reduce to
  self-location, giving MPS as "transparent self-location in a
  spatiotemporal frame of reference" (Windt's formulation, p. 7).
- **The prediction, and the test.** "Transparent spatiotemporal
  self-location is necessary and sufficient for bringing about a minimal,
  consciously experienced sense of selfhood" (p. 14). The test is a case of
  phenomenally opaque self-location; the paper asks whether any is known,
  and reports none.
- **Evidence is second-hand.** Out-of-body prevalence figures (Alvarado
  2000, Wolfradt) and one dream report; the paper itself says the
  categories in those surveys are ambiguous.
