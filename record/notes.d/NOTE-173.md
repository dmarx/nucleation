# NOTE-173: Griesemer & Shavit — Scaffolding individuality (3Cs)

## Contribution

The paper offers a vocabulary, not a model. It orders coordination, cooperation and collaboration as an idealized sequence of stages in an evolutionary transition in individuality (ETI). The stages are paired with three grades of group structure: collection, conexus and community. The authors read the MLS1/MLS2 contrast as a developmental progression, "from potentially unstable MLS1 situations into more stable MLS2 situations" (§2), rather than as rival formalisms. They extend Griesemer's sampling account of inheritance (propagules as generative samples of parental organization) from organisms to migrant social groups (§3). What is new is the combination: a staged interaction typology in which the last stage is marked by generative entrenchment and organizational closure. That typology is joined to a reproducer-based account of how such a community could be inherited.

## Key insight

Group-level individuality is not a matter of how strongly members interact but of what *kind* of interaction dominates. In coordination and cooperation, benefits can still be booked per member in the members' own currency. The lions' meat per lion is the paper's example: "the currency of cooperative interaction is the same as for individual action" (§1). In collaboration the group benefit (reliability, a team's win) "cannot even be recognized as group benefits without reference to the special interactions engendered by the group", and marginal attribution becomes "effectively meaningless" (§1). A community is a collective whose core interactions have become generatively entrenched, so that removing them is catastrophic. It therefore has organizational closure, and reproducing it requires exporting an organized sample of roles *and* interactions, not just of members (§3).

## Assumptions

- **The sequence is idealized and normative only in the embryologist's sense.** Coordination → cooperation → collaboration is "one possible pathway", like a normal table, and deviations are "heterochronic". How many real ETIs follow it is "an open empirical question" (§1).
- **It fits "egalitarian" (get-together) transitions better than "fraternal" (stick-together) ones.** Fraternal cases may run in other orders or in reverse (§1, after Queller & Strassmann 2009 and Szathmáry 2015).
- **Diversity ≠ heterogeneity** (after Shavit & Ellison 2021). Diversity is the relative abundance of member types. Heterogeneity is the variety of interactions and structural positions. Community needs the latter (§1).
- **Individuality is marked by organizational closure** in the sense of Moreno & Mossio (2015), which is adopted, not argued (§1, §3).
- **Inheritance is propagation by sampling** (Griesemer 2000). Offspring develop from generative samples of parental material parts, and an inheritance system is a sampling process evolved to make that development reliable. It need not propagate genes (§3).
- **Trait groups vs demes.** Only demic communities, not ephemeral trait groups, are candidates for higher-level reproducers (§2, citing Griesemer 2000 "units of evolutionary transition"). Which kind a given human grouping is, is treated as empirical.
- **Human focus with a general hope.** Humans are the target case "yet our hope is that the conceptual model applies to all life histories" (§1).
- **No formal setting.** There are no equations, no simulation and no data ("No data were reported").

## Key results

This is a conceptual paper, so the results are the positions argued or stated.

- **The 3Cs (§1).**
  - *Coordination* is "ordering in space and time" among members of a *collection* who co-occupy a place. The interaction is minimal, contributions are "mainly 'aggregative'", and the order may be self-organized, mutually imposed or externally scaffolded (queues, crosswalk stripes).
  - *Cooperation* is "operating together". Members enter the arena because individual goals need a group context, but there need be no shared goal, and effects are "interactive and non-aggregative … but only in limited ways". Benefit is still measurable per individual.
  - *Collaboration* is "labouring together" for a shared goal. In a community it yields a "sense of belonging" or trust, and "reliability is thus an emergent community property".
- **Table 1.** The columns are Stage 1 ("Low intensity of individual interaction"), Stage 2 ("Medium–high intensity"), and Stage 3 ("Intra-group interaction"), with a vertical label, "Transition to a new level-of-individuality perspective", between Stages 2 and 3.

  | | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Interaction | Coordination (co-ordination) | Cooperation (co-operation) | Collaboration (co-labour) |
  | Entity | Collection ("Co (two)-location") | Conexus ("Co (two)-nexus") | Community ("Com (together)-unity") |
  | Action | Ordered position | Joint operation | Shared labour |
  | Cause of action | Follow a rule, force or standard, externally or mutually imposed | Reconciliation within existing state, providing individual win-win situations | Maintain a transition through participation in a shared goal/aim/entity |

- **The structural sequence (§2).** A collection is pairwise binary interactions. A conexus ("connected collection") is a system of binary interactions forming "a web of junctions and meeting points". A community is "intersecting reference groups" in "a rich network of heterogeneous multi-way interactions where some interaction subsets have become entrenched so that most other interactions depend on them". Each step "further increases density and complexity of interaction structure".
- **MLS reinterpreted (§2).** There is "a rough positive analogy" between individual selection, MLS1 and MLS2 and the three grades. The disanalogy is that "the 3Cs are not necessarily about levels of selection. Rather, they are about levels of individuality and reproduction." In the paper's variant of classical group-selection models, migration can happen *within* generations and groups "develop" during the selection phase, so "selection and reproduction are more or less intertwined".
- **Two instabilities and three outcomes (§2).** Individuals move among reference groups, and reference groups move in trait space away from individuals. A conexus may (1) dissolve into coordination, (2) persist as an unstable conexus held by external scaffolding and high-connectivity hubs, or (3) self-stabilize into a community.
- **Generative entrenchment drives conexus → community (§2).** "Inter-actions" among conexuses become "intra-actions" (after Barad). The transition is "highly contingent, even if contingently irreversible". In human cases a transition "is marked by a change in the way (human) communication is organized (and propagated)".
- **Community reproduction (§3).** Following Caporael, a community "must be exported as an organized generative package larger than a single individual member", because no migrant can carry the whole structure "in their head". Integration means that removing node or interaction types breaks organization. The chain of inference is: community needs organizational closure, "Hence, community constitutes a new level of individuality."
- **The Neolithic thought experiment (§3).** Take a society of hunters, gatherers, farmers and shamans with heterogeneous interaction. A migrant group "might require" at least one of each type, the ongoing interaction types, and new group-specific capacities (fencing farmers, temple-founding shamans) to reassemble the culture at a new site.
- **Individuality and inheritance (§3).** Inheritance systems at new levels "seem likely to evolve in tandem" with new levels of individuality "in so far as the latter involve diverse members with heterogeneous interactions". The paper sets this against those who separate the two. It also suggests that Maynard Smith & Szathmáry's limited vs unlimited heredity "might be further characterized" by the mechanisms of the 3Cs. No such characterization is given.

## Method

There is no formal method. It is conceptual analysis by definition, analogy (population-genetic trait-group and deme models, embryological normal tables) and illustration (everyday examples, a Neolithic thought experiment). The main conceptual imports are generative entrenchment (Wimsatt), organizational closure (Moreno & Mossio), inheritance as sampling of propagules (Griesemer), truly social cognition and repeated assembly (Caporael), diversity vs heterogeneity (Shavit & Ellison), and scaffolding (Caporael, Griesemer & Wimsatt 2013).

## Concepts

- **Collection** — a grouping of individuals with the potential for more interaction but interacting "in minimal ways", through pairwise coordination. Membership lasts only while the interaction does (§1, §2).
- **Conexus** — "connected collection". It is a network of binary cooperative interactions forming a web of junctions. Its persistence is an unstable dynamic equilibrium, and it corresponds roughly to MLS1 group structure (§1, §2).
- **Community** — intersecting reference groups in a heterogeneous multi-way network with entrenched core interactions and organizational closure. It is the new level of individuality (§2, §3).
- **Coordination / cooperation / collaboration** — "ordinating" in space and time / "operating together" without a required shared goal / "labouring together" for a shared goal (§1).
- **Diversity vs heterogeneity** — the abundance of member types vs the variety of interactions and structural positions (§1).
- **Generative entrenchment** — a core interaction that later interactions depend on, so that perturbing it is catastrophic while changing later ones is tolerable (§2).
- **Reference group** — a trait or task group an individual belongs to. Individuals can move among them, and the groups can move away from individuals (§2).
- **Scaffolding** — a temporary structure or interaction framework supporting a process, extended to development, evolution and cognition (endnote 2).
- **Unithood** — the paper's generic word for the three grades collection/conexus/community, used alongside "phases of 'individuality'" (§2).
- **Endogenization** — Bourrat's term for bringing scaffolded features inside the collective. The paper relocates it to the "community rather than … a lower-level individual" (§2).

The paper's nearest neighbours in this record are Bourrat's individuality papers. ## Bearing on the record

- **Mereology ([LIT-124](../literature.d/LIT-124.md)).** The paper is tagged `mereology`, but it contains no mereology in the formal sense. It has no parthood relation, no composition principle and no discussion of when parts compose a whole. Its part–whole content is the ETI definition itself ("becoming part of a new reproducing collective while losing the capacity of independent reproduction"). Beyond that it offers a graded, interaction-based criterion for when a collective counts as one (entrenchment plus organizational closure), and the Barad-style re-description of inter-actions as intra-actions. That is a substantive answer to a special composition question for social and biological collectives, but it is given in process and organization terms, not mereological ones. A reader browsing mereology would find a case of the composition question, not a treatment of it. The tag is defensible under [ANTH-ADR-046](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-046.md)'s "justifiably appropriate" test, but it is secondary, and `philosophy-of-science` is right as the primary topic. Pair it with [LIT-142](../literature.d/LIT-142.md) (Meincke), which does argue parthood and count explicitly, for contrast.
- **Individuality cluster.** It gives [LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md), [LIT-049](../literature.d/LIT-049.md) and [LIT-157](../literature.d/LIT-157.md) a stated rival emphasis: community reliability and heterogeneity of interactions, against Darwinian properties plus individualizing mechanisms. It does not refute any of them, because no model or counterexample is given.
- **ML practice.** It carries nothing for ML practice. One paragraph (§1, and endnote 5 on Chalmers) speculates about a possible "AI–human transition" in which humans "find 'community' with AI". The authors call this "an empirical question of great complexity" and do not pursue it. No ANTH- document bears on it.

## Limitations

- The two most citable claims, non-circularity (C1) and individuality as a special case of collectivity (C2), are made only in the abstract. The abstract claims more than the body shows.
- The authors call it "not a fully-fledged model but only a first and rough sketch" (§1). They give no operational criteria that would sort an actual group into collection, conexus or community, so the empirical questions they pose (how many ETIs follow the path; trait group vs deme) cannot yet be put to data with this framework.
- Stage 3 is defined partly in psychological and intentional terms (shared goal, sense of belonging, trust, identity). That limits transfer to "all life histories" (§1) and sits awkwardly with the non-circularity claim.
- It is internally inconsistent on individuality vs inheritance timing (§2 vs §3), and there is a garbled key sentence in §2.
- The paper says it will "illustrate their explanatory power for other ETI case studies (independently studied in detail across this special issue)" (§2). It does not do so. Only the Neolithic case is sketched, and it is conditional.
- It says "hominid evolution" is central in the abstract, but the body does not discuss it.

## Open questions

- Can the among-members vs within-members criterion from the abstract be stated precisely enough to check the non-circularity claim? What would count as an interaction among members that is "not an instance" of one within them?
- Is there a measurable signature that separates a conexus from a community? Candidates are non-computability of per-member benefit, or the knockout effect of removing an interaction type. With such a signature the Neolithic case could be scored.
- Does the promised model of community "reliability" and endogenization into the community (§2) actually yield ETIs that Bourrat-style models do not?
- Which ordering holds, if either: individuality before inheritance (§2), or both in tandem (§3)? What case would discriminate between them?
- How much of the framework survives when the intentional vocabulary of Stage 3 is removed, as it must be for non-human transitions?

## Corrections to the seeded skim

- The dossier, the NOTE-173 summary and the [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md) summary all state "can be described without circularity" as a finding. In the full text, non-circularity is claimed in one abstract sentence: "We describe ET in a non-circular way, noting kinds of interactions among community members … that are not instances of those same interactions within community members". The body never mentions circularity again. It never uses the among-members vs within-members criterion. It gives no argument that the 3Cs avoid presupposing the higher-level unit. Stage 3 is in fact defined partly by a "shared goal", a "sense of belonging" and "group identity" (§1), which presuppose the group as their object. The claim is asserted, not argued.
- "Individuality is a special case of collectivity" is likewise abstract-only ("We argue for the explanatory benefits of treating 'individuality' as a special case of 'collectivity'"). The phrase does not recur in the body, and no explanatory benefit is set out or defended there. What the body supports is a reconstruction. Collectivity is a graded scale of "density, intensity, higher-level emergent 'jointness' and heterogeneity" (§2). Collections and conexuses are collectives but not individuals, and a community is the collective that reaches "organizational closure" (after Moreno & Mossio), hence "community constitutes a new level of individuality" (§3). The one body sentence that tries to state the thesis is garbled in the published text: "community can be seen as a different kind not of individuality, which is not only an issue of Darwinian properties at collective level" (§2).
- The skim treats "collection, conexus, community" as three levels of individuality. The text calls them "three levels of unithood or phases of 'individuality'" (§2). Table 1, however, draws a vertical bar, "Transition to a new level-of-individuality perspective", between Stage 2 (conexus) and Stage 3 (community). Only the community is a new individual. The paper also slips once: the closing summary says "coordination in collectives", not "collections" (§3).
- The skim misses the central mechanism. The conexus-to-community transition is driven by *generative entrenchment* (after Wimsatt 2007): "core" interactions come to be ones "later-evolved interactions will depend on for their existence and character, so that perturbations of it will tend to be catastrophic". The transition is "highly contingent, even if contingently irreversible" (§2).
- The skim also misses the §2 apparatus. It sets out two "unstable dynamic equilibria": individuals migrating among reference groups, and reference groups moving their trait boundaries away from individuals. It also gives three outcomes for a conexus: dissolution to coordination, persistence as an unstable scaffolded conexus, or stabilization into a community.
- The "in tandem" conclusion is weaker and less consistent than the skim says. §3 says inheritance systems "seem likely to evolve in tandem … in so far as the latter involve diverse members with heterogeneous interactions". Earlier, §2 says that a transition to a new level of reproduction "may indicate the emergence of a new level of individuality, but not yet of a transition in inheritance system", which "may require further adaptive evolution". So the paper itself allows the two to come apart in time.
- The abstract says "hominid evolution and learning" drive the argument, but the body has no discussion of hominid evolution. The human material is the Neolithic southern Levant (after Shavit & Sharon), modern firms, a sports team, political parties and academic credit. "Learning" is one sentence citing Watson & Szathmáry (2016) (§3).
- The Neolithic case does not "show" that founding groups must sample heterogeneous roles. It is a conditional thought experiment: sampling "might require" migrant groups to include each occupation type (§3).
- The skim's open question treats discipline formation (§1) as an analogy from biology to academic communities. In the text it is an example of a *reverse* pathway in "fraternal" cases, running collaboration → cooperation → coordination. The paper's focus is human socio-cultural evolution throughout, and biology enters mainly through population-genetic analogies (Wilson trait groups, Wright demes).
- The skim does not record that §2 engages Bourrat directly. The paper endorses his scaffolding and endogenization account (Bourrat, BJPS; Black, Bourrat & Rainey 2020) but faults his "reliance on classical island migration models" for neglecting Wright's shifting-balance tradition. It then proposes a "missing ingredient": community *reliability*, with endogenization "into the community rather than into a lower-level individual".
- Minor: [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md)'s summary reads "Griesemer et al. (2023)". There are two authors, James Griesemer and Ayelet Shavit.
