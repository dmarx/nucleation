# LIT-2140: Crossing the great divide: Coproduction, synergy, and development

Ostrom, E. (1996). Crossing the great divide: Coproduction, synergy, and development. *World Development* 24(6): 1073–1087. doi:10.1016/0305-750x(96)00023-x.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. Everything below is per Evans (1996), [LIT-2143](LIT-2143.md), read in
[NOTE-1665](../notes.d/NOTE-1665.md), unless it says Watson. Page numbers are those of the Evans
text read.*

- **Complementarity, per Evans.** "As Ostrom's production functions
  (Figures I and 2) show graphically, complementarity is the essential
  prerequisite of coproduction": inputs "sufficiently different (but
  equally necessary)" (p. 187).
- **The sewer case, per Evans.** Trunk lines were "beyond the collective
  efforts of local neighborhoods", and the whole network beyond the
  government's budget, so each side built a part. Officials' start-up
  effort and, after construction, "monitoring and maintenance required
  continuous face-to-face interaction" (pp. 187–188).
- **The Nigerian case, per Evans.** "To begin with, there is no
  complementarity", and the bureaucracy applies rules "which leave no room
  for initiative" (p. 195); Ostrom's "signals problem": "Public sectors
  typically rely on incentive systems that send very weak signals about
  performance to staff" (p. 196, quoting Ostrom).
- **Transplantation, per Evans.** "Ostrom notes, for example, that the
  effectiveness of condominium sewer systems is not confined to Northeast
  Brazil but has transplanted well to Kenya, Paraguay, and Indonesia"
  (p. 203). Neither neighbour shows it.
- **The sewers in the field (Watson 1995, not Ostrom).** Devolving feeder
  upkeep to residents "has not worked well even in the best cases"; the
  systems lasted where agencies stayed in contact ([LIT-2190](LIT-2190.md),
  [NOTE-1659](../notes.d/NOTE-1659.md)). Whether Ostrom drew on Watson is not verified.

## Standing in the record

Unfetched. Two neighbours were read in its place, and each stands in
for part of it only.

- **Evans (1996)**, [LIT-2143](LIT-2143.md), the special section's synthesis, stands
  in only for what Evans says of this article: his summary of its two
  cases, of its production functions and of its signals problem, in his
  framing of complementarity and embeddedness. It carries no numbers from
  the article.
- **Watson (1995)**, [LIT-2190](LIT-2190.md), stands in only for the sewer case's
  field data: seven Brazilian cities, costs, connection rates and the
  history of feeder upkeep.
- **Neither stands in** for Ostrom's own definition of coproduction, for
  her formal conditions (the production-function figures) or for the
  detail of the Nigerian case. The candidate account attributed to her
  (coproduction by citizens and officials outside one organisation beats
  either alone when their inputs are complementary, illustrated by the
  sewers and contrasted with Nigeria) is not filed as a THEORY until the
  article, or its 1997 GAIA reprint, is read.

It bears on the branch's argument as a case of support not meant to be
withdrawn: per Evans, the official side of coproduction is a standing
input, and the transient part is the start-up organising effort. That
qualifies [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) and supports [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md); see the two NOTEs.
