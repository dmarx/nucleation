# QUESTION-377: Can adding, removing, or reassigning organizational constraints cause a transition in the dimension or connectivity of the organization's feasible response space?

## Why it is a question

A: "A sequence of small architectural modifications may change the
number of available modes only modestly until a critical structural
threshold is crossed." The mechanical cases are real.

- **Kirigami.** Chen, Choi and Mahadevan ([NOTE-1574](../notes.d/NOTE-1574.md)) control the
  connectivity and the degrees of freedom of a cut sheet by which links
  remain, deterministically and at random, and find percolation-like
  transitions.
- **Origami facets.** Li and Choi ([NOTE-1564](../notes.d/NOTE-1564.md)) enforce or relax
  planarity on selected facets and find critical densities at which the
  structure's rigidity changes, depending on geometry, connectivity and
  the rule that picks the facets.

The organizational version would ask whether a few added, removed or
reassigned rules, roles or authorities leave the space of feasible
responses nearly unchanged until a threshold, then change its dimension
or split or join its components. A: "We'd need to specify an
organizational model to make this testable."

## What would answer it

A model of an organization whose feasible responses form a constraint
set, with a parameter for the density or placement of its constraints,
in which the dimension or the connected components of that set can be
computed as the parameter moves. A threshold would show as a jump in
either; a smooth change throughout would answer it the other way for that
model.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the resource-bounded realizability readings

The reading of Gopalan et al. ([NOTE-1584](../notes.d/NOTE-1584.md)), filed beside the pasted synthesis on resource-bounded realizability, gives a partial answer in the Boolean setting, by analogy only. Adding one relation type that is not tight to a tight language turns st-connectivity of the solution space from polynomial to PSPACE-complete (Theorem 2.9) and allows components of exponential diameter (Theorem 2.10). Schwerdtfeger's correction ([NOTE-1613](../notes.d/NOTE-1613.md)) restates the boundary for safely tight languages, and the hard side only grows. The jump comes from a change of constraint vocabulary, not of one instance, and it is in the complexity of connectivity, not in the dimension of a feasible set. The organizational model the question asks for is still missing. No status changes.
