# THEORY-014: In both formalisms of contextuality, classicality is the existence of a nonnegative version of a linear representation that always exists over the reals, so negativity is universal and only its unavoidability is nonclassical

## Source

Abramsky & Brandenburger (2011), [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md), Thms 5.4, 5.9, 8.1. Schmid et al. (2020), [LIT-003](../literature.d/LIT-003.md), Thm 2.8, Cor 3.5, Prop 4.4. [NOTE-017](../notes.d/NOTE-017.md) records that [LIT-003](../literature.d/LIT-003.md) attempts no formal comparison with [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md).

## What was actually shown

In the sheaf formalism every no-signalling model has a real signed global section, and noncontextuality is the existence of a nonnegative one ([LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md) Thms 5.4, 5.9, 8.1). In the GPT formalism every tomographically local GPT has a real-linear diagram-preserving representation, and generalized noncontextuality is the existence of a positive quasiprobabilistic one ([LIT-003](../literature.d/LIT-003.md) Thm 2.8, Cor 3.5, Prop 4.4). Both halves are proved in works this record has read. The shared shape — a linear representation that always exists, with classicality as its positivity — is the record's inference. Contextuality-by-Default gives a third instance, and one that does not need no-signalling: for every finite system of categorical random variables, signalling or not, a signed maximally connected quasi-coupling always exists (LIT-tmpn4n31, Theorem 6.1), and noncontextuality is the existence of a nonnegative one (Theorem 4.1; NOTE-tmpybbbx).

## What this does not say

- That the two representations are the same object. The sheaf section lives on global outcome assignments for one empirical model; the GPT representation lives on the state and effect spaces of a whole theory.
- That a sheaf-noncontextual empirical model yields a diagram-preserving ontological model of the corresponding GPT fragment, or the reverse.
- Anything beyond finite, tomographically local settings.
