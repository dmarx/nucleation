# LIT-tmph9a27: Changing Norms to Change Behavior

Miller, D. T., & Prentice, D. A. (2016). *Annual Review of Psychology*, 67,
339–361. DOI-10.1146/annurev-psych-010814-015013

Not read. What follows is taken from the abstract in OpenAlex's record and
has not been checked against the paper.

## Key takeaways

*From the abstract only.*

- **Scope.** A review of norm-based interventions: giving people
  information about the behaviour and attitudes of their peers, to reduce
  behaviour harmful to individuals (high alcohol consumption) or to the
  collective (high energy consumption).
- **Focus.** "Special attention to interventions designed to decrease
  college students' drinking and increase environment-friendly
  behaviors."
- **Claim as stated.** It identifies "the conditions under which norm
  information has the highest likelihood of changing the targeted
  behavior" and discusses why.

## Why this is here

CLAIM-415 holds that a person can be organized by a judgement they
attribute to a collective that does not hold it. Its present support is
Prentice and Miller's study of pluralistic ignorance (LIT-1370). This
review, by the same authors twenty years on, should say whether the
interventions that correct such a misperception work, and under which
conditions they do not, which bears on how durable the attributed
judgement is.
