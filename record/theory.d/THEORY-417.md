# THEORY-417: 'Personal identity' names several largely independent questions, among them who one is (characterization), what a person is, what it takes for one to persist, how many of us there are and what matters in survival; the persistence question concerns numerical identity, which 'remaining the same person' in the characterization sense presupposes, and posing it as 'a person at one time and a person at another' assumes that we are persons essentially

## Source

Olson (2002; substantive revision 30 June 2023), "Personal Identity",
*Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, [LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md), read in [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md).

## What was actually shown

**Seven questions (§1).**

- *Characterization*: "Who am I?" in the identity-crisis sense, a small,
  contingent and changeable subset of one's properties. The term is
  Schechtman's (1996: 1).
- *Personhood*: what it is to be a person, with Locke's "thinking
  intelligent being" the commonest answer.
- *Persistence*: what is necessary and sufficient for a past or future
  being to be someone existing now.
- *Evidence*: how we tell, memory against physical continuity.
- *Population*: how many of us there are now, raised by split brains,
  multiple personality and partial twinning.
- *Personal ontology*: what we are made of and what kind of thing we are,
  with seven candidate answers and "no consensus or even a dominant view".
- *What matters in survival*.

Discussions, the entry says, often leave unclear which one is at stake.

**The persistence question is about numerical identity (§2).** "Remaining
the same person" in the characterization sense "presupposes" numerical
identity, and concerns character. The persistence question is not about
the meaning of words, which would make its answer a priori and the same
for all possible people.

**Its usual framing begs a question (§2).** Asking what makes "a person at
one time and a person at another" the same assumes person essentialism.
With a Lockean definition of person, that implies you were never an embryo
and could not be in a vegetative state, and a memory criterion so framed
is "compatible with your surviving with no memory continuity at all, as
long as this happens when you are not a person".

**"Self" is set aside.** The word "is often used without any clear meaning
and will be avoided here".

**What could have come out otherwise.** "Remaining the same person" could
have been the persistence question in other words. It is not: it asks
whether a being keeps its character, which presupposes that it is
numerically the same being (§2).

## What this does not say

- **Which answer to the persistence question is right.** The entry lays out
  psychological-continuity views, brute-physical views and
  anticriterialism. It argues for animalism in §§6–7, and [NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md) rates
  the too-many-thinkers objection moderate and the reply to the transplant
  case weak to moderate. That argument is not part of this account.
- **That the characterization question has no metaphysics.** The entry
  places it outside persistence and so treats it as psychology. Schechtman
  ([LIT-468](../literature.d/LIT-468.md), Unavailable) holds the reverse, and her publisher says she "takes
  issue with analytic philosophy's emphasis on the first sort of question
  … to the exclusion of the second". The record has her only through other
  readings.
- **Anything about the self.** The narrative self, the minimal self and
  self-models are outside the entry by decision. It is a map of persons,
  not of selves, which is the separation [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md) keeps.
- **That the citations are clean.** §2 cites "Olson and Witt 2020"; the
  bibliography's only such entry is 2019 ([NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md), Limitations).

## Connections

- **Which question the record's works answer** ([NOTE-374](../notes.d/NOTE-374.md), Bearing on the
  record). Levin ([LIT-439](../literature.d/LIT-439.md)) and Clark and Chalmers ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)) answer the
  personal-ontology question about boundaries. Dennett's narrative self
  ([THEORY-691](THEORY-691.md)) answers the characterization and population questions.
  None of them answers the persistence question. Locke's criterion
  ([THEORY-546](THEORY-546.md)) and Lewis's stages ([THEORY-476](THEORY-476.md)) do; Parfit
  ([THEORY-447](THEORY-447.md)) and Dufner ([THEORY-464](THEORY-464.md)) are about what matters.
- **[THEORY-060](THEORY-060.md).** A life story as "the form a person's diachronic identity
  takes" is an answer to the characterization question, and [QUESTION-043](../questions.d/QUESTION-043.md)
  notes that it makes no claim about numerical identity. The map says why
  those are different claims.
- **Floridi** ([THEORY-650](THEORY-650.md)) holds that persistence questions are
  answerable only relative to a purpose. The entry rejects the related view
  that the persistence question is settled by conceptual means.
- **The population question** is where Dennett counts two selves in one
  body by interpretation, and where Meincke's pregnancy ([THEORY-448](THEORY-448.md))
  gives a count between one and two. The entry lists partial twinning as
  one of its cases.
