# NOTE-115: Meincke — A process view of pregnancy

## Contribution

Meincke asks how many individuals are present where there is a pregnant individual. Within substance ontology only two answers are available: two, on the dominant Containment View (the foetus sits in the gestating organism as in a container), or one, on Kingma's Parthood View (the foetus is a part of the gestating organism). She proposes a third: a pregnant individual is neither one nor two but something in between, because organisms are better understood as processes than as substances. Focusing on the Parthood View, she argues that a Process View, on which the pregnant individual is a bifurcating hypercomplex process, does better than either substance-based view.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction: separates the parthood/containment question from the "how many individuals" question, noting they are logically independent though usually paired.
- "The Containment View": traces it to Aristotelian hylomorphism, on which the foetus ("foster") is a substance from conception or about 14–16 days after.
- "Are fosters (substances that are) parts of substances?": argues the Parthood View either wrongly treats fosters like organs or, in its "Substances-as-Parts" version, contradicts itself when it tries to secure the foster–neonate identity.
- "Pregnant organisms: asymmetrically bifurcating hypercomplex processes": the gestating organism is a higher-level autopoietic process that maintains, and gradually disentangles from, a nested process of the same kind.
- "One or two?": uses the analogy of a river splitting — the count has no natural-number answer, and asymmetric dependence does not entail numerical oneness.
- Conclusions: this vagueness follows naturally from organisms' processual constitution, whereas substances impose a binary count.

## Open questions

- Check whether "something in between one and two" is ontic vagueness or indeterminacy of count, and how it squares with the vagueness options in the SEP mereology entry (§5).
- The view has ethical implications around pregnancy that the paper largely sets aside; any use should report them neutrally and as the author's own.
