# THEORY-tmp4utz4: The NTSB found the Tempe crash of an Uber test vehicle caused by its operator's distraction, to which the company contributed through a system design that relied on her to brake, the removal of a second operator, rare oversight and no countermeasures to automation complacency, all consequences of its safety culture, so that cause was divided between the operator and the organization that set her task

NTSB, Highway Accident Report HAR-19/03 (2019), [LIT-tmpmlje2](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmlje2.md), read in
[NOTE-tmpokcvk](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpokcvk.md).

## The finding

Probable cause: the operator's failure to monitor, "because she was
visually distracted throughout the trip by her personal cell phone".
Contributing: Uber ATG's "(1) inadequate safety risk assessment
procedures, (2) ineffective oversight of vehicle operators, and (3) lack of
adequate mechanisms for addressing operators' automation complacency—all a
consequence of its inadequate safety culture"; further, the pedestrian's
crossing and Arizona's oversight (p. v; p. 59). The design relied "on the
operator's intervention" to brake (p. v), and the culture contributed
"specifically to the vehicle operator's extended distraction" (p. 46).

## What it does not show

- **Blame.** The NTSB "does not assign fault or blame".
- **Where blame landed** in prosecution or the press, which the report
  does not cover.
