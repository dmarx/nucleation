# THEORY-1471: Banerjee, Hanna, Kreindler and Olken find, re-analysing seven randomized evaluations of government-run cash transfer programs in six countries, no systematic effect of the transfers on whether adults work or how many hours they work, for men or for women: pooled, a transfer worth 13.6% of household consumption changes participation by −0.4 points (90% interval −2.3 to +1.4) and weekly hours by about −5 minutes (−1.7 to +1.4 hours), ruling out moderate reductions for men while leaving a wide range for women

## Source

Banerjee, Hanna, Kreindler & Olken (2017), [LIT-2186](../literature.d/LIT-2186.md); read in [NOTE-1685](../notes.d/NOTE-1685.md).
Page numbers are the journal's.

## What was actually shown

- **Seven trials, one definition.** Household micro-data from seven
  cluster-randomized evaluations (Honduras, Morocco, Mexico twice,
  Philippines, Indonesia, Nicaragua), adults 16–65, work in the last week
  and weekly hours with non-workers at zero (pp. 157, 163–165).
- **Each programme** (Table 3, p. 170): no significant effect on hours in
  any; on participation only in Honduras, −0.0295 at the 10% level, which
  "is roughly what we may expect by pure chance" (p. 169).
- **Pooled** (Table 6, p. 173): scaled to 13.6% of consumption,
  participation −0.004 (5th–95th percentile −0.023 to +0.014) on a base of
  0.56; hours −0.077 (−1.734 to +1.356) on 21.1; men +0.001 and +0.470,
  with lower bounds of −0.020 and −1.702; women −0.008 (−0.039, +0.024)
  and −0.430 (−2.588, +1.611).
- **What could have come out otherwise.** A transfer that bought leisure
  would have shown up as a negative pooled effect with an interval clear
  of zero; the data rule out a fall in participation larger than 2.3
  points and in hours larger than 1 hour 42 minutes a week, at the
  one-sided 95% level.

## What this does not say

- That support never breeds dependence: support continued throughout,
  and nothing was measured after it stopped.
- That larger transfers, or transfers withdrawn steeply as earnings rise,
  leave work unchanged. The authors' own account (pp. 174–175) is that low
  implicit tax rates are one reason for the null.
- That women's work is unaffected: their interval is wide.
- That aid to states leaves their own effort unchanged. The finding is
  about households, and it bears against carrying the Samaritan's-dilemma
  logic of [THEORY-1480](THEORY-1480.md) and [CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md), or the "slack effort" route
  of [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md), down to them, not against state-level substitution.
- That transfers raise work: the gains from asset and graduation
  programmes are cited, not shown here.
