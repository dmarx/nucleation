# ARG-200: Gelfert and Nolting's report of Cassi's theorem, with Nachtergaele's proof and Halperin's limits, read as sources, shows that the spectral-dimension criterion of the claim about a representation's long-wavelength fluctuations is a theorem for spin models with a continuous symmetry in equilibrium, not Mermin and Wagner's own result, and holds only under conditions a trained representation is not shown to meet

## The inference

[CLAIM-842](../claims.d/CLAIM-842.md)'s criterion (d_s ≤ 2 gives diverging fluctuations, so no
conventional order) is offered as Mermin–Wagner read on a graph. The
readings, as sources, would say whether such a theorem exists and on
what conditions.

## Critical questions (source)

**Does the work say this, read in context?** Gelfert and Nolting report
that Cassi, applying Hattori et al.'s Gaussian field theory on general
networks to "fractal and disordered lattices whose spectral dimension is
less or equal to 2", shows that "at finite temperature no spontaneous
magnetization can exist for the classical O(n) and the ferromagnetic
(quantum) Heisenberg model", owing to "the infrared divergencies
displayed by the corresponding Gaussian models" (§3.4). And Hattori et
al.'s premise, "the only geometrical structure that spins can feel is the
one given by the Hamiltonian", is A29's point that the governing
dimension is the interaction graph's. Nachtergaele: "Translation
invariance is not required" (p. 5). So the graph version is a theorem,
Cassi's, not Mermin and Wagner's.

**Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** Not for a learned
representation. Halperin's conditions: a continuous symmetry whose
generator is a sum of local operators (orientational order escapes
because "the generator of these rotations cannot be written as an
unweighted sum of local operators"); finite second moments, which long
edges on an interaction graph could violate; and equilibrium, the
inequality following "from the quantum version of the fluctuation
dissipation theorem". [CLAIM-842](../claims.d/CLAIM-842.md)'s own caveats (no identified symmetry,
no equilibrium) are exactly these. And a Gaussian divergence can be
removed: in tethered membranes nonlinear coupling "removes the divergence"
(Halperin §II), and in Toner and Tu's flock the linear theory's d = 2
logarithm is removed by non-equilibrium convective terms ([THEORY-2199](../theory.d/THEORY-2199.md)).
At d_s = 2 exactly the XY case shows that what survives can be algebraic
order, set by defects no Gaussian energy contains (Drouin-Touchette,
[NOTE-2295](../notes.d/NOTE-2295.md)).

**Is the source in a position to know?** Gelfert and Nolting are a
review, and Cassi and Hattori et al. are known only through two of their
paragraphs; both are registered ([LIT-3061](../literature.d/LIT-3061.md), [LIT-3051](../literature.d/LIT-3051.md)), unread.
