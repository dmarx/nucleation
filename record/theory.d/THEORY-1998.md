# THEORY-1998: Cook holds that accidents in complex systems have several contributors and no isolable root cause, that attributing them to one reflects a social need to blame localized forces, that organizations leave the trade-off between production and risk ambiguous for practitioners at the sharp end to resolve, and that the practitioners' continuous defence against failure is seen chiefly after it lapses

Cook (1998; Revision D, 2000), [LIT-2783](../literature.d/LIT-2783.md), read in [NOTE-2083](../notes.d/NOTE-2083.md).

## The account

"there is no isolated 'cause' of an accident"; root-cause evaluation
reflects "the social, cultural need to blame specific, localized forces or
events for outcomes" (point 7). "Organizations are ambiguous, often
intentionally" about production and acceptable risk, and "All ambiguity is
resolved by actions of practitioners at the sharp end" (point 11). The
system functions "because people can make it function, despite the
presence of many flaws" (point 5); "In non-accident filled times, the
production role is emphasized. After accidents, the defense against
failure role is emphasized" (point 9).

## Bearing

- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: corroborates its control-and-duty part. The organization
  leaves the trade unresolved and the sharp end carries it; hindsight then
  calls the result error. It does not show where blame settles in fact.
- **[CLAIM-702](../claims.d/CLAIM-702.md)**: corroborates the visibility half: defending work is
  constant and seen chiefly when it lapses. Nothing on credit.
- **[QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md)**: bears on. If attribution by blame seeks localized
  causes, blame is a doubtful carrier of upstream provenance, which is the
  premise blameless review starts from. Cook does not show that tracing
  without blame does better.

## What it does not show

Anything by data. The treatise argues from experience in medicine and
other hazardous systems.
