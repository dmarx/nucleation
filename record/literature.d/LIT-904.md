# LIT-904: Preliminaries to a theory of action with reference to vision

M. T. Turvey (1977), "Preliminaries to a theory of action with reference to vision", in R. Shaw and J. Bransford (eds.), *Perceiving, Acting and Knowing: Toward an Ecological Psychology*, Hillsdale, NJ: Erlbaum (reprinted Routledge 2017) — DOI-10.4324/9781315467931. Preprint: Haskins Laboratories Status Report on Speech Research SR-41 (1975), pp. 1–49 (ERIC ED109722).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-826](../notes.d/NOTE-826.md).*

- **No keyboard executive.** The same impulses to a muscle give different
  movements in different states and loads, and the controller has far
  fewer degrees of freedom than the limbs, so muscles cannot be commanded
  one by one.
- **Coordinative structures.** "Reflexes and functional combinations of
  reflexes", relatively autonomous units; "a term borrowed from Easton
  (1972a) but used here with greater latitude" (p. 8).
- **Ballparks.** After Greene, the executive specifies equivalence classes
  of states, not states, and is ignorant of how lower levels realize them.
- **Tuning.** Higher levels set the gain, equilibrium and damping of
  self-regulating units. Reflex probes show the spinal segmental apparatus
  retuned, specifically and nonspecifically, in the 30–100 msec before a
  voluntary movement; Fel'dman's joint goes to a preset equilibrium
  whatever its initial conditions.
- **Vision.** Changing optical properties control locomotion and may set
  tunings directly; unchanging ones (affordances) select action plans.
  Argued, not measured.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-11 with the coordinative-structure readings, as the usual
source credited with the term. Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-826](../notes.d/NOTE-826.md), in the

The reading corrects the credit: Turvey borrows the term from Easton and
widens it from reflexes to functional combinations of reflexes. In his
sense a coordinative structure lies within one body, so its use for the
dancing couple ([CLAIM-175](../claims.d/CLAIM-175.md), [CASE-046](../cases.d/CASE-046.md)) would be a further widening by the
record. Reciprocal compensation, which [CASE-046](../cases.d/CASE-046.md) calls the signature of a
coordinative structure, is Kelso et al. ([LIT-902](LIT-902.md)), not this essay. Filed
from the reading: [THEORY-302](../theory.d/THEORY-302.md), control by tuning of autonomous units.
