# CLAIM-tmpcbm69: Whether a distinction survived is fixed by what the receivers can recover, and whether it can be shown to have survived is fixed by what the analyst's probes can identify, so the manuscript's thesis and the October fidelity clause, read so, answer different parts of one question

## The claim

This is the record's reply of 2026-10-10 to [CLAIM-tmp867uz](CLAIM-tmp867uz.md). It reconciles,
and it is a proposal: a reading of the October thesis that its words allow
and do not state.

[CLAIM-tmp867uz](CLAIM-tmp867uz.md) is right about [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s literal wording. [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) says
what survives is what "a receiving system can still reconstruct and act
upon"; [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s fidelity clause says "Fidelity concerns which
independently identifiable, decision-relevant relational distinctions
persist". On [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md)'s two cases the two give opposite verdicts, so read as
two answers to one part of [QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md) they compete.

There is another reading, and [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md) points to it: "Identifiability is
what makes a fidelity measure non-arbitrary". On that reading the two
conditions do different jobs.

- **Survival** is fixed by the receivers: a distinction survived a rendering
  for a population if that population can recover it from the rendering.
  That is [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s condition.
- **Certification** is fixed by the analyst: whether the distinction can be
  *shown* to have survived depends on whether the analyst's probes identify
  it. That is what "independently identifiable" adds, read as a condition on
  evidence.

Read so, [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) answers what survived, and [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s fidelity clause
answers what the analyst can show survived. These are different parts of one
question, which is what luria.yaml's blurb for `complements` asks: "answers a
different part of the same question, so the two belong together rather than
competing".

**[CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md)'s two cases, with verdicts.**

- *Identifiable but unusable.* The analyst's probes identify the
  distinction, and a receiver who has only the target text cannot recover
  it. Verdict: it did not survive for those receivers. The analyst can show
  that the distinction is in the rendering's structure, and can show that
  these receivers do not recover it; what the analyst cannot do is count it
  as surviving for them.
- *Usable but unidentifiable.* A receiver with side information recovers the
  distinction, and the analyst's probes cannot identify it. Verdict: it
  survived, for that receiver, and the analyst has no evidence of it. The
  fidelity measure is silent, not negative.

Neither case now has the two theses disagreeing about a fact. In the first
they agree that the analyst can identify something the receiver lacks; in the
second, that the receiver has something the analyst cannot certify.

## What it answers

[CLAIM-tmp867uz](CLAIM-tmp867uz.md)'s point that `complements` asserts the opposite of what the
theses say. Read as above, the relation is right on content, not only on the
ground of drafts that [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s summary gave. Whether to keep the relation,
and whether [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) should also list [QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md) in `answers`, is the
owner's decision; [CLAIM-tmp867uz](CLAIM-tmp867uz.md) stays Active as a statement about [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)
as written.

## What it does not say

- It does not say A218 meant this. A218 does not say which role
  "independently identifiable" plays. If A218 uses it to exclude recovered
  distinctions from what persists, the reading is not available, and that is
  the first half of the defeat condition.
- It does not say the literal reading is untenable. It says another reading
  is available, and that on it the two theses fit together.
- It does not say the receiver-relative condition is settled. Which receivers
  is [QUESTION-tmppstva](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmppstva.md)'s, and [CLAIM-074](CLAIM-074.md) holds that fidelity differs across
  them.
