# THEORY-tmp52xrv: Choroszewicz finds, from 2.5 years of ethnography of generative-AI tool trials in two Finnish public organizations, that test users were positioned as moral crumple zones: held responsible for the quality, verification and appropriate use of outputs from immature, opaque tools they could not shape, with poor outputs framed as user error, through five mechanisms: oversight and liability taken for granted, immature tools marketed as sparring buddies, trial-and-error learning offloaded to users, limited transparency, and a burden of vigilance against persuasive but fallible outputs

## Source

Choroszewicz (2026), LIT-tmpj3pjx, read in full; the reading is NOTE-tmpwcnyu.

## What was actually shown

Ethnographic observation (476 h, 109 interviews) of three tool trials. In them, "human oversight and
responsibility as liability were explicitly codified as a user burden" (p. 6), and "flawed and
low-quality outputs tended to be portrayed as the result of poor user interaction rather than
issues with the tool's design and development" (p. 6). Users' prolonged engagement could "function
as users' invisible work to compensate for the limitations of AI tools" (p. 6). Tools meant to save
time "instead generated additional workload" (p. 9).

Corroborates CLAIM-tmpnfjln in its allocation clause and, weakly, its asymmetric attribution: the
tools' promise was protected and poor outputs were put down to users. It does not corroborate the
claim's blame-after-visible-failure clause.

## What this does not say

- Nothing about blame after a harm: no failure with consequences and no sanction of a worker was
  observed. The positioning is prospective.
- Not that the pattern is uniform: the commercial tool's trial was better organized than the
  internal tools', and the author grants that the MCZ lens "may nevertheless oversimplify" (p. 10).
- Not about deployed systems, only early trials.
