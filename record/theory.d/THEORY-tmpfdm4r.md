# THEORY-tmpfdm4r: Dannenberg and colleagues show, in a thermodynamically self-consistent domain-level kinetic model of planar DNA origami, that cooperativity between staples from scaffold loop closure and coaxial stacking sharpens the folding transition and produces folding/melting hysteresis, and that the hysteresis is asymmetric, faster cooling or fewer staples lowering the annealing temperature far more than they raise or move the melting temperature, because binding rates are nearly temperature-independent while unbinding rates rise steeply with temperature

## Source

Dannenberg, Dunn, Bath, Kwiatkowska, Turberfield and Ouldridge (2015),
LIT-tmpdyrnb; read in NOTE-tmpyo994.

## What was actually shown

- **No cooperativity, no hysteresis** (Sec. III A): with γ = 1.5 and no
  stacking, annealing and melting curves nearly overlap, against the
  significant hysteresis of experiment.
- **Cooperativity widens it** (Table I): at γ = 2.5, stacking strength
  0 to 3 raises Tm − Ta from 0.4 to 2.5 °C and narrows the annealing width
  from 8.6 to 4.2 °C; removing the state-dependent loop term cuts it again
  (Table IV).
- **Asymmetry** (Sec. III D): 10 against 0.1 °C per minute lowers Ta by
  5.3 °C and raises Tm by about 1.3 °C; 2 against 20 nM staples lowers Ta
  substantially and leaves melting largely unchanged.
- **Mechanism** (Sec. II B): binding rates essentially constant, unbinding
  up about an order of magnitude per 2 °C.
- **Could have come out otherwise**: cooperativity could have shifted both
  transitions alike; the model separates them, and matches the scale of
  hysteresis in the authors' fluorescence data.

## What this does not say

- That the hysteresis reflects alternative stable states: it is kinetic,
  and shrinks with slower ramps.
- Anything about misfolds or traps, which the model cannot represent.
- That the parameters are measured: γ and n were chosen, not fitted.
- That it holds beyond planar designs with staples of at most two domains.
