## Neighbour N1: Mossio & Moreno (2010)

**Provenance.** "Organisational closure in biological organisms", Matteo
Mossio and Alvaro Moreno, *History and Philosophy of the Life Sciences*
32(2-3):269-288 (2010). HAL halshs-00792436, file
Mossio_Moreno_organisms_HPLS_FinalVersion.pdf, deposited 2018-09-19, HAL
licence "hal-authorisation-v1"; 16 pp., HTTP 200, 2026-11-22. Authors' final
version, not the journal's typeset text; pagination is the deposit's. Read in
full; references skimmed.

**Summary.** Self-maintenance (a constraint that helps maintain its own
boundary conditions) is common in physics; a flame has it. Organisms have a
stronger kind, organisational closure: a set of constraints each of which
maintains the boundary conditions of another that in turn maintains it.
Closure is necessary for organisms but underdetermines them; templates and
adaptive control are also needed.

**Key results.**
1. pdf p. 2: "whereas all organisms are a kind of FFE self-maintaining
   systems, not all FFE self-maintaining systems are organisms".
2. pdf p. 5: the flame keeps temperature above threshold, vaporizes wax and
   induces convection, so "flames can be said to be a physical example of a
   self-maintaining system".
3. pdf p. 5: "self-maintenance provides a naturalized grounding for some of
   the properties usually considered as being proper to biological systems,
   namely their teleology and normativity"; but pdf p. 6: "since in physical
   or chemical selfmaintaining systems one can hardly distinguish
   organizationally differentiated parts, the present claim is rather
   theoretical than operational."
4. pdf p. 7: "dissipative structures realize a minimal form of
   self-maintenance, in the sense of depending on the work of a single
   constraint acting on its own boundary conditions"; "The mutual dependence
   between a set of constraints is what we call an organisational closure."
5. pdf p. 7, the working definition: closed if constituted by constraints
   C1...Cn such that for each Ci some boundary conditions of its maintenance
   are determined by the immediate action of another Cj, "whose maintenance
   depends in turn on Ci as an immediate constraint". Three features (pdf pp.
   7-8): closure is partial ("organisational closure cannot be but partial");
   it holds among constraints, not processes (two billiard balls: "a causal
   cycle, but not an organisational closure"); dependence must be direct.
6. pdf p. 9: river and bed: "there is a single constraint, and then, no
   organisational closure"; the water cycle likewise.
7. pdf p. 12: a varied flame component changes nothing else, so "the flame
   will keep behaving in the same way in spite of various possible
   modifications of its components"; closure, unlike this, can grow
   complexity.
8. pdf p. 13: closure "underdetermines, in itself, the whole set of
   requirements" of an organism; adaptive control requires that "control
   relationships can be established only between modules whose
   characteristic time scales are sufficiently different".

**Assumptions.** That a constraint must be "an emerging property" of a
configuration to count (river, water cycle); that the flame's constraint is
one (the flame as a whole).

**Concepts.** far-from-equilibrium (FFE) self-maintenance; constraint;
work-constraint cycle (Kauffman); organisational closure; partial closure;
causal cycle vs closure; organisational self-maintenance vs biological
autonomy; dynamical decoupling.

**Limitations and gaps.** The flame is treated as a single constraint without
analysis: the wick and the molten wax pool, the candidates THEORY-072 names,
are not considered. The test for whether a structure is a constraint
("emerging" or "configurational" property) is asserted case by case (pdf p.
9), not given. Time scales appear only for control, not in the definition of
closure. Teleology is grounded in self-maintenance (result 3), which a flame
has, so the paper's own grounding does not yet exclude the flame; the book's
Introduction moves the grounding to closure (NOTE-1437, p. xxix).

## Neighbour N2: Bich, Mossio, Ruiz-Mirazo & Moreno (2016)

**Provenance.** "Biological regulation: controlling the system from within",
Leonardo Bich, Matteo Mossio, Kepa Ruiz-Mirazo and Alvaro Moreno, *Biology &
Philosophy* 31:237-265, DOI 10.1007/s10539-015-9497-8 (online 2015). HAL
hal-01185296, Regulation_web.pdf, CC BY-NC 4.0 (HAL cover), deposited
2015-08-19; 28 pp., HTTP 200, 2026-11-22. The authors' version ("The final
publication is available at Springer", pdf p. 2). Read in full: §§1-3, 5, 7
closely; §§4 and 6 (feedback; lac operon) at their conclusions; references
skipped.

**Summary.** Regulation is second-order control: a subsystem produced by the
organism but dynamically decoupled from its constitutive regime, triggered by
perturbations, that switches the system between viable regimes. It differs
from dynamic stability (distributed network compensation, feedback,
homeostasis), which belongs to the constitutive regime itself, the closure.

**Key results.**
1. pdf p. 2: "regulation consists in a specific form of second-order control,
   exerted over the core (constitutive) regime of production and maintenance
   of the components".
2. pdf p. 9, constraint defined: C constrains process P if "at a time scale
   characteristic of P, C is locally unaffected by P" and at that scale has a
   causal role on P (adapted from Mossio et al. 2013).
3. pdf p. 10: in organisms "biological constraints realise a circular
   organisation", the constitutive regime, citing Moreno & Mossio (2015).
4. pdf p. 14 (fn. 13): "feedback realises a circularity of processes, and it
   depends, in its basic instances, on the action of only one constraint
   (whose existence does not require that circularity). Organisational
   closure, instead, consists in a circular generative relation among
   constraints, and implies the mutual dependence of several constraints".
5. pdf pp. 17-18, requirements for regulation: R is produced by C; R is
   dynamically decoupled, operating "at a different dynamical scale and under
   different stoichiometric requirements than C"; R is triggered by
   perturbations; R shifts C between regimes; the new regimes cope.
6. pdf p. 23: "This special type of control, which we label regulation,
   cannot be accounted for by other forms of compensation, like distributed
   network responses or simple feedback loops."
7. pdf p. 6: regulation could be studied at many levels, "from proto-cells
   to multicellular systems, or even at the ecological sphere"; the paper
   restricts itself to prokaryotes.
8. pdf p. 24: decoupling lets regulation scale and so supports increase in
   complexity, which a distributed network cannot (paraphrase).

**Assumptions.** Closure as the constitutive regime is taken from the book and
Montévil & Mossio (2015); bacterial chemotaxis and the lac operon are taken as
minimal regulation.

**Concepts.** control (kinetic, spatial); constitutive regime; dynamic
stability; regulation; dynamical decoupling; stoichiometric freedom; robustness
vs homeostasis.

**Limitations and gaps.** "Dynamically decoupled" is defined by distinct rates
and stoichiometric freedom, with no measure of how distinct is enough. It is
not said whether ecosystems or colonies meet the five requirements (result 7
leaves it open), so the paper does not itself answer Baedke.

## How far they stand in

- For chapter 1 (closure): N1 is the authors' earlier statement; N2 gives the
  constraint definition with time scales that the book and LIT-582 use. Both
  restate the core claim; neither shows the chapter's own arguments.
- For chapter 3 (teleology): N1 only in passing (result 3). LIT-570 and
  LIT-1865, held, are better stand-ins.
- For chapter 6 (levels): N2 supplies what organisms add to closure
  (regulation), which is how the Introduction separates organisms from
  ecosystems ("only the requirements for closure", p. xxxi). It does not
  apply this to ecosystems. No stand-in for chapter 6 was reached.

## Bearing on named codes

- **THEORY-072** (Proposed). *Bears, mostly corroborates its reading of where
  the line falls, and answers part of its question.*
  (a) The flame: N1 places it as single-constraint self-maintenance (N1
  results 2, 4, 7), the condition CLAIM-395 formalizes. This is the authors'
  verdict, not the analysis the promote_when asks for: wick and wax pool are
  not examined (N1 limitations), so the first half of the promote_when stays
  undone.
  (b) "Differentiation as a separate condition or folded into closure":
  across N1 and N2 the authors fold the multiplicity of constraints into
  closure (several mutually dependent constraints, N1 result 4; N2 result 4),
  but they keep **differentiation at distinct time scales between
  subsystems** as a separate, further condition: control in N1 (result 8)
  and regulation in N2 (result 5). The time scale inside closure is only the
  one that makes something a constraint relative to a process (N2 result 2).
  THEORY-072's phrase "constraints that produce one another at different time
  scales" thus mixes the two: the authors' closure needs the constraint/process
  time-scale gap, and their organism needs a further gap between regulator
  and regulated. Worth a dated note on THEORY-072.
- **CLAIM-395** (Active): consistent; N2 result 4 is the authors saying a
  single-constraint loop is feedback, not closure.
- **LIT-167 / NOTE-148** (Baedke; closure too permissive): N1 concedes closure
  is necessary, not sufficient (result 8), and N2 names what must be added.
  Whether colonies or ecosystems have it is not argued: Baedke's objection is
  met only by assertion so far (the Introduction's p. xxxi).
- **NOTE-1443 / LIT-1865** (Mossio & Bich): N1 is the earlier source of the
  river-bed and water-cycle arguments NOTE-1443 records, with the same
  verdict.
- **LIT-120 / NOTE-093** (Bruckner's "Tessler" car, bacteria, flame checked
  against closure): N1's working definition (result 5) and N2's regulation
  requirements are the two checks to run.

## Candidate THEORY sentences

- N1: Mossio and Moreno (2010) hold that a flame maintains itself through a
  single constraint acting on its own boundary conditions, while an organism
  maintains itself through organisational closure, the direct mutual
  dependence of several constraints; closure is necessary for an organism
  but not sufficient, which also needs templates and control between parts
  working at different time scales.
  `promote_when`: the working definition (pdf p. 7) applied to a candle with
  wick and wax pool listed as candidate constraints, reaching the authors'
  verdict, or a later paper by the authors doing so.
- N2: Bich, Mossio, Ruiz-Mirazo and Moreno (2016) define biological regulation
  as second-order control by a subsystem the organism produces but which runs
  at its own rate and stoichiometry, is triggered by perturbations and
  switches the constitutive regime between viable states; feedback, network
  robustness and homeostasis belong instead to the constitutive regime.
  `promote_when`: the five requirements applied by an independent author to
  a case outside bacteria (a multicellular or ecological system) with a
  verdict each way.
