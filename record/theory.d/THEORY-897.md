# THEORY-897: Across seven computational learning studies selected without a systematic search, anxiety goes with altered adjustment of learning rate to uncertainty, mostly for aversive outcomes, while overall learning rate, choice consistency and outcome valuation show little relation to it

## Source

Brown, Price & Dombrovski, [LIT-1479](../literature.d/LIT-1479.md), read in full in [NOTE-1120](../notes.d/NOTE-1120.md)
from the NIH author manuscript (PMC10475148): the sections "Reinforcement
learning studies of anxiety" and "Anxiety as maladaptive aversive
uncertainty learning", and Table 2. The version of record could not be
fetched.

## What was actually shown

The authors tabulate seven studies that model learning under uncertainty in
samples with some clinical-level fear-based anxiety (Browning et al. 2015;
Brown et al. 2018; Homan et al. 2019; Gagne et al. 2020; Norbury et al.
2021; Abend et al. 2022; Zika et al., preprint). Across them:

- Differences lie in parameters for how learning adjusts to uncertainty
  (associability, volatility adjustment, latent-state formation). "Other
  parameters measuring overall learning rate, choice consistency, and
  outcome valuation show little relationship with anxiety."
- Studies with reward and loss conditions found differences in loss
  learning only (two) or in both (one).
- Under high volatility, anxious people learn slightly slower overall but
  accelerate after very surprising outcomes; under high noise they learn
  faster, especially after surprising losses, which the authors say is
  less consistent.

What could have come out otherwise: anxious participants could have shown
higher learning rates for punishment across the board, or higher
valuation of losses, which is what an excess-threat account expects. In
these studies, by the authors' reading, they mostly did not.

## What this does not say

- **That the literature was surveyed.** The studies were chosen from the
  authors' knowledge and searches, and the authors say they "do not
  represent a comprehensive, structured literature review following PRISMA
  guidelines".
- **That anxiety is a failure to tell noise from change.** That is the
  authors' reading of the pattern. They list two rival readings: that
  anxious people infer more information from negative outcomes (Pulcu &
  Browning 2019), or underestimate noise (Piray & Daw 2021).
- **That the finding is specific to anxiety.** One study found it more
  related to general internalizing symptoms than to GAD or depression; a
  computational meta-analysis they cite found higher punishment learning
  rates in both anxiety and depression.
- **Anything about avoidance.** These are learning tasks without
  avoidance. The link to avoidance is the authors' proposal, filed
  separately as [THEORY-910](THEORY-910.md).
