# LIT-tmp7ybhu: Commitments and Speech Acts

Robert M. Harnish (2005), "Commitments and Speech Acts", *Philosophica* 75,
11–41. DOI 10.21825/philosophica.82209.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpvf3w7.*

- **Two paradigms.** Austinian theories add rules or conventions to the
  utterance (Searle, Searle and Vanderveken, Alston). Gricean theories add
  expressed propositional attitudes (Strawson, Schiffer, Holdcroft, Bach and
  Harnish) (pp. 11–12). Bach and Harnish are "a mixed case", with Austinian
  notes (p. 12, n. 2).
- **The expressed-attitude theory goes "internal".** Communicative
  illocutionary acts are defined by the attitude expressed, and
  understanding them is recognizing it. "For S to express an attitude is
  for S to R-intend the hearer to take S's utterance as reason to think S
  has that attitude" (Bach and Harnish 1979, p. 15, quoted p. 19). Bach and
  Harnish "sharply distinguished" communicative from institutional acts
  (pp. 15, 22–23).
- **The normative theories leave commitment unexplained.** Searle's
  "imposing conditions of satisfaction on conditions of satisfaction"
  does not say how commitment is created (pp. 25–28). Alston's analysis of
  taking responsibility as subjecting an utterance to a socially entrenched
  rule fails for implicature and for responsibilities no rule fixes (pp.
  28–33).
- **The two may coincide.** In uttering e, S expresses the belief that P
  if and only if S takes responsibility for P being the case (p. 35). If
  so, "normative theory might at this level be simply another way of
  describing the same illocutionary facts" (p. 38).
