# Article 3. The Contracts We Become

## Relational invocation, stored coordination, and the formation of interdependence

Contracts are usually taken to be explicit agreements made by parties who exist before the agreement. This article states a different account, which the Nucleation record holds in parts. On that account an organization's processes can come to stand in contract-like relations that no one stated or agreed to. Those relations form from relied-upon regularities. They are contract-like only if they govern what happens on deviation, conflict or breach, and when they do, they can give a process a standing that does not depend on moral consideration. The article separates regularity, expectation, dependence and contract, so that no causal dependence is counted as a contract just because something depends on it. It then prices what a contract costs to form, keep, recognize, check, revise and leave, with the record's cost model of stored work, which is Active and run on three cases, and which holds the store fixed between uses. Last, it treats the invocation of a relationship as an operation that can reproduce, form or change the relationship it invokes, which that model does not price, and it sorts the ambiguity about which relationship an act invokes into three kinds. Everything this article says about contracts is Proposed. The cost geometry it applies is Active and demonstrated, and its organizational reading is a model, not a measurement.

## 3.1 Beyond explicit agreement

The owner's starting point, at U14, was an engineering concept: "a concept that might help us analyze these phenomena is the notion of service level agreements. contracts can form between processes. contracts can even operationalize standing through e.g. prioritization structure in the contract that governs resolution of disputes like races" (CLAIM-tmpkvzcn, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). Before this filing no entry held service-level agreements, contracts between processes or priority structures (ibid., "Where the record stands"). The proposal therefore adds a kind of party, the process, and a kind of standing, an operational one. Neither presupposes moral consideration or agency.

The second half of the same turn keeps the vocabulary apart from moral obligation. In the owner's words, it "mightnot make sense to describe these processes as having moral duties to each other precisely, but these systems of processes absolutely can enterpathological regimes of incompatible behavior requiring intervention and reorganization of commitments to resolve systemic frustration" (CLAIM-tmpe6yfm, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The hedge is "precisely". The claim does not say that no system of processes can bear duties. Nor does it say that every pathological regime is resolved by reorganizing commitments, since some regimes need resources or new capacities (ibid., "What it does not say").

The record's term for what forms is the **organizational contract**:

> a maintained relation among the processes of an organization that governs how they interact, explicitly agreed or formed implicitly from relied-upon regularities, and that is contract-like only if it governs deviation, conflict and breach and not only the ordinary course

(TERM-tmplpplx, Proposed, version 2). The term is the assistant's assembly of the owner's U14 and U16 with A's criterion, coined at the owner's "go ahead and coin the terms". Two exclusions fix its sense. It is not a legal contract, because "an organizational contract may be enforced by nothing but the dependencies it has created". It is also not the contractualist's contract, "an idealized agreement about what can be justified to each person" (ibid.). On the second exclusion, QUESTION-483's note of 2026-11-24 says the owner's contracts bear on the contractualism question only as "a contrast; no edge" (QUESTION-483, Open).

A49's §1 places the account among assume–guarantee contracts, relational contracts and psychological contracts, and argues that each has limits for a general process ontology. **The record cannot yet support that section.** The works are registered and unread:

- assume–guarantee contracts (LIT-tmpm8d98, Deferred);
- relational contracts (Baker, Gibbons and Murphy; LIT-tmp1xqzu);
- psychological and implied contracts (Rousseau; LIT-tmpwdxhm);
- convention (Lewis; LIT-473, Unavailable);
- priority inheritance (Sha, Rajkumar and Lehoczky; LIT-tmphhs41, Deferred).

What the record does say is where the owner's thesis departs from these neighbours. It departs in direction: "a contract reached from below, by the history of expectation and dependence, among parties that need not be agents able to agree" (CLAIM-tmp0d1fq, "Relation to existing work"). Whether that departure is new against the literature is not known until the literature is read.

The comparison A49 asks of the cost side, "against transaction-cost economics, incomplete-contract theory, dynamic optimization and existing resource-bounded planning frameworks", is not on the record either (old 03 §3.8). The record holds readings of Grossman and Hart and of Hart (LIT-2745, LIT-2776), read as "the right to decide what the contract leaves out, the residual control right, belongs to the owner of the relevant non-human assets" (THEORY-1979, Proposed), and CLAIM-728 (Proposed, interpretive): "a relationship fair in every specified term can still leave one party's terms at the other's discretion", from which CLAIM-tmpro73c sets itself apart (§3.7). It holds North's institutions, which "with technology … fix the costs of transacting" (THEORY-429, Proposed), Arthur's lock-in (THEORY-863, Active; §3.9) and Cohen and Levinthal's lock-out of a firm that stops investing (THEORY-1518, Active). It holds no reading of Coase or Williamson, and none of dynamic optimization or resource-bounded planning. The reading is LEAD-tmple60r (Open, stage idea, priority low; G3.3). This article does not supply the comparison from memory.

## 3.2 What makes a relation contract-like

If every stable interaction were a contract, the word would add nothing. A16 §13 says so: "mere behavioral regularity is insufficient to establish a contract-like relationship. Otherwise every stable causal interaction would qualify." It requires "evidence that the arrangement specifically governs counterfactual circumstances of deviation, conflict or breach", and names four marks (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl, Proposed, interpretive, A's; it rests on the owner's CLAIM-tmpkvzcn):

1. "A maintained relationship establishing reciprocal expectations or requirements."
2. "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation."
3. "Governance of circumstances in which requirements conflict."
4. "Some mechanism sustaining or revising that relationship."

**These conditions are necessary, not sufficient.** The claim's title says "contract-like only if it governs deviation, conflict or breach". A16 calls a relation that meets all four "a strong candidate", not a contract. TERM-tmplpplx's first version had read the marks as what makes a relation contract-like, and its version 2 corrected that, since the title "read them as sufficient ("because" for "only if")" (TERM-tmplpplx, history). The term and the claim still differ in one word: the term says "deviation, conflict and breach", the claim "or". As a necessary condition, "and" is the stricter. This article follows the claim. It uses the marks only as a filter. A relation that fails one of them is not a contract. A relation that passes all four is a candidate.

On breach, an Active neighbour shows why governing it is not a formality: "a breach can lower the injured party's position if it refuses to settle, so that the breaching party gains leverage over the resolution of its own breach" (CLAIM-716, Active, interpretive, A's, from another exchange). A relation whose terms say nothing about breach leaves that leverage to whoever breaches.

The assistant's note on CLAIM-tmp7hqtl shows why the filter is not yet a test. Take two controllers sharing a room, one heating and one cooling, with a deadband and a fixed rule that heating yields when both fire. They meet conditions 1 to 3, and they meet condition 4 if a tuning loop adjusts the deadband. "Whether that is a contract or a regulated regularity is exactly what the test was to decide." The note proposes a further condition, "that the requirements be revisable without replacing the processes (the contract, not the participants, is what changes), and that a breach be registered as a breach of the relation and not only as a deviation of one process". It gives this condition a test by intervention: change the terms, keep the processes, and see whether behaviour follows the terms. The note offers it "as the missing condition, not as settled" (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's). This article adopts the four marks as necessary. It flags the fifth as the assistant's proposal, and it does not use the fifth as a premise. The test has not been run; it is the change-of-terms probe of LEAD-tmpl9349 (§3.12).

A16 §6 offers a richer schema, C = (A, G, J, Π, Σ, M, E, R): assumptions, guarantees, jurisdiction, priority policy, suspension, monitoring, enforcement and revision. A16 calls it "a proposed extension for our inquiry, not the standard definition of an assume–guarantee contract" (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl). No entry defines how two such contracts compose. Assume–guarantee contracts compose and refine on (A, G), and LEAD-tmpbqj9u names the first formal step as saying how the other six components compose, "or to show that they reduce to (A, G) over a richer history space" (LEAD-tmpbqj9u, Open, stage idea). A49's §4 lists these components as "contractual structure". On the record they are a list, not yet a structure.

## 3.3 From regularity to maintained commitment

### The owner's route

The owner's U16 asked that the account "ensure we have space for characterizing implicit contracts, e.g. an unspoken understanding that evolved through evolution of expectations and assumptions based on consistently observed behaviors that evolved into dependencies" (CLAIM-tmp0d1fq, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The filing reads this as a three-step route:

1. consistently observed behaviour;
2. expectations and assumptions;
3. dependencies.

The claim's scope matters. It is "not that every regularity, expectation or dependence is a contract": "the owner's route ends in a contract only where the dependence comes to govern how the parties respond to its failure" (ibid., "What it does not say"). That last condition is §3.2's second mark. Joining the owner's route to A's marks in this way restates CLAIM-tmp0d1fq, because the claim states the condition itself (JR3.1). The claim's defeater keeps it modest: it is "not defeated by ... dependences that never became contracts: the claim is that the implicit route exists, not that every dependence takes it".

### Where an expectation can live

A20 §2 gives the owner's route a sense of expectation that needs no representation. Expectations can be represented, dispositional or structural, the last being "its successful operation presupposes another's output". The structural sense comes with a slogan: "**An expectation can be embodied in what an organization no longer needs—or no longer knows how—to do for itself.**" A20's case is a consumer that drops its buffer against a reliable producer: "Its organization now presupposes reliable delivery" (CLAIM-tmp18v1o, Proposed, interpretive, A's; not a member of the work).

The claim says it is more than capability loss. The size of the gap "says which deviations by the producer the consumer can absorb and which it cannot, which is what an implicit contract's tolerances are". Its defeater asks for exactly that: the reading has to predict something the bare loss does not (ibid.).

*Expectation, reconstructed against a known result.* If A20 §6's model shows the route, then with service held at 1, reliance should rise and independent capacity fall, and withdrawal should drop functioning to what capacity remains. The model fails as an illustration if functioning recovers on withdrawal. *Result.* The assistant reran A20's update rule at its default settings, with withdrawal at step 40: "independent capacity at withdrawal is 0.42 and functioning falls to a minimum of 0.34" (CLAIM-tmp18v1o). A20 states the model's limit itself: "It models unilateral dependency formation, not yet the formation of a reciprocally maintained contract."

The record holds one documented specimen of the same mechanism, outside a model. A provider "whose realized reliability exceeds its declared guarantee can lower the resilience of the organization built on it: those who depend on it stop maintaining the fallbacks the guarantee would require" (CLAIM-tmpb1wyc, Proposed, interpretive, A's, from A34 §2). Its only ground is the SRE book's global Chubby planned outage (CASE-tmp3xxqg, Proposed, documented). ARG-tmptccms (Proposed) weighs that support: the case holds as an existence result and not for how often it happens, and A34 itself says the arrangement is "not necessarily a mutually maintained implicit contract in our narrower sense". CLAIM-tmpzas6q (Proposed, interpretive, A's, from A36) carries the mechanism to constitutional conventions, where institutions rely on "historically reliable restraint beyond what law guarantees". For this article the specimen does one thing: it shows a structural expectation, in CLAIM-tmp18v1o's sense, in a documented system rather than only in A20's rerun.

### One-way dependence and reciprocal reliance

The record's held account of dependence formed by loss is CLAIM-603 (Proposed, interpretive, version 8; the assistant's, on an owner's remark of 2026-11-05). "Whether it is dependency or division of labour is decided by the supply, not the loss": one-way supply set from outside "leaves a dependence that nothing in the dependent's dynamics limits and that withdrawal punishes, while reciprocal loss ... leaves interdependence". The claim is held back by two notes: its title states the favoured loss too generally, and Moore's thesis is not held (CLAIM-603, note of 2026-11-06). The record's account of formerly autonomous partners coming to depend on one another is SYNTHESIS-053 (Proposed), which finds dependence, cohesion and host control "coming apart" in the documented symbioses. A51 asks that biological dependence and reciprocal maintenance be treated as "limiting cases rather than automatic examples of contractual normativity", and the record supports that reading: on CLAIM-603's own terms, a loss to reliable supply is "dependency or division of labour" by who supplies it, which says nothing of terms governing breach, and the demonstrated models of supply that does the work (CLAIM-602, CLAIM-675, Active; §3.10) are of a scalar capacity, not of a relationship. One reading in SYNTHESIS-053, THEORY-1050 (Active), has mutual dependence evolving ahead of reproductive cohesion in a model of an incipient endosymbiosis, "but only under the mapping" its authors choose.

A49's abstract lists five stages: "reliable regularity, acquired expectation, dependence, coordinated reliance and contractual organization". **No entry holds this sequence.** The nearest is A22 §12's six-stage normalization ladder (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, below). That ladder runs from "An exceptional accommodation" to "Enforced: Its withdrawal is treated as a violation", so it ends in enforcement, not in contract. "Coordinated reliance", A49's reciprocal stage, is defined by no entry. The first draft of this article read CLAIM-603's reciprocal loss as that stage. An independent reader found that the reading glosses an undefined phrase and predicts nothing (§ Joins, J3.1), and it is withdrawn.

This article composes the distinctions it needs from four entries. It treats them as distinctions, not as stages every relation passes through:

- *regularity* is what CLAIM-tmp7hqtl says is not enough;
- *expectation*, including structural expectation, is CLAIM-tmp18v1o's;
- *dependence*, one-way or reciprocal, is CLAIM-603's;
- *contract-likeness* is CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's necessary marks.

Reciprocity is not claimed as a condition of contract. CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's first mark asks for "reciprocal expectations or requirements", which is not the same as reciprocal loss.

### Relied on is not owed

The normalization ladder has a second part, and that part is what keeps the descriptive account apart from the moral one. "Repetition and reliance can turn a discretionary contribution into an operative obligation, whose withdrawal is treated as a breach, with no agreement ever made" (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, Proposed, interpretive, A's). But Expected, ReliedUpon, Committed and Owes are distinct: "The first three can contribute to the circumstances under which a duty arises. None establishes that duty automatically." And "One cannot necessarily acquire entitlement to another's activity simply by reorganizing oneself around its continued availability" (ibid.). An Active reading shows obligation without agreement in another register: after Mauss, "a gift carries no legal guarantee of return yet obliges one" (THEORY-1474, Active). No line of the record uses it yet, and this article cites it only as a neighbour.

This part rests on CLAIM-738 (Active, interpretive): "a demand's being asserted, maintained or recognized gives it no warrant, and a commitment is neither an obligation nor an authorization". A24 §11 names the gap between the two parts: C^op, "the actual contractual organization", against C^just, "an arrangement satisfying the relevant justified standards". "There is no reason to presume that these coincide" (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0). Nor does the claim say that a normalized contribution is exploited: "it could be a fair division of labor" (A22 §10, quoted there).

**The owner's turn on exploitation.** At U17 the owner asked to "analyze how exploitation fits into this picture, since it can expose relied upon expectations that create opposrtunities for asymmetric relationships". "Expose" has two readings, and the record holds both as alternatives, each Proposed, interpretive and the owner's, until the owner chooses or keeps both:

- **(a) exposure as vulnerability**: "Reliance exposes the party who relies: the expectations that implicit contracts and norms build up are relied upon, and relied-upon expectations create opportunities for asymmetric relationships, which exploitation takes up" (CLAIM-tmp05a1l);
- **(b) exposure as revelation**: "Exploitation can expose, in the sense of reveal, the relied-upon expectations an implicit contract kept tacit" (CLAIM-tmppx64t).

Reading (a) is about the opportunity reliance opens, and is "Not defeated by reliance that is never exploited". Reading (b) is about what exploitation makes visible. Neither says that a normalized contribution is exploited, so neither contradicts CLAIM-tmp0i6c0's "it could be a fair division of labor". What separates exploitation from dependence and from domination, and which declared standard gives the verdict, is QUESTION-468 (Open). This article does not answer it.

When reliance does make something owed is also open. QUESTION-tmprudey (Open; the assistant's, filling A's gap) sets out four grounds: knowing inducement, reasonableness, control, and vulnerability (the last held as CLAIM-671's supposition). It shows that the four "give different verdicts" in A22 §6's case of a volunteer. This article does not answer the question. The abstract's phrase "transforming the participants' future alternatives and vulnerabilities" is descriptive here. What follows from vulnerability is left to Article 8.

## 3.4 Operative norms, and how they outlast their reasons

The ladder's end state is an operative norm. A22 §2 separates two responses to the same discrepancy: "The first changes the expectation to accommodate reality. The second attempts to change reality to satisfy the expectation." The claim filed from it states both halves: "An expectation is an operative norm rather than a prediction when a discrepancy is met by bringing the behaviour back into line with the expectation instead of revising the expectation to fit the behaviour; which response a discrepancy gets, adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or revision, is the work of normative governance" (CLAIM-tmphq6hp, Proposed, interpretive, A's). The first half says what makes an expectation a norm. The second says that governing a norm is choosing among four responses, revision among them. The assistant links the claim to the record's direction of fit (TERM-151, Proposed), "a link A does not draw" (ibid.). The claim does not say an operative norm is legitimate. A22 §1 separates the "Operative norm" from the "Legitimate norm".

CLAIM-tmp0i6c0 already says that the ladder's end is an operative norm "in CLAIM-tmphq6hp's sense". The first draft of this article also read §3.2's second mark, "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation", as the norm's direction of fit. An independent reader found the reading trivial in one direction (a relation that corrects violations does respond differently to them) and denied by the draft itself in the other (renegotiation also differentiates, and CLAIM-tmphq6hp counts all four responses alike). The reading is withdrawn (§ Joins, J3.2). An Active neighbour bears on the same mark: for a discrepancy to change what an organization does, "three distinct achievements are needed and any can fail while the others hold": legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability (CLAIM-523, Active, interpretive). Any response to a violation, corrective or revising, presupposes them.

### A norm stabilized by the reliance it attracts

A22 §3 adds a stabilizer that grows with what others have built on the norm: "Compliance produces reasons for further compliance" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx, Proposed, interpretive, version 3, A's, with the assistant's checks). A22's model is x′ = σ(b + λg) − x, r′ = α(x − r), g′ = β(rx − g), with compliance x, reliance r, maintenance resources g, a baseline tendency to comply b and a feedback strength λ.

*Expectations, reconstructed against known results.* If the model carries A22's sentence, three things should hold:

- at its defaults there should be two stable states and a threshold between them;
- a practice should reach the high state only if it starts above the threshold;
- a practice established at a high baseline should hold the high state at baselines where a fresh practice dies out. The clause "can outlast a weakening of the reasons" fails if established and fresh practices collapse at the same baseline.

*Results.* First, at b = −3 and λ = 8 there are three equilibria: 0.048 and 0.993 are stable, and 0.689 is a saddle. Second, "Started at x = r = s, g = s², the system goes to the high state only for s above ≈ 0.689, the saddle", so "the 'sufficiently established practice' has to be established past roughly 69% compliance" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx). That grades the second expectation for starts on that diagonal only; the entry does not trace the threshold for other starting states. Third, on persistence, cite the corrected version. The assistant's first gap note read the model as letting a norm outlast "a weakening of its baseline support ... not its disappearance". The owner objected: norms do persist long after their original justification disappears, "as race-based power did in the United States after abolition" (CLAIM-tmp8l5s9, Proposed, empirical, the owner's; CASE-tmp3euq0, Proposed, a specimen). The recomputation that followed found: "The established norm holds above 93% compliance while unaided compliance falls from 27% to about 1.3%". The limit is that "below b ≈ −4.33 only the low state exists". "The sentence 'not its disappearance' above is withdrawn" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx, note of 2026-11-24).

The concession is the assistant's (CLAIM-tmps8ou2, Proposed, interpretive): "A norm's original justification and its baseline support come apart". A justification can vanish while replacement rationales and served interests keep support. The owner's case does not by itself show the reliance loop: "The norm was also maintained by violence, by disfranchisement and by the interests of those it served", and the case "does not separate them" (CASE-tmp3euq0). The case's standing is "documented", but its dates are "recalled and not checked against sources in this filing" (ibid.).

Two further limits apply. The model's "maintenance rule is supplied by the modeler" (A22 §5, quoted in CLAIM-tmpjg5gx), so it shows a candidate stabilizer and not how a standard forms. And the model is of a norm, not of a contract. Carrying it to a contract is now a claim of its own, filed from this article's join J3.3 after an independent reader admitted it by a separating case, narrowed: "An implicit contract's operative obligation, once normalized by the recipient's reliance, can be held by that reliance past the provider's original reasons for the contribution, where the provider cannot cheaply exit" (CLAIM-tmpu77ve, Proposed, interpretive; the wording is the assistant's, from the reader's narrowing). It rests on CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, CLAIM-tmpjg5gx and CLAIM-tmphq6hp. The condition on exit is what it adds: where the provider can leave cheaply, enforcement funded by the recipients' stake drives the provider out instead of holding the obligation. It inherits CLAIM-tmpjg5gx's limits, and it has not been tested. Beside it stands SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr's decline of A22 §22, which reads "dependency-mediated norm formation and asymmetric contractual organization" as a map of four held claims and its entrenchment clause as CLAIM-tmp4zii5's self-sealing. That decline tested entrenchment, not this claim (CLAIM-tmpu77ve, "Bearing").

## 3.6 Contractual structure and operational standing

### The vocabulary that is Active

Three Active terms carry the structure:

- **Precedence, jurisdiction and suspension.** "**Precedence:** among applicable constraints, which one controls? **Jurisdiction:** which constraints count as applicable to this case? **Suspension:** when may an otherwise applicable constraint be disabled?" It is "Not a ranking of rules" (TERM-017, Active).
- **Grant.** A conferral that "runs through a maintained relation of granting, delegation, eligibility or support" (TERM-101, Active).
- **Conferral.** "any effect one process has on another's share of future influence" (TERM-103, Active).

The record's account of the last two, and of how grants are counted, is SYNTHESIS-050 (Proposed). CLAIM-283 (Active, interpretive) separates "The precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it". The claim is stated for motives. A16 applies it to processes.

### Operational standing

A16 §3 defines the standing the owner said contracts can operationalize:

> a process's maintained eligibility, under an organizational arrangement, to have specified claims upon another process's activity or upon their shared governance become consequential. This need not imply moral consideration, consciousness or personhood.

(TERM-tmpgiy6r, Proposed, A's). It sets apart four quantities that "can come apart": asserted claim, operational standing, effective authority and moral standing. "A process might have contractual standing but be systematically starved of resources." Standing is also **not reducible to priority**: "A low-priority process could possess a protected guarantee of eventual service, an entitlement to escalate a missed deadline, or immunity from arbitrary cancellation. Standing concerns the structure of recognized claims, not simply one's position in a queue" (ibid.).

This narrows the owner's U14, which offered "prioritization structure" as the example. In the term's own words, "the prioritization structure is one way a contract secures standing, not what standing is" (TERM-tmpgiy6r, "Relations"). The owner's claim does not contradict the narrowing. It does not say "that a contract's priority structure exhausts the standing it secures", and its defeater is a showing that every entitlement such a contract secures "is fixed by the ordering alone" (CLAIM-tmpkvzcn). TERM-tmplpplx's version 2 brought the term into line with the narrowing: "Its priority structure is one place operational standing shows, though standing is not reducible to priority". In this article priority is one carrier of standing, not its home.

A parallel at the scale of whole organizations is stated for moral standing (TERM-083), not operational standing: "To have standing toward an agent is to have claims that can constrain what it is entitled to optimize, not only to enter its objective" (CLAIM-tmpa61rf, Proposed, interpretive, A's, from A48). Entering an objective is to that standing what a place in a queue is to operational standing. The record states the two separately, and this article does not join them.

The term leaves one thing open, and so does this article: "What makes an eligibility 'maintained' and 'recognized' without a representing process is not said" (TERM-tmpgiy6r).

### The priority-inversion construction

A16 §4 gives the construction (CASE-tmptpna1, Proposed, a stipulated construction, A's). Priority inversion is one of the nine coordination pathologies CLAIM-tmpzuzeh (Proposed, interpretive, A's) lists as candidate correspondences for organizations, each "with its own mechanism and remedy". The construction:

- three processes share one preemptive processor;
- L holds a lock that H needs;
- H must finish by time 5;
- L needs four units of work, M five and H one;
- only the arbitration rule changes, fixed priorities in one run and priority inheritance in the other.

*Expectation, reconstructed against a known result.* A16 reads the construction as granted authority without efficacy, relieved by "temporarily empowering a lower-priority process upon which it depends". If that reading is right, H should miss its deadline under fixed priorities and meet it under inheritance. The reading of the remedy as *relief* fails if the change only moves a cost onto another process. (The first draft also expected the two rules to give the same schedule when L holds no lock H needs. That holds by the definition of priority inheritance, which acts only on contention, and was not simulated; it is not counted as evidence here.)

*Result.* The assistant simulated both schedules unit by unit. Under fixed priorities the completions are "M at 6, L at 9, H at 10". Under inheritance they are "L at 4, H at 5, M at 10". So H and L each finish five units earlier and M four units later: "inheritance takes five units of delay off H, and off L, and puts four on M", and "The total delay summed over the three processes falls by six" (CASE-tmptpna1, note of 2026-11-24 correcting the earlier note's "four units of delay from H to M"). No schedule does better for both H and M, because ten units of work on one processor with H done by 5 leave M finishing at 10 at the earliest. So the case shows governance-induced frustration in CLAIM-357's sense "only because M has no deadline". Give M any deadline before 10 and "inheritance only chooses which commitment fails". For M the remedy is "a reallocation of delay, not a costless gain" (CASE-tmptpna1, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's). That is CLAIM-556's point (Active, interpretive): "dropping a demand is not relief". A16's table calls the outcome "Relief". This article does not, except in the conditional sense the note allows.

In A16's table, "The scheduler recognizes H's assigned priority" while "H remains unable to act because of its dependency". The first draft read this as an instance of TERM-tmpgiy6r's standing without effective authority. The case's gloss and the term's ("The arrangement recognizes its eligibility") match, so the reading is a relabelling that predicts nothing, and it is withdrawn as a join (§ Joins, J3.4). It does expose a conflict on the record. CASE-tmptpna1 calls that row CLAIM-283's "granted authority", distinct from efficacy, while TERM-tmpgiy6r maps CLAIM-283's split onto asserted claim against effective authority, which puts granted authority on the effective side. Which reading of CLAIM-283 holds is not settled, and this article takes neither.

### Contracts the ordinary course does not reveal

The two arbitration rules give one schedule whenever L holds no lock H needs, by the definition of inheritance. That makes the construction the separating case for a claim A16 §§15 and 19 put forward. The claim was narrowed by the assistant to the part the case supports: "An organization's characteristic structure can include the contracts maintained among its processes, which its ordinary behaviour does not reveal: two arrangements alike on the ordinary course can part when a contract is breached, an assumption fails or a process invokes its standing" (CLAIM-tmpersgx, Proposed, interpretive, version 2; argued in ARG-tmp2dpcv, Proposed).

The claim supersedes CLAIM-tmp1nubb (Superseded), which stated the same proposition for implicit contracts, and it carries that entry's six probes, which part arrangements alike on the ordinary course:

1. interruption of the relied-upon behaviour;
2. a substitute provider;
3. competing demands on the provider;
4. removal of an enforcer;
5. withdrawal of a discretionary contribution;
6. a request to renegotiate.

"Only the responses to these show 'whether a dependence is regulated, by what, and on whose terms' (A22)" (CLAIM-tmpersgx, note of 2026-11-24). The exchange's account, SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr (Proposed, version 3, items 6 and 7), integrates this with the owner's U14 and U16. Applying the probes to implicit contracts is therefore held and is not a join here. That the difference belongs to an organization's character, and not to its circumstances, is Article 1's question.

### What this article does not bridge

Operational standing presupposes no moral standing. TERM-124's domination (Proposed, the assistant's) has as one relatum "The party dominated, with standing (TERM-083)", and TERM-083 is moral standing (Proposed, the owner's, from an exchange of 2026-10-26). **No entry states how a process's operational standing relates to a party that can be dominated.** CLAIM-tmpe6yfm's hedge points one way, since processes need not owe one another duties "precisely". A16 §16 points the same way: "A scheduler may systematically starve a process. That can constitute a functional pathology without constituting an injustice to the process itself" (quoted in CLAIM-tmpe6yfm).

The far side of the gap is held. In the dependence-and-domination line, what sorts a dependence "is who controls the terms on which it is met, exit, renegotiation and the conditions of supply", not its magnitude (CLAIM-690, Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13). A dependence is presumptively dominating when three conditions coincide: participation in an important sphere rests on it, its controllers hold "consequential discretion", and the dependent lacks "effective means of protection, influence, challenge or justified withdrawal" (CLAIM-682, Proposed, interpretive, A's). The third condition is stated in CLAIM-523's three achievements (CLAIM-686, Active, demonstrated, the assistant's). SYNTHESIS-092 (Proposed) is the account. These entries are about parties with standing in TERM-083's sense. This article makes no claim about domination among processes. The bridge is left to Article 9, and it is a gap on the record, not a step this article supplies.

## 3.7 Ambiguous relational invocation

At U21 the owner asked to "integrate contract formation, persistence, and modification in our cost model. It might actually already be in there already under other names like ambiguous invocation of relationship" (QUESTION-tmpb7fx7, Open, the owner's). The filer checked both halves of the suspicion. Formation, persistence and modification were "held, for any form" (CLAIM-569, CLAIM-761, CASE-326, TERM-116, CLAIM-571). "Ambiguous invocation of a relationship: not held". The idea of this section is therefore the owner's. Its development, below, is A's.

A30 §3: "The ambiguity is not merely about what action is requested. It concerns **which relational organization is being enacted through the action**." A30 §5 sorts it into three kinds, each with its own remedy (CLAIM-tmp80ny9, Proposed, interpretive, A's, at the owner's U21):

| Kind | A30's definition | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Epistemic ambiguity | "A determinate arrangement exists, but its applicability is uncertain." | "more information" |
| Relational disagreement | "Participants embody incompatible specifications of their relationship." | "negotiation or arbitration" |
| Constitutive indeterminacy | "No established arrangement yet determines what the relationship requires." | "contract formation" |

A51 places this section on the record's pragmatic-transport accounts (SYNTHESIS-066, SYNTHESIS-068). The links the record states are narrower. CLAIM-tmp80ny9's "Where the record stands" names two: CLAIM-044 "separates ambiguity from contextuality for an utterance's readings", and "this claim moves the test from readings to the parties' specifications of a relationship"; CLAIM-151 makes "which communicative act an utterance performs depend on its relations to roles, norms and expectations", and "this claim asks which relationship it invokes" (both Proposed, interpretive). Neither account mentions contracts or invocation. That a relationship an act invokes is a communicative kind in SYNTHESIS-068's sense is not stated, and this article does not state it (G3.4).

The claim's defeater is a kind resolved by another kind's remedy alone, or a showing that the three cannot be told apart "by any observation of the parties' conduct under perturbation".

A32 §4 restates the division over Γ, the joint specifications compatible with what each party treats as governing. One admissible specification is a determinate relationship. Several is "Contractual ambiguity or under-specification". None is "Incompatible local specifications". The assistant's note shows that **the two divisions do not coincide**. An empty Γ is relational disagreement. "Several admissible specifications" covers both epistemic ambiguity and constitutive indeterminacy. What tells those two apart "is whether some arrangement is actually maintained, a fact of maintenance provenance (TERM-100)", and a deviation probe can show it (CLAIM-tmp80ny9, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's; TERM-100, Active). The article uses A30's three kinds. It treats Γ as identifying only the second kind, and it treats the probes of §3.6 as what separates the first kind from the third.

CLAIM-430's note gives the owner's perspectival invitation its place here: "An interaction can invoke an existing contract, invite a new contract, or ambiguously do both" (A32 §2). The note's own mapping puts the third case under constitutive indeterminacy and formative invocation (CLAIM-430, note of 2026-11-24). That mapping is held, so it is not a join (JR3.2).

### Who resolves the ambiguity

A30 §10 adds a variable that is not the degree of ambiguity: "It is **who controls the resolution of its ambiguity, when resolution becomes necessary, and who pays for it**." Its example: "treat an employee's recurring accommodation as discretionary when asked to compensate it, but as obligatory when the employee declines to provide it" (CLAIM-tmpro73c, Proposed, interpretive, A's, extending the owner's U17). The claim is "different in kind" from residual control in an incomplete contract (CLAIM-728, Proposed), because "no contingency is uncovered". It does not reach a verdict of exploitation. TERM-123 needs a declared standard, and A30 lists "productive under-specification" first among ambiguity's uses (CLAIM-tmpro73c).

### Productive misunderstanding

The owner opened a sidebar on 2026-11-24: "'productive misunderstanding' - identification and clarification of ambiguity as detection of and reorganization around novel consequential distinctions" (CLAIM-tmpupcws, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The claim names two achievements that come apart, detection and reorganization. It places the owner's "novel" distinction under constitutive indeterminacy, or under relational disagreement "when its resolution forms terms neither party held". The assistant answered it twice:

- **A qualification.** A misunderstanding "dates the distinction's relevance rather than its existence", and clarifying can coarsen as well as refine (CLAIM-tmpm76n5, Proposed, interpretive).
- **An objection at full strength.** "A misunderstanding can be productive because it is left unresolved", where each reading is satisfied on every case the cooperation meets (CLAIM-tmpgx2ej, Proposed, interpretive, counter).

At the owner's "file all three of your ideas", the assistant filed a join of the sidebar with A30's kinds and with control over resolution: "Resolving an ambiguity that no arrangement yet settles forms a contract, and the same act has two outcomes: resolved jointly, it is a productive misunderstanding that reorganizes the parties around a new distinction; resolved by one party who controls when and how, it is exploitation through ambiguity where the terms it sets fail a declared standard" (CLAIM-tmpisfi1, Proposed, interpretive, version 2, the assistant's; not a member of the work). The final clause keeps TERM-123's condition that exploitation is relative to a declared standard; version 2 restored it after this manuscript's drafter found it dropped (ibid., history). The claim was admitted by a discriminating prediction: "the share of benefit each party takes from the new terms tracks how far that party controlled the timing and terms of the resolution, not how ambiguous the relation was beforehand". The prediction has not been tested. Because the join is on the record, this article cites it and does not make it.

## 3.8 What a contract costs: the lifecycle as stored work

At U21 the owner asked to "integrate contract formation, persistence, and modification in our cost model" (QUESTION-tmpb7fx7, Open, the owner's). The filer found that half held: "Formation, persistence and modification: held, for any form" (ibid.). This section sets out the held half. §3.10 takes up the half that is not held, an invocation that changes what it invokes.

### 3.8.1 Stored work

The owner's thesis, from U17 of "Topological protection explained" (2026-11-18), is held in ChatGPT's wording (A39 §10):

> "A stabilized constraint can be stored work: by replacing later work with cheaper operations it changes which activities are realizable within a fixed per-task budget, so amortization becomes capability expansion without any added degrees of freedom or resources; the expansion is conditional on the costs of constructing, keeping, retrieving, verifying and revising the constraint and on the distribution of later demands, and the persistence that buys it can entrench" (CLAIM-761, Active, interpretive).

Its parts were held before U17: the owner's conjecture that "amortized retrieval is actually a kind of amortized computation" (QUESTION-095, Open); stored inquiry, "a stabilized specification is past inquiry stored so that later questions become cheaper to ask, and it can make other questions harder", a clause ChatGPT drew at A17 and A18 from the owner's thesis (CLAIM-475, Active, interpretive); the guard that "persistence is not amortization, which is shown only when earlier work replaces later computation, communication or recruitment" (CLAIM-443, Active, interpretive); and ChatGPT's accounting, "Filing an argument moves work across time ... so it is worth its cost only when expected reuse covers it" (CLAIM-303, Active, interpretive), cited here only for its accounting.

CLAIM-761's step is from totals to a budget. A question costing 12 from scratch and 3 with an acquired framework is out of reach under a per-task budget of 5 and then within it, "whether or not its construction has yet been repaid". "That is CLAIM-571's point that a threshold is a peak, not a total" (CLAIM-761; **J3.5**, a restatement). Two failures are kept apart: an operational one, which defeats the claim, and an economic one, which "defeats only the stronger reading that the investment was worthwhile, which the claim does not make" (ibid.). The expansion is relative to a budget; whether such an intervention creates a capability the system lacked "rather than make one it already had cheaper" is open (QUESTION-392, Open). An added hard constraint "cannot enlarge the set of admissible trajectories" at fixed dynamics (CLAIM-582, Active, demonstrated), so a stabilized constraint that lowers a cost has "changed the mechanism: it supplies cheaper operations ... and not only a restriction" (CLAIM-761). The claim does not support "That every emergent organization is such an investment" (ibid.). Nor is the expansion shown to be an expansion of freedom: whether "newly affordable operations widen substantive freedom, and for whom, is CLAIM-352's question" (CLAIM-761; CLAIM-352, Active, interpretive, the owner's, from U11 of the exchange of 2026-10-17; promoted at the sixteenth status review, whose rulings the owner delegated). And "stored" does not mean "slow": dynamical and economic separation "come apart both ways" (CLAIM-755, Proposed, demonstrated; A39 §3).

Two instances bear on contracts. The owner's learned local accommodation (CLAIM-358, Proposed, §3.5) is "A fourth kind of stored work", one that "stores neither an answer nor an index" (QUESTION-095, note, A39 §2; **J3.11**, a restatement). It is an amortization in CLAIM-303's sense, a lower total over repeated encounters; it names no per-task budget, so it is not read in CLAIM-761's sense. And a standing grant "pays it once for a domain before any question in it is formulated" (CLAIM-1016, Proposed, interpretive; Article 5, §5.5).

### 3.8.2 Three requirements and one cost model

"A form has at least three resource requirements, to construct it, to maintain it, and to move into or out of it, and none fixes another: a capability can be cheap to keep and costly to acquire, so having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it" (CLAIM-569, Active, demonstrated; the requirements are ChatGPT's proposal, the construction the filer's). In the construction, under a surplus of 3 a year, capability X (24 to build, 2 a year to keep) can be sustained and not acquired, and Y (6 to build, 4 to keep) acquired and not sustained: "Construction and maintenance rank in opposite orders". The claim says "at least three", and that none fixes another, not that they move oppositely (CLAIM-569, note of 2026-11-19). The thematic account is SYNTHESIS-112 (Proposed).

The record's cost model is LEAD-108's run, CASE-326 (Active, executed study; the assistant's, 2026-11-22). Its terms were "fixed before any case was priced": construction F at a price of work λ_F; upkeep U; the chance π that the store serves a demand; retrieval ρ and the cost of a miss; verification v with detection chance δ; staleness σ, loss L and revision R; erosion ε of the on-demand route; spill; the on-demand cost c; the price λ and budget B at the moment of demand; and "party", who pays each item. It reports feasibility, lifetime and distribution as three separate verdicts. Before the run LEAD-108 stated one criterion and no expectation about any case: "A case it cannot price without a new term is a counterexample to the single trade". The three cases were not sampled: they are the three that QUESTION-114 (Open) named as the test of whether one trade prices them all, and CLAIM-891 is filed as answering it, while the question stays Open. Its neighbour in physical database design is held as a reading: index selection "is NP-hard", and the tools choose "by searching configurations costed with the query optimizer's own estimates", with "no term for the one-time cost of building" (THEORY-345, Active). That last clause sets it apart from CASE-326, whose F is exactly that term. With the terms as fixed it priced a cockpit's speed bugs, a filed argument and a LEGO bin "without adding a term for any of them", once work is "priced by the moment it is done and checked against the budget for each demand, not only summed" (CLAIM-891, Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's). That the terms are complete is among what it "cannot show" (CASE-326). Erosion costs, per demand, "εc[(1 − π) + π(σδ + φ)]", so "it weighs little where demand is predictable, the store rarely wrong and checking cheap" (CLAIM-892, Active, demonstrated). The run was repeated on 2026-11-24 by a session other than the filer's, byte for byte (CASE-326, note of 2026-11-24). ## 3.9 Lock-in and exit

### 3.9.1 The geometry

"For a fixed architecture and one resource that must be held at each moment, the resource threshold for reaching a target configuration need not equal the resource requirement of the target: it is the least, over admissible transition paths, of the peak requirement along the path, and it is infinite when no admissible path joins the two" (CLAIM-571, Active, demonstrated). In ChatGPT's example both endpoints cost 0.7 to hold and every path passes a peak of 1.3, so at a budget of 1.0 each is feasible and neither is reachable from the other; the gap of 0.6 is reconfiguration slack (TERM-106, Active). For a cumulative expenditure, "a path costs its integral, not its peak", and the two can differ "by any factor" (CLAIM-646, Proposed, demonstrated); the six resource types "bound different functions of a path and are not one budget" (TERM-105, Active). Feasible, reachable and reliably reached are "three properties, each implied by the next and none implying it" (CLAIM-567, Active, demonstrated). Under directed moves "the least budget for going from one configuration to another can differ from that for coming back": in the filer's construction 1 one way and 3 the other (CLAIM-591, Active, demonstrated). The claim says "can differ", not that they usually do. A49's sentence that "the affordability of an organizational state does not establish the affordability of reaching it" restates CLAIM-569's title and CLAIM-571 (**J3.10**, a restatement).

### 3.9.2 Contractual lock-in without preference

A30 §8 applies the threshold to contracts. Replacing a critical provider can require keeping two providers at once, rebuilding expertise and moving data, so "The new contract would be cheaper to maintain, but the organization cannot afford the transition. This produces contractual lock-in without requiring any preference for the existing arrangement" (CLAIM-571, note of 2026-11-24: "An application that supports the claim's reading; no change"). The two providers held at once are the peak. The same passage adds that "renegotiation readiness might itself need to be maintained as an organizational capacity". Readiness is Article 2's subject, where the executed studies of reserve, redesign and use-following budgets are set out (CASE-315, CASE-343, CASE-412, CASE-397); this article takes only the pointer.

A30 §8 also says: "A relationship may be easy to enter, inexpensive to maintain and extremely expensive to leave" (in CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). The record reads this two ways and defines no directed revision cost:

- *as TERM-116's profiles* (Proposed): the incoming profile, "the cost of producing it from each other configuration", and the outgoing, "the cost of turning it into each", because "a system may be easy to assemble but hard to change once assembled"; its note of 2026-11-24 says that A30's party index "is an extension the term does not carry";
- *as a peak threshold*, by CLAIM-571's note above;
- *no revision cost*: CLAIM-569's note, corrected on 2026-11-24, reads "TERM-116 defines relational profiles, not a revision cost, and no entry defines R(C → C′)", and CLAIM-tmpsf9hp says the same.

What is open is which cost a contract's exit is: a profile of costs, cumulative under additive costs, or a peak along a path. CLAIM-646 shows they can differ by any factor, and which applies depends on which resource binds (TERM-105). No entry fixes that for contracts. That entry is cheap and exit dear then follows from CLAIM-591's "can differ" and adds nothing to it (**J3.9**, failed as drafted; filed as a dated note on TERM-116, 2026-11-24). The record's one organizational case of cheap entry and dear exit is an emergency regime "that can enter exceptional authority more cheaply than it can leave it", so that "the regime can outlast its justification" (CLAIM-tmp0gs0t, Proposed, interpretive; A34 §6 and A36 §15). It cites neither CLAIM-591 nor CLAIM-569, and its cost of leaving is argued, not priced.

Other routes keep an arrangement with no budget shortfall and no preference. They are neighbours, not joined to the threshold: option value under irreversibility, where "when the status quo irreversibly erodes the capacity to transform, the ranking can reverse" (CLAIM-tmp8mpgs, Active, demonstrated, at illustrative values); increasing returns, "once it has locked in no small change in returns alters later choices" (THEORY-863, Active, a reading of Arthur's model); practical compulsion "without any rule requiring it" (CLAIM-677, Proposed); and reliance on another's "historically reliable restraint" (CLAIM-tmpzas6q, Proposed; §3.3).

### 3.9.3 An obligation bars exit, and does not price it

"A genuine obligation enters a cost model of exit as a condition on which transitions are permissible, not as a cost: withdrawal can be feasible and affordable yet impermissible until alternatives are arranged or the duty is transferred, so a party who controls the circumstances that keep the restriction in place controls the other's exit without raising its price" (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6, Proposed, interpretive; A32 §13). It agrees with a held result: a normatively permitted path set gives "b*_N ≥ b*", so a normative restriction "can raise this claim's threshold and never lower it, as CLAIM-582 says of any added hard constraint" (CLAIM-571, note of 2026-11-17; the step is the assistant's). Its last clause has a held neighbour, which it cites: "One party can wrongfully bring about circumstances in which another incurs a genuine obligation, so that the second does right in discharging it and is still wronged" (CLAIM-703, Active, interpretive). It "does not answer QUESTION-472's second half", what separates such control "from ordinary reliance on someone's conscientiousness" (QUESTION-472, Open), nor does it say "that every obligation is a bar rather than a weight: defeasible obligations would need rules of priority" (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6). The account that holds these, SYNTHESIS-099 (Proposed), keeps QUESTION-472 Open. This is the exit side of §3.3's "relied on is not owed": where reliance has made something owed, leaving is barred and not priced. The four predicates this bar is one of, and costed reach indexed by actor and authority, are Article 5's (§5.6).

## 3.10 Stateful invocation

A30 §7 states the difference between invoking a relationship and reusing a stored result: "A cached result normally substitutes for a computation without intentionally changing the underlying problem. Contracts can change the problem" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, Proposed, interpretive, A's, at the owner's U21 and U22). It writes invocation as stateful:

  Invoke(q, C_t) → (a_t, C_{t+1}),  C_{t+1} = Φ(C_t, q_t, a_t, f_t).

A32 extends the response kernel that the record holds from another exchange, K_S(q) = Law(a, τ, c, S′ | do(q), S) (CLAIM-1156, Proposed, interpretive, A's), to K_C(q) = Law(a, τ, κ, C′, S′ | do(q), C, S), where κ is "the distribution of costs among the participating processes". The comparison follows CLAIM-1095's correction (Active, demonstrated, counter): the post-state is observed "only through its later responses", not compared directly.

A32 §19 divides the one operation by its effect:

- *reproductive* invocation "Exercises and maintains an existing relationship";
- *formative* invocation "Participates in establishing a previously unformed relationship";
- *transformative* invocation "Modifies the specification or governance of an existing relationship".

"A single interaction can instantiate all three" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). A49's formal centre writes the transition as Invoke(q, S_t) → (a_t, C_{t+1}, S_{t+1}), and its abstract says invocation can "exercise, reproduce, establish or modify" a relationship. These are A49's restatements of A32.

**Two limits.** First, "It does not say what the right update rule Φ is", and no entry defines Φ. Second, the claim leans toward the null that would deflate it, as A30 says itself (below).

### What the cost model does not price

ChatGPT's A30 §7 is the hinge from §3.8's stored work to contracts, and the record holds it in A's voice: "Invoking a relationship differs from reusing a stored result in that the invocation can change the relationship it invokes, so one operation can reproduce, form or transform a contract, and a stored-work cost model that holds the store fixed between uses prices a contract's lifecycle but not its invocation, which returns an act, a distribution of costs over the parties and the relationship's next state" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, Proposed, interpretive). CASE-326's store "is the same before and after a use, and σ and ε enter as fixed parameters" (ARG-tmpa5ped). For a contract, the erosion of the parties' alternatives and the inadequacy of the arrangement can be "produced by its invocations", and then "the per-demand cost is path-dependent and the fixed-parameter formula misprices it" (ibid.). CASE-326's own note of 2026-11-24 calls this "A qualification of scope, not of the case: for a store whose use changes it, σ and ε are not fixed." The two halves fit: CLAIM-569's note says the exchange adds nothing to the lifecycle (§3.8.3), and CLAIM-tmpsf9hp says the model misprices invocation. Where an agreement is externally enforced and invocation leaves it unchanged, "the model applies as it stands". And the cost of resolving *which* relationship an act invokes is not one parameter: "Only the first of CLAIM-tmp80ny9's three kinds is a retrieval or verification cost" (ARG-tmpa5ped). Resolving relational disagreement or constitutive indeterminacy costs a negotiation or a formation (§3.7).

### The test, what is already done, and the expectation

The test is LEAD-tmp14n26 (Open, stage idea; A30 §15, with the design the assistant's). It would make the store "a state updated by its own use", with ε and σ "functions of the history of invocations rather than fixed parameters", and then:

1. build two relationships matched in per-use cost and output at the start, one whose invocations leave it unchanged and one whose invocations expand its practical jurisdiction and erode the recipient's alternatives;
2. price each over a horizon, per party, including revision and exit at the end;
3. ask whether "time-varying ε, σ and R suffice" or a term of a new kind is needed.

**What the record has already done.** The lead is *done in part*. Erosion that depends on how long a supply has done the work is demonstrated for a capacity learned by doing (CLAIM-602, Active, demonstrated). Two forms of support that give the same capabilities now "can leave it different capabilities later" (CLAIM-675, Active, demonstrated), which is step 1's matched pair for a capacity rather than a contract. A state whose funding follows its own use is run in CASE-343 (Active, executed study), and CLAIM-921's note of 2026-11-24 carries it to "rarely invoked guarantees": "The contract might remain formally recognized while its guarantee becomes unrealizable" (A32 §9). The refresh's search by mechanism found nothing further: CASE-412 and CASE-397 (both Active) run budgets that follow use, but the arrangement's terms do not change by being invoked. What no entry does is the contract case: an invocation that expands a relationship's jurisdiction, priced per party, with the question whether a new term is needed (the lead's note of 2026-11-24).

**The expectation, stated before any result.** ChatGPT, whose claim it is, expects richer state to cover most cases: "the critical test is whether these require genuinely new cost terms, or merely richer state variables and transition mechanisms within the existing model. I suspect the latter will cover most of the cases" (A30 §15, in CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). The lead's outcomes:

- CLAIM-tmpsf9hp "is defeated" "if the two relationships' later costs never part, or part only by an amount the fixed-parameter formula with a re-estimated ε reproduces";
- the "richer state" reading is confirmed "if time-varying ε, σ and R suffice";
- a new term is needed "if a per-party cost of resolving or contesting the invocation does not reduce to them" (LEAD-tmp14n26).

If richer state suffices, an invocation that changes the store is CASE-326 with state-dependent parameters, and "relational invocation" names a reparametrization, not a new cost. The claim's defeat condition anticipates this, citing A32 §21: "relational invocation" may be "only a vocabulary" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp).

### Where the kernel leaves the account

A51 has this article culminate in CLAIM-1156's kernel. What the record holds is less. CLAIM-1156 is Proposed and interpretive; its claim is that "Two organizations that give the same answers can differ in what answering does to them", observed "through its later responses rather than compared as a state", as CLAIM-1095 (Active, demonstrated, counter) requires. K_C is A32's extension of it, held in CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, and the update rule Φ that would say how invocation modifies the relational state is defined by no entry. Whether relational invocation adds explanatory value beyond the existing kernel, A51's own remaining question, is therefore the same open item as LEAD-tmp14n26's: a richer state within the model, or a new term. This article does not answer it.

## 3.13 What the article claims, and with what standing

1. Contracts can form between processes and can operationalize a standing that is not moral (the owner's U14; CLAIM-tmpkvzcn, CLAIM-tmpe6yfm; Proposed, interpretive). Priority is one carrier of that standing, not its home (TERM-tmpgiy6r, Proposed).
2. A relation is contract-like only if it governs "deviation, conflict or breach and not only the ordinary course". The four marks are necessary, and whether they are sufficient is open (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl, Proposed, interpretive, with the assistant's proposed fifth condition, untested).
3. Contracts can be implicit, reached from relied-upon regularity, with expectations that can be structural (the owner's U16; CLAIM-tmp0d1fq; CLAIM-tmp18v1o, with one documented specimen in CLAIM-tmpb1wyc and CASE-tmp3xxqg; Proposed, interpretive). Being relied on is not being owed (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, Proposed, on CLAIM-738, Active). When reliance makes something owed is open (QUESTION-tmprudey), and so is which of the owner's two readings of U17 holds (CLAIM-tmp05a1l, CLAIM-tmppx64t).
4. An operative norm can be stabilized by the reliance it attracts, and in A22's model it can outlast most of its baseline support (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx as corrected at the owner's objection CLAIM-tmp8l5s9; Proposed). This is a property of a stipulated model. An implicit contract's obligation can be held the same way where the provider cannot cheaply exit (CLAIM-tmpu77ve, Proposed, interpretive, untested).
5. Arrangements alike on the ordinary course can part under breach, failed assumption or invoked standing (CLAIM-tmpersgx, Proposed, interpretive). Its separating case reallocates delay and does not relieve it (CASE-tmptpna1).
6. The ambiguity about which relationship an act invokes has three kinds with different remedies (the owner's U21; CLAIM-tmp80ny9, Proposed). Who controls its resolution can matter more than how ambiguous it is (CLAIM-tmpro73c, Proposed). Joint and one-sided resolution of constitutive indeterminacy may be one act with two outcomes, the second exploitation only where its terms fail a declared standard (CLAIM-tmpisfi1, Proposed, the assistant's, untested).
7. A contract's lifecycle is priced by the general cost model of stored work: formation, upkeep, recognition, checking, revision and exit, with who pays as a term (CLAIM-569, CASE-326, CLAIM-892, CLAIM-760, Active; the contractual reading ARG-tmpa5ped and the model's one-trade claim CLAIM-891, Proposed). The values are illustrative. Deferral is not yet one of the record's movements of work (QUESTION-tmpzhnqy).

9. Invocation can change the relationship it invokes, and the cost model prices it only once invocation returns the next state (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, ARG-tmpa5ped; Proposed). Whether a richer state suffices is open (LEAD-tmp14n26, not run, done in part).
