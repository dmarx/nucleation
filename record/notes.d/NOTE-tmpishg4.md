# NOTE-tmpishg4: Revonsuo — Binding and the phenomenal unity of consciousness

## Contribution

It sorts the "binding problem" into several problems. The main cut is
between binding as the brain's recovery of a stimulus's unity, which can
happen in an anaesthetized animal, and binding as the construction of a
unified experience, which can happen with no unified stimulus, as in
random-dot stereograms and dreams. It then separates three levels of
description and five kinds of phenomenal binding, and uses the scheme to
say which empirical work on synchrony bears on consciousness.

## Key insight

Stimulus-related and consciousness-related binding dissociate in both
directions: synchrony to a real object under anaesthesia has no
phenomenal unity to correlate with, and a hallucinated object is unified
with no unity in the stimulus. So evidence for one is not evidence for
the other, and only studies in which perceptual unity is illusory or
hallucinatory, in conscious humans, isolate the second.

## Assumptions

- **Phenomenal unity is real and describable** at its own level, "by
  describing actual contents of consciousness".
- **Lower levels matter to consciousness research only as mechanisms of
  phenomenal binding**: cognitive and neural descriptions are "of interest
  to consciousness studies only insofar as they can be ... related to
  integration at the phenomenal level".
- **A direct neural correlate of a content must differ when the content
  differs.** This premise drives §5's conclusion.

## Key results

- **Two conceptions** (§1). Stimulus-related binding (Zeki's formulation):
  "any neural processing that represents stimulus unity, regardless of
  whether the activity in question is conscious or unconscious".
  Consciousness-related binding (Damasio's): how "internal subjective
  percepts, as phenomenally unified entities" are constructed by the
  brain. They "do not necessarily have anything to do with each other".
  "As far as I can tell, there is only one author in the literature,
  namely, John R. Smythies, who clearly distinguishes these two types",
  as BP1 and BP2 (1994).
- **Three levels** (§2): phenomenal, neural mechanisms, cognitive
  mechanisms. "For consciousness studies, the binding problem is
  primarily the problem of integration at the phenomenal level"; for
  neuroscience it is integrating single-neuron activity into "functional
  neuronal groups"; for cognitive science, unifying the outputs of
  modules. Searle's objection to Crick and Koch is read as a confusion of
  levels.
- **Five kinds of phenomenal binding** (§3), each with a dissociating
  deficit: property binding (achromatopsia shows a feature can be lost
  alone; illusory conjunctions show failure), part binding (some
  prosopagnosia), semantic-conceptual binding (semantic dementia; vague
  dream objects), location binding (Bálint's syndrome, dorsal
  simultanagnosia) and serial or event binding (akinetopsia; identity
  changes in dreams). Spatial grouping (apperceptive agnosia) is given
  first and folded into property binding. Above them is the global unity
  of one world with one self in it, Metzinger's "phenomenal Holon".
- **Synchrony** (§§4–5). Human EEG studies of coherent stimuli show
  stimulus-related synchrony; illusory-triangle and Dalmatian studies
  are confounded with target detection. In his own study (Revonsuo et
  al., 1997; N = 8, 20 electrodes), viewing a random-dot autostereogram
  as coherent or incoherent gave no difference in 36–44 Hz power, while
  40-Hz power rose 500–300 ms before subjects reported the gestalt. He
  concludes that 40-Hz synchrony is not the direct neural correlate of
  the content of visual awareness, but accompanies its construction,
  which "lends support" to Engel et al.'s claim in the same issue that
  temporal binding is a prerequisite for access to consciousness.

## Concepts

- **Stimulus-related binding**: neural recovery of a stimulus's unity,
  conscious or not.
- **Consciousness-related (phenomenal) binding**: the integration of
  phenomenal contents into unified wholes.
- **Levels of description**: phenomenal, neural, cognitive.
- **Property, part, semantic-conceptual, location and serial binding**:
  the five local kinds of phenomenal unity; global unity is the Holon.

## Connections

The cut is Smythies's BP1/BP2, which Revonsuo credits and quotes. Metzinger's
"phenomenal Holon" (1995) is his source for global unity; the record
holds Metzinger's self-model account (THEORY-063), which keeps the
unity of experience apart from the self-model. The property, part and
location kinds are Treisman's (1996). Engel, Fries, König, Brecht and
Singer's paper in the same issue is the synchrony thesis he tests.

## Bearing on the record

- **Which paper draws the distinction.** This paper draws a distinction
  between integration that need involve no consciousness and phenomenal
  unity, and it is its main thesis, so the record's identification of A27's
  "Revonsuo's 1999 treatment" with this paper fits. Two cautions. Its
  terms are "stimulus-related" and "consciousness-related", not
  "functional" and "phenomenal"; "functional" appears only of neuronal
  groups. And Revonsuo does not claim the distinction as his: he credits
  Smythies (1994). Whether the issue introduction also draws it was not
  checked; that copy could not be fetched.
- **CLAIM-355, "It does not solve phenomenal binding".** The claim quotes
  A27's five bindings, of which "functional and temporal" are "the middle
  two". Revonsuo's five are a different list: property, part,
  semantic-conceptual, location and serial binding, all phenomenal. His
  serial binding is the nearest to A27's temporal; nothing of his is
  "functional". So A27's five-part scheme is not Revonsuo's; the record
  does not attribute it to him and should not. Revonsuo does support the claim's
  separation: neural integration of a response can be real while saying
  nothing about phenomenal unity.

## Limitations

- **A conceptual paper with one experiment**, the author's own, reported
  in summary. The null result on sustained viewing is an absence of a
  difference in scalp band power, which cannot rule out synchrony at
  scales EEG does not see; the paper does not discuss this.
- **The dissociations are single**, not double, for most of the five
  kinds, and the deficits are cited, not analysed.
- **The distinction is not original**, by the author's own account.
- **Errors and slips.** None of substance found. "Reprsentative" and
  "entites" are typos in the published text.
