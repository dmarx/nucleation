# LIT-tmpz3i32: The rubber hand illusion revisited: visuotactile integration and self-attribution

Tsakiris, M. & Haggard, P. (2005). *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance* 31(1): 80–91. doi:10.1037/0096-1523.31.1.80. PMID 15709864.

Not read: no lawful full text was reached (see the status note). The

## Why this is here

It is the classic statement that the rubber-hand illusion needs both
visuotactile synchrony (bottom-up) and a body-like object fitting one's own
body representation (top-down). Its drift measure is the paradigm's
standard implicit measure. Lush ([LIT-tmpozu5y](LIT-tmpozu5y.md)) argues that drift has never
been shown resistant to demand characteristics. In Lush's posted data,
people who were never stroked already expect more felt drift after
synchronous than asynchronous stroking ([NOTE-tmptyehi](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptyehi.md), the record's
computation). Lush also counts "top-down" accounts of this kind among
those that may be describing expectancy effects. A reading should check
whether any of its four experiments has a contrast whose demand is
plausibly matched. It bears on [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)'s C6. The free-energy self
([LIT-tmpetw17](LIT-tmpetw17.md), read in this batch, [NOTE-tmp69t3e](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp69t3e.md)) cites it for
rubber-hand ownership without re-examining it.
