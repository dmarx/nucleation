# THEORY-492: Life was forced into existence by geochemistry: core metabolism, rooted in the reductive citric-acid cycle, formed as a channel relieving the free-energy stresses abiotic processes keep building, as lightning relieves a charge separation, so its early steps would recur on any similar planet and selection on metabolism came before replication

## Source

Morowitz & Smith, *Complexity* 13(1):51–59 (2007), [LIT-528](../literature.d/LIT-528.md), read in full in
[NOTE-425](../notes.d/NOTE-425.md) from Santa Fe Institute Working Paper 2006-08-029 (7 August 2006).
The journal version was not seen and may differ. The formal results the
paper leans on (Smith 1998, 1999, 2005; Corbet & Morowitz 1972) are not in
the record and were taken as the paper describes them.

[NOTE-425](../notes.d/NOTE-425.md) concludes that "no new THEORY is indicated". This document files
the paper's thesis as Proposed, because it is a definite account of the
origin of life that the record's other dissipation papers do not state.

## What was actually shown

**The thesis.** "The continuous generation of sources of free energy by
abiotic processes may have forced life into existence as a means to
alleviate the buildup of free energy stresses." If so, life "had to emerge
on the earth" (C1, a conjecture, stated as one).

**The analogy.** Lightning and hurricanes are channels that carry charge
or heat between reservoirs far faster than diffusion. Metabolism is such a
channel "in the chemical domain", carrying redox electrons from H₂ to CO₂
and phosphate from polymer to orthophosphate.

**The evidence from metabolism (§II).** The anabolic core of autotrophs is
fewer than 500 molecules under 400 Da, rooted in the eleven carboxylic
acids of the citric-acid cycle. Run in reverse, the cycle "can duplicate
its own members from abiotic CO₂ and electrons", which the paper calls
network autocatalysis. The cascade built on it "leaves no energetic waste
products". The cycle is said to capture "about one third" of the redox
energy from CO₂ to acetate, fatty-acid synthesis another third and
methanogenesis the rest; these figures are stated, not computed (C2,
moderate as a description).

**The framework (§III).** Statistical mechanics as inference: maximum
entropy, with entropies allowed to depend on currents, predicts that
currents form, with Ohm's and Fourier's laws as the linear cases. With
positive feedback, thresholds appear that are phase transitions
"equivalent in all statistical respects to equilibrium phase transitions",
and the ordered state is a better channel. The principle is "not
equivalent to maximization of the inter-reservoir current".
Entropy-production principles "ultimately fail as general principles"
(C4, a summary of prior work, with no argument given here).

**The collapse (§IV).** The abiotic earth was metastable, and the emergence
of life was its "collapse" to greater stability, like phase separation in
an alloy. Later chemical transitions should be further, irreversible
collapses (C6, an analogy).

**Metabolism first.** Selective advantage came from interaction with
metabolites before replication was coordinated, which the authors note is
"directly opposite" to the RNA-world premise (C7, a hypothesis).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Nothing in the paper could have.
It reports no test. What could fail is the programme's open question: a
network supplied with geochemical free energy could relax without forming
the reductive cycle.

## What this does not say

- **That it has been shown.** The authors say the thesis "has not been
  quantitatively demonstrated", and that the framework has "few instances
  that usefully reach beyond equilibrium".
- **That present metabolism records its origin.** The case reads the
  structure of today's metabolism back into biogenesis, which it assumes
  ("modern metabolism recapitulates the process of biogenesis"). The
  authors also say that "universality due to descent from common ancestors
  becomes indistinguishable from universality due to evolutionary
  convergence", which takes away the observation that would tell the
  thesis from its rival.
- **That both energy sources were there.** The phosphate half is flagged as
  uncertain: polyphosphates are not reported at hydrothermal vents
  (fn. 5). The placement of methanogenesis is disputed (fn. 6).
- **That biomass is a thermodynamic state.** The "collapse" is an analogy;
  the authors say biomass is "probably" not such a state.
- **That living and non-living channels differ in kind.** The one asymmetry
  allowed is the "informational richness" of life's chemical substrate.
  Individuality is "a by-product of cellular form".
- **That entropy production is maximized.** The paper rejects that.

## Connections

- **Gradient dissipation ([THEORY-532](THEORY-532.md)).** Schneider and Kay read the
  biosphere as a channel too, and also reject entropy-production
  principles, but put a restated second law in their place.
- **Maximum entropy production ([THEORY-671](THEORY-671.md)).** The paper's Jaynesian
  framework is the same inference Dewar applies to paths ([LIT-521](../literature.d/LIT-521.md)), used
  here to predict currents rather than to derive MEP, and the paper rejects
  MEP. No `rivals` edge is declared, because the paper gives no argument
  against MEP of its own.
- **England's replication bound ([THEORY-679](THEORY-679.md))** argues that a
  fast RNA replicator is thermodynamically possible where a DNA one is not.
  It concerns which replicator, not whether replication came first.
- **Self-organized cycles.** The reductive cycle's network autocatalysis
  is the chemical counterpart of the spatially self-organized catalytic
  cycles of [LIT-036](../literature.d/LIT-036.md). Neither paper connects them.
- **The demarcation question.** On life's continuity with lightning, see
  [LIT-192](../literature.d/LIT-192.md) and [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md), and [THEORY-072](THEORY-072.md) for the record's account of where
  the line falls.
