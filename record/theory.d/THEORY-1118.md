# THEORY-1118: McKenna and Coates argue, with Dryden and with Buss and Westlund, that a condition on free agency stated only in the agent's present psychological structure is met by an agent whose structure a manipulator installed, so a mesh account must count that agent free or add a historical condition; Frankfurt, read first-hand, takes the first option

## Source

- McKenna & Coates, SEP *Compatibilism*, [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §§4.2.2, 4.3 and
  supplement §A, read in full in [NOTE-270](../notes.d/NOTE-270.md).
- Dryden, IEP *Autonomy* (2010), [LIT-297](../literature.d/LIT-297.md), §2.a.ii, read in full in [NOTE-266](../notes.d/NOTE-266.md).
- Buss & Westlund, SEP *Personal Autonomy* (2018 revision), [LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md), §3,
  read in full in [NOTE-267](../notes.d/NOTE-267.md).
- Frankfurt (1971), [LIT-692](../literature.d/LIT-692.md), read in full in [NOTE-545](../notes.d/NOTE-545.md): p. 20 and note 10.
- Frankfurt (2004), [LIT-293](../literature.d/LIT-293.md), the Tanner Lectures, read in full in
  [NOTE-264](../notes.d/NOTE-264.md): p. 172.

## The argument

A "mesh" or "snapshot" account makes free agency a relation among the
agent's present attitudes, and says nothing about how they came to be
there. A manipulator who installs the right mesh therefore produces an
agent the account must call free. [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md) argues this holds for every mesh
compatibilism it surveys (Frankfurt, Wolf, Fischer & Ravizza, Haji,
Wallace, Bok and Mele): each must say what is wrong with manipulation cases
or "bite the bullet". [LIT-297](../literature.d/LIT-297.md) makes the same objection against
hierarchical autonomy (the hypnotist case), and [LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md) argues that
coherence is not sufficient for minimal autonomy because the endorsement
itself can be manipulated.

Frankfurt takes the first option, first-hand. In 1971 his conception "appears
to be neutral" on how the will came to be, and note 10 says that if the
willing addict "has been made an addict by the deliberate and calculated
work of another", it "may be that both the addict and this other person
are fully responsible" (p. 20). In 2004: "What counts is our current
effort … and not the story of how we came to be" (p. 172). [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md) also
quotes his 2002 reply to Fischer, second-hand, to the same effect.

## What this does not say

- **That manipulated agents are unfree.** That is the premise the
  manipulation argument needs, and Frankfurt denies it. The argument forces
  a choice; it does not settle which option is right.
- **That historical conditions fail.** Whether any historical condition in
  the record escapes the argument is [THEORY-040](THEORY-040.md)'s second half, Proposed,
  held back for want of a first-hand reading of Pereboom's four-case
  argument or Mele's zygote argument.
