# ARG-065: That ordinary interpretation and response can hide a system's dispositions, their governance and their maintenance, and that interpretation and authority act on each other, favour one model of meaning and will only if that model predicts responses to combined interventions that separate models of matched complexity miss

## The inference

A55 §7 of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition",
second share (https://chatgpt.com/share/6aeea2bc-d280-83e9-ac1f-f6772adf263c):

1. "A system's current interpretations and actual responses may fail to
   reveal its dispositions to interpret differently under alternative
   contexts, its governance of those dispositions, or its means of
   maintaining them." [CLAIM-407](../claims.d/CLAIM-407.md) and [CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md) (Active, demonstrated) show
   that matched ordinary behaviour can hide governance, efficacy and
   maintenance; [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md) (Active) that a perspective's dispositions can
   outlast its enactment.
2. "Because interpretation and authority can influence one another, a joint
   model may predict responses to sequences of contextual and governance
   interventions that separate models miss." [CLAIM-297](../claims.d/CLAIM-297.md)'s loop, in which an
   operator's authority is updated by its performance on the
   interpretation, and [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md)'s instability, which "only the loop can
   do", are that mutual influence inside one coupled model.
3. So, A proposes, the joint model of [CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md) is the better explanation.

A's own verdict on step 3: "But that final **may** is the unresolved
point." A55 §7 reads [CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md)'s blocker rightly: "no decisive joint
prediction has been derived, and an existing laboratory model with frames,
operators, and a governor performed no better than a one-feature selector."
"Our discussion does not remove that blocker."

## Critical questions

- **Which rivals were considered?** Separate interpretation and governance
  models "under matched complexity and training information" (A55 §7), and
  [QUESTION-088](../questions.d/QUESTION-088.md)'s phenomenon-specific models, against which its answer is a
  held-out comparison with flexibility penalized.
- **Does a rival explain as well?** On the record, yes. Step 1's premises
  say what ordinary observation hides; separate models can represent a
  hidden governance bit ([CASE-125](../cases.d/CASE-125.md)) and a hidden allocation of efficacy as
  well as a joint one. Step 2's premises are results inside a coupled
  model, not comparisons with separate ones: they show the loop can do
  something, not that only a joint model predicts it. [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md) is about
  the persistence of perspectives and does no work for the joint model.
  The lab's one version with frames, operators and a governor "predicts
  exactly as a one-feature selector" ([CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md), note of 2026-10-29).
- **Would more evidence discriminate them?** Yes, and A55 §7 says how:
  evaluate on "held-out **compositions of context changes and governance
  interventions**", especially "history-dependent responses after an
  intervention that changes which processes can influence later
  interpretation". "If a simpler independent model predicts just as well,
  the proposed integration gains little support." That is [QUESTION-088](../questions.d/QUESTION-088.md)'s
  test with a target, and the second half of [CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md)'s defeated_if.

The argument stays Proposed until that test is run, and it carries no
weight for [CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md) before then.
