# ARG-tmpvn8p8: The symmetry that hides a protected beacon's miscalibration, applied to any number of channels reading one physical witness, shows that comparison identifies every difference among their errors and not their common mode, and so narrows the owner's point that physical phenomena are an independent witness that ends the regress of anchors

## The argument

[CLAIM-tmpklggb](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpklggb.md) shows one protected beacon's offset is hidden by a
symmetry: shifting the world and every offset together changes no report.
The shift acts on all channels at once, so adding channels that read the
same witness adds equations for the differences between their offsets
and none for the common shift. That is the rank result in [CLAIM-tmpnl4xk](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpnl4xk.md).
The owner's witness is therefore decisive for differences, and silent on
the frame. The frame is fixed by a standard, which ends the regress by
definition.

## Critical questions

- **Does the symmetry cover the witness case?** Yes, for channels whose
  errors are offsets, and for channels with known gains ([CLAIM-tmpklggb](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpklggb.md)).
  A channel whose error is not of a symmetric form, say a nonlinear
  distortion, can be partly identified from the shape of its errors. The
  claim is scoped to the offset case.
- **Does it reject the owner's point?** No. It says what the witness
  certifies (differences, invariants) and what must be added (a
  standard).
- **Is the metrology right?** It is cited from standard knowledge.
  Nothing in the claim rests on more than the statement that units are
  defined against fixed constants and calibrations are traced to them.
