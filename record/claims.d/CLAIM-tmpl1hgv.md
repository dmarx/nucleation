# CLAIM-tmpl1hgv: What a team's separable members supply is separable sites of intervention, not separable units of perspective: they make a team an experimental model of self-like organization, since members can be questioned, given evidence, denied access, replaced or removed one at a time, while the team's own dispositions may depend on relations among them that no member's isolated profile records

## The claim

A21 of the exchange "Explain First Contact RL"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b037165-3a1c-83ea-9d7c-311c7807acd3). For a
person, the record has to infer where one perspective ends and another
begins, and there may be no unique answer. A team comes with "a relatively
stable partition into collaborators": each can be asked a question
independently, their answers compared, their influence on one another
observed, and a participant removed or replaced. "That gives us a powerful
experimental advantage: we can distinguish changes occurring within a
collaborator from changes arising through collaboration."

The partition into members is not a partition into perspectives
(CLAIM-tmp7580l), and that is the point: "the team's boundaries are
accessible enough for us to intervene on its constituents, while its
collective organization remains something to be explained."

A models a team as T_t = (C_1(t), …, C_n(t), R_t, G_t), members, record and
governance, with a team-level dispositional profile D_T(q, h) =
P(Y_T | do(q), h), h the history of interactions and revisions. "D_T need
not be reconstructible from the independent response profiles of the
collaborators. Its behavior may depend on the relational organization that
exists only when they interact." The record may be one mechanism by which
that organization persists across interruptions and turnover
(CLAIM-tmpqr0tn).

## How a team can answer dissent

A21 §4 gives four responses of a team when one member is given evidence
against the team's current hypothesis:

- **fragmentation**: the change disrupts coordination and the team cannot
  reorganize;
- **conformity**: apparent agreement is restored by suppressing or
  ignoring the dissent;
- **centralized arbitration**: a designated authority settles it, at a
  supervisory cost;
- **adaptive integration**: the disagreement stays expressible and
  consequential, and the team adjusts its shared organization while
  continuing to work together.

"The team's integration isn't simply convergence upon a common belief. It
is a maintained capacity to coordinate through differences." The parallel
A draws is to a person: one disposition changes, another resists, and the
larger organization must accommodate, suppress, arbitrate or reorganize.

## Where the gap is

A gives no case for "D_T need not be reconstructible", and the condition
needs one stated precisely. A filled step, in the assistant's voice: what
fails is reconstruction from profiles measured in isolation, not from the
members' full conditional profiles. Two members each of whom, alone,
answers a question at chance, and who together answer it correctly because
each holds half the evidence and they exchange it, have a team-level
profile no isolated profile predicts; given each member's response
conditional on the other's messages, it is predicted. So the claim is that
team-level dispositions are not fixed by isolated profiles, and the
interventions that tell the difference are on the channels between
members (LEAD-tmph7m7s). The wording of this paragraph is the assistant's
(Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
