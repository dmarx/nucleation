# THEORY-tmp3ucjb: On a contested shared canvas, the artworks that last are those whose coalitions keep repairing them under continual overwriting, not those left unattacked, as Wu and Silva propose from the 2017 r/place lifecycles

## Source

Wu & Silva (2024; v3 2026), [LIT-1032](../literature.d/LIT-1032.md), read in [NOTE-724](../notes.d/NOTE-724.md). The paper's §6
and Fig. 8 present successful coalitions as showing collaborative and
adversarial actions together throughout their lives. [NOTE-724](../notes.d/NOTE-724.md) records it as
claim C6, "lasting artworks persist under sustained overwriting, not in its
absence", with weak support.

## What is asserted

That on a contested canvas, lasting is a matter of continued repair under
attack, not of being left alone: as a population, the artworks that survive
to the end are the ones whose coalitions kept repainting them while they
were overwritten.

## What the record holds toward it

[THEORY-216](THEORY-216.md), which is Active, holds what the log measured: early survival
is barely predictable from four coalition features, coalitions consolidate,
and *some* lasting artworks were held under sustained overwriting. The last
is shown in ten chosen lifecycles. No population measure, such as the share
of lasting artworks with sustained repair, is given.

## What this does not say

- That persistence under repair is the only way to last. Falmagne et al.
  ([THEORY-214](THEORY-214.md)) find some compositions safe through neglect on the 2022
  canvas.
- That the recovered coalitions are the real ones. They are inferred from
  co-placement.
- Anything about why coalitions consolidate.

## What would confirm or reject it

The promote_when. [QUESTION-169](../questions.d/QUESTION-169.md) asks it together with whether interaction
features restore early predictability. If it holds, [CLAIM-280](../claims.d/CLAIM-280.md) has its case
of persistence by contest and repair as a population fact, not an
illustration.
