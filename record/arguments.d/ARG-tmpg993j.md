# ARG-tmpg993j: The Yoneda lemma shows that relations constitute what a communicative act is

## The inference

In a specified category an object's full hom-functor fixes it up to
isomorphism. Read as "an object is determined by its relations", that
would make a communicative act what its relations make it, which is
[CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md): which act an utterance performs "is partly constituted by its
relations to participant roles, norms and expected uptake".

## Critical questions

- **Does the mapping premise hold?** Only as categorical identification.
  A191 §4: "Yoneda establishes the first. Our experiments investigate the
  second. Moderate communicative structural realism asserts the third.
  Neither of the first two logically entails the third." A187: "Yoneda
  establishes the first. It does not independently establish the second."
- **Are the model's assumptions met in the new domain?** No, and this is
  the question that fails. The record's reading of the lemma ([THEORY-032](../theory.d/THEORY-032.md),
  from [LIT-221](../literature.d/LIT-221.md), read in [NOTE-195](../notes.d/NOTE-195.md)): it fixes an object "only up to
  isomorphism, among objects the category already has". y(c) is indexed by
  every object of C. "So as a formal version of 'an object is determined
  by its relations' it presupposes the relata. It cannot establish
  constitution, and it gives elimination of the relata no support at
  all." Lam and Wüthrich ([LIT-222](../literature.d/LIT-222.md)) reach the matching conclusion for
  category theory generally: morphisms relate objects the category
  presupposes.

## Verdict

Rejected. [CLAIM-150](../claims.d/CLAIM-150.md) undercuts it: an ontology of intrinsically
individuated objects with relational properties reproduces the
categorical identification, so identification underdetermines
constitution. That is a claim about the subject matter, and it leaves the
lemma and [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) standing. The result suits the non-eliminative choice
the owner made at U54. What would establish constitution is A214 §13's
"Conceptual argument", which is named, not given ([QUESTION-028](../questions.d/QUESTION-028.md)).
