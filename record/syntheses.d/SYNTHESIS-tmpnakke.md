# SYNTHESIS-tmpnakke: What a record of a developing argument can secure is traceable change over its accountability structure, which is neither sufficiency for revision nor correctness: current conclusions and support topology do not fix revisions, a sufficient state makes history redundant only while its model and decisions hold, and what kept justifications can buy is a wider family of decisions and decisions a bounded agent could not otherwise make

## The account

A developing argument drops things legitimately, so a record of it cannot
aim at preservation: what it can secure is "change that is announced and
located" (CLAIM-189), and a manuscript is then a view checked against
that record rather than the argument's only copy (CLAIM-178). The
interlocutor's reading gives traceability an object: "the relevant
fidelity object is different", the accountability structure of what was
asserted, why, how it was challenged and what justified its revision
(CLAIM-250). Both are Proposed.

The exchange of 2026-10-14 (workbench entry of 2026-10-15) then fixed,
by construction, what such a record is and is not. Two histories can
agree on every current conclusion and call for different revisions
(CLAIM-294, CASE-093), and two records with the same support graph can
call for different revisions when one support fails (CLAIM-293,
CASE-089), so neither current conclusions nor the topology of
justification fixes revision. A state sufficient for a decision family
under a model makes the rest of the history worthless for those
decisions, and stops being sufficient when a source is discredited, the
model is revised or the decisions change (CLAIM-300, CASE-082).
Sufficiency, accountability and correctness are three properties, each
able to hold while the other two fail (CLAIM-299, with CASE-082, CASE-084
and CASE-093); CLAIM-299's body draws the link to CLAIM-189: "This says
traceability is not sufficiency either." What a kept record of
justifications can add is therefore not information for decisions the
current state already suffices for, but a wider family of decisions and
decisions a bounded agent could not otherwise afford (CLAIM-295, resting
on CLAIM-294 and CLAIM-300; Proposed). CASE-086 and CASE-090 illustrate
the two operations this supports: tracing what a defeated premise
carried, which propagates "obligations to review", and reopening an
alternative whose defeater is defeated.

What stays open is the optimum and the test: the least state a family of
revisions needs, and whether it looks like provenance, dependencies and
defeated alternatives (QUESTION-105); which mechanism a structured
record's advantage runs through (QUESTION-108, with CASE-091 as the
design); how to judge sufficiency when inquiry changes its own decision
family (QUESTION-112); and what a manuscript's choice of claims must
preserve (QUESTION-258).

## Admission

Not admitted. Each route was tried against the components as written.

- **Derived.** The valid inferences from the demonstrated claims and
  cases are already held. That what the record secures is not
  sufficiency is in CLAIM-299's body; that it is not correctness is
  CASE-084, which A6 called "a direct companion" to CLAIM-181 and
  CLAIM-190 (CLAIM-299's body; CLAIM-190 is Superseded). That keeping
  justificatory structure does not keep what revision needs is
  CLAIM-293 against CLAIM-250 and CLAIM-295, said in CLAIM-250's note of 2026-10-30. That history's value, where the state
  suffices, is not informational is CLAIM-295's title, resting on
  CLAIM-294 and CLAIM-300. That conclusions fall short and a sufficient
  state is enough for a fixed family, so the optimum lies between, is
  QUESTION-105's body ("bound it from both sides"). That this record's
  own `rests_on` and `grounds` record "that support exists, not how
  much" is CLAIM-293's summary, and that they propagate review and not
  belief is CASE-086. The remaining combinations are conjunctions, which
  are trivial. One candidate does not follow: that whether a dropped
  commitment was dissolution or loss (CLAIM-189) is relative to a
  decision family the argument itself changes (CLAIM-300, QUESTION-112).
  It needs a premise no component holds, that a legitimate drop is a drop
  of what is redundant for the argument's decisions; CLAIM-189 makes
  legitimacy a matter of rejection, and the record keeps what it drops
  with its status.
- **Hypothesis.** Taken whole, the account conjoins its components, and
  a conjunction cannot fail while every conjunct holds. The narrower
  integrating sentence, that what keeping the accountability structure
  buys is CLAIM-295's value, has three readings. As "can buy", it is
  stated: CLAIM-250's note says CLAIM-295 "gives it a possible
  instrumental justification", and CLAIM-295's summary says "this says
  what preserving it could buy". As "is worth keeping for", it turns on
  cost, which CLAIM-295 disclaims and leaves to QUESTION-098, a free
  parameter; as "buys only that", it is against CLAIM-295's own fallback,
  that the structure "might still be justified by inspectability,
  auditing, and shared human–agent coordination".
- **Integration.** The discriminating comparison the line names is
  CASE-091, and QUESTION-108 holds its three outcomes open. No component
  gives evidence for one outcome, so an integration settling it would
  have nothing to stand on.

**Whether the line falls into sub-parts.** It does not. Counting each
