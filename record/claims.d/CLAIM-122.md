# CLAIM-122: Will-organization is not one more higher-order preference: changing the conditions of efficacy changes behaviour while the competing preferences stay fixed

## The claim

"Suppose the underlying attitudes remain approximately constant while an
intervention changes who may halt deployment ... In both cases, practical
behavior can change because the conditions of efficacy change."

## What it does not say

That preferences play no part. It answers the individual-attitude version of
the objection only; see [CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md).

## Note of 2026-10-18: the supervisory test, and higher-order volition against metacontrol

The exchange of 2026-10-17 re-derived this claim three times without
citing it.

- **A7 §5 gave it a formal model.** u_t = Σ_i g_i(z_t, x_t) u_{i,t}, with
  competing proposals u_i and governance weights or gates g_i. The test:
  "**Hold the competing action proposals fixed, intervene on the
  governance relations, and measure whether the operative action
  changes.**" Its caveat: "Of course, an ordinary software scheduler can
  exhibit this. That would establish the functional distinction, not that
  the scheduler possesses will." That is [CLAIM-116](CLAIM-116.md): regulation, even
  higher-order regulation, does not establish a will.
- **A8 §5 separated the attitude from the machinery.** "**Higher-order
  volition is an attitude concerning what should be effective. Metacontrol
  is the causal machinery capable of altering what becomes effective.**
  They may be coupled; they are not identical." A8 uses "endorsement" in
  the reflective, Frankfurtian sense. The record's endorsement ([TERM-053](../terms.d/TERM-053.md))
  is an effect of one process on another's share of influence, and is not
  Frankfurt's, so a filing from A8 says higher-order volition. A8's
  candidate claim is this one with [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md), and was not filed. Its
  defeater is worth keeping: "cases in which maintained authority
  relations add no explanatory or predictive power beyond the first-order
  motives and evaluative states alone." That is this claim's defeater,
  widened from preferences to evaluative states.
- **A12's Proposition 2 is this claim restated.** "Construct two systems
  whose default action trajectories are identical", and "An intervention
  changing governance while holding proposals fixed can change future
  action in the second model without affecting the first." A15 §3.5
  ("Behavioral equivalence and organizational difference") kept it. No
  parameters were given.

[CASE-112](../cases.d/CASE-112.md) illustrates it: with the higher-order input held fixed, a
change in the structure of the competition switches the regime.

## Note of 2026-10-21: the first instance with numbers

Re-derived again, this time cited. A15 §1 of the exchange of 2026-10-20
cites the supervisory test ("hold competing action proposals fixed,
intervene on governance relations, and ask whether the operative action
changes"). A15 §2 then built it as a counterexample with parameters
(proposals u₁ = 0, u₂ = 1; an authorization input connected in one system
and not the other), and A16 §3 gave it noise, a closure statement and a
deficiency of 0.5 bit. That is filed as [CASE-tmpdc0tl](../cases.d/CASE-tmpdc0tl.md), which illustrates
this claim, and its general form as [CLAIM-tmppi0xf](CLAIM-tmppi0xf.md), with the profile it
asks a description to keep as [TERM-tmpf5k7x](../terms.d/TERM-tmpf5k7x.md). A17 made it its paper's
Proposition 1 and listed it as "Established by the paper's self-contained
finite counterexample", without the note of 2026-10-18, which already
holds A12's Proposition 2 in words. This is the first instance with
numbers. [CLAIM-116](CLAIM-116.md) still holds: A16, "K is simply a hidden mechanism
parameter."
