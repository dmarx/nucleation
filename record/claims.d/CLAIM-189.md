# CLAIM-189: Fidelity is the wrong measure of a developing argument: maturing drops and changes things legitimately, so what a record can secure is traceability, change that is announced and located, not preservation

## The claim

The transport line asks what survives a transformation, and its measures
reward preservation. A translation that drops a commitment has lost
something. An argument that drops a commitment may have matured. [TERM-052](../terms.d/TERM-052.md),
the owner's "crystallized argument", says so: "Maturing drops things
legitimately", and an item missing from a later crystallized argument "may
be dissolution, an item rejected as the argument matured. Or it may be
loss."

The transport line already allows for goals other than fidelity.
"Fidelity is one evaluative goal among adaptation, critique and parody"
([CLAIM-034](CLAIM-034.md)). Developing an argument is closer to those than to translation.
Applied to it, a fidelity profile would score every legitimate dissolution
as distortion, and would prefer an argument that never changed.

So the comparison in [CLAIM-191](CLAIM-191.md) holds for the mechanism of drift, how
unanchored restatement moves, but not for the goal. What the record
secures is not that the argument stays the same. It is that changes are
traceable: a superseded claim names its successor, a dropped claim keeps
its status and loses its `works` membership visibly ([CLAIM-178](CLAIM-178.md)), and a
change of thesis is a recorded supersession rather than a silent one.
Convergence is not preservation ([CLAIM-081](CLAIM-081.md)), and for an argument
preservation is not the aim either.

## What would answer it

A fidelity profile computed over the record's supersession chains that
separates announced from unannounced change, so that fidelity, suitably
defined, does measure what the record secures. Then the two claims would
not be in tension.

## Note of 2026-10-12: a disclaimer of the normative reading, left to the owner

PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) filed [CLAIM-252](CLAIM-252.md), Active, which says "The account does not say
which distinctions a translation ought to keep", and that what ought to be
kept "is set by the purpose the translation serves". [CLAIM-259](CLAIM-259.md),
Proposed, restates the decision term so that it "counts no improvement as
loss". This claim says a fidelity profile "would prefer an argument that
never changed", which reads the transport line's measure as a norm.
[CLAIM-252](CLAIM-252.md) disclaims that norm for the account. Whether this claim's
point survives the disclaimer, or narrows to the measure, is left to the
owner.
