# THEORY-2282: Small incentives doubled immunisation over camps

## The account

Banerjee and colleagues ([NOTE-2418](../notes.d/NOTE-2418.md)): "Improving reliability of
services improves immunisation rates, but the effect remains modest.
Small incentives have large positive impacts on the uptake of
immunisation services in resource poor areas and are more cost effective
than purely improving supply." The incentive "was mainly to reduce the
number of children dropping out after three injections."

## What it rests on

- Clustered randomization of villages, stratified by block; blind
  surveys; self-reports checked against scars, cards and logbooks.
- Costs to Seva Mandir, whose fixed camp day makes cost per child fall
  with attendance.

## What it does not say

- What incentives would do without reliable camps: "we could not
  introduce an incentive programme without simultaneously making the
  supply more reliable."
- That the result holds where baseline coverage is higher.
