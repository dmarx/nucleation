# LIT-tmp3deyb: The World of Emotions is not Two-Dimensional

Johnny R. J. Fontaine, Klaus R. Scherer, Etienne B. Roesch & Phoebe C.
Ellsworth (2007), *Psychological Science* 18(12):1050–1057 —
DOI-10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.02024.x

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* From the abstract:

- "Today, many researchers focus exclusively on two-dimensional models
  involving valence and arousal."
- "We show for three languages that four dimensions are needed to
  satisfactorily represent similarities and differences in the meaning of
  emotion words. In order of importance, these dimensions are
  evaluation-pleasantness, potency-control, activation-arousal, and
  unpredictability."
- "They were identified on the basis of the applicability of 144 features
  representing the six components of emotions": appraisals,
  psychophysiological changes, motor expressions, action tendencies,
  subjective experiences and emotion regulation.

What could not be checked without the text: the languages, the loadings,
how the potency-control dimension is defined, and whether the paper
discusses Mehrabian and Russell's dominance axis by name.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for a terminological reason. The record's account of
valence (CLAIM-tmpz8lli, TERM-tmp8gbrt) used "dominance" for an ordering
that wins a system's internal negotiation. The same word names the third
axis of Mehrabian and Russell's pleasure–arousal–dominance model, and the
component process model's own school uses it for Fontaine et al.'s second
dimension: "other dimensions that characterize the affective space are
arousal, dominance, and novelty (e.g., Fontaine et al., 2007, in press)"
(Shuman et al. 2013, p. 4, NOTE-tmpogww2). That dimension
corresponds to the power and control appraisals, the agent's capacity to
influence its situation. It is a different thing from the record's notion,
which concerns which of the system's own orderings prevails. The rename to
"ascendancy" keeps them apart. How the two interact is noted in
NOTE-tmpmobow: high control amplifies the efferent effects
of goal congruence.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
