# LIT-1275: What is embodiment? A psychometric approach

Longo, M. R., Schüür, F., Kammers, M. P. M., Tsakiris, M. & Haggard, P. (2008). What is embodiment? A psychometric approach. *Cognition* 107(3), 978–998. DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2007.12.004.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1004](../notes.d/NOTE-1004.md).*

- **The illusion has structure.** Across 131 people, ratings of 27
  statements after 60 s of brushing fell into four components in both
  conditions: embodiment of the rubber hand (26.3% of variance after
  synchrony), loss of own hand, movement and affect. A fifth, deafference
  (pins and needles, numbness, a less vivid hand), appeared only after
  asynchronous brushing.
- **Embodiment splits three ways.** A second analysis of the ten items
  loading on embodiment gave ownership, location and agency in both
  conditions. Ownership and location were positive after synchrony and
  negative after asynchrony. Agency, over a hand that never moved, was
  negative after both.
- **Ownership and drift go together.** Felt hand position moved 1.34 cm
  toward the rubber hand after synchrony and a non-significant 0.30 cm
  after asynchrony (n = 120). Location (β = .485) and ownership (β = .425)
  each predicted the drift; agency did not.
- **The rubber hand displaces the real one.** A "loss of own hand"
  component (paralysis, disappearance, loss of control) was higher after
  synchrony. The authors read this as a principle of "body constancy": the
  new hand replaces the old one rather than being added to it.
- **The method is the claim.** Structure that recurs across participants
  and conditions, and that predicts a behavioural measure, is offered as
  evidence that structured introspective report can measure a complex
  experience. No control separates that structure from expectation.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request for experimental work building
on the rubber hand illusion, alongside Lush 2020, Lush et al. 2020,
Ehrsson et al. 2022 with Lush & Seth 2022, Roseboom & Lush 2022, Rohde,
Di Luca & Ernst 2011, Abdulkarim & Ehrsson 2015 and Kalckert & Ehrsson
2012. Read in full: **Active**.

It is the first replication in the record with a large sample, named
tests and drift measured in both conditions, so it bears on
[THEORY-765](../theory.d/THEORY-765.md)'s promotion test, though not on its suggestion control. It
splits ownership from location within a hand, which is a part-level
analogue of Blanke & Metzinger's split between self-identification and
self-location ([THEORY-768](../theory.d/THEORY-768.md)); it measures nothing about the whole body.
Its "loss of own hand" component is a questionnaire sign of the trade-off
[THEORY-769](../theory.d/THEORY-769.md) predicts. It reports ownership without agency in the
condition means and the two cohering across people, and the authors draw
both conclusions; that bears on [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md) without testing its comparator.
It says nothing about interoception ([THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md)). Its argument that
covariance among reports reveals the structure of an experience is a
case for [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md). Haggard is also the author of [LIT-1009](LIT-1009.md), whose
agency is the narrow motor sense this paper's agency items do not reach.
The NOTE sets this out.

Filed alongside, earlier in the batch: Botvinick & Cohen 1998 ([LIT-1277](LIT-1277.md)),
Lenggenhager et al. 2007 ([LIT-1279](LIT-1279.md)), Blanke & Metzinger 2009
([LIT-1274](LIT-1274.md)), Apps & Tsakiris 2014 ([LIT-1262](LIT-1262.md)), Seth 2013 ([LIT-1263](LIT-1263.md)).

One of the sources of [THEORY-766](../theory.d/THEORY-766.md) (filed 2026-10-20), which restates the rubber hand synchrony effect as a difference in reports, separates drift from those reports, and leaves open whether expectation produces the difference.
