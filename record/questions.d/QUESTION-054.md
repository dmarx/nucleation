# QUESTION-054: Is there a governing level among an agent's regimes, a system that specifies control apart from the systems that carry it out, or is governance the interaction of the regimes themselves?

## Why it is a question

Three readings disagree, and no `rivals` edge marks it:

- [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md): "Emotion regulation is not a process separate from emotion
  generation: most of it is one motive state checking another, with no
  distinct regulating system above them."
- [THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md): which of model-based and model-free control wins "follows
  their relative reliability, not the preference of a supervising
  system".
- [THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md): control is "decided by a cost-benefit computation ... in a
  specification system distinct from the structures that implement the
  control".

The will-organization line speaks of a governing level. [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)
separates the operative regime, "the governing organization among
regimes", and the capacity to transform it. [CLAIM-099](../claims.d/CLAIM-099.md) separates
maintaining effective action from maintaining "the capacity to govern
among regimes". If [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md) are right, the governing
organization may be a pattern of interaction, not a system. If [THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md)
is right, it may be a distinct one.

What would count as an answer: a dissociation between specification and
implementation that meets [THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md)'s standard, a process change specific
to the specification system that precedes the outcome. Or a showing that
the line's claims need only a pattern of interaction, so that the
question does not bear on them.

## Note of 2026-10-12: typed partial answers

The THEORYs this question cites now carry `answered_by` edges. [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md) answer on the interaction side, and [THEORY-153](../theory.d/THEORY-153.md) (Beer's distinct metasystem) on the distinct-level side, beside [THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md). Beer's metasystem may be distributed, which bears on [THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md)'s durable governance without central control.

## Note of 2026-10-13: a candidate answer, with a residue

The exchange of 2026-10-13 built a model in which governance is the
interaction of the processes themselves. Each process endorses others,
and authority is computed from the endorsements ([CLAIM-285](../claims.d/CLAIM-285.md)). A4 took
it as Daw, Niv and Dayan's arbitration ([THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md)) generalized "from two
controllers to a population", and as Frijda's one motive checking another
([THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)). The residue is the aggregation rule. Keeping processes from
controlling their own authority needs a rule that no process controls
([CLAIM-282](../claims.d/CLAIM-282.md)). A3 §8: "operators may propose endorsement changes, but
their proposals should not give them unilateral control over their own
authority. A separate aggregation rule enforces the constraint." Whether
that rule is a governing level in this question's sense is open. Workbench
entry of 2026-10-13, 21:29.

## Note of 2026-10-18: an answer with both sides

The owner, at U45 of the exchange of 2026-10-17: "processes which can
navigate disagreement amongst themselves require fewer resources from the
system than processes which need to activate additional subsystems to
resolve disagreement." That presupposes both sides of this question: a
level that can be recruited, at a cost ([THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md)'s specification
system), and regimes that can settle a conflict without it ([THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md),
[THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md)). On this view a governing level exists but is not routinely
recruited when the regimes coordinate among themselves. A45: "The
higher-order governance remains available but ceases to be the main
mechanism producing each successful action." The institutional form is
[THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md)'s "cheap local conflict resolution" under nested layers of
governance; none of the exchange's turns cited it.

Filed as [CLAIM-358](../claims.d/CLAIM-358.md), Proposed, which answers this question in part.
It does not say which conflicts admit local settlement, or what recruits
the level when they do not. The residue of 2026-10-13, whether the
aggregation rule no process controls is itself a governing level, is
untouched.
