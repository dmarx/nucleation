# NOTE-tmph3w3u: The Harder Problem of Consciousness

## Contribution

The paper isolates a problem distinct from the hard problem. The hard
problem asks why a neural basis is the basis of this experience rather than
another or none. The harder problem asks how a naturalist who is also a
phenomenal realist can hold that physicalism is the default when the
question it would have to answer, whether a physically different functional
duplicate is conscious, is one we cannot even conceive evidence for. It is
tied to the problem of other minds and is "more epistemological".

## Key insight

The science of consciousness is built on one example, us. A science based
on us can generalise to creatures physically like us, but not to a
creature that shares only our superficial functional organization: to know
that its consciousness had a physical basis in common with ours we would
already need to know whether it was conscious. So the one test case that
could tell us what consciousness is, at the level that matters, is one we
have no way to test.

## Assumptions

- **Phenomenal realism (§I)**: consciousness is real and not given an
  armchair analysis in functional, representational or cognitive terms. It
  is "metaphysical realism about consciousness".
- **Naturalism (§IV(A))**: it is a default, and a defeasible one, that
  consciousness and conscious similarities are physical. The relevant
  sciences exclude parts of psychology that mention consciousness.
- **Anti-scepticism (§IV(C))**: "Undoubtedly, humans are conscious and rocks
  and laptops are not."
- **(C1)**: we have no reason to believe there is a deep physical property
  common to all possible realizations of our superficial functional
  organization.
- **A merely superficial isomorph is possible as far as we know (§V)**: an
  isomorph with respect to folk psychology and what it entails, and no more.

## Key results

- **(C2) Defeasibility (§V).** Superficial functional equivalence is a
  reason to attribute consciousness, but it can be "unmasked" without
  unmasking the equivalence. If all of an isomorph's realizers were states
  that in us are the bases of unconscious processes (the pituitary
  regulator as its pain), the reason would be cancelled or weakened.
- **Commander Data's specification (§V).** (i) superficial isomorph; (ii)
  merely superficial; (iii) realizers share no properties with our brain
  mechanisms that toasters lack; (iv) no part is itself an isomorph of us.
- **(C3)** Fundamentally different realization is not, per se, a ground of
  rational belief that Data lacks consciousness. **(C4)** We have no
  conception of such a ground either way: meta-inaccessibility, a term
  Block credits to Nida-Rümelin.
- **Epistemology of identity (§VII).** Water = H₂O and temperature = mean
  kinetic energy are accepted because assuming them explains more (why ice
  floats, why water expands before freezing) and rules out bad questions.
  "The epistemology of theoretical identity is just a special case of
  inference to the best explanation."
- **Disjunctivism fails epistemically (§VIII).** Amorphous hardening of glass
  is not counted as freezing, though a disjunctive explanation is
  available. An identity of a phenomenal property with "our realization or
  Data's" would explain nothing and would not rule out the question why we
  overlap in this respect rather than that.
- **Superficialism fails epistemically (§VIII).** Identifying pain with its
  superficial role could explain its common-sense effects but not its
  non-superficial ones (pain raising perceived pitch), and there is no
  reason those should be explained differently.
- **The harder problem (§IX).** Physicalism is the default and also
  inaccessible and meta-inaccessible. Second, it is the *subjective
  default* that Data is not conscious, while it is an open question that he
  is.
- **Laws do not settle it (§X(C)).** A physically different duplicate will
  obey some laws of our conscious states and not others. Whether Stevens's
  power law is a law of consciousness or of the human realizer is "of a
  piece with" whether Data is conscious.
- **What to do (§XIII).** Live with the tension; weaken naturalism (accept
  disjunctivism or superficialism on the strength of the problem); or
  restrict phenomenal realism, as in the Frege–Schlick view that
  phenomenal comparisons are meaningful only within a person, or only
  across naturalistically similar subjects.

## Concepts

- **deflationism**: armchair analyses of consciousness in non-phenomenal
  terms (functional, representational, cognitive).
- **phenomenal realism**: the denial of deflationism plus realism about
  consciousness.
- **naturalism**: physicalism as a default, challengeable.
- **superficial functional isomorph**: isomorphic to us in the causal
  relations folk psychology specifies, and in what that entails.
- **meta-inaccessibility**: lacking not only a ground of rational belief
  but any conception of one.
- **disjunctivism**: physicalism that allows consciousness to be a
  physically heterogeneous disjunction.
- **superficialism**: the phenomenal-realist claim that consciousness is the
  superficial functional role itself.
- **ground of rational belief**: stronger than a reason, weaker than rational
  certainty; enough for knowledge (§V).

## Connections

- **Troubles with Functionalism ([LIT-407](../literature.d/LIT-407.md), [NOTE-366](NOTE-366.md)).** The paper builds on
  Troubles' Functionalism/Psychofunctionalism distinction (n. 13) and rules
  out the homunculi-headed realization (condition (iv)). Block says the
  "universal psychology" discussion in Troubles had assimilated the
  exotic-creature question to the explanatory gap (§IX), which is the
  mistake this paper exists to correct.
- **Chalmers ([LIT-406](../literature.d/LIT-406.md), [NOTE-336](NOTE-336.md)).** Block cites *The Conscious Mind* for the
  hard/easy distinction and against supervenience as the framework (§XI).
  On the metaphysical upshot of the gap the two are rival: concept dualism
  with physicalism as default here, experience as fundamental there.
- **Searle.** The Chinese room is used to motivate meta-inaccessibility.
  Block agrees with Searle that consciousness is "a matter of the biology of
  the organism, not (just) its information processing", but says the
  problem "affects my view as much as his" (§V).
- **Sober (2000) and McLaughlin.** Attribution of minds by common-cause
  reasoning, or by inference to a shared material basis, is defeated in
  Data's case. Block's reply is that the common cause could be
  consciousness with a disjunctive or superficial basis.

## Limitations

- **Meta-inaccessibility is a premise.** Block says the line of thought
  leads up to it "without anything that I am happy to think of as an
  argument for it".
- **"Fundamentally different" is left vague** (§VIII), and with it what
  would count as Data's sharing a physical basis with us.
- **The argument is a conditional.** Deflationists may take it as a
  modus tollens against phenomenal realism, as Block allows.

## Open questions

- Is there a principled way to sort the properties the superficial
  isomorphism entails into candidates for the nature of consciousness
  (Nida-Rümelin's objection, §VIII)?
- Could a theory of human consciousness become objective enough to apply
  across physical constitutions? Block says we have no conception of how.
