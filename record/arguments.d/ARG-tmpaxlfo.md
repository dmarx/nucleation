# ARG-tmpaxlfo: Two workshops equal in dependence, integration and membership and different only in who controls the terms show that control sorts a whole-part dependence in both directions, given that one dependence can be organized more than one way

## Which sense of "whole"

TERM-074 sense 1, with a controlling agent or role, R, and a part with
standing, P, as in CASE-tmpzh595.

## The inference

1. One dependence can be organized as opportunity, vulnerability or
   leverage, and payoff coupling alone does not settle which (CLAIM-428).
2. Where a transformation increases a constituent's vulnerability to a
   responsible agent's discretion, and a principle grounds special
   obligations in that vulnerability, the agent's responsibilities are
   strengthened (CLAIM-671, supposed).
3. CASE-tmpzh595: two workshops equal in the whole's dependence on P
   (8 lost if P withdraws), P's dependence on the whole (P alone makes 0),
   the integration that produced it and P's membership. In A, P can
   withdraw and renegotiate; in B, R sets P's terms. In the toy model P's
   share moves by 1/2 per unit of the workshop's need in A and by 0 in B,
   and in B R can put P's payoff anywhere from 1 to 8.
4. So in the whole-on-part direction the workshop's need is P's hold in A
   and not in B, and in the part-on-whole direction P's dependence meets
   2's antecedent in B and not in A.
5. So the factor that differs, control over the terms, is what sorts the
   dependence in both directions, and the magnitude, which is the same,
   does not (CLAIM-tmp6p3ki).

## Critical questions

- **Is everything said to be held fixed really fixed?** Dependence is
  held fixed as what each side would lose by withdrawal, measured apart
  from whether it can withdraw. Under that measure, yes. Under a measure
  that counts the availability of exit inside dependence, B's P is more
  dependent, and the contrast varies two things. The claim and the case
  both say which measure they mean, and the claim's `defeated_if` (b)
  names the rival measure. P's realized stake also differs (4 against 1),
  but it is downstream of control, not a second factor set by hand.
- **Does changing the factor change anything else?** Control over exit
  changes P's realized share, as just said, and with it what P would lose
  in fact by a breakdown. It does not change the production, what P gave
  up to join (CLAIM-603's route) or P's membership (CASE-256's variable).
- **Is the case realistic, or only coherent?** Only coherent, by design.
  It is fictional so that it can hold fixed what no real case does.
  CASE-257 is near B for its enslaved contributors, but no real A is
  matched to it. The contrast shows that the outcomes can come apart at
  equal dependence. It does not show how often magnitude and control move
  together in fact.

## What the premises carry

- **Step 2 is a supposition.** CLAIM-671's principle is Goodin's and
  unread. Without it, step 4's part-on-whole half shows only that B's P is
  exposed to R's discretion, not that any responsibility follows. The
  conclusion's obligation clause is stated conditionally for that reason.
- **Step 1 carries the form, not the cases.** CLAIM-428's evidence is
  about trade between states. The contrast does not lean on that evidence,
  only on its point that one dependence admits more than one organization.

## Verdict

Proposed. The contrast supports the claim's central part: at equal
dependence and integration, control over the terms sorts the dependence
in both directions. It does not support a stronger reading, that
magnitude never sorts a dependence; the claim does not make that reading
and names the result that would show it wrong.
