# NOTE-tmp6vcnq: Structural Realism (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

## Contribution

The entry calls structural realism, by the lights of many realists and antirealists, the most defensible form of scientific realism, and surveys the field. It covers the realism debate the view responds to, the epistemic (ESR) and ontic (OSR) forms and their variants, their connections to case studies in physics and to metaphysics, logic and philosophy of mathematics, the main objections, and related structuralisms in other areas.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1–2: The no-miracles argument versus the pessimistic meta-induction. Worrall's case: Fresnel's elastic-ether theory to Maxwell's field theory, where the equations are retained and the ontology is not. Van Fraassen's constructive empiricism is the main antirealist foil.
- §3 ESR: structure as the limit of knowledge. §3.1 covers the Kantian lineage (Poincaré, Zahar); §3.2 covers the Russell/Maxwell/Ramsey-sentence formulation, and how the Newman objection threatens to trivialise it (the passage "Newman objection trivialises the Ramsey sentence formulation of ESR").
- §4 OSR (Ladyman 1998; French): the success of science is explained by modal and relational structure, not by reference to objects. §4.1 group theory and invariants (Poincaré, Cassirer, Born); §4.2 QFT (field quanta are not individuals; French & Redhead on metaphysical underdetermination); §4.3 general covariance and the hole argument; §4.4 the special sciences (Ladyman & Ross's real patterns and "rainforest realism"; Kincaid and Ross on the social sciences).
- §5 objections: collapse into standard realism (Psillos), the need for relata (Chakravartty, Psillos, Cao, and others), and others. §6 other structuralisms: informational structural realism (Floridi), causal and dispositional essentialism (Shoemaker, Bird, Mumford), and Sider.
