# NOTE-031: The Strongest El Niño Ever (Climate Brink)

## Contribution

Hausfather argues that, with the July model runs in, the developing 2026 El Niño is very likely to be the strongest on record, possibly by a wide margin. He compares the multi-model forecast with every event since 1877, looks at the per-model spread and at the relative index that removes tropical-mean warming, and traces how forecasts rose from March to July. He also notes that observed daily Niño 3.4 anomalies are already at "super El Niño" levels in mid-July. He expects most of the global-temperature effect in 2027, and ends by warning that the models are untested at this intensity.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Historical context (first figure): no event since 1877 has meaningfully exceeded 2.75 °C. The middle 80% of the 2026 forecast members sit at or above that record.
- Model spread (histogram): every model's median peak is at "super" El Niño strength. All but JAMSTEC SINTEX-F (2.2 °C) exceed the 2015-16 record, and CMCC (5.3 °C) is flagged as an outlier to discount.
- RONI (relative Niño 3.4): by this index 1982-83 holds the record (2.69 °C), but 11 of 14 models still forecast a record.
- Forecast evolution (March to July runs): each run came in warmer, with the peak median rising from about 2.8 to 3.6 °C, but the revisions are shrinking (+0.5, +0.14, +0.14), which suggests convergence. Observations kept outrunning the forecasts.
- Global temperature: given a 3–5 month lag, 2027 is likely to be the warmest year on record. There is about a 28% chance that 2026 edges out 2024. Caveat: model agreement is not verified skill.

## Open questions

- This is a dated forecast. Whether the event verified is now checkable, and a reader should check it rather than rely on the post.
- It has no ML content. The survey's "drop: a blog post" is reasonable. Its only possible use is as an example of ensemble forecasting outside the training distribution.
