# NOTE-1997: de Boer and Raaphorst (2023) — Automation and discretion

## Contribution

A quantitative test of the curtailment thesis, that automation curtails street-level discretion and so
changes how policy is implemented, using perceived discretion as the mediator.

## Key results

1. Abstract, p. 42: "automation (1) increases bureaucrats’ legal and accommodation style"; perceived
   discretion does not mediate; "automation does decrease" perceived discretion.
2. p. 49: the NVWA's INSPECT system generated verdicts; inspectors "could deviate from this verdict but
   only after getting permission from their supervisors"; "82 inspectors in our sample work with INSPECT".
3. p. 52 (Table 2): legal style β = 0.097, p = .044; accommodation β = 0.109, p ≈ .06; facilitation n.s.;
   "Hypothesis 1 is rejected."; perceived discretion β = −0.086, p = .069.
4. p. 53: indirect effects through perceived discretion all non-significant.
5. p. 55: automation "alters the choice architecture of street"-level bureaucrats, who must enter their
   observations in the system.
6. pp. 56–57: cross-sectional, "no conclusions can be drawn about causality"; only perceived, not granted,
   discretion measured.

## Assumptions

Self-reported automation use and style; single-item mediator; rule obedience as the control; inspectors as
street-level bureaucrats of a regulatory kind.

## Concepts

Curtailment thesis; discretion as granted, used and perceived; enforcement style (legal, facilitation,
accommodation); decision support technology; choice architecture.

## Bearing on the record

- [CLAIM-710](../claims.d/CLAIM-710.md): item 2 is a case where automation put a gate in front of the human's departure from the
  machine's verdict, the reverse of removing friction; it measures nothing about points of intervention.
- [CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md), [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md): item 5's explanation (the system steers through what it asks to be entered)
  is governance through a procedure's criteria rather than its decisions.
- The LIT's earlier gloss "without clearly changing enforcement behaviour" was wrong: style changed; the
  mechanism was not perceived discretion.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional; 82 treated; self-report throughout; one-item mediator; several results at p < .10; one
organization; INSPECT halted in 2019.
