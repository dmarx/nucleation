# LIT-2963: Scale-free networks are rare

Broido & Clauset (2019). Nature Communications 10, 1017. DOI
10.1038/s41467-019-08746-5; arXiv 1801.03400.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A invokes it without naming it at A10 for the finding that strongly scale-free real networks are uncommon. It bears on the exchange's caution that preferential attachment's pure power law is seldom what real networks show.

Read on 2026-11-22 in [NOTE-2263](../notes.d/NOTE-2263.md); the reading is [THEORY-2172](../theory.d/THEORY-2172.md). The
first summary's "preferential attachment's pure power law is seldom what
real networks show" is fair but narrower than the paper, which speaks of
power-law degree distributions in general, keeps preferential attachment
for citation networks, and limits "any mechanism that necessarily
produces power-law degree distributions". It supports [CLAIM-803](../claims.d/CLAIM-803.md)
([ARG-170](../arguments.d/ARG-170.md)) and does not bear on [CLAIM-841](../claims.d/CLAIM-841.md)'s thesis ([ARG-167](../arguments.d/ARG-167.md)).

Its verdict is disputed. Voitalov, van der Hoorn, van der Hofstad and
Krioukov ([LIT-3041](LIT-3041.md), registered, unread) define a power law as a
regularly varying distribution, [TERM-142](../terms.d/TERM-142.md)'s sense 1, where this paper tests
a pure power law above k_min; by their abstract, real scale-free networks
"are definitely not as rare" as the pure-power-law assumption makes them.
