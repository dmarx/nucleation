# LIT-978: Neural Collapse: A Review on Modelling Principles and Generalization

Vignesh Kothapalli (2022), *Transactions on Machine Learning Research (2023); arXiv:2206.04041*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-772](../notes.d/NOTE-772.md). A skim: the review's results are reports
of other papers, not checked against them.*

- **Two families of model (§3).**
  - In the unconstrained-features model the penultimate features are free
    variables. Under it, collapse is the global optimum or the gradient-flow
    limit for cross-entropy and MSE, with and without weight decay (Ji et
    al.; Mixon et al.; Zhu et al.; Tirer & Bruna; Han et al., [LIT-974](LIT-974.md)).
  - Local elasticity models feature drift with an SDE whose class coupling
    is chosen to favour separation.
  - Neither keeps the data. That is why neither can explain NC1 reaching
    about 10⁻² on CIFAR-10 and about 1 on ImageNet.
- **Collapse is not generalization (§4.1).** Networks trained with SGD,
  Adam and L-BFGS all collapse on the training set and differ in test
  error (Zhu et al.). Collapse on test data is slower and weaker (Hui et
  al.). The CDNV bound of Galanti et al. ties the two only for large
  per-class samples.
- **Collapse and transfer (§4.2).** Galanti et al. bound few-shot transfer
  error by source-class collapse, when source and target classes come from
  one distribution. Kornblith et al. (2021) find that ImageNet networks
  with more collapse transfer worse to other datasets.
- **No threshold (§4.1, §5).** There is no agreed value of NC1 or CDNV at
  which a network counts as collapsed. The review asks for one.
