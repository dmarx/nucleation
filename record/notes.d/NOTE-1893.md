# NOTE-1893: Fuentes (2016) — Dispossessed Lives: Enslaved Women, Violence, and the Archive

## Contribution

A methodological argument: the colonial archive is a product of the domination it records, its silences evidence of that system, so a method that requires a sufficient documentary base reproduces the erasure.

## Key results

1. Excerpt: "History is produced from what the archive offers." And: "The challenge this book has confronted is to write a history about what an archive does not offer."
2. Excerpt: "the system of slavery assumed enslaved people were criminals when they were instead victims of an impossible legal structure."
3. Excerpt: "It has not hewn to those historical practices that require a vast empirical base from which to generalize."
4. Interview: "To make such demands is to ignore and normalize the violence enslaved women endured and the epistemic violence produced from an archive in which they are distorted."
5. Interview: "They appeared in the archives as alleged criminals, enslaved prostitutes, tortured runaways, and victims of such violence that they were obscured from historical view."
6. Excerpt: "We cannot redeem or rescue them, but we can reconsider their pain."

## Assumptions

The archive's producers fixed what could be recorded and how; absence is structured, not random; ethical practice may go beyond what an empiricist standard of sufficiency licenses.

## Concepts

Archival power; archival and epistemic violence; erasure; the archive as mortuary (after Hartman); metanarrative.
