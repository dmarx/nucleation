# NOTE-tmptylts: Metzinger — Minimal phenomenal experience

## Contribution

The paper names a target, minimal phenomenal experience (MPE), and a
strategy, explanation by minimal models. The target is chosen because the
contemplative reports describing it claim "minimal dimensionality" together
with "maximal prototypicality" (pp. 2–3, n. 1). The paper gives a first
six-dimensional concept of MPE and a hypothesis about what MPE represents:
the organism's own tonic alertness. Its evidence is quoted report and
exegesis. It promises psychometric work, which became Metzinger 2024
([LIT-tmp3uosd](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3uosd.md)).

## Key insight

What an "empty" consciousness represents is the capacity to know. Tonic
alertness, "a graded functional property determining the capacity for
sustained attention", can exist without being represented, as in
unresponsive wakefulness. When it is represented, the result is
wakefulness, and "Consciousness is knowing that one is alert" (p. 33). The
paper makes this model the minimal case of experience. It is a model of
the system, but not of a self as subject: "MPE is non-egoic
self-modelling" (p. 36).

## Assumptions

- **Naturalism**, assumed "without having given an independent argument"
  (p. 7, n. 2).
- **Representationalism.** The reported contentlessness may itself be an
  appearance, and if there is a content "that can account for the reported
  experience of contentlessness, then Representationalism remains tenable.
  This is the line I will be pursuing" (p. 9).
- **Probabilistic concepts.** "Consciousness" has "not definitional but
  probabilistic structure". MPE is "a cluster concept" referring to a
  "phenomenological prototype", and membership is graded (p. 3, n. 1).
- **Report as data, provisionally.** Kriegel is quoted: "It is nonetheless
  open to the theoretician to challenge the practitioner's own description
  of the state she is in" (p. 9). The three methodological obstacles are
  named in footnote 27 (p. 38): embodied theory contamination, the lack of
  a positive characterisation, and performative self-contradiction. They
  are discussed elsewhere (Metzinger 2019, not held), not here.

## Key results

- **From MPS to MPE** (p. 2). Blanke and Metzinger's minimal phenomenal
  selfhood ([LIT-1274](../literature.d/LIT-1274.md)) has three features: identification with the body
  as a whole, spatiotemporal self-location and a weak first-person
  perspective. MPE is hypothesised to lack MPS and all other egoic
  self-consciousness, time representation and a spatial frame.
- **Six constraints** (pp. 7–23). PC1, Wakefulness, is "the phenomenal
  character of tonic alertness" (p. 9). PC2, Low Complexity, is "often
  described as the complete absence of intentional content" (p. 9). PC3 is
  Self-Luminosity, culturally variable and perhaps a metaphor for PC1
  (pp. 17–20). PC4, Introspective Availability, PC5, Epistemicity, "the
  phenomenal experience of knowing ... the subjective quality of
  confidence", and PC6, Transparency/Opacity, are drawn from Moore's
  "Refutation of Idealism" (pp. 20–23).
- **The negative profile** (pp. 10–11). Non-sensory, non-motor,
  atemporal, non-cognitive, non-egoic ("no localized unit of
  identification"), unbounded, and "aperspectival: no epistemic agent
  model".
- **No phenomenal oneness** (p. 13). The space "has neither centre nor
  periphery and ... cannot be introspectively individuated to create
  indivisible units of experience – not even a single one". It "lacks
  the phenomenal quality of singularity or numerical identity", so
  "non-oneness" is added to "non-duality".
- **No phenomenal contrast** (p. 13). "The method of phenomenal contrast
  ... cannot be applied to the phenomenology of tonic alertness": without
  wakefulness "there simply is no conscious experience altogether".
- **Not remembered** (p. 15). "The content of a full-absorption episode
  cannot be reported, because the self-referential mechanisms of forming
  an autobiographical memory are suspended". Only "the process of entering
  into it or of emerging out of it" can be represented in the
  autobiographical self-model.
- **The epistemic-space thesis** (pp. 26–27). MPE "is neither an internal
  model of a self-as-subject, nor a model of an object". The epistemic
  subject "simply is the organism or conscious system as a whole". MPE is
  "an internal model of an epistemic space currently opened by it".
- **Transparency for a contentless state** (pp. 30–31). If MPE had no
  intentional content and were transparent, "there would be nothing
  introspective attention could ever discover". If it were opaque, PC1 and
  PC3 would report "properties of the physical carrier itself", and some
  reports "may actually be reports about an entirely non-intentional
  process". The theory sketched: "'Appearance' results from a transparent
  representation of meta-confidence" (p. 31).
- **Three levels** (pp. 32–33). Arousal is a physical property, tonic
  alertness a functional one and wakefulness a phenomenal one. Tonic
  alertness "if it is not represented or internally modelled, can exist
  without the conscious experience of wakefulness (as in some cases of
  unresponsive wakefulness syndrome)" (p. 33). Tonic alertness "could be
  realized by very different physical properties in machines that create
  an integrated internal model of their own epistemic space as such"
  (p. 32).
- **Conclusion** (pp. 35–38). MPE is "an inner representation of tonic
  alertness". It is "non-egoic self-modelling: as such it is atemporal,
  selfless, and not tied to an individual first-person perspective"
  (p. 36). It "is aperspectival and it does not instantiate MPS, but it
  definitely has representational content" (p. 38). "Phenomenality is a
  transparent, temporally thin, and global representation of subjective
  confidence, relative to the abstract possibility of knowledge" (p. 38).
  Footnote 24 (p. 36) states that MPE is a counterexample to the
  "projective geometry" model of consciousness, since it is aperspectival.

## Concepts

- **Minimal phenomenal experience (MPE)**: the simplest conscious
  experience. The term is Windt's (2015), and the paper uses it more
  widely (p. 2).
- **Minimal model**: a model including "only the core causal factors
  giving rise to the target phenomenon" (p. 3).
- **Full-absorption episode**: a state at maximal Wakefulness and minimal
  Low Complexity, after which only awareness can be reported (p. 14).
- **Epistemic agent model (EAM)**: "a conscious internal model of the self
  as actively selecting targets of knowledge ... the origin of our
  consciously experienced first-person perspective" (p. 11, n. 7).
- **Unit of identification (UI)**: "whatever form of experiential content
  leads to phenomenological reports of the type: 'I am this!'". "If a
  conscious system has no UI ... it has no sense of self" (p. 10, n. 6).
- **Epistemic space model (ESM)**: MPE as an integrated model of the
  organism's set of epistemic capacities (p. 27).

## Connections

It builds on Blanke and Metzinger 2009 ([LIT-1274](../literature.d/LIT-1274.md)) and on Windt's coinage
of MPE. It draws on Moore 1903 for transparency, and on Forman, Stace,
Shear and Tibetan sources for the reports. [LIT-1354](../literature.d/LIT-1354.md) (Chandia-Jorquera et
al.) adopts its definition of pure awareness. Woods, Windt and Carter
([LIT-1351](../literature.d/LIT-1351.md)) test its premise that such goal states are contentless and
find them not to be. Its active-inference reading is shared with
Laukkonen et al. ([THEORY-882](../theory.d/THEORY-882.md)). Metzinger 2024 ([LIT-tmp3uosd](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3uosd.md)) is its
sequel, with the survey.

## Bearing on the record's own argument

- **[THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) (unity without a self-model).** This paper replaces the
  précis's "Selfless Snapshot" with a minimal case that is selfless and
  *not* experienced as one. It has no "phenomenal quality of singularity"
  (p. 13). It is still a model, and a self-directed one: "non-egoic
  self-modelling" (p. 36). For [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) the bearing is twofold. (a)
  If MPE is real, then on Metzinger's own later account there is
  consciousness that is not the integration of "one global world-model",
  since MPE models the system's capacity to know and not a world. So the
  first clause would be at best an account of ordinary waking experience,
  not of consciousness as such. (b) The second clause would hold only in
  the narrower form that phenomenal experience needs no egoic self-model.
  The inference is the record's. The paper does not mention the
  globality constraint.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md) (what each kind of evidence can establish).** The paper
  argues that MPE's own content is unreportable from memory (p. 15) and
  closed to the method of phenomenal contrast (p. 13). If both are right,
  its prototype is closed to the two standard routes of report-based
  evidence. It defers performative self-contradiction to other papers
  (n. 27). It also gives one dissociation the record can use. Tonic
  alertness without wakefulness, in unresponsive wakefulness, is a case
  in which a function is present and, on the paper's reading, experience
  is absent (p. 33). The reading of experience as absent rests on
  behaviour, which is [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)'s problem again.
- **[QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md) and [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) (what makes an organization
  experiential).** The paper's answer is a specific organization: a
  predictive model of the system's own epistemic capacities, temporally
  thin and integrated. Without it, alertness is not experienced (p. 33).
  That is a candidate for [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md)'s missing qualifying condition, in a
  form close to [TERM-073](../terms.d/TERM-073.md)'s interpretation (distinction, context,
  consequentiality). It is aperspectival, which [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md)'s part 3 does
  not allow as worded. It is not stated independently of report, so it
  does not meet [CLAIM-411](../claims.d/CLAIM-411.md)'s defeater either.
- **[QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md) (non-gluing and unity).** If MPE has no centre, no
  boundary and no oneness (p. 13), it is a case where the question of
  unity, glued or not, may not arise in phenomenal terms. One cannot ask
  whether local interpretations glue when there are no local
  interpretations. That is the record's inference. It marks a limit of the

## Limitations

- **No new data.** The sixteen case studies are quotations from published
  reports and from traditional and teaching texts. The author grants that "there are
  actually fewer paradigmatic reports of such experiences in the literature
  than previously assumed" (p. 38).
- **The methodological problems are deferred** (n. 27). The paper does not
  answer the performative worry, which is [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)'s.
- **A tension between PC2 and the conclusion.** Low Complexity is first
  read as "the complete absence of intentional content" (p. 9), and the
  conclusion says MPE "definitely has representational content" (p. 38).
  The paper resolves this by treating the content as abstract (epistemic
  capacity) and the reported contentlessness as an appearance (p. 9). The
  resolution is a choice of theory, not a finding.
- **Machine realisation is asserted as conceivable** (p. 32), with no
  argument for what would make a machine's model of its own epistemic
  space conscious rather than merely present.

## Open questions

- Is there a report-independent marker of a represented tonic alertness, as
  opposed to tonic alertness itself? The paper's account needs one to be
  testable on anyone not reporting.
- If full-absorption episodes cannot be remembered (p. 15), what are the
  reports of them reports of? The answer the paper implies, entry and
  exit, would make all the evidence evidence about transitions.
