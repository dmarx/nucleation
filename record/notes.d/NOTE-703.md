# NOTE-703: Forssten et al. — One Giant Leap from Mouse to Man

## Contribution

A review of probiotics acting on mood through the microbiota–gut–brain axis,
organized around why preclinical findings fail to translate. Its case
studies include strains whose human trials were null.

## Key insight

The rodent work shows that gut microbes *can* drive affect-related
behaviour. The human work has not shown that they *do*, robustly, at
doses and in populations that matter: "the translation of these findings
from mouse to man has proven difficult".

## Assumptions

- **Industry authorship.** Verbatim: "At the time of writing, all authors
  were employed by IFF Health & Biosciences. IFF Health & Biosciences
  manufactures and commercializes probiotics." The review is narrative,
  with no search method.
- **Healthy-volunteer trials.** These are taken as relevant to mood in
  general. The authors note that "most findings from these clinical trials
  cannot be extrapolated to patients with clinically diagnosed
  depression."

## Key results

- **Route.** "The vagus nerve is the most direct route of communication
  between the gut and the brain, thus enabling bidirectional
  communication." In mice the effects are lost after vagotomy.
- **Germ-free animals.** They show an exaggerated stress-hormone response
  that colonization normalizes. This is replicated across rodent strains.
- **Faecal transplant to rodents.** Abnormal behaviour "indicating at least
  partial transfer of the clinical psychiatric phenotype". The donors were
  few, and "the gut microbiome is the only humanized component of the
  rodent".
- **Healthy humans.** "A recent meta-analysis demonstrated that probiotic
  consumption could result in a reduction of subjective stress levels in
  healthy volunteers". Single trials report reduced reactivity to sad
  mood, perceived stress and anxiety, some only under stress or in
  subgroups.
- **Failures.** L. rhamnosus JB-1, after "comprehensive preclinical
  evidence", "was not found to have an effect on mood, anxiety, stress,
  sleep quality, memory performance, attention, or anti-inflammatory
  cytokine levels compared with the placebo." Another strain did not
  alleviate exam stress. A combination left depression scores identical to
  placebo.

## Connections

- **The owner's example** ([CLAIM-166](../claims.d/CLAIM-166.md), [CASE-047](../cases.d/CASE-047.md)). In animals, a
  lower-level collective changes affect-related behaviour, and a
  transplant moves the change with the microbes. That is the
  drive-from-below the owner describes. In humans the evidence that
  everyday affect and experience are moved by one's own microbiome is
  modest: small effects on *subjective* stress, which is experience,
  alongside clean nulls.
- **Felt affect** ([THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)). The vagus is an interoceptive channel, so this
  is a route from the microbiome into the bodily state that, on [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md),
  felt affect reports. That bearing is the record's.
