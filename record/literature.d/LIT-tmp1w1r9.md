# LIT-tmp1w1r9: Unto Others: The Evolution and Psychology of Unselfish Behavior

Sober, E. & Wilson, D. S. (1998). Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press.

Not read. The summary is drawn from the abstract or from what the work is
standardly cited for. A reading should check it first.

## Why this is here

The standard philosophical treatment of whether unselfish motivation exists, and of the line between evolutionary and psychological altruism, the owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)) has to cross. Hamilton ([THEORY-655](../theory.d/THEORY-655.md)) is the kin-selection side.

## Read in its place

The book cannot be read here, so the record holds its argument at second
hand:

- **A close critique.** Stich 2006 ([LIT-tmpgnooi](LIT-tmpgnooi.md), [NOTE-tmpeuwqz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeuwqz.md)) sets
  out the book's evolutionary argument for psychological altruism from
  parental care (pp. 296–320), quoting it at length, and argues that it
  leaves egoism open.

Missing still: the book's first half, on evolutionary altruism and group
selection; its own reading of the social psychology (Batson and his
critics); and its chapter on pluralism.
