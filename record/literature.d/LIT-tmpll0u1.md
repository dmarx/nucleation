summary: >-
  Meyer (2003; revised 2026). The SEP entry on what present people owe future and past people, which argues a position as well as surveying. Future people's contingency on our choices, the non-identity problem, defeats harm understood as making someone worse off than before or than otherwise. It does not defeat a threshold notion of harm, on which we harm a person by causing her to exist, or to be, below a normatively set threshold. Combined with the counterfactual notion, this gives a "disjunctive" harm. The threshold is best read as sufficientarian, in basic needs or capabilities, rather than egalitarian or prioritarian; Rawls's just savings principle is one version. The entry is weakly person-affecting, so it owes merely possible people nothing, and it grants that this gives no reason to prefer a flourishing future to none. That gap is filled by concerns that are not rights: Scheffler's afterlife conjecture, and an obligation owed to present and past people not to destroy the goods and practices on which future-oriented projects depend. Historical injustice is treated with the same notions of harm.
