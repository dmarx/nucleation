# CLAIM-tmpdga80: Experience is constituted by interpretive organization, not produced by it: an appropriately organized process in which signals acquire significance for a system is the experience, so whether a system can have experience becomes whether it can sustain that organization; the illusions and reappraisals that motivate it show that experience is constructed, not that construction is experience

## The claim

The owner, at U1: "if this assertion is accurate it follows that
ungrounded experience experience is possible and involves forming
interpretations and judgments from signals purporting to have been
produced by sensory apparatus. it further follows from this line of
reasoning that experience is fundamentally an interpretive act." The
proposal was to recast "questions about the possibility of experience as
questions about the possibility of forming judgements and
interpretations". A1 put the fork: "whether you regard experience as
*constituted by* interpretation, or interpretation as the mechanism that
*produces* experience". The owner, at U2: "yeah former reading".

The claim has four parts, each stated in the exchange.

1. **Constitution, not production.** A2: "The ongoing realization of
   \(\mathcal{I}\) in an appropriately organized system is the
   experience." A7 states it as an identity, "not a causal claim that
   \(\mathsf P\) produces some further entity \(E\)". Under production one
   asks how interpretation generates consciousness. Under constitution
   one asks "what entitles us to identify a realized
   \(\mathsf P\)-organization with consciousness". The claim takes the
   second question.
2. **Interpretation is organization, not judgement.** A1 recommended
   "interpretive organization, rather than judgment specifically", with
   judgement as "a higher-order operation within an interpretive system".
   A7 gave the definition the claim uses: "Interpretation is the
   context-dependent organization of information into distinctions that
   acquire significance through their relationships to a system's existing
   models, evaluative standards, and possible state transitions." Its
   three parts are distinction, context and consequentiality. A7 called
   the definition "deliberately nonphenomenal", which it must be: "Otherwise
   the constitutive theory would explain experience using a definition of
   interpretation that already contains experience."
3. **The organization must be perspectival.** Not every interpretation
   qualifies ([CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md)). A7's candidate: "a dynamically maintained
   system of interpretive relationships in which distinctions acquire
   coordinated significance relative to an operative reference frame of
   relevance, temporal continuity, and possible change". Its own table
   separates four levels: interpretation, coordination, perspectival
   organization, experiential constitution. Of the last step: "not a
   theorem derived from the first three. It is the central bridge
   principle the theory must defend." What makes an organization
   perspectival is [QUESTION-tmpc4j7p](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.md).
4. **The subject is constituted with the experience.** A2: "the
   organization of interpretive activity might constitute both
   experiential unity and the subject to whom that experience is
   attributed". A7's restatement of the owner's thesis: "Experience may be
   fundamentally a mode of organized interpretation rather than an act of
   interpreting", since an act "suggests a discrete event performed by
   something already capable of acting".

The owner's other premise, that sensory apparatus is necessary for
grounded experience, is restated as [CLAIM-tmpjw8ev](CLAIM-tmpjw8ev.md). Grounding concerns
what constrains an interpretation, not whether it is experiential. The
present claim does not rest on it. Ungrounded experience is a case the
thesis must cover, not a premise it needs.

## The evidence offered, and what it shows

A2 set the owner's three examples in a table, with what each varies. A7
added dreaming and hallucination.

- **Brain in a vat.** External causes vary while apparent experience is
  held fixed. It is a thought experiment, and the record holds no case
  for it. A2 noted Putnam's complication ([LIT-tmp0l6v1](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0l6v1.md)): the envatted
  brain's thoughts may not refer as ours do, so "the constitution of an
  experience and the determination of its referential content might come
  apart". That point belongs to [CLAIM-tmpjw8ev](CLAIM-tmpjw8ev.md).
- **Rubber hand illusion.** A2 read it as ownership changing while the
  body does not: "The result of interpretation can be the experience
  itself." A5, after reading the record, withdrew the strong reading.
  [THEORY-766](../theory.d/THEORY-766.md) separates reports of referred touch, reports of ownership
  and proprioceptive drift. Its standard controls do not equalize
  expectation ([LIT-1276](../literature.d/LIT-1276.md)). So the illusion "might change the constituted
  experience of bodily ownership, the interpretation of an unchanged
  experience, the reporting of an experience, or some combination". The
  constitutive reading needs the first, and the record's evidence does not
  yet pick it out.
- **CBT and reappraisal.** A2: "changing the interpretive organization
  changes the experience itself". A2 also gave the caveat: CBT "allows a
  causal reading in which cognition modifies an independently constituted
  emotional experience", so its success "is consistent with your
  constitutive thesis but doesn't uniquely establish it". The record holds
  both readings. [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) makes regulation part of generation;
  [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) keeps them apart. Bo et al. ([LIT-tmptaneb](../literature.d/LIT-tmptaneb.md)) find one largely
  subcortical system that reappraisal did not modulate. A3 drew the
  lesson that interpretation cannot be identified "exclusively with
  explicit cognition".
- **Interoceptive inference.** Not raised in the exchange. Seth's account
  ([THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md)) makes felt emotion "the integrated content" of
  predictions of interoceptive causes. It is the record's nearest reading
  to the claim for affect, and Barrett's theory of constructed emotion
  ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md); [LIT-tmphg31k](../literature.d/LIT-tmphg31k.md)) is the exchange's.

For that reason none of these readings is listed among the claim's
grounds. [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) are rival accounts of the same finding,
and [THEORY-766](../theory.d/THEORY-766.md) leaves expectation and report confounds open. They mark
what the thesis must explain, not support for its conclusion.

A7's verdict, which the claim adopts: "These motivate interpretive
construction. They do not, individually or collectively, prove interpretive
constitution." A3 separated three theses: content is interpretively
constructed (well supported); experience is constituted by interpretive
organization (this claim); the organization can be realized without
sensory or biological embodiment (not claimed).

## Where it sits in the record

- [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md) leaves open whether the integrated state is felt. A5:
  "Our discussion suggests extending this account, not replacing it." If
  the claim holds, a constructed owner does not mean there is no
  experience: "there might be real experience without an additional entity
  that owns it". The two complement each other.
- Metzinger's unity of experience without a self-model ([THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)) is
  part 4 already read: a perspective need not be represented as an owner.
  The exchange did not cite it.
- Veit's identity of felt valence with coordinated regulation
  ([THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)) is the one identity of this kind the record already
  reads. A5: the "decisive move would be to explore whether an analogous
  identity thesis can be generalized beyond hedonic valence."
- Strong representationalism is the standing position nearest the claim
  on the character of experience only ([LIT-tmpi7qws](../literature.d/LIT-tmpi7qws.md), read in
  [NOTE-tmpxf5do](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxf5do.md)). Lycan's version holds that a sensory quality can occur
  with nothing it is like to have it, and hands what makes a state
  experienced to a theory of awareness ([THEORY-tmptw1fe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmptw1fe.md)). It is nearest
  to part 1's "which experience", not to its "that there is one". Its
  named defenders also hold qualities wide, against the internal reading
  below. Block's Inverted Earth ([THEORY-095](../theory.d/THEORY-095.md)) shows that the
  claim's interpretation must be internal. Phenomenal character there
  stays fixed while externally individuated content inverts.

## The objections

- **Unconscious interpretation.** A3: "If we define interpretation in
  terms of inference, categorization, prediction, or the assignment of
  functional significance, then unconscious systems perform interpretation
  continually." Filed as [CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md).
- **Overgeneration.** Organizations, distributed software and language
  models may meet every condition (A5, A6, A7 §14). A7's standard is
  [QUESTION-046](../questions.d/QUESTION-046.md)'s: specify the conditions independently and accept
  the classifications that follow, "including uncomfortable ones".
- **The reverse direction.** Phenomenal intentionality makes experience
  ground meaning, not meaning constitute experience (A3). It asks whether
  anything can mean something "*to a system* unless there is already
  something it is like to be that system".
- **Against structuralism.** Negro ([LIT-tmp86j0r](../literature.d/LIT-tmp86j0r.md), read in [NOTE-tmp0dy5a](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0dy5a.md))
  argues against metaphysical structuralism about consciousness. He
  considers making judgements and cognitive capacities constitutive, and
  rejects it as aligning structuralism with eliminativism ([THEORY-tmp7uasf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7uasf.md)).
  The claim makes organization constitutive, not judgement, but it must
  still say why it is not eliminativism by another name. A4 drew from him the realization
  problem, whether "possessing a description of every relevant relation"
  differs from "instantiating the organized process itself". The claim
  answers with realized, not described, organization: in A7's words, "a
  causally realized structuralism, rather than a purely extensional
  structuralism". Aldé ([LIT-tmpac9l2](../literature.d/LIT-tmpac9l2.md)) argues that similarity relations do not
  fix character. That bears on the claim only if its relations are
  similarities, and A7 lists causal, counterfactual, temporal and
  evaluative ones.
- **Experience with little interpretation.** Minimal-content meditative
  states ([LIT-tmphw2n7](../literature.d/LIT-tmphw2n7.md); [LIT-tmpg6f7h](../literature.d/LIT-tmpg6f7h.md)), and pain or arousal that seem to come
  before categorization (A7 §15). These do not refute the claim, but they
  bar identifying interpretation with "rich conceptualization, explicit
  judgment, or self-narration" (A6).
- **Strong illusionism.** [THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md) could accept every dynamic the
  claim names and deny that any of it is phenomenal. A7: "That dispute
  cannot be resolved by terminology." It is underdetermined by
  third-person data, and the claim does not settle it.

## What it does not say

- It does not say that every interpretation is an experience. Part 3 is
  the qualification, and it is unspecified.
- It does not say that experience can be realized without a body or
  biology. A7: "We shouldn't confuse organizational constitution with
  unrestricted substrate independence." Nor does it say that a system
  equivalent in its input–output mapping, or isomorphic in a diagram, has
  the same experience. [THEORY-507](../theory.d/THEORY-507.md)'s invariance is a separate premise,
  and A5 noted that it is "not an argument that experience *is*
  functional organization".
- It does not say that experience is unreal, or that a self is required.
- It does not solve phenomenal binding. A7: "We have not dissolved the
  phenomenal binding problem merely by moving it into organizational
  language." What unity is under the claim is [QUESTION-tmpzje42](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpzje42.md).
- It does not say that graded organization means graded experience. A7:
  "graded organization does not by itself establish graded phenomenality".

## Note of 2026-10-22: the claim in the exchange's later terms

At U11 the owner asked "under our current characterization what is a
perspective? an interpretation?" The answers are filed as two Proposed
terms. Interpretation is [TERM-tmpgqmop](../terms.d/TERM-tmpgqmop.md), the sense part 2 uses. Perspective
is [TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md), in three senses: organizational, represented and
phenomenal. In those words the claim is A11's: "Certain organizational
perspectives are not merely associated with experience but constitute its
occurrence, character, and unity." The claim's bridge is from sense 1 to
sense 3. A11 named its weakest point: sense 1 is defined by "the conditions
governing interpretation", and sense 3 by "something being present or
manifest from a point of view". Whether these are one phenomenon is the
claim itself, and the exchange has not shown it.

A11 also restated part 4, the subject constituted with the experience, as
the claim's most general form: "an interpretation is not necessarily
something occurring *to* a pre-existing subject. A perspective may be
constituted through the organization of interpretive activity itself."

## Note of 2026-10-22: what the cited works say, once read

Eighteen of the twenty-two works the exchange cited were read on
2026-10-22 (curation entry of that day). None supports the identity. They
bear on it as follows.

- **Rival identities, not support.** IWMT ([THEORY-tmpopaql](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpopaql.md)) identifies
  consciousness with the coherent world model of an embodied agent. That
  is a rival identity that requires a capacity and history of coupling.
  Whyte et al. ([THEORY-tmpw20ca](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpw20ca.md)) speak only of a "correspondence" and
  state their criterion as conscious access in humans. Laukkonen et al.
  ([THEORY-tmpw19hw](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpw19hw.md)) offer conditions with no data. Their formal part is a
  hierarchical model with a precision parameter per level, which an
  ordinary filter would also satisfy.
- **The bridge is contested from the represented side.** On Graziano's
  illusionism ([THEORY-tmpncy2z](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpncy2z.md)), experience is the represented perspective
  of a real organization, [TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md)'s sense 2 of sense 1. Higher-order
  theories ([NOTE-tmpo7pcy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpo7pcy.md)) also make representing one's own state what
  makes it experiential. Both locate experience where this claim does not.
- **Character, not occurrence.** Lyre ([THEORY-tmpv5rmc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpv5rmc.md)) holds that
  structure fixes which experience a state is, and leaves whether there is
  one to a separate mechanism. That supports the claim's account of
  character and nothing more. Aldé ([THEORY-tmp4u3l2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4u3l2.md)) sets a test for any
  relational account of colour: it must yield the unique hues and the
  opponent structure.
- **Reappraisal.** In Bo et al. ([THEORY-tmptuggu](../theory.d/THEORY-tmptuggu.md), [THEORY-tmpak7ax](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpak7ax.md)) reported
  affect fell as cortical appraisal was reorganized. That fits the claim.
  It is limited only if the subcortical response that reappraisal left
  unchanged is part of the experience, and Bo et al. do not show that: it
  did not predict regulation success. Barrett et al. ([NOTE-tmpr1xjh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpr1xjh.md)) never
  mention CBT or reappraisal. The exchange supplied that link. Their
  wording moves between constitution and production.
- **Minimal content.** The meditation readings ([THEORY-tmp9y4pc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp9y4pc.md),
  [THEORY-tmp1lndf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp1lndf.md)) show reported content being trimmed, not interpretation
  being absent. What remains (calm, knowing, unity) is organization below
  concepts. Chandia-Jorquera et al. found no link between content ratings
  and the EEG, so the evidence is report. Woods et al. report that two
  meditators can describe one state differently, which a constitutive
  reading has to explain.
- **Overgeneration made concrete.** Gurnee et al. ([THEORY-tmp111ci](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp111ci.md)) find an
  access workspace in a language model, built from text alone. The claim
  must either accept that such a system qualifies or say what it lacks
  ([QUESTION-tmpc4j7p](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.md)).
- **Life.** Ward ([THEORY-tmpnwjjt](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpnwjjt.md)) classes the claim as informationalist,
  the side organicist enactivism rejects ([CLAIM-tmpjw8ev](CLAIM-tmpjw8ev.md), note of
  2026-10-22).

The defeater is not met by any of them.
