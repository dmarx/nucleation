# CASE-tmpzotci: The Washington Senators: the original club became the Minnesota Twins in 1961, an expansion club took its name in Washington and became the Texas Rangers in 1972, and the Montreal Expos became the Washington Nationals in 2005, so franchise, city and supporters each have a different successor

## The case

Checked on 2026-10-16 against the franchise histories
(en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Senators_(1901–1960),
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Senators_(1961–1971),
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos):

- The American League's original Washington club played 1901–1960. It
  was officially the "Nationals" for most of 1905–1955, with "Senators" in
  common use throughout. It moved to Minnesota as the Twins in 1961.
- An expansion club, also the Senators, played in Washington 1961–1971
  and moved to Arlington as the Texas Rangers in 1972.
- The Montreal Expos (1969–2004) moved to Washington as the Nationals in
  2005, whose Washington identity invokes the city's older baseball
  history while their franchise lineage runs through Montreal.

The owner's phrase was "a franchise that has changed locations and names
multiple times". A2's correction: "the Washington Senators were not a
single franchise that repeatedly relocated and changed names. The name
belonged to multiple distinct franchises." That is right.

## What it can show

That the continuities that ordinarily come bundled, legal franchise,
civic role and local tradition, can be separated, and that each then picks
a different successor. A2: "The league can stipulate that the Twins
inherited the original Senators' franchise history. It cannot thereby
determine that the original Senators' *fandom* continued uniquely as Twins
fandom." A 1961 supporter could follow the club to Minnesota, adopt the
expansion club, stop following baseball, or keep a loyalty to a team that
no longer existed. A5 §6: the target decides which continuity matters
([CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md)), and what is missing is a criterion that tells numerical
continuation, branching descent and independent reconstitution apart
([QUESTION-tmpn78rt](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpn78rt.md)).

It also tests [CLAIM-tmpdhfze](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdhfze.md): the 2005 Nationals' claim on old
Washington baseball is continuity by narrative and place, with no causal
path from the original club's organization. If that claim counts as
continuation, transmission is not necessary.

It cannot show how actual supporters divided, or that any fandom's
identity branched; that needs supporters' accounts, attendance and
retrospective identification (A4). Wegner, Delia and Baker on fans facing
a threatened relocation ([LIT-tmpkp0gw](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkp0gw.md), unread) is the nearest empirical
work the exchange found.
