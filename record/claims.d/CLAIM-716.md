# CLAIM-716: A breach can lower the injured party's position if it refuses to settle, so that the breaching party gains leverage over the resolution of its own breach: a settlement the injured party rationally accepts afterward can leave the breacher ahead, the leverage coming from the violation and not from any right to set the terms

## The claim

The owner's U25: "how does this developing theory model fait accompli
contract violation?" A55 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b) answered
with a mechanism:

> **One party acts contrary to an existing obligation, then uses the new circumstances created by that action to constrain the other party's practical choices.**

**The bargaining channel** (A55 §3). Let d_B(s) be B's payoff from
refusing a proposed settlement in state s. After a breach, B may face
litigation, partial compensation or a delay that makes performance
worthless, so d_B(s_after) < d_B(s_before), "a possible consequence of the
breach, not a universal property of breaches." B accepts a settlement
when its value to B is at least d_B(s_after). So "**A has changed B's
disagreement point by violating the agreement that was supposed to govern
their relationship**."

**A55's numbers**, checked: the entitlement is worth $100,000 to B; A
gains $40,000 by breaching; after the irreversible breach B expects to
recover $20,000 net; A offers $25,000. B rationally accepts (25 > 20). A
keeps $15,000 of its gain (40 − 25), and B bears an uncompensated loss of
$75,000 (100 − 25). "The injured party's rationality becomes part of the
mechanism through which the original violation is consolidated."

**The definition** (A55 §9), four conditions: "A party subject to an
existing obligation takes action contrary to it"; the action "changes the
injured party's feasible remedies, enforcement costs, or bargaining
alternatives"; this gives the breaching party "practical leverage over
the subsequent resolution"; and "The leverage derives at least partly
from the consequences of the violation itself." Formally, with 𝓡_B(s; H,
K, G) the remedial outcomes B can reach from s within horizon H, budget K
and arrangement G, a specified remedial objective J has J ∈ 𝓡_B(s_t^0)
and J ∉ 𝓡_B(s_t^a), the two states compared at the same time t, as
[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md) requires; strategic hold-up adds d_B(s_t^a) < d_B(s_t^0) and
evidence that A exploits it. A: "Neither alone establishes legal
liability, intent, or the ultimate moral judgment."

**Capture and externalization** (A57 §8): "**Remedial externalization:**
An actor exercises a capability while shifting the costs of discovering,
contesting, reversing, or compensating for its consequences onto others."
"**Remedial capture:** An actor's conduct changes the organization of
enforcement or bargaining so that the actor acquires practical leverage
over the resolution of its own disputed or wrongful conduct." "An
accidental injury might externalize substantial costs without granting
the injurer bargaining leverage. A strategic fait accompli may produce
both." This claim is capture.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)** (Active) holds the reachability half exactly: an
  intervention can make a specified corrective outcome costlier or
  unreachable while the governing standards stay as they were. It says
  nothing about bargaining. This claim adds the second channel, in which
  the injured party's own best response turns the changed state into
  terms. A57 §6 restates the reachability half as R_B(s(a)) ⊊ R_B(s(0))
  without the time index; A55 §9 keeps it, and the record's version is
  A55's.
- **[CLAIM-619](CLAIM-619.md)** (Proposed): an organization answers a challenge by
  reducing the capacity for future challenge. That acts on the
  challenger's later capacity; this acts on the value of refusing now.
- **[CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md)**: A55 §3 separates this from residual control: "the
  breacher creates a new bargaining position through an unauthorized
  action rather than simply exercising a residual right over an
  unspecified contingency."
- **[CLAIM-734](CLAIM-734.md)**: the same act seen as a change of allocation rule,
  from consent to compensation. That claim is about which mechanism
  governs the entitlement; this one is about the terms reached under it.
- **[CLAIM-722](CLAIM-722.md)** (F4): succeeding at making a remedy unreachable gains
  no power over what is owed. This claim is the descriptive mechanism the
  normative claim answers: the breacher does gain leverage, and that one
  says it gains no authority. A55 §8 has the bridge: B chose, it was B's
  best option, and "**The circumstances making that settlement B's best
  available option were legitimate.** This does not follow."
- **Resource-mediated hold-up** (A61 §5) reaches the same leverage by
  depleting B's means of pursuing the claim instead of changing the
  world: "The difference lies in **the provenance of the alternatives
  confronting B at the moment of choice**." That route is [CLAIM-748](CLAIM-748.md).

## What it does not say

- Not that every breach that moves options is coercive. A55 §6's table
  separates "**Compensated breach**" (an adequate, reliably enforceable
  substitute), "**Fait accompli with residual loss**" and "**Strategic
  remedial hold-up**"; only the last is this claim, and A: "Our theory
  should therefore avoid saying: *Every breach that changes bargaining
  options is coercive.*"
- Not that the settlement is void. A55 §3: "That doesn't automatically
  mean the settlement is legally void for duress."
- Not intent. The claim needs leverage that derives from the violation;
  that the breacher sought it is a further finding, which A's definition
  asks for only for "strategic" hold-up.
