# THEORY-1356: Collins and Frank propose that people learning stimulus–action rules by reinforcement spontaneously build latent task-sets cued by one input dimension, even when that structure gives no advantage during learning, so that a new context signalling an old task-set is learned faster (positive transfer) while a new context whose rule partly overlaps old task-sets draws errors that would be correct under an old task-set (negative transfer)

## Source

Collins and Frank (2013), [LIT-2012](../literature.d/LIT-2012.md); read in [NOTE-1545](../notes.d/NOTE-1545.md).

## What was actually shown

- **No advantage during learning.** A flat conjunctive solution is as good
  as a structured one during acquisition; structure building "does not
  seem optimal in terms of quantity of information to be stored and
  complexity of the model needed to represent structure" (p. 215).
- **Positive transfer.** Experiment 1 (N = 33): "we observed moderately
  but significantly faster learning in the transfer (C3) condition
  relative to the new (C4)" (t = 2.37, p = .024, pp. 208–209); replicated
  in Experiment 2 (N = 35).
- **Negative transfer.** An excess of neglect-context errors in the new
  context needing a partly overlapping task-set, significant over the
  whole group in Experiment 1; in Experiment 2 "the interaction typical of
  negative transfer was not significant, we got a similar trend" (p. 214).
- **Cued by one dimension.** The dimension with the larger reaction-time
  switch-cost predicted the kind of transfer (r = .39, p = .019, p. 211);
  in Experiment 2 the test was built on it.
- **Model.** The pattern is what the C-TS model predicts and the flat,
  dimension-expert and action-set models do not; model fits favoured
  structure, by "small" differences (p. 213).

## What this does not say

- That people perform Bayesian inference over task-sets: "We do not
  propose that humans solve the inference problem posed by such a
  generative model" (p. 194).
- That reuse follows a task-set's popularity, the Chinese-restaurant prior;
  the authors leave that untested.
- That everyone builds structure: Group 2, with no switch-cost asymmetry,
  transferred anyway, unexplained, and in the EEG re-analysis of the
  Experiment 2 participants 16 of 35 showed no transfer.
- That the structure is hierarchical in time: task-sets here are a
  within-trial abstraction over the state space, not options.
