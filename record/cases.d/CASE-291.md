# CASE-291: Identical harm, different histories of incapacity

## The case

A110 §11 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):

> Two officials make identical wrongful decisions.
>
> Both sincerely believe they are acting appropriately.
>
> Both lack access to the distinctions necessary to recognize the wrong.
>
> In the first case, the institutional limitation was not reasonably foreseeable or preventable.
>
> In the second, a superior deliberately removed an established review mechanism that would have identified the problem.

Held fixed: "the immediate act, harm, and frontline official's state of
knowledge". Varied: the history of the incapacity.

A's three predicted separations: "The affected party's underlying claim need
not differ. The frontline officials' culpability need not differ. But
responsibility for maintaining the defective mechanism may differ
substantially."

**Variant** (A110 §7, Case B): the review decays through an office's
negligence, "despite foreseeable risks and feasible maintenance", with
"fault attributable to the failure of maintenance, even if the individual
who later makes the mistake acts in good faith". A110 §7's three histories
(unavoidable limitation, negligent deterioration, deliberate insulation)
are this case's three arms; A108 §5's table adds a fourth, reasons
recognized and disregarded, which breaks the held-fixed knowledge and so
is not an arm.

## What it can show

Fictional, it shows that the separations are coherent: one act, one harm
and one state of knowledge are compatible with different upstream
responsibility, so [CLAIM-733](../claims.d/CLAIM-733.md)'s divergence can occur with nothing else
different. For the second arm, [CLAIM-714](../claims.d/CLAIM-714.md): the incapacity that leaves
the official blameless is the product of a superior's earlier decision.

It cannot show that the superior's removal breached a duty. The case says
"deliberately removed", not "wrongfully"; that needs the maintenance duty
([CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md), [ARG-114](../arguments.d/ARG-114.md)). As A says of its Case C, a finding that a
capability is lacking "is descriptive", and a finding of violation "requires
an additional normative premise". It also does not reach [QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md)'s
own case, a mechanism installed by an outside party: both arms here are
internal to one institution. It bears on that question by showing that
formation history changes who is responsible while present behaviour does
not.

It differs from [CASE-229](CASE-229.md) (Proposed), which holds harm and remedy fixed
and varies whether an institution kept its duty to maintain review, with
the institution as the bearer. Here the bearers differ within one
institution: frontline and superior.
