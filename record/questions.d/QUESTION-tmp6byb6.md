# QUESTION-tmp6byb6: Where an organization's parts are related by transports established independently of the analysis, and those transports have nontrivial holonomy, is the holonomy an error the organization tends to remove, or a persistent feature it maintains, and does its pattern predict the organization's response to intervention beyond what its pairwise relations and its dynamics predict?

## Why it is a question

LIT-tmpr8qf7 reads a nontrivial loop as corruption to be removed, and on
its benchmark it is, because the truth was built flat (THEORY-tmpy1s8c).
A2 of the exchange proposed the other reading for organized systems: "a
stable distribution of relational frustration", regulated,
compartmentalized or exploited, not eliminated. That fits CLAIM-471's view
that individuation need not need perfect agreement. For d > 1 the
mathematics allows it in a graded form: a connection can be frustrated on
some directions and compatible on others (CLAIM-tmp3lml8).

Three things keep it open.

- **The transports must not be the modeller's.** QUESTION-130's first
  objection applies unchanged. A holonomy computed from maps the analyst
  chose says nothing about the organization. Transports must be fitted per
  edge from paired observations, each edge independently, since fitting
  them from one set of global frames makes every loop trivial by
  construction, and they must be checked on observations not used to fit
  them. The companion paper's remark that an anchored orthogonal transport
  is identified "only on the anchored subspace" (LIT-tmpbihhf, p. 9) sets
  how many anchors are needed.
- **Holonomy is not dynamics.** One connection can sit in dissipative or
  conservative dynamics with different behaviour and identical holonomy
  (CASE-179's coupling regimes; CLAIM-481). Persistence of frustration has
  to be measured, not inferred from the connection.
- **Consistency-seeking revision can flatten without improving.** If the
  organization, or the analyst, revises the maps to reduce holonomy, it can
  reach agreement by changing how relations are expressed (THEORY-1088), so
  falling holonomy is not evidence of better organization (CLAIM-342).

## What would count as an answer

A system, simulated or measured, whose transports are fitted from held-out
observations, with nontrivial holonomy whose pattern is tracked over time
and under perturbation. Then a comparison of two such systems matched on
ordinary trajectories and on pairwise coupling, in the manner of
CLAIM-414's constructions, but differing in holonomy, whose responses to
interventions the holonomy predicts and the other models do not. A
negative answer is as useful: holonomy that adds nothing once the pairwise
couplings and the connection Laplacian's spectrum are known. CASE-179 is
the natural place to run it, as an added readout of its frustrated-maps
regime.
