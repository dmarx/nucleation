# THEORY-1482: Brown, Donner, Ribic and Bocetti project that the Kirtland's Warbler, recovered on plantation habitat that replaces suppressed wildfire, settles at a lower plateau when the supplied breeding habitat is cut, without going extinct from habitat loss alone, so that the species remains reliant on humans to keep its breeding habitat at its current quantity

## Source

Brown, Donner, Ribic & Bocetti (2019), [LIT-2155](../literature.d/LIT-2155.md); read in [NOTE-1674](../notes.d/NOTE-1674.md).

## What was actually shown

- **A model, run for 100 years**, of breeding on planted jack pine in
  Michigan and wintering in the Bahamas, with parameters from field
  studies and a validation against the 2001–2012 census.
- **Current management**: about 2,500 males; "no simulations fell below
  the critical threshold of 200 males" (§3).
- **Supply cut**: the population "initially declined, but stabilized at
  ca. 1,300 males" with climate-reduced habitat, and "at ca. 1,900 males"
  with management-reduced habitat (§3).
- **Habitat alone does not collapse it**: "loss of breeding habitat alone
  (up to 48.7%) did not cause extinction" (§4); with drier winters added,
  "98.1% of simulations fell below 200 males, and 9.9% went extinct" (§3).
- **Why the supply must continue**: the habitat-making fire regime is
  suppressed (§1). The model could have come out otherwise: a population
  that held its level on less habitat, by higher density, would have
  argued against reliance.

## What this does not say

- What happens if the supply stops: no scenario removes the plantations
  or cowbird removal entirely.
- That the plateaus are a property of the birds: ceiling density
  dependence makes population follow habitat area by construction.
- Anything observed after delisting: it is a projection.
- Anything from Scott et al. (2010), which it does not restate.
