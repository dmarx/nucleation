# ARG-tmps95tv: Clauset, Shalizi and Newman's fitting procedure and their finding that the lognormal is seldom excluded, as a source, support fitting the tail against alternatives before reading a mechanism, and the claim that a power law does not identify its mechanism only indirectly, while supplying none of the dynamical tests

## The inference

CLAIM-803: a power law does not identify its mechanism; read the
dynamics, after fitting the tail against alternatives such as the
lognormal. Ground: THEORY-tmpw4qgu.

## Critical questions (source)

**Does the work say this, read in context?** The fitting clause, yes:
"being roughly straight on a log-log plot is a necessary but not
sufficient condition for power-law behavior" (§4, p. 15), and Box 1's
three steps. The first clause, not in those words. It follows from what
they find: the lognormal "is not ruled out for any of our data sets except
the HTTP connections" (p. 26), so the tail often cannot separate even two
families, and for nine data sets "we must consider whether there is a
mechanistic or other nonstatistical argument" (p. 26).

**Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** For "fit first", yes.
For "read the dynamics", no: gain proportional to size, floors and
resets, and a cutoff that moves with system size are not discussed. The
nested test of a cutoff (§5.2) detects one in a sample; it says nothing
of how it scales.

**Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** Not as the
claim is worded, which cites the paper for fitting. ARG-141 did, in
resting the dynamical tests partly on this work; corrected there. The
authors also hold that form "may matter greatly" for inferring mechanisms
(p. 30), which the claim's "What it does not say" already allows.

## Verdict

Support for the fitting clause, indirect support for the first clause.
THEORY-tmpw4qgu replaces LIT-2987 in the claim's grounds.
