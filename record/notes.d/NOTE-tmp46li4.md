# NOTE-tmp46li4: Flack — Coarse-graining as a downward causation mechanism

## Contribution

It gives downward causation an operational form that avoids the charge of
non-materialism: the higher level acts only through components that
estimate it and tune to the estimate, so "the components are tuning to
patterns they perceive and hence are 'doing the work'" (§1). It splits the
notion into a weak form (apparent downward causation) and a strong one
(effective downward causation), lists criteria for each, and ties the
strong form to the origin of a new organizational level (§1, §7). It
proposes information-theoretic measures for how far an aggregate is built
by the collective rather than mirroring the environment, and for whether a
level is consolidating (§6). The macaque power example (§3) is a review of
the author's earlier work ([9,10,26,27]); no data are presented here.

## Key insight

The cause is the members' estimate, not the aggregate. "I propose we move
from simple feedback to downward causation when components tune behaviour
in response to estimates of coarse-grained, aggregate properties" (§1). The
aggregate is itself computed by the components ("endogenous
coarse-graining", §2), so the loop runs from microscopic behaviour, through
the components' own integrations of it, to a slow variable they estimate
and condition on. Slowness is what makes the tuning possible: "for tuning
to be possible the coarse-grained variables need to have a slow time scale
compared with (i) the microscopic behaviour producing them and (ii) the
computation used to estimate them" (§1, citing [8–10]).

## Assumptions

- **Adaptive systems are collective, heterogeneous and noisy** (§1): "It is
  not necessarily (it is perhaps rarely) the case that all components
  perceive similarly the aggregate properties describing the system or tune
  to them in the same way."
- **Coarse-graining has two defining properties** (§2): it is "lossy but
  true", introducing no outside information, and it is "an integration over
  component behaviour", which is what sets it apart from lossless
  compression. §6 then concedes that in Nature it "is unlikely to be a
  'true' simplification of the microscopic details as it is the physics
  sense", because components are imperfect.
- **The causal notion is operational, not a definition of causation.** "I
  will not discuss in this paper when the operational concept of downward
  causation I am proposing conforms to strict definitions of causality (e.g.
  [13,14])" (§1). [13] is Ay's refinement of the common cause principle,
  [14] Janzing et al. on quantifying causal influences.
- **An idealized observer** "with perfect information about the
  regularities in the system or environment" supplies the benchmark value
  of S (§6, §7).

## Key results

### Two forms of downward causation (§1)

- **Apparent (weak).** "I use the term apparent downward causation when
  tuning is partial and imprecise. In this weak or minimal form, only a few
  components need to be tuning their behaviour to estimates of aggregate
  properties for there to be some downward causation in the system." It
  "does not demand that estimates of aggregate properties be correct or
  even good predictors", and "components do not need to agree in their
  estimates".
- **Effective (strong)**, when (citing [8–10]): "aggregate properties are
  predictive of the future state of the system (slow variables);
  aggregate properties are robust to small perturbations; estimates of
  these variables are used nearly universally by all components to tune
  decision-making; components largely agree in their estimates of these
  variables; and as estimates converge there should be an increase in
  mutual information between the microscopic behaviour and the
  macroscopic properties."
- **Agreement in estimates is not agreement in strategy.** Components "need
  not be in agreement about how to best to tune to these variables", and
  "High agreement—high homogeneity—in strategy would likely make the system
  sensitive to perturbations and move it towards a critical point" ([12]).
- **Level.** "from an operational perspective it makes sense to start
  talking of a new level of organization when the criteria for effective
  downward causation are met" (§1). In its strong form downward causation
  "locally reduces entropy and creates order, which facilitates extraction
  of energy to do work" (§1); the paper does not return to the energy point.

### Coarse-graining (§2)

Temperature as the example; effective theories "by definition are agnostic
to system mechanics", and the ones of interest "have a basis in mechanism
but nonetheless ignore many of the details". "To distinguish coarse-graining
in Nature from coarse-graining by scientists, we call coarse-graining in
Nature endogenous coarse-graining [8]."

### The macaque power example (§3)

- **Power** "is operationalized as the degree of consensus among group
  members that an individual can win fights", encoded in "a network of
  subordination signals". The species is not named in this paper ("a
  well-study macaque society model system").
- **Two coarse-grainings.** A subordination signal is "a coarse-grained
  representation of the history of fight outcomes over some period", sent
  only when the sender perceives "a large asymmetry in fighting ability in
  its opponent's favour". A power score is "a course-grained representation
  of the signalling network" (sic). "The power distribution (DSP) results
  from the collective computation by all individuals of their power
  scores."
- **Fighting continues at a reduced rate** after signalling, "a mechanism by
  which the relationship might reverse in the future", likened to "a
  'background process' running on your computer".
- **Two phases**: "an information accumulation phase in which the
  individuals, like sensors, gather information semi-independently", and
  "a consensus or aggregation phase" in the signalling network.
- **Why coarse-grain.** Memory minimization: after signalling, the fight
  series "can (in principle, we do not yet know exactly how this works) be
  deleted from memory". And "uncertainty reduction through slow variable
  construction": "even though the coarse-graining is lossy, from a
  functional perspective information is gained because the signal to noise
  ratio is higher". "There is not yet a good language for discussing this
  transition."
- **Function.** A DSP predicts interaction cost "if it has mutual
  information with the underlying (and not directly observable)
  distribution of fighting abilities and changes slowly [27]". Its right
  skew makes policing accessible to those in the tail, who "are perceived
  to be disproportionately powerful and are unchallenged when they
  intervene".
- **Apparent downward causation** in the system "when the DSP has
  consolidated enough so that it changes more slowly than the microscopic
  fight behaviour producing it and at least one individual uses estimates
  of its power scores or other properties of the DSP ... to make
  predictions about interaction cost and tune behaviour".
- **Effective downward causation** "when/if (i) the collectively estimated
  DSP is robust to small perturbations in signalling patterns and has a
  slower time scale than the fight outcome network (many fights per hour
  with a per individual time scale on the order of hours [29]) underlying
  it and the signalling network used to compute it, (ii) the DSP predicts
  the cost of social interaction, (iii) individuals' estimates of
  properties of the DSP begin to converge ... and (iv) the mutual
  information between DSP and the underlying distribution of fighting
  abilities is high".
- **Convergence (iii) is more likely** with a fully connected fight
  network, similar dataset sizes, social learning, disparity in fighting
  ability, incentives to estimate accurately [27], similar computational
  capacity, "and as a result of positive feedback induced by the downward
  causation itself".
- **Pairwise effective downward causation.** Signal exchange "strongly
  predicts for signal sender tolerance by the receiver around resources"
  and reduces uncertainty about the relationship for both; "signal senders
  spend greater time in proximity to signal receivers than in the absence
  of signal exchange".

### Other systems (§4, §5)

- Fontana and co-workers' internal coarse-graining of molecular signalling
  into "fragments", "patterns that the system recognizes and uses" ([24,25]).
  "It is not yet clear however whether this work has anything to say about
  downward causation"; it does not say how fragments are produced.
- Mehta and Schwab's mapping between variational renormalization and deep
  networks via restricted Boltzmann machines ([35]), and Shwartz-Ziv and
  Tishby's information bottleneck with "a fast fitting phase" and "a slow
  generalization or compression phase" ([36]). Flack reads (§5) the layered
  networks as "each layer consolidated through coarse-graining and effective
  downward causation, such that the coarse-grained output one layer up,
  influences the microscopic behaviour one (and in some cases two layers)
  down". The difference she notes: "in our work the fitting phase is slow
  and the generalization phase is relatively fast". Her own open question:
  "whether effective downward causation in adaptive systems can increase
  symmetries at the microscopic level making the system as it consolidates
  increasingly amendable to coarse-graining".

### Measures and mechanisms (§6)

- **Figure 1's variables.** "E is the state of the environment. Mi are the
  strategies/behaviour/opinions of the microscopic components about E (and
  S...), S is the coarse-grained, aggregate variable. P is the algorithm
  that maps the environment onto component behaviour in M. C is the
  collective computation that aggregates the information in M and outputs
  S. D is the algorithm that captures both how the components, i, in M
  estimate S and use the estimate to tune their behaviour."
- **Constructed or mirrored.** Compare the collectively computed S with the
  idealized one, and "By calculating the conditional mutual information for
  these variables we can determine the contribution of the
  environment/some underlying constraint versus the collective computation
  to S." Where the environment contributes little, "components are
  effectively constructing their macroscopic worlds through collective
  behaviour and information processing". Power and stock price are
  "loosely tied" to a ground truth.
- **Predictions**, stated as expectation: where information processing
  dominates, macroscopic variables "will scale superlinearly"; with strong
  energetic constraints they will have high conditional mutual information
  with the environment and "scale sublinearly", as mass and metabolic rate
  do ([42]).
- **Consolidation.** "we can also quantify the extent to which a new
  organizational level is consolidating out of S by asking if the I(S;M|E)
  is increasing over time."
- **Time-scale separation as a mechanism.** "If the time scale associated
  with a coarse-grained or aggregate variable is very slow compared to the
  microscopic behaviour producing it, it effectively becomes the 'social'
  environment ... and components can tune to it regardless of whether it
  actually does a good job of summarizing the regularities in E or now in
  M—think of social institutions that are highly constraining and very hard
  to change or the power distribution in the monkey case study." It
  "facilitates learning and hence coordinated and organized behaviour".
  But "it seems likely the system will fail at some point if the aggregate
  properties components rely on for prediction do not capture the
  regularities in M or E—failure may just take longer if the time scale
  separation is very large." "In the monkey case study, both the signalling
  network and power structure have a much slower time scale than the fight
  dynamics."
- **Collective estimation as a second mechanism**, a wisdom-of-crowds
  variant that Flack calls "a somewhat trivial insight"; the interesting
  questions are the aggregation's details, since collective computation
  "may, as a result of higher order interactions and synergies, produce
  aggregate properties that bare little relation to the environmental
  states or underling constraints they are supposed to summarize" (sic).

### Conclusion (§7)

- Coarse-grained variables "promote accelerated rates of microscopic
  adaptation [8]": they let components fine-tune and "free components to
  search at low cost a larger space of strategies". RNA neutral networks:
  "structure changes more slowly than sequence" ([55]).
- **The level criteria restated** ([8,10]): estimates are "(functionally)
  good approximations of the idealized aggregate properties"; aggregates
  "sufficient to predict system dynamics at the macroscopic scale";
  components "rely to a greater extent on the coarse-grained descriptions
  ... than on microscopic behaviour"; estimates "largely in agreement"; and
  "mutual information between the consolidating higher level/layer and the
  microscopic behaviour one level down".
- "the consolidation of a new level of organization requires, in addition
  to downward causation (§1), that component estimates of aggregate
  properties ... are functionally good approximations of the aggregate
  properties as computed by an observer with perfect information". "an
  open and system dependent question is what constitutes 'good enough'."
- "the increase in correlation is due to the components increasingly
  perceiving the world in the same way—an effect that amplifies itself
  through effective downward causation."
- "The result is the slow variables become fundamental macroscopic
  properties that are a public good of sorts".

## Concepts

- **Endogenous coarse-graining** — coarse-graining "performed by the system
  (as opposed to a scientist) as an inference mechanism" (§1, §2, after
  [8]); Fontana's "internal coarse-graining" is the molecular counterpart.
- **Apparent downward causation** — at least some components tune to
  estimates of an aggregate that is slower than the behaviour producing it;
  estimates may be wrong and may disagree (§1, §3).
- **Effective downward causation** — the five §1 criteria: predictive (slow),
  robust, nearly universal use, agreement in estimates, rising micro–macro
  mutual information.
- **Slow variable** — an aggregate "predictive of the future state of the
  system" (§1); in §3 a variable that "changes more slowly than the
  microscopic fight behaviour producing it".
- **Power** — "the degree of consensus among group members that an
  individual can win fights" (§3).
- **Two-phase collective computation** — information accumulation, then
  aggregation or consensus (§3, §5).
- **'Social' environment** — an aggregate slow enough that components treat
  it as their environment, as distinct from the exogenous E (§6).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-327](../claims.d/CLAIM-327.md): two of its three stages are Flack's; the third is not.**
  Stage 1 (an observer finds a stable statistic) has no separate place in
  Flack. Her aggregate is computed by the members from the start
  ("endogenous coarse-graining", §2), so the observer enters only as the
  idealized benchmark (§6). Stage 2 (members recognize and use it) is her
  apparent downward causation, with two refinements the claim lacks: what
  members condition on is their *estimate*, which may be wrong and need not
  be shared (§1), and one member tuning is enough (§3). Stage 3, use that
  "preserves, transforms or regenerates" the variable, is not among
  Flack's criteria in either list (§1, §7). What reinforces itself in Flack
  is agreement: "positive feedback induced by the downward causation
  itself" makes estimates converge (§3), and correlation "amplifies itself
  through effective downward causation" (§7). Her stage beyond use is
  convergence, robustness, near-universal use and rising mutual
  information, and for a level, estimates good enough against the
  idealized aggregate (§7). The variable is maintained by members in
  Flack's example, since the signals that compose it are their behaviour
  and fighting continues as a "background process" (§3), but she does not
  make that a criterion. So [CLAIM-327](../claims.d/CLAIM-327.md) is the exchange's synthesis, with
  regeneration where Flack puts convergence. It also differs on what makes
  the variable a level: [CLAIM-327](../claims.d/CLAIM-327.md) says only the third stage does, and
  Flack says use alone is already (apparent) downward causation and a level
  needs the effective criteria. This paper does not settle whether Flack
  2012 says otherwise. Its own deference to [9] suggests the 2012 paper has
  the same criteria. [LIT-1122](../literature.d/LIT-1122.md) remains unread. [THEORY-tmp7ps0w](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7ps0w.md) states
  Flack's criteria.
- **[CLAIM-331](../claims.d/CLAIM-331.md): Flack builds slowness into the definition.** For her,
  slowness relative to the producing behaviour is a condition even of
  apparent downward causation (§3), and "slow variables" is the first
  effective criterion (§1). Its stated reason is enabling: "for tuning to be
  possible" (§1), asserted, citing [8–10]. That is stronger than [CLAIM-331](../claims.d/CLAIM-331.md)'s
  "a mark of a level and not its definition", and it is support of the kind
  [CLAIM-331](../claims.d/CLAIM-331.md)'s note of 2026-10-20 sets aside for slow feature analysis:
  slower by construction. The one empirical time scale given is the fight
  network's ("many fights per hour with a per individual time scale on the
  order of hours [29]", §3); the DSP's is only "much slower" (§6). The
  claim's caution "Not that a slow variable is a level" is Flack's too:
  slowness alone gives no downward causation without tuning.
- **[CASE-116](../cases.d/CASE-116.md).** Arm A, slow and unread, has no downward causation in
  Flack's sense, since nobody tunes (§1, §3), which agrees with the case.
  Arm B, read and rewritten and fast, falls outside Flack's account by
  definition, since she requires the variable to be slower than the
  behaviour producing it (§3) and than the computation that estimates it
  (§1). So arm B's "slowness is not necessary" contradicts a stipulation,
  not a finding. Flack's reason for the stipulation (that estimation needs
  a variable stable over the estimation window) is the thing arm B would
  have to meet: its members read Z_t directly, with no estimation step, so
  her reason does not bite there. Arm C, transmission without running, is
  not addressed.
- **[CLAIM-tmpmx2ql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpmx2ql.md): Flack states its first half for social systems.** §6:
  a very slow aggregate "effectively becomes the 'social' environment ...
  and components can tune to it regardless of whether it actually does a
  good job of summarizing the regularities", with institutions "highly
  constraining and very hard to change" as the example, and separation
  "facilitates learning and hence coordinated and organized behaviour".
  That is separation making a constraint, made effective only through
  tuning, which is the claim's "available, not effective". Flack adds a
  limit the claim lacks: a poorly summarizing aggregate will likely fail
  eventually, "failure may just take longer if the time scale separation is
  very large". On the second half (organization makes separation) she has
  only an open question: whether effective downward causation "can increase
  symmetries at the microscopic level making the system as it consolidates
  increasingly amendable to coarse-graining" (§5). Filed as
  [THEORY-tmphciwc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphciwc.md) (Proposed).
- **[CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md): the macaque case is an instance of endorsement summarizing a
  track record, but not of trust in its sense.** A subordination signal is
  a coarse-graining of fight outcomes, sent by the party that perceives
  itself weaker; power is the group's consensus aggregated from those
  signals; and fighting continues at a reduced rate so that the
  relationship can reverse (§3). That is [CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)'s "right a lot" loop
  with fights as the track record, a refresh process in place of a memory
  kernel, and memory minimization (the fight series can be dropped once
  signalled, "we do not yet know exactly how this works") as the forgetting
  of raw history. The tail's access to policing (§3) is durable,
  differentiated authority. Flack does not discuss early advantage, and the
  reliability side rests on mutual information with fighting abilities [27],
  which is not shown here. What is endorsed is the ability to win fights,
  not the competence of a policy, so the case is a structural analogue.
- **[CLAIM-334](../claims.d/CLAIM-334.md), [TERM-051](../terms.d/TERM-051.md) and [CLAIM-158](../claims.d/CLAIM-158.md): a fourth sense of "downward
  causation".** Flack's is neither Seth's Granger sense, nor ΦID's D, nor
  the interventional one. She leaves its relation to "strict definitions of
  causality" aside (§1). Its causal path runs through the members' estimates,
  which are states of the members distinct from S. That is the route
  [CLAIM-334](../claims.d/CLAIM-334.md) leaves open: one can intervene on what members believe of S
  without holding S's micro base fixed. Flack does not put it that way.
  "Downward causation" in Flack should not be compared with D or with
  Granger measures without saying so. Her apparent-to-effective gradation is
  a strength of tuning, not a spectrum of who drives ([CLAIM-158](../claims.d/CLAIM-158.md)).
- **[QUESTION-033](../questions.d/QUESTION-033.md): a proposed apportionment.** §6 proposes conditional mutual
  information to apportion S between the environment and the collective
  computation, and I(S;M|E) over time to track consolidation, which is the
  "how the apportionment changes over time" the question asks for. They are
  proposals and are computed for no system here.
- **[NOTE-153](NOTE-153.md)'s C8** cites Flack for higher-level causal power. This paper
  gives that power only through components' estimates (§1), which is
  compatible with Rizi's "not a metaphysical extra cause".
- No instruction for machine-learning practice is made; §5 reads two
  deep-learning papers for analogy only.

## Limitations

- **No data.** The macaque results are summarized from [9,10,26,27,29] and
  are not reproduced; no time scale is given for the DSP or the signalling
  network beyond "much slower" (§6).
- **The two lists of criteria differ.** §1's effective criteria include
  robustness and near-universal use. §7's level criteria drop robustness,
  replace near-universal use with relying "to a greater extent" on the
  coarse-grained description, and add that estimates be good approximations
  of the idealized aggregate. §7 says the level "requires, in addition to
  downward causation (§1)" the approximation condition, while §1 had said
  a level begins when the effective criteria are met. The paper does not
  reconcile them.
- **"Good enough" is left open** (§7), and the idealized observer's S is not
  defined for any system.
- **Physics slips.** "Temperature is the average speed of particles"
  (§2, rather than mean kinetic energy), and BCS theory is called a theory
  "of high temperature superconductivity" (§2). The XML also has typos
  ("course-grained", "bare little relation", "underling"). None bears on
  the argument.
- **Figure 1 was not seen**; its content is known only from the caption and
  §6.
- **The causal notion is not tied to any account of causation** (§1), so
  whether apparent downward causation is causation at all is not argued.
- The deep-learning section reads the cited papers through the author's
  framework. The information-bottleneck results it relies on ([36]) are
  reported as experiments that "suggested", and the paper says no more for
  them.

## Open questions

- Does Flack 2012 ([LIT-1122](../literature.d/LIT-1122.md)) include regeneration of the variable by its
  use among its criteria, or only convergence? A reading of it would settle
  [CLAIM-327](../claims.d/CLAIM-327.md)'s attribution.
- Is slowness needed for downward causation, or only for estimation? A case
  in which members read a fast variable without estimating it ([CASE-116](../cases.d/CASE-116.md) arm
  B) and tune to it would separate the two.
- Can I(S;M|E) be computed for the macaque data, and does it rise as the
  DSP consolidates (§6)?
- Do the superlinear and sublinear scaling predictions hold (§6)?
