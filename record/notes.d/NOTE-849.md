# NOTE-849: Seth & Bayne — Theories of consciousness

## Contribution

The review sorts the main neurobiological theories of consciousness by
what they aim to explain, so that it is clear where they are rivals and
where they are answering different questions. It sets out the evidence
each cites, the empirical debates that could separate them, and three
things theories must acquire before they can be tested properly:
precision, comprehensiveness, and a solution to the measurement problem.

## Key insight

Theories that look like rivals often have different targets: some
explain why a content is conscious, some explain what it is like, some
explain global states, some explain function. Where they do compete, the
evidence is filtered through report, which is itself one of the things in
dispute. So the main empirical debates are as much about what counts as
evidence as about the brain.

## Assumptions

- Only theories expressed in, or expressible in, neurobiological terms
  and consistent with known physics; quantum theories are set aside.
- The phenomenal/functional distinction is used as a distinction of
  explanatory targets, not a claim that the two are independent ("they
  are very likely not independent").
- Global versus local states, and why a state is conscious versus why it
  has its character, are the axes of comparison.

## Key results

As a review, its results are classifications and arguments.

- **Targets (Preliminaries).** Global states (wakefulness, dreaming,
  sedation; "regions within a multi-dimensional space", not levels);
  local states or contents; phenomenal versus functional properties; why
  a content is conscious versus why it has the character it has.
- **Higher-order theories.** A state is conscious by being the target of
  a suitable meta-representation. They mainly explain why some contents
  are conscious; character is fixed by what the meta-representation
  ascribes. Anterior, especially prefrontal, emphasis.
- **Global workspace theories.** Consciousness is wide availability to
  consumer systems through ignition and broadcast in a frontoparietal
  workspace. Explicitly theories of "conscious access"; "rarely
  attempted" phenomenal character. Open: what makes a workspace global.
- **Integrated information theory.** Axioms about experience yield
  requirements on a substrate; consciousness is the cause–effect
  structure of a maximum of irreducible integrated information (Φ).
  Posterior "hot zone". The most comprehensive in coverage, but implies
  non-biological systems "already exist" that are conscious, and Φ is
  "infeasible to measure, except in simple model systems". PCI is a proxy,
  "not equivalent to Φ", and its success is compatible with other
  theories.
- **Reentry and predictive processing.** Consciousness with top-down
  signalling; local recurrency in perceptual cortex suffices for
  experience, frontal regions for report. Predictive processing is a
  theory "for consciousness science" rather than "of consciousness".
- **Unity.** IIT treats consciousness as necessarily unified; GWT may
  account for it; HOT and PP "gesture" at it or overlook it.
- **Cerebellum.** All four families can explain why it is not implicated,
  so such explanations "lend specific support to a theory only if the
  account provided is more plausible than the accounts that might be
  provided by its competitors".
- **Front versus back.** Prefrontal activity and decoding favour HOT and
  GWT; no-report studies show diminished prefrontal engagement, and
  posterior activity predicts dream reports, favouring IIT and reentry.
  "At its heart is a disagreement about the relationship between
  consciousness and cognitive access." Decoding a content from an area
  "does not establish that the brain itself is 'reading out' those
  contents" from it.
- **Untestable predictions.** That posterior activity suffices without
  anterior contribution cannot now be verified, "since verification
  relies on subjective report (or at least, executive control in some
  form), which in turn requires anterior cortical activity". IIT's
  attribution of consciousness to simple systems "cannot be sensibly
  evaluated" without detection methods. IIT predicts that inactive (but
  not inactivated) neurons contribute to experience, testable in
  principle by optogenetics.
- **Timing.** Early (120–200 ms, "awareness negativity") versus late
  (250–400 ms, P3b, ignition) onset; early favours IIT and reentry, late
  favours HOT and GWT; unresolved.
- **Moving forward.** Theories are now used as "narrative structures".
  They need precision (computational models), comprehensiveness (affect,
  thought, dreaming, infants, animals, AI) and a solution to the
  measurement problem.
- **Box 3, the measurement problem.** Introspection is unreliable and
  unavailable for infants, patients, animals; theory-based validation is
  contested; so validation "seems to require a method … neither based
  solely on introspection nor on theoretical considerations", and of the
  proposals, "none is uncontroversial".

## Concepts

- **Global state.** An organism's overall conscious condition; preferred
  to "level" because it may be multidimensional.
- **Local state.** A conscious content.
- **Cognitive access.** A state's availability to a wide range of
  cognitive processes, usually including report.
- **Measurement problem.** Identifying whether a state is conscious
  (contents), or whether a system can be (creatures).
- **Narrative structure.** A theory used to interpret data rather than
  tested by it.

## Connections

The NCC programme (Crick and Koch 1990; Koch et al. 2016) is the
starting point; the no-report paradigm is Tsuchiya et al. (2015). The
adversarial collaboration programme (Melloni et al. 2021) is the route to
theory testing they endorse, which later produced COGITATE ([LIT-973](../literature.d/LIT-973.md)).
Chalmers's hard problem (Box 1) and illusionism (Dennett, Frankish) frame
what the theories attempt. Block's access/phenomenal distinction is
implicit throughout; Block is not cited.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)**, first part. [LIT-982](../literature.d/LIT-982.md) is registered as mapping "what
  each kind of evidence is taken to establish". The review does that for
  report, no-report, decoding, timing and lesion evidence, at the level of
  which theory each is taken to favour. It does not ask what each kind can
  establish about experience as opposed to access, beyond saying that
  this is the disputed point. Its contribution to the question is the
  structural limit: report-based verification cannot test
  posterior-sufficiency, and marker validation is circular. Filed as
  [THEORY-298](../theory.d/THEORY-298.md).
- **[THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md)** (report and no-report) is consistent with it and more
  specific; the review notes no-report results "but see" a counter-study
  and does not raise the post-perceptual-cognition objection [THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md)
  rests on.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)** says, for AI, that the dispute is "over what counts as
  evidence as well as the answer". Box 3 says the same for infants,
  patients, animals and organoids: the measurement problem for creatures.
  [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) could cite it as a general form.
- **[THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md)** (deficits): the review's lesion and brain-injury evidence
  is presented as evidence for theories, without the access caveat.
  Nothing here disputes [THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md).
- **COGITATE ([THEORY-245](../theory.d/THEORY-245.md), [THEORY-296](../theory.d/THEORY-296.md)).** The review predates it
  and points to the adversarial collaboration that produced it.
- **Second part of [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)** (systems without cortex or biology).
  The review notes IIT's implication of non-biological consciousness and
  lists animals, organoids and AI under the creature measurement problem,
  but offers no evidence that carries over; it says such predictions
  cannot now be evaluated.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- A review; it argues and classifies but reports no new data.
- Coverage of each theory is brief and partly by the proponents' own
  framing; its evidence summaries are selective by its own statement.
- Read from the accepted manuscript; the published text may differ in
  wording.

## Open questions

- A validation method for a consciousness marker that is neither
  introspective nor theory-based, which the review calls required and
  absent.
- What makes a workspace "global", which would fix GWT's predictions for
  infants, split-brain patients, animals and AI.
- Whether IIT's inactive-neuron prediction can be tested by optogenetics.
