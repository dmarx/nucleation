# LIT-610: The Weighted Likelihood Ratio, Linear Hypotheses on Normal Location Parameters

James M. Dickey (1971), *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 42(1):204–223 — DOI-10.1214/aoms/1177693507

## Key takeaways

- **The theorem (§3, (3.8)–(3.9)).** Reparameterise θ as (η, ζ) and test
  the sharp hypothesis H: η = η_H, given positive prior mass, against
  H̄: η ≠ η_H. The prior is a point mass on H inside a continuous prior f
  ((3.2), (3.5)). If the nuisance prior under H is the alternative's prior
  conditioned at η_H, g(ζ) = f(η_H, ζ)/∫ f(η_H, ζ) dζ (3.8), then the
  weighted likelihood ratio, the Bayes factor for H, is
  L_D(H) = P′(η_H | H̄, D)/P′(η_H | H̄) (3.9), exactly. The proof is one
  substitution of (3.8) into the null's marginal likelihood (3.6).
- **It is general.** The likelihood is any φ(D | θ) continuous in θ, η can
  be a vector, and H any smooth sharp hypothesis. Normal location
  parameters are the applications of §§4–5, not a condition of the
  theorem.
- **The condition, and where continuity comes in.** Dickey makes f
  meaningful on H by defining densities as elementary derivatives,
  limits of probability over volume on shrinking balls (3.3)–(3.4), and in
  the applications states (3.8) as a limit: the null's nuisance prior is
  "the limiting distribution" of the alternative's as the tested
  parameter goes to its null value (§4.1.2, §5.1.2). That is the
  continuity condition Wagenmakers et al. ([LIT-609](LIT-609.md)) write down, though
  Dickey never calls it continuity: he uses the word only for the
  likelihood (§2) and for the utility version of the theorem (3.9*).
- **The attribution is Dickey's own.** He calls the result "Savage's
  Density Ratio", says Savage found it as an exact general formula in
  unpublished 1963 notes, and credits the proof to Dickey and Lientz.
  Approximate forms go back to Jeffreys (1948), Lindley (1961) and Savage
  (1959, 1961).
- **Closed forms from conjugate priors.** A normal mean with known
  variance gives L_D(H) = (n1/n0)^½ exp(½σ⁻²Q) (4.6)–(4.7), which goes to
  infinity as the alternative's prior gets vague (n0 → 0). Unknown
  variance gives a ratio of Student-t ordinates (4.19). A linear
  hypothesis C_Hβ = η_H in the linear normal model gives (5.24), a
  Bayesian replacement for the F test built from prior and posterior
  "sums of squares between", and with σ² unknown an F-density ordinate in
  the stable-estimation limit (5.45). A new conjugate family (4.23) lets
  the mean and variance be independent a priori, at the price of
  Behrens–Fisher densities.
- **The hedges.** Savage's stable-estimation approximation needs only one
  prior ordinate, but "should not form the basis of a formal decision
  procedure without an evaluation of the quality of the approximation"
  (§1) and is unlikely to be realistic with many parameters (§3). Positive
  mass on a sharp hypothesis is "proposed as a good approximation to many
  prior opinions" (§3), not defended. There are no numerical examples.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the model-comparison batch,
and read the same day from a JSTOR scan the owner supplied ([NOTE-508](../notes.d/NOTE-508.md)).
No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

It is the original of the result the record reads in Wagenmakers et al.
([LIT-609](LIT-609.md)) and in Friston & Penny ([LIT-620](LIT-620.md)), and it agrees with both. The
question this entry used to leave open is settled: Dickey states the ratio
for general sharp hypotheses, with any continuous likelihood, so when
Friston & Penny and Bayesian model reduction ([LIT-614](LIT-614.md)) say they generalise
"the Savage–Dickey ratio", what they generalise is the reduced prior, from
a point mass to any narrower prior, not the setting. The point-mass clause
of [THEORY-081](../theory.d/THEORY-081.md) now cites it directly.

Two details of the paper's own citations are worth knowing before quoting
it. It dates Dickey & Lientz 1968 in the text, but its reference list
places that paper in *Ann. Math. Statist.* 41, the 1970 volume, which is
the year [LIT-609](LIT-609.md) gives. And the invariance result it cites as Dickey &
Lientz (1969) has no entry in its reference list.

Read it for §3 (pp. 207–210). The rest is conjugate algebra for
normal models, the Behrens–Fisher cases and a decision-theoretic
generalisation to utilities ((2.14), (3.9*)).
