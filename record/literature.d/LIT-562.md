# LIT-562: Pervasive negative effects of rewards on intrinsic motivation: The myth continues

Judy Cameron, Katherine M. Banko and W. David Pierce (2001), *The Behavior Analyst* 24(1):1–44 — DOI-10.1007/BF03392017

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp6ruuz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6ruuz.md).*

- **The core effect is not in dispute.** On interesting tasks, expected
  tangible rewards offered for doing the task lowered later free choice:
  K = 57, −0.35 before outliers, still negative after them (pp. 16–17).
  Deci, Koestner and Ryan ([LIT-557](LIT-557.md)) found −0.40 for the same contingency.
- **Coding decides the rest.** Re-coded into seven procedural categories
  (Table 1, p. 12), CET's performance-contingent category splits. Rewards
  for doing well and per unit solved undermine free choice. Rewards for
  surpassing a score do not. Rewards for exceeding others enhance it
  (pp. 16–18, Figure 2).
- **Per-unit undermining tracks earning less than the maximum**: −0.22
  (K = 14) against −0.03 (K = 6) when everyone earned it in full. The
  authors read this as failure feedback or time pressure (pp. 19–20, 23).
- **Low-interest tasks**: rewards raised free choice, 0.28 (K = 12). This
  is outside Deci et al.'s scope (p. 15).
- **Small either way**: about −0.30 for rewards for doing or doing well,
  roughly 47 seconds of an 8-minute free period (pp. 24–25).

## Standing in the record

Filed at the owner's request as the rejoinder to Deci, Koestner and Ryan
([LIT-557](LIT-557.md)) in the dispute over whether rewards undermine intrinsic
motivation, and read on the owner's instruction. It is `compared_against`
that paper: it re-analyses the same literature, merges the two databases
and sets its results beside Deci et al.'s contingency by contingency
(Figures 2 and 4). It restates and keeps the conclusion of the authors' own
1994 analysis ([LIT-556](LIT-556.md)), which stays unread. That relation is left
undeclared.

It is the source of [THEORY-tmpmshgc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmshgc.md), the account that the tightness of a
reward's stated contingency decides its effect, which rivals cognitive
evaluation theory ([THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md)). It is also the second source of [THEORY-046](../theory.d/THEORY-046.md),
the undermining effect on which both sides agree.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
