# NOTE-2409: The links between prenatal stress and offspring development and psychopathology: disentangling environmental and inherited influences

## Contribution

The first human study to cross-foster the prenatal environment. With egg
or embryo donation, the woman who is pregnant is not the child's genetic
mother, so a link between her stress and the child's outcome cannot run
through what she passed on at conception. The study tests which
prenatal-stress associations survive that removal.

## Key results

- **The logic** (Abstract): "If links between prenatal stress and
  offspring outcome are environmental, association should be observed in
  unrelated as well as related mother–child pairs."
- **Birth weight and gestation** (Results): late-pregnancy stress and lower
  birth weight in unrelated pairs, β = −0.189, p = 0.01, and in related
  pairs, β = −0.127, p = 0.002. Gestational age: β = −0.265 and −0.209.
  The interaction with relatedness was not significant.
- **Antisocial behaviour** (Results): unrelated β = 0.190, p = 0.02;
  related β = 0.186, p = 0.001. This was unchanged by adjustment for the
  mother's current anxiety and depression.
- **Anxiety** (Results): associated in both groups at first. After
  adjustment for the mother's current symptoms, unrelated β = 0.114,
  p = 0.145, and related β = 0.119, p = 0.008. Current maternal symptoms
  mediated the link (Sobel 2.48, p = 0.01, in the unrelated group).
- **ADHD** (Results): unrelated β = 0.095, p = 0.25; related β = 0.163,
  p = 0.001; interaction β = 0.157, p = 0.05. The authors read this as
  "attributable to maternally inherited factors".
- **Where epigenetics falls** (Discussion): "epigenetic effects are thought
  to be important. In this design, these will emerge as environmental
  effects to the extent that epigenetic changes can be induced by
  environmental modification and stochastic processes." This covers
  changes induced in the fetus by the pregnancy. A mark carried in the
  donor's egg covaries with the donor, not the carrier, so it would show
  as "inherited".
- **Limit** (Limitations): the design "can detect inherited effects and
  can demonstrate consistency with environmental links ... however, it
  cannot demonstrate causality".

## Assumptions

- Donors are unrelated to both parents, and nothing about the donor (her
  stress, her genes) is correlated with the recipient's stress.
- The fetus's genes in the unrelated group do not drive the mother's
  prenatal stress. The authors argue this is unlikely.
- Retrospective maternal ratings of stress at three stages of pregnancy
  are valid. Test–retest ICC was about 0.7, and late stress went with
  objective birth measures.

## Concepts

- **Prenatal cross-fostering.** Assisted reproduction splits the genetic
  mother from the gestational mother, so related and unrelated
  mother–child pairs can be compared.
- **Inherited versus environmental**, as the design defines them:
  "inherited" is whatever travels with the gamete, and "environmental" is
  whatever travels with the woman who carries and raises the child.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md), the routes** (evidence on a design). The donor-gamete
  design separates the uterine and rearing routes from everything carried
  at conception. It cannot separate a germline epigenetic route from
  genetic inheritance, because both travel with the gamete. In a
  trauma version, with an exposed egg or sperm donor and unexposed
  recipients, a difference in the children would show transmission through
  the gamete, DNA and marks together, with the womb and rearing removed.
  That would be more than any study the record holds. No study of
  trauma-exposed gamete donors was found ([CLAIM-1182](../claims.d/CLAIM-1182.md)).
- **The parenting route** (support). The anxiety result is a postnatal
  route visible even in unrelated pairs. The same design shows the
  rearing route directly for parental depression in Lewis et al. 2011
  ([NOTE-2370](NOTE-2370.md)).
- **[CLAIM-644](../claims.d/CLAIM-644.md)** (a parallel; the corroboration is [CLAIM-1017](../claims.d/CLAIM-1017.md)'s). The study shows what an
  "adoption-like" design can do, and that it divides the routes into two
  groups, not four.

## Limitations

- The exposure is ordinary prenatal stress, rated retrospectively on one
  scale, not trauma.
- Cross-sectional data, with mothers rating both stress and child outcomes
  except for antisocial behaviour.
- The unrelated group is small (205), and mothers in it were older, with
  more hypertension.
- The study measures the mother's side only. The sperm-donation group
  could test the father's genes but is not used for that here.
