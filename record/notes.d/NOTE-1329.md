# NOTE-1329: Hashimoto et al. — Word embeddings as metric recovery

## Contribution

It recasts word embedding as estimating the metric of a latent "semantic
space", the object psychometricians since Rumelhart and Abrahamson (1973)
had posited, rather than as producing features. Under a toy generative
model, a metric random walk over words, it proves that log co-occurrences
over a large window converge to squared latent distances (Lemma 1,
Corollary 6), and reads GloVe as weighted multidimensional scaling,
word2vec as stochastic neighbour embedding and PMI-SVD as classical MDS
under a norm assumption, each with the true embedding at a global optimum.
It adds a direct estimator (negative-binomial "metric regression"), a
reduction of manifold learning to word embedding (random walks on a
neighbourhood graph as sentences), and two small inductive-reasoning test
sets (series completion and classification).

## Key insight

A one-step co-occurrence can depend on distance through any unknown link
function h; a co-occurrence over many steps forgets h and depends on
distance through the heat kernel, whose logarithm is quadratic in
distance. "Intuitively, this result is an analog of the central limit
theorem for random walks. Note that, for this reason, we do not need to
know the link function h" (p. 277). So what log co-occurrence statistics
can identify is the metric, and the coordinates only up to the isometries
that preserve it.

## Assumptions

- **The generative model, eq. (1):** P(Xt = j | Xt−1 = i) =
  h(‖xi − xj‖²/σ), with h "some unknown, sub-Gaussian function linking
  semantic similarity to co-occurrence" (p. 277). The authors: "while the
  random walk is introduced over the words, it is not intended as a model
  of language but rather as a tool to understand the recovery problem"
  (p. 277). "This toy model ignores the role of syntax and function words"
  (n. 4).
- **Appendix A's setting:** points drawn i.i.d. from a density p with
  bounded log-gradient and a strict lower bound p₀ on a compact Riemannian
  manifold; a "spatial graph" whose edge i → j is present with probability
  h(ρ(xi, xj)²/σn(xi)²) (Definition 2), which covers ε-ball and k-nearest
  neighbour graphs; scale functions converging after rescaling, with a
  polynomial number of points in each neighbourhood (p. 282).
- **An open conjecture for directed graphs.** The marginal densities must
  be uniformly equicontinuous: "For undirected spatial graphs, this is
  always true (Croydon and Hambly, 2008), but for directed graphs it is an
  open conjecture from (Hashimoto et al., 2015b)" (n. 10, p. 282). A
  k-nearest-neighbour graph, used in §6.3, is directed.
- **A window growing with the vocabulary**, tn, so that every pair
  co-occurs in the limit (n. 5), with corpus size m → ∞ and vocabulary
  n → ∞ (n. 6).
- **Norm assumptions for SVD and negative sampling:** the true embedding's
  norms must match the unigram frequencies (p. 279; Lemma 7). The two
  statements differ: σ⁻¹‖xi‖² = log(Ci/√∑Cj) in §5.1, and
  ‖xi‖/σ² = (Ci/∑Cj)^½ in Lemma 7.
- **Dimension known:** GloVe "can recover the true metric provided that we
  set d correctly" (p. 278).

## Key results

### Embeddability of co-occurrence data (§3, pp. 275–276)

- Normalized PMI against MEN similarity judgments: Pearson r = 0.75
  (Fig. 2). The normalization, unigram frequency to the 3/4 power, was
  chosen because it "maximizes the linear correlation in Figure 2" (n. 2).
- Tversky and Hutchinson's nearest-neighbour statistics (Table 2):
  centrality C and reciprocity Rf. "Under the GS model … C ≤ 2 with high
  probability as the number of words n → ∞", "typical non-asymptotic
  values of C range between 1 and 3, while non-embeddable hierarchical
  structures have C > 10"; Rf "should be greater than 0.5" (pp. 275–276).

| source | C | Rf |
|---|---|---|
| free association | 1.51 | 0.48 |
| Wikipedia | 2.21 | 0.63 |
| word2vec corpus | 2.24 | 0.73 |
| GloVe corpus | 2.66 | 0.62 |

  The text reports only the corpora, "C ∈ [2.21, 2.66] and
  Rf ∈ [0.62, 0.73]", calls the agreement "surprisingly high … on all three
  statistics" (there are two), and does not remark that the free-association
  data's Rf = 0.48 is below its own 0.5 criterion, or that every corpus's C
  exceeds the asymptotic bound of 2 (p. 276). Including "the" raises
  Wikipedia's C from 2.21 to 3.46.

### Lemma 1 and Corollary 6 (pp. 277, 282–283)

- **Lemma 1:** there are aᵢ, bⱼ with
  −log Cᵢⱼ(tn) − aᵢ − bⱼ → ‖xi − xj‖² simultaneously over all i, j as
  m, n → ∞ (p. 277).
- **Corollary 6 (the precise form):** for any δ, γ, n₀ there exist t̂,
  n > n₀ and terms bⱼⁿ such that, with probability at least 1 − γ,
  |−t̂ log P(X at time t̂·gn⁻² = xj | X₀ = xi) − t̂bⱼⁿ − ρσ(xi, xj)²| ≤ δ
  uniformly over pairs, where ρσ is the geodesic under the local scale
  σ (pp. 282–283). It rests on convergence of the walk to an Itô diffusion
  (Theorem 3, from earlier work), convergence of marginal densities
  (Lemma 4) and Varadhan's small-time formula (Theorem 5). So the limit is
  approximate in t̂ and probabilistic in n, and the recovered distance is
  geodesic, scaled by t̂, not the Euclidean ‖xi − xj‖² of Lemma 1's
  statement unless the space is flat.
- **My check.** On 1,500 uniform points in the unit square with an
  unweighted ε-ball graph (ε = 0.08), I computed exact t-step transition
  matrices and fitted −log Pᵗᵢⱼ = c·‖xi − xj‖² + aᵢ + bⱼ over pairs closer
  than 0.3. At t = 1 the fit explains nothing (R² = 0.00): one-step
  co-occurrence carries no metric beyond the ball. At t = 4 and 8 it
  explains 94% of the variance, with slope roughly ∝ 1/t (98, then 41).
  At t = 40, when the walk spans the square, it falls to 75%. That matches
  the corollary: the relation emerges over many steps and holds while the
  walk's spread is small against the domain.

### Embedding algorithms as metric recovery (§5.1, pp. 278–279; Appendix B)

- **GloVe** is "a weighted multidimensional scaling (MDS) objective", and
  under Lemma 1 the true embedding x̂ᵢ = ĉᵢ = xᵢ/σ with zero biases is a
  global minimum (p. 278).
- **word2vec** (softmax skip-gram) is the stochastic neighbour embedding
  objective weighted by ∑ₖCᵢₖ, with the global optimum at
  x̂ᵢ = ĉᵢ = xᵢ(√2/σ) (p. 279; Theorem 8). For negative sampling the true
  embedding is a global minimum under a further norm assumption.
- **PMI-SVD** (Levy and Goldberg) recovers the embedding under the norm
  assumption, since Mᵢⱼ → ⟨xi, xj⟩; "Centering the matrix Mij before
  obtaining the SVD would relax the norm assumption, resulting exactly in
  classical MDS (Sibson, 1979)" (p. 279). Lemma 7 states recovery "under
  the same equivalence class", that is up to an orthogonal map A
  (p. 283).
- **What "consistent" means in Theorem 8.** The proof shows that the
  objective value at the true embedding comes within nδ₀ of a lower bound
  on the global minimum, with high probability (p. 284). It does not show
  that minimizers are close to the true embedding. Only Lemma 7 bounds the
  embedding itself.

### Metric regression (§5.2, pp. 279–280)

Cᵢⱼ ~ NegBin with E[Cᵢⱼ] = exp(−½‖xi − xj‖² + aᵢ + bⱼ) and
Var = E²/θ + E, fitted by SGD. Its second-order Taylor expansion resembles
GloVe, with the weight Cᵢⱼθ/(2(Cᵢⱼ + θ)) playing the part of GloVe's f
(p. 280). The paper writes GloVe's weight as min(Cᵢⱼ, 100)^{3/4} on
p. 278 and as max(Cᵢⱼ, xmax)^{3/4}, said to converge to xmax, on p. 280;
the second is a misprint (min, and the limit is xmax^{3/4}).

### Experiments (§6, pp. 280–281; Appendix C)

- Corpora of 2.4B, 6.4B and 5.8B tokens; 100K-word vocabularies;
  300 dimensions, window 5, unweighted windows for all methods (p. 280;
  Appendix C). Window 5 is far from the growing window the theory needs.
- **Table 3** (GloVe corpus), L2 / cosine accuracy: on Google analogies
  overall, regression 72.6 / 73.7, GloVe 69.6 / 73.7, SVD 51.2 / 54.3,
  word2vec 71.1 / 73.3; on SAT, word2vec is best (42.0 / 42.0), regression
  next (39.2 / 37.8); on classification regression leads with L2 (87.6);
  on sequence completion regression leads narrowly (58.3 / 59.0). The
  differences among the three good methods are a few points.
- **Table 4:** Isomap on a free-association graph (4,845 words) solves
  semantic Google analogies at 83.3% against 70.7% for the corpus
  embedding, and syntactic ones at 8.2% against 76.9% (p. 281). The
  corpus figures differ from Table 3's regression results (75.5 semantic)
  and the paper does not say on which subset they were computed.
- **MNIST** (4,000 images, 20-NN graph, 10 walks of 200 steps per vertex):
  5-NN label purity t-SNE 91.4, regression 75.3, SNE 72.8, word2vec 68.7,
  SVD 68.0, GloVe 64.3, Isomap 49, PCA 48 (Fig. 3; p. 281). The text says
  "reducing from D = 256 to two dimensions"; MNIST images have 784 pixels,
  so a downsampled version was presumably used, and it is not described.
- **New datasets:** 215 classification and 220 series-completion
  questions, built from WordNet relations and pruned by PMI (p. 280). PMI
  was used to build them, which may favour co-occurrence methods; the
  authors note only that they were built before training.

## Concepts

- **Semantic space** — "vector spaces over concepts where Euclidean
  distances between points are assumed to indicate semantic similarities"
  (p. 273).
- **Metric recovery** — estimating the latent distances (and coordinates up
  to isometry) rather than optimizing an embedding for a task (p. 274).
- **Geometric sampling (GS) model** — points drawn independently from a
  continuous density in a Euclidean space (Tversky and Hutchinson 1986,
  p. 275).
- **Centrality C** and **reciprocity Rf** — (1/n)∑ᵢ(∑ⱼNᵢⱼ)² and
  (1/n)∑ᵢ∑ⱼNᵢⱼNⱼᵢ, with Nᵢⱼ = 1 iff i is j's nearest neighbour
  (pp. 275–276).
- **Spatial graph** — Definition 2 (p. 282).
- **Ideal point** — the point a reasoning task asks for: B − A + C for an
  analogy, the centroid for a category, wₙ + (1/n)(wₙ − w₁) for a
  series (Appendix C.2, p. 284).

- **Levy and Goldberg (2014a, b)**: linear regularities beyond word2vec,
  and skip-gram with negative sampling as shifted-PMI factorization; the
  SVD case here extends the latter.
- **Arora et al. (2015)**: random walks and embeddings under isotropy
  assumptions; contrasted on p. 274 as not taking metric recovery as the
  object.
- **Rumelhart and Abrahamson (1973)** and **Sternberg and Gardner (1983)**:
  the psychometric semantic space and the ideal-point account of analogy,
  series and classification (Fig. 1, Table 1). Gentner ([LIT-1810](../literature.d/LIT-1810.md),
  p. 166) cites the same Rumelhart and Abrahamson model as unable to
  express higher-order relations.
- **Tenenbaum et al. (2000)** (Isomap) and **Hinton and Roweis (2002)**
  (SNE): the manifold-learning side.
- **Hashimoto, Sun and Jaakkola (2015a, b)**: the random-walk-to-distance
  results Appendix A relies on. The broader preprint arXiv 1509.05808 is
  not this work ([LIT-1801](../literature.d/LIT-1801.md)).
- **Alvarez-Melis and Jaakkola (2018)**, [LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md), by two of the same
  authors, aligns embedding spaces by Gromov–Wasserstein; this paper does
  not discuss alignment.

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md): the exchange's "precursor" reading is right, and this
  paper supplies its reason.** If embeddings recover a metric, two
  embeddings of one semantic space agree only up to an isometry (Lemma 7's
  orthogonal map) and per-word normalizers. An alignment that uses only
  within-space distances, as GW does, asks for exactly what such
  embeddings can share. The paper itself never compares two spaces, and in
  its model the isometry would be found by Procrustes as well; GW's
  advantage needs spaces whose metrics differ, which is outside this
  paper.
- **[CLAIM-046](../claims.d/CLAIM-046.md): support, and a fit with A6's caution.** What the
  co-occurrence observables identify here is the metric; the coordinates
  are fixed only up to isometry. That is [CLAIM-046](../claims.d/CLAIM-046.md)'s point that different
  internal states can produce identical observations, in a worked case.
  A6's caution is borne out by the paper's own numbers. The link from
  co-occurrence to similarity judgment is a correlation of 0.75 after a
  tuned normalization (Fig. 2), and a metric built from people's responses
  (free association, Table 4) behaves differently from the corpus metric:
  better on semantic analogies, near zero on syntactic ones. That is the
  kind of response-derived distance [CLAIM-046](../claims.d/CLAIM-046.md) points to, though free
  association is a judgment of similarity, not a receiver's response to a
  communicative act.
- **[CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md): this paper reaches identification, under a model the
  authors disown as a model of language.** Reconstruction is shown on
  benchmarks; identification up to isometry is proved for the random-walk
  model (Lemma 7; Corollary 6); nothing addresses whether the semantic
  space is the right kind. Theorem 8 is weaker than identification: it
  bounds the objective, not the embedding.
- **[CLAIM-113](../claims.d/CLAIM-113.md) and [THEORY-002](../theory.d/THEORY-002.md): an instance of identification up to a
  symmetry group.** What is recovered is the class of embeddings under the
  isometries of the latent space, the invariants of that group being the
  distances.
- **[THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md): a complement, through the logarithm.** [THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md) finds
  that a spectral embedding of co-occurrences that depend only on
  separation on a continuum yields Fourier modes, circles and ripples. This
  paper works with the logarithm of the co-occurrences, which under its
  model is quadratic in distance, and with classical MDS on that, which
  recovers the coordinates. The two results agree once it is seen which
  matrix is factorized; the record should not read them as rival
  accounts of the same embedding.
- **[THEORY-1154](../theory.d/THEORY-1154.md) (Gentner): the parallelogram rule is single-relation
  analogy.** The ideal point B − A + C treats a relation as one
  displacement vector; nothing in this paper represents a relation among
  relations, so its analogy results test Gentner's first two rules at most.
- **Should produce:** [THEORY-1128](../theory.d/THEORY-1128.md) (log co-occurrence under a metric
  random walk recovers the metric, and only the metric; Active) and
  [THEORY-1124](../theory.d/THEORY-1124.md) (corpus co-occurrences fit a Euclidean semantic space;
  Proposed).
- **Machine-learning practice.** The metric-regression method and its
  benchmark comparison (§§5.2, 6) are method evaluation for embeddings;
  the LIT carries the `anthology-candidate` flag for that part.

## Limitations

- **The generative model is a tool, not language** (p. 277), and the
  experiments use a window of 5 (Appendix C), not a window growing with
  the vocabulary.
- **The Euclidean-space evidence tests necessary conditions.** C and Rf
  are nearest-neighbour statistics that tree-like data violate; passing
  them does not rule out other non-Euclidean structure, and the
  free-association data fail Rf.
- **Theorem 8 bounds objective values only.** The word2vec "consistency"
  is weaker than recovery.
- **Directed graphs rest on an open conjecture** (n. 10).
- **Single runs, no error bars** in Tables 3–4 and Fig. 3, with fixed
  hyperparameters; GloVe's step sizes had to be reduced to avoid NaNs
  (Appendix C.1).
- **Unexplained details:** the MNIST dimension (256), the subset for
  Table 4, and the two norm assumptions. The series ideal point uses
  1/n where the average step over n words is 1/(n − 1) (p. 284).
- **Published date.** The paper's first page says "Published 7/2016";
  Crossref gives December 2016 (print). See [LIT-1801](../literature.d/LIT-1801.md).

## Open questions

- Does the log-quadratic relation hold for real corpora at the windows
  used in practice, or only for the theory's growing windows? A measure of
  how −log Cᵢⱼ scales with window size on a real corpus would say.
- Is the semantic space Euclidean, hyperbolic, or neither? A comparison
  of fit across geometries, with hierarchical vocabularies included, would
  test the hypothesis rather than its necessary conditions.
- Does Theorem 8 extend to recovery of the embedding itself, for example
  by a strong-convexity argument on the SNE objective near the truth?
