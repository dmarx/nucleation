# LIT-117: Agency, Shmagency: Why Normativity Won't Come from What Is Constitutive of Action

David Enoch (2006), *The Philosophical Review 115(2): 169–198* — DOI-10.1215/00318108-2005-014

## Key takeaways

- Constitutivism cannot ground normativity. Even granting that action has constitutive aims, one can ask what reason there is to be an agent rather than a "shmagent" (a near-agent lacking those aims), and the constitutive facts cannot answer that question without presupposing normative reasons.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Many philosophers interested in normativity hope that metaethical problems can be solved from within the philosophy of action, by deriving normative standards from what is constitutive of agency or action (constitutivism, associated with Korsgaard and Velleman). Enoch argues that this hope is unfounded. Even if action has constitutive aims or principles, facts about them explain normative truths only together with a reason to be an agent. A "shmagent", a being as similar to an agent as possible but lacking the constitutive commitments, could ask why it should be an agent at all. Constitutivism therefore cannot deliver normativity from non-normative materials.
