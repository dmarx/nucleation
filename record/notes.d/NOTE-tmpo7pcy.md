# NOTE-tmpo7pcy: Carruthers & Gennaro — Higher-Order Theories of Consciousness (SEP)

## Contribution

An encyclopedia entry on the family of theories that answer one question:
what makes the difference between a conscious and an unconscious mental
state of the same kind? The answer they share is a higher-order
representation of the state. The entry sets out four versions and the
objections each faces. It then turns to the claim that prefrontal cortex
realizes the higher-order states. It argues for little. Its one general
moral is that "different versions of a higher-order theory of phenomenal
consciousness need to be kept distinct from one another" (§7). Its
verdicts are that actualist HOT theory has not been refuted (§4) and that
the issue of sufficiency is where "the battle for phenomenal consciousness
may ultimately be won or lost" (§7.4).

## Key insight

Conscious states are states we are aware of. Most kinds of mental state,
perception and emotion included, also occur unconsciously, as in
blindsight, absent-minded driving and the agnosic patient D.F. So
whatever makes a state phenomenally conscious is something added to it,
and higher-order theories say it is the subject's non-inferential
awareness of the state. The entry's Transitivity Principle: "A conscious
mental state is a state whose subject is, in some way, aware of being in
it" (§2).

## Assumptions

- **Kinds of consciousness (§1).** Creature consciousness against
  state consciousness; intransitive (awake) against transitive (conscious
  of). The entry advises dropping talk of transitive creature
  consciousness. Phenomenal consciousness ("feel", after Nagel 1974) is
  set against functionally definable access consciousness (Block 1995).
  "Most theorists" hold that some states are access-conscious and not
  phenomenally conscious. The converse is disputed.
- **A cognitive level of explanation (§2).** Phenomenal consciousness
  "consists in a certain kind of intentional or representational content
  ... figuring in a certain distinctive position in the causal
  architecture of the mind". Mentality does not presuppose phenomenal
  consciousness, against Searle. Reduction is first to mentalistic terms,
  with a "second-step" reduction to neurophysiology often favoured.
- **Unconscious perception exists (§2).** Higher-order theorists "are
  united" on it. The evidence is the absent-minded driver (Armstrong);
  blindsight, with grasping in the blind field at "something like 80% or
  more of normal accuracy"; Milner and Goodale's two visual systems; and
  D.F., at chance on the orientation of a slot she can post a letter
  through almost normally.
- **Non-inferential awareness (§2).** A desire learned of from a
  psychiatrist is a target of higher-order representation without being
  phenomenally conscious, so the awareness must normally be
  non-inferential.

## Key results

- **The first-order alternative (§2).** For Dretske and Tye, phenomenal
  consciousness is analog, fine-grained content available to the
  first-order processes that guide thought and action. This explains
  ineffability: such contents "slip through the mesh of any conceptual
  net". First-order theorists have three answers to the unconscious cases.
  They can deny the data (Dretske), draw the line by availability to
  belief rather than to movement (Kirk), or "bite the bullet" and hold
  blindsight percepts phenomenally conscious though not access-conscious
  (Block, Tye, Nelkin).
- **The general thesis (§2).** "A phenomenally conscious mental state is a
  mental state that is (or is disposed to be) the object of a higher-order
  representation of a certain sort". The versions divide on whether the
  higher-order state is perception-like or thought-like, and on whether it
  must be actual or only disposed.
- **Higher-order perception, or inner sense (§3).** Armstrong, Lycan; after
  Locke. Advantage: it gives purely recognitional concepts of experience,
  which explain the explanatory gap and the conceivability of zombies by
  their "conceptual isolation". The entry gives three objections:
  - There is no phenomenology of inner sense. The entry calls this
    question-begging, since inner-sense theory denies that first-order
    states have a phenomenology of their own.
  - Inner sense could misfire, giving "It's red" and "It seems orange"
    together, which never appears to happen.
  - Carruthers's complexity objection. The scanner would be "almost as
    sophisticated and complex as the visual system itself", with no
    evolutionary story.
  "Lycan no longer holds HOP theory (Sauret & Lycan 2014)": attention to
  first-order states now seems to him sufficient.
- **Actualist higher-order thought (§4).** Rosenthal: a state is conscious
  when it actually causes, non-inferentially, a thought (generally itself
  unconscious) that one is in it. Several points follow.
  - **Inference.** Rosenthal rules out only conscious inference, so
    "unconscious self-interpretation is acceptable as a source of the
    conscious status of the states thereby attributed". Noticing the
    spring in one's step and inferring, unconsciously, that one is
    cheerful makes the cheerfulness conscious.
  - **Targetless thoughts.** Rosenthal takes a suitably caused thought to
    suffice even with no target state. The entry calls both features
    optional for the theory.
  - **Regress.** It is avoided because the thought is unconscious unless a
    third-order thought targets it in introspection (Figure 1).
  - **Dretske's spot.** Seeing the extra spot need not mean seeing that it
    is the difference.
  - **Richness.** A rich scene would need many thoughts. The best reply is
    Dennett's fragmentism, the "mere illusion of riches", and the entry
    doubts it. Still, "it cannot yet be concluded that actualist forms of
    HOT theory have been refuted", given change and inattentional
    blindness.
  - **"Light up".** How does thinking about a percept give it feel?
    Rosenthal points to wine-tasting and music courses, where new concepts
    change experience. The reply: oaky and oboe are first-order concepts,
    so the effect "appears to be a first-order phenomenon, not a
    higher-order one".
- **Dispositionalist higher-order thought, or dual content (§5).**
  Carruthers: a state is phenomenally conscious when it is available to
  cause higher-order thoughts about itself, perhaps held in a short-term
  store and made available by global broadcast.
  - **Consumer semantics.** Content depends on the systems that consume a
    state, so availability to a "theory of mind" consumer makes each
    percept "at one and the same time" an analog representation of green
    and of seeming green.
  - **Prosthetic vision.** The independent illustration is Bach-y-Rita's
    tactile-visual substitution. With the camera under their control,
    subjects' tactile patterns came to represent objects in space. "Here
    everything on the input side remains the same as it was when subjects
    first began to wear the device; but the planning and
    action-controlling systems have learned to interpret those states
    differently."
  - **Misrepresentation.** It cannot happen, since the higher-order content
    is "parasitic" on the first-order one.
  - **Costs.** It requires rejecting pure input semantics. Rosenthal
    objects that dispositions do not make us aware of anything. The
    objection that it makes feel non-categorical counts "equally against
    any representationalist theory".
  - "Carruthers no longer holds dispositional HOT theory or, for that
    matter, any form of higher-order theory", and defends first-order
    representationalism (Carruthers 2017).
- **Self-representational and hybrid theories (§6).** These deny that the
  higher-order state is a distinct state: the relation is "constitutive,
  or internal".
  - Identity versions: Carruthers's dual content, and Van Gulick's HOGS.
  - Part–whole versions: Gennaro's wide intrinsicality view, Kriegel, and
    Picciuto's quotational QHOT.
  - Kriegel posits a "ubiquitous conscious (but inattentive or
    peripheral) self-awareness" with every outward conscious state, which
    Gennaro doubts.
  - Kriegel requires that the integration be one "resulting from a
    psychologically real process (as opposed to a theorist's
    definition)".
  - A reconstruction on the model of the duck–rabbit: conceptual
    templates matched against analog input yield one state that represents
    the lines, a rabbit and "that one is experiencing something
    rabbit-like", which so becomes "self-presenting".
- **Targetless representations (§7.1).** Inner sense and actualist HOT
  face it; dispositional and self-representational views do not.
  - **Lycan and Rosenthal accept the consequence.** Lycan cites morphine.
    Rosenthal cites dental patients with dead nerves who feel pain until
    told.
  - **A critic's alternatives.** A vivid imagining of pain, or "a top-down
    expectation of pain causes a first-order experience of pain",
    placebo's opposite.
  - **Gennaro.** The first-order state must exist.
  - **Brown's HOROR** makes the theory non-relational. Rosenthal (2022)
    objects that "[i]t's the first-order state that's qualitatively
    conscious".
  - **Block (2011).** A thought that one is in pain should not be expected
    to have pain's "negative valence and high-arousal properties", so it
    is not sufficient for felt pain.
- **The rock (§7.2).** Thinking about a rock does not make it conscious.
  Replies: a rock is not a mental state (Lycan), or cannot be part of a
  complex higher-order state (Van Gulick, Gennaro).
- **Animals and infants (§7.3).** Higher-order thought seems to need
  mental-state concepts that most animals may lack. Gennaro's replies are
  simpler indexical higher-order thoughts, and comparative evidence of
  metacognition and mindreading: rhesus monkeys, scrub jays re-caching,
  episodic memory. Van Gulick's implicit higher-order representation
  over-generates: "It is implicit in the behavior of any creature that
  drinks, for example, that it is thirsty". The theory "does not say that
  having conscious states requires having introspective states".
- **Sufficiency (§7.4).** Creatures with the right higher-order
  representations but no phenomenal consciousness seem conceivable. The
  reply, shared with first-order theorists, is that reductive explanation
  needs only "good reason to think that the explained properties are
  constituted by the explaining ones, in such a way that nothing else
  needed to be added to the world". "But this is hotly contested
  territory." Somatoparaphrenia and thought insertion, where states are
  felt as not "mine", run against the HOT claim that the thought "I am in mental state M"
  accompanies "each and every conscious state".
- **Prefrontal cortex (§8).**
  - **For.** Prefrontal and parietal activity is higher in conscious than
    unconscious conditions, "often even when performance capacity is
    controlled for". In Lau and Passingham (2006), subjective reports
    were impaired with discrimination intact and tracked dorsolateral
    prefrontal cortex. In Rounis et al. (2010), TMS to prefrontal cortex
    affected "meta-visual awareness" but not first-order performance.
    The prediction: disrupting prefrontal cortex "should affect or
    eliminate sensory experiences without affecting performance".
  - **Against (localists).** Little prefrontal activity while absorbed in
    a film (Goldberg et al. 2006). Experience is not eliminated by
    extensive prefrontal damage or lobotomy. Prefrontal activity may come
    from report demands.
  - **A middle position.** Gennaro locates higher-order states outside
    prefrontal cortex: anterior cingulate, temporo-parietal junction,
    parietal cortex.
  - **The verdict.** The debate "continues", now with adversarial
    collaborations (Ball 2019). The entry adds that if prefrontal activity
    is required, "it is unlikely that most animals or even infants are
    conscious".

## Concepts

- **Transitivity Principle (TP)**: a conscious state is one whose subject is
  "in some way, aware of being in it" (§2).
- **Higher-order perception (HOP), or inner sense**: a faculty that scans
  first-order perceptions and produces analog higher-order
  representations of them (§3).
- **Actualist HOT**: consciousness by actually causing a non-inferential
  higher-order thought (§4).
- **Dispositionalist HOT, or dual content**: consciousness by availability
  to higher-order thought, which under consumer semantics gives each
  percept a second, "seeming" content (§5).
- **Self-representational theory**: one state represents the world and
  itself, by identity or as part and whole (§6).
- **Targetless (empty) HOT**: a higher-order representation with no
  first-order target (§7.1).
- **HOROR**: higher-order representation of a representation, whether or
  not the target exists (Brown 2015; §7.1).
- **Localist and globalist**: whether some conscious states need only local
  sensory activity, or need widespread activation (§8).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md): the claim's premise, and the most developed answer to
  it.** The entry's motivation (§2) is the claim's premise in the same
  cases: blindsight, automatic motor control (D.F.'s sensorimotor
  system) and the absent-minded driver.
  - **Both defeater branches are occupied.** The higher-order thesis is a
    stated criterion of the kind the claim's first branch asks for. The
    first-order theorists who "bite the bullet" (Block, Tye, Nelkin) hold
    the second branch, that blindsight percepts are experiential.
  - **Neither branch is won.** The higher-order criterion faces the same
    two failures the claim predicts for any candidate:
    - **Necessity.** Animals and infants (§7.3), and the localist lesion
      evidence (§8).
    - **Sufficiency.** The conceivable creature with the right
      higher-order states and no experience (§7.4), and the rock (§7.2).
  - **The evidence is report-tied.** The empirical case for it (§8)
    contrasts "conscious versus unconscious conditions" by report, and
    localists reply that the prefrontal activity is report. So it does not
    yet meet the claim's requirement that the criterion be "independent of
    report".
  - **Over-generation.** Van Gulick's implicit version (§7.3) does what the
    claim's defeater forbids: it lets the verdict spread to "every
    creature (no matter how simple)".
  - **The claim stands, and this sharpens it.** The leading named
    candidate has been abandoned by two of its main sources (Carruthers,
    Lycan) and is maintained by a third.
- **[CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md): three near-relatives, each with a difference.**
  - **Interpretation that confers consciousness.** On Rosenthal's actualist
    theory, "unconscious self-interpretation is acceptable as a source of
    the conscious status of the states thereby attributed" (§4). That is
    the literature's closest case of interpretation making a state
    conscious. But the interpretation is a self-ascribing thought, a
    higher-order judgement, which is the sense the claim puts aside. The
    claim treats judgement as "a higher-order operation within an
    interpretive system" (A1 of the exchange, not this entry). So the claim cannot adopt it without reversing its part 2.
  - **Content constituted by how a state is consumed.** Carruthers's dual
    content (§5) is constitutive, not causal: availability to a
    theory-of-mind consumer makes the percept "at one and the same time" a
    representation of seeming. His prosthetic-vision illustration holds the
    input fixed while consumer systems "have learned to interpret those
    states differently", and the content changes. That is the claim's
    picture for content: significance fixed by the organization that uses
    a signal. Two differences matter. The experiential dimension is
    conferred by a mentalizing system, which brings in the animals
    objection. And Carruthers has abandoned the view.
  - **Realized, not described.** Kriegel's requirement that the integration
    result "from a psychologically real process (as opposed to a theorist's
    definition)" (§6) is the claim's answer to Negro: realized, not merely
    described, organization.
  - **The standard of explanation.** The entry's reply on sufficiency
    (§7.4) is the claim's standard for an identity, that "nothing else
    needed to be added to the world". The entry says that this ground is
    where the issue "may ultimately be won or lost". It does not settle it.
- **[QUESTION-tmpc4j7p](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.md): the question's second contrast is contested.** The
  question separates constituting a perspective from representing one.
  Higher-order theories make the experiential difference consist in
  representing the state: the higher-order content gives "a dimension of
  seeming or subjectivity" (§3, §5, §6). On these views a representation
  of one's own state (nearer [TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md)'s sense 2) is what makes a state
  experiential. A self-representational theory goes further: the state is
  "self-presenting". So the question's separation is a substantive position
  against this family, and an answer to the question owes a reply to it.
  The question's overgeneration test applies to the family as it applies
  to the claim (§7.3).
- **[TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md).**
  - **The bridge from sense 2 to sense 3.** Higher-order theory is a
    standing attempt to make the bridge the TERM says must not be built
    into a definition: represented self-awareness (sense 2) as what makes
    phenomenal consciousness (sense 3).
  - **Berger's view.** Berger (2014) makes consciousness a property of
    persons, "how my mental states appear to me", not of states (§7.1).
    That is nearer a perspective-level reading.
- **[TERM-tmpgqmop](../terms.d/TERM-tmpgqmop.md).** The entry uses "interpret" in two senses, neither
  the TERM's.
  - **Rosenthal's "self-interpretation"** is inference from one's own
    behaviour to one's own state.
  - **Carruthers's consumer systems** "learned to interpret those states
    differently". That is a change in how a state is used, and it is the
    nearer one.
- **[CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md).** The pathologies of §7.4 bear on it. The HOT content "I am in
  mental state M" builds an owner into every conscious state. In
  somatoparaphrenia and thought insertion the ownership attribution fails
  while the experience stays. That fits [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)'s location of the owner
  in a representation that can fail without the binding failing. It is a
  problem for HOT theory, and the entry cites Gennaro's discussion of it,
  which was not read.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md), [THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md), [THEORY-098](../theory.d/THEORY-098.md).** §8 restates the prefrontal
  dispute that [THEORY-098](../theory.d/THEORY-098.md) takes a side on. It lists the same localist
  evidence (lesions, film-watching, report-driven activity) and the
  higher-order evidence the THEORY does not discuss (Lau and Passingham;
  Rounis et al.). It also reports the report confound [THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md) states:
  "it is crucial to show that the activity detected in the PFC during
  imaging studies pertains primarily to attention and report, not
  consciousness per se". It adds nothing that settles either, and it does
  not report COGITATE.
- **[THEORY-577](../theory.d/THEORY-577.md) and [THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md).** Inner-sense theory claims to explain the
  explanatory gap and zombie conceivability through purely recognitional
  concepts (§3). That is an attempt at the meta-problem [THEORY-577](../theory.d/THEORY-577.md)
  poses. Rosenthal's "Consciousness is about mental appearance" (§7.1),
  with targetless thoughts sufficing, is close to [THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md)'s
  quasi-phenomenal reading but keeps phenomenal consciousness real.
- **[THEORY-766](../theory.d/THEORY-766.md) (expectation).** The dental case (§7.1) has the shape of
  that THEORY's open question. A reported experience may be produced by
  the stimulation, by a thought about it, or by "a top-down expectation of
  pain" that causes a first-order experience. The entry does not decide
  among these.
- **[THEORY-tmptw1fe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmptw1fe.md)**, filed from the companion entry, says that a
  sensory quality needs awareness to be experienced. This entry is the
  account of that awareness it defers to. The entry also grants, for the
  driver, that one "may be possible to get oneself to believe" the
  perceptions are phenomenally conscious, so the case that THEORY rests on
  is not settled here either.
- **No THEORY filed.** The entry argues for no thesis of its own beyond
  the moral that versions must be kept apart. A THEORY on higher-order
  thought should come from a primary source (Rosenthal 2005; Lau and
  Rosenthal 2011; Brown, Lau and LeDoux 2019), none of which the record
  holds.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- A survey maintained since 2020 by Gennaro, whose own views (wide
  intrinsicality, animal metacognition, the "centrist" neural location)
  get the last word in several sections.
- Every empirical result is reported, with no effect sizes, and none was
  read here.
- §8 reports the prefrontal debate up to about 2023 and does not report
  the published results of the adversarial collaborations it mentions.
- Emotion gets one paragraph (LeDoux and Brown's HOROR for fear, §8), and
  affect enters only through Block's point about pain's valence (§7.1).

## Open questions

- Is there a criterion for "higher-order" that does not over-generate (Van
  Gulick's thirst) or under-generate (animals without mental-state
  concepts)? §7.3 leaves it open.
- What would separate Rosenthal's targetless dental pain from a top-down
  expectation that produces a first-order pain? The entry names both and
  no test.
- Does disrupting prefrontal cortex remove experience, or only metacognitive
  report of it? This is the theory's own prediction (§8). Rounis et al.
  report the second, and whether the first follows is open.
