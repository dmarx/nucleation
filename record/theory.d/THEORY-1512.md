# THEORY-1512: Alía and Esteban hold, from the randomized weaning trials of the 1990s, that ventilation can be withdrawn abruptly in about three quarters of patients whose cause of respiratory failure has improved, readiness being tested by a trial of unassisted breathing on T-tube or low pressure support, for 30 or 120 minutes alike, and immediate extubation after a passed trial shortening ventilation compared with gradual withdrawal; and that the patients who fail need gradual withdrawal while the causes are corrected, where SIMV was the least efficient method in both trials and once-daily T-tube trials or reductions of pressure support did better

## Source

Alía & Esteban (2000), [LIT-2215](../literature.d/LIT-2215.md); read in [NOTE-1701](../notes.d/NOTE-1701.md). It stands in
for Boles et al. (2007), [LIT-2213](../literature.d/LIT-2213.md), on withdrawal schedules, not on the
consensus' classification.

## What was actually shown

The review reports randomized trials; each could have gone the other way.

- **First trial.** 76% passed one 2-h T-piece trial in Brochard's trial
  (n = 456) and 76% were extubated on the first day in Esteban's (n = 546).
- **Trial form.** T-tube against pressure support of 7 cmH2O, 63% and 70%
  still extubated at 48 h (P = 0.14); 30 and 120 min "equivalent", with
  "reintubation rates of approximately 13% at 48 h".
- **Abrupt against gradual.** "immediate extubation after successful
  trials of spontaneous breathing expedites weaning and reduces the duration
  of mechanical ventilation as compared with a more gradual discontinuation
  of ventilatory support".
- **After failure.** Pressure support shortest in Brochard's ("5.7 ± 3.7
  days versus 9.3 ± 8.2 days" against T-piece and SIMV combined); T-tube
  trials best in Esteban's; in both, "SIMV is the least efficient technique
  of weaning".

## What this does not say

- **That SIMV is the most effective method.** The abstract says so; the
  body and the trials say the reverse, and the unread 2006 correction
  probably addresses it.
- **Whether pressure support or T-tube trials are better after failure.**
  The two trials disagree.
- **That rest by full support is harmless.** The review holds rest to be
  the only treatment of fatigue; later work on disuse atrophy (Jaber et
  al. 2011, [LIT-2211](../literature.d/LIT-2211.md)) bears against prolonged fully controlled rest.
- For the record it qualifies [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) on schedule (abrupt removal once
  the party can do the work) and bears against [CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md) where the capacity
  is fatigued rather than unused; both readings are the filer's.

## Note of 2026-11-06: the abstract and the body on SIMV

The abstract says that two randomized studies found SIMV the most effective
method of weaning in difficult-to-wean patients. The body says twice, and
the conclusion once, that it is the least efficient, and the trial figures
it gives (Brochard 1994: pressure support 5.7 days against 9.3 for T-piece
and SIMV combined; Esteban 1995: median 5 days on SIMV, 4 on pressure
support, 3 on T-piece) agree with the body. The title follows the body. The
2006 correction (doi:10.1186/cc4996) was not read; the discrepancy is
carried to THEORY-tmpvwl4g, which extends this entry.
