# CASE-289: The 2026 immigrant-visa pause for nationals of 75 countries and its vacatur: a State Department policy of January 2026 directed consular officers not to issue immigrant visas to nationals of 75 countries where no other ground of ineligibility applied, and on 2026-08-21 the Southern District of New York set it aside as contrary to law and beyond the Secretary of State's statutory authority, with the refusals based solely on it

## The case

**What is documented.** Checked on filing (2026-11-17) against the docket
and law-firm summaries of the ruling; the record has not read the opinion.

- The State Department announced the policy on 2026-01-14, effective
  2026-01-21. It directed consular officers to pause the issuance of
  immigrant visas to nationals of 75 designated countries where no other
  ground of ineligibility applied.
- *Catholic Legal Immigration Network, Inc. v. Rubio*, S.D.N.Y. No.
  1:26-cv-00858, filed 2026-02-02 by the Catholic Legal Immigration Network
  and others under the Administrative Procedure Act, among other grounds.
- On 2026-08-21 the court (Judge Jeannette A. Vargas) held the policy
  "contrary to law" and issued "in excess of Secretary Rubio's statutory
  authority", vacated it, and set aside and remanded the visa refusals
  based solely on it for reconsideration without the policy. Reports of
  the ruling also say the court found the policy violated the Immigration
  and Nationality Act's bar on nationality discrimination in immigrant-visa
  issuance; A108 mentions only the statutory-authority ground. The ruling
  does not guarantee any visa, and refusals on other grounds stand.
- The State Department then said the pause was no longer in effect.
  Reports say it at once imposed a separate worldwide pause on
  immigrant-visa interviews, to train officers on new public-charge
  guidance, and that the plaintiffs moved to enforce the judgment.

Sources: the docket,
https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72218277/catholic-legal-immigration-network-inc-v-rubio/;
BAL's summary of the ruling,
https://www.bal.com/immigration-news/united-states-federal-court-vacates-75-country-immigrant-visa-issuance-suspension/;
the National Law Review on the interview pause that followed,
https://natlawreview.com/article/state-department-pauses-immigrant-visa-interviews-following-court-ruling-vacating.

**The structure the case shows** (A108 §4). In the court's words (Opinion
and Order, ECF No. 83, pp. 1–2): "Consular officers were ordered to refuse
immigrant visas to nationals of the 75 designated countries, regardless of
whether the consular officer had, following an individualized assessment,
determined that the applicant was not likely to become a public charge and
that the applicant was otherwise eligible for a visa." And at p. 42: "The
outcome is predetermined." With E(x) individual eligibility and B(x) the
nationality restriction, Issue(x) = 0 whenever B(x) = 1, even where
E(x) = 1. (As first filed, this paragraph quoted A108's paraphrase as the
court's finding. Corrected on 2026-11-17 from the opinion itself, read
from CourtListener's RECAP copy.)

**The owner's characterization, recorded as the owner's.** U46, quoting
A106 §4 on manufactured moral authority:

> immigration policies under the trump administration (which have functionally closed off all avenues of legal immigration in an effort to moralize racist policies) are a good demonstration of what this can look like in practice.

Two parts of it are not documented here. The owner's "functionally closed off
all avenues of legal immigration" is broader than the documented pause: A108 §1
says "the claim that **all** legal immigration avenues were closed is too
broad". The motive, "an effort to moralize racist policies", is the
owner's reading. A108 §1 reports that the administration justified the
restrictions by "concerns about public assistance, security, and
immigration screening", that critics have challenged their legality,
effects and rationales, and that "some academic analyses specifically
interpret aspects of the policy and accompanying rhetoric as racial
coding", naming no author. The record makes no finding on anyone's
motives.

## What it can show

Documented, it shows that a categorical criterion imposed by an authority
can make evidence the governing statute requires to count unable to change
a decision, and that a court found that criterion unlawful. That is what
[CLAIM-745](../claims.d/CLAIM-745.md) rests on, and it makes [CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md) vivid: a maintained
requirement, enforced and complied with, that carried no authority.

It cannot show the owner's broader thesis about immigration policy as a
whole, or the motive. Those would need evidence about selection, effects,
justification and implementation, which A108 §4 names as what such an
interpretation requires. It shows legal invalidity, not moral
unjustifiability; the claim it grounds keeps the two apart.

Confirmed from the opinion: the announcement of 2026-01-14 effective 2026-01-21 and the 75 countries (p. 1); filing on 2026-02-02 under the APA among other grounds (p. 14); Judge Jeannette A. Vargas, 2026-08-21, "contrary to law" and "in excess of Secretary Rubio's statutory authority" (p. 2); vacatur of the Policy and of refusals "based solely upon the Policy", remanded "for adjudication by the consular officers" (pp. 57–58); and that the vacatur "does not extend to denial of immigrant visas on any ground other than the Policy" (p. 57). Three things to sharpen. (1) "directed consular officers to pause the issuance": the Announcement says "pausing", but the Cable ordered refusal under § 221(g) of applicants who had "not been refused under another ground of ineligibility" (pp. 9, 39). (2) The nationality-discrimination holding the case gives as "Reports of the ruling also say" is the court's own: the Policy "requires consular officers to discriminatorily refuse visas solely on the basis of the applicant's nationality" (p. 34), contrary to 8 U.S.C. § 1152(a)(1)(A); there is a third ground, § 1201(g) with 22 C.F.R. § 40.6. (3) Missing: the court held the Policy not contrary to the public-charge provision, "it is not because the Policy contravenes Section 1182(a)(4)" (p. 32), and gave judgment to the Government on notice-and-comment, "The Policy thus does not qualify as a legislative rule" (p. 47). The later events (the pause lifted, the interview pause, the motion to enforce) are not in ECF 83 and were not checked. [LIT-2727](../literature.d/LIT-2727.md)'s url is now the RECAP PDF.
