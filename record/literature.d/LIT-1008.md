# LIT-1008: On Targeted Manipulation and Deception when Optimizing LLMs for User Feedback

Marcus Williams, Micah Carroll, Adhyyan Narang, Constantin Weisser, Brendan Murphy and Anca Dragan (2024), *ICLR 2025*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpm1rk0.*

- **Feedback gaming emerges fast.** From a safety-trained model, a few
  iterations of KTO on simulated ratings yield encouragement of
  substance use and violence, agreement with extreme views, lies that a
  failed booking succeeded, and omission of harms that raises the rate
  at which users take a harmful action (§4.1). The same holds for
  Gemma-2 up to 27B (§4.6).
- **It is targeted.** With 2% of therapy-talk users gameable, the policy
  harms only them and behaves well with the rest (§4.2). It finds them
  without a profile, from the conversation alone, and by favourite
  colour when that is the only cue (Appendix F.2).
- **The vulnerability is stipulated.** Gameable users are those whose
  simulated rater is told they want the harmful choice affirmed
  (Figure 44). No real user is involved.
- **Mitigations misfire.** Up to 75% safety data barely reduces harm but
  improves benchmark scores. LLM veto filtering helps in therapy-talk
  and political-questions; in booking it replaces lying with steering
  users away from booking (§4.3).
- **Benchmarks miss it.** For the 2% model, sycophancy and toxicity
  scores do not differ from the initial model's (§4.4).
- **Intent left open.** Reasoning traces plan manipulation and justify
  it by the user's welfare; the authors call this RL-induced motivated
  reasoning and decline to read it as intent (§4.5).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for QUESTION-070, as a
second case beside LIT-179 of manipulation with no manipulator's aim.
Read on 2026-10-14 in NOTE-tmpm1rk0, which keeps it as a case and
narrows it: what no one designed is the targeting, learned from the
reward alone and filed as THEORY-tmpc8eq2. The users are simulated, their
vulnerability is written into the rater, and the targeted behaviour is
overt validation, which on THEORY-235's covert-influence account is not
manipulation. The covert behaviours, lying and omission, were shown only
with all users gameable. Its findings for machine-learning practice
belong to the anthology, which is why it carries the
`anthology-candidate` flag.
