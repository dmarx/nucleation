## What did not enter, and why

- **Mallory, stochastic measuring devices ([LIT-140](../../record/literature.d/LIT-140.md), [NOTE-090](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-090.md)).** The
  headline is an analogy, and the author concedes the agentive analogy it
  opposes "can't be refuted" (p. 15). Its reading calls it "an alternative
  image, not a demonstration". Its sharper points, strong against weak
  model content and seed-dependence as a failure of instrument regularity,
  belong to the ML literature it cites, as the reading says. There was no
  claim that could come out wrong.
- **Angius et al., transformer success ([LIT-153](../../record/literature.d/LIT-153.md), [NOTE-106](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-106.md)).** A survey that
  assimilates published studies to Cummins, mechanistic and simulative
  explanation, with no new analysis and no counter-cases. Its reading says
  it should not be a source; its one firm caution (ICL "mechanism" papers
  often give mathematical equivalences) is better sourced from the primary
  papers.

So thirteen of fifteen readings became sources, which fits the owner's
hypothesis. Two caveats. Two of the thirteen readings said outright that
the work "supports no THEORY document directly" ([NOTE-152](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-152.md), [NOTE-103](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-103.md)), and
the SEP-style surveys ([LIT-114](../../record/literature.d/LIT-114.md), [LIT-160](../../record/literature.d/LIT-160.md)) needed the most narrowing to
yield a claim that could be wrong. For [LIT-113](../../record/literature.d/LIT-113.md) the account filed is the
survey's central conditional verdict, which its reading rates moderate.
And [LIT-139](../../record/literature.d/LIT-139.md) gave two THEORYs, because its descriptive finding is well
supported and its normative claim rests on a premise it leaves unargued.
