# LIT-tmp0f102: On the logic of theory change: Partial meet contraction and revision functions

Alchourrón, C. E., Gärdenfors, P. and Makinson, D. (1985). On the logic of theory change: Partial meet contraction and revision functions. *Journal of Symbolic Logic* 50(2), 510–530. DOI 10.2307/2274239

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-tmpo6kr8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpo6kr8.md)). For a logically closed theory A, the contractions satisfying Gärdenfors's six basic postulates are exactly the partial meet contractions, A ∸ x = ⋂γ(A ⊥ x), where γ picks a nonempty subset of the maximal subsets of A not implying x (Observation 2.5); adding the two supplementary postulates gives exactly the contractions whose γ is generated by a transitive relation over those subsets (Corollary 4.5). Revision is defined from contraction by the Levi identity and inherits the corresponding postulates. The result needs, besides A, a selection function or ordering that A itself does not supply: different selection functions give different contractions of the same theory, and in the finite case distinct selection functions always give distinct contractions (Observation 4.6). The setting is one-step change of a single fixed theory; nothing is said about iterated change, about where the selection function comes from, or about the reasons for which a belief is held.

## Why this is here

A4 and A10 said the belief-revision literature matters to the exchange's
questions "as much as the recent prompting literature". It is registered
for [QUESTION-tmpga1fv](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpga1fv.md) and [CLAIM-tmp2rwpl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp2rwpl.md). The reading ([NOTE-tmpo6kr8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpo6kr8.md)) corrects
how it was first described here. AGM's state is not the closed theory
alone: a revision needs the theory plus a selection function or ordering,
and different ones give different results on the same theory. That backs
[CLAIM-tmp2rwpl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp2rwpl.md)'s general point that current conclusions do not fix their
revision. But what AGM adds is a preference over what to keep, not a
record of what each belief depended on, and the paper does not discuss why
beliefs are held; that point is Hansson's ([LIT-tmpr5312](LIT-tmpr5312.md)). For
[QUESTION-tmpga1fv](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpga1fv.md) it answers only a one-step version: a theory and a
transitive ordering suffice for single revisions.
