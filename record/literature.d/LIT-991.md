# LIT-991: Beyond the Moral Influence Theory? A Critical Examination of Vargas’s Agency Cultivation Model of Responsibility

Harry Harland (2020), *The Journal of Ethics* 24(4): 401–425.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpp7gou](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpp7gou.md).*

- **ACM is MIT renamed.** Each of Vargas's replies to the objections to
  the moral influence theory "can just as easily be made by supporters of
  MIT". His responsiveness to moral considerations, which may be
  unconscious and "exclusively affective", amounts on Harland's
  stipulation to intrinsic motivation to act morally. It draws the line of
  responsible agency where MIT does, taking in some animals and toddlers.
- **Moral drifters.** John and Fred internalise norms through social
  pressure, one false and one true. Neither is a moral agent; Fred is "a
  moral drifter". There are still "good reasons for holding them
  accountable", but doing so does not cultivate agency.
- **A dilemma.** A richer, Dworkinian notion of agency would separate ACM
  from MIT, but praise and blame are not shown to cultivate it.
- **The test is redundant.** Vargas fixes the relevance and proportion
  parameters of his counterfactual free-will test by what is co-optimal
  for the responsibility system. So the test cannot be applied until one
  knows whether blaming such agents is good policy, and then it is not
  needed.
- **Freedom inverted.** "A wrongdoer's blameworthiness is logically prior
  to his free will". The conclusion is stated for Vargas's model: "if we
  embrace ACM, enquiries into an agent's counterfactual freedom will
  become superfluous".
