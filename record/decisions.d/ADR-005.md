# ADR-005: anthology-candidate: a curation flag in the tag vocabulary

## Context

[ADR-001](ADR-001.md) keeps nothing in both records, so moving a work to the
anthology is a supersession: *Talagrand Meets Talagrand* went as
`LIT-064` → `ANTH-LIT-726`. Until now, the works somebody thought might follow
it were noted only in prose. The readings of `LIT-056`, `LIT-058` and `LIT-085`
each say so in their "what a deeper reading should check" section, and nothing
could list them.

The 2026-09-26 sweep of the papers-feed tracker for philosophy makes this
larger. Its seed dossiers judge some works to be chiefly about machine-learning
practice rather than a philosophical question that uses ML as its example. The
owner decided that everything from the sweep stays in nucleation for now, to be
transferred later if judged appropriate. The owner also asked for a tag, so
that a view could report on the candidates.

## Decision

Add `anthology-candidate` to `topics`, with a label and a blurb that say it is
a curation flag. Declare it as the only member of a `flags` tag group
(`require: any`: a label, not a rule, as in [ADR-003](ADR-003.md)).

- **It is carried after the work's topics, never first.** `primary_topic`
  derives from `tags[0]`, and a flag is not what a work is about. The lint does
  not enforce this position. It is a filing rule, like the rule of listing the
  topic the document is most about first.
- **It is a question, not a verdict.** Removing the tag records "stays here".
  Moving the work records the transfer: a `Superseded` status, with
  `superseded_by: ANTH-LIT-NNN` naming the anthology's copy.
- **Its tag page, `docs/literature/tags/anthology-candidate.md`, is the
  report.** `luria index` generates it like any other topic page, so no new
  machinery is needed.

## Alternatives

- **A separate field**, such as `candidate_for: anthology`. It says what it
  means more exactly, but every view in luria 0.31.0 is generated from `tags`,
  so a new field would need a report nothing generates.
- **A status.** Status says how the record stands on a work: read, placed,
  retired. A work can be `Active` here *and* a candidate for the anthology, so
  a status would lose one of the two facts.
- **Prose only, as before.** Prose is what could not be listed.

## Consequences

- `LIT-056` (*Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence*), `LIT-058` (I-MMSE) and
  `LIT-085` (NUTS) take the tag. Their readings already name the anthology as a
  possible home.
- The works from the philosophy sweep whose dossier judged them chiefly about
  ML practice take it when they are filed.
- The topic vocabulary now holds one word that is not a subject. The group label
  and the blurb are there so that a reader of the tag list is not misled.
