# THEORY-tmp0xl3k: In a Pólya-urn model of predictive policing, Ensign and colleagues show that allocating patrols by incidents discovered on patrol does not learn true crime rates: with equal rates the prior persists and with unequal rates the higher-rate region takes all, reported incidents only attenuate the loop, and dropping discovered incidents with the probability of patrol removes it

Ensign et al. (2017; FAT* 2018), [LIT-tmpsd8aw](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsd8aw.md), read in [NOTE-tmpwpvfg](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwpvfg.md).

## The result

"predictive policing algorithms only observe crime in neighborhoods they
patrol" (p. 1). Lemma 2: "the prior belief coupled with the lack of
feedback about the unobserved region prevents the system from learning
that the two areas are in fact identical". Lemma 4: with unequal rates the
process "will settle on region B with probability 1", "a classic 'go with
the winner' problem". Reported incidents "can attenuate the degree of
runaway feedback" but "cannot entirely remove it" (abstract). The repair
drops a discovered incident with the probability that police were sent
there (§3.5.1).

## What it does not show

- **Field behaviour.** Two regions, simulated; the authors grant that
  recorded incidents track the true rates, which favours the systems.
- **That the loop creates crime.** It amplifies a real difference or
  freezes the prior.
