# QUESTION-tmpw2rz2: How is a capability identified apart from the organizations that now hold it, so that conserving the capability can be told from conserving its occupant?

## What makes it open

CLAIM-tmpyakra holds that regulation should often conserve a capability
and the conditions of its reproduction, not the organization that now
performs it. To conserve a capability apart from its holder one must say
which capability it is without naming the holder. QUESTION-tmpn9t5a's
first unsettled item is the same thing from the other side: "the record
has no account of how a capability is individuated apart from the firms
that hold it." Its rare-invasion test measures something different
according to the answer.

**A candidate criterion**, from the manuscript's table of gaps:
individuate a capability by the demand it meets and the routes that
realize it, carrying CLAIM-916's step 4 to an ecosystem. Where a capacity
can be realized by substitutable routes, its constraint set is "the union,
over routes, of the states in which every member of that route stands",
so a capability would be the demand plus its set of routes, whoever stands
on a route. Two difficulties: a route may be held only by one firm, as
tacit knowledge is, which collapses the capability into its holder; and
the demand individuates the function only if the specification is given,
which is QUESTION-485's problem.

## What would count as an answer

A criterion that names a capability without naming a holder, and a case
where it separates conserving the capability from conserving the
incumbent. LEAD-tmpy0jkr's model needs one: "a declared bearer of
recovery, a firm or a capability".
