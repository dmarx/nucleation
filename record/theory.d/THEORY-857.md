# THEORY-857: Remedies for algorithmic governance offered as access or as transparency can run against the people they are meant to serve: inclusion in a data system also gives its operators access to the included, and transparency can be demanded of the governed while the governing system stays opaque, so what an access or transparency remedy does depends on who is made visible to whom

## Source

James, A., Hynes, D., Whelan, A., Dreher, T. & Humphry, J. (2023),
[LIT-1342](../literature.d/LIT-1342.md), read in [NOTE-1041](../notes.d/NOTE-1041.md).

## What was actually shown

An argument with illustrations:

- **Access.** LinkNYC's kiosks were offered as free Wi-Fi for poorer
  neighbourhoods and read, after Green (2020), as a way to collect and
  monetize users' data. Smart kiosks and poles "can increase the
  visibility of groups and even entire neighbourhoods through their
  interactions and data traces." The paper concludes: "Access is generally
  angled 'up' ... Access is however bidirectional, and access 'down' for
  powerful players is politically consequential." It quotes Benjamin on
  being "included, and hence more accurately identifiable, in an unjust
  set of social relations".
- **Transparency.** Under Robo-debt, the automated recovery of assumed
  welfare overpayments, "No credible claim to transparency was made ...,
  though transparency was arguably demanded of welfare recipients through
  the 'reverse onus'", the requirement that recipients disprove the debt.
  The scheme was eventually ruled unlawful. Elsewhere, procedural
  transparency is said to hide "the contractual relations through which
  these technologies are developed", where proprietary algorithms "often
  cannot be scrutinised".

## What this does not say

- **Not how often.** The cases were chosen to illustrate. Nothing in the
  paper estimates how often access or transparency remedies run backwards.
- **Not that transparency or inclusion is harmful as such.** The paper
  says such remedies are insufficient and open to reversal. Its
  recommendation is refusal, not opacity.
- **Not a measurement of legibility.** Neither direction is quantified.
  The connection to [TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)'s directed relation is the record's.
