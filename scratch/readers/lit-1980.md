## Summary

Three preregistered MTurk experiments (n = 111, 445, 447; US). For four
minutes participants press keys to earn ~60c for themselves; in one
condition each fourth keystroke also gives the Red Cross 1c, in another
it takes 1c from a planned donation (~15c either way), in the control
neither. Positive impact raised beneficence satisfaction and well-being
(positive affect, vitality, meaning). Negative impact raised beneficence
frustration (manipulation check, meta d = 0.84) but not ill-being
(negative affect d = 0.07, anxiety 0.09, depression 0.06, all CIs over
zero), and it also raised meaning, vitality and even beneficence
satisfaction. Study 3 finds the negative-impact group feels it "helped
science" more, read as compensation.

## Contribution

An experimental, not correlational, test of the frustration criterion for
beneficence: the same "dosage" of impact, positive or negative, randomised.
It adds an asymmetry finding: "the same 'dosage' of impact, which when
positive is capable of increasing participants well-being is not enough,
when negative, to increase participants ill-being to a similar degree"
(p. 9).

## Key results

- Hypotheses (p. 2): positive impact raises well-being, negative impact
  raises ill-being, each without necessarily moving the other side.
- Study 1 (p. 3): negative affect higher in negative-impact than neutral
  (Tukey p = .035), but the main effect fell to p = .068 controlling for
  gender. Underpowered for small effects (detects f ≈ 0.30).
- Study 2 (pp. 5–6): "we didn't find any parallel support for the
  hypothesis that having an antisocial impact would increase people's
  ill-being"; negative-impact participants felt more meaning than neutral
  (p = .046).
- Study 3 (pp. 7–8): no ill-being differences; negative-impact group rated
  "I helped scientific research" higher than neutral (p = .0498; .020
  with controls).
- Meta-analysis (§4, p. 8): "meta-analysis showed no effect of the
  negative impact condition on ill-being indicators. Instead, being in the
  negative impact condition seemed to increase positive affect, vitality,
  and meaningfulness as well as both beneficence satisfaction and
  beneficence frustration."
- A "minority of 'resisters'" pressed less in the negative condition and
  were excluded by a non-preregistered earnings cut; reanalysis with them
  left results unchanged (p. 6).

## Assumptions

- Felt antisocial impact can be induced by a small, instructed loss to a
  charity and is comparable in "dosage" to an equal gain.
- Immediate post-task self-reported mood is the outcome that would show
  ill-being.

## Concepts

beneficence satisfaction / frustration; prosocial vs antisocial impact;
dosage; compensation and rationalisation (defence); asymmetry of
enhancement and harm.

## Bearing

- **QUESTION-369**: the nearest the record now has to the "intervention
  rather than correlation" its answer calls for, for beneficence. The
  manipulation works on felt frustration but leaves ill-being unmoved,
  which fits LIT-1983's "wellness enhancer" reading. It still measures
  outcome by self-reported well-being, so it does not leave the
  well-being route.
- **LEAD-127**: an intervention study, but no functional deficit is
  measured, and no matched established need is thwarted for contrast. It
  half-meets "what would count": the thwarting is manipulated, the
  deficit is not functional.
- **LIT-1983**: an experimental follow-up by the same authors, agreeing in
  direction. It removes the entry-order and collinearity objections to
  LIT-1983 (randomised, no competence control), so its null is the
  stronger.
- **THEORY-052** / **CLAIM-368**: none directly; the three needs are not
  measured.

## Limitations (including gaps)

- One US online panel; self-report; trivial stakes (15c) and instructed
  harm, so responsibility can be shifted to the experimenter, as the
  authors say (p. 9). An ill-being null at that dose does not show
  beneficence frustration harmless at real doses.
- The asymmetry claim compares a gain and a loss assumed equal in felt
  size; the manipulation checks show a larger shift in satisfaction (d
  1.26) than in frustration (d 0.84), so the "same dosage" is asserted,
  not shown.
- The compensation account is supported by one single-item measure
  ("helped science") at p ≈ .05; mediation, preregistered as a hypothesis,
  is not reported as tested.
- Situational depression α = .52–.61, so the depression null is weak.

## Candidate THEORY

"Martela and Ryan (2021) find in three preregistered experiments that
having a small, instructed negative impact on a charity raises felt
beneficence frustration but not self-reported ill-being, while an equal
positive impact raises well-being, an asymmetry consistent with
beneficence enhancing wellness without its frustration harming it."

promote_when: a replication with real stakes, harm not attributable to an
instructor, outcomes beyond immediate self-report (behavioural,
physiological, or delayed), and a sample outside US online panels, that
again finds no ill-being effect; refuted if a design of that kind finds
negative impact raises ill-being comparably to how positive impact
raises well-being.
