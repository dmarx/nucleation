# NOTE-tmpptg2p: Smit, Helmich, Bringmann, Oldehinkel, Wichers and Snippe, read for whether individual time series show a transition into depression as a tipping point

## Contribution

The first test across many people of whether critical slowing down
precedes recurrence of depression within the person, after two
single-patient studies (Wichers et al. 2016, [LIT-tmpdpj4x](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdpj4x.md); Wichers, Smit
& Snippe 2020). It estimates sensitivity and specificity, which one
patient cannot give. The reading is [THEORY-tmp6agnf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp6agnf.md).

## Key results

- **Why within-person (pp. 4–5).** Earlier evidence was "conducted at the
  between-person level while aiming to inform us on a within-person
  process"; "EWS have been studied on the within-person level in only two
  individuals with a transition towards higher levels of depression".
- **Sample and data (pp. 6–8).** TRANS-ID Tapering: adults in remission
  who chose with their doctor to stop antidepressants. Five momentary
  assessments a day for four months; compliance median 88.5%, "a median of
  545 observations per individual (range = 344-609)". Transitions were
  rises in the SCL-90 depression scale that were reliable, persistent
  (three weeks, or treatment restarted) and judged clinically relevant by
  three raters, over six months.
- **Analysis (pp. 8–10).** Lag-1 autocorrelation and variance of negative
  affect with low arousal in a moving 28-day window, then a Mann–Kendall
  trend test, counted significant if p < .05 and τ > 0.1, on the windows
  before the transition. Preregistered, with 17 alternative settings.
  "leaving 19 participants with a transition, and 18 participants without
  a transition (N=37)".
- **Main result (p. 10).** "Out of the 19 participants with a transition,
  7 (36.8%) showed a significant increase in the lag-1 autocorrelation of
  NA low and 5 (26.3%) a significant increase in the variance"; of the
  18 without, 4 (22.2%) and 2 (11.1%). The group difference in trend was
  significant for variance (p = .038, d = 0.60) and not for
  autocorrelation (p = .090, d = 0.45). On average both trends were
  negative in both groups.
- **Timing (p. 11).** Where present, the trend "became significant 56.7
  and 37.8 days, respectively, before the start of the transition".
  Sudden transitions showed fewer signals than gradual ones, against the
  hypothesis.
- **Several signals together (pp. 11–12).** Over eight tests (four affect
  variables, two signals), participants with a transition averaged 2.79
  significant rises and those without 0.89; three or more would have
  flagged "10/19 (52.6%) of transitions, at the cost of only 2/18 (11.1%)
  of false positives", a post hoc rule.
- **Robustness (p. 13).** "the percentage of true positives (mean =
  32.9%, SD = 6.3%) was larger than the percentage of false positives
  (mean = 16.2%, SD = 9.9%)" across the 17 settings.
- **The authors' reading (p. 15).** "The absence of EWS in the majority of
  participants with a transition, may indicate that in the majority of
  patients, the recurrence of depressive symptoms is not a critical
  transition", but a large perturbation can cause a transition without
  slowing, so the absence "should therefore not be interpreted as evidence
  that individuals do not function like a dynamical system."

## Assumptions

- A transition into depression is a shift between alternative stable
  states, approached by a loss of resilience that shows as slower
  recovery from small perturbations.
- Momentary affect is a variable in which that slowing would show.
- Antidepressant discontinuation is a period of raised risk, not a
  manipulated driver: the dose is not used as a control variable.

## Concepts

- **Critical slowing down (CSD)**: slower recovery from perturbations as a
  system nears a tipping point.
- **Early warning signals (EWS)**: rising autocorrelation and variance,
  the measurable signs of CSD.
- **Replicated single-subject design**: the within-person analysis
  repeated in each person, then compared across people.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-058](../theory.d/THEORY-058.md) (its promote_when, route 1; meets the critical-slowing
  half in part).** The route asks for "Individual time-series data ...
  showing the signatures of an alternative stable state: critical slowing
  down (rising autocorrelation and variance among symptoms) before onset
  or relapse, or hysteresis". This is such data, before relapse, in 37
  people, with a preregistered analysis, and the signatures appear more
  often before relapse than without one. Three limits keep it short of
  meeting the route outright: the signals are in momentary affect, not
  "among symptoms"; they appear in a third of relapses, and the authors
  themselves read that as possibly "not a critical transition" in most;
  and the group difference holds for variance, not for autocorrelation.
  Whether this meets the route is the owner's call; no status is changed
  here.
- **[QUESTION-045](../questions.d/QUESTION-045.md) (evidence on the first half of its premise; does not
  answer).** The question's lock-in is a regime that "outlasts its
  trigger". This study shows within-person destabilization before entry
  into depression in some people; it says nothing about the persistence
  after entry that hysteresis would be, nor about a maintaining link. It
  is individual data, of the nearer kind.
- **[LIT-tmpzu3i3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzu3i3.md) / [THEORY-tmpan7c6](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpan7c6.md) (contrast).** Critical slowing is the
  sign of approaching a threshold; hysteresis is the asymmetry of the
  thresholds. The tapering design has a candidate control variable, the
  dose, which falls one way only, so it could not show a return path.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- **Read from the preprint**; numbers other than those in the abstract
  were not checked against the published version.
- **Low sensitivity**, about a third, in the authors' words "insufficient
  for clinical applications".
- **A downward drift** in both signals in most participants, perhaps from
  habituation to the questionnaire, which the authors think lowered the
  number of signals in both groups.
- **37 people** is large for this design and small for between-group
  tests; the autocorrelation difference did not reach significance.
- **The combined rule is post hoc** (three or more of eight tests), from
  the same data.
- **No manipulation and no return path**: the driver (discontinuation)
  is not measured as a continuous control variable and does not reverse,
  so the design cannot speak to hysteresis.
