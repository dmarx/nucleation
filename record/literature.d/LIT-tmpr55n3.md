summary: >-
  A textbook chapter. Social identity is "that part of an individual's self-concept which derives from knowledge of membership in a social group (or groups) together with the value or emotional significance attached to that membership" (Tajfel 1978). The theory has two parts. In the first, categorization is always self-relevant, and the motive for a positive social identity drives comparison with out-groups. That identity serves needs for certainty, self-esteem and meaning. In the second, members of low-status groups choose individual mobility, collective action or social creativity, depending on the permeability of group boundaries and the legitimacy and stability of status differences. Identification has cognitive, affective and behavioural components. Applications: identification improves health behaviour and buffers stress; at work it matters more than instrumental interdependence and motivates volunteers. An imagined common in-group raised Italian children's willingness to help an immigrant classmate.
