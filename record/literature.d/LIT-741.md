# LIT-741: Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems

C. S. Holling (1973), *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics* 4:1–23 —
DOI-10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245

## Key takeaways

- **Two properties, not one.** "Resilience determines the persistence of
  relationships within a system and is a measure of the ability of these
  systems to absorb changes of state variables, driving variables, and
  parameters, and still persist." "Stability, on the other hand, is the
  ability of a system to return to an equilibrium state after a temporary
  disturbance."
- **They can come apart.** The spruce budworm forest is "highly unstable
  and it is because of this instability that it has an enormous
  resilience". Insect populations in extreme climates fluctuate widely and
  absorb extremes. Great Lakes fisheries are stable, with low resilience.
- **Domains of attraction.** More realistic predator–prey models (lags,
  contagious attack, a prey reproduction threshold) produce bounded domains
  of attraction, outside which a population goes extinct. Holling takes
  distinct domains to be "not uncommon" in closed systems, while noting
  that open systems often recover by reinvasion.
- **Measuring resilience.** Two measures are proposed: the area of the
  domain of attraction, and how much the forces must change before every
  trajectory leads to extinction. Both concern the boundary, not the
  equilibrium.
- **Management.** An equilibrium-centred approach that secures a stable
  maximum sustained yield "might so change these deterministic conditions
  that the resilience is lost or reduced", so that a rare event triggers
  sudden change. A resilience approach keeps options open and does not
  presume the future is predictable.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a supplement to the essay on
organizational will. Its §6.1 claim, "stability is not viability", is
Holling's distinction made twenty years before Aubin's monograph. It is the
primary citation for the conceptual point, with Aubin ([LIT-714](LIT-714.md)) for
the formal one. Northstar's emergency regime, effective at suppression
while eroding maintenance, is Holling's maximum-sustained-yield story. The
account is [THEORY-132](../theory.d/THEORY-132.md).
