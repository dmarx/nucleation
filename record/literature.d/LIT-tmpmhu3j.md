# LIT-tmpmhu3j: Breakdown of Will

George Ainslie, *Breakdown of Will*, Cambridge: Cambridge University Press,
2001 — DOI-10.1017/CBO9781139164191 (the Cambridge Core edition). The précis
gives the paperback ISBN as 0-521-59694-7 and the length as 258 pp. Only the
year is known here, so `published:` carries it as the first of January.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description and the précis's outline, not a
reading.*

- **The publisher's description** (Cambridge Core), in part: "Ainslie argues
  that our responses to the threat of our own inconsistency determine the
  basic fabric of human culture. He suggests that individuals are more like
  populations of bargaining agents than like the hierarchical command
  structures envisaged by cognitive psychologists."
- **Structure, as the précis reports it**: three sections ("Breakdowns of
  Will", "A Breakdown of the Will", "The Ultimate Breakdown of Will") and
  twelve chapters. Chapters 2–4 criticize the conventional accounts and
  present hyperbolic discounting. Chapters 5–8 develop intertemporal
  bargaining and its evidence. Chapters 9–11 treat the will's side effects
  and premature satiation. Chapter 12 concludes.
