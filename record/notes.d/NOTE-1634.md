# NOTE-1634: Douglas, Bachelet and Church — A logic-gated DNA origami nanorobot

## Contribution

The first DNA origami device whose opening is gated by a programmable
molecular logic function tied to cell-surface inputs, shown as
conditional exposure of a payload in tissue culture. A hexagonal barrel
(196 staples on a 7308-base scaffold) in two domains, hinged at the rear
by single-stranded scaffold and fastened in front by two locks, each an
aptamer paired with a partly complementary strand. A conditional policy,
stay closed or open, is realized in static molecular hardware, the rule
set by which aptamer sequences fill the two locks.

## Key insight

The device computes nothing over time; its rule is a fact of its
construction. Each lock is a duplex that an antigen can pull apart; the
closed barrel stores strain and springs open once both locks give. Which
inputs open it is chosen by the designer through the aptamer sequences,
and how much key it takes is chosen through the lock length. The device
has a rule but no means to change it.

## Assumptions

- **Each lock is a two-state switch**, duplex or aptamer–target complex,
  following structure-switching aptamer precedent; opening is driven by
  strain stored in the closed barrel, not by an external energy input.
- **TEM on surface-adsorbed, negatively stained particles**, with
  conformational fractions from manual counting, represents solution.
- **Logic is AND only**, fixed at fold time; the device does not sense or
  alter its own lock composition.

## Key results

- **AND of two keys (p. 832, Fig. 2B).** "Both locks needed to be opened
  simultaneously to activate the robot. The robot remained inactive when
  only one of the two locks was opened. The lock mechanism is thus
  equivalent to a logical AND gate".
- **The rule set by sequence (pp. 832–833, Fig. 2C).** "The robot could be
  programmed to activate in response to a single type of key by using the
  same aptamer sequence in both lock sites. Alternatively, different
  aptamer sequences could be encoded in the locks to recognize two
  inputs." Six versions used "pairwise combinations of aptamer locks drawn
  from a set of three" aptamers: 41t, TE17 and sgc8c.
- **The opening (p. 831).** "When both aptamers recognize their targets,
  the lock duplexes dissociate, and the nanorobot—acting as an entropic
  spring—undergoes a drastic reconfiguration to expose its previously
  sequestered surfaces."
- **Lock length against sensitivity (p. 831).** "We found that shorter
  duplexes gave better sensitivity and faster activation rates, but at a
  cost of increased spontaneous activation"; 23 bp was chosen "because it
  displayed similar sensitivity to a shorter 16-bp duplex (activation at
  10 pM) without the unacceptable loss in sensitivity observed for longer
  duplexes (30, 37, and 44 bp required 1 nM)": a hundredfold more key for
  the longer locks.
- **The closed state needs a construction aid (pp. 831–832).** Two guide
  staples with 8-base toeholds "could be removed after folding" "by adding
  a 10:1 excess of fully complementary strands"; "folding with the aid of
  guide staples increased the yield of closed robots from 48% to 97.5%", "as
  assessed by manual counting" in TEM.
- **How open is told from closed.** By TEM morphology, and functionally by
  flow cytometry: closed, "the sequestered antibody fragments were not able
  to bind the cell surface"; opened by the right keys, the robot binds
  through its payload, "causing an increase in fluorescence".
- **Loading.** Payload linked "by adding linker-modified payload in molar
  excess to attachment sites and incubating at room temperature for 12
  hours", twelve sites in an inward ring.
- **Selectivity (p. 833, Fig. 2G).** In whole blood, "Off-target binding
  occurred in 0.6% of sampled cells."

## Concepts

- **Aptamer lock and antigen key**; aptamer–complement duplex against
  aptamer–target complex.
- **AND gate over molecular inputs**; the truth table as a conformational
  policy.
- **Entropic-spring reconfiguration**; scaffold hinge.
- **Guide staples**, set like a mousetrap at folding and removed by
  toehold-mediated displacement.
- **Payload sequestration against exposure**; functional readout of
  conformation.

## Connections

The barrel is a honeycomb multilayer design in the line of Douglas et al.
2009 ([LIT-2130](../literature.d/LIT-2130.md), [NOTE-1629](NOTE-1629.md)), whose week-long folding problem it
does not revisit. Its nearest sibling on the branch is Marras et al.
([LIT-2118](../literature.d/LIT-2118.md), [NOTE-1635](NOTE-1635.md), [THEORY-1438](../theory.d/THEORY-1438.md)), whose mechanisms are
switched by added strands and switched back by strand displacement, with
the cost of holding a configuration measured; this device opens once and
measures no holding cost. Both exclude or do not count misfolds and read
conformations from TEM on a surface. The mechanical origami logic of
[LIT-2082](../literature.d/LIT-2082.md) ([THEORY-1373](../theory.d/THEORY-1373.md)) is a different route to logic, reconfigurable by
moving switching elements, where here the rule is fixed in sequence. The
record's other aptamer mention, in the functional-information reading
[LIT-1148](../literature.d/LIT-1148.md), is conceptual. The device does not study its own folding
pathway, so it has no bearing on the held simulation [LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md)
([THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization.** That the authors built origami containers with
  recognition elements acting as logic gates, opening on cell-surface
  signals to expose a payload: supported (pp. 831–832, Figs 1–2). A's
  "proof-of-principle demonstration in cell culture, not an established
  clinical treatment": supported as proof of principle; the clinical clause
  is A's paraphrase, accurate but not in the paper, which says only that
  the prototype "could inspire new designs". That the object encodes a
  conditional response rather than a static shape: supported, the AND
  truth table. "A molecular device can implement a designed policy without
  being an autonomous governor of that policy.": A's interpretation, sound
  as an inference, not the paper's text; the paper does not discuss
  self-revision. A's remark that origami does not make its own staples:
  consistent with this paper, stated generally by A.

## Limitations

- **No melting or folding curves, times, hysteresis or cation figures** in
  the main text; all thermodynamic and kinetic detail is in the unread
  Supporting Online Material. The one quantitative cost given is the
  lock-length tradeoff.
- **Conformations by negative-stain TEM and manual counting**; misfolds not
  quantified in the main text.
- **AND only, two inputs, one-shot opening**; re-closing, cycling and the
  energy of the spring are not characterized.
- **Tissue-culture proof of principle**, in the authors' words a prototype
  that "could inspire new designs".
