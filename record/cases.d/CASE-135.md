# CASE-135: The Brookhollow Apartments fire, Wichita, 2023: a mayday after a stair roof collapses on two companies opens a second emergency inside a search for a trapped resident, and an independent review finds an unorganized rescue effort without finding that it changed the outcome

## The case

On 13 October 2023 the Wichita Fire Department responded to a fire at the
Brookhollow Apartments in Wichita, Kansas. A resident, Paoly Bedeski, was
trapped and died. During the search a stairwell collapsed and four
firefighters were caught, and a mayday was called.

Sedgwick County, with the City of Wichita, commissioned an independent
review from Jensen Hughes ([LIT-1374](../literature.d/LIT-1374.md)), covering every public-safety
agency from the first 911 call to the last engine leaving. It was presented
on 16 December 2024. As reported, it found shortcomings across emergency
communications and the fire department that "contributed to an
unorganized rescue effort", tension between emergency communications and
the agencies it serves, and a department culture lacking emphasis on
self-reflection and accountability. It said it "cannot conclude that the
shortcomings identified would have absolutely changed the ultimate outcome
of this fatal incident". (Press coverage, KWCH, 16 December 2024,
https://www.kwch.com/2024/12/16/city-wichita-releases-independent-audit-deadly-wichita-apartment-fire.)

A23 and A24 added details the record has not checked: that the
investigators described a mayday as "an emergency arising within another
emergency", that the mayday and the original incident were not managed
simultaneously and no clear announcement ended the mayday phase, and that
Wichita later adopted a mayday command procedure. They are kept here as
the exchange's, unverified.

## What it shows

- **Two regimes at once.** The original mission, search and suppression,
  and a new one, rescue of the organization's own members, both demand
  the response's authority. A24: the question is not only "whether the
  organization can switch regimes, but whether it can compose regimes
  whose demands become simultaneously relevant". [CLAIM-027](../claims.d/CLAIM-027.md) names the
  relations that decide which regime is effective; this case shows two
  that must both be. It is [QUESTION-078](../questions.d/QUESTION-078.md)'s question, differentiated
  coordination without synchronization, in a documented specimen.
- **Release as well as escalation.** If the exchange's detail holds, the
  mayday regime acquired authority without a clear way of giving it back.
  A24: flexibility depends "also on **the conditions under which they
  relinquish it**". [TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md) names suspension; it does not name release.
- **Act, procedure and capacity.** A review recommends; a department may
  adopt a procedure; whether the capacity is maintained is a third fact
  ([CLAIM-422](../claims.d/CLAIM-422.md)). A23: "A near-miss review may *recommend* reform without
  demonstrating that the institution actually changed."
- **The member who is also the beneficiary.** A24: "The firefighter
  matters as a person, not just as a functional part." The organism
  analogies for collectives fail here.

## What it does not show

- **Standing.** The fire, the mayday and the review's main findings are
  documented, from press coverage of the review. Anything beyond that,
  including the review's specific findings and recommendations, waits on
  the unread report. `standing: documented` is the event's, not the
  reading's. (Added the same day after a review the owner relayed.)

- That the safety culture changed. The owner's U23 premise was of a near
  miss that "resulted in a change to institutional safety culture"; A23
  weakened it to "enacted procedural and training changes", and this case
  does not establish even those.
- That the incident made a new collective agent. A37 withdrew the
  exchange's "temporarily constituted collective agent": "Coupling between
  organizations doesn't necessarily constitute an additional collective
  agent."
- The exchange's other fireground cases (Berkeley 2016, Leonardtown 2023,
  Cobb County 2022) are not filed. Their key details, such as a mayday on
  a channel the commander was not monitoring and a county-wide mayday
  guideline, could not be verified.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the review, read

- **The mayday.** The stair roof "collapsed on Engine 15 and Squad 15",
  and "There were no reported firefighters injured" ([LIT-1374](../literature.d/LIT-1374.md), p. 25).
  "Four firefighters caught" was the exchange's; the title is corrected.
- **A procedure that predated the fire.** The department's mayday policy
  said "During a mayday, no fire ground assignment should be abandoned"
  (p. 54); yet "search and rescue of Building 300 was not reprioritized and
  reinitiated for over eleven (11) minutes after the Mayday was called,
  despite the Mayday being under control within a few minutes" (p. 55).
  Dispatch had the same gap: "multiple dispatchers on multiple shifts were
  not exactly able to recall how to dispatch a second alarm" (p. 42).
  [CLAIM-422](../claims.d/CLAIM-422.md)'s procedure without maintained capacity, in one incident.
- **Release, documented.** The case's "release as well as escalation"
  depended on the exchange's detail. The review: "nor did Command announce
  when the Mayday was complete per WFD policy" (p. 63). The county's
  progress dashboard records a mayday command procedure adopted afterwards,
  and marks 37 of 40 recommendations "Implemented", which records adoption,
  not maintained capacity.
- **The phrase.** "an emergency arising within another emergency" is the
  exchange's paraphrase of "an emergency incident occurring within the
  larger context of an emergency incident" (p. 61).
