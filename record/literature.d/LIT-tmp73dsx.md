# LIT-tmp73dsx: Shared Cooperative Activity

Michael E. Bratman (1992), *The Philosophical Review* 101(2): 327–341 — DOI-10.2307/2185537. Reprinted as chapter 5 of Bratman, *Faces of Intention*, Cambridge University Press (1999), pp. 93–108.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, under the topic
`social-ontology` that [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added, as the first statement of Bratman's
planning-theory account of acting together.

`Deferred` because no lawful full text was reachable, so it is not read
and no NOTE is filed. The summary's three features of shared cooperative
activity are as search indexes and secondary summaries report them; they
are unverified against the paper.

What the record can say with a source is about the later form of the
account. Bratman's open-access précis of *Shared Agency* (2015, described
in [LIT-tmpq3vga](LIT-tmpq3vga.md)) gives the construction in five conditions: each intends
that we J; each intends that we J by way of each other's intentions,
mutual responsiveness and meshing subplans; each believes the intentions
are persistence-interdependent; they are; and all of this is common
knowledge. The précis calls the approach "reductive in spirit", and sets
it against Gilbert ([LIT-tmpe6ufg](LIT-tmpe6ufg.md)) and Searle ([LIT-tmpr2slv](LIT-tmpr2slv.md)), who appeal to
"basic new elements". How much of that construction is already in this
1992 paper is for a reading to establish.

Neighbours already in the record:

- **Tomasello** ([LIT-125](LIT-125.md), [NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md)). Bratman's commentary there argues that
  obligation may come from the "web of assurances" rather than from
  collaboration itself, and Pettit's commentary frames Bratman's model as
  the "normatively austere" side of a dilemma whose other side is Gilbert.
- **Bratman on autonomy** ([LIT-296](LIT-296.md), [LIT-297](LIT-297.md), read in [NOTE-267](../notes.d/NOTE-267.md) and
  [NOTE-266](../notes.d/NOTE-266.md)). Those encyclopedia entries report Bratman's planning theory
  of individual agency only in passing: harmony with long-term plans, and
  plans that partly constitute personal identity. This paper applies the
  same planning theory to acting together.

No instruction for machine-learning practice is known, so nothing here
points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.**

- Whether the 1992 paper already has the "intend that we J" condition and
  the meshing-subplans condition, or only the three features.
- How it handles coercion. Search summaries report that shared
  cooperative activity, unlike shared intentional activity, excludes some
  kinds of coercion; the précis does not say so, and it is unverified.
- Whether the account is claimed to give necessary conditions or only
  sufficient ones. The précis claims only sufficient ones.
