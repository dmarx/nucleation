# LIT-tmp83mpa: Bound states in the continuum

Hsu, C. W., Zhen, B., Stone, A. D., Joannopoulos, J. D. & Soljačić, M. (2016). Bound states in the continuum. *Nature Reviews Materials* 1: 16048. doi:10.1038/natrevmats.2016.48.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. What follows is from the neighbour, not from the review.*

- A BIC "lies inside the continuum and coexists with extended waves, but it
  remains perfectly confined without any radiation" (Sadreev, §1).
- It is a BIC exactly when its coupling to every continuum channel
  vanishes, by symmetry, by destructive interference, by Fabry–Pérot
  trapping or by accident (Sadreev, §3 and abstract).

## Standing in the record

A's characterization ("symmetry or destructive interference prevents its
coupling to those outgoing channels") matches the neighbour's definition
and two of its four mechanisms. It is unverifiable against this review.
It also omits that a true BIC needs exact parameters or symmetry, and
under perturbation it becomes a leaking quasi-BIC.

How far the neighbour stands in:

- **Sadreev (2021)**, LIT-tmpvudg9, Skimmed: carries the definition, the
  symmetry-protected and Friedrich–Wintgen mechanisms (worked in a
  two-level effective Hamiltonian), the Fabry–Pérot and accidental cases,
  and the collapse of the Fano resonance at the BIC point. It skips
  photonic-crystal BICs on purpose.
- **Not carried by anything read**: BICs as topological defects (vortices
  of far-field polarization) in momentum space, their robustness, the
  separability-protected type, the experiments, and lasing and sensing.
  On how robust a BIC is, the record should wait for this review or for
  Zhen et al. (2014), not fetched.
