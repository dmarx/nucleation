# NOTE-tmpko5k2: Parra Jounou and Tronto — Care ethics in theory and practice

## Contribution

As a neighbour: two sentences of Tronto's on privileged irresponsibility,
and her report of which book develops what.

## Key results

- On Moral Boundaries and otherness: "The danger of privileged
  irresponsibility is to cut off one's reaction to others."
- Closing answer: "privileged irresponsibility is a kind of bad care."
- Parra Jounou's introduction: "if we understand the function of
  democracy as not only sustaining the economy but also allocating care
  responsibilities equitably".
- On Caring Democracy's Bellamy coach (cited there at p. 44), whose riders
  think themselves "of finer clay": "Once you climb into it, you pretend
  that you don't know all those people pushing the carriage."
- Dates: "In 1992, after I finished writing Moral Boundaries"; Caring
  Democracy she "finished writing and submitted ... in 2010".

## Assumptions

None argued; an interview.

## Concepts

- **Privileged irresponsibility** as **bad care**.
- **Caring with**, care as recursive.

## Bearing on the record

- **Which of the originals' results it restates.** That privileged
  irresponsibility is Tronto's and runs through both books. Its carriage
  image is the nearest any text read comes to A17's "socially organized
  inattention", but it is an image, not an account, and the inattention
  phrase itself is in no text read.
- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: weakly, the image of not knowing those who do the
