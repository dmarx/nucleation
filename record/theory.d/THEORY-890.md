# THEORY-890: Culture shapes action less by setting its ends than by supplying a repertoire of skills, habits and styles from which people build strategies of action, so strategies outlast the ends they serve; in settled periods culture constrains through the cost of retooling, while in unsettled periods explicit ideologies govern action directly but structural opportunities decide which of the competing ideologies survive

## Source

Swidler (1986), [LIT-1367](../literature.d/LIT-1367.md), read in full in [NOTE-1075](../notes.d/NOTE-1075.md): *American
Sociological Review* 51(2), 273–286. Page numbers are the journal's.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was shown empirically; the paper argues. Its argument has three
parts, each resting on cited cases.

- **Against values.** In the culture-of-poverty debate the poor seem to
  share middle-class aspirations, so values cannot explain different
  conduct: "One can hardly pursue success in a world where the accepted
  skills, style, and informal know-how are unfamiliar" (p. 275).
- **Strategies outlast ends.** "If culture provides the tools with which
  persons construct lines of action, then styles or strategies of action
  will be more persistent than the ends people seek to attain" (p. 277).
  The case is the ascetic Protestant ethos, which "outlast[ed] its ideas"
  (p. 276).
- **Two models.** In unsettled periods ideologies "directly shape action"
  (p. 278), but "ideology forms around ethos, rather than vice versa"
  (p. 280), and "It is, however, the concrete situations in which these
  cultural models are enacted that determine which take root and thrive,
  and which wither and die" (p. 280). In settled periods culture and
  action are loosely coupled, and culture shows in "cultural lag": people
  do not take new opportunities that "would require them to abandon
  established ways of life" (p. 281).

What could have come out otherwise is in the paper's own research agenda
(pp. 282–284): who stays traditional longer after immigration; whether
belief systems break down when they stop regulating action rather than
when they stop describing the world. None of it is tested there.

## What this does not say

- **Not that values never matter.** Within an established way of life
  values "fine-tune the regulation of action" (p. 282).
- **Not how a strategy is carried.** The paper says culture works through
  "publicly available meanings" and not through "enduring psychological
  proclivities implanted in individuals by their socialization" (p. 283),
  but its notes borrow Bourdieu's habitus for the skills a strategy uses
  (nn. 9, 11). The account is compatible with either carrier.
- **Not what "structural opportunities" are.** The selection clause is
  credited to Skocpol (n. 17) and left undefined, so as stated it can
  explain any surviving ideology after the fact.
- **Not a measure of settledness.** The contrast is "not, of course,
  absolute" (p. 278).
