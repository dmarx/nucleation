# THEORY-tmpkdnvu: Ivern and Carbonell (2026), scoping 72 empirical studies of 2017–2026, catalogue 136 loot-box design features that the literature treats as problematic, in six categories (obfuscation, psychological manipulation, social influence and promotion, value, data or engagement manipulation, self-regulation obstacles, gambling resemblance), and find that the separate and joint effects of these features on players are largely unstudied

## The account

Ivern and Carbonell (2026), scoping 72 empirical studies of 2017–2026,
catalogue 136 loot-box design features that the literature treats as
problematic, in six categories, and find that the separate and joint effects
of these features on players are largely unstudied. "the risks associated
with LBs stem from the interaction of multiple features within broader
monetization systems, rather than a single design element" (p. 18).

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpzok4t](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzok4t.md); its key results are quoted there with
locations. The counts are of studies naming a feature, not of games or of
harm, and the journal and conference counts on p. 4 do not sum to 72.

## Bearing

On [CASE-248](../cases.d/CASE-248.md), as the list of design variables its study must record,
including odds and prices that vary by player.
