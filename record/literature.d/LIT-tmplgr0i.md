# LIT-tmplgr0i: The Organism

Jan Baedke (2025), *Cambridge University Press, Elements in the Philosophy of Biology (ed. Grant Ramsey)* — DOI-10.1017/9781009495035

## Key takeaways

- The organism is a "nexus concept" whose organizational (inward) and relational (outward) dimensions have to be integrated. Baedke argues this is best done by treating organisms as "overcomers" — agents that deliberately destabilize and reorganize their own organization — and not merely as self-maintaining "persisters". That lets organism-centred biology individuate organisms as distinct causal agents without replacing gene-centred biology.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Organisms are central to biology, yet it is hard to say what an organism is. After decades in which the gene dominated, fields such as epigenetics, niche construction theory and evo-devo again treat the organism as a causally efficacious, autonomous and active unit that shapes its own development and evolution. The Element examines this "return of the organism" as integrated history and philosophy of science. It covers conceptual, biotheoretical, historical, sociopolitical and anthropological dimensions, and proposes answers to the challenges facing organism-centred biology in the twenty-first century.
