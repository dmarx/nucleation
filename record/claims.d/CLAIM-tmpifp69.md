# CLAIM-tmpifp69: The selection that lets economically weighted demand set a product's design extends to news: where attention is the revenue, optimizing for it favours clickbait and works against investigative journalism, whose findings cannot be shown to be in demand before they exist and whose value falls largely to people who never pay for them

## The claim

The owner asked (U16) for the loot-box mechanism to be connected to the
attention economy, clickbait and the demise of investigative journalism.
A33's thesis, on that request: "In mobile gaming, a high-spending minority
can disproportionately determine what gets designed. In attention-driven
news, the equivalent economic power can accrue to the stories, audiences,
and behaviors that generate the most monetizable attention."

Three steps, as A33 gives them:

1. **Selection from packaging to capability (§2).** Optimization starts
   with headlines, then assignments, then "which reporting skills, beats,
   and investigative capabilities are retained". Robertson and colleagues'
   headline tests ([LIT-tmpox72u](../literature.d/LIT-tmpox72u.md)): each negative word raised clicks by about
   2.3%, a causal result about clicking only.
2. **Externality (§3).** A newsroom pays for an investigation and captures
   only part of its value; an investigation can be worth its cost to the
   public and not to the newsroom.
3. **Missing demand (§5).** "The public cannot demonstrate demand for an
   investigation whose findings are not yet known." An outlet that cuts
   investigations publishes fewer, sees little traffic for them, and cuts
   again: "The absence of discovered information becomes difficult to
   distinguish from the absence of demand for that information." This is
   [CASE-tmpd5eqf](../cases.d/CASE-tmpd5eqf.md).

## A's corrections, part of the claim's edge

- "Investigative journalism is being reorganized, not simply
  extinguished" (§7): nonprofit newsrooms grew while local papers closed.
  The loss is of local, continuous monitoring.
- The strongest rival (§10): "the loss of investigative capacity primarily
  reflects the collapse of older advertising subsidies, with click-driven
  optimization functioning as an adaptation to that shock rather than its
  principal cause."
- Christin's ethnography ([LIT-tmpw11wj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpw11wj.md)): newsrooms read metrics
  differently and sometimes cross-subsidize investigations, so "Metrics
  don't mechanically determine editorial decisions."
- A reversal (§7): a minority of values-driven subscribers can protect
  investigations by the same mechanism. "The concentration mechanism itself
  does not establish the quality of the outcome."

## Evidence, as A reports it

Bisiani and colleagues ([LIT-tmp1bfvb](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1bfvb.md)), falling originality and local
relevance across 27 Reach newspapers from 2019 to 2025; Hayes
([LIT-tmpk0xvf](../literature.d/LIT-tmpk0xvf.md)), scandal coverage down to about a quarter from 1990 to 2022
as reporting resources shrank; Gao, Lee and Murphy ([LIT-tmpn09dd](../literature.d/LIT-tmpn09dd.md)), higher
municipal borrowing costs after closures. All unread; observational except
the headline tests.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Scalco, Gesualdo, Cerqueti and Cinelli (2026), read

From the reading in [NOTE-tmp9yggi](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9yggi.md)
([LIT-tmpaodxg](../literature.d/LIT-tmpaodxg.md)), the reader's assessment
as checked by the filer: *qualifies*, in a useful way. The paper's method
can detect a void only where demand is **visible**, as searches or page
views; the claim's third step ("The public cannot demonstrate demand for an
investigation whose findings are not yet known") describes exactly the void
this method cannot see, because there is nothing to search for. So the work
supports the general frame the claim uses (information as a market of demand
and supply, where unmet demand is filled by lower-quality supply, result 4)
but marks the edge of measurability the claim depends on: a supply–demand
detector is blind to *latent* demand, which is the investigative case. It
*leaves open* the claim's causal story about newsroom economics; nothing
here concerns producers' incentives or investigative capacity, and the
quality link is correlational (result 5).

## Note of 2026-11-12: Gao, Lee and Murphy, read

From the reading in NOTE-tmpf5h7u (LIT-tmpn09dd), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **supports the externality step (step 2) and bears on
the claim's defeater; does not bear on attention optimization.** The paper
measures a public value of local news that the newspaper does not capture:
taxpayers pay about $650 thousand more per bond issue after closure, a cost
borne by people who were not (or not all) subscribers. That is exactly the
"value falls largely to people who never pay" premise, quantified for one
channel (accountability reporting on local government). It also bears on the
claim's strongest rival (A33 §10: loss of investigative capacity reflects
collapse of older advertising subsidies): the paper's instrument is
Craigslist's erosion of classified advertising, i.e. the advertising-subsidy
collapse itself causes the closures whose costs it measures. So it supports
the rival's account of *why* papers close, not the claim's
attention-optimization account; the claim's defeater (attention dependence,
apart from subsidy collapse, predicting capacity loss) is untouched. The
paper is about closure, not clickbait.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Robertson et al., read

From the reading in NOTE-tmpyh8fv (LIT-tmpox72u), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **supports step 1 narrowly (selection at the level of
packaging), leaves the rest open.** The paper shows a reliable, causal
incentive for negative wording at the headline level, and that a firm
optimizing CTR by A/B testing would select for it: the mechanism the claim's
first step names. It says nothing about assignments, beats, retained
capabilities or investigative journalism, externality or missing demand
(steps 2–3), nor about the claim's defeater (attention-revenue dependence
predicting loss of reporting capacity). Two details bear on the claim's
edge: the decline of CTR with platform age (optimized packaging loses force
as readers habituate) and the null for anger (the outrage-clickbait picture
is not what drives private clicking here). Use: grounds for "optimization on
clicks favours negative framing", not for "works against investigative
journalism".
