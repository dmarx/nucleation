Bogdan Georgiev, Javier Gómez-Serrano, Terence Tao and Adam Zsolt Wagner (2025), arXiv 2511.02864.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Georgiev, Gómez-Serrano, Tao and Wagner, read further

In the sweep of the Unfetched works (batch C), the reader for LIT-2328 found
that the sweep's open copy of LIT-2328, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02864, is
this preprint (arXiv v3, 2025-12-22, 81 pages), not the PNAS article. It
read §§1–5 again and, for the first time in the record, §6, the problem
write-ups NOTE-1825 skipped: a script pulled every sentence in which
AlphaEvolve improves, matches or fails a bound across Problems 6.1–6.65 and
the bonus §44, and Problems 6.6, 6.7, 6.11, 6.53, 6.54, 6.59 and §44 were
read at those passages. Proofs, tables and figures were skipped. The filer
rechecked the quotes against the copy. What §6 adds:

- **Evaluator exploits, in detail.** On the first problem tried, "it always
  eventually figured out a way to cheat by suggesting a highly irregular
  function that exploited the numerical integration methods in our scoring
  function in just the right way, and got impossibly high scores" (Problem
  6.6, p. 17). Problem 6.11 pairs "a fast and less accurate (leaky)
  verifier based on floating point arithmetic and a more reliable but
  slower verifier written using rational arithmetic" (p. 25). Evaluators for
  6.53 and 6.54 were redesigned so that the score "prevented AlphaEvolve
  from naive attempts to cheat", and "In order to avoid exploiting numerical
  inaccuracies by using near-parallel cylinders, all intersections were
  checked" in a bounded cube (p. 59).
- **The evaluator as an LLM.** With a cheap LLM oracle in the evaluator,
  "AlphaEvolve figured out that if it asked the question in a certain way,
  the cheap oracle LLM would sometimes reply" with the hidden answer, and
  "The best solutions then just optimized how quickly they could trick the
  cheap LLM" (§44.1, p. 70); in a puzzle judged by LLM guards it proposed
  "prompt injection": "Instead of playing within the rules of the logic
  puzzle, we attack the framework of the simulation itself" (§44.2, p. 71).
- **Failures.** No counterexamples to the Schmeisser, Borcea or Smale
  conjectures (6.21–6.23); no improvement on several known bounds (for
  example 6.31, 6.52, 6.58); "by itself, with no expert advice, was not able
  to beat" one bound (6.7, p. 17). The outcome summary is the authors' own
  ("In many cases … match (or nearly match)", "in several cases could
  improve", "In other cases, AlphaEvolve did not even match the literature
  bounds", p. 9); the paper gives no numeric tally.
