# CLAIM-959: Where membership in a whole is normatively thick, a collective institution that is an agent or a cooperative scheme its constituents belong to, constitution can ground an obligation the whole owes a constituent that it owes no outsider, under an independently justified principle of associative obligation, with the whole, where it can owe, as the bearer

## The reading

[CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md) is stated about a person who is, or is not, a constituent of an
organization. Read for a whole whose membership is normatively thick — a
collective institution that is an agent ([TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) sense 3), or a cooperative
scheme the constituent belongs to — "being a constituent" is not the bare
standing in relations that [CLAIM-960](CLAIM-960.md) reads in sense 1. It carries a
shared practice, a history, a mutual recognition. So varying constitution can
vary what is owed even with vulnerability, interests, rights and exposure held
fixed, and the matched pair need not come out flat. On this reading the case
yields its first result: "a genuinely whole-part ground of obligation", with
the rider that "what gives membership that significance must then be
explained".

## What the ground needs, and the bearer

The further premise the first result requires is a principle of **associative
obligation**: that standing in a certain kind of relationship, without consent
or a promise, can itself generate special obligations between the parties. The
record has not adopted such a principle. Its readings that point this way are
all of sense-3 wholes: Thompson's intergenerational groups, whose members'
children "acquire entitlements to its benefits" ([THEORY-1814](../theory.d/THEORY-1814.md)), and
Fineman's responsive state, owing through its constitutive role ([THEORY-1818](../theory.d/THEORY-1818.md));
[QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md)'s notes place both at sense 3 and find neither transfers to
sense 1. The bearer, where the whole is an agent, can be the whole itself — the
owner's review allowed that "for a corporation and its employees, the
corporation may be a plausible duty-bearer" — which is the reading
[CLAIM-877](CLAIM-877.md) makes first-class for the duty of answerability. Where the
thick whole is not itself an agent, the bearer clause falls back to the
controlling agents, as in the rival reading.

## Why it is supposed, and how it sits with the rival

The claim is a supposition: it holds only if an associative-obligation
principle is granted, as [CLAIM-671](CLAIM-671.md) is supposed on a vulnerability principle.
It does not contradict [CLAIM-960](CLAIM-960.md) term for term — that claim is about the
bare arrangement, this one about the thick whole — but the two are rival
readings of one case: they disagree on which sense of "whole" [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)
picks out, and so on whether its discriminating pair comes out flat. The
assistant leans to [CLAIM-960](CLAIM-960.md): the owner's default sense is 1
([QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md)), and the first result's ground needs a premise the record
has not taken. The owner chooses, and may hold both, scoped by the thickness of
the membership.

## What it does not say

- **That any associative principle holds.** None is adopted here; the readings
  cited support such grounds only for particular sense-3 wholes.
- **That a non-agent thick whole owes anything as bearer.** Only a whole that
  can owe bears the obligation; otherwise the controlling agents do.
- **That membership adds an obligation in the bare arrangement.**
  [CLAIM-960](CLAIM-960.md) denies it there, and this claim does not reach it.
