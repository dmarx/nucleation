# NOTE-tmpvxobt: Bovens and Zouridis (2002) — From Street-Level to System-Level Bureaucracies

## Contribution

A typology (street-level, screen-level, system-level bureaucracy) and the thesis that ICT relocates
administrative discretion to the designers of decision systems, with what that means for the
constitutional state.

## Key results

1. Abstract: "The street level bureaucrats have vanished." "System analysts and software designers are the
   key actors".
2. §2.2: the old discipline included ex ante steps: "The obligation to obtain the signature of their
   superior is another means to check the power of" low-level servants, plus ex ante legal review and
   mandatory ex post objection.
3. §3.1, student grants: "automation of a step in a process demands the standardization" of the steps
   before it; by the 1980s officials "merely pressed a button to indicate their acceptance of the
   computer’s decisions".
4. §3.2, traffic fines: fixed "regardless of the circumstances of the case"; appeals bring in a human, but
   "These cases are, however, far" and few between.
5. §4: "Only if the citizen should" lodge a complaint or objection does a specialized official enter.
6. §5.1–5.3: the system-level bureaucracy is the "zenith in legal rational authority", yet its designers
   are unaccountable policymakers, and "arbitrariness is not a question of a lack of impartiality, but of
   excessive" rigidity.
7. §6.2: "hardship clauses", customer panels, and a citizen's charter guaranteeing human assessment on
   request.
8. §7: "an explicit debate on the desirability of these transformations never did ensue"; at scale "such
   accountability is no longer feasible in each concrete case".

## Assumptions

Dutch legality doctrine; large agencies handling formalized legal transactions only; two cases from
Zouridis's dissertation, described qualitatively; conditionally programmed law and centralization favour
the change.

## Concepts

Street-, screen- and system-level bureaucracy; discretion of designers; digital rigidity; lex dura sed lex;
epistemic enslavement; informatization review; hardship clause; transparency as a constitutional ideal.

## Bearing on the record

- CLAIM-tmpuiaop: the clearest case in the chunk for the "exposes" reading. The steps that gave others a
  hold on a decision before it took effect (a superior's signature, ex ante legal review, a judge per
  case) are removed, and a human enters only after the decision, on objection (items 2, 5). The remedies
  reinstate intervention as a citizen-triggered step, not as delay for every case.
- CLAIM-462: execution is perfected while control of the designers is unsolved.
- TERM-tmp91lqy: item 2 gives textbook instances of friction in the term's sense.
- QUESTION-tmp9n1ac: item 8, the transformation adopted without debate, is the institutional analogue.
- CLAIM-tmpfmmkt: only the qualitative remark that case-by-case accountability is infeasible at scale.

## Limitations

A manuscript, not the published text; two Dutch cases; no measurement; pre-ML; remedies unevaluated.
