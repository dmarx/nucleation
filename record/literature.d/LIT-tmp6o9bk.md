# LIT-tmp6o9bk: A Pragmatist Theory of Evidence

Julian Reiss (2015), *Philosophy of Science, 82(3), 341–362 (July 2015)* — DOI-10.1086/681643

## Key takeaways

- Alongside the well-theorized "experimentalist" approach to evidence (Bacon, Mill, RCTs), biomedical and social science use a pragmatist approach that had never been articulated; Reiss develops it as a theory and supports it with the 1950s smoking–lung cancer controversy.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Reiss identifies two competing approaches to evidential reasoning in the biomedical and social sciences: experimental and pragmatist. Experimentalism has had extensive philosophical analysis and support from Bacon and Mill to recent work on causation and evidence. Pragmatism about evidence has been neither articulated nor defended. The paper sets out to fill that gap by developing a theory of pragmatist evidence, illustrated and supported with a case study of the 1950s controversy over smoking and lung cancer.
