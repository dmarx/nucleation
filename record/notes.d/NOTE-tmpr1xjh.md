# NOTE-tmpr1xjh: Barrett et al. — The theory of constructed emotion: more than a feeling

## Contribution

A reply, not a new statement of the theory. Against van Heijst et al.'s
proposal that basic emotion theory (BET) explains emotions as
"bioregulatory states of the body" and the theory of constructed emotion
(TCE) explains feelings, the sixteen authors argue that the split
misdescribes their theory, ignores evidence against BET, and rests on an
outdated evolutionary biology. Beyond the 2017 statement ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md)) it
states four things plainly: feeling and bodily regulation are one
mechanism, not two; the theory is holistic, not reductionist; its
evolutionary frame is the extended evolutionary synthesis; and the two
theories are incommensurable and cannot be integrated. It reports no data.

## Key insight

Separating an emotion into an objective bodily state and a subjective
feeling of that state is not neutral ground on which both theories can
meet. It is "the bifurcation of nature" (Whitehead), and it is already a
commitment of BET. On the TCE an instance of emotion is "a complex causal
ensemble (i.e., a whole-brain event) whose simultaneous features include
bodily regulation and the mental features of experience" (Error 1,
"Holism, not reductionism"). So there is no part of the emotion that is
the body's and another that is the feeling's.

## Assumptions

- **Allostasis is the brain's main function.** The brain anticipates the
  body's needs and prepares to meet them. "Allostasis is predictive and does
  not involve set points" (Error 1, "A brain regulates and coordinates the
  systems of its body"). Homeostatic subsystems are coordinated by it.
- **Efference copies.** Axons from "primary allostatic control neurons"
  (anterior cingulate is the example) send motor control signals to the
  spinal cord and, at once, prediction signals that build features of
  experience. The support is anatomical (Barbas 2015), cited (Error 1,
  "Feeling is an emergent feature of the brain's regulation of the body").
- **Population thinking.** Variation among instances of a category is
  structured by context and is real; a prototype is a summary (Mayr). BET is
  cast as typological thinking (Error 2).
- **Relational realism.** Mental features are "not properties of the
  intrinsic brain signals alone or of the incoming sensory signals alone but
  of their relations" (Error 1). "Relative" means only that instances of
  emotion "are conditioned on some perspective"; it does not mean subjective
  or arbitrary (Error 2, "Relational meaning"), after Barrett & Theriault
  (2025).
- **The extended evolutionary synthesis** against the modern synthesis:
  genes are "necessary but not sufficient", context is "fully causal rather
  than merely modulatory", and cultural inheritance and niche construction
  are evolutionary processes (Error 3).

## Key results

There are no new data. By section:

- **Error 1: more than a theory of feeling.**
  - The TCE contains hypotheses about emotions as "bioregulatory
    responses", but "Emotions are not special in this regard": every moment
    of life involves bioregulation. "Feeling cannot be meaningfully
    separated in any biologically justified manner from the brain's
    regulation and coordination of the various systems of the body."
  - Allostasis "does not cause separate instances of emotion but manifests
    them", as it manifests perception, cognition and decision.
  - The efference-copy hypothesis: "efferent copies of the same allostatic
    signals that control and regulate the body simultaneously create
    feeling and other features of lived experience", together with afferent
    and reafferent signals.
  - Mental features are compressions: "lower dimensional summaries of
    higher dimensional sensory and motor signals once their temporal and
    spatial redundancies have been removed".
  - Table 1 sets the TCE, van Heijst et al.'s description of it, and BET
    against Tinbergen's four questions. Its causal entry: categorizing
    creates regularities rather than detecting them, and the meaning
    it makes "manifests as features of allostasis, action, and lived
    experience".
- **Bifurcation and the blind spot.** The split implies that experience has
  "no significant causal role in the brain's bioregulation of the body".
  It has "manufactured" a correspondence problem, citing Gross (1998), Mauss
  et al. (2005) and Ventura-Bort et al. (2022): physical changes during
  instances of an emotion category rarely covary with its experience.
  "Heart rate can increase, decrease, or remain the same (relative to a
  baseline measurement) when people report feeling angry or afraid." The
  section also blames the blind spot for the "widespread failure of
  pharmaceutical companies to develop drugs that cure depression and
  anxiety" (citing LeDoux 2015) and for reading emotion from faces in
  hiring and in court.
- **Error 2: evidence against typological thinking.** Table 2 lists studies
  of variation within and similarity across emotion categories in
  peripheral physiology, brain signals, expressions, affective features,
  culture and words. "One cannot integrate a theory that requires unchanging
  prototypes with a theory that denies their existence." Even a single
  brain biomarker is "a statistical fiction" (after Clark-Polner et al.
  2017). The inference is stated as asymmetric: population evidence
  "disconfirms" emotion types, but commonality among instances would not
  show a gene or a dedicated circuit.
- **Ad hoc categories.** "A category is something you do, not something you
  have." A category event begins as a sample of brain-wide prediction
  signals with a similar control plan, "something like an action tendency",
  and ends as one situated instance selected by incoming signals. Signals
  such as facial movements and heart rate "have no inherent, biologically
  prepared emotional meaning"; meaning is relational. "A
  perspective-independent emotion does not exist in any meaningful sense."
- **Error 3: the extended evolutionary synthesis.** BET is placed in the
  modern synthesis, where "FEAR's expression or triggers can be modified or
  tweaked after the fact by lived experience, culture, or other phenomena
  such as regulation strategies". The TCE is placed in evo-devo: caregivers'
  allostatic support builds the niche in which infants learn emotion
  categories, words are "tools of niche construction", and humans are said
  to build more abstract categories than chimpanzees for reasons of niche
  and brain scaling. "All animals categorize", and "Even single-cell
  organisms can be said to categorize". Animal brains categorize incoming
  signals "and create lived experience (whatever that means for the animals
  in question)".
- **Incommensurability.** "Our actual theory cannot be integrated with BET
  because the two are incommensurate" (Kuhn, Feyerabend). Table 3 lists
  eight contrasts. On categorization: "Categorization involves constructing
  regularities, not detecting them." On causation: contextual factors are
  "fully causal and necessary".
- **The James–Lange example.** James's and Lange's theories were
  incommensurable, population against typological, and Dewey joined them by
  the same bifurcation. The authors call this "one of the great mistakes in
  modern psychology".
- **Conclusion.** "Emotions are not part of some perceiver-independent
  reality." In a survey of emotion scientists, "80% of the respondents
  endorsed typological beliefs (Ekman, 2016)".

## Concepts

- **Bifurcation of nature**: separating perceiver-independent physical
  states from perceiver-dependent feelings, so that feelings exist "only in
  a perceiver's mind" and are taken as epiphenomenal (Whitehead 1920).
- **Allostasis**: the brain's predictive regulation of the body for
  efficient energy use, without set points, as distinct from homeostasis.
- **Efference copy (here)**: the same allostatic control signal sent both to
  the body and as a prediction that helps construct features of experience.
- **Ad hoc category**: a momentary, situated grouping of instances that
  serve one function in one context, built in the brain as a dynamic event.
- **Typological and population thinking**: Mayr's terms. The first takes a
  category's prototype as real and variation as error; the second takes the
  variation as real and the prototype as a summary (Figure 1).
- **Relational realism**: instances of emotion are real and "conditioned on
  some perspective"; BET is cast as "traditional realism" (Figure 1).
- **Abstract feature**: a lower-dimensional feature generalizing over
  different concrete sensory and motor patterns, such as "threat" or
  "remove an obstacle" (footnote 8).

## Connections

It answers van Heijst, Kret & Ploeger (2025) in the same issue, and places
them in a line with Adolphs (2017), Damasio (2004) and de Waal (2019), who
also separate emotion from feeling. It rests on the authors' own earlier
work, chiefly Barrett 2017b ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md)), Barrett & Finlay (2018), Hutchinson &
Barrett (2019), Shaffer et al. (2022) and Barrett & Theriault (2025, the
philosophy of science behind "relational"). For evolution it draws on
Bateson & Laland (2013), Jablonka & Lamb, Laland and Ball (2024). It cites
Gross (1998), the *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* study of
antecedent- and response-focused regulation, not the 1998 review the record
holds ([LIT-554](../literature.d/LIT-554.md)). It does not cite Seth.

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), [NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md) (Barrett 2017).** The same theory. What this
  reply adds is emphasis and one explicit denial: the 2017 paper already
  put allostasis first; this one says outright that feeling is not a second
  phenomenon representing the first (C2, C3). It adds the evolutionary frame
  (C6) and incommensurability (C7). It does not repeat [NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md)'s C8, the
  hypothesis about reappraisal.
- **[THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md) (emotion categories as populations).** Error 2 and
  Table 2 restate its claim with a longer evidence list, all cited and not
  re-analysed, much of it from the authors' own groups. A further source for
  it, not a test. The asymmetry it states (variation disconfirms types,
  commonality would not confirm them) is worth adding there.
- **[THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md) (felt affect).** C2 takes the prediction side of
  [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)'s open question again, now as the efference-copy hypothesis. It
  offers no case where prediction and map come apart, so it does not meet
  that THEORY's promotion test.
- **The dispute between [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md).** The paper does not
  discuss emotion regulation, reappraisal or therapy. It bears on the
  dispute in three ways, none of them decisive.
  - **Separate regulation is placed on BET's side.** Regulation appears
    twice, both times as something BET uses: variation is put down to
    "regulatory efforts" (Error 2), and FEAR is "tweaked after the fact" by
    "regulation strategies" (Error 3; also the Figure 1 caption). A
    regulation process that acts on an already-formed emotion is part of
    the typological picture the authors reject. That agrees with
    [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)'s clause that there is no distinct regulating system, by
    implication only. It is not an argument about regulation.
  - **[THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md)'s evidence is read as an artifact.** The study cited for
    the correspondence problem, Gross (1998, JPSP), is the experiment
    [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md)'s cited contrast rests on (suppression lowered expression and
    raised sympathetic activation without reliably lowering experience).
    [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) reads that dissociation as strategies acting on different
    response channels of one emotion. This paper reads dissociations of that
    kind as what the bifurcation manufactures. The citation is in passing,
    one item in a list. The record has not read the 1998 experiment either.
  - **The counterfactual gets harder.** [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) needs an unregulated
    course to compare against. If allostasis "manifests" emotions rather than
    causing them, and every moment is bioregulation, there is no emotion that
    is not already the body being regulated. That is my inference, not the
    paper's. It sharpens the problem [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) already names in "What this
    does not say"; it does not settle it.
  - **Neither promotion test is met.** No dissociation, mediation or
    stage comparison is reported.
- **[LIT-tmptaneb](../literature.d/LIT-tmptaneb.md), [NOTE-tmphhgp9](NOTE-tmphhgp9.md) (Bo et al.).** The exchange set the two against
  each other: Bo et al. found amygdala, PAG and hypothalamus responses to
  aversive pictures unchanged by reappraisal while reported negative affect
  fell. Reading that as an unchanged component of the emotion, apart from
  the feeling that changed, is the bifurcation this paper rejects. On the
  TCE the subcortical response is one feature of a whole-brain event, not
  the emotion's objective part. So the reply does not refute that reading
  of Bo et al.; it shows that it presupposes BET's split. The comparison
  is mine.
- **[CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md) (experience constituted by interpretive organization).**
  The exchange called the TCE the nearest formulation of the constitutive
  reading for affect. The reading supports that in part. Meaning is
  relational, mental features are relations among signals, and allostasis
  "manifests" instances of emotion rather than causing them. That is
  constitution language. But the paper also says that categorizing gives
  meaning to signals "to create lived experience" (Table 1), production
  language, and it does not choose between the two. Its constraint is the
  one the exchange named: the meaning is not separate from bodily
  regulation. It does not mention cognitive behavioural therapy; that was
  the exchange's example, not the paper's.
- **[CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md) (broad interpretation admits processing not shown to be experiential).** The
  paper is a case of the objection. Categorization, its word for
  interpretation, reaches "single-cell organisms", and animal brains
  categorize "and create lived experience (whatever that means for the
  animals in question)". The theory extends categorization without limit
  and leaves experience unanalysed. It gives no condition under which
  categorization is experiential, so it offers nothing towards
  [QUESTION-tmpc4j7p](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.md).
- **[TERM-tmpgqmop](../terms.d/TERM-tmpgqmop.md) (interpretation).** The paper's categorization fits the
  term's three parts: a distinction (a category that "creates" regularities),
  a context (situated, ad hoc) and a consequence (it guides allostasis and
  action). It is not defined without experience, though: the categories
  are said to make experience.
- **[TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md) (perspective, three senses).** "Conditioned on some
  perspective" is used without saying which sense. In the Conclusion it is
  the perceiver's or the scientist's inherited categories, which is closer
  to the organizational sense than to the phenomenal one.
- **[THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md), [LIT-1263](../literature.d/LIT-1263.md) (Seth).** Not touched. "Interoception" occurs only in
  Table 1, in van Heijst et al.'s words.
- **THEORY warranted?** None filed. Its substantive claims are [THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md)'s
  and [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)'s, already held from [LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md). Incommensurability (C7) is a
  claim about two theories, argued and not shown. Suggested: add this

## Limitations

- A reply with no data. All evidence is cited, and Table 2's lists draw
  heavily on the authors' own groups.
- BET is characterized by its opponents, largely through van Heijst et al.,
  which was not read here. Whether BET as its own proponents state it
  requires "immutable" prototypes is not argued from their texts.
- The efference-copy hypothesis (C2) is stated, not tested, and nothing in
  the paper shows that feeling and bodily regulation cannot be dissociated.
  Incommensurability is asserted from a contrast table.
- Several claims go well past the evidence given: that the blind spot
  explains failed anxiety drug development, and the history of Dewey and the
  James–Lange theory.
- "Lived experience" and "feeling" are not defined, and the paper is
  explicit that what experience is for other animals is left open.

## Open questions

- What result would show that feeling and bodily regulation are, or are
  not, one mechanism? A manipulation that changes the felt features of an
  instance while its allostatic signals are held fixed, or the reverse,
  would bear on C2. None is proposed.
- If regulation strategies belong to the typological picture, what is
  reappraisal on the TCE? The 2017 paper's answer ([NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md), C8) is
  that it works through the same predictions. This reply does not say.
- How is a population of instances to be tested against a family of
  prototypes, so that the two theories are not merely redescribed?
  Incommensurability makes a shared test hard by its own account.
