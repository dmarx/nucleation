# LIT-807: A Discrete-Event Simulation of Claude Lévi-Strauss’ Structural Analysis of Myths Based on Symmetry and Double Twist Transformations

Jean-François Santucci, Albert Doja and Laurent Capocchi (2020),
*Symmetry* 12(10):1706, 23 pp., MDPI, CC BY 4.0 — DOI-10.3390/sym12101706

## Key takeaways

- **The representation.** A myth is a coupled DEVS model whose atomic
  models are mythemes, each two variables: a term a (a person, animal,
  divinity or thing) and a function x (its role). A myth is stored as a
  text file of such pairs; the reference myth is Lévi-Strauss's M1
  (Bororo), loaded by a MythGen model.
- **The transformations, as specified (§2.2).** Homology replaces term a
  by term b in every mytheme; inversion replaces a by "1/a", opposition
  by "a⁻¹", symmetry by "−a", in every mytheme. The worked examples show
  that each is the same substitution with a different label: ogre →
  "Sybille" (homology), ogre → devil (inversion, because "in the Corsican
  culture, the devil is often considered as having an inverse character
  to the ogre"). Adding and removing a mytheme are the other two
  operations. Functions are not transformed by any basic operation.
- **The canonical formula** f_x(a) : f_y(b) :: f_x(b) : f_{a⁻¹}(y) is
  glossed as the double twist (a term replaced by its opposite; a term
  value and a function value exchanged) and illustrated by The Jealous
  Potter (nightjar, woman, jealousy, potter; nightjar⁻¹ = ovenbird). It is
  listed among the operations a user may select, but the paper gives no
  rule for computing it on a mytheme list, and the example's key step
  (which bird inverts the nightjar) is ethnographic knowledge.
- **Who supplies the transformation.** Each TransformationADEVS instance
  holds a `transfolist` attribute naming the new myth and a list of
  (operation code, parameters); a chain of instances generates a variant
  step by step, and a Collector draws the resulting graph of myths. The
  Discussion says it outright: the inverse of the ogre, and every such
  value, "is not obtained from the software system", and without it "it
  is impossible to define the transformation". Simulation time only
  orders the chosen operations.
- **The validation (§5).** "We have been able to generate all the
  considered 70 myths" of the Mythologiques, and 28 Corsican folktales,
  "by selecting each time a set of transformations to be applied on a
  given myth"; correctness was checked against Lévi-Strauss's own
  variations and by "specialists of Corsican oral literature". No list
  of the myths, the transformations used, or any disagreement is given,
  and nothing is measured.

`what-survives-translation`) at the owner's request: the reader of
Doja, Capocchi and Santucci 2021 ([LIT-804](LIT-804.md), [NOTE-609](../notes.d/NOTE-609.md)) flagged it as
the paper in which the transformation engine summarised there is
described. It is read here on its own merits. See the curation entry of

Read on 2026-10-09 ([NOTE-632](../notes.d/NOTE-632.md)). No THEORY is filed: the paper
specifies software and reports that it reproduces analyses given to it,
which is not a finding about myth. It answers the 2021 reader's open
question: the "transformation model" is a substitution grammar over
(term, function) pairs driven by the analyst's choices, so the 2021
paper's summary was faithful and adds nothing it hid. It models
Lévi-Strauss ([LIT-775](LIT-775.md), unread here) and cites Propp's
*Morphology* ([LIT-828](LIT-828.md)) among earlier formalisations. Descola's point
that in myth the analyst makes the cuts ([LIT-822](LIT-822.md)) describes this
software exactly.
