# LIT-1081: Visual search and stimulus similarity

Duncan, J. and Humphreys, G. W. (1989). Visual search and stimulus similarity. *Psychological Review* 96(3), 433–458. DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.96.3.433

## Key takeaways

Not read: no lawful copy could be fetched (status note). Its abstract states the similarity account: search difficulty grows with target–nontarget similarity and falls with nontarget–nontarget similarity, along a continuum, with no sharp serial/parallel or feature/conjunction divide. Treisman and Gelade ([LIT-1082](LIT-1082.md), read) argue that conjunctions, not similarity, make search serial, so the record holds this disagreement having read only one side.

## Why this is here

The owner's LEGO parable ([CASE-094](../cases.d/CASE-094.md)): "if it's a single black brick in a box
of white bricks: ok, it's buried but its' still detectable." [CLAIM-310](../claims.d/CLAIM-310.md) uses
the feature/conjunction distinction to say what an arrangement buys:
sorting on one attribute leaves the wanted brick to be told from its
binmates by one feature only.
