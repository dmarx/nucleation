# NOTE-152: Le Bihan & Read — Duality and ontology

## Contribution

A duality is a formal map between the solution spaces of two empirically equivalent theories, and dualities are pervasive in string theory and QFT. Read naively, dual theories disagree about spacetime structure, fundamental ontology and mereology, which raises underdetermination questions. The authors give a non-technical survey of the possible ontological interpretations. They offer an enriched taxonomy, several of whose options are new to the literature.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1–2 (pp. 3–7): motivates the problem through quantum gravity and string dualities. A duality is defined as a map between solution spaces, and the "common core" is linked to De Haro & Butterfield's "bare theory" (fn. 13).
- §3 (pp. 8–10), Fig. 1: five dual solutions and their common core Mc are coloured by whether the worlds they represent are candidates for actuality. Cases (1)–(2) are underdetermination, (3)–(4) discrimination, and (5)–(6) nihilism, each with or without the common core's world. Discrimination is shown not to be disjoint from the common-core approach.
- §4–5 (pp. 11–14): antirealism and structuralism, then underdetermination. Super-empirical virtues do not remove candidate worlds, and metaphysical principles (PSR, PII) lack independent support.
- §6 (pp. 14–16): discrimination defended via PII plus gauge redundancy, so that one dual's ontology is preferred when the other has surplus structure.
- §8 (pp. 17–22): alternatives to the common core. §8.1 embeds the duals in a deeper overarching theory. §8.2, "pluralism", takes dual structures to be numerically distinct parts of one fragmented world, and faces an overdetermination objection by analogy with gauge-related electromagnetic potentials.
- §9 (pp. 22–23): the common-core approach dominates the literature, and the paper adds the overarching-theory and pluralist alternatives.

- Pluralism is flagged as novel but weak. Check whether later literature has developed it.
