# THEORY-2226: Two Winograd schemas joined in one story make a Bell-CHSH scenario over pronoun-and-word observables, and the one instance judged by crowd workers violates the inequality by about one standard deviation, with no-signalling imposed by an A/B-symmetric story and a response format that never elicits the pronouns' marginals

Lo, Sadrzadeh and Mansfield (2023), [LIT-3098](../literature.d/LIT-3098.md), read in [NOTE-2316](../notes.d/NOTE-2316.md).

## The account

A Winograd schema is a pair of sentences differing in one word, with a
pronoun whose referent the word decides by world knowledge. As a
measurement scenario it has two contexts and no shared observable, so it
is never contextual. Two schemas in one discourse give four observables,
(pronoun 1, word 1 or its alternate) and (pronoun 2, word 2 or its
alternate), and four contexts, the pairings of the two words: the
Bell-CHSH scenario. The items must be genuinely ambiguous, since
deterministic judgements are noncontextual.

On one instance (the cannibalistic or herbivorous animals, one of whom is
no longer hungry or alive), 348 valid Mechanical Turk responses over the
four contexts give a violation of 0.192 with a bootstrap standard
deviation of 0.176; 87% of resamples are positive.

## What could have come out otherwise

The violation could have been negative, and it is negative in 13% of
resamples; at this spread the data cannot tell it from
zero. Whether the marginals shift between contexts could not have come out
any way: the story is symmetric in A and B, respondents choose two of four
readings, and the 62 answers that broke the symmetry were discarded. Each
valid answer records only whether the two referents coincide.

## What it does not show

It does not show that human coreference judgements are contextual, at any
level of confidence a test would use. It does not show that they are
noncontextual either. And because the format removes the per-pronoun
answers, it does not show what a Contextuality-by-Default analysis of free
answers would find, where direct influences of the second word on the
first pronoun, which language data show as a rule ([LIT-842](../literature.d/LIT-842.md)), would be
counted against the violation.
