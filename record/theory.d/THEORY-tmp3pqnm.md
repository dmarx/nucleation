# THEORY-tmp3pqnm: If a mechanism's behaviour supervenes on its constituents' behaviours and constitution is not causal, every cause of that behaviour is also a cause of some constituent along another path, so no intervention can change the whole without separately changing a part; mutual manipulability by ideal interventions is then unsatisfiable, and once interventions are relaxed to admit these common causes, every model with constitutive dependence has a model without it that implies the same correlations under every manipulation, even in ideal experimental conditions

## Source

Baumgartner and Gebharter (2016), [LIT-tmpkhdpi](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkhdpi.md), §§3–5, pp. 10–20 of the
penultimate draft ([NOTE-tmpeljd8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeljd8.md)); Baumgartner and Casini (2017),
[LIT-tmps6sc3](../literature.d/LIT-tmps6sc3.md), §§2–3, pp. 6–11 ([NOTE-tmp5hk5o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5hk5o.md)); Baumgartner, Casini and
Krickel (2020), [LIT-tmp130h8](../literature.d/LIT-tmp130h8.md), §2, p. 6 ([NOTE-tmpz7pqv](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpz7pqv.md)). Romero 2015
([LIT-tmpk95bs](../literature.d/LIT-tmpk95bs.md), unread) is cited by all three for the same result.

## What was actually shown

**The premises.** (Sup): every change in a mechanism's macro behaviour Ψ
is necessarily accompanied by a change in some constituent behaviour Φᵢ.
(NC): Ψ and the Φᵢ are not causally related; Baumgartner and Casini derive
this from spatiotemporal overlap, and Baumgartner, Casini and Krickel from
Simultaneity ("Simultaneous changes cannot be causally related").

**Every cause of the whole is a common cause.** Let I change Ψ. By (Sup),
I's change in Ψ comes with a change in some Φⱼ, so on a difference-making
account I causes Φⱼ as well. One variable causing two can do so along one
path or two; (NC) rules out the single path I → Ψ → Φⱼ. So I is a common
cause of Ψ and Φⱼ (B&G pp. 11–12; B&C pp. 7–8; BCK p. 6).

**Mutual manipulability is unsatisfiable.** Craver's MM needs, for each
constituent, a possible ideal intervention on Ψ that changes Φᵢ. An ideal
intervention's paths to Φᵢ must all pass through Ψ (Woodward's IV.iii).
A common cause violates that. So no such intervention exists, and MM
entails that nothing constitutes anything (B&G's INEX, p. 13). Dropping
(NC) instead makes constitution an instantaneous, bidirectional causal
loop (Leuridan's conclusion, granted on p. 14).

**Relaxing interventions leaves the data uninformative.** Woodward's later
definitions let a common cause count as an intervention if its other path
runs through a supervenience base. MM is then satisfiable. But each
mutual manipulation is a common cause, and "For every model featuring
constitution there exists a pure common-cause model that entails the very
same correlations under manipulations" (B&C p. 10; B&G Fig. 5, p. 19).
The confounding is introduced by the manipulation itself, so it survives
an ideal experiment, which in causal discovery would remove it (B&C pp.
10–11).

**What could have failed.** A whole could have had a cause that left
every part unchanged. (Sup) rules that out by definition. Or the single
path through Ψ could have been allowed, which (NC) forbids. Each step is
deductive. I checked them against the definitions as the papers state
them.

**The asymmetry that survives.** The micro level can be changed without
changing the macro level, since several micro states may realize one
macro state. So bottom-up interventions that leave the whole fixed can be
surgical (B&G footnote 8, p. 13; B&C p. 12). What cannot exist is a cause
of the whole that leaves all parts alone.

## What this does not say

- **That constitution cannot be known.** It says first-order intervention
  data cannot show it. B&G and B&C each propose a second-order, abductive
  route ([THEORY-tmpy9dyw](THEORY-tmpy9dyw.md)), and BCK a sufficient condition under further
  principles ([THEORY-tmpv5g3z](THEORY-tmpv5g3z.md)).
- **That interventions inside a mechanism are uninformative.** The result
  concerns a whole and its parts changing together. A diachronic cause
  inside a system, such as a production pathway whose knockout changes a
  maintained quantity only over time, is a cause, and can be intervened
  on surgically. By B&G's own temporal clause (M**) (p. 17), it is not a
  constituent of what it later changes.
- **That the premises are agreed.** Leuridan rejects (NC); Krickel 2018
  ([LIT-tmp5bf7n](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5bf7n.md), unread) proposes reading mutual manipulability causally,
  between a component and temporal parts of the phenomenon. The result
  is conditional on (Sup) and (NC).
- **Anything about where a system's boundary lies.** All three papers take
  parthood as given (B&G p. 6; B&C footnote 1; BCK p. 5).
- **That simultaneity is sharp.** (NC) via Simultaneity draws the line
  between causation and constitution at zero delay. In a dynamical model a
  part's response takes time, and whether it counts as simultaneous
  depends on the time scale of the description ([CLAIM-tmp1qybv](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1qybv.md)).
