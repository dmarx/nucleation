# LIT-563: HDDM: Hierarchical Bayesian Estimation of the Drift-Diffusion Model in Python

Thomas V. Wiecki, Imri Sofer and Michael J. Frank (2013), *Frontiers in Neuroinformatics* 7:14 — DOI-10.3389/fninf.2013.00014

## Key takeaways

- The drift diffusion model can be fitted hierarchically: each participant's drift, threshold, starting point and non-decision time are drawn from group distributions estimated at the same time, so participants who resemble each other constrain each other's estimates, and every parameter comes with a posterior instead of a point estimate. The likelihood is the closed-form first-passage density (Navarro & Fuss approximation), with across-trial variability in drift integrated analytically and in starting point and non-decision time numerically.
- Parameters can be regressed on measurements trial by trial. In the tutorial, frontal theta power predicts a higher decision threshold on high-conflict trials, and that relation reverses when the subthalamic nucleus is stimulated (data of Cavanagh et al. 2011).
- In the authors' simulations hierarchical Bayes has the lowest recovery error and the highest detection rate for drift differences and covariate effects, against non-hierarchical Bayes, maximum likelihood and the χ²-quantile method, with the largest gains at small trial counts. Applying a t-test to the hierarchical estimates also raised detection, so the gain is credited to the pooling, not to the Bayesian test.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the estimation tool the
evidence-accumulation readings are applied with. It is a software and methods
paper for cognitive modelling. The boundary was checked: the model it
estimates is a model of human decisions, its use is to measure cognitive
processes and relate them to brain activity, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice. So it belongs here rather than in the anthology.
Its primary topic is `probabilistic-modeling` (hierarchical Bayesian inference
and MCMC outside deep learning). The package is at
https://github.com/hddm-devs/hddm.

It extends Ratcliff and McKoon ([LIT-576](LIT-576.md)): what HDDM estimates is that
paper's full diffusion model, drift v, threshold a, starting point z and
non-decision time t with across-trial variabilities sv, sz and st, and HDDM
cites it, with Ratcliff and Rouder 1998, as the model's statement. The method
it replaces, Ratcliff and Tuerlinckx's χ²-quantile fit to group-averaged data,
is the one Ratcliff and McKoon use. Ratcliff and Tuerlinckx (2002) is the
method HDDM was compared against, but it is not in the record, so no
`compared_against` is declared.

It is the bridge from the models to the parameters the other filings
interpret. Bogacz et al. ([LIT-569](LIT-569.md)) read threshold as caution set by
reward and starting point as prior odds; HDDM is how such parameters are
estimated from individual people's data, and its regression feature is how
they are linked to a neural signal trial by trial.
