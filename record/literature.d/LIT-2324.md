summary: >-
  Liu, Yang et al. (2025), ResearchBench: Benchmarking LLMs in Scientific
  Discovery via Inspiration-Based Task Decomposition. ResearchBench
  evaluates LLMs on three sub-tasks into which Yang et al. (2025b) decompose
  hypothesis discovery: retrieving "inspirations" (papers not known to be
  related to the research question), composing hypotheses from background
  plus inspirations, and ranking hypotheses. The decomposition is claimed
  "sufficient": solving the sub-tasks perfectly solves discovery, under the
  assumption that most hypotheses arise from a background plus inspirations.
  An LLM agent extracts question, background, inspirations and hypothesis
  from 1,386 papers (2024 onward, 12 disciplines). LLMs retrieve
  inspirations well (GPT-4o 45.65% hit ratio when keeping 4% of candidates),
  compose and rank only moderately; ranking suffers heavy position bias.
  Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
