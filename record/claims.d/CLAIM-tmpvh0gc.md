# CLAIM-tmpvh0gc: The account does not say which distinctions a translation ought to keep: it says how to tell which ones a rendering kept for stated tasks and receivers, and what ought to be kept is set by the purpose the translation serves

## The claim

This is the record's reply of 2026-10-10 to the second horn of
[CLAIM-tmpp41ad](CLAIM-tmpp41ad.md). It is decisive in the only way a disclaimer can be: it says
what the account does not claim, and is checkable against the account's own
texts.

[CLAIM-tmpp41ad](CLAIM-tmpp41ad.md) reads [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) two ways. Read as a criterion, the thesis says
which distinctions a translation ought to keep, and is a schema until the
family of tasks Q ([CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md)) is fixed. The record answers by declining that
reading. The account is descriptive: given stated tasks and receivers, it
says how to tell which distinctions a rendering kept. Which tasks and
receivers a translation ought to serve is not something the account decides.
It is set by the purpose the translation serves, its brief: a subtitle for a
comedy, a court interpreter's rendering of testimony and a scholarly crib of a
poem are owed different things.

The record already holds the split. [CLAIM-085](CLAIM-085.md), from A45 §8: the paper's two
goals are "a **normative theory of translation fidelity** and a
**descriptive theory of how interpreters construct communicative
significance**. LLMs can help test the second. Human communicative practice
remains essential for justifying the first." The manuscript leaves the choice
open: [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s summary says "the choice of observables and decision tasks
is stated to be normative and contestable (§11)". This entry draws the
consequence for the thesis. The account supplies the second theory and not
the first, so Q is an input to it, taken from the brief, and the revised
thesis ([CLAIM-tmpc1o4m](CLAIM-tmpc1o4m.md)) states Q for the study that tests it.

## What it answers

[CLAIM-tmpp41ad](CLAIM-tmpp41ad.md)'s second horn: that read as a criterion the thesis is a
schema, and that a descriptive defeat condition cannot test it. Both are
true of a normative thesis, and the account makes none. Its first horn, the
descriptive reading, is met by [CLAIM-tmpc1o4m](CLAIM-tmpc1o4m.md).

## What it does not say

- It does not say the normative question is unimportant. It says this
  account does not answer it.
- It does not say any purpose licenses any rendering. A brief can be
  criticised, and a rendering can fail its brief; the account measures the
  second.
- It does not say the record holds a normative theory. Skopos theory (Reiss
  and Vermeer) states purpose-relative norms for translation; it is not held
  here, and [CLAIM-023](CLAIM-023.md) records that it is absent.
