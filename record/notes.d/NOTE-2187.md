# NOTE-2187: Cheng, Gao, Tomasello and Kushnir — The Agency Gap (poster)

## Contribution

Operationalizes signatures of shared agency, commitment and signalling, in
a minimal game, and shows they and their link to efficiency fall with AI
partners whose agency is outcome optimization: the agency gap.

## Key results

1. **Premise.** "Humans evolved shared agency to solve this"
   coordination problem (Motivation, citing Tomasello 2022).
2. **Design.** A non-verbal game in which an alternative goal can
   appear mid-round; partners: individual-reward RL, joint-reward RL,
   GPT-4.1 mini, and GPT-4.1 mini prompted to infer the partner's goal;
   "(Every partner is described neutrally as “another player”)".
3. **Adults (N = 150).** Human–human pairs had the highest success,
   efficiency and commitment; "Commitment predict efficiency (p < .001)."
   Read off the layout: success 100.0 human against 98.9 with
   individual-reward RL; commitment 84.4 against 56.7.
4. **Children, 5–7 (N = 109, ongoing).** With the individual-reward
   partner they were less efficient (68.2 against 73.6 with joint-reward
   RL) and less committed (58.5 against 70.9), values read off the layout.

## Assumptions

Persisting toward the first goal measures joint commitment;
goal-disambiguating moves are signalling; neutral framing equates
perceived agency.

## Concepts

Agency gap; shared agency; joint commitment; signalling; asocial and
social tool; collaborative agent.

## Limitations

unfinished; one small LLM; commitment and signalling are proxies whose
link to shared intention is assumed; one paradigm.
