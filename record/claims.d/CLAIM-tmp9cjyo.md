# CLAIM-tmp9cjyo: Active inference already runs an internal loop: mental action selects among higher-level cognitive states and sets the precision of lower-level observations, and in a deep model each level is the environment of the one above, so a judgement–observation loop may be action and perception one Markov blanket inward

## The claim

The objection, at full strength. Active inference does not confine action
to the body. Sandved-Smith et al. ([LIT-tmpp54zu](../literature.d/LIT-tmpp54zu.md)) "cast mental action
as policy selection over higher-level cognitive states and add a further
hierarchical level to model meta-awareness states that modulate the
expected confidence (precision) in the mapping between observations and
hidden cognitive states". Parr and Friston ([LIT-tmpu6g9m](../literature.d/LIT-tmpu6g9m.md)), on the
standard reading, separate attention, the precision given to data, from
salience, the epistemic value of the action that samples them. In a deep
model, a higher level's outputs are a lower level's priors and its inputs
are the lower level's posteriors. The lower level is, formally, the higher
level's environment.

So a loop of judgement and observation is already there. The judgement is
mental action at the upper level. The observation is what the lower level
returns. "Cutting action out" draws the blanket around the upper level
and calls what crosses it judgement.

## What it does not say

- It does not show that every judgement can be represented this way. The
  representation needs one generative model whose likelihood fixes what
  each observation can be, with the mental action setting precision or
  choosing what to sample. If judgements produce outcomes that no single
  likelihood generates ([CLAIM-009](CLAIM-009.md)), the objection fails, and that
  is the result [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md)'s defeater (a) names.
- Neither work it grounds on has been read. Both readings are from the
  abstract or the standard citation.
