# NOTE-tmpb51ae: Ashford and Mulgan — Contractualism

## Contribution

An encyclopedia survey of contractualism in Scanlon's narrow sense, quoting
*What We Owe to Each Other* with page numbers, and of the objections to it
to 2018: redundancy and circularity, aggregation, demandingness,
responsibility, risk, animals and future people.

## Key results

- **The formula** (§1, quoting Scanlon 1998, p. 153): "An act is wrong if
  its performance under the circumstances would be disallowed by any set of
  principles for the general regulation of behaviour that no one could
  reasonably reject as a basis for informed, unforced, general agreement."
- **Wrongness** (§1): "wrongness consists in unjustifiability: wrongness is
  the property of being unjustifiable."
- **Not Rawls** (§2): "Contractualism, by contrast, invokes no veil of
  ignorance."
- **Scope** (§3): "contractualism covers only the realm of what we owe to
  one another."
- **Individualist restriction** (§3, quoting p. 229): the reasons that
  count are "various individuals' reasons for objecting to that principle
  and alternatives to it"; "it does not allow a number of lesser complaints
  to outweigh one person's weightier complaint."
- **Comparative** (§3.1): "If a principle imposes a certain burden (b1) on
  me, but every alternative imposes a greater burden (b2) on someone else,
  then b1 does not give me a reason to reject the principle."
- **Circularity** (§6): "'Because it is wrong' is not the kind of reason
  that can be fed into the contractualist apparatus, since it is not
  something that happens to individuals."
- **Substantive responsibility** (§4, quoting Scanlon 2015): "Making a
  promise, handing over some money, taking a risk, or choosing not to avail
  oneself of an opportunity can change a person's obligations to others,
  and theirs to him or her".

## Assumptions

It reports Scanlon's 1998 view and the literature to 2018, before
Scanlon's 2025 revision of the bilateral reading.

## Concepts

Reasonable rejection; informed, unforced general agreement; justifiability
to each; individualist restriction; non-aggregation; personal reasons
beyond well-being; the redundancy objection; the tie-breaker;
demandingness.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-483](../questions.d/QUESTION-483.md).** The scope ("only the realm of what we owe to one
  another") and the circularity answer (only reasons that happen to
  individuals are inputs) are what a reading of the record's account as a
  supplement to contractualism would rely on.
- **[QUESTION-484](../questions.d/QUESTION-484.md).** Rejection is judged against "alternatives", and a
  burden gives no reason to reject if every alternative imposes a greater
  one, so which alternatives are feasible enters the test. The entry does
  not discuss feasibility.
- **[QUESTION-485](../questions.d/QUESTION-485.md).** Contractualism's answer to where legitimacy comes
  from: justifiability to each, within what we owe one another.
- **[CLAIM-735](../claims.d/CLAIM-735.md).** Left open; beyond substantive responsibility (acts
  like promising "can change a person's obligations to others") the entry
  does not discuss rights or powers.
