# CLAIM-tmp9v9yl: Theories that purport to be originalist, unitary-executive or conservative can be shown in the framework's terms to fall short of what they purport, where what they preserve, unify or hold to is not what their own rationale says should be preserved, unified or held to

## The claim

U25 calls the theories "purported" and puts their names in quotation
marks. Read that way, the owner's thesis is an internal critique: a theory
that calls itself conservative can fail to conserve, one that calls itself
unitary can undermine what makes an organization one, and one that calls
itself originalist can hold to the text while what the text secured is
lost.

## Where the exchange takes this reading

A38 §8 ends on it, with Burke's sentence (LIT-tmpus6zs) as its warrant:
"A state without the means of some change is without the means of its
conservation." A38 §8 goes on: "The inability to change can therefore
become an inability to preserve. Likewise, the concentration of authority
intended to secure organizational unity can undermine the independent
processes through which adequate unity is maintained. And fidelity to an
organization's formative specification can be insufficient to preserve the
historically acquired capacities that constitute its continuing
realization."

Each of the three sentences is an application of a general claim filed
from the exchange: CLAIM-tmpa8tdg, CLAIM-tmp1wlu2 and CLAIM-tmpazcha.

## How it differs from reading (a)

Reading (a) (CLAIM-tmpgmxs0) needs the theories to hold the conflations
charged, which the objections filed from this exchange dispute
(CLAIM-tmpmp2wh, CLAIM-tmpqhxsz, CLAIM-tmpdzcj6). Reading (b) needs only
that each theory names a good it serves and that, in some conditions, its
commitments undercut that good. It is the stronger reading against the
theories' defenders, since it argues from their premises, and the weaker as
a verdict, since it holds only in the conditions it names.
