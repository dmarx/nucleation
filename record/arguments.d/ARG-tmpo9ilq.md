# ARG-tmpo9ilq: Matched-age revision rates, as evidence, show reliance and revision coupled

## The inference

Premise (CASE-307): at matched age, claims with inbound `rests_on` or
`grounds` edges take substantive revisions about 1.47 times as often as
claims with none, under every definition and sensitivity tried, and 148 of
the 303 revisions after a first edge fall within a day of it. Conclusion
(CLAIM-tmp4fc0k): on this record reliance and revision are coupled, so a
stability-generativity map of the claims must match on age and set apart
the revisions an edge's own session brings.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** If
  reliance and revision were independent, the matched-age rate ratio would
  sit near 1 and revisions would not cluster in the day after a first
  edge. Both differ: the ratio's interval excludes 1 in every variant, and
  nearly half the later revisions follow the edge within a day.
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** The rival that
  matters is that the excess is age, since young claims are both revised
  and cited more. CASE-307's stratification by age bin and its risk-set
  matching answer it. A second rival, that a few bulk commits drive it, is
  answered by the sensitivity that drops commits touching more than 20
  claims (1.40). What the evidence does not separate is why the two are
  coupled, scrutiny (CLAIM-864's reading) or recheck per edge
  (CLAIM-863's); the conclusion is stated so as not to need that.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** In part. CASE-307's
  hand-checks put the classifier's precision at about 0.7 by intent and 0.9
  by the letter, two of ten "substantive" changes being vocabulary sweeps.
  False positives would have to fall on cited claims to make the excess,
  and the lenient and title-only variants run the same way. The window is
  the record's first 46 days.

So the inference holds for the window counted, and is Proposed pending a
rerun on the longer history LEAD-tmpn6mi2 extracts.
