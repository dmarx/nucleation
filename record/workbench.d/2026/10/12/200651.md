**What started it.** PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121), one round of argument on the
`pragmatic-transport` line, was cut from main on 2026-10-10. By 2026-10-12,
six later PRs ([#122](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/122) to [#129](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/129)) had merged. The owner asked for [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) to be
reconciled. After the mechanical merge, they raised a further worry: [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121)
challenged the argument as it then stood, and the record's argument had
moved since.

**The mechanical merge.** Thirteen conflicts were resolved, all of them
additive: relation lists, directives and dated notes. Nine `uses:` entries
on six of [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121)'s claims were brought under [ADR-038](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-038.md) (merged in [#124](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/124) after
[#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) was cut). Each now gives a reason. One, [TERM-043](../../record/terms.d/TERM-043.md) on [CLAIM-tmp3wo5j](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp3wo5j.md), is
flagged for the owner.

**The discursive audit.** Three read-only audits read the merged branch:
main's new entries on the line, main's notes and edges on existing entries,
and [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121)'s own premises. [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121)'s premises about its own targets still hold.
Main did not version, narrow or supersede any entry [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) restated.

**The lesson.** A long-lived branch that argues against the record can be
merged mechanically while it disagrees with the record substantively. The
notes of 2026-10-11 show the other half of the risk: a synthesis written on
main while an argument PR was open read the record without the argument.
