# NOTE-tmpg8cdd: Umpleby — Vladimir Lefebvre's Theory of Two Systems of Ethical Cognition

## Contribution

An accessible secondary restatement of Lefebvre's two-systems account,
with no mathematics beyond a reference to a figure. It is testimony, not
a test.

## Key insight

Two parties can each act well by their own lights and misread each other,
when one values compromise and the other confrontation.

## Assumptions

- That the means/ends gloss captures Lefebvre's algebra, which Umpleby
  asserts without showing.
- That national ethical systems are "imprinted" early (p. 67).
- The evidence is anecdote: stories, films, summits.

## Key results

- **Thesis** (abstract, p. 65): "Lefebvre contended that the dominant
  ethical systems in the West and the Soviet Union were fundamentally
  different".
- **Generation** (p. 65): "Depending on whether the combination rule for
  the exponents is addition or multiplication, two quite different ethical
  systems are generated."
- **Means and ends** (p. 65): "In the first ethical system, the end does
  not justify the means. In the second ethical system, the end does
  justify the means."
- **Types** (p. 66): "In the first ethical system a saint is willing to
  compromise and has low self-esteem." "In the second ethical system a
  saint is willing to confront and has low self-esteem." Of the second:
  "This ethical system was dominant in the former USSR."
- **Negotiation** (p. 67): "In the first ethical system both sides search
  for where they can agree. In the second ethical system, there is a
  series of ultimatums".
- **Influence** (p. 67): "Lefebvre's theory was used at the highest levels
  in both the US and the USSR", asserted without a cited source.

## Concepts

Two systems of ethical cognition; a three-level structure of reflexion;
compromise and confrontation; self-esteem; saint, hero, philistine,
dissembler; reflexive control (a keyword only).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-703](../claims.d/CLAIM-703.md)** (governing an agent through its own sound values).
  Left open. Lefebvre models which choices count as good within a system,
  the kind of model of a target's evaluative criteria a reflexive
  controller would need, but nothing here links it to manipulation. A's
  "bridge" between reflexive control and manipulating moral reasoning is
  not supported by this paper.
- **[CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md)** (governing an organization through the criteria by which
  it weighs evidence). Left open: the two systems are a case of evaluative
  criteria differing, and nothing here goes further.

## Connections

Stands in for Algebra of Conscience (1982), [LIT-2663](../literature.d/LIT-2663.md), for its thesis
(two ethical systems, Western and Soviet) and its compromise and
confrontation typology, which it attributes to the book. It reproduces
neither the Boolean model nor the derivations, and gives no page
references to the book. It does not cite Lefebvre's 1980 paper,
[LIT-2697](../literature.d/LIT-2697.md), and cannot stand in for what that paper proves.

## Limitations

Secondary, brief and anecdotal, with claims of influence that cannot be
checked from it.
