# LIT-tmpncmdx: What Is Life? The Physical Aspect of the Living Cell

Erwin Schrödinger, *What Is Life? The Physical Aspect of the Living Cell*,
Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1944 (viii + 91 pp., with plates;
"based on lectures delivered under the auspices of the Dublin Institute for
Advanced Studies at Trinity College, Dublin, in February 1943", per the
title page as catalogued). A Cambridge reprint followed in 1945 (Library of
Congress 45006254), and Macmillan issued it in New York in 1945. The DOI
above is the 1992 Canto edition, *What is Life? With Mind and Matter and
Autobiographical Sketches* (print 31 January 1992, Cambridge Core 2013; DOI
10.1017/CBO9781139644129), the edition the record would read; *What is
Life?* is its first part, pp. ix–90. No catalogue record I reached gives a
day or month for the 1944 first edition, so `published:` carries the year,
as the first of January.

The brief's citation (1944, Cambridge University Press) is correct.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description, the contents and two
publisher excerpts, not a reading. What follows is what those sources say.*

- **Contents of *What is Life?*** (Canto 1992 pagination, from Cambridge
  Core): Preface (pp. 1–2); 1 The classical physicist's approach to the
  subject (3–18); 2 The hereditary mechanism (19–31); 3 Mutations (32–45);
  4 The quantum-mechanical evidence (46–55); 5 Delbrück's model discussed
  and tested (56–66); **6 Order, disorder and entropy (67–75)**; 7 Is life
  based on the laws of physics? (76–85); Epilogue: On determinism and free
  will (86–90). *Mind and Matter* (91–164) and the *Autobiographical
  Sketches* (165–184) follow in that edition.
- **Chapter 6, as far as the publisher's excerpt goes.** It opens with
  Spinoza (*Ethics* III, prop. 2) and a section "A remarkable general
  conclusion from the model". It says that Delbrück's molecular model
  "seems to contain no hint as to how the hereditary substance works", but
  that "there is just one general conclusion to be obtained from it, and
  that, I confess, was my only motive for writing this book." The excerpt
  stops before the conclusion. The book's negative-entropy passages, which
  the brief asks for, lie past it, and I have not read them.
- **The publisher's description** (for the Canto edition): the book is "a
  distinguished physicist's exploration of the question which lies at the
  heart of biology"; "written for the layman", it "proved one of the spurs
  to the birth of molecular biology and the subsequent discovery of the
  structure of DNA."

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as the starting point of the
"life as dissipation" literature the record is filling out. No anthology

practice. The Anthology of the SOTA does not hold it: a grep of its

`Deferred` because it was not read. This record files a book from its

does not rebuild a book from what others say about it.

What the record already says about it, at second hand, and what a reading
should check:

- **Schneider & Kay ([LIT-tmpq5e95](LIT-tmpq5e95.md))** open with it. They say Schrödinger
  separated "order from order" (heredity, the aperiodic crystal) from
  "order from disorder" (staying ordered by drawing on an outside flux) and
  that he expected new principles of physics. They present their paper as
  taking up "the task proposed by Schrödinger". A reading should check
  whether Schrödinger's "order from disorder" is what they make of it.
  Their account of him is a nonequilibrium-thermodynamics programme he did
  not state in those terms.
- **Friston ([LIT-tmpfy5q0](LIT-tmpfy5q0.md))** takes its epigraph from the book, cited to
  p. 2: "How can the events in
  space and time which take place within the spatial boundary of a living
  organism be accounted for by physics and chemistry?" Friston's answer
  makes the "boundary" statistical, a Markov blanket.
- **Morowitz & Smith ([LIT-tmphuean](LIT-tmphuean.md))** cite the 1992 edition for the
  "paradox" that biomass has lower equilibrium entropy than its abiotic
  matter yet has been stable for four billion years. They argue that
  equilibrium entropy is the wrong measure on a driven earth.
- **Nahas & Sachs ([LIT-192](LIT-192.md))** cite the 1992 edition in their bibliography;
  [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md) records no use of it in the text.

**Priority for a reading: high.** It is the common ancestor of every
"life feeds on negative entropy" claim in this batch, and three of them
quote or paraphrase it. Two things want checking against the text. The
first is what Schrödinger meant by "negative entropy" (he is often read as
meaning free energy; the text has to say). The second is whether chapter 7's "new laws of
physics" is a claim about thermodynamics at all, or about heredity. The
record's other unread foundation in the thermodynamics of information is
Landauer ([LIT-328](LIT-328.md)).
