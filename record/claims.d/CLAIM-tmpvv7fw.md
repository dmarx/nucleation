# CLAIM-tmpvv7fw: Standing to contest a harm matters only as a means of preventing or remedying harm, so defeating it wrongs those affected only through the harm it lets continue or risks, and where the harm's course is fixed by causes contest could not touch, reproducing the projections that defeat it adds no wrong beyond the harm

## The objection

CLAIM-614's defeater names the reduction: "whatever is wrong beyond the
harm reduces to the harm's continuation". This is that reduction at its
strongest, aimed at CLAIM-tmpq1213 and through it at CLAIM-614.

1. A procedure through which those affected contest a harm is valuable
   because, and to the extent that, it prevents harm, stops it, or
   remedies it.
2. So keeping those affected from contesting it is wrong because, and to
   the extent that, it lets harm continue that contest would have
   stopped, or raises the risk of it. The second clause meets the reply
   that the actual outcome was unchanged: ex ante, a missing channel
   raises expected harm, which is ARG-055's own caveat ("B's missing
   procedure would raise the risk of later harm").
3. Where the harm's course, actual and expected, is fixed by causes
   contest could not touch, the defeat of standing makes no difference
   to harm, and there is nothing it is wrong by.
4. So an organization that reproduces the projections hiding its harm
   wrongs those affected no more than one that merely continues the same
   harm, once expected harm is matched.

Its best support is the practice it generalizes. Courts let an error
stand when it made no difference to the outcome, and the cost of a
procedure is weighed against the harm it averts (CLAIM-624's condition 5
and CLAIM-520 make corrective capacity conditional on cost for that
reason).

## Where it is answered

ARG-tmpl0o7n concedes premise 3's case and denies
premise 1. It builds the case where expected harm is matched, and finds
that the record judges the defeat of standing a wrong there, as it already
does in CLAIM-723 ("even where the objection would have failed") and
THEORY-1972 ("The right to be heard does not depend upon an advance showing
that one will surely prevail at the hearing"). The harmless-error practice
does not carry premise 1: it governs whether a judgement is reversed, not
whether the party was wronged, and an error found harmless is still found
to be an error.

What would revive the objection: a showing that the record's judgements in
CLAIM-723 and THEORY-1972 are themselves only judgements of expected harm
under uncertainty, so that once the uncertainty is removed, as the
construction removes it, the judgement goes with it.
