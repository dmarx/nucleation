# CLAIM-tmpe2csg: Local descriptions that disagree are not thereby incompatible: whether there is an obstruction can be assessed only after the correspondence between their observations is fixed and independently validated

## The claim

A164, answering the owner's U47: "**apparent disagreement between local
descriptions does not establish a gluing obstruction until the appropriate
transition maps have been specified**". And: "**We should not test whether
two interpretations are identical before specifying how their observational
frames correspond.**"

A173 stated the same proposition twice, once as a ready-to-file claim and
once as a principle: "Different local descriptions do not constitute a
global compatibility obstruction merely because they assign different
values to apparently corresponding observables. ... A genuine obstruction
is the failure of compatible global realization after those
correspondences and the empirical admissibility constraints have been
imposed." A178 §1 made observational correspondence "the hinge of the
manuscript". A203 §13 repeats it: "**A disagreement between local
descriptions cannot be interpreted as a structural incompatibility until
the appropriate observational correspondences have been established.**"
A214 §10 and A218 §6 and Ch9.2 keep it.

A164 sorts disagreements into three classes, and "These categories should
not be conflated":

| Class | A164's test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinate difference | "compatible transition maps reconcile them" | inertial frames ([CASE-tmplyfo2](../cases.d/CASE-tmplyfo2.md)) |
| Structural transformation | "descriptions may remain comparable but no longer equivalent" | literary translation, cross-modal generation |
| Global obstruction | "cannot be jointly extended under the specified compatibility conditions" | sheaf contextuality ([CASE-035](../cases.d/CASE-035.md)) |

A178 §1 lists the same three as "Representational differences that
disappear under appropriate correspondence maps. Transformations that
conserve some relations while altering others. Empirical constraint
systems that do not admit a global realization satisfying all local
requirements."

The order of tests is the claim. First fix the correspondence between the
two descriptions' contexts and outcomes, the context part of a transport
([TERM-008](../terms.d/TERM-008.md)), and check that it is coherent with restriction, as
equivariance ([TERM-004](../terms.d/TERM-004.md)) requires. Then ask whether what is left of
the disagreement is a change of the act or a failure of joint extension.
Without the first step a transport theory cannot tell the three classes
apart. The first two classes are A18's passive and active transformations
([CLAIM-004](CLAIM-004.md)), which return here with a third class and an order.

## The proviso

A correspondence that is fitted to make the disagreement disappear decides
nothing. A173: "Transition maps must satisfy appropriate coherence
requirements and be independently justified or validated. They must not be
chosen solely to eliminate an observed incompatibility." A164: the
correspondences "must be independently anchored and empirically
validated". This is [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s remedy, carried from fidelity to
compatibility: anchor the correspondence to independently measured
distinctions, and evaluate it on tasks other than those it was fitted on.

## What it does not say

- It does not say translation is a change of coordinates. Lorentz maps are
  invertible and form a group; translation correspondences are directed and
  lossy. A164: "unlike Lorentz transformations, arbitrary translation maps
  can collapse distinctions". A178: "unlike changes of inertial
  coordinates, translation can erase distinctions and reorganize
  communicative structure". A203 §13: "Lorentz coordinate transformations
  are invertible symmetries, whereas translation can be directed and lossy."
  This is [CLAIM-035](CLAIM-035.md), and the reason the analogy of [ARG-004](../arguments.d/ARG-004.md) stays
  Rejected.
- It does not say relativity is evidence for the claim. The relativity case
  shows a disagreement with no obstruction once the maps are known. It does
  not show that translation has such maps.
- It does not say that every disagreement some map removes is a mere change
  of description. The map has to be anchored first.
- It does not say pragmatic judgements are contextual ([CLAIM-009](CLAIM-009.md)).

The claim carries a circularity risk, which its second defeater names. The
correspondence τ is the same object whose fit decides fidelity
([CLAIM-005](CLAIM-005.md)). If τ can only be validated by the fidelity verdict it is
used to reach, the order of tests is empty. That is [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) for
correspondences. A164's coordinate difference and structural transformation
are also the two horns of [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md), which the claim does not settle.
