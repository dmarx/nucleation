# TERM-tmpg31ao: measurement naivety: governing by a measure without an adequate account of how the measure relates to what it represents and of what using it to govern does; its forms are the inferences that what is measurable exhausts what matters, that predictive accuracy establishes evaluative adequacy, that improving an indicator improves its referent, that measuring leaves the measured unchanged, that local improvement is systemic improvement, and that choosing an objective makes it legitimate

## Definition

A46 §1, at the owner's U29: "an insufficient appreciation of the
relationship between a measurement, the phenomenon it represents, and the
consequences of using it to govern activity". Its six forms, each with the
entry that holds the corresponding point:

| Form | Mistaken inference | Held in |
|---|---|---|
| Representational | what is measurable exhausts what matters | CLAIM-348 |
| Statistical | predictive accuracy establishes evaluative adequacy | CLAIM-348 |
| Causal | improving the indicator improves its referent | TERM-108 (proxy) |
| Performative | measurement does not transform the measured | CLAIM-402, TERM-108 (reactivity) |
| Organizational | local improvement establishes systemic improvement | CLAIM-060 |
| Normative | selecting an objective establishes its legitimacy | CLAIM-tmpvmd97 |

## Use

In A46's chain, measurement naivety supplies an impoverished account of
success, greedy agency (TERM-tmpxr1ox) pursues it, and institutional
sociopathy (TERM-tmp72sq3) is what the resulting arrangement can become.

## Not to be confused with

Goodhart's law, which concerns what happens once a measure is optimized.
Measurement naivety is prior: the measure can be inadequate before anyone
optimizes it.
