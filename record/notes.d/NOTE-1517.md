# NOTE-1517: Voigt — How hard decisions shape our preferences

## Contribution

It proposes that the choice-induced preference change effect, the rise in
liking for a chosen item and fall for a rejected one after a choice between
items first rated equal, is formed during the decision rather than after it.
The traditional explanation is post-decisional dissonance reduction
(Festinger), with dlPFC adjusting vmPFC or striatal value at re-evaluation
(p. 1). Voigt's alternative: "preferences evolve dynamically as decisions
arise, potentially as a mechanism to prevent stalemate situations in
underdetermined decision scenarios" (p. 2).

## Key insight

When existing values cannot separate two options, the decision system
"extracts new information in the moment of choice via fixation on a
particular item in order to construct new preferences guiding upcoming
choices" (p. 2). The tie is broken by gaze, and gaze writes value.

## Assumptions

- **A serial architecture of five steps** (pp. 2–3, Fig. 1; the text's list
  numbers them "(1) … (2) conflict detection, (2) value updating, (3) …,
  (4)", a typo for five):
  1. value computation, vmPFC (with dlPFC, vStr);
  2. conflict detection, dACC, when the value differential is too small;
  3. value updating, by fixation, with precuneus and dlPFC;
  4. value-based decision, dlPFC, once the differential suffices;
  5. updated value representation, hippocampus, for remembered choices.
- **Fixation duration is causal in value-based choice**, taken from earlier
  work: "Fixation duration plays a causal role in value-guided choice (e.g.,
  Shimojo et al., 2003; Glaholt et al., 2009)" (p. 2), with Krajbich et al.
  2010's evidence-accumulation account.
- **Preference changes do not occur for easy choices**, "but only for hard
  decisions" (p. 2, from Voigt et al. 2019).

## Key results

The article reports no new data. Its empirical basis, as it states it:

- **From Voigt et al. 2019** (p. 2): preference changes "were predicted from
  activity in left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and precuneus while making
  hard decisions"; "the total and first fixation duration for of an item
  was predictive of its choice and, importantly, its subsequent change in
  value (when controlling for choice)"; changes became behaviourally
  relevant "only for choices that were remembered", with hippocampus
  activity. Hard choices took longer and engaged dACC (p. 2).
- **The attentional step** (pp. 2–3): "it is reasonable to assume that
  initially the precuneus … allocates spatial attention to the salient
  stimulus (i.e., bottom up) and forwards this information to the dlPFC",
  which "'holds' choice-relevant information in working memory". So
  "initial bottom-up shifts in spatial attention are explained by
  in-decisional precuneus activity controlling the reconstruction of new
  value information, which is forwarded to the dlPFC" (p. 3).
- **A reinterpretation of dlPFC** (p. 3): its role at re-evaluation in
  earlier studies "might simply reflect the computation of an upcoming
  value-based choice, but not necessarily the update of value itself as it
  was previously proposed".
- **Conclusion** (p. 4): "seemingly self-determined, subjective cognitive
  concepts, such as our preferences, might be emergent consequences from
  the particulars of the decision scenario itself and brain networks
  underlying value-based decisions".

## Claims

| C1 | Choice-induced preference change arises during hard choices, not only after them | moderate | Voigt et al. 2019 (fMRI, eye-tracking), cited; one study |
| C2 | Fixation duration on an item predicts its choice and its later change in value, controlling for choice | moderate | Voigt et al. 2019, cited; correlational |

## Concepts

- **Choice-induced preference change** — the post-choice spreading of
  ratings between items initially rated equal (p. 1).
- **Hard decision** — a choice in which "existing preferences are not
  sufficient to differentiate" the options (p. 1).
- **Endogenous preference formation** — change "in the absence of any
  additional external information about the choice options" (p. 1).

## Connections

- Izuma et al. 2010 and 2015, Mengarelli et al. 2015 (tDCS over left PFC
  diminishing the effect), and Harmon-Jones et al. 2015: the
  dissonance-based, post-decisional account it revises.
- Krajbich, Armel and Rangel 2010 and Shimojo et al. 2003: gaze in
  value-based choice, the "causal role" it imports.
- Voigt, Murawski, Speer and Bode 2019, *J. Neurosci.*: its own evidence.
  Not held by the record.

## Bearing on the record

- **Jennings 2026 ([LIT-1963](../literature.d/LIT-1963.md), [NOTE-1508](NOTE-1508.md), [THEORY-1341](../theory.d/THEORY-1341.md)): the use is
  broadly faithful on the mechanism and unfaithful on two points.**
  - Faithful: vmPFC and ACC judging whether the value difference suffices;
    dlPFC and precuneus raising attention on one item, "allowing for both a
    decision and an updating of values in the memory system" (Jennings
    p. 25) matches steps 2–5. "To prevent stalemate" is Voigt's phrase,
    though Voigt's subject is preference change ("preferences evolve …
    potentially as a mechanism to prevent stalemate", p. 2), not attention.
  - **Bottom-up, not top-down.** Voigt's attentional step is the precuneus
    allocating spatial attention "to the salient stimulus (i.e., bottom
    up)", with dlPFC holding the result in working memory (p. 3). Jennings
    uses dlPFC feedback as the mark of fluid interests and of attention
    proper, as against salience-driven selection by fixed interests
    (manuscript pp. 31–32). On Voigt's model the tie is broken by the
    salient item's capture of attention, which on Jennings's taxonomy is a
    fixed interest at work. That undercuts the step from Voigt to
    [THEORY-1341](../theory.d/THEORY-1341.md)'s criterion.
  - **Model, not demonstration.** Jennings presents it as "a model for how
    such conflict is resolved in the brain" (p. 25), which is fair, but
    [THEORY-1341](../theory.d/THEORY-1341.md)'s "updates the chosen option's value" rests on
    correlations (fixation predicts value change), plus a causal role for
    gaze imported from other studies. Voigt does not show that attention
    updates value; it proposes it, with correlational support.
  - Jennings's question "what determines whether attention falls on one
    option or the other?" (p. 26) has an answer in Voigt: salience,
    bottom up. Voigt's own conclusion is deflationary about the self:
    preferences "might be emergent consequences from the particulars of
    the decision scenario itself" (p. 4), emergence in the weak sense.
- **[CLAIM-540](../claims.d/CLAIM-540.md).** [NOTE-1508](NOTE-1508.md) read the mechanism as a causal update,
  a mixture that changes because of the choice. Voigt's evidence that the
  value changes because of the fixation, not merely with it, is the
  "controlling for choice" analysis of 2019 and imported gaze-manipulation
  results; within this article it is not an intervention.
- **[CLAIM-533](../claims.d/CLAIM-533.md).** If the stalemate is broken by which item captures
  gaze, the outcome is decided by a fluctuation in salience, which is the
  "uncontrolled fluctuations and outside inputs" the claim says
  amplification does not tell apart from the agent's effort.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- An Opinion article with no new data; the model is "tentative".
- Its main empirical support is one study by the author, not read here.
- The precuneus–dlPFC division of labour is assumed ("it is reasonable to
  assume", p. 3) from work on spatial attention and working memory, not
  tested on hard choices.
- The article does not say how the first fixation is determined beyond
  salience, nor what happens when neither item is more salient.

## Open questions

- Does manipulating gaze during choices between equally liked items shift
  later preference, and does disrupting precuneus or dlPFC (e.g. by TMS)
  abolish the in-decision update?
- Is the tie broken by salience or by a goal, i.e. bottom up or top down?
  That is the question on which its use in [THEORY-1341](../theory.d/THEORY-1341.md) turns.
