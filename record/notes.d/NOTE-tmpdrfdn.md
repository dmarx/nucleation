# NOTE-tmpdrfdn: Empathy, Altruism, and Group Identification

## Contribution

It gives the self-other merging hypothesis (SMH) a reading that survives
May's conceptual objection. Merging is group identification: forming a
self-conception as a member of a group that includes the other. It then
draws the consequence for the egoism–altruism dichotomy. Empathy-induced
helping, so understood, fits neither side. The paper proposes splitting
the distinction into an individual level and a group level.

## Key insight

Concern for another's welfare can be non-instrumental and still be
concern for one's own, when the other is part of a "we" one belongs to.
Egoistic helping to relieve one's own distress uses the other's welfare
as a means: my welfare is causally affected by hers but not made of it.
Group-identified helping is the reverse: "Reynie's first-person plural
welfare is constituted by, but is not causally influenced by, Elaine's
third-person singular welfare" (p. 9). Caring for a whole includes caring
for its parts for their own sake. So the act is altruistic toward Elaine
and egoistic toward "us".

## Assumptions

- **Batson's definitions** (p. 2). Altruistic: motivated "with the
  ultimate goal of increasing another's welfare" (Batson 2018, p. 22).
  Empathic concern: "other oriented emotion elicited by and congruent
  with the perceived welfare of someone in need" (2018, p. 29).
- **May's two conditions** (p. 4). An acceptable SMH must be behaviourally
  adequate, predicting and explaining the helping observed, and
  psychologically plausible, positing no unrealistic beliefs or desires.
- **Salice and Miyazono's account of identification** (p. 5): a
  Pushmi-Pullyu representation, descriptive and directive at once, not a
  belief.
- **The minimal group paradigm shows identification motivates pro-group
  behaviour** (p. 6), from trivial shared features such as preferring
  Kandinsky to Klee or over-estimating dots (Tajfel et al. 1971).
- **Neutrality about groups** (p. 5). Groups may rest on common features
  or on shared activities; the interpretation does not choose.

## Key results

- **The debate as the authors set it out** (pp. 2–4).
  - Batson's experiments: Katie Banks (Coke et al. 1978; misattribution of
    arousal) and Elaine (Batson et al. 1981; similarity and ease of
    escape). Stocks et al. (2009) answer the psychological-escape reply.
  - Cialdini et al.'s study, as reported (pp. 3–4). Arizona State
    introductory psychology students imagined a near-stranger, an
    acquaintance, a good friend or their closest family member ("a sibling if possible"),
    evicted from their apartment. They chose among seven levels of help,
    up to letting the person live with them rent-free. They rated
    "oneness" on a seven-point scale and on the IOS scale, plus empathic
    concern, sadness and personal distress. Closeness raised empathic
    concern and oneness, and both predicted helping. "Crucially, when
    oneness is statistically controlled, empathic concern is not
    predictive of helping", while oneness still predicts it with empathic
    concern controlled (p. 4). No statistics are given.
  - Cialdini et al.'s reading, quoted: empathy is a signal of oneness
    (1997, p. 491). SMH "seriously undermines the logic of the
    empathy-altruism hypothesis" by compromising "the distinction between
    selflessness and selfishness" (1997, p. 481). The helping is called
    "nonaltruistic", not egoistic (p. 4).
  - Cialdini et al. deny a physical merging: "What is merged is
    conceptual, not physical" (1997, p. 482, quoted on p. 4).
  - Batson's four versions of SMH (2018; note 4, p. 3): self-other
    identification; including the other in the self (Aron); seeing aspects
    of the self in the other (Cialdini); self and other as interchangeable
    exemplars of a shared group identity (Turner 1987). The authors place
    their reading in the fourth (note 6, p. 5).
  - On Batson et al. (1997): "Batson responded to Cialdini and colleagues
    by conducting experiments that cast doubt on SMH … and by indicating
    the methodological problems … (Batson, 2018, Chapter 10). These
    empirical issues about SMH, however, are outside the scope of this
    paper" (p. 4).
- **May's challenge** (p. 4; May 2011, 2018). Three readings of merging:
  - *peculiar belief*: X believes she exists in two bodies; unrealistic;
  - *indeterminate identity*: personal identities blur; empirically
    unclear, and helping needs Y represented as another person;
  - *property*: X believes some of her properties are in Y's body;
    unrealistic, and still needs an altruistic motive to explain helping.
- **The group identification interpretation** (pp. 5–6). When X
  empathizes with Y, "X group identifies with Y and thereby comes to
  conceive of Y's welfare as being constitutive of X's first-person plural
  (our) welfare" (p. 5).
  - It covers empathy-induced helping only, not all helping.
  - It allows identification without empathy, as in minimal groups.
  - It is adequate because Elaine and Kandinsky studies share a structure:
    participants learn of a similarity and are then offered a chance to
    help (p. 6).
  - It is plausible because it posits a shared social identity, not two
    bodies, and a PPR, not a belief (p. 5).
- **Causal versus constitutive** (pp. 6–7). Empathy may cause
  identification, by highlighting commonalities, which fits Cialdini's
  signal reading. Or identification may be part of empathy. On the
  second, after Salice and Montes Sánchez on pride, Elaine's suffering is
  the *focus* of Reynie's empathy and his social self is its *target*. The
  authors are neutral and find the constitutive reading more interesting.
- **Two objections** (pp. 7–8).
  - *Identification needs a desirable group, for self-esteem.* Reply:
    minimal groups give in-group favouritism with no esteem to gain, and a
    father's shame at his daughter's crime is identification that lowers
    esteem.
  - *Empathy reaches strangers outside any shared group*: the woman in a
    burning building, suffering children far away. Two replies. Either we
    do identify with them, through a salient similarity created by
    empathic affective mirroring. Or the helping is not empathy-induced
    but reasoned, as Bloom argues.
- **A new taxonomy** (pp. 8–11).
  - Empathy-induced, group-identification-driven helping (EGHB) is a kind
    of group-identification-driven helping (GHB). Reynie's helping is
    EGHB; Sticky's in the Kandinsky study is GHB but probably not EGHB.
  - Not egoistic in the usual sense: the motive is not one's first-person
    singular welfare. Self-esteem and reciprocity accounts are rejected.
    An evolutionary function of reciprocity is not a personal aim.
  - Not altruistic in the usual sense either: Elaine's welfare matters
    insofar as it is constitutive of "our" welfare.
  - So: altruistic at the individual level (concern for her welfare, not
    mine) and egoistic at the group level (concern for our welfare, not
    theirs). Salice and Satne's Alba, who does her part in a bridge her
    community decided on, is GHB of the same shape (p. 10, note 11).

## Concepts

- **self-other merging hypothesis (SMH)**: X helps Y out of concern for
  X's own welfare, which through merging includes Y's.
- **group identification**: acquiring a self-conception as a group member,
  shown in first-person plural talk ("we").
- **group identification interpretation**: empathizing with Y involves
  identifying with Y, so that Y's welfare is part of "our" welfare.
- **causal versus constitutive interpretation**: empathy causes
  identification, or identification is part of empathy.
- **focus versus target** (Salice and Montes Sánchez): what an emotion is
  directed at versus the self it is ultimately about.
- **GHB / EGHB**: helping driven by group identification; the subset
  induced by empathy.
- **individual-level versus group-level egoism/altruism**: whose welfare
  motivates (mine or hers), and whose plural welfare it serves (ours or
  theirs).

## Connections

- **Salice and Miyazono ([NOTE-tmpaso81](NOTE-tmpaso81.md)).** Miyazono's earlier paper
  supplies the account of identification. Its PPR, "you are a member of
  us; act as such", is what makes this reading non-doxastic (p. 5). The
  link has a cost. That paper separated *self-transformation* (being one
  of us) from *adopting the we-perspective* (reasoning about our good),
  and the team reasoner ("We intend") from the team benefactor ("I intend
  that we"). This paper's Reynie identifies with Elaine but acts for her,
  not with her. That looks most like a self-transformed team benefactor,
  a combination the earlier paper did not name. An inference.
- **Self-categorization theory ([THEORY-tmpo85ke](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo85ke.md)).** The authors place
  themselves in Batson's fourth version, Turner's interchangeable
  exemplars (note 6), and cite Turner 1982 for identification. But they
  stay neutral on what grounds groups, and their mechanism is similarity
  plus affective mirroring, not metacontrast.
- **Group entity versus category ([TERM-tmpko4rw](../terms.d/TERM-tmpko4rw.md)).** The "group" of
  Reynie and Elaine, and the Kandinsky-lovers, are categories formed by a
  shared property. The authors also allow groups grounded in shared
  activity (p. 5). Their argument does not need the distinction, but a
  test of it would.
- **Cialdini et al. 1997 ([LIT-tmpfxss5](../literature.d/LIT-tmpfxss5.md)).** The paper is a defence of its
  conclusion and reports one of its studies (pp. 3–4).
- **The SEP entry** (read the same day) reports May's objection and the
  numerical-identity argument this paper answers.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)).** This is the closest statement
  of the thesis in the literature the record holds, for the case of
  empathy. It bears on the claim at three points. These are inferences
  unless marked.
  - It gives the thesis a precise form. Collectivist or empathic helping
    is concern for a whole one belongs to, of which the other's welfare is
    a constituent. That is "self-interest on a larger self-identity" in
    the authors' words: egoistic "at the group level".
  - It splits the thesis's verdict. At the individual level the same act
    is altruistic, and concern for the other is non-instrumental (pp.
    8–9). So the thesis is not "no action is altruistic". It is "no
    altruism without an extended self that it also serves". Batson's
    empathy–altruism hypothesis, read at the individual level, is then
    compatible with the thesis (the authors say as much, note 3, p. 3).
  - It moves the dispute with [CLAIM-tmpybc4k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpybc4k.md). If empathy *constitutively*
    involves identification, no experiment can find empathy with
    identification absent, and Batson's controls for merging would have
    to be failed measures of identification. That rescues the thesis only
    by making it unfalsifiable for empathic helping.
- **The triviality objection ([CLAIM-tmprudek](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmprudek.md)).** The paper meets the
  objection's demand in one direction and fails it in the other.
  - Identification can occur without empathy (minimal groups, p. 5), so
    the criterion is not caring under another name.
  - But the paper gives no measure of identification apart from empathy
    and helping. Its reply to the strangers objection (p. 8) shows the
    risk: where empathy reaches a stranger, identification is inferred
    from a similarity the empathy itself creates. On the constitutive
    reading, empathic concern always counts as identification. Then "all
    empathic helping serves an extended self" is true by construction.
- **What happened when Batson controlled for merging.** Not reported: the
  authors say Batson et al. "conducted experiments that cast doubt on
  SMH" and declare the empirical issues out of scope (p. 4).
- **A dated note to [CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md) is proposed:**
  > Note of 2026-10-29: altruistic for her, egoistic for us. Miyazono and
  > Inarimori (NOTE-tmpdrfdn) read Cialdini's "merging" as group
  > identification. In empathizing with someone, one comes to count her
  > welfare as a constituent of "our" welfare, and helping is then
  > altruistic at the individual level and egoistic at the group level.
  > This is the thesis in a precise form, and it leaves Batson's
  > individual-level altruism standing. But on their constitutive reading
  > empathy always involves identification. So the thesis gains content
  > only if identification can be measured where empathy is present and
  > found absent.

## Limitations

- **Conceptual only.** No data. The empirical rebuttal by Batson et al.
  (1997) is explicitly not addressed (p. 4).
- **The adequacy argument is an analogy of procedure.** Elaine's helpers
  and Kandinsky-lovers both learn of a similarity and then help. That
  does not show that the same process explains both. The authors grant
  that empathy matters in one and not the other (note 7).
- **No criterion of identification apart from its effects.** The paper
  never says how to tell, in a given helper, whether identification
  occurred.
- **Cialdini's study is reported without numbers.** The paper's account
  of the oneness measure ("a seven-point scale" of "oneness" plus the IOS
  scale) differs from the SEP's quotation of Cialdini (1997: 484): the
  IOS scale plus a 7-point "we" item. The SEP quotes the original, so it
  is probably the more accurate.
- **Typos.**
  - "X's sorry for Y" for "sorrow" (p. 2). Footnote 2's list numbers "(4)"
    twice.
  - "motivated them reduce" (p. 3); "the father pride" (p. 7); "One might
    think is that" (p. 9).
  - Cialdini's "the distinction between selflessness and selfishness" is
    cited to p. 481 on p. 4 and to p. 482 on p. 9.
  - In the references, "Doris, J. M., Stitch, S." for Stich, with the SEP
    given as published by "Springer"; "Being one of us group
    identification" lacks its stop.

## Open questions

- Can group identification with a specific helped person be measured
  apart from empathic concern, for instance by first-person plural use or
  implicit self–other association, and does it carry the empathy–helping
  link when empathy is held fixed? That is the experiment the paper
  implies and does not run.
- Is the identified-with "group" here a category (shared property) or a
  group entity (shared activity)? Would helping differ between the two?
