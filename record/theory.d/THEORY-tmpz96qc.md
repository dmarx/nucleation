# THEORY-tmpz96qc: Shirt-Ediss, Torelli and colleagues report that, for four DNA origami designs each folded from three scaffold sequences with fully complementary staples under one protocol, the sequence that their energy model scored highest in off-target staple–scaffold, scaffold–scaffold, staple–staple and hairpin binding gave the fewest well-folded AFM objects in every design (0–20% against up to about 66–75%), and they attribute the loss to kinetic trapping and scaffold aggregation

## Source

Shirt-Ediss, Torelli et al. (2026), [LIT-tmp91912](../literature.d/LIT-tmp91912.md); read in [NOTE-tmp6ediz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6ediz.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Design held fixed**: for a 2D triangle and rectangle, a 3D tetrahedron
  and a 6-helix bundle, three scaffold sequences each, with complementary
  staples; "The only factor differentiating variants was the number of
  off-target bindings possible in each case."
- **Selection**: a de Bruijn scaffold, a Pareto-front Lambda region and a
  reverse-front Lambda region, scored on four off-target metrics and
  ranked over pools of 5000.
- **Outcome**: under one fast anneal, the reverse-front variants "nearly
  completely failed to fold into the intended target shapes in the buffer
  conditions used (only 0–20% well-folded AFM yield)"; the best variants
  reached 65% (triangle), 74.8% (tetrahedron) and about 66% (6-helix
  bundle). The Pareto Lambda variant beat the reverse-front one in every
  experiment; the Bonn replication agreed for triangle and rectangle.
- **Failure mode**: the reverse-front variants aggregated, with scaffold
  alone for triangle and rectangle, and in full reactions for three
  shapes.
- **Could have come out otherwise**: all variants could have folded alike,
  as isothermal gels without denaturation suggested, or the ordering could
  have reversed in some shape.

## What this does not say

- That off-target sites cause the failure by a known mechanism: the traps
  are hypothesised; "it is still possible to kinetically trap origami
  folding" is the authors' inference.
- That the score predicts yield: the metrics are "general heuristics for
  high-yield folding (but not absolute measures of it)", and the de Bruijn
  control lost to a Lambda sequence for the rectangle in Bonn.
- That GC content or the synthesis route is excluded as the cause.
- That it holds for large scaffolds, other buffers or other protocols.
