# LIT-1844: Identifying the Underlying Mechanisms of Change During Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT): A Systematic Review of Contemporary Mediation Studies

Stockton, D., Kellett, S., Berrios, R., Sirois, F., Wilkinson, N. & Miles,
G. (2019). *Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapy*, 47(3), 332–362.
doi:10.1017/S1352465818000553.

## Key takeaways

- Of ACT's six processes, acceptance has the most consistent mediation
  evidence, and the only evidence of mediation specific to ACT against
  CBT-type comparisons.
- Defusion and committed action mediate outcomes, but about as much in
  the comparison treatments, so they are not shown to be ACT's own.
- The self as context was never tested as a mediator in 2006–2015.
  Present-moment contact and values were tested once each.
- The methodological complaints of Hayes et al. 2006 still held: small
  samples, self-report, unreliable measures, and the mediator rarely
  shown to change before the outcome.

## Why this is here

[THEORY-050](../theory.d/THEORY-050.md) and [THEORY-051](../theory.d/THEORY-051.md) name Macri and Rogge's 2024 meta-analysis
([LIT-543](LIT-543.md)) as the test that could move them, and it is Unavailable. This
is the earlier systematic review of the same question.
