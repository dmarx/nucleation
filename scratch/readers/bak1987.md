## Summary

A four-page letter proposing that "dynamical systems with spatial degrees
of freedom naturally evolve into a self-organized critical point" (abstract,
p. 381), and that 1/f noise and fractal structure are its signatures. The
argument runs in three steps. First, a qualitative one with coupled damped
pendula: an array relaxed from a strongly unstable start stops at the
first "locally minimally stable" state. In 1D a kick just propagates to the
end and the dynamics is "trivial". In more dimensions a state of all
minimally stable sites would carry any perturbation everywhere, so it cannot
be the attractor. The system stops when the network of minimally stable
sites no longer percolates, and at that point there is no length scale
(p. 382). Second, lifetimes D(t) ~ t^(-α) give a spectrum S(ω) ~ ω^(-2+α)
(Eq. 1). Third, a cellular automaton, the sandpile: z(x,y) → z − 4 and each
neighbour +1 when z exceeds K, with z = 0 on the boundaries. It starts at
z >> K, relaxes until every z < K, and is then probed with single local
perturbations. The results are cluster sizes D(s) ~ s^(-τ), with τ ≈ 0.98
in 2D and 1.35 in 3D, and lifetime exponents α ≈ 0.42 in 2D and 0.90 in 3D,
hence S ~ ω^(-1.58) in 2D and ω^(-1.1) in 3D.

## Contribution

It names and proposes self-organized criticality: a critical point that is
an attractor of the dynamics, reached with no tuned parameter. It gives the
minimal-stability and percolation heuristic for why, and introduces the
sandpile automaton as the model. Numerically it finds power laws in
avalanche ("cluster") sizes and lifetimes in 2D and 3D. It conjectures that
SOC is "the common underlying mechanism" of 1/f noise, fractals and
turbulence.

## Key results (page, verbatim)

1. **The claim of no tuning** (p. 381): "The criticality in our theory is
   fundamentally different from the critical point at phase transitions in
   equilibrium statistical mechanics which can be reached only by tuning of
   a parameter, for instance the temperature. The critical point in the
   dynamical systems studied here is an attractor reached by starting far
   from equilibrium: The scaling properties of the attractor are insensitive
   to the parameters of the model."
2. **The thesis of generality** (p. 381): "We suggest that this
   self-organized criticality is the common underlying mechanism behind the
   phenomena described above" (1/f noise, fractals, turbulence).
3. **1D is trivial** (p. 382): "the system is stable with respect to small
   perturbations in one dimension and the dynamics is trivial."
4. **The mechanism, by percolation** (p. 382, italics in the original):
   "The system will become stable precisely at the point when the network
   of minimally stable states has been broken down to the level where the
   noise signal cannot be communicated through infinite distances. At this
   point there will be no length scale in the problem". It is likened to
   "the critical point at a percolation transition".
5. **Independence of details, asserted** (p. 382): "The arguments are quite
   general and do not depend on the details of the physical system at hand,
   including the details of the local dynamics and the presence of
   impurities".
6. **From lifetimes to the spectrum**, Eq. (1) (p. 382):
   S(ω) = ∫dt tD(t)/(1+(ωt)²) ≈ ω^(-2+α) for D(t) ~ t^(-α).
7. **The sandpile picture** (p. 382): "If the slope is too large, the pile
   is far from equilibrium, and the pile will collapse until the average
   slope reaches a critical value where the system is barely stable with
   respect to small perturbations." The rule: "There are no parameters
   since a shift in K simply shifts z."
8. **Cluster sizes** (p. 383): "power law D(s) ~ s^(-τ), τ ≈ 0.98. The fact
   that the curve is linear over two decades indicates that the system is
   at a critical point with a scaling distribution of clusters." In 3D,
   τ ≈ 1.35. Fig. 2: 50×50 and 20×20×20 arrays, 200 samples each, "The
   data have been coarse grained."
9. **The cutoff is finite size** (p. 383): "The falloff at the largest
   cluster sizes is due to finite-size effects, as we checked by comparing
   simulations for different array sizes." No collapse and no L-dependence
   are shown; the reference is to forthcoming work ("to be published").
10. **Lifetimes**, Eq. (2) (p. 383): with t^(1+γ) ≈ s,
    D(t) = (s/t)D(s(t))ds/dt ≈ t^(-(γ+1)τ+2γ) ≡ t^(-α). Here α ≈ 0.42 (2D)
    and 0.90 (3D), and "These curves are less impressive than the
    corresponding cluster-size curves". This gives S ≈ ω^(-1.58) in 2D and
    ω^(-1.1) in 3D (p. 384).
11. **Hedges in the conclusion** (p. 384): "More numerical work is clearly
    needed to improve accuracy, and to determine the extent to which the
    systems are 'universal,' e.g., how the exponents depend on the physical
    details." The paper "might be *the* underlying concept for temporal and
    spatial scaling in a wide class of dissipative systems with extended
    degrees of freedom."

Checked by the reader: Eq. (2)'s algebra. With s = t^(1+γ) and
D(s) = s^(-τ), (s/t)·s^(-τ)·(1+γ)t^γ ∝ t^(γ − (1+γ)τ + γ), as printed. The
spectra −2+0.42 = −1.58 and −2+0.90 = −1.10 match. γ is not reported, so
α cannot be checked against τ.

## Assumptions

- Local threshold dynamics on a lattice of dimension d ≥ 2 (1D is trivial).
- Fixed (dissipative) boundaries, z = 0. The toppling rule conserves z in
  the bulk (−4, then +1 to each of four neighbours), but the paper does not
  name conservation or boundary loss as a condition.
- Protocol: relax from a supercritical start (z >> K), then probe the
  stopped state with single local perturbations. The paper does not run a
  continuously driven steady state, and the words "slow drive" and
  "separation of timescales" do not appear.
- The perturbation grows within a cluster as t^(1+γ) ≈ s (assumed).
- The response weights are "s/t" (assumed).
- The power law is judged by eye from a coarse-grained log-log plot over two
  decades. There is no fitting method, no error bars and no comparison
  with alternatives.

## Concepts

Self-organized criticality; locally minimally stable state; domino effect
or avalanche; the critical state as an attractor; percolation of minimally
stable clusters; the cellular automaton (sandpile, "hour glass"); toppling
threshold K; fixed boundaries; cluster-size distribution D(s) ~ s^(-τ);
lifetime distribution D(t) ~ t^(-α); 1/f (flicker) noise, with
S(ω) ~ ω^(-2+α); fractals as minimally stable structures; finite-size
cutoff; universality (left open).

## Verdict on how the exchange characterized it

- **Mostly right.** LIT-2992's summary says "a slowly driven threshold
  system organizes itself to a critical state with power-law avalanche
  sizes and 1/f noise, without tuning." The threshold, self-organization,
  power-law avalanches and absence of tuning are all in the paper
  (results 1, 7, 8).
- **"Slowly driven" is the later framing, not the PRL's.** The PRL relaxes
  from z >> K and then probes with local perturbations (p. 382). It never
  states slow drive, timescale separation or conservation as conditions.
  So what QUESTION-508 and A24 §4 attribute to SOC (feedback
  dE/dt = h − ερ(E), separated timescales, conservation) is not in this
  paper.
- **"1/f" is loose, in the paper too.** The measured spectra are
  ω^(-1.58) (2D) and ω^(-1.1) (3D), and the paper itself puts "1/f" in
  quotes for the 2D result (Fig. 3 caption).
- **Newman's paraphrase is faithful.** NOTE-tmpa7yd2 quotes Newman:
  systems "arrange themselves so that they always sit at the critical
  point, no matter what state we start off in". This matches "an attractor
  reached by starting far from equilibrium" (p. 381).
- **A3's test list (CLAIM-803) is broadly true to the paper**
  ("thresholds, cascades, correlations, slow driving, and whether the
  event-size cutoff changes systematically with system size"). The paper
  has thresholds, cascades and a size-dependent falloff (result 9). Slow
  driving is not in it.
- A5's line that power laws can have "nothing to do with ...
  self-organized criticality" is A's, not the paper's. The paper argues the
  reverse for 1/f noise and fractals (result 2).

## Bearing on the named codes

- **QUESTION-508 (Open): qualifies; gives one partial answer and leaves the
  general question open.** BTW's answer is mechanism 4 and result 1: the
  relaxing dynamics stops at the first stable state, which is where
  minimally stable clusters stop percolating. Two things in it bear on the
  question. The answer is a stopping-at-marginal-stability argument by
  analogy with percolation, not A24 §4's feedback balance. And it requires
  d ≥ 2 (result 3), a condition the question's list lacks. The paper
  asserts independence of details (result 5), yet leaves universality open
  (result 11). It states no criterion of the kind the question asks for,
  and does not say that conservation or boundary loss matters.
- **CLAIM-841 (Proposed): contrast, and supports its "What it does not say"
  bullet.** It supports the bullet that SOC is "a route that ends at a
  critical point without outside tuning" (result 1). It runs against the
  thesis's spirit in two ways. It infers criticality from a straight
  log-log line, "linear over two decades indicates that the system is at a
  critical point" (result 8), the inference the claim and its "What would
  show criticality instead" section hold insufficient. And it proposes SOC
  as "the common underlying mechanism" of scaling, turbulence included
  (result 2), where CLAIM-841's table treats turbulence as a separate flux
  route. This is a historical contrast, not a defeater: the paper offers a
  conjecture and simulations, not an argument that the other routes fail.
- **CLAIM-795 (Proposed; grounds LIT-2992): supports A3's addition of the
  sandpile, numerically.** τ ≈ 0.98 (2D) and 1.35 (3D) come without any
  rich-get-richer rule or tuned parameter (results 7, 8). One caution: the
  mechanism is a chain reaction, "ever amplifying since the pendula
  generally are connected with more than two minimally stable pendula"
  (p. 382). That is local amplification, not size-proportional gain, so the
  claim's "no positive feedback" holds only in its rich-get-richer sense.
  None of the paper's exponents is 3/2 (compare CLAIM-815, CASE-303), and
  the paper offers no branching argument.
- **CLAIM-803 (Proposed; grounds LIT-2992): contrast in method; supports
  the cutoff test as a check.** The paper reads mechanism from the shape
  of the distribution (result 8), the practice the claim warns against. It
  does run one of the claim's dynamical checks: the falloff "due to
  finite-size effects, as we checked by comparing simulations for
  different array sizes" (result 9). It shows no data for that check. Its
  call for experiments on the spatial correlation length L(f) of noise
  (p. 384) is a dynamical test of the kind the claim wants. The work is a
  weak ground for this claim. It is cited as the source of the SOC test
  items, and as an example of what the claim criticizes.
- **NOTE-tmpa7yd2 (Newman): consistent.** Newman's attribution checks
  (see the verdict above). Newman illustrates SOC with the forest fire, and
  this paper has none: it has the pendula and the sandpile.

## Limitations

- The evidence is simulations on 50×50, 100×100 and 20×20×20 lattices, over
  two decades, coarse-grained, with no fit and no error estimates. The
  lifetime curves are "less impressive" (result 10).
- The finite-size check is asserted, not shown.
- The protocol is relaxation, then probing. It is not a driven steady
  state.
- The generality (results 2, 5) is argued, not demonstrated. Universality
  is left open (result 11).
- The "1/f" exponents are 1.58 and 1.1. The empirical systems in the first
  paragraph (resistors, the hour glass, the Nile, stars) are not compared
  with the model.
- Here τ is defined on the "number of affected sites" per local
  perturbation. τ ≈ 0.98 < 1 is not normalizable without the cutoff. The
  paper does not remark on this.
