# NOTE-2317: Brascamp, Blake and Knapen, unreportable rivalry switches, read for what removing awareness of a switch removes from fronto-parietal cortex

## Contribution

A rivalry display in which "perception demonstrably switches between these
inputs, yet where switches themselves are so inconspicuous as to become
unreportable, minimizing their executive consequences" (abstract). The
authors use it to ask whether fronto-parietal activity at a switch is its
cause or its consequence, and answer: consequence. In the record's terms,
it is a design that removes differential post-perceptual cognition about a
change while the change still happens.

## Key results

- **Rivalry without noticed switches** (Results, "Unreportable binocular
  rivalry switches"). Each eye sees a field of quasi-randomly moving dots;
  in the "same color" condition the two fields are statistically and
  chromatically identical. Brief motion pulses probe which eye dominates.
  Autocorrelation of dominance across pulses reproduces each observer's
  percept-duration distribution from the conspicuous "different colors"
  condition (15 observers), which is the evidence that rivalry occurs.
- **Detection at chance.** In a dual task, "Based on the sensitivity
  index, d', detection of switches in the same color condition could not
  be distinguished from chance". The authors' conservative reading: "observers detected perceptual switches in the same color condition about 8 times less frequently than in the different colors condition". The Bayes factor
  for d' = 0 is 1.1, so undetectability is not shown, only rarity of
  detection.
- **Fronto-parietal response follows reportability** (Results, "Brain
  responses"). The same-colour switch response in the dorsal and ventral
  attention networks and the right fronto-parietal assembly matched a
  prediction of one-eighth of the conspicuous response, "suggesting that no part of these assemblies' BOLD response reflects a neural cause of perceptual switches";
  mixed models gave Bayes factors of 8.6, 6.2 and 4.9 for no difference
  from that prediction.
- **The replay subtraction overestimates.** The traditional method,
  subtracting the response to replayed (physical) switches, predicts more
  same-colour response than was measured, so it "may thus lead to
  erroneous inferences of causality".
- **Interpretation** (Discussion): "a switch in perception may arise within the visual system, but noticing the change may rely on brain regions dedicated to behavioral responses."

## Assumptions

- That the same-colour condition is ordinary rivalry, inferred from matched
  alternation statistics rather than shown directly in visual cortex.
- Linearity of BOLD, so that a response "proportional to these switches'
  reportability" is the prediction for a pure-consequence region.
- That detecting a switch is the relevant post-perceptual event. Each
  dominant field is still seen; what goes unnoticed is the change.

## Concepts

- **Inconspicuous switch**: a change of the perceived content that the
  observer does not detect. It separates the change from awareness of the
  change, not experience from access.
- **Replayed switch**: a physical change mimicking a perceptual one, used
  as a consequences-only baseline.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md)** (supports, qualified). This is the "Brascamp et al.'s fMRI
  contrast" its promote_when calls "one instance". By the authors' own
  results, the fronto-parietal signal no-report designs would pick up at a
  switch scales with noticing it, which is that account's diagnosis. But
  the promote_when asks for "percept-specific prefrontal content",
  recorded with "electrocorticography or microelectrode arrays"; this
  study measured a univariate switch transient in BOLD. It does not meet
  the promote_when, and the first-hand reading it asked for is now done.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)** (evidence on the no-report kind). The study removes
  report and, more, awareness of the switch. It still cannot tell access
  from experience: each dominant field is seen and available throughout,
  and only the change goes unaccessed. Since the two fields are matched in
  every statistic, there may be no experienced change to be unaccessed.
  So it shows that a perceptual selection can occur with no executive
  signature, not that an experience can.
- **[QUESTION-061](../questions.d/QUESTION-061.md)** (background). The selection happened without reaching
  the regions "dedicated to behavioral responses", so a perceptual event
  was present to one system and, by BOLD, absent from another. It is not
  a content present to one consumer and absent from another; the content
  reached report at every probe.
- **Kapoor et al.** ([LIT-3094](../literature.d/LIT-3094.md), [NOTE-2318](NOTE-2318.md)) cite this design as one of
  the fMRI studies whose null results they set against their recordings.
  The two disagree about prefrontal cortex because they measure different
  things: this one a switch transient with switches unnoticed, that one
  percept-specific spiking with switches noticed.

## Limitations

- Six observers in the fMRI experiments, two of them authors.
- BOLD and univariate contrasts in localiser-defined regions; a
  percept-specific population code in prefrontal cortex would not show.
  Kapoor et al. press this point ([NOTE-2318](NOTE-2318.md)).
- "Undetectable" is not shown (Bayes factor 1.1); the fronto-parietal
  result is a proportionality, not an absence.
- Gap in the argument: the step from a response proportional to
  reportability to "no part of these assemblies' BOLD response reflects a
  neural cause" assumes that the
  cause, if there, would not also scale with conspicuity. The paper does
  not argue for this.
