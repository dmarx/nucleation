# CLAIM-tmpixmhp: Blame supplies provenance: responsibility for a harmful act and responsibility for producing the incapacity through which it was done can come apart and attach to different actors, so blame traced upstream to those who formed or maintained an agent's incapacity grounds holding them accountable where the agent who acted is not culpable

## The claim

The owner's U47 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), 2026-11-17,
after separating blame from accountability ([CLAIM-tmpsk25g](CLAIM-tmpsk25g.md)):

> "blame" gives us provenance for holding upstream actors accountable as well.

JoJo may not be culpable for the values his father raised him in. That
does not end the inquiry into blame: it moves it. Whoever formed the
incapacity through which the wrong was done may be blameworthy for that,
and their blame is what makes them accountable for the harm that followed.
The owner's word is "provenance": blame is how accountability finds its way
upstream.

A110 §3 develops it, crediting the owner ("Your observation about upstream
actors introduces a particularly important concept"). It names the concept
"**the provenance of normative incapacity**", lays out a chain (upstream
formation, maintained mechanism, wrongful action, continuing consequences,
responsibility assessment), and draws the general point:

> **Responsibility for a harmful act and responsibility for producing the incapacity through which that act occurred can diverge.**

A adds two limits that the claim keeps. "The diagram is not intended to
imply that responsibility runs only from the top to the bottom.
Responsibility can arise at several points independently." And "The mere
fact that JoJo learned values from his father does not logically establish
that every later action is the father's fault": the father's blame depends
on "what he did, understood, could foresee, and was obligated to avoid".

## On [QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md): a correction to A's attribution

A110 §3 goes on: [QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md) "asks whether an organization's externally
installed blindness differs normatively from blindness generated
internally", and "Your proposal suggests how to answer that question:
distinguish the origin of the mechanism, its present maintenance, its
operation, and the allocation of remedial duties." **The owner's U47 does not
mention [QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md) or the record.** The bearing is A's reading of the
owner's idea, not something the owner proposed.

The bearing is real, and narrower than A says. [QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md) asks whether an
installed mechanism changes *whose* the organization's counter-normativity
is. This claim does not answer that. It says that whoever installed or
formed the incapacity can be blameworthy for doing so, and accountable on
that ground, whatever is decided about the organization. It is evidence for
[QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md)'s second answer ("Formation too") only as far as the
installer's share is concerned. Hence `bears_on`, not `answers`. The
record's terms for the two histories are [TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md)'s formation provenance
(whose process produced the capacity) and maintenance provenance (whose
process keeps it).

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-tmpsk25g](CLAIM-tmpsk25g.md)** is what this rests on: without the separation of
  blame from accountability, the immediate agent's diminished blame would
  close the question.
- **[CLAIM-tmp0wx69](CLAIM-tmp0wx69.md)** (A110 §7) applies the claim to one actor across time:
  the incapacity that excuses a present decision can be the product of the
  same actor's earlier wrong.
- **[CLAIM-tmpa2l21](CLAIM-tmpa2l21.md)** (from A9 earlier in the exchange) holds second-order
  wrongdoing: structuring another's circumstances so that they commit the
  first-order wrong is itself a wrong. That is the upstream wrong of an
  arranger who pressures a competent agent. This claim covers the upstream
  actor who forms the incapacity, where the agent no longer recognizes the
  wrong.
- **[QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md)** asks what in a will's history would show it manipulated.
  This claim presupposes that some such history can be recorded; it does
  not say how.
- **[THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md)** (Wolf's Reason View, Proposed) locates JoJo's excuse in a
  present incapacity, not in his history. This claim agrees and adds that
  the history is where the blame goes.
- **[CASE-tmpolqas](../cases.d/CASE-tmpolqas.md)** (A110 §11 Case A) is the matched test: identical harm
  and frontline knowledge, different histories of the incapacity.

## What it does not say

- Not that the upstream actor is always to blame, or that blame always
  runs upstream. A formation can be unforeseeable or unavoidable (A110 §7's
  Case A, "Unavoidable limitation").
- Not that tracing provenance discharges the duty to correct. Assigning
  upstream blame does not discharge present remedial duties
  ([CLAIM-tmp4o53g](CLAIM-tmp4o53g.md)).
- Not that provenance is only a matter of blame. Formation and maintenance
  can be traced descriptively ([TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md)); this claim is about the use of
  blame in that tracing.
