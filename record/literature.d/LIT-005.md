# LIT-005: Adding causality to the information-theoretic perspective on individuality

Pierrick Bourrat (2024), *European Journal for Philosophy of Science (vol. 14, no. 1, art. 9)* — DOI-10.1007/s13194-023-00566-1

## Key takeaways

- The information-theoretic individuality measures of Krakauer et al. (2020) are associative, not causal. Replacing the observed past state with an intervened-on one (interventionist, Pearl-style) gives a causal information-theoretic measure of individuality, which the author applies to evolutionary transitions in individuality.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The paper builds on Krakauer et al. (2020), who define an individual as something that propagates information through time and derive information-theoretic measures from that idea. Bourrat identifies shortcomings in those measures, chiefly that they capture association rather than causation. He derives a causal version of the measure and shows how it can be implemented and extended to evolutionary transitions in individuality.
