## What A got wrong, across the works

- **Quotations.** None of the sentences A put in quotation marks occurs in
  any of the twelve papers. Each NOTE records A's as paraphrase.
- **Rival models.** The models A says were outperformed are often weak:
  - Kemp et al. (2010) compared only two simple baselines, by curve shape.
  - Lucas and Griffiths's rivals cannot vary across conditions.
  - In Baker et al., a cue-based heuristic matched the Bayesian model on
    Experiment 1's belief judgements.
  - In Jiang and Lucas, the ablation without transfer won the group-level
    comparison; the transfer account rests on the per-participant count.
- **"Hierarchical".** The word is A's label for Körding et al. and Baker
  et al., not the papers'. A places the hierarchical Gaussian filter
  beside HDDM, as estimation across participants. Its levels are hidden
  states inside one learner, and its parameters are fitted per subject.
  A's own table row, "Hierarchical Bayesian filtering", is the right place.
- **Mechanisms.** Gershman, Blei and Niv's model is a static mixture with
  no "transition". Context there is observed; the latent cause is what is
  inferred.
- **Experiments counted.**
  - Kemp et al. (2007) runs no experiments.
  - Körding et al.'s second "experiment" re-models Wallace et al. (2004).
  - Collins et al. (2014) re-analyses the participants of Collins and
    Frank's Experiment 2.
  - Mathys et al. (2011) fits no human data, and the 2014 paper fits one
    subject.
- **Lake et al.** The caveat that people may not use the same programs is
  A's. The authors suggest people do infer abstract motor programs.
- **Attribution.**
  - A's "Lucas and colleagues" is Lucas and Griffiths.
  - The registries give Kemp et al. 2007's second author as "Andrew
    Perfors"; the byline reads Amy Perfors.
  - The registries list only Mathys for the 2011 paper, which has four
    authors.

## What was left, and why

- **A's framing (§1).** The φ → θᵢ → Dᵢ schema, and the claim that hierarchy
  explains learning from little data and transfer, are the textbook
  framing. They are A's summary, not a finding, and the THEORYs hold what
  each paper shows.
- **A's criteria (§4).** Transfer, non-obvious predictions, comparison
  against simpler alternatives on held-out data, and recovery of
  meaningful latent variables. They are reasonable, but A applied them to
  the papers loosely, as above. They are not filed as a claim: this is the
  first time they turn up.
- **The four-way table and closing distinction (§§3, 5).** Abstract
  knowledge, latent structure, parameter estimation and filtering is a
  usable taxonomy, and the readings support it once the HGF is placed
  correctly. It is left in this entry rather than given a code, until it
  recurs.
- **A's reading list (§5).** It is advice and is not filed.
