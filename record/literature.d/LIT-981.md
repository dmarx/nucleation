# LIT-981: Geometric diffusions as a tool for harmonic analysis and structure definition of data: Diffusion maps

R. R. Coifman, S. Lafon, A. B. Lee, M. Maggioni, B. Nadler, F. Warner, S. W. Zucker (2005), *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 102(21), 7426–7431*.

## Key takeaways

*From the full reading, NOTE-tmpjjdsb (Read), which replaces NOTE-784.*

- **Symmetry is the starting point.** The kernel is required to be
  symmetric, positivity preserving and positive semi-definite. The Markov
  normalization a = k/v is not symmetric, but ã(x, y) = a(x, y)√(v(x)/v(y))
  is, with ã = Σ λ_i² φ_i φ_i (Eqs. 2–3).
- **Diffusion coordinates.** Φ_m(x) = (λ_i^m φ_i(x)) and D_m²(x, y) =
  ã^(m)(x, x) + ã^(m)(y, y) − 2ã^(m)(x, y), so ‖Φ_m(x) − Φ_m(y)‖ = D_m(x, y)
  exactly. This is not the inverse-stationary-weighted distance of the
  companion paper (LIT-1246); the coordinates differ by a factor √π. The
  truncation to j₀ coordinates is stated as D_m² = ‖x̌ − y̌‖²(1 + O(e^{−αm})),
  a relative error the neglected terms do not support in general
  (THEORY-745).
- **Geometry apart from density.** k^(α) = k_ε/(p_ε^α p_ε^α) (Eq. 13), with
  generator Δ − e^{(1−α)U} Δ e^{−(1−α)U} (Eq. 14): the graph Laplacian
  (α = 0), the backward Fokker–Planck operator (α = 1/2) or the
  Laplace–Beltrami operator (α = 1). Theorem 2.1 gives A_ε^{t/ε} =
  e^{−tΔ₀} + O(ε^{1/2}) on bandlimited functions for the α = 1 kernel, if
  each ball of radius √ε holds enough points. Both are stated without
  proof and referred to the companion.
- **"Directed diffusion"** means diffusion along a function's level sets
  through an anisotropic distance. It is not a non-reversible walk.
