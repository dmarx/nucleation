# LIT-tmp73an2: Effects of reward on intrinsic motivation—Negative, neutral, and positive: Comment on Deci, Koestner, and Ryan (1999)

Robert Eisenberger, W. David Pierce and Judy Cameron (1999), *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6):677–691 — DOI-10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.677

## Key takeaways

*From Cameron, Banko and Pierce (2001, [LIT-562](LIT-562.md)), not a reading. Their
account of their own earlier comment.*

- It distinguishes two control groups for the studies Deci et al. call
  performance-contingent. A complete control is told the performance
  objective and given feedback; a partial control is told neither ([LIT-562](LIT-562.md),
  p. 13).
- Comparing reward against each, it found one small difference on free
  choice. For rewards offered for exceeding others, reward against a partial
  control was positive and not significant, and reward against a complete
  control was "significantly positive" ([LIT-562](LIT-562.md), p. 13).
- It examined the procedures Deci et al. pooled as performance-contingent
  and found "very different effects on measures of intrinsic motivation"
  ([LIT-562](LIT-562.md), p. 10).
- An analysis in it "indicates that reward contingencies enhance perceptions
  of autonomy or self-determination", against cognitive evaluation theory's
  prediction ([LIT-562](LIT-562.md), p. 26).

## Standing in the record

Filed Deferred because it was not read. It is `compared_against` Deci,
Koestner and Ryan ([LIT-557](LIT-557.md)), whose meta-analysis it comments on in the same
journal issue. Their reply follows it there and is not in the record.

It matters for [THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md). That account's refutation clause asks for
rewards tied to a performance standard to raise free choice "against a
control group given the same performance feedback". By [LIT-562](LIT-562.md)'s account
this paper reports that comparison for rewards for exceeding others, and
finds it positive. A reading would say whether it does so with enough
studies, and with what effect size, to meet the clause. Until then nothing
should lean on it.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
