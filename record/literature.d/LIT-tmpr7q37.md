# LIT-tmpr7q37: The links between prenatal stress and offspring development and psychopathology: disentangling environmental and inherited influences

Rice, F., Harold, G. T., Boivin, J., van den Bree, M., Hay, D. F. & Thapar, A. (2010). *Psychological Medicine*, 40(2), 335–345.

## Key takeaways

- With egg or embryo donation, the woman who carries the pregnancy is not
  the child's genetic mother, so an association between her prenatal
  stress and the child's outcome cannot run through what she passes on at
  conception. The authors call this "prenatal cross-fostering".
- Late-pregnancy stress went with lower birth weight, shorter gestation
  and more antisocial behaviour in unrelated pairs too. The ADHD link
  appeared only in related pairs. The anxiety link was carried by the
  mother's current symptoms, a postnatal route.
- What the gamete carries, DNA and any epigenetic mark alike, falls on the
  "inherited" side of the comparison. The design cannot tell a germline
  epigenetic effect from a genetic one.
