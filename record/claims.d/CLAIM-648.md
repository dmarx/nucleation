# CLAIM-648: Bickhard gives debugging an endogenous norm

## The claim

The owner's debugger ([CASE-240](../cases.d/CASE-240.md); [CLAIM-649](CLAIM-649.md)) reads a silence
against a profile the designer supplied. Bickhard's account ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md))
has the same structure inside a self-maintaining system:

1. A far-from-equilibrium system that selects its next interaction by how
   it has differentiated its environment implicitly anticipates that the
   interaction will succeed, and "can find the predication false"
   ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)).
2. What it anticipates is fixed by what keeps it going. In
   [CASE-241](../cases.d/CASE-241.md)'s terms, the yield is at least the upkeep.
3. So the system holds an expected response profile that no designer
   wrote into it as a specification. A response that fails to come, or
   falls short, means by its own lights that the interaction failed. That
   is the owner's absence, read by the system that needs the response.
4. That profile and a designer's diverge exactly where the system's
   persistence and the designer's specification diverge. [CASE-241](../cases.d/CASE-241.md)
   gives one case each way: a flag where the system is not at risk (S2),
   and no flag where it dies (S4). On the silent patch (S3) they agree.

## Where it sits

- **It answers A5 §5 on the record's own reading** and bears on
  [QUESTION-128](../questions.d/QUESTION-128.md): norms of function and failure defined from an
  organization's own dynamics.
- **It complements [CLAIM-650](CLAIM-650.md).** That claim locates the false alarm's
  error in a non-factive meaning and, for a designed alarm, finds the
  norm the designer's. This claim offers the system's own anticipation
  as a non-factive meaning that no one designed.
- **Di Paolo ([THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)) and Barandiaran ([THEORY-217](../theory.d/THEORY-217.md))** would ask more:
  a graded norm needs adaptivity, and malfunction is measured against a
  normative field. The construction meets neither. The claim needs only
  Bickhard's all-or-nothing anticipation.

## What it does not say

- That any construction has a norm of its own. [CASE-241](../cases.d/CASE-241.md)'s
  dissolution condition is written by its modeller, as Kolchinsky and
  Wolpert concede of their viability function ([THEORY-1646](../theory.d/THEORY-1646.md)). The
  claim is about self-maintaining systems. The construction shows only
  that the two kinds of profile are different norms, not that a
  simulated one is owned.
- That such a system's failure is experienced. Nothing here bears on
  sentience. U1's question was about meaning without it.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Millikan and the SEP survey, read

<!-- inactive-ok-block: THEORY-2234 THEORY-2235 CLAIM-920 CASE-239 — Proposed; readings and the objection filed for LEAD-018, and the proposed study this claim does not yet cite -->
For [LEAD-018](../leads.d/LEAD-018.md) the record read the selection-based alternative to this
claim's organizational route: Millikan's biosemantics ([THEORY-2234](../theory.d/THEORY-2234.md))
and the SEP survey ([THEORY-2235](../theory.d/THEORY-2235.md)). It conflicts with this claim, and
the objection is filed as [CLAIM-920](CLAIM-920.md) (`objects_to` this claim). In
brief: on the etiological accounts, an anticipation is false by a norm
of the system's own only if selection or learning stabilized it, and a
system with no history has none (the Swampman case). That agrees with
this claim against the designer and narrows it: a profile "set by its
own upkeep" is the system's norm when past upkeep shaped it. [CASE-241](../cases.d/CASE-241.md)'s
forager, whose profile its modeller wrote, has its modeller's norm on
that account, which is this claim's own defeater; [CASE-239](../cases.d/CASE-239.md)'s learner
would have its own. The accounts come apart only for a self-maintaining
system with no history. The status is unchanged.
