# QUESTION-075: Which tasks and which receivers fix the human criterion against which a measure of communicative fidelity is validated?

## Why it is a question

The record's audit of 2026-10-10 found that the criterion in every central
defeat condition is a human judgement with no task and no receiver named.
[CLAIM-244](../claims.d/CLAIM-244.md): "Human judgements of communicative fidelity" ([CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md)),
"Human judgements of shared communicative kind" ([CLAIM-139](../claims.d/CLAIM-139.md)), while the line's
own theses say fidelity is task-relative ([CLAIM-128](../claims.d/CLAIM-128.md)) and relative to the
interpreter ([CLAIM-074](../claims.d/CLAIM-074.md)). [CLAIM-226](../claims.d/CLAIM-226.md): averaging a convenience sample
"measures the act for an unnamed population", and for interlingual cases the
source and target judges are different communities.

The record's replies answer part of it.

- [CLAIM-220](../claims.d/CLAIM-220.md) indexes the manuscript's thesis to a named population and
  stated tasks, and [CLAIM-204](../claims.d/CLAIM-204.md) does the same for the October thesis.
- [CLAIM-240](../claims.d/CLAIM-240.md) says a holistic judgement is well defined as fidelity under
  a population's default task, if the population is named and the weighting
  fixed in advance.
- [CLAIM-217](../claims.d/CLAIM-217.md) makes competent participants' uptake the standard of a kind,
  indexed to a population.
- [CASE-055](../cases.d/CASE-055.md) names populations, tasks and an invariance test for one
  study.

None of these says which tasks and receivers the line's claims are about in
general, as opposed to in one study. Three things are open.

- **Tasks.** Choice of act and choice of continuation are the study's tasks.
  Whether they exhaust what "recover and act upon" means, or stand for a
  wider family, is not argued.
- **Receivers.** Whether the claims are about the target language's ordinary
  readers, about the translation's intended audience, or about competent
  participants who know the standings in play ([CLAIM-217](../claims.d/CLAIM-217.md)), and whether
  these come apart.
- **Across populations.** How fidelity for one population bears on another:
  whether a rendering faithful for one community can be unfaithful for
  another, and what then is the fidelity of the rendering ([CLAIM-074](../claims.d/CLAIM-074.md)).

An answer names, for each central claim, a task family and a receiver
population, and states how a result for one population is carried to
another, or that it is not.

## Note of 2026-10-12: filed positions on main

Since PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) branched, main has filed two positions on whose receiver
counts. [CLAIM-160](../claims.d/CLAIM-160.md) separates the writer's projected receiver, which fixes
intended meaning at the write, from the actual reader, at whose read
fidelity is measured. [CLAIM-171](../claims.d/CLAIM-171.md) objects that the projected receiver is one
more reading. Neither names a task family or a receiver population, so
neither answers this question as posed. [CLAIM-160](../claims.d/CLAIM-160.md)'s projected receiver is
the source-side counterpart of "the translation's intended audience" named
above.
