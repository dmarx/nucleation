# THEORY-tmpicoxo: In Voigt's model of hard decisions, when two options are valued equally the value differential is too small to choose (conflict, dACC), and the tie is broken during the choice by fixation: precuneus allocates spatial attention bottom-up to the salient item and dlPFC holds it in working memory, which both settles the choice and raises that item's value, a change that lasts only if the choice is remembered (hippocampus)

## Source

Voigt (2022), LIT-tmphwtm4, pp. 1–4; read in NOTE-tmpic1x7.

## What was actually shown

Nothing new was measured. The article assembles:

- **From the author's 2019 study**: fixation duration on an item during a
  hard choice "was predictive of its choice and, importantly, its subsequent
  change in value (when controlling for choice)"; dlPFC and precuneus
  activity "was linked with online, trial-by-trial updates of preferences";
  only remembered choices produced lasting change, with hippocampus activity
  (pp. 2–3).
- **From others**: gaze "plays a causal role in value-guided choice"
  (Shimojo et al. 2003; Glaholt et al. 2009); dACC activity with decision
  conflict; dlPFC in working memory and top-down spatial attention,
  precuneus in "early bottom-up selection of spatial attention" (p. 2).
- **The synthesis**: precuneus "allocates spatial attention to the salient
  stimulus (i.e., bottom up) and forwards this information to the dlPFC"
  (p. 3), after which the decision "has transferred from a hard to a
  (relatively) easy one" (p. 3).

Each step could have come out otherwise: preference change could have been
purely post-decisional, or unrelated to fixation once choice is controlled.

## What this does not say

- **That attention causes the value update.** Within the article the link
  is correlational; the causal role of gaze is borrowed from studies of
  ordinary, not tied, choices.
- **That the tie is broken top-down.** The model's attentional step is
  bottom-up capture by the salient item; dlPFC holds what precuneus
  selected. It gives no support to dlPFC feedback as a marker of a
  non-salience-driven "fluid interest" (THEORY-tmptunid).
- **That an agent or subject resolves the tie.** Voigt's conclusion is that
  preferences "might be emergent consequences from the particulars of the
  decision scenario itself and brain networks" (p. 4).
