# NOTE-tmpirf1e: Our ways will not change: Future collective continuity increases present prosocial considerations

## Contribution

The paper carries Hershfield's future-self-continuity model from the
person to the group. Future collective continuity (FCC) is "the degree to
which one believes that different important events starting from the
ingroup's present and ending sometime in the future form a causal and
united temporal essence" (Introduction, "The structure of future
collective continuity"). It has three components, as future-self
continuity does: similarity, vividness and positivity. Three studies of
Italians relate it to attitudes toward foreigners and immigrants, the
third by experiment.

## Key insight

Expecting one's group to stay the same reassures the people most attached
to it, and the reassurance makes them less hostile to outsiders. Past
continuity, which earlier work tied to prejudice, makes the group's past
something to defend. Future continuity tells the attached that what they
value will survive, and lowers "collective angst", fear for the group's
future. The effect runs through attachment: the manipulation's mediated
effects held "for higher, but not lower identifiers" (Study 3, Moderated
mediation models).

## Assumptions

- **Collective continuity follows self-continuity's paths.** The extended
  self literature (Aron et al. 2004; Tropp and Wright 2001) is taken to
  license treating the in-group's future like the person's future self
  (Introduction).
- **The measure.** The Future Self-Continuity Questionnaire (Sokol and
  Serper 2020) with "you" replaced by "Italians" and "10 years" by "50
  years": similarity (4 items, "How similar are Italians now to what they
  will be like 50 years from now?"), vividness (3) and positivity (3), on
  seven-point scales (Study 1, Materials).
- **National identification** is the Roccas et al. (2006) scale's
  attachment ("I love Italy") and glorification subscales merged into one
  "composite of ingroup attachment" (Study 3, Materials).

## Key results

- **Study 1 (N = 202), correlational.** The three-factor structure fit
  (CFI = 0.98, RMSEA = 0.08) and a one-factor model did not. Similarity
  (r = .31), vividness (.21) and positivity (.40) went with beliefs that
  Italians help foreigners. In regression, similarity (b = .37) and
  positivity (b = .36) predicted; vividness did not (Results).
- **Study 2 (N = 200), correlational.** All three components went with
  less modern prejudice toward immigrants (r = −.29 to −.38) and less
  collective angst. Angst mediated similarity's (b = −.06, 95% CI [−.11,
  −.01]) and positivity's relations to prejudice. A manipulation was
  attempted and "was not effective" (footnote 1).
- **Study 3 (N = 252, preregistered).** National identification was
  measured first. Timelapse videos of Italian symbols fifty years on,
  unchanged or transformed, and a writing task listing ways Italians will
  stay the same or change.
  - Manipulation check: similarity 4.50 against 3.48, d = 0.90;
    vividness and positivity unmoved.
  - As preregistered: lower collective angst (d = −0.38) and outgroup
    anxiety (d = −0.46); prejudice (d = −0.21, p = .103) and collective
    action intentions (d = 0.20, p = .113) not significant. With
    political orientation as a covariate, outside the preregistration,
    both became significant (ηp² = .02 each).
  - Moderated mediation (Hayes model 7), which "diverged from our
    preregistration" (footnote 4): the indirect effect through angst was
    significant at +1 SD of identification (on prejudice b = −.75, 95% CI
    [−1.00, −.54]; on collective action b = .90, [.64, 1.20]) and not at
    −1 SD ([−.02, .41] and [−.51, .02]).
  - Identification correlated .14 with FCC similarity and .45 and .42
    with vividness and positivity (Table 3).

## Concepts

- **future collective continuity (FCC)**: the expected sameness, vivid
  imaginability and positive evaluation of one's group's future.
- **past collective continuity (PCC)**: the perceived connectedness of
  the group's past, in Sani et al.'s sense; earlier work links it to
  in-group protection and prejudice.
- **collective angst**: "fear and anxiety related to the ingroup's
  future" (Wohl and Branscombe 2009).

## Bearing on the record

- **LEAD-062: a neighbour of the test, not the test.** It is the one
  experiment the lead's search found that varies a continuity in time
  while measuring identification separately. But three things differ from
  what the lead asks.
  - The continuity is the group's with its own future, not the person's
    with future people or with a legacy.
  - The concern measured is for present immigrants, and the mediator is
    concern for the group's future, which continuity lowers.
  - Identification here is attachment and glorification, which is closer
    to caring than to the self's extent.
- **CLAIM-492: a continuity criterion that comes apart from caring,
  and whose effect depends on it.** FCC similarity correlated .14 with
  attachment and could be moved by manipulation, so it is a criterion of
  a temporal extent that can diverge from caring. But it moved attitudes
  only among the highly attached. On this one study, continuity mattered
  only where caring was already present. That is weak evidence for
  neither side of the thesis: the outcome is not concern for the entity
  whose continuity was varied, and the moderation analysis left the
  preregistration. It bears on what the claim asks for without
  supporting or bounding the claim, so it gets a dated note and no
  edge; no status changes.
- **QUESTION-301 and QUESTION-299: parallel.** A group's expected
  sameness is the collective analogue of Hershfield's future self. Nothing
  here concerns future people, legacy or obligation. An analogy only; no
  edge.
- **CLAIM-491: a contrast in mechanism.** The extended-self reading
  would expect continuity to raise care for what continues. Here it lowered
  fear for the group and, through that, hostility to outsiders. The
  authors' own gloss is strategic: openness may be "a pathway to protect
  the ingroup (e.g., its future reputation and status)" (Discussion). An
  interpretation by the authors, not tested.
- **The design this lead files** (CASE-tmp413kq) borrows its
  measure of continuity from the same questionnaire family and its
  check that continuity and caring are separable.
- **No ML instruction.**

## Limitations

- **Italians only**, recruited online (Study 1 through social media).
- **Self-reports and intentions**; no behaviour.
- **Analytic flexibility.** The preregistered tests of prejudice and
  collective action were not significant; they became so with an added
  covariate. The moderated mediation replaced the preregistered simple
  moderations, which the authors say point the same way (in the
  supplement, unread).
- **The manipulation check is the first mediator**, which the authors
  note, citing Lench et al. 2014; the indirect paths are correlational
  (footnote 5).
- **Effects small to medium**, by the authors' account.
- **Minor errors.** The Introduction cites "Malouida et al., 2021" and the
  Discussion "Malouida et al., 2017" for Maoulida et al. 2021. The Study
  2 abbreviation is "[CSA]" in one place and "[CAS]" in another. The
  Discussion says of Study 2 that FCC "negatively correlated with normative prosocial
  expectations", where the prejudice measure was what it correlated with
  negatively.
