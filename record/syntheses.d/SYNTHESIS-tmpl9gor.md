# SYNTHESIS-tmpl9gor: The flourishing line holds that neither preference satisfaction, the outcome assistance delivers now, nor a capability set at one moment measures how the lives an arrangement leaves open go; that worth is judged fallibly, so institutions may cultivate it but not impose it; and that whether a standard joining capability, worth and non-domination can fail stays open

*An account of the line flourishing-and-meaning, "what makes a life go well for the one living it and what makes it meaningful" (its blurb, ADR-099). It maps every in-force entry of the line and adds nothing (ADR-084). Most of the line was filed from A's turns in the exchange "Analyze technological civilization trap" (2026-11-13), and where a component says "the wording is A's; adopting it is the owner's call", that holds here too.*

## The account

### The standard the line's claims take part in

QUESTION-465 (Open) asks whether A's standard, that an arrangement "succeeds insofar as it widens people's substantive freedom to pursue worthwhile lives without domination", can be stated so that some outcome would count against it, and how its parts are weighed when capability, worth and non-domination conflict. It records that the integration "was audited on 2026-11-13 and admitted by no route of ADR-075", since "its own pluralism absorbs every counterexample", and so is filed as a question, not a claim. It names its parts: Sen's substantive freedom (THEORY-1852), Wolf's worth (THEORY-1836) and non-domination as a threshold for dependence (CLAIM-682, on another line). Its "Bears on" section names CLAIM-674, CLAIM-680, CLAIM-683, CLAIM-676 and CLAIM-675 as "the claims of this line that take part in it", and it calls CLAIM-674 and CLAIM-677's measure narrower sentences "that could fail". Its note of 2026-11-13 maps the exchange's recaps of the standard onto the record: CLAIM-352 for substantive freedom, CLAIM-674 for meaningful engagement, CLAIM-676 for participation, CLAIM-680 for pluralism and humility, TERM-120 (with CLAIM-688, off the line) for restraint, CLAIM-681 for "sovereign time" and CASE-263 for "regressive progress". "The question is unchanged."

CASE-263 (Proposed; stipulated thought experiment, A's, A8 §XII) is the test QUESTION-465 asks for on its first half: an innovation that raises output while raising insecurity and concentrating power, and a medical advance that adds dependence while greatly reducing suffering. A standard passes if it ranks the first below and the second above, "for reasons it states", which "separates the standard from growth and from anti-dependence". The case says what it does not test: how the standard weighs its own parts, since "in both innovations its parts agree", which is QUESTION-465's second half. It bears on QUESTION-465 and names CLAIM-681 as the dashboard's "sovereign time".

Under that standard the line's components answer five sub-questions.

### 1. What capability measures

- **CLAIM-352** (Active; thesis, interpretive; the owner's connection at U11 of the exchange of 2026-10-17, worked out by A11; lines will-organization first). Sen's opportunity aspect is what viable reachability can represent "only through a map from trajectories to valued functionings, which control theory does not supply"; will-organization gives a causal account of part of the process aspect, "not of its fairness or legitimacy". Its version-5 history note brings it to this line as "a claim about what capability needs, the line's subject". Its note of 2026-11-13 says the meaning question after Wolf "is beyond this claim" and that CLAIM-675 rests on it.
- **CLAIM-675** (Active; thesis, demonstrated; A's, A17 §V and A23 §6; lines transient-support and will-organization before this one). Two supports giving the same capabilities now can leave different capabilities later, where capacity is learned by doing and decays with disuse, so support "has to be assessed by the capability trajectory it produces". It rests on CLAIM-352 and CLAIM-605 (off the line), and its derivation is run in CLAIM-605's model (C = 0.156 against 0.7 at t = 5). Its body says the reachable set "itself moves under the support, so the opportunity aspect has to be read along the trajectory", reading CLAIM-352.
- **ARG-105** (Proposed; form evidence; the filer's verdict). Gardner's objection that Sen's preference for capability over achievement is unargued (THEORY-1850) "qualifies the framing" of CLAIM-675 and "leaves its derivation standing", since the two supports diverge in achieved functionings too. Its verdict: "capability trajectory" should be read as "the trajectory of what the party can reach and achieve", and CLAIM-675's tie to Sen through CLAIM-352 "carries Gardner's open question with it".
- **CLAIM-681** (Proposed; thesis, interpretive; A's, A8 §VI.C, A11 §18, A13 §VII.1). Time taken by activities a person "cannot reasonably refuse" limits what they can viably pursue apart from income and rights, so equal resources can leave unequal substantive freedom; "the contrast that matters is between time committed to demanding activity and time surrendered to necessity". It rests on CLAIM-352, applying its opportunity aspect to "a limit A adds and Sen's readings on the record do not name". It uses CLAIM-676 in its example: what assistance can add "depends on the time they have to use it". It names CLAIM-680's line for "a right to meaningful opportunity, not a guarantee of meaningful employment". Its note of 2026-11-18 says what holds it back: no mechanism for the title's "or smaller". It bears on QUESTION-465.

### 2. What satisfying preferences does not show

- **CLAIM-674** (Active; thesis, interpretive; A's, A9 §III and A10 §V; lines distributed-agency second). An arrangement can satisfy preferences more fully and raise reported well-being while narrowing engagement with what is worthwhile, so success at satisfying preferences "does not show that the lives an arrangement leaves open are going better". Grounded in THEORY-1836 (Wolf). Its body says the step to arrangements is A's (ARG-106) and that Marcuse (THEORY-1853) and Illich (THEORY-1861) corroborate "from other premises" (ARG-102). It complements CLAIM-677; it bears on QUESTION-465 and QUESTION-369. It names CLAIM-689 for whether a preference's origin matters and CLAIM-680 for which activities are worthwhile and who may say.
- **ARG-106** (Proposed; form source; the filer's verdict). Wolf's Tanner Lectures carry the premise of CLAIM-674 (fulfilment and meaning come apart), CLAIM-680 (no one in particular has authority over worth) and CLAIM-683 (meaning pursued for its own sake defeats itself), "but not their application to arrangements and institutions, which Wolf disclaims and which is A's". Its note of 2026-11-15 says the scope gap for CLAIM-683 is narrower since that claim was narrowed.
- **ARG-102** (Proposed; form source; the filer's verdict). Marcuse's false needs and Illich's needs that productivity cannot satisfy corroborate CLAIM-674's "separation of satisfaction from how lives go, not a measure of worth", since Marcuse's criterion runs partly on origin, "which CLAIM-689 says cannot decide alone", and Illich's on a level of conviviality. Neither supplies CLAIM-674's standard of worth, "which rests on Wolf (ARG-106)".
- **CLAIM-689** (Active; thesis, interpretive; A's, A9 §III). Whether a desire was socially formed and whether its object is worth desiring are separate questions; "a critique of produced wants cannot rest on their social origin as such", and can ask whether satisfying them engages people with anything worthwhile. Grounded in THEORY-1836. Its body presses Marcuse's criterion (THEORY-1853), noting that ARG-108 calls it contested and "this claim says where"; it sits at ADR-099's boundary with the authenticity line (QUESTION-057, off the line); it names CLAIM-680 for which objects are worthwhile. Its narrowing of 2026-11-18 put the wider clause in CLAIM-753.
- **CLAIM-753** (Proposed; thesis, interpretive; the record's own extrapolation, filed under ADR-047 at CLAIM-689's narrowing). A critique of produced wants "cannot rest on how they were formed, by whatever process, without appeal to the worth of their objects or to their effects". It rests on CLAIM-689. What holds it back is its rival, self-determination theory's introjection (THEORY-047), which is unanswered.
- **QUESTION-369** (Open; lines distributed-agency first). What would show that an experience is a basic psychological need, "something an organism's functioning requires, rather than something that reliably raises reported well-being", when self-determination theory interdefines need and well-being. Its history note brings it to this line as "a question of what makes a life go well". Its note of 2026-11-13 says CLAIM-674 bears on it "from outside self-determination theory": if reported well-being can rise while a life goes worse, effects on reported well-being cannot alone identify what a life requires; "It does not answer the question".

### 3. What assistance leaves the person

- **CLAIM-676** (Active; thesis, interpretive; A's, A9 §IV and §XI). Assistance delivering the same outcome can leave different participation in worthwhile activity, liberating, enabling or displacing, so it "is assessed by the participation it enables or displaces as well as by its outcomes". It complements CLAIM-675: "this one needs no dynamics", comparing now "by participation rather than capability" (its body; CLAIM-675's note of 2026-11-13 says the same from the other side). Vallor (THEORY-1847, THEORY-1856) corroborates. It names CLAIM-680 for which participation people have reason to value, and CLAIM-603 (off the line) as the liberating case. It bears on QUESTION-465.
- **TERM-120** (Proposed; A's, A13 §X). A *value-constituting limit*: a limit on what an agent can control that is part of what makes its relation to something worth having, set apart from limits that produce deprivation (to be overcome) and domination (to be contested). It draws on THEORY-1836 and THEORY-1866 (Rosa through Susen). Its "How it is used" section says CLAIM-676's displacing assistance is assistance that removes a value-constituting limit, and its last clause names CLAIM-680 for who may judge which limits are of which kind.

### 4. Who may judge worth, and what institutions may do with it

- **CLAIM-680** (Proposed; thesis, interpretive; A's, A9 §VII–§VIII and A10 §III, §IX). That some ways of living are more worthwhile, judged fallibly, "licenses institutions to cultivate the conditions in which people can engage with what is worthwhile, not to impose a conception of it". Grounded in THEORY-1836 for the fallibilism; the political boundary is A's (ARG-106). It names CLAIM-683 for what goes wrong when an institution measures meaning. Its note of 2026-11-17 makes it an instance of CLAIM-737 (off the line), and its note of 2026-11-18 says it can rest on that claim once CLAIM-737 is held. It bears on QUESTION-465.
- **CLAIM-683** (Proposed, version 2, narrowed at the owner's request; thesis, interpretive; A's, A9 §XII). When an institution makes measured meaningfulness "a target that shapes participants' incentives or self-understanding", it can induce proxy optimization (TERM-108's form) or self-conscious meaning-seeking (Wolf's paradox, THEORY-1836) that displaces engagement. ARG-106 argues how far Wolf carries it. Its body names protecting time, access and education as "the cultivation CLAIM-680 allows". It bears on QUESTION-465.

### 5. What an arrangement leaves open

- **CLAIM-677** (Proposed; thesis, interpretive; A's, A3, A8, A11 §4, A13 §II; lines distributed-agency first). A technology can become practically compulsory without any rule requiring it, so "the freedom an arrangement leaves is measured by what remains open to a non-adopter, not by what it lets adopters do". Grounded in THEORY-863. Its history note brings it to this line for its measure; "its mechanisms stay with distributed-agency". Its note on this line says CLAIM-674 "complements the measure from the other side", and that Marcuse's objection (ARG-108) "now has a claim on this line to press against: CLAIM-689". Its notes record two qualifications of "without any rule requiring it", from Cayley (THEORY-1862) and Energy and Equity (THEORY-1863, ARG-107), with the title kept.
- **ARG-108** (Proposed; form source; lines distributed-agency first; the filer's verdict). One-Dimensional Man corroborates CLAIM-677's mechanism and "argues against measuring freedom by the options left open, since what can be chosen and what is chosen are shaped as well", an argument of the kind the claim's second defeater describes, without meeting it. Whether the measure should narrow "to a necessary condition of freedom, is the owner's call".
- **QUESTION-462** (Open; lines transient-support second). Whether arrangements can make capability gains self-reinforcing, "the dependency ratchet runs the other way", past what threshold and on what supply. Its summary sets the loop as "the reverse of the ratchet by which adoption narrows alternatives (CLAIM-677)"; it bears on CLAIM-681 ("time, A's third link") and on CLAIM-687 (off the line), and it brings CLAIM-600, CLAIM-605 and CLAIM-673 (off the line) to what an answer needs.

### How the parts join

CLAIM-676 complements CLAIM-675 (its `complements`), and CLAIM-681 uses CLAIM-676 as its example. CLAIM-674 complements CLAIM-677 (its `complements`; CLAIM-677's note). CLAIM-689 is what CLAIM-677's note and ARG-102 set against Marcuse's criterion. CLAIM-680 is named by CLAIM-674, CLAIM-676, CLAIM-681, CLAIM-683, CLAIM-689 and TERM-120 as the place where the judgment of worth, and who may make it, is held. THEORY-1836 grounds CLAIM-674, CLAIM-680, CLAIM-683 and CLAIM-689, and is drawn on by TERM-120; ARG-106 argues the reach of that ground for three of them. QUESTION-465 is borne on by CLAIM-674, CLAIM-676, CLAIM-680, CLAIM-681, CLAIM-683 and CASE-263 (their `bears_on`), and itself names CLAIM-675 and CLAIM-677. QUESTION-369 joins by CLAIM-674 only (its `bears_on` and QUESTION-369's note); QUESTION-462 by CLAIM-677 and CLAIM-681.

## Not stated on the record

- **That the standard follows from the line's claims, or is their conjunction.** QUESTION-465 says the integration was admitted by no route; no entry derives it. The claims that bear on it are narrower sentences, by QUESTION-465's own account, not its parts proved.
- **A non-domination component on this line.** QUESTION-465 names non-domination as one of the standard's three parts, but the entry it cites, CLAIM-682, is on dependence-and-domination, as are CLAIM-687 and CLAIM-688, which its note maps. No in-force entry of this line states what non-domination contributes to how a life goes.
- **A rule for weighing the parts when they conflict.** QUESTION-465 names Sen's partial ordering (THEORY-411) as a candidate and the dashboard as declining to answer; CASE-263 says it does not test this. No entry states such a rule.
- **That CLAIM-677's measure is CLAIM-352's opportunity aspect.** The options left to a non-adopter look like a reachable set, but no entry identifies the two, or says whether the map to valued functionings applies to the non-adopter's options.
- **How CLAIM-677's measure ranks CASE-263's pair.** The case's second innovation adds dependence; no entry says whether it narrows what is left to a non-adopter, or how the measure ranks either innovation.
- **That CLAIM-683's measured meaning and CLAIM-674's satisfied preferences are the same failure.** Both separate a measured or reported quantity from engagement with worth, but no entry joins them.
- **That time under necessity (CLAIM-681) bears on the capability trajectory (CLAIM-675).** CLAIM-681 joins time to assistance through CLAIM-676, not to the trajectory; no entry says time is a condition of capacity kept by doing.
- **That CLAIM-689's separation of origin from worth bears on QUESTION-369.** Both separate what raises reported well-being from what a life needs or is worth, but neither names the other; QUESTION-369's tie to the line is CLAIM-674 alone.
- **That the self-reinforcing loop of QUESTION-462 would be assessed by CLAIM-675's trajectory.** QUESTION-462 cites CLAIM-605 and CLAIM-600 on the transient-support line, not CLAIM-675.
- **That TERM-120's value-constituting limits are what CLAIM-680 forbids institutions to remove or impose.** TERM-120 names CLAIM-680 only for who judges which limit is which; no entry says how cultivation and imposition apply to limits.
