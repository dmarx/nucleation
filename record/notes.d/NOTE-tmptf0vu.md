# NOTE-tmptf0vu: Takola & Schielzeth — Hutchinson's niche for individuals

## Contribution

The authors build an individual-level analogue of Hutchinson's population-level ecological niche. The individualized niche is the range of environmental conditions under which a given individual's expected lifetime reproductive success is at least one. It is chiefly an ecological concept of phenotype–environment match ("niche fit"), and fitness enters as the measure of that fit, not as a driver of selection. They work through four problems that arise when niches are scaled from populations to individuals: individuals are unique and so unreplicated; individual niches change over time; the space has more dimensions because conspecifics become niche axes; and the boundaries are set by expected (not realized) reproductive success, inferred by marginalizing fitness functions. The frame is the behavioural-ecology literature on consistent individual differences ("animal personality").

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction (p. 2): written "from the perspective of empirically working behavioral ecologists". The target is how individual differences contribute to population-level processes, not individual life histories. Inheritance and relative fitness are deliberately left out of the definition.
- "Working definition of the individualized niche" (p. 5): expected lifetime reproductive success ≥ 1 surviving offspring, with each offspring counted 0.5 per parent in outcrossers. The niche is the environment, not the phenotype. The phenotype mediates fit but is no part of the niche (citing Trappes et al. 2021).
- "The question of individual uniqueness" (pp. 6–7, Fig. 1): at the individual level a population hypervolume collapses to a point, the realized individualized niche. The potential individualized niche is a space of unobservable counterfactual outcomes. The partial fix is repeated measures and variance decomposition (repeatabilities) at the population level.
- "The question of time" (pp. 8–11, Figs. 3–5): lifetime niches miss developmental switches (density-triggered long wings, colour change for crypsis in grasshoppers). The paper distinguishes time-slice, prospective and trajectory-based niches.
- "The question of dimensions" (pp. 12–13, Fig. 6): as with Hutchinson, only environmental axes count. Conspecifics add intraspecific dimensions absent from the population niche. Axes are not restricted to fitness-relevant ones.
- "The question of boundaries" and Conclusions (pp. 14–17, Fig. 7): the boundary is the expectation of producing one descendant. The concept is not a rebranding of individual-differences research, because it concerns the environment and phenotype–environment match. The practical challenge is to find the few niche axes that matter.

## Open questions

- It is the natural companion to [LIT-tmpm2i91](../literature.d/LIT-tmpm2i91.md) (Trappes): individualized and potential niches are exactly the ones Trappes excludes from the NCT evolutionary niche. A nucleation note could treat the two together as a small cluster on niche concepts.
- Check how the counterfactual "potential niche" is meant to be estimated. The paper concedes it is unobservable, and the adequacy of the marginalization move (Fig. 7) is the philosophically interesting bit.
- It is more ecology-methodology than philosophy. A reader should decide whether the conceptual contribution (unit and level of a theoretical concept) earns it a note of its own or a line in a niche-concept cluster.
