# CLAIM-731: The cost of imposing a decision and the cost of questioning, suspending or reversing it are separate, so an institution can lower the first while the second stays the same or rises, gaining power over those its decisions affect with no change in its formal authority: friction moves from the exercise of power to its contestation

## The claim

A23 §2 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): a
termination that once needed evidence, HR and a meeting is triggered by a
score. "Management has dramatically reduced the cost of acting." The
employee must appeal, gather documents, find a lawyer and wait: "The
employee's cost of resisting may have increased." So "we should stop
treating friction as a single quantity":

- "**Execution friction:** The difficulty of imposing a decision."
- "**Contestation friction:** The difficulty of questioning, suspending, or
  reversing it."

"An institution's power may therefore increase even without any formal
expansion of its authority. It can act more often, more quickly, and more
cheaply than before, while those subject to its decisions cannot challenge
them at comparable speed or scale." The scale mismatch is structural: A21
§3, "The institution can make ten thousand decisions with relatively little
marginal effort", while each person wrongly decided must challenge one
decision at a time.

**The reform pair** (A23 §4). "**Reform A:** Automatically enroll eligible
people in a public benefit." "**Reform B:** Automatically terminate a
person's benefit when a risk-scoring model flags them as ineligible."
"Both reduce administrative processing costs. But they're not morally
equivalent." A removes friction from exercising a right; B removes it from
imposing a deprivation and leaves the cost of challenge where it was. A's
design rule, "asymmetric procedural design": make entitlements easy to
exercise, make withdrawing them or imposing significant harms "appropriately
demanding", and make challenge "accessible, inexpensive, and effective".
"**The aim is not more friction or less friction in general. It's a more
just distribution of friction.**"

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-462](CLAIM-462.md)** (Active): an arrangement can keep or increase its capacity
  for effective action while its capacity to be corrected degrades,
  because the two are maintained by different relations. This claim names
  one pair of such relations, the institution's cost per decision and the
  affected party's cost per challenge, and how automation moves them
  apart. It rests on [CLAIM-462](CLAIM-462.md) for the separation.
- **[CLAIM-710](CLAIM-710.md)** (the owner's) says that friction is where
  intervention is possible; this says that friction is not one quantity,
  and whose friction is removed decides whose power grows.
- **[CLAIM-727](CLAIM-727.md)** (from the same exchange) separates authority over a
  decision from authority over the conditions of its challenge. This is
  the cost-side counterpart: an institution can keep its authority as it
  was and still change what challenge costs.
- **[CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md)** (exploitation through access granted) is near Reform A's
  other face: access can carry encumbrances. The claim says nothing
  against making access easy.
- A party that can raise the other's cost of contesting while lowering its
  own cost of acting holds part of what [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md) calls a capacity to set
  the terms on which the other can contest.
- [CASE-285](../cases.d/CASE-285.md) proposes a study that varies speed alone.
- **[CLAIM-706](CLAIM-706.md)**, from the first share of the same exchange, is the
  nearest held entry and is distinct: it says that execution impedance,
  independent constraint and intervention opportunity are three properties
  of a step that vary independently, in the assistant's voice. This claim
  is A's and is about two parties' costs, the institution's per decision
  and the affected party's per challenge, and the power that moves when
  they part. The first filing mapped A23 §2 into [CLAIM-710](CLAIM-710.md)'s body and did
  not file it; it is filed here as A's.

## What it does not say

- Not that automation raises contestation friction. A23 §4: automation
  "could help accomplish both goals simultaneously", simplifying
  applications and organizing appeal evidence.
- Not that Reform B is always wrong. A23 §4: "The point is not that A is
  always safe or B is always unjustified. Both require safeguards against
  errors."
