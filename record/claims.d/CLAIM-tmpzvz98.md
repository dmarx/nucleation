# CLAIM-tmpzvz98: If contextuality in signalling data means contextuality that survives treating the signalling as error, the sheaf test's 0.148% is the more defensible figure for the BERT anaphora data and the 71.1% of Contextuality-by-Default 2.0 over-counts, since CbD calls contextual a model that is 99 parts a deterministic signalling model to one part PR box; the two routes then answer different questions, and formal contextuality on such data does not need Contextuality-by-Default

## The objection

LIT-844 says what the correction is for: SF "was used to quantify the
amount of fictitious contextuality contributing to the contextual
fraction due to signalling" (§3a). On this reading, a signalling model is
contextual when its obstruction survives any account of the signalling as
departure from a no-signalling model. A model that is mostly signalling
then does not qualify.

CbD 2.0 does not ask that. Take ε = (0.99, 0.99, 0.99). That is 0.01 of
the PR box mixed with 0.99 of D, the model in which every context is
deterministic and the marginals of each content disagree across its two
contexts. Δ = 1.98 < 2, so CbD 2.0 calls it contextual (CASE-tmpskejj).
In the BERT study, "the majority of the examples are concentrated at the
point (SF, ∆) = (1, 2)", where BERT is nearly certain (LIT-844, §6b and
Fig. 6), and the histogram of Δ "sharply peaks at 2". So the 71.1% is
likely to include many models of this kind, just below Δ = 2. Without
the per-instance data, how many cannot be counted.

On the error reading, then:

- **0.148% is the better figure.** It is a conservative one, as a
  sufficient test's should be.
- **The two routes answer different questions.** CbD asks whether
  same-content variables can be maximally coupled. The sheaf route asks
  whether contextuality survives charging the signalling.
- **CLAIM-037 claims too much.** The sheaf route does not need CbD to
  handle signalling data. It handles them by charging the signalling, as
  LIT-843's causal-past route does in another way.

## Where it is weak

- **The error reading is one choice among several.** In language data
  there is no apparatus whose errors the signalling could be. Signalling
  there is a context effect on a marginal, which is what CbD was built to
  allow.
- **The sheaf test is not exact even for the error reading.** The
  distance reading computed in CASE-tmpskejj, the nearest no-signalling
  model in the max norm, calls ε = (p, p, p) contextual up to at least
  p = 0.5, well past 1/6. So 0.148% under-counts by that measure too.
- **Vallée et al.'s own derivation was not read.** Whether it intends the
  error reading is taken from LIT-844's one sentence.
