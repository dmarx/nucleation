# CLAIM-1433: Heightened meaningfulness can come with a loosened frame or a locked one

*Alternative B. Alternative A is [CLAIM-1426](CLAIM-1426.md).*

## The claim

[THEORY-571](../theory.d/THEORY-571.md) places psychedelic states and depression at opposite ends of one
spectrum. Depression is a landscape of over-deep attractors; psychedelic
states are a flattened one. The relaxed beliefs account behind it says
psychedelics lower the weight of high-level beliefs, so the mind moves more
freely among framings.

Apophany, as [CLAIM-1385](CLAIM-1385.md) reads Conrad, sits at the other end. The
ability to step outside the current frame is lost, and closure comes early
on an unfinished form. It is an over-deep attractor reached suddenly.

Both produce the same felt quality: everything is meaningful, and what is
seen feels known. McGovern and colleagues (2024) call psychedelic insights
that feel true and are not "false insights", and trace their certainty to
what William James called the noetic quality. So the feeling is shared and
the dynamics are opposite. One state opens the frame and the other locks
it.

## How A and B come apart

| | A: same phenomenon | B: opposite dynamics |
|---|---|---|
| An offered alternative frame | refused, as in apophany | taken up more readily |
| Afterwards | delusion if the state does not end | revised beliefs, or false insights that fade |
| The feeling of meaning | the same | the same |

## What it does not say

That psychedelics cannot precipitate psychosis in the predisposed. On B,
that would be a flattened landscape in which a person falls into a deep
attractor they could not have reached before: a frame opened and then
locked.
