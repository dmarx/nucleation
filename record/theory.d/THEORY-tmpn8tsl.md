# THEORY-tmpn8tsl: Bovens and Zouridis find, in two Dutch executive agencies, that ICT turned street-level bureaucracies into system-level ones in which routine cases are decided without human interference and administrative discretion passes from case handlers to the system designers who translate law into algorithms, perfecting legality while raising the problems of controlling the designers and of rigidity in hard cases

## Source

Bovens and Zouridis (2002), Public Administration Review 62(2): 174–184 ([LIT-tmp24k63](../literature.d/LIT-tmp24k63.md)), read in the
authors' manuscript ([NOTE-tmpvxobt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvxobt.md)).

## What was actually shown

Two Dutch cases, described from Zouridis's dissertation: student grants and loans, and traffic fines. In
both, computerization first routinized officials' work ("merely pressed a button to indicate their
acceptance of the computer’s decisions", §3.1) and then removed them: "The street level bureaucrats have
vanished" and "System analysts and software designers are the key actors" (Abstract). A human enters only
"if the citizen should" object (§4). The authors argue that legality is perfected but that the designers'
choices are political and uncontrolled (§5.2) and that rigidity produces a new arbitrariness (§5.3); they
propose informatization review, "hardship clauses", and public access to the decision trees (§6).

Corroborates [CLAIM-tmpuiaop](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpuiaop.md) ("exposes"): the ex ante steps that let others act on a decision before it
took effect (a superior's signature, legal review, a judge) are what the transformation removed, leaving
only ex post objection. Corroborates [CLAIM-462](../claims.d/CLAIM-462.md): execution is perfected while the designers' choices escape
correction.

## What this does not say

No measurement of errors, appeals or outcomes. Scope is large agencies handling formalized legal
transactions, not discretionary services. It does not say intervention requires delay: its remedies are
citizen-triggered and upstream (review of the system, published decision trees). Read in manuscript; the
published text was not compared.
