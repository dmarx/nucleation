# THEORY-1324: In Ryan, Mims and Koestner's experiment, the same expected reward for doing well left less later free-choice engagement when given with controlling feedback than with informational feedback, and in both styles less than no reward with the same feedback: the interpersonal style of a performance-contingent reward changes its effect, and the reward lowers intrinsic motivation even when the information it carries is held fixed

## Source

Ryan, Mims and Koestner (1983), [LIT-1970](../literature.d/LIT-1970.md), *JPSP* 45, 736–750, pp.
744–748; read in [NOTE-1522](../notes.d/NOTE-1522.md).

## What was actually shown

**Design** (pp. 744–745). 96 students, randomly assigned, n = 16 per cell.
Rewarded subjects were promised $3 "if you do well" (informational) or "if
you perform up to our standards" (controlling). All feedback groups got
the same positive feedback after each puzzle, with "just as you should"
added in the controlling style. Unrewarded groups got the same feedback in
the same two styles. A no-feedback, no-reward group and a no-feedback,
task-contingent group completed the design.

**Free choice, seconds of 360** (Table 1, p. 746): informational feedback,
no reward 282.6, reward 229.4; controlling feedback, no reward 195.8,
reward 117.3; no feedback, no reward 164.1, task-contingent 113.4.

**Tests** (ANCOVA, initial interest covariate). Rewarded vs
feedback-matched unrewarded, F(1, 89) = 4.34, p < .05. Informational vs
controlling performance-contingent reward, F(1, 89) = 4.25, p < .05.
Controlling vs informational across the four feedback cells, F(1, 89) =
6.11, p < .02 (pp. 746–747).

**Checked.** From Table 1, with a pooled within-cell SD of 143.5 and no
covariate: framing among the rewarded F ≈ 4.88 (d ≈ 0.78); reward vs
matched controls F ≈ 3.37 (p ≈ .07; d ≈ −0.37 informational, −0.55
controlling). The reported tests agree with the table; the reward contrast
needs the covariate to reach p < .05.

**The manipulation reached felt pressure.** Pressure–tension 3.61
controlling vs 2.91 informational, F(1, 90) = 9.19, p < .005 (p. 748).

This could have come out otherwise: an informationally given reward could
have matched or beaten the unrewarded group with the same feedback, as the
reviewed studies of Karniol and Ross and Rosenfield et al. found against
positive-feedback controls (p. 741).

## What this does not say

- **That felt control mediates.** No mediation analysis; the pressure
  effect is reported for the information–control dimension across cells.
- **That it generalizes.** One task, one session, 16 per cell, college
  students; the self-report interest measure did not reach significance
  (p. 748).
- **Anything about rewards tied to a stated score or to beating others.**
  The criterion was "do well", unstated; [THEORY-154](THEORY-154.md)'s distinction is not
  tested.
