# CLAIM-tmpcrdhj: The conditions an effective description is commonly asked to meet are not of one kind: predictive sufficiency and approximation fidelity are adequacy relative to a declared target, metric and horizon; tractability restricts the operations under which adequacy is assessed; dynamical independence and memory closure are relations of the representation to the system, memory relative to a chosen projection; so a matrix over them as independent binary axes conflates kinds

## The claim

The owner's leave-one-out design ([QUESTION-tmp1nqby](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp1nqby.md)) treated five
"virtues" of an effective description as binary axes. At U4 the owner
noticed that working through it "has potentially already altered our
characterization of those "virtues"". A4: "Notice that \(P\) and \(A\) look
increasingly like **evaluation criteria**, while \(D\) and \(M\) describe
different aspects of **system–representation structure**. \(C\) describes
**resource accessibility**. These may not be five virtues of the same
logical kind." A5 took it further: approximation fidelity "is not
necessarily an independent property alongside predictive sufficiency. It
may specify the *degree* to which a prediction or dynamical relation is
preserved. Computational tractability need not be another virtue of the
same logical type. It can instead restrict the operations over which
preservation is evaluated." So A5 replaced them with one relation,
Adequate(T; 𝒬, ℐ, ℬ, ε, τ), which is the regime [TERM-tmpdxajb](../terms.d/TERM-tmpdxajb.md).

The kinds, with the record's support:

- **Adequacy for a target.** Predictive sufficiency is sufficiency for a
  declared future, and what is sufficient for one target need not be for
  another ([CLAIM-152](CLAIM-152.md)). Approximation fidelity is a degree in a declared
  metric over a declared horizon. [CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md): "adequacy is fixed by the
  evaluative target".
- **A restriction on operations.** Tractability is relative to a budget
  and a class of procedures. [CLAIM-304](CLAIM-304.md)'s distinction is recoverable in
  principle and infeasible to recover: the same representation passes or
  fails with the budget.
- **Relations of the representation to the system.** Dynamical
  independence compares the coarse variable's own past with the micro past
  for predicting its future ([THEORY-820](../theory.d/THEORY-820.md), [THEORY-733](../theory.d/THEORY-733.md)). Memory closure is a
  property of the coarse variable's own law ([CLAIM-397](CLAIM-397.md)) under a chosen
  projection. A5 wrote that "Memory closure and dynamical autonomy remain
  genuine structural conditions". That is not quite so for memory:
  different projections give different splits into Markov term and
  memory ([THEORY-752](../theory.d/THEORY-752.md)). [QUESTION-222](../questions.d/QUESTION-222.md) keeps the caution that the split into
  drift, memory and noise depends on the projection, so "an arbitrary norm
  of the memory kernel is not, by itself, a representation-invariant
  measure of emergence"; [CLAIM-397](CLAIM-397.md) is "Not about other projections".

So of the five, only dynamical independence needs nothing declared beyond
the micro state and the coarse-graining. A matrix over the five as binary
axes mixes a degree, a restriction and three relations, and a cell can be
filled or emptied by moving a parameter rather than by a fact about the
system. That is A3's own warning, "we could make an ablation succeed simply
by moving the goalposts", applied to the axes.

## What it does not say

- **Not that the five reduce to three.** A4: "This is not necessarily a
  reduction from five properties to three. Rather, it suggests that our
  initial coordinates were mixing different kinds of questions."
- **Not that the structural pair is free of choices.** Dependence needs the
  micro state named, and memory the projection.
- **Not that the leave-one-out question is empty.** Once the regime is
  fixed, each cell is a fact about a model class, and [QUESTION-tmp1nqby](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp1nqby.md)
  asks for them.
