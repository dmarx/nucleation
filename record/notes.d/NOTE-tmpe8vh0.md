# NOTE-tmpe8vh0: Haidt 2001, the social intuitionist model

## Contribution

- A model of moral judgment in which intuition is the default cause and
  reasoning the usual consequence (Figure 2), set against rationalist
  models (Kohlberg, Turiel) in which reasoning causes judgment (Figure 1).
- Four arguments against the causal primacy of moral reasoning (pp. 8–16).
- An account of where intuitions come from: partly innate, primate-derived
  and externalized in development; selected and tuned by culture through
  custom complexes and peer socialization in late childhood (pp. 17–22).
- Three proposed tests (p. 23): interfere with reasoning, vary the
  ecological validity of interviews, and look for consilience.

## Key insight

Moral reasoning is a lawyer, not a judge. People feel that something is
wrong, then search for reasons, and when the reasons are knocked down they
keep the judgment ("I don't know, I can't explain it, I just know it's
wrong"). Reasoning matters mainly socially: it changes other people's minds
by triggering new intuitions in them.

## Assumptions

- **Definitions (pp. 5–7).**
  - *Moral judgment*: "evaluations (good versus bad) of the actions or
    character of a person that are made with respect to a set of virtues
    held by a culture or subculture to be obligatory". The definition is
    left broad on purpose.
  - *Moral reasoning*: "conscious mental activity that consists of
    transforming given information about people in order to reach a moral
    judgment".
  - *Moral intuition*: "the sudden appearance in consciousness of a moral
    judgment, including an affective valence (good-bad, like-dislike),
    without any conscious awareness of having gone through steps of
    search, weighing evidence, or inferring a conclusion".
- **Dual process.** Intuition and reasoning are both cognition. Intuition
  is fast and its process inaccessible; reasoning is slow and partly
  conscious (Table 1).
- **Scope of the claim.** Descriptive only; the paper makes no claim about
  how judgments ought to be made (p. 3).

## Key results

- **The six links (pp. 7–8).** 1 intuitive judgment; 2 post-hoc reasoning;
  3 reasoned persuasion (one person's reasons trigger another's
  intuitions); 4 social persuasion (others' judgments shift one's own
  directly); 5 reasoned judgment (reasoning overrides intuition, rare); 6
  private reflection (role-taking triggers a competing intuition). Moral
  judgment is said to consist "primarily of links 1 through 4".
- **Four problems for rationalism.**
  1. *Dual process* (pp. 8–10): affective evaluation is fast and
     pervasive; impressions form from thin slices.
  2. *Motivated reasoning* (pp. 10–12): relatedness and coherence motives
     bias the search for evidence; people are "intuitive lawyers".
  3. *Post hoc* (pp. 12–14): people confabulate reasons for behaviour
     (Nisbett & Wilson); this yields the "wag-the-dog" and
     "wag-the-other-dog's-tail" illusions.
  4. *Action* (pp. 14–16): moral reasoning predicts moral behaviour weakly
     once IQ is partialled; psychopaths and VMPFC patients reason intact
     and act badly; empathy drives helping.
- **Harmless wrongs (p. 5).** In Haidt, Koller and Dias (1993), Americans
  and Brazilians condemned harmless offensive acts (eating one's dead dog,
  cleaning a toilet with the flag, masturbating with a chicken carcass then
  eating it), and affective reactions predicted judgments better than
  claims of harm. Haidt and Hersh found the same for sexual morality among
  liberals and conservatives, with "moral dumbfounding".
- **Manipulated intuition (p. 17).** Wheatley and Haidt: a hypnotically
  induced pang of disgust attached to a word raised both disgust and
  condemnation of stories containing that word.
- **Origins (pp. 17–19).** Primate prescriptive norms (de Waal); three of
  Fiske's four relational models have primate parallels (communal sharing
  with kin care, authority ranking, equality matching with Trivers'
  reciprocity); market pricing is uniquely human. The human novelty is
  third-party norm enforcement. "Once morality is located in a group's
  efforts to solve cooperation and commitment problems (Darwin; Frank)",
  interpersonal influence is required (pp. 18–19).
- **Development (pp. 19–22).** Fiske's models externalize in sequence;
  cultures keep some intuitions and lose others, like phonemes; custom
  complexes (purity in Orissa) and peers in a sensitive period of roughly
  ages 9–15 (Minoura) do the tuning.

## Concepts

- **social intuitionist model (SIM)** — the six-link model above.
- **moral dumbfounding** — stuttering, laughter and surprise at being
  unable to find reasons, without giving up the judgment (p. 5).
- **a priori moral theories** — "a pool of culturally supplied norms for
  evaluating and criticizing the behavior of others", from which post-hoc
  reasons are drawn (p. 13).
- **custom complex** — a customary practice together with the beliefs,
  values, sanctions and motives attached to it (Whiting & Child; Shweder).
- **somatic markers** — Damasio's body-state signals that tag options with
  feeling; the paper's proposed mechanism for intuition (p. 16).

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md), Bloom's objection.** Bloom says morality-as-cooperation says
  nothing about proximate mechanisms. This paper is one candidate mechanism,
  and nothing in it contradicts a cooperative ultimate function.
- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md), C3 and the open question.** [NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md) asks how often
  non-cooperative domains (purity, sex, harm) are moralized. This paper's
  reported studies say harmless sexual and purity violations are condemned
  in the United States and Brazil. That is evidence those domains are
  moralized, not evidence about why.

## Limitations

- The evidence is mostly about judgment in general, carried over to moral
  judgment by argument. The paper says so.
- The core studies are the author's own, reported briefly. Two were
  unpublished or in press at the time (Haidt, Bjorklund & Murphy 2000;
  Haidt & Hersh).
- Links 5 and 6 make the model hard to falsify: any case of reasoning
  changing a judgment can be assigned to them, and the model only says they
  are "rare".
- The "action problem" argues from correlations and clinical cases, not
  experiments.

## Open questions

- How often do links 5 and 6 occur outside interviews? The paper's own
  proposed test is ecological: real stories, known people, others present.
- Does the harmless-wrong effect survive when perceived harm is measured
  rather than stipulated? Dyadic morality ([LIT-tmp0aaqg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0aaqg.md)) says it does not.
