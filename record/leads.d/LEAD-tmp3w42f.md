# LEAD-tmp3w42f: Put a claimant's runway and a capacity learned by doing in one model, so that the cost of contesting and the cost of exit move together as substitutive supply erodes the claimant's capacity

## The gap

CASE-287: a claimant with 50 to spend at 10 a month "nets 60 if judgment
takes four months and nothing if the defendant can stretch it to seven",
so the cost of contesting can remove the remedy with the merits
unchanged (CLAIM-748). CLAIM-605 and CLAIM-tmp6nzs1: a party's capacity,
and with it the fallback its terms are bargained against, decays under
substitutive supply, with a deadline after which ending the supply does
not restore it. The two are held apart. A party that depends on its
supplier both to exit and to fund a challenge faces both at once, and
the article's J9.6 is the join not made.

## What it would take

1. **One state.** P's capacity C(t) as in CLAIM-605, and P's runway, its
   budget for contesting, funded from its stand-alone output, so that
   the runway shrinks as C decays.
2. **Two routes for P.** Exit, valued as in CLAIM-tmp6nzs1, and contest,
   valued as in CASE-287 with a constant hazard of relief.
3. **The question.** Whether the time at which contesting stops being
   affordable comes before or after CLAIM-605's deadline, and whether
   the supplier's ability to stretch the contest moves it.
4. **Controls.** Capacity-first supply; a runway funded from outside
   (legal aid) that does not depend on C.

## What would count

If contest becomes unaffordable before the deadline, the cost of
contesting forecloses the remedy while exit could still be restored, and
the two controls on the supplier (over exit, over contest) are one
control in the dynamic case. If after, the deadline governs both. Either
way CLAIM-748's siege gets a runway that the dependence itself shortens,
and CLAIM-731's separation of the cost of imposing from the cost of
questioning gets a dynamic case.
