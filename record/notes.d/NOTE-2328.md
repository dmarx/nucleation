# NOTE-2328: Karjus, Blythe, Kirby and Smith 2020 — Communicative need modulates competition in language change

## Contribution

A language-agnostic measure of where a rising word's frequency comes from,
and a test across five corpora that this depends on whether the word's
topic is itself rising. Before it, lexical competition was argued from
single cases (airplane against aeroplane); after it there is a systematic,
if weak, regularity: words in stable topics displace near neighbours, words
in rising topics do not.

## Key results

- **The measure (§2.3).** Normalised frequencies sum to one, so "the
  increase of one word is matched by or 'equalized' by the sum of
  decrease(s) of some other word(s)". Neighbours are ordered by cosine
  distance in an LSA space, normalised by the nearest neighbour's distance;
  the "equalization range" is where the cumulated decreases match the
  increase. A range of 0 means the nearest neighbour gave it all up.
- **The proxy for need (§2.4).** "Advection": the weighted mean log
  frequency change of the target's top 75 PPMI context words, which are
  removed from the neighbour list "to avoid autocorrelation".
- **Targets (§2.2).** ln(f_t2/f_t1) ≥ 2, f_t2 ≥ 200 per million, used in at
  least 80% of t2's time units, no outlying peaks; on average 270 per
  corpus (COHA 240, DTA 489, ERC 274, SYN2006PUB 257, Twitter 97).
- **Result (§3).** "In all models, advection is a significant predictor
  (p < 0.05) for the response variable of equalization range", describing
  "a moderate amount of variance (up to 11%)" over the controls; full-model
  R² between 0.17 and 0.25.
- **Cases.** airplane in 1916 to 1925: "The total increase of airplane is
  almost matched by the decrease of its nearest semantic neighbor,
  aeroplane" (Fig. 3); famed, famous and distinguished "all increase in
  tandem" (Fig. 1); funding "encroached the semantic field of
  appropriation" in the 1970s (§3).
- **No extinctions (§4.2).** Cases where a target's rise brings "the
  complete disappearance of a neighboring one, at least within the
  timespan of a generation, are almost non-existent".
- **Losers (Appendix).** Run on falling words in COHA, "a significant but
  even smaller correlation".

## Assumptions

- A word's meaning is one vector from co-occurrence (LSA); synonymy,
  antonymy, hyponymy and association collapse into one similarity.
- Corpus frequency change reflects change in use by a population, and a
  topic's frequency change reflects change in communicative need.
- Two adjacent time spans (10 years, 5 years or 30 days) capture the
  change; binning is chosen per word.

## Concepts

- **Equalization range**: the normalised semantic distance from a rising
  word within which neighbours' frequency losses sum to its gain.
- **Advection**: the mean log frequency change of a word's topic,
  the proxy for change in communicative need.
- **Competition between topics**: a rising word offset by falls in
  unrelated words, read as one topic gaining at another's expense.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md) (Saussure's value), promote_when.** The nearest study found
  in scale and design: an addition (a word that newly enters or spreads)
  and a measure of whether the response falls on the word's near
  neighbours or on words far from it, which is a within-field against
  outside-field comparison built into the measure. It does not meet the
  promote_when, for three reasons. The response measured is frequency, not
  "the use and interpretation of the neighbouring terms"; the paper itself
  names a neighbour that "might change meaning and continue to survive in
  another function" as uncontrolled. The "field" is a distributional
  neighbourhood, not a lexical field in the structuralist sense. And the
  dependence on topic advection says the neighbours yield only when the
  topic is stable. Bearing: evidence of the kind asked for, on the wrong
  quantity.
- **[CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md).** Its defeat condition's second clause asks for "a term whose
  range of use and interpretation stays fixed when a coexisting term of its
  field is added". famed and famous rising together is not that: both
  frequencies rose, and whether famous's range narrowed is not measured.
  Neither supports nor meets the defeat condition. A parallel only.
- **[CLAIM-242](../claims.d/CLAIM-242.md).** No bearing beyond background: the paper is about
  frequency in corpora, not utterance contrast.

## Limitations

- Correlational, as the authors say; the effect is small and the plot
  scatters widely.
- Frequency is the only response. Value, range of use and interpretation
  are not measured, and polysemy is collapsed into one vector.
- Removing topic words from the neighbour list removes some of the
  closest competitors (balloon for airplane), as the authors note.
- A gap in its own argument: that topic frequency change measures
  communicative need is assumed, not tested here; it rests on the unread
  Karjus et al. 2020a.
