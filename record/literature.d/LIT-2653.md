# LIT-2653: Probing Classifiers are Unreliable for Concept Removal and Detection

Kumar, A., Tan, C. & Sharma, A. (2022). Probing Classifiers are Unreliable for Concept Removal and Detection. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* 35: 17994–18008. arXiv:2207.04153.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1985](../notes.d/NOTE-1985.md).*

- **The headline** (abstract): such methods "are unable to remove the
  concepts entirely, and in some cases may fail severely by destroying all
  task-relevant features."
- **The mechanism** (Lemma 3.1): a max-margin probe for the concept "will
  use spurious features", main-task features correlated with it, even
  when the concept's own features suffice.
- **INLP's first step** (Theorem 3.2): "will corrupt the main-task
  features and this corruption is non-invertible with subsequent
  projection steps of INLP."
- **A clean classifier collapses** (§4.2): "for all values of κ, the
  main-task classifier's accuracy eventually goes to 50% random guess
  accuracy".

## Standing in the record

Filed from A's turn on concept erasure, which says that probe-based
removal can target correlated features, fail to remove the concept
completely, or damage useful information (paraphrased; the exchange was
not rechecked by the filer). The reader found all three failure modes in
the paper, with one slip in A's wording: the features the probe wrongly
targets are main-task features, relevant to the task and irrelevant only
to the concept, which is why removing them does harm. It criticizes INLP
([LIT-2619](LIT-2619.md)) and amnesic probing ([LIT-2621](LIT-2621.md)) directly.
[THEORY-1886](../theory.d/THEORY-1886.md) carries its central account.
