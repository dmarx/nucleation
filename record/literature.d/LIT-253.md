# LIT-253: When Does Closeness in Distribution Imply Representational Similarity? An Identifiability Perspective

Beatrix M. G. Nielsen, Emanuele Marconato, Andrea Dittadi, Luigi Gresele (2025), *NeurIPS 2025 (39th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems), per the arXiv v2 PDF footer; first appeared as arXiv preprint* — [ARXIV-2506.03784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03784)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-738](../notes.d/NOTE-738.md).*

- **Exact identification (Thm 2.2, a prior result restated).** For
  p(y|x) ∝ exp(f(x)ᵀg(y)) under a diversity condition, equal distributions
  give f(x) = Af′(x) and g₀(y) = A⁻ᵀg′₀(y), with A invertible. The
  embedding is fixed up to GL(M), and the unembedding up to the paired
  A⁻ᵀ and a translation.
- **Not stable under KL (Thm 3.1, Cor. 3.2).** Hold the embeddings fixed
  and let the unembedding norm ρ grow. Both softmaxes saturate on the
  same top label, so the KL divergence between the models goes to zero,
  and both models' loss on deterministic labels goes to zero.
  - The construction permutes the other labels, so the embeddings are not
    related by any continuous map, let alone a linear one.
  - How far apart they are is shown numerically: mean canonical
    correlation ≈ 0.001 while d_KL falls from 0.89 to 0.0001 (Table 1).
- **Stable under another distance (Thm 4.7).** The log-likelihood variance
  distance compares standardized log-probability differences with equal
  weight, not weighted by probability. d_LLV ≤ ε implies d_f,g ≤ 2Mε. The
  bound is non-vacuous only for ε < 1/(2M) and needs all labels but one.
- **The representational measure.** d_f,g is built on the log-odds
  coordinates Lᵀf(x), which are identified. It is constant on each
  equivalence class and vanishes up to rotation and per-coordinate
  scaling. Its core, m_SVD, equals the normalized Bures similarity of
  [LIT-250](LIT-250.md) on those standardized coordinates. That identity is the
  reader's, not the paper's.
- **Experiments are small.** They use 2- to 5-dimensional representations.
  - One CIFAR-10 pair shows permuted label clusters at similar test loss.
    Its KL is about 1.1 nats, so it is not small.
  - On a synthetic 2-D task, wider networks give closer distributions and
    more similar representations.
- **Scope.** Final embedding and unembedding only. The paper says hidden
  layers need identifiability results that do not exist yet.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 while pursuing, at the owner's request, the connection
*GNS, unitary equivalence and the convergence of representations* (see the
curation entry of that date). Read in full on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-738](../notes.d/NOTE-738.md).

It is held as the exact statement, for language-model-style softmax
outputs, that the distribution determines the representation only up to a
symmetry group, here GL(M) with paired A and A⁻ᵀ, not the orthogonal group.
It is also held as the proof that how stable this determination is depends
on the divergence. [THEORY-002](../theory.d/THEORY-002.md) cites it for the symmetry group and for the
KL negative result. [NOTE-738](../notes.d/NOTE-738.md) finds that [THEORY-002](../theory.d/THEORY-002.md) leaves out the
positive result (Thm 4.7), and that the paper's scope, the output layer
only, is narrower than [THEORY-002](../theory.d/THEORY-002.md)'s use of it for the Platonic hypothesis.
[THEORY-018](../theory.d/THEORY-018.md) cites it as starting from the same symmetry as Park, Choe and
Veitch ([LIT-304](LIT-304.md)), which is accurate. It bears on [QUESTION-027](../questions.d/QUESTION-027.md):
Cor. 3.2 is a case where the same decisions are reached with
representations that are not linearly related.
