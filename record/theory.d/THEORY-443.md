# THEORY-443: Being a biological individual is not being an organism: evolutionary (Darwinian) individuality and physiological individuality come apart, and each is found among parts of organisms and groups of organisms as well as among organisms, so a part or a group can be an individual of one kind and not the other

## Source

Wilson and Barker (2007; substantive revision 17 January 2024), "Biological
Individuals", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, [LIT-168](../literature.d/LIT-168.md), read in
[NOTE-114](../notes.d/NOTE-114.md).

## What was actually shown

**Not only organisms (§3.1).** Parts of organisms are "often enough,
themselves biological individuals", and so are the populations and
lineages organisms form. If compositional complexity is necessary for
organisms, "given that there are organisms, there must be some biological
individuals that are not organisms". The stronger form is conditional.

**Two kinds (§3.2).** Evolutionary against physiological individuality is
"the most commonly recognized distinction" (after Pradeu 2016). A Darwinian
individual is a member of a population with variation, heritability and
differential reproduction, a bearer and active reproducer of heritable
traits (Godfrey-Smith 2009). A physiological individual is "a functionally
integrated and cohesive metabolic whole, made of interdependent and
interconnected parts" (Pradeu 2016b: 807); the entry calls these living
agents. Organisms are "exemplars of (but not strictly identical to) both".

**The framework (§§4, 6–7).** Biological individuals "include exactly"
organisms, some parts and some groups of organisms, which overlap. With the
two kinds laid over them, nine regions result, each with an example: fruit
fly (organism, living, Darwinian); gene (part, Darwinian only); eusocial
insect colony (group, Darwinian only); lysosome (part, neither); a clade
such as Bryophyta (group, neither); heart (part, living only); coral reef
(group, living only); virus (part, both, by scaffolded reproduction);
squid with Vibrio (organism, living only). The authors say some regions
"may turn out … unoccupied".

**What could have come out otherwise.** The two kinds could have coincided
everywhere outside organisms. The placements the entry is least
contentious about, genes and hearts, have them coming apart.

## What this does not say

- **That all nine regions are occupied.** Regions 3, 7, 8 and 9 are each
  hedged ("perhaps", "more controversially", "plausible candidate"). The
  squid and Vibrio placement is offered and at once allowed to disqualify
  the pair as an organism.
- **Which parts and groups count.** "Some" is never given a criterion.
- **That genes are settled.** Bourrat ([LIT-157](../literature.d/LIT-157.md)) puts genes in multicellular
  organisms with hearts, as reproducees, which revisits region 2.
- **Anything about parthood.** "Part", "proper part", "compose" and "member"
  are used without definition, and the general metaphysics of individuality
  is "bracketed off" (§1).
- **That "living agent" means agent.** It is the entry's term for a
  physiological individual. In the record's terms ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)) an individual is
  not thereby an agent, and the entry's colonies are agents only "by the
  cognitive metaphor" (§7).
- **That the meta ratio measures individuality.** The authors' extension of
  Queller and Strassmann (§8) has no operationalization.
- **That the text is clean.** It places Buchnera in a "human" gut (§4), swaps
  anabolic and catabolic (§6.1), and misprints *Armillaria* (§2).

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)** asks, relative to a stated target, when a combined form
  is one system. This account constrains the answer for living systems:
  whether a part or a group is one individual depends on which kind of
  individuality is the target, evolutionary or physiological, and the two
  verdicts can differ for the same thing.
- **Pregnancy.** Meincke ([THEORY-448](THEORY-448.md)) is a case of the §4 overlap, one
  organism inside another, argued from the process ontology this entry
  notes and does not adopt.
- **Evolutionary individuality.** Clarke's view (§8), that something is an
  evolutionary individual if and only if it has mechanisms that inhibit
  selection among its parts and maintain selection between wholes, is the
  shape of [THEORY-141](THEORY-141.md) on major transitions and of [THEORY-135](THEORY-135.md) on suppressing
  cells that cheat the organism.
- **Agency.** [THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md) makes individuality one condition of minimal agency
  and not sufficient for it, which is the separation the entry's "living
  agent" label blurs.
- **Reefs.** [THEORY-128](THEORY-128.md) and [THEORY-130](THEORY-130.md) use the coral reef and holobiont
  cases this entry leaves open.
