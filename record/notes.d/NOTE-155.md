# NOTE-155: Trappes — Defining the niche for niche construction

## Contribution

Niche construction theory (NCT) hopes to transform evolutionary biology and to unify it with ecology, but the debate has concentrated on "construction" and neglected what the niche is. Trappes compares NCT's definition, the niche as the sum of selection pressures acting on a population, with the definitions ecologists use. The evolutionary niche is narrower than the ecological niche in general. Even compared with a restricted ecological concept it has a somewhat different extension, and she identifies three kinds of case where the two come apart: extreme habitat degradation, commensalism, and non-limiting or super-abundant resources. These differences limit how far NCT can unify the two disciplines.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction (pp. 1–3): the target is Odling-Smee, Laland and Feldman's *Niche Construction* (2003), which presents its niche definition as a "simple translation" of the ecological concept into selection-pressure terms. The paper asks whether that is true, because NCT's claim to unify ecology and evolution is at stake if ecological terms are used in non-standard ways.
- "Introducing the ecological niche" (pp. 5–7): builds a basic ecological definition (the biotic and abiotic conditions that permit the continued existence of an individual, population or species). It can be specified as realized or fundamental, split by reference unit (individual, population, species), or modified into social, establishment and developmental niches.
- "Contrasting niches" (pp. 7–10): only the *realized population* ecological niche comes close to the evolutionary niche. Fundamental, individualized and species niches all fall outside the NCT niche.
- Fig. 1 and "Three cases of non-coincidence" (pp. 10–14): (1) habitat degradation, e.g. lesser snow geese degrading their Arctic breeding habitat. Conditions that drive population decline exert selection, so they belong to the evolutionary niche, but they fall outside the realized ecological niche. (2) Commensalism, e.g. mirid bugs (*Tupiocoris notatus*) on *Datura wrightii*: the host tolerates the relationship, but it produces no fitness differences. (3) Non-limiting resources, e.g. current atmospheric CO2 for land plants: it supports persistence but exerts no selection pressure on the angiosperm stomatal response at present levels.
- "Niche construction across the conceptual divide" (pp. 15–17): results cannot be transferred directly between the disciplines. Each case has to be checked for reference unit, modality (actual or possible) and relevant effect (persistence or fitness differences). Recognising ecological niche construction as well opens up many kinds of construction, and the evolutionary significance of niche construction then becomes something to show case by case rather than something that follows from the definition.

## Open questions
