# QUESTION-364: When an agent's own processes revise its will-organization, what makes the result the continuation of that agent's will rather than a replacement authored by something else, and how can a history of authorization count when the standards for reading authorization are themselves revised?

## Why it is a question

The record separates endorsing a motive from making it effective
([CLAIM-047](../claims.d/CLAIM-047.md), [CLAIM-122](../claims.d/CLAIM-122.md)), explaining efficacy from settling whose the will
is ([CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md)), and endogenous self-legislation from legitimate
self-legislation ([CLAIM-485](../claims.d/CLAIM-485.md)). It holds that history matters to authorship:
[CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md)'s voluntary restriction differs from an imposed one only in how it
came about.

What it does not settle is the case the exchange's constructions made
concrete: an organization that revises the machinery by which it revises
itself. If the standards by which an earlier self authorized a change are
themselves among what changes, the later self cannot simply check the
history against them. A28: "Endogenous self-legislation and legitimate
self-legislation remain different achievements."

## What would count as an answer

A criterion of continuation for will-organization under revision of its
own authorizing standards, with a case it classifies as continuation and
one it classifies as replacement, and the reason the history of
authorization decides between them. [QUESTION-118](QUESTION-118.md)'s successor relations are
the collective analogue; [CLAIM-344](../claims.d/CLAIM-344.md)'s resolve, which keeps the capacity to
revise a commitment, is the nearest held account of what must persist.

## Note of 2026-10-31: legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability

The owner's synthesis from review of the filing is filed as a claim,
[CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md): legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability are distinct
achievements, none of which justifies a demand. It bears on this question
because a revision can be legible, effective and executable while who has
the authority to make it stays open.

## Note of 2026-10-31: historically situated corrective authority

Also from the owner's review. Two organizations can have the same present
decision procedures and make the same decisions while differing in how
those procedures were authorized. [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md) is the basic case: voluntary
precommitment against imposed restriction.

This question asks how authorization persists, or fails to persist,
through transformations of the very standards used to judge authorization.
Identifying the process now effective cannot settle that, and neither can
showing that the process is maintained internally ([CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)).
Three things are distinct:

  endogenous governance ≠ historically continuous authorization ≠
  legitimate authority.

The owner judged this possibly the most important remaining connection
between the record's account of will and its account of normativity.

## Note of 2026-10-31: standards that change by use, and two episodes from the parent paper

The readings filed on this date bear on this question three ways.

- **Authorizing standards can change with no act of revision.** Tsoukas
  and Chia hold that applying a category to a nonprototypical case is
  "an imaginative extension" that extends "the radius of application of
  the concept, thus transforming it", and that "the application of a
  concept is always a normative act" ([LIT-1913](../literature.d/LIT-1913.md), p. 574;
  [THEORY-1279](../theory.d/THEORY-1279.md)). Change of that kind "occurs naturally, incrementally,
  and inexorably through 'creep,' 'slippage,' and 'drift'" (p. 579). If
  the standards by which an organization reads authorization are
  categories of this kind, a history of authorization may contain no
  event at which the standard changed. Each application was authorized
  under the standard as it then stood. That sharpens the question. A
  criterion of continuation cannot assume that revisions of the
  authorizing standard are discrete acts that can be checked one by one.
- **A revision of the standard made in the act.** In the MSF meeting
  ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), pp. 1359–1363), Robert first applies MSF's standard,
  "MSF [is] ... not a university", "[they] are not here as educators"
  (p. 1362). He then extends it until an "ad hoc training program" falls
  inside, and ends with "at first sight, I don't have major objections"
  (p. 1363). The paper reads this as MSF's continued individuation. Under
  this question it is a candidate case of an organization's standard
  being revised by one of its members during the decision it governs. It
  cannot be classified from what is published, because nothing records
  what authority Robert had to reinterpret MSF's mandate. His "at first
  sight" may be a reservation of that authority.
- **The personal form of the question, answered by changing the unit.**
  The parent's climate example asks, "How can I go on as the person I
  know to be 'myself' now that I feel this urge?" Its answer is to
  "enlist friends ... and start a local nonprofit", placing the urge in a
  collective (p. 1356). The person continues by relocating the demand,
  not by revising their own will-organization. Whether that counts as
  continuation is filed as part of [QUESTION-365](QUESTION-365.md).

None of the three supplies a criterion. No status, title or warrant changes.

## Note of 2026-11-01: a standard of self-attribution installed before memory

Metzinger's chapter on mental action (LIT-tmppbt0s, pp. 20–21, read in
NOTE-tmpk5ghu) sketches, as one horn of a dilemma, how an adult comes to
read its own acts as self-originated. Caregivers ascribe veto and "ultimate
origination" ("You could have done otherwise!") "at a stage where, from a
scientific perspective, young children almost certainly do not possess
these capacities"; the ascription is internalized; and because
autobiographical memory begins only between two and four, "the process of
amnestic enculturation itself will never be introspectively available".
The result is "a strong hyperprior for autonomy and moral responsibility",
installed "before our conscious inner life-history has even begun". His
2015 paper calls personhood "a concept of social ontology", "constituted
not in brains, but in societies" (LIT-tmp0w298, p. 284).

This sharpens the question in a direction the notes of 2026-10-31 did not.
They showed standards changing by use with no act of revision. Here the
first standard by which an agent reads authorization of its own acts was
never authorized by that agent, and the history that installed it cannot
be consulted by it. A criterion of continuation cannot then start from a
first self-authorized standard and check the history forward, because on
this sketch there is none. It is a sketch, offered for the eliminativist
horn, not a result, and it supplies no criterion.

No status, title or warrant changes.
