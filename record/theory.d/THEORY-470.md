# THEORY-470: Platform decay of the enshittification kind is caused not by a change in the character of those who run platforms but by the weakening of four constraints that used to discipline them, competition, regulation, interoperability and the bargaining power of tech workers, so it is reversible if those constraints are durably restored

## Source

Doctorow (2024), [LIT-033](../literature.d/LIT-033.md), "Disenshittify or die!", the published crib of
his DEF CON 32 talk, read in [NOTE-041](../notes.d/NOTE-041.md). It is a speech text, not an argued
essay, and not the talk as delivered.

## What was actually shown

Nothing is measured. The talk argues from examples.

**The cause.** Tech bosses "were always that guy". What changed was the
environment that held "the enshittification lever" in place: "Not because
they used to be better people, but because they used to be subjected to
discipline." Firms are taken to maximise extraction subject to constraint
("charge the most, and deliver the least"), and digital platforms can be
re-priced and re-ranked at near-zero cost, "a twiddler's utopia".

**The four constraints, each said to be removed.**

1. **Competition.** Forty years of permissive antitrust; Facebook buying
   Instagram; Google's default payments.
2. **Regulation.** Captured by concentrated industries; the "we do it with
   an app" gambit; the Video Privacy Protection Act (1988) as the last
   federal consumer privacy law.
3. **Interoperability.** General-purpose computers make any
   "enshittificatory alteration" reversible by another program unless law
   forbids it; DMCA §1201, the CFAA and IP law forbid it. Hence "no one's
   ever installed a tracker-blocker for an app".
4. **Tech workers.** Their leverage came from scarcity, not organisation,
   and went with the layoffs; only a union is durable.

**The remedy.** Restore each: antitrust enforcement, an interoperability
mandate (the DMA), a federal privacy law with a private right of action,
right-to-repair laws, and a tech-worker union. "We can make good services
out of imperfect people."

**What could have come out otherwise.** A platform facing all four forces
could have decayed anyway. The talk examines none, and [NOTE-041](../notes.d/NOTE-041.md) notes that
nothing in it could show that.

## What this does not say

- **That the four are each necessary, or which matters most.** They are
  treated as jointly necessary with no way to tell them apart ([NOTE-041](../notes.d/NOTE-041.md),
  open questions).
- **That management does not matter.** The account denies a change in
  character, not that firms differ; it gives no comparison across firms.
- **That its figures hold.** Amazon's 29% and 45–51%, the 2,000-a-day
  shelf tags and the layoff totals are unsourced in the text (C3, C4).
- **What its legal examples say.** *United States v. Google* was decided
  in the District Court, not the D.C. Circuit (C6). The DMA is a
  Regulation, like the GDPR; the real difference is central enforcement by
  the Commission (C7). Several "wins" it counts were later reversed or
  narrowed.
- **That it was the delivered talk.** The crib may differ from the video.

## Connections

- **Extends [THEORY-150](THEORY-150.md)**, the 2023 essay's three-stage cycle. The essay
  had competition, interoperability and worker revolt as checks and
  regulation only as a remedy; the talk makes all four constraints and
  makes their loss the cause.
- **[THEORY-152](THEORY-152.md)** (exit and voice). Interoperability and competition lower
  the cost of exit; worker power is a form of voice inside the firm. The
  talk does not use Hirschman's terms.
- **[THEORY-480](THEORY-480.md)** is the record's primary document on one of the
  constraints in action: the remedy in the case the talk counts as an
  antitrust win chose data sharing over break-up.
