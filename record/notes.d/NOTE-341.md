# NOTE-341: The Computational Boundary of a "Self"

## Contribution

The paper offers a definition of an individual that does not depend on the
material it is made of: a Self is whatever pursues a goal, and its size is
the size of the goals it can pursue. It ties this definition to a
mechanism, bioelectric coupling through gap junctions, which the author's
laboratory has shown can steer pattern and tumour formation. And it draws
the consequence the other definitions avoid: a living system is not one
Self but a stack of nested Selves (molecular networks, cells, organs,
organisms, swarms), each with its own goals, cooperating or competing.
The definition and the consequence are new as a package. The experiments
it rests on are earlier work, cited.

## Key insight

Agency comes in levels and sizes, and the size is set by how far
information travels. A cell senses its own surface and keeps its own
variables in range. Coupled to its neighbours, it shares their
measurements, so the coupled group can sense and keep in range things no
cell can see, such as the length of a limb or the layout of a face. That
group is then a larger agent with larger goals. Nothing in the parts has
to be given up: the cells remain agents, now "equally selfish" but with
"the relevant self that they defend … bigger in terms of space, time, and
complexity". Cut the coupling and the larger agent dissolves into its
parts, which is the paper's account of cancer.

## Assumptions

The paper states three "core assumptions" (Introduction).

- **Evolutionary continuity.** Every capacity "emerged from simpler
  variants". Cognition is not binary, so describing memory or goals in
  aneural systems is not a category mistake. Failing to credit them is a
  "neganthropomorphic fallacy".
- **Pragmatism about metaphor.** Whether a cognitive description of a
  system is literal or loose is "an empirical question", settled by
  whether it "improves prediction and control in novel cases at the
  bench".
- **Cybernetic goals.** A goal is the set point of "a feedback system that
  operates to maximize some specific state of affairs", modelled as an
  attractor. No stronger teleology is assumed.
- **Third-person scope.** "Except for a few brief remarks at the end, no
  claims about consciousness … are made."

Two further assumptions are used without being stated as such:

- that the active-inference reading of cells (cells "minimize surprise and
  optimize variational free energy") is correct, cited to Friston and
  colleagues;
- that physiological coupling, not shared genome or shared fate, is what
  binds parts into a Self. The paper calls its definition "orthogonal" to
  evolutionary ones but then uses only coupling.

## Key results

There are no theorems, simulations or new data. What the paper offers is a
definition, a scenario, cited evidence and predictions.

- **Definition (Defining Individuation).** "The scale and types of goals
  that a system can pursue defines (determines) the boundaries and content
  of the putative 'agent'." The boundary is the "Center of Concern" or
  "cognitive light cone": "the most distant (in time and space) set of
  events that this system can measure and attempts to regulate". In
  advanced agents it is also "the boundary of the self-model". Fig. 2
  places a tick, a dog and a human on a time × space diagram.
- **Nesting (Fig. 2C; Multicellularity vs. Cancer).** "In this scheme,
  Individuals can overlap." A swarm, its animals, their organs, cells and
  intracellular networks "each have their own cognitive horizon". The
  paper adds that "most biological systems consist of multiple, nested
  selves [not one, as implied by Integrated Information Theory (Tononi,
  2008)]". It also says "there is not necessarily one unique primary level
  of organization", and that which level to describe is chosen
  empirically, as the one giving prediction and control "with the least
  effort".
- **Inner selves need partial isolation.** "Each inner agent maintains its
  own local Self only to the extent that it regulates (restricts)
  information flow from neighbors and thus establishes unique states that
  do not coincide with those of the collective." The Box puts it as a
  balance: too little sharing fails to bind, too much makes "a homogenous
  soup".
- **Evolutionary scenario (Agent's Back-Story).** The sequence runs from
  homeostasis (the "atom" of the hierarchy) to memory through delays and
  hidden layers, to anticipation, to multicellularity, to nervous systems
  as speed-optimised bioelectric networks. Multicellularity is said to be
  driven by "greedy infotaxis" and by surprise minimisation: "the least
  surprising object in the world is a copy of yourself" (citing Fields &
  Levin 2019).
- **Cited evidence for coupling as binding.**
  - Group-level decisions: left–right identity is randomised by whole
    domains, not cell by cell; melanocyte conversion happens body-wide.
  - Cancer: depolarising a specific cell population makes normal
    melanocytes metastatic in genetically normal tadpoles; optogenetic or
    constitutive hyperpolarisation prevents tumours from human oncogenes.
    Plastic barriers and gap-junction blockers induce tumours.
  - Bacterial biofilms coordinate by ion flows.
  - "most anesthetics … are gap junctional (bioelectric) uncouplers",
    offered as "no coincidence".
  - Swarm organisms (ants, slime moulds) show cognitive illusions like
    vertebrate brains, offered as a confirmed prediction of the scaling.
- **Predictions (Predictions and Research Program).** Among them: forcing
  electrogenic proteins and gap junctions into a unicellular organism
  should induce metazoan-like bodies; bioelectric genes should be
  conserved in regeneration across kingdoms; swarm organisms can be
  trained; "multi-human systems could have their own degree of
  cognition", testable with information theory and reinforcement
  learning; and "adaptive, useful robotics will require components that
  are themselves competent and goal-seeking".
- **Consciousness (What Does It Feel Like to be a Pancreas?).** "Nothing in
  this model explains" the Hard Problem. It is "natural to hypothesize
  that there is something-it-is-like to being a tissue or organ", with "as
  much proto-consciousness as a simple neural network". The model "does
  take a stand on the perennial 'combination problem' and can perhaps be
  seen as a form of panpsychism" (citing Chalmers 2013). What the stand is
  is not said.

## Concepts

- **Self / Individual** — "the set of parts that operate toward reaching
  specific goal states"; capitalised throughout.
- **Cognitive (computational) light cone, Center of Concern** — the
  spatio-temporal region whose events the system can measure and try to
  change.
- **Scale-Free Cognition** — the thesis that the same cybernetic processes
  (homeostasis, learning, goal pursuit) occur at every scale from molecular
  networks to swarms, and that the self–world boundary is the main
  "control knob". The term is credited to Christopher Fields.
- **Infotaxis** — moving or connecting so as to gather as much information
  as possible, "from as far away … as possible", to minimise surprise
  (after Vergassola et al. 2007).
- **Synapse (broadly defined)** — any structure that regulates the passage
  of signals according to the state of other such structures; gap
  junctions are the cellular case.
- **Neganthropomorphic fallacy** — failing to credit cognition to a system
  where doing so would improve prediction and control.

## Connections

- **Minsky.** Minsky's *Society of Mind* ([LIT-429](../literature.d/LIT-429.md); Singh's exposition
  [LIT-444](../literature.d/LIT-444.md)) is not cited. The comparison is still the point of filing
  this. Minsky's agents are mindless, and mind is what their organisation
  does. Levin's parts are agents with goals, and mind is what their goals
  become when shared. Both deny a single privileged controller. They
  differ on whether the parts are themselves minded, which is exactly the
  question the anti-nesting principle turns on.
- **Dennett and the homunculi.** The Discussion calls the view "consistent
  with Dennett's stack of homunculi" (Dennett 1991): each level is the
  same homunculus with "a tiny bit more reach in physical space … and in
  time". That is homuncular functionalism with the homunculi made
  biological and the stack read upward, not downward. The level-choice
  rule is Dennett's intentional stance (cited, 1987), which [LIT-220](../literature.d/LIT-220.md) states
  in its general form.
- **Hofstadter.** *Gödel, Escher, Bach* ([LIT-437](../literature.d/LIT-437.md)) is cited for
  collective states that "compute meta-system properties not accessible to
  the single agents", and for agents that "transcend one system and re-set
  its goals". GEB's "Ant Fugue", a colony that is a mind, is the
  literary ancestor of the paper's swarm examples, though the paper does
  not cite it for that.
- **IIT.** The paper cites Tononi 2008, not IIT 3.0 ([LIT-445](../literature.d/LIT-445.md)), for
  the single-Self picture. IIT 3.0 says that "of all overlapping sets of
  elements, only one set can be conscious", which is a claim about
  experience. Levin's nested Selves are a claim about goal pursuit. The
  two are not yet in contradiction. They meet only in the closing section,
  where Levin extends proto-consciousness to tissues and organs, and
  there he gives no argument.
- **Causal emergence.** The paper names Hoel et al. (2013, 2016) as the way
  to compare "the causal power of models at different levels" and to find
  the most efficacious level. [LIT-025](../literature.d/LIT-025.md) reviews that line. Using it would
  turn Levin's pragmatic level-choice into a measurement. The paper does
  not do this.
- **Brooks.** The robotics prediction cites Cudhea & Brooks (1986) for
  "modular designs with sub-goals". That is the same laboratory and
  architecture as Brooks's "Intelligence without representation"
  ([LIT-436](../literature.d/LIT-436.md)), though not that paper. Levin's complaint is that robots
  are made of "reliable but very dumb parts". Brooks's layers are exactly
  that, and Brooks counts it a virtue.
- **The record's Levin line.** [LIT-195](../literature.d/LIT-195.md) (world models and life–mind
  continuity, Levin a co-author) and [LIT-211](../literature.d/LIT-211.md) (the definition-of-life
  survey, Levin a co-author) build on this paper's continuum of cognition.

## Bearing on the record

The bridge between Minsky ([LIT-429](../literature.d/LIT-429.md)) and Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md),
[NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)) turns on whether a minded whole can have minded parts. This paper
is the record's clearest statement that, for **agency**, the answer is yes
and is the usual case. It sits on the **composition side**.

Against [NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)'s numbering:

- **C3 (a whole of sensing or conscious parts can be conscious): supports,
  for agency only.** Its nested Selves are wholes made of goal-pursuing
  parts. Its human-group proposal is the US case in functional terms.
  For experience it offers only the closing speculation.
- **C4 (Putnam's anti-nesting principle is unjustified): neutral.** Putnam
  is not discussed.
- **C5 (IIT's exclusion postulate has absurd consequences): neutral as
  argument, opposed in conclusion.** It rejects IIT's one-Self picture in a
  bracket and gives no reason, and so lends C5 no support.

A THEORY drawing the bridge can cite it for the composition criterion: a
whole is an agent when its parts share enough information to pursue goals
none of them can sense. It parallels Schwitzgebel's "entityhood-enough",
which is integration without spatial contiguity. A THEORY should not cite
it as an argument against exclusion, or as evidence that collectives
experience anything.

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **The light cone is not operational.** It is a diagram with a time axis
  and a space axis, and "much more work is needed to fully flesh out this
  rubric". There is no rule for measuring it, so the definition cannot yet
  decide a hard case such as a colony, a holobiont or a nation.
- **Goals are ascribed, not detected.** "It is of course non-trivial
  empirical work to ascertain goals." The cybernetic definition makes any
  homeostat a Self. The paper accepts this, but it leaves the criterion
  very liberal, which is the over-generation Schwitzgebel exploits.
- **The bioelectric evidence supports coupling, not Selfhood.** The cited
  experiments show that voltage and gap-junction coupling change pattern
  and tumour outcomes. That they change the *boundary of a Self* is the
  paper's interpretation of those results.
- **The IIT contrast is unargued, and aimed at an older statement**
  (Tononi 2008). The phenomenal and the agential readings of "Self" are
  never separated, so whether the paper disagrees with IIT at all is not
  settled by its text.
- **No model.** The infotaxis scenario and the in-silico evolution it
  proposes are not built.

## Open questions

- Can the cognitive light cone be measured for a collective, for instance
  from the reach of its sensing and the horizon of its prediction? A
  measurement would let the criterion include or exclude a nation, and so
  meet Schwitzgebel's challenge directly.
- Does Levin's account of agency at many levels conflict with IIT's
  exclusion at all, or does it only become a rival when "Self" is read
  phenomenally? The closing section's panpsychism remark suggests the
  latter, but it is not developed.
- Would causal-emergence measures ([LIT-025](../literature.d/LIT-025.md)) pick out the same levels as the
  goal criterion? If they diverge, which defines the Self?
