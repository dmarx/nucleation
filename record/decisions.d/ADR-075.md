# ADR-075: A synthesis in a filed source is audited and, when it integrates, filed as a thesis claim with its components mapped, rather than mapped to its source turns and left

## Context

[ADR-032](ADR-032.md) gave the record its own argument: "synthesis, objections
anticipated and answered, positions taken and given up". The machinery
holds the parts well. Each idea is a CLAIM, CASE, TERM or QUESTION with
a voice and a status. ARGs and `rests_on` connect them, and
`docs/argument-lines.md` renders the chains. What no entry holds is the
integration, the account the parts add up to.

The read-and-file skill made this worse without meaning to. A synthesis
in a source is mostly restatement, and step 2's ledger maps each item to
the turn it first appeared in. The synthesis itself then lands under
"left". It has happened repeatedly:

- the exchange "Organizational Nucleation and SLT" (curation of
  2026-11-04, 064306): A26's synthesis is left because it drops U1–U13
  and recasts three owner turns as joint findings;
- A's synthesis THEORY, left in the curation of 2026-11-04 (214041);
- A's synthesis paragraphs and closing slogans, left in the curation of
  2026-11-05 (004020) because their content was held;
- the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (curation
  of 2026-11-07, 015133). At U2 the owner had to ask A to reconstruct
  the record's "core synthesis". A then gave three "refreshed
  syntheses" (A37, A45, A52). A45 told the owner's U10, U11, U14, U15
  and U19 as the argument's own logic and dropped the owner's U4 and
  U16. It also misstated [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md)'s conditions.

Each of those faults was found by a reader comparing the synthesis with
the record. None was recorded against a synthesis, because none had an
entry. The one exception shows the alternative works. [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md) was
filed from "Assess SDT Coverage" as, in that filing's changelog, "A
Proposed thesis from the synthesis the owner asked for", and it has
since been revised at a status review.

## Decision

The read-and-file skill gains a synthesis audit, run after the idea
ledger and before the curation entry.

1. **Find every synthesis**: a turn, section or pasted text that states
   an overall account. Group the versions of one account; a revision is
   a new version, not a new thesis.
2. **Classify each distinct thesis** as *descriptive* (it says what the
   record holds), *supposed* (conditional on hypotheses holding) or
   *proposed* (it puts an account forward).
3. **Decompose it**: map each component to a code, with its status and
   voice.
4. **Audit each version** for:
   - **attribution**: whose each component is, and any owner contribution
     told as joint or as A's;
   - **coverage**: what the source contributed that the synthesis drops;
   - **fidelity**: components misstated against their codes;
   - **standing**: Proposed or unverified components presented as
     established;
   - **integration**: what the synthesis claims beyond its components.
     If that is nothing but labels, it is a recap.
5. **Admit it, or don't** (versions 2 and 3). A synthesis is filed as
   a CLAIM only if it earns an entry by one of three routes. Each route
   has its own test, and the audit says which route it passed:

   | route | test | warrant |
   |---|---|---|
   | **independent hypothesis** | a coherent case, stipulated if need be, in which every component holds and the integrating claim is false | `interpretive` or `empirical`; the case is the core of its `defeated_if` |
   | **derived result** | the integrating claim follows from the components by a valid inference that is not trivial and is not already held as a CLAIM | `demonstrated`; the body gives the derivation, and `defeated_if` names an error in it or a premise it needs |
   | **explanatory integration** | it states a dependency, mechanism or discriminating prediction that juxtaposing the components does not: an intervention or comparison whose outcome it settles and they leave open | per its evidence; `defeated_if` names that outcome going the other way |

   The second route is there because a valid derivation has no
   countermodel relative to its premises. From A ⇒ B and B ⇒ C, the
   inference to A ⇒ C can be a contribution, and no case has both
   premises hold and the conclusion fail. A's second review of the pull
   request made this point against version 2, which had only the first
   test. "Every component holds" means that no component's own
   `defeated_if` is met: a case that defeats a premise tests the premise,
   not the integration. A synthesis that passes no route is exhausted by
   its components. It is not filed. It and its audit stay in the
   curation entry, linked to the components. Three failures are guarded
   against:
   - **Salience without work.** The record's own legibility depends on
     how it files. A broad synthesis filed as one more CLAIM gains
     salience it has not earned, and it makes the distinctions it
     integrates harder to find.
   - **Losing a real integration to loose wording.** If a narrower
     integrating sentence passes where the stated one doesn't, the
     narrower one is filed.
   - **A free parameter.** A synthesis with an open slot, such as "a
     declared bearer", can absorb every case by choosing the slot, so no
     case can be stated. Fixing the slot can then turn it against its own
     premises. The dispositional reframing filed with this decision failed this way, and it passes neither of
     the other routes.

   A synthesis CLAIM filed in a contribution that has not yet merged, and
   that passes no route, is **withdrawn before merge**. Its file is
   removed, and its text and audit move into the curation entry.
   Retire-by-status protects codes that have merged, because others may
   depend on them. A temporary code has no dependants outside its own
   contribution, and the contribution repoints those it has. A synthesis
   CLAIM that has merged and is later found not admitted is retired by
   status, with a note saying it was not independently admitted and why.
   Not admitted is not refuted: its content may be true, and is held by
   the entries the note names. The status vocabulary has no value for
   this, so a merged case is left until it first happens. The proposal for
   a synthesis scheme, where "not admitted" would be a status of its own,
   is the likely answer.
6. **File the latest version of each admitted thesis** as one CLAIM,
   Proposed, with role `thesis` or `supposed`.
   - Its title states the integrating claim, not a label.
   - Its body gives the layers, each with its codes, voice and status,
     and the audit's corrections marked as corrections.
   - Its `rests_on` holds the component CLAIMs that are premises.
     Components that only illustrate are named in the body.
   - Its `defeated_if` states the admission case (step 5) and includes
     the objection that it only restates its components.
   - Earlier versions are listed in the body, not filed.
   - A descriptive synthesis is audited, not filed. The audit is the
     check it gets.
7. **Revise, don't duplicate.** When the record already holds a
   synthesis CLAIM for the account, a returning synthesis revises it as
   a new version. A version that adds nothing that passes step 5 revises
   nothing; its audit goes in the curation entry.
8. **The curation entry gets a section, "Syntheses audited"**: each
   synthesis, its classification, what was filed or why it was left, and
   the findings by check.

The load-bearing parts:

- **Voice is per component, not per synthesis.** A synthesis built in an
  exchange mixes the owner's ideas, A's and the record's. Adopting it is
  the owner's call. The audit makes plain what the owner would be
  adopting, and from whom.
- **The integration is the claim, and it must earn its entry by a route
  a reviewer can check.** A conjectured integration earns it by a case in
  which it can fail on its own, a derived one by a derivation, and an
  explanatory one by an outcome it settles. Without one of these, the
  CLAIM can only be defeated by defeating a component, and it adds
  nothing a reader of the components lacks.

## Alternatives considered

- **A new scheme for positions.** It would be a living document whose
  sections list the codes they integrate, each tagged with its standing,
  and whose versions are diffed. It may be where this ends up. But it
  costs a `luria.yaml` scheme and generated views. A synthesis CLAIM
  uses the machinery already in place: `rests_on` renders it in
  argument-lines, versions record its revisions, and `defeated_if` makes
  it answerable. The scheme is the next step if CLAIMs strain, for
  instance if per-component status or diffs between versions are wanted
  routinely.
- **File every version.** One exchange gave four summaries. Filing each
  would multiply near-duplicates and leave the reader to diff them. The
  latest version is filed, and the earlier ones are listed in it.
- **File only the owner's syntheses.** The owner rarely writes one. The
  owner asks for them, and A writes them. Leaving A's means leaving the
  integration, which is the problem.
- **Admit on the audit's judgement of "not exhausted"** (version 2). A's
  review proposed filing a synthesis "only when the audit identifies a
  distinct integrative proposition, explanatory dependency, or
  discriminating prediction that is not exhausted by its components".
  That is the right aim, but "not exhausted" is a judgement two auditors
  can make differently, and a filer who wrote the synthesis will tend to
  find it unexhausted. Requiring the case makes the judgement checkable:
  a reviewer can test the case.
- **Status quo.** Each synthesis is mapped and left. The record keeps
  paying to reconstruct its own account. Assistants keep being asked to
  do it from scratch, and their drift goes unrecorded.

## Consequences

- The skill's steps gain the audit, and the curation entry a section.
  The filer's brief carries the protocol, as it carries step 9's ARG
  rule.
- The exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" is the
  first filing under it. Its synthesis CLAIMs and audit are filed in the
  same contribution as this decision. The decision is proposed with its
  first application, so the filing can cite it and the application shows
  what the decision does.
- **Follow-up:** the filings listed under Context left syntheses
  unaudited. A sweep would audit them, file any distinct thesis, and
  revise rather than duplicate where an account recurs. That is a
  separate contribution ([ADR-071](ADR-071.md)).
- Synthesis CLAIMs are long-lived and will be revised. Their histories
  carry the account's development, which the workbench entries do only
  exchange by exchange.
