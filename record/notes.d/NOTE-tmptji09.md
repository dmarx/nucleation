# NOTE-tmptji09: The Haken–Kelso–Bunz (HKB) model: from matter to movement to mind

## Contribution

A compact, first-person statement, thirty-six years on, of what the HKB
model is, how it was extended, and what research it seeded. For this
record its contribution is that it puts the model's equations, regimes and
extensions in one readable place, standing in for the paywalled 1985 paper
and the 1995 book.

## Key insight

A system of many interacting parts can be described, near an instability,
by one or a few collective variables whose dynamics is low-dimensional. For
two hands that variable is relative phase. Its landscape has two wells at
slow speeds and one at high speeds; the loss of a well is a phase
transition, announced by growing fluctuations and slowing recovery. The
same landscape, tilted by a difference between the parts, has no wells at
all but still bends the trajectory: metastability, where parts both
coordinate and keep their own tendencies.

## Assumptions

- **Order-parameter description** near instabilities, after Haken's
  synergetics: fast components are "enslaved" to slow collective variables
  (§2).
- **Original HKB (Box 1, top row):** spatial and temporal symmetry,
  identical components (Δω = 0).
- **Coupled hybrid oscillators (Eqs. 4–6):** each limb ẍ + f(x, ẋ) with
  f = αẋ + βẋ³ + γẋx² + ω²x, coupled through (ẋ₁ − ẋ₂){A + B(x₁ − x₂)²};
  this yields Eq. (1) with a = −(α + 2βR²) and b = ½βR².
- **Movement frequency acts as the control parameter** through b/a.

## Key results

- **Eq. (1)–(2).** dφ/dt = −a sin φ − 2b sin 2φ; V(φ) = −a cos φ − b cos
  2φ. Fixed points at φ = 0 and φ = π for a, b > 0. Bistable for b/a >
  1/4; for b/a < 1/4 φ = π is unstable and only in-phase remains. Pitchfork
  bifurcation.
- **Eq. (3), stochastic HKB** (Schöner et al. 1986): additive Gaussian
  noise of strength Q. Fitted means, variances, relaxation times and
  switching times "with only a single free parameter" (§3.1, as reported).
- **Eq. (7)–(8), symmetry breaking:** dφ/dt = Δω − a sin φ − 2b sin 2φ,
  Δω ≈ ω₁ − ω₂. Switching becomes a saddle-node bifurcation; beyond it no
  fixed points remain but the potential keeps curvature: metastability.
- **Eq. (9)–(10), generalized HKB:** Kuramoto phase oscillators with HKB's
  second-order coupling added: dφ_i/dt = ω_i − Σ_j a_ij sin(φ_i − φ_j) − …,
  with a_ij, b_ij > 0 the two coupling strengths. The text layer truncates
  Eq. (10) after the first sum; the paper describes it as "a combination of
  Eqs. 7 and 9". Without the second-order term, Kuramoto "cannot handle the
  presence of both in-phase and anti-phase coordination and transitions
  between them" (Appendix).
- **Kuramoto versus HKB (§3.3).** Kuramoto's transition is disorder to
  order; HKB's is order to order, or order to metastable partial order.
- **Brain (§4).** Reported: phase transitions in MEG and EEG; activation of
  premotor, insula, pre-SMA and cerebellum scaling with the instability of
  anti-phase but not changing for in-phase at the same rate (Jantzen et al.
  2009); TMS switching anti-phase to in-phase only (Meyer-Lindenberg et al.
  2002).
- **Generality (§3.2).** The same multistability, transitions and
  metastability are reported within a person, between person and stimulus,
  between people, across species, and with virtual partners.

## Concepts

- **Order parameter / collective variable.** A low-dimensional relational
  quantity (here φ) carrying the information needed to describe the
  system's coordination.
- **Control parameter.** A parameter that, varied, moves the system through
  its patterns without specifying them (movement frequency); not an
  independent variable in the usual sense.
- **Intrinsic dynamics.** The coordination tendencies a system has before
  learning or instruction.
- **Functional information.** Meaningful information (intention, memory,
  perception) acting as an attractor in the same space as the intrinsic
  dynamics.
- **Metastability.** Coordination with no stable states, only "trapping"
  and "wrapping" tendencies.

## Connections

Its physics is Haken's synergetics; its biological problem is Bernstein's
degrees-of-freedom problem and Pattee's origin of coordination. Kuramoto's
model shares the synergetics origin (§3.3). Its descendants named in §5
include task dynamics, articulatory phonology, ecological dynamics and the
excitator model. De Jaegher & Di Paolo ([LIT-tmp9mw46](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9mw46.md)) build on the same
absolute/relative coordination from Kelso 1995. Raja et al.
([LIT-tmpumhsc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpumhsc.md)) use the HKB explanation of coupled oscillators as their
example of a dynamics already explained without a Markov blanket.

## Bearing on the record

- **Behavioral integration.** A quantitative case of many effectors acting
  as one through a collective variable rather than a central controller.
  The record's other `behavioral-integration` holdings are about control by
  competition, hierarchy or evidence accumulation; this is the dynamical
  alternative.
- **THEORY suggested** (reported, not filed; Proposed until the 1985 paper
  and Schöner et al. 1986 are read): in human rhythmic bimanual
  coordination, anti-phase loses stability at a critical movement
  frequency and switches to in-phase through a nonequilibrium phase
  transition, captured by dφ/dt = −a sin φ − 2b sin 2φ, with critical
  fluctuations and slowing as early signs.
- **Markov blankets ([LIT-tmpumhsc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpumhsc.md), [LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md)).** HKB is the record's worked
  example of explanation by a collective variable, the alternative Raja et
  al. set against the blanket partition.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- Retrospective and selective by its own account ("not a review"); the
  evidence for every empirical claim is in cited papers not read here.
- The claim that deriving HKB from lower levels settles the mechanism
  debate is asserted, not argued.
- The generalization to "mind" (§7) is suggestive language, e.g. Sheets-
  Johnstone's "ideational phase transitions".
