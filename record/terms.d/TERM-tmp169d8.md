# TERM-tmp169d8: exploitation, relative to a declared standard: one party's benefiting from an arrangement on terms that, as they bear on another party's labour, resources or standing, violate a standard declared with the judgment, so that with the arrangement, the beneficiary, the party exploited and the standard fixed the verdict can be false

## Definition

A judgment that an arrangement is exploitative has five relata, and is
well-defined only when all five are stated (CLAIM-363):

1. **The arrangement**, with its sense declared (TERM-074). Sense 1, a
   relational arrangement, is the default; it need not be an institution
   or an agent.
2. **The beneficiary**: the party, or the parties and roles, that benefit
   from the terms.
3. **The party exploited**, which must have standing (TERM-083). A cell
   or a subprocess is not exploited in this sense, though it may be used.
4. **The terms**: how the arrangement bears on that party's labour,
   resources or standing.
5. **The standard**, declared with the judgment: what the terms are
   judged against, such as a standard of fair exchange, of freedom over
   one's labour, or of non-encumbrance.

The arrangement is exploitative, relative to that standard, when the
beneficiary benefits from terms that violate it. Once the five are fixed,
the verdict is a fact about the case and can be false. That is CLAIM-363's
second part, and it is what keeps the term from being a label for
disapproval.

## What it is for

It lets an entry say "exploitative" without the record first settling
which standard is right, which is QUESTION-129's question and open. An
entry that uses the term names its standard. Two entries with different
standards can then disagree about a case without contradicting each
other, as A6 §4's four evaluations of one colonial production do (note on
CLAIM-363). QUESTION-tmpyesgg asks how the record identifies exploitation;
this term is the vocabulary that question is asked in, not its answer.

## What it is not

- **Not dependence.** A6 §2's dependency predicate is "Withdrawing the
  contribution disrupts or diminishes the production". An indispensable
  contribution does not by itself show exploitation, and compensation or
  acclaim does not show its absence (A6 §2).
- **Not domination.** Domination (TERM-tmpc9uv8) is a capacity over
  another's terms, used or not. A benevolent master dominates without
  benefiting on violating terms. A lender whose borrower could have
  refused at entry may exploit, on a standard of non-encumbrance, without
  holding a capacity over the borrower's later choices (CLAIM-669). The
  two often travel together and are separable.
- **Not instability or failure.** A stable, productive arrangement can
  exploit (CLAIM-546).
- **Not the bearer of the remedy.** The owner's review of #488 asked that
  the ground of an obligation be kept apart from its bearer. The
  beneficiary is a relatum of the judgment; who owes the exploited party
  a remedy is a further question. In a sense-1 arrangement with no agent
  that designed the terms, the benefit can accrue to parties who neither
  set nor control the terms, and the obligation may fall on whoever
  maintains or controls them, or on no one (QUESTION-460, CASE-256's third
  result).
- **Not a criterion with a test built in.** The term says what must be
  fixed for a verdict. It does not say how a violation is detected, which
  is QUESTION-tmpyesgg's question. CASE-tmpzh595 separates dependence from
  control; it does not detect exploitation.

## What would unsettle it

- A use the record needs in which exploitation is judged with no standard
  at all, so that the fifth relatum is idle. That would also be against
  CLAIM-363.
- A party exploited without standing, which the record needs to say is
  wronged: then relatum 3 is too narrow.
- Berenstain's "epistemic exploitation" (LIT-2264) is unread. As A
  describes it (the LIT's summary), disadvantaged people "can be compelled
  to do burdensome work explaining their oppression to more privileged
  audiences". That is labour, so relatum 4 would hold it. If the reading
  shows a benefit that is not on the terms of labour, resources or
  standing, relatum 4 widens.
- CLAIM-624's duty of answerability may turn out to be the standard most
  entries reach for. If so, a narrower term that fixes it would refine
  this one.
