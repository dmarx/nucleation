# LIT-1595: Late acquisition of mitochondria by a host with chimaeric prokaryotic ancestry

Pittis, A. A. & Gabaldón, T. (2016). Late acquisition of mitochondria by a host with chimaeric prokaryotic ancestry. *Nature* 531(7592): 101–104. doi:10.1038/nature16941.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1240](../notes.d/NOTE-1240.md).*

- **The measure.** A family's stem is the branch from its closest
  prokaryotic sister to LECA, divided by the family's median branch
  length inside eukaryotes. This removes rate only if rates before and
  after LECA are proportional, which the authors assume.
- **The order.** Bacterial families are shorter than archaeal
  (P = 1.38e-25); alphaproteobacterial families are shortest, and
  shorter than other bacterial ones at P = 0.044 ([THEORY-1102](../theory.d/THEORY-1102.md)).
- **Origin tracks location.** Alphaproteobacterial families go with the
  mitochondrion and energy production, other bacterial ones with
  endomembranes, archaeal ones with the nucleus and information
  processing. Matched comparisons say origin, not function, drives
  stem length.
- **Controls.** Plastid-derived families have shorter stems still, as
  the later plastid predicts. Adding one Lokiarchaeum genome shortens 30
  archaeal stems, which also shows that stem length depends on how close
  the sampled relative is.
- **What it cannot say.** Whether the non-alpha bacterial genes came
  with partners or by transfer, and anything about when partners became
  dependent on one another. Its closing claims, that the mitochondrion
  ended massive transfer and gave "the definitive selective advantage",
  are not analysed.

## Why this is here

The earlier statement of the mito-late explanation, "cellular complexity preceding mitochondrial acquisition", which A1 named and did not cite. What it shows is genomic chimaerism before the mitochondrion, not cellular complexity. [LIT-1566](LIT-1566.md) continues its mitochondrion-late order by a different method, and departs from it on the bacterial component: it dates most non-alphaproteobacterial duplications after the mitochondrion and argues against serial endosymbiosis. [LIT-1561](LIT-1561.md) is said to continue its argument, and Gabaldón is senior author on it; that is unchecked here. Its branch-length method has been disputed. The dispute is not in this paper, but the reading finds where the method is exposed: the assumption of proportional rates, and the dependence of stem length on how close the sampled relative is.
