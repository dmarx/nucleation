# THEORY-769: Ownership of a body part is a graded belief that it is the most probable common cause of correlated multisensory input, so that synchronous stimulation of another object moves some of that belief to the object and away from the real part

## Source

Apps & Tsakiris (2014), [LIT-1262](../literature.d/LIT-1262.md), *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral
Reviews* 41, read in full from the PMC author manuscript in [NOTE-991](../notes.d/NOTE-991.md):
§4 (the self as a prior), §5 (the illusions), §6 (anatomical predictions)
and §7 (caveats). Botvinick & Cohen (1998), [LIT-1277](../literature.d/LIT-1277.md), read in
[NOTE-1006](../notes.d/NOTE-1006.md), is the primary result the account is built to explain.

## What was actually shown

Apps and Tsakiris report no data and no model. They argue, within the
free-energy and predictive-coding framework, which they take as given
(§3), that one's own body is "the one which has the highest probability of
being 'me'", because no other object reliably evokes the same correlated
input across the senses. "The notion that there is a 'self' is the most
parsimonious and accurate explanation for sensory inputs" (§4).

On this account the rubber hand, enfacement and rubber-voice illusions are
one process. Synchronous stimulation leaves "considerable bottom up sensory
surprise" in one sense, multimodal areas explain it away, and the
representation of one's body part shifts (§§5.1–5.2, Fig. 1). "The driving
effect for each illusion is an increase in the probability that the other
object ... will be represented as part of the body and a decrease in the
probability that one's actual body will be represented as 'self'" (§5.2).
Context acts as a prior: the illusion is weaker or absent for a non-bodily
object, a hand of another skin tone, or a hand in an incongruent place
(§5.3).

What the account adds to the bare finding of [THEORY-765](THEORY-765.md) is two
commitments that could fail:

- **Ownership is graded.** It is a probability, not a switch.
- **It is shared.** What the other object gains, the real part loses. The
  authors cite cooling of the real hand and raised histamine reactivity as
  signs of this (§5.2).

## What this does not say

- **It is not tested.** "There is, as yet, little direct evidence that
  predictions and prediction errors related to self-stimuli are processed
  anywhere in the brain" (§7). "Empirical data neither largely supports
  nor refutes our account" (§8). Every finding the paper uses was gathered
  under other accounts.
- **The physiology is interpretation.** Cooling and histamine are read as
  falling probability of ownership. No mechanism links them, so they cannot
  carry the second commitment on their own. That is why the promotion test
  asks for another measure.
- **The prior can explain either result.** The account explains why the
  illusion fails for a box by a prior against boxes being hands. A
  different prior would explain the opposite result. Until priors are
  measured apart from the outcome they explain, this part of the account
  is not falsifiable, as [NOTE-991](../notes.d/NOTE-991.md) notes.
- **It says nothing about being a self.** The account explains how a body
  is modelled as one's own. It does not say why the model is experienced
  as a self rather than as a model, which is [THEORY-064](THEORY-064.md)'s claim.
- **It does not model interoception.** Interoception is named among the
  inputs but none of the worked cases uses it. That is [THEORY-767](THEORY-767.md).
- **It is about ownership, not agency.** It makes motor signals one kind of
  prediction among others, so that self-recognition needs no action. That
  is argued, not shown.

## Added on 2026-10-20

- **The finding it explains is narrower than first filed.** [THEORY-765](THEORY-765.md),
  which this account was built to explain, is Rejected and corrected by
  [THEORY-766](THEORY-766.md). Drift is not a measure of ownership reports, and the
  ownership report is the weakest of the illusion items and tracks trait
  suggestibility. An account that predicts drift and ownership from one
  belief now has two outputs to fit, not one.
- **The source's authors are on one side of an open dispute.** Tsakiris
  signs Ehrsson et al. (2022, [LIT-1264](../literature.d/LIT-1264.md)), which reads the rubber hand
  data as multisensory perception. Whether they are is [THEORY-766](THEORY-766.md)'s
  open question.
- **The cooling evidence is thinner than its citation.** Hohwy & Paton
  (2010, [LIT-1272](../literature.d/LIT-1272.md)), which the source cites for cooling of the real hand,
  report 0.13 °C in one comparison of four. It is significant only
  one-tailed and uncorrected, under a real foreign arm, not a rubber one,
  and with no ownership measure. Their box result, touch referred to a box
  only after the illusion has begun, is explained there by a prior that
  drops once the illusion is running. That is the after-the-fact move this
  account is warned against above. The promote_when already asks for a
  measure other than temperature, so the THEORY's standing does not change.
