# ARG-tmp3r9ay: The age-three pattern, equality stated as a norm but kept to only after collaboration, with mutual expectation and tacit salience unavailable at that age, is best explained by a joint commitment that motivates settling a division whose content is an already-known equal default

## The inference

Three findings stand together at age three. Children state equal division
as the norm yet keep more for themselves when the resource is a windfall
([THEORY-tmpitywd](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpitywd.md)). After collaborating they split equally, almost never
otherwise ([NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md)'s summary of Hamann et al. 2011). And they neither
reason recursively about mental states nor coordinate tacitly on salience:
those come at about 5 to 6 ([THEORY-tmpj4l2c](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpj4l2c.md), [LIT-tmpkwxfx](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkwxfx.md)). If the joint
commitment supplies the motive to settle the division and the known equal
norm supplies its content, all three follow: the norm is there before
collaboration, the commitment makes it bind, and nothing recursive is
needed.

## Critical questions

**Is this the best explanation of the evidence?** Three rivals:

- Tomasello's reading alone, obligation inside the joint commitment,
  explains the second finding but leaves the first and third unexplained:
  why the norm is known before it binds, and how the content is fixed
  without recursion. The claim adds rather than replaces.
- A Schelling focal point found by mutual expectation is ruled out at this
  age by the third finding.
- Killen's reading ([THEORY-tmpgv0cr](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpgv0cr.md)): fairness rests on independent
  judgment and peer conflict, not on the "we". It explains the known norm
  but not why collaboration, rather than conflict, triggers following it
  in these studies. It is a live rival for older children.

**Is the evidence reliable?** The motive behind the equal sharing has
never been measured: Killen and Smetana note that "the researchers did not
examine why 3-year-olds shared equitably" ([THEORY-tmpoak8v](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpoak8v.md)). The windfall
and collaboration findings come from different studies and designs. The
salience finding at age three rests on one study read only in its
abstract.

**Is there evidence the explanation would be false?** Equal division after
collaboration that did not depend on commitment, or a three-year-old who
did not hold the equal norm before collaborating, would defeat it. Neither
has been tested in the crossed design the lead proposes ([LEAD-tmpxakgu](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpxakgu.md)).

## Verdict

The explanation fits the age-three pattern better than either reading
alone, on interpretive grounds; its weakest step is the unmeasured motive.
Proposed.
