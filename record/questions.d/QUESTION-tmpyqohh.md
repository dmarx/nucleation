# QUESTION-tmpyqohh: Is the scale-free behaviour found at transitions related to chaos, and is it self-similarity?

## The owner's question (U4)

> Power laws often emerge at transitions between qualitatively different
> kinds of collective behavior.
>
> is there a relationship to chaos here? the scale-free behavior reminds
> me of self-similarity

The owner asks two things. Whether the power laws found at transitions
are related to chaos; and whether "scale-free" there is the same thing as
self-similarity. Both links are the owner's.

## Why it is a question

The exchange had by then given power laws from rich-get-richer growth,
multiplicative growth, critical branching and percolation (A1–A5), and
named criticality as one source. The record held chaos only as a
property of deterministic dynamics (THEORY-1332, CLAIM-376, CLAIM-533)
and criticality only through renormalization readings (THEORY-194,
THEORY-777) and critical slowing (THEORY-1315). Nothing related them, and
nothing said which of several things "scale-free" names.

## What would answer it

- For the chaos half: a statement of which of chaos, criticality and
  scale invariance imply which, with a separating case for each
  implication that fails, and the mechanisms by which they do co-occur.
  CLAIM-tmpyy261 offers this: they are independent, and meet at the
  period-doubling accumulation point (critical, not chaotic), in
  intermittency, and in turbulence. A6 §1 gave the independence as a
  table; ARG-tmp92gzv weighs its claim that the RG link is "not merely
  metaphorical".
- For the self-similarity half: a separation of what "scale-free" can
  mean. TERM-tmpyi882 does this. Self-similarity is its sense 2, a law
  unchanged by rescaling; the exchange's opening power laws were sense 1,
  a heavy-tailed distribution; the two come apart (CLAIM-tmpwl8y8).

The question stays open until the owner accepts or replaces those
answers.
