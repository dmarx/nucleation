# NOTE-tmpdpxch: Quantum Thermodynamics

## Contribution

The review introduces quantum thermodynamics as the extension of thermodynamics and nonequilibrium statistical physics to small, out-of-equilibrium, quantum ensembles. It notes that the field draws on statistical physics, many-body theory, mesoscopic physics and quantum information, which each bring their own tools. Topics covered include thermalisation of quantum systems, competing definitions of work, and the efficiency and power of quantum engines. It is pitched at postgraduate students and researchers.

- Structure: information and thermodynamics (Maxwell's demon, Landauer erasure, erasure with quantum side information, work from correlations and coherences); classical and quantum fluctuation relations; CPTP dynamics and thermalisation; single-shot thermodynamics and resource theory; quantum thermal machines (Carnot, Otto, Diesel, refrigerators) (Contents).
- Defines average heat and work by ⟨Q⟩ = ∫ tr[ρ̇H] dt and ⟨W⟩ = ∫ tr[ρḢ] dt; stresses that both are process-dependent, not observables (§2.1, Eqs. 2-3).
- Presents the Jarzynski equality as strengthening the second law to all moments of the work distribution, recovering the usual inequality via Jensen (§3.2).
- Discussion: the notions of work (ensemble Eq. 2, fluctuating Eqs. 15/24, single-shot Eq. 48, resource theory Eq. 50) are not unified. The single-shot and resource-theoretic optima coincide (§7).
- Open problems named: kinematic (typicality) vs dynamical (ETH) accounts of thermalisation, statistics vs engine performance, non-thermal baths (§7).
