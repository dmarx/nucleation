# LIT-tmpvjyqr: A Sociologist's Perspective on Domestic Violence: A Conversation with Michael Johnson, Ph.D.

Ooms, T. (interviewer) & Johnson, M. P. (2006). *A Sociologist's Perspective on Domestic Violence: A Conversation with Michael Johnson, Ph.D.* Center for Law and Social Policy (CLASP), from the May 2006 CLASP/NCSL conference "Building Bridges: Marriage, Fatherhood, and Domestic Violence".

## Key takeaways

- Three types, by control context (p. 3): "Intimate terrorism is the kind
  of intimate partner violence that involves a batterer who terrorizes and
  takes complete control of his partner through the use of violence in
  combination with other control tactics"; "Violent resistance is the
  violence involved when a victim of intimate terrorism fights back";
  situational couple violence "is the most common form".
- The gender debate dissolved (p. 4): the two camps "are talking about two
  completely different phenomena (intimate terrorism and situational couple
  violence), but both are using the same term (domestic violence)".
- Classification asks about control and fear, not acts (p. 7): "the power
  and control issues involved in their relationship, and ... the presence
  of fear".
- Couple interventions suited to situational couple violence "are not only
  inappropriate for intimate terrorism, they are dangerous" (p. 6).
