# CLAIM-778: An entry's local degrees of freedom are the features a restatement copies with error, neither pulled onto a convention nor copied exactly, and over its life cycle they are moved out of that part: the schema puts its form in the contracting part at filing, and its code, its verbatim reading and the relations that accrue on it put its content in the conserved part, so the revisions it can still take narrow like an inductive prior; an entry that has slowed works as a constraint on faster ones, and what narrows is its text, while its identity stays open to change by status

## The claim

The owner, at U1: "relate narrowing degrees of freedom by adding constraints
and thereby developing an increasingly narrow inductive prior towards desired
outcomes, to the evolution of an item on the record and how its local degrees
of freedom change over its life cycle".

**What the degrees of freedom are.** [CLAIM-772](CLAIM-772.md) splits the features of
an entry by what a restatement step does to them: it pulls them onto its own
fixed point (contracting), copies them exactly (conserved), or copies them
with error (neutral). Only the last kind is free: a contracted feature has
somewhere it goes, a conserved one stays where it was, and a feature copied
with error wanders, its spread growing as √n while its start is forgotten as
1/n ([CLAIM-779](CLAIM-779.md)). An entry's **local degrees of freedom** are those
features ([TERM-132](../terms.d/TERM-132.md)). The distribution over its next revision is
narrow when few of them remain.

**The life cycle moves features out of the free part.**

| Stage | Free (copied with error) | Moved to |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange text, not yet coded | everything | nothing yet |
| Filed (`luria new`) | content | form to the contracting part: fields, vocabulary, role, warrant |
| Coded and read verbatim | interpretation, scope, emphasis | the text to the conserved part |
| Proposed, with `promote_when` or `defeated_if` | which way its status goes | what would move it, conserved in the field |
| Active, with inbound `rests_on` | history notes, added relations | title and claim: a change costs work downstream, so in effect conserved |
| Superseded or Rejected | almost nothing | the body (retire by status, never by deletion) |

History notes say it in their own words: late in an entry's life they mostly
end "No other change."

**Two kinds of constraint, two parts.** Juarrero, after Gatlin
([THEORY-1335](../theory.d/THEORY-1335.md)), separates constraints that take a system away from
equiprobability from those that take it away from independence. The first
kind is the schema: the same for every entry of a scheme, a convention every
restatement contracts onto. The second is the relations: particular to the
entry, accruing after filing, each inbound `rests_on` an entry that has to
change if this one does. They are what make the text, in effect, conserved.

**Enabling turns into governing.** In Juarrero's account, enabling
constraints couple processes into a dynamic whose constraints then confine
the components ([THEORY-1335](../theory.d/THEORY-1335.md)). Coding an idea enables it: it can be cited.
Once claims rest on it, that web holds its text. The entry made the
constraint that binds it. [CLAIM-184](CLAIM-184.md) states the cost: anchors entrench.

**Slowing is what makes an entry a constraint.** [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md): where some
degrees of freedom settle fast relative to others, the relation they settle
into is a constraint over the window between the two time scales. Within a
session an assistant's framing is fast, and contracts onto its own
conventions; the filed entry is slow. Coding is the point at which an idea
passes from the one scale to the other (compare [CLAIM-191](CLAIM-191.md): a code restores
continuity), and a slowed entry can ground newer, faster ones.

**What narrows is the text, not the identity.** [CLAIM-031](CLAIM-031.md): "one changed
constraint can change the act" while most observables stay stable. A status
or role transition does that: a claim moved Active to Superseded, or thesis
to granted, keeps its body and changes what it is. [CLAIM-195](CLAIM-195.md) holds that what
counts as the same claim persisting is fixed by an amendable decision; the
record has put identity in the code and its controlled fields, not in the
prose. So credence and voice stay free after the text has all but closed,
and that is how the record makes a phase change ([CLAIM-114](CLAIM-114.md)) deliberately and
not by drift.

## What it does not say

- Not that narrowing is monotone. Correction and supersession reopen the
  degrees of freedom, but on a new entry: the old one is frozen, not
  loosened. A version that changes a title is the exception, and costs more
  the more inbound edges the entry has.
- Not that narrowing is good. Moving content into the conserved part too
  early is premature convergence; Proposed status, `promote_when` and
  `defeated_if` keep chosen features free on purpose, and [CLAIM-184](CLAIM-184.md) is the
  risk.
- Not that a settled entry was settled by being conserved. Contraction also
  settles, onto the convention; the two are told apart only by retention
  ([CLAIM-768](CLAIM-768.md)).
- The inductive prior is an analogy for the narrowing distribution over
  next revisions. A prior is soft and lint is hard; [DP-3](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-3)'s ladder (prose,
  convention, mechanism, guarantee) is the same hardening applied to the
  record's norms rather than its entries.

## Note of 2026-11-21: the narrowing as accrued predicates

At the owner's reframe, that adding predicates to an entry is adding
constraints, [CLAIM-785](CLAIM-785.md) restates this claim's narrowing by what an entry
says about itself rather than by what restatement copies: each predicate
filed on it ([TERM-134](../terms.d/TERM-134.md)) is a test its later versions must pass, and the
revisions open to it are the intersection of what passes. [CLAIM-783](CLAIM-783.md)
models that, with soft and hard predicates as one penalty at different
strengths. So the caveat under "What it does not say", that the inductive prior is an analogy and
that a prior is soft while lint is hard, is answered in that model: the
prior is the intersection weighted by enforcement, and lint is its hard
limit. Version 1's wording, "as constraints accumulate on it", is restored in
substance there. Nothing here changes.

## Admission

Audited at the synthesis review of 2026-11-21 ([ADR-075](../decisions.d/ADR-075.md), step 5) by a reader briefed neutrally.

**What it adds.** Most of the claim maps its premises. [CLAIM-772](CLAIM-772.md) already places content in the conserved part and form in the contracting part, and [TERM-132](../terms.d/TERM-132.md) already describes the life cycle as features leaving the free part. One sentence adds something: inbound `rests_on` and `grounds` edges that accrue after coding make an entry's title and claim, in effect, conserved. Substantive revisions become rarer after such edges arrive, while status stays free.

**The route, and a risk.** A realistic separating case passes. The premises concern unprompted copying error, and [TERM-132](../terms.d/TERM-132.md) puts deliberate, announced revision outside it. If heavily cited entries draw more audits and revisions, because scrutiny follows citation, substantive revision rises with inbound edges while every premise holds. The record has instances: [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md) was retitled in version 5 while cited, and [CLAIM-772](CLAIM-772.md) was rewritten twice. A rough count over current files gave this:

| Inbound edges | Claims | Share retitled |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 495 | 0.08 |
| 1–2 | 228 | 0.08 |
| 3–5 | 53 | 0.17 |
| 6 or more | 18 | 0.17 |

The count does not control for age or timing. If the pattern holds once those are controlled ([LEAD-199](../leads.d/LEAD-199.md)), it meets this claim's defeater (a), and shows the claim false rather than merely separate.

**A narrower sentence that would survive the scrutiny confound:** per audit or reframing an entry undergoes, the chance of a substantive revision falls as inbound edges accrue, because each edge adds a downstream recheck.

## Note of 2026-11-21: [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md) misstated

The same audit found two misstatements of [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md).

1. The body paraphrases its superseded version 4 ("settle fast relative to others … the window between the two time scales"). Version 5 says "settle faster than others by more than the rates vary among them", which adds that the gap between levels must exceed the spread within them.
2. The heading "Slowing is what makes an entry a constraint" goes against [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)'s own "Not that slowness makes a constraint effective". Slowness makes a constraint available, not effective.

Two smaller ones: [CLAIM-195](CLAIM-195.md) names codes, versions and supersession, not "controlled fields"; and [CLAIM-779](CLAIM-779.md)'s rates are carried over without its linear Gaussian model. No change of status.

## Note of 2026-11-22: [LEAD-199](../leads.d/LEAD-199.md)'s count meets defeater (a)

[LEAD-199](../leads.d/LEAD-199.md) was run ([CASE-307](../cases.d/CASE-307.md)): over the record's history to 2026-11-22, at matched age, claims with inbound rests_on or grounds edges take substantive revisions about 1.45 times as often as claims with none (Mantel-Haenszel by age, 1.47 [1.22, 1.77]; risk-set matched, 1.62 [1.33, 1.94]), title changes rise too, and the fall in revision rate over a claim's life tracks its age, not its edges. That meets defeater (a) as worded, by the route the admission above foresaw. Two readings are filed, for the owner to choose between: [CLAIM-864](CLAIM-864.md) (this claim is false as it stands) and [CLAIM-863](CLAIM-863.md) (the admission's narrower sentence, per audit, which the count cannot test). The status of this claim is left for the owner.
