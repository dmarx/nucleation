# LIT-1391: The Unspoken Whiteness of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu: The Gracies versus Luta Livre

Penglase, R. B. (2025). The unspoken whiteness of Brazilian jiu-jitsu: The Gracies versus luta livre. *Martial Arts Studies*, 18, 78–93. doi:10.18573/mas.300.

Read in full on 2026-10-22, in [NOTE-tmpvdjek](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvdjek.md). The summary is the abstract's and
the reading confirms it.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpvdjek](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvdjek.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **The rules were contested first.** In 1931 Rufino wanted no time limit,
  a pin and no gi; the Gracies won five-minute rounds, submission only and
  the gi. "the Gracies often insisted on rules that would benefit them and
  their fighting style", and the gi "also favored the Gracies in the ring"
  (p. 86). The referee gave the fight to Rufino (p. 87).
- **Race spoken through class and space.** The whiteness of jiu-jitsu
  "was 'spoken' through other registers, in particular class and urban
  space" (p. 80).
- **Different fortunes.** "jiu-jitsu ended up becoming far more
  economically profitable than luta livre" (p. 81); luta livre, "which
  never had such a strong financial foundation, largely went underground"
  (p. 89), after the 1991 defeat, a sympathetic press and "the underlying
  class and racial differences".
- **Absorption.** When jiu-jitsu took up no-gi, "the main technical
  contrast between the two arts collapsed" (p. 90), and the social
  difference remained.

## Why this is here

The owner proposed rival schools of martial arts as a case (U35). The replies drew from Penglase that the traditions' social positions, and not only their results, shaped their standing; that reading is the exchange's (A35: "the contrasting social positions of the traditions significantly affected their relative commercial and cultural fortunes"), not the abstract's, which is about racialization. The case is [CASE-130](../cases.d/CASE-130.md).

**Corrected 2026-10-22, from the reading.** A35's reading is the body's,
not only the exchange's: the paper says jiu-jitsu became "far more
economically profitable than luta livre" (p. 81) and names "the underlying
class and racial differences" among the causes of luta livre's decline (p.
89), beside the 1991 defeat and press bias, and does not weigh them. What
the exchange added was "significantly". The particulars [CASE-130](../cases.d/CASE-130.md) kept
unchecked (the 1931 rules dispute, the 1991 Grajaú event, the 1997 riot)
