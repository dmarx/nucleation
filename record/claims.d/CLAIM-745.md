# CLAIM-745: Categorical gates displace required reasons

## The claim

The owner's U46 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), quoting A106
§4's "Without that distinction, an institution could effectively manufacture
moral authority by creating and maintaining internal requirements"
([CLAIM-725](CLAIM-725.md)):

> immigration policies under the trump administration (which have functionally closed off all avenues of legal immigration in an effort to moralize racist policies) are a good demonstration of what this can look like in practice.

A108 §4 takes the documented part of it, the 75-country immigrant-visa pause
and its vacatur in *Catholic Legal Immigration Network, Inc. v. Rubio*
([CASE-289](../cases.d/CASE-289.md)), and states the structure. With E(x) for an applicant's
meeting the individualized eligibility requirements and B(x) for the
nationality-based restriction applying, the arrangement set Issue(x) = 0
whenever B(x) = 1, even where E(x) = 1. "This is not a full model of
immigration adjudication; other lawful grounds for refusal can exist."

> The important phenomenon is **decision-making insensitivity to evidence that the governing statutory scheme requires officials to consider**.

"Within the affected category, an applicant could produce evidence
satisfying the individualized criteria without that evidence changing the
categorical refusal." The general mechanism:

> **An authority can introduce a procedural requirement that displaces the relevance of the substantive reasons by which its decisions are supposed to be governed.**

It is distinct from "applying a demanding but justified eligibility
standard" and from "lawfully changing a governing standard through the
appropriate authority". A108 §1 adds the three steps that must be kept
apart: a criterion "has been officially imposed", "is legally authorized",
"is morally justified". "The second does not follow from the first, and the
third does not follow automatically from either." A108 §4 puts it as
Complies(a, D_G) ⇏ Justified_N(a), with D_G the institution's maintained
requirements: "An institution can become perfectly compliant with a
requirement it had no authority to impose."

## The moralization of administrative criteria (the owner's point, A's name)

A108 §4 credits the owner ("Your observation about racialized policy
rationales suggests another mechanism worth studying") and names it "**the
conversion of contested political objectives into apparently neutral
administrative criteria**". A rationale of fiscal responsibility,
security, merit or procedural integrity "does not independently establish
that the criterion accurately measures the concern, is applied
consistently, or is justified in light of its effects." The question it
leaves is "whether an institution's internal rule is being allowed to
**certify the legitimacy of the objective the rule was created to
advance**". The phrase generalizes the owner's "moralize racist policies";
the claim keeps the mechanism and leaves the motive to evidence (below).

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-725](CLAIM-725.md)** is what it rests on: a maintained requirement carries
  no warrant of its own. This claim is the case where the requirement is
  made a gate on an entitlement.
- **[CLAIM-086](CLAIM-086.md)** (Active): a constraint may govern conduct without governing
  the conditions under which it remains authoritative. Here the authority
  governs conduct through a criterion it had, by the court's finding, no
  authority to impose.
- **[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)** (Active): an intervention can make a correction costlier
  or unreachable while the standards stay the same. The gate is one way:
  the statutory standard stood, and evidence under it stopped counting.
- **[CLAIM-535](CLAIM-535.md)** (Active) and **[CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md)** (Active) hold the
  representational version: a policy acting on an observation that merges
  circumstances the standard separates cannot be acceptable in all of them.
  The gate is that merging done by decision, not by a limit of
  observation: the authority could see E(x) and set it aside.

## What it does not say

- Nothing about any official's motives. A108 §1: "the existence and
  operation of a restriction can be documented independently of a
  conclusion about the subjective motives of everyone who designed or
  implemented it." The owner's reading of the motive is recorded in
  [CASE-289](../cases.d/CASE-289.md) as the owner's; this claim does not depend on it.
- Not that the claim is shown by the case to hold of immigration policy
  generally. The owner's "functionally closed off all avenues of legal
  immigration" is broader than the documented pause; A108 §1 calls it "too
  broad".
- Not that every applicant had an entitlement to admission. A108 §1:
  "eligibility to apply for immigration benefits, a statutory entitlement to
  individualized consideration, and an entitlement to admission are
  different legal positions."

## Note of 2026-11-17: CLINIC v. Rubio, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1952 — new reading and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

CLINIC v. Rubio, ECF No. 83, read ([NOTE-2054](../notes.d/NOTE-2054.md), [THEORY-1952](../theory.d/THEORY-1952.md)): a documented specimen, now from the opinion rather than from reports. The Cable, as the court quotes it, directs that where "the applicant provides additional evidence that demonstrates he or she overcomes the public charge refusal, the consular officer should write detailed case notes . . . and refuse the applicant 221(g)" (p. 10). The court finds that "whatever determinations they make as to eligibility for the applicants subject to the Policy are ultimately irrelevant. The outcome is predetermined. The visa will be refused" (p. 42). One detail fits the claim's exact wording: the court held the Policy did not displace the individualized public-charge assessment (p. 32), so the required evidence was still assessed and could not change the decision; that is "unable to change a decision", not "not considered". On authority, the court rejects the Government's argument that officer-entered refusals preserve the consular role as "an exercise in Orwellian logic" (p. 40) and holds that the Policy "displaces consular officers from the role Congress assigned them" (p. 42), which bears out the claim's "that the criterion is imposed, and complied with, establishes neither its legal authority". The opinion says nothing on the Policy's moral justification, the claim's second half. No status changes.
