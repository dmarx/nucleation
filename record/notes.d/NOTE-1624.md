# NOTE-1624: Chen et al. — Topological mechanics of origami and kirigami

## Contribution

It extends topological polarization from spring networks to origami and
kirigami: a 1D origami strip with topologically selected edge folding,
tested in experiment; a conjecture that 2D triangulated origami cannot be
topologically polarized; and polarized kirigami with a flexible domain
wall between rigid regions.

## Key insight

In a structure whose constraints exactly balance its freedoms, where the
freedom lives is set by a topological invariant of the pattern, not by a
local surplus. Join two patterns of different invariant and the seam is
soft, or stressed, though nothing local has changed.

## Assumptions

- **The geometric limit**: "We will consider the mechanics of origami in
  the geometrical limit – folds will cost zero energy and faces do not
  stretch or bend." The experiment departs from it.
- **Periodic structures** with marginal (isostatic) counting.
- **A branch choice** of the vertex configuration space (σ_n = 1); the SI
  notes that two branches meet at the flat state.
- **In 2D**, flat-state degeneracy is broken by small random vertical
  displacements so that a linear rigidity matrix can be used.
- **A "balanced" unit cell** fixes the gauge.

## Key results

- **The strip is marginal**: "As each vertex contributes a DOF and a
  constraint, this origami structure has marginal rigidity." The
  displacement u(j) = 1 + cos f₂(j) obeys u(j+1) = κ u(j), κ a ratio of
  sines of the pattern angles, so the single degree of freedom is
  exponentially localized at one edge; the sign of ln κ is a topological
  polarization, and κ = 1 is the deployable case, such as a Miura-ori row.
- **Linear analysis fails**: "Unlike in periodic spring networks with
  marginal rigidity [17, 33, 34], a linear analysis is inadequate to
  capture the topology of the origami strip." "Coplanar hinges in the flat
  state are redundant constraints, and this results in extra zero modes at
  linear order which do not extend to higher order." A second-order
  rigidity matrix, of SSH form, is built instead.
- **Experiment**: in Mylar–PLA strips, "the decay lengths extracted from
  the fit are in good agreement with 1/l = ln κ"; a uniform bending mode
  appears at small γ.
- **2D origami**: "Periodic origami structures in 2D have the topology of
  the torus and thus χ = 0, which shows that triangulations are marginally
  rigid." For every periodic triangulation tested det R(q) is real, which
  forces zero polarization: "Though a proof of this statement for all
  triangulated origami eludes us, extensive numerical tests on a large
  number of distinct fold patterns bear out this conjecture." Hence "the
  only way to get an imbalance in the number of zero modes at the boundary
  of origami is by locally adding or removing constraints."
- **Kirigami**: cutting holes and merging triangles while keeping
  marginality gives unit cells of polarization (1, 0) and (0, 0). At a
  wall between them, "The zero modes render the heterostructure flexible in
  the vicinity of the domain wall (the mode depicted leads to out-of-plane
  wrinkling), while keeping it rigid further away." "By contrast, a domain
  wall between distinct patterns with equal polarization has no such
  localized modes (see SI)."
- **Soft or stressed**: "domain walls between structures with different
  polarizations create either “soft” lines along which the system easily
  deforms, or “stressed” lines which first bear the loads under applied
  strains [21]."
- **Outlook**: "two structures with different polarizations must be
  separated by globally flexible, i.e. deployable, realizations."

## Concepts

- **Origami**: rigid flat polygonal plates joined by hinges; **kirigami**:
  origami with holes.
- **Marginal rigidity**: an exact balance of degrees of freedom and
  constraints in a periodic structure.
- **Topological polarization** P_T: the winding number of the determinant
  of the rigidity matrix around the Brillouin zone; undefined at κ = 1.
- **Soft and stiff edge**: the edges of high and low deformation in the
  localized mode.

## Connections

It applies the topological polarization of Kane and Lubensky
([LIT-2078](../literature.d/LIT-2078.md), read in [NOTE-1601](NOTE-1601.md)) to folded structures, and its
second-order treatment of the flat state answers to the prestress and
second-order rigidity theory of Connelly and Whiteley ([LIT-2080](../literature.d/LIT-2080.md)). It
is a candidate independent case for Kane and Lubensky's account,
[THEORY-1408](../theory.d/THEORY-1408.md): the boundary modes it finds come from the topological
term.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures** (A, cited as S2).
  A says the paper finds a localized flexible mode at the interface
  between two rigid regions, predicted by a topological distinction.
  Right, but narrower:
  - the interface result is for kirigami; the paper conjectures that 2D
    triangulated origami has zero polarization and so cannot host it;
  - the flexible mode needs different polarizations on the two sides;
  - interfaces between different polarizations can equally be stressed
    lines that carry load, according to the wall's orientation.
  Missing from A: the 1D strip and its experiment; that linearization
  fails at the flat state; and the outlook that distinct phases must be
  separated in design space by deployable realizations.
- **On A's Maxwell–Calladine caveat**, that the count does not by itself
  guarantee finite-motion flexibility: this paper is the plainest support
  in the batch. At the flat state the count finds extra zero modes "at
  linear order which do not extend to higher order".
- **On redundant constraints and self-stress.** The synthesis credits
  Rocks and Mehta ([LIT-2112](../literature.d/LIT-2112.md)) with the point that redundant
  constraints can create states of self-stress; that is A's paraphrase,
  not in their paper, and Damavandi et al. ([LIT-2041](../literature.d/LIT-2041.md)) are the work
  that says it. Here redundant constraints, the coplanar hinges, produce
  spurious linear zero modes instead: the other side of the index
  identity, since at a degenerate configuration the rank drops and both
  counts rise. A's sentence covers only one side.
- **On A's Part 5 question**, under what conditions coupling two locally
  rigid subsystems creates new degrees of freedom at their interface: for
  this mechanical case the answer is specific. A flexible interface needs
  the two sides in topologically distinct phases, and with constraints
  locally balanced the opposite pairing gives stress, not freedom. A
  conditional answer is available; a blanket expectation that coupling
  rigid parts creates freedom is not.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md)** (composing interactions can turn an interior into a
  constraint). Qualifies: composing two rigid kirigami phases produces an
  effective freedom at the interface, or an effective stress line.
  Composition can create freedoms as well as constraints.

## Limitations

- **The 2D reality property is a conjecture**, supported by numerics, with
  no proof beyond the subdivision case.
- **The phases of the modified kirigami lattice** are left for future work.
- **The experiment departs from the ideal limit** (facet bending, crease
  thickness), giving a uniform-mode background at small γ.
- **The domain-wall result is numerical**, with no experiment.
- **Linear, or second-order for the strip, about one configuration**;
  finite folding paths are not analysed.

## Open questions

- Is every periodic triangulated origami unpolarized?
- Do the domain-wall modes of kirigami persist as finite motions, and can
  they be shown in experiment?
