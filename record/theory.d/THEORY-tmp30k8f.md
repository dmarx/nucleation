# THEORY-tmp30k8f: Tognazzini and Coates find no agreed account of what blame is, among cognitive, emotional, conative and functional theories, and hold that whether blaming is appropriate depends not only on whether its target is blameworthy but on the blaming interaction and on the blamer, whose standing hypocrisy, complicity in the wrong and meddling can undermine

## Source

The SEP entry "Blame" by Neal Tognazzini and D. Justin Coates (2014, rev.
2024), read in NOTE-tmp460sz as the stand-in for Scanlon's Moral Dimensions
(LIT-tmpnt5fk) and beside LIT-691 for Strawson (LIT-tmpbw6vo).

## What was actually shown

A survey's verdict, not a proof. Each family of accounts meets an objection
it has not answered. Against cognitive theories: "they risk conflating
blaming with judging blameworthy". Against Scanlon's conative account,
Wallace says it "leaves the blame out of blame", and there is the blame of
strangers. Protest theories risk circularity. For appropriateness, the
entry separates the blamed (agency; freedom, threatened by "coercion,
manipulation, situational pressures"), the interaction (proportionality,
evidence) and the blamer. On the blamer it reports Cohen's complicity list,
"you forced me to do it, you left me with no reasonable alternative", and
concludes that "The superior officer who orders a subordinate to do
something morally reprehensible is not in a position to blame the
subordinate for carrying out the order, even if civilians are."

That corroborates CLAIM-tmpnw7ez from the side of blame. A party who
structured another's circumstances so that the wrong became hard to avoid
loses standing to blame the actor, while the actor stays blameworthy to
others. Both responsibilities hold of one act. The source is independent of
the exchange. QUESTION-468 asks who answers for exploitation when the
arrangement is not an agent. The reported argument of Watson (2015) and
Beade (2019), that a state partly responsible for the conditions of crime
may lack standing to punish it, is a nearby institutional case.

## What this does not say

- Which account of blame is right. The entry endorses none.
- That the complicit party is responsible for the actor's act. Complicity
  is reported as defeating standing, not as a share in the act.
- Anything first-hand about Scanlon's or Strawson's texts beyond the
  quoted definition (Scanlon 2008: 128–29).
