# CLAIM-670: An arrangement can produce the dependence through which it exploits: by reliably supplying what a party then ceases to supply for itself, it creates a reliance on the provider that the party did not have before, and the provider can use that reliance to set terms the party could earlier have refused

## The claim

A9 §2 separated two mechanisms. Exploitation of an existing dependency: "A
party already depends on something another controls, and that dependency
is used to obtain benefits on unjust terms." Dependency-producing
exploitation: "An arrangement initially expands access or opportunity
but, through its terms and repeated operation, creates new
vulnerabilities or undermines the participant's ability to negotiate or
exit." And: "The second mechanism deserves independent investigation. It
is not automatically implied by the first."

[CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md) first stated the second mechanism, and was narrowed on
2026-11-12, after its one readable source, to a new encumbrance within a
need that predates the access. The owner's review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488) on 2026-11-13:
"I would preserve the distinction between producing a dependency and
producing a new encumbrance within an existing dependency. These may be
different causal mechanisms, and the record should not lose that
distinction merely because the first source better demonstrates the
second." This claim keeps the first.

## How it works

Two steps, the first held, the second not.

1. **The dependence is produced.** [CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md): a capability lost because
   another reliably supplies it is favoured to the one who loses it, and
   one-way supply leaves a dependence that nothing in the dependent's
   dynamics limits and that withdrawal punishes. So the party comes to
   rely on the provider for something it once did for itself.
2. **The dependence is used.** Once the party cannot cheaply resume
   supplying itself or switch provider, the provider can worsen the terms
   of supply. That is [CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md)'s coercive leverage, reached by a route
   that began as an offer the party had reason to accept.

The difference from [CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md) is where the dependence comes from: here
the arrangement makes it; there the need came first and the arrangement
added an encumbrance.

## What it does not say

- **What makes the terms exploitative.** That needs a standard the record
  does not supply ([CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md), [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)).
- **That the mechanism is common, or that any case read shows it.** None
  does yet.
- **That the colonial theatre is an instance.** Slavery's dependence
  predated it ([CASE-257](../cases.d/CASE-257.md)).

## Note of 2026-11-17: hold-up as a second route to produced dependence, and a misattributed source, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-728 — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17, Proposed -->

A53 §2 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b, 2026-11-16), at the owner's U24 ("labor theory, contract theory, and auction theory are feeling relevant"), relays the hold-up problem of incomplete-contract theory: a worker invests years in skills "useful primarily to one employer"; once the investment is made "the employee's outside options may be limited", and "The employer can attempt to renegotiate compensation, knowing the employee would lose much of their investment by leaving." A ties it to this claim ("It fits our discussion of dependency-producing exploitation"): "**The original exchange can be advantageous and apparently voluntary while progressively changing the power each participant possesses over later exchanges.**"

It is a second route to the dependence this claim is about. Here the provider's reliable supply leads the party to stop supplying for itself. In hold-up the party's own relationship-specific investment, often invited by the provider, makes leaving costly; the provider supplies nothing new. Both produce a reliance the party did not have and that the provider can use to set terms the party could earlier have refused, which is the claim's conclusion. A names no source; the standard ones are in the incomplete-contract work A cites beside it (Grossman and Hart 1986; Hart and Moore 1990), carried in [CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md), and none is held.

A49's provenance table pairs this claim with [CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md) in a row whose literature is Seamster and Charron-Chénier ([LIT-2525](../literature.d/LIT-2525.md)). Only [CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md) cites that work, through its reading of it, and this claim's summary says why: "Seamster and Charron-Chénier show the second mechanism, not this one." A49's body uses the work correctly, for access "extended on terms that reproduce or deepen vulnerability", which is [CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md)'s mechanism.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity', A22

<!-- inactive-ok-file: LIT-tmpgcwyt CLAIM-713 CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 LIT-2742 — Proposed, Deferred or Unfetched; the hold-up paper registered on 2026-11-24, the held claim on exploited commitment, the fallback claim filed with this, and Hart and Moore -->

A22 §7 (ChatGPT, 2026-11-24) cites this claim correctly for the first of
"two directions of dependency exploitation": "Provider exploits recipient
... The recipient loses alternatives. The provider acquires leverage over
the terms of supply", with "economic hold-up after relationship-specific
investment" as its example. The second direction, the recipient exploiting
the provider's commitment, it gives to [CLAIM-713](CLAIM-713.md).

A22 §9 relays hold-up a second time, now with a source and a model.

- **The source.** "Klein, Crawford and Alchian's 1978 work on appropriable
  quasi-rents" is registered as [LIT-tmpgcwyt](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgcwyt.md) (Deferred, identifier checked
  on Crossref). The note of 2026-11-17 above says no hold-up source was
  held; Hart and Moore 1990 has since been registered ([LIT-2742](../literature.d/LIT-2742.md),
  Unfetched), so that sentence is now out of date.
- **The model.** Equal-weight Nash bargaining over joint value V with
  outside options o_A and o_B gives u_B = o_B + (V − o_A − o_B)/2. As B's
  relationship-specific investment lowers o_B, B's share falls by half of
  each unit lost and A's rises by the same: with V = 20 and o_A = 4, B gets
  10, 9 and 8 as o_B is 4, 2 and 0 (checked with `python3 -I`). A22's
  caption keeps the verdict out of the model: "This establishes bargaining
  vulnerability, not by itself exploitation or injustice."
- **The point A draws.** "An arrangement can preserve the appearance of
  fair negotiation while its historical development progressively changes
  the conditions under which negotiations occur. The parties may continue
  to consent to the terms. But what they can afford to refuse has changed."
  That is this claim's conclusion, "terms the party could earlier have
  refused", in the bargaining model's terms.

The same computation, read the other way, is why an unexercised fallback
keeps a party's terms: [CLAIM-tmp2jhc1](CLAIM-tmp2jhc1.md). The claim is unchanged.
