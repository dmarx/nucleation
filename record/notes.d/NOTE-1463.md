# NOTE-1463: Liang, De Los Rios & Busiello — Thermodynamic space of reaction networks

## Contribution

Bounds of this kind were known for linear networks (Maes and Netočný) and
for catalytic ones, where the network is still a graph (the authors' own
earlier work). The paper proves them for arbitrary stoichiometry. Without
any kinetic information, a network's topology and its chemostatted driving
fix a region of concentration space, the **thermodynamic space**, that
contains every stationary state any choice of rate constants can produce
(eqs. 20, 24, 29). It then uses the region to bound bistability, chiral
imbalance, self-assembly yield and pattern contrast, to falsify candidate
topologies, and to infer a minimum driving from measured concentrations.

## Key insight

A steady current through a reaction must close into a cycle (an elementary
flux mode) that runs from fuel to waste, and every reaction along that cycle
pushes forward. So no step can take more of the cycle's free energy than the
whole cycle has (eqs. 15–17). Turning affinities into concentrations, the
stationary value of any product of concentrations must lie between what it
would be at equilibrium along the least favourable pathway and along the
most favourable one (eq. 28). Driving widens the space; it does not choose a
point in it.

## Assumptions

- **Open, dilute, mass-action networks, all reactions reversible**, with
  chemostatted species at fixed concentrations (§III A). The proofs use
  only the local detailed balance J⁺/J⁻ = exp(A/RT) (eq. 8), "not ... the
  specific form of the kinetics" (p. 5).
- **A stationary state with nonzero concentrations exists.** "Our framework
  does not capture the case of persistent oscillations, but it holds for
  CRNs exhibiting multi-stability" (p. 2).
- **Fixed chemostats.** The driving is a constant budget. How long it lasts
  is not considered.
- **Conformal decomposition** of the steady flux into elementary flux modes
  (EFMs), a standard result (§III B).

## Key results

- **Affinity bounds (eq. 20).** min(0, min_{E⁻_ρ} A^Y_e/e_ρ) ≤ A^ss_ρ ≤
  max(0, max_{E⁺_ρ} A^Y_e/e_ρ), over EFMs containing ρ, normalised by the
  number of times ρ is used. Equality holds at equilibrium and for
  reactions in no cycle.
- **Chemical probe (§IV A, eq. 24).** An auxiliary reaction ρ̂ compatible
  with the conservation laws, infinitely slow so it carries no flux but has
  a finite affinity, "analogous to a voltmeter in an electric circuit"
  (p. 9). Its affinity obeys a two-sided bound from the EFMs it closes.
- **Thermodynamic space (eq. 29).** K^min_{π} ≤ Π_i (x^ss_i)^{S_iρ} ≤
  K^max_{π}, with K_π = exp(−Δ_π G/RT) for each pathway π converting the
  species on one side of ρ (or ρ̂) into the other. Each bound "can be
  saturated ... when the dynamics is kinetically dominated by the
  corresponding reaction pathway" (p. 3). At equilibrium the two coincide,
  and "a non-zero width of the thermodynamic space is a signature of
  non-equilibrium conditions and a prerequisite for the emergence of complex
  behaviors" (p. 3).
- **Topology test (§IV C, Fig. 4).** A measured dimer amplification above
  Δμ/RT cannot come from the network without 2B ⇌ C, whose EFM uses the
  driven step twice and raises the ceiling to 2Δμ/RT. Exceeding a ceiling
  falsifies a topology; a missing reaction that does not re-use the
  driving is invisible.
- **Schlögl (§V A, eq. 36).** exp(−(μ°_X − μ_B)/RT) ≤ x^ss ≤
  exp(−(μ°_X − μ_A)/RT). Both stable and the unstable fixed point lie
  inside; at Δ_e G = 0 the bounds "coincide, collapsing the thermodynamic
  space and precluding bistable solutions" (p. 13). Checked: with
  k₁± = 8, k₂± = 1, b = 0.02, a = 0.8 the fixed points are 0.0234, 0.1787
  and 0.5979, inside [0.02, 0.8]; at a = 0.5 there is one, 0.0218; at
  a = b the space and the fixed point are both 0.02.
- **Chiral symmetry breaking (§V B, eq. 39).** |ln(r/s)| ≤ Δμ/RT, saturated
  as the dimerisation becomes fast; "the emergence of perfect homochirality
  ... necessitates an infinite driving force" (p. 15).
- **Self-assembly and patterns (§V C–D).** Trimer amplification is bounded
  by 3Δμ (eq. 46); reaction–diffusion contrast by tanh(Δμ/2mRT) (eq. 54),
  approached as D_u/D_v → 0.
- **Data (§VI).** GroEL–MDH concentrations: native dimer to monomer stays at
  equilibrium even with ATP, while native to misfolded is displaced and lies
  within the space (Fig. 9b–c). A photoacid: a minimum light-driven rate
  inferred from concentrations recovers 75–85% of the true one (Fig. 9f).

## Concepts

- **Elementary flux mode (EFM)** — a support-minimal vector in ker S_X: a
  minimal set of reactions that leaves internal species unchanged (§III B).
- **Cycle affinity A^Y_e** — the free energy the chemostats lose per turn of
  an EFM (eq. 14), independent of internal concentrations.
- **Chemical probe** — an infinitely slow virtual reaction used to read the
  affinity between species the network does not connect directly.
- **Thermodynamic space** — the region of stationary concentrations
  compatible with the network's topology and driving, for all kinetics.

## Connections

It uses the open-network framework of Rao and Esposito ([LIT-1889](../literature.d/LIT-1889.md),
[NOTE-1464](NOTE-1464.md)), whose emergent cycles are its driven EFMs, and the
hypergraph cycle geometry of Dal Cengio et al. ([LIT-1897](../literature.d/LIT-1897.md),
[NOTE-1476](NOTE-1476.md)). It generalises Maes and Netočný's bound for linear networks
and Liang and Pigolotti's flux-ratio bound (eq. 33). Prigogine's question,
which structure appears, is [THEORY-692](../theory.d/THEORY-692.md)'s; this paper bounds where any can
appear and does not choose.

## Bearing on the record

- **A13's use of it: half right, and the other half belongs to another
  paper.** The registration says A13 used it for "the intersection of the
  dynamically viable region with what thermodynamic driving permits". That
  is what the paper gives ([THEORY-1261](../theory.d/THEORY-1261.md)). A13 also used it for "an
  organization ... can still have a finite lifetime because its driving is
  exhausted". The paper says nothing of that: its chemostats are fixed and
  its states stationary. Exhaustion of driving, and the time at which it
  happens, is Remlein, Esposito and Avanzini ([LIT-1887](../literature.d/LIT-1887.md), [NOTE-1468](NOTE-1468.md)).
  The LIT's "Why this is here" should say so.
- **[CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md), rewritten.** The probe ∅ → c₁ has two pathways, from
  fuel through the catalysed reaction and from waste through reversed decay.
  So (d′/d)[W] ≤ c₁ ≤ (k/k′)[S]. In my rewriting ([S] = 3, unit rates) every
  fixed point, stable or not, lies in [[W], 3] for [W] = 0.01, 0.08 and 1. The
  lower edge is the extinct state's floor, set by the waste; the upper is the
  maintained state's ceiling, set by the fuel. The space is empty of width
  when [W] = 3, which is why the rewriting has a single equilibrium there.
  The construction's bistability needs a width it does not have below
  3.52 RT ([NOTE-1464](NOTE-1464.md)).
- **[CLAIM-273](../claims.d/CLAIM-273.md) and [THEORY-692](../theory.d/THEORY-692.md).** The paper makes concrete [THEORY-692](../theory.d/THEORY-692.md)'s point
  that thermodynamics bounds and does not select. Driving fixes a region;
  which state in it is occupied is "kinetically dominated" (p. 3). It is no
  support for maximum entropy production ([THEORY-671](../theory.d/THEORY-671.md)) or for driven
  adaptation ([THEORY-412](../theory.d/THEORY-412.md)) as selection principles, and no evidence against
  them.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md).** The bounds are attained in time-scale-separation limits:
  the Schlögl upper bound when reaction 1 is much faster than reaction 2
  (p. 13), the chiral bound as dimerisation becomes fast (eq. 40). A fast
  pathway reaches pseudo-equilibrium and fixes the concentrations the slow
  one must work against. That is the claim's first half in a chemical form,
  with a bound on how far the separation can carry it.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **Stationary states only.** Oscillations, chaos and transients are
  outside it, as the authors say (p. 2, p. 20).
- **Necessary, not sufficient.** Inside the space says nothing about whether
  a state is reached, stable or unique.
- **Saturation conditions** are argued from examples, not proved.
- **EFM enumeration grows combinatorially** (App. B); the bounds for large
  networks depend on targeted searches the paper does not supply.
- **Appendices skimmed.**

## Open questions

- Is there a lower bound on the driving needed for multistability in a
  given topology, as distinct from the space in which it must lie? The
  rewriting in [NOTE-1464](NOTE-1464.md) needs 3.52 RT; the space alone gives only
  "more than zero".
- What becomes of the space when the chemostats deplete ([LIT-1887](../literature.d/LIT-1887.md))?
