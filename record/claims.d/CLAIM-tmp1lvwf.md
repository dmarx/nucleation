# CLAIM-tmp1lvwf: Under any definition of interpretation that does not presuppose experience, most interpretation is unconscious, as blindsight and subpersonal perception and motor control show, so interpretive organization is not sufficient for experience, and the constitutive thesis is incomplete until it names the further organization that makes an interpretation experiential

## The objection

[CLAIM-tmpdga80](CLAIM-tmpdga80.md) defines interpretation without experience, so that the
identity is not circular. A3 named the cost: "Blindsight illustrates that
visual information can guide discrimination and behavior in the apparent
absence of corresponding conscious visual experience", and "much of
perception, motor control, and predictive processing involves
sophisticated information integration without clearly corresponding
conscious episodes." A1 had already pressed it: "A distributed
computational system could produce an impressively coherent interpretation
without there being anything it is like to be that system."

So the thesis has two options. It can take interpretation unrestricted
and count these cases as experience, which A1 said "would need to be
embraced as a substantive implication rather than allowed to slip in
unnoticed". Or it can restrict interpretation, and then it owes the
restriction. A3: the challenge "doesn't undermine the constitutive
thesis, but it forces a specification of which forms of interpretive
organization qualify."

## Why it stands

None of the candidates the exchange offered is yet a criterion:

- **Global availability.** The workspace view (A3). A3 added that it
  "isn't necessarily sufficient for phenomenal experience". The record's
  workspace and no-report readings ([QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)) are about access.
- **Integrated causal structure.** IIT (A3). The COGITATE result
  ([LIT-973](../literature.d/LIT-973.md)) challenged predictions of both IIT and the workspace view.
- **Higher-order or recursive modelling** (A3, [LIT-tmpx8g8s](../literature.d/LIT-tmpx8g8s.md)), with the risk of
  making experience depend on reflection.
- **Perspectival organization.** A7's own candidate. A7 conceded that "the
  existence of an operative frame does not yet prove experience. A
  corporation could possess such a frame."
- **Endogenous valuation** (A7 §12), with the thermostat as its warning:
  "The presence of a regulatory objective alone cannot be enough."

A7's formal statement of the gap: "No necessary-and-sufficient criterion
yet distinguishes experiential interpretive organization from
nonexperiential information processing." It called this "not a minor
omission".

## What it does not say

- It does not say that the thesis is false. Production views face the
  same cases. They say which processes produce experience, as the thesis
  must say which ones constitute it.
- It does not say that unconscious interpretation is a different kind of
  process. It may differ only in organization, which is what the thesis
  needs.
- It does not take sides on where to draw the line, or on whether the line
  is sharp. A7 left a graded account open.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the candidates tried since

U8–U11 of the same exchange tried four more conditions ([QUESTION-tmpc4j7p](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.md),
note of 2026-10-22): reciprocal integration, interventional sufficiency,
self-maintenance and cross-context answerability. None meets this claim's
defeater. Reciprocity also produces self-confirming capture ([CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md)).
Interventional sufficiency describes a regime adequately but "would not
automatically be a judgment about self-governance" ([TERM-067](../terms.d/TERM-067.md)). Self-maintenance was withdrawn as necessary
([TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md)). Answerability is untested. A8 stated the objection in this
claim's terms, as "the unconscious cognition objection": if unconscious
neural systems "instantiate all of it, we either need to accept more
consciousness than ordinarily attributed or acknowledge that our criterion
is insufficient. An appeal to reporting would merely recreate the
access-consciousness problem." A11's synthesis keeps the gap open:
"Which organizational properties distinguish those perspectives from
nonexperiential interpretive systems remains an open theoretical
question."
