# NOTE-tmpvf8za: Seamster and Charron-Chénier (2017) — Predatory Inclusion and Education Debt: Rethinking the Racial Wealth Gap

## Contribution

Defines predatory inclusion as a general mechanism of racial inequality and extends it from mortgages to education debt, with descriptive and regression evidence that the gap is not explained by attainment or socioeconomic controls.

## Key results

1. Definition ("Predatory Inclusion: Race and Debt in the United States"): "Predatory inclusion refers to a process whereby members of a marginalized group are provided with access to a good, service, or opportunity from which they have historically been excluded but under conditions that jeopardize the benefits of this access."
2. Same section: "In the long term, however, predatory inclusion reproduces marginalization for these groups while allowing already-dominant social actors to derive significant profits."
3. Abstract: "lenders and financial actors offer needed services to black households, but on exploitative terms that limit or eliminate their long-term benefits."
4. "Debt Patterns": "Black educational debt roughly tripled between 2001 and 2013, growing from $2,422 to $7,531 on average." And "Almost one in three black households (32 percent) held education debt in 2013, compared to less than one in five (18 percent) white households."
5. "Changes in Educational Attainment": "changes in educational attainment for black and white households between 2000 and 2013 do not explain racial gaps in student debt or their increase over time."
6. "Other Potential Explanations", on private lending: "Indeed, this is where we suspect a significant part of the inequality-generating action is."
7. Discussion: "Unlike homeowners, students cannot foreclose on their degrees and have their debt liabilities wiped clean." "Student debt is legally very difficult to discharge, even when declaring bankruptcy".
8. Discussion: households "will potentially be permanently saddled with debt repayment obligations that divert income from other uses and make it hard to obtain loans for other productive investments".

## Assumptions

Race is the household head's, as the survey codes it. The prior wealth and income gap is given: "Because of lower average income and wealth levels, black borrowers likely have to take on greater absolute and relative debt levels than their white counterparts". Higher education as a mobility tool with lower returns for Black graduates is cited, not tested.

## Concepts

Predatory inclusion; market incorporation; racialized debt; for-profit higher education; private and federal loans; nondischargeability.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmp16mkz** (exploitation through the access an arrangement grants): qualifies. It supports exploitation through inclusion that succeeds (the access is real, and the harm comes through it) and that widening access need not reduce it (access and the gap rose together). It does not support the claim's first version as worded: the need to borrow predates the access, coming from the wealth gap; what the access produces is a lasting debt that narrows later options, not reliance on the provider; exit is closed by bankruptcy law, not by the lender's terms; and the mechanism is conjectured. The claim is narrowed in its version 2, and the support judgement is ARG-tmpou39l.
- **CLAIM-428**: an offer of opportunity whose terms turn it into vulnerability, a case of the claim's framing. Not written as a relation.
- **QUESTION-tmpojwyk**: little bearing; the paper is about markets and households, not an organization's dependence on its subordinated members.
