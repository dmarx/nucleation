# THEORY-tmpo8don: Rice and colleagues report that, in children conceived by IVF, a mother's late-pregnancy stress went with lower birth weight, shorter gestation and more antisocial behaviour whether or not she was the child's genetic mother, while its link with ADHD symptoms appeared only when she was, and read the first as prenatal environmental and the second as inherited

## The account

Rice, Harold, Boivin, van den Bree, Hay and Thapar (2010), LIT-tmpr7q37.
779 IVF families: 387 homologous IVF, 187 sperm donation, 170 egg
donation, 35 embryo donation. With egg or embryo donation, the pregnant
mother is unrelated to the child (205 pairs). Late-pregnancy stress
predicted birth weight (unrelated β = −0.189; related −0.127), gestational
age and antisocial behaviour (0.190; 0.186) in both groups. It predicted
anxiety in both until the mother's current anxiety and depression were
adjusted, which then mediated it. It predicted ADHD symptoms only in
related pairs (β = 0.163 against 0.095; interaction p = 0.05).

## Evidence

A cross-sectional cohort with retrospective maternal ratings of stress,
validated against antenatal records of birth size. The test that could
have come out otherwise: in unrelated pairs there could have been no
association, as there was none for ADHD.

## What it does not show

- **It does not show germline transmission, or its absence.** The design
  sets what the gamete carries, genes and any epigenetic mark, against
  what the carrying mother supplies. A germline epigenetic effect would
  appear as "inherited", indistinguishable from a genetic one
  (CLAIM-tmpv8nto).
- It does not show causation, as the authors say. The exposure is
  ordinary stress, not trauma.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297's routes: the womb route is real for some outcomes, and the
donor-gamete design is the one design in the literature that removes both
the womb and rearing from a comparison of what the child inherits. It is
a ground of CLAIM-tmp3qsdg and CLAIM-tmpv8nto.
