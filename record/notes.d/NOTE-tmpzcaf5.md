# NOTE-tmpzcaf5: Chalmers — Foreword to Supersizing the Mind

## Contribution

The foreword is the first place either author says in print what the
extended-mind thesis does not need and where it is weakest. It names the
perception-action boundary as the one principled objection. It supplies a
contextualist reply that [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) did not have. It states that consciousness
probably does not extend, with a reason: no twin case. And it detaches the
thesis from functionalism about consciousness.

## Key insight

"Why did Otto walk north?" and "why did Otto reach for his notebook?" are
both good psychological questions. The first is best answered with an
extended belief and the second with an internal one. So whether the
belief is extended can depend on which question is asked, like a Necker
cube. On this view the deeper point of the thesis is an explanatory
unification, not a fact about where a belief is.

## Assumptions

- **No privileged skin-and-skull boundary.** Assumed, as in [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md).
- **Parity as a weak functionalism.** "If a state plays the same causal role
  in the cognitive network as a mental state, then there is a presumption
  of mentality, one that can only be defeated by displaying a relevant
  difference." Combined with the "observation" that there are no relevant
  differences, the thesis follows. The foreword says the observation "does
  not require functionalism for its support". It does not show that it
  holds.
- **Twin cases are the test of extension.** An argument for extended
  consciousness "would require twins with different states of
  consciousness".
- **Revisionism is allowed.** If a state shares a belief's important
  explanatory features, "then it really is a belief", whatever common sense
  says.

## Key results

- **The iPhone (opening).** It stores numbers and addresses, holds "my
  desires" (a memo of favourite dishes), calculates, settles arguments via
  Google, plans with its calendar, and is where he daydreams. "The iPhone is
  part of my mind already." If it were implanted, "all this would do is
  speed up the processing, and free up my hands".
- **History.** The 1998 paper grew from Clark's March 1995 manuscript "Mind
  and World: Breaching the Plastic Frontier", which already had the Tetris
  thought experiment and "the crucial Parity Principle". The paper was
  written in 1995 at Washington University. "The inspiration behind the
  original article was all his."
- **The footnote.** The order-of-belief footnote does not mean Chalmers
  rejects the thesis. He is "more confident of the thesis than I was a
  decade ago", though he does "not think the matter is completely cut
  and dried".
- **Objections that do not worry him.** That external processes work
  differently, that the mind will extend too far, and that the brain's core
  role will be lost.
- **The principled objection.** Perception is where the world affects the
  mind and action where the mind affects the world. If what lies outside
  these is not mental, Otto's notebook is outside the mind, because Otto
  must read and write.
- **Replies considered.** (1) [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)'s claim that Otto's access need not be
  perceptual "is surely too quick". (2) Inner perception and mental action
  can be compared to Otto's reading and writing, but an opponent can insist
  on "real perception and real action". (3) The Terminator reply: reading
  from a screen does not disqualify memory. An opponent "could hold the
  line", "and perhaps this opponent would have commonsense psychology on
  their side". In that case Clark's "commonsense functionalism" counts
  against the thesis.
- **The reply he favours.** "Not be afraid of a little revisionism": if
  Otto's extended state functions in explanation as belief does, it should
  be classed as belief. But perception and action do matter for some
  explanations, hence the Necker cube. Four semantic stories are offered
  for this: contextualism (the ascription is true in some contexts, false
  in others), always true, always false, and indeterminacy. The 7 × 8 = 56
  hesitation case shows the same context-dependence without extension.
  "Ultimately", these are "terminological questions". "The deeper point is
  that extended states can function in explanation in very much the same
  way that beliefs function." "This explanatory unification is the real
  underlying point."
- **Extending the thesis.** Something can be said for extended desire,
  reasoning, perception (the iPhone camera), imagination and mood, "if not
  an extended emotion".
- **Consciousness.** "No principled reason" why its physical basis could
  not extend, and it probably does in some possible worlds. But "it is
  unlikely that any everyday process akin to Otto's interaction with his
  notebook will yield extended consciousness, at least in our world", since
  no Olga/Twin Olga case works. "Perhaps" consciousness needs very
  high-bandwidth direct access. Dretske, Fisher and Martin's phenomenal
  externalisms are "largely independent" of active externalism. Hurley and
  Noë do not yield a twin case, so they at best show a weaker dependence.
  "I tentatively conclude that the extension of the mind is compatible
  with retaining an internal conscious core."
- **Presuppositions.** The thesis does not need functionalism about the
  mental, is compatible with physicalism and dualism, with connectionist
  and classical views, and with internalism and externalism about content.

## Concepts

- **Parity Principle** — named as such here ("the crucial Parity
  Principle"), attributed to Clark's 1995 draft, and glossed as a
  defeasible presumption of mentality. [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md) records that [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) never
  uses the name.
- **commonsense functionalism** — Clark's individuation of mental states by
  the roles commonsense psychology assigns them. The foreword says it may
  count against the thesis.
- **Necker cube effect** — mental states "counting as extended or not
  depending on our perspective and our purposes".
- **internal conscious core** — the tentatively held view that
  consciousness supervenes on the internal.

## Limitations

- It is a foreword: eight pages, and several positions are marked
  "perhaps" or "tentatively".
- The no-twin-case claim for consciousness is asserted after an unshown
  search.
- The contextualist reply is one of four semantic options, and the foreword
  declines to choose.
- The "no relevant differences" observation, which the parity argument
  needs, is asserted here, not argued.

## Open questions

- Which semantic story is right: contextualism, error, metaphor or
  indeterminacy?
- Can an Olga/Twin Olga case be built with a high-bandwidth or
  non-perceptual link? The 2019 chapter says yes for an implanted circuit.
- If belief-ascription follows explanatory purpose, does the same hold for
  self-ascription, and so for the extended self?
