# THEORY-tmps5brs: Purity is moralized in self-report: in morality-as-cooperation's own questionnaire studies, purity items are rated about as morally relevant as loyalty and authority items, and harmless purity violations are condemned, so that theory's prediction that non-cooperative behaviour is morally neutral fails unless purity is itself cooperative

## Source

Curry, Jones Chesters & Van Lissa (2019), [LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md), read in [NOTE-412](../notes.d/NOTE-412.md),
Studies 2 and 3a. The prediction is Curry (2016), [LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md), read in
[NOTE-408](../notes.d/NOTE-408.md), p. 40. The harmless-wrongs studies are reported in Haidt (2001),
[LIT-511](../literature.d/LIT-511.md), read in [NOTE-403](../notes.d/NOTE-403.md).

## What was actually shown

**The prediction.** "Behaviour that has nothing to do with cooperation …
will be regarded as morally neutral" ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md), p. 40). The chapter says MFT's
purity domain has "no apparent connection to cooperation" (p. 42).

**UK, n = 1,042 ([LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md), Study 2).** MFQ Relevance means on a 1–6 scale:
Care 4.63, Fairness 4.46, Authority 3.89, Purity 3.80, Ingroup 3.77. Purity
forms a distinct Judgement factor.

**US, n = 469 ([LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md), Study 3a).** MFQ purity Relevance mean 4.19, against
4.31 for ingroup and authority. The MFQ purity Judgement subscale has α =
.75, the highest of the MFQ Judgement subscales.

**The authors' reading.** Purity's relevance "appear[s] to contradict" the
theory and "remains to be explained" (p. 27). Their reply is that purity is
not a coherent domain and that disgust may serve several cooperation
problems (p. 40, endnote xv).

**Harmless wrongs ([LIT-511](../literature.d/LIT-511.md), p. 5).** In Haidt, Koller & Dias (1993),
Americans and Brazilians condemned harmless offensive acts, such as eating
one's dead dog, and affect predicted the judgments better than claims of
harm.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Purity items could have been rated
irrelevant, near the bottom of the scale. They were rated with loyalty and
authority, which the theory counts as cooperative.

## What this does not say

- **That morality-as-cooperation is refuted.** The theory also predicts that
  sexual, religious and dietary morality will turn out cooperative ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md),
  p. 40). If purity is cooperative, the observation is no counterexample.
  Fitouchi, André & Baumard ([THEORY-tmpbzrkk](THEORY-tmpbzrkk.md)) argue that it is, for puritanical norms.
- **How often societies moralize purity.** This is self-report from people
  asked about purity, in WEIRD online samples. It is not blind coding of what
  a society moralizes, which is the test [THEORY-tmp8vbpr](THEORY-tmp8vbpr.md) needs.
- **That disgust explains it.** Whether purity condemnation tracks disgust,
  harm or perceived self-control is the dispute between [THEORY-tmphcyt1](THEORY-tmphcyt1.md) and [THEORY-tmpbzrkk](THEORY-tmpbzrkk.md), and
  these data do not touch it.
- **That purity is one domain.** MFQ purity is a single factor in some
  samples. In others it merges with ingroup and authority; in the US sample
  of Study 3a the MFQ's Relevance items give two factors, one of them
  Ingroup/Authority/Purity.

- The harmless-wrongs studies are reported in Haidt's social intuitionist
  model ([THEORY-tmpjpjf8](THEORY-tmpjpjf8.md)) as evidence that affect, not perceived harm,
  drives moral judgment. Here they serve only as evidence that purity
  violations are condemned.

  Purity factor ([THEORY-tmpqgklr](THEORY-tmpqgklr.md)), whose cross-national comparisons are its least
