# SYNTHESIS-098: Remedies foreclosed before review, by faits accomplis, speed, friction and siege: acting before an opportunity to intervene can take effect is proposed as a wrong of its own, an irreversible act is proposed to convert a holder's consent into a claim to compensation, to give a reviewer genuine reasons to accommodate it, and to give the breacher leverage over its own settlement but no power over what is owed, an opponent can put a remedy out of reach by raising what pursuing it costs, the cost of imposing a decision and of contesting it can move apart, and the baseline against which terms are judged can be the actor's own product

*A part account of the dependence-and-domination and distributed-agency lines, on remedies foreclosed before they can be reviewed: by acting first, by outlasting, by moving friction, and by setting the baseline. Version 1 was filed at the interim synthesis review of 2026-11-17, before the readings and the reconciliation. Version 2 is the follow-up review of 2026-11-18. It adds the first filing's CLAIM-701, describes CLAIM-710 as it stands after the reconciliation (the curation entry of 2026-11-17, 20:57:46, its reconciliation section), and cites the readings as the support each component has, by the THEORY that carries the relation; a reading is not a component. Most components complement or rest on CLAIM-464 (Active, the owner's earlier observation that an intervention can make a corrective outcome costlier or unreachable before review while the standards stay the same). The account cites CLAIM-464 and does not integrate it. The line's account is SYNTHESIS-095; the sibling part, on answerability and contestability, is SYNTHESIS-103.*

## The account

**The wrong of acting first.** CLAIM-723 (the owner's, U27, with A59's
four conditions, three kinds of standing and the narrowing to one
mechanism of bullying; thesis, interpretive, Proposed) grounds on
THEORY-1972 and complements CLAIM-464, CLAIM-624, CLAIM-748, CLAIM-734
and CLAIM-716: where a party has standing to authorize, suspend or
contest an act, an actor who without adequate justification makes its
consequences irreversible before that standing can be exercised defeats
it, "even where the objection would have failed". Its body says
CLAIM-464 "holds the timing" and "is causal and says nothing about
standing"; it keeps the single act apart from "the wrong of
constructing a relationship in which such acts can routinely prevail",
which it calls CLAIM-624's. It names the family "wrongful unilateral
action", with remedial hold-up (CLAIM-716) and counter-corrective
domination (CLAIM-619) among its kinds (A59 §9).

*Readings.* Its ground, THEORY-1972 (Fuentes v. Shevin, 1972, which A59
cited and the record had not read), locates the wrong in the timing: the
hearing "must be granted at a time when the deprivation can still be
prevented"; the right "does not depend upon an advance showing that one
will surely prevail", which is the claim's "even where the objection
would have failed"; and its "extraordinary situations" match "without
adequate justification". Its scope is a constitutional rule on
state-assisted seizures of property.

**The rule converted.** CLAIM-734 (the connection the owner's, U26; the
form A's, A57, after Calabresi and Melamed; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed) grounds on THEORY-1962, bears on QUESTION-478, and complements
CLAIM-464, CLAIM-716, CLAIM-722 and CLAIM-723: an irreversible act
against a consent-protected entitlement leaves the holder in practice
only a claim to compensation, so the actor has changed the rule of
allocation "from consent to liability". Its body sets the pairs: it is
"the rule changing" and CLAIM-716 "the terms reached once it has";
CLAIM-723 "names the wrong in the order of moves; this one names what the
wrong converts"; and with CLAIM-722 "the two agree".

*Readings.* Its ground, THEORY-1962 (Calabresi and Melamed 1972, now
read), states the conversion mechanism in its §V. THEORY-1972 (Fuentes)
corroborates it by analogy: for hearings, the Court refuses to equate a
prior opportunity with compensation after.

**The reasons a reviewer is given.** CLAIM-701 (the assistant's, offered
at step 12 of the first filing and accepted by the owner on 2026-11-16;
thesis, interpretive, Proposed; lines distributed-agency and
organizational-normativity) rests on CLAIM-703 (SYNTHESIS-099) and
CLAIM-464, complements CLAIM-404, CLAIM-708 and CLAIM-726, and bears on
QUESTION-278: an actor that wrongfully creates circumstances in which
reversing its act would harm innocent third parties can give a reviewing
institution genuine reasons to accommodate those harms, as moral
blackmail gives its target a genuine obligation; the reviewer's sound
weighing does not discharge the actor's responsibility, and the reasons
are no larger than the circumstances support. Its body says it answers
in part the question CLAIM-464 leaves aside (what weight risks created by
the disputed conduct should have), keeps three propositions apart and
asserts the third only for wrongful acts with foreseeable costs, and
cites CASE-170 as an instance of the first two. Its Admission is a
separating case: harms that fall only on the actor give no genuine
reason. Its body calls CLAIM-726 distinct and complementary, a reviewer's
genuine reasons there and an audience's true accusation here. No reading
carries a relation to it.

**The leverage gained.** CLAIM-716 (A's, A55 §3 and §9, at the owner's
U25; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; also distributed-agency) complements
CLAIM-464, CLAIM-619, CLAIM-734, CLAIM-722 and CLAIM-728, and bears on
QUESTION-478: a breach can lower the injured party's position if it
refuses to settle, so a settlement it rationally accepts can leave the
breacher ahead, "the leverage coming from the violation". Its body says
CLAIM-464 holds "the reachability half exactly" and this adds the
bargaining channel, and it quotes A55 §8 against inferring from B's
choice that "The circumstances making that settlement B's best available
option were legitimate." No reading carries a relation to it.

**The power not gained.** CLAIM-722 (A's, A85 §5, A87 §2 and §10, A90 V
and VII, A97; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; lines
organizational-normativity and distributed-agency) rests on CLAIM-464,
grounds on THEORY-1984, complements CLAIM-716, CLAIM-734, CLAIM-720 and
CLAIM-743, and uses TERM-085: making a remedy unreachable gains a
wrongdoer no normative power over what is owed; the claim persists while
its remedy's correctability falls to zero, and a settlement reached
under the conditions the violation created does not authorize the
violation. Its body calls its third part "the normative complement" of
CLAIM-716, the conversion of CLAIM-734 "practical, not normative", and
A87 §10's "Litigation by attrition" the owner's siege, CLAIM-748.
*Readings.* Its ground, THEORY-1984 (Hohfeld 1913), is the distinction
A87 §2 uses, legal power against "the physical power to do the things
necessary"; Hohfeld analyses positive legal relations and says nothing
of moral claims or correctability.

QUESTION-475 (A's, A68 §4, from Fiss as the record read him; Open; line
distributed-agency) asks whether the capacity to make remedies costly can
bend, over time, the standard they are measured against. Its body says
that if it can, CLAIM-464's premise holds at a time and can fail over
time, and CLAIM-722 "would have a route by which it gains that power
after all"; it names Levinson (LIT-1592, Unfetched) as the reading to do
first. *Readings.* THEORY-1981 (Galanter 1974) bears on it: evidence for
a yes by another route, repeat players who "play for rules" and skew
precedent, and overload that "shields existing rules from challenge".
Its note says Galanter changes the stock of rules, not the standard.

**Siege.** CLAIM-748 (the owner's, U28; thesis, demonstrated, Proposed;
also distributed-agency) grounds in CASE-287, bears on QUESTION-479, uses
TERM-085 and TERM-105, and complements CLAIM-464, CLAIM-619, CLAIM-601,
CLAIM-723, CLAIM-716, CLAIM-722 and CLAIM-731: an actor can make a
legally recognized corrective objective unreachable for another, with
the merits, the law and that party's preferences unchanged, by raising
what pursuing it costs, in spending or in time, above what that party can
sustain. Its body records that A64 told the point as its own "key
insight"; sets "Temporal preemption" (CLAIM-723) and "Attritional
exhaustion" beside each other as sharing one result (A64's close); calls
A61 §5's resource-mediated hold-up CLAIM-716; and calls A61 §10's split
of adjudicative from attritional friction "related" to CLAIM-731.

*Readings.* Three THEORYs corroborate its mechanism, none its
threshold, the comparison of what the target can sustain with the time
to relief, which stays the exchange's construction: THEORY-1981
(Galanter: overload presses claimants to settle "by causing delay" and
"by raising costs (of keeping the case alive)"), THEORY-1923 (Cuéllar
2026, read in part: attacks that "levy a high cost on those targeted in
the meantime", costly rather than unreachable) and THEORY-1966 (Federal
Rules of Civil Procedure 11, 26 and 37: the law forbids papers and
discovery "to harass, cause unnecessary delay, or needlessly increase
the cost of litigation", a partial guard).

CASE-287 (A's constructions, A61 and A64, every number rechecked and
five of A's statements corrected by the filer; construction, fictional,
Proposed; also distributed-agency) bears on QUESTION-479: the four- and
seven-month game, the exponential race and the threshold. Its correction
of A64 §9's vulnerability gap requires the baseline course to be among
the opponent's admissible strategies, and it points to CLAIM-724 for
"the adversarial quantity itself".

**The measure.** CLAIM-724 (A's, A76 VIII.4 and A81 §3; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; lines realizability and dependence-and-domination)
complements CLAIM-537, CLAIM-568 and CLAIM-748, bears on QUESTION-479 and
uses TERM-085: against a challenged party who can act on the means of
correction, correctability is robust to a declared class of opposition,
depends on who moves first, and splits permitted from merely available
opposition, failure against the first showing the institution inadequate
and against the second a wrong by the opponent as well. Its body says it
gives "the measure on which a siege succeeds and the split that says
whether the siege was a wrong", and that its third part "says where the
answer enters" to QUESTION-479: "in what goes into Σ_permitted". No
reading carries a relation to it.

**Whether siege is wrong.** QUESTION-479 (A's, asked at the close of
A61, A64 and A68; Open) refines QUESTION-478: may a party under a
justified constraint set or raise the cost another must bear to make it
effective, and within which limits? Its body names A64's Candidate C as
"a normative proposal requiring independent justification", says A64
§10's definition moves the question into a counterfactual course "which
must itself be defended", and lists CLAIM-727 (in SYNTHESIS-103) as what
an answer denying the party such authority "would rest on". *Readings.*
THEORY-1966 (the Federal Rules) bears on it with a partial answer in
positive law, confined to US federal civil procedure: not "for any
improper purpose", within proportionality, binding both sides.
THEORY-1981 (Galanter) bears on it too.

**Friction.** CLAIM-710 (the owner's, U11; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed; line distributed-agency) complements CLAIM-523, CLAIM-462 and
CLAIM-464: automation reduces procedural friction, yet friction is where
an opportunity to intervene is exposed, and perhaps what it consists in,
so removing friction can remove the points at which a decision can still
be interrupted. The first filing kept both of the owner's strengths,
"exposes" and the hedged "characterizes", attributed the stronger form to
the owner (A21 §2, A26 §VI), and mapped A21 and A23 in the body without
adopting them; the continuation's reading of the same turn (A21 §1, §2
and §5, A23 §4) was folded in at the reconciliation. Its body lists
CLAIM-731, CLAIM-740, CLAIM-715, CLAIM-730 and CASE-285 as filed from it
and says it does not rest on them, and names CLAIM-711 (the first
filing's, distributed-agency, outside this account) as the objection to
its "characterizes" reading. *Readings.* THEORY-1938 (Bovens and
Zouridis 2002: automating a step left one point of intervention, a
formal objection the affected person must open) and THEORY-1947 (Citron
2008) corroborate it.

CLAIM-731 (A's, A23 §2, at the owner's U12; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed; first line distributed-agency) rests on CLAIM-462 and
CLAIM-710, complements CLAIM-706 (the first filing's, outside this
account), and uses TERM-124: the cost of imposing a decision and the cost
of contesting it are separate, so an institution can lower the first and
gain power with no change in its formal authority. *Readings.*
THEORY-1947 (Citron) corroborates it: automation lowers the cost of
deciding while challenge comes to need costly decoding of the system.

CLAIM-730 (A's, A33 §4 and §8; supposed, interpretive, Proposed; first
line distributed-agency) rests on CLAIM-478 and CLAIM-423, complements
CLAIM-711, and uses TERM-124: a slowly revised rule over a fast process
can dominate those it harms with no one acting, and, where the rule is
open to revision on demand, keep the fast process accountable instead.
Its body calls CLAIM-731 "the same asymmetry stated as cost".
*Readings.* THEORY-1931 (the HKMA's discussion paper, 2026, read in part)
corroborates both halves as regulatory guidance.

CASE-285 (A's proposed study, A33 §10 C; stipulated, proposed-study,
Proposed; first line distributed-agency) is a variant of CASE-003,
illustrates CLAIM-731, CLAIM-730 and CLAIM-710, and bears on
QUESTION-278: the same overseer, a slow and a fast acting institution,
then precommitted limits, automatic suspension and pauses. Its body says
it could support CLAIM-730's second clause over its first, and its
summary calls it a test of CLAIM-464 and CLAIM-462. It is not run. The
reconciliation recorded it as distinct from the first filing's CASE-280,
which varies foreclosure at fixed speed.

**The baseline.** CLAIM-747 (A's, A102 §6 and A106 §9, at the owner's
U44; thesis, interpretive, Proposed) rests on CLAIM-670 and CLAIM-464:
the alternatives against which a relationship's terms are assessed can
be products of that relationship, so an actor cannot justify its terms by
the narrowness of options it helped narrow without justification, and the
assessment must separate the existence of a dependence, the legitimacy of
its creation, the authority exercised through it and the responsibilities
of maintaining it. Its body says it rests on CLAIM-464 "for the parallel
point about assessment". No reading carries a relation to it.

**Neighbours not integrated.** The first filing filed, in the
distributed-agency line only, CLAIM-704, CLAIM-705, CLAIM-706, CLAIM-711,

## Arm B

Run again with the readings. No candidate passes ADR-075's test.

- **QUESTION-479's bearings** (CLAIM-748, CASE-287, CLAIM-724, and
  CLAIM-727 from SYNTHESIS-103, now with THEORY-1966 and THEORY-1981).
  The sentence tried: a party under a justified constraint may raise the
  cost of making it effective only by opposition the procedural rules
  permit, and its authority over the constrained conduct does not make it
  the one to say what is permitted. Both halves are stated already, in
  CLAIM-724's body ("in what goes into Σ_permitted") and CLAIM-727's.
  The Federal Rules give "permitted" a content, "not for any improper
  purpose", but only for one jurisdiction's civil procedure, and that
  content is the reading's, recorded in QUESTION-479's dated note, not an
  integration of claims. Which strategies are permitted morally stays a
  free parameter, and it is the question. Fails.
- **Siege is contestation friction raised by the opponent** (CLAIM-748,
  CLAIM-731, with THEORY-1981). The sentence tried: the cost an actor
  raises in a siege is the cost of contesting, so siege is CLAIM-731's
  asymmetry worked from the opponent's side. Separating case, realistic
  and from a reading: Cuéllar's executive attacks (THEORY-1923) levy costs
  on targets that are not costs of contesting, such as lost contracts,
  and can still put the corrective objective beyond what the target can
  sustain, which CLAIM-748 counts. As an identity the link fails on a
  case the claim allows; as "siege can work through contestation cost" it
  is Galanter's, a reading, and adds nothing to the components. Fails.
- **The capacity to make remedies costly bends the rules over time**
  (QUESTION-475, CLAIM-722, with THEORY-1981). The derivation runs
  through Galanter alone; it is recorded in the question's dated note as
  evidence for a yes, and it concerns the stock of rules, not the
  standard. A claim resting on one reading is not an integration; the
  record's relation for it is the THEORY's `bears_on`. Not a candidate.
- **Preemption and attrition as two routes to one result.** Already
  stated, in CLAIM-748's body, from A64's close. Nothing filed.
- **The chain from fait accompli to settlement** (CLAIM-723, CLAIM-734,
  CLAIM-716, CLAIM-722, now with CLAIM-701 beside them). Each link of
  the chain is stated pairwise in the components' complements and bodies;
  CLAIM-701's link to the others is listed above as not stated, and it
  adds a reviewer's reasons, not a step of the chain. Nothing filed.
- **The hold-up's baseline is endogenous** (CLAIM-716 with CLAIM-747).
  Fails as trivial: it instantiates CLAIM-747 with CLAIM-716's
  mechanism, and its content is A55 §8 in CLAIM-716's body and
  CLAIM-722's third part.
- **Speed alone separates action from correction** (CLAIM-731,
  CLAIM-730, CLAIM-710, CASE-285). Already stated, as CASE-285's
  prediction, and untested; the readings corroborate the claims, not the
  prediction. Nothing filed.

## What it does not say

- That any of these claims is accepted: all are Proposed, and the
  questions Open; the readings corroborate or ground parts of them, each
  as its THEORY says.
