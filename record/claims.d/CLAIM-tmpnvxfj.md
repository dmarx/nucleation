# CLAIM-tmpnvxfj: Pragmatic translation fidelity is the preservation, under corresponding communicative conditions, of the ways an utterance transforms interpretation, interpersonal alignment and the possibilities for subsequent interaction

## The claim

A40 §XV: "**Pragmatic translation fidelity is the preservation, under
appropriately corresponding communicative conditions, of the ways an utterance
transforms interpretation, interpersonal alignment, and the possibilities for
subsequent interaction.**" Its operational core, A39 §4.3: "**A pragmatically
significant difference between utterances is a difference in the observable
communicative transitions they make possible under specified conditions.**" And
the line that returns to the refrains: "the translation that best preserves what
a poem *does* may differ from the translation that best preserves what its
words *say*."

Its defeat condition is A40's own: the paper "would stand or fall on a
particularly clear claim: **A model of translation quality that preserves the
structure of communicative trajectories should explain or predict some
judgments that cannot be adequately explained by models restricted to static
semantic and pragmatic similarity.**" The manuscript does not state that
stake. The nearest thing is §10 Case I's success criterion,
"incremental prediction of held-out human communicative-fidelity judgments",
which is weighed against embedding similarity, not against static pragmatic
similarity.

## Why it was replaced

A45 and outline v3 (A48) extended it to learned interpretive dispositions
([CLAIM-tmpizns9](CLAIM-tmpizns9.md)), and after the owner's U20 objection it became the transport
thesis ([CLAIM-tmpt20oo](CLAIM-tmpt20oo.md)). The manuscript's thesis ([CLAIM-tmpww3q0](CLAIM-tmpww3q0.md)) keeps the observational turn and the
"possibilities for subsequent interaction" (as expected replies and uptake,
§2), and drops the transformation-of-states framing for observables and their
relations.
