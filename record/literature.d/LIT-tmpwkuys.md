# LIT-tmpwkuys: Feelings change: Accounting for individual differences in the temporal dynamics of affect

Peter Kuppens, Zita Oravecz & Francis Tuerlinckx (2010), *Journal of
Personality and Social Psychology* 99(6):1042–1060 — DOI-10.1037/a0020962

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* From the abstract:

- **Three processes.** "It is hypothesized that individuals are
  characterized by an affective home base, a baseline attractor state
  around which affect fluctuates. These fluctuations vary as the result
  of internal or external processes to which an individual is more or
  less sensitive and are regulated and tied back to the home base by the
  attractor strength." Individual differences in "affective home base,
  variability, and attractor strength" are proposed to underlie
  individual differences in affect dynamics.
- **Method.** The account is evaluated "by means of a diffusion modeling
  approach in 2 extensive experience-sampling studies on people's core
  affective experiences", over long (Study 1) and shorter (Study 2)
  timescales.
- **Reach.** The parameters are related to personality and emotion
  dispositions, and the abstract draws implications for affective
  dysfunction in psychopathology. Lange et al. (2020, LIT-tmp39gs5)
  cite it for the constructionist claim that core affect varies
  continuously.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for QUESTION-tmpsqwu5, which asks how to measure the
three marks of ascendancy that TERM-tmp8gbrt takes from Frijda:
priority, persistence and resumption. DynAffect gives two of them an
established statistical form. Attractor strength is a rate of return to
a baseline after perturbation, which is the shape of resumption.
Variability around the home base is the converse of persistence. But
what returns in DynAffect is an affective state, a location in core
affect, not the authority of one ranking over the system's conduct. To
measure resumption in the record's sense, the same model would have to
be fitted to a different time series: for example, the share of an
organization's or an animal's conflict decisions that follow the
candidate ranking ≿_v, with the home base as its baseline ascendancy
and the attractor strength as its resumption. Whether the paper's
model allows that change of variable is a question for the reading. So
is the exact form of its diffusion model, which the abstract does not
give.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
