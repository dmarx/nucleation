# THEORY-2103: Perspective-taking grows from joint attention

## The account

Infants share attention before they know anything of perspectives. Joint
attention builds common ground with a partner, so children first track
what a partner has experienced with them and only later what the partner
can see; co-presence leads them to over-attribute shared perception. By 3
they take another's differing view; only at about 4.5, with false belief,
do they hold two views of one object together.

## Evidence

A theoretical chapter reviewing the authors' and others' developmental
experiments.
