# LIT-055: Who Goes Nazi?

Dorothy Thompson (1941), *Harper's Magazine (August 1941 issue)* — <https://harpers.org/archive/1941/08/who-goes-nazi/>

## Key takeaways

- Thompson argues that susceptibility to Nazism follows character and situation, not race, nationality or class. Secure, self-possessed people with an inner code do not "go Nazi"; the humiliated, envious, success-chasing or spoiled do.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Thompson, a journalist who had reported from Germany, Austria and France, turns a "macabre parlor game" into an essay. She walks around an imagined American society party and predicts which guests would join a Nazi movement in a showdown. She sets aside race and nationality as predictors, and says Nazism appeals to a type of mind and is partly the disease of a post-war generation. Through a series of character sketches she contrasts those with a settled personal code, security or happiness with those driven by resentment, social insecurity, opportunism or vanity. She concludes that "nice people don't go Nazi", and that what decides it is something in the person.
