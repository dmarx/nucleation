# THEORY-tmplw93g: Scanlon now holds that an act is wrong when someone could reasonably reject, on personal reasons weighed against others' opposing reasons, any principle permitting it, that besides the wrong of not complying with an established and needed practice there is a distinct wrong of failing to do one's part in establishing a needed norm, and that the judgments of sufficiency and relevance this rests on are substantive, which weakens the view's reductive explanatory power

Scanlon, "Contractualism and Intuitionism" (2025), LIT-tmpgoyge.

## The account

The facts of morality "are facts about the sufficiency of various reasons
for rejecting a principle when various opposing reasons are taken into
account" (pp. 6–7). Where a needed practice is established, "it is wrong
not to comply with that practice" (p. 21); where it is not, "What is wrong
in these cases is not, or not just, failing to do what a norm would require
but also failing to do one's part in getting such a norm established"
(p. 22). "The moral nature of this idea of relevance weakens the
explanatory power of a reductive view" (p. 54).

## What it does not show

- Which norm one must help establish, or at what cost: the duty is
  imperfect (n. 41).
- Anything about rights, powers or authority beyond a passing remark
  (p. 31).
- The final wording: the paper is an unpublished draft.
