# TERM-066: interpretive capture, of an organization's evaluation: the condition in which the processes that read evidence, score constituents and allocate authority are driven by the interpretation or party being evaluated more than by evidence independent of it, so that authority amplifies or holds an interpretation without a gain in fidelity; judged against a standard specified apart from the captured process

## Definition

An organization's evaluation is captured when what decides how its
evidence is read, how its constituents are scored and how authority is
allocated among them is the interpretation, or the party, under
evaluation, more than evidence that does not pass through it. [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md)'s
instability, βγ > (1 − α)(1 − δ), is a possible mechanism of it, not its
definition: the instability shows only that the symmetric state does not
hold, and a system can give growing authority to a better-supported
interpretation by the same loop. Likewise [CLAIM-340](../claims.d/CLAIM-340.md)'s κ′ > κ̄ (the
prevailing interpretation bends the reading of the evidence more than the
predictions scored against it) is a condition under which capture can
arise. The verdict that an evaluation is captured needs, in addition, the
standard from outside: an independently characterized failure of
evaluative independence or of fidelity to evidence that does not pass
through the evaluated interpretation. [CLAIM-297](../claims.d/CLAIM-297.md) states the outcome:
authority amplifies an interpretive advantage "without any gain in
fidelity to independent evidence".

Two conditions belong to the term:

- **A standard from outside.** Capture is judged "against a separately
  specified institutional purpose" (A20 §8) or, for the record's
  operators, against fidelity to evidence that the captured process does
  not generate. Without such a standard every stable allocation of
  authority would count as captured or none would.
- **Durability through the organization's own processes.** A20 §3: "the
  organization reproduces the altered allocation through processes that
  appear to be its own". A single influenced decision is not capture.

**The word in the record.** [CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md): "Capture, if it starts at symmetry,
needs both arrows of [CLAIM-297](../claims.d/CLAIM-297.md)'s circuit". [CLAIM-340](../claims.d/CLAIM-340.md): "If it is (κ′ > κ̄),
capture returns". [QUESTION-100](../questions.d/QUESTION-100.md): "stable expertise" against
"self-reinforcing interpretive capture". [CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md) gives no criterion "for
when entrenched authority is capture and not merited trust". [CLAIM-359](../claims.d/CLAIM-359.md)
speaks of "safeguards against capture". None defines it.

**The sense not adopted.** The exchange also used the institutional
sense: "Regulatory capture concerns the subordination of regulatory
decisions to particular interests rather than the regulatory
institution's designated public purposes" (A20 §1, after the OECD's
"policy capture"), and Stigler's theory of regulation demanded by industry
(A20 §6; [LIT-1324](../literature.d/LIT-1324.md), Unavailable on 2026-10-21). That sense is
purpose-relative and need not involve any interpretation–authority loop:
a decision bought outright is captured in it.

The OECD report, read on 2026-10-21 ([LIT-1325](../literature.d/LIT-1325.md), [NOTE-1043](../notes.d/NOTE-1043.md)), words
it so: "Policy capture is the process of consistently or repeatedly
directing public policy decisions away from the public interest towards
the interests of a specific interest group or person", and its first
chapter adds "by the intent and action of this group or person". Its three
criteria, after Carpenter and Moss: "An overarching policy goal(s) must be
explicitly defined", "The captor (group) must intend to extract rents",
and a shift of the process away from the goal. Against A20's paraphrase
that adds intent, a third difference between the senses beside the
purpose-relative standard and the loop: this term needs no intent, since
[CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md)'s loop can be the mechanism of a capture nobody intended. The first criterion is
this term's standard from outside, made explicit, and "consistently or
repeatedly" is its durability condition.

Other readings of the day fall on the institutional side. Schram and Ule's
capture is assigned, a policymaker's payoff set equal to the operator's,
with no loop and no intent condition ([NOTE-1053](../notes.d/NOTE-1053.md)). Besley and Prat's
"captured" press receives a transfer in exchange for silence, one-shot: the
decision bought outright ([THEORY-837](../theory.d/THEORY-837.md)). Short, Hill and Brown's is
diversion from the public interest through an adopted criterion, judged
against measured public values, which they distinguish from public
preferences ([THEORY-859](../theory.d/THEORY-859.md), Proposed; [THEORY-833](../theory.d/THEORY-833.md)). Barnehl and
Schumacher add a coercion condition, "an outlet has to be coerced by an
external actor to alter its editorial choices", under which a merely
agreeing outlet is not captured, a condition this term lacks; their
measure gives the outside standard operationally, with uncoercible
opposition outlets as one pole, though alignment is not causal
identification ([THEORY-836](../theory.d/THEORY-836.md)). Szeidl and Szucs show why an outside
reference is needed: their slant is measured against a critical reference
outlet that nets out the neutral level ([THEORY-860](../theory.d/THEORY-860.md)). Di Tella and
Franceschelli's within-newspaper comparison is the weakest form of such a
standard, blind to a bias every paper shares ([THEORY-851](../theory.d/THEORY-851.md)). This definition takes from it only the outside
standard. If the owner wants both, the institutional one would be a
narrower TERM that `refines` this one, with the evaluator a public
institution and the interest a party outside it.

A21 §7 split three things the term must keep apart: "**Interpretive
advantage**" ("Conceptually established; not necessarily capture"),
"**Interpretation–authority coupling**" ([CLAIM-330](../claims.d/CLAIM-330.md)'s loop) and "**Epistemic
institutional capture**" ("Inappropriate durable influence over an
institution's evidence-production or evaluation processes, judged against
its declared purposes and standards of independence"). This term is the
third, generalized beyond institutions.

## What it is not

- **Not concentration.** A25 §8: "We should avoid using *capture* as a
  synonym for *concentration*." Authority can be concentrated because it
  is merited.
- **Not merited trust.** [CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)'s same loop "might develop both
  well-calibrated expertise and undeserved authority". Whether a standard
  internal to the organization can tell them apart is [QUESTION-087](../questions.d/QUESTION-087.md).
- **Not dominance by a better-supported account.** A21: "A particular
  interpretation can prevail because it is better supported."
- **Not an editorial perspective or ordinary selection.** A21: "A news
  organization can be ideologically committed without being captured",
  and "ordinary editorial selection is unavoidable".
- **Not protective lock-in** ([TERM-020](TERM-020.md)), which it complements. Lock-in is
  a regime that "generates the conditions that justify its own
  continuation" and reduces the capacity to leave it, whoever is served.
  Capture is about whose interpretation the evaluation serves. A regime
  can be either without the other.
- **Not endorsement** ([TERM-053](TERM-053.md)). Endorsement is the channel: any effect
  of one process on another's share of influence. Capture is a regime of
  that channel.

## What would settle it

The owner's choice between the fidelity-relative sense taken here and the
purpose-relative institutional sense, or an answer to [QUESTION-087](../questions.d/QUESTION-087.md) that
supplies the standard. A use in an argument that needs the term to be
adopted would also force the choice.
