# NOTE-2107: Miyato and others 2024 — Artificial Kuramoto Oscillatory Neurons

## Contribution

A neuron model for deep networks: each unit is a vector on a hypersphere,
updated by a generalised Kuramoto rule and renormalised after each step,
with the layer's input entering as a bias direction.

## Key results

- The conditioning input acts as a bias: "In physics lingo, C is often
  referred to as a “symmetry breaking” field."
- Readouts take norms, since states that differ by a global phase should
  read the same.
- "both the readout module and conditional stimuli C are essential for
  stable training" (appendix), and, as a limitation, "AKOrN would not
  perform well on memory tasks" (§7).

## Assumptions

General coupling matrices between oscillators, not necessarily
isotropic.

## Concepts

Kuramoto synchronization; symmetry-breaking field; binding by
synchrony.

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-2787](../literature.d/LIT-2787.md).** This is the oscillator architecture ChatGPT
  attributes to the owner's U6 example, with the bias that A12 says erases
  domain walls. Here the bias helps training. No Z2 symmetry and no walls,
  so it stands in for none of that work's results.
- **[THEORY-1507](../theory.d/THEORY-1507.md).** Whether walls between oscillator domains are protected
  depends on whether the dynamics leave a disconnected set of vacua, not
  on the sphere the units live on. With isotropic coupling the vacua form
  a connected sphere and walls are unprotected; the general coupling here
  need not be isotropic. That is the filer's reasoning, not the paper's.

## Limitations

Experiments not read; the note covers the architecture only.
