# CLAIM-tmpkivlz: Who harmed whom, act by act, and who had power over whom, in the ongoing relationship, are separate questions: both parties can be culpable for harmful acts while one of them dominates the other, so neither a tally of acts nor the allocation of power settles the other

## The claim

A3 §7 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b)
separates two moral questions. "**Question 1: Who harmed whom?** This
focuses on individual actions and consequences." "**Question 2: Who had
power over whom?** This focuses on the ongoing relationship. Who could set
the rules? Who had to accommodate the other's moods? Who could impose
consequences? Whose autonomy was being restricted?" Under the first, "Both
people can be guilty." Under the second, "the answer may be profoundly
unequal, even when both people have done harmful things."

The claim is that these are independent assessments. Each act keeps its
own verdict, which a finding of domination neither erases nor fixes. A3
"Where I land": "You can recognize someone's harmful actions without
pretending the relationship was symmetrical. Conversely, recognizing a
power imbalance shouldn't mean denying their agency or excusing every
harmful thing they did." The method follows: "Neither method should
replace the other. Both the specific physical acts and the larger pattern
matter."

Why they separate, in the record's terms: [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md) makes domination a
capacity, "used or not", to set the terms on which another can act, exit,
contest or renegotiate. A count of acts, however complete, counts uses. A3
§3's six questions (can each say no, keep friends, spend money, leave the
room, disagree, leave the relationship) ask about that capacity, not about
acts.

**A's rider, A7 §9.** A goes further: "acts of domination, acts of defense,
failures of care, and failures of honesty aren't straightforwardly
commensurable", so "there may be no uniquely correct answer to the
question *Who was the worse person?*", while "There can nevertheless be
clear answers to more specific questions". That is responsibility as a
profile without a total order. It is the shape [CLAIM-354](CLAIM-354.md) gives freedom
(a partial order, "not a scalar"); the claim does not need it and does not
assert it.

## How it sits with the record

- **[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)** supplies the capacity sense of domination that makes the
  separation hold; the claim is the first in the record to set it against
  an act ledger in a relationship between two persons.
- **[CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)** is a test for domination by an arrangement's controllers;
  **[CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md)** separates the magnitude of a dependence from who controls
  its terms. This claim separates a third thing, harm done act by act,
  from both.
- **[CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md)**'s last clause, "domination is another's capacity to govern
  those same relations, used or not", is the general statement the second
  question applies.
- The senses of responsibility at stake (for a particular act, for the
  pattern or conditions) are [TERM-tmp8pnnr](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8pnnr.md)'s; a verdict in one sense does
  not carry to another.

## What it does not say

- Not that domination is easy to establish. A3 §6: "human motives aren't
  always clear"; "power isn't one-dimensional"; a 2023 systematic review
  found "mixed support for established typologies".
- Not that being dominated licenses any response. A3 §4: "Experiencing
  abuse does not give someone unlimited moral permission to hurt their
  partner."
- Nothing about any real couple. The exchange began from the Depp and Heard
  dispute (U1, A2); the claim needs only the constructed case, and the
  record files no case naming them.

Each THEORY now `corroborates` this claim, which does not rest on any of them.

- **Johnson (2006)**, standing in for Johnson 1995 ([LIT-tmpozb57](../literature.d/LIT-tmpozb57.md), Unfetched) and 2008 ([LIT-tmp5254i](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5254i.md), Unavailable), types violence by control context, not acts: to classify a couple's violence, ask "about the power and control issues involved in their relationship, and about the presence of fear"; "all three types of violence can be dangerous, even life-threatening", so a dangerous act does not fix the type.
- **Hardesty et al. (2015)**, with Johnson: "A risk of the count approach is (mis)classifying highly controlling abusers as “low control” when they rely on very few control tactics but enforce them relentlessly"; and with the frequency and severity of violence controlled, "the group differences reflect the control context rather than differences in violence". That is the claim's point in an operational form: a tally of acts, even of control tactics, does not settle who dominates.
- **Stark (2015)**, standing in for Stark 2007 ([LIT-tmp6y633](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6y633.md)): "Interventions focused on discrete assaults (rather than course of conduct) miss the significance of abuse"; tactics that "taken alone, may not rise to the level of abuse" make the pattern "in combination".
- **Mazinani & O'Connor (2026)**: "bidirectional violence refers only to relationships in which both partners were reported to have used violent acts. The term does not imply equivalence in motive, coercive control, severity, consequences, or responsibility." An interpretive guard, not a test.

None of the four takes up the claim's other half, that the dominated party's own acts keep their verdict. Johnson allows only that "in the case of violent resistance a case can sometimes be made that the violence was justified".

Two works cited beside the claim carry less. **Lohmann et al. (2023)** ([NOTE-tmphdvuc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphdvuc.md), [THEORY-tmp0cj7p](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp0cj7p.md)), which A cited for harms of coercive control "of its own", finds associations with PTSD and depression no stronger than psychological abuse's and no causal link; its THEORY carries no relation. **Stover et al. (2025)** ([LIT-tmpftjt6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpftjt6.md), abstract only) defines "bidirectional" as acts reported by either partner, with rates from 10% to 96.6%; its rates do not show how often both partners are culpable. The body's "A3 §6 ... a 2023 systematic review found mixed support for established typologies" remains unchecked.
