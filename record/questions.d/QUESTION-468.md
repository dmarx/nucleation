# QUESTION-468: How does the record identify exploitation in an organization: what separates it from dependence and from domination, which declared standard supplies the verdict, and who, if anyone, answers for it when the arrangement is not an agent?

## The question

Three parts, which an answer has to keep apart.

1. **What separates exploitation from dependence and from domination.**
   A6 §2 of the exchange "About Plantation Paradise" listed five
   predicates, contribution, dependency, cooperation, exploitation and
   domination, and said "These are distinct predicates" (kept in the note
   on [CLAIM-546](../claims.d/CLAIM-546.md)). The record has no way to tell, in a case, which of them
   hold. An indispensable contribution does not show exploitation, and
   "compensation or public acclaim" does not show its absence (A6 §2).
2. **Which standard supplies the verdict.** An exploitation verdict is
   relative to a standard declared with it ([TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md); [CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)). The
   record supplies none, and A6 §6 put the investigation's aim as
   "Separating causal dependence from violations of an independently
   justified labor or freedom standard". Which standards are admissible,
   and whether conflicts between them have better resolutions, is
   [QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md). This part asks the narrower thing: for the record's own
   cases, which standard the record uses, and whether one is enough.
3. **Who, if anyone, answers for it.** The owner's review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488): "A
   whole can be the ground or object of a normative judgment without
   necessarily being the bearer of the corresponding obligation." The
   beneficiary of exploitative terms, the agent that controls the terms
   and the bearer of a duty to remedy can be three different parties. In
   a sense-1 arrangement ([TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)) that no agent designed, the benefit
   can accrue to parties who neither set nor control the terms.
   [QUESTION-070](QUESTION-070.md) asks the same of manipulation, when no member intends it.

## Which sense of "organization"

[TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) sense 1 by default: a relational arrangement, which need not be
an institution or an agent. The party exploited must have standing
([TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)); the predicates apply to persons and to collectives with
standing, not to cells or subprocesses, which can be used but not, in
this sense, exploited. Each entry that answers a part of the question
should say which sense of "organization" it means.

## Why it is a question

What the record holds bounds it without answering it.

- **Stability is not fairness.** Stable coexistence does not imply
  cooperation, and Hawks keep taking the resource at equilibrium
  ([CLAIM-546](../claims.d/CLAIM-546.md)). Viability does not say what ought to persist ([CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)).
- **One dependence, several organizations.** The same dependence can be
  organized as opportunity, vulnerability or leverage ([CLAIM-428](../claims.d/CLAIM-428.md)).
  [CLAIM-690](../claims.d/CLAIM-690.md) adds that control over the terms, not magnitude, sorts it
  in both directions of a whole-part relation, and [CASE-264](../cases.d/CASE-264.md) builds the
  contrast. That separates dependence from domination. It does not
  separate either from exploitation.
- **Routes into exploitation.** Through access whose terms leave an
  encumbrance that narrows exit ([CLAIM-669](../claims.d/CLAIM-669.md)); through supply that produces
  the reliance it then uses ([CLAIM-670](../claims.d/CLAIM-670.md)). Both say how, given a standard;
  neither says which standard.
- **Domination has a clause, not a criterion.** [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s "another's
  capacity to govern those same relations, used or not", now proposed as
  [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md) with a declared standard of answerability.
- **Answerability.** [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s second-order duty is to keep violations
  knowable, contestable and correctable, a verdict given only an
  independently justified standard. It is a candidate for the standard of
  answerability in domination; it does not say what exploitation is.

None says how, in a given organization, the record tells that a party is
exploited rather than only dependent or only dominated.

## Kept apart

- **Ground and bearer.** Part 2 is about the ground: by what standard the
  terms are wrong. Part 3 is about the bearer: who owes a remedy. [CLAIM-671](../claims.d/CLAIM-671.md)
  and [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md) bear on the second for vulnerability and membership.
- **Control and integration.** The owner's review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488): "deeper
  integration need not monotonically increase vulnerability". A part that
  gave up a capability to a supplier ([CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)) is dependent; whether it
  is dominated depends on who controls the terms, and whether it is
  exploited on the standard declared.

## What would count as an answer

- For part 1, a test that, in a matched pair, tells exploitation from
  dependence and from domination, as [CASE-264](../cases.d/CASE-264.md) tells dependence from
  domination. A6 §6's proposed comparison is one: "two theatrical
  organizations with similar artistic outputs but different labor
  arrangements could help isolate exploitation from performance."
- For part 2, an entry that names a standard the record adopts for a
  class of cases, with its warrant, or one that shows the record can work
  with declared standards case by case.
- For part 3, an account of where the duty falls when the arrangement is
  not an agent: on its maintainers, its controllers, its beneficiaries,
  or on no one.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Tognazzini and Coates, read

On "who, if anyone, answers for it when the arrangement is not an agent":
the SEP entry "Blame" reports an argument that an institution's complicity
in the conditions of wrongdoing removes its standing to blame (Watson 2015,
Beade 2019, on the state). That presupposes the institution can be
complicit, so it answers for the conditions at least to the extent of
losing standing. Relation: bears_on (on the new THEORY).

## Note of 2026-11-16: Liedo, Van Grunsven and Marin, read

Offers one answer to "which declared standard": Brake's, "Exploitative labor happens when someone
benefits from the work of someone to the detriment of her interests", with the benefit taken as
"economic profit, avoidance of the fair share of work, or social credit" (p. 6). It also offers a
structural source (privileged irresponsibility) and a reason exploitation goes unidentified: it is
masked by the exploited party's satisfaction (p. 8). [TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md) could cite Brake's standard as one
declared standard. Relation: **bears_on**.

## Note of 2026-11-17: a candidate standard for exploitation through commitments, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-713 CLAIM-707 — Proposed or Open; filed from the first share of the same exchange (PR 510) and reconciled with this filing on 2026-11-18 -->

A15 §8 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (2026-11-15) asks what
separates "legitimate reliance" on someone's conscientiousness from
exploiting it, and answers on four dimensions: "**Consent**"
("Responsibilities are knowingly and reasonably accepted" against
"Obligations are imposed through manufactured dependency"), "**Burden**",
"**Control**" ("They are accountable for outcomes they cannot meaningfully
control") and "**Exit**" ("Exiting is made to appear equivalent to harming
innocent people"). Its summary: exploitation can consist in "**appropriating
the practical benefits of someone's moral commitments while denying them
control over the conditions under which those commitments are
exercised**".

For this question's second part, the four are a candidate declared
standard in [TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md)'s sense, for one kind of case: exploitation through
moral commitments. [CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md) is the mechanism they would judge. For
the first part, what separates exploitation from domination,
[QUESTION-472](QUESTION-472.md) asks whether control over the circumstances that trigger
someone's obligations is domination too. For the third, who answers when
the arrangement is not an agent, [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md) says where blame tends to
land instead.

The question stays Open.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity', A22

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-1209 CLAIM-1309 CLAIM-1219 CLAIM-1244 — new; the claims filed on 2026-11-24 that bear on this question -->

A22 §19 (ChatGPT, 2026-11-24) answers the first part of the question, what
separates exploitation from dependence, with three levels, keeping the
record's restriction of moral exploitation to [TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md):

| Level | A22's characterization |
|---|---|
| Functional appropriation | "One process benefits from another's maintained activity." |
| Asymmetric contractual organization | "Benefits, burdens, dependencies or revision authority are unevenly distributed." |
| Normative exploitation | "A party benefits through terms that violate a justified standard concerning another party with relevant standing." |

"Functional appropriation is widespread and not intrinsically
pathological. Asymmetry may be productive, justified or necessary.
Normative exploitation requires a further argument." The levels let the
record describe "exploitation-like dynamics in computational systems,
evolutionary ecology and subpersonal organization without prematurely
attributing moral duties or moral patienthood to their constituents."

This is a sorting of the question's terms, not an answer to its second and
third parts, which standard and who answers. The filing of 2026-11-24 adds
four claims that bear on the question: the owner's U17, filed as two
readings ([CLAIM-1209](../claims.d/CLAIM-1209.md), reliance as exposure; [CLAIM-1309](../claims.d/CLAIM-1309.md), exploitation
as revelation); identification of an implicit contract by perturbation
rather than routine behaviour ([CLAIM-1219](../claims.d/CLAIM-1219.md)); and the regulatory surplus
whose capture is a candidate mechanism of exploitation ([CLAIM-1244](../claims.d/CLAIM-1244.md)).
The question stays Open.
