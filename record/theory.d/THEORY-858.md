# THEORY-858: Insulating a regulator from future political revision fixes in place the interests of whoever designs the insulated regime: it relieves the underinvestment that electoral pressure on prices causes when the designers serve consumers, and entrenches capture, removing the electoral pressure that would correct it, when they serve the regulated industry

## Source

Schram, A. & Ule, A. (2024), [LIT-1340](../literature.d/LIT-1340.md), §5: "regulatory independence is
beneficial when policymakers favor consumers but may be detrimental if they
favor the industry", and "if the regulatory interests align with
infrastructure operators, a flexible regulator may be more efficient." Read
in [NOTE-1053](../notes.d/NOTE-1053.md).

## What was actually shown

A stylized game with equilibria for two boundary cases of voting (the
paper's Table 2, derived in an appendix this reading did not check), and
the laboratory result [THEORY-856](THEORY-856.md). Both bear on the account only for a
regulator whose independence is a price fixed at the start, with
policymakers' alignment assigned.

## What this does not say

- **Not that independent agencies are captured more often.** The paper
  cites the worry that independence raises "the chance of its capture by
  the industry", and does not test it.
- **Not that political control is better.** The account is conditional on
  the designers' alignment, and the authors' own result is that without
  capture independence helps.
- **Not that electoral pressure corrects capture.** In the lab it was
  partial, and the authors expect it to be weaker in real elections.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md).** A route by which formal independence protects
  capture rather than evaluation: independence from one authority (voters)
  installed by an agent of another (the industry).
- **[CLAIM-410](../claims.d/CLAIM-410.md).** Independence is a change in who holds revision. This
  account says the effect of removing the electorate's share of revision
  depends on who held rule-making when it was removed.
