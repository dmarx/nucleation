---
title: 'The write-manuscript protocol was first written from one manuscript''s README, not from the record of how that manuscript was made'
created: '2026-11-23T03:45:00'
tags:
- retro
---

**Going wrong, found by the owner.** Asked to document a procedure for
fleshing out a manuscript from the record, the assistant wrote
[ADR-143](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-143.md) and the write-manuscript skill from the first manuscript's
README, its review file and its directory layout. The owner asked whether
it took into account the procedure actually used: the notes about that
process, the retrospectives and curation entries on it, and its commit and
pull request messages. It did not. The README is that draft's own summary,
written by the same hands, and says what was done, not what went wrong or
what the reviews of it recommended.

**Why it happened.** The README looked like a complete account of the
procedure, so it was taken as the source rather than as one source. A
procedure is what practice converged on, and the record keeps that
convergence in its journals (curation, devlog, retrospectives) and in git,
not in the artifact.

**What was done.** A reader was sent to mine those records and compare them
with the skill; the skill and the ADR are revised on its report in the same
pull request, before the new manuscript's review.

**What would catch it.** When writing down a procedure from practice, read
the record of the practice (journals, retrospectives, commits, pull
requests) as well as its product.
