# NOTE-tmpynmwh: Barad — Nature’s Queer Performativity

## Contribution

A short statement of agential realism, in Barad's own words, aimed at
queer theory and ecocriticism. It extends performativity from human
practices to "all matter(ings)" (p. 31) and puts "queerness" in the
indeterminacy of identity, causality, space and time, not in human
sexuality alone. For this record its value is that it states the agential
cut, intra-action, phenomenon and agential separability first-hand
(pp. 31–32, 46–47), where the record had them only at second hand.

## Key insight

There are no things before relations. A phenomenon is an entanglement in
which "relata only exist within phenomena as a result of specific
intra-actions" (p. 49, note 28). Where classical thought takes the line
between subject and object as given in advance (the Cartesian cut), Barad
takes every such line as enacted, locally and within the phenomenon, by an
intra-action: the agential cut. It separates and binds in one movement
("cut things together-apart", p. 32), so separation is "exteriority-within",
not absolute. Accountability then attaches to the cuts that are made and
what they exclude.

## Assumptions

- **Bohr's philosophy-physics, read ontologically.** The objective
  referent of "wave" and "particle" is not an object with inherent
  properties but "what he called a phenomenon – the
  entanglement/inseparability of 'object' and 'apparatus' (which do not
  preexist the experiment but rather emerge from it)" (p. 42). That this
  inseparability is ontological, and not a limit on description, is
  Barad's reading, asserted here and argued in Barad 2007.
- **Quantum entanglement as the model of all relation.** Phenomena are
  entanglements "in the technical sense of 'quantum entanglements'" (p. 32),
  and the shift "is not necessarily circumscribed by the scale of the
  microscopic realm" (p. 45; note 56 distinguishes the ontological question
  from whether classical laws explain a regularity).
- **Diffractive reading.** Agential realism is "not a straight read of
  physics" but insights from physics and from poststructuralist theory
  "read through one another" (p. 46, note 58).
- **Performativity as iterative intra-activity**, against Butler's
  "iterative citationality" (p. 49, notes 24 and 27).

## Key results

The essay has no results of its own. Its claims, in order:

- **The nature/culture divide undoes itself (pp. 27–31).** The legal and
  moral category "crimes against nature" both places the human outside
  nature and condemns acting like a beast, so its logic trips over the very divide it
  is meant to secure (pp. 29–30).
- **The cut cannot presuppose the human (p. 31).** What is needed is
  "accountability for the cuts that are made", and "Whatever a 'cut' is
  must not assume a prior notion of the 'human'". The posthumanist point is
  "not to blur the boundaries between human and nonhuman" but "to
  understand the materializing effects of particular ways of drawing
  boundaries".
- **The agential cut (p. 32).** Quoted in full: "A specific intra-action
  enacts an agential cut (in contrast to the Cartesian cut – an inherent
  distinction – between subject and object) effecting a separation between
  'subject' and 'object'. That is, the agential cut enacts a 'local'
  resolution within the phenomenon of the inherent ontological
  indeterminacy. Crucially then, intra-actions enact agential separability
  – the local condition of exteriority-within-phenomena." And: "Identity is
  a phenomenal matter; it is not an individual affair."
- **Intra-action (p. 32).** "The notion of intra-action (in contrast to the
  usual 'interaction', which presumes the prior existence of independent
  entities/relata)" reworks "causality, agency, space, time, matter,
  discourse, responsibility, and accountability".
- **Queer critters (pp. 33–38).** Lightning, whose upward and downward
  leaders make "a communication that has neither sender nor recipient until
  transmission has already occurred" (p. 35); stingray receptor cells that
  seem to anticipate a signal (from Kirby 2001); Pfiesteria, whose toxicity
  "only 'are' in relationship to fish" so that, on Schrader's account, an
  epistemic uncertainty is "an ontological indeterminacy" (pp. 37–38).
- **The quantum leap (pp. 39–41).** Read as a discontinuity "that exists in
  neither space nor time", which "torques" the relation of continuity and
  discontinuity (pp. 40–41).
- **Which-path and quantum eraser (pp. 41–44).** With a which-slit detector
  the pattern is a scatter pattern; without one, or after the which-slit
  information is erased, a diffraction pattern. Barad: "This is direct
  empirical evidence that identity is not fixed and inherent, but
  performative" (p. 43), and the eraser shows "the atom's identity, its
  ontology, is never fixed, but is always open to future and past
  reworkings". Against the eraser's proponents she argues the past is not
  changed or restored: the post-erasure pattern "is not the same as the
  'original'", and "the trace of all measurements remains even when
  information is erased" (p. 44).
- **Cutting together-apart (pp. 46–47).** "Agential cuts – intra-actions –
  don't produce (absolute) separation, they engage in agential
  separability – differentiating and entangling (that's one move, not
  successive processes)." "Agential cuts radically rework relations of
  joining and disjoining" (p. 46).
- **Ethics (pp. 46–47).** "There are no 'acts against nature' ... there are
  only 'acts of nature' (including thinking and language use)";
  accountability is "an ethico-onto-epistemological commitment to
  understand how different cuts matter" (pp. 46–47).

## Concepts

- **Phenomenon** — "material-discursive intra-actions" that "extend across
  ... space and time"; "ontologically primitive relations – relations
  without preexisting relata" (p. 32, p. 49 note 28). Bohr's word, which
  Barad takes over.
- **Intra-action** — action within a phenomenon, as against interaction
  between independently existing entities (p. 32).
- **Agential cut** — what an intra-action enacts: a separation between
  "subject" and "object" that is a local resolution of ontological
  indeterminacy within the phenomenon (p. 32). Not an absolute separation:
  "cutting together/apart" (p. 46).
- **Cartesian cut** — "an inherent distinction" between subject and object
  (p. 32), the classical assumption the agential cut replaces.
- **Agential separability** — "the local condition of
  exteriority-within-phenomena" (p. 32); a separability that is "a matter
  of irreducible heterogeneity" without reducing difference to sameness
  (p. 46).
- **Spacetimemattering** — the imploded term for space, time and matter
  taken as entangled, not separate (p. 49, note 25).
- **Apparatus** — used here only of the two-slit set-up and its which-slit
  detector (pp. 41–43). The essay gives no general definition.

## Connections

The essay presents itself as a companion to Barad 2010 (note 57) and points
to Barad 2007 ([LIT-1657](../literature.d/LIT-1657.md)) for the physics (notes 49, 53), and to Barad 2003
([LIT-1653](../literature.d/LIT-1653.md)) for posthumanist performativity (note 23). It draws its
nonhuman cases from Vicki Kirby (*Quantum Anthropologies*, 2011) and Astrid
Schrader (2010). Its quantum physics is Bohr's complementarity read as
ontology, the line Faye and Jaksland ([NOTE-1220](NOTE-1220.md)) and Jaksland ([NOTE-1230](NOTE-1230.md))
examine in the record. Its view of performativity is set against Butler's.

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-087](../terms.d/TERM-087.md): partly.** The term waits on "a reading of Barad herself that
  confirms or corrects the third sense". This reading confirms the core in
  her own words: an agential cut is enacted by an intra-action within a
  phenomenon, it is "a 'local' resolution within the phenomenon of the
  inherent ontological indeterminacy" (so ontological, not epistemic), it
  yields "agential separability – the local condition of
  exteriority-within-phenomena", and it is defined against "the Cartesian
  cut – an inherent distinction – between subject and object" (p. 32),
  which matches [TERM-087](../terms.d/TERM-087.md)'s first sense. It does not confirm the rest. The
  essay never defines an apparatus in general (the word is used only of the
  two-slit set-up), never says that a cut is movable or that "no agential
  cut is an inherent distinction", and never mentions Heisenberg. Those
  parts still rest on Faye and Jaksland's quotations from Barad 2007. One
  difference to note: Barad reads Bohr's phenomenon as an ontological
  inseparability of object and apparatus "which do not preexist the
  experiment" (p. 42), whereas [TERM-087](../terms.d/TERM-087.md), after Faye and Jaksland, holds
  Bohr's own separation fixed and epistemic. That is a disagreement between
  Barad and her critics about Bohr, which the term already reports, not a
  correction of the term.
- **[CLAIM-476](../claims.d/CLAIM-476.md).** It calls luria an apparatus "in Barad's constitutive
  sense" for a record's objects. The essay supports the sense of cut the
  claim uses (a differentiation enacted within a phenomenon, not
  presupposed), but gives no general account of an apparatus, so it does
  not supply the constitutive sense the claim names. It says nothing about
  records.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **An essay, not an argument from evidence.** The physics is described
  informally and the interpretive step from Bohr's complementarity to an
  ontology of phenomena is asserted here and referred to Barad 2007. The
  macroscopic cases are taken from secondary accounts and read by analogy.
- **What "evidence" means here.** The claim that the eraser is "direct
  empirical evidence" of performative identity holds only on Barad's
  reading of Bohr; a reader who takes the which-path result as a fact about
  what can be jointly measured gets no such conclusion from it.
- **Terms are defined in the notes or by reference**: phenomenon (note 28),
  spacetimemattering (note 25), diffraction (notes 30, 58). Apparatus, the
  word [TERM-087](../terms.d/TERM-087.md) most needs, is not defined.
- **The version.** Barad says the Qui Parle printing is not the version she
  submitted (notes 1 and 15); this reading is of the authorized one only.

## Open questions

- Does Barad 2007 define the apparatus and the movability of the cut as
  Faye and Jaksland quote her? Only a reading of [LIT-1657](../literature.d/LIT-1657.md) settles it.
- Is the agential cut ontological in a sense that differs from a movable
  Heisenberg cut taken realistically? The essay does not compare them.
