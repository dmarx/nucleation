# CLAIM-241: Partial constitution by uptake makes competent participants' uptake a defeasible standard of a communicative kind, not the criterion: where the act's other constituents, such as its institutional conditions, diverge from how even informed participants take it, their verdicts can be wrong, and a standard must say whose competence counts, for which class of acts, and how divided verdicts are combined, which a majority does not

## The objection

At U62 the owner asked "how would you suggest we update or modify the
theses?" and argued nothing. The assistant's answer (A240) recommended for
[CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md): "Retain as Proposed; qualify its constitutive claim",
because "Competent uptake is an important standard, but participant
disagreement and institutional conditions complicate it". Its first
refinement says how:

> **First, don't equate competent uptake with a simple majority vote.** A
> communicative act's institutional or normative conditions can sometimes
> diverge from how a sampled audience interprets it. I would phrase uptake
> as a *defeasible, practice-grounded individuation standard*, specifying
> whose competence matters and for which class of acts.

Its draft of T1 carries the same point as a qualification: "Competent uptake
is a central practice-grounded criterion for identifying these kinds, but it
is not presumed infallible, unanimous, or sufficient for every kind of
institutional act" ([CLAIM-215](CLAIM-215.md)). Its revised proposal at U61 (A232
§2.1) says the standard "requires identifying the relevant community,
competence conditions, interpretive circumstances, and tasks. It does not
mean that every judge's response is correct, or that there is always one
uncontroversial classification."

**The target is step 3 of [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md).** On [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md), which act an
utterance performs is "partly constituted by its relations to participants,
norms and expected uptake". Step 3 concludes that competent participants'
verdicts "are not one more probe of a fact fixed elsewhere", and so that
"the judges ... are the criterion a probe family is tested against". The
premise does not carry the conclusion. If uptake is one constituent among
several, it is part of what makes the fact, and so are the norms and the
standings. Where those diverge from how even informed participants take the
act, the participants' verdicts are wrong about it. Partial constitution
gives uptake a vote, not the last word. This undermines the inference; it
does not rebut [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md).

## What the target already has

Some of the point is met.

- "It does not say participants are infallible about institutional status."
  The judges are told "the standings and norms in play", so a misreading
  from ignorance of a standing is excluded.
- "It does not say the standard is one population." Which population is
  [QUESTION-075](../questions.d/QUESTION-075.md)'s.
- Its defeat condition counts verdicts that "split between populations in
  ways no stated condition captures".

## What it lacks

- **A rule for divided verdicts within a population.** The claim does not
  say how verdicts are combined. The discriminating study models
  disagreement ([CASE-055](../cases.d/CASE-055.md): "Judge and group random effects"), and
  [CLAIM-226](CLAIM-226.md) objected to pooling judges, but the standard itself is
  silent. A majority is one rule. It is not the only one, and it is not
  argued for.
- **A class of acts.** The claim covers "a communicative kind" in general
  and names teasing in its defeat condition. [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) version 2's "can
  change the act" is "carried by acts with standing conditions", and
  whether teasing is constituted by relations is left open
  ([QUESTION-073](../questions.d/QUESTION-073.md)).
- **Defeasibility.** Informed participants can be wrong in a way the
  claim's "made known to them" does not reach. The act's other
  constituents can diverge from informed uptake: its institutional
  conditions, or the speaker's intention if [QUESTION-073](../questions.d/QUESTION-073.md) goes that
  way. Where they do, the standard misclassifies.

[CASE-054](../cases.d/CASE-054.md) bears on whose competence counts: "There is no evidence that
a Crow would recognise a Dogon joking exchange as the same thing. That
bears on [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md), which takes competent participants' uptake as the
standard."

## What it does not say

- It does not say competent uptake is not a standard, or that the analyst's
  probes are a better one. It says the standard is defeasible.
- It does not say a judge's verdict is wrong whenever judges disagree.
  Disagreement can track a difference in population or standing.
- It does not say which rule for combining verdicts is right, or which
  class of acts the standard covers. It says the standard must state both.

## Where it stands

Open. The fix is not a change of wording. [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md) exists to close the
first horn of [CLAIM-206](CLAIM-206.md): a kind that outruns every probe, the judges
included, could not be confirmed or defeated by them. It closed that horn
because competent uptake "is not a family the kind can outrun". If the
standard is defeasible, the kind can outrun it where the other constituents
diverge, and part of the horn is open again. That reaches [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)
version 2 and the ontological clause of [CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md), which take up the
same reply. [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md) itself grants: "A kind that outruns even
competent uptake is not excluded by argument here".

What would answer it: an argument that, for the acts the line studies, the
other constituents cannot diverge from the uptake of participants who know
the standings and norms in play; or a version of [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md) that states
the class of acts and the rule for divided verdicts, and says which test
still has a criterion when the standard is wrong. [QUESTION-073](../questions.d/QUESTION-073.md) and
[QUESTION-075](../questions.d/QUESTION-075.md) bear on both.

## Note of 2026-10-12: the interlocutor's reading, as advice

The interlocutor's reading of 2026-10-12, relayed by the owner, makes this entry's point a second time: "constitutive dependence is not the same as epistemic authority. Participants can collectively constitute a practice without individually possessing infallible access to its structure." It accepts competent uptake only "as a locally privileged criterion, conditional on a specified practice and population". It adds a case: "A formal notice may acquire legal force upon issuance, even if unread." Recorded as advice; the record does not take it. [CLAIM-255](CLAIM-255.md) files the reading's three-way split.
