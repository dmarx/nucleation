## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read in arXiv v1. The authors model a hiring screen's pipeline as a structural causal model and show on American Community Survey data that changing the salary cap that defines a positive label, the definition of a private-sector worker, or the model-selection metric moves the counterfactual explanations of two retrieval-based methods (DiCE-kdtree, NICE) more than changing the methods' validity threshold; changing the validation strategy moves them about as much (RQ1, Fig. 2), and agreement among explanations falls (RQ2, Fig. 3). From this they argue that as justification or recourse such explanations "preclude consideration of whether the decision-maker ought to have acted differently", and that recourse "is relational and involves multiple parties". They do not claim a defect in CEs as such. The exchange's account (A19 §7) is accurate; "shift responsibility" is A's wording. The Conclusions' "a single CE method and metric" does not match the body. See the NOTE and THEORY filed from this reading.
