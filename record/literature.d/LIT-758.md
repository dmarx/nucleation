# LIT-758: Issues as Elements of Information Systems

Werner Kunz and Horst W. J. Rittel (1970), *Issues as Elements of Information Systems*, Working Paper No. 131, Institute of Urban and Regional Development, University of California, Berkeley — <https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5cj786v8>

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmptx0xy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptx0xy.md).*

- **Issues are the atoms.** An issue has the form of a question, arises
  from a controversial statement, is specific to a situation, and is
  "raised, argued, settled, 'dodged', or substituted".
- **Objections are new issues.** I₂ is a direct successor of I₁ when it
  "challenges a statement made in support of one of the positions
  maintained in view of I₁". Other relations: generalisation, relevant
  analogy, and compatibility of positions across issues.
- **Four kinds of issue.** Factual, deontic, explanatory, instrumental.
  Questions of fact are kept apart until someone doubts an answer.
- **Why not a thesaurus.** The discourse runs on "ad hoc vocabulary which
  is rapidly changing, defying thesaurization", whose central terms are
  "proper names for long stories" that depend on context.
- **Numbered items.** "All the items are numbered consecutively to ease
  reference", with relations kept as matrices and drawn as issue maps.
- **Subsystems.** An issue bank of living, settled or abandoned, and
  latent issues; an evidence bank; a handbook of model problems; a topic
  list; an issue map; a documentation system.
- **Field report.** Three manual systems in government use, with "eager
  acceptance by the users although the implementation induced major
  changes in organization and working styles". Nothing about cost.
