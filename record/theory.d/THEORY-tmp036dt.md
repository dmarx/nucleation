# THEORY-tmp036dt: Psychopathy as the PCL-R measures it is one dimensional construct rather than a type: its items form four correlated factors, interpersonal, affective, lifestyle and antisocial, explained by one superordinate factor, and its affective features are tied psychometrically, genetically and longitudinally to its antisocial ones, so that no one component is its essence

## Source

Hare and Neumann (2008), "Psychopathy as a clinical and empirical
construct", *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* 4:217–246
(LIT-tmp57loc), read in full in NOTE-tmp1fxmh. Hare receives royalties
from the PCL-R (p. 240).

## What was actually shown

A review, not a study. It reports others' analyses and the authors' own.

- **The instrument.** The PCL-R is "a clinical construct rating scale
  that uses a semistructured interview, case history information, and
  specific scoring criteria" (p. 219): twenty items scored 0–2.
- **Four factors.** Interpersonal (glibness and superficial charm,
  grandiose sense of self-worth, pathological deception, conning and
  manipulation),
  affective (lack of remorse or guilt, shallow affect, callousness and
  lack of empathy, failure to accept responsibility for actions),
  lifestyle (need for stimulation, parasitic lifestyle, lack of realistic
  long-term goals, impulsivity, irresponsibility) and antisocial (poor
  behavioural controls, early behaviour problems, juvenile delinquency,
  revocation of conditional release, criminal versatility) (pp. 219–220).
  The first two make Factor 1 and the last two Factor 2, and one
  superordinate factor explains all four (pp. 220, 233–234).
- **No essential component.** The review answers the view that the
  affective features are the disorder's core: "a growing body of
  literature indicates that the affective features of psychopathy are
  intimately tied—i.e., psychometrically, genetically, and
  longitudinally—to the undercontrolled and fundamentally antisocial
  features of psychopathy. Thus, there appears to be no basis for holding
  one component of psychopathy as more essential than other components"
  (p. 231).
- **A continuum.** Psychopathic traits "are best viewed as existing on a
  continuum" (p. 234).
- **Against antisocial personality disorder.** The PCL-R combines
  personality traits with antisocial behaviour, where the diagnosis of
  antisocial personality disorder emphasises the behaviour (p. 219).

What could have come out otherwise: a factor structure in which the
affective items stood apart from the antisocial ones, or a taxon rather
than a dimension.

## What this does not say

- That the affective features are unimportant. The claim is that they
  are not *more* essential than the rest.
- That the structure holds outside the populations the PCL-R was built
  on. Much of the evidence is forensic, and several antisocial items
  read criminal history.
- Anything about corporations. Its bearing on the record's question is
  the record's inference: if no component is the essence, then a
  corporation matching only the conduct items (callousness, failure to
  accept responsibility, conning, parasitic lifestyle, irresponsibility)
  matches part of the construct to a degree, and with no affective
  counterpart sits nearer the behaviour-led diagnosis than the
  construct. Several antisocial items are developmental (early behaviour
  problems, juvenile delinquency) and have no corporate counterpart but
  by analogy. And the instrument's method, an interview, does not
  transfer at all, so a corporate "score" is an analogy, not an
  assessment.
