# CLAIM-415: A person can be organized by a judgement they attribute to a collective that does not hold it: the audience in a self-model can be a false unity as the owner can, and a judgement can shape a person who rejects it, because resisting it makes it consequential

## The claim

The owner, at U39: "connect these ideas to how an individual's
perspective can be influenced by groupthink and herd mentality, or even
just the perception of a plurality of opinion where one may not exist
(e.g. because of a loud minority). then examine the phrase 'I am whatever
you say I am'."

A39 corrected the framing first: "a loud minority producing a false
impression of consensus is not automatically *groupthink*. Groupthink
concerns problematic decision-making within groups; the apparent-consensus
problem often begins with how individuals infer what others believe." The
mechanisms it then set out are on file or registered: pluralistic
ignorance (Prentice and Miller, [LIT-1370](../literature.d/LIT-1370.md)), the majority illusion
(Lerman et al., [LIT-1390](../literature.d/LIT-1390.md)), and the silencing of a majority by a vocal
minority with private beliefs held fixed (Vilone and Polizzi, [LIT-939](../literature.d/LIT-939.md),
read as [THEORY-242](../theory.d/THEORY-242.md)). A39: "The community can acquire a misleading public
perspective without its members acquiring corresponding private beliefs."

The claim takes two steps beyond them.

1. **The audience in a self-model.** [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md) says a self-model can
   attribute real, distributed activity to a single owner the
   organization does not contain. A40: "The imagined owner need not be the
   only misleading entity in the self-model. The imagined social audience
   may also be organized into a false unity. A person can therefore find
   themselves governed by the attributed judgment of a collective that is
   not, in fact, united in holding that judgment." Cooley
   ([LIT-1379](../literature.d/LIT-1379.md)) already holds that the audience in the self is imagined
   and can be collective: the looking-glass self, "a very large and
   interesting class of cases" of the social self, runs through imagined
   judgements of others. The claim adds that the others imagined can be a
   unity no one composes ([TERM-075](../terms.d/TERM-075.md)'s imagined audience), which is [QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md)'s question ("when is the
   credit due to the population as organized?") asked from inside the
   person credited.
2. **Rejection does not free.** The lyric is Eminem's "The Way I Am"
   (2000), which A39 identified. Of its three readings, the third is the
   claim's: "A person can be shaped by a judgment they reject, because
   opposition to the judgment becomes an organizing feature of their
   activity." A40: "I can become partly organized by what you say I am,
   even when I refuse to believe you." Hacking's looping effects, which
   the record holds through [CLAIM-402](CLAIM-402.md), give the self-confirming reading.
   The resistance reading is the one the record lacked: the judgement
   acquires authority over conduct ([CLAIM-349](CLAIM-349.md)'s second term) without
   being believed. Cooley states the step: "non-conformity may be
   regarded as a remoter conformity" (ch. VIII). [CASE-142](../cases.d/CASE-142.md) works it.

A39 also gave the reading that restores agency: "I can recognize the
judgment, investigate its grounds, and refuse to let its apparent
popularity substitute for its actual credibility." That is [CLAIM-349](CLAIM-349.md)'s
adaptive regulation applied to social judgement: keep access, make
authority conditional on evidence. And the record's own form of the
point: "Repeated visibility, numerical prevalence, and independent
corroboration are different things" (A39), which [ADR-044](../decisions.d/ADR-044.md)'s `corroborates`
was written to keep apart.

## What it does not say

- **Not conversion.** A39: an apparent consensus becoming a real one is
  "possible, not necessary. The Vilone–Polizzi model … establishes
  distortion of expression without modeling private-belief conversion."
  Prentice and Miller's men moved toward the misperceived norm; their
  women did not.
- **Not that the attributed collective attitude is new.** List's §3.6,
  falsely attributed common attitudes, is in the record's reading
  ([NOTE-554](../notes.d/NOTE-554.md)), with Prentice and Miller as its example; [CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md) is a
  fanbase credited with a stance it has no procedure to form. A39
  proposed it as "a fourth analytical object" without citing either.
- **Not a term, as A39 coined it.** A39's "recursive social
  self-modeling" was offered "as a way of integrating established
  mechanisms, not as a new discovery". The objects it runs between are
  filed as [TERM-075](../terms.d/TERM-075.md)'s senses 2 and 3, not under that name.
- **Not that the majority illusion is the loud minority.** Of the claim's
  grounds, [LIT-1390](../literature.d/LIT-1390.md) supports only the route by which a rare attribute
  looks like a local majority through network position; the loud
  minority is [LIT-939](../literature.d/LIT-939.md)'s silencing.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the readings

- **The majority illusion is not pluralistic ignorance.** Lerman et al.
  ([LIT-1390](../literature.d/LIT-1390.md)) show a structural route to a perceived majority: many nodes
  see a majority among their neighbours while the attribute is rare
  overall, and each sees its neighbours correctly. The mechanism is
  degree, not volume, so it does not support the "loud minority" example,
  and it measures no belief or conformity. The attributes on its real
  networks are assigned by the authors. Pluralistic ignorance ([LIT-1370](../literature.d/LIT-1370.md),
  not reached) is a misjudgement of what others hold, not a skewed sample
  seen accurately. The exchange and the paper both run the two together.
- **Cooley holds more of the claim than credited.** The audience in the
  self is always imagined, and can be collective: "There is no separation
  between real and imaginary persons" ([LIT-1379](../literature.d/LIT-1379.md), ch. III). Step 2,
  "rejection does not free", is his: "non-conformity may be regarded as a
  remoter conformity" (ch. VIII).
- **But less than attributed.** The claim says Cooley "makes the
  self-conception run through imagined judgements of others". For Cooley
  the looking-glass self is one class of the social self, which he defines
  by self-feeling, and "I am whatever you say I am" is his unstable
  extreme, vanity: "becoming, so far as he can, what any flatterer says
  he is" (ch. VI). What remains the claim's own is a collective unity its
  supposed members do not compose.
