summary: >-
  Wilie, Cahyawijaya, Ishii, He and Fung (2024), Belief Revision: The
  Adaptability of Large Language Models Reasoning. Belief-R tests whether
  language models revise a conclusion when a new premise defeats it, and
  keep it when a new premise does not. Following Byrne's suppression task,
  each item gives two premises licensing modus ponens or modus tollens (time
  t), then a third premise (t+1) that either adds a requirement (the prior
  conclusion should be suppressed: "belief update") or an alternative (it
  should stand: "belief maintain"). About 2K items, annotated by majority
  vote. ~30 models are evaluated. Most models that do basic inference well
  fail at revision; larger LLMs reach at most about 50% on BREU, the mean of
  update and maintain accuracy; and models better at updating tend to be
  worse at maintaining. Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
