# SECTION-005: From productive dependence to constitution

## Manuscript text

The preceding examples are about causal mechanisms and the evidential limits of reduced dynamical models. They do not by themselves establish metaphysical constitution. It is tempting to conclude that, because a relation causally sustains an organization, it partly constitutes the identity of an individual. But such an inference needs a further premise identifying which relations are identity-constituting for the kind under discussion.

At least three notions should be kept apart. A relation may be *identificatory*, permitting a scientist to track a pattern. It may be *causally relevant*, influencing the pattern's behavior. It may be *constitutive*, partly determining what entity exists. A fourth notion, *mechanistic constitution*, concerns components and activities organized into a mechanism producing a phenomenon; its extension need not coincide with relational constitution of an individual. The mechanistic-constitution literature, especially Craver and subsequent criticism of mutual manipulability, should therefore be treated as a direct interlocutor rather than an incidental citation.

A university department provides a simple contrast. Interactions of friendship, proximity, scholarly collaboration, employment, and authorized office may all causally matter. They do not all necessarily constitute the department in the same sense. Two organizations with identical members may differ because the relations establishing their offices and authority differ. Conversely, a particular office can remain constituted by a legal authorization relation that the officeholder does not personally maintain.

The relevant philosophical proposal is a *defeasible constitutive warrant*: specify the kind of candidate individual, identify the relations alleged to constitute it, investigate how those relations are realized and maintained, test their relevance to continued existence or transformation of that kind, and compare competing boundary proposals. None of these steps alone is a sufficient metaphysical test; together they make a claim accountable to more than an appealing diagram of dependencies.

A candidate boundary can be used provisionally without circularity. What would be circular is to count every productive mechanism inside the chosen boundary as constitutive merely because it was included. Causal evidence can vindicate or disconfirm a constitutive hypothesis relative to its declared premises. It cannot alone supply those premises.
