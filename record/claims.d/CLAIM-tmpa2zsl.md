# CLAIM-tmpa2zsl: Evidence a judgement–observation loop did not select can have been selected by someone who models it, so the channel that anchors the loop is the channel through which it can be controlled: an anchor needs evidence whose selection is independent of the interpretation it tests, by anyone, and in the linearized model an adversary tracking the target's lean with gain ρ adds ρ to κ′

## The claim

[CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) holds that a judgement–observation loop is kept honest by
evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation
it tests. [QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md)'s candidates for such evidence in one mind
include input the loop did not select (candidate 1) and other people's
reports (candidate 5).

Reflexive control uses exactly those channels. Lefebvre and Lefebvre
(1984), as Chotikul quotes them ([LIT-tmpnwiw8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnwiw8.md), read in [NOTE-tmppxn3n](../notes.d/NOTE-tmppxn3n.md)),
define it as "conveying to a partner or an opponent specially prepared
information to incline him to voluntarily make the predetermined
decision". Lefebvre (1968), as quoted there, describes it as "providing him
with the grounds by which he is able to logically derive his own decision,
but one that is predetermined by the other side". Thomas (2004) and
Lefebvre's own books could not be read lawfully ([LIT-tmp0gbc8](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0gbc8.md), [LIT-tmpvq5lt](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvq5lt.md),
curation entry of 2026-10-17). In Lefebvre's formalism, as
the free front matter of *Algebra of Conscience* states it ([LIT-tmpvp0b1](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvp0b1.md)),
an individual has an "inner world" of "images of this individual, of his
partner, and of the relationships between them", which "may have their
'inner worlds'", and "By changing a formula we can change both the
behavior and the structure of the inner world of an individual". The
images are part of the agent's choosing apparatus, not only beliefs about
the other side. The sister record reads it as level-k recursive modelling
([MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md)). The adversary
"injects signals" into the target's observation stream to control its model,
"such that" the target's policy "maps to an action benefiting" the
adversary. The evidence the target receives is unselected by the target and
selected by someone who models the target's interpretation.

So the anchor condition has to be strengthened. The evidence must be
independent of the interpretation it tests, not only in that the loop did
not choose it, but in that no one chose it as a function of that
interpretation.

## In the linearized model

[CASE-106](../cases.d/CASE-106.md) scores committed predictions against evidence read as
r = e + κ′m. Let an adversary supply part of the evidence as a function of
the target's lean, e = e₀ + ρm + b, where ρ is how far the signal tracks the
target's current interpretation and b is a constant push. Linearizing
gives (checked with sympy, scratch/formal/check.py's method):

  γ = 4ηs(κ′ + ρ − κ̄),  with a constant drive 4ηs·b on authority.

The model covers one of reflexive control's methods, not all of them.
Chotikul lists ten (p. 82, in the report's own outline, not quoted from the 1984
report). Only one is an evidence term: "transferring false information
about the real situation". In the model that is the constant push b. It
leaves stability alone and moves the equilibrium toward the specialist
whose hypothesis the controller favours, so the target's loop works as
designed and settles where the controller wanted. A lean-tracking term
like ρ appears in the source only as a condition for a deception to be
accepted: it must conform to the target's strategic assumptions (p. 70).

The other methods act elsewhere in the operator model ([CLAIM-336](CLAIM-336.md)):

- **On the goal and the frame.** "Creating a goal for the opponent" and
  "creating the doctrine for the opponent" act on the query and the
  operators, not on the evidence.
- **On the decision.** "Transferring a decision" acts on the read-out.
- **On the loop itself.** "Destructive" control is meant to "destroy,
  paralyze, or neutralize" the target's decision procedures (p. 81).

The report also says, without a citation, that control "may be conducted
effectively without a chain of feedback" (p. 81), though its own Figure 15
draws a loop with feedback. Open-loop control of this kind is chosen in
advance from a model of the target. That is [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s "anyone's model of
it" in its plainest form.

## The trade

[MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md) names it: an agent whose model "is not observation-driven" is
immune to this, "and also ... not adaptive". In this record's terms, the
channel that anchors a loop ([CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)) is the channel through which
it can be controlled. What separates an anchor from an attack is not the
channel but whether whoever fills it selects the evidence as a function of
the target's interpretation.

## Defences

[MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md) names three structural defences, "restricting the observation
channel, requiring costly signals, or acting on commitments fixed in
advance", and argues that recursive depth is not one: "Depth is an arms
race, not a defence". In the model:

- **Commitment** keeps the evidence gain ([CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md)) but does not remove ρ.
  And it is itself a target: "creating the doctrine for the opponent"
  supplies the commitments.
- **Costly signals** bound how far b and ρ can be pushed.
- **Restricting the channel** lowers the share of evidence an adversary
  supplies.

None makes the evidence independent. Each limits how much of it can be
adversarially selected. The Soviet sources, as Chotikul reports them,
treat depth as the defence the sister record denies it is: "the advantage
... lies on the side having the highest rank of reflection" (p. 80).
Neither they nor Chotikul consider escalation.

## What it does not say

- It does not say all unselected evidence is adversarial. Most is not. The
  claim is that "unselected by the target" does not certify independence.
- Bayesian persuasion ([THEORY-404](../theory.d/THEORY-404.md)) is the cooperative limit. There the
  sender commits openly to a signal and the receiver knows the design, and
  persuasion still moves beliefs only in ways whose average is the prior.
  Reflexive control drops the open commitment and the receiver's knowledge
  of the design. That is why it can steer.
- Lefebvre is quoted at second hand, through Chotikul's public-release
  report. His books and Thomas (2004) could not be read lawfully.
