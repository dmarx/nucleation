# NOTE-tmpl4pbc: Ward — What is enactivism?

## Contribution

The paper corrects a taxonomy its own author helped spread (Ward,
Silverman & Villalobos 2017). It separates two questions usually run
together. One is where the processes underlying cognition are: in the
skull, or across brain, body and world. The other is what the meaning of
mental states fundamentally depends on: informational properties, or
organizational properties distinctive of living things. On the second
question the enactive approach of Varela, Thompson & Rosch's *The
Embodied Mind* (TEM) is organicist. Most other "embodied" views are not.
Two further commitments, experiential primacy and reciprocity, set it
apart from other organicist views as well.

## Key insight

"Embodied" is not the same as "organic". A view can put cognition in
brain–body–world loops and still hold that those loops matter only
because of the information they carry. Ward places embodied predictive
processing, extended functionalism, cybernetics and most ecological
psychology there. Enactivism, by contrast, holds that information
processing is meaningful "only in virtue of" its inherence in living
activity (§5). The sensorimotor slogan that gave enactivism its usual
definition is, on this reading, TEM's least distinctive commitment.

## Assumptions

- **TEM is the reference text.** Enactivism means the view of TEM as
  developed by Varela, Thompson and Di Paolo. Ward stipulates that
  "enactivism" unqualified will mean views combining continuity, primacy
  and reciprocity (§1).
- **"Fundamental dependence" can be weak.** The organicist/informationalist
  split is about what the meaning of mental states "fundamentally depends
  on". A methodological reading, that good explanation must appeal to
  organizational properties distinctive of living things, is enough: "The
  weaker reading is necessary and sufficient for membership of the
  ‘organicist’ grouping" (§3). The metaphysical reading is "an optional
  add-on".
- **Distinctive, not merely characteristic.** Organicism requires
  properties that set living things apart from inorganic nature. Features
  found in both, such as feedback loops, do not count (§§3–4).
- **Information is meant broadly.** Informationalism covers any view that
  treats cognition as processing of, or dealing with, information, and
  takes information to be precise enough to bear the theory's weight
  (§3).

## Key results

The paper argues; it reports no data. By section:

- **§§1–2, the lump-and-split taxonomy.** Enactivism became one branch of
  an anti-cognitivist "embodied cognition", defined by "cognition emerges
  from sensorimotor activity" (Shapiro & Spaulding 2025). Footnote 3 traces
  this to TEM's own "nutshell" on p. 173, which Ward calls "a poor nutshell
  definition of enactivism" when read out of context.
- **§3, the second axis.** Informationalists hold that the meaning of
  mental states depends on informational properties; organicists hold
  that it depends on organizational properties distinctive of living
  things. The axis is "orthogonal" to the cognitivist–embodied one.
- **§4, sorting the views (Figure 1).**
  - Embodied predictive processing is informationalist: bodily and worldly
    factors "are important only insofar as they help implement the
    hierarchical pattern of information flow upon which cognition
    ultimately depends".
  - Cybernetic views appeal to feedback cycles "found throughout organic
    and inorganic nature, cycles which can just as well be implemented by
    non-living systems like autonomous drones, or suitably constituted
    and embedded LLMs". Those properties are "characteristic rather than
    distinctive of living systems", so these views are not organicist.
  - Basal cognition and ecological psychology are split: organicist only
    where biological self-maintenance, not information processing, is "in
    the explanatory driving seat".
  - Footnote 6: biological naturalism of the kind that says consciousness
    depends on computations only an organic substrate can implement
    (Milinkovic & Aru 2026; Seth 2026) is not thereby organicist. "Such
    views tend to see biological properties as relevant only as
    implementations of the computational properties that remain in the
    explanatory driving seat".
- **§5, continuity.** The bacterium swimming up a sugar gradient: a
  self-producing network whose activity is oriented to keeping itself
  going, so that sugar has "an organism-relative meaning". From Thompson &
  Stapleton: "cognition is grounded on the sense-making activity of
  autonomous agents". Ward grants that "Just how and whether this crucial
  argument for life/mind continuity works is complex and controversial".
- **§6, primacy and reciprocity.** Lived experience is both the primary
  explanandum and the evidential base. But experience is not a fixed
  point: "the structure of the lived experience that we are trying to
  understand is malleable and shaped by whatever contingent and evolving
  cultural apparatus for making sense of things currently surrounds us".
  Scientific concepts are part of that apparatus. The illustration is
  meditation: TEM's revised introduction prefers a reading on which
  "mindfulness practices should be understood as skillful ways of enacting
  certain kinds of embodied states and behaviors in the world, not as
  inner observation of an observer-independent mental stream". Hence
  "Reports of mindfulness practitioners should be taken not as privileged
  insights into primal experiential truths". Ward is explicit that whether
  enactivists are right that informationalism leaves a gap "is not
  something I aim to establish here".
- **§7, payoffs.** Enactivism is both a research programme and a
  philosophy of nature, and blurs the line between them. It does not ban
  informational or representational explanation: TEM grants "the need for
  a symbolic level" while calling such descriptions approximate. Its own
  claims about minimal subjectivity "are not immutable truths but partial
  perspectives". The conclusion: "The lesson is not that informationalism
  is wrong, or to be unequivocally discarded; it is that it is partial".

## Concepts

- **Lump-and-split taxonomy**: lumping views that stress embodied
  sensorimotor interaction as "enactivism", then splitting them into
  autopoietic, sensorimotor and radical variants (§1).
- **Informationalism**: the meaning of mental states depends on
  informational properties, and "information" can be made precise enough
  to bear the theory (§3).
- **Organicism**: the meaning of mental states depends on organizational
  properties *distinctive* of living things, at least as a methodological
  requirement on explanation (§3).
- **Characteristic versus distinctive**: properties living things have
  and the inorganic also has, against properties only living things have
  (§§3–4).
- **Life–mind continuity, experiential primacy, reciprocity**: the three
  commitments Ward takes to define enactivism (§1).
- **Sense-making**: the generation of organism-relative meaning by the
  integrated activity of a self-maintaining system (§5).

## Connections

The paper reads TEM (1991; revised edition 2017) through Thompson's *Mind
in Life* (2007), *Waking, Dreaming, Being* (2014) and *The Blind Spot*
(Frank, Gleiser & Thompson 2024), and Di Paolo's work on adaptivity and
sense-making. It sets enactivism against Hutto & Myin's radical
enactivism, Noë's and O'Regan's sensorimotor views, Clark's and Seth's
predictive processing, Gibsonian ecological psychology and Lyon's basal
cognition. It names Villalobos (2013) among cyberneticists hostile to
informational talk, and Villalobos co-wrote the 2017 taxonomy Ward now
disowns. The record holds none of these, apart from Seth's work: Seth
2014 and 2021 are placed on the informationalist side, which bears on the
record's Seth readings (LIT-1263; LIT-135).

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpjw8ev (grounding is a relation, apart from occurrence).** The
  exchange cited this paper for life–mind continuity and the primacy of
  experience, and drew from it the enactive challenge to the claim: that
  an organization can be held without current input does not show it
  could arise without an embodied history. The paper does not make that
  argument. It does not discuss brains in vats, development, grounding,
  or experience without current input. The challenge is the exchange's
  inference from the enactive view, and should be cited as such. Read on
  its own, the paper's objection to the record lies elsewhere. The claim
  keeps the developmental question open and says so, which the paper
  cannot fault. What the paper would contest is the claim's other half:
  that what makes an interpretive organization experiential need not be
  what grounds it. For an enactivist, meaning, and with it experience,
  depends on the organization's inherence in a living self-maintaining
  system (§5), not on its informational structure.
- **CLAIM-tmpdga80 and TERM-tmpgqmop (interpretation as organization of
  information).** On Ward's map the constitutive thesis is
  informationalist. TERM-tmpgqmop defines interpretation as
  differentiating information within an operative organization, and A7
  separated the thesis from any requirement of biology. That puts the
  thesis with predictive processing and cybernetics, and against
  enactivism, however much it speaks of organization. The cut is
  sharpened by TERM-tmpe36gg: self-maintenance was withdrawn as a
  necessary mark of perspective, which is the step an organicist would
  refuse. The paper does not show the organicist is right (§6), so this
  locates the record's thesis rather than refuting it.
- **Reciprocity as a constitution claim.** One strand runs the other way.
  Ward holds that the vocabularies we use to understand ourselves "pre-
  and re-shape the experiences themselves" (§1, developed in §6). That is a claim that
  interpretation partly constitutes the character of experience, at the
  level of culture rather than of a single system's processing. It is
  closer to CLAIM-tmpdga80's direction than the exchange's use of the
  paper suggests, though it is asserted, not argued.
- **QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.** Ward's distinctive/characteristic distinction is a
  ready test for any proposed answer. A condition that drones or "suitably
  constituted and embedded LLMs" could meet is, for the enactivist,
  characteristic of life and not distinctive, and so cannot be what makes
  meaning. The question's own constraint, that the separation must not be
  drawn from the verdict on machines, cuts against that test too.
- **The meditation readings (LIT-tmphw2n7, LIT-tmpg6f7h).** §6's reading of
  contemplative reports, as enacted within a tradition's vocabulary and not
  as observation of a fixed inner stream, bears on both. Woods, Windt &
  Carter code teachers' descriptions, and Chandia-Jorquera et al. give
  participants a scale with its top defined as "free from content". On
  Ward's view neither reaches an experience prior to its description.
- **THEORY-767 and the Seth line.** Ward's footnote 6 places Seth's
  biological naturalism on the informationalist side. The record's
  interoceptive-inference reading (THEORY-767; LIT-1263) is, on this
  taxonomy, embodied but not enactive.

## Limitations

- A taxonomic and exegetical paper. It argues for a way of carving the
  field and for what TEM meant. It does not argue that organicism is
  true, and says so (§6).
- The placements are rough. Ward says the partition does not yield "a neat
  and unequivocal sorting" (§4), and many theorists leave their stance
  indeterminate.
- Life–mind continuity is described, not defended. The argument is "one
  way it can be sketched", and its critics are cited, not answered (§5).
- Reciprocity, the paper's most striking commitment, is illustrated by the
  history of TEM's readings of Buddhism, not by evidence that vocabularies
  change experience.
- Read in the accepted manuscript, without page numbers.

## Open questions

- What would show that a property is distinctive of life rather than
  characteristic of it? Without a criterion, the organicist line can be
  redrawn whenever a non-living system meets the last one.
- Can the weak, methodological organicism make any claim about which
  systems have minds or experience, or only about how to explain the ones
  that do?
- Is reciprocity testable: does adopting a vocabulary change experience,
  or only reports of it? The meditation literature (LIT-tmpg6f7h) is one
  place to look.
- Where does a self-maintaining but non-living system, an organizational
  closure built in silico, fall on the axis?
