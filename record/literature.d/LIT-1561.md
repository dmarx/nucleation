# LIT-1561: Gene ancestries reveal diverse microbial associations during eukaryogenesis

Bernabeu, M., Manzano-Morales, S., Marcet-Houben, M. & Gabaldón, T. (2026). Gene ancestries reveal diverse microbial associations during eukaryogenesis. *Nature* 656(8127): 399–405. doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10639-9.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1237](../notes.d/NOTE-1237.md).*

- **Two more donors.** Under thresholds chosen to maximize assignments to
  Alphaproteobacteria and Asgard, Myxococcota and Planctomycetota hold
  comparable shares in every dataset ([THEORY-1105](../theory.d/THEORY-1105.md)).
- **Not misplaced mitochondrial genes.** With Alphaproteobacteria removed
  from the database, their genes fall to Gammaproteobacteria first; the
  new donors do not fit that pattern.
- **Waves, by stem length.** Asgard first, then Planctomycetota, then the
  mitochondrial ancestor and Myxococcota. The ordering depends on stem
  lengths as relative times, which Tobiasson et al. dispute.
- **Viruses as carriers.** About 4.5% of families have a viral sister, 74%
  of them giant viruses, some with prokaryotic genes behind them.
- **Donors, not shown partners.** "our data do not provide information on
  the host or endosymbiotic nature of any partner."

## Why this is here

A2 cites it as the distributed side of the live disagreement, and rates it "High". A2's own caution, kept here: a history of horizontal gene transfer does not by itself show a past endosymbiosis.

The reading confirms the caution: the authors make it themselves. Like [LIT-1558](LIT-1558.md), the paper places the mitochondrion late, and both have bacterial genes arriving before it. The disagreement between them is over how those genes are grouped and timed ([NOTE-1237](../notes.d/NOTE-1237.md)).
