### Summary
An actor-critic agent gets a second network, the "question network" (meta-parameters η), that reads FUTURE observations o_{t+1:t+j} and outputs 128 cumulants (arctan) and discounts (sigmoid). Together with the current main-task policy these define on-policy GVFs. A linear "answer network" on the shared encoder learns to predict them by multi-step TD. η is trained by a meta-gradient: θ is unrolled through L=k=10 inner updates (RL loss plus answer loss), and η descends the sum of the main-task RL losses over those updates ("area under the curve"; Alg. 1, Eq. 3). There are two settings. In "Representation learning" the encoder gets ONLY answer-loss gradients. In "joint learning" (57 Atari games, IMPALA) it gets both.

### Contribution
(1) The question network: a learned parameterization of auxiliary-task targets, updated by meta-gradients through the main-task loss. (2) Evidence that unrolled (non-myopic) meta-gradients are needed: k=1 or 2 fails. (3) Atari results for representation-only and joint training.

### Key results (Paper.pdf unless noted)
1. Representation from discovered questions alone suffices (§4.1, p. 8, Figs. 2-3): "in the two gridworld domains the resulting policies were optimal (see Figure 2); in the Atari domains the resulting policies were comparable to those achieved by the state of the art IMPALA agent after training for 200M frames". Caveat read off the figures: the x-axes run to 1e7 frames (gridworld, against "A2C (3M)") and 2e9 frames (Atari, against "IMPALA (200M)"). The paper concedes the reference "learned faster" (p. 8).
2. Hand-designed and random questions fail in that setting (p. 8): "in Atari (shown in Figure 3) the handcrafted auxiliary tasks, now including a “Pixel Control” baseline (green), resulted in almost no learning."
3. Myopic meta-gradients fail (p. 8, Fig. 5 right): "the representation learned with k = 1 and k = 2 was insufficient for the final policy to do anything meaningful." Longer is not always better: "the largest unroll length k = 50 performing worse than k = 25", conjectured to come from "increased variance of the meta-gradient estimates". In Puddleworld (supp. Fig. 9) k=10 was best and k=20 and k=50 much worse.
4. Number of questions matters (p. 8, Fig. 5 left): "Too few questions (d = 2) did not provide enough signal"; "too many questions (e.g. d = 128) made learning slower". All other runs fix "d = 128 and k = 10".
5. Area-under-curve against final-loss meta-objective (p. 8, Fig. 4): "both approaches can be effective, but we found that optimizing area under the curve to be more stable."
6. Joint learning (§4.2, p. 9, Fig. 6): "None of the auxiliary tasks outperformed IMPALA on each and every of the 57 games." On the top 10 pixel-control games: "large gains in 6 out of 10 games, small gains in 2, and losses in 2." Read off Fig. 6 right (approximate): at N=57 the Discovered-GVFs median (~210%) is below IMPALA and Reward Prediction (~221%) and above Pixel Control (~205%). It leads only on the top-N subsets N=10-40, which are ranked by pixel-control gain. The text does not state the N=57 ordering.
7. Supp. §6.6, Fig. 8: in gravitar and frostbite the representation-only agent "significantly outperforms also the plain “IMPALA” agent (trained for 200M frames)". 3 seeds; no significance test is reported.

### Assumptions
- Environments: Puddleworld (continuous 2-D, 5 actions), Collect-Objects (four-room grid, 2 ordered objects, episodes ≤40), Atari ALE (6 games for representation-only; 57 for joint).
- Architecture: A2C, 5-step, 16 threads (gridworlds); IMPALA, 20-step, 200 actors (Atari). Linear policy, value and answer heads. Representation runs use an independent question network that mirrors the agent's hidden layers. Joint runs feed the question network the ResNet's last hidden layer (two MLPs, 256/128).
- "Question" = a vector of cumulants u_t and discounts γ_t computed from future observations; the policy is fixed as the current main-task policy (on-policy). In gridworlds the discount is hand-tuned and only cumulants are learned (§3.2).
- "Useful" = lowers the summed main-task RL pseudo-loss over the next L inner updates (Eq. 3). No other criterion: no generality, interpretability or transfer.
- Meta-horizon: unroll L=k=10 updates (Table 1), truncated. η gets one update per k θ-updates.
- Key experiment: the encoder is trained only by the answer loss (§4.1: "the parameters of the encoder network are unaffected by gradients from the main-task updates"). But η is trained on the main-task loss, so main-task information still reaches the encoder, through the questions. The paper does not discuss this route.

### Concepts (quoted)
- GVF (§1, p. 3): "Generalized value functions, or GVFs, generalize the standard value function to allow for arbitrary cumulant functions of states in place of rewards, and are specified by the combination of such a cumulant function with a discount factor and a policy." The abstract says "general value functions".
- Question/answer in a value function (§1): "the question is “what is the discounted sum of future rewards under some policy?” and the answer is the approximate value function."
- Cumulant (intro, p. 1): "any goal might be formulated in terms of a scalar signal, or cumulant (White, 2015), whose discounted sum must be maximized"; prior work estimated "discounted sums of suitable handcrafted functions of state, cumulants in the GVF terminology".
- Discount: specified in γ_t as an output of the question network (§2.1: "computes the values of a set of cumulants ut and their corresponding discounts γt").
- Question network (§2.1, p. 3): "It takes as inputs j future observations ot+1:t+j and, through the meta-parameters η, computes the values of a set of cumulants ut and their corresponding discounts γt". The terminology "is derived from work on TD networks (Sutton & Tanner, 2005)".
- Answer network (§2.4, p. 5): "an answer network that, given the state xt approximates the GVF answers." On use (§2.1): "the question and answer networks are only used during training, and neither is needed for action selection."
- Non-myopic meta-gradient (§2.2, p. 3): "Such meta-loss may be evaluated after one update (myopic) or k > 1 updates (non-myopic)." "changing the meta-parameters η at one time step affects not just the immediate update to θ on the next time step, but at all future updates."

### Verdict on the abstract
Accurate in substance, with three qualifications.
(a) "sufficient, on their own": true of final performance in 2 gridworlds and 6 Atari games, but it took ~10x the frames on Atari, and the main-task loss shapes the encoder indirectly through the meta-learned questions.
(b) "better than popular hand-designed auxiliary tasks": true in the representation-only setting, where those tasks were never meant to stand alone. In joint training it holds on pixel-control-favoured subsets.
(c) "improve the data efficiency": shown as final score at 200M frames on top-N subsets. Over all 57 games the median is not above IMPALA (plot-read).
"Discover their own questions" means a learned parameterization within a fixed family of 128 on-policy GVFs over the observation stream. The paper does not analyse what was discovered.

### Bearing on the record
- CLAIM-754 (inquiry frontier): a parallel at most. The paper's "questions" are prediction targets: auxiliary-loss targets, chosen purely for their instrumental effect on a fixed main task, never pursued, and never used for action ("neither is needed for action selection"). The agent does not formulate questions in TERM-092's sense, determinate enough to pursue with its resources. It tunes 128 targets inside a fixed expressible family. What it does show is that the set of targets an agent learns to predict can itself be learned and can change what the agent later does (via the representation). That is a mechanism, not a measure, for moving something query-like. The paper never measures any frontier.
- CLAIM-1009 (answer frontier): operationalizes the split architecturally. A separate question network and answer network map onto query and answer components. The "Random GVFs" baseline separates them. The answer network can learn answers to fixed random questions, yet the representation fails. Question choice therefore matters independently of answering capacity. The paper reports no answer accuracy, so the answer side is not measured.
- CLAIM-1003 (losing the ability to ask is harder to detect): qualifies by contrast. Here bad questions are fully detectable, because a declared main task scores them, and the meta-gradient relies on that signal. The claim's hard case is the one with no downstream task signal, which the paper does not have.
- CLAIM-756 (acquisitions valued by effects on later ones): operationalizes a narrow form, and gives empirical support for the myopic failure mode. Questions have no value of their own. η is valued only by what answering them does to subsequent main-task learning over k updates. The myopic valuation (k=1, 2) fails to find useful questions (result 3). But the "later acquisition" is the same fixed main task, not a different later one, and nothing shows a step with negative direct return inside the best policy.
- CLAIM-758 (overconsolidation): no bearing beyond parallel. There is no later task family, no plasticity or forgetting measure, and no long-horizon stabilization.
- QUESTION-495 / LEAD-011 / CLAIM-1197 / CLAIM-1130 / CLAIM-1036: sidesteps rather than supplies. The meta-objective is a fixed external valuation (main-task loss), which meets CLAIM-1197's condition 2 without resource costs. The truncated k=10 unroll gives a finite objective (condition 3) by truncation, not by a discount that outruns growth. Attainment (condition 4) is not addressed: local gradient descent with no optimality claim. The task law (condition 1) is the fixed environment, though the paper notes the cumulants are non-stationary because the policy and η change. CLAIM-1036 is excluded by construction: what the agent predicts changes, but the judge (main-task loss) does not. The finding that extends the line: longer meta-horizons can do WORSE in practice (k=50 < k=25; Puddleworld k=20, 50 < k=10). The authors conjecture estimator variance. So even where a horizon-defined optimum exists, the method aimed at it can degrade as the horizon grows. That is a fifth, practical concern (estimability) that CLAIM-1197's four conditions do not name.
- SYNTHESIS-105: a candidate ML instance for the line. What an agent is made to predict changes what it can later learn to do on the main task.
- TERM-131 (learning): consistent. The random-question baseline is a clean counterfactual: the same answering machinery and the same experience, with a different retained organization.
- NOTE-2415 / THEORY-2305 (Lippl and Lindsey): a parallel. Joint learning is multi-task learning with a learned auxiliary task. The paper adds empirical evidence that the choice of auxiliary target decides whether useful features form at all. It offers no theory of which features are reused.

### Limitations and gaps
- 3 seeds everywhere. No confidence intervals and no significance tests, yet the text says "significantly".
- Representation-only comparisons pit ~2e9-frame runs against a 200M-frame reference line.
- Main-task signal leaks to the encoder through η. This undercuts "on their own" as a test of question-driven representation, and the paper does not discuss it.
- Baseline hyperparameters were chosen on the A2C/IMPALA baseline. Joint-run auxiliary hyperparameters were searched on 10 games.
- Fig. 6 subset selection is ranked by pixel-control gains. All-57 medians do not favour the method.
- Discovered questions are never inspected or characterized. The paper gives no evidence that they are "questions" in any interpretable sense.
- d and k are sensitive and non-monotone, and the optimum differs across the two gridworlds. The text calls them "consistent".
- On-policy GVFs only. Compute and memory costs of the unroll are not reported.
- Joint-learning "data efficiency" is shown only as final score at a fixed budget. There are no learning curves for the joint runs.

### Candidate THEORY
statement: Veeriah and colleagues show that an actor-critic agent can meta-learn, by gradients unrolled over ten updates through its main-task loss, the cumulants and discounts of 128 on-policy general value functions. Predicting these, with no main-task gradient reaching the encoder, yields representations on which linear policies reach the main-task-trained reference in two gridworlds and six Atari games, though with up to ten times the frames, where reward prediction, pixel control and fixed random questions barely learn. One- and two-step (myopic) meta-gradients fail.
promote_when: An independent replication in which questions (auxiliary-task targets) discovered by meta-gradient, used as auxiliary tasks, improve main-task learning over the same agent with fixed random or hand-designed auxiliary tasks.
