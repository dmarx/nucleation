# THEORY-586: The ψ-ontology theorems exclude only overlap of the distributions that distinct quantum states induce over ontic states; overlap is sufficient but not necessary for reading ψ as knowledge, so a model in which the state is ψ-ontic in Harrigan and Spekkens' sense can still have ψ represent partial knowledge

## Source

Hance, Rarity & Ladyman (2021, v4 2022), [LIT-090](../literature.d/LIT-090.md), read in full in [NOTE-072](../notes.d/NOTE-072.md).

## What was actually shown

The paper is a conceptual argument with no theorem.

**Informal terms (§II).** A term is ontic when "taken to represent how the
world is independently of our knowledge of it to some extent or other",
epistemic when "taken to represent knowledge or information about the
world to some extent or other". Thermodynamic entropy and proper mixtures
are offered as terms that are both.

**Formal terms (§III).** In the ontological-models framework, ψ-ontic means
disjoint supports for distinct states (Eq. 5); ψ-epistemic means overlap
for some pair (Eq. 4). These are contradictories by definition.

**The gap (§IV).** Overlap implies the weaker condition Eq. 6: on Λψ some
response function differs from the Born probability for ψ. Models without
overlap can satisfy Eq. 6 as well. So "there is nothing in Harrigan and
Spekkens' formal definition for a model being ψ-ontic ... which is
necessary or sufficient for a model to have wavefunctions representing
reality" (p. 4).

**Conclusion (§V).** PBR and the other no-go theorems "do not rule out all
epistemic models". The paper accepts PBR's result "given certain
assumptions" and reads its conclusion as about overlap.

**What could have come out otherwise.** PBR's own text could have tied its
conclusion to a broader notion than overlap. It does not: PBR defines
"mere information" as overlap (PBR p. 2) and leaves the QBist option open
(PBR p. 5), which [NOTE-072](../notes.d/NOTE-072.md) records as compatible with this reading (C5).

## What this does not say

- **That some ψ-ontic model is epistemic in a precise sense.** The only
  example, Schlosshauer–Fine's ψ-dependent models, is cited, not exhibited
  (p. 4; [NOTE-072](../notes.d/NOTE-072.md), C4).
- **That Eq. 6 captures "ψ represents knowledge".** [NOTE-072](../notes.d/NOTE-072.md) shows that
  Eq. 6 holds exactly when ψ is predictively incomplete, so any
  deterministic hidden-variable model, Bohmian mechanics included, counts
  as epistemic, against the paper's own §I. Hence Proposed.
- **That ontological models cannot reproduce interference or the bomb
  tester.** Appendix A says so. [THEORY-403](THEORY-403.md)'s explicit nonnegative,
  local model reproduces the bomb tester in the fragment it covers.
- **Anything about PBR's assumptions.** The paper never discusses
  preparation independence, which is where PBR is most open to challenge.
