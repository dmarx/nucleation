# NOTE-tmpujosr: Clark — Intrinsic content, active memory and the extended mind

## Contribution

The paper is the first reply by either author of [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) to the published
criticism. It sets the terms of the later debate in three ways. It names
"the Parity principle". It says the thesis concerns material vehicles, not
contents (note 1). And it recasts the argument as a challenge: show why
Otto's notebook is not just an unusual realiser. It also introduces the
"ecological control" picture of the brain that Clark's book develops.

## Key insight

The critics granted parity and then looked for something the notebook
lacks. Each candidate (intrinsic content, active updating) is either
missing from some states we already count as cognitive, or would not
matter if it were missing inside the head. So the question returns to
parity, and parity is meant to "command rational assent" as a release from
"bio-chauvinistic prejudices".

## Assumptions

- **Vehicles, not contents.** Having a contentful state is a property of a
  whole active system. The thesis concerns the material aspects that
  enable it (note 1).
- **Parity is not argued.** "We did not argue for this principle, except by
  providing illustrations and defences against counter-arguments" (p. 2).
- **Functionalism with hybrid realisers.** Following Weiskopf, functionalists
  "have long been open to unusual realizers", including silicon-biological
  hybrids (p. 3).
- **Content is "as content does"** (Prinz and Clark 2004). Intrinsic content
  is "essentially fuzzy and indistinct" (p. 4).

## Key results

- **Parity, restated (p. 2).** "If, as we confront some task, a part of the
  world functions as a process which, were it to go on in the head, we
  would have no hesitation in accepting as part of the cognitive process,
  then that part of the world is (for that time) part of the cognitive
  process. (Clark and Chalmers 1998: 8)". Note 3 calls it a "veil of
  metabolic ignorance".
- **Intrinsic content, first reply (p. 4).** It is unclear the notion is
  intelligible. Where it is, nothing makes it neural: artificially evolved
  creatures, and a silicon neuron that worked within 14 biological neurons
  of a Californian spiny lobster (Szucs et al. 2000), suggest silicon can
  carry it.
- **Second reply (p. 5).** In-the-head problem solving with an imagined Venn
  diagram uses a conventional representation, and is cognitive. For the
  harder case of Otto's dormant entry: Martians who store bit-mapped images
  of text in biological memory would count as remembering, so Otto should.
  "All that matters, for some conventional encoding to count as the vehicle
  of a dispositional belief, is that it be appropriately linked, at
  run-time, to representations whose content is ... intrinsic."
- **Third reply (pp. 5–6).** Adams and Aizawa concede (2001: 50) that it is
  unclear how much of each cognitive state must involve non-derived content.
  "This concession, I submit, removes the entire force of the appeal to
  intrinsic content", since EM never claimed a cognizer made wholly of
  notebooks.
- **Active memory (pp. 6–7).** The worry (O'Brien, Dartnall, Weiskopf):
  biological memory revises itself and propagates updates, and a notebook
  does not. Reply: a system that updated only at recall time ("compilation"
  rather than "interpretation") would be "every bit as rational as we
  are". Otto updates at run-time too. Concession: the flow of revision is
  "restricted and local", and "the notebook's lack of inbuilt links and
  pathways makes it unlike biological memory".
- **The weakened claim (p. 7).** Otto-and-the-notebook "exhibit enough of
  the central features and dynamics of a normal agent having ... the
  dispositional belief". A world in which face memory were never
  reconstructive would still count those memories as cognitive, so the
  static notebook can count too.
- **The larger picture (pp. 7–10).** Against Dartnall's spade ("my-spade-and-I
  do not get the prize"), the line between a constantly available tool and
  a limb is "fuzzy at best". Macaque rake use (Iriki et al. 1996) and
  stick-induced near-space remapping (Berti and Frassinetti 2000) show
  tools entering body maps. Brains are "open-ended controllers" practising
  "ecological control". Note 7 reports MIT "memory glasses" that cue names
  below conscious awareness and improve memory in mildly Alzheimic patients
  "by about 50%", credited to Chalmers. Persons are better identified with
  Rovane's "rational point of view", and their identification with the
  biological is a Lockean "forensic matter".

## Concepts

- **material vehicle** — the enduring material aspect of a system that
  enables it to have a contentful state.
- **Parity principle** — the name given here to [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)'s §2 sentence.
- **active dispositional memory** — the critics' point that biological memory
  is integrative and reconstructive.
- **ecological control** — conscious control that devolves detail to
  non-conscious mechanisms, body mechanics and external devices.
- **open-ended controller** — a brain that computes, moment to moment, which
  resources are available and under its control.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md).** Indirect only. Rovane's "rational point of view",
  which Clark favours for persons, comes from a book (not held here) that
  is usually cited for allowing group persons. That bears on the agency half of the account, which the
  readings there already grant, and not on phenomenal consciousness, which
  the paper does not discuss.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Imagined cases throughout.** Martians, compiled updating and
  non-reconstructive face memory are parity arguments, and their force is
  parity's.
- **The empirical material is suggestive.** One silicon neuron in a lobster
  ganglion, and body-map remapping from tool use, are about sensorimotor
  integration, not belief.
- **It answers Adams and Aizawa's first mark, not their second.** Their
  claim that the processes differ in kind is met only by the active-memory
  concession and the "below the level" reply.

## Open questions

- What is the level "to which the philosophical functionalist should
  attend", and does memory science's level (interference, learning curves)
  fall below it? Rupert says no.
- Does the "rational point of view" criterion for persons extend to groups,
  and if so, what does it say about group consciousness?

## Corrections

- none to a seeded skim (there was no seed)
- **The parity quotation.** Clark quotes [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) (p. 8) as ending "that part
  of the world is (for that time) part of the cognitive process", with "were
  it to go on in the head" and "accepting as". [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md), reading the
  authors' web version, gives "were it done in the head", "recognizing
  as" and "(so we claim)". Adams and Aizawa ([LIT-tmpss0cg](../literature.d/LIT-tmpss0cg.md), p. 46) quote the
  same wording as [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md), with an ellipsis where the hedge stands. So this paper's version is a paraphrase
  presented as a quotation, or it follows a typeset text that differs from
  the web version; I could not check the typeset 1998 text. It should not
  be cited as the 1998 wording.
- **The concession, quoted.** Clark quotes Adams and Aizawa as "it is unclear
  to what extent every cognitive state of each cognitive process"; their
  text (p. 50) has "each cognitive state". The sense is the same.
