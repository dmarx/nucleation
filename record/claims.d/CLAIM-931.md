# CLAIM-931: Unset scores keep the loop from locking in

## The claim

[CASE-353](../cases.d/CASE-353.md) builds [CASE-099](../cases.d/CASE-099.md)'s loop inside one mind. Authority
attenuates readings against it and is scored against the dominant
interpretation, and the world is out of the loop's reach. With the senses
alone it locks in on every seed. The case gives [QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md)'s candidates
one switch each, with each one's stated objection built in.

- **Anchors.** Commitment before evidence (candidate 3), processing
  signals (candidate 4) and exogenous capture (the second half of
  candidate 1). In a loop with evidence, any one of them was enough to
  prevent lock-in, in 37 to 42 of 44 configurations per parameter variant.
  Removing it from a minimal loop made the loop lock in.
- **Evidence without anchoring.** The sensory channel (the first half of
  candidate 1), the body (candidate 2) and testimony (candidate 5) arrive
  whatever the loop does. Alone or together, they lock in at the default.
  Removing one from a loop that does not lock in leaves the loop blind,
  or locks it in only when capture goes with it or one channel is left
  unanchored.
- **Redundancy.** With all present, no single removal makes the loop lock
  in, in any of 19 variants.

The anchors share what the channels lack: each scores or weights the loop
by something the loop's authority does not set. The committed prediction
is fixed before the evidence. Disfluency registers error, not content.
Capture is triggered by raw surprise. The channels' arrival is unselected,
but how far each counts is decided by the authority under test. So, of
[CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s two routes, "arrival or scoring", in this model only the
second anchors once authority sets attention. Unselected arrival anchors
only when it carries its own weight past attention, as capture does.

## Two readings for [QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md)

The question asks for "a loop that does not lock in, with action held
fixed, and a demonstration that removing a given candidate makes it lock
in." Which candidate that names is the owner's call.

- **Reading A: it answers within the model.** The loops that remove one
  candidate and lock in exist: sense + commitment, body + processing
  signals, sense + capture and others. The candidates that work are 3, 4
  and the capture half of 1. Candidates 2 and 5, and the bare channel of
  1, do not anchor. They supply what the anchors score.
- **Reading B: it shows no single candidate is the anchor.** In the loop
  with all five, no removal makes it lock in. A demonstration on a loop
  built to be minimal shows what the modeller put in. What the run
  supports is that a scoring route which authority does not set is
  needed, and that any of three supplies it. Which one a mind uses stays
  open.

## What it does not say

- That minds have these mechanisms, or have them in this form. Each
  candidate is built to the question's description. The difference that
  decides, whether authority sets the weight, is built in.
- That action is irrelevant. With action held fixed, contingency between
  intention and outcome is absent by construction. [CLAIM-339](CLAIM-339.md)'s
  empirical form, the extinction hypothesis, concerns that contingency,
  and the model is silent on it.
- That the anchors are independent scoring in either sense [TERM-147](../terms.d/TERM-147.md)
  separates. The committed score reads the evidence as attended, and
  capture compares the input with the interpretation. What they share is
  only that the loop's authority does not set their weight.
- That commitment works whatever κ′ − κ̄. Here it did across κ̄ from 0 to
  0.8, where [CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md)'s linearization would predict a positive
  coupling. The evidence gain outweighed it. Why was not diagnosed, and
  [CLAIM-340](CLAIM-340.md) is a statement about symmetry, which this does not test.
