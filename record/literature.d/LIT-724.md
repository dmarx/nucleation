# LIT-724: The Communication Structure of Epistemic Communities

Kevin J. S. Zollman (2007), *Philosophy of Science* 74(5):574–587 —
DOI-10.1086/525605

## Key takeaways

- **The learning problem.** Agents choose between a well-understood action,
  whose payoff is the same whatever the truth, and a new one that may be
  better or worse. They learn only from the outcomes of what they and their
  neighbours actually do, and update by Bayes. If everyone takes the old
  action, no informative evidence arrives again (§2).
- **Bala & Goyal's result.** On an infinite line convergence to the truth
  is certain. Adding a "royal family" connected to everyone makes failure
  possible, because its misleading results reach everybody at once (§2).
- **Finite simulations.** For 3–12 agents, cycles succeeded more often than
  wheels, and wheels more often than complete graphs, over 10,000 runs per
  size. Complete graphs converged much faster (§3.1, Figures 2–3).
- **Density, not centrality.** Across every network of up to six agents,
  lower density and lower clustering predicted success. Degree variance did
  not, so the hub's centrality is not what does the harm (§3.2).
- **A trade-off.** The most reliable graphs were minimally connected; the
  fastest were complete or nearly so (Figures 5–6). Which matters more "will
  depend on the circumstance" (§3.2).
- **The robustness claim.** Footnote 7 says the ordering of the three
  networks holds "for any setting of the parameters". [LIT-733](LIT-733.md) shows
  that it does not.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request: the founding result of the
"research on epistemic networks" an essay's §8.2 cites for the claim that
more communication does not always improve collective judgment. The essay's
wording, "complicates any simple claim", is as strong as the evidence
allows. Rosenstock et al. ([LIT-733](LIT-733.md)) found that the effect holds only
where inquiry is hard, and the account [THEORY-143](../theory.d/THEORY-143.md) is stated with that
limit.
