# NOTE-2184: Recht — Announcing The Irrational Decision

## Contribution

An insider's case that current AI and decision science descend from one
1940s formalisation of rationality, with the normative claim that its
domain of validity is narrow.

## Key results

1. **The formalism.** "Hence, the mathematical version of rationality
   reduces all decision-making to a statistical analysis of risk."
   (Announcing).
2. **The thesis.** "We decided people should make decisions like
   computers. The problem is, this decision was irrational." "The vast
   majority of decisions can’t be outsourced to computers." (Announcing).
3. **Scope.** "Mathematical rationality helps frame simple, small world
   decisions" (Announcing); "Analytic tools from computer science,
   philosophy, mathematics, and statistics are inherently local in their
   validity"; "It is that such methods have sweet spots" (Mathematical
   Pluralism).
4. **Utility as prediction.** "Flood and Dresher proved it unpredictive
   of human behavior in 1950" (Mathematical Pluralism).
5. **Probability.** On Jaynes's comparison: "The only reasonable answer
   to Jaynes’ question is: “I don’t know.”" (The Rational Landscapes).
6. **Language models.** "But natural language is not rational
   language." (The Rationality of the Language Machines).

## Assumptions

Rationality has older, non-mathematical senses; a formalism's usefulness
is local; political and value questions are not optimisation problems.

## Concepts

Mathematical rationality; small-world and large-world decisions; sweet
spots; mathematical pluralism; the RCT as measurement; cyborg decision
making.
