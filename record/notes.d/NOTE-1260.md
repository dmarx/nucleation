# NOTE-1260: Psychometric validation of the Loyola Generativity Scale in Chilean teachers: evidence for a three factor structure

## Contribution

The first validation of the Loyola Generativity Scale (LGS) for Chilean
teachers. It translates and adapts the scale, removes four items and
proposes a three-factor structure in place of the two factors the
authors attribute to the original. Its claim of interest to the record
is that a "Generative Legacy" factor, motivation toward symbolic
continuity, separates from "Generative Action", concrete generative
behaviour and commitment.

## Key insight

Within a single generativity scale, wanting to leave something that
lasts and doing generative things come apart as related but distinct
factors (r = .448, p. 8). Generative Legacy "represented motivations
related to symbolic permanence and societal contribution" (p. 8).

## Assumptions

- **The original LGS** (p. 4): 20 items, answered on a 4-point scale from
  "never applies to me" to "very often applies to me", covering concern
  for future generations, productivity, community involvement and
  perceived personal impact. The authors say it has two factors,
  Positive Generativity (17 positively worded items) and Generative Doubts
  (3 negatively worded), citing McAdams and de St. Aubin (1992, p. 1008).
  The record cannot check this against the 1992 paper.
- **Ordinal items** call for polychoric correlations and robust
  unweighted least squares rather than maximum likelihood (pp. 4–5).
- **Sampling adequacy as a filter.** Items with an MSA below .50 share
  too little variance and are removed (p. 5).
- **Teaching is "inherently generative"** (p. 2), the premise for
  choosing teachers.

## Key results

- **Sample** (pp. 3, 5–6; Table 1). 430 elementary teachers with three
  or more years' experience, in municipal and subsidized private schools
  in four regions (Metropolitan 39%, La Araucanía 26%, Maule 19%, Los Ríos
  16%), from about 47,600 in those regions. Stratified by region and
  school type, then voluntary: a convenience sample. 76% women; 66%
  aged 30–49; 76% urban. Response rate unknown.
- **Translation** (p. 4). Forward translation, reviewed by three experts.
  No back-translation is mentioned.
- **Split-sample check** (p. 5; Table 2). The Solomon method split the
  sample into two halves of 215; ratio communality index S = 0.9789; KMO
  .8318 and .8143.
- **Items** (p. 5; Table 3). Item means ranged from 1.644 to 3.456.
  - Four items were removed for sampling adequacy: GEN5, GEN9, GEN11
    and GEN14. Their MSA values (.589 to .769) are above the stated .50
    cut (p. 5); the lower bounds of their bootstrap intervals (.341 to
    .475) are the only ones below it, so that is presumably the rule
    applied, though the text does not say so.
  - On the 16 remaining items: determinant 0.0019; Bartlett χ²(120) =
    2645.4, p < .001; KMO .872 (bootstrap 95% CI .831–.905).
- **Number of factors** (pp. 6–8; Figure 1; Table 4).
  - Eigenvalues 5.804, 1.674, 1.337, then 1.029. The scree plot's elbow
    and the Hull method chose three.
  - One factor: 36.3% of variance, χ²/df = 4.431, RMSEA = 0.089.
  - Two factors: 46.7%, χ²/df = 3.137, RMSEA = 0.071.
  - Three factors: 55.1%, χ²/df = 1.792, RMSEA = 0.043, CFI = 0.989,
    NNFI = 0.983, GFI = 0.991, AGFI = 0.985, RMSR = 0.041. Close-fit test
    p = 0.83 for RMSEA < .05.
  - Four factors fit slightly better (61.5%, RMSEA = 0.032) but were
    rejected as a weak, poorly defined over-extraction.
- **The three factors** (p. 8; Table 5), standardized loadings.
  - Generative Inefficacy: GEN2, GEN13, GEN15 (0.617–0.801). Reverse-coded
    items on low impact and lack of usefulness.
  - Generative Action: GEN1, GEN6, GEN7, GEN12, GEN16, GEN17, GEN18,
    GEN19 (0.426–0.745). Creativity, productivity, teaching, community
    engagement.
  - Generative Legacy: GEN8, GEN10, GEN20 (0.605–0.735).
  - Correlations: Inefficacy with Action −.340, with Legacy −.295; Action
    with Legacy .448 (95% CI .370–.538).
- **Reliability** (p. 9; Table 6). ORION .795, .863 and .824; factor
  determinacy .892, .929 and .908.
- **Not reported in the body.** The item wordings, the factor means and
  the "consistently high levels of generativity across demographic
  groups" the abstract describes. These may be in the Supplementary
  Material.

## Concepts

- **generativity**: "the enduring concern for contributing to the
  well-being, development, and continuity of future generations" (p. 2).
- **Generative Legacy**: the authors' third factor, motivation toward
  "symbolic permanence and societal contribution".
- **Generative Action**: concrete prosocial behaviour and commitment.
- **Generative Inefficacy**: doubts about one's generative impact,
  measured by reverse-coded items.
- **MSA, Hull method, ORION, FDI**: item sampling adequacy; a
  fit-against-parsimony rule for the number of factors; reliability of
  factor scores; correlation of factor scores with the latent factor.

## Connections

- **McAdams and de St. Aubin 1992 ([LIT-1777](../literature.d/LIT-1777.md))**, the work this note
  stands in for. The paper gives the scale's format and cites the 1992
  abstract: the LGS "showed strong positive associations with reports of
  actual generative acts" (p. 4, quoting p. 1003). It does not report the
  1992 samples or statistics.
- **Kruse and Schmitt 2012**, read alongside, restates the model the
  scale was built from and uses the scale in an intervention.
- **Kotre (1984), *Outliving the Self***, is in the references, the
  source of the phrase "outliving the self" behind generativity's
  symbolic-immortality motive. Not held by the record.

## Bearing on the record

- **The legacy question ([QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md)).** Its first answer
  criterion is a test that separates the readings: measure felt
  continuity with one's legacy, and ask whether concern follows that
  measure or caring alone. As inference:
  - **A candidate measure exists.** A three-item Legacy factor, about
    symbolic continuity, separates from an eight-item Action factor in
    this sample. If its items are about outliving oneself and Action's
    about doing for others, the two could be pitted against each other:
    does generative action track the legacy motive?
  - **The paper does not run that test.** It reports only the
    correlation, .45. Action and Legacy are both self-reports, so the
    correlation cannot say which drives which.
  - **The items are not printed**, so the record cannot tell whether
    Legacy measures a self extended in time ("my work will live on") or
    a concern for the future that is not about oneself. That difference
    is the one between the thesis's reading and Scheffler's.
- **The triviality objection ([CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)).** A legacy factor
  separable from action is a criterion of the temporal self's extent
  that might come apart from caring. It is a candidate only: one sample,
  exploratory, items unseen.
- **The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md)).** Nothing changes yet. The
  thesis would predict that generative action rises with the legacy
  motive; the data are compatible with that and with its reverse.
- **A dated note on [QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md)**, jointly with Kruse and Schmitt:
  the proposed text is in the Kruse draft. No note is needed on
  [CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md).
- **No THEORY filed.**
- **No ML instruction.**

## Limitations

- **Called confirmatory, done exploratory.** The number of factors was
  chosen from scree plot, eigenvalues and the Hull method, solutions of
  one to four factors were compared, and "rotated factor variances" are
  reported (pp. 6–9). That is exploratory factor analysis, run in FACTOR.
  The split halves were used only to check equivalence, not to confirm a
  structure found in one half on the other.
- **Two items disappear.** Sixteen items survive the MSA screen and
  enter the matrix tests (Bartlett's df of 120 fits 16 items). The final
  solution has 14 (Table 5; "the 14 retained items", p. 9). GEN3 and GEN4
  are not mentioned again, and no reason is given.
- **A wording factor is a rival reading** (an inference from Table 3).
  The six items with the lowest means are GEN2, GEN5, GEN9, GEN13, GEN14
  and GEN15 (1.64 to 2.21), the pattern of negatively worded items. MSA
  removed three of them; the other three are the whole of Generative
  Inefficacy. That also sits oddly with the authors' account of three
  negatively worded items in the original.
- **The MSA rule is misstated.** The text says items with MSA below .50
  were removed, but the four removed items have MSA between .589 and
  .769; only their bootstrap lower bounds fall below .50 (Table 3).
- **Sample**: convenience, teachers only, cross-sectional, self-report;
  no measurement invariance test; no criterion measure of generative
  behaviour.
- **Unrelated literature in the introduction**, such as generativity in
  machine learning and a study of autism (Hocaoglu and Güvendir, cited
  for generativity across groups).
- **Typos.** "Relaibility" (Table 6 title); "No binary" and
  "Ungraduated" (Table 1); "47.600" for 47,600 (p. 3). The education rows
  of Table 1 sum to 404 of 430 (94%), so a category is missing.
- **Generative AI** (Microsoft Copilot) was used in writing, as the
  authors declare (p. 12).

## Open questions

- What do GEN8, GEN10 and GEN20 say? If they are about being remembered
  or outliving oneself, the Legacy factor is a measure of the extended
  temporal self; if about the future's good, it is not.
- Does Legacy predict generative behaviour, measured by McAdams and de
  St. Aubin's acts checklist, over and above Action?
