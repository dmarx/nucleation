# CLAIM-462: An arrangement of institutions can keep, or increase, its capacity for effective action while its capacity to be effectively corrected by others degrades, because the two are maintained by different relations, so the first is no evidence of the second

## The claim

A8: "an organization of authority has at least two different capacities:
the capacity to **produce effective actions** and the capacity to **remain
answerable for those actions through other institutions**. These
capacities need not develop together." And: "A complex institutional
system can preserve or even increase its capacity for effective action
while losing its capacity for effective correction." A9 separated four
capacities that "are not different degrees of one quantity": effective
action, effective restraint (preventing a change before it happens),
effective correction (reversing or remedying it once it has) and
effective reconstruction (restoring the capacities, relations and
opportunities it damaged).

It rests on two claims inside one organization. [CLAIM-099](CLAIM-099.md): maintaining
a regime of effective action is not maintaining the capacity to govern
among regimes. [CLAIM-422](CLAIM-422.md): a corrective act, a procedure and a maintained
capacity to correct are three things, so a correction can be recorded and
not maintained. This claim says the capacity to be corrected is also held
in relations the arrangement's effective action does not need: a court's
orders reaching an enforcer who will act on them, information reaching a
reviewer, a foreign partner's cooperation being requirable. A9: "The
institutions are formally continuous. The organization through which they
exercised mutual constraint is not necessarily continuous."

The cases show the pattern, not its frequency. At USIP ([CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md)) the
executive's capacity to take control was effective, and the court's
capacity to restore control was weighed against the risk the taking had
created. At CECOT ([CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md)) a transfer was coordinated across two
governments, and its reversal had no comparable procedure. At the East
Wing ([CASE-170](../cases.d/CASE-170.md)) demolition was quick and review slow. A9: "the mechanisms
that produce a harmful outcome may operate much faster than the mechanisms
authorized to evaluate and reverse it", which is [CLAIM-423](CLAIM-423.md)'s timescales
deciding who holds authority.

The degradation is hard to see. A8: it "can become visible only when a
court orders an action reversed, a person seeks release from custody, an
institution attempts to recover its former functions, or a community seeks
restoration of rights after displacement", which is [CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md)'s method:
arrangements alike in ordinary operation part under the intervention the
capacity is for. It is the same reason [TERM-069](../terms.d/TERM-069.md)'s probe family must include
the probes that matter: "challenge", "noncompliance" and "restoration" as
well as "ordinary action" (A9).

## What it does not say

- **That self-governance is enough, or that one corrector is the ideal.**
  A9: "An institution can govern itself effectively while resisting every
  attempt by others to hold it answerable", and the ideal "may instead be
  a differentiated arrangement of organizations whose authority over one
  another is limited, conditional, and independently contestable"; a
  government modelled as one agent can hide the distribution that makes
  correction possible ([CLAIM-418](CLAIM-418.md)).
- **Which corrections are owed.** A9: a model of remedial reachability
  "would not decide which institutional actions are legitimate", nor that
  "every legally valid court order should be implemented by any means
  available" ([CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md)).
- **That effective answerability means challengers win.** A9: "A
  functioning system can legitimately reject challenges"; the requirement
  is that correction "should depend on appropriately constituted and
  reviewable procedures—not simply on whether the actor exercising power
  consents to being corrected".
- **That it is new.** Cover ([LIT-1601](../literature.d/LIT-1601.md)) on interpretation and enforcement,
  Pettit ([LIT-1614](../literature.d/LIT-1614.md)) on contestability, Hillebrecht ([LIT-1607](../literature.d/LIT-1607.md)) and
  Rosenberg ([LIT-1615](../literature.d/LIT-1615.md)) on compliance and courts' limits, all unread, may
  hold it already; A9's preferred next step was the question first
  ([QUESTION-278](../questions.d/QUESTION-278.md)), "rather than prematurely declare" a result.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Cover, read, holds part of it

Of the four works "What it does not say" lists as unread, Cover, [LIT-1601](../literature.d/LIT-1601.md),
is now read ([NOTE-tmpns25g](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpns25g.md)); Pettit, [LIT-1614](../literature.d/LIT-1614.md), Hillebrecht, [LIT-1607](../literature.d/LIT-1607.md), and
Rosenberg, [LIT-1615](../literature.d/LIT-1615.md), are Unavailable. Cover holds a version of this claim
for courts. A court's capacity to correct by judgment depends on the role
structure that carries judicial words into deeds through others, which is
a different relation from the one that makes the judgment right. So the
doubt that the claim is new is partly borne out. Cover does not treat the
correction of one institution by others, which is this claim's subject.
His account fits [CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md), one of the claim's grounds, closely: the
police and the security contractor acted on the executive's papers, and a
court's word could become a deed only if they would act on its. The claim
is unchanged and stays Proposed.
