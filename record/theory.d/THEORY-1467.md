# THEORY-1467: Mussmann and colleagues argue, from greater prairie chickens in Illinois, that supplying individuals by translocation while the habitat threat persists gives only ambiguous or transitory gains and perpetuates conservation reliance, which can fall below a reliance threshold only if extrinsic threats and intrinsic small-population limits are relieved together

## Source

Mussmann, Douglas, Anthonysamy, Davis, Simpson, Louis & Douglas (2017), [LIT-2183](../literature.d/LIT-2183.md); read in [NOTE-1670](../notes.d/NOTE-1670.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The population after supply.** From 1831 shed feathers at six leks,
  2010–2013: "4 year average N = 79", "average Ne = 13.1", migration
  "less than 1%", twelve family groups with relatedness above half-sib.
  Only 73 birds were recorded in 2011, and further translocations were
  begun and suspended (§1.4).
- **No genetic rescue signal.** Heterozygosity "did not differ among
  contemporary, pre- and post-translocated populations, whereas allelic
  diversity did" (abstract). This could have come out otherwise: higher
  heterozygosity after translocation would have supported rescue.
- **The argument.** Translocations "invoke long-term intensive
  management (i.e. the perpetuation of conservation reliance)" (§4.4);
  "Two factors establish the baseline for conservation reliance, and both
  must be alleviated in tandem" (§4.4, fig. 5); intrinsic remedies alone
  "promote rather than alleviate conservation reliance" (§5).

## What this does not say

- That a reliance threshold exists or where it lies: fig. 5 is a
  conceptual diagram, and nothing in the data measures a threshold,
  collapse point or hysteresis.
- That relieving the habitat threat would end reliance: no population
  here had its threat relieved, so the "in tandem" half is argued, not
  observed.
- That translocation caused the decline: the habitat threat persisted
  throughout, and the pre-translocation baseline is 32 birds over 19
  years.
- Anything from Scott et al. (2010): the definition and the 84 % figure
  are cited to Goble et al. (2012).
