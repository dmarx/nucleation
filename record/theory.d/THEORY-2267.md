# THEORY-2267: Frischmann and Benesch argue that state rules requiring friction with a functional purpose in digital code, architecture and interfaces can be content-neutral time, place and manner restrictions subject to intermediate First Amendment scrutiny; they justify such friction chiefly by the time it gives individuals to deliberate, report the line between friction that only imposes a cost and friction that blocks passage until a condition is shown and so opens a governance seam, and offer a framework and examples but no evidence of their own

## Source

Frischmann and Benesch (2023), [LIT-3136](../literature.d/LIT-3136.md), 25 Yale J.L. & Tech. 376;
read in [NOTE-2359](../notes.d/NOTE-2359.md).

## What was actually shown

An argument in law and a framework, not a finding. The legal part
applies the US doctrine of time, place and manner restrictions (Ward,
Reed, City of Austin) to rules that require friction in digital design:
content-neutral rules (delays triggered by geography, by social-graph
distance or by a count of forwards; alerts; comprehension checks;
greyscale settings) would face intermediate scrutiny, while a trigger
defined by content would not qualify. The framework describes any
friction measure by six parameters: type, direct effect on subjects,
design, purpose, scope and governance. Friction is justified as
"resistance" that "affords us opportunities to stop and think" (p. 388),
and is said to provide "opportunity/space for governance" (p. 387).
Quoting Frischmann and Ohm, it separates a speed bump, which makes a
driver bear a cost and does "not involve a seam or enable governance",
from a checkpoint or CAPTCHA, which blocks passage until a condition is
shown (p. 405 n. 89). The effects it cites are others' (speed bumps,
security warnings) or companies' own reports (WhatsApp, Twitter).

## What this does not say

- That friction opens a point at which anyone other than the person
  passing can intervene. Its friction is chiefly for that person's own
  deliberation; the gates it describes let the party that set them check
  the person passing, not the reverse. It is not evidence for [CLAIM-710](../claims.d/CLAIM-710.md)'s
  procedural friction between parties.
- That any measure works. The article asks for the research.
- That the First Amendment test of content neutrality settles which
  frictions are just; it is used as a constraint on drafting.

## Bearing

Corroborates [CLAIM-715](../claims.d/CLAIM-715.md)'s premise (friction aimed at individuals'
thinking leaves the institution's authority untouched): even friction the
state would impose is imposed on users' deliberation. Bears on
[QUESTION-471](../questions.d/QUESTION-471.md) through the seam distinction. Bears without a relation on
[CLAIM-710](../claims.d/CLAIM-710.md) and [TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md), and on [THEORY-1937](THEORY-1937.md) as the same programme given a
regulator; see the NOTE.
