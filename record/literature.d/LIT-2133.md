# LIT-2133: Failure Mechanisms in DNA Self-Assembly: Barriers to Single-Fold Yield

Majikes, J. M., Patrone, P. N., Kearsley, A. J., Zwolak, M. & Liddle, J. A. (2021). Failure Mechanisms in DNA Self-Assembly: Barriers to Single-Fold Yield. *ACS Nano* 15(2): 3284–3294. doi:10.1021/acsnano.0c10114.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1633](../notes.d/NOTE-1633.md) (main text, captions and tables; the supplement unread).*

- **A blocked state is observed.** The abstract reports "a predicted, but
  previously unobserved blocked state" that "acts as a limit on yield for
  single folds".
- **More staple, less fold, past a point.** "the yield of the folded state
  increases as a function of staple concentration up to about 5× the
  scaffold concentration", then falls; all three fold distances "fall
  below 50 % yield at high staple:scaffold ratios".
- **An equilibrium effect at this scale.** "We do not anticipate this to be
  a kinetic effect as anneals performed two times slower replicated the
  trend (SI Fig S4)."
- **Whole origami escapes, by hypothesis.** A notched rectangle "still
  forms at high fidelity even at 100x staple excess"; the authors propose
  that blocking strands are displaced only along a ‘nucleation front’, and
  leave open whether the blocked state is "either thermodynamic or
  kinetic" as a barrier in whole origami.
- **The cheap probes cannot see it.** "our reporter cannot distinguish
  between the blocked state and a scaffold without a staple", and gels
  "failed to identify any conditions that could distinguish the circular
  and figure-eight states".

## Standing in the record

Filed for the realizability inquiry as the cleanest case on the record
of a target that is feasible and reached, yet reached by under half the
molecules because of an equilibrium competitor ([CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md)), and of a
resource that has a window: staple excess helps up to a turnover and then
hurts, at a single fold, without the time budget of [CLAIM-594](../claims.d/CLAIM-594.md). It
extends Majikes et al. 2020 ([LIT-2117](LIT-2117.md)), which measured the holding
side of one fold, to the reaching side. It supports [CLAIM-557](../claims.d/CLAIM-557.md) on what a
single channel lumps together, and offers a hypothesis for [QUESTION-380](../questions.d/QUESTION-380.md)
(nucleate where fold distances are short). A's synthesis cites it as a
precedent for kinetic trapping; the reading finds the single-fold blocked
state equilibrium, the kinetic reading a hypothesis for whole origami
only. Filed from it: [THEORY-1457](../theory.d/THEORY-1457.md). No instruction for
machine-learning practice.
