# CLAIM-030: An organized whole's operative intention is not the expectation of its members' intentions; it is produced by systemic mechanisms that can evolve apart from what the members intend

## The claim

U12: "taking an expectation over the lower level intentions doesn't actually
produce the higher level intention. the higher level intention is manifested
out of systemic mechanisms that may evolve completely divorced from the
intentions of lower level agents, influenced by environmental pressures."

The final manuscript keeps it as its first conclusion from Northstar:
"collective action can change while formal policy, available information, and
constituent attitudes remain approximately constant" (Section 5.10).

## What it does not say

That the members do not matter, or that the whole is an agent. Whether it is
one is [QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md).

## Note of 2026-10-11: organizational drift as evidence

[THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md) (the normalization of deviance at NASA) and [THEORY-144](../theory.d/THEORY-144.md)
(Rasmussen's drift toward the boundary of acceptable performance) are added
as grounds. Both describe an organization's operative course moving through
systemic mechanisms, reinterpretation of anomalies and migration under cost
pressure, that no member intended. A synthesis review also noted a parallel
in the nucleating-argument line: [CLAIM-177](CLAIM-177.md) records theses that moved
"without a turn arguing either move". Whether that is a case of this claim,
or the owner's unrecorded change of mind, is open.

## Note of 2026-10-12: evidence now linked

[THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) now grounds this claim. The group's view corrects the inconsistency any mechanical aggregation of its members' views would produce. It partly duplicates [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md).
