# NOTE-tmpn9ky5: Chakravartty — Ontological Priority and non-eliminative OSR

## Contribution

Chakravartty examines non-eliminative ontic structural realism, which keeps objects but gives relations ontological priority over them. He argues that this priority thesis is as problematic as the eliminative OSR it was meant to improve on. The result is a dilemma for anyone hoping OSR, in either form, will give realism a way forward. The argument is mainly conceptual, but he closes by showing that the physics cited for the view does not escape it.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §10.1 (pp. 187–190): sets up SR, then ESR versus OSR, then eliminative versus non-eliminative OSR. The epigraph is the Cheshire cat's "grin without a cat".
- §10.3 (pp. 192–194): the only general enough dependence relation is "determination of identity". Principle (2): relata are less fundamental than their relations iff the relata depend on the relations for their identity and not vice versa. Saunders and Ladyman & Ross are cited as holding that objects are "purged of their intrinsic natures".
- §10.4 (pp. 194–196): dispositional essentialism seems to help, but it individuates properties by dispositions for relations, and those dispositions are intrinsic.
- §10.5 (pp. 196–202): bundle theories plus dispositional identity still leave intrinsic features. Relata with no intrinsic features at all amount to eliminativism, so the conceptual space for non-eliminative OSR runs out (Fig. 10.1).
- §10.6 (pp. 202–206): extrinsic dispositions (entanglement on the orthodox reading) would help only if all properties were extrinsic, or if the intrinsic ones did not fix identity. Mass and charge are state-independent intrinsic properties, so quantum identity is not extrinsic. He is also sceptical of generalising "lessons from physics" to all sciences.
