# LIT-tmppbuqq: Dynamic mechanistic explanation: computational modeling of circadian rhythms as an exemplar for cognitive science

Bechtel, W., & Abrahamsen, A. (2010). Dynamic mechanistic explanation: computational modeling of circadian rhythms as an exemplar for cognitive science. *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A*, 41(3), 321–333 — DOI-10.1016/j.shpsa.2010.07.003

Read in full in NOTE-tmpbljat.

## Key takeaways

- **Mechanism, extended to dynamics.** "The orchestrated functioning of the
  mechanism, manifested in patterns of change over time in properties of
  its parts and operations, is responsible for one or more phenomena"
  (p. 323). The model's variables are chosen properties of parts and
  operations, and that choice "anchor[s]" the model to the mechanism.
- **Six contributions of models** (pp. 324–328). C1: show that an
  identified mechanism can produce the phenomenon. C2: explore parameter
  space. C3: indicate which identified parts are essential and so "should
  count as components of the responsible mechanism". C4: explain a disorder
  by an altered parameter. C5: show how independent oscillators couple.
  C6: explore how relations between mechanisms are lost and restored.
- **Grounded against de novo.** Circadian models describe a mechanism
  whose parts were found by laboratory work. Cognitive models posit their
  parts and are checked only by fit. So "much more is asked of
  computational models in cognitive science than in circadian research,
  but they benefit less from empirical constraint" (p. 329). The
  past-tense debate shows the result: without constraint from parts,
  conflicts between models "are often irresolvable" (p. 330).
- **A model describes, it does not mirror.** A circadian model does not
  itself generate circadian rhythms. Its variables track properties of the
  mechanism (p. 329, n. 5).

## Standing in the record

The record's maintenance constructions (CASE-189, CASE-190) are dynamical
models whose variables, "constraint capacities", are posited, not found.
In this paper's terms they are de novo, how-possibly models. CLAIM-522 and
CLAIM-519 say that the same effective dynamics can be realized by
different mechanisms. This paper says what tells them apart in practice:
independent evidence about the parts and operations. Fit and dynamics
alone do not.
