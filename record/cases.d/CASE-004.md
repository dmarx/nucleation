# CASE-004: Two people, an empty wallet: shared complicity, normative authority, and an underspecified relation

## The case

A97: "Condition A, **Shared complicity**: Two friends sit together with an empty
wallet between them. Both are amused by a weekend of overspending. Their
expressions convey mutual understanding, not condemnation. Condition B,
**Normative authority**: Two people sit together with an empty wallet between
them. One sternly admonishes the other for overspending. Their expressions
convey judgment and embarrassment. Condition C, **Underspecified relation**: Two
people sit together with an empty wallet between them after a weekend of
overspending." Observation contexts: objects and actions; affect and alignment;
role and authority; interactional meaning; plausible continuation.

## What it can show

That "These prompts overlap substantially in concrete scene elements but
prescribe different interpersonal configurations", so object-level evaluation
cannot tell a faithful image from one that reverses the relation ([CLAIM-013](../claims.d/CLAIM-013.md),
[CLAIM-123](../claims.d/CLAIM-123.md)). Manuscript §7 keeps A and B: "two friends sharing amused complicity and
... an adviser criticizing someone may describe nearly identical visible
objects". Without condition C there is no measure of which relation a
generator supplies when none is specified.
