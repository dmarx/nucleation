# THEORY-669: Reading the universal quantum state as nomological escapes the costs of reading it as a field only by paying in the theory of laws: on the strong reading, modelled on the classical Hamiltonian, a time-dependent ψ makes laws time-indexed unless the universal state is time-independent, as Wheeler–DeWitt would make it; on the weak and functionalist readings ψ need be neither unique nor time-independent, but only within an extended Humeanism whose criterion would equally make classical fields nomological

## Source

Chen (2018; *Philosophy Compass* 2019), [LIT-204](../literature.d/LIT-204.md), read in full in [NOTE-175](../notes.d/NOTE-175.md).
Belot (2011), [LIT-100](../literature.d/LIT-100.md), pp. 7–9, read in full in [NOTE-110](../notes.d/NOTE-110.md). Allori (2021),
[LIT-200](../literature.d/LIT-200.md), read in full in [NOTE-154](../notes.d/NOTE-154.md).

## What was actually shown

**The map (Chen §§3–5).** Realist readings of the universal wave function
in non-relativistic quantum mechanics fall into ontological readings
(high-dimensional field, multi-field on 3-space, spacetime state realism,
Hilbert-space vector), nomological readings and a sui generis reading.
Which is open depends on the solution to the measurement problem and on
whether fundamental space is high- or low-dimensional (Table 1, asserted,
with one cell inconsistent with the text).

**The strong nomological reading.** ψ has the status of the classical
Hamiltonian: it generates the motion and suffers no back-reaction (Dürr,
Goldstein and Zanghì; Goldstein and Teufel). Belot shows what it costs in
current theory (pp. 7–9): time-indexed law-propositions, and grades of
necessity, ψ being "more like an initial condition" than the Schrödinger
equation. He ranks them: time-indexing is "the more daunting obstacle";
grades of necessity are tolerable on Lange-style accounts. Both Belot and
Chen point to quantum gravity, where Wheeler–DeWitt ĤΨ = 0 (Chen eq. 11)
would make the universal state time-independent. Chen notes that whether
it holds is open, and that the arrow of time would need revision (fn. 24).
Chen's own variant takes a simple initial mixed state and gives up purity
and time-independence of the generator.

**The weak nomological reading (Chen §4.2).** ψ is a variable of the Humean
best system: it need only be the simplest of the competitors, not
time-independent or unique. The objection that trajectories carry far less
information than ψ is answered by the agreement physicists reach on ψ from
pointer facts. The reading has been defended only within extended
Humeanism. Chen reports the objection that it is "too cheap": the same move
would banish classical electromagnetic fields (fn. 28).

**The functionalist variant (Allori).** ψ is whatever plays its empirical,
nomological and explanatory roles; it is "an ingredient in the law, rather
than a law itself", one of several possible realizers, as a potential is up
to a constant (§§3.2–3.4). An ingredient may be time-dependent and
non-unique, so Belot's and Maudlin's objections lose their grip. [NOTE-154](../notes.d/NOTE-154.md)
places the view in Chen's weak cell, which Allori's own fn. 46 aligns with
Esfeld's and Bhogal and Perry's Humeanism.

**What could have come out otherwise.** One of the readings could have
answered both costs: a time-independent universal state already in hand, or
a criterion separating ingredients of the law from material posits. None
supplies either.

## What this does not say

- **That ψ is nomological, or is not.** Chen's survey weighs no verdict and
  says the list may not be exhaustive (§6). Belot's own conclusion is
  "inconclusive" (§3).
- **That Allori answers the too-cheap objection.** She cites Chen (2019) and
  does not address it. Her reply to objection 4 (p. 20) embraces classical
  fields as bookkeeping devices whose explanatory force belongs to the
  law, which is the case the objection treats as a reductio ([NOTE-154](../notes.d/NOTE-154.md)). Her
  text is also inconsistent on whether ψ is nomological (pp. 11, 20–21).
- **That the functionalist view bypasses PBR.** The bypass is asserted
  (fn. 32) by calling the ontic/epistemic dichotomy false; PBR is not
  engaged in its own terms ([THEORY-605](THEORY-605.md), [THEORY-586](THEORY-586.md)).
- **That the configuration-space problem disappears.** Allori asserts it
  "never arises" (p. 17) and then lets configuration space re-enter through
  nonlocal nomological facts (objection 5).
- **That Table 1 is reliable cell by cell.** Ten cells are "?", and the Sm
  cell for the strong reading contradicts §4.1 ([NOTE-175](../notes.d/NOTE-175.md)).
