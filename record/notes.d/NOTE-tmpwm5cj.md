# NOTE-tmpwm5cj: Grueneisen et al. — “I Know You Don’t Know I Know…” Children Use Second-Order False-Belief Reasoning for Peer Coordination

## Contribution

The first demonstration that children recruit higher-order false-belief
reasoning for peer coordination.

## Key results

1. **Coordination needs recursion, in theory.** "Required for coordination
   is thus what is metaphorically described as a “meeting of minds”
   (Schelling, 1960), or the formation of mutual expectations of each
   other’s behavior." (p. 288).
2. **Salience at 5 and 8.** Their in-press study: dyads of 5- and
   8-year-olds, "In the absence of any communication, children
   deliberately used the salient option as a focal point for achieving
   coordination with their partner" (p. 288).
3. **First order.** "Children chose the marked box signiﬁcantly more often
   in the experimental condition (55.7%) than in the control condition
   (7.7%)" (p. 291).
4. **Second order.** "children chose the marked box signiﬁcantly more
   often in the experimental condition (46.2%) than in the control
   condition (19.2%)" (p. 291).
5. **Conclusion.** "this study shows that 6-year-olds can reason
   recursively about mental states as a means to coordinate" (p. 292);
   cooperative contexts may help: "one possibility is that children
   generally deploy their higher order TOM abilities more readily in
   cooperative than in competitive contexts" (p. 292).

## Assumptions

Choosing the falsely believed box indicates false-belief reasoning;
comprehension questions scaffold it.

## Concepts

Coordination problem; mutual expectations; first- and second-order false
belief in action.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's question**, the upper anchor: recursive mutual expectation
  in coordination is shown at 6, about half the children succeeding;
  nothing like it is shown at 3.
- **[THEORY-tmpqxwyk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpqxwyk.md)** (Schelling), support at 6 and over: children
  coordinate by meeting of minds, as Schelling describes; the authors cite
  Schelling and Lewis for the need for recursion.
- **[THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)**, background: the authors suggest cooperation helps
  children deploy higher-order reasoning.

## Limitations

Low overall success; no difference between first- and second-order tasks;
scaffolding by comprehension questions; one German sample.
