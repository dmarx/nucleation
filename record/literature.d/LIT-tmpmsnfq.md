summary: >-
  Horsthemke (2018). A Comment. Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance is documented in plants, nematodes and fruit flies; in mammals it must pass the Weismann barrier and two waves of reprogramming. Rodent reports mostly await independent confirmation, and the human ones (grandparental food supply, a Prader–Willi imprint) rest on unique cohorts. The Dutch Hunger Winter shows fetal programming, not germline transmission. Familial epimutations usually turn out to be secondary to a genetic variant. A proof needs: genetic, ecological and cultural inheritance excluded (F3 after an exposed pregnant female, F2 after an exposed male, with IVF and fostering); the factor found in pure germ cells; and the factor shown to cause the effect. In humans only genetic effects can be excluded. Cultural transmission very likely outweighs any epigenetic inheritance; the author remains sceptical.
