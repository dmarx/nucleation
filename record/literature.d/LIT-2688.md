# LIT-2688: Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing

Danielle Ensign, Sorelle A. Friedler, Scott Neville, Carlos Scheidegger and Suresh Venkatasubramanian (2017; FAT* 2018). Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing. arXiv:1706.09847; *Proceedings of the 1st Conference on Fairness, Accountability and Transparency*, PMLR 81 (2018).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of 2026-11-17; the reading of 2026-11-16 is below.*

- "predictive policing algorithms only observe crime in neighborhoods they
  patrol" (p. 1).
- Lemma 4: unequal rates lock in, "a classic 'go with the winner'
  problem"; Lemma 2: equal rates leave the prior in place.
- Reported incidents "can attenuate the degree of runaway feedback" but
  "cannot entirely remove it without the interventions we suggest"; the
  repair drops discovered incidents with the probability of patrol.

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read in arXiv v3. The paper proves, in a two-region urn with patrols that discover crime at its true rate, that a system retrained on its own discovered incidents learns where it looked rather than where crime is: under equal rates the outcome is a draw fixed by the initial counts (Lemma 2), under unequal rates it goes wholly to the higher-rate region (Lemma 4, a "go with the winner" problem). Reported incidents help only if discovered ones are down-weighted (§3.4.1). A rejection-sampling filter on discovered incidents repairs it (§3.5), and PredPol simulations on Lum and Isaac's Oakland data show both the runaway and the repair (§5). The authors' own limit: crime "reporting is noisy and skewed by area and by type of crime", and then "the solutions might not continue to work" (§6). What the exchange says (A19 §6) is accurate; its further claim that such algorithms turn effects of institutional treatment into properties of people is A's, since in this model policing changes nothing about the people. See the NOTE and THEORY filed from this reading.

## Reading of 2026-11-17

Read a second time on 2026-11-17 by the continuation's readings and reconciled with the reading above on 2026-11-18: [NOTE-2025](../notes.d/NOTE-2025.md) now carries both readings and [THEORY-1927](../theory.d/THEORY-1927.md) both accounts. What that reading recorded here:

**The exchange's characterization: faithful.** A19's "deployment choices
can bias subsequent observations and produce runaway feedback" is right.
Its gloss, that the classification "can help create the evidence
subsequently used to justify" it, fits Lemmas 2 and 4, with one sharpening:
the loop amplifies a real but small difference, or freezes the prior where
there is none; it does not create crime. The analogy to interpersonal
coercive control is A's.
