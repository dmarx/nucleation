# THEORY-tmpmyusz: Natali, Naiseh, Cabitza and Frischmann propose that AI decision support be designed with friction, deliberate inefficiencies at critical points of the interaction such as pauses before AI output, cognitive forcing functions and seamful design, to counter automation bias and preserve users' judgement, agency and accountability, and set an agenda for calibrating it, reporting no evidence of their own

## Source

[LIT-tmphus2f](../literature.d/LIT-tmphus2f.md), read in the open-access published version, HHAI 2025, pp. 518–521.

## What was actually shown

Nothing empirical: the poster defines designed friction ("deliberate inefﬁciencies designed to foster engagement, critical reason[ing], and user agency"), lists mechanisms from the literature (prosocial friction and speed bumps, slow and seamful design, programmed inefficiencies, cognitive forcing functions, AI provocations), and proposes an agenda: design principles, calibration against cognitive load, effects on skill retention and autonomy, evaluation criteria, cross-cultural study.

## What this does not say

- That friction works: the evidence is in the cited work (Buçinca et al., held as [THEORY-1765](THEORY-1765.md)), not here.
- Anything about procedural or institutional friction between parties, or about who may intervene in a process; its friction is inside one user's interaction with an AI.
- How friction is to be defined beyond added inefficiency.
