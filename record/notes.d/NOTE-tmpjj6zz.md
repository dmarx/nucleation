# NOTE-tmpjj6zz: The Viable System Model: Its Provenance, Development, Methodology and Pathology

## Contribution

A first-hand account of where the Viable System Model came from, of the
method Beer claims makes it a scientific model rather than an analogy, and
of its use as a diagnostic: organizational "pathology" read off as the
failure of one of five subsystems. It is a reflection on the model, not a
statement or test of it.

## Key insight

That an organization exists says nothing about whether it is viable. Beer's
test is structural: whether it has the regulatory functions any
independently viable system needs, at each level of recursion, and whether
they are connected with requisite variety. A system can lack one and go on
existing for a while, reacting without foresight.

## Assumptions

- Viability can be characterised by invariances that hold across brains,
  firms, states and cells; mapping "across these boundaries" is
  legitimate (p. 5).
- Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety is the central law of regulation (p. 7).
- A system's elements can be treated by their function under role
  constraints, not their identity, so people's free will does not break
  the model (p. 9).
- Boundaries, the number of subsystems and the identity of System Five are
  nominated by the observer (p. 11).

## Key results

- **Recursion** (pp. 4, 10, 16): "In a recursive organizational structure,
  any viable system contains, and is contained in, a viable system". The
  topology must be an exact replica at each level.
- **Five subsystems** (p. 10), necessary and sufficient for any system
  "capable of maintaining its identity independently of other such
  organisms within a shared environment". System One's elements are its
  "autopoietic generators" and are themselves viable systems. The whole
  interacts with its environment through its Systems One and Four "and
  not otherwise".
- **Identity by closure** (p. 11): System Five closes the system and "only
  this determines an identity". Allende told Beer that System Five was
  "the people".
- **Requisite variety in three places** (p. 7): blocks of variety,
  channels and transducers. These become the first three principles of
  organization (Appendix 2).
- **Pathologies** (pp. 12–13), quoting *Brain and Strategy* (1983):
  removing any of the five functions from a subsidiary may kill it;
  System Five may fail to represent the whole to the larger system; without
  Two, the units of One fight; without Four, Three and Five collapse into
  each other. Beer's gloss is that Five people are usually promoted from
  Three and "dive down" into its problems, leaving a metasystem that
  "responds reactively" and is "incapable of planning and foresight (Four)
  and will and judgment (Five)".
- **Takeovers** (p. 12): autonomy "does not mean zero interference", and
  the cohesion of two recursions inevitably takes up the new subsidiary's
  variety.

## Concepts

- **viable** — "capable of independent existence" (pp. 3–4).
- **variety** — the number of distinguishable states of a system (p. 6).
- **requisite variety** — "Only variety can destroy variety" (p. 7);
  a regulator short of it must attenuate or amplify.
- **recursion** — the nesting of viable systems within viable systems.
- **metasystem** — Systems Three, Four and Five together; its collapse is
  the "decerebrate cat".
- **yo-yo technique** — testing the model by mapping fresh systems down to
  it and back (p. 5).

## Connections

It restates the model of *Brain of the Firm* ([LIT-tmpvuucm](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvuucm.md), unread) and
*The Heart of Enterprise*, and rests on Ashby's law and Sommerhoff's
directive correlation.

**Aubin's viability ([THEORY-tmp4t87t](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4t87t.md)).** The two uses of "viable" differ.
Aubin's is a property of a constraint set relative to a dynamics: a set is
viable if from each of its states some evolution stays in it, and the set
is fixed independently of the dynamics. Beer's is a property of an
organization's regulatory structure: whether it has the five subsystems,
connected with requisite variety, that keeping an identity independently
needs. Aubin's theory has no subsystems or recursion; Beer's has no
constraint set or kernel. They meet at two points, as the record reads
them. Both separate being somewhere now from being able to stay: Beer's
"The fact that the societary system is there does not guarantee that it
will always be there" (p. 12) is a state outside a viability kernel, in
Aubin's terms. And both leave the hard part outside the theory: Aubin takes
the constraint set as given, and Beer says the boundaries and the identity
fixed by System Five are nominated by the observer. No relation is
declared.

**Argyris ([THEORY-tmpley53](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpley53.md)).** Beer reports that danger points
"deliberately concealed out of embarrassment or self-serving" tend to
signal themselves to the diagnostician (p. 12). Argyris's defensive
routines are the opposite claim: the concealment is itself made
undiscussable. The collapse of Five into Three, a metasystem that reacts
and cannot judge, resembles an organization confined to single-loop
correction. That parallel is the record's; Beer does not draw it.

**Ostrom ([THEORY-tmpsk5aw](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpsk5aw.md)).** Ostrom's nested enterprises and Beer's
recursion both make nesting a structural mark of what lasts. Ostrom's is a
correlate of survival in common-pool institutions; Beer's is asserted as a
necessary condition for any viable system. No relation is declared.

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-tmpyl7eo](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyl7eo.md). For the owner's essay:

- **§6.1** (stability is not viability). Beer draws the distinction in
  organizational terms: systems that are there but not "effectively there"
  operate "at the margins of viability" and grow "less and less viable in
  front of everyone's eyes" (p. 12).
- **§8.3** (recursive self-governance). Recursion is the model's central
  principle, though Beer's recursion is structural nesting, not the
  essay's revision of standards by procedures that are themselves revised.
- **§7.1** (corporate endorsement). Beer locates identity in System Five's
  closure and says nominating its components is hard because "who are
  'we'?" has different answers for shareholders, employees and others
  (p. 11). That names the essay's question without answering it.
- **§6.3** (extraction from above). The takeover passage says the higher
  recursion inevitably takes up the subsidiary's variety, whatever is
  promised about its autonomy.

## Limitations

By its own account the paper neither expounds nor validates the model.
The evidence is the author's consultancy, recalled, and a list of other
users. The claim that five subsystems are necessary and sufficient sits
beside the admission that "five" is arbitrary. The copy lacks one page and
the figures, including Figure 4, the diagram the VSM section is built
around.

## Open questions

What result would count against the model: Beer invokes falsifiability but
names no observation the model forbids. How System Five is to be
identified in a contested organization other than by the observer's
imputation, which Beer calls "probably idiosyncratic".
