# THEORY-1980: In a larger and more diverse sample than the original studies, preschool marshmallow-test wait-time predicts adolescent achievement only modestly, and the association is largely explained by family background, the early home environment and concurrent cognitive and behavioral measures

Watts, Duncan & Quan (2018), [LIT-2752](../literature.d/LIT-2752.md).

## The analysis

Wait-time at 54 months (capped at 7 minutes) against age-15 achievement
and behavior, n = 918, with the headline sample the 552 children of
mothers without a college degree. The bivariate association was "only
half the size of those reported in the original studies, and was reduced
by two-thirds" with controls (abstract), and non-significant with
concurrent 54-month controls. A large residual association would have
supported the original reading; it was not found.

## What it does not show

- **What the task does measure.** The authors suggest something "rather
  distinct from self-control"; trust or reliability is not measured.
- **Causation either way.** Wait-time was "not exogenous".
- **The higher-SES sample's dose-response.** Most of those children hit
  the 7-minute ceiling.
