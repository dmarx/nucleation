# NOTE-tmpgy7n9: Fricker — Powerlessness and Social Interpretation

## Contribution

The full argument for hermeneutical injustice, from the chapter of the
book that defines it. The paper separates a hermeneutical disadvantage
that is unjust from one that is bad luck. It gives a definition that
grows from a systematic case to a generic one that also covers
incidental cases. It sets out the wrong, both primary and secondary. The
précis ([NOTE-tmpaz74m](NOTE-tmpaz74m.md)) gave only the systematic definition.

## Key insight

A gap in shared concepts disadvantages everyone formally, as a missing
free dental service does. It becomes an injustice where it falls unevenly
because some people were kept from helping to make the shared meanings.
That exclusion, marginalisation, is what makes it unjust. It need not be
a lifelong feature of a social group. One moment of powerlessness in one
patch of experience is enough for the incidental kind.

## Assumptions

- **Social experience.** The domain is "our understanding of our own
  social experiences" (p. 1). The definitions all concern "some
  significant area of one's social experience".
- **Injustice needs a wrong as well as a harm.** "For something to be an
  injustice it must be harmful but also wrongful, whether because
  discriminatory or otherwise unfair" (p. 3).
- **Marginalisation is coerced.** Opting out by choice does not count
  (p. 6).

## Key results

- **Bad luck against injustice (pp. 3–5).** Harasser and harassee share
  the lacuna, but only she is disadvantaged by it. A person with a
  misunderstood medical condition suffers "a hermeneutical disadvantage
  that is, while collective, especially damaging to them in particular",
  but "theirs is a poignant case of circumstantial epistemic bad luck".
  What makes Wood's case unjust is that "unequal relations of power
  prevented women from participating on equal terms with men in those
  practices by which collective social meanings are generated".
- **Hermeneutical marginalisation (p. 6).** When "there is unequal
  hermeneutical participation with respect to some significant area(s) of
  social experience, members of the disadvantaged group are
  hermeneutically marginalized". It may be "only fleetingly, and/or only
  in respect of a highly localized patch". It "is always socially
  coerced" and "always a form of powerlessness, whether structural or
  one-off". It afflicts people "qua one social type, but not another"
  (p. 7).
- **The definitions (pp. 7–12).**
  - Systematic, first form: "the injustice of having some significant
    area of one's social experience obscured from collective
    understanding owing to persistent and wide-ranging hermeneutical
    marginalization".
  - Systematic, second form: "... owing to a structural prejudice in the
    collective hermeneutical resource".
  - Generic: "the injustice of having some significant area of one's
    social experience obscured from collective understanding owing to
    hermeneutical marginalization". It "covers both the systematic case
    and the incidental case". "The systematic case is central", but
    "Incidental hermeneutical injustices can be every bit as harmful as
    systematic ones."
- **The incidental case (pp. 9–11).** Joe, in McEwan's *Enduring Love*,
  is stalked. The police's "interrogative flow chart" and Clarissa's
  doubt trivialise it ("my grievance would have to be poured into the
  available bureaucratic mould"). "This has nothing to do with any
  general social powerlessness", since "his social identity is that of
  the proverbial white, educated, straight man". "Still he is none the
  less up against a one-off moment of hermeneutical marginalization."
  Incidental cases "stem not from any structural inequality of power but
  rather from a more one-off moment of powerlessness".
- **No culprit (p. 13).** "Whether the injustice is of the systematic or
  the incidental kind, it involves no perpetrator, no culprit." It
  happens "when some actual attempt at intelligibility is handicapped by
  it".
- **The primary harm (pp. 13–14).** "Hermeneutical lacunas are like holes
  in the ozone—it's the people who live under them that get burned." The
  primary harm is "a situated hermeneutical inequality: the concrete
  situation is such that the subject is rendered unable to make
  communicatively intelligible something which it is particularly in his
  or her interests to be able to render intelligible."
- **Secondary harms (pp. 15–17).** Practical harms: Wood's lost transfer,
  job and benefits. Epistemic harms: lost confidence, and so lost
  knowledge on any confidence-including account of knowledge, and
  stunted intellectual courage.
- **Construction of identity (pp. 17–23).** Through Edmund White's *A
  Boy's Own Story*: authoritative constructions can "constitutively"
  construct one's social identity (what one counts as), and perhaps
  "causally" (what one becomes). Dissonance, which is possible because
  constructions hold unevenly, is "the starting point" of consciousness
  raising. Hermeneutical injustice "undermines the subject in his
  capacity as an interpreter of his own social experience".

## Concepts

- **Hermeneutical lacuna.** A gap in "collective hermeneutical
  resources" where the name of a social experience should be.
- **Hermeneutical marginalisation.** Coerced unequal participation in
  making social meanings for some area of experience. It is a form of
  powerlessness.
- **Systematic and incidental.** Marginalisation that pursues the subject
  through other dimensions of social life, against a one-off, localised
  moment.
- **Hermeneutical hotspot.** A place where "the powerful have no interest
  in achieving a proper interpretation".
- **Situated hermeneutical inequality.** The primary harm: a gap that is
  formally general and falls on some in practice.
- **Constitutive and causal construction.** What one counts as, against
  what one is made into.

## Connections

The examples come from Brownmiller's memoir (Wood), McEwan (Joe) and
Edmund White. The paper draws on Montmarquet for intellectual courage, on
Lehrer for self-trust, and on Langton and Haslanger for social
construction. Fricker's later chapter ([NOTE-tmp3a0fd](NOTE-tmp3a0fd.md)) restates the cause
as "hermeneutical marginalisation in relation to some area of social
experience" and adds that the injustice survives good-faith listening.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmplo3ff](../theory.d/THEORY-tmplo3ff.md) states the systematic case as if it were the whole.**
  Its title and "What this does not say" make the gap's being "owed to
  the relative powerlessness of a social group to which the subject
  belongs" necessary. It also says a gap owed to "an individual's
  idiosyncrasy" or to a design choice "that falls on no group" does not
  meet the definition. This paper's generic definition is wider. It
  needs only "hermeneutical marginalization", which may be "one-off",
  "fleeting" and "highly localized", and its Joe case is a socially
  powerful man. Two parts of the précis-based account survive: a gap
  with no marginalisation behind it is still excluded, as bad luck, and
  the domain is still "social experience". [THEORY-tmplo3ff](../theory.d/THEORY-tmplo3ff.md)'s
  promote_when names this point ("admits local and institutional gaps")
  as one that would refute it as a statement of Fricker's account. This
  paper, drawn from the book's final chapter, meets that refuting
  condition for the 2006 text. Whether the book keeps it is unread.
  [THEORY-tmp0d8b5](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp0d8b5.md) states the wider definition.
- **[QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md), part 2.** Its present text follows the précis: the gap
  "must be owed to the relative powerlessness of a social group". On the
  generic definition, the question becomes whether a contributor whose
  move fits no scheme suffers even a one-off, localised marginalisation
  in making the shared meanings, here the record's grammar. Joe's
  grievance, "poured into the available bureaucratic mould", is formally
  close to a move that has to be filed in a closed grammar. Two
  conditions remain:
  - the obscured thing must be a significant area of the contributor's
    *social experience*, not just any idea;
  - the marginalisation must be "socially coerced". A contributor who
    chooses not to file is a hermit, not marginalised.
- **[NOTE-098](NOTE-098.md)'s gloss of Leonelli** ("marginalisation of ways of knowing")
  is still wider than Fricker's. Her domain is social experience, not
  ways of knowing in general.
- **Nothing here is ML practice.**

## Limitations

- The incidental case rests on one literary example, and the paper calls
  the systematic case "central".
- It is drawn from a chapter "forthcoming" in 2006. The published
  chapter may differ, and it was not read.
- "Significant area of social experience" and "socially coerced" are
  left without criteria.

## Open questions

- Whether an institution's own procedure (the police's flow chart) is
  the marginalising agent in Joe's case, or only its occasion. The paper
  says the injustice has "no perpetrator", but the flow chart is
  someone's design.
- What makes a patch of experience "significant" enough.
