# LIT-tmplt3pf: Is the United States Phenomenally Conscious? Reply to Kammerer

Eric Schwitzgebel (2016), *Philosophia* 44(3), 877–883 —
DOI-10.1007/s11406-016-9725-8. Author's manuscript (28 April 2016) at
https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzPapers/KammererReply-160428.htm.

## Key takeaways

- **Concern 1: it may not exclude actual groups.** Kammerer allows some
  group consciousness, so he and Schwitzgebel may agree on the main
  point, that some actual groups of people are conscious if materialism
  is true. Even the US may escape: condition A needs citizens to
  consciously represent *the entity* at issue, a vague-boundaried
  concrete whole of residents and their artefacts. When people think
  "the United States" they may refer to the government, the nation or
  the land instead.
- **Concern 2: flickering group consciousness.** If "required" in
  condition A is read loosely, a member (Leo) whose motivation to keep
  working comes to depend on representing the group switches the group's
  consciousness off and on with no change in group-level processing. If
  it is read strictly, Kammerer's own theory lets non-conscious parts
  fill the roles, so condition A is never met. Any theory that lets
  consciousness vary without higher-level functional change invites
  fading or flickering qualia cases (cf. Chalmers 1996).
- **Concern 3: the justification.** Applied generally, the simplicity
  rule ("ascribe consciousness only when other subjects' states cannot
  explain the behaviour") is the causal-exclusion problem, and would
  threaten human consciousness and baseballs breaking windows. Applied
  only to consciousness, it is ad hoc. And group-level explanation is
  often simpler (why the army landed here).

**Where it sits.** It answers Kammerer's "How a materialist can deny that
the United States is probably conscious" ([LIT-409](LIT-409.md), read in [NOTE-362](../notes.d/NOTE-362.md)),
which answered Schwitzgebel's "If materialism is true, the United States
is probably conscious" ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md), read in [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md)). It is a reply that
contests the principle, not one that builds on it, so no `extends:` is
declared: the paper does not present itself as carrying Kammerer's work
further, and it does not show the principle false, so `corrects:` would
overstate it. In order of the record's numbering of Kammerer's claims
([NOTE-362](../notes.d/NOTE-362.md)):

- **Against C2 (the US verdict).** [NOTE-362](../notes.d/NOTE-362.md) says the verdict rests on an
  asserted counterfactual, condition B. This reply presses condition A
  instead: whether citizens represent the right entity at all. The two
  objections are independent, and either alone defeats the verdict.
- **Against C3 (the principle allows some group consciousness).**
  Schwitzgebel takes the concession as agreement on his main point, which
  is about some actual groups, not the US in particular.
- **Against C6 (the Occam-style justification).** The reply's third
  concern is the strongest attack on it in the record. It is the half of
  [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s `promote_when` that asks for "the Occam-style rule Kammerer
  gestures at, worked out".

**The flicker argument and organisational invariance.** The second
concern has the form of Chalmers's fading and dancing qualia argument
("Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia", [LIT-392](LIT-392.md), read in
[NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md)), which Schwitzgebel cites only as Chalmers 1996: consciousness
that varies while function stays fixed. [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) records that
organisational invariance "leaves Kammerer's principle standing", because
the US is not an isomorph of anything. This reply finds a variation in
which group-level function is held fixed by construction, and so turns
an invariance-style argument against the principle after all, at least
on a loose reading of "required".

**The book's later reply.** *The Weirdness of the World* ([LIT-216](LIT-216.md), read in
[NOTE-188](../notes.d/NOTE-188.md)) answers Kammerer again, more briefly, as "motivated post-hoc",
with the Planck-sized-person case. That is this paper's third concern
compressed, plus a new counter-case; the reference argument and the Leo
case are not repeated there by [NOTE-188](../notes.d/NOTE-188.md)'s account.

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice, and it does not
belong in the anthology.
