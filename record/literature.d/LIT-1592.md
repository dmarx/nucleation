# LIT-1592: Rights Essentialism and Remedial Equilibration

Levinson, D. J. (1999). Rights essentialism and remedial equilibration. *Columbia Law Review*, 99(4), 857. doi:10.2307/1123480.

Not read. The summary is drawn from what the work is standardly cited
for and from the exchange's description of it. A reading should check it
first.

## Why this is here

A8: "If a person retains a formal right but the institutional mechanisms for exercising it are systematically unavailable, has the relevant freedom persisted?" It bears on [CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md)'s opportunity aspect of freedom and on [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md).

## Note of 2026-10-29: anticipated by Fiss, now held

Fiss's "The forms of justice" (1979), LIT-1600, read in NOTE-tmpafym3,
states the thesis this work is standardly cited for, twenty years before
it. Fiss holds that rights and remedies "jointly constitute the meaning of
the public value", and that because a structural remedy depends on actors
outside the court, "no judge is likely to decree more than he thinks he
has the power to accomplish … He will tailor the right to fit the remedy"
(pp. 50–58). That is remedial equilibration under another name. Whether
Levinson cites Fiss, and how his account differs, is for a reading of this
work, which is still Unfetched. The status is unchanged.
