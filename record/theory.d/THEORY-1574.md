# THEORY-1574: Bostrom argues that on aggregative, time-neutral views of value reducing existential risk is a global priority, and that it is undersupplied for lack of motivation as well as understanding, since its benefit is a pan-generational public good whose future beneficiaries cannot pay and moral sentiment is insensitive to scope

## The account

An existential risk "threatens the premature extinction of Earth-originating
intelligent life or the permanent and drastic destruction of its potential
for desirable future development" (p. 15), in four classes: extinction,
permanent stagnation, flawed realisation and subsequent ruination. What
makes it bad is that it would "destroy the future" (p. 17).

On aggregative, time-neutral value, Earth alone could hold at least 10^16
future lives, so a millionth of one percentage point of risk reduction is
worth at least a hundred times a million lives (pp. 18–19). Maxipok follows:
"Maximise the probability of an 'OK outcome'" (p. 19). Other perspectives
concur, among them Adams' shared project of humanity and custodial duties:
"We do not want to be the failing link in the chain of generations" (p. 23).

"Mitigation of existential risk is hampered by a lack of understanding, but
also by a deficit of motivation" (p. 26). Safety is a pan-generational
public good: even a world state captures only the benefits to people now
alive, and future beneficiaries cannot pay. "Moral motivations, too, may
fail to measure up", through scope insensitivity (pp. 26–27).

## Evidence

The risk estimates, 10–20% this century, are subjective, with one informal
poll (p. 15, n. 1). The axiology is assumed, and the other perspectives are
surveyed in a page. Scope insensitivity is cited to a review, and note 37's
guess that concern would be the same under a guarantee against extinction is
a conjecture ([NOTE-1321](../notes.d/NOTE-1321.md), Limitations).

evidence for the claim: "Bostrom grants the thesis its prediction
([NOTE-1321](../notes.d/NOTE-1321.md)). Concern for existential safety is undersupplied because people
capture only their own share of it: concern reaches as far as the self's
stake." The claim does not rest on it, so the reading corroborates the claim
([ADR-058](../decisions.d/ADR-058.md)). It is weak support: the diagnosis is a public-goods argument and
an untested claim about sentiment, and Bostrom says nothing about the self.
