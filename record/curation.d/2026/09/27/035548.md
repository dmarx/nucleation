## Bricken & Pehlevan, *Attention Approximates Sparse Distributed Memory*

[LIT-tmpynu35](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpynu35.md), [NOTE-tmpk6gen](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpk6gen.md). Also held in the anthology as [ANTH-LIT-641](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-641.md).

**Reason for a second entry** ([ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md) requires one). The anthology reads the
paper for attention practice, mainly its QK-norm retrodiction. This record
reads it as associative memory:

- Kanerva's content-addressable retrieval;
- the cerebellar mapping;
- the SDM read as a kernel smoother, set against van Rijsbergen ([LIT-262](../../record/literature.d/LIT-262.md))
  and the kernel THEORY documents.

### What the reading found

- **The paper never says "kernel", but its construction is one.** The
  weight SDM gives each stored memory is the overlap of two Hamming balls,
  which is a positive-definite kernel of distance. Locally it is approximated
  by exp(β·cos), the von Mises–Fisher kernel. Softmax attention with unit
  vectors and a fitted β is then a normalised kernel smoother over the
  memories.
- **Neuron-level SDM is linear attention with a fixed binary feature map.**
  This is the reader's observation, not the paper's.
- **The approximation holds over a narrower range than the paper claims.**
  - It holds for distances below the radius d, not up to 2d. At n = 1000
    the fit over the full range has R² ≈ 0.68.
  - The overlap is a staircase, equal at distances 2k−1 and 2k.
  - The paper's analytic β is off by about 2–2.5×, because it approximates a
    far-tail binomial with a Gaussian. The β the experiments use comes from
    regression instead.

  These are the reader's recomputations.
- **The biology is weak.** The cerebellar mapping restates Kanerva's rather
  than re-arguing it. For attention it would need about 2⁶⁴ neurons, which
  the paper itself calls implausible. How a circuit would write every token
  in one forward pass is not addressed.
- **Against van Rijsbergen.** His tr(ρP) is a broad degree-2 cosine kernel
  that ranks documents and is normalised over the query. SDM and attention
  use a sharp, tunable kernel, return a reconstructed vector rather than a
  ranking, and normalise over the stored memories. Same family, different
  job.

### The anthology's entry needs correcting

This record reports these and does not edit the anthology ([ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md)).

1. **The interpolation claim.** [ANTH-LIT-641](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-641.md) and [ANTH-THEORY-097](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/theory.d/THEORY-097.md) say the
   learned QK-norm band β ∈ [10, 25] interpolates between SDM's three
   optima. The paper claims it only for two of them, the critical-distance
   and signal-to-noise optima. I checked this against the text. Its Fig. 7
   caption says the memory-capacity optimum, β = 35.5, is "much larger than
   the largest" learned βs.
2. **The residual stream.** [ANTH-LIT-641](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-641.md) says the paper interprets "the
   residual stream" through the SDM frame. The paper never mentions it (also
   checked). Its §4 covers the feed-forward block, LayerNorm, value norms,
   multi-head attention and stacking.
3. **Where the optima come from.** [ANTH-THEORY-097](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/theory.d/THEORY-097.md) credits all three to
   Kanerva's book and his 1992 review. The paper cites Kanerva (1993) for
   two of them and derives the memory-capacity optimum itself.
4. **The GPT-2 check.** The abstract's "we confirm … in pre-trained GPT2"
   overstates Appendix A.2. GPT-2's effective β mostly lies in [3, 12],
   below the QK-norm band, and GPT-2 does not normalise its queries and keys.

## ZipNN: Lossless Compression for AI Models

[LIT-tmpbmt5h](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpbmt5h.md), [NOTE-tmpiq4s5](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpiq4s5.md). Not held in the anthology. It is tagged
`anthology-candidate` because it is chiefly about ML practice.

### What the reading found

- **The exponent carries almost all the redundancy.**
  - About 40 of its 256 values occur, and 12 cover about 99.9% of
    parameters. The exponent compresses to about 33%, while sign and
    mantissa stay near 100%.
  - Whole models compress to about 66% (BF16), 83% (FP32) and 34–48% for
    rounded "clean" models.
  - On Llama-3.1-8B, ZipNN is 17% smaller and 62% faster than Zstd.
- **The information theory is thin.**
  - The paper never computes an entropy or compares its codes to one.
  - Its explanation of the exponent skew is asserted, not argued. The paper
    itself says "No matter the reason".
  - Part of that explanation is wrong: Adam's ε does not add noise to
    weights, and 2⁻²³ is exponent 104, not 99.
  - Its "ExaByte per year" savings claim read "per month" in v1, which the
    paper's own 6 PB/day figure rules out.
- **No bearing on the record's description-length works.** Lossless coding
  of the float format compresses how the numbers are written, not the
  function the network computes. It does not touch Kolmogorov complexity or
  MDL ([LIT-224](../../record/literature.d/LIT-224.md), [LIT-225](../../record/literature.d/LIT-225.md), [LIT-238](../../record/literature.d/LIT-238.md)), or the lossy compressibility of the
  Sefidgaran bounds ([LIT-233](../../record/literature.d/LIT-233.md), [LIT-236](../../record/literature.d/LIT-236.md)).
