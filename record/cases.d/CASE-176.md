# CASE-176: The United States Institute of Peace, 2025–2026: its board removed and its building entered by DOGE staff with police assistance before any court ruled, nearly all staff dismissed, a district court's judgment that the takeover was unlawful stayed on appeal, and the institution renamed while its legal identity continued

## The case

The owner, at U5: "examine the case of the US Institute for Peace through
the lens of our framework"; at U6: "another interesting facet here is the
involvement of the DC metropolitan police and FBI, and the judge's
concern regarding the potential for police instigated violence in their
ruling on whether or not to grant a TRO."

Congress created USIP in 1984 as an independent national institute for
preventing and resolving violent conflict, governed by a bipartisan board
of Senate-confirmed appointees and ex officio members. Executive Order
14217 of 19 February 2025 directed it to reduce to its statutory minimum.
In mid-March 2025 the administration removed the appointed board members
and installed new leadership; on 17 March DOGE staff entered the
headquarters with the help of the District's Metropolitan Police; on 19
March Judge Beryl Howell declined a temporary restraining order; at the
end of March nearly all staff were dismissed and the building was moved
toward the General Services Administration. On 19 May 2025 Howell held
that the takeover exceeded the administration's authority; on 27 June a
divided D.C. Circuit panel stayed her judgment pending appeal. The
institution was later presented as the Donald J. Trump Institute of
Peace.

The exchange added particulars the record has not checked, kept as the
exchange's:

- **The TRO hearing** (A6). FBI agents, MPD officers and State Department
  security had helped secure the building. Howell criticized the resort to
  armed law enforcement and declined the order, on the plaintiffs' failure
  at that stage to show likelihood of success and irreparable harm, and
  asked whether an order excluding the new occupants could provoke an
  "armed battle" if they refused to leave. A6: that concern "was not a
  judicial finding that police violence was certain, nor the sole stated
  ground of denial." The owner's "police instigated violence" is the
  owner's gloss of that concern.
- **The footage** (A6). In February 2026 a D.C. Superior Court judge ordered
  MPD to release body-camera footage, and Marisa Kabas published nearly six
  hours of it on 6 March 2026, showing officers working through locked
  doors and escorting USIP staff out; in it the new acting president,
  Kenneth Jackson, says "It's a private building, and that's why we need
  MPD." NPR reported in March 2025 that both sides had asked MPD for help,
  that the acting U.S. Attorney directed police to documents supporting the
  new leadership, and that MPD treated the incumbents as unauthorized
  occupants.
- **The contractor** (A5). A *Security Management* investigation reported
  that USIP's guards came from a contractor with a far larger State
  Department contract, that its personnel let DOGE in despite USIP having
  suspended the contract, and that DOGE had threatened its federal
  contracts.
- **The consequences** (A5). Programs in some 26 conflict zones stopped;
  staff in Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger were left without support; during
  the brief return of the old leadership after the May ruling about 25
  staff came back, funds stayed inaccessible and overseas offices stayed
  closed (AP). DOGE publicized a contract with the Afghan scholar Mohammad
  Halimi as support for the Taliban; ProPublica reported that the Taliban
  then detained and beat three of his relatives.
- **The façade** (A5). On 30 September 2026 the D.C. Circuit barred carving
  the President's name into the façade while litigation continued; the
  lettering had already been added.

## What it shows

- **Continuations that did not move together** ([CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md)). A5's table:
  juridical identity, building and name continued; personnel, programs,
  leadership, internal procedures and partner relations did not. A5: "What
  persists legally need not be what persists functionally." It is the
  record's second documented case of the respects coming apart, after the
  Senators ([CASE-105](CASE-105.md)); unlike that one it has, so far, no second
  successor, only a hypothetical one (former staff continuing the work
  elsewhere), so it shows the respects separating and not plural succession
  (note of 2026-10-27 on [CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md)).
- **Control is not competence** ([CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md), [CLAIM-099](../claims.d/CLAIM-099.md)). A5: "Control
  over organization ⇏ Competence to reproduce organization." The new
  leadership had the building, systems and payroll, not the mediation
  expertise or the trust of partners in conflict zones, which A5 calls
  relationally realized.
- **A boundary held by an outside dependence** ([CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md), [CLAIM-405](../claims.d/CLAIM-405.md)). The
  guard on USIP's door answered to a larger customer. To explain routine
  security the boundary is USIP and its contractor; to explain the
  takeover it must include the contractor's other obligations.
- **Authority as a profile** ([CLAIM-410](../claims.d/CLAIM-410.md)). Rule-making (the statute),
  appointment and removal (disputed), interpretation (officers deciding
  whose documents counted), enforcement (MPD, FBI), adjudication (the
  courts) and organizational control (the new leadership) were held by
  different bodies and did not move together. A6: "The police did not have
  to resolve the constitutional controversy correctly in order to make one
  side's claim practically effective." The legibility of a claim to an
  enforcer ([TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)) stood in for its validity.
- **Enforcement before adjudication changes the remedy** ([CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md)). The
  legal dispute was the same after 17 March; the distribution of physical
  control was not; and the risk of reversing it became one consideration
  before the court. A6: "The means used to establish control can therefore
  change the practical conditions under which that control can be
  challenged."
- **A reclassification with consequences outside** ([CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md)). The Halimi
  contract, described by payee history and amount, read as suspicious; its
  purpose, the scholar's later record and the danger of publicity were
  merged out. A5: "A description sufficient for identifying a potentially
  controversial expenditure may be radically insufficient for evaluating
  the consequences of publicizing it."

## What it does not show

- **The legal outcome.** The district court's judgment is stayed, not
  reversed, and the appeal was pending when the exchange ran. Nothing here
  says the takeover was lawful or unlawful.
- **That police acted unlawfully.** A6: the mechanism "does not establish
  that MPD knowingly acted unlawfully"; one legal commentator defended the
  officers as answering an apparent trespass dispute.
- **How much was lost for good.** A5's defeaters stand: capacities carried
  into successor organizations, the same functions performed by new staff,
  or partner relations simply reactivated would weaken a claim of
  destruction. Nor are later conflicts attributable to the closure without
  evidence. A5: "The loss of a functioning peacebuilding relationship is an
  institutional change even when we cannot calculate how many future deaths
  or conflicts it might have prevented."
- **That the renamed institution's activities lack value.** A5: they "must
  be evaluated on their own merits".
- **Standing.** The order, the removals, the entry with police assistance,
  the TRO denial, the May ruling and the stay are documented public events;
  the particulars above and the reading are the exchange's. A6 named the
  evidence a reading should start from: the 19 March transcript, MPD's
  explanation, the body-camera footage, the May opinion and the appellate
  record. A6 also proposed the comparison that would test the mechanism:
  disputes in which police declined to enforce a contested leadership claim
  until a court ruled, against those in which they acted at once.
