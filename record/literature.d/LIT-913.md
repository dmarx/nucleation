# LIT-913: On the Concept of Coordinative Structures as Dissipative Structures: I. Theoretical Lines of Convergence

Peter N. Kugler, J. A. Scott Kelso and M. T. Turvey (1980), "On the Concept of Coordinative Structures as Dissipative Structures: I. Theoretical Lines of Convergence", in G. E. Stelmach and J. Requin (eds.), *Tutorials in Motor Behavior*, Amsterdam: North-Holland, pp. 3–47 — [doi:10.1016/s0166-4115(08)61936-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0166-4115(08)61936-6)

## Key takeaways

*Unread. From the title and the literature that cites it.*

- **The proposal.** A coordinative structure is not built by a controller
  out of independent parts. It is a self-organizing pattern, a dissipative
  structure in Prigogine's sense, sustained by a flow of energy.
- **Why the record wants it.** It is the link between the motor-control
  sense of "coordinative structure" and the coordination dynamics the
  record already reads ([THEORY-069](../theory.d/THEORY-069.md), [LIT-601](LIT-601.md)). Any extension of the term to
  dancing couples or firms should start from what it claims here.
