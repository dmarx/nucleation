# CLAIM-tmp6phbn: Participation on a shared canvas thins selectively: people keep coming back while art they invested in survives, so unaffiliated corruption falls over the event and the remaining participants are weighted toward maintainers, a cooling schedule the population produces for itself

## The claim

The owner, disagreeing with [CLAIM-tmp4zg6p](CLAIM-tmp4zg6p.md)'s first version, which said the
canvas has no thermal decay:

> The time horizons for these experiments were always relatively short, no
> more than days or weeks. There functionally is a kind of thermal decay at
> work: random participation. Part of what differentiates /r/place from the
> psychological model is that there's a dropoff in participation over time,
> creating a survival function that biases towards user's sustaining
> participation if the art they are invested in persists. If users were
> never invested in any of the art to begin with, they are not likely to
> hang around, so the "temperature" of the random corruption is much higher
> at the beginning of the experiment than at the end.

Three parts:

1. **Unaffiliated placement is a decay.** Over the short horizon of an
   event, placements that serve no plan corrupt any pattern left alone. That
   is the canvas temperature ([TERM-tmpzxb0m](../terms.d/TERM-tmpzxb0m.md)).
2. **It cools.** Uninvested participants leave, so T falls over the event.
3. **The cooling is selective.** Whether a participant stays depends on
   whether their art persists. Surviving art keeps its maintainers, and
   failed art loses them. The population that remains is weighted toward
   people defending something.

The third part is a feedback from the pattern to the flow that maintains it,
the mark of [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md)'s self-maintaining rung, and so it answers
[QUESTION-tmpgjjah](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpgjjah.md) in part. The readings show the other route of the same
feedback, recruitment. The Mona Lisa group wrote its forum's URL on the
canvas as a guide, and within two hours newcomers said they had come because
of it (Litherland and Mørch, [THEORY-tmpbrxww](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpbrxww.md)). Wu and Silva find coalitions
consolidating into fewer, larger ones over the 2017 event ([THEORY-tmp7w5tq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7w5tq.md)),
which is what selective retention would produce, though it does not show
it.

Read together with the dissipative-structure claim, an event looks like
annealing. Order specified elsewhere is laid down while the noise is high.
Most of it is erased, under 7% of updates surviving ([THEORY-tmp7w5tq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7w5tq.md)). What
remains settles as the temperature falls, and the falling is produced by the
same selection that decides what remains.

## What it does not say

- It has not been tested. The released logs (2017, 2022, 2023) would allow
  a survival analysis: each participant's hazard of not returning, with the
  survival of the pixels they placed as a time-varying covariate, and the
  rate of placements fitting no artwork over time.
- It does not say all decline is selective. Novelty wears off for everyone,
  and per-user cooldowns of 10 or 20 minutes ([CASE-tmpd83bj](../cases.d/CASE-tmpd83bj.md)) change rates
  for reasons unrelated to art.
- It does not carry over to the psychological model unchanged. The owner
  names the dwindling population as what separates r/place from a mind,
  whose processes do not leave. Whether something in a mind plays the part
  of a cooling population, such as fatigue, or attention withdrawn from
  failed projects, is open. It is not assumed.
- It does not say the cooling is good for the canvas. A schedule set by
  who stays favours whatever their art is, which is [CLAIM-tmpf1063](CLAIM-tmpf1063.md)'s
  entrenchment loop at the level of the population.
