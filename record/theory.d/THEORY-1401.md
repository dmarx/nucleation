# THEORY-1401: Bogetoft and Hougaard hold that an organization's measured technical inefficiency can be a rational choice of on-the-job slack traded against profit, so that under price-based or restriction-based control a rational unit still uses an efficient underlying plan, and its observed inputs reveal partial information about how it values the different kinds of slack

## Source

Bogetoft and Hougaard, Rational Inefficiencies (KVL working paper 2001/14,
revised 2002), LIT-2096; read in full in NOTE-1576. Registered as a
stand-in for Bogetoft and Kerstens (2022), LIT-2076.

## What was actually shown

- **The premise, argued** (§1): "we see inefficiency and in particular the
  allocation of inefficiency (slack) among different inputs as the result
  of a rational choice made by that firm" (p. 3), with rationales from the
  organizational, rent-seeking and bureaucracy literatures.
- **Efficient underneath** (Propositions 1 and 2): under a price-based
  regime the underlying plan is allocatively efficient. Under a
  restriction-based regime it is technically efficient, with supporting
  prices when the acceptable set is convex. These are derivations within
  the model. Checked in NOTE-1576 v2. Proposition 2's proof passes from the
  slack set to all of A − L with only "By w ≥ 0". The step holds because L
  is closed upward, so A − L is closed downward. Without convexity the
  restriction regime still gives a technically efficient underlying plan
  (the Observation, p. 17).
- **Revealed trade-offs** (Proposition 3): observed slack must be at least
  as good, by the unit's value function, as every other feasible slack
  vector, which constrains the value function partially.
- **No data** (§8, p. 31): estimating the models "on actual data sets" is
  left "for future research".

## What this does not say

- That observed inefficiency is in fact rational: it is an interpretation
  the model allows, and the authors grant the waste reading may be right
  and the rational one "too soft".
- Which slack is useful and which wasteful: "The distinction between
  valuable and non-valuable waste is a challenging one." (p. 5). That is
  Bogetoft and Kerstens' later question, unread here.
- That slack held as a buffer pays off in contingencies: slack is valued as
  held, not tested by what it does when needed.
- That the value function fitted under one regime predicts behaviour under
  another, which the paper assumes.
