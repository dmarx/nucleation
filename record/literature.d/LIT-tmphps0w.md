# LIT-tmphps0w: Epilogue: Analogy as the Core of Cognition

Douglas R. Hofstadter (2001), *in Dedre Gentner, Keith J. Holyoak and Boicho
N. Kokinov (eds.), The Analogical Mind: Perspectives from Cognitive Science,
MIT Press, pp. 499–538 (book DOI 10.7551/mitpress/1251.001.0001)* —
DOI-10.7551/mitpress/1251.003.0020

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for Douglas Hofstadter's work in
this record. It is the short statement, under its own title, of the thesis
the owner named: that analogy-making is the core of cognition. It closes a
volume edited by the founder of the structure-mapping approach to analogy
(Gentner), the approach whose program, the Structure-Mapping Engine,
Hofstadter's group had criticised nine years earlier for working on
hand-built representations ([LIT-tmph3vnp](LIT-tmph3vnp.md)).

`Deferred` because no lawful full text could be reached (see the history
note), so no NOTE is filed and nothing is said here about the chapter's
argument beyond its title and place. Tagged `cognition` only: the title
alone justifies it, and further tags wait for a reading.

Where it would sit:

- The 1992 critique ([LIT-tmph3vnp](LIT-tmph3vnp.md), read in full) already argues the
  weaker half of the thesis: that analogy-making is a perceptual process
  going on "constantly in the background of the mind", not "a heavy weapon
  wheeled out occasionally". A reading should check how far this chapter
  goes beyond that, to the claim in its title.
- Copycat ([LIT-tmpsfaz3](LIT-tmpsfaz3.md)) is the group's program for the thesis; Hofstadter
  and Sander's *Surfaces and Essences* (2013), being filed beside this work,
  is its book-length statement.
- Minsky's frames ([LIT-tmpf92fs](LIT-tmpf92fs.md)), filed in the same session, are the other
  classic account in the record of how a mind brings stored structure to a
  new situation. Whether this chapter engages frames is for a reading.
