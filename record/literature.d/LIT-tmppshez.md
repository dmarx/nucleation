# LIT-tmppshez: The Frankfurt Cases: The Moral of the Stories

Fischer, J. M. (2010). The Frankfurt Cases: The Moral of the Stories.
*The Philosophical Review*, 119(3), 315–336.
doi:10.1215/00318108-2010-002.

## Key takeaways

- The lesson of the Frankfurt cases is conditional: if determinism rules
  out responsibility, it is not by eliminating alternatives. It does not
  by itself establish compatibilism.
- A deterministic Frankfurt case need not beg the question: assume
  determinism, stay agnostic on whether it removes alternatives, and let
  determinism plus Black's device remove them together.
- What matters is robust alternatives; a mere flicker, such as showing a
  different involuntary sign, does not ground responsibility.
- Fischer concedes that causal determination may matter to responsibility
  in another way, "lack of 'sourcehood'" (p. 331).

## Why this is here

[THEORY-316](../theory.d/THEORY-316.md) states Pereboom's causal-history incompatibilism from a partial
reading of *Living without Free Will* ([LIT-1006](LIT-1006.md), Unavailable) and waits on
the rest of its Chapter 1 and on a reply from the leeway side. This paper
reports Pereboom's buffer case and Widerker's dilemma, and is the
compatibilist's statement of what the cases do and do not show.
