# LIT-1365: National Incident Management System, Third Edition

U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Federal Emergency Management Agency (2017). *National Incident Management System*, third edition, October 2017. https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/is700b/handouts/national_incident_management%20system_third%20edition_october_2017.pdf.

Skimmed on 2026-10-22 in [NOTE-1072](../notes.d/NOTE-1072.md): the Introduction and the
Command and Coordination component read in full.

## Why this is here

The owner's U23 proposed NIMS as a temporary collective with well-defined boundaries. A24 drew from it that "Coordinated action need not require that all practical authority flow through one undifferentiated controller." Bears on [CASE-135](../cases.d/CASE-135.md) and [CASE-134](../cases.d/CASE-134.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** NIMS supports A24 in part. Under
Unified Command "there is no one 'commander.'" and each agency keeps its
authority; functions are delegated; the Safety Officer can stop unsafe
acts. But the doctrine keeps a single command function, a single Incident
Action Plan and unity of command ("each individual only reports to one
person"), and leaves "Ultimate responsibility" for safety with command.
Authority is divided among those who hold the command function, not
spread so that no one holds it. For [CASE-134](../cases.d/CASE-134.md), the transfer of command is
formalized with a briefing that "captures essential information for
continuing safe and effective operations". For [CASE-135](../cases.d/CASE-135.md), the Brookhollow
review's freelancing and self-dispatch depart from NIMS's rule that
resources not requested "should refrain from spontaneous deployment". See
[NOTE-1072](../notes.d/NOTE-1072.md).
