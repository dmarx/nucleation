## The verdicts

- **(a)** Yes, narrowed. "refinement types are given only to programs
  which are already well-typed in ML" (abstract); refinements are
  "subtypes which one can think of as subsets" (p. 1); an annotation is
  accepted only when the inferred refinement already lies below it
  (RESTRICT, Figure 2, p. 8). Narrowed: the refinements are a finite
  lattice declared in advance, "necessary for the decidability of
  refinement type inference" (p. 2), not arbitrary predicates; they are
  of a type, not of one term; and no evidence is carried.
- **(b)** It matches. "an expression has type σ ∧ τ if it has both type σ
  and type τ" (p. 3); "?e ∧ std is equivalent to ?e" (p. 5); ?e and
  stdpos meet only at ⊥. The witness must be a value, since soundness
  holds only "if e evaluates to v" (p. 7). The paper does not treat an
  empty intersection as a conflict.
- **(c)** Everything is inferred ("first performs an ML type inference
  pass and then a refinement type inference pass using abstract
  interpretation", p. 7); annotations are checked against what was
  inferred. Both are word to world, and nothing moves a term ("we do not
  extend the language of programs for ML (only the language of types)",
  p. 1). Not a bidirectional system; the correspondence waits on the
  Dunfield and Krishnaswami reading.
