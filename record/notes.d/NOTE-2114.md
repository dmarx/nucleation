# NOTE-2114: Ibrahim 2013 — Collective Capabilities: What are they and why are they Important?

## Contribution

A short position piece in the Human Development and Capability Association's e-bulletin. It defines collective capabilities, following Evans (2002), Stewart (2005) and Ibrahim's own 2006 paper, as capabilities produced by collective agency whose benefits accrue to individuals and the group, and that no individual could have alone. It argues that collectivities have intrinsic as well as instrumental value for capabilities. It answers Sen's and Alkire's objections, and states five conditions under which generating collective capabilities is good. It ends by asking that the evaluative space be extended from the individual to the collectivity.

## Key results

- Definition, p. 4: "Collective capabilities thus need to be seen as those capabilities that result from the exercise of collective agency and whose benefits accrue to the individual and the collectivity. They are capabilities that the individual alone would neither have nor be able to achieve (Ibrahim, 2006)."
- Two effects on individuals, p. 5: "First, the act of choosing the life that one ‘has reason to value’ might be a collective rather than an individual act (Evans, 2002). Secondly, these capabilities affect the individual’s perception of the good".
- Sen's objection, p. 6: Sen "rejects the concept of collective capabilities and prefers to label those capabilities that result from social interaction as ‘socially dependent individual capabilities’."
- Alkire's objection, p. 6: a claim that a group "provided a collective capability" "may overlook some significant dis-benefits or heterogeneities".
- Conditions, p. 7: "The generation of collective capabilities needs to be through the free and voluntary participation of the collectivity members, rather than through force or coercion." Unequal distribution is acceptable when it is not "due to deliberate exclusionary practices". With Ballet et al. 2007, "changes in collective capability can occur even if individual capabilities remain unchanged".
- Conclusion, p. 7: "The evaluative space of human capabilities therefore needs to be extended from the individual to the collectivity."

## Assumptions

- Sen's capability framework, with agency and freedom as central. Collectivities have intrinsic value, which is asserted with support from De Herdt, Deneulin and Cleaver, not argued from first principles.
- Normative throughout. Empirical support is by citation (Kabeer 2003; Ibrahim 2008), not shown.

## Concepts

- Collective capability; collective agency; socially dependent individual capability (Sen's alternative term).
- Group capabilities (Stewart), relational capabilities (Dubois et al.), external capabilities (Foster and Handy).
- The evaluative versus the instrumental importance of collectivities.

## Bearing on the record

- [CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md): qualifies. [CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md) keeps the individual as the unit and splits Sen's freedom into opportunity (through a map to valued functionings) and process (partly will-organization). Ibrahim marks a contested extension beyond that: capabilities that exist only through collective agency, and an evaluative space extended to the collectivity. Her first condition (free, voluntary participation, not coercion) is a process-aspect requirement on how a collective capability arises. Sen's own reply, "socially dependent individual capabilities", keeps [CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md)'s individualism intact, so the claim is not defeated. But it leaves open whether a collective's reachable set is a further object [CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md) does not cover. The held reading [NOTE-259](NOTE-259.md) (Robeyns) already records Sen's distinction.
- [CLAIM-760](../claims.d/CLAIM-760.md): supports its third category. A collective capability, one of the "capabilities that the individual alone would neither have nor be able to achieve", is capability expansion that is neither work saved nor work transferred. Alkire's "dis-benefits or heterogeneities" objection is a boundary point like the claim's: whose capabilities are counted decides whether the collective achieved anything.
- How far it stands in for [LIT-2823](../literature.d/LIT-2823.md): for the 2006 paper's definition and its place in the debate, yes. For the 2006 framework (collective action, institutions, social capital) and the three Egyptian self-help cases, no.

## Limitations

- Four pages in a newsletter, not peer-reviewed in the journal sense, with no data.
- A later restatement, seven years on, which may differ from the 2006 paper in emphasis.
- It does not engage with whether collective capabilities can be measured.
