# ADR-057: Status review is ranked by reliance, derived from the record each pass, covers relations as well as statuses, and changes status in the pull request that decides it

## Context

The owner asked on 2026-10-27 for opportunities to change item statuses
or relations: "claims that a lot of interesting downstream material
depends on but which is currently in proposed state". Then: "the record
has changed materially since that last audit was interrupted. also, that
was the first time we utilized that process, and would likely benefit from
changing it."

The audit of [ADR-047](ADR-047.md) ran from 2026-10-19 to 2026-10-21. Measured on
origin/main at 99ce85e6f against the queue's own record revision
(3064087c, version 172):

- **The record moved.** 611 commits came after the queue's revision. They
  added 326 THEORYs, 91 CLAIMs, 63 QUESTIONs, 60 CASEs and 32 TERMs, and
  modified 103 CLAIMs and 168 THEORYs.
- **The decisions went stale.** 230 of the 308 decision files judge an
  entry that has changed since its `reviewed_revision`. [ADR-047](ADR-047.md) called
  such a review stale, and nothing re-ran one.
- **It ranked the load-bearing claims last.** Counting transitive
  dependents along `rests_on`, `grounds`, `uses` and `presupposes`, 15 of
  the 19 most-relied-on Proposed entries never entered the queue. [CLAIM-027](../claims.d/CLAIM-027.md)
  has 19 dependents, [CLAIM-177](../claims.d/CLAIM-177.md) 13 and [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md) 13. The waves ordered
  candidates by kind of evidence, and the record's own interpretive theses
  came last, in wave 5. Of the 32 reviewed there, 22 were retained. NOTES
  records the reason: "[ADR-047](ADR-047.md) gives arguments no audit route, and
  interpretive promotions go to the owner."
- **The owner questions were never asked.** All five decided promotions
  that are still undone ([CLAIM-322](../claims.d/CLAIM-322.md), [CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md), [CLAIM-046](../claims.d/CLAIM-046.md), [CLAIM-241](../claims.d/CLAIM-241.md), and
  [THEORY-073](../theory.d/THEORY-073.md)'s narrowing) wait on an owner ruling. About forty such
  rulings sit in one string, the queue's `handoff.next_step`, and no pull
  request put them to the owner.
- **Relations were out of scope.** On the same tree, 49 edges have an Active
  CLAIM resting on or grounded in a Proposed entry. Four claims use
  Superseded TERMs. 37 Open QUESTIONs list an Active answer. The audit did
  not look for any of these.
- **Separating review from promotion was costly.** The queue's history
  cites 114 pull requests, numbered [#165](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/165) to [#308](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/308), over three days.

## Decision

**The worklist is derived and kept nowhere.**
`record/status-review/worklist.py` reads the frontmatter on a freshly
fetched main and prints five sections:

- **Load-bearing Proposed entries**: Proposed CLAIMs, THEORYs and TERMs,
  ranked by their transitive dependents along `rests_on`, `grounds` and
  `uses` (for CLAIMs and ARGs) and `presupposes` (for THEORYs). Each
  entry shows its premises the record does not hold and its live
  objections.
- **Credence inversions**: Active CLAIMs whose `rests_on` or `grounds`
  names something not Active.
- **Retired premises**: live entries that need something Superseded or
  Rejected, with the live successor reached along `superseded_by`.
- **Open questions with an Active answer.**
- **Unblocked Proposed CLAIMs**: all premises held and no live objection.

Nothing is persisted between passes except the record itself, so record
growth is absorbed by rerunning the script. Work in progress is the open
pull request.

**A pass reviews clusters, starting from the most relied-on.** A cluster
is a load-bearing entry, its premises the record does not hold, and its
live objections. It is reviewed from the bottom up, because a claim cannot
go Active over a Proposed premise. Each pass also takes whatever the
other sections list for the entries it touches. A pass is sized to what a
reviewer can read in one sitting: about five clusters.

**Every claim has a route, interpretive ones included.** An interpretive
thesis is proposed for Active when four things hold:

1. Every premise is Active.
2. Each live objection is answered by an Active counter, or the thesis is
   narrowed to concede it.
3. Nothing in the record meets its `defeated_if`.
4. The owner assents.

The pull request is where the owner assents. It lists each interpretive
status change separately, and merging it is the ruling. Demonstrated and
empirical entries follow [ADR-047](ADR-047.md)'s evidence rulings, which stand
unchanged:

- a checked step-by-step re-derivation meets a request for a proof;
- one study read in full suffices for a title that claims no more than it
  measured;
- a narrowing's dropped claims go to Proposed entries.

**Relations are in scope.** A pass decides each item it takes from the
other sections:

- **An inversion** is resolved by demoting the conclusion, promoting the
  premise, or changing the edge. A demonstrated conclusion that only
  borrows a framing from its premise is conditional, and it says so.
- **A retired premise** moves to its successor, or the entry says why the
  old sense is meant.
- **An Open question with an Active answer** becomes `Answered`, or gets a
  note saying what part is still open.

While reading a cluster, the reviewer also turns body citations that do the
work of a premise or an objection into the matching field.

**Reasoning lives on the entries.** A status change carries a dated
history note on the entry. An entry kept Proposed gets the blocker that
holds it, stated as a missing result or an unanswered objection and not
as "needs more evidence", in a dated note in its body. The pass as a whole,
including what it looked at and left alone, is one workbench entry. There
are no per-code decision files.

**Owner rulings are a checklist in the pull request.** A ruling the pass
needs goes in the pull request description, one checkbox each, with the
proposed answer already made in the diff where one exists. A ruling still
open when the pull request merges is filed as a QUESTION. It is not left in
prose.

## Forcing functions: making the backlog visible to the lint

The owner asked, the same day, for rules in the luria configuration that
would stop this backlog from forming again. The backlog was possible
because of a blind spot, and the lint could have exposed it. The blind
spot has three parts, each measured on the same tree:

- **The age signal could not fire.** The record's first commit is dated
  2026-09-25, and `luria.stale_days` is unset, so it defaults to 90. No
  document in a record 32 days old can be 90 days old, so the
  pending-documents headline has never named one as stale. The report
  ranks 1,075 undecided documents by age and by raw citation count. It
  does not rank them by how much rests on them. This is DP-15's
  absence reading as success.
- **Nothing measures reliance.** "A lot depends on it" is a transitive
  count along `rests_on`, `grounds`, `uses` and `presupposes`. No report or
  warning class computes it, so the claims the owner asked about were
  invisible to every tool.
- **Nothing checks credence across an edge.** Reference fields check that
  a target is present, resolves and is in force. "In force" counts
  Proposed as in force. So an Active claim resting on a Proposed one (49
  edges), or an Open question with an Active answer (37), is no finding
  at all.

Proposed mechanisms, in the order they can be adopted:

1. **`luria.stale_days: 14`.** This is configuration only, under luria
   1.1.0. The headline starts counting stale documents at the record's
   own pace, 611 commits in the week before this decision. It is a
   report, and it gates nothing.
2. **A field that says what holds a Proposed CLAIM back.** It would be
   `held_back_by`, prose, `required_when: {status: [Proposed]}`, and it
   is configuration only. A THEORY already has `promote_when`. A CLAIM
   has `defeated_if`, which says what would sink it, but nothing says
   what would float it. The field moves the blocker from a ledger onto
   the entry, where the lint can ask for it. Adopting it means backfilling
   278 Proposed CLAIMs, or rolling it out under a baseline.
3. **Pattern-backed warning classes (a luria change).** `patterns` are
   already written in Cypher and checked against the schemes. Today they
   feed only chains and views. A pattern declared with `report: true`
   would make each match a row of its own warning class. That class is
   then acknowledgeable, mutable, and gateable by `fail_on` and
   `baseline` like any other. The three credence checks become
   configuration:

   ```yaml
   patterns:
     inverted_premise:
       match: (c:CLAIM {status: 'Active'})-[:rests_on]->(p:CLAIM {status: 'Proposed'})
       report: true
     inverted_ground:
       match: (c:CLAIM {status: 'Active'})-[:grounds]->(p:THEORY {status: 'Proposed'})
       report: true
     answered_but_open:
       match: (q:QUESTION {status: 'Open'})<-[:answers]-(c:CLAIM {status: 'Active'})
       report: true
   ```

   This is the general mechanism: a blind spot found later becomes a
   pattern and a class, with no luria release. Under `baseline`, the
   count may fall and may not rise.
4. **Reliance in the pending report (a luria change).** A
   `luria.reliance` list names the fields that carry dependence. The
   pending-documents table gains a column of transitive dependents and
   sorts by it. A `load-bearing` warning class names any undecided
   document with at least `luria.load_bearing` dependents (for example
   5). `worklist.py`'s first section is the prototype, and it would retire
   once luria computes this.

5. **An acknowledged retired premise expires.** The four claims that
   use Superseded TERMs ([CLAIM-004](../claims.d/CLAIM-004.md), [CLAIM-042](../claims.d/CLAIM-042.md), [CLAIM-061](../claims.d/CLAIM-061.md) and [CLAIM-064](../claims.d/CLAIM-064.md))
   are not unseen. Each carries an `inactive-ok-file` directive, "the
   definition current when this claim was written". That is true, and it
   silenced the one finding that asked whether the claim still holds in
   the successor's sense. luria's directives already take `until <date>`,
   and the `expired-directives` class reports one that has lapsed. The rule
   is that a directive excusing a retired `rests_on`, `grounds` or `uses`
   target carries an `until`. As a convention this needs no change to
   luria. As a check it would be a luria change: a directive class that
   requires `until`.

**What was adopted.** On 2026-10-27 the owner said "go ahead with all
the things, use your best judgement":

- **Mechanism 1 is adopted** with this decision.
- **Mechanism 2 is declined.** Most of what holds a Proposed claim back is
  already in its graph: a premise not held, or a live objection.
  Mechanism 3 reports those from the source. A field that restated them
  would be a hand-kept projection of the graph and would drift (DP-3).
  This is the failure the old ledger's decision files showed. The residue
  a graph cannot hold, such as a missing comparison or an unread source,
  goes in a dated note on the entry, as this decision's first pass did.
- **Mechanisms 3, 4 and 5 are luria changes.** They are opened in the
  luria repository and adopted here when a release carries them.
- **Mechanism 5 is also adopted now, as a convention.** An `inactive-ok`
  directive that excuses a retired target held in `rests_on`, `grounds`
  or `uses` carries `until <date>`, no more than 30 days out.

## Alternatives considered

- **Resume [ADR-047](ADR-047.md)'s queue.** It holds 308 decided candidates and no
  pending ones. A new triage under its rubric would re-rank a record a
  third larger by evidence class, and that order puts the claims the owner
  asked about last.
- **Keep the ledger and add a reliance wave.** This fixes the order but
  keeps the two costs that were measured: decision files that drift from
  the entries they judge, and a hand-maintained queue whose owner questions
  went unasked. [ADR-047](ADR-047.md) rejected a derived queue because it "cannot record
  what is in progress or what to do next". Under this decision the pull
  request records what is in progress, and the worklist, rerun, says what
  to do next.
- **Review in one pull request and promote in another.** [ADR-047](ADR-047.md) kept
  them apart so that a review could succeed with no status changed. That
  is still allowed: a pass may change nothing. But the separation cost
  about 140 pull requests over three days, and the five promotions that
  needed the owner were never put to the owner. A pull request that
  proposes the change and names the ruling puts both in front of the
  reviewer at once.
- **The curation journal for the pass narrative.** Its blurb is why
  things entered or left the record. The workbench's blurb is working
  sessions on the record's own arguments, which is what a status review
  of claims is.

## Consequences

- No ledger can drift from the record, because there is no ledger. The
  cost is that a pass leaves no machine-readable disposition, so the next
  pass sees a retained claim only as still Proposed. The dated note in the
  entry's body says why.
- ADR-047 is Superseded. The owner accepted this decision on
  2026-10-27.
  `record/promotion-audit/` stays as the history of the first audit. Its
  five undone promotions and its open owner rulings are carried into the
  first pass under this decision, as rulings.
- The worklist's sections are fired once on a fixture record by
  `record/status-review/test_worklist.py` ([DP-4](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-4)).
- Ranking by reliance favours entries that are cited, not entries that
  are important and new. A new thesis with no dependents yet does not
  surface here. The worklist is one input to choosing what to review, and
  it does not decide the choice.
