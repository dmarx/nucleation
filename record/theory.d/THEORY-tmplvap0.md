# THEORY-tmplvap0: An appraisal of an event's pertinence to the agent's concerns elicits the emotion, as a state of action readiness, and emotions differ by their mode of action readiness

## Source

Frijda, Ridderinkhof & Rietveld (2014), [LIT-540](../literature.d/LIT-540.md), read in full in [NOTE-433](../notes.d/NOTE-433.md):
"Action readiness elicitation", "States of action readiness", "The aims of
impulsive actions" and the Conclusion. The paper extends Frijda's The
Emotions ([LIT-546](../literature.d/LIT-546.md)), and says so: "action readiness and changes in action
readiness form the key to what we call 'emotions'". The book itself is
unread, and so is Lazarus's Emotion and Adaptation ([LIT-550](../literature.d/LIT-550.md)). Neither can
carry this account, and both are named so that a reader knows where the
fuller statements are.

## What was actually shown

The paper has no data of its own. Its claims, in the order it gives them:

- **Elicitation** (C2 in [NOTE-433](../notes.d/NOTE-433.md)). An event becomes emotional when it is
  appraised as pertinent to a concern, meaning a goal, need or value. This
  happens "in a fraction of a second", and largely "automatically and
  non-consciously". "In the absence of concern pertinence, there is no
  motive, no motive state, no change in action readiness; in short, no
  emotion." Inside the theory this is close to a definition.
- **What is elicited** (C1). A state of action readiness: "not
  representations of desirable states, but actual preparations for
  action". It has control precedence, meaning persistence, resumption
  after interruption, and priority over other actions.
- **Emotions differ by mode.** Hostility corresponds to anger, avoidance
  to fear, submission to awe, and disinclination to act to apathy. The
  mapping is given by examples, and the record holds no defence of it
  against variation between instances (Limitations in [NOTE-433](../notes.d/NOTE-433.md)).
- **Cited evidence.** Rated control precedence tracks rated intensity
  (Sonnemans & Frijda 1994; C4 in [NOTE-433](../notes.d/NOTE-433.md)). Observers rate facial
  expressions for action tendencies about as consistently as they do for
  emotion labels (Tcherkassof et al. 2007). The record has read neither
  study.

Gross's review ([LIT-554](../literature.d/LIT-554.md), [NOTE-429](../notes.d/NOTE-429.md)) shows that this order of explanation,
evaluated situation then response tendency, was the field's consensus model
in 1998. His Figure 1 is "a distillation of major points of convergence"
across Lazarus, Frijda and others. That shows how widely the account was
held. It is not evidence that the account is true.

## The rivalry, stated exactly

Barrett ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md)) names causal appraisal theories "highly doubtful",
because "meaning does not trigger action, but results from it". The rival
is [THEORY-tmpx3ps2](THEORY-tmpx3ps2.md). Neither paper cites the other, so the record holds the
relation as `rivals` ([ADR-022](../decisions.d/ADR-022.md)). They are closer than the labels suggest:

- **Both are predictive.** Frijda: "A motive itself can be conceived of as
  being set to expect a particular input, along the lines proposed in
  predictive coding theory". Both have actions anticipated through their
  exteroceptive and interoceptive sensory consequences.
- **Both let instances vary, and both group them by function.** "When
  angry one can kick, shout insults, turn one's back." Frijda's mode is
  what such equifunctional actions share.
- **They disagree on two points.** The first is order: in Frijda an
  appraisal of the event generates the readiness, while in Barrett the
  prediction, including its action plan, comes first and meaning is its
  result. The second is where the unity lies: for Frijda in the agent's
  readiness to change its relation to the object, and for Barrett in the
  perceiver's goal-based concept. The second point is the one a result
  could decide, which is why `promote_when` asks for it.

Whether the first point is fully empirical is open. Frijda's appraisal
already covers "possibilities for action that the event affords", so his
appraisal and Barrett's
action-first prediction may partly redescribe one process.

## The agency holdings

The paper's second thesis is that impulsive action is purposive with "no
explicit goal representation" (C3). The appraised object stays "before
one's eyes or before one's mind's eye" and guides the action, as an
intention in action (Searle 1983, Pacherie). What makes the action
purposive is its "pragmatic anticipation", meaning its predicted sensory
effects. This puts the account between two positions the record holds.
Brooks ([LIT-436](../literature.d/LIT-436.md), [NOTE-351](../notes.d/NOTE-351.md)) builds robots with no goals at all, whose
coherence is "in the eye of an observer". Frijda keeps a real motive in
the agent, carried by anticipation and not by a represented end state.
He also says that "assuming an action to have a motive is an inference
from observing a variety of equifunctional actions". That is Dennett's
intentional strategy ([LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md), [NOTE-350](../notes.d/NOTE-350.md)) applied to emotion, with the
difference that Frijda takes the motive inferred to be a real state.
Barrett calls the same inference, from action to emotion, the "mental
inference fallacy". On that point the two rivals disagree about method as
well as substance.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say that appraisal is deliberate or conscious.** Frijda's
  appraisal is fast and mostly non-conscious. The rival's charge is about
  causal order, not about deliberation.
- **It does not say that emotions have a neural signature.** The
  account's candidate for what instances share is functional, a mode of
  readiness, so Barrett's Table 1 evidence against neural essences does
  not reach it directly.
- **It does not cover feeling.** The paper leaves out emotional experience
  (Limitations in [NOTE-433](../notes.d/NOTE-433.md)). The felt layer is [THEORY-tmp2gk4g](THEORY-tmp2gk4g.md), which is
  compatible with this account.
- **It does not include the paper's claim about regulation**, which is
  that much regulation is one state of readiness checking another. That
  claim is [THEORY-tmppzrsd](THEORY-tmppzrsd.md), and the constructionist side holds it too, so
  it does not depend on this account winning.
- **Lazarus's version is not held.** Concern pertinence here is Frijda's
  term. Whether Lazarus's relational themes are the same account is not
  something the record can say until [LIT-550](../literature.d/LIT-550.md) is read.
