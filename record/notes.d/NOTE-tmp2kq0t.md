# NOTE-tmp2kq0t: Tomasello — The role of roles in uniquely human cognition and sociality

## Contribution

It brings the shared-intentionality programme to the sociologists' concept
of social role, and uses one organizing idea, the dual-level structure of
shared goal and individual roles, to unify perspectival and objective
cognition, relational concepts, fairness and moral identity. A synthesis
with no new data.

## Key results

1. **Roles need cooperation.** "the notion of social role only arises
   in the context of cooperative endeavors" (§2, p. 4); chimpanzee group
   hunting gets "a “lean” reading" (§2.1, p. 5): no joint goal, so no
   roles.
2. **Children and chimpanzees.** "children are able to form a joint
   agency toward a joint goal in ways that chimpanzees are not" (§2.2, p.
   7).
3. **Perspectives.** "apes and very young human infants only imagine or
   track the epistemic states of others; they do not understand different
   perspectives on a common situation" (§3.2, p. 12).
4. **Fairness.** "Self-other equivalence is the cognitive basis for a
   sense of fairness" (§4.1, p. 13); role ideals are "the first, if still
   delimited, socially shared normative standards" (p. 13); collaboration
   takes "a normative turn" at around three (p. 13).
5. **Joint commitment.** "each of us agrees to join the other in
   judging whichever of us does not perform her role in the ideal way as
   normatively defective" (§4.1, p. 14).
6. **Strategy and morality.** "Children's strategic impression
   management is not moral; it is strategic." (§4.2, p. 15).
7. **The admitted gap.** "I have provided no evidence here that playing
   local roles in spontaneous collaboration with" others is a prerequisite
   for societal roles (§5, p. 16).

## Assumptions

Roles exist for a participant only if represented; Bratman, Gilbert and
Searle describe the psychology; ape–child task contrasts reveal species
differences; the two-step evolutionary story is called speculative (p. 5).

## Concepts

Dual-level structure; joint and collective intentionality; joint
commitment; role ideals; self-other equivalence; role-governed categories;
triangulation; objective perspective; moral identity; generalized other
(Mead).

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)** (Tomasello 2020, obligation), the same account in
  short: role ideals, self-other equivalence, joint commitment as
  collaborative self-regulation and guilt, with the same experiments, plus
  the cognitive half (perspectives, objectivity, thematic concepts).
- **[TERM-080](../terms.d/TERM-080.md)**, the joint and collective split maps onto the
  participatory and institutional senses of collective intention.
- **[THEORY-414](../theory.d/THEORY-414.md)** and **[LIT-473](../literature.d/LIT-473.md)**, a contrast: roles and institutions
  need shared intentionality and normativity, not only equilibrium
  expectations; coordination without a joint goal is the lean chimpanzee
  case.
- **[THEORY-1605](../theory.d/THEORY-1605.md)** (group identification) and **[THEORY-457](../theory.d/THEORY-457.md)** (partner
  choice and division), parallels.
- **[LIT-tmpxajf5](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxajf5.md)**, a stand-in for its thesis (§4.1–4.2, pp. 13–15), not
  for its transactional causal model.

## Limitations

A review by the theory's author, almost all evidence from his lab; the
prerequisite claim is unsupported by his own account; the cognitive claims
are argued, not tested; captive chimpanzees; no cultural variation.
