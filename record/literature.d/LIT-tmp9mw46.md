# LIT-tmp9mw46: Participatory sense-making: An enactive approach to social cognition

Hanne De Jaegher and Ezequiel Di Paolo (2007), *Phenomenology and the
Cognitive Sciences* 6(4):485–507.

The brief's citation (Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences 6, 2007) is
correct.

## Key takeaways

- **The definition.** "Social interaction is the regulated coupling between
  at least two autonomous agents, where the regulation is aimed at aspects
  of the coupling itself so that it constitutes an emergent autonomous
  organization in the domain of relational dynamics, without destroying in
  the process the autonomy of the agents involved."
- **The interaction can outlast the wishes of its members.** Two people
  mirroring each other's sidesteps in a corridor are held in the encounter by
  its own dynamics until some other channel breaks the symmetry.
- **Participatory sense-making.** "The coordination of intentional activity
  in interaction, whereby individual sense-making processes are affected and
  new domains of social sense-making can be generated that were not
  available to each individual on her own", on a spectrum from orientation
  (pointing out) to joint construction of meaning (charades).
- **Against the "Rear Window" picture.** Social understanding is not, in the
  first instance, one detached individual working out another's mind;
  mirror-neuron explanations re-describe the problem at the neural level.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the enactive part of the
agency batch. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

It is tagged `cognition` first (it is an account of social cognition),
then `social-ontology`, because its central claim is about what a social
interaction *is*: an entity with its own autonomy, not reducible to the
behaviour of its members. That is a claim about a social thing, made from
cognitive science rather than from collective-intentionality theory. It is
tagged `agency` because the interactors must remain autonomous agents for
the interaction to count as social, and coercion that destroys an agent's
autonomy ends the social interaction. It is not tagged `self`: the closing
remark on co-modulation of self-in-interaction and other-in-interaction is
a few sentences.
