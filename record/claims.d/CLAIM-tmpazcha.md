# CLAIM-tmpazcha: Specification fidelity can coexist with drift

## The claim

A40 §10: "The historical specification of an organization and the
relationships acquired through its continuing realization are
distinguishable objects. Historical fidelity cannot independently establish
present organizational adequacy or the legitimate scope of continuing
authority."

## Its argument

- **Development under a fixed text** (A38 §1). With T₀ the original text,
  C_t the operative "contractual ecology", E_t the environment and a_t the
  participants' activity, C_{t+1} = Φ(C_t, T₀, E_t, a_t): "Even while T₀
  remains unchanged, the capacities, dependencies and relationships through
  which its provisions operate can change substantially."
- **Semantic fidelity with functional drift** (A40 §2.3). With R_t =
  Φ(T, E_t, O_t) the response profile, T_{t+1} = T_t does not give
  R_{t+1} = R_t. A40 lists six objects of fidelity (semantic, procedural,
  institutional, functional, relational, normative) and says "These
  invariants can come apart": a provision's wording and meaning can be kept
  while "a protection that previously depended upon surrounding
  circumstances" is lost.
- **Five objects** (A40 §1): text, meaning, law, organization and
  justification, "related but not interchangeable".
- **Intertemporal standing** (A40 §2.1). From (1) a provision meant m, (2)
  it helped establish arrangement O, (3) the present organization descends
  from O, it does not follow that (4) m "ought to govern the present
  organization in a particular way". (4) needs "an account of why the
  historical specification possesses continuing authority, what its
  jurisdiction encompasses, and under what conditions it can legitimately
  be supplemented or revised". A40: "causal ancestry, legal continuity and moral
  authority remain distinguishable."
- **The contract analogy** (A40 §2.2). Reconstructing two parties' original
  agreement does not characterize every obligation, capacity or dependency
  their cooperation later produced.

## Where the record and the literature hold it

- [TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md) separates formation from maintenance provenance, and [CASE-191](../cases.d/CASE-191.md)
  found that they come apart and that only the second can be read off a
  present organization. This claim adds that neither settles authority.
- [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) holds the parallel for viability: what keeps an arrangement
  going does not establish what ought to persist.
- The drift A40 §2.3 describes is the institutional-change literature's;
  A40 cites Mahoney and Thelen ([LIT-tmpdsowf](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdsowf.md)). The legal treatment of
  practice acquired after ratification, historical gloss and liquidation, is
  Bradley and Siegel's ([LIT-tmp4h6yz](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4h6yz.md)), cited at A38 §1. A40's contribution,
  by its own account, is to connect drift to "contractual dependence, the
  cost of correction and organizational character".

## What it does not say

That historical meaning lacks authority, or that fixation is false. A38 §1:
"Our framework need not reject fixation." The claim is a non-entailment,
and sophisticated originalist theory concedes it (A40 §2 reports Solum's
separation of semantic, legal and normative claims). That concession
matters for the critique built on it, [CLAIM-tmpr2qzg](CLAIM-tmpr2qzg.md), and its objection,
[CLAIM-tmpmp2wh](CLAIM-tmpmp2wh.md).
