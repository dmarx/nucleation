# LIT-tmp0l030: Dirty Hands Defended

Linda Eggert (2023; issue 2025). Dirty Hands Defended. *Journal of Moral Philosophy* 22: 1–20. DOI 10.1163/17455243-20234097. Open access, CC BY 4.0.

## Key takeaways

- An act can be permissible and still wrong (p. 14): "a wrong action, such
  as the transgression of an actual, intact right, may be permissible
  under certain circumstances, without this rendering it any less wrong."
- The remainder grounds remorse and repair (p. 15), and dirty hands are
  "not unique to political activity" (p. 11).
- "Duties conflict as a result of things that happen, including other
  people's actions" (p. 17); public policy should work to reduce such
  situations (p. 18).
- That paradigm cases involve someone else creating the predicament is an
  aside (p. 18), and its generality is deferred.

**The exchange's characterization: partly faithful.** A9's "She notes
that such predicaments sometimes arise precisely because one person has
wrongfully created circumstances in which another must violate a moral
duty to avoid something worse" is accurate as a report of a two-sentence
aside (p. 18). She does not develop it, defers whether dirty hands always
arise this way, and cites de Wijze for the stronger view without adopting
it. Nothing in the paper says that creating such a predicament wrongs the
person placed in it, so it does not support [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md)'s second-order
wrong; it gives only the agent's side, a remainder beside the arranger's
part. It leaves [CLAIM-703](../claims.d/CLAIM-703.md) open.
