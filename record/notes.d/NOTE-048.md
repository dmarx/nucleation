# NOTE-048: Law Dork on DHS v. D.V.D. (South Sudan removals order)

## Contribution

It is same-day legal journalism with an explicit viewpoint. It reports the Supreme Court's grant of the Government's "motion for clarification" in the third-country-removal class action. It quotes both separate writings, and it sets the order beside two other events of 3 July 2025: the budget bill's passage, and DOJ's FOIA release of Attorney General Bondi's TikTok non-enforcement letters. It reads the three together as a sign that "constitutional government ... is no longer in effect".

## Key insight

The post's reading is that the order extends the Court's emergency-docket interventions by stripping a district court of its remedy for violations of an injunction the Court later stayed. The post treats this as enlarging executive power and shrinking district courts' ability to enforce their own orders. The order's own reading is narrower. A stayed injunction cannot be enforced by a remedial order whose purpose is to coerce compliance with it.

## Assumptions

These are the authorities each side relies on.
- **Majority (per curiam).** Nken v. Holder, 556 U. S. 418, 428 (2009). United States v. Mine Workers, 330 U. S. 258, 295, 303 (1947). General Atomic Co. v. Felter, 436 U. S. 493, 497 (1978). United States v. Fossatt (1859). Deen v. Hickman (1958). In re Sanford Fork & Tool Co. (1895).
- **Kagan, concurring.** Mine Workers, 330 U. S., at 294–295; Worden v. Searls, 121 U. S. 14 (1887).
- **Sotomayor, dissenting.** The Fifth Amendment. Trump v. J. G. G. Supreme Court Rules 20(1) and 23(3). A. A. R. P. v. Trump (Alito, J., dissenting), used against the majority. Lackey v. Stinnie. Mine Workers' "central message" that injunctions "must be obeyed ... however erroneous".
- **The post's premises.** South Sudan is a country the administration "advises Americans not to travel" to (asserted, not sourced). The men were "sent out of the U.S. in violation of an injunction" (consistent with the per curiam's account of the District Court's finding).

## Key results

The post against the order, point by point.
- **Holding.** Post: the district court "could not enforce its remedy order ... because the Supreme Court later stayed the underlying injunction". Order: "The May 21 remedial order cannot now be used to enforce an injunction that our stay rendered unenforceable." **Accurate.**
- **Vote.** Post: "an apparent 7-2 vote". Order: unsigned; Kagan "vote[s] to grant"; Sotomayor and Jackson dissent. **Accurate, with the right hedge.**
- **Number of men.** Post: eight. Per curiam: the violation finding concerned "six class members". Dissent: eight. **The post follows the dissent without saying so.**
- **What the remedial order required.** Post: "that due process be provided". Per curiam: "specified procedures ... tailored to the circumstances". Dissent: "adequate notice and an opportunity to be heard". **A fair paraphrase.**
- **Effect.** Post headline: "send eight men to South Sudan with no process"; body: "Permission had been granted"; "seven justices are allowing eight people to be sent to South Sudan". Order: it decides only the enforceability of the remedial order. It "ha[s] no occasion to reach the Government's other requests for relief", and it says nothing about what process the men receive or where they go. **This is the post's interpretation of the order's practical effect.** It is consistent with the dissent's description of what the Government "wants to do". But lumping Kagan into "seven justices are allowing" drops her stated reason: she still thinks the stay was wrong and votes only because "a district court [cannot] compel compliance with an order that this Court has stayed".
- **Quotations.** All four are verbatim against the order: Sotomayor on torture or death; "ignores the Court's Rules ... creates new law on civil contempt"; Kagan's concurrence, with an accurate ellipsis; "Other litigants must follow the rules, but the administration has the Supreme Court on speed dial." **Accurate.** "Indefensible" in the dissent is aimed specifically at "the Court's continued refusal to justify its extraordinary decisions in this case, even as it faults lower courts". The post's "their colleagues' actions" is a fair gloss.
- **Kagan's earlier position.** Post: Kagan "had joined Sotomayor's June 23 dissent". Order: Kagan says she "voted to deny the Government's previous stay application" and cites that dissent. **Consistent**; that she joined it is not stated in this order.
- **Djibouti.** Post: "held in Djibouti at a U.S. military base". Order: the dissent says the Government is "currently holding [them] in Djibouti". "Military base" is not in the order.
- **Omissions.** The majority's reasoning and its route to mandamus. The dissent's Rule 23 and Rule 20 arguments beyond the one-line quote. The dissent's point that the remedial order was never appealed. Footnote 1 on criminal contempt. The dissent's charge that the majority rests on "a single line of dictum" from 1947 on "a matter of first impression".

## Concepts

- **third-country removal**: removal to "a country other than a person's country of origin or another country where they have legal status" (the post's definition). The injunction's wording was a "country not explicitly provided for on the alien's order of removal" (per curiam).
- **motion for clarification**: the Government's vehicle here. The dissent argues it was in substance a new request for relief that should have gone first to the lower courts (Rule 23(3)).
- **remedial / civil-contempt order**: the May 21 order. The majority treats it as coercive civil-contempt relief that "falls with" a stayed injunction. The dissent notes that criminal contempt would survive.

## Connections

It is one post in Law Dork's running coverage of the D.V.D. litigation. It links the June 23 stay post, the Djibouti post and the CASA post. It relies on a DocumentCloud copy of the Bondi letters released in response to a FOIA request by Tony Tan. The primary authorities are those listed under Assumptions.

## Bearing on the record

It carries nothing for ML practice and no instruction for the Anthology of the SOTA. If the record keeps legal primary sources on emergency-docket practice, the order should be filed and this post cited, if at all, as contemporaneous commentary. Any document that quotes the post's "with no process" should instead cite what the order decides.

## Limitations

- It is commentary written the same day, framed by a strong viewpoint ("alarming", "absurd or horrifying"). The majority's reasoning is absent, so a reader of the post alone cannot judge the dissent's criticisms.
- It uses the dissent's count of the men (eight) and does not note the per curiam's "six".
- The "Thursday, in context" section joins three unrelated events by date. Its TikTok claims rest on letters I did not read.
- The post does not source the travel-advisory claim.

## Open questions

- Why do the per curiam ("six class members") and the dissent ("eight") differ? Possibly the violation finding and the remedial order covered different subsets of the men on the flight. The district-court docket (ECF 118, 119) would settle it.
- Does the rule the majority applied, that a coercive civil-contempt remedy falls with a stayed injunction, stand as precedent, given that the dissent calls it a question "of first impression" answered by dictum? Later citation of the order would show.

## Corrections to the seeded skim

- The dossier leaves the reporter citation as "unverified". The order's own caption reads "Cite as: 606 U. S. ____ (2025)", so the volume is confirmed and the page is not yet assigned.
- The dossier (following the post) describes a remedial order "requiring process for eight men". The per curiam says the District Court found the Government violated the injunction by "failing to provide six class members a 'meaningful opportunity' to assert CAT claims", and that the May 21 order concerned "those individuals". The dissent says "eight class members" and "eight noncitizens". The post and the dossier both use the dissent's figure without noting the difference. The order itself does not reconcile the two numbers.
- The dossier's "apparent 7–2 vote" is right to keep "apparent". The order is unsigned and records no vote. Kagan's concurrence says "I vote to grant", and only Sotomayor and Jackson are recorded as dissenting. The alignment of the other six is inferred, not stated.
- The dossier's summary says the Court "held" the order unenforceable "because the Court had already stayed the underlying injunction". That is correct. But the dossier, like the post, omits the grounds: Nken v. Holder (a stay "divest[s]" an order "of enforceability"); United States v. Mine Workers (a civil-contempt remedy that serves to "coerce" compliance falls with the injunction); General Atomic v. Felter (such a claim is "properly addressed here"); and the statement that further relief may be sought "through mandamus".
- Missing from the dossier: the dissent says the Government "did not appeal" the remedial order, that the Government skipped the lower courts contrary to Rule 23(3), and (footnote 1) that the majority does not dispute that criminal contempt survives an order being set aside. The post omits all three, and they are the dissent's main legal points.
