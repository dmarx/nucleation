# ARG-tmpml5si: Contested open-source successions, in which participants who judged a new steward illegitimate moved their work to another successor, are evidence that the participants' verdicts help make the succession

## The inference

1. [CASE-tmpcr92d](../cases.d/CASE-tmpcr92d.md) proposes comparing successions the community contested
   with ones it accepted. Its measures include community identification,
   behavioural migration and "the delay between a loss of recognized
   legitimacy and a loss of productive capability".
2. A's candidate instance, from A36: in May 2021 "an ownership dispute
   and change in operational control triggered the resignation of most
   Freenode volunteer staff. They established Libera Chat, and many
   communities followed." A: "Much of the operational, social, and
   normative continuity followed Libera."
3. A36 §5: "A contributor who migrates to a fork changes the productive
   capacity of both branches." So "**community judgments about identity
   can be causally productive of the organizational differences they
   describe**".
4. So participants' verdicts on which successor continues a project help
   make it so ([CLAIM-tmpjw473](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjw473.md)).

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** Yes, as
  the evidence stands. The instances are A's reports, unverified here, and
  [CASE-tmpcr92d](../cases.d/CASE-tmpcr92d.md) lists what has to be checked before the case can be
  documented. Granting them, the Freenode move is expected if verdicts did
  no causal work. The staff could have left over working conditions, over
  control of the infrastructure they ran, or because their colleagues did.
  For Redis and Valkey, A itself notes that "some major Valkey
  participants had clear commercial reasons to oppose the Redis licensing
  decision".
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** No, not yet, and
  this is the question that decides the verdict. The rival is [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s
  criterion on its own: a collective persists by causal transmission of
  the organization that produces it. On that criterion the Freenode case
  picks Libera because the volunteer staff, the producing organization,
  moved. [CASE-tmpcr92d](../cases.d/CASE-tmpcr92d.md) says so: "under [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s criterion, causal
  transmission of the producing organization, the Freenode case picks
  Libera, because the volunteer staff moved". Nothing in that verdict needs
  the staff's judgement of legitimacy to have caused the move. In this
  instance the two cannot be separated, because the verdict and the move
  were one act by the same people: the staff's resignation was both their
  judgement and their departure. [CLAIM-tmpjw473](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjw473.md)'s own defeat condition
  names the rival as a restatement risk too: [CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md) together with
  [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md), "with nothing added about succession". What would tell them
  apart is [CASE-tmpcr92d](../cases.d/CASE-tmpcr92d.md)'s design, not its instances. Verdicts expressed
  by people other than the movers, or before the move, that predict who
  moves beyond what cost and commercial incentive predict, would support
  the claim. Statements that follow the move and add nothing to a cost
  model would defeat it.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** Not migration. A36 §7:
  "contributor migration is insufficient on its own. We would need
  contemporaneous statements or surveys explicitly addressing legitimacy,
  continuity, or betrayal, and should distinguish those judgments from
  cost-driven migration." The design's identification measure
  (contemporary statements, discussions, surveys) is the right kind, on
  [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)'s discipline that the verdict be measured by evidence that
  does not pass through the migration it is meant to explain. Its timing
  is what matters. A statement made after a move may be the move's
  rationale, not its cause.

Proposed. The instances, unverified, do not discriminate the claim from
[CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s criterion alone. The proposed design could, and the verdict
waits on its being run. If it is not run, this argument should be
Rejected as far as the instances go.

## What it does not show

- That the verdict alone decides a succession, which the claim does not
  say either.
- Which successor, if any, is the project persisting. That stays
  [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md)'s question.
