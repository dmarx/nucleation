# THEORY-2214: Beneficence is an enhancer, not a need

## The account

Martela and Ryan (2019) find that frustration of beneficence, measured as
feeling one has harmed others, correlates with ill-being but adds no
variance to it once autonomy, competence and relatedness frustration are
controlled. On that ground they tentatively propose that beneficence is a
basic wellness enhancer, whose satisfaction raises well-being but whose
frustration has no unique cost, rather than a basic psychological need.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-2304](../notes.d/NOTE-2304.md); its key results are quoted there with
locations. The null depends on controlling competence frustration, which
correlates .81 with beneficence frustration (footnote 2, p. 124), and on
entering the three needs first. The authors' 2021 experiments
([THEORY-2213](THEORY-2213.md)) reach the same null for ill-being without that control.

## Bearing

On [QUESTION-369](../questions.d/QUESTION-369.md), as the record's worked case of a candidate need tested by
the frustration criterion: evidence that the criterion can exclude a
candidate, and an instance of the question's circularity, since the test
runs on the same self-reported well-being.
