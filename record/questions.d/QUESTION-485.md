# QUESTION-485: From where does a relationship, a capability or a restriction derive its legitimacy, and from where its adequacy?

## Why it is a question

The owner's U31, after A68's account of how resistance to correction can
be organized: "from whence does a relationship, capability, or
restriction derive legitimacy and/or adequacy?"

The record's argument on dependence and domination has been treating some
interventions, obligations and restrictions as justified, and asking how
an arrangement makes them effective. It has not said what makes them
justified. A69: "we’ve been treating some rights of intervention,
obligations, and restrictions as *justified*, but we haven’t yet said
what makes them so." Its nearest entries mark the gap from the other side:

- **[CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)** (Active): viability does not establish what ought to
  persist. A71 §1 states it ("A system's success at reproducing itself
  cannot serve as sufficient evidence of its right to reproduce itself")
  and attributes it to [QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md), which cites [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md).
- **[CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)** (Active): a normative judgment is well-defined once its
  relata are fixed. A70 lists them as "relationship, perspective, context,
  and standard"; the claim's are the organization or standpoint, the
  standard, the scale and the context.
- **[CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md)** (Active): adequacy is fixed by the standard and not shown
  by stability or performance.
- **[QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md)** (Open) asks whether relationally indexed judgments can
  be objective about the standards themselves. This question is narrower
  in object (authority over relations, capabilities and restrictions) and
  wider in asking for adequacy as well.
- **[QUESTION-454](QUESTION-454.md)** (Open): what makes authority to decide for one with
  standing who cannot decide legitimate. One case of this question.
- **[QUESTION-464](QUESTION-464.md)** (Open): which procedure fixes the purposes an
  arrangement's members would endorse. A procedure of that kind is one
  candidate source.

## A's survey (A71), carried as A's

**Three things to keep apart.** "**Legitimacy concerns the justification
of authority; adequacy concerns the satisfaction of standards; efficacy
concerns causal accomplishment. None can be inferred from either of the
others without additional premises.**" The pairing of legitimacy with
adequacy is the owner's; efficacy is A's third.

**Five candidate sources of legitimacy** (A71 §2), of which A says "I
wouldn't collapse them into one principle yet":

- **A. Consent and self-authorization.** "**Consent can confer authority
  only where the consenting party possesses the relevant authority to
  confer, and where the conditions of consent preserve its legitimating
  significance.**" [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md) is A's example.
- **B. Justifiability to those affected**, after Scanlon's contractualism:
  principles that those subject to them could not reasonably reject.
  Applied to the litigation case ([CLAIM-748](../claims.d/CLAIM-748.md)), a rule permitting
  litigants to impose unnecessary costs when profitable is rejectable; a
  rule permitting burdensome but necessary evidence is not.
- **C. Equal moral standing and non-domination**, after relational
  egalitarianism and republicanism ([TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)). A warns that non-domination
  cannot be defined as freedom from illegitimate power without
  circularity.
- **D. Protection of independently valuable interests**, after the
  capability approach. "The difficulty is that identifying a valuable
  outcome doesn't automatically establish who has authority to impose
  it", which is [CLAIM-680](../claims.d/CLAIM-680.md).
- **E. Role-based responsibility and vulnerability.** "**Creating a
  dependency can generate or strengthen responsibilities without granting
  legitimate authority over the dependent.**" (the note of 2026-11-17 on
  [CLAIM-671](../claims.d/CLAIM-671.md)).

**Three grounds for one claim** (A71 §3): "**The claim's ground:** Why is
B owed something?", "**The authority's ground:** Who is entitled to
determine whether A has fulfilled the obligation?" and "**The
enforcement's ground:** Who may compel compliance, and under what
conditions?" Formally, Permitted_N(a, x) against Authorized_N(a, b, x):
"Neither necessarily entails the other." For a restriction this is
[CLAIM-737](../claims.d/CLAIM-737.md).

**Adequacy** (A71 §4). For the corrective requirements D_N^corr of a
standard N, an arrangement G is adequate when every j in D_N^corr has
α_j(G; s, K, H) ≥ 1 − ε_j. That is [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)'s correctability set at a
declared reliability, which the record holds. It does not establish that
the requirements are justified, as A says, and adequacy now does not imply
adequacy later.

## What would count as an answer

- **A source, or an ordered set of them**, that says for a relationship,
  a capability and a restriction what grounds authority, with its scope:
  which of A's five apply to which objects, and what decides between them
  when they conflict. A71 §7's schema (justified, authorized, permissible
  and answerable as jointly sufficient within a declared framework) is a
  form for an answer, not one.
- **A separation of the two halves**, if legitimacy and adequacy turn out
  to need different kinds of answer. A71 §12 proposes that: a theory of
  standing and authority from moral and political philosophy, and a
  theory of organizational adequacy where the record's formal apparatus
  already works. F4's [CLAIM-746](../claims.d/CLAIM-746.md) (four separable judgments about an
  authority) and [QUESTION-482](QUESTION-482.md) (what makes the allocation of authority
  to evaluate an actor legitimate) are on the first half.
- **A use of the owner's standing criterion.** A71 does not test its
  sources against the kinds of standing A59 §2 separated for the owner's
  U27 (consent, intervention, consultation; [CLAIM-723](../claims.d/CLAIM-723.md)), though
  "Authorized" needs exactly that.
- **A reduction**, showing that legitimacy is adequacy to some standard,
  would dissolve the pairing. A71 denies it: "none establishes that moral
  justification is reducible to realizability."
