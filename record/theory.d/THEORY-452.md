# THEORY-452: A thing with many equally good candidate boundaries can be counted as one in two ways that cover each other's failures: supervaluation makes 'there is one cat' true on every precisification though no candidate is definitely the cat, and counting by almost-identity, meaning extensive mereological overlap, makes it true enough where the candidates overlap almost completely

## Source

David Lewis (1993), "Many, but almost one", [LIT-1015](../literature.d/LIT-1015.md), in *Ontology,
Causality and Mind*; read in full in [NOTE-722](../notes.d/NOTE-722.md) from the 1999 reprint
(*Papers in Metaphysics and Epistemology*, pp. 164–182), with all ten
footnotes.

## What was actually shown

**The problem.** "Many surfaces are equally good candidates to be the
boundary of the cloud." Either all candidates count, and there are many
clouds, or none does, and there are none: "We have many things or we have
none, but anyway not the one thing we thought we had." Geach's 1001 cats are
the same problem once the hairs are questionable rather than plucked.

**Two rivals set aside.** Constituter dualism is unparsimonious, and the
constituters stay "all too cat-like", which gives 1002 cats. Vague objects
get no correct conception: each of three pictures replaces the vagueness
with precision, and the precise candidates are still cat-like.

**Supervaluation.** Vagueness is semantic indecision. A sentence is
super-true "if and only if it is true under all ways of making the unmade
semantic decisions". Each intended interpretation puts exactly one candidate
in the extension of "cat", so "there is just one cat" is super-true. The cost
is granted, not removed: "Although it is super-true that something is a cat
on the mat, there is nothing such that it is super-true of it that it is a
cat on the mat." The rule is a defeasible presumption that pragmatics can
suspend.

**Almost-identity.** "The real opposite of identity is distinctness" in the
sense of non-overlap. Overlap is a spectrum, credited to Armstrong. The
candidates sit near the identity end, so "The cats are many, but almost one",
which is true for most contexts. Almost-identity is overlap, "not a relation
of partial indiscernibility". It "is not in general an equivalence relation.
Many steps of almost-identity can take us from one thing to another thing
that is entirely distinct from the first." Its threshold is left undecided.

**Each needs the other.** Fred's house with and without the garage overlap
substantially but are not almost one, so supervaluation must decide.
Definite descriptions ("The cat on the mat includes hair h17") get their
truth-value gap only from supervaluation: "It's hard to see how else to get
it." In the other direction, "The supervaluation rule comes natural only when
the alternative interpretations don't differ too much", which almost-identity
secures.

**What could have come out otherwise.** One remedy could have handled every
case alone. The garage case and the gappy description show that neither
does, and that is the essay's argument for combining them.

## What this does not say

- **That the problem arises everywhere.** Footnote 6 grants that a more
  eligible referent would win by default. The problem bites only where
  "nature is gradual, no handy joint in nature picks out one of the c's from
  all the rest". Where there is a natural joint, the account is not needed.
- **Where the threshold for "almost" lies.** Lewis leaves it, and whether
  almost-identity is an equivalence among the actual candidates, "very
  sensibly" undecided. The account cannot count anything until a context
  supplies both.
- **That almost-identity is closeness in some metric.** It is shared parts.
  Two nearby subspaces in general position share only the zero vector, yet
  the angle between them is small. Borrowing the counting move does not
  borrow the relation ([NOTE-722](../notes.d/NOTE-722.md)).
- **That the cost of supervaluation is removed.** Something is the cat and
  nothing is super-truly the cat. Lewis accepts this, he does not explain it
  away.
- **That it holds without its assumptions.** It assumes perdurance and an
  unrestricted supply of precise candidates (footnote 4). Those who deny
  either, such as van Inwagen, are pointed to, not answered.
- **That it is evidence for the record's units.** The essay is about counting
  and reference in ordinary language. It has no metric, no dynamics and no
  threshold.

## Connections

- **The record's units.** [NOTE-722](../notes.d/NOTE-722.md) finds the map onto [CLAIM-164](../claims.d/CLAIM-164.md) partly fair.
  For perturbed copies of one gap-isolated subspace, "almost one" fits, and
  the gap supplies the threshold Lewis leaves open. For vectors inside a
  degenerate cluster it does not, because they can be orthogonal; there
  supervaluation fits, and "names nothing" would be "refers
  indeterminately". Both bearings are the record's.
- **[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md).** The temporal version of the problem applies to the
  duration of a combined form, whose clearing of a threshold has no sharp
  end. Fred's house is the nested-block case. [NOTE-722](../notes.d/NOTE-722.md) reads Lewis's
  context as the role a stated target plays there, and says that
  resemblance is the record's. No `answers` edge is claimed, because the
  essay supplies remedies for counting, not a criterion for the question.
- **[LIT-446](../literature.d/LIT-446.md)** (Survival and Identity, [NOTE-373](../notes.d/NOTE-373.md)) already counts by a relation
  other than identity and handles degrees by supervaluation, so this essay
  generalizes a move made there for persons.
- **Fiat boundaries.** Smith and Varzi ([LIT-1024](../literature.d/LIT-1024.md), [THEORY-622](THEORY-622.md)) also hold
  that vagueness is a matter of demarcation, not of the material, and
  that every candidate aggregate is precise.
