# THEORY-2207: Rheinberg, Manig, Kliegl, Engeser and Vollmeyer find by experience sampling of 101 employees, with flow measured by all its components, that flow is higher at work and happiness/satisfaction higher in leisure, that across single reports the two correlate weakly positively, and that goal-directedness of the current activity raises flow in both contexts while leaving happiness unchanged at work and lowering it in leisure; they read the paradox of work as partly an effect of how often work is goal-directed

## Source

Rheinberg, Manig, Kliegl, Engeser and Vollmeyer (2007), *Zeitschrift für
Arbeits- und Organisationspsychologie* 51(3): 105-115, [LIT-3071](../literature.d/LIT-3071.md), read
in [NOTE-2297](../notes.d/NOTE-2297.md).

## What was actually shown

Seven random signals a day for a week, 4,603 reports from 101 employees;
at each, the ten-item Flow Short Scale and two happiness/satisfaction
items. Flow M = 5.24 at work against 4.81 in leisure; happiness 4.95
against 5.15 (§4.2). Across single reports the relation is "nicht negativ
sondern leicht positiv", r = .36 raw, .30 within-person (§4.1).
Goal-directedness raises flow at work, F(1, 1276) = 213.9, and not
happiness, F = 0.9; in leisure it raises flow, F(1, 2124) = 121.8, and
lowers happiness, F(1, 2084) = 19.2 (§4.3). 74% of work signals were
clearly goal-directed, against 46% of leisure signals (§5).

## The account

The authors' explanation of the paradox is partial and stated in the
subjunctive: work is mostly goal-directed, goal-directedness lifts flow
without lifting happiness, so work has more flow and not more happiness.
Why goals lower happiness in leisure they leave open; a gap between the
present and the goal in view is offered post hoc and said not to explain
why the effect is absent at work.

## Where it bears

On [CLAIM-441](../claims.d/CLAIM-441.md)'s third exit ([NOTE-2297](../notes.d/NOTE-2297.md), "Bearing on the record"): flow
and happiness are reported together at the signal, so the record's only
in-episode data put some of flow's positivity in the episode. Weak
evidence, since the correlation is pooled over all activities and the
flow score has no enjoyment item. Engeser and Baumann ([THEORY-2208](THEORY-2208.md))
test the lag directly, on what appears to be the same sample.
