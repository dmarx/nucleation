# THEORY-tmppv8yl: Vestring and colleagues report that in healthy adults ketamine, given 90 to 150 minutes before an approach-avoidance conflict task with experimenter-set threat probability and loss, raised approach, flattened its decline with threat probability and steepened its decline with loss, while lorazepam raised approach and flattened both

## Source

Vestring et al. (2026), [LIT-tmpxug14](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxug14.md), read in [NOTE-tmpkouzt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpkouzt.md).

## The account

In 34 healthy adults tested after no drug, intranasal ketamine (50 mg)
and oral lorazepam (1 mg), both drugs increased approach in Bach et al.'s
approach-avoidance conflict task. Ketamine reduced the slope of approach
on threat probability (β = 0.13, p = 0.005) and increased its slope on
loss magnitude (β = −0.17, p = 0.001), and raised token gains; lorazepam
"attenuates adaptive adjustments to both threat probability and loss
magnitude". The authors read ketamine as "reducing reliance on
probabilistic threat cues" and as reflecting plasticity-facilitated
adaptation.

## What it does not show

Threat probabilities were learned, not told, and no rating of them was
taken, so whether ketamine lowered the learned estimate or the weight
given to it is open. The plasticity reading is the authors' and is not
tested here.
