# NOTE-tmpjtl4f: Corfield — Duality as a category-theoretic concept

## Contribution

Nagel in 1939 described how projective duality freed mathematicians from thinking they had to describe intuitive elements, but philosophers have largely ignored duality since, even as it spread through twentieth-century mathematics. Corfield argues that a reassessment is overdue. Category theory has much to say about duality, through arrow reversal, dualising objects and adjunctions, alongside changes in how mathematics understands identity and structure. Physicists' use of "duality" does not always match mathematicians', but the mathematical analysis is still useful, and category theory is an important language for philosophy of physics.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (p. 55): historical instances (dual Platonic solids, De Morgan duality, the Fourier transform, projective duality with Pascal/Brianchon), leading into Nagel's thesis.
- §2 (pp. 56–57): philosophers on mathematical duality since Nagel.
- §3 (pp. 57–59): physicists use "duality", "symmetry" and "reciprocity" loosely. He prefers to reserve "duality" for involutions with structural reversal, not mere equivalence, as in homological mirror symmetry read as an equivalence of A∞-categories. Six worked mathematical examples (algebraic geometry, number, linear, logic, convex geometry, analysis), after Willerton.
- §4 (pp. 59–61): all the constructions revolve around adjunction, though there is no single monolithic account. Set is not self-dual, and Rel and monoidal non-cartesian categories (Baez's "Quantum Quandaries") are more natively dual settings. T-duality relates to Fourier–Mukai. Mirror symmetry's Z2 action and electric–magnetic S-duality (a vestige of SL(2,Z)) are only partly dualities in the mathematician's sense. Predicate logic is "far from optimal" for such moduli-space sameness questions.
