# NOTE-1585: Lawvere — Metric spaces, generalized logic, and closed categories

## Contribution

The identification of metric spaces with categories enriched in the
extended non-negative reals under addition, now called Lawvere metric
spaces. With it, the general theory of enriched categories (V-functors,
the Yoneda embedding, Cauchy completion) specializes to metric-space
theory, and the same scheme over truth values gives posets and logic. The
2002 commentary extends it toward dynamics and optimization.

## Key insight

"I noticed the analogy between the triangle inequality and a categorical
composition law" (commentary, p. 1). Composition X(a, b) + X(b, c) ≥
X(a, c) and identity 0 ≥ X(a, a) are the only axioms a metric needs; the
rest of the usual definition is extra. The closed interval [0, ∞] with ≥
as maps, + as tensor and truncated subtraction as hom "constitute a bona
fide example of a complete, symmetric, monoidal closed category V"
(p. 1).

## Assumptions

- V a complete and cocomplete symmetric monoidal closed category; for
  metric spaces V = ([0, ∞], ≥, +, 0).
- Infinite distances allowed (the analogue of an empty hom-set);
  separation and symmetry not assumed.
- One tensor per base: R (sum), the central example, and R_cart (max),
  named for ultrametric spaces (p. 152).

## Key results

- **Only two axioms** (commentary, p. 2). Metric spaces satisfy only the
  two axioms for a V-category, and "the evidence is compelling that the
  usually-given more restrictive definition was too hastily fixed."
- **Asymmetry** (p. 2). "A metric space can always be symmetrized (by one
  of two methods, + and max), but it is often better to delay that until
  the last stage of a calculation, because the natural asymmetry carries
  considerable information"; and "many constructions arising in dynamics
  as well as many constructions in analysis lead naturally to
  non-symmetric metric spaces".
- **Paths** (p. 2). "profunctor composition is Bellman-Fenchel
  convolution", and the commentary names "free V -categories (generated by
  V -graphs)", which for this V are the shortest-path closures of weighted
  graphs.
- **Cost** (pp. 2–3). Truth values relate to distances "in terms of the
  cost or work required to transform or move one point to another"; the
  inclusion of truth values "interprets “false” as infinite distance or
  “unattainably expensive”."
- **Sublevel sets** (p. 3). "Every presheaf has in particular its zero
  set", and its sublevel sets.
- **The axioms** (original p. 136). "Then the laws satisfied by X are
  greater-than relations X(a, b) + X(b, c) ≥ X(a, c), 0 ≥ X(a, a)."
- **The base** (p. 145). "Our central example R has as objects all
  non-negative real quantities (including ∞), as morphisms a → v the
  greater-than-or-equal-to relations a ≥ v, and as « tensor » a ⊗ u = a + u
  the sum of quantities." And (p. 147): "an R-category is an arbitrary
  (generalized) metric space."
- **The table** (p. 138). Metric spaces, categories and posets set side by
  side as V-valued categories: nonnegative real quantities under "≧",
  "sum" and "zero"; abstract sets under mapping, cartesian product and
  the one-element set; truth values under entailment, conjunction and
  true.
- **The Fréchet axioms dropped** (p. 138). "None of our results in this
  paper will depend on the additional Frechet axioms"; "Allowing ∞ among
  the quantities is precisely analogous to including the empty set among
  abstract sets"; "The non symmetry is the more serious generalization,
  and moreover occurs in many naturally arising examples, such as
  X(a, b) = work required to get from a to b in mountainous region X."
- **Normed categories** (pp. 139–140). The fundamental property
  "|f| + |g| ≥ |fg|" gives a metric, the infimum of the norms of the
  morphisms from a to b. "We will leave as an exercise for the reader to
  define a closed category S(R) such that « normed categories » are just
  S(R)-valued categories" (p. 140).
- **Functors** (p. 149). An R-functor "is an arbitrary distance-decreasing
  map (Lipschitz map of Lipschitz constant ≤ 1)".
- **The max base** (p. 152). "the underlying category of R does admit the
  cartesian closed structure R_cart in which u ⊗ v = max(u, v)", with
  Hom(a, v) = v if v > a and 0 if a ≥ v. "An R_cart-category is just an
  ultrametric space, so that all of our general results may be
  reinterpreted as applying to ultrametric spaces." The identity
  R_cart → R is a closed functor, "a + v ≥ max(a, v)".
- **Yoneda** (p. 154). "The Yoneda embedding is, for any closed V and any
  V-category A, a V-full-and-faithful V-functor"; for metric spaces it is
  "an isometric embedding of A into the space R^(A^op) where the last is
  equipped with the sup metric."
- **Cauchy completeness** (p. 163). "In order that a metric space Y be
  Cauchy-complete, it is necessary and sufficient that every adjoint pair
  of bimodules ... be induced by a V-functor".
- **Free V-categories** (p. 165). The hom is a coproduct over all finite
  sequences of vertices; for R the distance is "inf [γ(x, x1) + γ(x1, x2)
  + ..... + γ(xn, x′)] the well-known « least-cost » distance."

## Concepts

- **V-valued (enriched) category**: hom-objects X(a, b) in V, composition
  X(a, b) ⊗ X(b, c) → X(a, c), identity k → X(a, a).
- **Metric space**: a [0, ∞]-valued category.
- **Normed category**: a category with norms on morphisms, |f| + |g| ≥
  |fg| (original p. 140).
- **Symmetry** as structure, not a property: given isomorphisms
  X(a, b) → X(b, a).

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization** (Response 3, §2). A: "Lawvere-style metric
  enrichment gives the familiar additive shortest-path model, while more
  general ordered monoidal structures allow different composition laws."
  A's sentence, not Lawvere's. The first half is right: the base is
  ([0, ∞], ≥, +), and the free V-category on a V-graph is the shortest-path
  metric. The second half is right with a correction: if path closures are
  to exist the base must be a quantale (complete, with ⊗ distributing over
  joins), not just an ordered monoid (Marsden & Zwart, Definition 1;
  Seven Sketches §2.5.3). A links Oxford's archive and names no Lawvere
  work; the Oxford item is most probably Marsden & Zwart.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md)** (threshold = infimum over paths of the peak along the
  path): supports. *Lawvere names this base* (R_cart,
  p. 152). In
  ([0, ∞], ≥) joins are infima, and max(a, inf_i b_i) = inf_i max(a, b_i),
  so ⊗ = max distributes over joins and ([0, ∞], ≥, max, 0) is a
  commutative quantale. Taking max along a path and the infimum over paths,
  the hom of the free category it generates on the configuration graph is
  exactly the claim's B*(A, B), which is Lawvere's free V-category formula
  (p. 165) specialised to R_cart. The reader's step is reading max as a
  peak capacity.
- **[CLAIM-585](../claims.d/CLAIM-585.md)** (a Pareto frontier of sufficient budgets): supports,
  and sharpens what an enrichment must use. *The reader's, in neither
  source:* a product of two such bases gives each hom the componentwise
  infimum over paths, which no single path need achieve. For the claim's two
  paths with peaks (3, 1) and (1, 3) it reports (1, 1), which suffices for
  neither. So the hom-object must be an upward-closed set of budget vectors
  (a quantale of up-sets, joined by union), which is what the claim already
  uses. A's pair law (max, +) on peak and expenditure does not flag this.
- **A's "resource-graded process category"**: Lawvere's normed category is
  one established form of it, keeping each morphism with its norm rather
  than only the best cost between two objects (see [NOTE-1602](NOTE-1602.md)).
- **Asymmetry.** A: "Ordinary topological connectivity is symmetric;
  computational reachability need not be." Lawvere supports this from the
  side of costs: his metrics are non-symmetric by default. A turns to
  directed homotopy for it; Lawvere's framework already carries it in the
  hom-objects.
- **[TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md)** (six resource types): qualifies. A capacity (max) and a
  cumulative expenditure (+) are each a quantale base; a replenishable
  reserve, ḃ = s(t) − c, is not a context-free composition law, which
  matches A's remark that reserves must be tracked "as part of the state".
- **[LIT-803](../literature.d/LIT-803.md)** (Seven Sketches; [NOTE-601](NOTE-601.md) skimmed chapter 2 at its opening
  only): chapter 2, read in part for this filing (§§2.1, 2.2.3,
  2.3.3–2.3.4, 2.5.3, 2.6), holds the textbook statement of A's sentence:
  Lawvere metric spaces as Cost-categories, the shortest-path metric of a
  weighted graph, and other bases giving other path laws (Exercise 2.63's
  widest path).
- **[LIT-2047](../literature.d/LIT-2047.md)** (sublevel-set persistence of energy landscapes): related
  only through the merge level as a minimax path cost; Lawvere's sublevel
  sets of presheaves are a different use.

  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Lawvere's.** No algorithms or complexity; two bases only, sum (the
  central example) and max (R_cart); foundational and partly programmatic: the idea "can be
  developed considerably further" (commentary, p. 1).
- **This reading.** The 1973 pages were read from an OCR transcription;
  quoted passages were checked against the page images, the rest was not,
  and the diagrams of §1 were not checked symbol by symbol.
