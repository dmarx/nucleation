# NOTE-tmpuba7m: Being one of us: we-identities and self-categorization theory

## Contribution

It finds a class of cases that self-categorization theory claims and
fits badly: the "we" of close relationships. It argues that
depersonalization, the theory's "basic process underlying group
phenomena", is not necessary for a we-identity. It thereby limits the
use phenomenologists have made of the theory as an account of the "we"
in general. It adds a four-condition definition of we-identity and an
externalist sketch of close-relationship identity.

## Key insight

To be "one of us" with a spouse or an old friend is not to see each
other as interchangeable. Relationships "cannot survive substitution of
their participants" (Kolodny 2003, p. 148). The bond grows by each
becoming tuned to the other's particularities and so less replaceable,
which is the reverse of depersonalization. If the "we" can be built
either way, a theory of one way is not a theory of the "we".

## Assumptions

- **We-identity defined by four conditions** (§2):
  - (i) membership in a social group, broadly "an entity constituted by
    and only by people" (Epstein 2019);
  - (ii) awareness, at least at times;
  - (iii) endorsement;
  - (iv) variable affective salience.
- **Close relationship defined normatively** (§4): robust (participants
  not substitutable), non-instrumental, reciprocal concern, with
  emotional vulnerability, open-endedness and common knowledge. Not
  necessarily dyadic. Fn. 16: non-antagonistic cases only.
- **SCT is read as committed to depersonalization being general.** León
  cites Turner et al. (1987, p. 50; 1994, pp. 455, 460) and the claim
  that group size is no theoretical restriction (1987, p. 103, fn. 12).

## Key results

- **The theory, as quoted (§3).** Page numbers are Turner et al. 1987's.
  - SCT asks "how individuals are able to act as a group at all"
    (p. 42).
  - Social identity is "those aspects of an individual's self-concept
    based upon their social group or category memberships together with
    their emotional, evaluative and other psychological correlates"
    (p. 29).
  - Self-representations are "'switched on'" (p. 44), with "an inverse
    relationship between the salience of the personal and social levels"
    (p. 49), a "psychological discontinuity" (p. 66, fn. 7).
  - Depersonalization is a "change from the personal to the social level
    of identity" (pp. 51, 204); Turner et al. keep the word "in lack of a
    better alternative" (p. 204, fn. 9).
  - "Five individuals in a room have just as much become a group ... if
    they are behaving in terms of a large-scale category membership they
    share ... as if in terms of an emergent group whose norms and
    defining features are unique to that specific collection of five
    people (a friendship group ...)" (p. 103, fn. 12).
- **León prefers a continuum to levels** (§3). Following Huddy (2001),
  personal and social identity are entangled, so it is better to treat
  them as two ends of an "identity continuum" (Oakes 2002; Turner et al.
  1994, p. 456).
- **Phenomenology's uptake** (§3). Zahavi (2019) and Salice & Henriksen
  (2015) take the "we" to require downplaying interpersonal differences,
  "social uniformity", and link depersonalization to Husserl's
  "self-alienation". Walther's (1923) "public person" and Heidegger's
  "the One" are earlier versions of the same idea.
- **The argument** (§4).
  1. SCT claims to cover groups of any size, friendship groups included.
  2. Close relationships yield we-identities.
  3. Their members grasp each other as non-substitutable and
     complementary.
  4. Depersonalization is perceiving oneself and others as
     interchangeable exemplars.
  5. So close-relationship we-identities do not arise by
     depersonalization, and depersonalization is not necessary for a
     we-identity.

  León grants that close others share features, may act under an ad hoc
  group prototype (Peter facing down a stranger at a picnic), and often
  form relationships against a background of shared social identity.
  The relationship-science evidence cited is couple resilience: "the more
  individuated or differentiated the 'I' is, the more flexible and
  adaptive the 'we' will be" (Skerrett & Fergus 2015, p. 25).
- **The SCT reply and its cost** (§4). SCT can put close relationships
  under personal identity (Hogg 2001, p. 133). That is coherent within
  SCT, but then SCT is not an account of the "we" in general, which is
  what phenomenologists used it for.
- **The positive sketch** (§5).
  - Husserl's *Zweieinigkeit*: "two human beings who build a life unity,
    not two lives next to each other".
  - Integrationist extended-mind criteria: individualizing, reciprocal,
    enduring, reliable coupling (Heersmink 2015; Sterelny 2010), applied
    agent to agent (Skorburg 2017).
  - Transactive memory in couples (Wegner et al. 1991).
  - Grief as a "partial loss of self" (Fuchs 2018; Ratcliffe 2023).
  - Socially extended identities need heterogeneity: "if individuals who
    constitute a socially extended system were to level down the
    differences between them, the system would most likely collapse"
    (§6).

## Concepts

- **we-identity**: an aspect of a subject's self-conception held as a
  group member, at times salient, endorsed and cared about (§2).
- **close personal relationship**: robust, non-instrumental, reciprocal,
  emotionally vulnerable, open-ended concern, as common knowledge (§4).
- **personalized we-identity**: a we-identity arising from mutual
  individualization rather than depersonalization (§5).
- **individualization / entrenchment**: mutual fine-tuning of two agents
  to each other's particularities, making each less substitutable (§5,
  after Sterelny 2010).

  close relationships are groups without interchangeable members, and
  draws a different moral: groups are perceived from collective
  intentionality. León would extend the boundaries of persons instead.
  Both agree that SCT's equivalence-class account misses some groups.

  perspective of others" (p. 460). That sits badly with the Husserlian
  "self-alienation" the phenomenologists pair with depersonalization.

## Limitations

- **No data.** The relationship research is cited, not analysed, and the
  case for C3 is conceptual.
- **The dispute may be verbal.** SCT can assign close relationships to
  personal identity (Hogg 2001), and León concedes this is coherent
  within SCT. The disagreement is then over whether SCT was ever a theory
  of the "we" in general. León shows phenomenologists used it as one; he
  shows less clearly that Turner et al. claimed it, beyond the
  group-size passage.
- **Non-antagonistic relationships only** (fn. 16).
- **The extended-mind sketch inherits that debate's contested criteria.**

## Open questions

- Does a measured we-ness in couples correlate with, or against,
  self-stereotyping as a couple? A study measuring both in the same dyads
  would test whether personalization and depersonalization are opposite
  routes or can co-occur.
