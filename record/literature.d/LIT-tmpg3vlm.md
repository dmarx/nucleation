# LIT-tmpg3vlm: Transcranial stimulation of the frontal lobes increases propensity of mind-wandering without changing meta-awareness

Axelrod, V., Zhu, X. & Qiu, J. (2018). Transcranial stimulation of the
frontal lobes increases propensity of mind-wandering without changing
meta-awareness. *Scientific Reports* 8: 15975. DOI
10.1038/s41598-018-34098-z.

Read in full in NOTE-tmp36b7e.

## Key takeaways

- **Replication.** "By using a larger sample size and by conducting the
  experiment in a different country and language, we fully replicated the
  key original findings" (Abstract).
- **Meta-awareness unchanged.** "We found no significant difference
  between conditions (one-way ANOVA main effect: F(2,82) = 1.11; p =
  0.33 ...) ... a Bayes factor of 0.25, indicating substantial evidence in
  support of H0" (Results). The interaction of stimulation with
  meta-awareness level had a Bayes factor of 0.132.
- **Low meta-awareness goes with more mind-wandering**, in every condition
  (Fig. 3C), which is a correlation across probes, not an intervention.
- **The authors' own caveats**: the meta-awareness question always came
  second, after the mind-wandering question, which may bias it.

## Standing in the record

Registered for LEAD-141 as the nearest the literature comes to a test of
Metzinger's H1 (LIT-1945, p. 14). It does not test H1, which needs the
agent model's availability blocked; it shows instead that the one
manipulation cited for H1 changes reported mind-wandering without a
measurable change in meta-awareness. It conflicts with the pre-registered
null of LIT-tmp9cpfz, which was submitted before it appeared and does not
discuss it.
