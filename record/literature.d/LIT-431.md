# LIT-431: Time and the Observer: The Where and When of Consciousness in the Brain

Daniel C. Dennett and Marcel Kinsbourne (1992), *Behavioral and Brain
Sciences* 15(2), 183–201, a target article with open peer commentary and the
authors' response (the issue's pages run to 247). Drafted at the Bellagio
Study Center in April 1990. Dennett's copy is in the Tufts Digital Library.

## Key takeaways

- **Two models (§1.1).** *Cartesian materialism*: discard Descartes's
  dualism but keep a central place where the contents of consciousness are
  presented, so that the timing of arrival there fixes subjective order.
  *Multiple Drafts*: discriminations are distributed in space and time, are
  made only once, and are revised by many processes in parallel; there is
  "a parallel stream of conflicting and continuously revised contents", no
  draft of which is canonical.
- **Content and vehicle (§2.1).** The time a content is represented is not
  the time it represents, as a dated letter to Calcutta tells of a truce
  signed before a battle whose news came first. The brain can infer order
  by "content-sensitive settling" and need not re-stage events in their
  represented order for an inner observer.
- **Orwell and Stalin (§2.2).** A false content can be added before
  experience (a Stalinesque show trial) or after it (Orwellian revision of
  memory). At the time scale of colour phi and metacontrast both accounts
  fit all the data, including the subject's own, so "there is really only a
  verbal difference between the two theories".
- **Libet (§3).** Backward referral and the subjective delay of intention
  are re-read as failures to keep the time of representing apart from the
  time represented. Grey Walter's "precognitive carousel" shows the same
  point with a task the brain normally performs.
- **Conclusion (§4).** "The representation of sequence in the stream of
  consciousness is a product of the brain's interpretative processes, not a
  direct reflection of the sequence of events making up those processes."

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request for Dennett, as the article form
of the Multiple Drafts model. It is the model Chalmers names in a sentence
in "Facing Up" ([LIT-406](LIT-406.md), read in [NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md)) as one more theory of an
easy problem.

**On the society-of-mind bridge ([THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)).** The brief asks whether
"no central Cartesian theatre" makes consciousness a society-of-agents
product in Minsky's sense, and whether that bears on the anti-nesting
question. My reading:

- *Not in Minsky's sense, though in his company.* The paper denies a
  central place, controller or audience, as Minsky's memo ([LIT-399](LIT-399.md))
  and Brooks ([LIT-436](LIT-436.md)) do. It cites *The Society of Mind* ([LIT-429](LIT-429.md))
  once, for the question which "finish line" fixes order when A beats B to
  one and B beats A to another (cf. Minsky 1985, p. 61). But its units are
  not agents with competences. They are "content-fixations",
  discriminations and "editing" processes, and the paper never calls them
  agents, a society, or homunculi. What it gives consciousness is not a
  society's product but an interpretive product: the narrative the
  probes elicit.
- *It supplies a regress argument that is close in shape to Kammerer's
  anti-nesting principle ([LIT-409](LIT-409.md)).* A central conscious subsystem
  would be "the first step in an infinite regress of too-powerful
  homunculi": its work would be either mysterious or decomposed into
  subsystems "that begin to duplicate the tasks of the 'nonconscious' parts"
  (§1.1). That forbids a conscious part that re-presents the whole's
  contents for the whole. It does not forbid minded parts as such. A
  citizen is not a Cartesian theatre for a nation. So it is no help in
  refusing Schwitzgebel's upward move ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md)). The pairing with Kammerer
  is mine.
- *It cuts against the exclusion postulate.* The THEORY counts IIT's
  exclusion postulate, derived from an axiom that experience has definite
  borders, as one form of anti-nesting. This paper argues that below a few
  hundred milliseconds there is no fact about which contents are "in"
  consciousness at an instant, and that a cortical "mode shift" criterion
  timed by outside observers is still the Cartesian Theatre if its timing
  is taken as definitive (§3.2). A definite-borders axiom applied to time
  is what the paper denies. My inference; the paper predates IIT.
- *Its criterion for an observer is organisational.* "When a portion of the
  world comes in this way to compose a skein of narratives, that portion of
  the world is an observer" (§2.2). Nothing in that definition is
  restricted to brains. So, on my reading, the paper sides with the
  functionalist premise the THEORY says both directions share.

**On *The Emotion Machine* ([LIT-432](LIT-432.md), read in [NOTE-348](../notes.d/NOTE-348.md)).** Its
"Immanence Illusion" (that no resource can know what another is doing "right
now") quotes Dennett and Kinsbourne. This is the paper it quotes.

**On Hofstadter.** Douglas Hofstadter is among those thanked for comments on
drafts. Nothing in the text connects to GEB ([LIT-437](LIT-437.md)) or *I Am a Strange
Loop* ([LIT-423](LIT-423.md)).

**On Damasio.** The paper cites "Damasio 1989a" for there being no single
place through which all causal trains pass to deposit their contents "in
consciousness". The reference list was not in the PDF I read, so I cannot
say which 1989 Damasio paper that is. The record holds one candidate, the
convergence-zone proposal "Time-locked multiregional retroactivation"
([LIT-385](LIT-385.md), filed unread); the identification is unverified.

The book-length version, with the other implications the paper sets aside
("Dennett 1991b"), is *Consciousness Explained* ([LIT-443](LIT-443.md)).

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice, and it does not
belong in the anthology.
