# THEORY-208: Agency is multiply realisable without cognition or mental states, and AI is a new kind of it

## Source

Floridi (2025), [LIT-897](../literature.d/LIT-897.md), read in [NOTE-702](../notes.d/NOTE-702.md): §1 for the thesis,
§4 for the criteria, §§5–13 for the kinds, and §12 for artificial agency.
The criteria are taken from Floridi & Sanders (2004), [LIT-898](../literature.d/LIT-898.md), read in
[NOTE-701](../notes.d/NOTE-701.md), which the 2025 paper follows and loosens.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested. The paper is a conceptual taxonomy, and it says
outright that it assumes its premise: "assuming it is [a form of agency],
what its nature is" (p. 2).

- **The criteria.** Interactivity is necessary for every kind: "without
  some form of interaction, there can be no agency" (p. 8). Autonomy and
  adaptability are graded, and are absent from natural agency, where there
  is "no autonomy or adaptability … only interactions" (p. 9). Agency is
  not defined by necessary and sufficient conditions (p. 3).
- **The kinds.** These are natural (rivers), biological (dogs), animal
  social (ant colonies), artefactual (smart thermostats), human individual,
  human social (corporations), artificial (LLMs) and social artificial
  (Agentic AI). They form "a branching pattern" (p. 17), not a ladder.
- **AI's place.** "A computational, goal-driven form of agency defined by
  human purposes" (p. 18). It learns statistically and openly, and it
  cannot choose its own goals (p. 19). It has no consciousness,
  intelligence or understanding (p. 19).

What could have come out otherwise: a stated level of abstraction under
which artificial agency failed to differ from artefactual agency except in
degree. The paper writes none down, so nothing could.

## What this does not say

- **Not that AI is an agent because it is intelligent, or that it is not
  intelligent because it is an agent.** The denial of intelligence is a
  separate premise, asserted (pp. 18–20), and the account is only as
  strong as that premise. In a weak sense the paper concedes AI is
  intelligent (p. 2). On its adverbial reading, intelligence is "behaving
  intelligently" (p. 3). The denial therefore needs the strong,
  human-level sense.
- **Not that the three criteria sort the kinds.** They separate natural
  agency from the rest and nothing else. The other distinctions use origin,
  substrate, goal source and mental states.
- **Not an answer to the permissiveness objection** ([CLAIM-010](../claims.d/CLAIM-010.md)). The
  account accepts it: rivers and thermostats are agents. It answers by
  subdividing agency into kinds, not by restricting what counts.
- **Not a criterion for collective agency.** Colonies, corporations and
  Agentic AI are described as collective agents, and no test says when a
  group is one ([QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md)).
- **Not about selfhood or personhood.** Those enter only as features of
  the human kind (pp. 14–15).
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Connections

- **[THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md) (organisational minimal agency)** is a rival. On it, a
  system serving an externally fixed norm is not acting, and that is
  exactly how this account describes AI.
- **[THEORY-173](THEORY-173.md) (large models are a social technology, not agents)** is a
  rival. It agrees that large models have no understanding and no goals
  of their own, and denies agency because its criterion requires
  self-set goals.
- **[THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md).** The agency here is the thin kind that account treats as
  uncontested.
