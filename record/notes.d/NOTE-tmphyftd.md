# NOTE-tmphyftd: Adewuyi (2026) — review of Vallor, The AI Mirror

## Contribution

Reportage of the book, chapter by chapter, with two mild criticisms.

## Key results

1. §2 [book p. 10]: AI mirrors humanity "not as we ought to be or could be, but as we already are and have long been".
2. §3 [p. 67]: "AI has the potential to change our human character".
3. §4 [p. 117], the reviewer's paraphrase: moral deskilling is "a situation in which humans lose the capacity for moral reasonings".
4. §5 [p. 200]: the danger of losing "faith in our capacity to be any better than our mirrors".
5. §5 [p. 165]: democratic moral improvisation "guided by practical wisdom".

## Assumptions

The reviewer takes the book's virtue-ethical frame as given.

## Concepts

AI mirror; moral deskilling; intellectual virtue; autofabrication; practical wisdom.

## Bearing on the record

Moral deskilling is a support that does the work (algorithmic decision) shrinking a capacity kept by exercise (moral reasoning). It bears on whether [CLAIM-605](../claims.d/CLAIM-605.md)'s model applies to people ([CASE-tmpf71w4](../cases.d/CASE-tmpf71w4.md)), not on the derivation.
