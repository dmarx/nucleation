# LIT-994: Imbalance Trouble: Revisiting Neural-Collapse Geometry

Christos Thrampoulidis, Ganesh R. Kini, Vala Vakilian and Tina Behnia (2022), *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 35* (NeurIPS 2022).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-764](../notes.d/NOTE-764.md).*

- **The invariant is the logits (Theorem 1).** For the
  unconstrained-features max-margin problem at any (R, ρ)-STEP imbalance,
  ŴᵀĤ = Ẑ, with Ẑ[c, i] = 1 − 1/k for the true class and −1/k otherwise.
  Then ĤᵀĤ = UΛUᵀ and ŴᵀŴ = VΛVᵀ from Ẑᵀ = UΛVᵀ, and embeddings collapse
  to class means.
- **The ETF is the balanced special case (Corollary 1.3).** Otherwise,
  majority classifiers are longer than minority ones (Lemma 3.1). The
  minority classifiers' cosine, (R − 7)/(R − 7 + 2k(2 + √((R+1)/2))), rises
  with R and reaches 1 only as R → ∞ (Eq. 4).
- **Regularization matters under imbalance (Propositions 1–2).** No finite
  ridge gives SELI; the ridge-regularized geometry depends on λ, and tends
  to SELI as λ → 0. With balanced data it is the ETF at every λ.
- **Minority collapse (§H).** It does not occur for small regularization
  at finite imbalance. It needs R above a threshold that rises with the
  minority fraction and with 1/λ.
- **Measured (Figures 2, 4–7, 13; Table 3).**
  - In the unconstrained-features model and in ResNet-18 and VGG-13 on
    imbalanced MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR10, NC1 holds.
  - The geometry ends closer to SELI than to the ETF.
  - The approach slows as R grows, and is worse for embeddings than for
    classifiers.
  - Minority test error on CIFAR10 rises from 13.8% at R = 1 to 98.1% at
    R = 100.
- **Not shown.** That gradient descent reaches SELI, which is conjecture
  C1. Nothing is run with more than one seed.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-051](../questions.d/QUESTION-051.md), and read in [NOTE-764](../notes.d/NOTE-764.md). Its
geometry is filed as [THEORY-286](../theory.d/THEORY-286.md). It answers part of [QUESTION-051](../questions.d/QUESTION-051.md)'s
first half: under cross-entropy, the preferred last-layer geometry is
fixed by the labels' simplex encoding, and the simplex ETF of [THEORY-083](../theory.d/THEORY-083.md) is
its balanced case. It bears on [QUESTION-080](../questions.d/QUESTION-080.md): under weight decay and
imbalance the end geometry depends on the regularizer, and the slow
approach makes measured geometry depend on when training stops. It shows
that [THEORY-083](../theory.d/THEORY-083.md)'s promote_when, which counts imbalanced replications as
tests of equiangularity, needs restricting to balanced classes.
