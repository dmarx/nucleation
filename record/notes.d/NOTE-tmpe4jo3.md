# NOTE-tmpe4jo3: Russian Hybrid Warfare

## Contribution

The report argues that the US misreads the Russian approach to war. Washington treats hybrid war as activity below the level of armed conflict. Russian military writing, by contrast, treats it as a strategic-level effort to reorient a target state's governance and alignment, with conventional forces included and all instruments serving an information campaign. Drawing on Russian military journals, the author says the Kremlin believes hybrid wars dominate twenty-first-century conflict, sees itself as the target of one, and is adapting its institutions accordingly. The report ends with recommendations for US policy.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- The executive summary (pp. 8-9) quotes the Russian military definition of hybrid war: all actions, up to and including conventional forces in regional conflicts, are subordinate to an information campaign. It also lists Kremlin adaptations: centralizing decision-making, "patriotic consciousness" campaigns, expeditionary capability, and deniable PMCs and proxies.
- The introduction (p. 13) warns that if the US deters the kind of war Russia does not intend to fight, it can suffer defeats "even as its defense strategy technically succeeds". It says Europe-only case studies miss the global scope of Russian hybrid war (Syria, Libya, Venezuela).
- Russian hybrid war theory (pp. 14-24) covers definitions, the Kremlin's perception that a hybrid war is being waged against Russia, whole-of-society mobilization, kinetic conflict within hybrid war, and diversifying participants. On p. 20 it cites Russian theorists (Bartosh, Kartapolov) who write openly about offensive hybrid war doctrine.
- The implications section (pp. 25-27) recommends that the US treat hybrid wars as unified multi-domain wars rather than splitting them by "domain", counter Russian information campaigns openly, remove the deniability of PMCs and proxies such as Wagner, the DNR and the LNR, avoid "false red lines" on using conventional forces, and reassess global force posture.

## Open questions

- It is an interpretive framework built largely on Russian open-source military journals. A deeper reading should check how representative the cited theorists are of official doctrine.
- It was published in September 2020, before the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine. That invasion is a natural test of the report's claim that Russia seeks to avoid major conventional war.
- ISW is a think tank with a stated policy perspective. The recommendations are advocacy and should be read separately from the descriptive analysis.
