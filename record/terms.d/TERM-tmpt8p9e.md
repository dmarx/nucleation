# cross-scale concordance, relative to declared parties, claims and horizon: of an encompassing organization's operation and development with the independently justified claims of those who constitute, sustain, depend on or are affected by it, together with the effective answerability of its governing arrangements to those claims; not alignment, harmony or Pareto improvement

## Definition

A23 §7, offered as "a candidate definition, not a settled result":

> Cross-scale concordance is a multidimensional, historically situated
> relationship between an encompassing organization and parties who
> constitute, sustain, depend upon, or are affected by it.
>
> The relationship is concordant insofar as the organization's operation
> and development remain compatible with independently justified claims
> of those parties; its governing arrangements provide appropriate
> protections and opportunities for effective agency; and its responsible
> agents retain feasible means of recognizing, contesting, and correcting
> violations of those claims.

> Its evaluation is relative to declared parties, claims, circumstances,
> and temporal horizons. Different arrangements may be incomparable across
> some dimensions.

Two components, which A23 states at the outset: **normative
compatibility** ("whether the relationships are justified") and
**effective institutional answerability** ("whether the organization is
capable of responding when they are not"). "Neither can replace the
other."

## The job it does

It names the relation between a whole's development and its parts' claims
that [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md) and [QUESTION-461](../questions.d/QUESTION-461.md) ask about, so that a finding about one
encompassing organization can be stated as a finding about this relation.
Its parties are [TERM-012](TERM-012.md)'s three relations kept apart: internal
(constituents), dependency (what the organization relies on) and
affected-party. A23 §6 splits its time dimension three ways: present,
developmental (how the organization changes the parties' later
capabilities, [CLAIM-tmp6wuql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6wuql.md)) and reflexive (whether its governing
arrangements can recognize and correct emerging conflicts).

## What it must not be confused with

- **Alignment.** A23: "concordance cannot simply mean alignment between a
  whole and its parts. A totalitarian society might achieve extraordinary
  alignment through coercion and cultural conditioning." Agreement
  produced by removing the alternatives is not concordance; [SYNTHESIS-023](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-023.md)'s
  objection, that non-domination cannot be read from low supervisory cost
  but only under intervention, applies here too.
- **Harmony or the absence of dissent.** A23 §12: "Concordance is
  therefore not the absence of tension between wholes and parts. It is a
  property of how those tensions are organized and addressed." Quiet can
  be learned discrimination or a disabled alarm ([CASE-129](../cases.d/CASE-129.md)).
- **Pareto improvement**, which [TERM-012](TERM-012.md) already excluded.
- **Fewer recognized claims.** An organization does not become more
  concordant by ceasing to recognize a claim that conflicted with its
  priorities (A23 §8); against a fixed reference set of claims, that is
  [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md) and [CLAIM-556](../claims.d/CLAIM-556.md)'s point that dropping a demand is not relief.
- **Corrigibility as such.** A23 §10, after [CLAIM-511](../claims.d/CLAIM-511.md): "An institution's
  corrigibility is an important possible means of legitimacy, not an
  independently sufficient definition of goodness." The answerability
  component is [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s duty with its conditions, not a demand for
  correction everywhere ([CLAIM-520](../claims.d/CLAIM-520.md)).
- **[TERM-012](TERM-012.md)**, which this rivals. [TERM-012](TERM-012.md) was absorbed into [TERM-006](TERM-006.md)'s
  thick form ("which is where its circularity worries came from") and
  deferred with the authenticity line. This definition borrows no
  ownership criterion and leaves the standards to be declared.

## What it leaves open

The standards. A23 §11: "We need a standard of legitimacy that cannot be
derived solely from successful organization." Concordance relative to
declared claims is well defined ([CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)); which claims are justified
is [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)'s, and who owes what to whom is [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md)'s. A
"degree" of concordance is at most a partial order ([CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md)'s treatment
of freedom; A23 §8).
