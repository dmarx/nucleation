# THEORY-1186: Across computational studies of exploration in reward-based bandit tasks, mostly in sub-clinical samples and not yet replicated, directed exploration tends to rise with trait or cognitive anxiety and to fall with state or somatic anxiety

## Source

Chou, Wilson & Smith (preprint June 2024; NBR 2024), [LIT-1483](../literature.d/LIT-1483.md), read in
full from the PsyArXiv preprint in [NOTE-1127](../notes.d/NOTE-1127.md). The primary studies were not
read. The authors' hedged reading of about eight bandit studies is
[THEORY-917](THEORY-917.md), which this entry extends.

## The claim

The review proposes that the direction of the relation between anxiety and
directed exploration depends on the facet of anxiety: "many results could
be seen to suggest that trait/cognitive anxiety measures promote
exploration, while state/somatic anxiety deters exploration. However,
findings are mixed" (p. 18). Its summary puts it as trait or cognitive
anxiety that "may" increase, and somatic or state anxiety that may
decrease, directed exploration (pp. 28–29). [NOTE-1127](../notes.d/NOTE-1127.md) records it as claim
C1, weak to moderate: mixed results, different tasks and measures, no
replication.

This entry states the pattern as a tendency of bandit tasks generally,
which is the review's suggestion taken as its conclusion. It says nothing
about random exploration, and nothing about exploration under threat.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, carried from [THEORY-917](THEORY-917.md) unchanged: the same participants
doing more than one exploration task, with every facet of anxiety measured
by the same instruments, replicated in a second sample. [QUESTION-330](../questions.d/QUESTION-330.md) asks
the same. The promote_when also asks that random exploration be unrelated,
a condition kept from the old wording; this entry does not assert it.
