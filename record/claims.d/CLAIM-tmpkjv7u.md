# CLAIM-tmpkjv7u: A dependence becomes presumptively dominating by those who control it when three conditions coincide: participation in an important sphere of life rests on the arrangement, those who control it hold consequential discretion over the dependent, and the dependent lacks effective means of protection, influence, challenge or justified withdrawal; this is a test for domination by an arrangement's controllers, not for every unfreedom, and impersonal constraint that no one holds is outside it

## The claim

A13 §II, after the mechanism filed as [CLAIM-tmpbypt7](CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md):

> I'd propose that a technological dependency becomes presumptively
> dominating when three conditions coincide:
>
> 1. **Essentiality:** Continued participation in an important sphere of
>    life substantially depends on the system.
> 2. **Asymmetrical authority:** Those controlling the system possess
>    consequential discretion over dependent individuals.
> 3. **Inadequate accountability:** Those individuals lack sufficiently
>    effective means of protection, influence, challenge, or justified
>    withdrawal.

And: "What matters is not only the degree of dependence but the
institutional relationship through which that dependence is governed."
The claim generalizes "technological" to any dependence, which A's own
examples allow.

The exchange reached it by steps. A2 proposed that a free civilization is
"one in which their dependence on society does not become a mechanism of
domination"; A3 §V offered voice, exit, competence and security as what
prevents it; A6 §III and A8 §III defined freedom as non-domination; A11
§12 asked that dependence be "justified, accountable, and compatible with
meaningful agency". A13 is the first to state a test.

## Which sense of "arrangement"

[TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)'s sense 1 by default: the arrangement a party depends on is a
relational arrangement of processes, roles and infrastructures, which need
not be an institution or an agent. The second condition names who holds
discretion over it, "those controlling the system", and they are agents;
the arrangement itself is not assumed to owe or to dominate anything, as
[QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md) keeps the bearer apart from the ground.

## How it sits with what the record holds

- **[CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md)** defines domination as another's capacity to govern the
  relations by which an agent governs itself, "used or not". The second
  and third conditions here are that capacity, unchecked. The first,
  essentiality, is new: it says which dependences raise the political
  question, so a dependence on an inessential service can carry
  [CLAIM-545](CLAIM-545.md)'s domination without meeting this threshold.
- **[CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md)**: the same material dependence can be organized as mutual
  opportunity, competitive vulnerability or coercive leverage. This claim
  says which organization of an essential dependence is objectionable.
- **[CLAIM-669](CLAIM-669.md) and [CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md)**: an arrangement can exploit through the
  access it grants, or produce the dependence it exploits. Those are
  routes into the condition this claim tests for.
- **[QUESTION-461](../questions.d/QUESTION-461.md)** asks when an organization's dependence on its
  subordinated parts gives them authority. This claim runs the dependence
  the other way, the party depending on the arrangement, and bears on the
  question's second branch: what keeps a dependence from turning into a
  claim on the arrangement is, on this account, the absence of the third
  condition.

## What it does not say

- Not that essential dependence is bad. A's water system is the example
  of one that is not.
- Not how much accountability suffices. "Sufficiently effective" is a
  parameter the claim leaves open; A23 later tied it to answerability
  ([CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)).

Corroborates.

## Note of 2026-11-13: Marcuse and Illich, read

- **Marcuse, One-Dimensional Man, read in full** ([THEORY-tmpoxssh](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpoxssh.md),
  [NOTE-tmpf1tpw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpf1tpw.md); [ARG-tmpsh7he](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpsh7he.md), version 2). The book does not support
  reading Marcuse as describing constraint that no one holds, which is how
  version 2's history, its `defeated_if` ("Marcuse's administration") and
  its title's last clause place him. The "objective order of things" is
  "itself the result of domination"; the project for the whole is chosen
  "primarily" by "those groups which have attained control over the
  productive process". What Marcuse adds is different: control exercised
  through the choice of a society's project, not over particular
  dependents; veiled ("the technological veil conceals the reproduction of
  inequality and enslavement"); and by controllers who are themselves
  caught in it ("a vicious circle which encloses both the Master and the
  Servant"). His place is restated: he marks the claim's limit because
  discretion over a project, out of sight, is not the discretion over the
  dependent that the second condition names, and leaves nothing visible
  for the third condition's means of challenge to reach. The relation
  stays `bounds` for that reason ([ARG-tmpsh7he](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpsh7he.md) gives the argument). On the
  third condition, the book both supports and qualifies: plural
  institutions make "possibilities of escape and redress" calculable (ch.
  2), but "Free election of masters does not abolish the masters or the
  slaves" (ch. 1), and a channel for complaint can integrate by turning a
  general grievance into "a treatable and tractable incident" (ch. 4).
  What "effective" means in the third condition is pressed, not settled.
  Status unchanged; whether the title's last clause should be restated at
  a later version is the owner's call.
- **Illich, Tools for Conviviality, a two-page excerpt** ([THEORY-tmpww16o](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpww16o.md),
  [NOTE-tmp8ceuj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8ceuj.md)): "Neither a dictatorial proletariat nor a leisure mass can
  escape the dominion of constantly expanding industrial tools." It places
  the constraint in the tools, whoever controls them, another limit of the
  narrowed test; it carries `bounds` to this claim.

## Note of 2026-11-13: stand-ins, read

- **Crawford, the 2017 interview** ([THEORY-tmp8m1e4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp8m1e4.md), [NOTE-tmpp5kql](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpp5kql.md)). Corroborates, as a reported case of all three conditions in Crawford's diagnosis, not argued: smart cities "controlled by what could be seen as a cartel of ICT companies" (discretion), where "citizens have become more and more captive and dependent in their everyday life" (essentiality), and his call for public scrutiny of "the intention and effects of all that programming" (accountability lacking). "This power belongs to those who design our environment."
- **Illich, Energy and Equity** ([THEORY-tmpyot08](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyot08.md), [NOTE-tmp0cqdl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0cqdl.md)). Bounds it, beside the excerpt of Tools for Conviviality ([THEORY-tmpww16o](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpww16o.md)): radical monopoly is "hidden, entrenched, and structuring", a dependence no one holds, outside the claim's test since version 2 ([ARG-tmpsh7he](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpsh7he.md)). Illich's shoe case straddles the boundary: the industry holds no discretion, but "Teachers or party officials" who deny access to the barefoot do.
- **Winner** ([THEORY-tmpvlt2n](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpvlt2n.md)): consistent with the narrowing, no relation. The bomb's "centralized, rigidly hierarchical chain of command" is authority inside a system, not discretion over dependents outside it; practical necessity ("Fine, but that’s no way to run a railroad") is how accountability erodes, which bears on the third condition; the tomato harvester's concentration, which no one intended, is the impersonal constraint the claim puts outside its test.
- **Dijkema's review** ([THEORY-tmpnm1gx](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpnm1gx.md)) and **Vallor's Noema essay** ([THEORY-tmpt34ma](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpt34ma.md)): leave it open; "monopolized attention" is market power over attention, and the ideology Vallor names is impersonal.

## Note of 2026-11-13: the third condition in the record's terms, and a scaling rule

- **[CLAIM-tmpub743](CLAIM-tmpub743.md)** (the assistant's) checks whether the third condition
  is the failure of [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)'s legibility, volitional efficacy or
  correctability for the dependent's claims. In part: such a failure at the
  controllers leaves the dependent without effective influence and
  challenge, and at every party able to constrain them without
  protection, but [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) states necessity only, so the converse is not
  derived, and justified withdrawal is exit, which none of the three
  covers. On the natural reading of "lacks effective means of protection,
  influence, challenge or justified withdrawal", as lacking all four, the
  condition also needs exit to fail.
- **[CLAIM-tmpwgiwq](CLAIM-tmpwgiwq.md)** (A's, from A13 §VI.3) puts a slope beside this
  threshold: the accountability owed rises with how indispensable the
  arrangement has become, the first condition.
