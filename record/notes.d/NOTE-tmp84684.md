# NOTE-tmp84684: Orosz 2025, software job openings at a five-year low

## Contribution

(Blog post; no abstract.) Using Indeed's aggregated postings index (January 2020 = 100) via FRED, the author shows software development vacancies at about 65% of the 2020 baseline in the US, with similar drops in the UK, France and Germany and an exception in Australia. He compares other sectors and weighs explanations: interest rates, US Section 174 amortization, over-hiring during the pandemic, GenAI tooling, and smaller teams. He also questions how representative Indeed's data is.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Headline figures (first section): 65% of the January 2020 level, 3.5× fewer than the mid-2022 peak, 8% down year on year. Cross-country charts follow.
- The author argues Section 174 cannot be the main driver, because the drop began in 2022 and also appears outside the US.
- "Comparison with other industries": all jobs +10%, banking −7%, sales −8%, marketing −19%, software development −34%, while construction (+25%), accounting (+24%) and electrical engineering (+20%) grew.
- "Why have software dev job vacancies dropped?": interest rates are the main explanation. GenAI impact is described as uncertain, with companies possibly in "wait and see" mode. Salesforce's flat engineering headcount is noted with its commercial incentive to talk up AI. Linear (25 engineers) and Bluesky (13) are cited as small-team examples.
- Data caveat: Indeed's coverage may be falling. The author cites Microsoft's listings on Indeed as undercounting and treats the data as "directionally correct".
- "What's next?": scenarios for productive smaller teams, stagnation, and LLMs broadening software creation by non-developers. The author expects LLMs contribute "somewhat" to stalled hiring.

## Open questions

- It is an attributed practitioner's reading of labour-market data, not a study. The claims about AI's labour effect are speculative by the author's own account.
- If this topic is filed, the primary sources (Indeed Hiring Lab / FRED series) and peer-reviewed labour-economics work would be better anchors.
