# LIT-1397: Fallibility, reflexivity, and the human uncertainty principle

Soros, G. (2013). Fallibility, reflexivity, and the human uncertainty
principle. *Journal of Economic Methodology*, 20(4), 309–329.
doi:10.1080/1350178X.2013.859415.

Read in full on 2026-10-29 ([NOTE-1362](../notes.d/NOTE-1362.md)).

## Why this is here

[CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md) needs the general condition under which a judgement's
circulation bears on its own truth. Soros's reflexivity is one statement of
it: a view held by participants is both an attempt to know a situation and
an intervention in it. His point that the two functions interfere is the
reason [CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md) keeps visibility as evidence about a future and not
as a warrant: where circulation shapes the outcome, the outcome is open,
and a forecast's popularity can make it more likely or, through
counter-moves, less.
