# NOTE-140: Gao — The Meaning of the Wave Function

## Contribution

Gao asks whether the wave function is ontic, epistemic or something else, and, if ontic, what physical state it represents. Part one reviews the rival views critically, gives a new argument for the ontic view from protective measurements, and derives the free Schrödinger equation from spacetime translation invariance. Part two interprets the wave function as describing random discontinuous motion (RDM) of particles. Part three asks whether this ontology accounts for definite experience and how it fares under relativity.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Contents (pp. iv–vi): ch. 2 covers ψ-epistemic views and the ψ-ontology theorems (PBR, Hardy), and ch. 3 criticises the nomological view. Ch. 4 gives the protective-measurement proof of reality, ch. 5 derives the Schrödinger equation, and chs. 6–7 cover charge density and RDM. Ch. 8 treats the measurement problem and a discrete energy-conserving collapse model, with Penrose and CSL analysed. Ch. 9 treats relativity and a preferred Lorentz frame.
- Preface (pp. 1–2): the RDM idea goes back to Gao (1993). The main ideas were discussed at workshops hosted by the *International Journal of Quantum Foundations*.
- Epilogue (pp. 165–167): protective measurement (Aharonov & Vaidman 1993) gives a definite, non-probabilistic link between the wave function and results. With the ontological-models assumption, this implies ψ is real. He also argues that realism about classical mechanics then requires realism about quantum mechanics for consistency.
- Epilogue (pp. 167–168): wave-function realism in Albert's configuration-space sense struggles with our 3D impressions. RDM develops the Monton/Lewis "property of particles in 3D space" suggestion and should in principle be experimentally distinguishable from a field ontology, via self-interaction of charged wavepackets.

## Open questions

- A leading ψ-ontic argument (protective measurement), which should be checked against the critical literature on protective measurement. Whether the ontological-models assumption does the real work is the key question.
- RDM is a minority ontology. Chen ([LIT-204](../literature.d/LIT-204.md)) lists Gao (2017) among the book-length treatments. Compare with Dorato's events ([LIT-176](../literature.d/LIT-176.md)) and Allori's functionalism ([LIT-200](../literature.d/LIT-200.md)).
- Chapter 3's critique of the nomological view bears on Allori ([LIT-200](../literature.d/LIT-200.md)).
