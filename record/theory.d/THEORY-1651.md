# THEORY-1651: Liang and colleagues estimate, with a corpus-level maximum-likelihood method, that after ChatGPT's release between 6.5% and 16.9% of peer-review text at major ML conferences was substantially modified by LLMs, with no significant rise in Nature-portfolio reviews, and that such text is more common in reviews written near the deadline, with low confidence, without citations, by reviewers who do not answer rebuttals, and in reviews most similar to the paper's other reviews.

## The account

Liang and colleagues estimate, with a corpus-level maximum-likelihood method, that after ChatGPT's release between 6.5% and 16.9% of peer-review text at major ML conferences was substantially modified by LLMs, with no significant rise in Nature-portfolio reviews, and that such text is more common in reviews written near the deadline, with low confidence, without citations, by reviewers who do not answer rebuttals, and in reviews most similar to the paper's other reviews.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-1757](../notes.d/NOTE-1757.md); its key results are quoted there with locations.
