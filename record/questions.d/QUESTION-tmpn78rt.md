# QUESTION-tmpn78rt: When a collective reproduces its organization through the replacement of all its members, what must stay causally continuous for the later organization to be the earlier one persisting, and not a similar one produced anew, and how is the question settled when several later collectives each continue it in a different respect?

## Why it is a question

A5 §7: "**When a collective reproduces its organization through
replacement of constituents, what must remain causally continuous for the
later organization to count as persistence of the earlier collective
rather than production of a similar new one?**" A5 noted that it "does not
presume that a conserved scalar γ exists. It does not equate reproduction
with identity. And it is compatible with competing answers from
organizational closure, causal continuity, structural stability, and
participant recognition."

It sits between [QUESTION-035](QUESTION-035.md)'s "how long does it last" and
[QUESTION-043](QUESTION-043.md)'s coherence measure for a person whose life story changes.
A5: "Those two questions nearly meet at the fandom example."

The branching part comes from the Washington Senators ([CASE-tmpzotci](../cases.d/CASE-tmpzotci.md)):
franchise, city and supporters each have a different successor. A2: "This
suggests that fandom identity is not always an equivalence relation with
sharp boundaries." A4's weaker and safer form: "Multiple successors do not
imply that numerical identity itself branches. The weaker and more
defensible claim concerns plural successor relations." [CLAIM-002](../claims.d/CLAIM-002.md) holds
the textual analogue, identity across retellings as a family of relations
(artifact, transmission, function).

## A candidate answer for the branching part, not filed as a claim

A4's H5, in its weak form: "When a collective's institutional,
geographical, genealogical, and cultural continuity relations diverge,
multiple later collectives can possess genuine but different relations of
succession to the earlier collective, without all being numerically
identical to it."

- **Limit (A4).** "Multiple successors do not imply that numerical
  identity itself branches. The weaker and more defensible claim concerns
  plural successor relations."
- **Defeated if (A4).** The respects "either consistently select the same
  successor in relevant cases, or the account cannot give a non-arbitrary
  explanation of what each relation preserves."
- **The respects, from the Senators case** ([CASE-tmpzotci](../cases.d/CASE-tmpzotci.md)):

  | respect | what carries it | successor of the original Senators |
  |---|---|---|
  | franchise | the league's legal succession | the Minnesota Twins |
  | civic role | the city's place in the league | the expansion Senators |
  | interpersonal transmission | supporters, families, the press | divided, unknown |
  | narrative and place | the city's baseball history | the 2005 Nationals, by claim |

- **An asymmetry (A2).** A league can designate the franchise successor by
  convention, but "No comparable authority can compel all supporters to
  adopt an authorized successor allegiance": franchise succession is
  official, fandom succession "distributed and negotiated".
- **Why not a claim.** A5 §6 advised "Case first". The record has one
  documented case and no data on how supporters divided. It becomes a
  claim when a second case is filed, or when the owner says so.

## What would count as an answer

A criterion that distinguishes, in stated cases, numerical continuation,
branching descent and independent reconstitution (A5 §6), and that is not
satisfied by every causal descendant. It should return verdicts on the
three Senators lineages and on the 2005 Nationals, whose claim on old
Washington baseball is narrative rather than organizational. Candidate
materials named in the exchange: organizational routines (Feldman and
Pentland, [LIT-tmp43amb](../literature.d/LIT-tmp43amb.md)), imprinting (Marquis and Tilcsik, [LIT-tmpr346c](../literature.d/LIT-tmpr346c.md)),
identity trajectories (Cloutier and Ravasi, [LIT-tmpnvksw](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnvksw.md)), all unread; and the record's own closure reading ([THEORY-221](../theory.d/THEORY-221.md)),
with A5's caution that "recruiting and cultural transmission may
regenerate characteristics while depending heavily on external causes".
