# CLAIM-tmp2tzy8: Governance can improve and still fall behind what it governs

## The claim

A4 §14: "The danger might not be that AI develops while humans decline in
absolute terms. It might be that AI-mediated organizations develop faster
than the human capacities required to govern them." It ties this to "the
inquiry's work on timescale separation, response deadlines,
reconfiguration costs and adaptive readiness", and concludes: "A
governance system can be improving and still be becoming inadequate. That
observation might be more important for alignment than the deskilling
hypothesis itself."

## What it needs

The second sentence is true by arithmetic once there is a required level
that grows: if held capacity grows at rate g and required capacity at
d > g, the shortfall grows at d − g while the held capacity improves. The
claim's content is therefore the premise that the required capacity grows
with productive complexity, and the comparison, that this gap matters
more than an absolute decline. Neither is measured. What counts as
required is relative to a declared demand, as for TERM-167's M_D, and who
declares it is QUESTION-485's question.

## What it does not say

That human capacity does not decline; A4 §§6–9 keep atrophy as one
trajectory among four (CLAIM-tmpz33db). And the claim concerns the
constitutional capabilities of A4 §12 (TERM-tmp35z0s), not all of them.
