# CLAIM-1224: The unit of scholarly contribution is not the unit of publication: a paper is a communication artifact and a contribution a change in the state of knowledge, and corrections, narrowings, newly found dependencies, integrations, maintenance and retirements become recognizable as contributions once claims and their revisions have identities apart from the manuscripts that present them

## The claim

A21 of the exchange "Hyper article interpretation"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b064f1b-9ea8-83e8-951e-634ed41675b6), under
"The unit of contribution is not the unit of publication":

> A paper is a communication artifact.
>
> A contribution is some change to the state of knowledge.

A contribution "can also involve showing that an earlier result was wrong.
Or narrowing a claim to the domain where it actually holds. Or identifying a
previously unrecognized dependency between two theories. Or improving a
method so that other people can use it reliably. These things don't always
produce independently publishable papers." Hence "giving claims, arguments,
results, and their revisions identities independent of the manuscripts that
present them": "If the contribution is identifiable, we can recognize it
without requiring somebody to package it as a new paper."

Two further parts of A21 belong to the same claim:

- **Retired claims carry content.** "The original claim can be superseded
  without losing the information learned from investigating it." In a
  record, a superseded claim stays connected to what replaced it, so the
  correction says something about the theory's boundary that the surviving
  wording does not.
- **Maintenance needs an end condition.** "Sometimes good maintenance means
  retiring a bad claim, merging redundant lines of work, or demonstrating
  that an avenue of investigation has been exhausted. Otherwise, we'd simply
  replace *publish more papers* with *grow your research corpus forever*."

## A misstatement in the turn

A21's own example: "An early theory of translation turns out to be too
strong. Rather than disappearing, the old claim remains connected to the
objection that defeated it and the narrower claim that replaced it." In the
record, [CLAIM-006](CLAIM-006.md) is Superseded by [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md). Its objection, [CLAIM-004](CLAIM-004.md), is
still Proposed, because it "gives no criterion" for its own distinction. So
the record holds that the claim was replaced, not that the objection
defeated it. A16 had this right ("remains *Proposed*"). The example still
illustrates the claim, which needs only the supersession.

## What it does not say

- Not that every edit is a contribution. A21: "This doesn't mean everybody
  who edits a document gets equal credit."
- Not where credit attaches. That is [CLAIM-1273](CLAIM-1273.md) (A5): at the level
  where the contribution occurred, with a citation naming the inference and
  its version. This claim says which contributions there are to credit.
- Not how contributions are to be weighed. That is [CLAIM-1223](CLAIM-1223.md) and
  [CLAIM-1229](CLAIM-1229.md).
- Who contributed what is something a record can represent. What any
  collaborator believes it cannot ([CLAIM-668](CLAIM-668.md)). A21's point that stewardship
  is not ownership, and that provenance must tell originator, contributors
  and maintainers apart, meets [CLAIM-617](CLAIM-617.md) on the succession of stewards.
