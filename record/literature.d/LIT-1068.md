# LIT-1068: Outcome Indistinguishability

Dwork, C., Kim, M. P., Reingold, O., Rothblum, G. N. and Yona, G. (2021). Outcome indistinguishability. In *Proceedings of the 53rd Annual ACM SIGACT Symposium on Theory of Computing (STOC 2021)*. [ARXIV-2011.13426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13426)

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-885](../notes.d/NOTE-885.md)). A predictor p̃ is outcome indistinguishable (OI) relative to a class A of distinguishers when no A ∈ A can tell an individual with Nature's outcome from the same individual with an outcome drawn from Ber(p̃_i). The paper sets out four levels by how much access the distinguisher has to p̃: none, the prediction on the sampled individual, oracle queries, or the code. The lower two levels are equivalent to multi-accuracy and multi-calibration over closely related classes. At those levels OI predictors exist with size O(s/ε²), whatever Nature's complexity. At the oracle level, under fine-grained hardness of clique counting or BPP ≠ PSPACE, some efficient distinguisher classes force any OI predictor to be nearly as expensive as Nature's own predictor. That gives a strict separation between oracle access and sample access. OI never requires p̃ ≈ p*. A predictor is good only relative to A, and with the paper's construction a weak A gives a coarse predictor that a richer A would refute. The paper covers binary outcomes, a fixed representation of individuals and single samples (multi-sample OI is left mostly to future work).

## Why this is here

The owner asked whether material downstream of computational indistinguishability, or the signal detection literature, could help with [CLAIM-302](../claims.d/CLAIM-302.md) and the bounded-observer questions. Read for [QUESTION-103](../questions.d/QUESTION-103.md). It makes "a model is sufficient for a bounded observer" precise, which [CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md) and [QUESTION-104](../questions.d/QUESTION-104.md) reach for. It is machine-learning theory and carries the anthology flag; it is filed here for that question ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md), [ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md)).

The reading ([NOTE-885](../notes.d/NOTE-885.md)) adds and narrows. How strong an observer is depends on its access to the predictor as well as its budget: with oracle or code access a cheap observer can force the predictor to be as complex as nature, which qualifies [QUESTION-104](../questions.d/QUESTION-104.md). The paper says nothing about signal detection, and proves no minimal or unique sufficient partition.
