# CLAIM-tmpuxcp6: When an organization's record of winning moves cycles, the failure is organizational nonintegrability, distinct from a perspective's own preference instability and from switching of the governing regime; it becomes organizational tension only when realized as costly reversal against demands the organization maintains, and cognitive dissonance only when that tension is detected and appraised as aversive; learning can relieve it by accommodation, by entrenched precedence or by cheap periodic coordination, which observed coherence does not tell apart, and ambivalence and nonintegrability entail neither one another

## The claim

The owner, 2026-10-23: "rather than asserting potentials always have to
exist, could we characterize cycles as a kind of internal tension or
cognitive dissonance?"

The record already says when a potential exists: exactly when the finite
relation of observed wins is acyclic ([TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md), [CLAIM-434](CLAIM-434.md)). What it lacked
was a reading of the cyclic case.

**The structural fact.** Three perspectives, each transitive: productivity
W ≻ R ≻ S, recovery R ≻ S ≻ W, belonging S ≻ W ≻ R. Pairwise majority
decides W over R, R over S and S over W. No strict ordering agrees with all
three wins, though no perspective is inconsistent: Condorcet's cycle. Call
a set of locally effective relations that no single transitive ordering
over the declared options can represent *organizationally nonintegrable*.
Its degree, under declared weights, is the minimum weighted feedback-arc
fraction of the window's wins, zero exactly when an ordinal potential
exists ([TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md)). Not the cyclic part of a Hodge decomposition, which can
be nonzero on an acyclic record ([CLAIM-434](CLAIM-434.md)).

**Three instabilities, not one.**

| kind | where it lies | diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| preference instability | one perspective reverses its own ordering | does the same process reverse under matched conditions? |
| collective nonintegrability | perspectives' rankings combine into cyclic decisions | can all contested wins fit one ordering? |
| governance instability | the governing regime switches over time | does control keep switching so no course is sustained? |

The Condorcet example has the second and neither of the others. A fixed
cyclic rule need not oscillate if the sequence of choices that would
traverse the cycle never arises; conversely, governance can oscillate
while each moment's decisions admit an ordering.

**From structure to tension to dissonance.** Cyclic structure ≠ realized
cycling ≠ costly tension. A cycle becomes organizational tension when each
win undoes the last: recurrent arbitration, reversed commitments,
switching costs, abandoned courses. Even realized cycling can be healthy,
as legitimate alternation of work, rest and company is, so the line
between periodic coordination and costly reversal needs reference to the
demands maintained ([TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md), [CLAIM-368](CLAIM-368.md)'s frustration), not graph theory.
Dissonance is a further step: on the action-based model (Harmon-Jones),
conflicting cognitions are aversive chiefly when they imply incompatible
actions. That fits, but only conditionally: unresolved consequential
conflict plus its detection and aversive appraisal can yield dissonance.
Dissonance can also arise with no three-option cycle, from acting against
a belief. "Cognitive dissonance" keeps its psychological meaning;
nonintegrability and tension name the substrate.

**What learning does to a cycle.** Learned governance ([CLAIM-tmp0qupq](CLAIM-tmp0qupq.md)) need
not remove a cycle. It can

- accommodate: a conditional policy over richer contexts admits an
  ordering that the projected labels did not;
- entrench: one perspective learns to dominate, choices become
  transitive, dissent goes frustrated or loses access;
- coordinate: a cheap, predictable periodic schedule keeps the cycle.

The first two can produce the same acyclic record, so observed coherence
cannot distinguish accommodation from suppression ([CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md);
[CLAIM-tmpd6q2w](CLAIM-tmpd6q2w.md)).

**Ambivalence is not nonintegrability.** Ambivalence is coexisting
evaluations of opposed significance. It occurs where one perspective
consistently prevails, and a cycle need not involve opposed valences at
all. [CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md)'s reading of ambivalence as "no ordering prevails" should be
narrowed accordingly ([CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md)'s note of 2026-10-23).


The action-based model of dissonance is [LIT-tmp2lytb](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2lytb.md), unread.

## What it does not say

- That a potential is the default and a cycle a pathology. They are
  regimes.
- That the degree of nonintegrability measures tension or felt discomfort.
  How it relates to arbitration cost, reversal frequency, persistence,
  negative affect and reported dissonance is empirical.

## Note of 2026-10-24: at which level a cycle lies, and a fourth relief

From the evaluation and learning-in-games readings filed the same day.
- **Mean dynamics and runs.** A cycle in the mean dynamic is not a cycle in
  any actual run. Cross learners lock on to a pure profile even in matching
  pennies ([THEORY-tmphhvqf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphhvqf.md)). Conversely, Sato and Crutchfield's fixed
  learning rules produce cycles and chaos with no switching of regime
  ([THEORY-tmpev90a](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpev90a.md)). An apparent alternation between cooperation and
  competition can be one chaotic regime, which bears on the
  governance-instability row of this claim's table.
- **Level.** Collapsing each strongly connected component of the win graph
  to a point leaves an acyclic quotient ([NOTE-tmpxufhw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxufhw.md)). So an ordinal
  potential always exists one level up, and this claim should say whether
  its cycles lie within components or between them. The minimum
  feedback-arc fraction and the component structure are different facts.
- **Any cycle can arise.** Where parties can recognize each other before
  the outcome is fixed, every pattern of wins and losses is realizable
  ([THEORY-tmp2ela3](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp2ela3.md)).
- **A fourth way to relieve nonintegrability.** In matching pennies, a
  harmonic game, LOLA's learning-aware learners settled at the mixed
  equilibrium where naive learners kept cycling ([THEORY-tmpaod5d](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpaod5d.md)). Taking
  account of how the others learn joins accommodation, entrenchment and
  periodic coordination. Mixing, as the value between populations orders
  hull-nested populations, is a related route ([THEORY-tmpxh6cj](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxh6cj.md)).
