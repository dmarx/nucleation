The owner supplied the text of van Rijsbergen's *The Geometry of Information
Retrieval* ([LIT-262](../../record/literature.d/LIT-262.md)): front matter, Preface, Prologue, chapters 1–6 and the
index. The appendices and the annotated bibliography were not included.

The book had been seeded that morning from the publisher's excerpts and one
review. It is now read, and it is `Active` with a `Read` note ([NOTE-239](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-239.md)).

## Two corrections to what the record said

1. **Riesz is the book's own point.** The seed attributed the
   functional-as-vector identification to Kantor's review. Chapter 6 (p. 74)
   states it, citing Riesz & Nagy: query matching is a linear functional, and
   so an inner product with a vector.

2. **The book models non-commuting measurement in retrieval.** The
   contextuality curation entry of this date reported Kantor's review as
   saying the book has no retrieval analogue of non-commuting measurements.
   That is not true of the book:
   - relevance and aboutness are explicitly allowed not to commute
     (AR ≠ RA), and the sequence A → R → A can change the second answer
     (pp. 21–22);
   - chapters 2 and 5 develop compatibility and the failure of distribution.

   What the book lacks is data showing these effects in retrieval, not the
   notion. That entry is left as written, since the journal is history, and
   this one corrects it.

## What the reading adds to the record's threads

- **The book never discusses contextuality.** It is therefore not tagged
  `contextuality`. But its order effects are exactly the question-order
  setting that Contextuality-by-Default analyses ([LIT-264](../../record/literature.d/LIT-264.md), [THEORY-013](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-013.md)). That
  analysis found a Hilbert-space model of question order to predict no
  contextuality. Whether retrieval judgements show the book's order effects
  at all is untested, by the book or anyone the record holds.
- **GNS.** The book does not mention it and works in the opposite direction,
  from state to probability via Gleason. [THEORY-004](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-004.md)'s GNS link stays an
  inference.
- **The strongest parts are two chapters.** Chapter 5 proves that the
  subspace conditional is a Stalnaker conditional, equal to the Sasaki hook.
  Chapter 6 recasts standard retrieval operations as trace calculations.
  Everything else is a language offered, not a result shown.
