# NOTE-tmpe2f2g: Kermavnar et al. — A systematic map of need-fulfillment scales

## Contribution

A catalogue: the original self-report scales of basic or fundamental
psychological need fulfillment for a healthy general population, with each
scale's theory, need states, domain, structure, response format and
validation, from 1976 to 2023. Its use is as a finding aid and as a list of
the field's measurement faults, which it states plainly. It does not
evaluate any scale's validity beyond the steps the developers reported.

## Key insight

The instruments are where the field's conceptual distinctions get made or
lost. Four states are distinguished in theory (Table 1): satisfaction and
frustration, which are experiences, and support and thwarting, which are
conditions in the social environment. The scales mostly measure the first,
often mix the others into it, and the oldest and most used sometimes label a
reverse-worded satisfaction item "frustration". "If our review highlights one
key point, it is the urgent need to reduce this ambiguity and confusion"
(§5).

## Assumptions

- **Scope (§2.2).** Healthy general populations in a social context; scales
  for migrants, refugees, patients, extreme conditions and
  technology-mediated fulfillment are excluded (§4.7). English-language,
  peer-reviewed papers; Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, ProQuest.
- **A scale is "original"** if a study developed and validated it; adapted
  versions are "secondary" (§2.2).
- **The review takes no position** on the one-dimensional versus
  two-dimensional model: "We neither endorse nor critique this theoretical
  shift … the evidence remains inconclusive" (§5).

## Key results

- **Counts (§3).** 121 eligible studies: 32 primary, describing 31 original
  scales (the BMPN has two), and 89 secondary studies adapting 20 of them.
  23 scales are SDT-based, 8 on alternative theories (Murray, Maslow, ERG,
  the Psychology of Working Theory, DRAMMA).
- **Most cited (§3.1)**, Web of Science and Scopus on 21 August 2023: the
  BNSW-S (Deci et al. 2001; 1,045 and 1,142), the BPNSFS (Chen et al. 2015;
  1,038 and 1,114), the "Sheldon scale" (Sheldon et al. 2001; 819 and
  1,069).
- **States measured (§3.2.2).** Satisfaction only, 21; satisfaction and
  frustration, 4 (BPNSFS, GNSF, NSFS, PNSS-S); support, 3; frustration only,
  1 (PNFS-PA, Chung et al. 2020); thwarting, 1 (PNTS, Bartholomew et al.
  2011); support and thwarting, 1 (IBQ, Rocchi et al. 2017). "All the
  alternative questionnaires exclusively focused on assessing need
  satisfaction."
- **History (§3.1).** Thwarting enters the scales in 2011 (Bartholomew et
  al.), satisfaction-and-frustration in 2015 (Chen et al.), frustration
  alone in 2020. "Need unfulfillment" (Bhavsar et al. 2020) was not
  confirmed as a separate dimension.
- **Needs (Table 4).** Autonomy in 27 scales, relatedness 24, competence 23;
  more than 30 others appear once or a few times (self-esteem 2, novelty 2,
  safety 2, morality 1, meaning 1, and so on).
- **Structure (§3.2.3).** Most scales have three factors (15); seven have
  six; items range from 6 to 68. Unipolar response scales 14, bipolar 15.
- **Validation (§3.3).** Content validity assessed in 15 SDT studies and 5
  alternative ones; quantitative content analysis in five; face validity
  never quantified. CFA in 23 studies, nine without prior exploratory
  analysis; one CFA on the same sample as the EFA. Some CFAs had fewer than
  100 respondents (DRAMMA, 85; one of BPNS's, 81; one of GNSF's, 84).
- **Faults (§4.2.2–4.3.3).**
  - "Satisfaction/frustration" and "support/thwarting" "were at times used
    interchangeably" (Costa et al. 2015; Bhavsar et al. 2020 named).
  - Double-barrelled items mixing frustration and thwarting, e.g. "I feel
    incompetent (frustration) because of the things I am told (thwarting)",
    adapted from the PNFS-PA.
  - Items meant for satisfaction that assess thwarting, e.g. "There were
    people telling me what I had to do" in the BMPN (§4.3.3).
  - The BPNSFS: Chen (2013)'s version measured satisfaction only, with four
    positively and four negatively coded items per need; "the 12 negatively
    coded items were repurposed to represent need frustration" (§4.2.2).
    The review quotes Murphy et al. 2023 (p. 129): the distinction "is
    likely primarily driven by item-keying direction".
  - Ten satisfaction scales use reverse-scored negative items, which
    Sheldon and Gunz read as "absence of satisfaction" and Van den Broeck et
    al. as "need frustration" (§4.3.3).
- **Recommendations.** "Positive descriptions of need frustration rather
  than negative descriptions of need satisfaction" (§4.3.3). The IBQ and
  the GNSF are named "examples of good practice" (§5).

## Concepts

- **Need fulfillment** — the review's umbrella for all four states.
- **Need satisfaction / frustration** — experiences, "intrapersonal".
- **Need support / thwarting** — the social environment's "active and
  direct way of fostering/undermining one's needs", "interpersonal"
  (§4.2.2, after Vansteenkiste & Ryan 2013).
- **Need dissatisfaction**, **need unfulfillment** — low satisfaction
  without frustration; the latter "results from need-indifferent
  interpersonal behaviors" (§4.2.3). Not established as a separate
  dimension.
- **Cross-paths** — support with frustration, or thwarting with
  satisfaction, e.g. "The people in my life pressure me to do things their
  way" with "I do things the way I want" (§4.2.2).

- **Bartholomew et al. 2011** (LIT-tmp6as8m): the PNTS, the one thwarting

- **Sheldon et al. 2001** (LIT-tmpd3l9z): the "Sheldon scale", ten candidate
  needs; noted as having failed to extract its expected ten factors and

- **Elson et al. 2023**, "the toothbrush problem", as the motive.
- **Vansteenkiste, Ryan and Soenens 2020** (LIT-561): the nine criteria are
  mentioned (§1, "five 'basic' and four 'associated' criteria") and not
  applied.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-tmpee04a (what would show a need is a need).** The review does
  not answer it and says the field has not: "a current lack of consensus of
  what constitutes a fundamental psychological need" (§1), with typologies
  differing on innateness, universality and interrelatedness (§4.1.1). It
  adds one fact the question can use: every scale it found is a self-report
  of experience or of perceived environment, and none measures a functional
  deficit. So the question's proposed answer, "a functional deficit, not a
  report, that follows thwarting of the candidate need", has no instrument
  among the 31. The review also records that candidate needs are proposed
  because "the frustration of ARC alone may not adequately predict
  individuals' ill-being" (§4.1.2): the well-being route the question
  doubts, stated as the motive.
- **QUESTION-131.** The review's four-cell table (Table 1) is the
  psychology's version of the question's rows, with one difference: it
  places thwarting in the environment and frustration in the person, where
  the question places thwarting on a constituent's demand. Its cross-paths
  (thwarting without frustration, frustration without thwarting) are the
  separations in each direction the question asks for, stated as examples,
  not shown. The review also says measures of external conditions "do not
  always predict personal experiences" (§1).
- **THEORY-052.** It corroborates Murphy et al.'s point that the BPNSFS
  frustration scale was not built for the two-dimensional construct, and
  adds that scales for frustration proper are few and recent (2020). It
  does not assess whether need frustration predicts psychopathology.
- **THEORY filed from this reading**: THEORY-tmpbkq7u (Active), what the
  review counted and the faults it documents. It is also a second source
  for THEORY-tmp62iwr.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **The search is narrow by design.** Title words "need*" and one of six
  measurement words; English; healthy populations. Scales of need
  frustration in clinical samples are out of scope (§4.7).
- **Descriptive only.** No scale's validity is tested or compared; the
  "good practice" verdict rests on wording and format.
- **Small inconsistencies in the text.** The overview is said to cover
  "1978 to the present" (§1), but the earliest scale is dated 1976 (§3.1,
  §4). The BMPN is classed SDT-based in Table 3 and counted among the
  "alternative questionnaires" for its autonomy, competence and relatedness
  items in §3.2.1. The conclusion's "24 eligible primary studies based on
  SDT" squares with the 23 SDT scales only because one scale has two
  primary studies.
- **Murphy et al.'s conclusion is restated more firmly than Murphy states
  it**: they "deemed BPNSFS invalid for use as a measure of the
  dual-dimension theory precisely for this reason" (§4.3.3). Murphy et al.
  say "probably" and that the matter cannot be settled with existing data.

## Open questions

- Would a frustration scale written with positive descriptions of
  frustration (the review's recommendation) separate from satisfaction once
  keying is balanced?
- How should the midpoint of a bipolar frustration or thwarting scale be
  read (§4.3.4)? The review leaves it open.
