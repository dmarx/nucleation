# NOTE-180: Clark & Chalmers — The Extended Mind

## Contribution

Where does the mind end and the world begin? Rather than the skin-and-skull boundary or Putnam–Burge semantic externalism, Clark and Chalmers defend "active externalism": external features that are coupled to the organism and drive cognition in the here and now are part of the cognitive system. They first argue that cognitive processes can extend into the environment, and then that mental states, specifically standing beliefs, can too. The paper closes by drawing out consequences for language, social cognition and the self.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 Extended Cognition: three Tetris-style rotation cases (mental rotation, a rotate button, a neural implant) are held to be on a par. Kirsh and Maglio's "epistemic actions" support this. The parity-style principle: if a part of the world functions as a process which, "were it done in the head", we would count as cognitive, then it is part of the cognitive process.
- §3 Active Externalism: a coupled system in which removing the external component degrades competence just as removing part of the brain would. This is contrasted with Putnam and Burge's distal, historical, passive externalism. Language is treated as a "cognitive vortex", on the analogy of a tuna exploiting eddies.
- §4 From Cognition to Mind: Inga recalls from memory that the museum is on 53rd Street, while Otto, who has Alzheimer's, consults a notebook. The differences between the cases (perceptual access, phenomenology) are argued to be "shallow", so Otto believes it too. Denying Otto's belief pushes one toward counting only occurrent beliefs.
- §5 criteria: the resource is a constant in the agent's life, directly available, automatically endorsed and (more arguably) consciously endorsed in the past. The Internet "is likely to fail on multiple counts", and intermediate cases may be indeterminate.
- §5 extensions: socially extended belief (an interdependent couple, the Woodens as "memory bank"). Language is "not a mirror of our inner states but a complement to them". An extended self: Otto "is best regarded as an extended system". There are moral consequences, since interfering with someone's environment may be like interfering with their person.

## Open questions

- It is the foundational text for extended-cognition questions about AI tools: whether a user's LLM assistant or memory store can satisfy the availability, trust and endorsement criteria. Birch's [LIT-111](../literature.d/LIT-111.md) and Register's [LIT-138](../literature.d/LIT-138.md) (BCIs as body parts) sit downstream of it.
- The trust and endorsement criteria are the pressure point for AI-mediated cognition. Hallucination-prone tools ([LIT-212](../literature.d/LIT-212.md)) would fail "automatic endorsement" only if users stop endorsing, which cuts both ways.
