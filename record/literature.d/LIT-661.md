# LIT-661: Git Re-Basin: Merging Models modulo Permutation Symmetries

Samuel K. Ainsworth, Jonathan Hayase and Siddhartha Srinivasa (2023),
*International Conference on Learning Representations* (ICLR 2023) —
[ARXIV-2209.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836)

## Key takeaways

- **Alignment removes barriers, where the network is wide enough.** Weight
  matching chooses permutation matrices P_ℓ to maximize Σ_ℓ ⟨W_ℓ^A,
  P_ℓ W_ℓ^B P_{ℓ−1}ᵀ⟩_F. That problem is NP-hard with no polynomial-time
  constant-factor approximation for L > 2 (Lemma 1). Solving for one P_ℓ at
  a time is a linear assignment problem, and cycling through the layers until
  nothing changes terminates (Algorithm 1, Lemma 2). It uses no data and takes
  seconds to minutes: 3 s for an MLP, 33 s for ResNet-50, 194 s for a 32×-wide
  ResNet-20 (App. A.5). After matching, MNIST MLPs and CIFAR-10 ResNet-20s at
  large width show zero barrier, the first zero-barrier connection between
  independently trained ResNets (Figs. 2, 4).
- **Where it fails.** At 1× width neither VGG-16 nor ResNet-20 becomes
  linearly connected (Fig. 4). ImageNet ResNet-50 keeps a barrier, reduced
  67% from naive interpolation (§5.1). Connectivity is absent early in
  training and appears gradually (Fig. 3). The authors conclude that
  permutations are "a necessary piece, though not a complete picture", and
  guess that general linear relationships between activations are at work.
- **A counterexample** (§4, App. A.6). A two-dimensional classification task
  with two hidden layers of two ReLU units has two perfect solutions that no
  permutation connects linearly: one tests x₂ first and x₁ second, the other
  the reverse. LMC is a property of the solutions training reaches, not of
  architectures. The authors note this does not contradict Entezari et al.'s
  conjecture, whose restriction to SGD solutions is "load-bearing".
- **Merging.** ResNet-20s trained on disjoint, biased halves of CIFAR-100
  merge into a model with better test loss than either and better calibration,
  though not better accuracy (§5.4). Merging five MNIST MLPs at once with
  Algorithm 3 lowers test loss by 43% (Table 3).

## Standing in the record

**Second reading under [ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md).** The anthology holds this paper as
[ANTH-LIT-333](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-333.md) (read 2026-09-15, its [ANTH-NOTE-116](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/notes.d/NOTE-116.md)), for practice: a toolkit for
aligning networks before averaging them. This record reads it for the
landscape question: how far it confirms the single-basin conjecture, and what
its negative results (thin models, early training, the counterexample, the
ImageNet barrier) say about the shape of the solution set.

**The anthology entry misdescribes the paper**, as this reading finds it. Per
[ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md) this is reported here and in [NOTE-532](../notes.d/NOTE-532.md), and not edited across the
boundary:

- [ANTH-LIT-333](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-333.md) calls weight matching a "Hungarian algorithm" applied "layer by
  layer" and lists among its limits that "greedy layer-by-layer is not
  globally optimal". The paper's weight matching is coordinate descent over
  all layers, repeated until convergence (Algorithm 1). Appendix A.7 argues
  *against* greedy one-pass layer-by-layer matching, with a counterexample,
  and shows that a single pass does much worse.
- It says multi-model merging "requires pairwise anchoring". The paper gives
  a merge-many algorithm (Algorithm 3, App. A.10).
- It reports near-zero barriers "across architectures (MLPs, CNNs, VGGs,
  ResNets)", and merged models "within 1–2%" of the individual ones on
  CIFAR-10. The paper gets zero barriers for VGG-16 and ResNet-20 only at
  large width, none at 1×, and a remaining barrier on ImageNet. This reading
  found no 1–2% figure.

The paper **extends** Entezari et al. ([LIT-652](LIT-652.md)). It states their
conjecture as its own Conjecture 1. Where their simulated annealing lowered
barriers only for shallow nets, its weight matching removes them for wide
ResNets, and its counterexample sharpens their restriction to SGD solutions.
It reads Benton et al. ([LIT-656](LIT-656.md)) as showing that SGD solutions form a
connected volume, and Entezari et al. as conjecturing that this volume is
convex modulo permutation.

Its finding that LMC appears only after training sits uneasily with Entezari
et al.'s Theorem 3.1, which proves it at random initialization. Ferbach et al.
([LIT-680](LIT-680.md)) reconcile the two: the width their bound needs at
initialization is far beyond practical widths. Its picture of connectivity
emerging during training parallels Frankle et al. ([LIT-654](LIT-654.md)), who saw the
same for copies sharing an initialization.

The paper anticipates using merge-many "along the lines of 'model soups'";
the anthology holds model soups as [ANTH-LIT-675](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-675.md). The paper carries merging
instructions, hence the `anthology-candidate` flag ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)), although the
anthology already holds it.
