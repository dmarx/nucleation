# CASE-134: Northstar's commander handoff: twelve hours into an incident the commander goes off shift and hands command to a colleague, and the response continues without disruption and with no one continuous through it

## The case

The owner, at U22: "A CASE we explored some time ago (in part to probe
notions of corporate phenomenology) that could be worth revisiting now
was an engineering organization responding to a critical internal
incident." A22 found Northstar ([CASE-023](CASE-023.md) and its variants [CASE-002](CASE-002.md),
[CASE-003](CASE-003.md) and [CASE-036](CASE-036.md)) and proposed this variant:

"Imagine Northstar has been responding to a major failure for twelve
hours. The incident commander goes off shift and transfers command to a
colleague. The original commander no longer directs the response. The new
commander inherits the current incident state, ongoing obligations,
outstanding hypotheses, and authority to coordinate the teams. The
incident continues without any significant disruption."

Under the Incident Command System such transfers are routine and
formalized ([LIT-1365](../literature.d/LIT-1365.md)).

## What it shows

A22: "**The handoff illustrates continuity of organization without
establishing continuity of experience.**" If one asked where the
response's point of view is, the commander is the tempting answer, and
the handoff defeats it: "Did the supposed corporate experience transfer
from one human being to another? That seems like an implausible conclusion
from the operational facts alone." It also argues "against identifying the
organizational perspective with whichever employee happens to hold the
most comprehensive report".

So the response's organization persists through a change of every
person in its central role, as [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s collectives persist through a
change of members. The representation of one continuous commander, which
reports and logs may carry, is a represented owner ([CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)) in the
small.

## What it does not show

- That the response is a further agent. A37 withdrew the exchange's
  "temporarily constituted collective agent".
- Anything about experience. The case shows only that no person is
  continuous; it neither locates nor rules out experience elsewhere
  ([QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md), Deferred).

## Note of 2026-10-22: what NIMS asks of a handoff

The stipulated handoff can be set against the documented doctrine:
"When command transfers, the transfer process includes a briefing that
captures essential information for continuing safe and effective
operations, and notifying all personnel involved in the incident"
([LIT-1365](../literature.d/LIT-1365.md), p. 22). The doctrine fixes what is transferred as information
and notification; what the case adds is that a briefing transfers the
record of an arrangement, not the capacity the outgoing commander had
built.

## Note of 2026-10-26: records preserved or erased at the handoff (reported)

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CASE-166 — Proposed; the reported study, cited as open -->

T20 of the exchange "Experimental Infrastructure Review" reports running
this case's handoff in a simulation ([CASE-166](CASE-166.md)): "An incident
commander can transfer their role to another employee while
preserving—or losing—the group's records." During a telemetry outage the
records-preserved arm estimated the company's state with an MSE of
0.00411, against 0.03398 with the records erased. But "that improvement in
organizational knowledge often doesn't change the selected action".

That fits this case's note of 2026-10-22: a briefing "transfers the record
of an arrangement, not the capacity the outgoing commander had built".
The records carried the estimate, and what the response did hardly
depended on them. T20 put it the other way round ("preserving a capacity
for interpretation is not equivalent to exercising that capacity
successfully"), calling the records the capacity. T18 also placed
Northstar "already considered in CASE-134"; the company is [CASE-023](CASE-023.md), and
this is its handoff variant. Reported, not reproduced: T20's "PerspectiveLab v2.11" is not held,
and dmarx/PerspectiveLab holds no v2.10 or v2.11 (its latest import is
c54617e, v2.9). The case is unchanged.
