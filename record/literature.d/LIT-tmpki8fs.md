summary: >-
  Akerlof & Kranton (2000). Adds identity, "a person's sense of self", to the utility function: U = U(own actions, others' actions, I), where I depends on one's assigned social categories, how well one's own traits match the category's ideal, and how far one's own and others' actions follow the category's prescriptions. Violating one's prescriptions costs utility (Is); others' violations cost it too (Io), and the loser may pay to "respond", harming the violator. A two-person game shows when such responses deter. Applied to occupational segregation by sex, to "oppositional" identities chosen by the socially excluded, and to wives who earn more yet still do most of the housework, it yields predictions standard models do not. Evidence is illustrative: ethnographies, a 70-household vignette survey, census shares and a Panel Study of Income Dynamics regression. Identity is modelled as self-image to be maintained, not as concern for a group's welfare.
