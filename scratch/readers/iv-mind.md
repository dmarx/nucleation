NB the PDF uses fi/fl ligatures; quotes below are with ligatures normalised to "fi"/"fl".

Summary: Computational functionalism (CF) says executing certain computations suffices for consciousness. Block separates a functional (second-order) role from its first-order "subcomputational" realizer (AND gate built electrically vs mechanically) and names the "meat hypothesis": consciousness depends directly on the biological realizers. Since we can only extrapolate from ourselves, and we have both roles and realizers, extrapolating on roles favours AI and on realizers favours cognitively simple animals; the two candidate classes compete. The major theories (GWT, higher-order pointer, local recurrence, IIT) are "meat-neutral". Answering "realizers are computational too": simulating gravity produces no force; going down the hierarchy eventually reaches non-computational physics. As a guess at what meat is, he turns to the first animals: ctenophores (comb jellies, sister to all animals per 2023 chromosome evidence) have a largely purely electrical nerve net, other animals electrochemical; electrochemical processing seems to have a functional advantage, though neither inhibition (Box 2) nor learning (cnidarian evidence weak) clearly explains it; fluctuations in ionic "chemical soup" and large-scale dynamics (Box 3, Godfrey-Smith) are candidates. Conclusion: modest; some aspect of our biology is necessary for consciousness in us and might be necessary, period; AI could still suffer or have agency without consciousness.

Contribution: a framing (role vs realizer as two extrapolation bases) that makes the AI-vs-simple-animal attribution a forced trade-off, and the label "meat-neutral" for the major theories; an evolutionary pointer (electrical vs electrochemical nervous systems) as where to look for the realizer.

Key results (page = printed TiCS page):
- Abstract, p. 298: "Current theories of consciousness are 'meat-neutral'"
- Role versus realizer, p. 299: "The meat hypothesis is that our consciousness does depend directly on the subcomputational properties"
- p. 300: "the problem with functionalism is that it focuses on the roles, neglecting the realizers"
- p. 301: "The role and the realizer hypotheses are equally plausible"
- p. 301: "From the point of view of the meat hypothesis, functionalism has it backwards"
- p. 298 (margin): "We should be investigating what is special about electrochemical computation."
- First animals, p. 304: "electrochemical processing has a functional advantage over purely electrical processing"
- Concluding remarks, p. 306: "there is an opposition between AI and simple animals"
- p. 307: "There is little doubt that some aspect of our biology is necessary for consciousness in us"
- p. 307: "suffering may not require consciousness"
Assumptions: phenomenal consciousness is real (illusionism set aside, flagged as an outstanding question); attribution must extrapolate from the human case; realizer = realizing mechanism, not stuff (other ions could do); gradualism in the evolution of consciousness.
Concepts: computational functionalism; meat condition / meat hypothesis / "subcomputationalism"; role vs realizer, second- vs first-order property; core vs total realizer; meat-neutral theory; extrapolation base; electrochemical vs electrical nervous system; Myriazoa, ctenophore-sister hypothesis.

Bearings:

- THEORY-507 (Chalmers, organizational invariance via fading qualia, LIT-392): contrast; Block holds the realizer may matter even under identical fine-grained organisation.
- THEORY-252 (CF implies idle clamp changes consciousness, LIT-1000): parallel, another argument against CF.
- THEORY-577 (biological theories struggle to account for hard-problem judgments): contrast; Block concedes the meat hypothesis leaves the Hard Problem but says functionalism only appears to close it.
- THEORY-043 / THEORY-092 / THEORY-095 (from Block's own LIT-407, LIT-642, LIT-634): background; same author's long anti-functionalist line, now as an open necessity hypothesis.
- THEORY-090, THEORY-101 (affective/hedonic origin of consciousness, simple animals): parallel; Block's realizer route also favours simple animals.

Candidate THEORY sentence: Block argues that because we can attribute consciousness to other systems only by extrapolating from ourselves, and our consciousness may depend on our computational roles, on their subcomputational biological realizers, or on both, the major theories of consciousness are meat-neutral and choosing roles favours conscious AI while choosing realizers favours conscious simple animals.
promote_when: an argument or result that shows, for some conscious system, whether its consciousness varies with its computational roles while the biological realizers are held fixed, or with the realizers while the roles are held fixed.

How far it stands in: it is the book's thesis in compact form (the paper says it precedes "a book-length treatment to be published under the title Consciousness Itself (Solms, in press)", i.e. the book that became The Hidden Spring). It carries the brainstem/affect/free-energy core; it lacks the book's narrative, case material, the chapter on building an artificial conscious system, and any revisions after 2019.

Summary: Two impediments to the hard problem are removed. (1) Philosophical: on dual-aspect monism the brain does not cause experience; mind and brain are two appearances of one function, to be described abstractly (mathematically). (2) Scientific: consciousness is not cortical. Decorticate animals and hydranencephalic children are conscious and emotional; small brainstem lesions (parabrachial/ERTAS) abolish consciousness; DBS of PAG/ERTAS evokes affect. So consciousness is generated in the upper brainstem and is fundamentally affective; cortex supplies memory images and stabilises ("binds") consciousness. Mechanism: an extended homeostasis. Systems with a Markov blanket minimise free energy (self-evidencing); this gives proto-selfhood but not feeling. Feeling arises with complexity: multiple homeostatic needs must be compartmentalised (categorical "flavours") and prioritised in novel contexts where action is a bottleneck; this prioritisation is precision-weighting (ω), physiologically ERTAS neuromodulation. Deviation from a settling point (rising uncertainty) is felt as unpleasure, return as pleasure; "consciousness is felt uncertainty". Cognitive consciousness is reconsolidation in progress ("consciousness arises instead of a memory-trace").
Contribution: a localisation (upper brainstem/PAG, not cortex) joined to a formal function (precision optimisation in free-energy minimisation) as one dual-aspect mechanism for affect-first consciousness.

Key results (page = article page):
- p. 2: "the brain does not “produce” or “cause” consciousness"
- p. 3: "who ever heard of an unconscious feeling"
- p. 4: "Consciousness persists in the absence of cerebral cortex, as does volitional behavior."
- p. 5: "the so-called level of consciousness is a function of variational free energy"
- p. 6: "we must conclude that consciousness is generated in the upper brainstem"
- p. 7: "I do not believe that thermostats are conscious."
- p. 7: "In short, consciousness is felt uncertainty."
- p. 10: "there is nothing about such proto-mental dynamics which explains the emergence of feeling"
- p. 11: "Expected free energy just is uncertainty about the consequences of any putative action."
- p. 12: "precision (consciousness) controls the influence of prediction errors on action (motivation) and perception (attention)"
- p. 12: "Consciousness itself is affective."
- p. 13: "it is easy to envision an organism (or machine) in which precision values are set in such a way that the system’s responses to prediction error are automatized"
Assumptions: dual-aspect monism; the double-dissociation logic of lesion localisation; behavioural signs (emotion, preference) as adequate evidence of consciousness in decorticate subjects; Friston's free-energy formalism; homeostasis = minimising free energy.
Concepts: dual-aspect monism; affect as elemental consciousness; ERTAS; PAG "decision triangle" (with superior colliculi); homeostatic/emotional/sensory affects (Panksepp); SEEKING; Markov blanket; precision (ω) as felt affect; compartmentalisation into qualitative "flavours"; felt uncertainty; reconsolidation.

Bearings:
- THEORY-090 (basic affective consciousness realised subcortically, upper brainstem and thalamus, first function alarm; LIT-626, LIT-388): supports, independent clinical and formal case; contrast on function: Solms makes feeling the means of choice in novel contexts, THEORY-090 says "no choice between options".
- THEORY-045 (felt affect = experience of the body's regulatory state; prediction vs map unsettled): supports, taking the prediction side (felt residual prediction error/uncertainty).
- THEORY-093 (Damasio: homeostatic feelings conscious in themselves; LIT-628): parallel; Solms credits Damasio and Panksepp, adds the precision mechanism.
- THEORY-101 (hedonic valence as common currency for trading off options): parallel, but Solms holds the opposite of a common currency: needs must be qualitatively compartmentalised ("10 × X is worth more than 10 × Y").
- CLAIM-440 (valence presupposes contested rankings, common currency as negotiation ordering): parallel to Solms's prioritisation of competing needs in a bottleneck.
- THEORY-882, THEORY-883 (active-inference accounts of consciousness: precision-weighted competition; posteriors that inform policy selection): parallel, same framework, but Solms locates consciousness in affect/precision itself, not in contents.
- THEORY-073 (a Markov blanket does not individuate a system): qualifies Solms's derivation of selfhood from the blanket (p. 10).
- THEORY-098, THEORY-096 (perceptual consciousness in sensory cortex / global workspace): contrast, cortico-centric.
- NOTE-187 (Veit reply citing Solms 2021 PAG "selection triangle"): background; the record already leans on the book secondhand.
Limitations: a hypothesis-and-theory paper; equations are schematic; decorticate consciousness inferred from behaviour; heavy Freud apparatus in footnotes; not the book.

Candidate THEORY sentence: Solms argues that consciousness is generated in the upper brainstem and is fundamentally affective: feeling is the precision-weighting by which an organism that minimises free energy prioritises among its homeostatic needs in unpredicted contexts, so that rising uncertainty about a need is felt as unpleasure and falling uncertainty as pleasure.
promote_when: evidence showing whether loss of the upper-brainstem arousal systems abolishes felt affect while loss of cortex spares it, and whether reported pleasure and unpleasure track decreases and increases in the expected uncertainty about the need at issue.

Summary (from stand-ins): Recht calls "mathematical rationality" the view, built with the first computers after WWII, that decisions under uncertainty are statistical risk analyses: model the unknown as probability, put all goals on one currency, maximise expected return. The core technologies (linear programming, game theory, detection and estimation theory, SGD, neural nets, the randomised trial, information theory) were invented in that window, and computers were built to systematise decisions this way, so people came to be measured against machines. The persistence of this ideal is the "irrational decision": the methods are locally valid ("sweet spots": regulatory bureaucracy, drug approval, transparent allocation, fair games) but fail for most decisions, especially political or value-laden ones. Probability as the foundation of uncertainty fails at comparability (Jaynes's earthquake vs fish-catch: "I don't know"); utility maximisation was refuted as a predictive theory almost at once (Flood and Dresher 1950). He urges mathematical pluralism and a "cyborg" decision-making that uses computational tools grounded in human judgment. LLMs do not depart from this: built by maximum likelihood and A/B-test optimisation, yet natural language is not rational language.
Contribution: an insider's intellectual history tracing current AI and decision science to one 1940s formalisation, with the normative claim that its domain of validity is narrow.
Key results (verbatim):
- (a): "Hence, the mathematical version of rationality reduces all decision-making to a statistical analysis of risk."
- (a): "We decided people should make decisions like computers. The problem is, this decision was irrational."
- (a): "The vast majority of decisions can’t be outsourced to computers."
- (a): "Mathematical rationality helps frame simple, small world decisions"
- (b): "Analytic tools from computer science, philosophy, mathematics, and statistics are inherently local in their validity"
- (b): "It is that such methods have sweet spots"
- (b): "Flood and Dresher proved it unpredictive of human behavior in 1950"
- (c): "The only reasonable answer to Jaynes’ question is: “I don’t know.”"
- (d): "But natural language is not rational language."
Assumptions: rationality has older, non-mathematical senses; usefulness of a formalism is local and pragmatic; political and value questions are not optimisation problems.
Concepts: mathematical rationality; actuarial agent ("utilitycoin"); small-world vs large-world decisions (via Raginsky/Savage); sweet spots; mathematical pluralism (Cartwright's dappled world); external validity; the RCT as measurement device (Neyman); cyborg decision making.

Bearings:
- THEORY-1947 (Citron: automated administrative decision systems make unreviewed rules), THEORY-1938 (Bovens & Zouridis, system-level bureaucracy), THEORY-1857 (Vallor via Adewuyi: algorithmic decision and moral deskilling): parallel; Recht supplies the intellectual history of the formalism those systems run on.
- CLAIM-710 (automation removes procedural friction where intervention is exposed): parallel to the "outsourcing" critique.
- THEORY-491 (randomisation adds little beyond removing allocation from experimenters) and THEORY-451 (Cartwright: singular causal claims need evidence on each step): parallel; Recht's narrow RCT "sweet spot" and dappled pluralism.
- THEORY-534 (MLE optimality was never one theorem): parallel; same deflation of a foundational optimality story.
- THEORY-359 (resource-rational derivation of cognitive mechanisms by expected utility vs cost): contrast; the program Recht calls mathematical rationality applied to minds.
- CLAIM-440 / THEORY-101 (common currency of valence): contrast; Recht rejects a universal currency for goals.
- THEORY-1920 (contestable-AI design framework): parallel on keeping humans in decisions.
Limitations: stand-ins only; blog register; the historical claims (which technologies, who invented what when) are asserted, not documented in the posts.

Candidate THEORY sentence: Recht argues that the mathematical rationality built with the first computers, which casts every decision under uncertainty as maximising expected utility under a probability model, is valid only in narrow settings such as regulation and engineering, so that outsourcing most decisions, and political or value-laden ones in particular, to computation built on it is itself irrational.
promote_when: the book's own text (or a lawful chapter) read, with the passages where Recht states the scope of mathematical rationality and the grounds on which decisions outside it cannot be delegated to computation.
