# Article 7. Memes That Make Worlds

## Pragmatic transport, cultural nucleation, hyperstition, and operative norm formation

**Abstract.** A transmitted expression can keep a relation, rebuild a distinction, settle on a convention that remembers nothing of its source, or help something new get organized. This article argues that these outcomes come apart, and that the theory of memes worth having is one that keeps them apart. It starts from what is known formally about transmission: a chain can forget where it began while staying far from it, so convergence is not preservation, and agreement between a source and its carriers is not by itself evidence that anything was carried. On that floor it states a conjecture: that a meme acts less as a replicator than as a seed around which organization forms, carrying one characteristic value of a perspective, so that its persistent reproduction can seed related but distinct perspectives rather than copies of one. Three sharper results follow. Under reversal of a social attribution with its grounds held fixed, conformity and defiance lie in the same component of a response, and the size of that component measures how far the attribution sets the terms of response; this makes exact one part of the phrase "I am whatever you say I am", that conforming and defying alike let the attribution set the terms. In a model of hyperstition, circulation amplifies its own realization exactly when the product of the gains exceeds the product of the decays, and a realization outlasts its representation only if the realized organization has a second stable state of its own. In a simulation, only engagement measured under randomized exposure told a class that reshaped what it describes from one that merely won on the measures its own use supplied; concentration, fit and stability did not. The last step, to norms, is weaker than it looks: in another simulation, restoring a deviation, proposed as the mark of a shared perspective, was passed alike by plain conformity, by referee enforcement and by checking against the world, and whether that bears on restoration as the mark of an operative norm is open. Apart from coded testimony on one case and a coding of eleven open-source successions from public records, every study reported here is a model.

---

### 7.1 Introduction

The word "meme" was coined for a cultural replicator by analogy with the gene (Dawkins 1976; LIT-tmpjl855), and the analogy carries a picture with it. A meme is a unit; it is copied; it is selected by how well it gets copied; its success is its frequency. On that picture the interesting question about a slogan, a joke or a phrase is how far it spreads and how faithfully.

This article asks a different question, in the form a conversation on the subject arrived at: "How do transmissible structures such as memes take part in forming, maintaining and transforming the organizations through which they become consequential, and when does the seed's own persistence matter to the organization's?" (QUESTION-tmprvnp0). "Meme" is used here without deciding in advance between its senses: "a replicator copied between carriers, a seed around which organization forms, or a transmissible expression of a perspective's characteristic organization", and among the roles a meme can play, "seed, scaffold, constraint, product, carrier or probe" (TERM-tmpet5z8). The question has three parts that the replicator picture runs together. What does circulation keep of its source? What can a transmitted expression do besides be copied: can it help an organization form, or a perspective? And when what an expression says comes true, or comes to be enforced, how is that told apart from a regime of evaluation that only makes it look true?

The argument runs in the order its steps depend on each other. Section 7.2 sets out what is known about transmission chains, which supplies a caution that governs everything after it. Section 7.3 states the conjecture that a meme is a seed and a characteristic value, and what formal limits bound it. Section 7.4 takes the phrase "I am whatever you say I am" as a worked case of an attribution that organizes the person it is about. Section 7.5 treats hyperstition, a representation that helps bring about what it represents. Section 7.6 takes up the distinction the whole inquiry needs, between a referent that has really changed and an evaluation regime that only confirms itself, and reports the one run that separates them in a model. Section 7.7 carries the argument to norm formation and finds the step weaker than it appears. Section 7.8 says what the argument does not establish and states the strongest objection. Section 7.9 concludes.

### 7.2 What circulation keeps

The memetic argument should not begin with a new theory of memes. There is a developed theory of transmission, organized as an account of pragmatic transport in twelve parts (SYNTHESIS-066), an account of communicative kinds (SYNTHESIS-068) and an account of transmission, convergence and anchoring across several lines of inquiry (SYNTHESIS-115). What it gives the memetic argument is less a theory of memes than a caution about evidence.

**Convergence is not preservation.** The central result is formal. A transmission chain has two quantities that are easily confused: how much its present state retains of its starting state, and how far its present distribution has drifted from the starting distribution. These vary independently. "A chain can forget its initial state while its distribution stays far from where it began, so convergence is not preservation and losing information about the origin is not degradation" (CLAIM-270). The same chain "can also stay close in distribution while scrambling which individual started where" (CLAIM-270). Read for serial reconstruction, in which each retelling rebuilds rather than copies, the result says that "transmission is not degradation" (CLAIM-081): a story that has changed greatly may have changed for reasons that have nothing to do with error.

**Local fidelity does not bound global drift.** A natural rescue is to measure each step: if every retelling is close to the one before, the chain should stay close to its start. It need not. "The same local error can be damped, accumulated or amplified, and local similarity can coexist with large global drift" (CLAIM-056), and the bound that measures each step "measures departure from a reference transport, not whether the act survives" (CLAIM-056). Even that reading needs qualifying. Its compositional predictions "are upper bounds whose constants cannot be estimated from serial-reproduction data in a metric fixed beforehand, so the compositional half of the success criterion cannot fail" (CLAIM-208), and a chain "reorganizes observational constraints rather than only accumulating metric error: a text can change greatly while keeping a few consequential constraints, and one changed constraint can change the act" (CLAIM-031). What survives a retelling is better described as a few constraints that matter than as a distance from the original.

**What does keep the start.** Something does. "An observable conserved exactly at every step and not constant on the support of the starting law keeps information about the start at every generation", while "a bounded conservation defect does not prevent forgetting" (CLAIM-635). The proposal built on this is that forgetting a source and anchoring to it are "one contraction, told apart by what each restatement step conserves exactly" (CLAIM-772): a chain that contracts everywhere except on what it copies without error keeps the start's value on that part and forgets the rest.

**Where chains settle.** Where a chain goes when it forgets is described by a cultural attractor, "a shared decoding convention rather than a preserved historical object" (TERM-027). Reaching one "is evidence of forgetting the start" (TERM-027), because an attractor is where the chain goes whatever its start. The term is taken from an analysis, not from Sperber's work, which has not been read for this article (§7.8).

**Reconstruction filtered by bias.** Serial transmission is reconstructive and "filtered by the transmitters' inductive biases" (CLAIM-090). That proposal is held back by a missing measurement: no chain on hand ends on a prior measured on its own transmitters beforehand, and the study that would supply one is still to be found or run (LEAD-187). Convergence to the prior is proved only for learners who sample from their posterior (Griffiths and Kalish 2007; THEORY-155; ARG-035). Chains of human function learners forget their starting function and converge on a favoured one, "not shown to be convergence to their prior" (Kalish, Griffiths and Lewandowsky 2007; THEORY-489). In two experiments on risk messages passed down chains, "Content got shorter, less accurate and more divergent; the evaluative signal survived and drifted negative" (Moussaïd, Brighton and Gaissmaier; Jagiello and Hills; CASE-068).

**Significance by role and uptake.** What act an utterance performs is "partly constituted by its relations to participant roles, norms and expected uptake" (CLAIM-151). This is a proposal; the study that would discriminate it, with renderings built to dissociate wording, proposition, stance and transport structure (CASE-055), "has not been run" (SYNTHESIS-066).

The caution this floor supplies can be put in one sentence. That an expression is still circulating, or that its carriers now agree, says nothing by itself about whether anything of its origin survived. The formal side of this is derived. The human side is known only from other people's experiments.

### 7.3 Seeds, values and invitations

**The conjecture.** The article's central proposal is that "a meme can act as a nucleation site: a seed around which beliefs, practices and relations organize, and not only a unit that copies itself" (CLAIM-tmp1ng5k). In the gloss that accompanied it, the seed "only needs to provide a structure around which existing information can assemble" (CLAIM-tmp1ng5k). A second proposal says what kind of structure a meme is. Borrowing the representation-theoretic notion of a character, which records how a representation responds to each transformation, it reads memes as "characteristic values of perspectives: each records how a perspective responds to one kind of transformation of a situation, so a family of memes samples a perspective's character without being it" (CLAIM-tmp4mk0t). A rival reading takes a meme to be a whole character (CLAIM-tmphi3mx). Both are kept, with the value reading primary (CLAIM-tmp4mk0t). Both are conjectures.

**Three persistences.** If a meme is a seed, three things can persist and need not persist together: "the persistence of the seed, of the organization it nucleates and of the perspective it carries are distinct phenomena" (CLAIM-tmpgvsgg). Two readings support the separation. A collective that replaces its members persists, when it does, "by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by resemblance: an organization of the same kind that arises independently is not its continuation" (CLAIM-329). And a figure's influence can persist "as a story about them, as a perspective transmitted from them, and as later organizations that descend from their activity, and causal descent is not perspectival inheritance: a movement founded to oppose someone can descend from them without continuing their perspective" (CLAIM-420). In assembly, the separation is derived: "Persistence and nucleating productivity in the committor sense vary independently" (CLAIM-595). An intermediate can last a long time and contribute nothing to what forms.

**Copying and organizing come apart.** "A viral meme produces copies; a generative meme produces structures" (CLAIM-tmp4uj40). The claim is that the two come apart, so that a widely copied meme "can organize little and a rarely copied one much"; it does not say they are opposed. A meme can carry "a portable specification of a governance or inquiry protocol … which is not a contract but one around which contracts can be organized" (CLAIM-tmpdq0ah). One proposed instance is the belief that fiduciary duty requires maximizing shareholder value, which "can bind a corporation more tightly than the applicable law does … and so help produce the organizational character later presented as a legal necessity" (CLAIM-tmpyqjn0). A meme can also change "the conditions under which later memes are assessed" (CLAIM-tmp158r8). The seed picture has a precedent in a proposal about arguments, that "the nucleation threshold of an argument is whatever makes a structure persist" (CLAIM-185), and the same precedent supplies its cost: "Anchors entrench" (CLAIM-184).

**The seed window, formally.** What can a seed do in the simplest model? In a model in which a maintained organization has one stable maintained state beside an unmaintained one, and a meme enters as a transient forcing, "a transient seed leaves a maintained organization only inside the bistable window and only by carrying the state past the unstable threshold" (CLAIM-tmpq1lyz). Because "the meme enters only as a scalar forcing", "the model expresses the environment's receptivity but not which organization a seed nucleates" (CLAIM-tmpq1lyz). This bounds what a one-state model can say about memes. It cannot tell one seed's organization from another's, and so it cannot even state the diversification taken up below. Models that do vary the seed against a fixed receiver exist, but for assembly, not culture: adding a seed already in its target state "can halve the chance of exact assembly" (CASE-389), and two seams each productive alone can together be a trap, with a fixed stock of components sliding the realized form "from one fold to the other with the attainable set unchanged" (CASE-374).

**What a character can and cannot carry.** The character reading inherits the limits of characters. They "classify finite-dimensional representations of a finite or compact group up to isomorphism only over a field of characteristic zero; they forget the isomorphisms, fix neither the group nor a permutation action, and classify kinds, not tokens or positions" (CLAIM-132; SYNTHESIS-077). A second derived limit is sharper for perspectives: "The character of a perspective's representation detects the transformations it is blind to, not the states it merges", and "a projection can merge states no transformation relates while its character shows no blindness at all" (CLAIM-tmpqbaic). And comparing characters across carriers "needs one group identified on both, which is fixed only up to automorphism", so "the same character" is relative to a chosen correspondence (CLAIM-249). All three bound both readings. A meme recognized as the same value in two carriers is the same only up to what a character forgets, and a perspective's character can miss the very merging that makes it a perspective.

**Fidelity against convergence.** The character reading suggests a measure of memetic fidelity: "Memetic fidelity can be the preservation of characteristic relations rather than the copying of content" (CLAIM-tmpjitli), with success read as a source and a carrier that agree in their characteristic relations. Section 7.2 limits what such agreement can show. A chain can arrive at an agreement whatever its start (CLAIM-270; TERM-027). So agreement in characteristic relations between source and carrier is not by itself evidence that those relations were transmitted; they may be where reconstruction tends. Evidence of transmission needs the start to matter: different sources leading to different endpoints under the same reconstruction, or a relation the chain conserves at every step, of the kind that keeps information about the start (CLAIM-635). This is CLAIM-270's distinction between retention and drift applied to the fidelity measure, and it adds nothing to it (CLAIM-tmpjitli, note of 2026-11-25); for collectives, the same point is that resemblance is not continuation (CLAIM-329). Its practical consequence is that a test of the fidelity claim, such as transferring chosen memes between matched populations (LEAD-tmpqucxc), must vary the source while holding the reconstruction fixed.

**Reproduction diversifies.** The value reading has a further consequence, stated as a thesis: "persistent reproduction of a meme can nucleate related but distinct perspectives (e.g. 'death of the author' type phenomena)" (CLAIM-tmp590sw). The mechanism proposed for it is that, reproduced, "the value travels without the rest of the character that produced it. Each carrier that takes it up completes it from its own character" (CLAIM-tmp590sw). Circulation then "can diversify the perspectives it touches rather than copy one". The reader's side is already argued: "an unread text has possible meanings and no fixed one" (CLAIM-171). What the thesis adds is "that the meanings become maintained perspectives, not passing readings" (CLAIM-tmp590sw). Its nearest analogue is the separation of causal descent from perspectival inheritance (CLAIM-420): an organization can descend from a source without continuing its perspective. Barthes's essay, which names the phenomenon, is registered and not yet read (Barthes 1967; LIT-tmpflbme), so the gloss of it here is not a reading. The thesis fits the fidelity point from the other side. If reproduction diversifies, then agreement among carriers is even weaker evidence of transmission than §7.2 alone implies.

**The carrier against the concept.** Diversification has a public face. "Symbolic stability can persist while conceptual stability fails" (CLAIM-621), as in the reported variation in how the contested expression "anti-vax" is applied and taken up (CASE-222): an expression can keep circulating while the concept it carried splits among rival uses. That proposal may be only a restatement (CLAIM-621) of the earlier point that "the persistence of a product and the persistence of the organization producing it are separate facts" (CLAIM-280). The question it raises is whether rival communities using one expression share "one concept with context-dependent extension, several historically related concepts sharing an expression, or an organization of the rivalry among the uses" (QUESTION-439; SYNTHESIS-025). A proposed study would exchange interpretive rules, histories and institutions between populations matched in behaviour (CASE-148); it has not been run. There is one field study of the corresponding distinction for organizations. Coding eleven open-source successions from their public records, it found that "the community's recognition followed the maintainers and not the name": where the creator or the core maintainers moved, the successor became the recognized line, where only part moved recognition split, "and no successor took the original's name except by reunion" (CLAIM-1158; CASE-411). A name is a carrier; what persisted as the recognized project was the organization producing it.

**Meaning and will.** A broader thesis puts this together: "the coupling of meaning and will runs through what is transmitted and can remake the concepts, perspectives and organizations it runs in", "without that persistence showing truth, consensus or autonomy" (CLAIM-620). Its own defeater lists the objection that it "only restates" an earlier thesis that meaning and will are one model (CLAIM-336), with the categories of §7.6 confirmed through use (CLAIM-620). It is stated here as the frame the rest of the article fills in, not as a result.

**Perspectival invitation.** At the scale of a single encounter, there is a proposal of how an interaction can make a new way of relating available: "An interaction can make available a way of relating that its participants' operative perspectives did not hold, by giving each a role to enact in it, so that taking part can come before, and help produce, the reinterpretation it later seems to express" (CLAIM-430). Its case is Franklin's favour, and its read evidence includes field experiments in which canvassers who exchanged personal narratives did what argument did not (Kalla and Broockman; CASE-147). It keeps apart "whether the change stays with the partner, generalizes to a category, or is reproduced beyond the encounter" (CLAIM-430). Whether a transmitted expression can carry such an invitation beyond the encounter, and so whether the invitation is the seed at the scale of one meeting, is the third of those questions, and it is open.

### 7.4 "I am whatever you say I am"

The phrase names a person organized by what others say of them. It also names the suspicion that the "you" is plural when it may not be: "the perception of a plurality of opinion where one may not exist". The phrase is a worked case of every distinction so far, because the attribution is a transmitted expression, the person is what it may organize, and the plurality is what circulation may fake.

**What is proposed.** "A person can be organized by a judgement they attribute to a collective that does not hold it: the audience in a self-model can be a false unity, and a judgement can shape a person who rejects it, because resisting it makes it consequential" (CLAIM-415). The claim depends on four selves being kept apart: the attributed self others hold, the one the person imagines they hold, the imagined audience, and the one the person takes up as their own (TERM-075). Three constructions fix the cases. In one, "two people with similar dispositions hear the same negative characterization, one told it comes from a few critics and the other believing it speaks for nearly everyone who matters to them, so that the words are fixed and only their attributed breadth differs" (CASE-140). In another, a label confirms itself through others' treatment (CASE-141). In the third, a label is confirmed by the resistance of someone who rejects it (CASE-142), with the limit "Not shown: that resistance always confirms" (CASE-142). The proposal sits within a wider account of what a collective is credited with, which "need not be what its members hold" (SYNTHESIS-052).

**What is read.** The claim's three clauses are not equally grounded. The false unity has read support. In a model on scale-free networks with fixed private beliefs, a hard-core minority drives most of the majority into silence, so that "a spiral of silence can make a small, fixed minority opinion look far more widespread than it is without any member changing belief" (Vilone and Polizzi 2024; THEORY-242). When an attribute is concentrated on well-connected nodes, "many nodes can see it in a majority of their neighbours though it is globally rare" (Lerman, Yan and Wu 2016; THEORY-887), though the attributes there are assigned by the authors, so the result shows what topology permits and nothing about what people perceive. Prentice and Miller's study of pluralistic ignorance about campus drinking is registered and not available (Prentice and Miller 1993; LIT-1370). Attributed plurality can also be produced rather than misperceived: a vocal minority "can fix the fanbase's public stance through a group process and not only through biased sampling of who speaks" (CLAIM-493), a derivation from self-categorization theory (Spears; THEORY-1608). The third clause is another matter. That resisting a judgement makes it consequential "has no read result: it rests on Cooley's statement and the constructed [CASE-142]" (CLAIM-415, note of 2026-10-30).

**Three routes.** An attribution can come true of a person in three ways that the phrase runs together. "**Self-fulfillment:** I acquire characteristics that were initially misattributed to me"; "**Adversarial constitution:** I become organized through my opposition to the characterization"; and "**Interpretive self-sealing:** Whatever I do is taken as confirmation, regardless of whether my character changes" (CLAIM-415, note of 2026-11-24). "Only the first necessarily involves realization of the attributed content." The resisting person of CASE-142 "can be the second or the third", and which it is "turns on whether the person became hostile or was only read so"; the self-confirming label of CASE-141 "shows the first and third together" (CLAIM-415, note of 2026-11-24).

**The self-governing response.** Against all three, a perspective governs itself here by making the authority of others' judgements "depend on their grounds, not on their visibility, repetition or attributed plurality; neither absorbing every judgement nor resisting every one meets this" (CLAIM-425). Absorbing and resisting fail alike because "both let the judgements set the terms of response" (CLAIM-425).

**The formal result.** That last phrase can be made exact. Conformity (a response equal to the attribution) and opposition (a response equal to its negative) transform alike when the attribution is reversed. The derived statement is this: "Under reversal of a social attribution with its grounds held fixed, every response splits uniquely into a part that reverses with the attribution and a part that does not: conformity and defiance lie wholly in the first and a response governed by the grounds alone wholly in the second, so the size of the reversing part, not its direction, measures how far the attribution sets the terms of response" (CLAIM-tmpj4pw3). "Its sign does not enter: defiance scores as conformity does." And reversing the attribution together with its grounds "measures nothing" (CLAIM-tmpj4pw3). This is the precise form of CLAIM-425's point that absorbing and resisting "both let the judgements set the terms of response", and it is a condition any test of the phrase must meet: the grounds have to be held fixed while the attribution is reversed.

**What the result does not decide.** The reversing part measures how far the attribution sets the response. It does not say by which route. A person whose reversing part is large may have become what the label says, may be organized against it, or may simply be read through it. The routes differ in whether the referent changed, which is the distinction of §7.6.

**A seeded capacity to revise.** The phrase may also do the opposite of what it describes. The proposal is that "a capacity for reflexive self-governance can itself be seeded by a transmissible expression … but holding the representation, or believing oneself autonomous, is not having the capacity, which shows only in effective revisions of dependencies met after the exposure" (CLAIM-tmp5tjtg). It rests on a reading that reflexive governance "is a capacity some organizations of perspective have and others lack … and the capacity to revise those standards is not the capacity to revise them well" (CLAIM-424). On this proposal, a meme about being organized by attribution can be a seed of the capacity to see it, and only what the person later revises shows whether it was.

**The test, with its expectation.** The study the formal result calls for has not been run. It would present the same evidence about a person with a characterization in one direction or the other, and estimate each respondent's reversing part (LEAD-tmp0w114). Its expectation, stated before any run: "Conformity and defiance should show reversing parts of opposite sign and similar size; [CLAIM-425]'s self-governing response a small one together with responsiveness to changes in the grounds" (LEAD-tmp0w114). Its falsifier: "If defiant respondents show no larger reversing part than indifferent ones, [CLAIM-415]'s 'resisting it makes it consequential' loses its measurable form" (LEAD-tmp0w114). A proposed study of the label "anti-vax" is likewise not run (CASE-230).

### 7.5 Hyperstition

The phrase concerns a judgement about what someone is. Hyperstition concerns a judgement about what will be: "a positive feedback circuit including culture as a component", whose ideas "function causally to bring about their own reality" (Carstens and Land 2009; NOTE-1378). The self-fulfilling prophecy is the older name for one case of it (Merton 1948; NOTE-1409), and reflexivity in markets is another (Soros 2013; NOTE-1362).

**What hyperstition changes.** It qualifies the self-governing rule of §7.4. "Where a judgement's circulation helps bring about what it says, its visibility, repetition and attributed plurality are evidence about what it will bring about" (CLAIM-426). So the rule that a judgement's authority should rest on its grounds and not on its circulation holds for judgements of what already is, "and not without qualification for judgements of what someone or something will become" (CLAIM-426). The read sources give "no condition for telling which judgements' circulation helps make them true" (CLAIM-426, note of 2026-10-30).

**When circulation amplifies, in a model.** A condition can be given in a model. Let m be a representation's salience and r its realization, with ṁ = αr − μm and ṙ = βm − νr. Then "the representation's circulation amplifies its own realization exactly when the product of the two gains exceeds the product of the two decay rates, so hyperstitional efficacy is a property of the coupling between a representation and the activity that can realize it, not of the representation" (CLAIM-tmpvic6m). The condition is αβ > μν. The claim says what it is not: "Not a way to tell, in a case, which judgements' circulation helps make them true." "The gains are not observed" (CLAIM-tmpvic6m). It is a mechanism shown possible, not a law of cultural evolution, and it narrows the open question of CLAIM-426 without closing it (CLAIM-426, note of 2026-11-24).

**Whether the realization outlasts the representation.** A second proposal says what a hyperstition's success consists in. What a representation helps bring about "can come to be maintained by processes of its own and outlast the representation's circulation … or it can stay dependent on continued circulation; which of the two holds is a fact about maintenance provenance, told apart by withdrawing the circulation once the realization has formed" (CLAIM-tmpyp5bu). The linear model cannot show the first outcome. Derived: "coupling alone never lets a realization outlast its representation: once circulation stops the realization decays at its own rate whatever the gains were; it persists only if the realized organization has a self-maintaining loop of its own strong enough to have a second stable state, and the realization crossed that loop's threshold while the representation still circulated" (CLAIM-tmpnws9d). In its check, with a self-maintenance strength of 2.5, above the threshold of 2, realization was 0.806 at withdrawal and settled at 0.724; with a strength of 1.5 it went to zero (CLAIM-tmpnws9d). The same result limits the withdrawal test: "Withdrawal before the threshold is crossed ends the realization in both kinds of system, so a single withdrawal at an unknown stage does not discriminate" (CLAIM-tmpnws9d).

**The general condition, and its precedents.** The hyperstition result is an instance of conditions already known for transient support. It is a construction in which a transient encounter forms a connector "maintained by what it enables", in hyperstition's variables (CLAIM-tmpnws9d; CASE-189). More generally, "transient support can be withdrawn safely only if it started a process that keeps itself going on a flux that will stay available, was large enough to carry the supported party past any threshold that process has", among further conditions, and "persistence after withdrawal is needed to tell which, though slow reversion can mimic it" (CLAIM-600). Executed models in assembly bear the condition out. In one, a scaffold that forms and holds a nucleus is withdrawn for good at chosen states, and "the assembly becomes independent of the support before it is complete only where what it has built holds the nucleus or no longer needs it" (CLAIM-865; CASE-308). In another, a loop that makes its own teachers closes and "withdrawal becomes safe on crossing a separatrix" (CASE-365), while a nucleus that only repairs the constraint of its own formation "divides the supply needed … but never to independence" (CLAIM-952). And when a capacity is installed by an outside pulse and then maintains itself, it "is told apart only by a knockout during formation" (CASE-191); after the fact the two are the same organization. That is the same limit as CLAIM-tmpnws9d's single withdrawal at an unknown stage. None of these models is about memes. What they show is that the hyperstition condition is not special to hyperstition.

**One shape, and why it does not pick out nucleation.** The outlasting of a realization is one of several results with the shape of a second stable state. A capacity lost past a threshold "returns only under conditions better than those it was lost under" (CLAIM-463), on a construction of a fold in boundary maintenance (CASE-111). A norm in a stated model, once established past a threshold, holds where a weakly established one would not (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx); a norm's justification and its support come apart, so "a norm that outlasts its justification need not have outlasted its support, and a bistable norm ends by a push past its threshold rather than by fading" (CLAIM-tmps8ou2). In that model a norm outlasts a weakening of its baseline within the bistable range, not the loss of its baseline. An anchor, likewise, "may act by nucleation, helping a new response organization form that then maintains itself, so its influence outlasts the anchor" (CLAIM-tmpggu4v). That these are one shape is a restatement: each entry states its own bistability, and the same observation has been made before of a fold and a hysteresis loop (SYNTHESIS-074). It does carry a caution. Threshold models of contagion "already produce critical masses and hysteresis" (CLAIM-tmpgvsgg), so "a threshold or a lasting regime after withdrawal is not by itself evidence of nucleation" (LEAD-tmpiktom). Outlasting tells a self-maintaining realization from a circulation-dependent one; it does not tell a seed from a threshold.

**The nearest data.** No field case of hyperstition with a withdrawal is known to this article, and the search for one is set out but not done (LEAD-tmp5msck). The nearest data are of two kinds. In a pre-registered randomized experiment on 817 play-money prediction markets, "about 59% of a ±5-point price shock that no trader's information supported was still present after 60 days", and a model of finitely many risk-averse traders gives such persistence "even with no learning, and the data cannot tell that from learning" (Rasooly and Rozzi 2025; THEORY-267). Whether "the traders come to believe the shocked price" is exactly the question of §7.6 asked of real people, and it is open (QUESTION-212). And over all Reddit posts and comments from 2005 to 2019, links to subreddits that did not yet exist "preceded the creation of a sixth of the subreddits later created" (Krohn and Weninger 2022; THEORY-2070), which the authors report "as correlation, not cause"; the links are proposals or jokes, not judgements (CLAIM-426, note of 2026-11-19).

### 7.6 A changed referent against apparent confirmation

Every step so far has met the same difficulty. A meme that seems to have organized something, a label that seems to have come true of a person, a prophecy that seems to have fulfilled itself: each could instead be a regime of evaluation that reads its own effects as confirmation. The inquiry needs to tell a genuine transformation of the referent from apparent confirmation produced by the evaluation regime. That distinction is drawn in three forms: a criterion, a decomposition and a run.

**The competition over tests.** The general form is a reading: "Competing perspectives compete over the tests that measure them as well as under them: success under an evaluative regime is evidence of competence relative to that regime, a fair test need not be neutral across competences, and a perspective that gains influence over the test can change what later success shows" (CLAIM-416). Its mechanism is that when authority is updated "by how well it performs on the interpretation it helped make dominant", the coupling "can amplify an initial interpretive advantage without any gain in fidelity to independent evidence" (CLAIM-297). In a model of two institutions, an evaluator's reading can be captured even with "informational independence", which "does not prevent it while a resource channel runs" (CLAIM-1202).

**The criterion.** For categories, the consequence is a criterion of evidence. "Predictive success of a category across contexts warrants treating it as a real effective kind only when the evidence in those contexts does not pass through the category's own use, or when the effect of that use is modelled: a category that speakers or institutions act on can produce the regularities that confirm it" (CLAIM-402). The claim "defeats one inference and is not a criterion of kindhood". Its premise is that what keeps a loop from confirming itself "is evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests" (CLAIM-342). For metrics: "**Improved correspondence between a measure and its object does not establish that the measure was originally adequate, or that the resulting transformation is desirable**" (CLAIM-402, note of 2026-11-24). In the read case, law schools under a public ranking "changed what they did in order to move their rank" (Espeland and Sauder 2007; THEORY-862), and a ranking can raise its own validity through the reactions it provokes (THEORY-854). Formally, a deployed model that changes the distribution it is scored on can settle at a performatively stable point that "need not minimize the risk it actually incurs" (Perdomo et al. 2020; THEORY-839), and retraining converges to such a point only under stated conditions (THEORY-861).

**The decomposition.** The change in what a learned distribution says can be written as two terms. One is "performative change of the world sampled". The other is "endogenous change of how it is represented". These "need different interventions to separate: change the reporting regime with the policy fixed, or change the policy with raw outcomes observed independently" (CLAIM-402, note of 2026-10-24). An analysis of hyperstition makes a parallel cut, separating "a changed reality (hyperstition) from a changed reading of evidence (interpretive capture, confirmation bias) and from counterperformativity", and names the danger: "the causal consequences of a representation become evidence for it, while the formative role of the representation becomes increasingly illegible" (CLAIM-426, note of 2026-11-24).

**The run.** The distinction has been run once, in a simulation built for it (CASE-367), from a case of genres in recommender systems (CASE-296) and the hypothesis that collapsing differences that do not matter is how classes become real (SYNTHESIS-108). Its criterion was set before the run: "Classes that reshape preferences only when used supports the reading of [CLAIM-762] the case was filed for" (LEAD-030). There were two worlds, one with genres planted in 600 users' preferences and one with clusters made by exposure alone, each run in five arms over four seeds. The classes were "Inferred once" from raw engagement counts and then held fixed (CASE-367). The decisive probe showed each user 20 items at random, so the engagement gap it measured did not pass through the classes' use.

The results, start to end in the taste world, were these (CASE-367). Steering exposure by the classes over fixed preferences raised concentration from 0.42 to 0.90 and fit to raw counts from 24.5 to 41.1 millinats per cell. The random-exposure gap moved from 0.352 to 0.357, identical to every digit to the unsteered arm, "because the runs share random numbers and the preferences did not move". With users and creators adapting, the same steering raised the gap to 0.482, and the true gap rose from 0.344 to 0.466. The same adaptation without the classes lowered the gap to 0.231. So "in this model the classes reshape preferences only when used", which is what the criterion said would count. The loop reinforced planted genres, created a small distinction where there had been none, and erased one when exposure followed popularity. Clusters that exposure alone had made fitted raw counts better than planted genres (9.6 against 3.7 millinats) and were perfectly stable (adjusted Rand index 1.00). A closure test, conditioned on exposure and scored on randomized exposure, passed the planted genres (0.86) and failed the exposure-made clusters (−0.01).

Two claims are drawn from it, both empirical and both about the model: "only engagement under randomized exposure tells classes inferred, classes used and classes that reshape preferences apart" (CLAIM-954), and "Fit to engagement data and stability over time favour clusters that exposure made … so neither can warrant an engagement category as a kind" (CLAIM-955). The randomized probe is necessary in the model, "not sufficient: classes reshaped by their own use pass it too" (QUESTION-028, note of 2026-11-22). And the case's own limit: "The laboratory shows that the measures can tell the cases apart when the cases are known, not that real genres are reshaped" (CASE-367).

**What the run shows, and what it does not.** Concentration and fit rose in both the fixed-preference arm and the adaptive arm; only the probe that did not pass through the classes separated them. That is the run's result, and it is "an instance of [CLAIM-402]'s worry, not an answer to it" (CLAIM-954). Its place in the decomposition needs stating carefully. Because the classes were held fixed, how the world is represented did not change in any arm; both arms lie in the first term, performative change of what is sampled, and differ in whether preferences moved with it. The randomized probe is the decomposition's second separating intervention, "change the policy with raw outcomes observed independently", applied inside that term. The run has no arm for the representational term; re-inferring the classes from the engagement the steering produced would supply one (CLAIM-954, note of 2026-11-25). Nor does the fixed-preference arm instantiate interpretive self-sealing in the sense of §7.4, since there behaviour changed under a fixed reading, whereas self-sealing is a changed reading of fixed behaviour. The adaptive arm illustrates self-fulfillment at the scale of a population: the referent was made to fit the class. Adversarial constitution has no arm (CLAIM-954, note of 2026-11-25).

A constructed case shows the same loop running toward loss. A newsroom that allocates reporting by measured demand cuts investigations, publishes fewer, sees little engagement with them, and "takes that as evidence they are not wanted, until it no longer has the sources or expertise to see what needs investigating" (CASE-247). The regime's measures confirm while the capacity they ought to measure is lost.

**A changed referent is not a vindicated category.** The run carries one caveat that matters for everything earlier. The classes that reshaped preferences were reshaped by use, so they are also the case CLAIM-402 warns of, a category that produces the regularities confirming it. A changed referent is a fact about the world, not a warrant for the category or its makers. Persistence does not make "the fiction true when it circulated" (CLAIM-tmpyp5bu).

### 7.7 From regularity to operative norm, and not to legitimacy

A transmitted expectation can do more than describe or come true. It can come to be enforced. Norm formation is treated at length elsewhere in this series (Article 3); three steps matter here.

**The marker.** "An expectation is an operative norm rather than a prediction when a discrepancy is met by bringing the behaviour back into line with the expectation instead of revising the expectation to fit the behaviour" (CLAIM-tmphq6hp). It needs "no represented belief about what ought to happen". The marker is a direction of fit: a description is written to fit what it is applied to, a requirement makes what it is applied to fit it (TERM-151).

**Reliance as stabilizer.** "A norm can be stabilized by the reliance it attracts" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx). Read against the case of race-based power after American abolition (CLAIM-tmp8l5s9; CASE-tmp3euq0), whose historiography is still to be read (LEAD-tmpuuj6e), the model's verbal conclusion had to be corrected: "A norm's original justification and its baseline support come apart" (CLAIM-tmps8ou2). The general lesson is that a model's verbal conclusion "often reads a parameter as the concept it was introduced to stand for" (CLAIM-tmpyxemn), and an older objection says the same of models in which operators maximize an objective the modeller fixed: "the norms the organization appears to maintain are set by the modeller, not by the organization" (CLAIM-288).

**Not legitimacy.** Neither step makes a norm good. The marker is "Not that an operative norm is legitimate" (CLAIM-tmphq6hp); the stabilizer, "Not that the stabilized norm is good" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx). What matters is what becomes stabilized, whose interests it serves and whether revision remains possible. The ladder from repetition to the point where "Its withdrawal is treated as a violation" makes something owed "only under a further principle" (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0). Whether an organization's own dynamics can supply norms of function and failure, and what more practical and moral authority need, is an open question (QUESTION-128).

**What restoration does not show.** The marker invites an observational test: watch whether deviations are brought back. A simulation ran the corresponding test for a different property, whether a population shares a perspective (CASE-362; LEAD-087). Its criterion included a restorative condition, that a minority's deviation "is corrected back toward the shared structure" rather than averaged (TERM-148), and a failure rule committed before any code was written: "if plain conformity passes condition 3 … the criterion fails to make the distinction" (CASE-362). Plain conformity passed. It restored a minority's deviation with a dilution of 0.024, against an averaging benchmark of 0.25 and a threshold of 0.125. "Enforcement by a referee (0.031) and checking against the world (−0.030) also passed. The condition does not separate them" (CLAIM-946). Restoring a deviation "does not tell a population whose members evaluate one another's interpretations from one whose members copy one another's answers" (CLAIM-946). Where the coupling does show is "in how fast the deviants are drawn back and in whether the others' response follows the standard" (CLAIM-945). The case says of itself: "Not real groups" (CASE-362).

**Whether this bears on norms is open.** It is tempting to carry the result over: if copying, enforcing and checking all restore a deviation, observed restoration cannot tell an enforced norm from a majority copied. The carry-over does not go through as it stands. The objects differ: the simulation restores interpretive prototypes, the marker restores behaviour against an expectation. And the marker has a free slot. If "bringing the behaviour back into line" means only that the deviant's behaviour returns, copying meets it; if it means, as the norm model's source has it, that others' activity is recruited to restore compliance, then enforcement is separated from copying by construction (CLAIM-tmphq6hp, correction of 2026-11-25). Until the marker says which, no case can be stated either way. Two results point to what a better test would be. One is a principle: "Recovery under interventions on state cannot show where maintenance is realized … only interventions on the production mechanisms, relative to a declared boundary, separate the two" (CLAIM-522). On it, the test of an operative norm is to knock out others' corrective activity and see whether the expectation still holds, not to watch restoration. The other is a marker in a neighbouring setting. Three simulated populations with the same turnover and aggregate statistics, one responding alike to a common input, one transmitting its norms to its replacements and one held by an institution (CASE-101), were run through a priced lagged block test, which found the population held by delayed transmission, with its lag (CLAIM-926; CASE-347). The test's limits are as much a part of the result: it "rejects a commonly driven population only while the common input is observed, and gives a commonly driven population and an institutionally maintained one the same boundary" (CLAIM-926). Whether either result bears on an organization's operative norms is not decided here.

### 7.8 What the argument does not establish

**It does not establish anything about memes in a real population.** The formal results are derivations or model results. The executed studies are simulations whose mechanisms "were written into the simulator" (CASE-367), whose groups are "Not real groups" (CASE-362), and which are documented "as a computation on a stated model" (CASE-347). The chain computations that illustrate the reading of serial reconstruction use hand-chosen matrices, "not fitted to text, pretrained models, or human measurements" (CASE-017). The hyperstition results hold for a stated pair of equations, and the amplification condition "is a condition on gains in a model, not a way to measure them" (CLAIM-tmpvic6m). Two studies do touch real people and organizations. One is the coding of eleven open-source successions (CASE-411). The other is a study of how a fan's redub of a cartoon put a line into a feature film three months later (CASE-100), from production testimony coded by rules written first and a circulation-network model; the one observation the case gives "favours circulation over a list that names the distributors by at most about five to one, and by under two if the case is known for the video's fame" (CASE-410; CLAIM-1026). Its criterion, set before the run, was that "A route through circulation rather than one contributor supports [CLAIM-332]" (LEAD-188); the testimony gives a route that ends in one contributor and is silent on its middle, so it is read two ways, one through circulation with weak support (CLAIM-1022) and one as an entry on the list (CLAIM-1052), and the choice between them is open (LEAD-188, note of 2026-11-23). The case's network spreads videos over a fixed contact network and records no ties formed among circulators, so it cannot show the new relations that a nucleation thesis predicts (LEAD-tmpiktom, note of 2026-11-25). The human evidence elsewhere is read, not gathered: transmission chains run by others (CASE-068; THEORY-489), one interview study of rankings (THEORY-862), a correlational study of Reddit (THEORY-2070), and a randomized experiment on play-money markets (THEORY-267).

**Replacing participants is done in part.** A study with turnover matched across populations found "the transmitting population … with its lag" (CASE-347). Its criterion was "A procedure that recovers the known boundaries and rejects the commonly driven population" (LEAD-026). It met that criterion only in part: the procedure recovers the transmitting and institutional populations, but "rejects the commonly driven members alone only while their driver is observed" (LEAD-026, note of 2026-11-22). What is transmitted there is a norm in a model, not a meme.

**The rival theories have not been read.** The literatures a reviewer of the memetic thesis would press first are not read for this article: Dawkins (LIT-tmpjl855), Sperber's epidemiology of representations and cultural attraction, Boyd and Richerson's dual-inheritance theory, complex contagion, and Schweitzer and Andres on group formation as a phase transition (Schweitzer and Andres 2022; LIT-tmpfj6uj). The reading is set out and not done (LEAD-tmplsxtr). The reading of the majority illusion cites threshold models of contagion (Granovetter 1978; Watts 2002; Centola) and itself simulates no contagion (NOTE-1085). Until the rivals are read, the nucleation reading has no published rival to beat in this argument.

**The strongest objection.** The objection that matters most is that threshold models of contagion already do everything the seed thesis claims. They produce critical masses, sudden spreads and hysteresis: the persistence of an adoption after its trigger is withdrawn (CLAIM-tmpgvsgg). Every outlasting result in §7.5 has the shape a threshold model also has (§7.5). A seed that "nucleates" an organization may be a trigger that tipped a population already near its threshold, and nothing more. The conjecture of §7.3 has no answer to this yet, and its own defeater says so: the transmission models it is compared with must "include threshold (complex-contagion) models, which already produce critical masses and hysteresis" (CLAIM-tmpgvsgg). What would answer it is a relational measure. The study that would test it (LEAD-tmpiktom) states its expectation before any run: "New persistent relations formed after exposure, beyond what the fitted threshold model predicts, would favour [CLAIM-tmpgvsgg]. Adoption patterns the threshold model fits, with no relational excess, would meet its defeater" (LEAD-tmpiktom). The study would also have to vary the seed while holding the receiver fixed, which the one-state model cannot express (CLAIM-tmpq1lyz), and to withdraw the seed at known stages, since a single withdrawal at an unknown stage does not discriminate (CLAIM-tmpnws9d; CASE-191).

A second objection comes from the floor itself. If agreement between carriers is weak evidence of transmission (§7.3), then much of what the seed thesis would point to as its evidence, a shared value among carriers, is weak evidence of a seed. The conjecture survives this only by predicting divergence where a replicator theory predicts agreement (CLAIM-tmp590sw). That is a prediction, and it has not been tested.

**How far prior work reaches.** The view that this series' experiments are "largely already satisfied by prior work" (CLAIM-tmpyp0ju) holds here for the formal results and for one simulated separation of a changed referent from apparent confirmation. It does not hold for memes in any real population.

### 7.9 Conclusion

A theory of memes that starts from transmission rather than from replication arrives at a different set of questions. Circulation can forget its source while staying far from it, so neither persistence nor agreement shows that anything was carried (§7.2). A meme may instead be a seed around which an organization forms and a characteristic value of a perspective, which travels without the rest of the character that produced it and is completed differently by each carrier; this is a conjecture, bounded by what characters forget and by what a one-state model of seeding can express (§7.3). The phrase "I am whatever you say I am" has an exact core: with the grounds held fixed, conformity and defiance are one component of a response, and its size is the measure of an attribution's hold (§7.4). Hyperstition has a condition in a model, and its success, outlasting its representation, requires a second stable state that threshold models also have (§7.5). What separates a referent that changed from an evaluation regime that confirms itself, in the one model where it was run, is evidence that does not pass through the class's own use (§7.6). And observed restoration is too weak a mark of a norm, though whether the result that shows it weak for shared perspectives carries over to norms is open (§7.7). Neither an operative norm nor a stabilized one is thereby legitimate.

What the argument does not supply is a study of memes in a real population against a threshold-contagion baseline with a relational measure. That is the test on which the conjecture stands or falls.
