# CLAIM-725: A demand's satisfaction condition does not carry its warrant: representing a requirement by the histories that satisfy it fixes what meets it, not its source, who bears it, to whom it is addressed or why it binds, so that the joint satisfiability of demands cannot certify that any deserves fulfilment, and an institution's maintained requirements cannot certify their own authority

## The claim

A106 §4 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b). A demand d_i
is represented by its acceptance set A_i = {h : Sat(d_i, h)}, the histories
in which it is satisfied. "A demand may express a justified obligation, a
technical requirement, an organizational objective, or simply a party's
request. Its inclusion in a model does not imply its moral authority."

The forest: an owner's minimum return, a neighbour's easement, a
community's sustainable extraction and a manager's exclusion of a
disfavoured group are all representable. "But they differ in normative
provenance." The easement may express a claim-right, the sustainability
requirement a collective duty; the return may be legitimate "without giving
the owner a right to achieve it at others' expense", and the exclusion may
be unjustified. "The compatibility problem can tell us whether the demands
can be fulfilled together. It cannot establish that each deserves
fulfillment."

So A puts provenance into the formal object: d_i = (A_i, Source_i,
Bearer_i, Addressee_i, Warrant_i), which "distinguishes the requirement's
content from the reasons it is supposed to have normative force", and:

> Without that distinction, an institution could effectively manufacture moral authority by creating and maintaining internal requirements.

## Two kinds of compatibility (A106 §4, folded in)

A then separates "**Requirement compatibility:** Can the specified demands
be satisfied together?" from "**Normative compatibility:** Can the
arrangement satisfy the applicable obligations while respecting the rights
and authority structures that govern how satisfaction may be achieved?"
"The second is not merely the first with additional high-priority
preferences": a right to authorize a decision "may require a particular
procedure or response to a choice, regardless of whether the same physical
endpoint could have been produced without authorization." That is
[CLAIM-741](CLAIM-741.md)'s point about rights that protect a choice; this claim
supplies the other half, that what is being made compatible may lack
authority in the first place.

## How it sits with the record

- **[TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)** (Active) is the term for frustration, the failure of an
  organization's demands to be jointly realizable; its acceptance sets are
  over transformations. A says its own representation "generalizes the
  acceptance-set representation in Nucleation's Active [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)". It is a
  re-indexing from transformations to histories more than a
  generalization. Nothing in the claim depends on the difference.
- **[CLAIM-738](CLAIM-738.md)**, filed from the part before, holds that a demand's
  presence in an organization's maintained set gives it no warrant. This
  claim is the formal reason, stated for the record's own apparatus: the
  representation the apparatus uses for a demand cannot carry the warrant,
  so compatibility results built on it cannot either.
- **[CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)** (Active) says its three achievements establish nothing
  about whether the demand or the revision is justified, and **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)**
  is "a verdict only given an independently justified standard". This
  claim says where in the formal object that standard has to enter.
- **[CLAIM-745](CLAIM-745.md)** applies it: an administrative criterion imposed as a
  gate is a maintained requirement whose imposition establishes neither
  its legal authority nor its justification. The owner's U46 attached the
  immigration specimen ([CASE-289](../cases.d/CASE-289.md)) to this sentence.
- Bears on [QUESTION-485](../questions.d/QUESTION-485.md): it rules out one source of legitimacy, an
  institution's own maintained requirements, and shows that adequacy in
  the sense of joint satisfiability cannot supply a demand's warrant,
  which is evidence against reducing legitimacy to adequacy.

## What it does not say

- Not that compatibility analysis is idle. It tells which demands can be
  met together; it cannot tell which should be.
- Not who supplies the warrant. That is the independently justified
  standard the record does not yet have ([QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)).
