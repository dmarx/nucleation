# Article 2. The Contracts We Become

## Implicit contractual organization and the developmental constitution of interdependence

Contracts are usually taken to be explicit agreements made by parties who exist before the agreement. This article states a different account, which the Nucleation record holds in parts. On that account an organization's processes can come to stand in contract-like relations that no one stated or agreed to. Those relations form from relied-upon regularities. They govern what happens on deviation, conflict and breach, and so they can give a process a standing that does not depend on moral consideration. They can also change when they are used. The article separates regularity, expectation, dependence and contract, so that no causal dependence is counted as a contract just because something depends on it. It then treats the invocation of a relationship as an operation that can reproduce, form or change the relationship it invokes, and it sorts the ambiguity about which relationship an act invokes into three kinds.

## 2.1 Beyond explicit agreement

The owner's starting point, at U14, was an engineering concept: "a concept that might help us analyze these phenomena is the notion of service level agreements. contracts can form between processes. contracts can even operationalize standing through e.g. prioritization structure in the contract that governs resolution of disputes like races" (CLAIM-tmpkvzcn, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). Before this filing no entry held service-level agreements, contracts between processes or priority structures (ibid., "Where the record stands"). The proposal therefore adds a kind of party, the process, and a kind of standing, an operational one. Neither presupposes moral consideration or agency.

The second half of the same turn keeps the vocabulary apart from moral obligation. In the owner's words, it "mightnot make sense to describe these processes as having moral duties to each other precisely, but these systems of processes absolutely can enterpathological regimes of incompatible behavior requiring intervention and reorganization of commitments to resolve systemic frustration" (CLAIM-tmpe6yfm, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The hedge is "precisely". The claim does not say that no system of processes can bear duties. Nor does it say that every pathological regime is resolved by reorganizing commitments, since some regimes need resources or new capacities (ibid., "What it does not say").

The record's term for what forms is the **organizational contract**:

> a maintained relation among the processes of an organization that governs how they interact, explicitly agreed or formed implicitly from relied-upon regularities, and that is contract-like only if it governs deviation, conflict and breach and not only the ordinary course

(TERM-tmplpplx, Proposed, version 2). The term is the assistant's assembly of the owner's U14 and U16 with A's criterion, coined at the owner's "go ahead and coin the terms". Two exclusions fix its sense. It is not a legal contract, because "an organizational contract may be enforced by nothing but the dependencies it has created". It is also not the contractualist's contract, "an idealized agreement about what can be justified to each person" (ibid.). On the second exclusion, QUESTION-483's note of 2026-11-24 says the owner's contracts bear on the contractualism question only as "a contrast; no edge" (QUESTION-483, Open).

A49's §1 places the account among assume–guarantee contracts, relational contracts and psychological contracts, and argues that each has limits for a general process ontology. **The record cannot yet support that section.** The works are registered and unread:

- assume–guarantee contracts (LIT-tmpm8d98, Deferred);
- relational contracts (Baker, Gibbons and Murphy; LIT-tmp1xqzu);
- psychological and implied contracts (Rousseau; LIT-tmpwdxhm);
- convention (Lewis; LIT-473, Unavailable);
- priority inheritance (Sha, Rajkumar and Lehoczky; LIT-tmphhs41, Deferred).

What the record does say is where the owner's thesis departs from these neighbours. It departs in direction: "a contract reached from below, by the history of expectation and dependence, among parties that need not be agents able to agree" (CLAIM-tmp0d1fq, "Relation to existing work"). Whether that departure is new against the literature is not known until the literature is read.

## 2.2 What makes a relation contract-like

If every stable interaction were a contract, the word would add nothing. A16 §13 says so: "mere behavioral regularity is insufficient to establish a contract-like relationship. Otherwise every stable causal interaction would qualify." It requires "evidence that the arrangement specifically governs counterfactual circumstances of deviation, conflict or breach", and names four marks (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl, Proposed, interpretive, A's; it rests on the owner's CLAIM-tmpkvzcn):

1. "A maintained relationship establishing reciprocal expectations or requirements."
2. "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation."
3. "Governance of circumstances in which requirements conflict."
4. "Some mechanism sustaining or revising that relationship."

**These conditions are necessary, not sufficient.** The claim's title says "contract-like *only if*". A16 calls a relation that meets all four "a strong candidate", not a contract. TERM-tmplpplx's first version had read the marks as what makes a relation contract-like ("because it governs deviation"), and its version 2 corrected that to "only if" (TERM-tmplpplx, history). The article uses the marks only as a filter. A relation that fails one of them is not a contract. A relation that passes all four is a candidate.

The assistant's note on CLAIM-tmp7hqtl shows why the filter is not yet a test. Take two controllers sharing a room, one heating and one cooling, with a deadband and a fixed rule that heating yields when both fire. They meet conditions 1 to 3, and they meet condition 4 if a tuning loop adjusts the deadband. "Whether that is a contract or a regulated regularity is exactly what the test was to decide." The note proposes a further condition, "that the requirements be revisable without replacing the processes (the contract, not the participants, is what changes), and that a breach be registered as a breach of the relation and not only as a deviation of one process". It gives this condition a test by intervention: change the terms, keep the processes, and see whether behaviour follows the terms. The note offers it "as the missing condition, not as settled" (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's). This article adopts the four marks as necessary. It flags the fifth as the assistant's proposal, and it does not use the fifth as a premise.

A16 §6 offers a richer schema, C = (A, G, J, Π, Σ, M, E, R): assumptions, guarantees, jurisdiction, priority policy, suspension, monitoring, enforcement and revision. A16 calls it "a proposed extension for our inquiry, not the standard definition of an assume–guarantee contract" (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl). No entry defines how two such contracts compose. Assume–guarantee contracts compose and refine on (A, G), and LEAD-tmpbqj9u names the first formal step as saying how the other six components compose, "or to show that they reduce to (A, G) over a richer history space" (LEAD-tmpbqj9u, Open, stage idea). A49's §4 lists these components as "contractual structure". On the record they are a list, not yet a structure.

## 2.3 From regularity to maintained commitment

### The owner's route

The owner's U16 asked that the account "ensure we have space for characterizing implicit contracts, e.g. an unspoken understanding that evolved through evolution of expectations and assumptions based on consistently observed behaviors that evolved into dependencies" (CLAIM-tmp0d1fq, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The filing reads this as a three-step route:

1. consistently observed behaviour;
2. expectations and assumptions;
3. dependencies.

The claim's scope matters. It is "not that every regularity, expectation or dependence is a contract": "the owner's route ends in a contract only where the dependence comes to govern how the parties respond to its failure" (ibid., "What it does not say"). That last condition is §2.2's second mark. Joining the owner's route to A's marks in this way is a restatement of CLAIM-tmp0d1fq, not a join, because the claim states the condition itself (JR1). The claim's defeater keeps it modest: it is "not defeated by ... dependences that never became contracts: the claim is that the implicit route exists, not that every dependence takes it".

### Where an expectation can live

A20 §2 gives the owner's route a sense of expectation that needs no representation. Expectations can be represented, dispositional or structural, the last being "its successful operation presupposes another's output". The structural sense comes with a slogan: "**An expectation can be embodied in what an organization no longer needs—or no longer knows how—to do for itself.**" A20's case is a consumer that drops its buffer against a reliable producer: "Its organization now presupposes reliable delivery" (CLAIM-tmp18v1o, Proposed, interpretive, A's; not a member of the work).

The claim says it is more than capability loss. The size of the gap "says which deviations by the producer the consumer can absorb and which it cannot, which is what an implicit contract's tolerances are". Its defeater asks for exactly that: the reading has to predict something the bare loss does not (ibid.).

*Expectation, before the result.* If A20 §6's model shows the route, then with service held at 1, reliance should rise and independent capacity fall, and withdrawal should drop functioning to what capacity remains. The model fails as an illustration if functioning recovers on withdrawal. *Result.* The assistant reran A20's update rule at its default settings, with withdrawal at step 40: "independent capacity at withdrawal is 0.42 and functioning falls to a minimum of 0.34" (CLAIM-tmp18v1o). A20 states the model's limit itself: "It models unilateral dependency formation, not yet the formation of a reciprocally maintained contract."

### One-way dependence and reciprocal reliance

The record's held account of dependence formed by loss is CLAIM-603 (Proposed, interpretive, version 8; the assistant's, on an owner's remark of 2026-11-05). "Whether it is dependency or division of labour is decided by the supply, not the loss": one-way supply set from outside "leaves a dependence that nothing in the dependent's dynamics limits and that withdrawal punishes, while reciprocal loss ... leaves interdependence". The claim is held back by two notes: its title states the favoured loss too generally, and Moore's thesis is not held (CLAIM-603, note of 2026-11-06).

A49's abstract lists five stages: "reliable regularity, acquired expectation, dependence, coordinated reliance and contractual organization". **No entry holds this sequence.** The nearest is A22 §12's six-stage normalization ladder (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, below). That ladder runs from "An exceptional accommodation" to "Enforced: Its withdrawal is treated as a violation", so it ends in enforcement, not in contract. "Coordinated reliance", the reciprocal stage, has no entry of its own.

This article composes the distinctions it needs from four entries. It treats them as distinctions, not as stages every relation passes through:

- *regularity* is what CLAIM-tmp7hqtl says is not enough;
- *expectation*, including structural expectation, is CLAIM-tmp18v1o's;
- *dependence*, one-way or reciprocal, is CLAIM-603's;
- *contract-likeness* is CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's necessary marks.

Reading CLAIM-603's reciprocal loss as A49's "coordinated reliance" is the article's join, not the record's (J1). The join adds no claim that reciprocity is needed for a contract. CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's first mark asks for "reciprocal expectations or requirements", which is not the same as reciprocal loss.

### Relied on is not owed

The normalization ladder has a second part, and that part is what keeps the descriptive account apart from the moral one. "Repetition and reliance can turn a discretionary contribution into an operative obligation, whose withdrawal is treated as a breach, with no agreement ever made" (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, Proposed, interpretive, A's). But Expected, ReliedUpon, Committed and Owes are distinct: "The first three can contribute to the circumstances under which a duty arises. None establishes that duty automatically." And "One cannot necessarily acquire entitlement to another's activity simply by reorganizing oneself around its continued availability" (ibid.).

This part rests on CLAIM-738 (Active, interpretive): "a demand's being asserted, maintained or recognized gives it no warrant, and a commitment is neither an obligation nor an authorization". A24 §11 names the gap between the two parts: C^op, "the actual contractual organization", against C^just, "an arrangement satisfying the relevant justified standards". "There is no reason to presume that these coincide" (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0). Nor does the claim say that a normalized contribution is exploited: "it could be a fair division of labor" (A22 §10, quoted there).

When reliance does make something owed is open. QUESTION-tmprudey (Open; the assistant's, filling A's gap) sets out four grounds: knowing inducement, reasonableness, control, and vulnerability (the last held as CLAIM-671's supposition). It shows that the four "give different verdicts" in A22 §6's case of a volunteer. Article 2 does not answer the question. The abstract's phrase "transforming the participants' future alternatives and vulnerabilities" is descriptive here. What follows from vulnerability is left to Article 8.

## 2.4 Operative norms, and how they outlast their reasons

The ladder's end state is an operative norm. A22 §2 separates two responses to the same discrepancy: "The first changes the expectation to accommodate reality. The second attempts to change reality to satisfy the expectation." "An operative norm might be distinguished partly by the organization's disposition to preserve it against discrepancies rather than simply revise it in response to them" (CLAIM-tmphq6hp, Proposed, interpretive, A's). The assistant links this to the record's direction of fit (TERM-151, Proposed), "a link A does not draw" (ibid.). The claim does not say an operative norm is legitimate. A22 §1 separates the "Operative norm" from the "Legitimate norm".

The article reads §2.2's second mark, "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation", as this distinction: a relation meets the mark when a violation recruits correction rather than revision. CLAIM-tmp0i6c0 already says that the ladder's end is an operative norm "in CLAIM-tmphq6hp's sense". No entry says that CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's second mark *is* the norm's direction of fit. That reading is the article's join (J2). It is also weaker than an identity. A relation can respond differently to violation without enforcing, for instance by renegotiating, and CLAIM-tmphq6hp counts "adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or revision" all as normative governance.

### A norm stabilized by the reliance it attracts

A22 §3 adds a stabilizer that grows with what others have built on the norm: "Compliance produces reasons for further compliance" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx, Proposed, interpretive, version 3, A's, with the assistant's checks). A22's model is x′ = σ(b + λg) − x, r′ = α(x − r), g′ = β(rx − g), with compliance x, reliance r, maintenance resources g, a baseline tendency to comply b and a feedback strength λ.

*Expectations, before the results.* If the model carries A22's sentence, three things should hold:

- at its defaults there should be two stable states and a threshold between them;
- a practice should reach the high state only if it starts above the threshold;
- a practice established at a high baseline should hold the high state at baselines where a fresh practice dies out. The clause "can outlast a weakening of the reasons" fails if established and fresh practices collapse at the same baseline.

*Results.* At b = −3 and λ = 8 there are three equilibria: 0.048 and 0.993 are stable, and 0.689 is a saddle. So "the 'sufficiently established practice' has to be established past roughly 69% compliance" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx). On persistence, cite the corrected version. The assistant's first gap note read the model as letting a norm outlast "a weakening of its baseline support ... not its disappearance". The owner objected: norms do persist long after their original justification disappears, "as race-based power did in the United States after abolition" (CLAIM-tmp8l5s9, Proposed, empirical, the owner's; CASE-tmp3euq0, Proposed, a specimen). The recomputation that followed found: "The established norm holds above 93% compliance while unaided compliance falls from 27% to about 1.3%". The limit is that "below b ≈ −4.33 only the low state exists". "The sentence 'not its disappearance' above is withdrawn" (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx, note of 2026-11-24).

The concession is the assistant's (CLAIM-tmps8ou2, Proposed, interpretive): "A norm's original justification and its baseline support come apart". A justification can vanish while replacement rationales and served interests keep support. The owner's case does not by itself show the reliance loop: "The norm was also maintained by violence, by disfranchisement and by the interests of those it served", and the case "does not separate them" (CASE-tmp3euq0). The case's standing is "documented", but its dates are "recalled and not checked against sources in this filing" (ibid.).

Two further limits apply. The model's "maintenance rule is supplied by the modeler" (A22 §5, quoted in CLAIM-tmpjg5gx), so it shows a candidate stabilizer and not how a standard forms. And the model is of a norm, not of a contract. Reading it as the persistence of an implicit contract's operative obligation goes through CLAIM-tmp0i6c0's end state and CLAIM-tmphq6hp's norm. No entry draws that chain (J3).

## 2.5 Complementary adaptation

Its third part limits it: "Invitation is not establishment". Its notes say that "Neither defeat condition has been tested" (notes of 2026-10-30 and 2026-11-06). ## 2.6 Contractual structure and operational standing

### The vocabulary that is Active

Three Active terms carry the structure:

- **Precedence, jurisdiction and suspension.** "**Precedence:** among applicable constraints, which one controls? **Jurisdiction:** which constraints count as applicable to this case? **Suspension:** when may an otherwise applicable constraint be disabled?" It is "Not a ranking of rules" (TERM-017, Active).
- **Grant.** A conferral that "runs through a maintained relation of granting, delegation, eligibility or support" (TERM-101, Active).
- **Conferral.** "any effect one process has on another's share of future influence" (TERM-103, Active).

CLAIM-283 (Active, interpretive) separates "The precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it". The claim is stated for motives. A16 applies it to processes.

### Operational standing

A16 §3 defines the standing the owner said contracts can operationalize:

> a process's maintained eligibility, under an organizational arrangement, to have specified claims upon another process's activity or upon their shared governance become consequential. This need not imply moral consideration, consciousness or personhood.

(TERM-tmpgiy6r, Proposed, A's). It sets apart four quantities that "can come apart": asserted claim, operational standing, effective authority and moral standing. "A process might have contractual standing but be systematically starved of resources." Standing is also **not reducible to priority**: "A low-priority process could possess a protected guarantee of eventual service, an entitlement to escalate a missed deadline, or immunity from arbitrary cancellation. Standing concerns the structure of recognized claims, not simply one's position in a queue" (ibid.).

This narrows the owner's U14, which offered "prioritization structure" as the example. In the term's own words, "the prioritization structure is one way a contract secures standing, not what standing is" (TERM-tmpgiy6r, "Relations"). The owner's claim does not contradict the narrowing. It does not say "that a contract's priority structure exhausts the standing it secures", and its defeater is a showing that every entitlement such a contract secures "is fixed by the ordering alone" (CLAIM-tmpkvzcn). TERM-tmplpplx's version 2 brought the term into line with the narrowing: "Its priority structure is one place operational standing shows, though standing is not reducible to priority". In this article priority is one carrier of standing, not its home.

The term leaves one thing open, and so does this article: "What makes an eligibility 'maintained' and 'recognized' without a representing process is not said" (TERM-tmpgiy6r).

### The priority-inversion construction

A16 §4 gives the construction (CASE-tmptpna1, Proposed, a stipulated construction, A's):

- three processes share one preemptive processor;
- L holds a lock that H needs;
- H must finish by time 5;
- L needs four units of work, M five and H one;
- only the arbitration rule changes, fixed priorities in one run and priority inheritance in the other.

*Expectations, before the result.* A16 reads the construction as granted authority without efficacy, relieved by "temporarily empowering a lower-priority process upon which it depends". If that reading is right, H should miss its deadline under fixed priorities and meet it under inheritance. If the two rules are distinct contracts that the ordinary course does not reveal, they should give the same schedule when L holds no lock H needs, and different schedules when it does. The reading of the remedy as *relief* fails if the change only moves a cost onto another process.

*Result.* The assistant simulated both schedules unit by unit. Under fixed priorities the completions are "M at 6, L at 9, H at 10". Under inheritance they are "L at 4, H at 5, M at 10". "A16's numbers are right. What its table leaves out is M": the relief "moves four units of delay from H to M". No schedule does better for both, because ten units of work on one processor with H done by 5 leave M finishing at 10 at the earliest. So the case shows governance-induced frustration in CLAIM-357's sense "only because M has no deadline". Give M any deadline before 10 and "inheritance only chooses which commitment fails". The remedy is "a reallocation of delay, not a costless gain" (CASE-tmptpna1, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's). That is CLAIM-556's point (Active, interpretive): "dropping a demand is not relief". A16's table calls the outcome "Relief". This article does not, except in the conditional sense the note allows.

The construction also illustrates operational standing, though the case does not cite the term. In A16's table, "The scheduler recognizes H's assigned priority" while "H remains unable to act because of its dependency". That is standing without effective authority, the first of TERM-tmpgiy6r's two divergences. CASE-tmptpna1 cites CLAIM-283 and TERM-tmpgiy6r cites CLAIM-283. No entry says that this case is an instance of the term (J4). The construction shows nothing about the term's stronger clause, since every entitlement in it is a priority.

### Contracts the ordinary course does not reveal

The two arbitration rules give one schedule whenever L holds no lock H needs. That makes the construction the separating case for a claim A16 §§15 and 19 put forward. The claim was narrowed by the assistant to the part the case supports: "An organization's characteristic structure can include the contracts maintained among its processes, which its ordinary behaviour does not reveal: two arrangements alike on the ordinary course can part when a contract is breached, an assumption fails or a process invokes its standing" (CLAIM-tmpersgx, Proposed, interpretive, version 2; argued in ARG-tmp2dpcv, Proposed).

The claim supersedes CLAIM-tmp1nubb (Superseded), which stated the same proposition for implicit contracts, and it carries that entry's six probes, which part arrangements alike on the ordinary course:

1. interruption of the relied-upon behaviour;
2. a substitute provider;
3. competing demands on the provider;
4. removal of an enforcer;
5. withdrawal of a discretionary contribution;
6. a request to renegotiate.

"Only the responses to these show 'whether a dependence is regulated, by what, and on whose terms' (A22)" (CLAIM-tmpersgx, note of 2026-11-24). The exchange's account, SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr (Proposed, version 2, items 6 and 7), integrates this with the owner's U14 and U16. Applying the probes to implicit contracts is therefore held and is not a join here. That the difference belongs to an organization's character, and not to its circumstances, is Article 1's question.

### What this article does not bridge

Operational standing presupposes no moral standing. TERM-124's domination (Proposed, the assistant's) has as one relatum "The party dominated, with standing (TERM-083)", and TERM-083 is moral standing (Proposed, the owner's, from an exchange of 2026-10-26). **No entry states how a process's operational standing relates to a party that can be dominated.** CLAIM-tmpe6yfm's hedge points one way, since processes need not owe one another duties "precisely". A16 §16 points the same way: "A scheduler may systematically starve a process. That can constitute a functional pathology without constituting an injustice to the process itself" (quoted in CLAIM-tmpe6yfm). This article makes no claim about domination among processes. The bridge is left to Article 9, and it is a gap on the record, not a step this article supplies.

## 2.7 Ambiguous relational invocation

At U21 the owner asked to "integrate contract formation, persistence, and modification in our cost model. It might actually already be in there already under other names like ambiguous invocation of relationship" (QUESTION-tmpb7fx7, Open, the owner's). The filer checked both halves of the suspicion. Formation, persistence and modification were "held, for any form" (CLAIM-569, CLAIM-761, CASE-326, TERM-116, CLAIM-571). "Ambiguous invocation of a relationship: not held". The idea of this section is therefore the owner's. Its development, below, is A's.

A30 §3: "The ambiguity is not merely about what action is requested. It concerns **which relational organization is being enacted through the action**." A30 §5 sorts it into three kinds, each with its own remedy (CLAIM-tmp80ny9, Proposed, interpretive, A's, at the owner's U21):

| Kind | A30's definition | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Epistemic ambiguity | "A determinate arrangement exists, but its applicability is uncertain." | "more information" |
| Relational disagreement | "Participants embody incompatible specifications of their relationship." | "negotiation or arbitration" |
| Constitutive indeterminacy | "No established arrangement yet determines what the relationship requires." | "contract formation" |

The claim's defeater is a kind resolved by another kind's remedy alone, or a showing that the three cannot be told apart "by any observation of the parties' conduct under perturbation".

A32 §4 restates the division over Γ, the joint specifications compatible with what each party treats as governing. One admissible specification is a determinate relationship. Several is "Contractual ambiguity or under-specification". None is "Incompatible local specifications". The assistant's note shows that **the two divisions do not coincide**. An empty Γ is relational disagreement. "Several admissible specifications" covers both epistemic ambiguity and constitutive indeterminacy. What tells those two apart "is whether some arrangement is actually maintained, a fact of maintenance provenance (TERM-100)", and a deviation probe can show it (CLAIM-tmp80ny9, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's; TERM-100, Active). The article uses A30's three kinds. It treats Γ as identifying only the second kind, and it treats the probes of §2.6 as what separates the first kind from the third.

CLAIM-430's note gives the owner's perspectival invitation its place here: "An interaction can invoke an existing contract, invite a new contract, or ambiguously do both" (A32 §2). The note's own mapping puts the third case under constitutive indeterminacy and formative invocation (CLAIM-430, note of 2026-11-24). That mapping is held, so it is not a join (JR2).

### Who resolves the ambiguity

A30 §10 adds a variable that is not the degree of ambiguity: "It is **who controls the resolution of its ambiguity, when resolution becomes necessary, and who pays for it**." Its example: "treat an employee's recurring accommodation as discretionary when asked to compensate it, but as obligatory when the employee declines to provide it" (CLAIM-tmpro73c, Proposed, interpretive, A's, extending the owner's U17). The claim is "different in kind" from residual control in an incomplete contract (CLAIM-728, Proposed), because "no contingency is uncovered". It does not reach a verdict of exploitation. TERM-123 needs a declared standard, and A30 lists "productive under-specification" first among ambiguity's uses (CLAIM-tmpro73c).

### Productive misunderstanding

The owner opened a sidebar on 2026-11-24: "'productive misunderstanding' - identification and clarification of ambiguity as detection of and reorganization around novel consequential distinctions" (CLAIM-tmpupcws, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The claim names two achievements that come apart, detection and reorganization. It places the owner's "novel" distinction under constitutive indeterminacy, or under relational disagreement "when its resolution forms terms neither party held". The assistant answered it twice:

- **A qualification.** A misunderstanding "dates the distinction's relevance rather than its existence", and clarifying can coarsen as well as refine (CLAIM-tmpm76n5, Proposed, interpretive).
- **An objection at full strength.** "A misunderstanding can be productive because it is left unresolved", where each reading is satisfied on every case the cooperation meets (CLAIM-tmpgx2ej, Proposed, interpretive, counter).

At the owner's "file all three of your ideas", the assistant filed a join of the sidebar with A30's kinds and with control over resolution: "Resolving an ambiguity that no arrangement yet settles forms a contract, and the same act has two outcomes: resolved jointly, it is a productive misunderstanding that reorganizes the parties around a new distinction; resolved by one party who controls when and how, it is exploitation through ambiguity" (CLAIM-tmpisfi1, Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's; not a member of the work). It was admitted by a discriminating prediction: "the share of benefit each party takes from the new terms tracks how far that party controlled the timing and terms of the resolution, not how ambiguous the relation was beforehand". The prediction has not been tested. Because the join is on the record, this article cites it and does not make it. The word "exploitation" in its title carries TERM-123's condition of a declared standard, which CLAIM-tmpro73c keeps and CLAIM-tmpisfi1's title does not state.

## 2.8 Stateful invocation

A30 §7 states the difference between invoking a relationship and reusing a stored result: "A cached result normally substitutes for a computation without intentionally changing the underlying problem. Contracts can change the problem" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, Proposed, interpretive, A's, at the owner's U21 and U22). It writes invocation as stateful:

  Invoke(q, C_t) → (a_t, C_{t+1}),  C_{t+1} = Φ(C_t, q_t, a_t, f_t).

A32 extends the response kernel that the record holds from another exchange, K_S(q) = Law(a, τ, c, S′ | do(q), S) (CLAIM-1156, Proposed, interpretive, A's), to K_C(q) = Law(a, τ, κ, C′, S′ | do(q), C, S), where κ is "the distribution of costs among the participating processes". The comparison follows CLAIM-1095's correction (Proposed, demonstrated, counter): the post-state is observed "only through its later responses", not compared directly.

A32 §19 divides the one operation by its effect:

- *reproductive* invocation "Exercises and maintains an existing relationship";
- *formative* invocation "Participates in establishing a previously unformed relationship";
- *transformative* invocation "Modifies the specification or governance of an existing relationship".

"A single interaction can instantiate all three" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). A49's formal centre writes the transition as Invoke(q, S_t) → (a_t, C_{t+1}, S_{t+1}), and its abstract says invocation can "exercise, reproduce, establish or modify" a relationship. These are A49's restatements of A32.

**Two limits.** First, "It does not say what the right update rule Φ is", and no entry defines Φ. Second, the claim leans toward the null that would deflate it. A30: "the critical test is whether these require genuinely new cost terms, or merely richer state variables and transition mechanisms within the existing model. I suspect the latter will cover most of the cases" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp).

What the existing cost model prices is ARG-tmpa5ped's subject (Proposed, a formal application; the inference is A30's, the critical questions the assistant's). The model prices formation, upkeep, recognition, checking and revision. It does not price an invocation that changes the store: "the model's store is the same before and after a use, and σ and ε enter as fixed parameters". Where an agreement is externally enforced and invocation leaves it unchanged, "the model applies as it stands". The critical questions also find the mapping "loose" for ρ: "Only the first of CLAIM-tmp80ny9's three kinds is a retrieval or verification cost" (ARG-tmpa5ped). So the Invoke transition cannot treat ambiguity as a single retrieval parameter. Resolving relational disagreement or constitutive indeterminacy costs negotiation or formation.

The test is LEAD-tmp14n26 (Open, stage idea; A30 §15, with the design the assistant's). It would build two relationships matched in per-use cost and output, one whose invocations leave it unchanged and one whose invocations expand its jurisdiction and erode the recipient's alternatives. It would then ask whether their later costs part by more than "the fixed-parameter formula with a re-estimated ε reproduces". **It has not been run.** The pricing, including what the stateful store costs and who pays, is Article 3's.

## 2.9 Contractual identity

A49's §7 asks what makes a contract persist "through renegotiation, personnel changes and substitution of implementation". **No claim on the record states an answer.** The only hold is the assistant's proposed fifth condition on CLAIM-tmp7hqtl: the requirements should be "revisable without replacing the processes (the contract, not the participants, is what changes)", with its test, "change the terms and keep the processes, and see whether behaviour follows the terms" (§2.2). That condition is about telling a contract from a regularity, not about its persistence. A persistence condition would be its converse: replace the processes, keep the terms, and see whether the relation's responses to deviation survive. CLAIM-tmpersgx's second probe, a substitute provider, is the nearest the record comes. This section is a gap and states no thesis.

## 2.10 The formal centre, and what would test it

A49 proposes to "Construct comparative systems exhibiting mere coupling, unilateral dependence, coordinated adaptation and maintained conditional commitments. Probe whether deviation produces different regulatory responses." No such systems have been built. The record holds parts of the design:

- one construction for each of two of the four systems: A20's dependency model for unilateral dependence (CLAIM-tmp18v1o), and the two arbitration rules for maintained conditional commitments (CASE-tmptpna1);
- the assistant's two controllers, a candidate for mere coupling that passes the four marks (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's note);
- the probes, in CLAIM-tmpersgx;
- the governance architectures, from fixed priority to a "Reflexive contract ecology", in LEAD-tmpbqj9u (Open, stage idea: "the arms and measures are named, the model is not");
- the shared organizational state, in LEAD-tmpfq5cj (Open, stage idea: "the state is named, its dynamics are not").

No lead holds A49's four-system comparison. Its expectations can be stated before anything is run. Matched on the ordinary course, the four systems should part under CLAIM-tmpersgx's probes in this order:

- under interruption, mere coupling and unilateral dependence should show no correction, only a drop in functioning;
- coordinated adaptation should show both parties' functioning fall;
- maintained conditional commitments should recruit correction toward the terms.

The account fails if the probes do not part the systems, or if they part them as well on the ordinary course. It also fails if the two controllers respond to a change of terms with processes kept exactly as a maintained commitment does, since §2.2's filter would then have no test to back it. These are expectations for a study that has not been run. They are not results.

## 2.11 What the article claims, and with what standing

1. Contracts can form between processes and can operationalize a standing that is not moral (the owner's U14; CLAIM-tmpkvzcn, CLAIM-tmpe6yfm; Proposed, interpretive). Priority is one carrier of that standing, not its home (TERM-tmpgiy6r, Proposed).
2. A relation is contract-like only if it governs deviation, conflict and breach. The four marks are necessary, and whether they are sufficient is open (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl, Proposed, interpretive, with the assistant's proposed fifth condition).
3. Contracts can be implicit, reached from relied-upon regularity, with expectations that can be structural (the owner's U16; CLAIM-tmp0d1fq; CLAIM-tmp18v1o; Proposed, interpretive). Being relied on is not being owed (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, Proposed, on CLAIM-738, Active). When reliance makes something owed is open (QUESTION-tmprudey).
4. An operative norm can be stabilized by the reliance it attracts, and in A22's model it can outlast most of its baseline support (CLAIM-tmpjg5gx as corrected at the owner's objection CLAIM-tmp8l5s9; Proposed). This is a property of a stipulated model.
5. Arrangements alike on the ordinary course can part under breach, failed assumption or invoked standing (CLAIM-tmpersgx, Proposed, interpretive). Its separating case reallocates delay and does not relieve it (CASE-tmptpna1).
6. The ambiguity about which relationship an act invokes has three kinds with different remedies (the owner's U21; CLAIM-tmp80ny9, Proposed). Who controls its resolution can matter more than how ambiguous it is (CLAIM-tmpro73c, Proposed). Joint and one-sided resolution of constitutive indeterminacy may be one act with two outcomes (CLAIM-tmpisfi1, Proposed, the assistant's, untested).
7. Invocation can change the relationship it invokes, and the cost model prices it only once invocation returns the next state (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, ARG-tmpa5ped; Proposed). Whether a richer state suffices is open (LEAD-tmp14n26, not run).

No item is Active. A49's "Primary contribution", "A theory of contracts as organizational achievements rather than necessarily explicit antecedent agreements", is on the record as a set of Proposed claims whose defeaters have not been tested.
