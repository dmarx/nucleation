# NOTE-797: Mashour & Alkire — Evolution of consciousness and emergence from anesthesia

## Contribution

The paper adds a proposal, not a result. Consciousness could be studied
in its evolutionary emergence by watching it return after general
anaesthesia, which the authors call "a reproducible model system that can
be observed in real time over the course of hours". It joins that
proposal to a reading of the human emergence data and of comparative
neuroanatomy, and concludes that birds and early mammals are capable of
phenomenal consciousness.

## Key insight

Recovery from anaesthesia runs in order: "from primitive homeostatic
functions (such as breathing) to evidence of arousal (such as
responsiveness to pain or eye opening) to consciousness of the environment
(as evidenced by the ability to follow a command) to higher cognitive
function". The authors read that order as phylogeny replayed: "General
anesthesia represents a way of turning back the evolutionary clock". If
the first structures to come back are ancient and conserved, then so is
the core of consciousness.

## Assumptions

- **What a model needs.** The authors set three conditions: "(i)
  consciousness emerges from unconsciousness at a discrete and measurable
  point, (ii) phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness are
  closely juxtaposed or collapsed, and (iii) assessment of neural
  structure and function is possible." Condition (ii) is asserted of
  emergence, not shown.
- **Command-following marks consciousness.** "The emergence of
  consciousness (as judged by the return of a response to command)". They
  grant "the contamination of access consciousness, because language is
  involved in assessing return of consciousness after anesthesia".
- **Emergence shows the core.** Studying the return, not the loss,
  avoids "nonspecific deactivations due to drug effects".
- **Homology carries function.** Structures homologous or analogous to the
  human core (brainstem, thalamus, hypothalamus, "association neocortex
  (or equivalent) capable of recurrent processing") are taken to support
  the same consciousness in other species.
- **Self-recognition indicates self-awareness**, with the stated caveat
  that mirror behaviour may be explained by "simple knowledge of one's
  body".

## Key results

The paper reports no data of its own. Its empirical claims are cited:

- **The emergence PET study (Långsjö et al. 2012, ref. 65).** Healthy men
  recovering from propofol or dexmedetomidine. The return of a response to
  command "was correlated primarily with activity of the brainstem (locus
  coeruleus), hypothalamus, thalamus, and anterior cingulate (medial
  prefrontal area)". "Surprisingly, there was limited neocortical
  involvement". "Frontal–parietal connectivity appeared to be the key
  cortical response", confirmed by EEG (Lee et al. 2011, ref. 70).
- **Waking from sleep (Balkin et al. 2002, ref. 72).** "Midline arousal
  structures of the thalamus and brainstem also recovered function well
  before cortical connectivity resumed."
- **The authors' own caution.** The PET correlates "may reflect an
  arousal-related response to the test stimulus itself rather than a
  direct awareness of the stimuli that is occurring in the thalamus".
- **Anaesthesia and integration.** Phi "is predicted to decrease during
  sleep and seizures; preliminary evidence suggests it also decreases
  during anesthesia (ref. 25)". Anaesthetics preferentially suppress
  long-range and frontal-to-posterior feedback connectivity (refs. 26,
  38–40).
- **The inference to other species.** "The recent experiments with general
  anesthesia in humans suggest that phylogenetically ancient structures in
  the brainstem and diencephalon—with only limited neocortical
  involvement—are sufficient to support primitive consciousness." Synapsids
  and sauropsids diverged "∼315 million years ago". Birds show episodic
  recall, theory of mind and, in magpies, mirror self-recognition, and
  have a pallium with recurrent processing "not found in reptiles". So
  "consciousness evolved along two independent lineages", and "the
  foundations required for both consciousness of the world and
  consciousness of the self may have formed as early as the amniote
  radiation".
- **The conclusion.** "By 'reverse engineering,' we postulate that early
  mammals and birds possessing these structures (or their equivalents) are
  capable of phenomenal consciousness."
- **Human uniqueness.** It is put down to richer frontal interconnectivity
  and a dominant anterior-to-posterior flow "dominant in humans (39) but
  not in rodents (38, 102)". "The role of information flow in
  consciousness is unclear at this time."

## Concepts

- **Phenomenal and access consciousness.** After Block: "Phenomenal
  consciousness is subjective experience itself, whereas access
  consciousness is that which is available to other cognitive processes
  (such as working memory or verbal report)."
- **Awareness and arousal.** Awareness is "the content of consciousness",
  arousal "brain activation and level-of-consciousness". The authors
  divide phenomenal consciousness into these two processes.
- **Primitive (core) consciousness.** The form that returns first after
  anaesthesia, judged by command-following. It is not defined
  independently of that marker.
- **Motility (motricity).** Proposed, after Merleau-Ponty, Llinás and
  Buzsáki, as "the evolutionary anlage of consciousness".
- **Emergence.** The return of consciousness after anaesthesia, as
  opposed to induction.

## Connections

It belongs to Mashour's anaesthesia work, which reads anaesthetic loss of
consciousness as the disruption of frontal-to-parietal feedback (Ku et al.
2011, Lee et al. 2009 and 2011). It sides with the Cambridge Declaration
on Consciousness (2012) and with Darwin on continuity, and it uses Merker's
hydranencephaly argument (ref. 75), which the record holds through
[THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md). It uses IIT's Phi as a "guiding paradigm" and proposes
measuring it during emergence. It cites Block (2007) for the
phenomenal/access distinction.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md) (deficit evidence).** The paper is an example of what
  the question asks about, and it confirms [THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md)'s limit in its own
  words.
  - Its marker of consciousness returning is "the return of a response to
    command". That is a marker of access. The authors grant that access
    "contaminates" the model, and their condition (ii) needs phenomenal
    and access consciousness "closely juxtaposed or collapsed" at
    emergence. It is assumed, not shown.
  - So the recovery evidence establishes what returns with access. Its
    move to "primitive phenomenal consciousness" is the transfer [THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md)
    calls a fallacy when made from a deficit.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md) (transfer to systems without cortex).** The paper's
  answer is "yes, by homology". Birds qualify through a pallium with
  recurrent processing, and through behaviour (episodic recall, theory of
  mind, mirror self-recognition).
  - That is architectural and behavioural evidence. [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) holds
    that architectural evidence presupposes the disputed theory.
  - [THEORY-100](../theory.d/THEORY-100.md) holds a report correlate in the crow's nuclear pallium.
    This paper adds no data about birds, only the anatomical argument.
  - It never applies the anaesthesia model to another species. It
    proposes doing so, for example by measuring Phi at the human threshold
    and comparing other species' waking values.
- **[LIT-965](../literature.d/LIT-965.md)'s earlier description was loose.** The status note said the
  paper uses emergence as "deficit and recovery evidence about
  consciousness in species without a human cortex". It uses human
  emergence data only. The step to other species is by comparative
  anatomy, not by anaesthesia evidence from them.
- **[LIT-965](../literature.d/LIT-965.md)'s earlier summary was loose too.** It took the abstract's
  "found at the earliest points of vertebrate brain evolution". The body
  concludes something narrower: "early mammals and birds", with
  foundations "as early as the amniote radiation". It says it is
  "unlikely that paramecia and sea squirts have phenomenal experience".
  And "degree and not kind" sits beside Gallup's "qualitative
  psychological difference among primates", which the authors quote, and
  their own view that human consciousness "seems unique".
- **[THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md).** The cited emergence data are consistent with its
  subcortical clause: ancient structures with "only limited neocortical
  involvement" suffice. But the marker here is command-following, not
  felt alarm, and the authors keep "limited neocortical connectivity and
  recurrent processing" in the core. So the paper is not a second source
  for [THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md)'s "before and without cortex".
- **[THEORY-096](../theory.d/THEORY-096.md).** Anaesthetics suppressing frontal-to-posterior feedback
  is cited as a correlate. [THEORY-096](../theory.d/THEORY-096.md)'s own promote_when sets anaesthesia
  data aside as consistent with all the leading theories. This reading
  agrees, and adds nothing to that account.
- **No THEORY is filed.** The paper is a perspective. Its one empirical
  finding (C2) is second-hand, from a study the record does not hold. Its
  conclusions are argued by homology at a strength that would make a
  THEORY a restatement of opinion. The record already holds what bears on
  it: [THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md) on recovery evidence, [THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md) on the subcortical core,
  and [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) and [THEORY-100](../theory.d/THEORY-100.md) on architectural transfer. Långsjö et al.
  (2012), the primary source, would earn a reading if the question needs
  the emergence result first-hand.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- No new data. Every finding is cited, and the central one is a single
  PET study.
- The marker of return is a response to command. The model cannot tell
  returning experience from returning responsiveness, and the authors
  name the confound.
- "Recapitulates phylogeny" is an analogy. The authors themselves reject
  Haeckel's recapitulation for ontogeny in the introduction.
- The step from homologous structure to phenomenal consciousness assumes
  that the structures do the same work across species.
- The abstract's conclusions are stronger than the body's.

## Open questions

- Does the order in which function returns after anaesthesia match the
  phylogenetic order of the structures that return? The paper asserts the
  parallel and does not test it.
- Can a report-free marker of returning experience be fixed before
  emergence, so that experience and responsiveness can come apart? Without
  one, the model inherits [THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md)'s limit.
- Run in another species, would the model show the same subcortical-first
  order? The authors propose this and do not do it.
