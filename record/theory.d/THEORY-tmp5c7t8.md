# THEORY-tmp5c7t8: Industry can bias a scientific community toward a worse treatment without corrupting any individual, by selectively funding researchers whose methods already favour its product, provided researchers differ in method and both influence and the training of newcomers follow productivity; an independent funder that discounts industry-funded work counteracts this, and one that funds on productivity alone makes it worse

## Source

Holman & Bruner (2017), *Experimentation by Industrial Selection*, [LIT-744](../literature.d/LIT-744.md),
read in full in [NOTE-574](../notes.d/NOTE-574.md). What its model and figures showed is [THEORY-146](THEORY-146.md),
which this entry extends.

## The claim

The paper names a route by which industry biases science that is not
corruption: industrial selection. Where researchers honestly differ in how
they measure success, funding those whose measure already favours a
product makes them more productive, more influential with their peers, and
more likely to train the next generation, so the community drifts toward
their method while no one's judgment changes ([NOTE-574](../notes.d/NOTE-574.md), C1–C2). Without
methodological diversity it does not happen (C3). An independent funder
that rewards productivity counting industry-funded work makes it worse,
and one that discounts that work counteracts it (C4). The authors offer
the antiarrhythmic drug disaster as a real case of the mechanism, read from
a secondary history (C6).

The evidence is one bandit model at a few parameter settings, with the
funder comparison at one setting, and one historical case. The claim is
about real research communities.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, which is [QUESTION-315](../questions.d/QUESTION-315.md): evidence from real fields that
traces methods through funding and training lineages, and comparisons of
funders whose merit assessments do or do not count industry-funded work.
More simulations of the same model family, and case histories read through
the model, cannot settle it.
