# LIT-126: The Category Error in Contemporary AI Safety Discourse and Why Non-Sentient Systems Cannot Be Moral Machines

Armando Vieira (2026), *PhilArchive preprint (PhilArchive lists it as forthcoming in AI & Society; unverified)* — <https://philarchive.org/archive/VIETCE>

## Key takeaways

- Talk of AI systems as "moral machines" or possible moral patients is a Rylean category error that mistakes functional sophistication for phenomenal consciousness. AI safety should therefore be reframed around impacts on sentient beings rather than around the systems' own moral status.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

AI safety discourse increasingly treats AI systems as possible bearers of moral status, calling them "moral machines" and debating their rights and responsibilities. Vieira argues that this conflates functional sophistication with phenomenal consciousness, the sentience he holds is required for moral patiency. Drawing on the philosophical-zombie tradition, AI ethics and critiques of anthropomorphism, he argues that current AI systems are "moral zombies", able to simulate moral behaviour without the experiences that ground moral concern. He takes up objections from relational ethics, from human–AI interaction studies and from global perspectives on moral status, and recasts AI safety as a problem of ontological and ethical clarity rather than value encoding.
