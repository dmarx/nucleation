# CLAIM-tmplkh2i: Many of the line's defeat conditions cannot in practice be met: the comparative ones are null results with no margin over an observable set the thesis may re-choose, and the possibility theses' are universal negatives that no finite study establishes

## The objection

Three shapes of defeat condition, and one variant, cannot be met by any study
the programme could run.

**Nulls with no margin.** [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md), [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md), [CLAIM-005](CLAIM-005.md) and [CLAIM-147](CLAIM-147.md) are
each defeated when a rival predicts the judgements "as well as" the theory.
That is a null hypothesis. A failure to find a difference does not show there
is none, and showing that two predictors are equal needs an equivalence test:
a margin δ fixed in advance, and enough data that a difference larger than δ
would have been detected. Without a margin the condition is either never met,
since any difference in the theory's favour, however small, means the rival
did not do "as well", or met by any underpowered study, since no detected
difference counts as "as well". No entry
in claims, questions, cases or terms states a margin, a power or a sample
size. The only hits for "power" are "predictive power" and "discriminating
power" ([CLAIM-098](CLAIM-098.md), [QUESTION-027](../questions.d/QUESTION-027.md)).

**An observable set the thesis may re-choose.** [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s "What it does not
say": "It does not say the observables are fixed in advance". [CLAIM-128](CLAIM-128.md)
leaves the weights of the fidelity profile to the task: its defeat condition
is met if the theory forces a single weighting "instead of leaving it to be
chosen per task". So after a failure, a new set of observables or a new
weighting restores the thesis. Preregistering the cover (manuscript §10, as
[QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) reports) binds one study. It does not bind the thesis, whose
defeat condition is stated over "pragmatic observables" in general. The
exchange had the remedy and lost it. [CASE-029](../cases.d/CASE-029.md): A49 said the failure conditions
belonged in the methods "as **predefined decision rules**", but "outline v4
(A52) lists only outcomes", after U19's instruction to withhold hedging
([CLAIM-095](CLAIM-095.md)).

**Universal negatives for possibility theses.** Several supporting theses say
only that something can happen, and are defeated only if it never does, or if
the opposite always does:

- [CLAIM-033](CLAIM-033.md) (a translation "can broaden" one dimension and narrow another):
  defeated if renderings "never gain specificity along another";
- [CLAIM-087](CLAIM-087.md) (cross-modal reconstruction "can improve access"): defeated if
  access "never rises above the text-only baseline at any stage";
- [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md) (translation "may preserve local meanings" while changing global
  structure): defeated if preservation of the local distributions "always
  carries" preservation of global compatibility;
- likewise [CLAIM-117](CLAIM-117.md) ("never diverge"), [CLAIM-081](CLAIM-081.md) ("always move together"),
  [CLAIM-054](CLAIM-054.md) ("never separate"), [CLAIM-038](CLAIM-038.md) ("Every empirical model") and
  [CLAIM-044](CLAIM-044.md) ("Every pattern").

A thesis of the form "there is a case where X" is confirmed by one case and
refuted only by "in no case X", which no finite study establishes. The
exchange's own synthetic constructions were of exactly that kind: [CLAIM-028](CLAIM-028.md)
calls them "exact existence constructions for three of the manuscript's
claims". So the proposed experiments can neither defeat these theses nor add
to them beyond a construction. What the main thesis needs from them is
something else. [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) rests on [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md), and [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s defeat condition
is comparative: for the pragmatic observables to beat text similarity in real
translations, these effects must be common and large there. None of these
theses states a prevalence or a magnitude.

**Impossibility claims.** Two defeat conditions are modal universals.
[CLAIM-077](CLAIM-077.md) is defeated if "No family of observable distinctions can be fixed
independently of the transports being compared"; [CLAIM-143](CLAIM-143.md)'s second disjunct
is that "no correspondence can be validated independently of the fidelity it
is used to measure". No finite record of failures shows that something cannot
be done, so the admissibility thesis cannot fail by its own condition. A
variant is [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md)'s: two realizations agree "on every independently
anchored relational invariant tried" and are judged different acts. That can
be met, but meeting it shows only that the invariants tried were incomplete,
not that no invariant is complete, which is what the thesis says.

Granted, because every point is read off the wording of the defeat
conditions and of [CLAIM-028](CLAIM-028.md) and [CASE-029](../cases.d/CASE-029.md).

## What it does not say

- It does not say these theses are false, or untestable in principle.
- It does not say [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) is settled either way.
- It does not say [CLAIM-095](CLAIM-095.md)'s instruction was wrong. It says the decision rules
  it swept out were not hedges.
- The remedy is the owner's: each comparative `defeated_if` restated with a
  margin and a prespecified set of observables and weights, each possibility
  thesis given a claim of prevalence or magnitude that a study could defeat,
  and each impossibility condition replaced by an observable one.
