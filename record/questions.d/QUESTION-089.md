# QUESTION-089: When does a process that destabilizes a whole's prevailing pattern improve the organization rather than only disrupt it, and does that depend on others able to build on what it opens and on authority that can move to them?

## Why it is a question

A4 §9B: "**When does dissent improve the organization rather than merely
destabilize it?** ... A dissenting operator may re-noise a region, but its
contribution depends on whether the system can subsequently exploit the
newly accessible alternatives. A potential prediction is that re-noising is
most valuable when paired with translation and synthesis capacity, and when
authority can be dynamically reallocated to those operators." A3 §6 gave the
case. A skeptic that cannot overturn an emerging consensus endorses a
process that can generate alternative hypotheses, and "The population can
become more epistemically robust precisely because a dissenting operator
successfully delegates authority to a different operator." A7's H3 and its
second result: "constructive dissent improves adaptation".

It refines [QUESTION-041](QUESTION-041.md). That question asks which channels keep a whole's
course correctable. This one asks when correcting through them helps, since
A3 §9 granted that "A re-noising operator may be locally successful but
globally harmful". The record's readings on it are March's exploitation
crowding out exploration ([THEORY-142](../theory.d/THEORY-142.md)), Longino's critical practices
([THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)) and Hirschman's exit and voice ([THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md)).
[CLAIM-279](../claims.d/CLAIM-279.md) holds that "destabilizing" is relative to the pattern
defended. So the question must fix whose adaptation counts. An answer gives
the conditions, and a case where the same dissent helps under them and harms
without them.

## Note of 2026-10-23: dissent in hidden-profile groups, from abstracts

Cited at A5 of the exchange of 2026-10-23, "Framework Overview Request" as results that meet part of [CASE-108](../cases.d/CASE-108.md)'s aim.
Neither study was reached; both are read from their PubMed abstracts.

- **Schulz-Hardt et al. (2006, [LIT-tmpefjd9](../literature.d/LIT-tmpefjd9.md)).** Groups whose members agreed
  on a wrong option before discussion "hardly ever solved the hidden
  profile"; dissent raised solution rates "even if none of these individual
  prediscussion preferences were correct", mainly through greater
  discussion intensity. A case where destabilizing a consensus helps even
  when the dissenter is wrong.
- **Mojzisch and Schulz-Hardt (2010, [LIT-tmp4zxlj](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4zxlj.md))** bound it: learning the
  others' preferences at the start impeded solution, through less attention
  to the information exchanged, even when another member favoured the
  correct option.
- **Lu, Yuan and McLeod (2012, [LIT-tmp1k4zi](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1k4zi.md))**, a meta-analysis of 65
  studies and 3,189 groups: hidden-profile groups were eight times less
  likely than fully informed groups to find the correct solution, and
  pooling of unique information predicted decision quality.

Together they suggest the condition is whether dissent drives the handling
of information, not whether positions are visible. The question's second
half, about others building on what dissent opens and about authority
moving to them, is untouched. A THEORY waits for a reading.
