summary: >-
  Corominas-Murtra, Hanel & Thurner (2015), Understanding scaling through history-dependent processes with collapsing sample space, PNAS (Crossref: doi 10.1073/pnas.1420946112, 2015-04-13); arXiv 1407.2775. A sample-space-reducing (SSR) process is one whose reachable states shrink, nested, as it unfolds: from state i it jumps uniformly to a state below i, restarting at the top on reaching 1. Such a process visits state i with probability exactly 1/i, proved by induction, Zipf's law in rank. Mixing each step with probability 1 − λ with a uniform jump over all N states gives stationary occupation asymptotically ∝ i^(−λ), so the rank exponent is the share of nested steps and the frequency exponent is (1 + λ)/λ ∈ [2, ∞). Examples are word order in sentences (λ ≈ 0.9 fitted to one book), random walks on a directed network and fragmentation. Offered as a route to Zipf's law without multiplicative, preferential or critical mechanisms; the abstract's "necessarily" outruns the proof, which covers uniform jumps only. The strict process is a Markov chain with no reinforcement. Here as item 5 of the record's catalogue of feedback-free mechanisms (exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws", A5).

## Note of 2026-11-22: Corominas-Murtra, Hanel and Thurner, read

Read in NOTE-tmpyqqhq. The registration's summary was accurate for the
construction it named; the reading adds that the exponent is tunable,
p(i) ∝ i^(−λ) with λ the share of nested steps, that "history-dependent"
in the title means state-dependent (the strict process is a Markov chain,
P(j | i) = 1/(i − 1)), and that the abstract's "necessarily lead to Zipf's
law" is proved only for uniform jumps.
