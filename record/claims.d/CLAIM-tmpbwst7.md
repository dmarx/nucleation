# CLAIM-tmpbwst7: Context-sensitive interpretation, order effects and noncommuting framing operations do not by themselves establish nonclassical probability or quantum contextuality

## The claim

Manuscript §9: "Generally AB≠BA even in a finite classical Markov system;
therefore order sensitivity alone does not establish quantum contextuality or
incompatible quantum measurements."

## How it was built

1. **A21** flagged it and went on regardless: "order effects alone don't
   establish quantum contextuality. Classical models with memory or state
   changes can also produce them."
2. **A24**, after the owner opened the record at U11: "Context-sensitive
   meaning ⟹ Quantum contextuality. That implication is unjustified." The
   ground was [THEORY-013](../theory.d/THEORY-013.md): once context-dependent marginals are separated out,
   behavioural, social and word-meaning data published as contextual show none.
   And from [LIT-312](../literature.d/LIT-312.md), Aerts and Gabora's Hilbert-space concepts, whose worked
   model uses "projectors diagonal in a single basis" so that its fits establish
   nothing beyond classical probability. Hence A24's ladder of evidential
   burden: framing effects, order effects, Hilbert-space models with "predictive
   utility against classical baselines", formal contextuality. "a Hilbert-space
   representation alone does not establish nonclassicality."
3. **A27**: "Neither phenomenon is inherently quantum."
4. **A30**, against the owner's [CLAIM-tmpjwomz](CLAIM-tmpjwomz.md): [CASE-tmp11vtm](../cases.d/CASE-tmp11vtm.md), and "observing order
   dependence is sufficient to establish noncommutativity of the effective
   operations, but not sufficient to establish nonclassical probability" ([TERM-tmpnvqh8](../terms.d/TERM-tmpnvqh8.md)).

## Dropped on the way, worth keeping

- **[THEORY-013](../theory.d/THEORY-013.md) as ground.** The manuscript states the conclusion without the
  empirical reason to expect it, which is what the record supplied.
- **The discriminating tests.** A30 named the QQ equality, "a parameter-free
  constraint", as "a more discriminating prediction than order dependence
  alone", and the Grand Reciprocity equations as a test quantum models can
  fail. The manuscript §9 asks for "independently specified constraints"
  without naming any. [LIT-316](../literature.d/LIT-316.md) (Busemeyer and Bruza) is in the record for
  these and was not named. A30's figures for the QQ tests come from search
  results and are not checked here.

## What it does not say

That pragmatic judgements are classical. Whether they are formally contextual
is [CLAIM-tmp1ycte](CLAIM-tmp1ycte.md), still open.
