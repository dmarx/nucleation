# CLAIM-tmpbypt7: A technology can become practically compulsory without any rule requiring it: as infrastructures, competitors and expectations adapt to its adoption, the alternatives left to those who decline it shrink, so the freedom an arrangement leaves is measured by what remains open to a non-adopter, not by what it lets adopters do

## The claim

The exchange "Analyze technological civilization trap"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af6b47c-8b0c-83ea-ae04-3e3eb2649597,
2026-11-13) returned to one mechanism in four turns. A3 §III:

> Something that originally expanded choice gradually becomes a
> prerequisite for ordinary participation.

A13 §II named it "the dependency ratchet" and gave it three parts:

- **Infrastructural adaptation.** Supporting institutions reorganize around
  the technology (roads, fuel and parking for the automobile), which makes
  it more useful and its alternatives less practical. "The crucial point is
  that a technological arrangement can become compulsory without any legal
  requirement to use it."
- **Competitive pressure.** Firms, workers and states adopt because rivals
  do, so adoption is "formally voluntary but strategically compelled".
- **Cultural normalization.** "What initially appears optional becomes
  expected", until the arrangement is hard even to recognize as contingent.

A13 places the point of no return where "the cost of restoring
alternatives exceeds what individual participants can reasonably bear".
A11 §4 gave the measure the claim keeps:

> The relevant measure of technological freedom is not merely what a
> technology allows someone to do, but what possibilities remain available
> when they choose not to use it.

## Which sense of "organization"

Where A speaks of society or institutions "reorganizing" around a
technology, the sense is [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)'s sense 1, a change in a relational
arrangement of infrastructures, practices and expectations, not an act of
a collective institution (sense 3). No one need decide it; that is the
claim's point.

## What the record held

- **Lock-in between competing technologies.** Arthur's model ([THEORY-863](../theory.d/THEORY-863.md)):
  increasing returns make one technology take the market, not necessarily
  the better one, and no small reversal undoes it. That is a choice
  between two technologies, not between adopting and abstaining; what it
  carries to this claim is [ARG-tmp7y8r4](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp7y8r4.md).
- **Path dependence in politics** ([THEORY-885](../theory.d/THEORY-885.md)), and protective lock-in, a
  regime that shifts the evidence for its own necessity ([CLAIM-445](CLAIM-445.md),
  [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)).
- **An arrangement that produces the dependence it exploits** ([CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md)),
  and dependence organized as opportunity, vulnerability or leverage
  ([CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md)). Those are about a party that relies on a provider. This
  claim is about the party who declines: what the adopters' reorganization
  leaves to someone who does not take part.

## What it does not say

- Not that dependence is unfreedom. A says repeatedly that some
  dependencies (medicine, the grid, sanitation) enlarge what people can
  do; the claim measures what is left to a non-adopter, and a technology
  can enlarge that too.
- Not that the mechanisms always run. A13: "A proposed mechanism of
  technological lock-in, not a universal law of innovation."
- Not when compulsory dependence is objectionable. A11 §4: "compulsory
  dependence requires justification"; the threshold A proposed for that is
  [CLAIM-tmpkjv7u](CLAIM-tmpkjv7u.md).

## Note of 2026-11-13: the readings of 2026-11-13

Readings registered from the exchange and read, or read through stand-ins, on 2026-11-13. Each that gives evidence carries `corroborates` to this claim.

- **Illich through Cayley** (THEORY-tmpxwo9k): the test of radical monopoly, "whether one can still find an alternative or decently do without", is this claim's measure; the car environment its infrastructural mechanism; alternatives made "unimaginable" its cultural one. **It qualifies the title.** Illich's burial example ends in law: "Now legislation is being passed to make the mortician's ministrations compulsory." A de facto monopoly can later be codified, so "without any rule requiring it" describes how the compulsion can arise, not that it stays unwritten. The title is kept; this note records the qualification.
- **Ellul 1983** (THEORY-tmpypzp0): outside the technical city "the only choices left" are two. Ellul goes further than the claim: particular choices "no longer have much significance".
- **Rosa** (THEORY-tmp0dvpy): people keep pace "para evitar la pérdida de opciones y conexiones", the non-adopter's loss of options, and standing still means falling behind.
- **Moen** (THEORY-tmpzuniv): the car city and the arms race, the infrastructural and competitive mechanisms, from a source independent of the exchange.
- **Morozov** (THEORY-tmps3a0q): once most people self-track, declining is "no longer an autonomous decision"; mechanism and measure, by example.
- **Crawford 2006** (THEORY-tmpiihve): Ford "destroyed the possibility of an alternative way of working"; competitive mechanism.
- **Postman** (THEORY-tmp8mi75): curricula reorganized around tests; technologies become mythic.
- **Kelly** (THEORY-tmp5l4e4): a foil, not evidence. His "More ways, more choices" measures the options technology gives its adopters, the measure this claim sets aside; it neither meets the claim's defeater nor answers it.
- **Lanier** (THEORY-tmpvajjd) and **Hui** (THEORY-tmpyuoss): Lanier names lock-in in software without the claim's mechanism; Hui's bifurcated technological futures say lock-in to one path is not necessary, without a mechanism that keeps a non-adopter's options open. Points of contact, no relation.
- **Feenberg** (THEORY-tmpa59p3): lock-in depends on the prevailing technical code, which shifts the emphasis to contestability; consistent with the claim.
