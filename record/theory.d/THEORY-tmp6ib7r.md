# THEORY-tmp6ib7r: The minimal phenomenal self is global self-identification with the body, self-location and a weak, geometrical first-person perspective; body-part ownership leaves it untouched, autoscopic phenomena and full-body illusions displace its parts, and because those illusions are passive, agency only causally enables the minimal self and is not part of it

## Source

- Blanke & Metzinger (2009), "Full-body illusions and minimal phenomenal
  selfhood", *Trends Cogn. Sci.* 13(1):7–13, [LIT-tmpypm1w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpypm1w.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-tmpyzsg9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyzsg9.md). An Opinion piece.
- Lenggenhager et al. (2007), [LIT-tmpeggnf](../literature.d/LIT-tmpeggnf.md), read in [NOTE-tmp9c2fc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md): the
  experiment the account cites for manipulating global ownership.

## What was actually shown

**The analysis.** Minimal phenomenal selfhood (MPS) is "the experience of
being a distinct, holistic entity ... possessing a body and a location in
space and time". It has three parts: self-identification with the whole
body, self-location, and a weak first-person perspective (1PP), the
geometrical origin of an egocentric model. The strong 1PP, the self as
directed at an object, needs MPS.

**The clinical sorting.** In autoscopic hallucination all three parts are
normal. In heautoscopy all three are abnormal and can alternate or
bilocate. In out-of-body experience all three sit at the illusory body.
The lesion sites differ by phenomenon, from small cited series.

**The experiments.** Lenggenhager et al. shifted reported
self-identification with a filmed body, and a walking-error measure taken
as self-location. Ehrsson shifted felt location to the camera. Neither
induced an out-of-body experience or touched the strong 1PP.

**The agency step.** The illusions are passive, so "a passive,
multisensory and globalized experience of 'owning' a body is sufficient
for minimal conscious selfhood". If global owning is the simplest form of
self-consciousness, as the authors propose, then agency is not
constitutive of it.

What could have come out otherwise: the clinical phenomena might not have
sorted cleanly by the three parts. They do, though the sorting was made
with these categories. The experiments might have failed to shift
self-identification. They shifted it for one's own filmed body, and
barely for a mannequin.

## What this does not say

- **That global ownership is the simplest self-awareness as a matter of
  fact.** It is proposed as a research strategy, and the definition of MPS
  makes it so.
- **That body ownership in general is the simplest self-awareness.** The
  paper excludes body-part ownership, the rubber hand, from MPS.
- **That a system without agency could have a minimal self.** The
  evidence comes from adults who are agents. The paper concedes agency
  enables MPS "in practically all humans", and its own definition of MPS
  includes being "capable of global self-control".
- **That the three parts are each necessary.** Box 1 says so, and then
  says perhaps not.
- **Anything about transparency.** The account assumes Metzinger's
  transparent self-model ([THEORY-064](THEORY-064.md)) and does not test it.
