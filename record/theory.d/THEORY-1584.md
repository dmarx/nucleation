# THEORY-1584: Botzung and colleagues report that rival basketball fans recalled the same plays with valence set by their allegiance, recalled plays good for their own side more accurately and confidently, and that rated intensity, not valence, modulated amygdala and hippocampal activity at retrieval

## The account

Allegiance, not the event, fixes whether a remembered play is good or
bad: "the same exact event can be classified as either positive or
negative according to the perspective of the fan" (introduction). Fans
remember their own side's good moments better, reversing the usual
finding with simple stimuli. Across the rivalry the authors find "an
emotion-induced resonance in brain activity across rival fans while
reflecting on emotionally intense plays" (Conclusions).

## Evidence

- Recall 74% overall; no group difference in accuracy, confidence or
  rated intensity (Table 1).
- Ratings went the way allegiance predicts for 88.9% of correctly
  recalled shots.
- More correct memories were positive than negative (P = 0.042), with
  higher confidence (P = 0.002) and intensity (P = 0.001).
- In medial temporal regions of interest, intensity had a main effect (P
  < .001) and valence did not (P = .096).

## What it bears on

on. No question or claim names it, and the question on rivalry (whether
rival fans in fact return it) is not what the paper measures, so no
relation is filed from here.
