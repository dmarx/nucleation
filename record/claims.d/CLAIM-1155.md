# CLAIM-1155: Suppressing standing wrongs beyond the harm

## The claim

[CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md), the owner's, says that an organization which harms and also
reproduces the projections that make its harm non-legible commits a
counter-normativity distinct from the harm. Its note of 2026-11-18 says
what holds it back: it "gives no argument that reproducing the projections
wrongs those affected beyond the harm's continuation". This claim gives
one.

Three things are kept apart:

- **H, the harm.** The setback the organization's activity does to those
  affected.
- **C, the harm's continuation.** The further harm that follows because
  the harm goes unrecognized and so uncorrected.
- **D, the defeat of standing.** Those affected cannot make their claims
  about the harm count with the organization: the claims are merged into
  states the organization's projection does not distinguish ([CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md)),
  or are recognized and cannot move action, or are moved and cannot be
  acted on ([CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)'s three achievements, [CLAIM-403](CLAIM-403.md)'s rungs).

The reduction [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)'s defeater names says that whatever is wrong
beyond H is C. This claim says D is a wrong of its own, under three
conditions:

1. **Standing.** Those affected have standing to contest the harm with
   the organization: to bring it, to be heard on it, or to be consulted
   ([CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md)'s consent, intervention and consultative standing; the
   weakest is enough). The claim takes this as a condition. It does not
   derive it from the harm's being a wrong, since [CLAIM-738](CLAIM-738.md) says a right
   does not by itself generate a warranted demand, and each such passage
   "needs its own warrant".
2. **Reproduced occlusion.** The organization reproduces the projection
   as part of its self-maintenance ([TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)), so what it keeps going
   includes the conditions under which their claims fail. Legibility is
   [TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)'s: legible to the organization's operative procedures, not to
   everyone.
3. **A feasible alternative.** Some representation the organization
   could maintain would let the claims register. The unavoidable case is
   left open (below).

Under these conditions, reproducing the projection wrongs those affected
in their standing as claimants on the organization. The wrong is
**directed**: it is done to them, and its object is the conditions under
which their claims can count, not the interests H sets back.

## Why D does not reduce to C

The reduction would make D wrong only by what it causes: more harm, or a
higher risk of it. Then where contest could not have changed the harm's
course, D would add nothing; that is the objection [CLAIM-1184](CLAIM-1184.md), filed
beside this claim. [ARG-228](../arguments.d/ARG-228.md) builds the case: a harm already complete, a remedy fixed
by a regulator acting on its own tests, and two organizations alike in
everything but the projection through which they register complaints. C,
and the expected C, are matched by construction, which closes the gap
[ARG-055](../arguments.d/ARG-055.md) left open, where "B's missing procedure would raise the risk of
later harm". D is present in one and not the other.

That D is still a wrong is the judgement the record already makes of the
same form elsewhere. [CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md) holds that defeating a party's standing to
contest is a wrong "even where the objection would have failed", and that
"absence of an objection and absence of an effective opportunity to object
are different facts". Fuentes v. Shevin ([THEORY-1972](../theory.d/THEORY-1972.md)): "The right to be
heard does not depend upon an advance showing that one will surely prevail
at the hearing." [CLAIM-727](CLAIM-727.md) adds why the organization's own say-so cannot
settle it: authority over a decision "does not carry authority over the
conditions on which it may be challenged". What is new here is the
mechanism. [CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md)'s foreclosure works by timing, an act made
irreversible before the objection can arrive. This one works by
representation: the objection arrives and has no category to arrive in.

**If D is a harm.** A reader may count being unheard as a setback to an
interest of its own, a harm. Then D is a second harm, distinct from H and
not C, and [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md) survives: its defeater is the reduction to the harm's
continuation, not to harm in general. What the claim loses on that reading
is only the word "not a harm", which its title does not use; it says D is
directed at something other than the interests H sets back.

## The boundary test: gaslighting

A9 §7 of the exchange "About Plantation Paradise" proposed, after Kincaid
and Stalion's review of the gaslighting literature ([LIT-2532](../literature.d/LIT-2532.md)), a test:
"whether organizational counter-normativity identifies a genuinely
different mechanism ... potentially without strategic manipulation or any
single actor intending the obstruction."

**Not read.** On 2026-11-22 OpenAlex listed two open copies: the
publisher's (onlinelibrary.wiley.com, which served a Cloudflare challenge
on 2026-11-12 and was not retried) and a submitted version in the
University of Texas at Tyler's repository
(https://scholarworks.uttyler.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1016&context=mgmt_fac),
which answered with a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) and was not retried.
Europe PMC holds no record of it. So the test is run here against what the
record holds, and the review's own conceptualizations stay unchecked.

D differs from gaslighting, in the senses the record holds, on two counts
that do not depend on the review:

- **No intending actor.** Fricker's "intentional gaslighting" is
  deliberate manipulation of others' credibility judgements ([THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md):
  Ripley works on Greenleaf's prejudice). D needs no aim: it is constituted
  by the reproduced projection and attributed through the organization's
  self-maintenance, as Anderson's structural testimonial injustice is
  constituted by an un-refreshed list, "through no one's fault"
  ([THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md)), and as [CLAIM-406](CLAIM-406.md) holds such a loop with "no continuing
  prejudiced act". [CLAIM-657](CLAIM-657.md)'s two routes need no deliberate suppression
  either. In the construction, the projection is reproduced because the
  organization's metrics reward low incident counts ([CLAIM-445](CLAIM-445.md)'s loop),
  and no one aims at the complainants.
- **Its object is the organization's uptake, not the target's self-trust.**
  Gaslighting works on its target's confidence in their own perception or
  judgement. D does not need those affected to doubt anything: A's
  qualification on [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md) is that they "may correctly recognize the
  harm", and the pathology persists "because these perspectives are denied
  the conditions needed to become practically effective". The affected
  can know exactly what was done to them and still be wronged in this way.

So the boundary test, as far as it can be run without the review, is
passed: D is a different mechanism from strategic or relational
gaslighting. Whether the review's structural or institutional sense of
gaslighting is D under another name is what a reading must settle. If it
is, D has a literature, and [QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md)'s question, whether an organized
whole's manipulation is the same wrong when no member intends it, has a
neighbour on the record that answers its analogue yes.

## What it does not say

- **That those affected have standing.** It is a condition. Which
  standards confer it is [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)'s matter, as for [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md).
- **That unavoidable reproduction wrongs them.** Where no feasible
  representation would let their claims register, the claim says nothing.
  [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md) leaves that open on purpose, and [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)'s condition 5
  excludes it. One way the owner could settle it is Thomson's distinction,
  not filed: the standing is infringed whether or not the projection is
  avoidable, and violated only where it is.
- **Who bears the wrong.** The claim speaks of "the organization" as
  [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md) does; whether that is an arrangement whose controllers bear it
  or an institution that is an agent is [CLAIM-876](CLAIM-876.md) and [CLAIM-877](CLAIM-877.md)'s choice.
- **How large the wrong is,** or that reproducing the projection
  aggravates it beyond a single occlusion. The construction shows
  reproduction is the organization's, not that it weighs more.

## Its relation to [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)

[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md) states the wrong from the duty's side: a breach of "a
second-order duty of answerability". This claim states it from the
claimant's: the defeat of a standing they hold. Where the organization's
duty and their standing are correlative, the two describe one wrong, and
they complement each other. Two things are this claim's own: it needs no
duty to maintain means of review, only the claimants' standing, so it
reaches an organization whose general duty of answerability is in doubt;
and its non-reduction rests on matched expected harm, not on a stipulated
duty, which is the gap [ARG-055](../arguments.d/ARG-055.md)'s critical questions name.

**Alternative, the owner's choice.** If standing and duty are taken as one
relation seen from two ends, this claim restates [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md) and is defeated
as a thesis of its own (its defeater says so). Then the ARG beside it
should conclude [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md) and [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md), and this claim be retired. The
assistant leans to keeping it: the claimant's side is the one [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)'s
words, "those affected", are on, and it does not wait on [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)'s
answer about which organizations owe answerability in general.
