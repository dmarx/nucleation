# NOTE-121: Elshatlawy et al. — Generalized theory of observers

## Contribution

The paper proposes a formal framework unifying the notion of an observer across physics, computer science and philosophy. Starting from cybernetic feedback models, it gives an operational definition of minimal observers, explores their role in shaping foundational concepts, and identifies what would be left undefined without them. Drawing on quantum gravity, digital physics, second-order cybernetics, and ruliological and pregeometric approaches, it argues observers are indispensable reference points for measurement, reference frames and emergent complexity.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §4.1 (ch. pp. 52–53): history from Aristotle and Bacon to relativity, quantum measurement and cybernetics, where observation becomes a prerequisite for intervention in a feedback loop.
- §4.5.2 Definition 4.1: a minimal observer is a tuple (X, Y, Z, f, g, B) — internal states, inputs, outputs, transition and output functions, and a boundary — with non-trivial sensing, action, internal dynamics and feedback closure; a thermostat is the worked example (§4.5.3).
- §4.6.2: lists what would not be defined without observers — internal vs external, definite outcomes, reference frames, hierarchical layering, coarse-graining — linking to Wolfram's foliation-choosing observer and Chris Fields' boundary-based view.
- §4.7.1: by Definition 4.1 an electron is not an observer (no internal state-update mechanism or boundary); §4.7.2 speculates observers anchor local measurement and emergent geometry in pregeometric/computational universes.
- §§4.10–4.11: theorems on observer equivalence and complexity, comparison with Kantian, Husserlian and Wittgensteinian views and existing observer theories, replies to regress and reductionism objections; the conclusion calls the framework "definitive, self-contained".

## Open questions

- Bridges agency (cybernetic sensor–actuator loops) and the observer problem in physics; contrasts sharply with Rovelli's RQM ([LIT-177](../literature.d/LIT-177.md)), where any physical system, electrons included, is an observer.
- Check whether the "theorems" are substantive or definitional, and whether the claim that coarse-graining requires observers survives objective coarse-graining accounts (cf. [LIT-150](../literature.d/LIT-150.md)).
- Companion to the same authors' pregeometry paper ([LIT-102](../literature.d/LIT-102.md)).
