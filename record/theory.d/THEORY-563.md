# THEORY-563: Consciousness is a real pattern in the brain's content-bearing states, a description that trades predictive accuracy for compression, and so coarse that describing the brain through it is metaphor; on that reading there is no Cartesian theatre, whether a given content was conscious can lack an answer when two descriptions compress and predict equally, and first-person reports are fallible as literal descriptions of the brain but authoritative as an author's

## Source

Taylor (2024), "Consciousness interpreted: an interpretation of Dennett's
view of consciousness", *Philosophical Psychology* 39(3), 828–846,
[LIT-461](../literature.d/LIT-461.md), read in full in [NOTE-379](../notes.d/NOTE-379.md) from the version of record (online-first
pagination 1–19). Quotations from *Consciousness Explained* ([LIT-443](../literature.d/LIT-443.md)) and
*From Bacteria to Bach and Back* ([LIT-424](../literature.d/LIT-424.md)) are Taylor's; the record holds
both unread.

## What was actually shown

**The hub (pp. 2–4).** A real pattern, after Dennett's "Real Patterns"
([LIT-220](../literature.d/LIT-220.md)), is a description of a system more efficient than its bit map,
whether or not anyone concocts it. Three of its features do the work: the
trade-off between compression and accuracy, several patterns in one
system, and existence independent of description. Consciousness is taken
to be such a pattern in the brain's drafts, its content-bearing states.
The anchor is *Consciousness Explained* p. 460: pains "are as real as
haircuts and dollars and opportunities and persons and centers of
gravity".

**Multiple drafts (Box 2; pp. 4–9).** A pattern is distributed, so there is
no Cartesian theatre, as a Sicilian dragon is not a place on the board.
Whether a draft is conscious is "an open question", because being part of
a pattern depends on relations to other states. And in metacontrast
masking, the Orwellian reading (seen, then overwritten in memory) and the
Stalinesque reading (never seen, edited before experience) compress
equally and predict the same, so neither is the correct description. This
replaces Dennett's first-person operationalism, which Taylor sets aside.

**Global workspace (pp. 9–10).** Nothing in the three multiple-drafts claims
conflicts with workspace theory, given a distributed model of working
memory that keeps sensory content in sensory cortex while broadcasting it.

**User-illusion and authority (pp. 10–15).** A user-illusion is a real
pattern coarse enough that describing the system through it lapses into
metaphor. Reports are therefore fallible read as literal descriptions of
the brain, and authoritative read as unwitting fictions, where the subject
has an author's authority. Qualia eliminativism, on this reading, is an
attack on one picture of consciousness, peripheral to the positive view.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The six claims (Box 1) could have
resisted any consistent reading, as Block and Schwitzgebel charged. The
paper shows one consistent reading exists. The masking claim could fail
for a case in which the readings predict different downstream effects.

## What this does not say

- **That the view is true, or Dennett's.** "My goal is not to argue that
  the theory is correct" (p. 1), nor that it is exactly what Dennett meant
  (p. 2).
- **That the masking indeterminacy holds of real cases.** It assumes the
  two readings differ only in whether the disc was conscious, with no
  downstream difference. Taylor calls that "somewhat simplistic" (n. 8,
  citing Haase and Fisk 2015). If they differ in prediction, one reading
  wins and there is a fact ([NOTE-379](../notes.d/NOTE-379.md), C2).
- **Which pattern consciousness is.** A system has many real patterns. The
  paper says only "a user-illusion", and does not say what makes a pattern
  conscious rather than, say, the belief pattern the intentional stance
  finds. Without that it supplies no criterion of the kind [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) or
  [QUESTION-046](../questions.d/QUESTION-046.md) asks for.
- **That reports are fictions in fact.** That the coarseness of the pattern
  forces metaphor, and so makes a sincere report a fiction rather than a
  false belief, is stated, not argued.

## Connections

- **Real patterns.** [THEORY-036](THEORY-036.md) holds that Dennett's compressibility
  criterion admits almost every non-random pattern, with projectibility and
  scale-relativity added later. On this reading consciousness inherits that
  liberality unless the "user-illusion" condition narrows it.
- **Illusionism.** Frankish ([THEORY-633](THEORY-633.md)) counts Dennett as strong
  illusionism's leading defender; Taylor never uses the word and reads the
  denial of qualia as peripheral. The two agree only if Taylor's
  "consciousness" is Frankish's inclusive sense, with phenomenal properties
  excluded ([NOTE-384](../notes.d/NOTE-384.md), [NOTE-379](../notes.d/NOTE-379.md)). On qualia, see [THEORY-415](THEORY-415.md).
- **The self.** Dennett's centre of narrative gravity ([THEORY-691](THEORY-691.md)) is
  the answer Taylor names to the worry that a user-illusion needs a user,
  and does not develop.
- **Timing.** [THEORY-103](THEORY-103.md) records that Block holds the parallel question for
  partial report, whether uncued letters were seen before the cue, to have
  an answer by inference to the best explanation. That is the move this
  account denies for masking when the descriptions tie.
