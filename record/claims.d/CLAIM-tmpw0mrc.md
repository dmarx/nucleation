# CLAIM-tmpw0mrc: The threshold criterion measures coupling strength, and coupling is not constitution: a metronome driving a musician, or a nerve driving a muscle, forms a strongly coupled block that clears the threshold without being one system

## The claim

Simon's criterion compares the size of interactions inside a group with the
size of interactions across its edge. A metronome that a musician follows
closely has a large coupling into the musician and none back. Group the two,
and the block can be nearly decomposable from the room with a wide gap. The
same holds for Adams & Aizawa's nerve and muscle ([LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)), their case of
coupled but not part. [CLAIM-tmp8tsw5](CLAIM-tmp8tsw5.md) then counts both as one system. The
record's entrainment claim ([CLAIM-tmptsdgt](CLAIM-tmptsdgt.md)) counts neither, because it
requires each side to adjust the other. A pacemaker that "entrains the
rest" is not thereby one with them ([NOTE-414](../notes.d/NOTE-414.md)).

## Why it is still open

There is a direct reply, and it is not yet filed. Require the block to clear
the threshold in *both* off-diagonal directions inside it: the musician must
drive the metronome as well as the reverse. That imports entrainment's
mutuality as a condition on the matrix. It would make the criterion
directional, and it would answer this case. Whether it over-excludes is
untested. A notebook, a tool and a leader-follower pair are all asymmetric,
and the owner's dancing couple has a lead and a follow. Until a directional
version is stated and tried on those cases, the objection stands.
