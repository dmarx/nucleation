# CLAIM-tmpfoatc: In linear models, integrated information with the exclusion rule separates an organized whole from its parts by how strongly its seams couple relative to its parts' integration, not by whether the parts are modules or minds, so applied by one procedure it denies a loosely coupled modular mind unity as it denies a group, and grants a tightly coupled group unity at its members' expense

## The claim

[CASE-tmp5fglr](../cases.d/CASE-tmp5fglr.md) applied autoregressive integrated information and IIT's exclusion rule by one procedure to a linear model of a modular mind and to a group of three such minds. In both, the whole is the subject once coupling across its seams passes a threshold, and its parts are excluded; below it the parts are the subjects. The whole's integration is fixed by its seams almost alone, and the group's threshold is higher only because its parts are more integrated than the mind's modules. Near a threshold one link flips the verdict, and the subject can be a set straddling two members.

So this measure and rule, motivated within one system and not by the verdict on groups, answer [QUESTION-046](../questions.d/QUESTION-046.md)'s "same procedure" demand but not its "different sides" demand: they separate tightly from loosely coupled wholes, by degree. Whether a real modular mind and a real group fall on different sides is then an empirical question about how strongly their seams couple relative to their parts, which the record does not hold. This confirms, for one measure, the cost [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) gives the architectural route: a criterion that marks a group down for loose coupling marks a modular mind down too.

## Scope

Demonstrated in the models computed: linear Gaussian networks of three-unit modules, Phi_AR as the measure, the size-normalized and unnormalized cuts. It is not shown for IIT's own Phi, for nonlinear or larger systems, or for the slow and symbolic links between persons.
