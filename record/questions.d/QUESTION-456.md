# QUESTION-456: How can the set of demands an organization's compatibility is judged against be established defensibly, when who counts as a claimant, what each demand is inferred to require and what it is taken to mean are themselves shaped by the organization's own governance?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)'s complex is defined for a given set of demands D, each with an
acceptance set A_i. [CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md) requires a fixed reference set and an explicit
correspondence before and after change. Neither says how D is fixed. A42
names three ways the fixing can fail, each with a literature registered
unread:

1. **Who is represented.** An aggregate can hide a small group's failure,
   the reverse of the whale case: "a small statistically underrepresented
   group can disappear inside it" (meta-metrics in intersectional
   fairness, [LIT-2451](../literature.d/LIT-2451.md)).
2. **How demands are inferred.** "behavioral observations are evidence
   about a demand, not automatically the demand itself": Â_i, the inferred
   acceptance set, is not A_i, and behaviour produced under selective
   exposure can make the second error hide the first (Caliari,
   [LIT-2363](../literature.d/LIT-2363.md); Freer and Nosratabadi, [LIT-2435](../literature.d/LIT-2435.md)).
3. **What demands mean.** "semantic substitution": an organization can
   meet a value by changing its operational test while keeping its name,
   A_original ≠ A_operative, which is neither relief nor deletion in
   [CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md)'s sense (Moats and colleagues, [LIT-2361](../literature.d/LIT-2361.md)).

What would count as an answer: conditions under which a demand set, its
claimants and its acceptance sets can be fixed independently of the
organization's governance, or a procedure (A42 §8 sketches six steps, from
identifying claimants to justifying the resolution) whose outputs can be
checked against a reference set the organization did not choose.

## Bearing

- [QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md) asks whether relationally indexed judgments can be objective
  about the standards themselves; this asks how the standards enter the
  analysis at all.
- [CLAIM-662](../claims.d/CLAIM-662.md): identification is costly and can be penalized.
- [CLAIM-627](../claims.d/CLAIM-627.md) (jurisdiction over a concept's contexts partly settles whether
  its uses are compatible): the third failure is a case of it.
- [CLAIM-537](../claims.d/CLAIM-537.md)'s adjustable and nonadjustable compatibility under
  disturbance: A42 §6 carries it to uncertainty about the demands'
  interpretation, with K_uniform ⊆ K_each ⊆ K_some, and asks whether an
  institution can act defensibly across interpretations without deciding
  among them. The answer is yes exactly when the demand set is in
  K_uniform.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Moats et al. (2026), read

From the reading in [NOTE-1760](../notes.d/NOTE-1760.md) ([LIT-2361](../literature.d/LIT-2361.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: *supports* its third failure mode ("what demands
mean") with ethnographic instances, and adds two the question does not name:
**pushing** (a demand presumed held by the claimants but imposed on them, a
failure in *who* holds the demand) and **projection** (the demand's
acceptance set is so loose that incompatible readings coexist, so A_i is not
fixed at all). *Leaves open* the question's sought answer: the paper offers
no procedure for fixing D independently of governance; its remedy is
ethnographic consultation and "curated comparison", which is closer to A42
§8's step "identify claimants" than to a check against an unchosen reference
set. Result 9 bears directly: fixing a demand's meaning too tightly is
itself narrowing.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Fanning & Raworth (2025), read

From the reading in [NOTE-1782](../notes.d/NOTE-1782.md) ([LIT-2454](../literature.d/LIT-2454.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **bears on it, leaves it open**. Disaggregation
changes the verdict: the global aggregate hides that the clusters causing
overshoot and those bearing shortfall are different. The paper also shows
the unit of claimant constrains measurement (ecological boundaries cannot be
disaggregated comparably; footprints are a constructed substitute). It does
not address how the set of demands is defensibly established; the 35
indicators are taken from SDG data and planetary boundaries science.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Freer and Nosratabadi, read

From the reading in [NOTE-1797](../notes.d/NOTE-1797.md) ([LIT-2435](../literature.d/LIT-2435.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: supports and sharpens it. What an observer can infer
from behaviour depends on an assumption about attention that the behaviour
cannot itself fix. Under the weakest assumption almost nothing is inferred,
and the inferred relation is model-relative. This gives formal content to
"behavioral observations are evidence about a demand, not automatically the
demand itself". It does not address who fixes the attention set, so
selective exposure shaped by an organization is left open.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Boxer, McFowland and Neill, read

From the reading in [NOTE-1741](../notes.d/NOTE-1741.md) ([LIT-2344](../literature.d/LIT-2344.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **supports the question's first failure mode and
offers a partial procedure**. The paper is a worked instance of "An
aggregate can hide a small group's failure": aggregate calibration or
error-rate parity can coexist with a significant subgroup violation (results

partly answers "who is represented" without the auditor choosing the
subgroups. It does not answer the question: the protected attribute, the
covariates that define subgroups and the fairness definition (separation or
sufficiency) are still chosen by the auditor, and the outcome label may
itself be biased by the institution (result 8), which is the question's

## Note of 2026-11-12: Rockström, Denton, Norström et al., read

From the reading in [NOTE-1796](../notes.d/NOTE-1796.md) ([LIT-2422](../literature.d/LIT-2422.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **bears directly as a worked instance of a procedure,
without answering the question.** The Earth Commission is an attempt to fix
a demand set from outside any one organization: a scientific body sets
"safe" and "just" boundaries, specifies claimants beyond the organization's
own (other species, future generations: the 3 I), and then "transcribes"
them into budgets allocated to actors. That is close to what the question
says would count as an answer, "a procedure ... whose outputs can be checked
against a reference set the organization did not choose". But the paper also
shows the question's difficulty: the choice of control variables, of justice
criteria (minimum, medium, limitarian levels; "acceptable" and
"unacceptable" inequalities) and of allocation principles is itself made by
the Commission and left open; it states that its review will ask "which
approaches risk reinforcing existing injustices". So: support for the claim
that external reference sets are being constructed, and evidence that fixing
who counts and what a demand means remains a governance choice one level up.
The finding that three just boundaries are stricter than safe ones is a
concrete case where adding claimants (people harmed) tightens the acceptance
set.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Md Fahim Sikder, read

From the reading in [NOTE-1779](../notes.d/NOTE-1779.md) ([LIT-2434](../literature.d/LIT-2434.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **bears on all three failures, mostly as illustration;
supports the first.** 1. *Who is represented*: the Credit Default result is
a direct, quantified instance of the question's first failure: a demand (FPR
parity) met for each single attribute is not certified for the sex × age
groups. The paper adds a second mechanism the question does not name: small
groups are not refused, they are *uncertifiable* (n_g < 30), so a group can
be both at risk and statistically illegible; the auditor's sample size
decides whether its demand can be checked at all. 2. *How demands are
inferred*: the LLM-guided discover-then-confirm design is a procedural
answer to one part of the worry: group hypotheses generated by one process
are tested on data that process did not see. It does not address who chooses
the candidate groups' attributes in the first place. 3. *What demands mean*:
the paper fixes the criterion (FPR/TPR parity) and lets ε be set by a
regulator; the choice among incompatible criteria (Chouldechova, cited) is
outside the tool. It shows how much rides on that choice (calibration buys
FPR parity at a reported TPR cost) without resolving it.
