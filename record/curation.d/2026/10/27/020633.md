## Decisions

**The companion posts are filed separately.** Each of causal scrubbing's
three companion posts is its own Alignment Forum post with its own
address, and the two results posts add an author (Tao Lin) whom the main
post and the appendix do not have. The record files one LIT per work, a
source names one work, and a NOTE is one reading of one work, so each is
its own LIT, `extends` LIT-1549. Folding them into LIT-1549 would have
hidden three readings inside one NOTE and given one source to four works.

**The Markov IBP's year.** The record's "Van Gael et al. 2009" (CASE-165,
NOTE-1172, NOTE-1175) comes from the IBP review, LIT-1556, which cites
"volume 21, 2009", the year of the printed NIPS volume. The paper, which
introduces the Markov IBP, appeared at NIPS 21, the conference of December
2008 (pp. 1697–1704), and the proceedings' BibTeX says 2008. It is the
same work. The record dates it 2008-12-01, to the month, since the PDF
carries no date, following LIT-677's precedent for a NeurIPS paper. The
NeurIPS site files the first author as "Gael"; the PDF's "Van Gael" is
used.

## Errors found in the works

Numbers and claims were checked where cheap; the scripts are the readers'
own and are not part of the record.

- **Hase et al.** Table 3's FT-1 Tracing Reversal R² (0.067) disagrees with
  Figure 6 (29.4), and §5.3's "58.5% average" uses the figure's value; the
  figure's cell equals Table 6's, and Table 3's FT-5 row matches Table 6's
  digit for digit, so one row was taken from the wrong table. The
  conclusion is unaffected. The edit layers are given with 25 in §4.2 and
  24 in Appendix A, and n = 652 is 32.6% of 2,000 records, not of "about
  10%" of CounterFact.
- **Geva et al.** Only a wording slip ("rank … increases" where the rank
  improves). Noted as weaknesses: one annotator per key, no random-direction
  control.
- **MEMIT.** Slips ("token T" for S, "GPT-XL", "Figure D" for Figure 10,
  "Speficity"); CounterFact called "21,919 factual statements" where they
  are counterfactual records; the GPT-NeoX clamp names ‖h‖ for δ. Eq. 14
  was checked to be the joint least-squares solution, and every S is the
  harmonic mean of its parts.
- **Curve Detectors.** The firing rate is "10%" in the opening and 11%
  elsewhere; "only a couple convolutional layers deep" contradicts "five
  convolutional layers" (the second is right); typos. The causal reading of
  feature visualization is overstated, as in Zoom In.
- **Attribution Patching.** "d_head 6" for 64; two formulas use an
  activation where its gradient belongs; the translation heads of the
  induction-heads paper are placed in GPT-J, where they are in that paper's own 40-layer model; Vig
  et al.'s title misquoted.
- **Auditing for hidden objectives.** Team 1's prompt is quoted two ways;
  the SFT set is 74,177 samples and "≈70,000"; C.2's "28% increase" is 28
  percentage points (a 165% relative increase).
- **Paren balance checker.** The horizon test is stated backwards twice;
  experiment 2b's loss is 0.56 in the text and 0.53 in the table; row 2a
  gives 87.2% where 88% is printed; "class-balanced" contradicts about 26%
  balanced.
- **Induction heads.** "K individually (94 -> 97%)" where by the post's
  numbers K went from 91% to 94%; the introduction's headline 89% is the
  bonus hypothesis, the main result's 86% hypothesis 4.
- **Across the companion posts.** The appendix says both results posts use
  the randomized-loss baseline. The induction post's 0% is the heads run
  on another sequence (a 0.053-nat gap), the paren post's a 4.30-nat gap,
  so "35% to 86%" and "93% to 72%" are on different scales; LIT-1549's
  summary sets them side by side. The appendix's Lemma 1 says "product
  distribution" where its proof shows the joint.
- **The infinite factorial HMM.** Both of the sequential buffet's printed
  predictive rules are wrong: the stay-on rule should have γ where δ is
  printed, and the switch-on rule is c01/(c00 + c01 + 1), not
  c00/(c00 + c01); checked as exact ratios of the paper's eq. (6), the
  printed stay-on rule gives 0.21 where the exact value is 0.86. Also "∝"
  for "∼" in (11), and typos.

## A question for the owner: THEORY-1011

THEORY-1011, ROME's storage reading, is Proposed, and its promote_when
says it is "refuted if edits of equal quality are available at layers that
tracing does not implicate". Hase et al. report exactly that, in GPT-J on
652 CounterFact facts: ROME rewrites at over 96% at every layer but the
last, and the per-fact tracing effect adds 0.1 point of R² for ROME at
layer 6 (0.947 to 0.948) and at most 3.2 points across methods and
objectives (THEORY-tmpg87ki). The localization part of THEORY-1011 meets
its own refutation clause. Its conjecture that "any middle layer could
store a given fact equally" is not refuted, and is close to Hase et al.'s
own "many layers could store a fact" (THEORY-tmpiz2n7). The readers
suggest an ADR-047 disposition of `split` or `needs-correction` rather
than a flat rejection. **The status was not changed**: what to do with
THEORY-1011 is the owner's decision, and its dated note of 2026-10-27
records the evidence and leaves it open.
