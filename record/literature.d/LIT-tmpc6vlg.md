# LIT-tmpc6vlg: A Multicomponent Conceptualization of Authenticity: Theory and Research

Michael H. Kernis and Brian M. Goldman, "A Multicomponent Conceptualization
of Authenticity: Theory and Research", in Mark P. Zanna (ed.), *Advances in
Experimental Social Psychology*, vol. 38, Academic Press (Elsevier), 2006,
pp. 283–357 — DOI-10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38006-9. Crossref gives only the
year, so `published:` carries 2006 as the first of January.

The brief's citation is correct (title, authors, venue, volume, year).
*Advances* is a serial of invited review chapters, so this is a book
chapter with a volume number, not a journal article. At 75 pages it is
long; no shorter lawfully readable statement by the same authors was found
(see Standing in the record).

## Key takeaways

*Registered from two papers that report this chapter and that I read in
full, not from the chapter. Each point says who reports it.*

- **Definition.** Authenticity is "the unimpeded operation of one's true
  self in one's daily enterprise" (Heppner, Kernis, Nezlek, Foster, Lakey &
  Goldman 2008, quoting Goldman & Kernis 2002). Schlegel and colleagues
  ([LIT-tmpzyy82](LIT-tmpzyy82.md), p. 474) paraphrase it the same way and call the programme
  "based on self-determination theory".
- **Correlates.** Self-reported authenticity relates positively to
  self-actualization, self-concept clarity and self-esteem and negatively to
  distress (Schlegel et al., p. 474). Dispositional authenticity correlates
  r = .47 with level of self-esteem (Heppner et al. 2008, p. 1141, citing
  this chapter). Belief that one's behaviour is authentic relates to
  judgments of meaning (Schlegel et al., p. 474).
- **The measure.** The Authenticity Inventory, version 3, has 45 items, for
  instance "For better or for worse I am aware of who I truly am" and "I
  frequently pretend to enjoy something when in actuality I really don't"
  (Schlegel et al., Study 4, α = .94).
- **The components.** The chapter is commonly summarized as distinguishing
  four components: awareness, unbiased processing, behaviour and relational
  orientation. Schlegel and colleagues name "the awareness aspect" and the
  claim that authenticity includes "awareness and acceptance of both one's
  positive and negative attributes" (p. 487). I have not seen the other
  three named in a source I read.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the main social-psychological
theory of authenticity. It is psychology with no instruction for
machine-learning practice, so no anthology topic holds it. The Anthology of
the SOTA does not hold it: a grep of its literature.d for "Kernis" and
"authenticity" found nothing.

`Deferred` because it was not read. A lawful related paper that was read
is Heppner, Kernis, Nezlek et al. (2008), "Within-Person Relationships Among
Daily Self-Esteem, Need Satisfaction, and Authenticity", *Psychological
Science* 19(11):1140–1145, from Nezlek's reprint page at William & Mary. It
is empirical, uses two ad hoc daily authenticity items rather than the
AI-3, and does not state the four-component theory, so it cannot stand in
for the chapter. It reports one finding worth keeping beside this entry: the
item "throughout the day I was in touch with my 'true self'" correlated
more strongly, within persons, with each autonomy item than with the other
authenticity item, so the authors analysed the items singly (p. 1142).
That is a discriminant-validity problem between felt authenticity and SDT's
felt autonomy, at the level of the daily items.

**Where it bears.**

- **SDT's autonomy ([LIT-559](LIT-559.md), [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)).** The authors place authenticity
  inside self-determination theory. If authenticity and autonomy are the
  same construct, the chapter adds a measure of [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)'s integration;
  if they differ, it should say what authenticity has that self-endorsed
  regulation lacks. The awareness and unbiased-processing components,
  which concern self-knowledge rather than motive, are the likely
  difference. [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md) warns that integration may be measurable only as
  reflective endorsement; the AI-3 is a self-report of exactly that kind.
- **The true self-concept ([LIT-tmpzyy82](LIT-tmpzyy82.md)).** Schlegel and colleagues measured
  AI-3 authenticity alongside an implicit measure of true self-concept
  accessibility. The two were uncorrelated (r = .05) and each predicted
  meaning in life independently. So whatever the AI-3 measures, it is not
  how readily the person's own true-self traits come to mind.
- **The rival measure.** Wood and colleagues' Authenticity Scale
  ([LIT-tmpr2weu](LIT-tmpr2weu.md)) is the person-centred alternative, built on Rogers rather than
  SDT. No comparison between the two has been read here.
- **The philosophers.** Taylor ([LIT-tmprf69i](LIT-tmprf69i.md)) makes authenticity depend on
  horizons of significance; nothing reported of this construct includes
  one.

**Priority for a reading: medium-high.** It is the construct the batch's
