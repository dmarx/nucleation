# LIT-tmpictnr: Self-Regulation and the Problem of Human Autonomy: Does Psychology Need Choice, Self-Determination, and Will?

Richard M. Ryan and Edward L. Deci (2006), *Journal of Personality* 74(6):1557–1586 — DOI-10.1111/j.1467-6494.2006.00420.x

## Key takeaways

- **Autonomy is endorsement, not independence.** "Autonomy is not defined by
  the absence of external influences but rather by one's assent to such
  influences or inputs" (p. 1560). One can be "autonomously dependent, or
  forced into independence" (p. 1561).
- **Autonomy is graded.** "Within SDT, autonomy for any given action is a
  matter of degree" (p. 1562), along the continuum of the 2000 paper
  ([LIT-tmpckq0r](LIT-tmpckq0r.md)): external, introjected, identified, integrated, intrinsic.
- **Heteronomy can come from inside.** Heteronomy is "regulation from
  outside the phenomenal self, by forces experienced as alien or pressuring,
  be they inner impulses or demands, or external contingencies" (p. 1561).
- **Autonomy is reflective endorsement, so it can be checked counterfactually.**
  A driver shifting gear on a nonconscious cue acts autonomously because,
  "were she to consider it reflectively, she would wholly endorse the
  action". A man primed into a purchase against his values acts under
  control (p. 1572).
- **Choice is not autonomy.** "One could have only one option ... and yet
  feel quite autonomous so long as one truly endorses that option"
  (p. 1576).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the SDT paper that engages
the philosophy of autonomy. It is where the record's agency holdings and
its motivation holdings meet. It `extends` Ryan and Deci (2000),
[LIT-tmpckq0r](LIT-tmpckq0r.md): it takes that paper's continuum as the definition of autonomy
it defends ("Ryan & Deci, 2000", cited for the continuum on p. 1562 and as
the framework in the abstract), and it would have nothing to defend without
it.

The engagement with Frankfurt, which the owner flagged, is slighter than
the paper's framing suggests. Frankfurt (1971) is cited twice (pp. 1559,
1560), only as one author of the "hierarchical accounts"; the analytic
detail is Dworkin's (1988), including "decisive identification", the move the
record's Frankfurt holdings attribute to Frankfurt's "Identification and
Wholeheartedness" of 1987 ([NOTE-264](../notes.d/NOTE-264.md) calls it "decisive identification",
[NOTE-267](../notes.d/NOTE-267.md) reports it as "decisive commitments"). It is not attributed to him
here. The record's first-hand Frankfurt is the 2004 Tanner
Lectures ([LIT-293](LIT-293.md)), which this paper predates. [NOTE-tmpvdmv2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvdmv2.md) sets out how
SDT's position relates to Frankfurt, to [THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md) and to [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md).
