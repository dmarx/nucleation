# LIT-tmpbkl3k: Complex cooperativity in DNA origami revealed via design-dependent defectivity

Majikes, J. M., Hasni, A., Haridas, S., Robertson, J. W. F., Pintar, A. L., Zwolak, M. & Liddle, J. A. (2026). Complex cooperativity in DNA origami revealed via design-dependent defectivity. *Nucleic Acids Research* 54(4): gkag052. doi:10.1093/nar/gkag052. Preprint: bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2025.04.23.650292.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmppwpd0.*

- **Defectivity is a proxy.** "We equate the defectivity, or φ, to the
  average number of missing or blocked staples for a design", but the
  authors "use this only as a very approximate substitute"; "The primary
  utility of φ is to facilitate comparison between designs."
- **Skew predicts.** "simply increasing the number of short folds at the
  expense of medium folds merely creates additional kinetic traps for the
  topologically necessary long folds"; the rotated design with the most
  short folds "performs objectively worse".
- **Funnel, not path.** The collapse "supports the hypothesis that 2D
  origami is more funnel-like than path-like"; "the aggregate properties of
  event distributions in origami matter more than their precise
  propagation during assembly."
- **Probes trade resolution for cost.** "yield measurements have an
  inverse relationship between their resolution and both their cost and
  throughput"; on a gel "only a significant amount of damage will shift
  the band".

## Standing in the record

Registered from A's second paste on DNA origami, which cited it for
distinguishing positive and negative cooperative mechanisms. The reading
confirms the four mechanisms, as the literature's, and adds the paper's
own result: defectivity across 34 designs of one target is predicted by
aggregates of the first-fold penalty distribution. It supports CLAIM-567
(one feasible target, design-dependent reliability), qualifies CLAIM-583
(the designs share the target, not the compatibility complex) and
CLAIM-185 (more early anchors made outcomes worse), and bears on
QUESTION-380. It extends Majikes et al. (2021) and Majikes et al. (2020),
LIT-tmp0hqya, and pairs with the review of Cumberworth and Reinhardt
(2025), LIT-tmp7dfy2, which disagrees on how much blocking matters.
