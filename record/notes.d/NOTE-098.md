# NOTE-098: Leonelli — Philosophy of Open Science

## Contribution

The Open Science movement seeks wide dissemination, scrutiny and reuse of research components for the benefit of science and society. Leonelli examines what OS principles and practices do in contemporary research and how they bear on the epistemology of science. After reviewing the worries that drove calls for openness, she argues that the common reading of openness as resource-sharing can constrain epistemic diversity and worsen epistemic injustice, producing unreliable and unethical knowledge. She proposes instead that openness means establishing judicious connections among systems of practice. This rests on a process-oriented view of research as a tool for effective and responsible agency.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Contents: §1 Introduction; §2 Rethinking Communication (motivations for OS, the dominance of "closed", commercialized publishing); §3 Rethinking Practice; §4 Rethinking Values; §5 Rethinking the Philosophy of OS; §6 Conclusion.
- §3 gives four implementation cases, centred on open data: 3.1 COVID-19 data sharing ("access wars"), 3.2 technology and quality standards ("the mangle of practice"), 3.3 crop data linkage and bioprospecting, 3.4 the reproducibility "crisis".
- §4 defines epistemic diversity as ways of differing that affect how knowledge is developed, understood or enacted. It argues that epistemic diversity is vital to how science works, tied to epistemic (in)justice, and a precondition of any workable OS.
- §5 argues that openness-as-sharing presupposes an object-oriented view of science built on commodified, tradeable outputs. Leonelli's alternative is a process-oriented view in which research fosters collective agency grounded in trust, and openness is the search for judicious connections. Fig. 2 inverts the usual ordering of OS values.
- §6: implementation difficulties are not merely practical or ethical but rooted in philosophical assumptions. Outputs are "temporary signposts" of inquiry, and OS should attend to who counts as a contributor and how objects are handled and interpreted.
