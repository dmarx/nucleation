**What the two readings changed.** The first draft of the record's claim
said a corporation has nothing that "registers" the costs it imposes on
others. The Wells Fargo report showed that wrong: the bank held the
signals for twelve years (a 2004 internal investigation, a 2012 HR
project, ethics-line trends, a deleted termination count) and none had
authority over the sales model. The claim now speaks of authority, after
[CLAIM-349](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-349.md). The report also showed the harm earned little, so Bakan's
mechanism, harm done when its benefits outweigh its costs, is not needed
and is not what happened there.

Hare and Neumann's review deny that the construct has an essential
component: the affective features are tied to the antisocial ones. So a
corporation that shows only the conduct matches part of the construct,
to a degree, and sits nearer the behaviour-led diagnosis. That went into
the claim's third part and strengthened the objection.

**Not done.** [CASE-014](../../record/cases.d/CASE-014.md) (Microsoft) stays Deferred; the documented case is
Wells Fargo instead, because a public investigation of the firm's own
conduct exists for it. The Wells Fargo reader sent the owner's email
address once to sec.gov, as the contact in a User-Agent header for one
request; it should not have, and it read a mirror instead.

**Copies only the owner can reach.** Velasquez 1983 and 2003 (PhilArchive

**Revised the same day, at the owner's correction.** The note above says
the claim was moved to "in part", nearer the behaviour-led diagnosis,
because a corporation shows only the conduct. The owner: "one of the
specific things our framework equips us to do is to assign affect to
corporation-like things." [CLAIM-039](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-039.md), Deferred since 2026-10-04 with
reopening left to the owner, is Proposed again on that word. The claim
now assigns psychopathy's affective features functionally, after Frijda
([THEORY-055](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-055.md)): a harm to others reaching the corporation's decisions as
one of its own concerns is functional remorse, and reaching them only as
a threat to its other concerns is functional fear. A corporation with
only the second has threat without remorse, which is the construct's
profile across both factors, not part of it. The objection was rewritten
to the point that now carries weight: that for a corporation, whose
every concern was installed for its own ends, remorse may not be
distinguishable from prudence.
