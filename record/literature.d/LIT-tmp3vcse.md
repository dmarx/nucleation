# LIT-tmp3vcse: Memorandum Opinion (remedies), United States v. Google LLC (D.D.C., No. 1:20-cv-03010-APM, ECF No. 1436) [descriptive title]

U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, Judge Amit P. Mehta (2025), *U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia* — <https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1436.0_1.pdf>

## Key takeaways

- Having found Google liable under Sherman Act §2 in 2024, the court imposed a remedy that bars exclusive distribution contracts covering Search, Chrome, Assistant and the Gemini app and requires some search-index and user-interaction data sharing and syndication to "Qualified Competitors". It declined to order a Chrome or Android divestiture, a payment ban or choice screens, and it set a six-year term.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

This is the remedies-phase memorandum opinion that followed the court's 2024 liability ruling that Google unlawfully maintained monopolies in general search and search text ads through exclusive default-distribution agreements. The court says generative AI changed the remedies proceedings: GenAI chatbots are a nascent competitive threat, and part of the court's aim is to keep Google's search dominance from carrying over into GenAI. The court accepts Google's proposed remedies with modifications and adopts some of the plaintiffs' proposals. It rejects structural divestitures and several behavioral remedies as overreaching or unconnected to the violation. It orders the parties to submit a revised final judgment by September 10, 2025.
