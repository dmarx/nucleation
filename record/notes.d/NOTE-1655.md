# NOTE-1655: Rogers et al. — India country study of sustainability and exit strategies in Food for Peace projects

## Contribution

India is the study's test of phase-over: handing a project's functions to
a state that already has resources, capacity and a legal duty to serve.
It shows that these conditions at the national level are needed and still
not enough; local implementation and a linkage that runs before exit
decide the outcome.

## Key insight

The project's own organization need not survive, only its functions, and
they survive by being moved onto a supply that will last. Phase-over "depends,
in part, on the resources, capacity, and commitment (motivation) of
government entities" (p. 5), and it "depends critically on effective local
implementation" (p. 5): the same national programme gave opposite results
in different states.

## Assumptions

- **Design (pp. 1–2).** CARE and CRS ran FFP projects in India from 1997;
  each gave its last cycle, a phase-out plan (POP), to transition into
  Government of India programmes. Exit in 2009 (CARE) and 2010 (CRS); the
  endline survey replicated two years later (2011, 2012) in a subset of
  states, with qualitative rounds at both points.
- **The receiver is permanent and funded.** The Indian state is taken as a
  lasting phase-over entity; the project's own organization is not
  required to persist.
- **No counterfactual.** Endline is compared with follow-up in a subset of
  states.

## Key results

1. **States differ more than time does (p. 2).** "the aggregate results
   for all states in the study mask substantial state-by-state
   differences"; "differences across states were often larger than any"
   endline-to-follow-up "change within a given state", and "no one state
   saw consistent improvements" or consistent declines.
2. **Routines the state adopted (p. 2).** "more than 95 percent of
   frontline anganwadi workers reported attending monthly sector
   meetings", yet attendance varied by state, "from 9.7 percent in
   Chhattisgarh to 92.7 percent in Andhra Pradesh". Home visits were
   "sustained or improved", "reaching 40 percent of mothers at
   follow-up".
3. **Growth monitoring less well kept (p. 3).** "an overall participation
   rate of 47.4 percent"; not reducing feeding during diarrhoea rose "to
   71.8 percent overall".
4. **CRS health: phase-over worked (p. 3).** Institutional deliveries
   "rose from 20 percent to 76 percent between baseline in 2002 and the
   end of the POP in 2010, continued to increase to 87 percent at
   follow-up in 2012", credited to health activists "whose pay is linked
   to improvements in these indicators".
5. **Malnutrition not moved (pp. 3–4).** In CARE areas weight-for-age
   "rates rose over the life of the project, and endline rates were
   unchanged at follow-up". In CRS areas one stunting measure was
   "unchanged (remaining at 40%)" and the overall rate fell to "35% at
   follow-up", with three of seven states rising; wasting went "from 25
   percent to 22 percent". Care practices "showed no significant effect
   on the probability of a child being stunted".
6. **Agriculture through the employment guarantee (p. 4).** Land under
   irrigation went "from 8.7 percent at POP endline in 2010 to 21.7
   percent at follow-up in 2012"; the share of farmers selling reached
   "close to 100 percent in three of the four states studied".
7. **School meals: a late linkage (p. 5).** "only a few of the schools
   formerly supported by CRS had successfully obtained authorization to
   participate in the Mid-Day Meals program", and "some schools did not
   start the application process until close to the end of the POP".
   Enrolment, retention and new teaching methods were nonetheless kept.
8. **What the receiver brought (p. 5).** The Indian programmes combined
   "resources dedicated to the programs", "functional infrastructure" at
   national and state level (capacity), and "a legal commitment to provide
   services (motivation)". Yet success "depends critically on effective
   local implementation".
9. **Linkage decides it (p. 5).** "where linkages had not been clearly
   established", "the activities did not continue post-project".
10. **Timing (p. 5).** "transitions should be gradual", and
    project-strengthened "systems should be operating independently prior
    to project exit".
11. **Pay (p. 6).** "motivation for service provision can be maintained
    through pay-for-performance and salary models"; indicators tied to
    activists' pay "were well maintained or improved".
12. **Recommendations (pp. 6–7).** "resources, capacity, and motivation
    must be present at every level down to the most local"; engage
    government "from the beginning" of the project "and allow a gradual
    transition to independent functioning".

## Concepts

- **Phase-out plan (POP)**: a whole final project cycle given to
  transition.
- **Phase-over**: transferring responsibility to an existing entity, here
  national programmes: the Integrated Child Development Service, with
  its anganwadi workers, the National Rural Health Mission, the National
  Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA) and Mid-Day Meals (p. 5).
- **Motivation** as a legal commitment, for the state, or as pay, salary
  or pay for performance, for workers.
- **Local implementation variance**: one national system, different
  outcomes by state.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (independent of what founded it, never of what feeds it).**
  Qualifies it. Independence from the founding donor was reached by
  moving the supply to a domestic supplier, the national programmes; the
  functions still depend on a feed, only a different one. Consistent
  with the claim's wording, and a reminder that "never of what feeds it"
  allows the feed to change hands.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) (when a partial organization becomes independent of the
  intervention that established it).** Answers it in part: phase-over
  succeeded where the receiver had resources, capacity and a legal
  mandate and the linkage was operating before exit, and failed where the
  linkage was begun late.
- **[CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md) (assembling and persisting are separate).** Supports it:
  delivery systems persisted while impact on stunting never assembled.
- **[QUESTION-386](../questions.d/QUESTION-386.md) (funding rules that lose capabilities).** Bears on it:
  pay for performance kept the paid-for indicators (deliveries,
  immunisation); the brief does not say whether unpaid functions
  suffered.
- **[QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md).** No case here: no nucleus regenerates its own
  constraints; the state supplies them.
- Filed: [THEORY-1496](../theory.d/THEORY-1496.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- An executive summary of a two-year follow-up in a subset of states,
  with no comparison group.
- The project's effect on nutrition was never established, so what is
  assessed is persistence of activities, not of impact.
- The variance between states is reported, not explained.
- Two years after exit says nothing of longer persistence.
