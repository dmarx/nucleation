# CLAIM-494: Empathic concern for another produces motivation whose ultimate goal is the other's welfare, and it does so when perceived self–other merging is controlled, so not all helping is concern for an extended self

## The claim

Batson's empathy–altruism hypothesis: feeling empathic concern for a
person in need evokes a motive whose ultimate goal is that person's
welfare, not one's own. Against Cialdini et al. ([LIT-1728](../literature.d/LIT-1728.md)), Batson et
al. ([LIT-1700](../literature.d/LIT-1700.md)) report that the empathy–helping link holds with
perceived self–other merging controlled. If so, the owner's thesis
([CLAIM-491](CLAIM-491.md)) fails for at least some helping: there is no extended
self for the motive to serve.

## What it does not say

- **Not that most helping is altruistic.** One altruistic motive is
  enough to defeat a universal thesis.
- **Not that the 1997 exchange is settled.** Cialdini et al. measured
  oneness differently. Which measure captures the self's extent is the
  crux that [QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) asks about.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the SEP's report

Doris, Stich, Walmsley and Schulz ([NOTE-1274](../notes.d/NOTE-1274.md)) report that Batson et al.
found methodological problems in Cialdini et al. and, in "a pair of
experiments" that avoided them, found empathy's role in helping "clearly
evident". They give no numbers, and the paper is still unread; the summary
above now leaves the count to the SEP. They argue that none of the four
merging measures in the exchange (IOS, "we", perceived similarity, a
trait-rating difference) tests for perceived numerical identity. On their
view only that would make helping egoistic, so neither side measured what
egoism needs. The owner's thesis claims less than identity, an extended
self-identification, so the argument leaves it open. It does put the
thesis to saying why such a self counts as the agent's own.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

Its evidence, Batson et al. 1997 ([LIT-1700](../literature.d/LIT-1700.md)), is Unavailable and known
only from the SEP's report ([NOTE-1274](../notes.d/NOTE-1274.md)), which gives no numbers. The paper
it answers, Cialdini et al. ([LIT-1728](../literature.d/LIT-1728.md)), is now read ([NOTE-1444](../notes.d/NOTE-1444.md)) and
cuts against it: with oneness controlled, empathic concern predicted no
willingness to help in any of twelve relationship conditions, near
strangers included. That weighs against the objection without defeating
it, because the helping was imagined and the reply criticised that
paradigm. What would settle it is a read result, the reply or a later
study, in which empathic concern predicts real helping with oneness
measured as Cialdini et al. measured it.
