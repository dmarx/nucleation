# THEORY-913: In small fixed-effect meta-analytic pools without correction for publication bias, responses specified in if-then plans ran more immediately (d = .77, k = 7), held up under a concurrent cognitive load (d = .85, k = 7) and ran without conscious intent (d = .72, k = 3) compared with responses under a goal intention alone, and the planned cues were detected, attended to and remembered better (d = .80, k = 6); the authors read this as control of the response handed in advance to the cue

## Source

Gollwitzer & Sheeran, [LIT-1497](../literature.d/LIT-1497.md), read in full in [NOTE-1124](../notes.d/NOTE-1124.md) from
the KOPS scan of the published chapter: pp. 83–86, 97, 99–100 and 106,
with Table V.

## What was actually shown

- **The then-component (Table V, p. 97).** Against participants with the
  goal intention only, planned responses were more immediate (d = .77,
  k = 7), efficient under a concurrent load (d = .85, k = 7) and
  independent of conscious intent (d = .72, k = 3, from subliminal
  priming of the cue or of the goal; pp. 85–86).
- **The if-component (Table V).** Detection, discrimination,
  accessibility, attention and memory for the planned cue, d = .80 over 6
  tests, three of the five measures from one test each.
- **Mediation (p. 106).** Cue accessibility mediated goal attainment in
  one study and, with the strength of the cue–response link, in a second.
  Immediacy, efficiency and lack of intent had not been tested as
  mediators.
- **The authors' reading.** Forming the plan "involves a strategic
  abdication of effortful action control" (p. 84); "forming an
  implementation intention means deciding these issues in advance, thereby
  delegating the control of goal-directed behavior to specified situational
  cues" (p. 100).

What could have come out otherwise: planned responses could have been no
faster than intended ones, or slowed by a concurrent task as deliberate
responses are.

## What this does not say

- **That the processes produce the effect on goal attainment.** Only
  accessibility and link strength were tested as mediators, in two
  studies.
- **That the effects are as large as reported.** Fixed-effect pooling,
  small k, many tests from the authors' groups, and no correction for
  publication bias; the 2024 review finds that bias substantial overall
  ([THEORY-918](THEORY-918.md)).
- **That the plan makes control unconditional.** The same chapter reports
  plan effects gated by the goal intention ([THEORY-904](THEORY-904.md)).
- **That plans are habits.** The authors' figure is "instant habits"; the
  habit-like features were measured, not the history that makes a habit.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-443](../claims.d/CLAIM-443.md).** The claim cites implementation intentions as
  pre-arranged links of situation to response. This is what the links do,
  as measured: a response fixed in advance and triggered by its cue, with
  later deliberation and effort saved. In the claim's terms it is a prior
  over actions, not over which process governs; and the efficiency and
  conserved-capability results are measured savings of later work, the
  evidence the claim says persistence alone cannot give, for a cached
  action.
- **[CLAIM-358](../claims.d/CLAIM-358.md) and [QUESTION-095](../questions.d/QUESTION-095.md).** The plainest case the record holds of a
  decision cached and reused at lower cost.

## Note of 2026-10-30: the dropped clause

<!-- inactive-ok-block: ADR-047 — Superseded by ADR-057, which keeps its evidence rulings; cited for the four kinds -->
The tenth status review's narrowing dropped one clause: that "the planned
cues became more accessible (d = .80, k = 6)". It was imprecise. Table V's
if-component pools detection, discrimination, accessibility, attention and
memory for the planned cue, three of the five measures from one test
each, so the d = .80 is not an estimate of accessibility. That is the
record's mis-statement of the table, not a wider claim the source makes,
so it is kind 3 under [ADR-047](../decisions.d/ADR-047.md) and gets this note only. The review's other
changes, naming the fixed-effect pooling, the missing bias correction and
the goal-intention comparison, make explicit what the old title left
implicit and drop nothing. Whether the process effects survive correction
and mediate goal attainment was not claimed by either title and stays
[QUESTION-308](../questions.d/QUESTION-308.md) (kind 4).
