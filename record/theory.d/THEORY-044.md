# THEORY-044: In relational frame theory the self is verbal behaviour's content, its process and its context, and the self as context is a perspective learned from deictic relations that cannot itself be found as content

## Source

- Barnes-Holmes, Barnes-Holmes, McHugh and Hayes (2004), [LIT-541](../literature.d/LIT-541.md), read in
  full in [NOTE-430](../notes.d/NOTE-430.md). The three selves are on pp. 367–369, and the deictic
  reading of defusion on pp. 370–371.
- Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda and Lillis (2006), [LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-431](../notes.d/NOTE-431.md) from an early manuscript. Self as context is on p. 8.

The RFT book ([LIT-549](../literature.d/LIT-549.md)), to which every concept in both papers is cited, is
filed unread.

## What the sources say

**Three selves.** RFT defines "the conceptualized self (i.e., the self as
the content of verbal relations); the knowing self (i.e., the self as the
ongoing process of verbal relations); and the transcendent self (i.e., the
self as the context of verbal relations)" ([LIT-541](../literature.d/LIT-541.md), p. 368). The worked
example is a woman deciding whether to end her marriage. Deciding from
self as content, she stays because she is "the type of woman who simply
cannot live without a man". Deciding from self as process, she leaves
because she has accurately noticed that she has long been unhappy, without
asking what kind of person she wants to be. From self as context she can
contact both, "but neither obtain absolute control over her decision", and
only from there can she see that protecting the first is feeding the
hostility the second reports (pp. 368–369).

**The context is a learned perspective.** "As a result of deictic frames
such as I-You, Now-Then, and Here-There human language leads to a sense of
self as a locus or perspective." "'I' emerges over large sets of exemplars
of deictic relations, but since this sense of self is a context for verbal
knowing, not the content of that knowing, it's limits cannot be
consciously known" ([LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md), p. 8). The manuscript cites "growing evidence
of its importance to language functions such as empathy, theory of mind,
sense of self", but the citation is an unfilled placeholder ("see xxx"),
and its citation of trained perspective-taking is "(xx)" ([NOTE-431](../notes.d/NOTE-431.md),
Corrections).

**Fusion is content standing in for the context.** "When we respond to our
thoughts as I HERE NOW, we are in a sense caught up in them – we are not
perceiving those thoughts and feelings from a perspective, but rather they
are the perspective." Defusion moves them to I THERE THEN, so that they are
"thoughts and feelings that we had, rather than providing our current and
only perspective" ([LIT-541](../literature.d/LIT-541.md), pp. 370–371).

**What is evidence and what is not.** The three selves are definitions
within a theory, and the marriage case is constructed. The claim that the
perspective is learned from deictic relations is RFT's empirical claim, and
in the record it rests on studies cited and not read. Part 3 of the 2004
paper, which reads ACT's processes through RFT, is labelled by its authors
"no more than a heuristic" (p. 369). So the conceptual account is stated
clearly enough to compare, and its empirical half is unsupported here.

## Where it meets the record's views of the self

- **Self as content is the narrative self.** Gallagher ([LIT-453](../literature.d/LIT-453.md), [NOTE-377](../notes.d/NOTE-377.md))
  separates the narrative self, extended in time through memory and story,
  from the minimal self. Dennett ([LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md), [NOTE-364](../notes.d/NOTE-364.md)) makes the self a
  "centre of narrative gravity", an abstractum the story coheres around,
  and Hume ([LIT-451](../literature.d/LIT-451.md), [NOTE-386](../notes.d/NOTE-386.md)) makes its identity "fictitious", supplied by
  the imagination. RFT's conceptualized self is this self described from
  the outside: a network of verbal relations whose subject is oneself.
  Where the record's views differ is on its value. ACT treats domination by
  it ("defending a conceptualized self", [LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md), p. 7) as part of
  inflexibility. That converges with Strawson ([LIT-452](../literature.d/LIT-452.md), [NOTE-383](../notes.d/NOTE-383.md)), for whom
  self-narration tends to falsify the self it describes (C7), and with
  Dennett's claim that self-interpretation can change the self (C4). It
  sits awkwardly with Gallagher's richer narrative self, which includes
  self-deception by design, and with the ethical narrativity views Strawson
  attacks, of which the record holds Schechtman ([LIT-468](../literature.d/LIT-468.md)) unread.
- **Self as context is what cannot be found as content.** Hume, looking
  inward, "never can catch myself at any time without a perception"
  ([NOTE-386](../notes.d/NOTE-386.md)), and concludes there is only the bundle. RFT accepts the
  observation and draws a different conclusion: what cannot be found among
  the contents is the perspective from which they are found, whose limits
  "cannot be consciously known". Metzinger ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)) gives the
  same structure in self-model terms. A first-person perspective is a
  transparent model of the intentionality relation, one the system cannot
  recognise as a model (C8). On both accounts the perspective is not an
  object of experience, and on neither is it a substantial self. RFT calls
  it a behavioural context; Metzinger calls it a model. Neither cites the
  other, and the mapping is the record's.
- **The deictic "here" follows the point of view.** In Dennett's "Where Am
  I?" ([LIT-405](../literature.d/LIT-405.md), [NOTE-338](../notes.d/NOTE-338.md)), where a person is follows the point of view, and
  the narrator cannot think "here" of the vat that holds his brain. On
  RFT's account the same deictic terms are what the perspective is learned
  from. Both treat "here" as fixed by where one sees from. RFT does not
  discuss the body, and the comparison is the record's.
- **The open seam is the minimal self.** Gallagher's minimal self is
  pre-reflective and embodied. He reports neonates "less than an hour old"
  imitating faces as evidence of a primitive self before language
  ([NOTE-377](../notes.d/NOTE-377.md), C5), and Metzinger roots the self-model in non-linguistic motor
  control ([NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md), C4). If RFT's self as context were that minimal self,
  its claim that "'I' emerges over large sets of exemplars of deictic
  relations" would contradict this evidence. The sources support a
  narrower reading. What RFT says is learned is a *sense* of self as
  perspective, a context "for verbal knowing", and the evidence it points
  to concerns empathy and theory of mind, which are reflective,
  self–other achievements. On that reading self as context is a verbal
  layer that presupposes the minimal self and does not replace it, and the
  two are compatible. The "transcendent, spiritual" language of [LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md)
  reaches further than that reading, and nothing in the sources decides
  between them. `promote_when` says what would.
- **No substantial self is required.** Like the bundle, the narrative
  abstractum and the self-model, RFT's three selves are functional: what
  verbal behaviour is about, its ongoing activity, and the perspective it
  is emitted from. Nothing in the account needs a self as an entity.
  Shanahan ([LIT-466](../literature.d/LIT-466.md)) reads the dissolution of every candidate site of an
  AI's self as Buddhist emptiness. ACT, which calls the perspective
  "transcendent" and fosters it by mindfulness, is the clinical relative of
  that reading, though neither paper says so.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say self as context is therapeutic.** That is a separate,
  empirical claim, [THEORY-051](THEORY-051.md), with its own evidence.
- **It does not say RFT has shown the perspective is learned.** The
  sources assert it and cite studies the record has not read.
- **It does not settle personal identity.** The three selves say nothing
  about persistence over time, fission or what matters in survival
  which the record's personal-identity readings address. Strawson's Episodics, who do not
  experience the self they are as having been there in the past, could have
  all three selves.
- **It does not answer the regress about the agent's standpoint.**
  Deciding "from self as context", with neither content nor process in
  "absolute control", looks like a candidate for the standpoint [THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)
  argues no higher-order attitude can supply. A perspective is not an
  attitude, so the regress may not apply to it. Whether it can then make a
  decision the agent's own is a question neither source asks.
