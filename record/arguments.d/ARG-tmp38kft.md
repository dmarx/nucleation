# ARG-tmp38kft: Broido and Clauset's finding that few network degree distributions pass a severe power-law test shows that a power law or a scale-invariant law does not by itself indicate criticality

## The inference

CLAIM-841: a power law does not by itself indicate criticality, since
other routes remove a characteristic scale without tuning. Ground offered:
THEORY-tmphcnj3, through the folded-in bullet "Many reported scale-free
networks are not".

## Critical questions (source)

**Does the work say this, read in context?** No. Criticality, phase
transitions and the claim's routes are absent from the paper. Its subject
is whether degree distributions are power laws, not what a power law
indicates.

**Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** Not even for the bullet
as worded. 46% of data sets are Super-Weak and the lognormal comparison
is inconclusive for 40%; the paper says many "may in fact be log-normal
networks". It shows that many networks reported as scale free show little
statistical evidence of it, and its verdict on rarity is itself disputed
(LIT-tmpvmrkv).

**Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** Its bullet
did, slightly. The work was never in the claim's grounds.

## Verdict

Rejected as a route to the thesis. The bullet is reworded in the claim.
