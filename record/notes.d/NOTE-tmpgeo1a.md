# NOTE-tmpgeo1a: Segundo A.P.G. v. Bondi show cause hearing

## Contribution

The transcript records a February 3, 2026 show-cause hearing before Judge Jerry W. Blackwell in St. Paul. The hearing covered five habeas matters: Segundo A.P.G. (26-cv-603), Oscar O.T. (26-cv-167), Jose L.C.C. (26-cv-244), Roman N. (26-cv-282) and Juan V.A.C. (26-cv-645). The respondents include the Attorney General, the DHS Secretary and ICE officials. The court says the hearing concerns compliance with its orders, not policy. It walks through each case in which the government did not file the required 48-hour release confirmations, delayed releases, or imposed release conditions the court had not ordered. Government counsel describe workload, lack of training and difficulty getting the agency to comply. Petitioners' counsel ask for sanctions. The court takes the matter under advisement.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Opening remarks (pp. 5-8): the court says it called the hearing because of repeated noncompliance. It states that an enforcement operation of this scale carries a corresponding obligation to make each detention lawful, and that the government cannot "detain first and then sort out lawful authority later".
- Segundo A.P.G. colloquy (pp. 11-14): release was ordered on January 27, with a status update due within 48 hours. No update was filed, and the court followed with a text order and a show-cause order. Government counsel (Ms. Le) says she did not receive the order in time.
- A further matter (pp. 20-26; I did not pin down which of the five cases from the pages sampled): release was ordered on January 15, and the person was released on January 28, 13 days later. The court presses counsel on an unexplained "safety concern". Counsel cites fear that protesters would appear at the airport.
- Jose L.C.C. (pp. 29-32): the government concedes it imposed release conditions the court had not ordered, which had to be struck. Counsel describes difficulty getting the agency to comply and says she had no orientation or training in the role (p. 17).
- Petitioners' counsel (pp. 41-46) argue that the problems are systemic, refer to the court's list of about 90-96 order violations in January, and ask for sanctions. The court takes the matter under advisement and adjourns at 2:16 p.m.
