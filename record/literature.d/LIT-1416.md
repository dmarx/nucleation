  Kleinman, Liu & Redding (2024; NBER 2020). Asks whether countries that become more economically dependent on a trade partner realign politically toward it. Dependence is the elasticity of a country's real income to the partner's productivity, from a constant-elasticity trade model with sectors and input-output links; about 30 percent of pairs are "enemies", with negative elasticity. Instrumented by a counterfactual Chinese productivity shock (119 countries, 1980–2010) and by the falling cost of air relative to sea transport (country pairs, 1970–2012), greater exposure raises UN voting similarity (0.094 SD per SD in the pair panel), lowers strategic rivalry except ideological rivalry, and raises formal alliances; bilateral trade does not proxy for it.

## Why this is here

The exchange carried the favour from persons to states: trade as a standing exchange that comes before, and then shapes, political alignment. It cited this paper for economic alignment causing political alignment (UN voting, rivalries), with quasi-experimental variation from China's integration and air-transport costs. The abstract agrees on the direction, the causal claim and both sources of variation. **Flag:** the abstract names no outcome measure; "UN voting" and "rivalries" are not in it and are unchecked.
