  e.g. Instagram's public denial of shadowbanning; (iii) neo-normative,

# NOTE-tmp7mfz7: Liang, Li, Aroles and Granter (2025) — Content Creation within the Algorithmic Environment: A Systematic Review

## Contribution

Names visibility, allocated by opaque algorithms, as the main instrument of control over content
creators, a group usually seen as autonomous, and gives a critical account of why creators'
attempts to model and game the algorithm reinforce rather than undo that control.

## Key results

- p. 1 (Abstract): research question "How does algorithmic power maintain its dominance and shape
  the nature of work for content creators?"; thesis "we argue that the fantasies fabricated by
  neoliberalism justify, endorse and ultimately support the dominance and dynamic power of
  algorithms over creators in content creative platforms".
- p. 2: algorithmic control on platforms works through customers, "with visibility serving as a
  form of governance".
- p. 4: "A key aspect of algorithm management in content creation is the use of visibility, but
  this is easily overlooked"; of gig work generally, "algorithms match workers and customers
  without recourse to (human) managerial involvement".
- p. 8 (Method): "We finally retained 47 articles as our sample".
- p. 12: "visibility functions as the main currency within virtual communities".
- p. 13: "Although some creators claim to be ‘experts’ in algorithm manipulation, this is less
  effective than they think"; "platforms are generally regarded as the authoritative interpreter
  of the algorithm", so a platform statement collapses a folk theory; "Instagram issued a public
  statement denying the existence of shadowbanning".
- p. 15: "This form of neonormative control appears to represent a ‘free control’".
- p. 17: "Autonomy exists but only to the extent that it is embedded in algorithmic management".
- p. 19 (Limitations): "most of the reviewed articles did not specify the platform studied", and
  "some popular platforms, such as TikTok/Douyin, did not feature in this article".
- p. 20 (Conclusion): "the dominant power status of algorithms remains unchallenged".

## Assumptions

A critical-theory frame (neoliberalism as regime; fantasmatic logics) is applied, not tested;
themes are built by the authors' active categorisation and stabilised against that framework.
Sample limited to business and management journals on the ABS list, English only, no
proceedings or books, so communication and HCI work (where most creator-algorithm studies live)
is largely out.

## Concepts

Algorithmic management; visibility as currency and governance; folk theories of algorithms;
reverse engineering; shadowbanning; neo-normative control and "free control"; individualisation
(responsibilisation, quantification); hegemonic ideology (universalism, disembeddedness); beatific
fantasies; dislocation and suturing (Tirapani and Willmott; Glynos and Howarth).

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** A's account ("a systematic review identifying algorithmic visibility as managerial power over
content creators who have no conventional manager") is broadly right. The paper does say visibility
is the key instrument of algorithmic management and a "form of governance". The phrase "no
conventional manager" is A's: the paper's nearest words are about gig work generally ("without
recourse to (human) managerial involvement"), and it stresses that creators are seen as
autonomous entrepreneurs. A omits the paper's main thesis, that neoliberal "fantasies", folk
theories included, sustain the control, and its narrow sample.

- **[CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md)** (an organization can be governed from outside without any decision being made for
  it, through the evidence its procedures receive or the criteria by which they weigh it; the
  interpretation–authority loop can close between institutions). **Supports by analogy, at the
  level of individual workers, not organizations**. Both routes appear: platforms govern what
  creators see of their own performance (metrics, visibility; informational), and creators
  internalise metric success as the criterion of good work ("free control"; evaluative). The
  shadowbanning episode is a clean instance of the interpretation–authority loop: the platform is
  "the authoritative interpreter of the algorithm" whose statement dissolves creators'
  independently built theory of how they are being evaluated. The review's evidence is qualitative
  and second-hand (47 articles, mostly platform-unspecified), so this is illustration, not a test.
- Found in recheck: **[THEORY-150](../theory.d/THEORY-150.md) / [THEORY-470](../theory.d/THEORY-470.md)** (enshittification): the review's "market
  rationality underpinning visibility" (engagement- and profit-driven allocation of visibility,
  popularity bias) describes the business-customer side of Doctorow's account from the creators'
  side. It does not address stages or decay over time. Weak, illustrative bearing.
- **[LIT-tmpc2x78](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc2x78.md)** (Montero-Porras et al.): complementary. Liang supplies the qualitative picture of
  visibility as the reward V that the platform controls; Montero-Porras model creators' investment
  as a response to it. The filer may note the pairing.
- **[CLAIM-332](../claims.d/CLAIM-332.md)** (a loosely organized population changes an outside institution without collective
  intention): folk theories are collaboratively built and circulated, but the review finds they
  fail to change platforms ("easily undermined"), a negative instance at best.

## Limitations

Small, discipline-restricted sample; the platform is usually unspecified; no TikTok/Douyin; themes
fitted to a pre-chosen critical framework, so the "hegemony" conclusion is interpretive rather than
derived from the reviewed evidence; no quality appraisal of included studies is reported.
