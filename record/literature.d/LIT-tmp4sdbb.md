# LIT-tmp4sdbb: A systematic review of human avoidance learning: Cognition, computation, and methods

Mancinelli, F. & Bach, D. R. (2026). A systematic review of human avoidance learning: Cognition, computation, and methods. *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 190: 106916. doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106916. Preprint: PsyArXiv, doi:10.31234/osf.io/yqcd4_v1 (2025).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpk2tkp. Page numbers are the preprint's.*

- **Scope.** 116 articles (1939–2024) of "experimental procedures
  involving healthy humans" (p. 7) with shock or loud noise; a systematic
  review under PRISMA, not a meta-analysis. Phenomena are rated A to C for
  robustness (Fig. 4).
- **Against two-factor theory.** Avoidance is learned without ending the
  CS (8 studies, A); it transfers to stimuli never trained with it; it
  appears after Pavlovian learning alone (A).
- **Protection from extinction (A).** With avoidance available, "42%
  (Malloy & Levis, 1988) and 47% (Williams & Levis, 1991) of participants
  did not extinguish a unary and costless AR by the 500th
  would-be-unpunished trial" (p. 18), and those allowed to avoid "retain
  higher US expectancies" (p. 18). More effective responses last longer,
  "putatively because effective ARs provide fewer opportunities to detect
  changes in contingency" (p. 28).
- **Avoidance spreads to the safe stimulus (B).** Offering a response for
  a never-punished stimulus, then withdrawing it, raises threat
  expectancy for that stimulus (pp. 19–20); signalling is a confound.
- **Not purely inferential.** Some avoidance persists after signalled
  outcome omission, without habits forming: "the evidence also contradicts
  a purely inference-based account" (p. 25).
- **Paradigms too simple.** Mostly instructed, signalled button presses;
  the authors call for multi-step, naturalistic tasks.

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it at turn U14, unnamed, as a 2026 systematic review of 116 human avoidance-learning studies giving the strongest support to expectancy- and inference-based accounts. The abstract confirms the count and the verdict for inference-based accounts against associative ones; it does not name expectancy accounts separately, and it adds that the inference-based theories are themselves incomplete.
