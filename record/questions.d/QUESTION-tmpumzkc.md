# QUESTION-tmpumzkc: When a habit is modelled as a prior over policies that grows by counting, do the counts fitted to an animal's or a person's own choice history predict when behaviour becomes insensitive to devaluation, and when it still switches at once?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-923 shows, in one simulated T-maze task with hand-set parameters,
a habit learned by counting that persists after devaluation once acquired,
while an agent devalued earlier switches at once. Nothing is fitted to
behaviour, and the authors concede that the scheme probably makes no new
behavioural predictions. Whether the count-driven prior is how habits form
is a part of what QUESTION-088 asks of process models of habit.

## What would answer it

An empirical test, not another simulation: animals or people whose choices
are fitted by this scheme, with the habit's prior and transitions estimated
from their own history, showing that the amount of prior practice the
fitted counts imply predicts insensitivity to devaluation, and that the same
fits predict immediate switching when devaluation comes before that point.
The account of habit is refuted if devaluation sensitivity is lost under
training regimes where the fitted policy counts stay low, or kept where they
are high. A simulation in another task cannot settle it.
