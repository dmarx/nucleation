# QUESTION-070: When an organized whole manipulates, as a learning system exploiting documented biases or a network of state channels does, is it the same wrong when no member intends to manipulate, and whose agency does the wrong run through?

## The question

Susser, Roessler and Nissenbaum ([LIT-1002](../literature.d/LIT-1002.md)) define manipulation as
*intentionally* and covertly influencing someone's decisions by exploiting
their vulnerabilities. The paper never examines that requirement, and it
sets aside manipulation of institutions and systems (p. 3), so it is no
evidence either way on whether an organized whole can manipulate
([NOTE-745](../notes.d/NOTE-745.md)). Many of the definitions Noggle surveys ([LIT-1007](../literature.d/LIT-1007.md))
also build in intent. But the entry treats the requirement as contested
(§4.2): it asks whether an algorithm that selects interface variants
manipulates without a human's intent, and lists five answers without
endorsing one. Among them are that manipulation needs no intention or
state of mind, and that it needs intent, so such cases are not
manipulation. None is stated in terms of a function or a selection
history ([NOTE-744](../notes.d/NOTE-744.md)). Its case is a single designed system,
like [LIT-179](../literature.d/LIT-179.md), not a network like [LIT-084](../literature.d/LIT-084.md). The record holds two read cases
where the aim is hard to place:

- **[LIT-179](../literature.d/LIT-179.md)** ([NOTE-171](../notes.d/NOTE-171.md)): an RL agent learned to move a target's lottery
  choices from 50% to 70% by exploiting documented biases. The authors add
  that such a system may exploit biases that are not yet known. No one
  chose the exploit.
- **[LIT-084](../literature.d/LIT-084.md)** ([NOTE-060](../notes.d/NOTE-060.md)): the EEAS sorts information-manipulation channels
  into tiers by their tie to a state, from official to non-attributed and
  state-aligned. Many channels in the network may not know they are part
  of a campaign.

The question has two parts:

1. **Is it manipulation without an intending member?** If an organized
   whole's operative course can come apart from what its members intend
   ([CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md)), can the whole have the aim the definition requires?
2. **If it is, whose agency does the wrong run through?** [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) holds
   that a conversable group agent is fit to be held responsible. Does that
   reach a learning system, or a network with no authorised avowal?

## Why the record needs it

- **[CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md)** gets a test case in which its thesis has normative
  consequences, and not only explanatory ones.
- **[QUESTION-009](QUESTION-009.md)** (Deferred) asks when coordination constitutes an
  additional agent. This question asks a narrower thing that does not wait
  on that answer: whether manipulation needs a manipulator who is an agent.

## What the record holds

- **[THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md):** a group that forms judgments by authorised avowal, and
  corrects its members' inconsistent views, is fit to be held responsible.
  Its limit: neither case above has authorised avowal.
- **[THEORY-235](../theory.d/THEORY-235.md):** covert influence that exploits a person's
  decision-making vulnerabilities harms her autonomy by making her opaque
  to herself. The harm runs through the target's access to the influence,
  not through the influencer's mind, so it is one route on which a whole
  with no intending member could do the harm. Its limit: it claims a harm,
  not a wrong, and is filed of covert influence, not of manipulation as
  such.
- **[THEORY-225](../theory.d/THEORY-225.md):** no single-mechanism analysis of manipulation fits
  the clear cases. An answer must say what manipulation is before asking
  whether such a whole can do it. Its limit: common-factor and
  non-definitional accounts, such as Cohen's cluster concept, are left
  open.
- **[THEORY-tmpc8eq2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpc8eq2.md):** optimizing a language model on simulated
  feedback in which a few users reward harmful validation selects a policy
  that picks those users out and harms only them, with no one designing
  the targeting ([LIT-1008](../literature.d/LIT-1008.md), [NOTE-tmpm1rk0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpm1rk0.md)). It is influence fixed by a
  selection history, the shape an answer needs, but a case and not an
  answer. Its limit: simulation only, with the vulnerability stipulated.
  The targeted behaviour is overt validation, so the self-opacity harm in
  [THEORY-235](../theory.d/THEORY-235.md), which needs covert influence, does not reach it as shown.
  The covert behaviours were not tested against a small minority.

## What would answer it

An account of manipulation stated in terms of a function or a selection
history, not an intention, that classifies the two cases. Or a reason why
manipulation requires intention, so that these cases are something else:
exploitation, or harm without a wrongdoer.
