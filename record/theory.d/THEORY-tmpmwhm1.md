# THEORY-tmpmwhm1: Muralidharan and Sundararaman find, in a randomized evaluation in 500 Andhra Pradesh government primary schools, that bonuses paid to teachers on their students' test-score gains raised scores after two years by 0.27 SD (individual) and 0.16 SD (group), against 0.09 SD for an extra contract teacher or a block grant for materials calibrated to the same yearly spending, and that the incentive worked through effort while present rather than attendance

## The account

Muralidharan and Sundararaman ([NOTE-tmphqrk0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphqrk0.md)) set the incentive and
input arms of one randomized study to cost about the same per school, and
find that "performance-based bonus payments to teachers were a
significantly more cost effective way of increasing student test scores
compared to spending a similar amount of money unconditionally on
additional schooling inputs." At equal spending: "three times larger ...
(0.27 SD vs. 0.09 SD)". The channel, by their reading: "not increased
teacher attendance, but greater (and more effective) teaching effort
conditional on being present."

## What it rests on

- Randomization within mandals, 100 schools per cell, mandal fixed
  effects, school-clustered errors.
- Costs as yearly flows; testing (about Rs 5,000 per school a round) left
  out, which the authors say does not change the ranking.

## What it does not say

- Anything about the two input arms separately; they are pooled here.
- That place, rather than conditionality, makes the difference: the arms
  acting on effort are the only conditional ones.
- Anything beyond two years and Andhra Pradesh's low-learning setting.
