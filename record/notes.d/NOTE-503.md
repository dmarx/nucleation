# NOTE-503: Block — Advertisement for a Semantics for Psychology

## Contribution

It states what a semantics for cognitive science should explain, as eight
desiderata, and argues that only two-factor conceptual role semantics (CRS)
is even aimed at all of them. It also gives the first explicit argument
that one-factor, long-arm conceptual role semantics (Harman) is equivalent
to the two-factor theory, and it gives the systems reply to the Chinese
room a theory of what the system's symbols would mean.

## Key insight

Split meaning into what is in the head and what relates the head to the
world, and many disputes dissolve. Narrow meaning, conceptual role, is what
twins share and what explains their behaviour; reference is what differs
between them. Objections that conceptual roles are about symbols rather than
the world (Fodor's Tom Swift, Dretske's "cream") hit only a one-factor
theory: in two-factor CRS the referential factor does that job (pp. 637–639).

## Assumptions

- **Representationalism, minimal (pp. 641–642).** Thoughts are structured:
  representational states "constitute a combinatorial system". This is
  "empirical and contingent", motivated by cognitive science's success and
  by productivity (entertainable n × m = q sentences number on the order of
  forty-six digits). Rules need not be represented, and some reasoning may
  be primitive.
- **Token materialism** (p. 617) and a focus on the brain.
- **A language module** that sets up a "standard association" between
  English and mentalese expressions, characterisable non-intentionally
  (pp. 632–634); conceptual roles of English are those of their mentalese
  associates.
- **Idiolect narrow meaning**, not public language meaning (p. 633).
- **No analytic/synthetic distinction**; meaning similarity should be a
  "multidimensional gradient", as decision theory replaced belief with
  degrees of belief (pp. 624, 629; n. 16).

## Key results

- **The eight desiderata (pp. 616–621).** (1) meaning and reference/truth;
  (2) what makes expressions meaningful; (3) relativity to representational
  system; (4) compositionality; (5) meaning and mind/brain; (6) autonomous
  versus inherited meaning; (7) knowing, learning and using; (8) why
  narrow and wide meaning bear differently on reference and explanation.
- **Narrow and wide individuation (pp. 619–625).** Narrow groups tokens that
  attribute the same properties by the same descriptions; wide groups those
  that attribute them to the same individuals. Narrow meaning is better for
  explaining decisions; wide meaning predicts where world–behaviour laws
  hold regardless of description. The tour-bus variance argument supports an
  individualistic scheme in psychology. Narrow meaning may be an "aspect or
  determinant" of meaning rather than a kind of meaning; the "fallacy of
  subtraction" charge is "merely verbal" (p. 626).
- **CRS (pp. 627–635).** Conceptual role is abstracted causal role,
  illustrated for the material conditional by three inference tendencies;
  the "crucial question" is what counts as same and different role, faced
  with the collateral-information problem (pp. 628–629). Social meaning
  (Burge's arthritis) goes to the referential factor or a third, social
  factor (p. 630). Field's conditional-probability version is set aside:
  Bayesian change of degree of belief cannot model generation of new
  hypotheses (pp. 630–631).
- **Two factors or one (pp. 635–639).** Harman's long-arm roles need normal
  context, experts and borrowed-reference chains; chop off the external part
  and the difference from two-factor theory is "merely verbal".
- **Meaning and reference (pp. 643–644).** The conceptual role of names
  makes the causal theory of reference true (Kripke's Moses intuitions are
  facts about how names function), so conceptual role determines the nature
  of the referential factor, and the function from context to reference,
  without determining reference.
- **Learning (pp. 644–648).** CRS explains why evidence of correct use is
  evidence of knowing meaning, and rejects premise 2 of "Fodor's Paradox"
  (learning a word is hypothesising a definition in known terms), so it
  avoids the conclusion that 'meson' and 'zipper' are innate. Learning
  Newtonian mechanics: no definitions, only use.
- **Meaningfulness and the competitors (pp. 649–660).** Non-reductionist
  theories "put off the semantic issue". Gricean theories need a
  naturalistic account of intentional content; Searle's "equivalent causal
  powers" is not one, and the Loar–Schiffer version is as empirically exposed
  as CRS if there is a language of thought. Fodor refutes Dretske–Stampe;
  Fodor's own theory fails on epistemic idealisation (Riemannian space,
  charmed quarks need the right theory, not only rubbed noses) and on
  teleology (a swamp-baby has meaning without evolutionary design; meiotic
  drive shows selection need not aim at truth), and it cannot separate
  nomologically correlated contents (electrical and thermal conductivity).
- **Relativity and pictures (pp. 661–663).** Meaning and syntax are both
  relative to system because both are functional (a reader of odd and one of
  even characters reads "CDAOTG" as CAT and DOG). So a pictorial
  representation can express an abstract concept "so long as the processors
  that act on it ignore the right specificities", against Piaget and
  Premack.
- **Autonomous and inherited meaning; the Chinese room (pp. 664–667).** Both
  are conceptual role, and external roles are inherited from internal ones.
  The systems reply lacks a theory of what would make the system's symbols
  meaningful; CRS supplies one, and distinguishes simulating a Chinese
  speaker's information processing from emulating it. "What makes Searle's
  argument sound so convincing is that it is difficult to imagine a version
  ... that is a genuine instantiation or emulation rather than a mere
  simulation." Searle's target, symbol manipulation, is wider than Block's in
  1978, which was Ramsified commonsense functionalism.
- **Explaining behaviour by functional states is not circular (pp. 667–669)**,
  as genes and reinforcers show: a web of effects, and independent
  identification of realisers.

## Concepts

- **Conceptual role** — "total causal role, abstractly described", limited
  to relations mediating inference, decision making "and the like" (p. 628).
- **Narrow / wide meaning** — individuation by description versus by
  referent (p. 619); narrow is "in the head" in the sense of supervening on
  physical constitution (p. 620).
- **Autonomous / inherited meaning** — meaning that needs no translation into
  the language of thought, versus meaning that does (p. 617).
- **Short-arm / long-arm roles** — roles stopping at the skin, versus
  Harman's roles reaching into objects and community (p. 636).
- **Standard association** — the mechanical mapping between English and
  mentalese expressions set up by the language module (p. 632).

## Bearing on the record

- [NOTE-117](NOTE-117.md)'s counter-model to Toon is functional role semantics as Block
  states it here and in [LIT-625](../literature.d/LIT-625.md). Drayson needs only that CRS can let
  mental representations take content from social use; p. 630 offers that
  through the referential factor or a third, social factor, but does not
  develop it.
- For the record's AI-consciousness readings ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md), [NOTE-052](NOTE-052.md)), p. 635 is
  a philosopher holding functionalism for intentional states while denying
  it for experience, the split Butlin et al. do not consider.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- No identity conditions for conceptual roles; the collateral-information
  problem is posed and left (pp. 628–629). The paper says so: it is an
  advertisement.
- The normative-versus-actual question about roles is raised and set aside
  (p. 631).
- The social factor is mentioned, not built.
- Arguments against the competitors are compressed; Fodor's theory is
  criticised in an unpublished version (n. 61).

## Open questions

- What makes two conceptual roles the same, and can a similarity gradient do
  the work of identity for psychological explanation?
- Is the "small part" of conceptual role that fixes the function from
  context to reference (p. 670) the same for all natural-kind terms, as Block
  tentatively suggests?
