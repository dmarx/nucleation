# NOTE-tmptk2u9: Biological organisation as closure of constraints

## Contribution

Autopoiesis and Rosen's closure to efficient causation had said organisms
are organisationally closed, but not which entities close, at what level, or
how closure differs from any causal cycle. The paper gives closure an
explicit definition in terms of constraints, picked out by time-scale
symmetries, and states conditions (direct dependence; strictness) that
separate it from a cycle of processes and from looser mutual benefit.

## Key insight

Split the causes acting in an organism into what is consumed or transformed
(processes) and what shapes the transformation while staying the same at
that time scale (constraints). Organisms are the systems whose constraints
are themselves produced, at other time scales, by processes other
constraints of the same system shape. That mutual production among
constraints is closure, and it is a different causal regime from the
thermodynamic flow it rides on.

## Assumptions

- **Symmetry as the criterion of a constraint.** Conditions I–II of
  Definition 1 can be met "after (properly justified) quantitative
  approximations" (footnote 9): a constraint is only approximately
  conserved.
- **Time scale is decisive; spatial scale and level of description are not,**
  "on a first approximation" (footnotes 15–16 allow exceptions, including
  strong emergence, left for another paper).
- **Constraints are mostly limiting at long time scales and generative at
  biological ones;** this is how the paper reconciles itself with Juarrero.
- **Direct dependence is required** for closure, justified by the degree of
  integration of biological systems; "we do not provide in this paper a
  theoretical justification for this claim" (§4).
- **Closure is context-dependent:** realised relative to boundary conditions,
  including external constraints (C5 in the §5 diagram).

## Key results

- **Definition 1 (constraint), §3.** C constrains A → B at τ iff (I) A → B
  and A_C → B_C are asymmetric with respect to B at τ, and (II) the aspects
  of C responsible are temporally symmetric with respect to A_C → B_C at τ.
  Hence no flow of matter or free energy from C to the process at τ; the
  heart's "pumping" is re-described as its architecture constraining the
  conversion of cardiomyocyte chemical energy into blood kinetic energy.
- **Definitions 2–3 (dependence), §4.** C1 depends on C2 if C2 constrains,
  at τ2, a process producing aspects of C1 relevant at τ1; directly if no
  later process at τ2 maintains that aspect. Slow (τ2 > τ1) and fast
  (τ1 > τ2) dependence both occur; alkaline phosphatase is the fast example.
- **Definition 4 (closure), §5.** Overall closure: every member depends
  directly on another member and is generative for another. Strict: not
  splittable into two closed sets. A closed set must contain at least one
  constraint with τs − τd > 0 and one with τs − τd < 0. Constraints subject
  to closure "constitute biological functions".
- **Closure versus cycles (footnote 25).** In the hydrologic cycle entities
  transform into each other but "do not act as constraints on each other".
- **Boundaries (§6).** Lower bound: ascribe closure only to maximally closed
  systems in the available description. Indirect mutual dependence (the
  hunting/cooking group) gives overall but not strict closure.
- **Tendency to closure (§6.1).** K(V) counts dependencies between
  constraints subject to closure located in volume V; δK(V, l) by spatial
  scale l peaks within each organism and collapses at its edge (Figure 1,
  two toy cells sharing two functions at a larger scale). Conjectured to be
  higher for a unicellular eukaryote than a metazoan cell, for a bacterium
  than a biofilm.
- **Levels (§6.2).** Individual cells fail both conditions of Definition 1
  with respect to the organism's processes (negligible effect; symmetries at
  larger scales), so cell and organism closures separate while nesting. Two
  closures that are separated and hierarchically nested are two levels of
  organisation.
- **Invariance and variation (§7).** Closure is invariant over any
  sufficiently long interval while the constraints realising it vary.

## Concepts

- **Constraint.** An entity that changes a process while its relevant aspects
  are conserved through that process at the process's time scale.
- **Dependence / generative.** C1 depends on C2 if C2 constrains the
  production of C1's relevant aspects; C2 is then generative for C1.
- **Closure (overall, strict).** As Definition 4.
- **Maximally closed system.** The set of all mutually dependent constraints
  in the available description.
- **Tendency to closure.** δK(V, l), the gain in constraint dependencies per
  increment of volume at spatial scale l.

## Connections

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-536](../literature.d/LIT-536.md) (Biological Autonomy).** This reading stands in for the book's
  chapter 1, with the caveat in the LIT note: the paper refers to the book
  for whether closure defines organisms.
- **The flame.** The paper does not mention flames. My reading, not the
  paper's: Definition 4 needs every member to be both dependent and
  generative within the set, so a system whose only constraint is one
  macroscopic pattern (the 2009 paper's description of a flame) cannot
  realise closure; whether a flame has more than one constraint at distinct
  time scales is an empirical question the paper does not take up. This is
  the check [NOTE-093](NOTE-093.md) asked for (Tessler, bacteria, candle flame), still
  undone.
- **Baedke ([LIT-167](../literature.d/LIT-167.md)).** §6.1–6.2 is the paper's answer to the holobiont
  objection; [NOTE-148](NOTE-148.md) records that Baedke draws his overcomer contrast with
  "Montévil et al. 2016", not with this paper.
- **Markov blankets ([LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md); [LIT-tmpxq8pk](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxq8pk.md); [LIT-tmpumhsc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpumhsc.md)).** A rival
  boundary criterion. Raja et al. argue blankets cannot represent a boundary
  the system produces; closure is defined by production among constraints.
  No paper in the record compares the two on the same system.
- **THEORY suggested** (reported, not filed): organisms are separated from
  dissipative structures by closure among multiple constraints at distinct
  time scales (with differentiation, per [LIT-tmp9gfzl](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9gfzl.md)), not by
  far-from-equilibrium self-maintenance alone.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- No real organism, cell or protocell is described as a closed network; the
  examples are fragments and toy diagrams.
- Direct dependence is required without justification, by the authors'
  admission.
- The tendency-to-closure measure is a count with no stated way to identify
  or weight dependencies, applied only to Figure 1.
- Closure is not claimed to be necessary and sufficient for life.

## Open questions

- Does any non-living system (a flame, an autocatalytic set, a
  Belousov–Zhabotinsky medium, Friston's simulated soup) realise strict
  closure under Definitions 1–4?
- Can δK(V, l) be computed for a real symbiosis, and does it agree with the
  boundaries biologists already draw?
