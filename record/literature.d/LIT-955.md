# LIT-955: Tangled webs: A practical investigation of graph tangles.

Michal Salter-Duke (2024), *PhD thesis, Te Herenga Waka—Victoria University of Wellington*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpv7vus](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpv7vus.md).*

- **What a tangle is.** For an order k, a choice of side for every cut of
  weight below k, such that no three chosen "small sides" cover the graph
  and no "big side" is a single vertex. It points to a region without
  listing its vertices. For each k a graph has either such a tangle or a
  carving of width below k, not both (tangle–tree duality).
- **An algorithm.** Cuts are enumerated in order of weight
  (Vazirani–Yannakakis) and oriented by a pruned search. Four proved
  reductions (hanging trees, small forests, interchangeable leaves) make
  graphs of about 200 vertices feasible in under a day. It remains
  exponential.
- **Communities.** Tangle communities resemble those of standard methods
  where community structure is strongly delineated (yeast PPI), and not
  where it is weak: the karate club, C. elegans and college football show
  one tangle per order at every order reached. Similarity falls as order
  rises past a network's best order, and each region seems to have its
  own best order.
- **Trivial tangles.** A vertex of degree at least θ that low-order cuts
  can separate is a tangle of order θ. Groups of one to three vertices
  recur, often at a network's periphery.
- **Images.** In three-shade greyscale a one-pixel-wide line cannot be
  distinguished by any tangle, except at the image's edge or corner. One
  tangle picks out the Mona Lisa's eye. Higher colour resolution pushes
  meaningful tangles beyond the orders that can be computed.
- **A boundary needs a further rule.** Vertices are assigned to a
  tangle's community if they lie on the big side of every distinguishing
  cut, with inheritance from lower orders. That rule is the author's.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md), and
read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmpv7vus](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpv7vus.md). The seed note called it a rival
criterion for when a combined form is one system and where its boundary
lies. The thesis tests that criterion and finds it wanting in practice.
Tangles see strongly delineated regions. They leave weak structure
undivided, pass single high-degree vertices, and give no boundary without
an extra assignment rule. [THEORY-tmpaf2sz](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpaf2sz.md) is filed from it. For [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)
it shows that a single global threshold of strength suits some regions and
not others.
