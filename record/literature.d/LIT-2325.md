summary: >-
  Khatua, Zhu et al. (2026), CooperBench: Why Coding Agents Cannot be Your
  Teammates Yet. CooperBench gives two coding agents, each in its own
  environment, two features on the same repository state that are logically
  compatible but touch overlapping code; they can message each other in real
  time, and success requires their merged patches to pass both features'
  tests. 652 tasks from 12 libraries in 4 languages. Against a "Solo"
  baseline (one agent does both features), cooperating agents do markedly
  worse ("curse of coordination"); the gap is largest at middle difficulty
  and grows with more agents. A communication tool reduces merge conflicts
  but does not raise success. Failures trace to three capability gaps:
  communication, commitment and expectation (modelling the partner). Read
  for the exchange "luria experiment design".
