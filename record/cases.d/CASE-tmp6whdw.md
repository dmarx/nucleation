# CASE-tmp6whdw: Tatiana's letter in Eugene Onegin III: вы to ты and back, lost in Kline and half kept in Spalding

Found on 2026-10-10 by a search the owner asked for: a documented case of
the half of a change of cover that [CASE-tmpxnd3y](CASE-tmpxnd3y.md) lacks, a question the
source decides that a rendering makes undecidable, for [CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md).

## The case

Pushkin, *Евгений Онегин*, chapter III, the letter between stanzas XXXI and
XXXII. Russian text from ilibrary.ru, https://ilibrary.ru/text/436/p.4/index.html
(fetched 2026-10-10).

- **Opening, вы.** «Я к вам пишу — чего же боле? … Вы не оставите меня.»
- **The break, ты.** «То воля неба: я твоя; / Вся жизнь моя была залогом /
  Свиданья верного с тобой; / Я знаю, ты мне послан богом». The ты runs on
  through the dream passage («Ты в сновиденьях мне являлся») and «Кто ты,
  мой ангел ли хранитель».
- **The return, вы.** «Кончаю! Страшно перечесть… / Стыдом и страхом
  замираю… / Но мне порукой ваша честь, / И смело ей себя вверяю…»

The letter never says that she changes address. The pronouns alone carry
it. So the Russian decides two relational questions: at what point she
drops propriety, and whether she takes it back at the end.

**A rendering that loses both.** A. S. Kline, *Eugene Onegin* (2009),
https://www.poetryintranslation.com/PITBR/Russian/Onegin3.php. Opening: "I
know it's in your power today / To punish me with your derision." At the
break: "It's Heaven's will: I am yours." At the end: "On you alone I must
depend / Boldly trusting in your honour…". Kline's letter has no "thou",
"thee", "thy" or "thine" (checked by search of the page text).

**A rendering that keeps the break and loses the return.** Henry Spalding,
*Eugene Onéguine* (London: Macmillan, 1881), Project Gutenberg #23997,
https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/23997. Opening: "I write to you! Is more
required?", with "you" through the first part. At the break: "'Tis Heaven's
will and I am thine. … I feel thou wast by God sent down". At the end: "But
boldly I myself resign: / Thine honour is my countersign!". Spalding's
"Thine" stands where the Russian has «ваша».

**Readers of the source on the switches.** M. Naydorf, «Наблюдения над
смыслом Письма Татьяны к Онегину»,
https://sites.google.com/site/marknaydorftexts/kulturologiceskij-analiz/nabludenia-nad-smyslom-pisma-tatany-k-oneginu,
an undated, unrefereed essay that cites Lotman's commentary: «Татьяна
переходит на «ты»… обращается, собственно, не к Онегину, которого знает
очень мало, а к герою своей девической мечты», and at the end «Татьяна как
бы возвращается от «воображения» к «реальности», вновь переходит на «вы»».
Russian school analyses seen only as search snippets call the ты "резкий,
внезапный … неосознанный" and say "под конец она опять переходит на «вы»";
**unverified**, and evidence of how the text is taught, not scholarship.

**The value of English "thou".** Brown and Gilman (1960), p. 253: "The
pronoun 'thou' is reserved, nowadays, to prayer and naive poetry". Beside
"by God sent down" and "Guardian angel", Spalding's "thou" reads as
devotional to a modern reader, which lies nearer Naydorf's dream-hero
reading than to plain intimacy.

**Why Pushkin's «Ты и вы» does not serve.** The lead given to the search was
Pushkin's lyric «Ты и вы» (1828): «Пустое вы сердечным ты / Она, обмолвясь,
заменила». The poem names its pronouns, so every rendering has to keep the
switch explicit; Givental and Wilson-Egolf
(https://math.berkeley.edu/~giventh/verse/thou.pdf): "Sweet thou for an
empty you / She humorously substituted". No rendering can make the question
"did she change her address?" undecidable without dropping the poem's
subject. What English endangers there is the *value* of the poles: "thou"
is now the elevated form, so a modern reader can take the direction of the
slip backwards. That is distortion of an answer to a question both sides
can decide, which [CLAIM-tmpx3m7e](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpx3m7e.md) measures apart, not a loss of cover. The
letter is the better case because it never names the pronouns.

## What it can show

The half of a change of cover that [CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md) calls "the source decides
what the target cannot", which [CASE-tmpxnd3y](CASE-tmpxnd3y.md) does not supply and which
rested until now on [CASE-010](CASE-010.md). The question is of the claim's own kind, the
speaker–addressee relation. On Kline's text "At which line does she drop
formality?" and "Does she take it back at the end?" have no textual
answer; on the Russian both have one. No other question's answers need
move: the words of the letter are close in all three texts. The three
texts also make a graded set, as [CLAIM-tmpx3m7e](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpx3m7e.md) wants: the Russian decides
both questions, Spalding the first only, Kline neither.

## What it cannot show

- **Two conditions are met only partly or not at all.** The texts are
  attested, and the renderings that lose the switches are read directly.
  But the attestation that the source decides the question for its readers
  is one essayist and unchecked school notes; Lotman's commentary and
  Nabokov's, the obvious authorities, were not read. And there is no
  evidence at all about English readers: that Kline's readers cannot answer
  is inferred from the text, not measured. The claim's test is reader
  agreement above a threshold, and neither side's agreement is measured.
- **The strongest reply.** The letter is itself framed as a translation.
  Tatiana writes in French («Итак, писала по-французски»), and the narrator
  calls his Russian «Неполный, слабый перевод» (Spalding: "A feeble version
  read below"). So the вы/ты switch is the narrator's rendering of a
  vous/tu switch, and Russian readers are reading a text that announces it
  is already a rendering. A defender could add that context decides the
  intimacy anyway: "I am yours" and the passion around it tell an English
  reader where she lets go, so the pronoun is redundant at the break. The
  first point does not touch the case, since what is compared is the
  Russian and the English, each as its readers have it. The second is the
  better objection, and it is why the return is the cleaner item: "trusting
  in your honour" does not mark a retreat in English, and only «ваша»
  marks it in Russian.
- **What would close the gap.** The reader test the search report sketches,
  which could be items in the discriminating study ([CASE-tmp1uzfn](CASE-tmp1uzfn.md)). Ask
  Russian readers of the source, and matched English readers of Kline and of
  Spalding: "Does her attitude to Onegin change in the last four lines? If
  so, how?" and "At which line does she become most intimate?". The case
  supports the claim if Russian readers agree above the threshold fixed in
  advance (a return to formality; intimacy at «я твоя»), Kline's readers
  fall below it on both, and Spalding's agree on the second question and
  not the first. It is the reply's case if Kline's readers agree on the
  second question from context alone.
- **It is not contextuality,** as [CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md) says of any change of cover,
  and it measures no distortion.
