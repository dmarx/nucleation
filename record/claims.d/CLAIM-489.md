# CLAIM-489: A sign's communicative significance is fixed by its contrasts within a system rather than by correspondence to a referent

## The claim

This is [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md) as first filed. [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md) granted, on reading Saussure,
that "purely differential" is said of signifier and signified taken apart,
that the sign as a whole is a positive fact, and that Saussure does not set
value against reference. [ARG-034](../arguments.d/ARG-034.md) holds the inference from that reading to
this title Rejected. [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md) now states only what the reading carries:
value, as distinct from signification, is fixed by contrast.

Two parts are left here. The first is that significance in use, not only
value in the language system, is fixed by contrast. [CLAIM-242](CLAIM-242.md) and [ARG-033](../arguments.d/ARG-033.md)
take that up. The second is that contrast works instead of reference.
[CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s body already qualified it from [LIT-809](../literature.d/LIT-809.md) and [LIT-860](../literature.d/LIT-860.md): contrast
together with reference, not contrast instead of it.

## Why it was Proposed

Its source in the manuscript is Lévi-Strauss ([LIT-775](../literature.d/LIT-775.md)), which the record
has not read. The reading it once leaned on, Saussure's, does not carry it.

## Note of 2026-10-29: rejected on its own condition

Its defeat condition is met by a reading the record holds. In Bergen,
Goodman and Levy's Experiment 1 ([THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md), Active; [NOTE-613](../notes.d/NOTE-613.md)), a novel
symbol is read as the unnamed object only through its contrast with an
alternative tied to the other object by resemblance: "the contrast works
only because one alternative is iconically tied to an object." Contrast did
its work through the correspondence the title says it replaces. What
survives is [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s narrowed claim about value, and [CLAIM-242](CLAIM-242.md)'s
use-level counterpart, which sets contrast beside reference rather than
against it.
