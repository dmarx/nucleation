# CLAIM-tmpx4kjt: The upstream provenance of a failure can be carried without blame: an investigator barred from assigning blame or liability traced the Tempe operator's distraction to her company's safety culture and the state's oversight and addressed its recommendations to them, so blame is one carrier of upstream provenance and not its only source

## The claim

The owner's U47 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse": ""blame" gives
us provenance for holding upstream actors accountable as well"
(CLAIM-733). The owner's U48 then proposed blameless culture as a case.
Blameless practice tries to keep what the owner's sentence credits to
blame, the trace upstream, by causal tracing alone: "contributing causes",
in the SRE chapter's words, "without indicting any individual or team".
If that works anywhere, blame is one carrier of provenance and not its
source.

It works in one documented case. The NTSB's report on the Tempe crash
(CASE-292, read in NOTE-2034):

- **No blame, by rule.** "The NTSB does not assign fault or blame for an
  accident or incident"; its investigations "are not conducted for the
  purpose of determining the rights, liabilities, or blame of any person or
  entity" (49 CFR 831.4(c)).
- **No liability, by statute.** "No part of a report of the Board ... may
  be admitted into evidence or used in a civil action for damages
  resulting from a matter mentioned in the report" (49 U.S.C. § 1154(b),
  read in NOTE-tmp45cjg).
- **Upstream all the same.** The probable cause is the operator's
  distraction; contributing are Uber ATG's risk assessment, oversight of
  operators and lack of countermeasures to automation complacency, "all a
  consequence of its inadequate safety culture", and the Arizona
  Department of Transportation's "insufficient oversight". The culture
  contributed "specifically to the vehicle operator's extended
  distraction" (pp. v, 46).
- **Repair upstream.** Its recommendations (p. 60) go to NHTSA, the state
  of Arizona, the American Association of Motor Vehicle Administrators and
  Uber ATG ("Complete the implementation of a safety management system").
  None goes to the operator.

So the provenance of the operator's incapacity, in TERM-127's causal and
constitutive senses, was traced to the company and the state, and the
remedial question, who should act, was answered upward, by a body that
could blame no one. ARG-tmp67frz sets out the inference and its critical
questions.

## Its relation to CLAIM-733

Proposed as a qualification of CLAIM-733, it turned out not to deny
anything CLAIM-733 states. CLAIM-733's own "What it does not say" already
has "Not that provenance is only a matter of blame. Formation and
maintenance can be traced descriptively". What this claim adds is a
documented instance at the level of an institution, with the conditions
that made it work: an investigator independent of the parties, forbidden
to blame, and fenced off from liability. Hence `complements`, not
`objects_to`. CLAIM-733 says what blame can carry; this says what can
carry it without blame.

Cook (THEORY-tmpl5x2t) gives a reason to expect the two to differ in
practice. Root-cause attribution, he argues, reflects "the social, cultural
need to blame specific, localized forces or events", so blame tends to
stop near the event. That is no evidence against CLAIM-733, which is about
blame traced upstream, but it is a reason to doubt that blame is usually
the carrier that reaches there.

## What it does not say

- **That CLAIM-733 holds only where no independent investigator exists.**
  That was the stronger form proposed. One specimen shows that blame is not
  the only carrier. It does not show that, where such an investigator
  exists, blame adds nothing: blame may still be what makes an upstream
  actor act on the trace, which is this claim's second defeater.
- **That internal blameless reviews reach upstream.** The NTSB is outside
  the organizations it investigates. Whether a review commissioned by the
  organization reaches above its own commissioners is CLAIM-tmp5g7xc's
  question, and the claims complement each other.
- **That no blame fell on the operator.** The company suspended her (NOTE-2034,
  p. 27); prosecution and press are outside the report and unread.
- **That the NTSB's language is neutral.** "Inadequate" and "insufficient"
  are evaluations. What the Board withholds is the assignment of fault and
  liability, not judgement of practices.
