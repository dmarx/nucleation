# LIT-tmpyhay6: Pervasive negative effects of rewards on intrinsic motivation: The myth continues

Judy Cameron, Katherine M. Banko and W. David Pierce (2001), *The Behavior Analyst* 24(1):1–44 — DOI-10.1007/BF03392017

## Key takeaways

*From the PubMed abstract, not a reading.*

- Its stated purpose is "to resolve differences in previous meta-analytic
  findings" between Cameron and Pierce (1994, [LIT-tmp3x51d](LIT-tmp3x51d.md)), who found
  negative effects "limited and could be easily prevented in applied
  settings", and Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999, [LIT-tmp58ot3](LIT-tmp58ot3.md)), who show
  "pervasive negative effects of reward".
- Its results, as the abstract states them: "in general, rewards are not
  harmful to motivation to perform a task"; rewards for low-interest tasks
  enhance free-choice intrinsic motivation; on high-interest tasks verbal
  rewards have positive effects; "Negative effects are found on
  high-interest tasks when the rewards are tangible, expected (offered
  beforehand), and loosely tied to level of performance"; when rewards are
  linked to level of performance, intrinsic motivation increases or does
  not differ from control.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the rejoinder to Deci,
Koestner and Ryan ([LIT-tmp58ot3](LIT-tmp58ot3.md)) in the dispute over whether rewards
undermine intrinsic motivation. It is `compared_against` that paper: the
abstract says the new meta-analysis was made to resolve the differences
between its findings and Deci et al.'s, which is a comparison the authors
ran. Its relation to the authors' own 1994 analysis ([LIT-tmp3x51d](LIT-tmp3x51d.md)) is left
undeclared until it is read.

`Deferred` because it was not read. Nothing in the record should lean on it
beyond its abstract. On the abstract's own terms, the two analyses agree
that expected tangible rewards loosely tied to performance undermine
interest in an interesting task; they disagree on whether that effect is
pervasive or circumscribed.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
