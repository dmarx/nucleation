# LIT-tmp2v8qs: Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self

Seth, A. K. (2013). Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 17(11), 565–573. DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2013.09.007.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpi27ob](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi27ob.md).*

- **Emotion as inference.** Felt emotion is "the integrated content" of
  top-down predictions of interoceptive signals across levels, and
  "emotional content is determined by active inference on the likely
  internal and external causes of changes in the physiological condition
  of the body" (p. 567). This is offered as a generalisation of appraisal
  theories, from James and Lange to Schachter and Singer.
- **Against bottom-up accounts.** Damasio, Craig and Critchley ground the
  self in representations of the body too, but "none identify emotional
  states with top-down inference" (p. 569). Their hierarchy is "largely
  bottom-up".
- **Active interoceptive inference.** Descending predictions act as set
  points for autonomic reflexes, as proprioceptive predictions do for
  motor reflexes in active-inference motor control. This requires
  lowering the precision of, or attention to, interoceptive prediction
  errors (p. 567, Figure 2).
- **The anterior insula as comparator.** The anterior insula is proposed
  as comparator, on indirect evidence: anticipation and prediction-error
  responses in fMRI, and false cardiac feedback that raises both its
  activity and the emotional salience of stimuli (pp. 567–569). The
  article says plainly that there is "not yet any direct confirmatory
  evidence" (p. 567).
- **Body ownership.** The rubber hand illusion is read as revision of a
  multisensory self-model to cut precision-weighted prediction error, and
  expecting correlated input is enough to induce it. People with lower
  heartbeat-detection sensitivity are more susceptible to it. A virtual
  hand flashing in time with the heartbeat is owned more, by
  questionnaire and by proprioceptive drift, unless finger movements are
  mapped to it. A whole-body version gave the same result (pp. 569–570,
  Figure 3).
- **Psychopathology (Box 3).** Depersonalization is proposed to come from
  imprecise interoceptive predictions, and Cotard's delusion from
  explaining away persistent interoceptive error.

## Standing in the record

Filed and read on 2026-10-19 at the owner's request, to fill the record's
gap around the rubber hand illusion and bodily self-consciousness. It is
the work that joins the record's interoception cluster to body ownership.
It was filed with Botvinick & Cohen (1998), Lenggenhager et al. (2007),
Blanke & Metzinger (2009) and Apps & Tsakiris (2014), all four of which
it cites or anticipates (Apps & Tsakiris as the 2013 online version).

Where it meets the record, set out in the NOTE:

- **Seth's later view** ([LIT-135](LIT-135.md), [NOTE-169](../notes.d/NOTE-169.md)). The 2024 case for biological
  naturalism starts from interoceptive predictive processing. This is that
  view's 2013 statement, about emotion and self and not yet about
  consciousness in general.
- **Felt affect** ([THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)). It takes the prediction side of the open
  question [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md) leaves, prediction or map. It names Damasio's
  account ([LIT-388](LIT-388.md), which it cites) as bottom-up, and it cites the
  false-feedback evidence [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)'s promotion test asks for, at second
  hand. Barrett's later account ([LIT-542](LIT-542.md), [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)) cites it.
- **The self-model** ([LIT-450](LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md), [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)). It gives
  Metzinger's body-ownership claim ([NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md), C6) a mechanism and an
  interoceptive route. It also gives a self-model that includes the
  body's interior, which Metzinger's article names only as an unconscious
  layer.
- **Active inference** ([LIT-1181](LIT-1181.md), [NOTE-960](../notes.d/NOTE-960.md)). It carries Friston's
  account of motor control to autonomic control.
