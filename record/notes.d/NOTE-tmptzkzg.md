# NOTE-tmptzkzg: Martela and Ryan (2019) — Distinguishing between basic psychological needs and basic wellness enhancers

## Contribution

- A beneficence-frustration scale, "the feeling that one's behaviors and
  actions have caused harm to others" (p. 117), four items, separable by
  factor analysis from beneficence satisfaction and from the three need
  frustrations.
- The first test of the frustration criterion for a candidate need.
- A conceptual distinction between a basic need and a "basic wellness
  enhancer", set beside self-determination theory's older distinction of
  deficit needs (pp. 130–131): a deficit need such as safety protects
  against ill-being without enhancing growth; a wellness enhancer does the
  reverse.

## Key results

- **Criteria (pp. 117–118).** Two that a candidate "must minimally meet":
  its satisfaction uniquely enhances well-being, its frustration uniquely
  harms it. Further criteria include being "a predictor of wellness rather
  than an indicator", and frustration is "an active thwarting" rather than
  a lack of satisfaction.
- **The satisfaction criterion is taken as already met** by Martela and
  Ryan 2016b: "beneficence as a candidate need has passed one crucial test"
  (p. 117).
- **Study 1 (pp. 121–122).** Two factors, satisfaction and frustration,
  correlating −.462; the frustration items are "reverse-scored". Frustration
  correlates −.71 with self-esteem, which the authors find "surprisingly
  high".
- **Study 2 (pp. 123–126).** Beneficence frustration correlates .81 with
  competence frustration, "noteworthy given that the items measuring these
  constructs don't have much content overlap" (p. 124). Entered last, it
  adds almost nothing (ΔR² ≤ .003 for six of seven outcomes). The exception,
  a positive relation to meaning in life, is read as "a suppression effect
  likely due to multicollinearity" (pp. 125–126).
- **Footnote 2 (p. 124).** Without competence frustration as a control,
  beneficence frustration does predict several outcomes: "it is especially
  competence frustration that accounts for the insignificant relations".
- **Study 3 (pp. 126–128).** The same null for negative affect and
  situational depression and anxiety, about "the single unhappiest event"
  of the past two weeks; full mediation by the three needs.
- **Conclusion (p. 128).** "in no case was it independently connected to any
  well-being or ill-being indicator when controlling for the contribution
  of the three basic need frustrations". Of the three established needs,
  "all three of them seem to be independently connected" (p. 129).
- **Three explanations (p. 129).** A design fault, judged unlikely;
  self-deception about harming others, which would make the self-reports
  unreliable; or effects running through the three needs.
- **The proposal (pp. 130–131).** Wellness enhancers are "universal
  conditions for improving human thriving, the satisfaction of which should
  lead to more optimal development … But their frustration in life might
  not directly make that life miserable." Beneficence "might be better
  considered a basic wellness enhancer" (p. 130); "we tentatively propose"
  the distinction (p. 131); "it is premature to make any conclusions of
  whether beneficence should be seen as a wellness enhancer" (p. 130).
- **Limitations stated (p. 130).** Cross-sectional self-report, and
  "participants were from the United States"; the remedy named includes
  "manipulating beneficence frustration".

## Assumptions

- The established frustration scales validly measure the three needs'
  frustration.
- Incremental variance in cross-sectional self-report regressions is the
  test of the frustration criterion, with the established needs entered
  first.
- Beneficence frustration is having harmed others, not being prevented
  from helping; the alternative is named (p. 129).

## Concepts

Basic psychological need; basic wellness enhancer; deficit need versus
growth need; need satisfaction versus need frustration; incremental
validity; full mediation; suppression effect.

## Bearing on the record

- **On the record's characterization.** NOTE-440's second-hand account is
  right in substance, with two corrections (filed as a dated note on
  NOTE-440): the paper's term is "basic wellness enhancer", and the classing
  is a tentative proposal the authors call premature, not a verdict. The
  LIT's "frustration adds no variance" is right for Studies 2–3. The
  search-result abstract in LIT-1983's history (version 2) is confirmed.
- **QUESTION-369**: a worked instance of the question's circularity, not an
  answer. Need-hood is decided by incremental self-reported ill-being, and
  the three needs win partly by entry order. Footnote 2 shows the null rests
  on controlling competence frustration, which correlates .81 with
  beneficence frustration, so "adds no variance" cannot tell "derived from
  competence" from "shares measurement with competence". The paper names
  the remedy the question asks for, manipulation, and does not attempt it;
  NOTE-tmpqf55c (the authors' 2021 experiments) does.
- **LEAD-127**: correlational, with no functional deficit; it does not meet
  "what would count".
- **NOTE-1502** (Sheldon et al.): consistent; there too competence carried
  the unique variance, and here competence frustration absorbs beneficence
  frustration.
- **THEORY-052**: corroborates its premise that each of the three
  frustrations predicts ill-being, on cross-sectional self-report of the
  kind its promote_when excludes. Not promotion evidence.
- **NOTE-1530** and **NOTE-1515**: the negatively keyed, reverse-scored
  frustration items and a null that turns on a .81 correlation between two
  frustration scales are the measurement worries those notes hold.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional, self-report, MTurk, US only, as the authors say. LIT-1980
  is registered as a Central European test.
- **Entry order is asserted, not argued.** Beneficence is entered last, so
  shared variance is credited to the three needs by design, and mediation
  with beneficence as cause presumes a direction a cross-sectional design
  cannot show.
- **Collinearity is not taken up as a rival reading.** The two frustration
  scales may share a negatively keyed self-evaluation factor (compare the
  −.71 with self-esteem); no latent or bifactor model is tried.
- **Asymmetric standards.** Earlier satisfaction findings are accepted,
  while the frustration null is offered as possibly the measure's fault.
- **The new category is defined by the null** it is introduced to explain.
- Appendix 1's items were not seen in this copy.
