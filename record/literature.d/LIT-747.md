# LIT-747: Responsibility, Moral and Otherwise

Susan Wolf (2015), "Responsibility, Moral and Otherwise", *Inquiry*
58(2):127–142 — DOI-10.1080/0020174X.2015.986852. Given as the Routledge
Lecture in Philosophy, Cambridge, 21 February 2013; read in the author's
typescript.

## Key takeaways

- **Two distinctions, not one** (p. 2). The familiar contrast between
  causal and moral responsibility "seems to me to blend two apparently
  independent distinctions together": merely causal vs deep, and moral vs
  nonmoral. Quality-of-will accounts are right about moral blame but have
  "nothing to say" about deep responsibility for nonmoral traits (p. 3).
- **Self-disclosure** (pp. 5–6). One is deeply responsible when the result
  "expresses or reflects" one's "(inner) self"; she takes Watson's name,
  "the self-disclosure view". Love for an artist "qua artist" is as
  reactive as resentment and gratitude (p. 9), and asking whether James
  could have done otherwise than write *The Portrait of a Lady* "seems
  utterly bizarre" (p. 10).
- **Not two levels** (pp. 13–19). Watson's two-level view puts
  attributability first and accountability, which adds control, on top.
  Wolf thinks it "mistaken" (p. 15). Accountability needs only that "(1) he
  acts as he ought not to, (2) he was in a position to know that he ought
  not to so act, and (3) it was up to him whether to act in that way or
  not" (p. 17). Her stipulated psychopath "may be accountable for his
  psychopathic behavior without his psychopathic character being
  attributable to him at all" (p. 18), and it is "not obvious" that the
  ability to choose involved "requires anything metaphysically robust"
  (p. 19).
- **The hunch** (pp. 21–23). The self that is "a fit object of reactive
  attitudes is an 'intelligent' self", one with "the same (or better)
  powers of perception, reason, and imagination that we do". The
  psychopath "can't get it", so the objective attitude fits him. If depth
  survives, it lies in "an intelligent self's unimpeded response to the
  world" (p. 23).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as the lawful Wolf text beside
*Freedom Within Reason* ([LIT-685](LIT-685.md)), which stays Deferred. It is not a
substitute for that book: the lecture does not use "Reason View", "the
True and the Good" or "sanity", and it argues a different question. The
account it supports is [THEORY-147](../theory.d/THEORY-147.md).

It bears on [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md), the Reason View as the record reports it at second
hand. This lecture splits what [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md) treats as one condition. Fitness
for the reactive attitudes needs an intelligent self; accountability, on
Wolf's own stipulated case, does not. Any resemblance between the
intelligent self and the earlier sanity condition is the record's
observation, not a claim the lecture makes.

For the owner's essay, it bears on §8, which opens with Wolf's JoJo. §8.1
distinguishes responsiveness to information from responsiveness to
reasons, and Wolf's intelligent self is a condition on perceiving and
understanding the world, not only on obeying a rule.
