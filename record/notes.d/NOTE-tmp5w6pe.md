# NOTE-tmp5w6pe: Bestwick — The Nationals Should Stop Claiming Expos History

## Contribution

A supporter-writer's argument that the club should claim Washington's baseball tradition since 1901 rather than the Expos'.

## Key results

- **The legacy.** "The legacy of the Washington Nationals goes back not to Canada, but to the rich baseball history" that happened in Washington; "many would attempt to convince us that this legacy belongs to Minnesota or Texas".
- **What the club does.** It has "been awkwardly drawing upon both the Expos and the Senators rather than just picking one and sticking to it"; it honours "Senators greats along with Homestead Grays legends like Josh Gibson and Cool Papa Bell."
- **What he asks.** "harken back instead to the proud Major League tradition that has existed in Washington since 1901." "I'd much rather see a Senators jersey."

## Assumptions

None of the formal kind. Statements by an interested party are taken as
their stated position, not their motive.

## Concepts

Continuation of a club by franchise (legal and statistical succession)
against heritage by place (a city's baseball history).

## Bearing on the record

The strongest supporter claim on the Senators found: their legacy belongs to Washington, against the Twins' and Rangers' claim and against the Expos', but as a city's tradition, with no claim that the Senators' records or 1924 title are the club's. Coded H-SEN, strongly, and D-EX, and not C-SEN (statement 14).

## Limitations

One writer's opinion; it does not report how supporters divide. Its long sentences were returned by the fetch tool in excerpts.
