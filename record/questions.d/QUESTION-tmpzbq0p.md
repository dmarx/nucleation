# QUESTION-tmpzbq0p: Is the response an implementation intention specifies gated by the goal intention it serves, so that with the goal deactivated or weak the cue alone does not produce it, and does the gate survive the plan's being acted on many times?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-904](../theory.d/THEORY-904.md) holds that Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006) conclude, from a
handful of studies, that plans affect performance only with a strong and
activated goal intention, and that the 2024 review found plan effects
larger with strong goal intentions than with weak ones, by a between-test
difference of d = .79 over eight tests. Neither is a test in which the
plan effect is absent with the goal deactivated, in a sample that could
show such a null. And the authors leave open what happens once the plan has
been acted on many times, where a habit could fire without the goal.

This bears on [CLAIM-443](../claims.d/CLAIM-443.md)'s distinction between a prior over actions and a
prior over which process governs: a cached mapping licensed by an active
goal is a third arrangement between them.

## What would answer it

A test that crosses plan against no plan with the goal intention
activated against deactivated, or strong against weak, measured or
manipulated before the cue arrives, in samples large enough for a null in
the weak or deactivated cells, finding the plan effect present only with
the goal; preferably repeated after the plan has been acted on many times,
since repetition is where a cue-triggered response could come loose from
its goal. The gate is refuted if planned responses run as strongly with the
goal deactivated, or if after repetition they fire regardless.
Between-test comparisons of motivation in a meta-analysis cannot settle it.
