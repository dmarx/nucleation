# LIT-2145: Applied nucleation facilitates tropical forest recovery: Lessons learned from a 15-year study

Holl, K. D., Reid, J. L., Cole, R. J., Oviedo-Brenes, F., Rosales, J. A. & Zahawi, R. A. (2020). Applied nucleation facilitates tropical forest recovery: Lessons learned from a 15-year study. *Journal of Applied Ecology* 57(12): 2316–2328. doi:10.1111/1365-2664.13684.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. What follows comes from the works that cite it, not from its
own text.*

- **Canopy.** Schubert et al. (2024) cite it for "plantations have
  significantly greater overall canopy cover" than the other treatments.
- **Minimum nucleus.** Holl et al. (2024) cite it, with Zahawi &
  Augspurger (2006), for "a minimum nuclei size of approximately 64 m2"
  in tropical moist forest.
- **Cost.** Holl et al. (2024) cite it for nucleation's cost scaling to
  the area planted.

## Standing in the record

Unfetched. The 16–18-year census of the same experiment, Schubert,
Zahawi, Oviedo-Brenes, Rosales & Holl (2024), [LIT-2177](LIT-2177.md), read in
[NOTE-1660](../notes.d/NOTE-1660.md), stands in for its long-term recruitment results and goes
past them. It does not stand in for this paper's canopy-cover, seed-rain,
bird or cost data, or for its 15-year figures. The 64 m² minimum is cited
in the record only at second hand (Holl et al. 2024, [LIT-2172](LIT-2172.md)).
