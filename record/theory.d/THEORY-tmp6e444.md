# THEORY-tmp6e444: Between tests, implementation intentions in the contingent if-then format had larger effects than plans that schedule when, where and what without linking cue to response (d = .43 against .29); rehearsing the plan once raised the effect, keeping a copy to revisit lowered it (.31 against .44), and adding how or for how long to a time-and-place cue roughly halved it

## Source

Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024), LIT-1488, read in full in NOTE-1131.
The between-test differences the review measured are THEORY-908, which
this entry extends.

## The claim

The authors read their moderator contrasts as effects of how a plan is
formed. On format, they conclude "that contingent or if-then planning leads
to superior impact on outcomes" (p. 180), because the link "causes
participants not only to become perceptually ready to meet the cue but also
mentally link that cue with the prepared response" (p. 165). On formation,
one rehearsal helps and more adds nothing, and "when participants had the
opportunity to revisit their plans (e.g., because they retained copies),
those plans were less effective" (p. 180), which they read, with the
smaller effect of plans that add how or for how long, as deliberation
undermining automaticity.

What THEORY-908 holds is the contrasts themselves: contingent .43 against
schedule .29, rehearsed .45 against .33, recorded .31 against .44, time
and place .46 against .24 with how or how long added. They come from
different studies, outcomes and settings, no within-study comparison is
pooled, the estimates are not corrected for publication bias, and the
deliberation account was not measured.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, which QUESTION-309 also asks: random assignment of
format and of recording within one design for the same goal, sample and
outcome, and a test of what rehearsal works through. More between-test
moderator analyses cannot answer it.
