# NOTE-343: Rupert — Challenges to the Hypothesis of Extended Cognition

## Contribution

The paper gave the opposition to [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) its standard form. It names the
alternative (the hypothesis of embedded cognition, HEMC). It makes the
burden explicit: HEC must earn its place against HEMC by explanatory power,
because conservatism otherwise favours HEMC. And it tests the claim
against the experimental psychology of memory rather than against
intuitions.

## Key insight

Everything the extended-mind cases show about how heavily thinking leans
on the world can be said by an organism-centred theory that treats the
world as the thing being leaned on. So the extended view must show that a
category spanning brain and notebook does real explanatory work. Memory
research suggests it does the opposite: the differences between remembering
and looking things up are what memory science studies.

## Assumptions

- **HEC is a substantive empirical hypothesis.** The bare possibility of
  extended cognition is granted and set aside (§I). HEC is construed as
  literal mereological extension: cognitive states "literally
  comprise—as wholes do their proper parts—elements in that environment"
  (p. 1).
- **Individual subjects only.** Hutchins's socially distributed cognition is
  excluded (§I).
- **Conservatism.** Other things equal, prefer the hypothesis that needs no
  new ontology (J. pp. 395, 405–06, by the author's outline).
- **Explanatory kinds are those that support induction and explanation in
  science** (note 43). Rupert avoids "natural kind", so that microstructural
  essentialism cannot settle the matter trivially.
- **Memory science is a fair test.** Memory is "a fundamental cognitive
  process, subserving virtually all other important cognitive functions"
  (§V), including Otto's kind of standing belief.

## Key results

- **HEMC versus HEC (§II).** HEMC: cognitive processes "depend very heavily,
  in hitherto unexpected ways, on organismically external props". HEC: the
  unit of analysis is organism plus environment. Inferring HEC from HEMC by
  "epistemological dependence" is invalid: understanding Nazi Germany's
  invasion of Poland needs 1920s German economics, which is not part of the
  invasion.
- **Content externalism is a different thesis (§III).** Neither entails the
  other. Russellian content can seem to put the horse in the belief, but
  McGinn's view shows one can hold worldly constituents of content without
  external mereological parts of the mind. HEC is about "nuts-and-bolts"
  processing, not content.
- **The past-endorsement dilemma (§IV).** Directory assistance satisfies
  [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)'s first three criteria, so without the fourth, Otto believes
  every listed number: "It is absurd". Keep past endorsement, and it is
  arbitrary to require it for extended but not internal belief (Clark and
  Chalmers's own worry), and, worse, it makes internal consciousness "the
  ultimate source of cognitive authority", so the notebook is "best
  accommodated by HEMC". Note 34 adds that the constancy criterion is too
  strong even for internal beliefs. Note 36 answers Lycan's suggested fix
  (the subject caused the marks) and Clark's "personal tailoring".
- **Conversation (§V).** Holding a model of a long conversation needs
  internal working memory (Ericsson and Kintsch's long-term working
  memory). An interlocutor keeping it on paper would "destroy the dynamics
  of normal conversation". Rowlands's view that working memory is mostly
  external fits the data poorly.
- **Negative transfer (§V).** In A-B/A-C paired-associate learning, internal
  memory shows interference. A written list shows none, and no learning
  curve: lists are "learned" at once. "List-losing curves" will not match
  forgetting curves. The differences are in learning time and access time,
  "at the very heart" of memory research.
- **Not malfunction (§VI).** The generation effect (Bobrow and Bower 1969),
  a success of memory, has no external analogue: a subject consulting a
  notebook gets the same answer whoever wrote the context sentence.
- **Generic memory (§VII).** A kind like "has some sort of access to the
  information that P" would cover notebooks. But internal systems share
  power laws of learning and forgetting and Rescorla-Wagner learning that
  external stores lack. Psychologists who split memory into systems tend to
  drop the superordinate kind, not widen it. The man who lives in the
  library does not "know" its contents in any sense that explains his
  behaviour better than an embedded account.
- **Functionalism (§VIII).** A three-premise functionalist argument for HEC
  is valid, but premise 2 (some realisers are external) fails both ways.
  Commonsense functionalism forbids seeing one's own memory ("Yesterday I
  saw my memory of last week's trip to the beach"). Psychofunctionalism
  takes its roles from memory science, whose roles external stores do not
  fill.
- **Cognitive systems (§IX).** Taking extended systems as the objects of
  cognitive science sits badly with AI, which wants self-contained systems
  that work across environments, and with developmental psychology, which
  explains the skills of "a single coherent system, one with historical
  integrity". The conclusion: "HEMC provides the best interpretation" for
  now, and "without the strength of cognitive science's successes
  supporting it, the hypotheses of extended mind and self seem weak
  indeed."

## Concepts

- **HEC** — the hypothesis of extended cognition, "human cognitive
  processing literally extends into the environment".
- **HEMC** — the hypothesis of embedded cognition: heavy, unexpected
  dependence on external props, with the organism as the cognitive system.
- **conservatism** — prefer the theory with fewer new commitments, other
  things equal.
- **past-endorsement criterion** — [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)'s fourth feature, which the
  dilemma turns on.
- **generic memory** — a coarse kind, "a subject remembers P if and only if
  the subject has some sort of access to the information that P".
- **internal privilege** — the organism's consciousness as the final
  authority on what counts as belief.

## Limitations

- **The empirical case concerns fine-grained kinds.** A defender can grant
  every difference and claim a coarse kind. Rupert's reply to that (§VII)
  is a challenge plus conservatism, not a demonstration.
- **No experiment on external memory is cited or run.** The external side
  of the comparisons is argued by thought experiment ("there is no reason to
  think...").
- **The dilemma's second horn needs conservatism** to get from "the
  organism is privileged" to "prefer HEMC".
- **Only the author's version was read.** Journal page numbers are given
  only where the author's outline supplies them.

## Open questions

- Would a cognitive science built on generic kinds predict anything an
  embedded account cannot? Rupert's challenge is to name one result.
- Does any external store show interference, a generation effect or power-law
  forgetting? A digital store with fuzzy, reconstructive retrieval might.
  That is the experiment the paper's argument invites.
