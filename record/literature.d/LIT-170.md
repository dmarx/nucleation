# LIT-170: Unitary inequivalence as a problem for structural realism

Steven French (2010), *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part B: Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics 43(2), 121–136 (May 2012)* — DOI-10.1016/j.shpsb.2011.09.001

## Key takeaways

- Unitarily inequivalent representations in QFT need not defeat structural realism. The realist can either adopt conventional (Lagrangian, cut-off) QFT, following Wallace, or take the "Swiss Army knife" line of Ruetsche, on which the structure spans all the inequivalent representations. The second option pushes her toward a profoundly modal conception of structure that also illuminates spontaneous symmetry breaking and quantum statistics.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The existence of unitarily inequivalent representations in QFT has been offered, notably by Howard, as a serious problem for structural realism. Ladyman's founding example relied on the Stone–von Neumann equivalence of matrix and wave mechanics, and that equivalence fails for systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom. French sets out two responses: adopt conventional QFT, or take the relevant structure to span the space of inequivalent representations. He draws on spontaneous symmetry breaking and on superselection and quantum statistics, and suggests how the two options might be combined.
