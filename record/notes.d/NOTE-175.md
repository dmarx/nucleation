# NOTE-175: Chen — Realism about the Wave Function

## Contribution

A taxonomy, not a thesis. Chen fixes the object of study as the universal wave function in non-relativistic QM (§1, third and fourth remarks). He separates realism from instrumentalism and epistemicism, and orders the realist proposals by ontological category rather than by interpretation. He then crosses the categories with the three realist theories, and with the choice of fundamental space, in one table (Table 1).

## Key insight

Which reading of ψ is open to you depends on two prior choices: which solution to the measurement problem you adopt, and whether you take fundamental space to be high- or low-dimensional. Only the sui generis reading is compatible with every theory and with both spaces, and that is because it says the least.

## Assumptions

- Non-relativistic QM, spinless, with distinguishable particles in the main text. Fn. 4 notes that indistinguishable particles make unordered configuration space the natural domain, and fn. 5 sets spin aside.
- "The wave function" is the universal wave function; subsystem wave functions are derivative (§1).
- Realism is left "open-ended" (§1). The measurement problem is the triad P1–P3 of §2.2 (ψ is complete; ψ always obeys the Schrödinger equation; outcomes are unique). BM denies P1, GRW denies P2 and EQM denies P3.
- The strong nomological reading rests on a simplicity premise. Chen says that fundamental laws should be simple, and that the demand is neutral between Humeans and non-Humeans (fn. 25).

## Key results

- **§3.1 high-dimensional field** (Albert 1996; Loewer, Ney, North). Its challenges are empirical coherence and the emergence of 3-space. Albert's answer is the triplet grouping of coordinates in the interaction Hamiltonian (eq. 8). Maudlin's objection is that the view reifies the overall-phase and constant redundancy; fn. 18 says Chen's comparativism avoids it. The reading is incompatible with GRWm, GRWf and Sm (fn. 16).
- **§3.2 multi-field on 3-space** (Forrest 1988; Belot [LIT-100](../literature.d/LIT-100.md); Hubert and Romano 2018). ψ assigns properties to N-point regions of R³. Because N-element subsets are unordered, the reading enforces permutation invariance automatically.
- **§3.3 spacetime state realism** (Wallace and Timpson 2010). Systems carry density-matrix properties. The objection is redundancy: reduced states can be derived from the universal state, which is in tension with parsimony (p. 12).
- **§3.4 Hilbert-space vector** (Carroll and Singh 2018). Recovering space and objects is "even more difficult" than from configuration space. The reading is "more speculative" and motivated by quantum gravity (pp. 12–13).
- **§4.1 strong nomological** (Dürr, Goldstein and Zanghì; Goldstein and Teufel). The model is the classical H: ψ generates the motion, there is no back-reaction, and it is time-independent under Wheeler–DeWitt ĤΨ = 0 (eq. 11). The costs: it is open whether WDW holds, and the arrow of time has to be revised (fn. 24). Chen's own (2018b) variant takes a simple initial mixed state, as simple as the Past Hypothesis. It gives up purity and time-independence of the generator.
- **§4.2 weak nomological** (Miller, Esfeld, Bhogal and Perry, Callender, Esfeld and Deckert). ψ is a best-system variable; it need not be time-independent or simple simpliciter, only the simplest of the competitors. The objection (Albert, Maudlin, Dewar) is that trajectories carry far less information than ψ, and ψ does not supervene on them. Chen replies that physicists nonetheless agree on ψ from pointer facts. The reading has been defended only within extended Humeanism.
- **§5 sui generis** (Maudlin 2013). ψ is neither ontological nor nomological.
- **§6**: none of the options wins, and the list may not be exhaustive.

## Concepts

- **Ontological interpretation**: ψ is part of the fundamental *material* ontology, alongside particles, fields, events and properties (p. 8).
- **Multi-field**: an assignment of properties to regions made of N points of 3-space, which may be disconnected (p. 10).
- **Strong nomological**: ψ has the same status as the classical Hamiltonian function. It is simple and ideally time-independent, and both Humeans and non-Humeans can accept it (pp. 13–14).
- **Weak nomological**: ψ is a dynamical variable in the Humean best system. It need only be the simplest of the competitors (pp. 15–16).
- **Empirical coherence**: a theory should not undermine the evidence for it, which here comes from 3-dimensional instrument readings (p. 9).

## Connections

Chen's map is the frame the other three works in this batch fill in. Belot ([LIT-100](../literature.d/LIT-100.md)) runs the same comparison inside primitive-ontology Bohmian mechanics, and Chen cites him for the multi-field reading. Allori's wave-functionalism ([LIT-200](../literature.d/LIT-200.md)) is a variant of the weak nomological reading. Her own fn. 46 aligns it with Esfeld's and Bhogal and Perry's Humeanism, so fn. 28's "too cheap" argument applies to it directly. Poltorak ([LIT-164](../literature.d/LIT-164.md)) takes Chen's options as the test cases for his closure argument. The epistemic readings set aside in §1 are those that PBR ([LIT-062](../literature.d/LIT-062.md)) constrains. Chen's gloss that PBR shows them "incompatible with the empirical facts" is broader than the theorem's framework-relative result; see [LIT-090](../literature.d/LIT-090.md) for the dispute over what "epistemic" covers. The Hilbert-space option (§3.4) is the programme Carroll sets out in [LIT-123](../literature.d/LIT-123.md). Gao's ψ-ontic view ([LIT-121](../literature.d/LIT-121.md)) and Dorato's event and power reading ([LIT-176](../literature.d/LIT-176.md)) are the book-length and dispositional relatives. Chen names Gao (2017) as a book-length treatment (fn. 29).

**Metaphysics.** The work presupposes realism about the wave function and surveys theses about fundamental space (high- versus low-dimensional), ontological category, the nature of laws (Humean versus non-Humean) and the emergence of 3-space. The `metaphysics` tag is justified. The primary tag should stay `quantum-foundations`: the object is what quantum theory's central posit is.

## Limitations

- Non-relativistic only; relativistic complications are deferred to Myrvold (2015) (fn. 2).
- It is a survey: the objections are listed rather than weighed.
- Table 1 is asserted, and at least one cell is inconsistent with the text (C7).
- It covers the realist family only, and says the list may not be exhaustive (§6).

## Open questions

- Can the weak nomological reading be made plausible outside extended Humeanism (p. 16)?
- Can the strong nomological reading survive without Wheeler–DeWitt time-independence?
- What would settle the "?" cells of Table 1? That needs case-by-case arguments, for instance whether a Hilbert-space-fundamental ontology can host Bohmian particles.

## Corrections to the seeded skim

- The dossier glosses the three families as ψ being "a physical object (ontological), something law-like (nomological), or an entity of its own kind". Chen says (§3, p. 8) that "ontological" is a misleading label: the four ontological readings put ψ in the fundamental *material* ontology. The nomological and sui generis readings "are compatible with the position that the wave function is part of the ontology", just not in the category of particles or fields.
- The "too cheap" objection (fn. 28, p. 16) is reported as "One argument is", not endorsed. In the body Chen says only that one "could argue" the weak nomological reading is less realist, and that it "could still be realist" if extended Humeanism is realist (p. 16).
- Table 1 check (the dossier asked for one). There are 10 "?" cells: Hilbert-space vector for BM, GRWm, GRWf and Sm; strong nomological for GRW0, GRWm, GRWf and S0; weak nomological for GRW0 and S0. One cell does not match the text: §4.1 (p. 15) says the strong nomological case "may also be possible" in both Everettian and GRW theories with additional ontologies, yet Sm is marked ✓ while GRWm and GRWf are marked "?". The ✗ entries for the high-dimensional field under GRWm, GRWf and Sm do match fn. 16.
- The Hilbert-space option (§3.4) is Carroll and Singh (2018), "Mad-dog Everettianism". That is not the held Carroll essay [LIT-123](../literature.d/LIT-123.md), although the programme is the same.
