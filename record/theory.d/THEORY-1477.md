# THEORY-1477: Planted tree nuclei facilitate forest recovery because, once established, they keep attracting seed dispersers and improving microsites, whereas bird perches and bat boxes leave no improved conditions and decay within years; nuclei must also spread, which is slow, unpredictable and rarely measured, and in tropical moist forest a nucleus needs about 64 m² to attract birds and shade out grass

## Source

Holl, Reid, Zahawi, de Siqueira & Brancalion (2024), [LIT-2172](../literature.d/LIT-2172.md); read in [NOTE-1676](../notes.d/NOTE-1676.md). A stand-in for Corbin & Holl (2012), [LIT-2179](../literature.d/LIT-2179.md), unread.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was shown with new data; this is the authors' synthesis.

- **Mechanism** (p. 2): nuclei facilitate recovery by "attracting seed
  dispersing animals thereby enhancing seed dispersal, creating favorable
  conditions for seedling recruitment both within and at the edge of
  nuclei", and by "spreading over time".
- **Tree against perch** (p. 2): perches and bat boxes "do not improve
  local conditions for seedling establishment and growth" and have
  "short-term impacts (e.g. many bird perches decompose within a few
  years)"; "once established, trees attract dispersers and modify
  microsite conditions continuously".
- **Spread** (p. 2): nuclei must "not only establish but also spread over
  time, which may be slow and highly unpredictable", and "few studies
  have monitored seedling establishment adjacent to actively restored
  patches to assess whether they increase in size".
- **Minimum** (p. 3): "approximately 64 m2 is needed to attract seed
  dispersing birds and shade out pasture grasses in tropical moist
  forests", cited to Zahawi & Augspurger (2006) and Holl et al. (2020),
  [LIT-2145](../literature.d/LIT-2145.md), and said to "vary with ecosystem type and disperser
  behavior".
- What could have come out otherwise: a measured perch effect lasting as
  long as a tree's, or nuclei smaller than 64 m² drawing birds, would
  undo the account; neither is tested here.

## What this does not say

- That a nucleus becomes self-maintaining: the planted trees' own
  regeneration is not covered (see Schubert et al. 2024, [LIT-2177](../literature.d/LIT-2177.md)).
- That nucleation is independent of outside supply: it depends on
  dispersers and seed sources that are not reintroduced, and does not fit
  "where key seed dispersing fauna have been extirpated" (p. 4).
- That 64 m² holds outside tropical moist forest.
- Anything about what happens after maintenance ends, or about cost,
  which the authors report as contested.

## Note of 2026-11-06: what holds it back

The title is general, and its source, Holl and colleagues' opinion
article, argues it from cited studies without data of its own. It waits
on a primary study read in full, such as Holl et al. (2020), [LIT-2145](../literature.d/LIT-2145.md),
still Unfetched, or Zahawi and Augspurger (2006), showing the
persistence contrast, the 64 m² minimum and the spread its condition
names.
