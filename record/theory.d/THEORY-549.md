# THEORY-549: A structured group is not identical to its members: two groups of one kind can have the same members at every time and still be two, which theories that identify a group with a plurality, fusion, aggregate or set of its members cannot allow, and a view of groups as realizations of structures with functionally defined nodes can

## Source

Katherine Ritchie (2013, online 10 October 2012), "What are groups?",
[LIT-479](../literature.d/LIT-479.md), read in full in [NOTE-390](../notes.d/NOTE-390.md). Page numbers are those of the Online
First PDF (1–16). Carried forward, with an amended membership definition,
in Ritchie (2020, online 2018), [LIT-486](../literature.d/LIT-486.md), read in full in [NOTE-389](../notes.d/NOTE-389.md).

## What was actually shown

**The criteria (p. 3).** Any theory of groups must allow that (1) a group
can have different members at different times and (2) in different worlds;
(3) groups exist temporarily and (4) contingently; (5) they are located;
and (6) Coincidence: two groups of the same basic kind can be extensionally
coincident and non-identical. Each is supported by one ordinary example
(pp. 2–3). Only Coincidence is said to separate groups from tables and
trees (p. 4).

**The refutations, strong given the criteria.**

- Non-singular pluralities fail (1) and (2), since some people are not
  identical to more or other people, and meet (6) only if arrangements are
  added (pp. 5–6).
- Fusions (Sider) fail (6), since fusion identity is extensional. The
  Supreme Court and a committee of exactly the Justices, with the same
  members at every time, would be one group (p. 7). Counterparts "qua" a
  sortal do not rescue them (p. 8).
- Burge aggregates fail (1) for member-components and fail (6) when the
  member-components are the same (pp. 9–10).
- Effingham's sets fail (5) unless some sets are concrete, meet (3)–(4) only
  by stipulation, capture (6) only "in spirit", and face a Benacerraf
  problem (pp. 10–12).

**The positive view (§6).** A structure is nodes joined by functional
relations (pitch-ball-to, has-more-power-than). Some things realize it iff
each occupies a node and every node is occupied (p. 14). Two groups are
identical at a time only with the same members in the same intra- and
inter-structural relations (p. 15). It meets all six criteria (p. 15). It
also explains why groups usually have many members: most group structures
contain an irreflexive relation, which needs two occupants (p. 14).

**The 2018 amendment.** [LIT-486](../literature.d/LIT-486.md) keeps the view for "organized social groups"
and makes membership collective, so that two groups can share a structure
(its n. 22). It adds that occupation can be normative: members are
functionally related "or at least normatively bound" as the structure
requires (p. 411).

**What could have come out otherwise.** One of the four rivals could have
met Coincidence without giving up its account of identity. None did. The
fusion case is the cleanest: its identity condition is extensional by
design.

## What this does not say

- **That the criteria are established.** Each is defended by one case.
  Coincidence rests on the claim that a student "is in two clubs" (p. 3).
  A reader who holds that the chess club and the nature club with the same
  members are one group under two descriptions loses nothing to the
  refutations. Uzquiano's alternative is set aside, not answered (n. 1).
- **That groups are structures.** Groups are realizations of structures.
  The grain of structures is left open among three options (p. 13), and
  persistence through a change of structure is not given beyond
  "continuity of the realization" (p. 14).
- **That the view survives vacancies.** Occupation is defined holistically:
  one thing occupies a node only if every node is occupied (p. 14). A team
  one player short has, as written, no members ([NOTE-390](../notes.d/NOTE-390.md), Limitations).
- **That these are social groups.** The intention condition that would
  separate social groups from structured material objects "might be added
  to any of the views" (p. 4) and is not part of the view.
- **Anything about agency or minds.** The paper never mentions them. That a
  group is distinct from its members holds of every structured group, agent
  or not. So "over and above the members", in the sense of numerical
  distinctness, is settled before agency comes up, and List & Pettit's
  claim ([LIT-391](../literature.d/LIT-391.md)) says something further ([LIT-479](../literature.d/LIT-479.md), Standing). That reading
  is the record's.
