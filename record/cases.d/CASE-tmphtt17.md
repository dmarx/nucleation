# CASE-tmphtt17: Google SRE's cap on operational work

## The case

From the SRE book's Introduction ([LIT-tmpeybfl](../literature.d/LIT-tmpeybfl.md)), checked against the web
edition on 2026-11-24:

> Google caps operational work for SREs at 50% of their time. Their
> remaining time should be spent using their coding skills on project
> work. In practice, this is accomplished by monitoring the amount of
> operational work being done by SREs, and redirecting excess operational
> work to the product development teams: reassigning bugs and tickets to
> development managers, [re]integrating developers into on-call pager
> rotations, and so on. The redirection ends when the operational load
> drops back to 50% or lower. This also provides an effective feedback
> mechanism, guiding developers to build systems that don’t need manual
> intervention.

And: "This approach works well when the entire organization—SRE and
development alike—understands why the safety valve mechanism exists".

## The readings (A's)

A34 §7: "The protected engineering allocation is consequently a
constitutional constraint: it preserves resources for the continuing
transformation of the organization." A34 §8 reads the return of
operational work as ending a hidden subsidy: "Development produces
changes. Operations absorbs the consequences of those changes. Successful
correction can conceal the regulatory burdens introduced by development."
Returning the pager "provides the operational team with a form of
standing concerning the terms under which it continues to supply its
service". A34 maps it to [CLAIM-760](../claims.d/CLAIM-760.md) (work transferred rather than saved),
[CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md), [CLAIM-690](../claims.d/CLAIM-690.md) (control over terms), [CLAIM-731](../claims.d/CLAIM-731.md) and [TERM-122](../terms.d/TERM-122.md).

A34 also says: "In extreme cases, an SRE team can ask developers to
resume exclusive on-call responsibility until a service meets appropriate
operating standards." The Introduction checked here supports the
redirection of excess work, not exclusive handback; where the book or
workbook says the stronger thing was not checked.

## Bearing

It illustrates [CLAIM-tmpjoaj3](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjoaj3.md)'s remedy, both halves: the reservation and
the return of overflow. It illustrates [CLAIM-690](../claims.d/CLAIM-690.md), the operating team
holding a term of the support relation (the cap) and an exit-like
response when the term is breached, and [CLAIM-760](../claims.d/CLAIM-760.md), the return making
visible work that the development side's metrics did not carry. A34
cautions: "A particular division of engineering work is not automatically
exploitative." The hidden subsidy itself was introduced at A22 of the
exchange and is filed by another group.
