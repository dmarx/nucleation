summary: >-
  Chalupka, Perona & Eberhardt (2015; AISTATS 2016). A micro cause I and
  micro effect J, both discrete and possibly huge, are confounded by a
  hidden H. The fundamental causal partition groups i by P(J | man(i))
  and j by P(j | man(i)) across all i; its classes are the fundamental
  cause C and effect E, and do(C = c) is any manipulation into the
  class. E is claimed to be the minimal sufficient partition for P(J |
  man(i)), and (C, E) the coarsest pair that recovers it losslessly.
  The fundamental causal coarsening theorem extends the UAI 2015 one to
  the effect side: given the observational partitions, both causal
  partitions fail to coarsen them only on a null set. Subsidiary
  variables are coarsenings of (C, E) with unambiguous effects; two that
  do not interact compose into the fundamental partition. A simulated
  100-neuron population responding to bars in images is recovered. No
  distribution over interventions is assumed.

# LIT-1833: Multi-Level Cause-Effect Systems

Chalupka, K., Perona, P. & Eberhardt, F. (2016). Multi-level cause-effect
systems. In *Proceedings of the 19th International Conference on
Artificial Intelligence and Statistics (AISTATS 2016)*, PMLR 51, 361–369.
arXiv:1512.07942

Read in full on 2026-10-29 ([NOTE-1420](../notes.d/NOTE-1420.md)). One account was drawn from it
and from [NOTE-1415](../notes.d/NOTE-1415.md): [THEORY-1169](../theory.d/THEORY-1169.md).

## Why this is here

Eberhardt and Lee ([LIT-1839](LIT-1839.md), [NOTE-1413](../notes.d/NOTE-1413.md)) recommend causal feature
learning as the alternative to effective information. [QUESTION-204](../questions.d/QUESTION-204.md)'s
second half asks whether a coarse-graining chosen by a causal criterion
agrees with an independent interventionist test of which level the
effects track. This is the paper that defines the test for cause and
effect together, which is the form Hoel's coarse-grainings of a
transition matrix need, and that says it assumes no intervention
distribution. Its partition of causes by P(y | do(x)) is the optimum of
the causal information bottleneck ([LIT-1802](LIT-1802.md), [THEORY-1125](../theory.d/THEORY-1125.md)).
