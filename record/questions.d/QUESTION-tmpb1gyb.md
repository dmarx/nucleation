# QUESTION-tmpb1gyb: Does an independent study of large, exogenous, unconditional windfalls in US administrative data, from lotteries or another as-good-as-random source, find over a multi-year horizon a marginal propensity to earn out of unearned income of roughly −0.5, the windfall largely smoothed rather than spent at once, a per-dollar earnings response rising with pre-win income and falling with prize size, and no increase in self-employment with profits above about $15,000?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-1498 reports one study of US lottery winners, with consumption
inferred from the budget identity and wealth imputed from capital income,
over five years. The authors find effects about twice Sweden's.

## What would answer it

An independent study of as-good-as-random windfalls in US data finding a
marginal propensity to earn near −0.5, smoothing, the same gradients and
no rise in profitable self-employment would answer yes. Observed rather
than imputed wealth or consumption would strengthen the smoothing part.
