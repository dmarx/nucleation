# THEORY-tmpdfele: Costa, Yetter and DeSomer report that sons born after the Civil War to Union soldiers held as prisoners when exchanges had stopped had about 1.11 times the older-age mortality hazard of non-prisoners' sons, with no effect on daughters, and favour an epigenetic route by elimination without measuring one

## The account

Among 19,903 children of 6,367 Union Army veterans, born after 1866 and
surviving to 45, sons of men held in Confederate camps after exchanges
stopped in mid-1863, when conditions were worst, had a mortality hazard of
1.110 against non-prisoners' sons (P = 0.009). Sons of prisoners captured
when exchanges were quick did not differ (1.016), and daughters showed no
effect in any specification. Within families, sons born after the war died
at 2.225 times the rate of brothers born before it (P = 0.056), a
magnitude the authors ask to "be interpreted with caution". The effect was
1.204 among sons born in the second quarter and nil in the fourth, which
the authors read as adequate late-gestation maternal nutrition reversing
it. Socioeconomic, family and maternal controls left it unchanged. By
elimination, the authors find the pattern most consistent with epigenetic,
possibly Y-linked, transmission.

## Evidence

The paper (NOTE-1255): Cox models on linked service, pension, census and
death records, stable across four specifications, though the contrast with
exchange-period prisoners' sons weakens to P = 0.06–0.08 with fuller
controls. The channels the authors exclude are mostly tested in the SI,
which was not seen. No biological measurement is made, and there is no
data on how the fathers raised their sons.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297, the germline and womb routes. A father's hardship before
conception is associated with sons' mortality, but the design cannot
separate the germline from care or culture; and the quarter-of-birth
result puts the womb inside the paternal story. The authors' untested
remark that Civil War prisoners "returned home to a grateful nation. Their
status as heroes may have given their experiences meaning." is read in the
question's notes as a group's story buffering transmitted harm.
