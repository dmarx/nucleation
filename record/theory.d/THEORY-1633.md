# THEORY-1633: Bostrom argues that delay in settling the local supercluster loses potential lives at a vast rate, but that on a total, undiscounted view even a small cut in existential risk outweighs any feasible acceleration, while on a person-affecting view, given some chance that people now alive share the cosmic future, safety must be balanced against speed

## The account

Each second's delay in settling the Virgo Supercluster loses the potential
for about 10^29 lives, or over 10^13 if all must be biological (§I). On any
evaluation that aggregates and is "not relativized to a particular point in
time (no time-discounting)" (§II ¶3) that is an enormous loss.

But "If our actions have even the slightest effect on the probability of
eventual colonization, this will outweigh their effect on when colonization
takes place" (§III ¶3): a percentage point of existential risk reduction is
worth a delay of over 10 million years, and for standard utilitarians
"priority number one, two, three and four should consequently be to reduce
existential risk".

On a person-affecting view, extinction is bad for what it does to people who
exist or will exist. Given a chance that some now alive live "for perhaps
billions of subjective years", safety is very important but must be balanced
against speed: "it is not enough that humankind survives to colonize; it is
crucial that extant people be saved" (§IV ¶6).

## Evidence

A short argument from estimates: 10^13 stars, 10^42 operations per second
per star, 10^17 per human brain (§I). The person-affecting conclusion
depends on a probability of radical life extension, put at not less than 1%,
and on non-declining marginal utility of lifespan (§IV ¶4–5).
