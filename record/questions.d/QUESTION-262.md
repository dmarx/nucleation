# QUESTION-262: When cooperation across a relation's prevailing perspective changes how one party takes the other, does it change only the state of that party's interpretive organization or also its coupling, how far outcomes may revise the governing interpretation, and can taking part be told experimentally from receiving information of equal favourable content?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md) says an interaction can make available a way of relating
the participants' perspectives did not hold, and [CLAIM-429](../claims.d/CLAIM-429.md) says a
durable change acts on the coupling, not only the state. Neither says how
to tell, in a person or a relationship, which happened. The existing
evidence does not separate them: contact reduces prejudice and often
generalizes ([LIT-1413](../literature.d/LIT-1413.md)), narrative exchange outlasts argument
([CASE-147](../cases.d/CASE-147.md)), but neither isolates a change in how outcomes revise the
interpretation.

## What would count as an answer

A5's contrasts, each holding the favourable information fixed:

- **Information against cooperation.** Does taking part change later
  behaviour more than equivalent favourable information?
- **One-sided favour against mutual coordination.** Does reciprocal
  responsiveness matter beyond the act?
- **Local against general.** Does cooperation change responses to other
  members of the category ([CASE-146](../cases.d/CASE-146.md))?
- **Belief against authority.** Do participants endorse different
  propositions, or change whose testimony can move their later judgements
  ([CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md)'s stages)?
- **Immediate against reproduced.** Do changes survive separation,
  conflict and return to the original social setting?

Measured, A5 added, by conduct and not only self-report: behaviour toward
unfamiliar group members, responses to disconfirming evidence, and
willingness to grant epistemic standing, scored by evidence that does not
pass through the interpretation under test ([CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)). A5 also proposed
an order manipulation: cooperation then debate, against debate then
cooperation, since framing operations need not commute ([CLAIM-052](../claims.d/CLAIM-052.md)).

An answer at the level of states, if every effect is reproduced by a
changed starting point with fixed coupling, would defeat [CLAIM-429](../claims.d/CLAIM-429.md).

## Note of 2026-10-29: the contact review and the field experiments, read

Two works this question leans on are now read, and neither supplies its
contrasts.

- **Paluck, Green and Green**, [LIT-1420](../literature.d/LIT-1420.md) ([NOTE-tmpsnvbw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpsnvbw.md)). On randomized
  evidence with delayed outcomes, "contact reduces prejudice" holds in
  direction and is qualified in size: 0.25 for ethnic and related targets,
  near zero in the pre-registered field trials. "Often generalizes"
  ([LIT-1413](../literature.d/LIT-1413.md), Unavailable) is untested there: the review's outcomes mostly
  concern the group met, and its pooling does not separate generalization
  to the group from generalization to other groups. No included study
  isolates taking part from receiving information.
- **Kalla and Broockman**, [LIT-1407](../literature.d/LIT-1407.md) ([NOTE-tmpwypf3](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwypf3.md)), behind [CASE-147](../cases.d/CASE-147.md). In
  Experiment 2 a stranger's video narrative did as well as both parties
  telling stories, so the contrast between information and cooperation is
  not the one its arms draw. It measures no behaviour and no
  generalization to other groups.

The question is unchanged and stays Open.
