## 2. Contracts between processes, and operational standing (Art. 2 §§1, 4)

- **CLAIM-tmpkvzcn** (Proposed, interpretive, thesis; **the owner's, U14**; member). "contracts can form between processes. contracts can even operationalize standing through e.g. prioritization structure in the contract that governs resolution of disputes like races." Its "Where the record stands" says no entry held service-level agreements, contracts between processes or priority structures before it. It says "Standing is held as moral standing in TERM-083", and that this proposal "gives it an operational sense that presupposes neither moral consideration nor agency". A16 narrows it: priority inversion "is not technically identical to a race condition". Its second part is **CLAIM-tmpe6yfm** (Proposed; the owner's, U14; member): processes "need not owe one another moral duties for a system of them to enter a pathological regime of incompatible behaviour", which takes "intervention and a reorganization of their commitments".
- **CLAIM-tmp7hqtl** (Proposed, interpretive, thesis; A16 §13, A's; rests on CLAIM-tmpkvzcn; member). "mere behavioral regularity is insufficient to establish a contract-like relationship. Otherwise every stable causal interaction would qualify." It names four marks: "A maintained relationship establishing reciprocal expectations or requirements"; "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation"; "Governance of circumstances in which requirements conflict"; "Some mechanism sustaining or revising that relationship". "The contract need not be explicitly represented, linguistically articulated or consciously agreed to." A16 §6's schema C = (A, G, J, Π, Σ, M, E, R) is "a proposed extension for our inquiry, not the standard definition of an assume–guarantee contract" (LIT-tmpm8d98, Deferred). **The assistant's gap-fill note** adds two controllers with a deadband and a fixed tie-break, which meet all four marks. The missing condition it proposes is "that the requirements be revisable without replacing the processes (the contract, not the participants, is what changes), and that a breach be registered as a breach of the relation", and it says this is "offered as the missing condition, not as settled". This note is the record's only hold on Art. 2 §7, "contractual identity" (curation map).
- **CLAIM-tmpersgx** (Proposed, version 2, interpretive, thesis; A16 §§15, 19, A's, narrowed by the assistant; rests on CLAIM-tmpkvzcn, CLAIM-tmp7hqtl and CLAIM-364; grounds CASE-tmptpna1; member by Arts. 1 and 10). "An organization's characteristic structure can include the contracts maintained among its processes, which its ordinary behaviour does not reveal". "Two systems with identical ordinary behavior may diverge when a contract is breached, an assumption fails, or competing processes invoke their standing." It was admitted by a separating case (ADR-075). **It supersedes CLAIM-tmp1nubb** (A22 §15, Superseded: "An implicit contract cannot be read off consistent behaviour"). It takes in that entry's six probes: interruption, a substitute provider, competing demands, removal of an enforcer, withdrawal of a discretionary contribution, and a request to renegotiate. "Only the responses to these show 'whether a dependence is regulated, by what, and on whose terms' (A22)." The supersession was at the owner's word on duplicates ("your discretion how to best handle merges"), and its wording is the assistant's.

## 3. Implicit contracts: formation, expectation and norm (Art. 2 abstract, §§2–3)

- **CLAIM-tmp0d1fq** (Proposed, interpretive, thesis; **the owner's, U16**; member). "importantly, we should ensure we have space for characterizing implicit contracts, e.g. an unspoken understanding that evolved through evolution of expectations and assumptions based on consistently observed behaviors that evolved into dependencies". It is a three-step route: consistently observed behaviour, then expectations and assumptions, then dependencies. It does not say "that every regularity, expectation or dependence is a contract", nor that an implicit contract binds anyone morally. Its neighbours, all unread: relational contracts (LIT-tmp1xqzu), psychological and implied contracts (Rousseau, LIT-tmpwdxhm) and convention (Lewis, LIT-473, Unavailable). It also names the Black Queen (CLAIM-603) and incomplete contracts (CLAIM-728, Proposed). "The owner's addition to these is the direction: a contract reached from below ... among parties that need not be agents able to agree."
- **CLAIM-tmp18v1o** (Proposed, interpretive, thesis; A20 §2, A's; **not a member**). Expectation is "represented", "dispositional" or "structural ('its successful operation presupposes another's output')". "**An expectation can be embodied in what an organization no longer needs—or no longer knows how—to do for itself.**" It claims to be more than capability loss: the gap's size "says which deviations by the producer the consumer can absorb", which is an implicit contract's tolerances. The assistant reran A20 §6's model: "independent capacity at withdrawal is 0.42 and functioning falls to a minimum of 0.34". A20 itself says the model shows "unilateral dependency formation, not yet the formation of a reciprocally maintained contract."
- **CLAIM-603** (Proposed, version 8, interpretive, thesis; the assistant's, 2026-11-05; held; member by Arts. 2 and 9). - **CLAIM-430** (Proposed, interpretive, thesis; **the owner's perspectival invitation**, U2 of "Ben Franklin Effect Explained", 2026-10-22, with the phrase "coined in A3"; held; member by Arts. 2 and 7). ### Tensions to keep visible

1. **Sufficiency against necessity.** CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's marks are necessary ("only if"). TERM-tmplpplx's definition reads them as what makes a relation contract-like ("because it governs deviation"). The assistant's own note says that the four "do not yet exclude" the two-controller case. SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr version 2 corrected the same overstatement.
2. **The accounts lag.** SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr says of A20 §16's "organizational contract" that "no TERM holds the words", but TERM-tmplpplx now does. SYNTHESIS-095 and SYNTHESIS-038 integrate none of the exchange's contract entries.
3. **CLAIM-tmp1nubb is Superseded but still a member** (its `works` field, and the curation's Art. 2 row). Its probes now live in CLAIM-tmpersgx, which is a member by Arts. 1 and 10 and not by Art. 2. The prompt's list leaves CLAIM-tmpersgx out of Article 2 too.
4. **What standing is.** TERM-tmpgiy6r says standing is "not reducible to priority", which narrows the owner's U14 ("operationalize standing through e.g. prioritization structure"). TERM-tmplpplx says "Its priority structure is where operational standing lives". The article should say whether priority is one carrier of standing or its home.
5. **Two "standing"s, one domination.** Operational standing presupposes no moral standing, and TERM-124's dominated party has standing "(TERM-083)". The bridge Article 2 defers to Article 9, from a process's operational standing to a party that can be dominated, is not stated anywhere.
6. **Is the frustration relieved or reallocated?** A16 reads CASE-tmptpna1 as "relief". The assistant's check finds that this holds only while M has no deadline. Otherwise inheritance "only chooses which commitment fails" (CLAIM-556: "dropping a demand is not relief").
7. **Ambiguity is sorted twice.** CLAIM-tmp80ny9's three kinds and A32's Γ trichotomy do not coincide (the assistant's note). ARG-tmpa5ped adds that only epistemic ambiguity is a retrieval cost, so the formal centre's Invoke cannot treat ambiguity as one parameter.
8. **Persistence after the reasons go.** CLAIM-tmpjg5gx's correction stands against its own earlier note. Cite the corrected version: the norm outlasts a fall in its baseline support from 27% to about 1.3%, but not every baseline.
