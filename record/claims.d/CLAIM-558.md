# CLAIM-558: A procedural capability that operation seldom calls on decays with the time since its last use, and practice in the interval slows the decay, so a capability kept for rare events is maintained by practice outside operation, not by operation

## The claim

Three parts, of different strength.

1. **Decay with disuse.** A learned procedure with a motor component loses
   performance with the time since it was last used. Tatel's
   meta-analysis ([LIT-2079](../literature.d/LIT-2079.md), read in [NOTE-1560](../notes.d/NOTE-1560.md); [THEORY-1424](../theory.d/THEORY-1424.md)): for
   accuracy the effect size "generally increases in magnitude by 0.08 per
   month of infrequent use", and "one can expect about half of initial
   skill acquisition gains to be lost after between 6 and 9 months of
   infrequent use".
2. **Practice in the interval helps.** "Performance opportunities during
   the retention interval were a statistically significant moderator of
   the relationship between retention interval and effect size for
   accuracy-based performance measures" (Tatel), for accuracy only.
3. **So rare capabilities need practice outside operation.** Oskar and
   colleagues ([LIT-2019](../literature.d/LIT-2019.md), [NOTE-1612](../notes.d/NOTE-1612.md); [THEORY-1404](../theory.d/THEORY-1404.md)), citing frequency
   studies: "For high acuity low occurrence (HALO) procedures,
   supplemental training beyond regular clinical shift experience is
   needed". If operation calls on a capability less often than its decay
   allows, operation cannot be what keeps it.

The first two are the readings' findings for individual clinicians'
skills. The third, and its extension to a capability an organization
keeps for rare events, are the filer's.

## Why it is the record's

[CLAIM-422](CLAIM-422.md) separates a corrective act, an executable procedure and a
maintained capacity, and says an organization can hold the second
without the third. This claim gives one mechanism for the gap: the
procedure stays written while the people who would perform it lose it
through disuse. It also says why the gap does not show in ordinary
operation. The skill is not called on, so its loss is not observed until
a retention test or a real contingency, the kind of intervention
[CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md) says is needed to expose differences matched trajectories
conceal. Telling a warranted reserve from wasteful redundancy, A's
[QUESTION-395](../questions.d/QUESTION-395.md), then needs a measure of maintenance as well as of
holding.

## What it does not say

- That decay is linear beyond a year. Oskar and colleagues: "Only two of
  the included studies (5.5%) described skill retention or decay beyond a
  one-year timeframe."
- How often practice is needed. Oskar and colleagues find the literature
  cannot yet say that, and give no rates.
- That an organization's capability decays as an individual's skill
  does. A team's capability depends also on coordination, equipment and
  procedure; the claim covers the skill part only, and the extension is
  interpretive.

Adopting it is the owner's call.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on aid dependency and self-sufficiency

Read at the owner's request to look to aid and development for theory on how aid-dependent organizations become self-sufficient; the works were chosen by the assistant.

- **Qualitative support at the scale of states and projects.** Under repeated help the recipient "loses skill and motivation over time" (Ostrom et al., [NOTE-1647](../notes.d/NOTE-1647.md)). Where donors run budgets, "over time, real developmental capacities may atrophy within the administration." (Moss et al., [NOTE-1661](../notes.d/NOTE-1661.md)). "If the revenue-raising function is permitted to decay", a weak state is exposed (Moore, [NOTE-1663](../notes.d/NOTE-1663.md)). In Kenya, health workers' service fell as refresher training lapsed ([NOTE-1650](../notes.d/NOTE-1650.md)). None gives a rate.
- **Used.** [CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md) rests on this claim's disuse term to model aid crowding out capacity learned by doing.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on reintroduction and ecological recovery

Read at the owner's suggestion to look to reintroduction and ecological recovery for what makes support safe or hazardous to remove; the works were chosen by the assistant. The assistant's synthesis is [CLAIM-600](CLAIM-600.md).

- **Selection is a faster route than disuse.** Araki et al. ([NOTE-1668](../notes.d/NOTE-1668.md)) judge relaxed selection, the genetic form of disuse, too slow to explain hatchery fish's loss of fitness in the wild. Christie et al. ([NOTE-1652](../notes.d/NOTE-1652.md)) show active selection for captive success instead. Where support changes what is rewarded, a capability can be lost faster than disuse alone would lose it.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings in medicine, on disuse

Extended from skill to tissue. Under full ventilatory support the diaphragm's fibres atrophy within 18–69 hours of inactivity, and force falls by about a third by day 6 in Jaber's group's measurements ([NOTE-1702](../notes.d/NOTE-1702.md)). Jaber et al. note that resting the diaphragm under full support was once thought restorative, a view since "completely turned on its head". No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on assistance and skill

Supported and extended. Disuse need not mean rare operation: it can sit inside daily work when automation does a subtask (Macnamara et al., [NOTE-1710](../notes.d/NOTE-1710.md)). The decay is larger for cognitive than for procedural skill, and it may go unnoticed by the performer. The FAA's advice that pilots use manual skills on most flights is practice kept up beside the support, this claim's remedy. No new decay rates are given; the record's rates are still Tatel's ([LIT-2079](../literature.d/LIT-2079.md)). No status changes.
