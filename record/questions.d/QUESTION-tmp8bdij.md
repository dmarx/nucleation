# QUESTION-tmp8bdij: Across real symbioses, does the partner with the larger and faster-growing population become obligate first more often than chance, and does the other partner's dependence follow the balance of births and deaths inside the collective, as Athreya, Czuppon and Gokhale's model predicts?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1051](../theory.d/THEORY-1051.md) holds what Athreya, Czuppon and Gokhale's model shows. No growth
parameters, carrying capacities or within-collective rates are measured in
any real symbiosis, and the authors call measuring births and deaths inside
the collective "imperative". [CASE-173](../cases.d/CASE-173.md) and [NOTE-1236](../notes.d/NOTE-1236.md) report none for
*Buchnera*.

## What would answer it

Comparative or experimental evidence relating the partners' population
growth parameters to which becomes obligate first, across independent
symbioses, with the faster-evolving partner (larger population, faster
growth) found obligate first more often than chance; and, for at least one
symbiosis, measured rates of partner birth and death inside the collective,
with the host's dependence matching the model's prediction from their
balance (deaths dominating: host fully dependent; births dominating: host
independent). The model's own simulations, or the observation that symbionts
are often more obligate than hosts, cannot settle it, since symbionts live
inside hosts for other reasons. It is refuted if slower-evolving partners
are as often obligate first, or if host dependence is unrelated to
within-collective demography.
