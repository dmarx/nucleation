# CLAIM-1134: Intelligence is epistemic richness

## The claim

The owner's words, at U32b, the first turn of the branch after A60: the whole turn, "intelligence" as epistemic (ecological) richness. The turn follows A60's account of richness ([TERM-158](../terms.d/TERM-158.md)) and the owner's thesis that diversity measures it ([CLAIM-1034](CLAIM-1034.md)).

In this reading, "as" is identity. An organization is intelligent to the degree that its ways of recognizing possibilities, formulating questions and realizing answers are rich, in [TERM-158](../terms.d/TERM-158.md)'s sense: maintained, differentiated and viable. The parenthesis says the richness is the ecological kind, so intelligence is the epistemic dimension of ecological richness.

## Alternatives

The turn is a phrase, and it admits two readings that the record would file differently:

- **This entry**: intelligence *is* epistemic richness.
- **[CLAIM-1102](CLAIM-1102.md)**: intelligence is to be *understood through* epistemic richness, as a property of an ecology of inquiry and not only of one problem solver.

A62b answers the second and objects to the first: "But I'd distinguish epistemic richness from effective intelligence." Its answer is [CLAIM-1106](CLAIM-1106.md), which `objects_to` this entry. The owner chooses which reading was meant.

## Standing

Proposed, the owner's. If this is the reading meant, [CLAIM-1106](CLAIM-1106.md)'s objection stands against it: on A62b's own §6, an organization "possesses considerable latent epistemic richness but may exhibit poor collective intelligence" when its authority structure keeps its findings apart.
