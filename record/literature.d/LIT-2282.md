summary: >-
  Wu et al. (2024), LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term
  Interactive Memory. A benchmark of 500 hand-written questions, each
  embedded in a coherent, length-configurable history of user–assistant
  sessions (≈115K tokens in the S setting; 500 sessions, ≈1.5M tokens, in
  M), testing five abilities: information extraction, multi-session
  reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention. In a
  pilot, commercial memory assistants (ChatGPT, Coze) and long-context LLMs
  lose much accuracy relative to reading only the evidence. The authors then
  decompose memory design into indexing, retrieval and reading, with four
  control points (value, key, query, reading strategy), and show gains from
  storing rounds rather than sessions, expanding keys with extracted user
  facts, time-aware query expansion, and Chain-of-Note reading over
  JSON-formatted items. Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
