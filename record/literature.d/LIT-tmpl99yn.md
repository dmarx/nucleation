# LIT-tmpl99yn: Shaping the Institutional Mind

Deborah Tollefsen (2025), "Shaping the Institutional Mind", *Philosophies*
10(5):112 — DOI-10.3390/philosophies10050112

## Key takeaways

- **The question** (§1). Attributing knowledge and belief to institutions
  is "extremely useful and ubiquitous". Fictionalism leaves its usefulness
  unexplained.
- **The shared error** (§§1–2). Summativists, functionalists such as List
  and Pettit, and critics of group minds all hold McGeer's "standard view":
  ascription is causal explanation of pre-existing internal states.
- **The regulative view** (§3). After McGeer, Zawidzki and Andrews, folk
  psychology is "mind-making rather than mind detecting". Telling a friend
  that one intends to go to the gym is a commitment, not a report. Extending it to
  institutions means "We are shaping them to be more and more agent-like."
  The view is assumed: "I do not have space to argue" for it.
- **Corporate criminal law** (§4.1). Under respondeat superior a
  corporation could escape conviction by dispersing knowledge. Under the
  collective knowledge doctrine in *United States v. Bank of New England*,
  "no individual human being knew that p, but, according to the courts,
  the bank was said to know that p". Banks then built software, rules and
  training to detect reportable deposits. "The courts were not
  identifying a pre-existing mental state".
- **Institutional narratives** (§4.2). A narrative "sets up expectations
  for its behavior moving forward". Philip Morris's 1999 repositioning
  and public counter-narratives both regulate.
- **Agnosticism** (§5). *Groups as Agents* argued, on interpretivism, that
  organizations are agents. This paper "can, however, remain agnostic"
  about institutional minds: what is regulated may be only individual
  behaviour.

It is a different kind of claim from the record's group-agency accounts.
[THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md) and [THEORY-tmpe1ql0](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpe1ql0.md) say what is true of group agents; this
paper says why the attributions work, and leaves the reality question
open. Tollefsen classes List and Pettit's functionalism under the standard
view she rejects.

For the owner's essay, it bears most on §8.4, externalized governance: a
court's attribution of knowledge changed the internal structure of the
institutions it was attributed to. It also bears on §7.1, where a
corporation's self-ascription in a narrative works as a commitment.
