# THEORY-tmpf7stg: A newer, less constrained presidential adjudication has emerged in the United States, in which presidents make fact-specific, high-stakes decisions about particular targets with limited judicial oversight as doctrine expands White House control of agencies and constraining norms erode, raising the risk of executive coercion that courts could check through a harder look built from existing doctrine

Cuéllar (2026), [LIT-2706](../literature.d/LIT-2706.md).

## The argument

Presidents "make fact-specific, high-stakes decisions about sanctions,
investigations, contracts, legal interpretations, and enforcement, with
limited or minimal judicial oversight" (p. 597). Doctrine has widened
presidential control of agencies, and "These norms are quickly eroding"
(p. 617). The risk is "executive coercion" (p. 626). The remedy is a
"harder look" that still calls "for a lighter touch" than review of
rulemaking (p. 643), with civic actors who "create friction and slow
down" edicts (p. 648).

## What it does not show

- **Systematic evidence.** The essay argues from current examples; the
  part that documents erosion was skimmed in this reading.
- **That review can come in time.** By its own account the harder look
  "generally would only occur after" final agency action (p. 645).
- **That a remedy becomes unreachable.** Its mechanism is cost before
  correction, not a change in what correction can achieve.
