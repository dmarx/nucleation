summary: >-
  A systematic review of the mortality-salience (MS) hypothesis of terror management theory, that reminders of death increase worldview defense. An uncorrected random-effects meta-analysis of 635 effects reproduces Burke et al.'s (2010) moderate effect (g = 0.81, about r = .37, against their r = .35 over 277 studies), but a funnel plot shows strong asymmetry. Bias corrections disagree. Selection models and WAAP-WLS give a small effect, about r = .18; PET-PEESE finds none. A p-curve of 825 focal tests shows that some studies test real effects, at an average power of 25%; z-curve gives an expected replication rate of 19% and an expected discovery rate of 8%, near the 5% of a literature of null effects. Five of nine preregistered replications succeeded, and Many Labs 4 did not. The authors conclude the literature holds some non-zero effects, much smaller and far more heterogeneous than published, and that most studies are underpowered.
