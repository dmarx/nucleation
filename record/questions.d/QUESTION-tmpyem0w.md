# QUESTION-tmpyem0w: Does the free-riding structure of truth-and-obstinacy debate survive with more than two debaters or with strategies richer than fixed, memoryless premise and conclusion rules?

## Why it is a question

Handfield, Garcia, Hilbe and Yeo ([LIT-966](../literature.d/LIT-966.md), read in full in [NOTE-795](../notes.d/NOTE-795.md)) model
deductive debate as a game whose payoff trades true beliefs against changes
of mind. In their two-debater, three-proposition model, the most
truth-conducive equilibrium is often a snowdrift: one debater argues boldly
and the other free-rides with a compromise strategy, and in a narrow band of
mixed equilibria a higher weight on truth lowers equilibrium accuracy
([THEORY-290](../theory.d/THEORY-290.md)). Every exact result is for two players and n = 3, and the
strategies are fixed rules over current positions. The paper's own "ideal
cooperative debater", who targets the partner's false beliefs, lies outside
that space. [NOTE-795](../notes.d/NOTE-795.md) lists both extensions among its open questions. Until
2026-10-19 they were [THEORY-290](../theory.d/THEORY-290.md)'s promote_when. [THEORY-290](../theory.d/THEORY-290.md)'s body disclaimed
them, so the clause moved here when the title was narrowed to the model.

## What would answer it

An analysis or experiment that measures accuracy and revision costs
separately and finds the same free-riding structure among more than two
debaters, or with richer strategies than fixed, memoryless premise and
conclusion rules. In such a study, a participant facing a bold partner must
argue less boldly, collective accuracy must fall short of what mutual
boldness would give, and the shortfall must vanish when the cost of revision
is removed. It would answer no if, with strategies that can target the
partner's false beliefs (the paper's "ideal cooperative debater"), the
truth-optimal profile is an equilibrium wherever the truth weight is
positive.

What would not answer it: more runs of the two-person, three-proposition
model, and observational data on debates, which cannot measure revision
costs.
