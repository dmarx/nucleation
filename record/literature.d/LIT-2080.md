# LIT-2080: Second-Order Rigidity and Prestress Stability for Tensegrity Frameworks

Connelly, R. & Whiteley, W. (1996). Second-Order Rigidity and Prestress Stability for Tensegrity Frameworks. *SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics* 9(3): 453–491. doi:10.1137/S0895480192229236.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1588](../notes.d/NOTE-1588.md) (in part). Sections are the paper's.*

- **The hierarchy.** "We demonstrate a hierarchy of rigidity--first-order
  rigidity implies prestress stability implies second-order rigidity
  implies" rigidity, and "Examples show that none of these implications
  are reversible, even for bar frameworks" (abstract).
- **The duality.** A test "which combines information on the self stresses
  and the first-order flexes of a framework to detect second-order
  rigidity" (abstract), a special case of the "Farkas alternative" (§1.4).
- **An engineering test corrected.** Pellegrino and Calladine's rank
  conditions are "necessary but not sufficient for prestress stability"
  (§1.3).
- **Coincidence.** With a one-dimensional cone of first-order flexes or of
  proper self-stresses, second-order rigidity implies prestress stability
  (Proposition 5.3.1).
- **Constraints that stress.** Turning cables into bars kept a nontrivial
  first-order flex but added proper self-stresses, with which the
  framework "is even prestress stable" (§6.2).

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures cites it, rightly, for the
hierarchy and its irreversibility. It is the mathematical ground for the
synthesis's caveat that a Maxwell–Calladine count does not by itself
settle finite motion or stability, and its §6.2 is the cleanest instance in
this batch of added constraints creating self-stress rather than removing
freedom, the point the synthesis attributes to Rocks and Mehta
([LIT-2112](LIT-2112.md)), who do not make it. Filed from it: [THEORY-1389](../theory.d/THEORY-1389.md). No
instruction for machine-learning practice.
