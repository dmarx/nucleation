# TERM-040: communicative kind, as a repeatable relational organization that numerically distinct events realize, distinct from a token and from a structural position

## Definition

A communicative kind is what two realizations share when they count as the
same act: a repeatable organization of relations among speakers, audiences,
norms, commitments and expected responses. Numerically distinct events realize
it. A218 §1: "We distinguish numerical identity of concrete events from
structural identity of repeatable communicative kinds. A translated
performance is not literally the same event as its source, although both may
realize corresponding relational organizations." A218 §2 names the cases
where it is clearest: "promising, reprimanding, authorizing, teasing, and
establishing conversational commitments".

Three things are kept apart.

- **Kind.** A194 §6: "**Structural kind:** What invariant pattern does this
  realization instantiate?"
- **Position.** "**Structural position:** Which relational role does this
  particular object occupy within the larger configuration?" Promisor and
  promisee are positions in one kind.
- **Token.** The historical event. A191 §2: "A particular promise made at a
  particular time has a concrete history, speaker, audience, and occurrence."

The split first appears at A191 §2, as type against token: "Our transport
theory often concerns preservation of **act type** across realizations,
rather than numerical identity of a single historical event." A194 §3 displays
it as token identity ≠ structural type identity ≠ representational
coordinates, and §6 adds position: "This prevents the theory from using a
single invariant signature to solve two different individuation problems."

## What it is not

- Not the observations of it. A communicative object as an empirical system
  of constraints ([TERM-042](TERM-042.md)) is a pattern of observations, and a kind is what
  those observations are of.
- Not an observational class ([TERM-014](TERM-014.md)). A218 Ch7's core claim: "A
  structural kind may be real while remaining indistinguishable under an
  insufficient family of empirical probes." An equivalence class relative to
  a family of measurements cannot be.
- Not a position. A kind has positions; it is not one.
- Not a token. A translation and its source are two events of one kind, not
  one event.

The term has a gap. A218 never defines a kind formally. The nearest is §3.1:
"Represent candidate relational kinds through structured systems with
designated participants, relations, compositions, and transformations." What
makes two such systems the same kind is the open part.
