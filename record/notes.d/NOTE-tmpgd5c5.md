# NOTE-tmpgd5c5: Chirkov, Ryan, Kim & Kaplan — Differentiating autonomy from individualism

## Contribution

It is the primary study behind SDT's reply that autonomy is not
individualism. It measures autonomy as the relative internalization of
one's reasons for a practice, applies it to practices of each of
Triandis's four cultural orientations, and shows in four countries that
more autonomous enactment of *any* type of practice, collectivist and
vertical included, goes with more well-being (pp. 97–99, 104–106).

## Key insight

Autonomy and independence are on different axes. Dependence versus
independence concerns whether one relies on others; autonomy versus
heteronomy concerns whether one endorses what one does. "One can be
autonomously dependent" (p. 98), and one can conform to group norms
volitionally if one endorses them, as one can obey a traffic officer
volitionally if one values traffic laws. So a collectivist practice can be
enacted autonomously, and the cultural critique of SDT's autonomy need
"equated SDT's constructs of autonomy and self-determination with making
choices independently from one's reference group" (p. 97).

## Assumptions

- **Autonomy is relative internalization**, measured per practice by the
  Self-Regulatory Questionnaire of Cultural Practices: "Why do you or would
  you do this?", four reasons rated 1–5, combined as the relative autonomy
  index −2·external −1·introjected +1·identified +2·integrated (pp. 101–
  103). The "integrated" reason reads in part "I feel free in choosing and
  doing it, and feel responsible for the outcomes" (p. 102).
- **Intrinsic motivation is excluded** as not pertinent to internalization
  of instrumental practices (p. 99).
- **Culture is measured as perceived norms**: participants rated how
  important and frequent each practice is among "most Americans [Koreans,
  Russians, Turks]" (p. 102).
- **Well-being** is a composite of standardized SWLS, Short Index of
  Self-Actualization, Rosenberg self-esteem and six CES-D items (reversed)
  (pp. 102, 104).
- **Measurement comparability by MACS**, accepting strong invariance at
  CFI ≈ .89–.90 by a difference-in-fit criterion (pp. 102–104).

## Key results

### Samples (Table 1, p. 101)

- USA 195, Russia 159, Turkey 94, South Korea 111; university students;
  73% women in the US and Russian samples, 27% in Korea, 43% in Turkey.
  The Turkish sample was much poorer (48% "low–low" income; 68.5% of
  fathers without high school).

### Perceived cultural norms (p. 103, Table 3)

- Turkish data on perceived practices were "noncomparable" and excluded
  from that comparison. Relative to the US (latent means), Koreans saw
  their culture as more horizontally (0.42) and vertically (0.75)
  collectivist and less horizontally (−0.71) and vertically (−0.22)
  individualist; Russians as more vertically collectivist (0.32) and less
  vertically individualist (−0.59). Russia "a mixed model".

### Internalization of practices (pp. 103–104, Table 4)

- Across samples, horizontal practices mean index 4.68 against vertical
  1.60, t(557) = 27.20, p < .0001. Unpredicted: individualist above
  collectivist within each dimension (horizontal 5.08 vs 4.45; vertical
  2.01 vs 1.19).
- US students internalized most practices least (raw vertical
  collectivism −0.71). Men internalized vertical practices more than women
  in three of four samples.

### Autonomy and well-being (pp. 104–105, Tables 6–7)

- Composite well-being regressed on one internalization index, three
  country contrasts and their interactions, one model per practice type:
  β = .24 (horizontal individualism), .24 (horizontal collectivism), .27
  (vertical individualism), .29 in Table 6 (vertical collectivism);
  R² ≈ .19–.20; none of the twelve country × internalization interactions
  significant (Table 6 betas −.08 to .00).
- Within each country, all sixteen internalization betas positive and
  significant: Korea .23–.28, Russia .17–.24, Turkey .34–.42, USA .15–.22
  (Table 7).
- With overall internalization and the likelihood of performing
  individualist and collectivist practices: only internalization (β = .17)
  and collectivist practice (β = .09) were significant; no interactions
  (p. 105). No gender × internalization interactions.
- Country contrasts carried more well-being variance than autonomy (Contrast
  1, US against the rest, β ≈ .37–.44).

## Concepts

- **Autonomy** — behaviour "experienced as willingly enacted", fully
  endorsed; its opposite is **heteronomy**, not dependence (p. 98).
- **Dependence / independence** — reliance or non-reliance "on others for
  guidance, support, or needed supplies" (p. 98).
- **Reflective vs reactive autonomy** (Koestner) — endorsement against
  resistance to influence (p. 98).
- **Horizontal / vertical; individualism / collectivism** — Triandis's
  equality versus hierarchy and individual versus group priority (pp.
  99–100).

## Connections

- **Iyengar and Lepper 1999** ([LIT-tmp0rsz4](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0rsz4.md)) is the paper's chief target.
  It reports them as finding that experimenter-imposed choice undermined
  intrinsic motivation in both Asian and Anglo American children, and that
  choice by trusted others "uniquely enhanced intrinsic motivation for the
  Asian group", with the interpretation focused on the latter (p. 97). This
  is Chirkov et al.'s account; the original is not held.
- **Oishi 2000** and **Miller 1997** are criticized for measuring autonomy
  as individualist values or as freedom from all influence (pp. 97–98).
- **Sheldon et al. 2001** ([LIT-tmpd3l9z](../literature.d/LIT-tmpd3l9z.md)), cited for event autonomy
  predicting well-being in Korea and the USA (p. 99).
- Reis et al. 2000 ([LIT-tmpdbw3y](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdbw3y.md)) and Sheldon, Ryan and Reis 1996 are
  cited as the only diary studies of the needs, both American (p. 107).

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-441](NOTE-441.md)** holds this study second-hand (p. 1577 of Ryan and Deci
  2006; its C8), as "Autonomous reasons predicted well-being with no moderation by culture
  in Russia, the United States, Turkey and South Korea". The primary text
  bears that out. Two qualifications for anyone leaning on it: the samples
  are 94 to 195 students per country, and "no moderation" is twelve
  non-significant interaction terms, not a bound on their size.
- **[THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md) and [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)** (internalization and regulation): primary
  evidence that the internalization continuum, scored as a relative
  autonomy index, predicts well-being outside the US, for practices the
  participants did not choose.
- **[QUESTION-tmpee04a](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpee04a.md).** The paper is about whether autonomy's link to
  well-being is universal, not about what makes autonomy a need. Its
  argument for need-hood is the universality of the link, which is one of
  the nine criteria; it still uses well-being as the criterion, and the
  autonomy measure's top anchor includes "I feel free", which is close to
  a well-being report.
- **The definition carries the thesis.** The claim that autonomy is not
  individualism is true by SDT's definition; what is empirical is that the
  so-defined autonomy predicts well-being for collectivist practices too.
  The paper tests the second; the first is argued.

## Limitations

- University students; cross-sectional ("it is also conceivable that high
  well-being leads people to view their behaviors as more autonomous",
  p. 107); four cultures only (the authors').
- **Measurement invariance was accepted below conventional cut-offs**: for
  well-being, strong invariance RMSEA = .11, CFI = .89 (p. 104); for the
  autonomy index CFI = .90. Turkey's perceived-norm data were not
  comparable.
- **The four internalization indices are never entered together**, and
  their betas are nearly identical (.24–.29), which suggests a general
  person-level tendency to give autonomous reasons more than a
  practice-specific effect; the paper does not report their
  intercorrelations.
- **A reporting error.** The text for vertical collectivism (p. 105)
  repeats the vertical individualism figures word for word (β = .27,
  F = 28.55 for the index; Contrast 1 .44, F = 82.50; Contrast 2 .30,
  F = 33.10), while Table 6 gives vertical collectivism .29, .43 and .25.
- The practices were rated hypothetically ("would you do"), so autonomy
  for practices one does not perform is included.

## Open questions

- Would the relation hold with outcome measures that are not self-reports,
  and in non-student samples, in Africa and South America as the authors
  ask (pp. 107–108)?
- Does the relative autonomy index for cultural practices carry variance
  beyond a general autonomy orientation?
- Do longitudinal designs show autonomy preceding well-being across
  cultures?
