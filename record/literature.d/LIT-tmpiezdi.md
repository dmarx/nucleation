# LIT-tmpiezdi: Modifying avoidance in social anxiety: The effects of a cognitive-behavioural instruction on extinction learning

Sridhar, Biagi & Wake (2026). Behaviour Research and Therapy 203, 105088.
DOI 10.1016/j.brat.2026.105088.

## Key takeaways

- A brief instruction to make non-avoidance responses "to test your
  expectancy" did not significantly change avoidance during avoidance
  extinction (Instruction Type F(1, 88) = 1.46, p = .230); at +1 SD of
  social anxiety the difference was p = .066, labelled exploratory
  (§§3.2.3, 3.4.3).
- It did not change US expectancy after the instruction (Instruction Type
  F(1, 92.38) = 0.07, p = .788) or skin conductance in the extinction
  test (§§3.3, 3.4.5).
- The abstract and conclusion state the instruction "reduced avoidance
  behaviour among individuals with higher social anxiety"; the reported
  tests give that only as a non-significant trend.

## Standing in the record

Filed by LEAD-047's search as the nearest design to an intervention on a
defensive process's authority with threat belief measured, for
CLAIM-446. It falls short of the lead's criteria: belief is measured and
unchanged, not held fixed, and the behavioural effect is not reliable.
