# LIT-1267: Reply to: No specific relationship between hypnotic suggestibility and the rubber hand illusion

Lush, P. & Seth, A. K. (2022). Reply to: No specific relationship between hypnotic suggestibility and the rubber hand illusion. *Nature Communications* 13, 563. DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-28178-y. Reply to Ehrsson et al. (2022), *Nat Commun* 13, 564.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1003](../notes.d/NOTE-1003.md).*

- **The disagreement is interpretation, not data.** The authors accept
  Ehrsson et al.'s null relation between hypnotisability and the
  synchrony effect as a replication of their own, and call the drift
  reanalysis insensitive.
- **The asynchronous condition is not a control for expectation.**
  Participants expect more from synchronous stimulation, so the
  difference between conditions confounds timing with demand. In the
  target sample the expectancy difference did not vary with
  hypnotisability (b = -0.16, SE 0.09, P = 0.072, B = 0.07), which by
  their account explains the null.
- **Two routes to the same report.** The proposal is that participants
  high in phenomenological control meet the demand by having the
  experience, and those low in it by response bias or compliance. Low
  suggestibles' residual illusion is put down to the second route.
- **Ownership is the weak item.** Mean agreement was 1.9 and 1.2 for the
  two referred-touch items and 0.7 for ownership (seven-point scale, maximum 3);
  ownership agreement rose 0.76 points per SWASH point and reaches 1 only
  above SWASH 2, the top 31%.
- **Implicit measures are not exempt.** Expectancies cover drift and skin
  conductance as well, by the authors' cited work, and could cover 2AFC
  and cross-modal congruency tasks, which have not been tested for it.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request for experimental work building
on the rubber hand illusion, with the comment it answers, Ehrsson et al.
2022 ([LIT-1264](LIT-1264.md)), declared as its rival reading of the same data. The
target article, Lush et al. 2020, Lush's 2020 Collabra paper and Roseboom
& Lush 2022 are being filed alongside. Read in full: **Active**.

It does more to [THEORY-765](../theory.d/THEORY-765.md) than the comment it answers. That
THEORY's promote_when accepts either expectations measured or matched, or
trait suggestibility measured with the synchrony effect surviving it.
This reply shows, with data, that the second can be met while the first
fails, and that its authors' account predicts exactly that. The NOTE
proposes tightening the promote_when, and sets out the bearing on the
other three rubber-hand THEORYs.

Anil Seth, its second author, wrote Seth 2013 ([LIT-1263](LIT-1263.md)), the source
of [THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md), whose cardio-visual evidence is a synchronous against
asynchronous comparison of the kind this reply says "offers no protection
against demand characteristic effects". Manos Tsakiris, of Apps &
Tsakiris 2014 ([LIT-1262](LIT-1262.md)), is on the other side, an author of the
comment.

One of the sources of [THEORY-766](../theory.d/THEORY-766.md) (filed 2026-10-20), which restates the rubber hand synchrony effect as a difference in reports, separates drift from those reports, and leaves open whether expectation produces the difference.
