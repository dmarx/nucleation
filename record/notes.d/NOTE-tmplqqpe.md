# NOTE-tmplqqpe: Blanke & Metzinger — Full-body illusions and minimal phenomenal selfhood

## Contribution

The paper names a target for the science of bodily self-consciousness,
minimal phenomenal selfhood (MPS), and breaks it into three dimensions
that can be measured or reported separately: self-identification with the
body as a whole, self-location, and a weak first-person perspective. It
then sorts the existing evidence by whether it touches those dimensions.
The rubber-hand illusion and somatoparaphrenia, the field's main tools for
ownership, are put on the side of *partial* ownership and said not to
reach MPS. Out-of-body experience, heautoscopy and the new full-body
illusions are put on the side of *global* ownership. From that sorting it
draws a thesis: agency is a causal background to selfhood, not part of
it.

## Key insight

The simplest self is a whole-body thing: being identified with one body,
located where it is, and seeing from inside it. Owning a hand is not a
small piece of that; it can be disturbed while the self stays whole, and
the self can be displaced while every part is still one's own. On this
view a body-part illusion and an out-of-body experience are different
kinds of evidence, and only the second reaches selfhood.

## Assumptions

- **The self-model theory.** Concepts and the strong 1PP come from
  Metzinger's *Being No One* ([LIT-467](../literature.d/LIT-467.md)) and its précis. MPS is "the
  representational content" of an integrated state that "for the first
  time makes the body available for attention and global control", and
  it is felt as a self because the body model "is phenomenally
  transparent because it cannot introspectively be recognized as a model"
  (p. 8).
- **Strategy: isolate the simplest form first.** "Given a complex domain
  such as human self-consciousness, we propose that an optimal strategy
  is likely to be the (initial) isolation of the simplest form of the
  target phenomenon" (p. 7). The rest of the paper depends on MPS being
  that form.
- **Reports and drift measure the dimensions.** In the full-body illusion,
  "Questionnaire scores were used to quantify self-identification and
  perceived body position (drift) to quantify self-location" (p. 12). The
  patients' dimensions are read from their descriptions (Box 2).
- **"Necessary" in the metaphysical sense.** The agency thesis is about
  "the minimal supervenience base for phenomenal selfhood" (p. 12), and
  the Box 1 definitions separate what is necessary from what is only
  "causally enabling" (p. 8). Evidence from adult humans is taken to bear
  on that base.

## Key results

The paper has no data of its own. Its moves, in order:

- **Definition (p. 7).** "MPS is a phenomenal property, namely the
  conscious experience of being a self. It is the experience of being a
  distinct, holistic entity capable of global self-control and attention,
  possessing a body and a location in space and time."
- **Three readings of "first-person perspective" (p. 8, glossary).** The
  weak 1PP is "a purely geometrical feature of an egocentric model of
  reality", a centre of projection. The strong 1PP appears when the system
  "is internally represented as directed at an object component"; it "is
  exactly what makes consciousness subjective". The cognitive 1PP adds a
  self-applied concept of a subject and is set aside. "Having MPS is a
  necessary condition for the strong (and the cognitive) 1PP, but not for
  the weak 1PP because the weak 1PP is itself a constituting factor for
  MPS."
- **Transparency and opacity (pp. 8, 10).** "If parts of the conscious
  body-model become phenomenally opaque, that is, if they are experienced
  as a representation ... the dimension of self-identification and
  consequently MPS can be weakened" (p. 8). From the autoscopic data,
  "there seems to be a continuum between transparency and opacity, which
  determines how realistically the content of a body representation is
  experienced and how strongly the dimension of self-identification is
  expressed" (p. 10).
- **No substantial self (p. 8).** MPS and other self-representations are
  "not static internal copies of some mysterious thing or individual
  substance ('the' self), but only ongoing processes of tracking and
  controlling global bodily properties". Global properties are those of
  the system as a whole, like wetness, which no H2O molecule has.
- **Box 1: necessity and sufficiency (p. 8).**
  - "The existence of a conscious, transparent and global
    body-representation available for attentional access is a sufficient
    condition for MPS."
  - "We claim that spatiotemporal self-location, self-identification and
    weak 1PP are necessary and sufficient for MPS. If there are further
    necessary conditions, then our claim is false."
  - Agency: "The temporally preceding experience of agency and of global
    motor control (e.g. in childhood) causally enables MPS in practically
    all humans, but it is not causally necessary". Out-of-body
    experiences and the experimental manipulations "demonstrate that
    agency only belongs to the causal background for MPS". What
    constitutes MPS "could also take place in a disembodied, non-neural,
    artificial system possessing no effectors".
- **Partial against global ownership (pp. 9–10).**
  - Somatoparaphrenia, after right temporo-parietal damage, "affects only
    the contralesional (mostly left) hand (or foot), and never
    ipsilesional body parts" (p. 9).
  - In the rubber-hand illusion the fake hand stroked in synchrony with
    the occluded real hand can produce felt touch, reported ownership and
    a displacement of felt hand position. "The RHI is abolished when the
    stroking is asynchronous" (p. 9).
  - Both "are only associated with changes in partial ownership and do
    not enable the investigation of MPS as the most fundamental sense of
    selfhood". MPS is "not manipulated in healthy subjects during the RHI
    because these studies do not experimentally manipulate self-location,
    self-identification or the weak 1PP" (p. 9).
  - Neurons: fingertip fields in area 3b, larger fields in area 5, and
    VIP cells that may encode "the entire or large parts of the body
    surface". "We argue that cells in area VIP and 5 are of importance for
    self-identification and other MPS-dimensions, whereas cells
    representing fingertips or hands are involved in partial ownership"
    (pp. 9–10). The vestibular system is added for global body
    representation (p. 10). This is a proposal, with no study of it cited.
- **Neurology varies MPS along its dimensions (pp. 9–11, Box 2, Figs 1–2).**
  - *Autoscopic hallucination*: a second own body is seen, but all three
    dimensions are normal and insight is preserved. Lesions centre on
    right parieto-occipital or temporo-occipital cortex (7 patients).
  - *Out-of-body experience*: self-location and the weak 1PP are at the
    illusory, usually elevated body, with "a sense of disembodiment";
    self-identification is with the illusory body; all three dimensions
    and the strong 1PP are pathological, and there is usually no insight.
    Lesions mainly right temporo-parietal (n = 12).
  - *Heautoscopy*: self-location, weak 1PP and self-identification can sit
    at either body, at both, or alternate rapidly; patients report "being
    split in two selves". Lesions mainly left temporo-parietal. On the
    authors' reading this is "a brief double instantiation of MPS (two
    selves)" that "does not occur in any other form of autoscopic
    phenomenon" (p. 11).
  - The dimensions dissociate: an auditory weak 1PP can sit apart from the
    visual one, and in one stimulation case the patient had his visual
    weak 1PP at his body "but self-location 2 m behind his body as
    represented" (p. 12).
- **Full-body illusions (pp. 11–12, Fig. 3).** In Lenggenhager et al.'s
  set-up, participants see through a head-mounted display their own
  back, a fake body or an object stroked synchronously or asynchronously
  with their own back. With synchronous stroking "they felt as if the
  virtual body was their own (illusory self-identification) and showed
  drift towards it (illusory self-location)". "Both full-body illusion
  measures were also observed for a fake body ..., but not (or less so)
  for a rectangular object. The full-body illusion was abolished during
  asynchronous stroking." In Ehrsson's set-up, stroking at the unseen
  chest and in front of the camera induced "the experience of being at
  the position of the camera" behind the body. "Both studies revealed
  visual capture": self-identification followed where the stroking was
  seen. The protocols induced neither an out-of-body experience nor an
  autoscopic hallucination, "but rather a state that is comparable to
  heautoscopy", and "neither of them managed to manipulate what we have
  called the strong 1PP" (p. 12). Stratton's 1899 mirror experiment is
  credited as the precursor.
- **Conclusion (p. 12).** "The reviewed clinical and experimental data
  show how a passive, multisensory and globalized experience of 'owning' a
  body is sufficient for minimal conscious selfhood." "Agency might be a
  sufficient condition for MPS, but it does not belong to the
  metaphysically necessary part of the minimal supervenience base".
  "Bodily agency is a causally enabling, but not a constitutive
  condition, for phenomenal selfhood." Attentional access is speculated
  to be necessary for the move from MPS to the strong 1PP.

## Concepts

- **Minimal phenomenal selfhood (MPS).** The conscious experience of being
  a self in its simplest form; a whole-body representation "functionally
  available for global control" (p. 8).
- **Self-identification.** "The degree to which an organism identifies
  with the content of a global body representation" (glossary).
- **Self-location.** "A determinate volume in space, normally localized
  within the bodily boundaries as represented", within which the origin
  of the weak 1PP lies (glossary). Also temporal, which is not treated.
- **Weak 1PP.** The geometrical origin of an egocentric, usually
  visuospatial, model of reality.
- **Strong 1PP.** The system represented as directed at an object; for a
  phenomenal self as subject, a "phenomenal model of the intentionality
  relation" (p. 8).
- **Partial against global ownership.** Ownership of a body part against
  identification with the body as a whole.
- **Global property.** One that "can only meaningfully be ascribed to a
  given system as a whole" (p. 8).
- **Causally enabling condition.** A condition with a causal role that is
  "not strictly necessary" (Box 1), as against one in the minimal
  supervenience base.
- **Agency** (glossary). "Global motor control, including the subjective
  experience of action, control, intention, motor selection and the
  conscious experience of will."

## Connections

The paper puts Metzinger's self-model theory to work on Blanke's clinical
series of out-of-body experience and heautoscopy, and on the 2007
full-body illusions. It takes the rubber-hand paradigm from Botvinick and
Cohen (1998) and Tsakiris and Haggard (2005), and cites Gallagher's *How
the Body Shapes the Mind* and Pacherie's phenomenology of action as the
views on which agency is needed for a minimal self. The immunity
literature (Shoemaker 1968; Mizumoto and Ishikawa 2005) is cited for the
reading of out-of-body experience as an "error through misidentification"
(Box 2). Metzinger's "Self models" ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md)) is cited in the glossary and
text.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md) and [LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md).** [NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md)'s C6, "Body ownership can be
  manipulated by multisensory conflict and is the simplest form of
  self-awareness", cites this paper. The paper supports the manipulation
  (C5) and narrows the "simplest form": it is *global* ownership, and the
  rubber hand, the best-known manipulation of ownership, does not reach it
  (C2). [NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md)'s own text already says "global body ownership"; the
  claim table's wording is the looser one. [NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md)'s C7, on phantoms in
  congenital limb absence, gets nothing here: Brugger's chapter is cited
  only for hemibody ownership.
- **[THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) ([LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), [NOTE-460](NOTE-460.md)).** The paper is the self-model theory's
  empirical programme for its most basic level. It also bears on the
  theory's prediction, that a wholly opaque self-model would abolish
  selfhood. It offers a graded version (C7), that opacity weakens
  self-identification. Its evidence is the realism of autoscopic
  phenomena and insight into them, which are reports, so it does not
  answer [NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md)'s open question of how transparency could be tested
  apart from reports of selfhood. It names no manipulation of opacity.
- **[THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md) ([LIT-453](../literature.d/LIT-453.md), [NOTE-377](NOTE-377.md)).** Gallagher's minimal self has two
  parts, ownership and agency, which come apart. Blanke and Metzinger
  split ownership instead, into part and global, and make agency no part
  of the minimal self (C8). The two views disagree on what the minimal
  self is made of, not on any finding. Second, the paper calls
  out-of-body experience "an error through misidentification". That is
  misidentification of the body one identifies with. On the immunity
  principle as [NOTE-377](NOTE-377.md) states it, "I" used as object can misidentify, so
  this is not a counterexample to [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md)'s immunity clause. The paper
  says, though, that the strong 1PP is also pathological in out-of-body
  experience. It does not say whether that reaches "I" as subject.
- **[LIT-1009](../literature.d/LIT-1009.md) ([NOTE-827](NOTE-827.md)).** Haggard's review holds that
  involuntary movement gives ownership without agency (its C1). That agrees with
  this paper's thesis that a passive multisensory sense of owning a body
  suffices for minimal selfhood. The two reach it from opposite sides:
  Haggard by removing the motor command, Blanke and Metzinger by removing
  movement altogether.
- **What C8 rests on.** The thesis that agency is not constitutive is
  read off adults whose agency was intact and merely not used during the
  illusion, and patients who had agency before their lesions. The paper
  grants that agency "causally enables MPS in practically all humans".
  So the evidence shows that MPS can be altered without agency playing a
  part at the time. It does not show that a system that never had agency
  could have MPS. That further claim ("a disembodied, non-neural,
  artificial system possessing no effectors") is conceptual.
- **Sibling filings.** Lenggenhager et al. (2007), Botvinick and Cohen
  (1998), Apps and Tsakiris (2014) and Seth (2013) are filed on the same
  day. The first two are the primary studies behind C5 and C2. Their
  readings should check the paper's descriptions of them, which this
  reading did not do.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology. The remark on artificial systems is a conceptual
  possibility.

## Limitations

- An opinion piece in a short format. No effect sizes, sample sizes or
  statistics are given for the full-body illusions. The lesion series are
  small (7 and 12, and one count unreadable in the extracted text).
- **Box 1 is internally loose.** The three dimensions are claimed
  "necessary and sufficient" under the heading "Necessary conditions that
  are not sufficient". The next paragraph says "Perhaps one or more of
  them are not individually necessary." The text also says agency "might
  be a sufficient condition for MPS" (p. 12), so there is more than one
  sufficient route on the authors' own account.
- **The dimensions blur in the experiments.** The text labels Ehrsson's
  felt location at the camera "illusory self-identification" (p. 12).
  The Figure 3 caption says the same set-up "diminished
  self-identification with the virtual body". Being at the camera's
  position is self-location or the weak 1PP's origin on the paper's own
  definitions.
- The text cites Box 2 for opacity (p. 8), but Box 2 does not discuss it;
  it discusses insight.
- The claim that the rubber hand leaves MPS untouched rests on what those
  studies did not measure, not on a measurement that found it unchanged.
- The authors review their own work. The theory is largely Metzinger's,
  and the clinical series and one of the two experiments come from
  Blanke's group.

## Open questions

- Does the rubber-hand illusion really leave self-location and
  self-identification unchanged? It would take a study measuring the MPS
  dimensions during it.
- Can opacity of the body model be manipulated, apart from asking
  whether the experience feels real? The prediction of C7 needs it.
- Can the strong 1PP be manipulated experimentally? The paper predicts
  that multiple, spatially incongruent perspectives and prolonged
  exposure might do it (p. 12).
- Would a person with little or no history of bodily agency, such as
  someone with severe congenital paralysis, show full-body illusions?
  That would test C8 beyond what adult data can.
