# TERM-173: measurement naivety

## Definition

A46 §1, at the owner's U29: "an insufficient appreciation of the
relationship between a measurement, the phenomenon it represents, and the
consequences of using it to govern activity". Its six forms, each with the
entry that holds the corresponding point:

| Form | Mistaken inference | Held in |
|---|---|---|
| Representational | what is measurable exhausts what matters | [CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md) |
| Statistical | predictive accuracy establishes evaluative adequacy | [CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md) |
| Causal | improving the indicator improves its referent | [TERM-108](TERM-108.md) (proxy) |
| Performative | measurement does not transform the measured | [CLAIM-402](../claims.d/CLAIM-402.md), [TERM-108](TERM-108.md) (reactivity) |
| Organizational | local improvement establishes systemic improvement | [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md) |
| Normative | selecting an objective establishes its legitimacy | [CLAIM-1334](../claims.d/CLAIM-1334.md) |

## Use

In A46's chain, measurement naivety supplies an impoverished account of
success, greedy agency ([TERM-179](TERM-179.md)) pursues it, and institutional
sociopathy ([TERM-170](TERM-170.md)) is what the resulting arrangement can become.

## Not to be confused with

Goodhart's law, which concerns what happens once a measure is optimized.
Measurement naivety is prior: the measure can be inadequate before anyone
optimizes it.
