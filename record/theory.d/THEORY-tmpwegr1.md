# THEORY-tmpwegr1: Griffith, Redding and Van Reenen find, in a panel of manufacturing industries in twelve OECD countries over 1974–1990, that industries further behind their industry's productivity frontier grow faster the more R&D they do, so that R&D raises the rate at which frontier technology is absorbed as well as the rate of own innovation, and that for a country at about half the frontier's productivity over half of R&D's estimated return comes through this absorption

## Source

Griffith, Redding and Van Reenen (2004), [LIT-tmpzbgxy](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzbgxy.md); read in
[NOTE-tmp7qron](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7qron.md). A stand-in, for the core claim only, for Cohen and
Levinthal (1990), [LIT-tmpwa0ib](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwa0ib.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The interaction.** In 1801 country-industry-year observations,
  1974–1990, with country-industry fixed effects and year dummies,
  productivity growth rises with lagged R&D intensity times the lagged
  TFP gap: δ2 = 0.596 (s.e. 0.333) in the base specification, "positive
  and statistically significant at the 10% level"; 1.00 (0.346) with
  skills and hours adjustment; 0.815 (0.350) with human capital; 0.814
  (0.385) with R&D lagged two years (Table 2, p. 889). It could have been
  zero or negative.
- **The share.** From column (6), absorption is 54% of R&D's total effect
  for Finland, whose TFP the United States' is just over twice, 48% for
  Norway, 40% Italy, 14% the United States (Table 3, p. 891).
- **The authors' conclusion.** "R&D stimulates growth directly through
  innovation and also indirectly through technology transfer." (§VI,
  p. 893).

## What this does not say

- That R&D causes the catch-up; the causal reading is conditional, "Under
  the assumption that the association between productivity growth and R&D
  is causal" (p. 890).
- That little catch-up happens without own R&D and skills. The fall of the
  gap term from 0.080 to 0.013 once the interactions are in (Table 2,
  column 6) invites that reading; it is the reader's, not the authors'.
- Anything about firms, which are not observed, or about Cohen and
  Levinthal's cumulativeness, path dependence or lockout.
- What happens to absorption when a unit stops or outsources its R&D; no
  such case is in the sample.
