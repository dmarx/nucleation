# THEORY-895: Incentive sensitization: repeated drug exposure can make the mesolimbic 'wanting' system lastingly hyper-reactive to drug cues and contexts, so that cue-triggered incentive salience grows, persists for years and even increases during abstinence, while 'liking' need not rise and may fall; sensitized 'wanting' can oppose the person's cognitive desire, and incentive salience can run without any reportable change in feeling

## Source

Berridge and Robinson (2016), "Liking, wanting, and the
incentive-sensitization theory of addiction", *American Psychologist*
71(8):670–679 ([LIT-1461](../literature.d/LIT-1461.md)), read in full in [NOTE-1109](../notes.d/NOTE-1109.md) from
the NIH author manuscript (PMC5171207). It is unpaginated, so locations
are by section.

## What was actually shown

A review. The primary studies were not read here.

- **The dissociation it starts from.** Dopamine depletion left 'liking'
  reactions to sugar "completely normal" while abolishing motivation, and
  stimulation raised 'wanting' without raising 'liking' (introduction;
  held in [THEORY-893](THEORY-893.md)).
- **Sensitization, and its independence from pleasure.** "Once induced,
  sensitization is very long lasting, and possibly even permanent."
  "‘Liking’, by contrast, need not increase with sensitization, and may
  even decrease" (Addiction Distorts ‘Wanting’ Versus ‘Liking’).
- **Independence from the person's goals.** Incentive salience "can occur
  either in opposition to a cognitive desire or even unconsciously in
  absence of any cognitive desire". Sensitized 'wanting' persists "even if
  the person cognitively doesn’t want to take drugs, doesn’t expect the
  drugs to be very pleasant, and even long after withdrawal symptoms have
  subsided" (after Figure 1; Addiction Distorts).
- **Reactive and context-gated.** "A sensitized dopamine system is not
  always hyper-active, but it is hyper-reactive to drug cues and
  contexts", and a novel test context can prevent its expression
  (Addiction Distorts; Disentangling Reports).
- **It grows in abstinence.** "if anything, sensitization grows for some
  time during abstinence", which the authors say a withdrawal account
  cannot explain (Disentangling Reports).
- **In people.** Cue-triggered striatal responses are enhanced in addicts.
  PET studies report that "the greater the cue-induced dopamine release
  the greater the craving" (Sensitization in Human Addiction, quoting
  Leyton and Vezina 2013).
- **Beyond drugs, weakly.** Sensitization "may in some cases occur without
  drugs", and dopamine agonists in Parkinson's disease produce
  compulsions without reported pleasure that fade when the drug stops
  (Other Behavioral Addictions?).

What could have come out otherwise: cue reactivity in abstinent addicts
fading with withdrawal; relapse tracking expected pleasure; hedonic
reactions rising with pursuit.

## What this does not say

- **That every addiction is sensitization.** It is "specifically meant to
  explain individuals who have near-compulsive levels of urge to take
  drugs" (Addiction Distorts), and "only about 30% of people who take
  cocaine" become long-term addicts (Individual vulnerability).
- **That behavioural addictions are sensitization.** The authors call the
  evidence "emerging" and the mechanism "not yet understood".
- **That 'wanting' is unvalenced.** The same circuitry produces "fearful
  salience" (Motivational Salience in Desire Versus Dread). The theory is
  about the strength of the pull, not its sign.
- **That the human evidence measures dopamine directly.** "most reports of
  such hyper-reactivity used fMRI measures, which do not directly measure
  dopamine" (Sensitization in Human Addiction).
- **Anything about organizations.**

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

The record holds sensitization only as Berridge and Robinson's review
reports it; [NOTE-1109](../notes.d/NOTE-1109.md) read none of the primary studies. No primary study
the record reads shows, in animals, a lasting rise in cue-triggered
pursuit after intermittent exposure with taste reactivity flat and
expression gated by context, or, in people, cue-triggered dopamine release
measured by PET that tracks craving while hedonic ratings stay flat. A
split into what the authors propose would free nothing: [CLAIM-438](../claims.d/CLAIM-438.md),
[CLAIM-437](../claims.d/CLAIM-437.md) and [CLAIM-447](../claims.d/CLAIM-447.md) use sensitization as a fact, and the last two rest
on Proposed claims besides.
