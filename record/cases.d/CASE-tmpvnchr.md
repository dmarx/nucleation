# CASE-tmpvnchr: Chinese martial traditions through the Cultural Revolution: practices restricted, teachers persecuted and teaching driven underground, then after 1978 a reconstruction of Shaolin martial arts through heritage policy and tourism in which local practitioners and authorized institutions dispute who carries the tradition

## The case

The owner, at U37: "another potentially interesting case to examine in
the neighborhood of the martial arts material is the (various)
perspectival interruption(s) caused by the Chinese Cultural Revolution."

The Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) included the campaign against the
"Four Olds", in which teachers, traditional authorities, cultural
practices and artefacts could be attacked. For Shaolin martial arts the
record has one registered source, Su ([LIT-tmp8mi9i](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8mi9i.md), unread), whose
abstract reads them as having served imperial authorities before 1949,
been "restricted and forbidden to practise in Mao's era from 1949 to
1978", and been reconstructed since 1978 under UNESCO's heritage
conventions, China's heritage policy and tourism, as "Shaolin Kungfu" in
the service of contemporary political and economic needs, with "experts"
and local residents positioned unequally. Su's period of restriction is
the whole Mao era, not only the Cultural Revolution.

The exchange added particulars the record has not checked: that
state-led standardization of martial arts began before 1966 (the
24-form taijiquan routine of 1956); that official wushu demonstrations
abroad resumed from 1974; that a local practitioner recalled practising in
secret during the Cultural Revolution and demonstrating for visiting
Japanese martial artists in 1979; and that the 1982 film *The Shaolin
Temple* widened public interest in the monastery. They are kept as the
exchange's.

## What it shows

A37 separated four interruptions in one period:

- **A, the teacher remains but teaching becomes dangerous.** "The skill
  hasn't necessarily vanished. But its **capacity for social
  reproduction** has been drastically reduced." Enactment and reproductive
  interruption ([CLAIM-tmpj73lm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpj73lm.md)).
- **B, the techniques persist but their authorization changes**, from a
  master–disciple relation to standardized instruction by a coach. "**the
  same outwardly recognizable martial practice can become embedded in a
  different organization of significance and authority**." That is
  [CLAIM-tmp8lheg](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8lheg.md)'s character kept apart from its lineage and its
  institution.
- **C, the practice survives in a marginal carrier.** Local practitioners,
  monastic authorities and heritage institutions each claimed the
  tradition on different grounds. "**Which survivals were subsequently
  authorized to count as the continuation of the tradition?**" A practice
  may keep historical continuity and lose the authority to represent it.
- **D, reconstruction becomes a source of authenticity.** Restored
  buildings, schools, films and tourism shape what later practitioners
  learn as authentic, which is [TERM-027](../terms.d/TERM-027.md)'s cultural attractor and
  [CLAIM-tmp6bxtr](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6bxtr.md)'s contest over the test, in a heritage regime.

## What it does not show

- **Equivalence with ordinary interruption.** A37: "The human costs are
  morally central. Any comparison to ordinary cognitive interruption
  concerns specific structural relations, not equivalence of experience or
  consequence."
- **One deletion and one restoration.** A37: "The historical processes are
  neither one uninterrupted tradition nor one clean deletion followed by
  restoration."
- **The particulars above, or Su's argument.** Su is registered unread,
  and the taijiquan study A37 cited for the change from shifu to coach was
  not identified and is not filed.
- **Standing.** The period and the reconstruction are documented; the
  four-way reading is the exchange's.
