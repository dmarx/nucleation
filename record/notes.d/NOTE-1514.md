# NOTE-1514: Klyubin, Polani & Nehaniv — Keep your options open

## Contribution

It recasts empowerment ([LIT-1965](../literature.d/LIT-1965.md)) as an interventional quantity, adds
*contextual* empowerment with "objective" (state) and "subjective" (sensor
history) contexts and a constructible context automaton, and tests the idea
in three scenarios: cart-pole, sensor evolution, and a hardware robot.

## Key insight

"Empowerment quantifies (1) a potential rather than an actual information flow
in the system … (2) it looks at information that could be generated
specifically by the agent itself" (p. 11). It is a property of the
embodiment, not of any controller: "it does not depend on a particular
action-selection mechanism" (p. 11).

## Assumptions

- The perception–action loop as a causal Bayesian network, arrows read "as
  actual causal mechanisms" (p. 2); actions chosen through an independent
  source Z_t, so information found downstream must have come from it.
- **n-step empowerment (eq. 1, p. 3):** ℰ(Aⁿ_t → S_{t+n}) = max_{p(a_t, …,
  a_{t+n−1})} I(A_t, …, A_{t+n−1}; S_{t+n}), open-loop ("the actuation
  distribution is selected 'blindly'"); feedback variants are possible "though
  the computation becomes more intricate".
- **Contextual empowerment (eq. 2):** ℰ(Aⁿ_t → S_{t+n} | C) = Σ_c p(c)
  ℰ(Aⁿ_t → S_{t+n} | C = c), C a non-descendant of the actions. "This can be
  interpreted as the channel capacity with side information known to the
  sender … and the receiver."
- Finite variables; capacity by Blahut's algorithm.

## Key results

- **Eq. (3), p. 4:** contextual ≥ context-free, since the context-free case is
  the one that ignores the side information. (Re-derived: for actions
  independent of C, I(A; S | C) = I(A; S, C) ≥ I(A; S), so Σ_c p(c) max
  I(A; S | C = c) ≥ max Σ_c p(c) I(A; S | C = c) ≥ max I(A; S).)
- **Objective against subjective (p. 3).** State-specific empowerment, which
  "implies access to 'objective' state information", is "never smaller (but in
  general larger)" than the global value; the agent's own sensor-action
  history "constitutes an upper limit on what the agent can possibly know"
  (p. 4). A context automaton is a finite-memory filter of that history,
  generalizing ε-machines; its quality is the gain over context-free
  empowerment, computable without outside variables.
- **Cart-pole (pp. 4–7).** Two actions ±10 N held for 3τ, 10 actions, the
  pole's angle observed in 101 bins; ensembles of 5⁴ perturbed initial
  states. Empowerment drops to zero past a critical angle (Figure 2). Over
  angle and angular speed, the maximum is off-centre, 0.07 bit above upright
  (Figure 3), but those states "are unreachable through the intrinsic dynamics
  of the agent", like Garden-of-Eden states. A greedy controller choosing the
  action whose successor state has the higher 10-step empowerment "performs
  classic pole-balancing" (Figure 4), a horizon of 1 + 10 steps and "no
  'overarching plan'".
- **Sensor evolution (pp. 7–9).** As in 2005: blobs to arcs at about d = 12,
  read as "analogous to a phase transition" (Figure 5); arced sensors present
  but inferior below d = 11.
- **AIBO (pp. 9–11).** Head tilt as action, infra-red distance as sensor,
  1,000 actions with a book placed for 100 of every 200. Over |A|, |S| ∈
  {2, …, 15}: context-free 0.52–2.03 bits, book-contextual 0.61–2.15,
  best evolved two-state automaton 0.56–2.15, with fitness ℰ(A → S | C) −
  I(A; C) to penalize dependence induced by undersampling. Where the book
  matters, the automaton's gain approaches the book's (Figure 7).
- **Discussion.** Empowerment identifies states from which the agent can best
  "home into a suddenly emerging goal" (p. 12): from upright the pole can be
  made to fall right; not conversely. The horizon is "the main free
  variable". A weak form of the principle, organisms approximating
  informationally optimal dynamics, is proposed; a strong one, empowerment
  computed by biology, is "unlikely".

## Claims

| C3 | The highest-empowerment cart-pole states are not reachable under the agent's own dynamics | moderate | numerical, stated as robust to parameters (p. 6) |

## Concepts

- **Context** — a variable of non-descendants of the actions used as side
  information.
- **Context automaton** — a finite-state machine fed (S_t, A_t) whose state
  serves as context.
- **Open-loop empowerment** — capacity with actions chosen without feedback.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md).**
  - *Reachability against sustainability.* The cart-pole shows state-wise
    empowerment and what the agent can sustain coming apart: the maxima are
    unreachable, and the agent's own dynamics settle on upright. This is the
    claim's point that the viable alternatives, not states taken one at a
    time, are what count ([CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)), found inside the empowerment
    literature.
  - *A partial order in empowerment's own terms.* Objective ≥ subjective
    (history) ≥ automaton ≥ context-free is an ordering by what the agent can
    read, which is [CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md)'s note of 2026-11-02 and [CLAIM-537](../claims.d/CLAIM-537.md)'s I ≥ III
    ≥ II, in bits.
  - *Not a defeater.* Empowerment is a scalar, but it is horizon-dependent
    ("the main free variable") and ignores cost and viability constraints
    unless they are built into the channel, so it does not fix the claim's
    other dimensions.
- **[CLAIM-537](../claims.d/CLAIM-537.md).** The claim's three conditions have an information
  analogue here: the side-information capacity (eq. 2) is the adjustable
  case with the agent reading the context, context-free is the static case,
  and the automaton is the observed case with a bounded reader. The
  correspondence is of structure; the paper has no tolerance model.
- **[TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md).** The AIBO result is a constructed legibility: an internal
  context that comes to carry an external distinction only because the
  distinction modulates the agent's influence ("if it does not modulate the
  influence of the agent on the world, the context change is not considered
  relevant", p. 13). That is legibility relative to a task the agent did not
  choose, the capacity of its own channel.
- **No instruction for ML practice** (empowerment as an RL intrinsic reward
  came later and is not in this paper).

## Limitations

- Computation needs a "detachable" world model (p. 11); the AIBO
  experiment was arranged so the causal effect could be read from
  observation.
- Each scenario is one small model; no comparison against other intrinsic
  utilities.
- Open-loop throughout.

## Open questions

Closed-loop and interleaved empowerment; how a sustainable system's
horizon is set; whether actuator evolution under energetic costs shows the
selective pressure the authors predict (p. 13).
