# QUESTION-379: When does a coherent family of local observations and constrained interactions correspond to an object the system can construct or sustain, that is, when is such a presheaf representable in a category of realizable systems, and when it is, is the object constructible within the available resources?

## Why it is a question

A (§6): "Suppose we have a collection of local observations and
interactions arranged into a presheaf F: 𝒞^op ⟶ Set." "We can ask whether
there exists an object A such that F ≅ Hom_𝒞(−,A). If so, F is
representable." "But not every presheaf is representable. And in a
category of physically realizable constrained systems, asking whether a
given relational profile is representable becomes a substantive
question." A separates three situations: a collection of local
descriptions "may be internally compatible. It may also support a
coherent global mathematical description. Yet there may be no physically
realizable object in the chosen category that has the desired relational
profile." A's guard: "sheaf gluing and representability are different
conditions. The existence of a global section does not generally imply
representability, and representability alone does not establish physical
feasibility."

A's two questions:

> When does an apparently coherent family of constrained interactions
> correspond to an object that the system can actually construct or
> sustain?

> When such a realization exists, is it constructible within the available
> computational and physical resources?

The record holds the separation and not the question. [CLAIM-264](../claims.d/CLAIM-264.md): "being
representable and having a global section are independent." [THEORY-032](../theory.d/THEORY-032.md):
Yoneda identifies an object "among objects the category already has", so
a profile no object has is outside what it identifies. [CLAIM-471](../claims.d/CLAIM-471.md) keeps
gluing apart from the physical generation of the relations glued. On the
resource side, [CLAIM-569](../claims.d/CLAIM-569.md) separates the costs of constructing,
maintaining and moving into a form, and [CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md) separates assembly,
persistence and regeneration; neither asks whether a specified profile has
a realization at all. [CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md)'s recursion, that constraints are
themselves realized and maintained, is the frame in which the category of
realizable systems is not given in advance.

## What would answer it

- **A representability criterion for a stated category of realizable
  systems**: a condition on a presheaf of local observations, checkable
  from the observations, under which some realizable object has exactly
  that profile; or, for a stated class, a proof that some glued profiles
  have no realization.
- **An instance in a declared toy**: a family of local constraints on
  folding modules or constraint networks that glues to a global section
  of the observation presheaf while no assembly in the declared class
  realizes it.
- **The resource version**: for representable profiles, the least cost of
  constructing a representing object, under one of the declared resource
  laws ([TERM-114](../terms.d/TERM-114.md)), and a case where it exceeds the budget though the
  object exists.

## What would not answer it

- **A failure of gluing.** That is local incompatibility, which the record
  already handles ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md); [CLAIM-538](../claims.d/CLAIM-538.md)); the question is about profiles
  that glue.
- **Representability in a category of all mathematical models.** The
  question is about a category of what can be built or sustained, and the
  answer turns on which category that is.
