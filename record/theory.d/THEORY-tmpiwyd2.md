# THEORY-tmpiwyd2: Walking through a doorway into a new room, rather than as far within one, lowers recognition of an object being carried, through retrieval interference between the event models of the two rooms that both hold it, not through a longer retention interval, a passing cost of updating, or loss of the encoding context; outside the originating paradigm, with passive movement and no extra load, it has not appeared

## Source

Radvansky & Copeland (2006), [LIT-1392](../literature.d/LIT-1392.md); Radvansky, Krawietz & Tamplin
(2011), [LIT-1384](../literature.d/LIT-1384.md); Pettijohn & Radvansky (2016), [LIT-1385](../literature.d/LIT-1385.md); each read in
full ([NOTE-tmpljxnu](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpljxnu.md), [NOTE-tmpktq6q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpktq6q.md), [NOTE-tmpjvmd8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpjvmd8.md)). McFadyen et al. (2021),
[LIT-1387](../literature.d/LIT-1387.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmph147i](../notes.d/NOTE-tmph147i.md), for the limits. Page numbers are
the journals'.

## What was actually shown

- **The pattern.** In desktop virtual rooms, a probe for the carried object
  drew more errors and slower answers after a doorway than after crossing
  a large room (2006 Experiment 2: errors .05 to .14); the object just set
  down was not affected. "Thus, making a spatial shift disrupted processing
  of currently relevant information" ([LIT-1392](../literature.d/LIT-1392.md), p. 1154). The same pattern
  held in both 2016 experiments, "largely confined to the associated
  probes" ([LIT-1385](../literature.d/LIT-1385.md), p. 2123).
- **Alternatives that could have won and did not.**
  - Retention interval: doorway trials were not slower to travel; if
    anything the no-shift trials took longer ([LIT-1385](../literature.d/LIT-1385.md), Experiment 1).
  - A passing cost of updating: a 3-second pause before the probe left the
    effect unchanged ([LIT-1385](../literature.d/LIT-1385.md), Experiment 2), against the 2006 paper's own
    effort-of-updating explanation.
  - Loss of the encoding context: returning to the room where the object
    was picked up did not help (A′ .90 against .92 for a plain shift,
    p = .20), and two new rooms were worse than going and returning
    ([LIT-1384](../literature.d/LIT-1384.md), Experiment 3). Memory tracked "the number of new areas
    entered" ([LIT-1384](../literature.d/LIT-1384.md), p. 1640).
- **The account.** On the event horizon model the carried object is in two
  event models, the room where it was picked up and the room it is carried
  into, and a recognition probe must choose between them: "The effect is a
  result of the competition between these two event models during
  retrieval, even though they are both pointing to the same response"
  ([LIT-1385](../literature.d/LIT-1385.md), p. 2125).
- **The limits.** In head-mounted virtual reality with passive movement and
  identical rooms, doorways did not lower hits (Bayes factors 3–6 for the
  null); under a counting load they raised false alarms to recombined
  probes, which the authors read as interference ([LIT-1387](../literature.d/LIT-1387.md), Experiments 1
  and 2). Watching or walking a curtained hallway did not affect memory for
  a 16-item spatial arrangement (Experiments 3 and 4).

## What this does not say

- **That people forget their intentions when they enter a room.** No study
  here tests a goal or intention. The task is recognizing colour–shape
  labels for two objects, and the effect is a few percentage points of
  errors.
- **That the effect is robust.** It replicates within one lab and its
  paradigm, with few trials per cell (as few as 5 or 6), and with room
  changes that also changed wall pattern and room size. The one
  independent multi-experiment test in the record changed several things
  at once and found no loss of hits. Independent positive studies exist
  (Lawrence & Peterson 2016; Seel et al. 2019, cited in [LIT-1387](../literature.d/LIT-1387.md)) and are
  not held here.
- **That interference has been shown.** It is reached by eliminating two
  alternatives. No condition yet separates it from deactivation of the
  prior event model by an opposite prediction.
- **That it is event segmentation theory's mechanism.** EST
  ([THEORY-tmpg9wtv](THEORY-tmpg9wtv.md)) predicts that what precedes a boundary becomes harder
  to retrieve because the model is reset. The cost here falls on what is
  carried across the boundary.
- **That location is irrelevant.** The return result says the encoding
  room does not reinstate access. On the authors' account each room entered
  is still a new event model, so the unit is room-bound. Whether a change of
  task without a doorway does the same (A37's proposal at [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md)) has not
  been tested in these papers.
