# LIT-1564: Chimeric origins and dynamic evolution of central carbon metabolism in eukaryotes

Santana-Molina, C., Williams, T. A., Snel, B. & Spang, A. (2025). Chimeric origins and dynamic evolution of central carbon metabolism in eukaryotes. *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 9(4): 613–627. doi:10.1038/s41559-025-02648-0.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmphab0m.*

- **LECA had the whole pathway set**, with redundant enzymes at several steps.
- **Asgard did contribute.** ADPGK, APGM, enolase and RPIA nest with Asgard
  (bootstrap 39–97%); GPMI and pyruvate kinase do so ambiguously. Most are
  cytosolic, and most have since been replaced in many lineages.
- **The mitochondrion contributed more.** Seventeen alphaproteobacterial
  enzymes, ten of them in the TCA cycle, mostly mitochondrial
  (THEORY-tmpyr6q4).
- **Other donors, none dominant.** Planctomycetota–Verrucomicrobiota,
  Chlamydia, Fusobacteriota and others give single enzymes.
- **Origin no longer predicts compartment.** Host-derived enzymes work in
  the mitochondrion and symbiont-derived ones in the cytosol.
- **Syntrophy is consistent, not shown.** The cytosol–mitochondrion split is
  what every symbiogenetic model predicts.

## Why this is here

A1 ranked it fifth, for metabolic integration during eukaryogenesis, and for discriminating syntrophy-first from complexity-first scenarios. With [NOTE-159](../notes.d/NOTE-159.md), the record's only reading on eukaryogenesis.

The reading corrects both points. The paper bears on metabolic integration, but it does not discriminate syntrophy-first from complexity-first: it says nothing on timing, and its syntrophy support would hold under either. NOTE-tmppkn6v and NOTE-tmpe5t7q are now readings on eukaryogenesis too (NOTE-tmphab0m).
