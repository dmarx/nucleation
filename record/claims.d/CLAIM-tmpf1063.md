# CLAIM-tmpf1063: Durable authority among an organization's processes need not be a primitive: trust can be the temporal regularity of endorsement that policies earn by being right often, and the same loop entrenches whatever gained influence early

## The claim

A4 §5, reading the record's note on Montévil and Mossio ([LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)):
"For our model, that means distinguishing persistent authority structures
from transient endorsement updates". The owner, at U5: "why? I'd expect
persistent authority to be an emergent property here as 'right a lot'
policies become distinguished into 'trusted' policies."

A5 §1 granted it: "We don't need two kinds of authority, one transient and
one persistent. We need an authority-allocation process whose dynamics can
produce different degrees of persistence." With endorsements updated toward
a learned assessment of each operator's suitability,
Ė_ji = η_j[Q_ji − E_ji], "**Trust is then an emergent temporal regularity of
endorsement**, not an independently imposed authority category." Momentary
endorsement, established trust and institutionalized authority "are
descriptions of different dynamical regimes, not different ontological
primitives."

A6 §1B gave the knob, a memory kernel over the endorsed operator's track
record. "A slowly decaying memory kernel means established trust persists
despite occasional failures. A rapidly decaying kernel makes endorsement
highly responsive to recent behavior." And A6 §1C separated latent authority
from the influence currently exercised. "an operator can remain highly
*trusted* while being temporarily inactive."

A5 named the failure mode in the same loop. One chain is good policy, then
endorsement, then greater influence, then more chances to show competence.
The other is early advantage, then greater influence, then more visibility,
then greater endorsement. "This is how a system might develop both
well-calibrated expertise and undeserved authority."

The claim rests on [CLAIM-tmppy0ey](CLAIM-tmppy0ey.md). The slow route by which an operator's
authority changes, through others' endorsements, is the route by which
trust accumulates.

## What it does not say

- It does not say which loop dominates in a given organization. That is an
  empirical question about memory kernels and feedback.
- It does not give a criterion for when entrenched authority is capture and
  not merited trust. The record's nearest result is protective lock-in, a
  regime that "generates the conditions that justify its own continuation"
  ([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md), [CASE-036](../cases.d/CASE-036.md)). A11 §4 asked for a criterion grounded in the
  organization's own good. [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) disclaims that viability alone
  supplies one.
- It does not say the record holds a theory of credibility among
  contributors. [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md) asks the same question about the record's own
  contributors.
