# CLAIM-039: Affective states can be properties of organized systems, characterized functionally as Frijda characterizes emotions, without implying subjective experience

## The claim

U19: affective states as "properties of agents/systems rather than subjective
experience". U20 asked for Frijda's characterization to be mapped onto a
distributed corporate agent rather than relying on loose examples.

## Why it is set aside

The paper moved from affect to will by the evening of 2026-10-04. The
assistant also found prior art (A64): it "can no longer present genuinely
collective, non-phenomenal affect as the paper's principal novel conjecture".
Deferred, not rejected: it is a different paper.

## Note of 2026-10-12: grounds and a difficulty

[THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md) and [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md) now ground this claim. [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md) supplies the Frijda characterization the claim depends on (a mode of action readiness), not evidence that organizations meet it. [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md) holds that a group emotion, being a functional kind, "need not be felt", though "no actual case has yet been shown".

The difficulty: [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md) (Veit) identifies hedonic valence with a coordinated regulatory state. On that view a functional valence state is a felt one, which is the uncoupling this claim denies. It is not a ground, so no edge is written. It is limited to hedonic valence in animals, while this claim uses Frijda's action readiness. If the line is taken up again, it is the objection to answer first.
