## The boundary

None of the 22 is held in the Anthology of the SOTA. I grepped a clone of
its record/ taken 2026-10-09 (commit 1cffe8f) for every author, arXiv id and
title. GenEval and T2I-CompBench are named there only in prose inside other
entries, and the rest are not named at all.

The four text-to-image works are machine-learning papers. The anthology's
`generative-modeling` and `analysis-and-evaluation` topics could hold them,
so by default they belong there. They are filed here because the owner asked
for the manuscript's references to be filed in this record. Each carries
`anthology-candidate` last ([ADR-005](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-005.md)), which leaves the move to a later
decision.

Their topics here are the nearest this vocabulary has, and that fit is
loose. `representation-learning` stands for attribute binding in GenEval
and T2I-CompBench. `probabilistic-modeling` stands for the energy-based
composition and the conditional Schrödinger bridge. The vocabulary has no
word for evaluating generative models. I did not add one, because these
works are expected to leave for the anthology.

The other 18 fit existing words: `information-theory`, `mathematics`,
`probabilistic-modeling`, `contextuality`, `linguistics`,
`philosophy-of-language`, `social-science` and `cognition`. `linguistics`
is a loose fit for the two translation books. Its blurb names the study of
language itself, and translation theory is adjacent to that. If more
translation studies arrive, that is the second instance that would justify
a `translation` word.

## How deeply

Every entry is registered, not read. Bibliographic details were checked
against arXiv, Crossref and Open Library. Dates follow
[ADR-002](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-002.md), and books take the first edition's year, as [LIT-473](../../record/literature.d/LIT-473.md)
does. Five points need stating:

- **Takeaways of two kinds.** Where an abstract or a publisher's description
  was reachable, the takeaways come from it and say so. For the classics
  with no deposited abstract, they state what the work is standardly cited
  for, and say that too: Austin, Blackwell, Du Bois, Goffman, Gutt,
  Hofstadter, Lévi-Strauss, Saussure, Shannon, Wyner and Ziv, and Cover and
  Thomas.
- **T2I-CompBench.** The arXiv record 2307.06350 now carries the journal
  version, T2I-CompBench++. The title and authors filed are those of the
  conference version (v2), which is what the manuscript cites.
- **Saussure.** The 1916 Payot first edition could not be found in a
  catalogue record reachable from here. The source is the Open Library work
  record.
- **Gutt and Lévi-Strauss.** The years of the first editions (Blackwell
  1991, Plon 1958) were not checked against a catalogue.
- **Two-part and later-edition works.** Shannon's two parts have two DOIs,
  and the entry's DOI field holds part I. Cover and Thomas, and Gutt, are
  cited in their second editions, and the entries carry the DOI of the
  cited edition.

## What a reading should check

These entries deliberately say nothing about the manuscript. The owner wants
each work read first on its own merits, so that the reading is not tilted by
the use the owner expects to make of it. So a reading of any of these works
answers what the work itself establishes, and leaves the manuscript out. How
a work serves the manuscript is a later and separate question.

The text-to-image works still need a decision about where they belong:
either they move to the anthology, or the record gains a word for them. That
question is about their subject, not about the manuscript, and a reading
can settle it.
