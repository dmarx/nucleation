# NOTE-tmpl7udu: Perez-Zapata et al. — Meeting and Missing Minds: Children and Adults Use Alignment of Intuitions to Solve Pure Coordination Games

## Contribution

A developmental test of pure coordination without communication.

## Key results

1. **Against regress.** Hierarchical reasoning "risks a form of regress
   whereby each person will be trying to anticipate the other’s
   perspective" (p. 4).
2. **Earliest age.** "we tested children aged 4-5 because this is the
   earliest age at which previous evidence suggested children might be
   able to succeed on strongly-constrained coordination games." (p. 7).
3. **Finding.** "Present findings reveal for the first time that children
   as young as 4-5 years of age can solve pure coordination problems that
   have no single obvious answer." (p. 32).

## Assumptions

Alignment with same-age others measures coordination; the partner was a
puppet.

## Concepts

Pure coordination; alignment; group-based reasoning.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's view**, a qualification: tacit coordination appears from
  about 4–5, not 3; no three-year-olds were tested, the partner was a
  puppet, and which reasoning children used is not tested.
- **[THEORY-1630](../theory.d/THEORY-1630.md)**, a parallel: the authors present "we" reasoning as
  escaping the regress.

## Limitations

Preprint; puppet partner; the attribution of group-based reasoning to
five-year-olds is the authors' gloss on prior work.
