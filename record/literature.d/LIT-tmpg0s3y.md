# LIT-tmpg0s3y: Exposing Pluralistic Ignorance to Reduce Alcohol Use Among College Students

Schroeder, C. M. & Prentice, D. A. (1998). Exposing pluralistic ignorance
to reduce alcohol use among college students. *Journal of Applied Social
Psychology*, 28(23), 2150–2180. doi:10.1111/j.1559-1816.1998.tb01365.x.

## Key takeaways

- Telling entering students that their peers are privately less
  comfortable with campus drinking than they appear was followed by less
  self-reported drinking months later than a decision-making discussion
  was.
- The correction did not lower the comfort students attributed to the
  average student, relative to control. The authors infer that it
  weakened the norm's grip, measured as the moderating role of fear of
  negative evaluation, rather than moving the norm.
- They locate the consequential error in the perceived uniformity of
  peer opinion, the "illusion of universality", more than in its
  perceived centre.
- First-year men reduced pluralistic ignorance by revising their view of
  the average student, not their own attitude. That is the opposite
  direction from the second-year men of Prentice and Miller 1993
  (LIT-1370).

## Why this is here

CLAIM-415 cites Prentice and Miller 1993 (LIT-1370) for men who moved
their private attitudes toward a misperceived norm, the one result it
names of private change under a misperceived consensus. That paper is
Unavailable. This one, by Prentice and a student, restates the 1993
finding (p. 2153) and runs a longitudinal comparison of its own on the
same campus.
