# LIT-tmpddovi: The Problem of the Many

Peter Unger (1980), *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 5, 411–467 — DOI-10.1111/j.1475-4975.1980.tb00416.x

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- **The problem.** For an ordinary object with a fuzzy edge, many slightly
  different candidates have equal claim to be it.
- By parity, either all of them are clouds (many clouds) or none is (no
  clouds). Unger argues for the second, nihilist horn.
- It is the standard source of the problem; Lewis (1993; [LIT-tmp0l1qi](LIT-tmp0l1qi.md)), filed beside it, is
  the standard reply.

## Standing in the record

Filed for [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md), where a combined form's boundary lies. The record
answers with a gap-isolated block relative to a target ([CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md), [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)),
and a block's edge is not sharp: many nearly equal partitions pass the same
test. That is Unger's problem in the record's terms. The survey of the
Mathematics of Meaning corpus found the corpus's thermostat, with three
equally defensible system boundaries, to be the same problem at small
cardinality ([MOM-LIT-017](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-017.md)).

A reading should check whether the argument depends on the candidates being
equally good, which a priced criterion might break by ranking them.
