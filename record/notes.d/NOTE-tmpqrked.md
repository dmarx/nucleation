# NOTE-tmpqrked: Damasio & Damasio — Homeostatic feelings and the biology of consciousness

## Contribution

A compact statement of the Damasios' "new theory" of consciousness, the
same thesis as their book Feeling and Knowing (2021, cited as ref. 7) and as
the Neural Computation paper [LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md). What it adds to Damasio and Carvalho
(2013, [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)) is the step from feeling to consciousness, feelings as what
make every content conscious, and a physiological reason why feelings
differ in kind from exteroceptive images: in interoception the mapped
object can act on the map.

## Key insight

Ordinary images of the world tell you what is out there but not whose image
it is. Homeostatic feelings do both at once, because they are about the body
and are partly made of it: the gut that the brain maps can also reach the
mapping neurons directly, chemically and electrically. A continuous stream
of such feelings, even the faint feeling of simply existing, tags every
other content as belonging to this organism, and that tagging is what
consciousness is.

## Assumptions

- **A definition of consciousness.** Consciousness is the spontaneous
  identification of mind contents with an owner ("Consciousness"), not
  integration, scale of integration or attention. Everything else follows
  from this choice, and the essay does not argue for it beyond setting it
  against the alternatives it names.
- **Sensing versus minding.** Sensing needs no nervous system and "dispenses
  with representations"; minds are streams of mapped images and need nervous
  systems ("Sensing and minding").
- **Homeostatic feelings are conscious in themselves** ("How is
  consciousness made", paragraph 2): "Homeostatic feelings are not conscious
  because they borrow consciousness from some other process."
- **A threshold.** Feelings support continuous consciousness when above a
  threshold, and are generated continuously in the awake state.

## Key results

There are no results in the experimental sense. The claims and their
offered support:

- **The identification claim** ("How is consciousness made", paragraphs 1–5).
  Feelings provide "the identification of a particular mind with a
  particular organism"; self-consciousness builds on this when perceptions,
  memories and reasoning are connected to the organism and to its history.
  Support: argument from the definition.
- **The evolutionary claim** (paragraph 3). Homeostatic feelings were "the
  inaugural phenomena of consciousness", selected because their knowledge
  allowed life regulation to be guided "in an overt manner". Support: none
  given beyond the advantage stated.
- **The hybrid mechanism** ("How do living organisms generate homeostatic
  feelings?", Table 1). Interoceptive pathways: poorly myelinated or
  unmyelinated (Aδ/C) axons; non-synaptic as well as synaptic transmission;
  slow (seconds to hours) as well as fast time scales; blood–brain barrier
  absent or with gaps; monoamines, acetylcholine and neuropeptides.
  Exteroception, movement and cognition: well myelinated (Aα/Aβ),
  predominantly synaptic, very fast (μs–ms), a continuous barrier,
  glutamate and GABA. Support: the anatomical contrast, cited to Carvalho
  and Damasio (2021).
- **Coma** ("Impaired consciousness", paragraph 1). Focal damage to the
  posterior brainstem (nucleus tractus solitarius, parabrachial nucleus,
  periaqueductal grey) causes coma by compromising integrated body
  representations and "severing ... their links to the exteroceptive mental
  contents"; "small brainstem lesions can have a devastating effect on
  consciousness, whereas even extensive lesions of the cerebral cortex may
  not". Support: Parvizi and Damasio (2001, 2003).
- **Anaesthesia** (paragraph 2). General anaesthetics act on "the plain
  'sensing and detecting' process", the membrane level that is the first
  link of the chain to feeling, and so suspend interoception and
  exteroception together; they halt plant activity too. Support: Baluška et
  al. (2016).

## Concepts

- **homeostatic feelings**: feelings generated by life regulation, from
  interoception, distinguished from emotional feelings. Salient ones
  (hunger, thirst, pain, well-being, malaise, desire) and those near
  equilibrium ("feelings of life/existence").
- **sensing/detecting**: response to conditions, possible without a nervous
  system or representations; shared by bacteria and plants.
- **minding**: having representations ("mapped patterns" or "images") built
  with a nervous system.
- **hybrid**: of a feeling, being at once neural/representational and
  non-neural/bodily, because the mapped object and the map "commingle".

## Limitations

- **No data and no test.** The essay does not say what result would refute
  the identification claim.
- **The definition carries the argument.** A theory of access or of
  integration is not refuted by being told consciousness is something else.
- **Coma and anaesthesia do not separate the accounts.** Brainstem lesions and
  anaesthetics abolish arousal and every cortical process downstream as
  well, so their effects are what every theory predicts.
- **Plant evidence.** That anaesthetics halt plant activity shows they act on
  membranes; it does not show where in an animal their effect on
  consciousness lies.

## Open questions

- Can feelings of existence be shown to be present when exteroceptive
  contents are conscious, and absent when the same contents are processed
  without awareness? That is the test the "lending" claim invites.
- Would blocking interoceptive signalling while sparing arousal abolish
  consciousness of exteroceptive images, as C2 requires? Spinal or vagal
  deafferentation cases bear on this, and the essay does not discuss them.
