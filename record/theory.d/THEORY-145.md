# THEORY-145: Organizations that espouse revising their governing values characteristically fail to, because the theory-in-use people actually act on keeps premises tacit and tests self-sealing, and the resulting defensive routines make the inconsistency, and its undiscussability, undiscussable; so errors are corrected within the governing values (single-loop) while the values themselves go unexamined

## Source

Argyris (2002), [LIT-745](../literature.d/LIT-745.md), read in [NOTE-561](../notes.d/NOTE-561.md). His 1977 *HBR*
article, [LIT-739](../literature.d/LIT-739.md), is unread.

## What was actually shown

Argyris ([LIT-745](../literature.d/LIT-745.md)) states the theory and illustrates it with one
workshop.

- **The distinction.** Learning is detecting and correcting error.
  Single-loop correction keeps the governing values; double-loop correction
  changes them.
- **The mechanism.** People's espoused theories vary, while the theory they
  act on, Model I, does not. Model I aims at unilateral control, winning
  and suppressing negative feelings while appearing rational. It produces
  evaluations and attributions that are never illustrated or tested.
  Because it is skilled, it is unaware of itself. Organizational defensive
  routines (mixed messages, made undiscussable, the undiscussability made
  undiscussable) protect people from embarrassment and from discovering its
  causes. Individual and organizational routines sustain each other.
- **The illustration.** Thirty-four CEOs, asked to help a role-played COO
  who admitted his errors, gave abstract advice. They attributed his
  failure to learn to his closedness without testing that, and blamed him
  as he had blamed his board.

The theory exposes itself to refutation: Argyris says a theory-in-use is a
design that should admit no exceptions, so one would disconfirm it. No
independent test is reported.

## What this does not say

- **Not that single-loop learning is bad.** Model I may be preferred for
  routine, single-loop work. The claim concerns embarrassing, non-routine
  problems.
- **Not that structure fixes it.** The claim is the reverse: egalitarian
  structures drift back to control unless individual theories-in-use
  change, and vice versa.
- **Not independently established.** Universality rests on the author's
  earlier studies. The case is interpreted by the facilitator who ran it,
  and the quasi-experiment is proposed, not run.

- **Drift ([THEORY-144](THEORY-144.md)).** Rasmussen's defences erode unseen because

  declared; they are different mechanisms with a similar result.
