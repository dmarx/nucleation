# CASE-133: American party realignment: each major party kept its name and organizational continuity across the twentieth century while its coalition and its stance on civil rights changed, and the change began in state parties, unions and constituencies before it reached national leaders

## The case

The owner, at U29, named among "super stable" organizational
perspectives "the modern democratic party being the torchbearers for the
party that was historically called the republicans (i.e. the party of
lincoln) vs. the modern republicans aligning with the historical
confederacy, entrenched racist currents in the deep south". A29 corrected
the framing: "The parties did not exchange their memberships like two
teams swapping uniforms. Different political coalitions developed,
migrated, split, and recombined through institutions that retained their
historical names." The owner, at U30, took that sentence up as the point
where the record "touches on character theory".

The documented part, from Schickler ([LIT-1380](../literature.d/LIT-1380.md), unread): the
Republican Party is organizationally continuous with Lincoln's, the
Democratic Party with the nineteenth-century Democrats. Their coalitions
and their positions on civil rights changed over the twentieth century.
From the late 1930s, Northern state Democratic parties, industrial unions
and new urban constituencies, Black voters among them, tied New Deal
economic liberalism to civil rights, and Northern Democrats in Congress
moved before national party leaders did. The Civil Rights Act of 1964 and
the Voting Rights Act of 1965 came after that shift, and white Southern
voters moved toward the Republican Party over the decades that followed.

Two works the exchange set beside it, both unread: Acharya, Blackwell and
Sen ([LIT-1388](../literature.d/LIT-1388.md)) on the persistence of attitudes in former slaveholding
counties, which raises the question of transmission against association,
and Taylor ([LIT-1381](../literature.d/LIT-1381.md)) on the women's movement in abeyance, a
perspective that persisted dormant between its active periods.

## What it shows

- **Carrier and character come apart.** A party's name, organization and
  legal continuity persist while its coalition and stance change. In
  [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md)'s terms, the carrier's continuity did not guarantee
  continuity of its operative perspective: some orientations and
  constituencies persisted, others migrated or transformed, and the
  parties' internal perspectives were heterogeneous throughout. An
  orientation can acquire a new carrier. A40: "A
  perspective may acquire a new institutional carrier. A carrier may
  acquire a new perspective. And that acquisition may transform both."
- **Persistence by transmission, not resemblance.** [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md) says a
  collective that replaces its members persists by causal transmission.
  Realignment is the case where the organization was transmitted and its
  perspectives were only partly, so organizational persistence and perspectival
  persistence need separate criteria.
- **Change from below.** On Schickler's account the change ran from state
  parties and constituencies up to national leaders, which is
  [CLAIM-423](../claims.d/CLAIM-423.md)'s point that components on different timescales hold
  different authority.

## What it does not show

- That the parties swapped perspectives, or that a modern party is the
  "torchbearer" of a historical one. The replies declined both, and the
  case does not support them.
- That attitudes in the South today are transmitted from slavery rather
  than associated with it. Acharya et al. argue for transmission; the
  record has not read them.
- The owner's other U29 cases (religious fundamentalism, anti-vax,
  fascism, progressive social liberalism, the Southern strategy, Mormon
  control of Utah). No sources for them were registered, and none is
  filed.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the sources, after the readings

- **The documented part is still unread.** Schickler ([LIT-1380](../literature.d/LIT-1380.md)) and Taylor
  ([LIT-1381](../literature.d/LIT-1381.md)) are now Unavailable: no lawful copy was reached. The
  publisher's description of Schickler says the new alignment had
  crystallized "by the mid-1940s"; the case's "From the late 1930s" is
  unconfirmed.
- **Acharya et al., read ([LIT-1388](../literature.d/LIT-1388.md)).** They give the case its best evidence
  that carrier and character come apart: high-slave counties were more
  Democratic while "the Democratic Party was the racially conservative
  party" (p. 634), and are more Republican now; the slavery effect on the
  Democratic vote weakens around 1950. The case's line that they "argue
  for transmission" is accurate.
- **Sewell ([LIT-1383](../literature.d/LIT-1383.md))** makes the case's point for the state, not the
  parties: one constitution across sharply distinct party systems (p. 24).
