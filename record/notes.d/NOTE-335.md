# NOTE-335: Putnam — Psychological Predicates

*Skimmed: an excerpt of the 1975 reprint, not the 1967 original. Enough to
state the hypothesis and condition (3) verbatim; not enough to say what the
elided text contains.*

## Contribution

It is the founding statement of machine-state functionalism about pain. Pain
is the state of an organism with a certain functional organisation, offered
as an empirical hypothesis that beats the brain-state hypothesis on
plausibility. The hypothesis also carries the first anti-nesting principle in
the functionalist literature, introduced by stipulation.

## Key insight

The brain-state theorist must find one physical-chemical state shared by
every possible pain-feeler: mammal, octopus, extraterrestrial. The
functionalist needs only a shared organisation, and behaviour is evidence for
shared organisation and weak evidence for shared physics. Once pain is
organisation, though, anything organised the right way qualifies, swarms
included. Condition (3) is where Putnam stops that.

## Assumptions

- **Probabilistic automata** with sensory inputs and motor outputs. States
  are "specified only 'implicitly'", by the transition probabilities of the
  Machine Table.
- **Description.** A Description of a system S is any true statement that S
  has states S1…Sn related to one another and to inputs and outputs by a
  given Machine Table. That table is S's Functional Organization relative to
  the Description.
- **Method.** Philosophy puts forward "schemata for hypotheses", not
  finished theories. Identification is accepted for its fruitful predictions
  and questions.

## Key results

The hypothesis, verbatim from the excerpt:

> (1) All organisms capable of feeling pain are Probabilistic Automata.
> (2) Every organism capable of feeling pain possesses at least one
> Description of a certain kind (i.e., being capable of feeling pain is
> possessing an appropriate kind of Functional Organization).
> (3) No organism capable of feeling pain possesses a decomposition into
> parts which separately possess Descriptions of the kind referred to in (2).
> (4) For every Description of the kind referred to in (2), there exists a
> subset of the sensory inputs such that an organism with that Description
> is in pain when and only when some of its sensory inputs are in that
> subset.

- The organisation must include something like a "preference function", an
  "inductive logic" and "pain sensors" whose inputs have high disvalue.
- "The purpose of condition (3) is to rule out such 'organisms' (if they can
  count as such) as swarms of bees as single pain-feelers."
- Condition (1) "is, in fact, empty, since everything is a Probabilistic
  Automaton under some Description".
- The functional-state hypothesis "is not incompatible with dualism": a
  body plus a soul can be a probabilistic automaton.
- Against the brain-state hypothesis: one psychological predicate applicable
  to mammal and octopus with different physical correlates collapses it, and
  "it seems to me overwhelmingly probable that we can do this".

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Pain is a functional state of a whole organism, not a brain state | hypothesis, argued as more plausible | comparative plausibility argument (octopus, extraterrestrials) |
| C2 | No pain-feeler has parts that separately possess the pain-capable Description | stipulation | one sentence of motive, the bees (in the passage read) |
| C3 | The functional hypothesis is less vague and more investigable than the brain-state hypothesis | assertion | "I contend, in passing" |
| C4 | The functional state explains pain behaviour, not merely correlates with it | assertion | methodological section |

## Concepts

- **Description / Functional Organization / Total State** — relative to a
  Machine Table; one physical system can realise many.
- **Anti-nesting condition (3)** — a condition on the *organisation* of
  parts, not on whether they feel pain.

- **IIT ([LIT-185](../literature.d/LIT-185.md))**: the exclusion postulate is a later anti-nesting rule.
  Putnam's gives the verdict to the parts and denies it to the whole; IIT's
  gives it to the level of maximal integration.

## Bearing on the record

It is the anti-nesting principle itself, so a THEORY drawing the
Minsky–Schwitzgebel bridge should quote it from here, in Putnam's words,
and not in Schwitzgebel's paraphrase. Clause (1)'s emptiness ("everything
is a Probabilistic Automaton under some Description") is the liberalism
that (3) is patching. That is worth quoting beside Block's case.

## Limitations

- An excerpt of a reprint, with elisions, and with summaries by the course
  between excerpts. Whether the 1967 printing differs is unknown.
- The text just before the bees sentence is elided ("…"), so this reading
  cannot confirm that no other motive for (3) is given.
