# NOTE-tmpdb4eb: Dorsch, Coeckelbergh and Vierkant (2026) — Does AI undermine democratic deliberation?

## Contribution

A mechanism linking engagement optimization to the erosion of epistemic
agency through metacognitive confidence, offered in place of the isolation
version of the filter-bubble story; a reliabilist, epistemic-luck framing of
the harm; and design proposals. Author contributions (p. 17): Dorsch
"developed the central theoretical framework and drafted the manuscript".

## Key results

1. **Thesis (abstract).** AI threatens deliberative democracy not by
   isolating citizens in filter bubbles, "but because it hijacks the
   capacities through which beliefs are evaluated".
2. **The strong filter-bubble hypothesis fails (p. 6).** Users "are
   routinely exposed to cross-cutting political content … often more
   frequently than consumers of traditional media".
3. **Engagement is fluency (p. 8).** "Optimizing engagement therefore also
   involves optimizing processing fluency."
4. **Untested by its own account (p. 9).** "Our claim is straightforward,
   though not yet directly tested empirically"; "The argument is therefore
   theoretical rather than empirical"; "we proceed on the conditional
   assumption that such effects do occur".
5. **Second-order appraisal (p. 10).** Fluency "operates not primarily at
   the level of first-order endorsement of particular claims, but rather at
   the level of second-order appraisal"; "fluency does not make opposing
   claims more persuasive; it makes their rejection feel more epistemically
   warranted". Curation "is not simply altering which beliefs individuals
   hold, but reshaping the standards by which beliefs are assessed".
6. **Asymmetric credibility (p. 11).** "the real filter bubbles are not
   bubbles of exposure, but bubbles of asymmetric credibility and
   evaluative framing", set beside Nguyen's epistemic bubbles and echo
   chambers.
7. **Mindshaping (pp. 12–13).** "To shape the mind is not merely to
   influence what people think or how they behave, but to reshape the
   standards by which thinking itself is evaluated"; "Processing fluency
   thereby becomes the operative standard by which the reliability of one's
   own epistemic stance is tacitly assessed".
8. **Curation answerable to truth or to expectation (p. 13–14).** Maps and
   signage are useful "because their curation is guided by ground truth"; a
   map organised around where users expect things to be makes arrival "a
   matter of luck".
9. **Reflective luck (p. 14).** "no phenomenological difference exists"
   between metacognitive feelings from reliable processes and from systemic
   biases; the mechanism "operates below the threshold of awareness", so
   users "remain reflectively unable to determine whether the underlying
   process tracks truth".
10. **The harm named (p. 4).** "not merely a crisis of misinformation … but
    a crisis of epistemic agency".
11. **Remedies (pp. 15–16).** Friction must be context-sensitive, since some
    "may themselves be epistemically corrosive"; an optional "Socratic
    mode"; Pol.is-like platforms, criticised for "prioritizing consensus
    over justificatory reasoning" and reducing deliberation "to preference
    aggregation".
12. **Corroboration (pp. 11–12).** Gen Z polarization and Blue Rose
    Research's 2024 poll, attributed by its researchers to TikTok; a
    footnote concedes it does not measure affective polarization.

## Assumptions

- Engagement is driven by, and so optimizes, processing fluency (p. 8),
  asserted from the fluency literature, not shown for recommender systems.
- Deliberative democracy depends on intellectual humility.
- Reliabilism about justification, for the epistemic-luck argument.
- Content mocking an opposing group is processed fluently by the in-group.

## Concepts

Processing fluency; truth effect; metacognitive confidence; epistemic
stance; affective versus ideological polarization; asymmetric credibility
bubbles; mindshaping; managerial versus evaluative epistemic control;
epistemic friction; veritic and reflective epistemic luck; intellectual
arrogance and humility; deliberative AI.

## Bearing on the record

- **On the LIT's characterization.** A's account, that the deeper concern is
  interference with capacities for evaluating beliefs and reasons, not
  informational isolation, is accurate; it is the abstract's thesis. It
  leaves out that the argument is avowedly untested and conditional, and
  that its target is the strong, isolation form of the filter-bubble
  hypothesis, not Nguyen's echo chambers, which it extends.
- **[QUESTION-441](../questions.d/QUESTION-441.md)**: bears on part 2 (what makes it reproductive) and leaves
  parts 1 and 3 open. The paper gives a mechanism by which a system
  reproduces the conditions under which its harm is not legible to those
  it harms: miscalibration undetectable from within, and an optimization
  that resets the standard by which users would judge it. But the bearer is
  "AI", "algorithms" or "engagement architectures", never an organization as
  agent, and no wrong of the organization is stated.
- **[LEAD-050](../leads.d/LEAD-050.md)**: partly met. It states a wrong apart from misinformation
  harm, the corruption of epistemic agency, but a wrong done to epistemic
  agency, not a wrong of governance. Its contrast between curation
  answerable to ground truth and curation answerable to predicted
  expectation (pp. 13–14) is the nearest it comes to a governance norm.
- **[THEORY-1653](../theory.d/THEORY-1653.md)** (Gauthier et al.'s field experiment on X): held evidence
  against the paper's motivating link from algorithmic curation to
  affective polarization, which the paper does not cite.
- **[THEORY-662](../theory.d/THEORY-662.md)** (fluency as an inference whose cause is not given to
  introspection): support, from the cognitive side, for the paper's
  reflective-luck premise.
- [ADR-098](../decisions.d/ADR-098.md)'s `philosophy-of-technology` tag is confirmed: the paper is about
  design that steers its users.
- "echo chamber", "epistemic friction", "Pol.is", "epistemic luck" and
  "intellectual humility" are held nowhere else in the record.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **The central link is assumed.** That engagement optimization maximizes
  fluency is asserted, and everything after §3 proceeds "on the
  conditional assumption" that its effects occur.
- **Fluency is stretched to cover outrage**, which risks making the account
  unfalsifiable unless fluency is measured apart from engagement.
- **The asymmetry step is asserted.** Why fluency raises confidence in
  rejecting opponents rather than in their claims is argued from the
  absence of convergence, which has rival explanations.
- **Corroboration is weak**, and experimental evidence such as
  [THEORY-1653](../theory.d/THEORY-1653.md)'s is not engaged.
- **No agent of the wrong** is named, which limits its use for
  [QUESTION-441](../questions.d/QUESTION-441.md)'s third part.
- **Remedies are untested.**
