# THEORY-538: A system believes and desires when its behaviour is reliably and voluminously predictable by treating it as a rational agent with the beliefs and desires it ought to have, and the success of that strategy is an objective fact though adopting it is free; the more richly a system is connected to its world, the fewer interpretations fit, so thermostat and person differ in degree

## Source

Dennett, *True Believers: The Intentional Strategy and Why It Works*,
[LIT-398](../literature.d/LIT-398.md), read in full in [NOTE-353](../notes.d/NOTE-353.md). The text read is the revised 1987 text
as reprinted in *Mind Design II* (1997), not the 1981 printing, so claims
about what the 1981 essay contained are not supported here.

## What was actually shown

**The strategy (pp. 61–64).** Treat the object as a rational agent; ascribe
the beliefs and desires it ought to have given "its place in the world and
its purpose"; predict what practical reasoning recommends. A true believer
is "a system whose behavior is reliably and voluminously predictable via
the intentional strategy" (p. 59), a believer "in the fullest sense".

**Objectivity (pp. 66–67).** The lectern is excluded because the strategy
gives no predictive gain over what "we antecedently" knew. Against the worry
that cleverer observers would not need it: "The decision to adopt the
intentional stance is free, but the facts about the success or failure of
the stance, were one to adopt it, are perfectly objective."

**Real patterns (pp. 68–71).** Martian super-physicists who predict the
ticker tape but not the intentional patterns "would be missing something
perfectly objective": the interchangeable ways of ordering 500 shares, and
the "societal pivot points" where an outcome turns on what people believe.

**Degree (pp. 72–75).** A thermostat has about half a dozen belief-like
states that can be reinterpreted. Enriching its sensors and behaviour
shrinks "the class of indistinguishably satisfactory models" until a unique
interpretation is "practically (but never in principle)" fixed. "There is
no magic moment." In degenerate cases there is no fact of the matter which
beliefs a person has, and that this is so "is itself a matter of objective
fact" (p. 71).

**Mechanism (pp. 75–77).** Why the strategy works is answered by design for
a thermostat and by evolution for people. Whether a language of thought
produces the pattern is a separate empirical claim, for which the reasons
are "not overwhelming".

**What could have come out otherwise.** Little, since the essay argues and
reports nothing. What it predicts is that the strategy pays on people,
animals and chess programs and not on lecterns, which is common ground.

## What this does not say

- **Anything about experience.** A well-predicted machine is a believer;
  the essay says nothing about whether it feels ([NOTE-353](../notes.d/NOTE-353.md), on [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md)).
- **That groups are believers.** The criterion would admit a firm or a
  state whose group-level stance pays, and Dennett never applies it to
  one. Its institutional examples are reached through individuals.
- **How much prediction is enough.** "Reliably and voluminously" is not
  quantified, and the baseline the lectern fails against is informal.
- **That intentional patterns are invisible to physics.** The Martian case
  assumes it.
- **That most beliefs are true, as a count.** The "less than ten percent"
  false is a guess that depends on a way of counting beliefs Dennett
  doubts can be made (p. 63).
- **That the inner mechanism is a language of thought.** Dennett expects
  "some version" to prove correct and keeps that claim separate from the
  reality of the pattern.

## Connections

- **Rival: psychologism ([THEORY-092](THEORY-092.md)).** Block holds that whether behaviour
  is intelligent, "or the mark of any mental property", depends partly on
  how it is produced, and his lookup machine is the case. This account
  fixes belief by predictive success, with no condition on production. The
  only escape the essay offers is its baseline: whether a lookup table's
  behaviour is predicted more cheaply some other way. It does not discuss
  the case. [THEORY-092](THEORY-092.md)'s `promote_when` asks for exactly this reading.
- **Rival: causal powers ([THEORY-638](THEORY-638.md)).** Searle holds that a system
  running a formal program has no intentionality whatever its behaviour,
  and that ascription to a humanlike robot is withdrawn once the program is
  known. On this account it would not be withdrawn if the strategy still
  paid.
- **Turing ([THEORY-433](THEORY-433.md))** gives a behavioural criterion of the same
  family for thinking, without a theory of belief behind it.
- **Real patterns.** [THEORY-036](THEORY-036.md) examines the later "Real Patterns"
  criterion, compressibility against the bit map, which this essay's
  Martians anticipate.
- **Selves.** Dennett's self as a centre of narrative gravity
  ([THEORY-691](THEORY-691.md)) applies the same interpretive method to the self.
- **Agency under composition.** [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) cites this criterion for why
  every reading it surveys grants agency in both directions.
- **Language models.** Goldstein & Lederman ([LIT-206](../literature.d/LIT-206.md)) restate the
  criterion without a stance, measuring the gain against a mechanical
  explanation rather than against what was known before; they exclude
  thermostats, which Dennett includes as degenerate cases.
