# NOTE-tmpvzqac: Kuhn — Second Thoughts on Paradigms

## Contribution

Exemplars as a cognitive component distinct from rules: what problem
practice transfers is an acquired similarity relation, "logically and
psycho logically prior" to any criteria for it (pp. 9–10 of the copy).

## Key results (pages of the printout)

1. **Laws as schemas.** "They are not so much generalizations as
   generalization-sketches, schematic forms" (p. 4).
2. **Later problems made cheap.** The student "discovers a way to see his
   problem as like a problem he has already encoun[tered]"; "Once that
   likeness or analogy has been seen, only manipulative difficulties
   re[main]" (p. 8).
3. **The thesis.** "Once a new problem is seen to be analogous to a
   problem previously solved, both an appropriate formalism and a new way
   of attaching its symbolic consequences to nature follow." (p. 8)
4. **History.** Galileo, Huyghens and Bernoulli each modelled a problem
   solution on an earlier one (p. 8).
5. **What exemplars teach.** "That ability to recognize group-licensed
   resemblances is, I think, the main thing stu[dents acquire]" (p. 9);
   "Only after a number of such problems have been assimilated, can a
   student or a professional proceed to iden[tify other Newtonian problems
   for himself]" (p. 15).
6. **Knowledge in the relation.** "This knowledge can thereafter be"
   embedded "not in generalizations or rules, but in the similarity
   relationship itself." (p. 14)
7. **Where it fails.** "That expectation may be violated, perhaps during a
   visit to Australia." (p. 14)
8. **Rules as a substitute.** "Unless he is extraordinarily careful he
   will weaken the community's cognition by doing so." (p. 16); rules have
   uses: "That strategy might save valuable data-processing time." (p. 22)

## Assumptions

Communities identifiable apart from paradigms; the Johnny case transfers
to abstract sciences "if in a less pure form"; learning needs a teacher
who says which stimuli go together.

## Concepts

Disciplinary matrix; exemplar; generalization-sketch; correspondence
rules; learned similarity relation; quality space; group-licensed
resemblance.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-756**, corroborates the premise as interpretive and historical
  illustration: exemplars are acquisitions whose value is that later
  problems become tractable. Kuhn measures no cost and does not compare an
  exemplar's direct return with its effect, so nothing bears on the
  claim's reversal.
- **TERM-131**, supports interpretively: a change in retained perceptual
  organization that alters what the learner can later do, with the
  knowledge in the organization rather than in statable rules.
- **CLAIM-758**, analogy only: a learned relation serves its environment
  and costs when it shifts (the Australia line); Kuhn prefers similarity
  as more flexible than rules, so the trap he names is not
  consolidation.
- **CLAIM-754** and **TERM-117**, parallels: agreement on a formalism for
  a new problem exceeds any conjunction of past cases; an exemplar is a
  partial organization defined by what it does to later formation.
- **THEORY-673** and **THEORY-770**: this is the primary source of the
  "generalization-sketch" those readings apply to the Price equation.
- **THEORY-1358** and **THEORY-1363** (Kemp and colleagues on learning to
  learn): a formal parallel to Kuhn's informal one.
- **QUESTION-028**, parallel: learned clusters carry information that
  nature offers no swan-geese.
