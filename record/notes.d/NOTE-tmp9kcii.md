# NOTE-tmp9kcii: Doctorow, "Disenshittify or die!" (DEF CON 32)

## Contribution

The talk opens by asking what happened to the "old, good internet" and describes the enshittification cycle. Platforms are first good to users and lock them in. They then shift value to business customers, and finally claw value back from both. Doctorow names the mechanism "twiddling": altering a service's back end to change prices, rankings and terms. He argues that firms were once restrained by competition, regulators strong enough to discipline them, users' ability to interoperate and modify, and scarce tech workers who could refuse. Antitrust retreat, regulatory capture, IP law and mass layoffs removed each restraint. He closes by asking hackers to fight for interoperability rights and to organise as workers.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- (Pluralistic text, opening) The stages of enshittification, illustrated with Facebook feeds, Amazon search ads, "most favored nation" pricing clauses, HP ink DRM and Apple parts pairing. He rejects the "if you're not paying, you're the product" framing, arguing that paying business customers are squeezed too.
- (middle) "Twiddling" is presented as the pathological mechanism, and the "four forces" as the core argument: competition, regulation, interoperability and worker power.
- (middle) On legal barriers to interoperability, he cites DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention (up to 5 years and $500k for a first offence, in his account) and terms-of-service and computer-crime laws. Examples of adversarial interoperability include ad-blockers, jailbreaks and battery-unlock kits.
- (closing) Tech workers' power came from scarcity and ended with layoffs, so he calls for a union (Tech Workers Coalition, Tech Solidarity). The final line: "restore competition, regulation, interop and tech worker power".
