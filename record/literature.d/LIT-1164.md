# LIT-1164: Causal Reasoning in Graphical Time Series Models

Michael Eichler and Vanessa Didelez (2007), "Causal Reasoning in Graphical Time Series Models", *Proceedings of the 23rd Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence*: 109–116 — [ARXIV-1206.5246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5246)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-941](../notes.d/NOTE-941.md).*

- **Causality as intervention (Defs. 2.1, 2.5).** The causal effect of
  Xa(t) on Xb(t + h) is the mean of Xb(t + h) under an intervention
  regime σa(t): atomic, conditional on the past, or random.
- **The assumptions that do the work (Assumption 2.3).** (i) Whether one
  intervenes in Xa(t) is independent of the past of the whole system and
  of the other variables at t, which excludes instantaneous causality.
  (ii) The future depends on the intervention only through the system's
  present and past. They hold only if the system V includes every
  relevant process, observed or not, and the paper says they must be
  checked in each application.
- **Granger to intervention (Lemma 4.10, Conclusion).** If X satisfies the
  global Granger-causal Markov property for a mixed graph, a missing edge
  a → b means Xa(t) has no effect on Xb(t + 1), and a ∉ an(b) means no
  effect at any horizon. A present edge is only "potentially causal".
- **Identification (Theorems 3.1, 4.11, 4.13).** A set S whose observed
  past blocks every an(b)-pointing back-door path identifies the effect
  from observational quantities. With S = V this always holds, by (ii). A
  front-door criterion covers a latent confounder (Example 4.14); its
  proof is deferred.
- **Gaussian case (Example 3.2).** The identified effect of setting
  Xa(t) = x* is Φ_ba^{(h)}(1)x*, a coefficient of the best linear h-step
  predictor from S's autoregression.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-16 to check [NOTE-010](../notes.d/NOTE-010.md)'s C5, and read the same day.
It qualifies C5. Measuring every relevant variable is one of the
conditions, not all of them. Instantaneous causality must also be absent,
and interventions must be exogenous changes of the system. Even then, the
result is that Granger noncausality implies no intervention effect, and
that the effect is identified from the observational predictor. It does
not make the two analyses one analysis. Filed as [THEORY-704](../theory.d/THEORY-704.md);
the record's own statement is [CLAIM-333](../claims.d/CLAIM-333.md).
