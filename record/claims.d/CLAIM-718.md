# CLAIM-718: A normative verdict on a relationship can be taken as a premise in order to find what in the relationship's organization makes it true: hold behaviour and outcome fixed across cases judged differently, look for the organizational difference, then try to break it with cases where verdict and difference come apart; this finds the organizational marks of normative terms without a prior measure of them, and is circular without the last step

## The claim

The owner, U19 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), after
A37's account of controlled opposition:

> measurement challenges aside, we can still examine scenarios from the perspective of trying to ascertain what kinds of properties we feel productive vs coercive scenarios should exhibit. what are desirable properties for an organization of relationships? instead of assuming a toolkit for objective measurement even exists, let's instead examine how we would describe various relationships if we took for granted that our particular normative judgement about that relationship was accurate?

That is the method: bracket measurement, take the verdict as given, and
ask what the relationship must be like for the verdict to hold. A39
restated it as a reversal of the usual order, from "*Given an
organizational arrangement and a set of measurable properties, can we
determine whether it is coercive?*" to "**Suppose we are correct that
this relationship is coercive. What is it about the organization of the
relationship that makes this judgment intelligible?**", and named it:
"I'd call this a *normative morphology of relationships*". "This doesn't
mean treating our intuitions as infallible. It means provisionally taking
a normative judgment as given so that we can investigate its
implications."

**The guard against circularity is A's** (A39 §9): "We need to
distinguish **using a normative judgment as an investigative premise**
from **using it as the explanation of the properties we're
investigating**." Three steps:

1. "Hold visible behavior and outcomes as constant as possible": paired
   cases alike in actions, consequences and participants, with opposite
   stipulated verdicts.
2. "Look for explanatory differences in organization": who controls the
   dependencies, how obligations are allocated, whether objections take
   effect, what happens when capacities run out.
3. "Try to break the proposed explanation": "Could the supposedly
   productive relationship lack the property we've identified? Could a
   coercive relationship possess it?"

A's example of the third step: the ability to exit looks essential to a
legitimate relationship, but a parent cannot simply leave a child, "So
the mere absence of unrestricted exit cannot be sufficient evidence of
coercion." The mark is then revised, not the verdict. A's summary:
"**We use normative cases to refine the structure of our concepts, rather
than pretending the concepts are already measurable variables.**"

**Why it rests on [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md).** The method needs the verdict to be
well-defined, and [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md) holds that it is once its relata are fixed.
A49 §2 lists what fixing them takes for a judgment about a capability
(holder, capability, affected parties, arrangement, standard, history and
horizon; the note of 2026-11-17 on [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md)). Without that, a stipulated
verdict has no content to test a mark against.

## How it sits with the record

- **The record already uses it.** [CASE-264](../cases.d/CASE-264.md)'s two workshops, [CASE-229](../cases.d/CASE-229.md)'s two
  institutions (and [ARG-055](../arguments.d/ARG-055.md) on it) and [CASE-263](../cases.d/CASE-263.md)'s regressive progress all
  stipulate a verdict across a pair alike in what is observed, and look
  for the organizational difference. [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md) is the pair in which the
  same restriction is self-imposed and imposed. This claim states the
  method those cases use, and its third step, which none of them states.
- **[CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md)** (Proposed) and **[CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md)** (Active): matched ordinary
  trajectories conceal organizational differences that show only under
  intervention or in history. That is why the method's first step holds
  behaviour fixed: the differences it looks for are of the kind ordinary
  behaviour does not show.
- **[QUESTION-468](../questions.d/QUESTION-468.md)** asks how the record identifies exploitation and which
  standard supplies the verdict. The method is one answer to the first
  part: by stipulated pairs, with the standard declared in the
  stipulation. It does not answer the second.
- **In the exchange**, the method was used at once: the marshmallow task's
  three readings ([CASE-281](../cases.d/CASE-281.md)), the three hospitals ([CASE-288](../cases.d/CASE-288.md)) and
  A59 §8's counterexamples to wrongful unilateral action
  ([CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md)), which A introduces with "this returns us to the
  normative-morphology method you've been advocating".

## What it does not say

- Not that the stipulated verdicts are right. The method finds what would
  make a verdict true; whether the standard behind it is justified is
  [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)'s question, and A49 §2 says so: "determining which
  standards ought to govern remains a separate task of moral and political
  reasoning."
- Not that the marks found are measures. A39 §9: "These aren't yet
  measurements." A mark that survives the third step is a candidate for
  measurement, no more.
- Not that the method is new. Reasoning from cases to principles and back
  is old in ethics; what the claim records is the owner's use of it to
  find organizational marks, and the rule that a mark must survive cases
  built to separate it from the verdict.
