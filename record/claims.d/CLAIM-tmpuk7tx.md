# CLAIM-tmpuk7tx: A loosely organized population can change what an outside institution does through its members' separately motivated acts of making, selecting and passing things on, without forming a collective intention or judgment, when the organized pattern of those acts, and not only the acts of identifiable individuals, earns its place in explaining the change

## The claim

A7: "**Distributed cultural uptake.** A loosely organized population can
alter the subsequent behavior of an external institution when its
members' locally motivated acts of creation, selection, and circulation
change the institution's informational or incentive environment; the
resulting influence does not entail that the population formed a
collective intention or corporate judgment."

The case is the owner's ([CASE-tmpj323r](../cases.d/CASE-tmpj323r.md)): two fans made a parody, many
people spread it for their own reasons, and the film's makers put its
line into a reshoot. A7: "Those locally intelligible actions collectively
changed the informational environment in which the film's creators
operated." A7's ladder places it:

| rung | Juggernaut |
|---|---|
| aggregate behaviour | many shared or enjoyed the parody |
| collective amplification | sharing shaped its spread |
| **collective causal efficacy** | the spread made the line salient to the film's makers |
| collective intention | not established |
| corporate agency | not established |

"This is a conceptual progression, not a logical entailment chain." The
claim is that the third rung can hold without the fourth: "the
distinction between **having effects as a collective and acting as a
collective agent**".

## The defeater, and what was added to it

A7's defeater: "the alleged collective influence disappears once the
contributions of identifiable creators, distributors, and institutional
decision-makers are modeled, with no additional explanatory or predictive
contribution from the distributed circulation process." A7 said it "needs
refinement. A micro-level description can always include every
transmission event. The challenge is whether modeling the propagation
network contributes a causally meaningful and explanatorily economical
structure, rather than requiring irreducibility."

The refinement filed here is the record's: the network model must beat
the list of individual contributions under [CLAIM-154](CLAIM-154.md)'s reification rule,
its effect clearing the Occam cost of keeping it. Without a price the
thesis could not be defeated ([CLAIM-278](CLAIM-278.md)'s complaint, in another place).
The rule's Gaussian form will not transfer unchanged to a propagation
network (A12 said the same of its use for fandoms), so the defeater fixes
the kind of test and not yet its threshold.

## What it does not say

- **That the fandom acted.** A7 §4: "The fandom's identity boundary and
  the causal boundary of a particular collective effect may differ." The
  efficacious system may be a passing coalition of makers, sharers and
  production staff across the fandom's edge ([CLAIM-152](CLAIM-152.md)). Whom the
  effect is credited to is [QUESTION-tmpxrbo1](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpxrbo1.md).
- **That the circulation caused the decision.** The production route in
  the case is documented only as a second-hand quote from the
  screenwriter.
- **An operative intention.** [CLAIM-030](CLAIM-030.md) is about an organized whole's
  intention parting from its members'; this needs no intention at all.
  Drift inside an organization ([CLAIM-192](CLAIM-192.md)) and a crowd steering an avatar
  ([CASE-075](../cases.d/CASE-075.md)) are effects inside the population's own activity; this is an
  effect on an outside institution.
