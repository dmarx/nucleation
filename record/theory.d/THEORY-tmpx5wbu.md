# THEORY-tmpx5wbu: Schuhr finds in a laboratory experiment with real money that intertemporal choices presented as a free series of two or three paired decisions, chosen at once, are more patient in their first choices than the same choices presented singly, and that forcing the paired decisions to match lowers patient choice, which he reads as reward bundling done by the chooser rather than imposed

## Source

Alexander Schuhr (2015), doctoral thesis, University of Cape Town,
LIT-tmpj7m62, read in part in NOTE-tmpjem53.

## What was actually shown

Georgia State undergraduates chose between smaller-sooner and
larger-later money on multiple price lists in eight treatments, one
session each (n = 29-37). In the bundling treatments each row held two or
three decisions with the same amounts and successively later dates, made
at once and paid together; in the free ones (T4, T6) they could differ,
in the forced ones (T5, T7) they had to match. First choices in bundling
treatments were more often larger-later than baseline choices; a finite
mixture model gave BUNDLED +0.307 (p = 0.019) and FORCED -0.330
(p < 0.001) on the probability of exponential discounting, with no effect
of the payout scale (Table 24).

## The account

Ainslie's reward bundling, as the thesis puts it: "Reward bundling in
human subjects is a psychological process prompted by the presentation
of each decision as part of a series of comparable decisions" (p. 130).

## What it does not show

That the present choice is treated as a predictor of choices made later,
by a later self. The series is chosen all at once.
