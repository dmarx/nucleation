# CASE-154: One representation, two tasks, one bounded observer: with X₁, X₂ independent fair bits and an observer that reads one coordinate and cannot compute XOR, the raw pair U = (X₁, X₂) gives error 50% on Q_A = X₁ ⊕ X₂ and 0% on Q_B = X₁, and the compressed V = X₁ ⊕ X₂ gives 0% and 50%; a legibility regime can enable one perspective's distinction while disabling another's, with no task-independent sense in which it is better

## The case

X₁, X₂ independent fair bits. A bounded observer can read one coordinate
of what it is given and cannot compute XOR. Two tasks: Q_A = X₁ ⊕ X₂ and
Q_B = X₁.

| task | error from raw U = (X₁, X₂) | error from compressed V = X₁ ⊕ X₂ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Q_A | 50% | 0% |
| Q_B | 0% | 50% |

From U the observer reads X₁, which is independent of X₁ ⊕ X₂, so Q_A is
at chance, and Q_B is exact. From V, Q_A is exact, and V is independent of
X₁, so Q_B is at chance. An ideal observer solves both from U. The bounded
one cannot. This is [CLAIM-199](../claims.d/CLAIM-199.md)'s point made two-sided: a transformation can
create usable information for a restricted observer ([TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)) and, for
the same observer, destroy usable information for another task.

## What it can show

That a learned legibility regime can be competence-enhancing for one
perspective and disabling for another, with no task-independent sense in
which it is better or worse. An institution's reporting can get better at
forecasting output and worse at registering a grievance; a defensive
process can learn an excellent threat representation that is poor at
finding safe engagement.

## What it cannot show

That a regime that disables a perspective is a governance failure. Before
calling it one, it has to be shown that the representational resources
could have served both demands at once ([CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md)'s structural conflict
against avoidable shortfall). A real resource limit is a different failure
from an authority structure that excludes a feasible alternative.
