# THEORY-tmp1x94l: 'Unit of selection' names four separable roles, interactor, reproducer, manifestor of adaptation and beneficiary, and being an interactor at a level does not require engineering adaptations at that level, so showing that a level lacks them does not show it is not a unit of selection

## Source

Lloyd, "Units and Levels of Selection", *Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy* (2005; substantive revision 2024), [LIT-160](../literature.d/LIT-160.md), read in full in
[NOTE-184](../notes.d/NOTE-184.md).

## What was actually shown

**Four questions (§2, §5).** What is the interactor, an entity that
"interacts, as a cohesive whole, directly with its environment in such a way
that replication is differential" (Hull 1980: 318)? What is the reproducer,
Griesemer's generalisation of the replicator? What manifests accumulated
engineering adaptations at a level? Who benefits in the long run? The roles
are "distinct and separable". One entity may fill several.

**The interactor is adaptation-neutral (§2.3).** It needs only a higher-level
character whose relation to fitness cannot be reduced to lower-level
characters, detected by contextual analysis. It does not need engineering
adaptations: accumulated, design-like change beyond the ancestral range of
variation, as against any trait that is a direct product of selection.

**The diagnosis traced through the debates (§4).** G. C. Williams's 1966
attack was on group benefit, and his critics "never considered groups as
interactors, only as interactors-cum-manifestors"; Williams later accepted
the interactor criterion (1990: 504). The paleontologists' early demand for
"emergent properties" in species selection fused the same two roles. The
current definition of species selection is an interactor definition, with
geographic range as the standard trait. Dawkins's question is the beneficiary
question, and he accepts vehicles as the "proximal subjects of selection".

**What could have come out otherwise.** A dispute could have survived the
separation of roles intact. Lloyd's reading is that the ones she examines do
not. She chose the disputes and reads them from one side.

## What this does not say

- **That the separation is accepted.** Godfrey-Smith's Darwinian populations
  deny that the unit question is ambiguous (2009: 111). Lloyd's reply, that his
  analysis still uses all four roles, is an assertion with selective quotation
  ([NOTE-184](../notes.d/NOTE-184.md), C9).
- **That the conflation is "the most common error".** That is asserted, not
  counted (C2).
- **That kin and group selection models are not equivalent.** Her case rests on
  faulting Dugatkin & Reeve (1994). The opposing technical literature is
  quoted, not refuted (C4).
- **That the cheater problem is a fallacy.** The support is a result reported
  secondhand and one survey of mutualisms (C5).
- **That the multilevel Price equation should be abandoned.** That is credited
  to Griesemer in correspondence and left as "an open question" (C7).
- **What a biological individual is.** The entry keeps units of selection apart
  from individuality on purpose (§4.3, n. 99). Its view is functional-role
  pluralism about the relata of selection, not a theory of identity.
