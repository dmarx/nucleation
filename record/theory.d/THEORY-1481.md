# THEORY-1481: Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock argue that when external funding and legitimacy are granted for adopting the forms of capable states (best-practice laws, agencies and procedures) rather than for demonstrated performance, recipient organizations engage in isomorphic mimicry, producing a capability trap in which state capability stagnates or declines while reform and aid continue, and that it is escaped by locally defined problem-solving through authorized experimentation, tight feedback and broad engagement (PDIA)

## Source

Andrews, Pritchett & Woolcock (2012; World Development 2013), [LIT-2187](../literature.d/LIT-2187.md); read in [NOTE-1664](../notes.d/NOTE-1664.md), working-paper pages.

## What was actually shown

- **An argument, with illustrations.** Since the 1980s funds have been
  tied to ex ante reform commitments "open to visible evaluation in
  relatively short time periods" (p. 6), scored against scripts (WGI,
  Doing Business, PEFA) that "have essentially closed the space for
  novelty" (p. 6). Local agents "are rewarded so handsomely for complying
  with externally mandated 'forms' (appearances)" (p. 7) that function is
  not pursued.
- **An equilibrium claim**, stated not modelled: once sprung, there is no
  incentive "for any one organization or leader or front-line agent to
  break out" (p. 5).
- **The escape**, argued from Lindblom, Kingdon, Ostrom, Greenwood et al.
  and Jin et al.: small, cheap steps on locally nominated problems, lessons
  fed back at once, and broad engagement, since central decision-making
  lowers diffusion (pp. 13–18).
- **A boundary**: donor projects "nearly always succeed" at logistical
  tasks (p. 19) and founder on implementation-intensive ones (p. 20).

## What this does not say

- That PDIA works: no evaluation, and the worked case is hypothetical.
- That aid as such, or its volume, causes the trap: the argument is about
  what aid is conditioned on.
- How long a trap lasts, or how fast capability decays when aid is
  withdrawn: no data on either.
- That form-keyed support always degrades capability: logistical tasks and
  reducing government are named exceptions.
