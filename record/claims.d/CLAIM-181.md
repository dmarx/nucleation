# CLAIM-181: The record fixes the grammar an argument is expressed in, though not its shape: its closed vocabulary of schemes, roles and relations formalizes early, and a move that fits no CLAIM, TERM, ARG or declared relation is either forced into one or lost

## The claim

Shipman and Marshall ([LIT-756](../literature.d/LIT-756.md)) found that users of interactive systems
resist formalizing. When they are made to formalize early, the structure
that results belongs to the system and not to them.

The crystal analogy needs care here. A seed crystal does not pass its
shape on. It passes on its lattice: new material joins it in the seed's
arrangement and orientation, which is why seeding a solution selects one
polymorph over another. The macroscopic habit is set by how fast each face
grows, and that depends on supersaturation, temperature and impurities.
Near equilibrium, the slowest-growing faces bound the crystal and the
seed's outline is forgotten. Far from equilibrium, flat faces become
unstable and branch, and the branching amplifies fluctuations in growth
conditions, not features of the seed. A snowflake takes its six-fold
symmetry from the lattice and its arms from the path it took through
temperature and humidity. (General physics, stated from memory. No
crystal-growth work is held in this record.)

Mapped onto the record, the seed is the schemes and relations, and they
fix the lattice. Every move is expressed as a CLAIM, a TERM, a CASE or an
ARG, joined by `rests_on`, `objects_to`, `supersedes` and the other
declared relations, or else it is left uncoded. Which claims exist, and
how the argument branches, are set by the exchange: the owner's questions,
the pace, what gets read. The objection reaches the first and not the
second.

Within that reach there is evidence for it:

- The record decides what counts as a thing. A move becomes an entry, or a
  "flourish, not coded".
- [ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md) rejected roles for literature beyond premise (foil, method,
  analogue, boundary marker), though they "recurred", so they stay in
  prose.
- A thesis must state `defeated_if` when it is filed, which may come
  before the argument knows what would defeat it.

If the objection holds, part of what the record shows as the argument
settling is the argument being re-expressed in the record's grammar.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the record decides the argument's conclusions, or which
  claims the argument contains. The analogy that would support that was
  wrong.
- It does not say a closed grammar is a defect. A lattice is what makes a
  crystal a crystal. The question is what fails to crystallize in it.

## What would answer it

Two kinds of finding would answer it. One is a move that mattered in the
exchange and that the record could not hold, or could hold only by
distorting it. The other runs the opposite way: every case of drift the
record caught was drift by the exchange's own standards, and not only by
the record's.

## Note of 2026-10-11: what Ostwald's seeds say

Ostwald 1897 ([NOTE-709](../notes.d/NOTE-709.md)) supports the narrowed counter, within a
limit it did not state. Only the solid itself or a strictly isomorphous
solid seeds, and isomorphous seeds work to the same dilution as identical
ones, so what a seed passes on is its lattice. But a seed decides only in
the metastable zone. Past the labile limit the new phase forms without
one, in the nearest form. Read across, the record's grammar would decide
how an argument crystallizes only while the exchange is metastable. That is
an analogy, and the paper does not license it.

## Note of 2026-10-11: the grammar decides what can anchor

The owner's reframing ([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)) strengthens this counter. Only what
fits one of the record's schemes can be given a code, so only that can
persist as an anchor and have later structure grow on it. Structure that
fits no scheme can still form in the exchange, as Ostwald's labile zone
forms a phase with no seed, but it does not last and nothing grows on it.
The record then shapes the argument by deciding what can anchor, not only
what form a thing takes when it is filed.

## Note of 2026-10-11 (later): the grammar as a constraint set

Viability theory ([THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md)) gives this counter a precise form. Whether
something persists is relative to a constraint set fixed independently of
the dynamics, and the theory does not supply that set. For an argument, the
record's schemes, codes and supersession rules are that set: they say what
counts as the same claim persisting. [CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)'s note draws the
consequence. Persistence, and so anchoring, is always persistence as
something, and the record's grammar decides what.
