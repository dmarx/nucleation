  is of A's relevant class. A reproducer is a reproducee with reproductive
  autonomy, meaning it can reproduce across a range of backgrounds. A

# LIT-157: Reproducees, reproducers, and Darwinian individuals

Pierrick Bourrat (2025), *Synthese 205(2), article 62* — DOI-10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w

## Key takeaways

- Being reproduced (a reproducee), being able to reproduce with some autonomy across backgrounds (a reproducer), and being a Darwinian individual (a reproducer with high reproductive causal control) are distinct statuses — so genes in multicellular organisms are, like hearts, mere reproducees that serve larger reproducers, and material overlap is needed for reproduction by reproducers but not by reproducees.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Bourrat examines the concept of reproduction in evolution. He distinguishes objects that are reproduced (reproducees), objects that reproduce by virtue of some reproductive autonomy (reproducers), and Darwinian individuals, which are reproducers with a high degree of reproductive causal control. Applied to genes, viruses and cells, the distinction explains why they differ in reproductive status. It also supports Griesemer's view that reproduction requires material overlap.
