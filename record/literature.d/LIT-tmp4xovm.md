# LIT-tmp4xovm: Manipulation Cases in Free Will and Moral Responsibility, Part 1: Cases and Arguments

De Marco, G., & Cyr, T. W. (2024). "Manipulation Cases in Free Will and
Moral Responsibility, Part 1: Cases and Arguments". *Philosophy Compass*
19(12), e70009. DOI 10.1111/phc3.70009. Open access, CC BY 4.0.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp5wtbq](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5wtbq.md).*

- **Case 1, verbatim** (from Fischer et al. 2024, 98): neuroscientists
  "press a button just before he begins to reason about his situation",
  producing "a strongly egoistic reasoning process, which they know will
  deterministically result in his decision to kill White". Plum is
  "stipulated to satisfy various compatibilist conditions", among them
  reasons-responsiveness and conformity to higher-order attitudes.
- **Cases 2 to 4, by description.** Case 2: Plum "pre-programmed" at the
  start of life, like Mele's Ernie. Case 3: an egoistic society
  "instills in him the very same tendencies". Case 4: ordinary
  determinism, "without any manipulators in the picture".
- **Pereboom's argument is to the best explanation.** He argues that
  adjacent cases do not differ relevantly, and then that "the best
  explanation of the Plums' lack of responsibility for their decisions is
  that the decisions were causally determined by factors outside of the
  Plums' control" (n. 23).
- **Mid-life and original design.** Plum and Sweet Sally are manipulated
  mid-life. Ernie is designed as a zygote, so a bypassing view cannot
  separate him from a normal determined agent, since nothing he has
  developed was bypassed.
- **The soft-line worry.** For any necessary condition a soft-liner
  offers, a new case can meet it. A natural-force case (Natalie, changed
  by an electromagnetic storm) has no manipulator, which answers
  manipulator-focused views and the worry that reactions to the
  manipulator distort judgements about the victim.
- **Hard lines.** These are Frankfurt's ("do not depend on the origin"),
  Fischer's (the intuitions track autonomy, not responsibility),
  McKenna's reversal from agnosticism about Case 4 back to Case 1, and
  mitigation views.
- **Wider reach.** Parallel cases with indeterminism press libertarians
  too. Todd's modified argument needs only that blame be mitigated.
