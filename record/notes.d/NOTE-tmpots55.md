# NOTE-tmpots55: Shavit & Sharon — ETI and the Neolithic Revolution (2023)

## Contribution

It applies the 3Cs sketch of the companion paper ([LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md)) to an
archaeological case, the earliest Neolithic, and argues that an ETI
perspective lets the debates over a single cause, the tempo and the
role of intention be bypassed: several causes can lead to the same
transition. It also poses the transition's preliminary anomaly, how a
lifeway costly to each individual could evolve at all.

## Key insight

Farming became a group-level trait that individuals could not leave
once the group's traits depended on one another: "group characteristics
such as economic and social stratification, religion, and larger group
size and territory that theoretically could exist separately ... can no
longer do so in practice", and farming "becomes entrenched in one's
personal and community lifeway"; "In short, the ‘point of no return'
has passed" (§1(c)).

## Assumptions

- The ETI frame: Szathmáry's three phases, West et al.'s question of how
  selfish interests are overcome, Griesemer's 3Cs, Michod's fitness
  decoupling and Black et al.'s scaffolding, used together (§1(a)).
- "Individual" and "group" are context-relative: an individual is "an
  idealized type" of group member, a group is nested in groups (§1(a)).
- Group-level cultural evolution needs both selection and inheritance at
  the group level, so it "cannot occur at this cooperative stage"
  (§1(c)).

## Key results

- **The stages assigned to periods** (§1(c)). Coordination: Early and
  Middle Epipalaeolithic bands of 20-25 with ephemeral boundaries.
  Cooperation: the Natufian, with sedentism, diverse technology and
  individually varied artefacts (the Jordan River Dureijat fish-hooks),
  hence low group-level inheritance. Collaboration: the PPNA and PPNB,
  with distinct Khiamian and Sultanian cultures, group "signatures", and
  group fitness decoupled from individual fitness "at least for some
  crucial traits".
- **Paths into the third stage** (§1(c)): "This condition can arise via
  several non-exclusive causal pathways": patchy ecological conditions
  scaffolding cultures, inter-group selection decoupling group from
  individual fitness, or in-group institutions maintaining division of
  labour, a canonical group signature and bias against other groups.
- **Why the farmer does not revert** (§1(c)): enforced group pressure or
  "personal internalization of groupthink or group-identity"; and group
  selection overriding individual selection, so that fitness depends
  "primarily on group membership".
- **Entrenchment** (§1(c)): mutual dependence of stratification,
  religion, size and territory; reverting "threatens one's self as well
  as one's community as a whole".
- **What the frame buys** (§2): debates over cause, tempo and intention
  are "not contradicting options that require resolution"; the
  Neolithic is not reducible to agriculture or domestication.
- **Its own standing**: "We will not examine to what degree and in what
  respects the Neolithic Revolution fits the ETI criteria" (§1); the
  model "seems to unknowingly function as a simplifying ‘inference to
  the best explanation’ tool" (§1(c)); "that is all we seek" (§2).

## Concepts

- **Coordination, cooperation, collaboration**: as in [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md);
  collaboration is "co-labouring toward a shared goal" with division of
  labour (§1(a)).
- **Group signature**: a canonised artefact or style marking a community,
  inherited at the group level, possibly non-practical (§1(a), §1(c)).
- **Ecological scaffolding**: used loosely, for climate change affecting
  fitness differences between settlements (§1(c)).

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md)'s open status (the worked case it needs)**: this paper does
  not score any Natufian or Neolithic site against the 3Cs by measurable
  criteria. It assigns periods to stages by fit with a published
  synthesis (Belfer-Cohen and Goring-Morris), and says it does not test
  the fit. The case that could settle [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md) is still missing.
- **[CLAIM-531](../claims.d/CLAIM-531.md)** (bears on it, without testing it): the entrenchment the
  paper describes is mutual, group traits that "can no longer" exist
  separately, which is the cycle of mutual maintenance [CLAIM-531](../claims.d/CLAIM-531.md) says an
  entrenched and closed organization has. It shows nothing about
  whether entrenchment without such a cycle is possible; it simply does
  not separate the two.
- **[CLAIM-529](../claims.d/CLAIM-529.md)** (both routes in one case): the paper explains staying
  by interaction criteria (group fitness decoupled, division of labour)
  and by attitude criteria (internalized group identity, a group
  signature) side by side, without separating them. A reading for the
  claim's contrast, not a test.
- **[CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)** (a parallel): the shaman remark, that a community without
  one is doomed "unless ‘the shaman' is not a specific person but has
  already became embedded in routine group maintenance", is a social
  version of endogenization: a function held by one member, then by the
  organization's routine.
- **[CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) and [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)** (an analogue): the "point of no return",
  a lifeway that later structure grows on until leaving it is too costly,
  is the shape [CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) gives anchors that entrench. An analogy, no
  edge.

## Limitations

- No data analysis; the stage assignments are interpretive and the
  mapping to 3Cs criteria is asserted. Group fitness, inheritance and
  decoupling are never measured.
- The ecological scaffold is invoked as climate, not as structure that
  imposes Darwinian properties on groups.
- The bypass of the cause debate is argued only by the possibility that
  several causes reach one outcome; that does not show the debates are
  unanswerable.
- Black et al. is misdescribed as an experiment.

## Open questions

Could the Natufian-PPNA sequence be scored for group-level inheritance
(fidelity of a community signature across daughter sites) against
within-group variation, as the paper's own prediction of "relatively
low fidelity in transmitting group culture" at the cooperative stage
invites?
