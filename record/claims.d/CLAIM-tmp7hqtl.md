# CLAIM-tmp7hqtl: A relation between processes is contract-like only if it governs deviation, conflict or breach and not only the ordinary course: it maintains reciprocal requirements, responds differently to their satisfaction and violation, settles which prevails when they conflict, and is sustained or revised by some mechanism; its formation, which need not be explicit or agreed, nucleates a constraint on the processes' later interaction

## The claim

A16 §13: "A relationship between processes can initially require ongoing
negotiation. Eventually, the processes may establish an arrangement that
regulates their subsequent activity and is maintained through that
activity." "The contract need not be explicitly represented, linguistically
articulated or consciously agreed to. A functional equivalent may arise
through learned coordination, reciprocal adjustment and maintained
allocations of authority."

"However, mere behavioral regularity is insufficient to establish a
contract-like relationship. Otherwise every stable causal interaction
would qualify. We should require evidence that the arrangement
specifically governs counterfactual circumstances of deviation, conflict or
breach." A strong candidate would exhibit:

1. "A maintained relationship establishing reciprocal expectations or
   requirements."
2. "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation."
3. "Governance of circumstances in which requirements conflict."
4. "Some mechanism sustaining or revising that relationship."

Its figure runs conflict, accommodation, maintenance: "Formation of a
potentially constitutive constraint". A16 §6 offers a richer schema for
such a contract, C = (A, G, J, Π, Σ, M, E, R): assumptions, guarantees,
jurisdiction, priority policy, suspension, monitoring, enforcement and
revision, saying "This is a proposed extension for our inquiry, not the
standard definition of an assume–guarantee contract" (for which see
[LIT-tmpm8d98](../literature.d/LIT-tmpm8d98.md)). A16 §14 adds meta-contracts that govern formation and
revision, distributed rather than a supervisor's (noted on [CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md)).

## Where the record stands

- [CLAIM-358](CLAIM-358.md) holds that a recurring conflict admitting a stable
  accommodation can be settled among the processes more cheaply than by
  escalation; this claim says what the settled accommodation must do to
  count as a contract. [CLAIM-277](CLAIM-277.md) holds durable authority as the regularity
  of grants; [CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md) that never-objecting and negotiating processes part
  only under intervention, which is why condition 2 and the counterfactual
  test carry the weight.
- Nucleation as the formation of a self-maintaining constraint is
  [QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md)'s subject; the claim offers contract formation as one
  instance.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the assistant's gap fill

The four conditions are each necessary on A16's account, but A16 presents
them as marks of "a strong candidate", and they do not yet exclude
everything its own test is meant to exclude. Two heating and cooling
controllers sharing a room, with a deadband and a fixed rule that heating
yields when both fire, meet conditions 1 to 3 (each requires the other to
stay out of its band, each responds to violation, the tie-break settles
conflict), and meet 4 if a tuning loop adjusts the deadband. Whether that
is a contract or a regulated regularity is exactly what the test was to
decide. What the four conditions lack is a condition on the relation's own
standing as an object: that the requirements be revisable without
replacing the processes (the contract, not the participants, is what
changes), and that a breach be registered as a breach of the relation and
not only as a deviation of one process. Both are tests on interventions:
change the terms and keep the processes, and see whether behaviour follows
the terms. This is offered as the missing condition, not as settled. The
note's wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it
is the owner's call.

## What it does not say

It does not say that every stable relation between processes is a
contract, nor that a contract's formation is always beneficial: A16 §12
notes that a contract "requiring impossible performance could instead
induce resource exhaustion, deceptive reporting or cascading failure."
