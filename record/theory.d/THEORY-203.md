# THEORY-203: In data generated by a random hierarchical grammar, the tokens that Bayes-optimal noising and denoising changes do so in blocks set by the depth of the latent that changed, and the block size, measured as the correlation length of the changes, diverges at the noise threshold where the top-level class is lost, as a power of the distance to it whose exponent is log(branching) over log(slope of the level-to-level belief map at its unstable fixed point); a Gaussian field with power-law spectrum has instead a correlation length of changes that grows with noise to the end of the process

## Source

Sclocchi, Favero, Levi and Wyart (2024; ICLR 2025; JSTAT 2025),
[LIT-886](../literature.d/LIT-886.md), §3, App. A.2 (Eqs. 20–54), App. B, Figs. 2, 8–11, as read in
[NOTE-691](../notes.d/NOTE-691.md). The model, the BP denoiser and the annealed map are those
of [LIT-884](../literature.d/LIT-884.md) ([THEORY-199](THEORY-199.md)).

## What was actually shown

**The setting.** The Random Hierarchy Model of [LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md) with known rules,
branching s, v symbols per level, m rules per symbol, in the regime where
the class threshold of [THEORY-199](THEORY-199.md) exists: s m (v − 1)/(v^s − 1) < 1 and
L → ∞. Leaves are noised (the ε-process: belief 1 − ε + ε/v on the true
symbol), a new datum is sampled from the exact posterior by belief
propagation, and each token is marked changed or not. The object is the
connected correlation C(r, ε) of two tokens' change marks over
trajectories, averaged over data, and its sum, the dynamical
susceptibility χ.

**The calculation.** In the annealed approximation, the joint law of two
tokens' changes is a product of 2 × 2 matrices through their lowest common
ancestor (Eq. 51). Near the threshold ε* the upward belief p_ℓ stays near
the repulsive fixed point p* of the map F for ℓ̃ ≈ −log|ε − ε*| / log F′(p*)
levels before flowing to recovery or to chance. Equating the correlation
length with the block a latent at that depth spans, ξ ≈ s^ℓ̃, gives
ξ ∼ |ε − ε*|^(−ν), ν = log s / log F′(p*) (Eq. 54): the form of a
renormalisation-group correlation-length exponent, with s as block factor.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Exact BP on rule draws at
v = 32, m = 8, s = 2, L = 9 could have departed from the mean field or
shown no peak; it matches the mean-field C(r, ε) and χ(ε), both peak at
ε* ≈ 0.74 with C(r, ε*) ∼ r^(−1), and rescaling distances by Eq. 54's ξ
collapses the curves for ε between 0.60 and 0.90 (Figs. 2a, 9). With
masking noise instead of the ε-process, the peak sits where the class
reconstruction drops (Figs. 2b, 10), and it does not depend on whether
sampling is by BP or by backward diffusion with the exact score
(Fig. 8). For a Gaussian random field with spectrum ‖k‖^(−a) and exact
score, the changes are the resampled modes above κ*(t) =
(1/α_t − 1)^(−1/a), so the length grows monotonically and χ is largest at
the end (App. B, Fig. 11): there, a peak at an intermediate noise does not
occur.

## What this does not say

- **Not proved.** The exponent comes from linearising an annealed map and
  identifying an escape depth with a correlation length; the critical decay
  exponent (a = 1) is fitted, not derived. The agreement with exact BP is
  one parameter set at s = 2. Recomputing from Eqs. 22 and 54 at that set
  gives ν ≈ 1.89 where the paper quotes ν ≃ 1.78 ([NOTE-691](../notes.d/NOTE-691.md),
  Limitations), an unexplained gap.
- **Not about trained diffusion models.** The denoiser knows the grammar,
  as in [THEORY-199](THEORY-199.md).
- **Not that a χ peak implies a hierarchy.** The only non-hierarchical
  contrast is the Gaussian field. A flat model with a class transition
  (a mixture of many classes) is not analysed, and could plausibly give χ
  a peak at its transition without a power law across scales. The
  discriminating signature in the model is the power-law C(r) at the peak,
  not the peak alone.
- **Not that text or images are generated by such a grammar.** The
  susceptibility peaks in a masked text diffusion model and an ImageNet
  diffusion model (read through CLIP patch embeddings) are consistent with
  it, over about one decade of distance and without a control.
- **Not about attribute hierarchies.** The hierarchy is part–whole; it
  leaves [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md) where it was.
- **Not an instruction.** It describes what an ideal denoiser's changes
  look like, not how to build or use one.

## Connections

It is a consequence of [THEORY-199](THEORY-199.md): the threshold that account places on
the class becomes, here, a critical point with a diverging length. It is
set beside [THEORY-201](THEORY-201.md), whose correlations are static, between tokens of
the data; these are dynamical, between changes, and the paper's
Gaussian-field contrast is its case that static power-law correlations do
not produce them. The exponent formula makes the level-to-level belief
map a coarse-graining flow with one relevant direction, the kind of
object [THEORY-194](THEORY-194.md) is about (which coarse-graining keeps the relevant
variables), though here the flow is fixed by the grammar rather than
chosen. The source of the observable is the physics of glasses, where a
peaking dynamical susceptibility marks cooperative rearrangement.
