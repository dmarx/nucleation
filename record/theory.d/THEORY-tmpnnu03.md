# THEORY-tmpnnu03: Cognition has two necessary marks, involving non-derived content and a distinctive kind of causal processing, and as a contingent fact only processes in the brain have both, so notebooks, screens and tools coupled to a thinker are not parts of cognition, and human-plus-tool systems will support no science of their own

## Source

Adams & Aizawa (2001), "The bounds of cognition", [LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md), read in full in
[NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md). Adams & Aizawa (2010), "Defending the Bounds of Cognition",
[LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md), read in [NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md) from a draft that predates the published chapter.

## What was actually shown

**The method ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) p. 46).** The bounds of cognition "must be determined
by finding the mark of the cognitive, then seeing what sorts of processes
in the world have the mark". Parity is granted as "true and obvious": it
shows only that being in the brain is not the mark.

**Mark 1, non-derived content ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) §2.1).** Cognitive states "must
involve intrinsic, non-derived content". The support is a claimed "fairly
broad consensus" (Dretske, Fodor, Searle, Millikan) and arguments against
Dennett's stance view. The 2010 text says the condition has "calculated
openness": states must *involve* such content, not consist of it, and a
process with none is non-cognitive ([NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md), C2).

**Mark 2, causal processing ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) §2.2).** Sciences sort phenomena into
causally homogeneous kinds, so cognition "must be discriminated on the
basis of underlying causal processes". The analogies are Bacon on heat and
neo-Lamarckians confusing inheritance with infection.

**Applied ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) §3.1).** Otto's notes have "merely derived content", so
they "do not ... constitute beliefs or memories". Tetris blocks on a
screen "are not representations at all ... they are the blocks to be fit
together". Otto's retrieval uses motor and visual processing that Inga's
lacks. Cognition is like muscle contraction, which stays in the arm when
shears are used, and unlike some digestion, which is transcorporeal in
spiders.

**Against convention as a source of mental content ([LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)).** A mental
image of Euler circles need not get its content by convention; the
dependence on a prior convention is historical, like a mental image of a
car depending on the car's invention. A convention needs access to "the
would-be syntactic item" it gives meaning to, and "we have no way to
identify particular tokens of brain states qua syntactic items".

**No science of tool use ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) §4; [LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)).** "There are no laws
covering humans and their tool use over and above the laws of intracranial
human cognition and the laws of the physical tools." Tools are artifacts,
an open-ended set, so a brain-tool science would have to cover "a motley",
not "a multiplicity".

**What could have come out otherwise.** An external store could have been
shown to carry content not fixed by convention, or a regularity over
human-plus-tool systems could have been named. The authors cite no study
either way.

## What this does not say

- **That cognition cannot extend.** Transcranial cognition is "logically
  and perhaps nomologically possible"; the claim is a "boring contingent
  empirical fact" that there is none.
- **That the marks are established.** Both are assumed, and the authors say
  little about what non-derived content is ([NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md), Limitations). They
  concede ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) p. 50) that it is unclear how much of each cognitive
  state must involve non-derived content, and Clark's reply ([LIT-403](../literature.d/LIT-403.md)) uses
  that concession against them.
- **That the processing differences are measured.** The 2001 paper cites
  no experiment on external memory, and the 2010 contrast rests on
  consumer electronics ([NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md), C5). Rupert ([LIT-422](../literature.d/LIT-422.md)) supplies the
  empirical version ([THEORY-tmpgrmvp](THEORY-tmpgrmvp.md)).
- **That there will be no science of tool use.** "Slim to none, we
  speculate": a bet ([NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md), C8).
- **Anything about consciousness or groups.** Consciousness is set aside
  "without argument" (p. 48). The motley argument concerns artifacts, and
  organisms are not artifacts, so it does not by itself count against a
  nation as a cognitive system ([NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md), Bearing on the record).

## Connections

- **What it presupposes.** The coupling point ([THEORY-tmpma6ht](THEORY-tmpma6ht.md)) is why a
  mark is needed at all. This account supplies one candidate.
- **Rival.** Chalmers's sensorimotor statement ([THEORY-tmpegs18](THEORY-tmpegs18.md)) counts
  Otto's notebook into his standing beliefs. [LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §2 answers the
  derived-content objection in one line: biological memory's content is
  derived from conscious states too.
- **Neighbour, not ally on this premise.** Rupert's embedded cognition
  ([THEORY-tmpgrmvp](THEORY-tmpgrmvp.md)) agrees that internal and external processes differ in
  cognitively relevant ways, and rejects the derived-content argument as
  question-begging ([LIT-422](../literature.d/LIT-422.md), n. 87).
- Clark's 2005 reply ([LIT-403](../literature.d/LIT-403.md)) and his book ([LIT-412](../literature.d/LIT-412.md), unread) are the
  answers on the other side.
