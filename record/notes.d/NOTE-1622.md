# NOTE-1622: Murray and Sastry — Steering nonholonomic systems with sinusoids

## Contribution

A constructive, open-loop method for steering nonholonomic systems: motion
in a Lie-bracket direction is produced by periodic inputs whose frequencies
are chosen so that only the wanted bracket gets a net, zero-frequency
component. It defines chained systems, proves them controllable and
steerable by sinusoids, gives sufficient conditions for converting a
two-input system to chained form by feedback, and shows by example that
simple sinusoids are not enough for general higher-order systems.

## Key insight

The directions a system can move in at an instant and the configurations
it can reach are different spaces. The gap is closed by motion: going
round a loop of inputs leaves a net displacement in a direction no single
input provides, "the Lie bracket directions correspond to rectification of
harmonic periodic motions of the driving vector fields" (§III-B, p. 708).

## Assumptions

- **Driftless, input-affine kinematics**: the inputs are velocities, and
  the input fields are smooth and linearly independent on an open set U.
- **Regular filtrations**: the ranks of the bracket distributions Gᵢ are
  constant on U. The results are local, and singular configurations (a
  steering angle of ±π/2) are excluded.
- **Free space.** The obstacle-avoidance problem, the piano mover's
  problem, is set aside (§I).
- **Open loop.** Trajectories are computed, not stabilized; Fig. 9 shows
  the effect of a small initial-condition error.
- **Near-canonical form.** Nonlinear terms are handled by harmonic
  corrections only where the system is close to canonical or triangular
  form.

## Key results

- **The reformulation** (§I, p. 701): "Roughly speaking, we would like to
  convert the constraint specification from describing the directions in
  which the system cannot move to those in which it can." Ignoring the
  constraint fails: "If we attempt to ignore the constraint, the paths
  generated by a path planner may not be feasible" (pp. 700–701).
- **The bracket** (§II-A, p. 702): "Thus, the Lie bracket is the
  infinitesimal motion that results from flowing around a square defined by
  two tangent vectors."
- **Chow's theorem** (§II-A, p. 702): "Roughly speaking, Chow's theorem
  states that if we can move in every direction using Lie bracket motions
  (possibly of higher order than one), then the system is controllable."
  The filtration, degree of nonholonomy and growth vector classify how
  deep the brackets must go: the kinematic car has growth vector (2, 3, 4)
  (Ex. 2, p. 704), the hopping robot (2, 3) (Ex. 1, p. 703).
- **The method** (abstract, p. 700): "trajectories use sinusoids at
  integrally related frequencies to achieve motion at a given bracketing
  level." Algorithms 1 and 2 steer first- and second-order canonical
  systems, motivated by Brockett's optimal controls for the first-order
  case; Algorithm 3 steers chained systems (Prop. 5).
- **Its limit** (§IV-A, p. 709): for a free nilpotent system of order 4
  with two inputs it is "not possible to get motion only in the x 7
  direction using simple sinusoids."
- **Feedback** (§V, p. 714): "Closed-loop control of nonholonomic systems
  is very difficult, in part because of fundamental restrictions which
  prohibit the existence of smooth feedback controllers which
  asymptotically stabilize a point."
- **Existence and construction** (§V, p. 715): "Although the existence of
  a trajectory is guaranteed by the appropriate controllability conditions,
  construction of a trajectory for systems with drift is still an open
  problem."

## Concepts

- **Nonholonomic constraint**: a velocity constraint that "cannot be
  written as an algebraic constraint in the configuration space" (p. 701);
  a holonomic one integrates to a level surface hᵢ(x) = cᵢ.
- **Controllable** (p. 702): for any two points of U some input steers one
  to the other in finite time.
- **Filtration** G₁ = Δ, Gᵢ = Gᵢ₋₁ + [G₁, Gᵢ₋₁]; **degree of
  nonholonomy**, the step at which it stops growing; **growth vector**,
  the ranks of the Gᵢ (pp. 702–703).
- **Chained system** (§IV-B): a canonical form in which each state's
  derivative is the previous state times one input.

## Connections

It builds on Chow's theorem and Frobenius's for reachability, on
Brockett's optimal-control result for first-order systems, and on Laumond
for the car with trailers, whose N-trailer parking problem it says
"remains unsolved" (p. 701). The classical holonomic planning problem, the
piano mover's, is named and set aside.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures** cites it, as M11,
  for Lie brackets, and quotes "The number of directions in which a system
  can move immediately is not the same as the number of states it can
  reach." No such sentence is in the paper: it is A's paraphrase. As a
  paraphrase it is right; the paper's construction rests on that gap. A
  misses two points the paper makes.
  - **Bracket directions cost cycling inputs.** They are reached only by
    loops of input, and higher brackets need higher harmonics and more
    cycles. Reach has a price in motion, which is closer to A's later
    "accessible at acceptable cost" than A says.
  - **The existence of a path is not a construction of one.** Chow's
    condition guarantees a trajectory; building one is a separate problem,
    solved here only for chained and near-canonical systems, and open for
    systems with drift (p. 715). A's own distinction between a feasible
    and a reachable target recurs here one level up: reachable does not mean
    steerable by a given class of inputs.
- **[CLAIM-345](../claims.d/CLAIM-345.md)** (equal controllability rank does not fix the cost of
  control): supports and qualifies. The rank condition settles
  reachability; the effort to move along a higher bracket grows with the
  amplitudes and the number of cycles, so rank-equal systems can differ in
  cost. The paper computes no cost beyond Brockett's first-order case, so
  the support is in kind.
- **[CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md)** (freedom in degrees along several dimensions): A's gloss
  calls them "several independently variable dimensions"; the claim does not say
  independent, and what it asserts is a partial order by inclusion of what
  can be viably reached. The paper supports separating instantaneous
  admissibility from reachability; it is silent on viability, having no
  state constraints.
- **[CLAIM-495](../claims.d/CLAIM-495.md)** (holonomy on a graph connection): related by the loop. A
  bracket is the displacement left after a small closed loop of input, a
  continuous anholonomy. The analogy holds only as far as this: going round a
  loop need not bring you back; [CLAIM-495](../claims.d/CLAIM-495.md) is about discrete compatibility, not about
  using loops to reach new states.
- **[CLAIM-542](../claims.d/CLAIM-542.md)** (sensitivity does not fix the allocation of control): the
  same family as [CLAIM-345](../claims.d/CLAIM-345.md), in the linear case; supports in kind only.
- [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md), [CLAIM-365](../claims.d/CLAIM-365.md) and [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md), which A cites in the same part, bear
  on nothing in this paper. [THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md) (viability kernel) is unrelated: no
  state constraints here.
- Filed: [THEORY-1432](../theory.d/THEORY-1432.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The authors'**: the chained-form conditions are only sufficient (a car
  with more than one trailer is not covered); simple sinusoids fail for
  general higher-order systems; closed-loop control is hard and systems with
  drift are open; "the study of nonholonomic motion planning is in its
  infancy." (p. 701).
- **Local and idealized**: results on a regular neighbourhood, no
  obstacles, kinematic rather than dynamic examples, and no cost or
  optimality analysis beyond the first-order case.

## Open questions

- How does the least effort to reach a configuration grow with the bracket
  depth it needs? A cost analysis of the sinusoidal steering, or of optimal
  steering, would say.
- When a path exists, is there a constructive method for systems with
  drift? The paper leaves this open.
