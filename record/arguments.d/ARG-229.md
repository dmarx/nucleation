# ARG-229: Mulligan and colleagues' methylation difference in grandchildren of women pregnant during the 1980 violence, whose mothers were exposed only in utero, by inference to the best explanation, shows a germline route of violence trauma working apart from the care of a parent who lived through it

## The inference

1. Grandchildren of women pregnant during the 1980 Hama violence differ
   from children of families who left Syria before 1980 at 14 methylation
   sites, at genome-wide significance in two models ([THEORY-2299](../theory.d/THEORY-2299.md)).
2. Their mothers were fetuses at the time and did not live through the
   violence as persons.
3. Of the explanations, a germline route, in which the violence reached
   the oocytes forming in the fetal mothers, needs no lived experience in
   the middle generation and predicts a difference in the third.
4. So the germline route is the best explanation of the difference, and it
   works apart from the care of a parent who lived through the trauma.

## Critical questions

- **Is the explanation better than its rivals?** Not shown. Four rivals
  predict the same difference:
  - *the womb, one generation on*: the mothers' own in-utero exposure
    altered their development, and so the uterine environment they gave
    their children. This is [QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md)'s womb route, displaced by a
    generation;
  - *the grandmothers' care*, of the mothers and in three-generation
    households of the grandchildren;
  - *the group*: a 1980 refugee community whose history is Hama, against
    families who left before;
  - *cohort and selection*: different years of flight, ages and
    socioeconomic status, which the authors could not adjust.
  The study measures none of these, so it cannot rank them.
- **Is the evidence reliable?** Moderately. There are two models and
  Bonferroni correction, but 18 children from 10 families, and 6 of the 14
  sites are lost when controls are restricted to children.
- **Has enough been investigated?** No phenotype, no maternal behaviour, no
  community measure, and no gametes.
- **Would a weaker conclusion follow?** Yes: that the difference does not
  require a parent who lived through the trauma. Premise 2 secures that
  much, and it is the last clause of [CLAIM-1103](../claims.d/CLAIM-1103.md). The step from there
  to "the germline", which the claim's first clause takes, is the one that
  fails.

## Verdict

Proposed, with the assistant's lean that the inference does not carry
its germline conclusion, and carries only the weaker one. The owner
decides. If it is rejected, the question of whether the Syrian design
separates any route rests with [CLAIM-1017](../claims.d/CLAIM-1017.md).
