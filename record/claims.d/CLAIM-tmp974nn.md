# CLAIM-tmp974nn: On selection-based teleosemantics, a self-maintaining system's anticipation can be false by a norm of its own only if selection or learning stabilized it; upkeep in the present, with no such history, sets no norm, so the endogenous profile of the owner's debugging case is a norm only for a system whose own past upkeep shaped it

## The objection

[CLAIM-648](CLAIM-648.md), on Bickhard's account ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)): a self-maintaining system
that anticipates an interaction will sustain it holds an expected
response profile "set by its own upkeep", and the response's absence
"means by its own lights that the interaction failed".

The selection-based account reads this differently:

1. **Falsity needs a function, and function needs history.** Millikan:
   "attributions of truth and falsity ... make sense only by reference to
   function, hence by reference to a history of selection" ([NOTE-tmpqexrv](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqexrv.md),
   p. 9). The survey: on etiological theories "an item's function is
   determined by its history of selection or by past selection of things
   of that type" ([NOTE-tmp60j0q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp60j0q.md), §2).
2. **Learning counts.** Producer and consumer may be shaped "by natural
   selection or by learning" and "maintain their current settings
   (selection through learning)" ([NOTE-tmpqexrv](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqexrv.md), p. 5). Shea counts a
   system's past contributions to its own survival as stabilizing a
   function ([NOTE-tmp60j0q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp60j0q.md), §3.5).
3. **Present upkeep alone is not enough.** The Swampman case: a replica
   with no history has no functions and so no content ([NOTE-tmp60j0q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp60j0q.md),
   §4.2). A self-maintaining system whose anticipation is set only by
   what its upkeep now requires, and was never stabilized by that
   requirement, is in the same position.
4. **So the profile is the system's own only with a history.** The
   anticipation can be false by the system's own lights when the
   system's past upkeep, through learning or a selection-like retention,
   is what fixed which response it anticipates.

## What it does to [CLAIM-648](CLAIM-648.md)

- **It narrows, it does not refute.** The teleosemanticist agrees with
  [CLAIM-648](CLAIM-648.md) against the designer: a norm can come from the system's own
  history and not from anyone's intention. The disagreement is over
  whether present organization suffices. That is the difference between
  [THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md) ("no history or observer needed") and the etiological
  accounts.
- **[CASE-241](../cases.d/CASE-241.md)'s forager.** Its profile was written by its modeller, and
  it has no history of learning or selection. On this account its
  anticipation has the modeller's content, which is the case [CLAIM-648](CLAIM-648.md)'s
  own defeater names ("only one more designer's profile"). The forager
  [CASE-239](../cases.d/CASE-239.md) proposes, which revises its criterion by learning, would
  have a profile stabilized by its own past, and on this account its
  norm would be its own.
- **Where the two accounts come apart.** Only for a system that
  maintains itself and has no history: a swamp forager. For any system
  with a past of upkeep that retained what worked, both accounts can
  call the profile endogenous, for different reasons.

## What it does not say

- That Millikan or Neander discuss [CLAIM-648](CLAIM-648.md)'s case, organizational
  accounts, or Bickhard; neither text read does. The application is the
  assistant's.
- That the etiological account is right against Bickhard's. The survey
  reports non-etiological teleosemantics (Nanay, Piccinini) that drop the
  history requirement, and Bickhard's own case against etiology.
- That a designer's intention gives a designed system's signal its
  content on Millikan's account. Her derived proper functions are not in
  the chapter read, and the survey sets artefacts aside.
