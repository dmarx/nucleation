# CLAIM-tmpadjsm: Understanding in a learning system is the developmental value of its acquired organization, what it makes cheap to learn next, so a frozen exact rule knows every value of its function without understanding it, and understanding entails greater future learnability

## The claim

QUESTION-493 asks what it is for a *learning system* to know and to
understand. For such a system, on this reading, understanding is what its
acquired organization makes cheap to learn next: the developmental
frontier of CLAIM-754, moved by what was learned. CASE-tmpkttf2's rule
learner understands x² because x² + c costs it one example; the table
learner does not because it costs every entry. CASE-tmphawe9's frozen
circuit, which can learn nothing, knows every value of x² and does not
understand x².

Two reasons against CLAIM-tmpf4pun's tracking criterion:

1. **Only change escapes the table.** Stable tracking can be tested only
   over a family of perturbations, and over any finite family a table can
   match it, which is CLAIM-519's result for world models. A changed
   target lies outside every table the system holds, so developmental
   value is the one criterion a table cannot meet. THEORY-438's tracking
   is evidence of understanding to the degree its perturbations approach
   a change of target.
2. **It is the question's scope.** The question is about learning
   systems, and the developmental reading says what understanding is in
   one: the part of its organization that pays off in further learning.
   A system that cannot learn is outside that scope, so denying it
   understanding in this sense is not a counterexample but a boundary.
   The injured person CLAIM-tmpf4pun cites still learns, procedurally and
   in the moment, and is not a frozen circuit.

## What it does not say

- Not that the frozen circuit lacks knowledge: it knows every value, on
  QUESTION-493's candidate criteria.
- Not a different verdict on any learner in LEAD-001's set: on every one
  the two readings agree.
