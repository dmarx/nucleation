# NOTE-tmp7rprz: Chalmers — Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia

## Contribution

The paper turns the standoff over absent and inverted qualia, intuition
against intuition, into an argument. A functionalist cannot win by
appealing to intuitions about the China brain. But anyone who denies the
functionalist conclusion for an isomorph must accept one of two strange
things: sudden disappearance of experience, or a dissociation between
experience and judgment in a system working perfectly. The paper makes the
thought experiment precise (the switch, the ten-percent step) and states
what is and is not concluded: natural, not logical, necessity. It also
lists the gaps it leaves open.

## Key insight

Experience and the judgments a system makes about it are tied together
tightly enough that you can notice a significant change in your experience
when you attend to it. An organisation-preserving swap cannot change
judgments, by construction. So it cannot change experience either, unless
experience can float free of everything the subject could ever say or
think about it. The argument turns a premise about self-knowledge into a
constraint on the psychophysical laws.

## Assumptions

- **Functional organisation (§1)** is a set of abstract components, a
  set of states for each, and dependency relations among the components,
  inputs and outputs. A physical system realises it when it can be divided
  into components whose causal dependencies mirror the specification.
- **Grain (§1).** Fine enough to fix behavioural capacities and
  dispositions. "In the brain, it is likely that the neural level
  suffices, although a coarser level might also work." The neural level is
  used for illustration, and "the arguments generalize". The generality is
  asserted.
- **Corresponding states (§1).** Isomorphs must be in corresponding
  states at the time (so a sleeping twin does not count).
- **Empirical, not logical, possibility (§2).** The question is what the
  laws of nature allow.
- **Feasibility in principle (§3).** A silicon chip can perform a neuron's
  local input–output function, with transducers. If it cannot, a silicon
  isomorph is impossible and the principle is not engaged, so this is not
  a premise the conclusion needs.
- **Judgment supervenes on organisation (§§3–4).** "Unless one is a
  dualist of a very strong variety, beliefs must be reflected in the
  functioning of a system". So an organisation-preserving change cannot
  add a belief such as "I can't see anything" or "my qualia just flipped".
- **Reliable self-knowledge (§§3–4).** A rational, unimpaired, attending
  subject is generally right about its own experience, and can notice a
  significant change in it.
- **Continuity of fundamental laws (§3, footnote).** Known laws make one
  magnitude depend continuously on another, and "there is no way to
  compound continuity into discontinuity".
- **Empirical premise (§5).** Some systems, me or some biological systems,
  are in fact conscious.

## Key results

All are thought experiments and plausibility arguments. The author says
that "these arguments do not constitute conclusive proof" (§2).

- **Fading qualia (§3).** If Robot, a silicon isomorph of me, lacks
  experience, then in the replacement series either:
  - qualia suddenly disappear: one neuron, or a few molecules, switches off
    a whole field of experience. That needs a brute discontinuity at an
    arbitrary point; or
  - qualia fade. Joe, halfway along, says "bright red" and experiences
    "tepid pink"; says he hears loud noise and experiences "a distant
    rumble". He is "utterly out of touch with his conscious experience, and
    is incapable of getting in touch".

  Everyday fading, in drowsiness or down the evolutionary tree, comes with
  a matching change in function. Joe's does not. Blindness denial is set
  aside, because its subjects are impaired and Joe is not. The sorites
  charge is answered: the argument accepts a continuum and rejects the
  intermediate cases for independent reasons.
- **Extension to populations (§3).** The same series works with neurons
  replaced by tiny homunculi, or external normal-sized ones with radio
  links, "ending up with a network of homunculi that is essentially
  equivalent to the population controlling a robot". The homunculi can
  then "double up", keeping written records, until one homunculus does all
  the work, as in the Chinese room. "If the causal organization of the
  original system is preserved, even if it is only among a system of marks
  on paper, then the same arguments suggest that the system will have
  experiences. (Of course, we should not expect the homunculus itself to
  have the experiences; it is merely acting as a sort of causal
  facilitator.)"
- **Dancing qualia (§4).** Fading does not touch inversion, since
  intermediate inversions preserve the structure of experience. Instead,
  among the 10%, 20%, … 90% systems, two neighbours must differ
  noticeably, because red cannot become blue in ten unnoticeable steps.
  Install the neighbour's silicon circuit as a backup with a switch.
  Flipping it changes experience but not organisation, so "I am not
  suddenly disposed to say 'Hmm! Something strange is going on!'". The
  same construction works against absent qualia, from vivid to tepid. If
  that were possible, qualia might be dancing all the time, since low-level
  physiology changes constantly and only function is held fixed by
  evolution. "The only place to draw a principled line is at the
  functional level."
- **Loopholes conceded (§4).**
  - *Speed*: a much faster or slower isomorph cannot be swapped in.
  - *History*: a physical twin with a different past cannot be reached by
    a switch, though a fading series can vary history.
  - *Very mild inversions*: changes smaller than one noticeable step. The
    step could be at most 20–25% before personal identity is in question,
    but if colour depends on a small area, one swap covers it.
  - *Unattended qualia*: these could invert unnoticed.

  Each is called coherent but unmotivated.
- **Nonreductive functionalism (§5).** Logical necessity cannot be
  reached. Fading and dancing qualia are intelligible. And even if they
  were impossible, the premise that some brains are conscious is
  empirical, so only the conditional could be necessary. So functional
  organisation "suffices for conscious experience with natural necessity".
  That is "just as compatible with certain forms of property dualism … as
  with certain forms of physicalism". Consequence: systems duplicating our
  organisation are conscious "even if they are made of silicon,
  constructed out of water-pipes, or instantiated in an entire population",
  and biochemistry is "at best indirectly relevant".

## Concepts

- **Organizational invariance** — experience is invariant across systems
  with the same fine-grained functional organisation (§1).
- **Functional isomorph** — a system sharing another's organisation at a
  grain fine enough to fix behavioural dispositions, in corresponding
  states (§1).
- **Absent / inverted qualia** — an isomorph with no experience /
  different experience (§2).
- **Fading qualia** — experience that degrades along a replacement series
  while function stays fixed (§3).
- **Suddenly disappearing qualia** — experience that vanishes at one step
  (§3).
- **Dancing qualia** — experience that flips with an organisation-neutral
  switch, unnoticed (§4).
- **Nonreductive functionalism** — organisation determines experience
  with natural necessity, without reduction (§5).
- **Causal facilitator** — the role of a homunculus who implements an
  organisation without being its subject (§3).

**Composition and nesting.** This is the paper's main bearing on the
record's bridge. The §3 extension describes a whole made of minded parts:
people with radios, each standing in for neurons. On the paper's argument
that whole has the experiences of the person it replicates, and the
homunculi keep their own. The paper never states an anti-nesting principle
or argues against one. Its result is incompatible with any principle that
forbids a conscious whole from having conscious parts as such, because the
replacement series passes through wholes of that kind with no
organisational change. The single-homunculus case separates two
relations a part can have to a whole. A part can *implement* the whole's
organisation, like the record-keeping homunculus, whose own experiences
are not the system's. Or a part's *own* capacities can be what the whole's
capacities consist in. The paper's verdict covers the first relation only.

## Connections

- **Block ([LIT-tmpc6np5](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc6np5.md), [NOTE-tmpxt8ou](NOTE-tmpxt8ou.md)).** The paper's target, cited as
  "Block (1980)" in the text and "Block (1981)", the *Readings* reprint,
  in the references; the original is 1978. Its gloss, that Block "argues
  that it is bizarre to suppose that this would somehow give rise to a
  group mind", is not Block's ground: Block's doubt rests on dissimilarity
  and design, and survives replacing every homunculus with a gate (n. 19).
  The paper's population series is Block's n. 17 case, a billion people
  each simulating a neuron, the one where Block says intuition "seems to
  founder". Block's main China system is a machine-table realisation, and
  the paper reaches it only through the asserted generality of grain.
- **Searle.** The water-pipes, the Chinese room, and *The Rediscovery of
  the Mind* pp. 66–67 for a fading scenario in which beliefs stay true but
  impotent. The paper rejects that scenario: an unchanged organisation has
  "no room" for the new beliefs.
- **Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md), [NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)).** Not cited (it is later). It
  supplies the premises the conscious-US argument needs against
  neurochauvinism and contiguism, and it refutes absolute anti-nesting for
  isomorphs. It does not supply the step from isomorphism to similarity of
  kind; see Bearing.
- **Chalmers's later objection in Schwitzgebel §6 ([NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)).** That
  principle says a conscious system's capacities must come largely from
  the relations among its subsystems, not from the subsystems' own
  capacities. Its test case is the "two-seater homunculus". The 1995
  single-homunculus verdict is consistent with it, because there the
  homunculus is a facilitator executing recorded relations, not a source
  of the capacities. The later principle reads as the restriction this
  paper's §3 already observes. That reading is mine; neither text links
  them.
- **Kammerer ([LIT-tmpdpmaj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdpmaj.md), [NOTE-tmpu7aha](NOTE-tmpu7aha.md)).** His functional anti-nesting
  principle works with supervenience on functional organisation, and notes
  that this is "compatible with Chalmers's naturalistic dualism". It
  survives this paper. A homunculus in a neuron's role does not
  consciously represent the whole, in a role only it could fill, since a
  chip could fill it.
- **IIT ([LIT-tmpzhi2q](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzhi2q.md), [NOTE-tmpxik42](NOTE-tmpxik42.md)).** IIT's claim that a conscious
  system can have an unconscious functional equivalent (C7 there) is a
  denial of this paper's principle. The two cannot both be true for
  fine-grained isomorphs.
- **Seth ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md)).** Seth doubts substrate flexibility. This paper is the
  standard argument for it, for isomorphs only.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md).** The question to settle is whether organizational
  invariance, if true, licenses Schwitzgebel's upward move. It does not.
  It licenses the replacement cases only:
  - *Licensed:* a fine-grained functional isomorph of a conscious being
    is conscious with the same experiences. That holds whatever realises
    it, including a population of minded people (§3). So it refutes
    absolute anti-nesting, that is, Putnam's stipulation and IIT's
    exclusion where it would deny the whole, for isomorphs.
  - *Not licensed:* any system whose organisation is merely similar in
    kind or degree to a conscious one. The United States is not an
    isomorph of a human or a rabbit, and no organisation-preserving series
    reaches it. Schwitzgebel's step ([NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md), C1 and C7) needs a principle
    about *which* organisations suffice. The paper explicitly leaves that
    open (C9).
  - *Consequence for the THEORY:* if invariance holds, the anti-nesting
    route survives only in functional forms like Kammerer's. The dispute
    between a society of mind and a nation then becomes wholly
    architectural, which is the THEORY's second route. Invariance cannot
    settle it, because neither is an isomorph of the other or of us.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md).** The paper is the canonical argument for the conditional
  "an exact functional isomorph of a conscious system is conscious". No
  current AI system is such an isomorph, so the paper does not touch
  [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)'s claim that current evidence cannot settle AI consciousness.
- **[LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md).** No direct bearing.
- No ML instruction; not for the anthology.

## Limitations

- It is a plausibility argument throughout, by the author's own framing
  (the Schrödinger's-cat comparison, §2).
- The noticing premise is supported by familiar cases. An opponent can
  hold that self-knowledge is reliable only for the biological systems it
  was observed in, which is the question at issue.
- The generality of grain is asserted. The series is built neuron by
  neuron, and the move to coarser realisations, including Block's
  machine-table China system, is not shown.
- The population and single-homunculus extensions take one paragraph each,
  and they stipulate that organisation is preserved throughout.
- History and speed are open loopholes, conceded.
- It says nothing about which organisations are conscious. So it cannot
  adjudicate any case that is not an isomorph: animals, groups, AI systems.

## Open questions

- Is there a principled extension from isomorphism to similarity, for
  example a continuity principle over organisations and not over
  replacements? That is what would carry the paper's force to
  Schwitzgebel's US. The paper does not attempt it.
- Does the dancing-qualia argument survive a grain change, from neurons to
  machine-table states? That would decide whether it answers Block's main
  case or only n. 17.
