# QUESTION-260: Under what conditions is an organizational perspective constitutive of an agent's practical organization, rather than one of the perspectives that influence the agent, and can a perspective be agentic in its own right under the record's competing criteria of agency?

## Why it is a question

A38: "**What makes an organizational perspective the perspective *of* an
agent, rather than merely a perspective that influences the agent?** A
martial tradition influences a fighter. But it is not necessarily an
agent itself. A political culture influences a government. But it is not
identical to the government. A professional perspective influences an
engineer. But the engineer can contest, revise, and sometimes refuse to
enact it." A39 proposed the record's wording: "Under what conditions is
an organizational perspective constitutive of an agent's practical
organization, rather than merely one of the perspectives influencing that
agent? Can an organizational perspective be independently agentic, and
how would competing criteria of agency classify such cases?"

[CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md) holds that a perspective is not thereby an agent and that
the boundaries need not coincide. It does not say where a perspective
belongs to an agent. Two things keep this open:

- **The record has two criteria of agency.** [TERM-044](../terms.d/TERM-044.md) (Active) and
  [TERM-063](../terms.d/TERM-063.md) (Proposed) need not classify the same systems, and A39 advised
  against settling them here. An answer has to say how each classifies a
  tradition, an interaction and an institution's operative perspective.
- **A38's other half is unsettled.** Whether "Every sufficiently rich
  exercise of agency involves an organization of practical significance"
  depends on how broadly significance is read. On the enactive reading
  ([TERM-063](../terms.d/TERM-063.md)), "Agency and significance emerge together."

It refines [QUESTION-093](QUESTION-093.md), which asks which of predictive autonomy,
interventional autonomy, organizational closure and self-individuation a
coalition must have to be an agent. This asks it of perspectives, and
adds the converse: when a perspective is part of an agent and not beside
it. A38 suggested where the answer may lie: "a self-governing agent isn't
an entity that possesses one privileged perspective, but an organization
capable of exercising and sometimes changing its agency across a
plurality of perspectives". That is [CLAIM-424](../claims.d/CLAIM-424.md)'s capacity, offered as
a criterion, and not yet argued.
