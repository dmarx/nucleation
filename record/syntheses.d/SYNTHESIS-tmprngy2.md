# SYNTHESIS-tmprngy2: An organized individual that persists through changes of mode persists, on the record's proposed account, as the particular organization that keeps its repertoire realizable, inside it or outside it, and its identity over time as the causal transmission of that organization; locally realizable histories need not glue, and a coherent history can be terminal; and synchronization phenomena are offered as a laboratory that separates dependence and suppression by withdrawal, though a drive fixes where control lies and not whether it is coercive

*A part account of the realizability line: the part filed from the
exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (2026-11-06 to 07),
with A's question on representability from the paste of 2026-11-05. Most of
its claims are first in distributed-agency and carry realizability because
they bear on this line's questions. Every claim in it is Proposed. The
line's account is built over it and its four sibling parts.*

## The account

- **What persists.** CLAIM-611 (A's thesis across U6 to U13, corrected by
  the filer; interpretive, Proposed): what persists through changes of
  state and mode "is the particular organization that keeps its repertoire
  realizable, not the repertoire it keeps, nor any state, mode or
  boundary", and it "may lie inside the individual or outside it". Its body
  places the integration: "The locus of an individual's persistence
  through change of mode is layer 3 acting on layer 2." It returned from
  SYNTHESIS-003 (Superseded) at ADR-084, and its "Admission" section gives
  the case that admitted it.
- **Identity over time.** CLAIM-615 (A's integration, much of it the
  owner's U14 to U16, corrected by the filer; interpretive, Proposed),
  resting on CLAIM-611 and CLAIM-593: identity over time is "carried by
  the causal transmission of the relations that keep its repertoire
  realizable", so "a structuralism about individuals has to be historical".
  Of CLAIM-593's two directions, "The first carries over whole; the second
  only as consistency".
- **Histories.** CLAIM-625 (A's, with the reader's qualification;
  demonstrated, Proposed): over time intervals the viable histories form a
  sheaf, yet a viable history extends "exactly when its endpoint can stay
  viable for the remaining time, so a coherent history can be terminal"; it
  is "CLAIM-131's distinction carried from contexts to time intervals".
  CLAIM-623 (A's, with the reader's qualification; demonstrated, Proposed):
  forgetting a hidden mechanism can turn a sheaf of histories into a
  presheaf of realizable observations that is not a sheaf, so "a gluing
  failure need not show a disagreement on an overlap or a cycle of three
  contexts". ARG-074 (the assistant's wording on A's construction;
  existence, Proposed) concludes it from CASE-224 and adds that "a gluing
  failure need not show a disagreement on an overlap, and need not have an
  overlap".
- **The laboratory.** CLAIM-616 (the owner's U4, with A's qualification;
  interpretive, Proposed): synchronization phenomena can serve as a
  laboratory for the record's organizational distinctions, and "a drive
  fixes where control lies, not whether it is legitimate". CASE-225 (A's,
  Proposed): a forced Stuart–Landau oscillator in which "dependence for
  timing, dependence for performance and suppression of a latent capacity
  are three things", told apart only by withdrawals. ARG-072 (the
  assistant's wording; the thesis the owner's and the qualification A's;
  analogy, Proposed) concludes CLAIM-616 from CASE-225 and two readings:
  "What transfers is that the distinctions are coherent and can be
  separated by intervention".

What stays open is QUESTION-379 (A's, Open): when a coherent family of
local observations corresponds to an object the system can construct or
sustain, and whether it can be built within the resources available; and
QUESTION-437 (A's, Open), which refines it: whether a gluing failure of a
reduced variable identifies an omitted capacity, and can be told apart from
modelling error.
