# THEORY-499: The breakup of the Eulalia parent body beside the Jupiter 3:1 resonance, 785–965 Myr ago, can plausibly have supplied the cluster of large lunar craters dated near 800 Ma, but shown only for the most favourable of five model solutions, the one carried through, with an average largest lunar impactor too small for Copernicus, and with the cluster's absolute date inherited from Copernicus alone

## Source

Bottke, Vokrouhlický, Dykhuis & Zellner (2026), [LIT-009](../literature.d/LIT-009.md) (*The Planetary
Science Journal* 7, art. 171), read in full in [NOTE-002](../notes.d/NOTE-002.md) from the arXiv
preprint 2606.05036v1. Figures were read through their captions.

## What was actually shown

**The crater cluster (§2.1).** Seven lunar craters of D ≥ 20 km have mean
model ages of 760–820 Ma, from Copernicus (93.1 km) down to Al-Khwarizmi
(22.5 km). Their ages are N(1) superposed-crater densities scaled to
Copernicus = 800 Ma, the one crater of the seven dated by Apollo samples.
The scale is checked on two other sample-dated craters, Aristillus and
Autolycus.

**The source.** The Eulalia family is re-identified among overlapping
families. Yarkovsky/YORP chronology fits give five solutions with ages
T = 785, 855, 965, 920 and 865 Myr. The text says 800–900 Ma; only two of
the five fall in that range ([NOTE-002](../notes.d/NOTE-002.md), corrections). The parent body broke
up just inside the J3:1 resonance, so part of its debris went straight in
and more drifted in over 10⁸ years.

**The shower, Solution 5 only (§§5–6).** Table 1 gives 6,995 D ≥ 2 km and
833 D ≥ 5 km fragments into the J3:1. That is after a WISE incompleteness
factor F1 ≈ 2.3, which the authors call "a knob" and set by eye, and before
a ≈1.5× collisional correction. Impact probabilities per resonance entrant
are 5.4×10⁻³ (Venus), 4.2×10⁻³ (Earth) and 0.6×10⁻³ (Mars), from 61,065
test particles. The Moon is taken as 1/20 of Earth by cross-section, not
simulated. The largest expected impactor is about 8 km on Earth and Venus,
4.5 km on Mars and ">3 km" on the Moon. Most impacts fall in about
700–850 Ma.

**The comparison (§6.4).** In 10,000 trials of seven lunar projectiles,
the observed crater sizes "generally fall within the 1σ envelope".
Copernicus "would require a larger projectile" than the average.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The best solution could have
delivered too few multi-km bodies to make the cluster, or delivered them at
the wrong time. It did not. Whether the other four solutions would have is
not reported: "If Solution 5 cannot produce an adequate impact shower, the
results from Solutions 1–4 are unlikely to be relevant" (§6.2).

## What this does not say

- **That the shower happened.** The paper shows that a source exists which
  could have produced it. No crater of the cluster but Copernicus has a
  sample age. The only compositional link is Morgan et al.'s 1973 trace
  elements in Apollo 12 material, which the paper calls "compelling albeit
  circumstantial".
- **That the cluster is at 800 Ma.** It is a cluster in N(1). If
  Copernicus's ray-sample age were not its formation age, every date in it
  would move together.
- **A central estimate.** Only the most favourable of five solutions is
  propagated, F1 is tuned by eye, and the size distribution is extrapolated
  at both ends. [NOTE-002](../notes.d/NOTE-002.md) reads the result as an existence argument.
- **That three-quarters of the family went into the J3:1.** That fraction
  is a geometric first approximation (§3.1). The simulations report
  absolute yields, not the fraction.
- **Anything settled about Earth, Mars or Venus.** Links to the Bitter
  Springs Anomaly, Snowball Earth, eukaryote diversification, Martian
  caldera volcanism and Venus resurfacing are flagged by the authors as
  "necessarily speculative" (§7).
- **That such showers leave the dating clock alone.** The argument that a
  family shower raises the large-impactor flux for 10⁸ years while changing
  small-crater density by only about 6% (App. A.2) is a back-of-envelope
  estimate.

## Connections

- The model leaves out a possible second, lower-(e, I) half of the family,
  which could raise the flux "potentially by a factor of two" (§3.2.2). The
  shower could be larger than modelled as well as smaller.
- The template event is the 465.8 Ma L-chondrite breakup, whose source
  family the paper leaves open. The record holds no reading of it.
- No other THEORY in the record bears on impact chronology.
