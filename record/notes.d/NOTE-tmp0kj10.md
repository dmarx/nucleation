# NOTE-tmp0kj10: Précis of Breakdown of Will

## Contribution

The précis compresses the twelve chapters of *Breakdown of Will*
([LIT-tmpmhu3j](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmhu3j.md)) into one argument. It starts from a single empirical premise,
that vertebrates discount delayed rewards hyperbolically, and derives from
it three things usually treated as primitives: a divided self, a will, and
the will's characteristic failures. It also proposes to replace classical
conditioning with reward as the selector of "involuntary" processes such as
pain, emotion and appetite.

## Key insight

If value is roughly divided by delay, a smaller reward that is imminent can
outweigh a larger later one, and the same person will prefer each at a
different time. Those time-bound preferences grow into "interests" that act
strategically against each other. A person who sees that today's choice
predicts tomorrow's can make each choice stand for a whole series of
choices, and summed hyperbolic curves favour the larger, later series. So
willpower is a self-enforcing contract among successive selves, made of
self-prediction, and it is as fragile as any such contract: one defection
lowers the credibility on which the next cooperation depends. "Will, in
short, is a bargaining situation, where credibility is power" (§8.1).

## Assumptions

- **Hyperbolic discounting is basic** (§3). Value is inversely proportional
  to delay plus a small constant (Mazur 1987, one free "impatience"
  parameter). Fits with an exponent on the denominator are reported, but the
  exponent is "usually close to 1.0".
- **Rough additivity.** The discounted effects of each reward in a series
  "simply add" (Mazur 1997). The précis says the bargaining model needs
  "almost no extra assumptions-- only rough additiveness" (§6.1).
- **Self-awareness.** Bundling by perception requires "a degree of
  self-awareness probably unavailable to nonhumans" (§5). Animals show
  commitment devices, but not, on this account, will.
- **A functional definition of reward**: "that which increases the
  likelihood that the processes it follows will recur", stripped of its
  connotation of pleasure (§4.1). The account of pain and emotion depends on
  this.
- **A common currency.** Note 1 cites Shizgal and Conover: "the utility of
  all competing resources must be represented on a single, common
  dimension".

## Key results

The précis is a theoretical synthesis. Its results are arguments, with
cited experiments as support.

- **Preference reversal** (§3). Hyperbolic curves predict a "strong but
  temporary" preference for the smaller, sooner (SS) reward just before it
  is due. The précis cites observations in animals and in people choosing
  between juice, video-game access, relief from noise and money.
- **Commitment in animals** (§5). Pigeons choose in advance an option that
  removes the SS alternative (Ainslie 1974). Rats press a bar committing them
  to 0.5 s of shock 40 s later instead of 5 s of shock 45 s later, rather
  than leave the choice open and then, almost always, take the imminent
  0.5 s (Deluty et al. 1983).
- **Four tactics of self-control** (§5): external constraints, attention
  control, emotion control, and will. Only will is "at once the strongest
  and most versatile".
- **Bundling** (§§5–6). Choosing for a series raises the relative value of
  larger, later (LL) rewards under hyperbolic but not exponential curves
  (Figures 3A–3B). Students chose LL money more often when choosing for five
  weeks at once (Kirby & Guastello 2001). Rats chose LL more when choosing
  for a block of three trials (Ainslie & Monterosso 2003). Bundling makes
  summed curves look "more exponential" (Figure 5).
- **Exceptions and bright lines** (§6.1). Short-range interests find
  exceptions and long-range interests define principles. A rule of no
  alcohol is more stable than two drinks a day because "the line between
  some drinking and no drinking is unique (bright)".
- **Five models of will compared** (§8): null (Ryle; Becker & Murphy),
  organ (Baumeister & Heatherton), resolute choice (Bratman; McClennen),
  pattern-seeking (Rachlin) and intertemporal bargaining. Bargaining is
  "the only model that explains both temporary preference and adequate
  incentive to overcome it from the properties of the rewards involved".
- **Kavka's toxin** (§8.1). Intending is classifying an act as a precedent,
  staking the value of the series on it. The subject can sincerely intend
  to drink the toxin because reneging injures the credibility of her later
  intentions. "Only intertemporal bargaining makes it affirmatively
  rational."
- **Side effects of will** (§9): rules overshadow goods-in-themselves (§9.1);
  rules magnify lapses into "lapse districts" (§9.2); rules motivate
  misperception of lapses (§9.3); rules may serve compulsions, since
  "well-marked criteria may win out simply because they offer more
  stability" (§9.4). Hence "rationality is elusive" (§9.5).
- **Premature satiation** (§§10–11). An organism that discounts
  hyperbolically consumes a self-generated reward too fast. Emotional reward
  stays strong only when its occasions are uncertain or "mysterious". This
  is offered as the reason beliefs feel like facts, why other people move
  us, and why some goals can only be reached indirectly.

## Concepts

- **interest**: "the processes selected for by a particular kind of
  reward"; named only where rewards that dominate at different times
  conflict (§3.1).
- **picoeconomics**: "a micro-microeconomics" of the internal marketplace
  (§3.1).
- **personal rule**: a rule to treat all members of a category alike,
  enforced by the stake that each choice puts on the whole category (§6).
- **bundling**: summing the discounted values of a series of rewards by
  perceiving one choice as predicting the rest (§5).
- **lapse district**: an area of behaviour where a person no longer dares
  attempt will, fenced off to protect the rule elsewhere (§9.2).
- **premature satiation**: harvesting emotional reward "before it's ripe",
  so that appetite is wasted (§§10–11).

## Connections

- **Schelling.** The précis cites Schelling (1960) for "limited warfare"
  among successive motivational states. Schelling's own account of
  self-command, read here as [LIT-tmpwkj6a](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwkj6a.md) ([NOTE-tmpacu07](NOTE-tmpacu07.md)), describes the
  same divided chooser from the side of tactics. Schelling's stark cases
  (the pain button, the stroke device) are commitment devices of
  Ainslie's first kind. His New Year's resolution, which "raises the stakes"
  by violating "one's personal constitution", is a personal rule.
- **Berridge.** The précis takes Berridge and Robinson's "wanted" but not
  "liked" behaviours (1998) as instances of its itch-like cycle, and rejects
  their reading of them as classically conditioned. Dayan and Berridge
  ([LIT-tmpmlzuo](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmlzuo.md)) defend Pavlovian prediction as model-based. They do not
  reduce it to instrumental reward, so the two disagree about conditioning.
- **Dual controllers.** Daw, Niv and Dayan ([LIT-tmpk0t5p](../literature.d/LIT-tmpk0t5p.md)) reject the
  economic picture of an "irrational" limbic system fighting a "rational"
  prefrontal one, and give the two controllers the same ends. Ainslie
  rejects the same picture for a different reason: there are many
  controllers, and their ends differ only by time.
- **Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen ([LIT-tmpncuk4](../literature.d/LIT-tmpncuk4.md))** treat patient
  intertemporal choice as a "default override" needing costly control from
  a dedicated specification system. That is close to the "organ" model the
  précis rejects. The two accounts are not tested against each other in
  either text.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)** (regulation is not separate from generation). The précis
  supports its architecture from outside emotion research. Self-control is
  the interaction of first-order interests, and there is no governor:
  "Such interests are not options chosen by an overarching ego, the top
  down model". It also addresses the theory's stated largest gap, which
  state wins: the one with "the greatest discounted reward at a given
  moment", with bundled future rewards added by self-prediction. It differs
  from the theory in one respect. The extra motive that will recruits is
  not a concurrent emotion but an expectation about one's own future
  choices. Whether that is "one motive state checking another" or a
  distinct kind of process is a real question for the theory. The précis's
  account is filed as [THEORY-tmpgx68d](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpgx68d.md), and [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) says where it stands.
- **[THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md)** (higher-order attitudes cannot confer ownership by order
  alone). The précis does not discuss ownership. It does reject
  hierarchical, faculty-based accounts of the will's force, by a regress of
  the same shape: an organ of will "has to be guided by some evaluation
  process outside of motivation". It also suggests Bratman's resolute
  choice reduces to bargaining. If so, a planning agent's authority would
  rest on the recursive stake of precedent and not on the order of an
  attitude. That bears on [THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md) without deciding it.
- **Self-determination theory ([LIT-558](../literature.d/LIT-558.md), [LIT-560](../literature.d/LIT-560.md); [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md), [THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md),
  [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)).** Two points of contact, neither cited by either side. The
  will's first side effect, choices "detached from their immediate
  outcomes" with "an aloof, legalistic quality", resembles introjected
  regulation ([THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)). The indirection puzzle, in which "Trying to have
  fun usually spoils the fun", parallels the undermining of intrinsic
  motivation ([THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md)). The mechanisms are different, so this is a
  convergence of phenomena, not support for either account. SDT also denies
  that will is "a non-brain-related force" ([LIT-560](../literature.d/LIT-560.md), as read in [NOTE-441](NOTE-441.md)).
  Ainslie's will is likewise not a force but a pattern of reward.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **A précis, not the argument in full.** The précis says it is "necessarily
  selective". Derivations are referred to the book and to Ainslie (1992).
- **The central mechanism is recursive and hard to test.** The précis says
  so (§8). Its best evidence is that it resolves philosophers' thought
  experiments, which shows coherence, not truth.
- **Reported, not re-examined.** The hyperbolic-discounting literature is
  cited, not reviewed. Whether hyperbolic fits beat alternatives such as
  quasi-hyperbolic or two-system models is not discussed.
- **The commentaries were not read.** BBS printed objections and a response
  with the précis, and none of them is reflected here.
- **The account of emotion (§§4, 10–11) is the most speculative part.** Its
  model of pain as a fast cycle of reward and non-reward is offered "in
  theory at least" and is not tested.

## Open questions

- Does bundling by self-prediction, rather than an external
  choose-for-the-series manipulation, produce the predicted shift? Kirby
  and Guastello's suggestion condition gave "an intermediate degree of
  self-control", which is suggestive only.
- Can a lapse district be shown to follow a first failure of a personal
  rule, as the account predicts, rather than to precede it?
- What do the BBS commentaries object to, and does the response meet them?
