# LIT-1279: Video Ergo Sum: Manipulating Bodily Self-Consciousness

Lenggenhager, B., Tadi, T., Metzinger, T. & Blanke, O. (2007). Video ergo sum: manipulating bodily self-consciousness. *Science* 317(5841), 1096–1099. DOI 10.1126/science.1143439.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-999](../notes.d/NOTE-999.md).*

- **From hand to whole body.** The rubber hand illusion manipulates
  ownership and location of a body part, "a part-to-whole relationship",
  and so "did not experimentally manipulate selfhood per se". This
  experiment aims at the whole body: global self-attribution and global
  self-localization.
- **Synchrony moves felt self-location.** In study I, after synchronous
  stroking participants returned to a point 24.1 ± 9.0 cm (mean ± SEM)
  toward the seen body, against 12.5 ± 8.5 cm, not significant, after
  asynchronous stroking. The first three questionnaire items, on where
  the touch was felt, what caused it, and whether the seen body felt like
  one's own, were answered positively only after synchronous stroking.
- **Body-likeness matters.** In study II, synchronous stroking drew
  participants toward their own filmed body (17.1 cm) and a mannequin
  (25.4 cm), but only 14.3 cm, not significant, toward a white
  rectangular object. People felt the touch on the object but did not
  feel it was their body. The authors read this as bottom-up integration
  with a cognitive constraint, "rejecting a pure Bayesian account".
- **Not an out-of-body experience.** No participant reported overt
  disembodiment or a change in visual perspective. The authors call the
  result closer to heautoscopy and propose that a full out-of-body
  experience needs more, such as visual-vestibular conflict. Their
  pointer to the temporo-parietal junction is speculation; nothing was
  imaged.
- **The effects are modest.** The drift is about a tenth of the 2 m
  distance; in study II the own-body condition did not differ between
  synchronous and asynchronous stroking; and critics in the 2008 e-letters
  argued that the result may be recognising oneself in a video, not an
  illusion of ownership.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-19 at the owner's request to fill the record's gap
around the rubber hand illusion and bodily self-consciousness. It is the
experiment that Metzinger's "Self models" ([LIT-450](LIT-450.md), read in [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md))
cites for its claim C6, that body ownership can be manipulated by
multisensory conflict and is the simplest form of self-awareness.
Metzinger is a co-author. The reading supports the first half of C6 as
[NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md) graded it, moderate, with smaller and less consistent effects
than the citation suggests. The second half, that global ownership is the
simplest self-awareness, is imported in this paper from Metzinger's
book, not tested.

It bears on [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) only in the way that theory's `promote_when` already
anticipates: it shows the bodily self-model can be manipulated, not that
it is all a self is. It bears on [LIT-1186](LIT-1186.md), an attempted out-of-body
perception study, by drawing a line between illusory self-location and
the disembodiment that defines an out-of-body experience. It carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice.
