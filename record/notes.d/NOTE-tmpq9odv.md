# NOTE-tmpq9odv: Longo et al. — What is embodiment? A psychometric approach

## Contribution

The paper is the first to measure the experience of the rubber hand
illusion with many items in a large sample and to ask what latent
structure the reports have. It gives the field a 27-item questionnaire,
a decomposition of "embodiment" of a hand into ownership, location and
agency, and a link between the ownership and location components and
proprioceptive drift. It also finds that the asynchronous control is not
a neutral baseline: it has an experience of its own, deafference.

## Key insight

Owning a seen hand is not one feeling. In report, it is a cluster of
three, the hand being mine, being where my hand is, and being under my
control, which move together across people but can be pulled apart. The
illusion moves the first two and not the third. On the authors' reading,
"'me-ness' is not reducible to 'here-ness'" (p. 992), and the new hand
replaces the old one instead of being added to it.

## Assumptions

- **Item covariance across people reveals the structure of an
  experience.** "To the extent that participants do not agree, no
  structure should emerge. Conversely, to the extent that structure does
  emerge ... it demonstrates agreement (however implicit) across
  participants regarding the structure of their experience" (p. 994).
  This is the inference the whole paper rests on.
- **The items cover the experience.** The 27 statements came from free
  reports of five pilot participants and from hypothesised constructs
  such as ownership and agency (p. 982). The authors grant that "there
  are no doubt additional components of embodiment which were not
  covered by our questionnaire" (p. 995). No item asks about the whole
  body, self-location in the room or perspective.
- **PCA with varimax rotation, run separately per condition.** The number
  of components came from "the scree plot and eigenvalues" (p. 982).
  Varimax makes the components orthogonal by construction, so component
  scores are uncorrelated within a condition.
- **The secondary split.** Items loading above .50 on embodiment (items
  1–10) were re-analysed alone (p. 986). Three subcomponents were kept,
  though by Table 2 the second and third have eigenvalues of 0.75 and
  0.66 (synchronous) and 0.84 and 0.71 (asynchronous), against 6.49 and
  6.07 for the first. Before rotation, then, one component carries about
  65% and 61% of the variance of these ten items (my arithmetic from
  Table 2's eigenvalues). The paper does not state the criterion for
  keeping components below 1.
- **Design.** Within subjects: each participant did both conditions, in
  alternating order (66 synchronous first, 65 asynchronous first), on the
  right hand (N = 67) or left (N = 64). Induction was 60 s at about 1 Hz;
  asynchrony was 180° out of phase (pp. 981–982). The first two items
  were always "enjoyable" and "interesting"; the rest were randomised.
- **Sample.** "Current and prospective students" recruited at university
  open days, 75 of 131 female (p. 981).

## Key results

- **Top-level structure (Table 1, pp. 983–986).** Synchronous: four
  components, 55.3% of variance; embodiment of rubber hand 26.3%
  (eigenvalue 9.52), loss of own hand 12.2%, movement 9.0%, affect 7.8%.
  Asynchronous: five components, 59.4%; embodiment 24.6% (9.74), then
  deafference 9.6%, movement 9.2%, loss of own hand 8.4%, affect 7.6%.
  Deafference (items 23–25: pins and needles, numbness, "the experience
  of my hands was less vivid than normal") loads only in the asynchronous
  analysis (.580, .780, .711).
- **Item 27 loads on nothing.** "It seemed like I was feeling the touch of
  the paintbrush in the location where I saw the rubber hand being
  touched", close to the item the original report found most strongly
  affirmed, has no loading above .50 in either condition (communalities
  .199 and .277; Table 1, p. 985). Item 8, that the touch "was caused by
  the paintbrush touching the rubber hand", loads on embodiment and then
  on location. The paper does not comment on item 27.
- **Ownership, location, agency (Table 2, p. 987).** Synchronous rotated
  variance 35.4%, 24.8%, 18.8% (79.0% in all); asynchronous 34.3%, 23.7%,
  18.2% (76.2%). Ownership: items 1–5 (looking at my own hand, rubber hand
  resembling mine, belonging to me, being my hand, being part of my body;
  loadings .696–.811 synchronous). Location: items 6–8 (.776, .831, .670).
  Agency: items 9 ("I could have moved the rubber hand if I had wanted",
  .843) and 10 ("I was in control of the rubber hand", .745). In the
  asynchronous analysis items 1 and 6 load on both ownership and location.
- **Presence by condition, top level (pp. 988–989, Fig. 1).** Condition ×
  component interaction F(3, 390) = 69.24, p < .0001 (partial η² ≈ .35,
  my computation). Embodiment was "significantly positive following
  synchronous stroking, but significantly negative following asynchronous
  stroking": about +0.7 against −1.2 on the −3 to +3 scale, read off
  Fig. 1. Loss of own hand was higher after synchrony, about +0.1 against
  −0.3, so near neutral in both. Movement was more negative after
  synchrony (about −2.4 against −1.2); affect positive in both (about
  +1.6 against +1.35). Deafference after asynchrony, about +0.3.
- **Presence by condition, subcomponents (p. 989, Fig. 2).** Condition
  F(1, 130) = 110.69, p < .0001; component × condition F(2, 260) = 3.60,
  p < .05 (partial η² ≈ .04, my computation). "Component scores for
  ownership and location were positive following synchronous stroking,
  but negative following asynchronous stroking. Agency scores were
  negative in both conditions." Read off Fig. 2: ownership about +0.35
  against −0.55, location about +0.7 against −0.65, agency about −0.55
  against −1.1. So synchrony raised agency scores too, by roughly as much
  as the others, without bringing them above zero.
- **Sex and laterality (p. 990).** Women agreed more with items overall
  (−.055 against −.324; F(1, 127) = 4.06, p < .05), which the authors
  relate to women being "slightly more suggestible than males". The
  effect did not interact with component or synchrony. Laterality had no
  effect. Suggestibility was not measured.
- **Proprioceptive drift (p. 990).** n = 120 (11 missing or unusable).
  Pre-test bias toward the midline 0.58 cm, t(119) = 2.43, p < .05.
  Further displacement toward the rubber hand after synchronous stroking
  1.34 cm, t(119) = 4.56, p < .0001; after asynchronous 0.30 cm, t(119) =
  1.20, n.s.; difference t(119) = 3.22, p < .005 (dz ≈ 0.29, my
  computation). Drift was measured before the questionnaire.
- **Report predicts drift (pp. 990–991).** Regressing the synchronous
  minus asynchronous difference in drift on the differences in component
  scores: embodiment β = 0.609, t(115) = 3.40, p < .001; movement β =
  0.290, p = .096; loss of own hand and affect n.s. Among the
  subcomponents: location β = .485, t(116) = 3.02, p < .005; ownership
  β = .425, t(116) = 2.85, p < .01; agency β = .110, n.s. "Proprioceptive
  displacement therefore appears to be a genuine by-product of
  embodiment, but also related specifically to body ownership, but not
  agency" (p. 991).
- **Body constancy (pp. 991–992).** From loss of own hand: "the novel
  rubber hand is incorporated by functionally suppressing the existing
  hand, rather than by adding an additional 'supernumerary' hand". Item
  19, "I had three hands", loaded on movement, not on embodiment.
- **Touch goes with location, not ownership (p. 992).** Because item 8
  loaded on location, "somatic sensory experiences are tied to location,
  not to ownership", which the authors compare with somatoparaphrenic
  patients who feel touch on a hand they disown.
- **Two causes (p. 993).** The asynchronous condition counts as conflict
  only if "some rudimentary form of ownership of the rubber hand is
  elicited merely from the visual perception of the rubber hand", so
  "there are at least two types of cause of the rubber hand illusion:
  purely visual information from the perception of the hand in plausible
  configuration, and multisensory synchrony."
- **Ownership and agency, both ways.** "We provided the first direct
  empirical evidence confirming the intuition that body ownership and
  agency reflect dissociable components of embodiment" (p. 991). And:
  "ownership and location along with the sense of agency grouped
  together in the omnibus PCA ... This experiential link between the
  senses of ownership and agency belies the traditional view that these
  are distinct" (p. 995).

## Concepts

- **Embodiment** — "the sense of one's own body" (p. 979), here measured
  as the experience of a rubber hand as part of one's body. The authors
  grant the word has other senses in cognitive science (p. 995).
- **Ownership** — items 1–5: the rubber hand belongs to me, is my hand,
  is part of my body, looks like mine, and I seem to be looking at my own
  hand.
- **Location** — items 6–8: my hand is where the rubber hand is, or the
  reverse, and the felt touch is caused by the brush on the rubber hand.
  It is the location of a hand, not of the self.
- **Agency** — items 9–10: felt capacity to move and control the rubber
  hand. No movement occurs, so these are judgements of possible control,
  not the experience of controlling an act.
- **Loss of own hand** — items 12–16: unable to move my hand, my hand has
  disappeared, out of my control, couldn't tell where it was, and
  (negatively) could have moved it.
- **Deafference** — items 23–25: pins and needles, numbness, hands less
  vivid than normal.
- **Body constancy** — the authors' proposed principle that an
  incorporated object replaces an existing part.

## Connections

The study is by Haggard's group, then at UCL, and builds on Botvinick &
Cohen (1998), which it cites as the source of the illusion, and on
Tsakiris & Haggard (2005) and Tsakiris, Prabhu & Haggard (2006) on
ownership and agency. It takes Gallagher (2000) as the source of the
ownership–agency distinction and Jeannerod (2007) as a view on which
location is constitutive of selfhood, which it argues against. It sets
itself against accounts that tie bodily self to a single system, naming
Damasio's visceral interoception (p. 993). None of the cited works was
read here.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmpef8lf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpef8lf.md) (Proposed), the synchrony effect.** This is the
  first replication in the record that gives what the first half of the
  promotion test asks for: a sample size (131, 120 for drift), named tests
  (repeated-measures ANOVA, t-tests, regressions) and drift measured in
  both conditions. Effect sizes are not reported as such but can be
  computed from the statistics given. It **partly meets** the test and
  does not meet its core: there is no measure or matching of
  expectations, and no trait suggestibility measure. The sex effect,
  which the authors relate to suggestibility, did not interact with
  synchrony, but that is not a suggestibility control. The design is
  within subjects, so each participant saw both conditions and could
  infer which was meant to work. It **bears against the drift refutation
  clause**: drift was 1.34 cm after synchrony and 0.30 cm, not
  significant, after asynchrony, so drift did not appear as strongly
  under asynchrony. It also differs from the 1998 report in ways the
  THEORY should note: 60 s of brushing rather than ten minutes, and item
  27, close to the original's most strongly affirmed item about referred
  touch, did not load on any component.
- **[THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md) (Proposed), graded common-cause ownership.** The
  loss-of-own-hand component, higher after synchrony, is a sign on a
  measure other than temperature or histamine that the real hand loses
  something as the rubber hand gains ownership, which is what the
  promotion test's second result asks for. It **partly meets** that
  requirement and no more. The component sits near neutral in both
  conditions, its items are about paralysis, disappearance and control,
  not about the real hand ceasing to be mine, and it did not predict
  drift. Because varimax makes components uncorrelated, the paper cannot
  show the real hand's loss tracking the rubber hand's gain across
  people. Nothing is varied parametrically and no model is fitted, so the
  first, model-based half of the test is not touched. The authors' "two
  causes", visual plausibility and synchrony (p. 993), fit the THEORY's
  use of a prior, but are inferred from other studies, not shown here.
- **[THEORY-tmpom3z2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpom3z2.md) (Proposed), interoception.** No bearing. There are no
  interoceptive items or measures. The authors reject linking bodily self
  to "a single somatic sensory system such as visceral interoception"
  (p. 993), but that is an argument against singularity, not evidence
  against an interoceptive contribution.
- **[THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md) (Proposed), minimal selfhood as whole-body
  identification.** It **does not meet** the first promotion condition
  and does not refute it: no item measures whole-body self-location or
  self-identification, so it is another rubber-hand study that did not
  look. What it adds is a part-level parallel, which is the record's
  pairing and not the authors': ownership (identification with a hand)
  and location (where the hand is felt) are separable within the hand,
  much as Blanke & Metzinger separate global self-identification from
  self-location ([LIT-tmpnbv1d](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnbv1d.md), [NOTE-tmplqqpe](NOTE-tmplqqpe.md)). That fits the THEORY's
  dimensions at a smaller scale; it is no evidence that the hand reaches
  the whole. It says nothing on the second, developmental condition.
- **[THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md) (Proposed), ownership and agency.** The condition means
  give ownership above neutral and agency below it after synchrony, which
  is consistent with the distinction. The structure also shows the two
  cohering: the agency items load with ownership on the top-level
  component (.651 and .740), and synchrony raised agency scores along
  with ownership. The authors say both that the two are dissociable and
  that their grouping "belies the traditional view that these are
  distinct". The agency measured is felt capacity to move a hand that
  never moved, not the sense of initiating an act, so the comparator half
  of [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md) is not tested. Haggard's own later review ([LIT-1009](../literature.d/LIT-1009.md),
  [NOTE-827](NOTE-827.md)) takes agency in the narrow motor sense these items do not
  reach, and [THEORY-322](../theory.d/THEORY-322.md) is about that sense; neither is touched.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md).** The paper's central argument (p. 994) is that
  structure in the covariance of introspective reports across people
  "demonstrates agreement ... regarding the structure of their
  experience", and that its relation to drift "attests to its validity".
  This is report evidence, and it does not separate experience from
  report. Items with similar wording can cluster because people read them
  alike, and a shared expectation of what the synchronous condition
  should feel like would produce shared structure too. The drift link is
  convergence with a behavioural measure that is itself not a measure of
  experience. The works by Lush and colleagues being filed alongside
  address the expectation side.
- **[NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md), claim C6.** A second, larger primary demonstration that
  ownership of a body part moves with multisensory conflict. Like
  Botvinick & Cohen it concerns a part, not the global ownership C6
  calls "the most simple form of self-awareness".
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **No control for expectation or demand.** Within-subjects design,
  predictable intended answers, no expectation or suggestibility
  measure. The authors invoke suggestibility only to explain the sex
  difference.
- **The three-way split is thin.** It comes from a second rotation of
  ten items whose first unrotated component carries most of their
  variance, with two components below an eigenvalue of 1 retained.
  Different rotation or retention choices could give a different split.
  The paper reports no reliability coefficients and no confirmatory
  analysis.
- **Same sample, two analyses.** The "convergent evidence" that the same
  components appear in both conditions (p. 986) comes from the same 131
  people rating the same items twice, so it is not an independent
  replication of the structure.
- **Small item-to-person ratio for PCA.** 27 items and 131 people, about
  five per item.
- **Short induction.** 60 s, with drift measured before the questionnaire,
  so the questionnaire reflects the experience after a delay.
- **Figure values are approximate.** The component means are given only
  as bar charts; the values above are read off them. Item means are in a
  supplement not read here.
- **A hand, not a body.** The authors say the structure "may or may not
  generalise" to other senses of embodiment (p. 995). It says nothing
  about self-location or perspective.

## Open questions

- Does the four-component structure, and the ownership–location–agency
  split, survive a confirmatory factor analysis in an independent sample?
- Does the structure survive when expectations of the two conditions are
  measured or matched? If participants expecting the illusion produce the
  same pattern without synchrony, the structure is of expectation, not
  of the experience.
- Would whole-body items (where am I, which body am I) move with the
  hand-level ownership and location components, or stay put? That is the
  measurement [THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md)'s promotion test asks for.
- Is loss of own hand traded against rubber-hand ownership person by
  person? An oblique rotation or a direct correlation of item scores would
  show it; the orthogonal analysis here cannot.
- Why did the classic referred-touch item load on nothing?
