# LIT-tmp3n00g: Microchimerism in the human brain, quantitative assessment and single nuclei profiling establish cell types and diversity

Sami B. Kanaan, Ashley McDonough, Coline Gentil, Jeff Ojemann, Haynes
Heaton and others (2026). "Microchimerism in the human brain, quantitative
assessment and single nuclei profiling establish cell types and diversity."
bioRxiv preprint, v1 2026-06-10, v2 2026-10-01.
DOI-10.64898/2026.06.05.730225.

## Key takeaways

*Not read.* From the abstract: maternal cells are common in the human brain,
take neuronal (notably L2/3 intratelencephalic) and microglial or
perivascular macrophage identities among others, differ transcriptionally
from host nuclei, and persist into old age at diminishing frequency. A at
A11 §4 notes that the authors leave orthogonal genetic validation, spatial
localization and cell fusion or artefact open. That is unverified.
