# CASE-tmprzyow: The doorway effect: memory for objects just handled is worse after walking through a doorway into another room than after walking as far within one, returning to the first room does not restore it, and some replications find no effect

## The case

The owner, at U37: "I think we also may have landed on a likely (at least
partial) simple, high-level explanation (or at the very least intuition)
for The Doorway Effect (i.e. connect perspective and grounded
experience)."

The studies, from their abstracts, all registered unread:

- **The finding.** Radvansky and Copeland ([LIT-tmpw4lki](../literature.d/LIT-tmpw4lki.md)): in a virtual
  environment, memory for objects just picked up or set down was worse
  after walking through a doorway than after walking the same distance
  within a room, read as the updating of situation models at a boundary.
- **Not the context of encoding.** Radvansky, Krawietz and Tamplin
  ([LIT-tmpl1ntz](../literature.d/LIT-tmpl1ntz.md)): the effect held with smaller displays and in a real
  environment, and returning to the original room did not improve memory,
  against an encoding-specificity account.
- **Not time or disruption.** Pettijohn and Radvansky ([LIT-tmpl8f6c](../literature.d/LIT-tmpl8f6c.md)):
  travel times were similar and a 3-second delay did not change the
  pattern, which the authors attribute to interference at retrieval among
  event models that share elements.
- **Not always.** McFadyen et al. ([LIT-tmpm7ybd](../literature.d/LIT-tmpm7ybd.md)): four experiments, in
  immersive virtual reality with and without a working-memory load, in
  video and in real movement, found no significant doorway effect; under
  load, false alarms to mismatched probes rose after a doorway.
- **The theory behind it.** Event segmentation theory (Zacks et al.,
  [LIT-tmphg1bq](../literature.d/LIT-tmphg1bq.md)): a rise in prediction error is perceived as an event
  boundary, and boundaries update the event models that guide attention
  and memory.

A37 also cited a 2026 virtual-reality study reporting an event-boundary
effect; it was not identified and is not filed.

## What it shows

A37's reading, which the owner's U37 prompted: "The intention hasn't
necessarily disappeared from memory. It may have lost accessibility
because the current situation is being organized through a different
event model, one in which the intention's earlier contextual supports are
no longer active." So "an inaccessible intention is not necessarily an
erased intention", and information retained, information currently
accessible and information deployed come apart, which is [TERM-055](../terms.d/TERM-055.md)'s
distinction. That is activation interruption ([CLAIM-tmpj73lm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpj73lm.md)).

The return-to-the-room result matters most for the record's account. A37:
"So our theory shouldn't reduce perspective to location. **The relevant
context is an organized, goal-sensitive relationship to the
environment—not merely the current coordinates of the body.**" A37
proposed two discriminating variants: a doorway crossed while a salient
task continues, and a change of task with no doorway.

## What it does not show

- **That the effect is robust.** McFadyen et al. found none under four
  conditions. A37: "it's important not to propose that doorways invariably
  reset memory."
- **A new explanation.** A37: "That isn't yet a new scientific
  explanation. It's a way of interpreting established event-cognition
  findings through our broader organizational-perspective framework." The
  owner's U37 offered it as at least partial, or as intuition, and the
  case keeps that.
- **That Minsky's K-lines ([NOTE-352](../notes.d/NOTE-352.md)) or the doorway options of
  hierarchical reinforcement learning ([THEORY-688](../theory.d/THEORY-688.md)) are its mechanism.**
  A37 used both as analogies and said so: the first "is a theoretical
  account of memory, not an established neural mechanism for the doorway
  effect"; the second are "computational results about hierarchical action
  learning, not experiments on human doorway forgetting".
- **Standing.** The studies are documented; the reading is the exchange's,
  and the papers have not been read.
