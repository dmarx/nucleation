# NOTE-492: Block — Functional Role and Truth Conditions

## Contribution

It turns the "Advertisement"'s objection to Harman into a general
convergence result: the private-use and public-use programmes in functional
role semantics, pursued until they handle Twin Earth, the travelling
speaker, borrowed reference and the division of linguistic labour, become
the same two-factor theory. That makes room for narrow content inside any
use theory, without arguing that narrow content is explanatory.

## Key insight

Whatever extra machinery a long-arm role needs to get reference right is a
theory of reference in disguise. Subtract it and what remains is the
internal role, which is the same in the twins. So the choice between "one
factor" and "two" is a choice of bookkeeping, and n. 31 says which
bookkeeping suits which purpose.

## Assumptions

- **Thinking in a representational system**, though not necessarily
  sentences in the brain: "structured states" suffice (p. 160).
- **Functional role** includes inferential role "plus more", including
  causal relations to inputs and outputs; a reductionist version must state
  it non-intentionally (pp. 160–162).
- **Putnam's Twin Earth verdict** that the two dialects' 'aluminum'
  utterances differ in truth conditions is granted (p. 167).
- **Public use "plus private use"**: Block sees no non-behaviourist reason to
  include thinking out loud and exclude thinking quietly (p. 174).

## Key results

- **Five facts explained (pp. 162–166)**, as listed in the LIT. Functional
  role semantics supports no Davidsonian truth-maximising or Dennettian
  rationality-maximising charity (p. 166).
- **Non-reductionist failure (pp. 168–169).** Defining a term through other
  terms' truth conditions cannot separate 'aluminum' from twin 'aluminum',
  since the defining terms share extensions; with synonyms it only moves the
  question to 'munimula'.
- **Two-factor option (pp. 170–171).** The internal factor explains the
  shared behaviour (sticking a bus token in the fuse box on either planet);
  the second factor, for example a causal theory, explains the difference.
  "The functional role factor determines the nature of the referential-
  truth-conditional factor (without determining reference or truth
  conditions themselves)." Accepting it does not literally require narrow
  content (p. 171).
- **Metaphysical picture for private use (pp. 172–173).** Inner
  representations are "semantically active" (Haugeland); public tokens have
  life only in our deliberate use.
- **Public use (pp. 174–178).** Movie-camera public use is identical on both
  planets; semantic descriptions of use beg the question; long-arm roles work
  only with "normal context" (Harman) or "dominant causal source" (Evans,
  Devitt), with borrowed reference reaching back to Moses, and with experts
  for elm and beech. The internal part, "at least partly inferential", is the
  same for both communities and "can be identified with the narrow meaning
  ... if narrow meaning exists"; the external part "looks very like a
  traditional theory of reference" (p. 178).
- **Appendix (pp. 179–181).** Burge cases cannot be traced to different
  causal sources, since the counterfactual language change may never reach
  Fred. "Functional role in a language" must be cashed through the structure
  of deference; it cannot be an average, since a whole community can misuse a
  word ('brisket'). Individual roles still matter: the fortnight case and
  'tharthritis'.

## Concepts

- **Use theory of meaning** — functional role, conceptual role or procedural
  semantics (p. 157).
- **Inferential role** vs **functional role** — the latter adds causal links
  to inputs and outputs (p. 160).
- **Long-arm / short-arm roles** — roles extending into the world versus
  stopping at the sense organs and effectors (p. 175).
- **Functional role in a language** — a community-level role shaped by the
  structure of deference, not an average of individuals (p. 180).

## Bearing on the record

- [NOTE-117](NOTE-117.md) (C3 and its limitations) can now point to this LIT. The paper
  supports Drayson's claim that functional role semantics can let content
  depend on communicative use, but on Block's own conclusion that dependence
  sits in the referential or community factor, while the role of the inner
  representation is narrow. If Toon's worry is that inner representations
  are "locked away" from convention, Block's answer is that their content is
  two-factored, and only one factor is locked away. That reading is mine.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- The convergence argument is by cases (traveller, names, experts); it is
  not shown that every reference-fixing device has a long-arm analogue that
  separates cleanly.
- Narrow content is defended against no objection; p. 171 lists the
  objections and declines to answer them.
- The appendix is a sketch, with no account of how deference fixes roles.

## Open questions

- Is the "functional role in a language" of the appendix a third factor, or
  can it be absorbed into the referential factor, as the "Advertisement"
  (p. 630) left open?
- Campbell's reply (Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society 88, 1987/8) is
  not in the record.
