# TERM-tmphg3oi: moral worth, and which sense: intrinsic moral value, status-based dignity, or moral merit

## Definition

A claim about moral worth says which of three it means (A12):

1. **Intrinsic moral value.** The value a being has in itself, not as a
   means.
2. **Status-based dignity or respect.** What is owed to a being because of
   the kind of being it is, whatever it does.
3. **Moral merit or praiseworthiness.** The worth of a being's conduct or
   character, earned and lost by what it does.

A12: "Someone's conduct may be blameworthy without diminishing the basic
respect they are owed. An organization may produce valuable outcomes
without itself having the moral standing of a person. A non-agent may
possess morally considerable interests without having intentions or moral
responsibility."

## What it is not

A12's exclusions: instrumental usefulness, organizational effectiveness,
legality, virtue, moral patienthood and moral responsibility are not
synonyms for moral worth. In particular an **organizational achievement**
([TERM-tmpzgft9](TERM-tmpzgft9.md)) is not worth in any of the three senses: A11 classed an effective
coercive apparatus as one. **Moral standing** ([TERM-tmpiiy3n](TERM-tmpiiy3n.md)) is whether a being
is owed consideration at all, which senses 1 and 2 may ground and sense 3
does not.
