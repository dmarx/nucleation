# NOTE-1682: Azam, Devarajan and O'Connell — Aid dependence as learning by doing in tax collection

## Contribution

The paper sets out "to give precise analytical content to the widely used
concept of aid dependence" (p. 13). It separates two senses. In one, every
recipient is dependent, since a cut in aid hurts now, and that dependence
"represents a Pareto optimum" (p. 9). In the other, aid holds back
institutional learning: "excessive aid levels hold back the recipient's
institutional development" (p. 11). It shows that the second can persist
as an equilibrium path that meets every necessary condition of the
planner's problem, ties graduation to initial institutional capacity, and
frames the move toward graduation as a distribution problem between donor
and recipient. Its stated aim is a middle way: "neither the gap-filling
view nor the dependency view provides an adequate account of the dynamics
of aid and development" (p. 3).

## Key insight

Aid fills the gap between what the government spends and what it collects,
and the collecting is how the revenue ministry learns. So aid that
displaces revenue at the margin displaces the experience that makes revenue
cheap. Kaldor's question, as the paper puts it, "Will developing countries
learn how to tax?" (p. 3), gets the answer: "The answer depends on initial
conditions, on how much aid is received, and most importantly, on the
degree to which donor and recipient internalize learning-by-doing
externalities in the recipient's public sector" (p. 3).

## Assumptions

Equations here were read from the page images (see the history).

- **Surplus (p. 4, eq. 1).** Linear marginal benefit and cost with unit
  slopes: S(r, g; B, θ) = (Bg − g²/2) − (θr + r²/2). B is the intercept of
  the marginal social benefit of spending, θ the intercept of the marginal
  social cost of taxation, "the tax inefficiency parameter".
- **Aid fills the fiscal gap (pp. 4, 6).** Aid and taxes are the only
  finance: g = r + a (4.2). Aid is a grant, government to government, and
  cannot be negative.
- **Learning by doing (p. 5, eqs. 2–3).** "We capture this feature by
  introducing learning-by-doing of the type studied by Arrow (1962) and
  others." R_t = ∫_{−∞}^t r_s e^{−α(t−s)} ds and θ = 1/R_t; α ≥ 0, and "a
  lower value corresponds to greater institutional memory". Hence (4.1),
  θ̇ = αθ − θ²r, equivalently Ṙ = r − αR. Learning is in tax collection
  only, and efficiency rises without bound with experience.
- **The planner (p. 6, eq. 4).** A global planner maximizes
  ∫_t^∞ [βS − a] e^{−δ(s−t)} ds: recipient welfare weighted by β, aid
  costing the donor one for one, discount rate δ. The recipient attaches
  no cost to aid (p. 9).
- **No incentive channel.** Low revenue is the planner's choice, not moral
  hazard; there is no accountability or political-economy mechanism.

## Key results

1. **First-order conditions (p. 6).** βS_g = 1, so g = B − β⁻¹, and
   spending is constant along any optimal path; βS_r = λθ² − 1,
   so r = β⁻¹ − θ − β⁻¹λθ², the extra term being the one "which reflects
   the learning-by-doing externality associated with revenue collection".
   Dynamics: ṙ = (α + δ)[r − β⁻¹] + δθ (7), ȧ = (α + δ)[a − B + 2β⁻¹] − δθ
   (8), θ̇ = αθ + θ²(a − B + β⁻¹) (9). Equation (7) follows from (4.1) and
   (6.2)–(6.3); rechecked.
2. **Two steady states or none (p. 7).** "There are two such points if
   memory is sufficiently long; otherwise there are none." The exact
   condition (Appendix, p. 13) is 4αβ² < γ⁻¹ with γ = δ/(α + δ); α ≤ 1/4
   suffices when β ≤ 1.
3. **Saddle and trap (p. 7).** The low-aid equilibrium P "is a saddle point
   while the high-aid, low-tax-efficiency equilibrium M is locally stable";
   transversality holds at both. Planner welfare is higher at P by
   (r_P − r_M)/2δ, and "the saddle path converging to P is the planner's
   optimum for sufficiently low values of the discount rate".
4. **Kept capacity and aid (pp. 7–9).** On θ̇ = 0, a = B − β⁻¹ − α/θ, i.e.
   θ = α/(g − a): kept experience is own revenue over the decay rate.
   Steady-state aid a_P = Max[0, B − (β⁻¹/2)(3 + √(1 − 4αβ²γ))] (11), and
   θ_P = α/r_P = α/(B − β⁻¹ − a_P). Revenue needed "to keep a given level of
   accumulated experience intact" rises with α, and "With g fixed at its
   optimal level, greater current revenue implies lower aid" (p. 8). Weaker
   memory and a higher discount rate raise steady-state aid; the authors
   call the first "less obvious" (p. 8).
5. **The recipient prefers the trap (p. 9).** "the recipient is actually
   better off at M. Hence all the benefits in going from M to P accrue to
   the donor." This because "in our model the recipient attaches no cost to
   aid"; and "if the recipient faced a possible cutoff in aid, he would
   rather be in a high-institutional-development state, P."
6. **Myopic, gap-filling aid (pp. 9–10).** "What if the planner ignored
   institutions and institutional development altogether?" Then
   g_s = B − β⁻¹, r_s = β⁻¹ − θ and a_s = g* − β⁻¹ + θ: aid rises one for
   one with inefficiency. Above a threshold θ₁ "the economy will require
   increasing amounts of aid. Institutions will deteriorate until domestic
   revenue mobilization is driven to zero and aid finances all of public
   spending." Then "The process does not stop here, however, since taxation
   capacity now gradually disappears as cumulative tax experience is driven
   to zero." Below θ₁ the economy converges to M₁, "which is a low-aid,
   high-institutional-quality state", with less aid than even P. The myopic
   planner is "continuously "surprised" by the evolution of institutions
   over time" (p. 10).
7. **Up-front correction (pp. 11–12).** "the optimal strategy is to reduce
   aid "up front," making a horizontal jump" onto the saddle path. The
   recipient's surplus falls at once and the planner's payoff falls in the
   short run, but "Initial losses persist for some period but are more than
   offset in present value terms by gains due to faster institutional
   development" (p. 12).
8. **A rational trap (p. 12).** Paths to M are an infinity of solutions to
   the necessary conditions. "Expectations are fully rational on such
   paths." The planner underrates how far θ can be cut, a "failure of
   ambition", and "Graduation does not occur, for the simple reason that
   institutional development is too slow."
9. **Conclusions (p. 13).** "If aid displaces domestic institutional
   development at the margin, an aid relationship that ignores this link
   will at best slow down eventual graduation." With weak initial capacity
   "the attraction of gap-filling aid to both parties will be strong enough
   to displace the recipient's existing institutional capacity, locking
   donor and recipient into a permanent situation of high aid and low
   institutional capacity." And "A Pareto improvement can be achieved only
   if prospective gains can be redistributed towards the recipient without
   overly compromising institutional development."
10. **Policy (summary page).** "In some cases a short-term reduction in aid
    may increase a country's chances of graduating from aid."
11. **Data (Table 1, p. 2).** Median net ODA/GNP, 34 sub-Saharan African
    countries: 3.00 % (1960s), 7.53 %, 11.24 %, 15.30 % (1990s); the seven
    High-Performing Asian Economies fall from 1.14 % to 0.02 %. Descriptive;
    nothing is tested.

## Concepts

- **Aid dependence**, in two senses: that a cut hurts now, a Pareto optimum
  (p. 9); and that aid holds back institutional development (p. 11).
- **Graduation**: leaving high aid as domestic revenue replaces it.
- **Tax inefficiency θ**: the intercept of the marginal social cost of
  taxation, the reciprocal of accumulated experience.
- **Institutional memory**: low α, slow depreciation of experience.
- **Gap-filling aid**: aid set to spending minus statically chosen revenue;
  in this paper it is the harmful case.
- **Failure of ambition**: rational paths to M that underrate the long-run
  gain at P (p. 12).

## Connections

It draws on Arrow (1962) for learning by doing, on Kaldor (1963) for the
question (the paper words it "Will developing countries learn how to tax?";
Kaldor's title, in its references, is "Will underdeveloped countries learn
to tax?"), on McKinnon (1964) for the self-limiting gap view, and on Bauer
and Friedman for the dependency view. It extends the argument informally to
learning by doing in manufacturing under the Dutch disease: "As in our case,
graduation will be postponed or prevented altogether" (p. 4), the
infant-industry neighbour of Juhász's blockade study, [LIT-1489](../literature.d/LIT-1489.md) and
[THEORY-919](../theory.d/THEORY-919.md). In this batch: Moss, Pettersson and van de Walle (2006) cite it
for learning by doing in the public sector, with a paraphrase (below);
Moore (2004) supplies the fiscal-sociology side it lacks; and Ostrom,
Gibson, Shivakumar and Andersson (2002) the incentive channel, the
Samaritan's dilemma, that its planner model leaves out.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (supplied maintenance).** Qualifies it. Here the outside
  supply is not a neutral repair: aid does the work that capacity is kept
  by. Under full substitution experience decays at rate α, as [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md)'s
  capacity does on withdrawal, but here the decay runs while the supply
  continues.
- **[CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md) (the lead's learned-capacity model).** Mapping R → C,
  r → P, α → μ, a → A. Its first point, that kept capacity falls with the
  share of work aid does, is in this paper in unsaturated form: θ = α/(g − a)
  on the θ̇ = 0 locus, θ_P = α/r_P at the steady state. Its second point,
  recovery time growing with the length of full substitution, is not here:
  under Ṙ = r − αR recovery time is bounded as the substitution lengthens,
  so the point rests on the claim's cap of work by capacity, which this
  model lacks. Its third point is not here, and the vocabulary clashes:
  this paper's "gap-filling aid" is the harmful rule, aid filling the gap
  after revenue is chosen statically. The paper has a real trap, a
  threshold on initial capacity and a welfare conflict, which the claim's
  model, with exogenous aid, does not.
- **The aid/revenue ratio.** The phrase "level of aid in relation to
  overall revenue", which Moss, Pettersson and van de Walle (2006)
  attribute to this paper, is their paraphrase and does not appear in it.
  Here the ratio is implicit, a/r with g fixed, and initial institutions
  enter through θ₀ against the threshold θ₁.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) (independence from the founding intervention).** Bears on
  an answer: graduation comes where accumulated experience lets revenue
  replace aid, and depends on initial capacity against θ₁, on memory α and
  on whether the learning externality is internalized.
- **[QUESTION-399](../questions.d/QUESTION-399.md) (self-maintaining nucleus).** Bears on it: P is
  self-reinforcing, revenue building the efficiency that justifies revenue,
  but it is a saddle reached on one path; M is a stable low-capacity state.
- **[CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md) (capability decays with disuse).** Supports it in another
  domain: experience "does not decay at all" only when α = 0 (p. 5).
- **[QUESTION-386](../questions.d/QUESTION-386.md) (allocation by observed activity).** Bears on it: the
  myopic rule funds the observed gap, so aid rises as capacity falls and
  locks the trap in.
- **[CLAIM-520](../claims.d/CLAIM-520.md) (the value of keeping a corrective capacity is
  conditional).** Qualifies it: keeping tax capacity pays the far-sighted
  planner, while the recipient alone prefers M unless a cutoff in aid is
  possible.
- **[CLAIM-516](../claims.d/CLAIM-516.md).** Partly parallel: a productive self-loop in which, without
  own work, capacity decays to zero; nothing on the closure boundary.
- **[TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md).** Experience is a stock built from a rate with depreciation.
- **[TERM-117](../terms.d/TERM-117.md).** Initial capacity below θ₁ acts as a nucleus threshold for

## Limitations

- **No evidence.** Pure theory with one descriptive table and one figure of
  data; the country readings are illustrative and untested.
- **Strong functional forms.** Unit slopes, θ = 1/R, fixed spending, so
  every gain from learning goes to the donor as lower aid: "the benefits of
  learning-by-doing on the revenue side accrue to the donor in the form of
  lower aid" (p. 13).
- **One mechanism.** Learning in tax collection only; no incentive,
  accountability or donor-practice channel.
- **Printed typos** (checked against the page images and symbolically):
  - p. 8 defines "γ = δ/(2α + δ)"; the Appendix and the derivation give
    γ = δ/(α + δ).
  - The Appendix's roots r = β⁻¹ ± β⁻¹(1 − 4αβ²γ)^{1/2} lack a factor ½;
    the roots of r² − β⁻¹r + αγ = 0 are (β⁻¹/2)(1 ± √(1 − 4αβ²γ)),
    consistent with (11).
  - p. 9 writes g* − B⁻¹ for g* − β⁻¹ in the ȧ = 0 condition.
  - p. 7's exact condition is printed "α < (α + δ/4β2δ)", for
    α < (α + δ)/(4β²δ).
  - Smaller slips, the reader's or this filing's: the Appendix replaces r²
    with "β⁻¹r + αγ" where the quadratic gives β⁻¹r − αγ (the term cancels
    from the welfare difference, so the result stands); Kimbrough is cited
    1987 in the text and 1986 in the references.
- **Status.** A working paper whose cover asks that it "should not be cited
  without the permission of the authors"; no later published version was
  looked for.

## Open questions

- Does tax capacity in aid-dependent states actually depreciate with
  disuse and grow with collection, as R does? A panel of revenue
  administration capacity against aid shares would tell.
- With a capacity ceiling on revenue (work capped by capacity), does the
  saddle P survive, and does recovery time after full substitution grow
  with its length? The lead's model says the second; this paper's cannot.
- What compensation scheme moves a recipient from M to P without undoing
  the learning? The paper poses it and does not solve it.
