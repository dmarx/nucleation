# NOTE-tmpgi6rv: Tomasello et al. — Two Key Steps in the Evolution of Human Cooperation: The Interdependence Hypothesis

## Contribution

The first compact statement of Tomasello's two-step, collaboration-first
account of human cooperation, joining a game model (the Stag Hunt), a
fitness mechanism (stake in partners plus partner choice, instead of
reciprocity) and a shared-intentionality account of norms and
institutions.

## Key results

1. **The thesis.** Human cooperation and its psychology "all derive from
   mutualistic collaboration (with social selection against cheaters)"
   (abstract, p. 673); "it is collaboration, not altruism, that creates the
   many coordination problems" (p. 674). "A better model for real-life
   cooperation is the Stag Hunt" (p. 674).
2. **Joint goals and commitment.** "To form a joint goal, we must know
   together that each of us has the goal of working with the other"; "once
   they have formed a joint goal, humans are committed to it" (p. 677).
3. **Reputation, redefined.** Against free riders: "The answer is, of
   course, social selection by means of reputation." It is "not reputation
   for altruism or anything else external, but simply reputation for being
   a good collaborator" (p. 679).
4. **Helping without reciprocity.** "Mutualistic collaboration thus
   provides a safe context within which basic tendencies to help others
   could evolve" (p. 679). The stakeholder rule sB > C "changes the math
   because my selfish benefits depend on the well-being of selected others"
   (p. 680), and "reciprocity cannot explain uncontingent acts of altruistic
   helping" (p. 680).
5. **Reciprocity demoted.** "the usual suspects—kin selection, sexual
   selection, direct reciprocity, and indirect reciprocity— play only minor
   roles" (p. 680); in Table 1 reciprocity appears only in the apes' column
   (p. 681). "Kinship and reciprocity do not get you culture." (p. 685).
6. **Step two.** "group identification is a scaled-up version of the “we”
   intentionality" (p. 682); "social norms are nothing more than
   conventionalizations" of first-step social selection (p. 683); "I am
   sanctioning myself or praising myself as an emissary of the group."
   (p. 684). Cultural group selection acts on conventions and norms only
   afterwards (p. 685).
7. **Partner market.** "a kind of market for collaborative partners such
   that anyone with a poor reputation will be avoided" (p. 686). "This
   account is, of course, speculative." (p. 686).

## Assumptions

The Stag Hunt is the right model; early collaborations were small enough
that free riding limited itself; chimpanzees stand in for the last common
ancestor; the developmental order in WEIRD children suggests the
phylogenetic order; partner choice was possible and reputation as a
collaborator hard to fake; demography triggered the second step.

## Concepts

Interdependence hypothesis; obligate collaborative foraging; Stag Hunt;
joint and collective intentionality; stakeholder helping (sB > C); partner
choice and social selection; group-mindedness; norms as conventionalized
social selection.

## Bearing on the record

- **LIT-495** and Alexander's 1985 account, a contrast: collaboration
  first and reciprocity last, where Alexander puts indirect reciprocity
  first. They converge on audience-based partner choice and on intergroup
  competition, and differ on what the return is and what is reputed.
  Alexander is not cited. The reader observes, and the filer agrees, that
  the paper's first-step mechanism is functionally close to indirect
  reciprocity (observers choose partners by what they saw; the evidence
  cited is a competitive-altruism study), and at the second step it draws
  on an indirect-reciprocity model (Panchanathan and Boyd) for punishing
  non-punishers; the difference from reciprocity is asserted, not
  modelled.
- **THEORY-495**, background: the evolutionary scaffolding of the
  obligation account, with an early form of its "emissary of the group"
  idea; nothing on obligation as such.
- **LIT-504**, a stand-in for the book's evolutionary core, not its
  moral-psychology chapters.
- **THEORY-457** (Baumard and colleagues, mutualistic morality by
  partner choice), the closest parallel.
- **THEORY-567** (Nowak) and **THEORY-663** (Trivers), contrasts: the
  stakeholder mechanism is none of Nowak's five rules, and the
  reciprocity-first baseline is set aside without citing Trivers.
- **LIT-514** (Boehm), a parallel: cited for counter-dominance; partner
  choice comes first and punishment second, against Boehm's
  punishment-driven social selection.
- **THEORY-414** (Guala, institutions as Stag Hunt equilibria) and
  **LIT-473** (Lewis, cited for conventions), parallels to the second
  step.
- **LIT-047 and NOTE-034** (morality-as-cooperation), a parallel:
  cooperation-first, from one mutualistic root rather than seven games.

## Limitations

Speculative by its own account; evidence mainly from captive chimpanzees
and WEIRD children; the stakeholder model leaves free riding on helping
open; the line between reputation as a collaborator and indirect
reciprocity is not modelled; the comments by Hawkes (kin and cooperative
breeding) and Sterelny (timing of the second step) were not read.
