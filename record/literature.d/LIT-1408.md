  Pettigrew (1998). Reviews Allport's contact hypothesis (equal status, common goals, cooperation, authority support) and names four problems: selection bias in cross-sectional studies, an expanding list of conditions that are facilitating rather than essential, no account of process, and no account of how effects generalize. Proposes four processes (learning about the outgroup, changed behaviour as the precursor of attitude change, affective ties, and ingroup reappraisal, whose outcome it calls "deprovincialization"), three sequential strategies for generalization (decategorization, salient categorization, recategorization), and a longitudinal contact theory in which Allport's four conditions and a fifth, friendship potential, are essential and other factors facilitating. In the author's European surveys the path from outgroup friendship to less prejudice is stronger than the reverse.

# LIT-1408: Intergroup Contact Theory

Pettigrew, T. F. (1998). Intergroup contact theory. *Annual Review of Psychology*, 49, 65–85. doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.49.1.65.

Read in full on 2026-10-29 ([NOTE-tmpmwtvy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpmwtvy.md)).

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it for deprovincialization: contact with an outgroup leads one to reappraise one's own group, seeing its ways as one way among others. The abstract names this process "ingroup reappraisal" and lists "changed behavior" as a process in its own right, which is the Franklin shape: conduct first, and the view revised after. **Flag:** the word "deprovincialization" is not in the abstract; whether the review uses it is unchecked. *Checked 2026-10-29 ([NOTE-tmpmwtvy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpmwtvy.md)):* it does, once (p. 72): optimal contact shows that ingroup norms "turn out not to be the only ways to manage the social world", which "can reshape your view of your ingroup and lead to a less provincial view of outgroups in general ('deprovincialization')". Bears on [CLAIM-275](../claims.d/CLAIM-275.md) and [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) (a perspective revised by being made one of several).
