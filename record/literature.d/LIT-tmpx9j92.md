# LIT-tmpx9j92: One Giant Leap from Mouse to Man: The Microbiota–Gut–Brain Axis in Mood Disorders and Translational Challenges Moving towards Human Clinical Trials

Sofia D. Forssten, Arthur C. Ouwehand, Shannon M. Griffin and Elaine Patterson (2022), *Nutrients* 14(3): 568 — [doi:10.3390/nu14030568](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14030568)

## Key takeaways

*Read in full; see [NOTE-tmp88f2b](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp88f2b.md).*

- **The route.** "The vagus nerve is the most direct route of communication
  between the gut and the brain, thus enabling bidirectional
  communication."
- **Animals.** Faecal transplants from depressed patients into rodents
  produce behavioural abnormalities, "indicating at least partial transfer
  of the clinical psychiatric phenotype". But "the gut microbiome is the
  only humanized component of the rodent".
- **Humans.** "A recent meta-analysis demonstrated that probiotic
  consumption could result in a reduction of subjective stress levels in
  healthy volunteers". Strains with strong preclinical evidence have
  failed in trials. For L. rhamnosus JB-1: "not found to have an effect on
  mood, anxiety, stress, sleep quality, memory performance, attention, or
  anti-inflammatory cytokine levels compared with the placebo."
