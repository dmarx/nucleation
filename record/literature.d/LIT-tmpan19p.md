# LIT-tmpan19p: A Theory of the Evolution of Social Power: Natural Trajectories of Interpersonal Influence Systems along Issue Sequences

Friedkin, N. E., Jia, P. & Bullo, F. (2016). A theory of the evolution of social power: Natural trajectories of interpersonal influence systems along issue sequences. *Sociological Science* 3: 444–472. doi:10.15195/v3.a20.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp6p1t2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6p1t2.md). Page numbers are the journal's; S for
the supplement.*

- **The mechanism.** "Success in influencing group issue outcomes elevates
  the individual’s self-weight" (p. 445): closure on the next issue is set
  by influence centrality on the last.
- **In simulation, autocracy.** Even with who-influences-whom redrawn at
  random every issue, all 14,000 simulated systems converged to one
  closed member, though disturbances can change who (S1–S2).
- **In the laboratory, the individual step.** In 30 four-person student
  groups over 15 choice dilemmas, prior centrality predicted next-issue
  closure, more strongly for cumulative centrality as the sequence went on
  (Tables 2–3, pp. 457–458).
- **Structure shifted; consensus did not.** Networks thinned (84% to 67%
  density by issue 11) and discussions shortened, but "In our data, the
  probability of reaching consensus does not alter along the issue
  sequence" (p. 467).
- **Efficiency is not quality.** The account does not address "whether
  the evolution of a group toward greater efficiency is associated with a
  decrease or increase of collective intelligence" (S11).

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it at turn U19 as the laboratory support for DeGroot–Friedkin social-power dynamics, whose model is held as [LIT-1146](LIT-1146.md) and whose single-author precursor is [LIT-1404](LIT-1404.md). The abstract confirms that it reports experiments consistent with the theory.

**Corrected 2026-10-24, from the reading.** Two corrections. The
experiment's model of each discussion is Friedkin–Johnsen, with members
anchored to their initial opinions, not DeGroot; its predicted attractor
is a single closed member, where the DeGroot–Friedkin model of [LIT-1146](LIT-1146.md)
predicts power ordered by centrality and autocracy only for a star. And
what the laboratory supports is the individual step, closure following
prior influence, with structural trends; the predicted end state was not
reached. For [CLAIM-tmpd6q2w](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpd6q2w.md) it supports the self-reinforcing baseline and
the point that faster settlement is not better integration. Filed from
