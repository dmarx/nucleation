# THEORY-1932: The NTSB found that the probable cause of the 2018 Tempe crash of an Uber ATG test vehicle was its operator's failure to monitor the road because she was distracted by her phone, that the automated driving system was designed to rely on the operator to avoid collisions, and that Uber ATG's inadequate safety culture, through poor risk assessment, rarely exercised oversight and no countermeasures to automation complacency, contributed to the crash and to the operator's distraction itself

## Source

NTSB/HAR-19/03, [LIT-2664](../literature.d/LIT-2664.md); the reading is [NOTE-2034](../notes.d/NOTE-2034.md).

## What was actually shown

An investigation's causal findings. The design "precluded activation of emergency braking for
collision mitigation, relying instead on the operator’s intervention" (p. v). The probable cause
was "the failure of the vehicle operator to monitor the driving environment and the operation of
the automated driving system because she was visually distracted throughout the trip by her
personal cell phone" (p. v). Attentive, "she would likely have had sufficient time" (p. vii). ATG's
safety culture "created conditions—including inadequate oversight of vehicle operators—that
contributed to the circumstances of the crash and specifically to the vehicle operator’s extended
distraction during the crash trip" (p. viii). The pedestrian's impaired crossing and Arizona's
oversight also contributed.

## What this does not say

- Nothing about blame: "The NTSB does not assign fault or blame for an accident or incident".
- Nothing about moral crumple zones or how the public, the employer or prosecutors treated the
  operator, beyond her suspension and layoff (p. 27).
- It does not excuse the operator: her distraction was "a clear example of deliberate engagement in
  visual distraction" (p. 43).
