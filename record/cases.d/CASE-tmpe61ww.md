# CASE-tmpe61ww: The telephone game

## The case

U20: "that's just a similarity constraint, not an equivalence. This is why the
game 'telephone' works." A50: "translation can preserve recognizable structure
without preserving identity, and ... small local deviations can accumulate into
large global changes." A52 made it running case B, beside Henley and the
language model.

## What it can show

The game is a familiar practice, so the case is documented in that sense. In
the exchange it is used as an idealized illustration, not as data. It shows
that each step can be close to its predecessor while the end is far from the
start ([CLAIM-tmpghha4](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpghha4.md)), and that the working relation is similarity within ε rather
than equivalence ([CLAIM-tmpumy4f](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpumy4f.md)). Bartlett's serial reproduction and Kirby, Cornish
and Smith's iterated learning, brought in at A50, are its experimental
versions (manuscript §8).
