# NOTE-tmplo8zf: Bruening 2018, lexicalism: wrong and superfluous

## Contribution

The lexicalist hypothesis holds that the grammar component producing words is distinct and strictly separate from the one producing phrases. Bruening argues it is wrong for three reasons: phrasal syntax often feeds word formation, phrasal syntax can access subword parts, and claims that word formation and phrasal syntax follow different principles are false. It is also superfluous, because the facts it was supposed to explain have independent explanations. The most parsimonious model follows: one combinatorial component that builds both words and phrases.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1: the hypothesis is attributed to Chomsky (1970) and is foundational in HPSG, LFG, Simpler Syntax and some minimalist models. Its core is one-way encapsulation, where the word system's output feeds phrasal syntax and never the reverse.
- §2 Error 1: phrasal syntax feeds word formation, with phrases inside compounds and zero-derivation (§2.1) and resultatives (§2.2).
- §3 Error 2: phrasal operations target subword units. §3.1 covers coordination and ellipsis of word parts (Italian "infra e ultrasuoni", German "Freund- oder Feindschaft", English "pre- and post-revolutionary"). §3.2 covers focus.
- §4 Error 3: claimed differences in principle fail, covering locality of affixes, adjunct treatment, headedness and idiosyncrasy (§§4.1–4.4, against Williams 2007 and Chomsky 1970).
- §5 Superfluous: Bresnan & Mchombo's (1995) lexical-integrity arguments are answered one by one. The "no phrase constraint" (*[AP quite happy]-ness) is explained by the selectional properties of individual morphemes (§5.6). Key point: the constraints that really hold make no reference to "word".
- §6 Conclusion: a hypothesis that is both incorrect and does no work should be discarded.

## Open questions
