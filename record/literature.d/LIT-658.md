# LIT-658: Knowledge is a Region in Weight Space for Fine-tuned Language Models

Almog Gueta, Elad Venezian, Colin Raffel, Noam Slonim, Yoav Katz and Leshem Choshen (2023), arXiv preprint — [ARXIV-2302.04863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04863)

## Key takeaways

- **Fine-tuning moves a model in a direction set by the data.** With task
  vectors (fine-tuned minus pretrained weights) compared by cosine, 280
  models fine-tuned on 12 datasets with 20 seeds each cluster by dataset with
  98% accuracy, and models from three task families cluster by family with
  90% (§4). Clusters follow the kind of data and not its amount (§4.1, App.
  C). Euclidean distance did not give clear clusters, which the authors put
  down to weight-norm growth during training (§2.3).
- **The space between fine-tuned models is as good as the models, or
  better.** Lines between pairs (§5.1) and samples from the convex hull of a
  group (§5.2) keep low loss on the group's data, and often lower loss than
  the endpoints: on MNLI every hull sample beats every model fine-tuned on
  another dataset, and hull samples beat the MNLI-fine-tuned models in 88% of
  comparisons. Loss is compared through a "generalized loss", a linear probe
  refitted on the frozen encoder for each target dataset (§3).
- **The region is small and its walls are steep.** Extrapolating along the
  same lines past the endpoints raises the loss quickly (§6, App. G), so the
  fine-tuned models lie near the edge of a basin with a flat floor. Starting
  further fine-tuning from the region's centroid beats starting from the
  pretrained model on 9 of 12 datasets (§7, Table 2).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the owner's batch on mode connectivity and model merging, under
the `loss-landscapes` topic that [ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md) added for it. The anthology could
hold it as model merging; this record holds it for what it says about the
shape of the solution set, so it carries the `anthology-candidate` flag.

It generalises the shared-start linear connectivity of Frankle et al.
([LIT-654](LIT-654.md)), whom it cites for the dependence of linear paths on a shared
origin, from a line between two runs to a convex region spanned by many
fine-tuning runs from one pretrained model. The paper credits Entezari et al.
([LIT-652](LIT-652.md)) for the linear connectivity of same-dataset fine-tuning (§1),
which is not the setting that paper studies: it conjectures linear
connectivity modulo permutation for networks trained from different random
initialisations. See the reading. It reads Mirzadeh et al.
([LIT-651](LIT-651.md)) as the multitask precedent, and Benton et al. ([LIT-656](LIT-656.md)) as
the precedent for low-loss volumes rather than lines. No relation is
declared: none of these is a design it builds on or a result it measures.

Its §8 offers the region picture as an explanation of weight averaging in
practice: model soups ([ANTH-LIT-675](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-675.md)) average models from one dataset region,
and robust fine-tuning by interpolation ([ANTH-LIT-674](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-674.md)) moves within the
region of the pretrained model. TIES-Merging ([LIT-675](LIT-675.md)), by an
overlapping author group, cites it among the works showing that separately
trained weights can be interpolated without a rise in loss (its §2). Its region claim is the one a THEORY would rest on; see the report
on theory candidates.
