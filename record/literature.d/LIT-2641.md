# LIT-2641: When Are Concepts Erased From Diffusion Models?

Lu, K., Kriplani, N., Gandikota, R., Pham, M., Bau, D., Hegde, C. & Cohen, N. (2025). When Are Concepts Erased From Diffusion Models? *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* 38: 16521–16545. doi:10.52202/085713-0497. arXiv:2505.17013.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1987](../notes.d/NOTE-1987.md).*

- **Robust to text, not to context** (§3.6): "Methods such as GA,
  TaskVectors, STEREO, and RECE, appear robust to text-based attacks ...
  Yet TaskVectors and RECE resurfaced the erased concept under inpainting
  and diffusion-completion probes".
- **The diagnosis** (§3.6): "most current erasure methods operate through
  guidance-based avoidance rather than true destruction of underlying
  representations".
- **Erasure is relative to the probe** (§6): "knowledge undetectable
  through one technique can often be recovered through others."
- **The converse worry** (§5): a strong enough optimization-based probe
  "may be able to induce generation of a concept the model did not even
  encounter during training".

## Standing in the record

Filed from A's turn on concept erasure, which says the paper evaluated
seven methods across 13 concepts and more than 100,000 generations, that
apparently erased concepts could reappear under alternative generation
conditions, and that it recognizes the discovery-versus-induction problem
(paraphrased; the exchange was not rechecked by the filer). The reader
found the seven methods, 13 concepts, reappearance and the named limit
in the paper; the generation count is the project page's ("over 100K
generations"), not the paper's. [THEORY-1909](../theory.d/THEORY-1909.md) carries its central
account.
