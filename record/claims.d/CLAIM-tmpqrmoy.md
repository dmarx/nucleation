# CLAIM-tmpqrmoy: In one school system, interventions of equal yearly cost that acted at different points of how schools produce learning differed threefold in effect: bonuses acting on the effort of teachers already present did more than an extra teacher or materials, so where an equal budget enters an organization can matter more than its size

## The claim

The case (CASE-tmpjfl6r; THEORY-tmpmwhm1) is a school system in which
learning is produced through staff, materials and the effort of staff
already present. Three arms of one budget entered at three of these
places. The arm at effort did three times as much: "The individual
incentive program spent the same amount per school as the input programs
but produced gains in test scores that were three times larger than those
in the input schools (0.27 SD vs. 0.09 SD)." The authors place the
mechanism at effort, not presence: "not increased teacher attendance, but
greater (and more effective) teaching effort conditional on being
present."

In CLAIM-562's terms, budget is the amount, held equal, and the effect is
fixed by which part of the structure the budget engages. A budget count
would rank the three arms equal; they are not.

## How it relates

- **CLAIM-562**: corroborates its first half in an organization, which its
  own text left as "an analogy". Independent of the mechanical systems
  it rests on.
- **CLAIM-tmphbmpy** objects: the arms differ in instrument as well as in
  place, and the design does not separate the two.

## What it does not say

- That effort is the binding constraint in schools generally.
- Anything about the two input arms separately, which this paper pools.
- That adding an intervention can hurt; no school had two.
