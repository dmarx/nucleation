# QUESTION-399: Under what conditions does a productive partial organization become a self-maintaining nucleus, one whose continued operation contributes to regenerating the very constraints that make its productive influence possible?

## Why it is a question

A productive nucleus ([TERM-117](../terms.d/TERM-117.md)) changes later formation and owes
nothing to closure. A separates four phenomena: "These can overlap, but
none is automatically equivalent to another."

| Phenomenon | A's defining property |
|---|---|
| Nucleation | a critical or productive intermediate changes progression toward a new structure |
| Autocatalysis | a product enhances the rate of its own production |
| Organizational closure | constraints produce and maintain one another in a closed dependency structure ([TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)) |
| Regeneration | a system restores components or capacities after damage or turnover ([CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md)) |

The question is what has to be added to the first for the last two to
follow.

## What the record has

- **DNA origami nucleates, and regenerates only from supply.** Schneider,
  Möritz and Dietz's nucleus speeds folding. An earlier version of this
  entry said nothing in the origami read here repairs itself. That is wrong.
  Scheckenbach et al. 2021 ([NOTE-1636](../notes.d/NOTE-1636.md)), read later in this pull request, show folded
  origami replacing labels and missing staples from a pool of strands in
  solution, and keeping 76 % intact over 11 days in serum against 12 %. The
  parts all come from outside, and the repair stops being specific at high
  damage rates ([CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md)).
- **Replication without regeneration.** Ni et al. ([NOTE-1630](../notes.d/NOTE-1630.md)) copy
  tile patterns from supplied tiles under an imposed thermal program, and
  nothing replaces a damaged copy.
- **Closure.** [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md) states closure of constraints, and [CLAIM-516](../claims.d/CLAIM-516.md)
  states when capacities that produce one another survive turnover.

## What would answer it

- A system, molecular or organizational, in which a nucleus that speeds
  formation also, once formed, takes part in producing or repairing the
  constraints of its own formation. Then a measure of when that loop
  closes: the disturbance or turnover rate it survives, and what supply it
  still needs from outside.
- The other way: an argument that productivity and self-maintenance trade
  off, for instance because reversibility serves one and stability the
  other.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on aid dependency and self-sufficiency

Read at the owner's request to look to aid and development for theory on how aid-dependent organizations become self-sufficient; the works were chosen by the assistant.

- **Candidate loops.** The fiscal contract, in which revenue funds the state, the state serves taxpayers and their consent funds revenue, is the readings' self-maintaining loop for a state (Moore, [NOTE-1663](../notes.d/NOTE-1663.md); Moss et al., [NOTE-1661](../notes.d/NOTE-1661.md)). Aid stands in for its input and can keep it from closing. Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock ([NOTE-1664](../notes.d/NOTE-1664.md)) propose small wins on local problems that loosen constraints over time, asserted, not measured. The Kenya savings groups ([NOTE-1650](../notes.d/NOTE-1650.md)) trained new groups after exit, a nucleus that reproduced.
- **What closing the loop costs.** [CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) shows that upkeep drawn in by the structure's own state has an ignition threshold, a collapse point in supply and an outage it cannot recover from. A nucleus that becomes self-maintaining in that sense trades smooth dependence for thresholds.
