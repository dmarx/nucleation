# QUESTION-265: Is "psychopathic" a category error when said of a corporation, or can the construct, its affective features included, be assigned to a corporation functionally, and if so, what in a corporation would remorse, or its absence, be?

## Why it is a question

The owner, 2026-10-23: "how's the nucleation record on topics like
corporate psychopathy? qua treating the corporation as a sociopathic
entity." It was not, before this filing. Every "psychopath" in the record
was a person's disorder or Wolf's stipulated case ([THEORY-147](../theory.d/THEORY-147.md)).

The thesis needs three premises, and the record holds unequal parts of
them.

1. **The corporation is an agent.** French ([THEORY-131](../theory.d/THEORY-131.md)) and Pettit
   ([THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md)) say so; List's result on aggregation ([THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md), Active)
   and [CLAIM-271](../claims.d/CLAIM-271.md) say a group's attitudes can be no member's. Velasquez ([LIT-1429](../literature.d/LIT-1429.md),
   [LIT-1432](../literature.d/LIT-1432.md)) denies it: responsibility needs a mind that forms intentions and a
   body it controls, and a corporation has neither.
2. **Its wrongs are its own, not distributed to its members.** Isaacs
   ([LIT-698](../literature.d/LIT-698.md)) on collective guilt, and Floridi ([LIT-899](../literature.d/LIT-899.md)) on harm no node
   intends.
3. **It lacks the moral emotions.** Huebner ([THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md)) allows a group
   functional, unfelt emotions, and has found no actual case; List
   ([NOTE-340](../notes.d/NOTE-340.md)) doubts a group agent is phenomenally conscious. Nothing in
   the record addresses remorse, guilt or empathy in a corporation.

The third premise is where "psychopathic" gets its force. The record's
own framework bears on it: [CLAIM-039](../claims.d/CLAIM-039.md), reopened by the owner on
2026-10-23, characterizes affect functionally, after Frijda ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)),
as an appraisal of an event's pertinence to a concern eliciting action
readiness, and so lets affect be assigned to a corporation. The owner:
"one of the specific things our framework equips us to do is to assign
affect to corporation-like things." The question is then not whether a
corporation can lack remorse but what its remorse would be, and how it
would differ from its fear of sanction.

It also has a rival that shares its name. Boddy ([LIT-1431](../literature.d/LIT-1431.md)) says
"corporate psychopaths" are people, individuals with psychopathic traits
who rise in corporations. An answer has to say whether a corporation's
harms need its members' traits at all.

## What would count as an answer

A claim that says, of the clinical construct's features (Hare and
Neumann's four factors, [THEORY-892](../theory.d/THEORY-892.md)), which can be said of a corporation and in what sense. It should say whether the absence it names is a deficit or a design. It should say
what does the work in a corporation that a moral sense does in a person,
and tested on a documented case, it should separate the corporation's
contribution from its members'.

## What would not answer it

A list of corporate wrongs, however long, since a person can do wrong
without psychopathy. Nor would the thesis restated as a metaphor without
saying which features transfer, or a verdict on whether corporations are
bad.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Mulgan, read

[LIT-1874](../literature.d/LIT-1874.md) ([NOTE-2306](../notes.d/NOTE-2306.md); [THEORY-2216](../theory.d/THEORY-2216.md), which `bears_on` this question)
gives **a third position** beside [CLAIM-431](../claims.d/CLAIM-431.md) and [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md). Mulgan, after
Bakan, says many for-profit enterprises "are closer to psychopaths than to
normally functioning human adults" (p. 906): they act and plan but feel no
empathy. A group whose management structure has no mechanism for
recognising and responding to moral reasons "is irredeemably psychopathic.
(And a psychopathic corporate group, unlike its human equivalent, has no
moral standing at all.)" (p. 910), and "nonpsychotic individuals can
together constitute a psychopathic corporate group" (p. 914). So
"psychopathic" is assigned structurally, not through affect, and its
consequence is to deny the group moral agency, not to ground blame. On step
1, it adds to Velasquez's premises as the record holds them (mind and body)
the slippery-slope argument and Searle's account of consciousness, both
second-hand. The question stays open.

## Note of 2026-11-23: the sanction-lift test, run and not met

The test [CLAIM-431](../claims.d/CLAIM-431.md) and [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md) both name for what remorse in a
corporation would be, whether harms keep being weighed once a sanction
lifts, was run on open sources by [LEAD-101](../leads.d/LEAD-101.md) ([CASE-405](../cases.d/CASE-405.md)). No case holds
reputation and benefit fixed, so it does not decide between them. What it
adds to this question: reversion after a sanction lifts is predicted by
both answers ([CLAIM-1169](../claims.d/CLAIM-1169.md)); a monitor's effect on violations does not
outlast the monitor ([CLAIM-1136](../claims.d/CLAIM-1136.md)); and remorse, if a corporation has
it, would show only in a redesigned corporation's post-lift conduct when
keeping the procedure no longer pays. The question stays open.

On the owner's U29, which put "institutional sociopathy" beside
measurement naivety, greedy agency and enshittification, A46 cites this
question and offers a functional definition that does without the clinical
attribution: competence in pursuing its own objectives with a systematic
failure "to give independently justified claims of affected parties
appropriate practical authority". Its distinctive step is that the
institution may understand those it harms very well: "It may involve the
ability to understand others without allowing their interests to govern
activity in the ways their standing warrants." That is filed as a
candidate answer, CLAIM-tmptrn75; it locates the deficit after the harm is
tracked, where CLAIM-432 locates it in what the appraisals track.

**A misreading.** A46 §14: "The record explicitly identifies the absence of
an account of corporate remorse as an open problem." The question's first
section said so on 2026-10-23, but the record has since filed CLAIM-432,
CLAIM-431 (remorse cannot be told from prudence) and CLAIM-tmpsuj5n (when a
sanction-lift test separates them), recorded in this question's note of
2026-11-23. A46 §7's remorse profile (recognition, attribution, corrective
mobilization, restoration, self-formation, persistence, the changes "survive declining
public attention and personnel turnover") restates CLAIM-tmpsuj5n's
persistence criterion and adds restoration and attribution; its own test
("A costly remedy undertaken solely to avoid reputational damage ... does
not establish that the institution would respond appropriately when
wrongdoing remains commercially advantageous") is CLAIM-tmpsuj5n's
condition. A46 §13's perturbation profiles are LEAD-tmp466iv, beside
LEAD-101. The question stays open. The wording of this note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session), reporting A's (ChatGPT's) text; adopting what it reports is the owner's call.
