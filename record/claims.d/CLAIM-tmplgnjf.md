# A demand specification is not a regulation: regulations are constraints on conduct meant to induce constraints on outcomes, and a regime succeeds only if its conduct constraints make the demanded outcomes reliable under the participants' responses, so a rule can be perfectly enforced and fail its purpose

## The claim

A31 §1: a specification "might demand worker safety, low environmental
damage, reliable provision of essential goods, meaningful opportunities
for competition, and the capacity to adapt to new technologies. Those are
not themselves regulations." Regulations are "the implementing
mechanisms: enforceable standards, disclosures, liability rules, rights,
prohibitions, incentives, monitoring procedures, and revision
mechanisms."

A31 §3 writes the two kinds of constraint side by side: c_k(x, a) ≤ 0 on
conduct, and ∫ f_j dμ_g ≤ b_j on the distribution μ_g of histories the
regime g induces. "The first specifies what participants must do. The
second specifies what the ecosystem must achieve." A31 §2 strengthens
joint satisfiability, that some attainable history meets every demand,
to reliability under strategic behaviour and disturbance:
P_g(Γ_H ∈ V_j) ≥ 1 − ε_j for each j. "This makes regulation a problem of
steering the distribution of possible futures, rather than specifying one
ideal future."

A33 §6 carries selective stabilization ([CLAIM-785](CLAIM-785.md), about an entry's
predicates) to regulation by analogy: constrain the dimensions in which
deviation does unacceptable harm and leave free those that support
experimentation and differentiation. An outcome constraint leaves the
technology open, and a technology prescription is sometimes needed
because the risk cannot be measured by an outcome test. "The choice
depends on observability, enforceability, and uncertainty."

## What it does not say

It does not say that outcome constraints are better than conduct
constraints, or that enforcement is code validation: A31 §8 says "Legal
interpretation, discretion, political legitimacy, and contested
interests cannot be collapsed into automated consistency checking." The
reliability requirement presupposes a model of participants' behaviour;
[CLAIM-tmpx9nrj](CLAIM-tmpx9nrj.md) separates what that model says from what is possible at
all.
