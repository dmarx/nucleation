# LIT-1916: A Process Theory of Organization

Hernes, T. (2014). *A Process Theory of Organization*. Oxford: Oxford University Press. doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199695072.001.0001.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from metadata, not a reading.* The table of contents shows:

- Part I on assumptions of organization theory that "do not work for
  explaining organizing in a world on the move", and assumptions to
  replace them: becoming, ongoing temporality, endogeneity of process.
- A chapter on "Temporality and Process" (pp. 75–98), which holds the
  p. 93 passage the parent quotes.
- A chapter on articulatory modes and agency in Part II, with sections on
  intersubjective, practical, material, textual and tacit articulation.

## Why this is here

Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy 2024 ([LIT-1902](LIT-1902.md)) take from it:

- their epigraph and central question, "How can there be agency without
  agents?" (p. 93, quoted pp. 1349, 1358 and 1363);
- the claim that agency is not attributed to actors that appear "larger,
  more central, more important, or more resourceful" (p. 15, quoted p.
  1350);
- agency as "the outcome of the encounter as it reaches closure and
  becomes an event, thereby giving meaning to other events, while
  defining the encounter as well" (p. 93, quoted p. 1353);
- agency as taking place when events are "articulated", a form of
  communication (pp. 1350, 1363).

Whether they use it faithfully cannot be judged without the book. One
thing can be seen from the parent alone. The epigraph quotes Hernes
voicing a reader's objection ("Some readers may object, 'But where are
the agents?'"). The parent then twice cites "without agents" as something
"Hernes (2014, p. 93, emphasis added) pointed out" (pp. 1358, 1363). That
turns a question Hernes anticipates into a thesis he asserts. It may be
fair, if he goes on to answer the question that way, but it cannot be
checked here. The parent says Hernes "hints at" the relation between
agency and communication "yet does not theorize it" (p. 1353), which is
its stated gap.
