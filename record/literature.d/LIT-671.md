# LIT-671: Explaining Landscape Connectivity of Low-cost Solutions for Multilayer Nets

Rohith Kuditipudi, Xiang Wang, Holden Lee, Yi Zhang, Zhiyuan Li, Wei Hu,
Sanjeev Arora and Rong Ge (2019), *Advances in Neural Information Processing
Systems 32* (NeurIPS 2019) — [ARXIV-1906.06247](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06247)

## Key takeaways

- **Dropout stability implies connectivity** (Theorem 1). A solution is
  ε-dropout stable if, for each layer onward, some half of the units can be
  kept and rescaled and the rest zeroed with loss rising by at most ε. Two
  such solutions are joined by a path whose loss never exceeds the worse
  endpoint by more than ε. The path walks each network to its half-zeroed
  version (Lemma 1; the number of segments grows with depth), uses the zeroed
  half as scratch space to permute units freely at no loss (Lemma 4, five
  segments), and then crosses between the two half-networks (Lemma 2, eight
  segments).
- **Noise stability gives simpler paths** (Theorems 2–3). Under a noise
  stability condition adapted from Arora et al.'s compression bounds (layer
  cushion, interlayer cushion, activation contraction, interlayer
  smoothness), a network can be interpolated straight to a randomly
  dropped-out version, and two ε-noise-stable networks are joined by 10
  segments with barrier Õ(ε). Routing through a narrow network that already
  achieves low loss gives 13 segments and a barrier that shrinks with the
  dropout fraction.
- **Not all minima are connected** (Theorem 4). For any student width h and
  any convex loss, a dataset labelled by a two-unit teacher exists on which
  the student's global minimizers are not connected. Overparametrization alone
  does not give mode connectivity.
- **The conditions roughly hold in practice** (§6). Three-layer, 32-filter
  convnets on MNIST are fairly dropout stable without any dropout in
  training, and a VGG-11 trained with channel-wise dropout on CIFAR-10 is
  noise stable; the constructed path between two such VGG-11s keeps low loss,
  while the straight line reaches loss 2.34 and 10% accuracy.

## Standing in the record

Filed with the batch [ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md) opened `loss-landscapes` for. It **extends**
Garipov et al. ([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)) and Draxler et al. ([LIT-653](LIT-653.md)) from
observation to explanation: it sets out to prove the connectivity they found,
for multilayer nets and realistic widths, from properties trained networks
already have. Its explanation is a formal version of Draxler et al.'s
"resilience" and "redundancy" arguments. A network that can spare half its
units can move the other half anywhere, including into any permutation,
without a barrier.

Its counterexample is the first statement in the batch that connectivity is a
property of *found* solutions. Git Re-Basin ([LIT-661](LIT-661.md)) reaches the same
conclusion for linear connectivity modulo permutation with a counterexample
of its own. Ferbach et al. ([LIT-680](LIT-680.md)) take up the link between dropout
stability and linear connectivity, showing that networks with independent
neuron weights become dropout stable at the same widths at which they become
linearly connected.

It carries `learning-theory` because its conditions are the noise-stability
quantities of compression-based generalization bounds: the property that
explains generalization is shown to explain connectivity too.
