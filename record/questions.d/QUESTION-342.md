# QUESTION-342: In an independent replication of the 2 × 2 removal of shared music and of visual contact in dancing dyads, or a reanalysis of the public data under another movement decomposition, do the music-driven and partner-driven components stay distinct with an equivalence test bounding their interaction near zero, and does a temporal measure other than periodicity (phase lags, directional measures) still fail to tell them apart while a spatial one succeeds?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-891](../theory.d/THEORY-891.md) reports Bigand et al. ([LIT-1424](../literature.d/LIT-1424.md), read in [NOTE-1093](../notes.d/NOTE-1093.md)): in 35
dyads, shared music and visual contact each raised synchrony in different
movement components, with no interaction detected, and the components'
spatial direction, not the periodicity of their synchrony, classified
which factor drove them. No interaction was bounded, one decomposition was
used, and one temporal representation was tried. It sharpens part of
[QUESTION-063](QUESTION-063.md): whether removing drive and channel separately keeps
discriminating, and whether every timing measure fails.

## What would answer it

An independent replication with the same 2 × 2 logic (shared music
removed, visual contact removed, each independently) in a new sample, or
the public data (IIT Dataverse, doi:10.48557/UR2GBG) reanalysed under a
different movement decomposition, showing that the music-driven and
partner-driven components stay distinct, that an equivalence test bounds
their interaction near zero, and that a periodicity measure still fails to
classify them while a spatial one succeeds. Independence fails if a
powered test finds an interaction; the timing result fails if another
temporal representation (phase lags, directional measures) does tell the
two kinds apart. Neither says anything about the direction of the
partner-driven coupling.
