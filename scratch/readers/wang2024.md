## Summary

The paper characterizes the outlier ("spike") eigenvalues and eigenvectors
of a nonlinear spiked covariance model K = GᵀG, whose rows have covariance
Σ with finitely many bounded spikes and arbitrary nonlinear dependence
across coordinates, and shows a "Gaussian equivalence" with the linear
spiked model of Bai and Yao: an outlier exists iff z′(−1/λᵢ) > 0, it sits
at z(−1/λᵢ), and its eigenvector's squared overlap is φ(−1/λᵢ) (Theorem 6,
formal Theorem 12). Two applications: (i) in a deep fully-connected network
with random Gaussian weights at linear width, a spike in the input Gram
matrix propagates layer to layer through a recursion with a BBP-type test
at each layer; spikes that fail it are absorbed into the bulk, and the
number of spikes and their alignment with the input signal never increase
with depth (Theorem 3, Corollary 4): a "curse of depth" at random
initialization. (ii) After O(1) GD steps with η ≍ 1 on a two-layer network,
the first-layer weights are approximately W₀ plus a rank-one term; the
test-data CK then has a spike, above a threshold, whose eigenvector aligns
with the test labels (Theorem 5), proving Conjecture 4 of Ba and colleagues
(LIT-tmpnwvnx).

## Contribution

1. First-order limits and the BBP transition for spikes of nonlinear
   spiked covariance models, not only weak convergence of the bulk.
2. A deterministic equivalent of the resolvent for spectral arguments on
   the positive real line outside the support, and a "no outliers" theorem
   (Theorem 9), extending Chouard and Schröder and colleagues.
3. Spike propagation through depth at random weights (Theorem 3).
4. The test-data CK spike and its label alignment after a few GD steps
   (Theorem 5), verifying [BES+22, Conjecture 4].

## Key results (section, verbatim)

- **Motivation** (§1): spikes "are often the primary spectral features
  (signal) of interest, because they pertain to low-rank structure of the
  underlying learning problem (e.g., class labels or the direction of the
  target function)".
- **Per-layer phase transition** (§2.1, after (2.7)): "The condition
  zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) > 0 describes the 'phase transition' phenomenon for spike
  eigenvalues in this model, where spikes i ∈ Iℓ−1 with zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) > 0
  induce spike eigenvalues in the CK matrix Kℓ of the next layer, while
  spikes with zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) ≤ 0 are absorbed into the bulk spectrum of Kℓ."
- **Theorem 3(c)**: the squared overlap of layer ℓ's spike eigenvector with
  the input spike eigenvector tends to ∏ₖ φₖ(si,k−1), a product of factors.
- **Corollary 4** (GMM-type signal-plus-noise input X = Σθᵢaᵢbᵢᵀ + Z): Kℓ
  has a spike for θᵢ "if and only if θᵢ > γ0^{1/4} and i ∈ Iℓ".
- **Curse of depth** (§2.1, numerical illustration): "IL ⊆ · · · ⊆ I0 and
  φℓ(si,ℓ−1) ∈ (0, 1), so the number of spike eigenvalues of Kℓ induced by
  K0 and the alignment of the spike eigenvectors of Kℓ with the true class
  label vectors {bi} are both non-increasing in the network depth ... In
  other words, at random initialization, the input signal diminishes as the
  depth of the NN increases."
- **Remedies, empirical** (Fig. 2): larger width preserves alignment over
  more layers; "gradient descent training on the weight matrices also
  restores and even amplifies the informative signal in the CK matrix of
  each layer; specifically, after 50 steps of GD training (yellow curve),
  the alignment between the class labels and the leading eigenvector of Kℓ
  may increase through depth." This is simulation (µP, η = 0.1), not a
  theorem.
- **Activation conditions** (§2.1, on (2.5)): "if bσ = 0, then spiked
  eigenstructure does not propagate across the NN layers in our studied
  regime of bounded spike magnitudes"; E[σ″(ξ)] = 0 is assumed because
  otherwise "Kℓ may have spike eigenvalues even when the input K0 has no
  spiked structure" ([BP22]); "We assume E[σ″(ξ)] = 0 for clarity, to avoid
  characterizing also such uninformative spikes across layers."
- **Trained weights** (§2.2, from [BES+22]): W_trained ≈ W₀ + (ηbσ/n)Xᵀya⊤,
  a change "O(1) in operator norm and approximately rank-1".
  Proposition 35 (App. F): "The largest singular value smax(W) falls outside
  the limit of its empirical singular value distribution if and only if
  θ1 > γ0^{1/4}".
- **Theorem 5**: the test-data CK "has a spike eigenvalue if and only if
  θ1 > γ0^{1/4} and z′(−1/λ1) > 0", with label alignment (2.14) > 0.
  Fig. 3(b): "above a phase transition threshold, the alignment ⟨û, ỹ⟩²
  ... increases with the learning rate or number of GD steps; in addition,
  alignment also increases with the training set size n."
- **Gaussian equivalence** (§3): "an exact asymptotic equivalence between
  spiked spectral phenomena in a nonlinear spiked covariance model with
  those of a linearly defined (possibly Gaussian) model."

## Assumptions

- Proportional limit n/dℓ → γℓ; fixed depth L; i.i.d. N(0,1) weights
  independent of X (§2.1). Input τn-orthonormal with τn·n^{1/3} → 0, a
  fixed number r of spikes of bounded size outside the bulk support.
- σ twice differentiable, bounded σ′, σ″; Eσ(ξ) = 0, Eσ² = 1; bσ ≠ 0 and
  E σ″(ξ) = 0 (e.g. odd σ such as tanh).
- §2.2: isotropic Gaussian inputs, single-index labels σ*(β*ᵀx) + noise,
  second layer frozen, fixed T and η_t ≍ 1 (not the maximal-update
  η = Θ(d) of Dandi and colleagues), independent test data.
- General model: independent rows, concentration of quadratic forms.

## Concepts

Conjugate kernel (CK); nonlinear spiked covariance model; BBP phase
transition per layer (z′ > 0); spike absorbed into the bulk; spike
propagation and its product of overlap factors; "curse of depth" at random
initialization; Gaussian equivalence; deterministic equivalent and
generalized resolvent; "master equation" for spikes; uninformative spikes
from non-odd activations ([BP22]); pre-trained (test-data) CK.
"Bulk" is used by count throughout ("spectral bulk", "bulk spectrum").

## Verdict on how the exchange characterized the work

- **A32 §6** (in CLAIM-799): work tying learned features to spiked
  random-matrix models, so "the spectral perspective is not merely an
  analogy. But they don't establish that DNN representations generally
  behave as active matter, or that their learning dynamics are critical."
  **Fair.** §2.2 proves a spike in the test-data CK of trained weights and
  its label alignment (Theorem 5); the paper says nothing of active matter,
  criticality or dynamics beyond O(1) GD steps. Its "phase transition" is
  the BBP outlier criterion, an estimator-side event.
- **LIT-2984's summary**, "how spikes propagate, grow or vanish through
  nonlinear layers": **half right; correct it.** Propagate and vanish, yes
  (Theorem 3). Grow, no, not in the theory: at random weights the number
  of spikes and their alignment are "non-increasing in the network depth".
  Growth through depth appears only in Fig. 2(b), after GD training, as a
  simulation. "Spike eigenvalues of bounded size" is the paper's own
  scope.
- **LIT-2984's "how a BBP-type transition carries through a deep
  network"**: **accurate**, for random weights: a BBP-type test applies at
  each layer, recursively.
- **Park and colleagues** (NOTE-tmpzethm) cite it as prior BBP-in-networks
  work: **accurate.**
- **Dandi and colleagues** (NOTE-tmpdac0o) call it a sibling not cited:
  **accurate**; it is the bounded-step (η ≍ 1) sibling of their η = Θ(d)
  analysis. Note the reverse: Wang and colleagues cite Moniri and
  colleagues (LIT-tmp4kae5) as "an asymptotic regime different from ours
  where the learning rate and spike eigenvalues diverge".

## Bearing on the named codes

- **CLAIM-799: supports, and adds a cascade.** (1) The BBP criterion
  recurs at every layer: a spike yields a CK outlier only if
  zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) > 0, else it is "absorbed into the bulk". (2) Signal before
  outlier, again: after GD, W_trained ≈ W₀ + (ηbσ/n)Xᵀya⊤ for any η > 0,
  yet the weights' top singular value separates only if θ1 > γ0^{1/4}
  (Prop. 35), and the test-data CK has an outlier only if additionally
  z′(−1/λ1) > 0 (Theorem 5). Two detectability thresholds in series,
  weights then features, both above the moment the update exists: a third
  instance of the claim's separation, beside Park's. (3) A caution the
  claim should carry: Theorem 3 does not say what a sub-threshold
  (absorbed) spike leaves in layer ℓ's bulk or eigenvectors, so "the
  signal is lost with depth" is the paper's reading of outlier alignment,
  "at random initialization, the input signal diminishes as the depth of
  the NN increases", not a statement that decodability is lost. The claim
  separates the two; the paper does not, but proves only the spectral
  side. Grounds: the claim cites LIT-2984; the reading can replace it.
- **NOTE-tmpzethm (Park and colleagues): parallel.** Fig. 3(b)'s
  threshold is in "learning rate × steps", the analogue of Park's critical
  time t_c for an outlier that appears while the student converges
  throughout; here at η ≍ 1 and O(1) steps, a different and smaller
  regime. Park's citation of it is correct.
- **NOTE-tmpdac0o (Dandi and colleagues): contrast, complementary
  regimes.** At η ≍ 1 the test-data CK's bulk law in Theorem 5 is built
  from the untrained ν0 = bσ²⊗ρMP_γ0 ⊕ (1−bσ²), so learning shows as one
  outlier beside an unchanged bulk: the regime CLAIM-799's Dandi note
  calls the one where the outlier picture "is closest to the whole story".
  Dandi's η = Θ(d) reshapes the bulk. Both are Gaussian single-index,
  two-layer, one/few steps, odd activations.
- **TERM-tmpf5ymd ("bulk"): an instance of the bulk by count, no new
  sense.** "Spectral bulk", "absorbed into the bulk spectrum". The
  outliers, few and largest, are the bulk by weight and here carry the
  signal. One addition worth a line: the paper names "uninformative spike
  eigenvalues" ([BP22], non-odd σ), outliers by weight that carry no input
  structure, and assumes them away (E σ″ = 0).
- **CLAIM-tmp2r6ur (owner's truncated band): leaves open; background, with
  one parallel.** No per-direction discrimination is measured and the
  bottom of the spectrum is not discussed. Parallel to the stop-word half:
  for non-odd activations the CK can have top eigenvalues "even when the
  input K0 has no spiked structure", i.e. top directions that carry no
  task signal; reported from [BP22] and excluded by assumption, not shown
  here. In the paper's own settings the top outliers are the signal
  (class labels, target direction), which runs against "top = weakly
  discriminating", as in Dandi.
- **CLAIM-tmp9atj9 (rival a, top directions track data PCs in weak
  regimes): weak parallel, leaves open.** At random weights the CK's spike
  eigenvectors are attenuated copies of the input Gram's spike
  eigenvectors (Theorem 3(c)): an untrained network's top directions are
  inherited from the data's principal structure. Fig. 2(b): training
  "restores and even amplifies" alignment with labels. But in the GMM the
  input's top PC *is* the label direction, so the paper cannot separate
  "tracks PCs" from "tracks the task", which is the point of the rival.
- **CLAIM-tmpufbx6 (rival b, the floor is the estimator's): parallel, weak.**
  Whether a spike is an outlier depends on aspect ratios γℓ = n/dℓ:
  wider layers keep alignment over more layers (Fig. 2(a)) and alignment
  rises with n (Fig. 3(b)). So where a spectrum's resolvable part ends
  moves with sample and width, as the rival says of a floor. But this is
  about top outliers, not the bottom of a spectrum; it does not reach the
  band.

## Limitations

- Random Gaussian weights for the depth result; the training result is
  two-layer, O(1) steps, η ≍ 1, frozen second layer, isotropic Gaussian
  inputs, single-index target.
- Bounded spikes only, fixed number r; proportional widths.
- E σ″ = 0 and bσ ≠ 0 assumed: uninformative spikes (non-odd σ) and
  the case bσ = 0 are outside the theorems.
- First-order limits only: no fluctuations, no finite-n rates for the
  threshold region, no statement on what an absorbed spike leaves in the
  bulk or in sub-threshold eigenvectors.
- The GD-through-depth gain (Fig. 2(b)) and the width remedy (Fig. 2(a))
  are simulations, not theorems.
- Proofs in Appendices C–F not checked by this reader.

## Candidate THEORY

"In a fully-connected network with i.i.d. Gaussian weights at linear width
and an odd-type activation (bσ ≠ 0, E σ″ = 0), a bounded spike in the
input Gram matrix yields an outlier eigenvalue of layer ℓ's conjugate
kernel only if it passes a BBP-type test z′ℓ > 0 at every layer up to ℓ,
spikes that fail are absorbed into the bulk, and the squared overlap of
each surviving outlier eigenvector with the input spike is a product of
per-layer factors in (0, 1), so the number of outliers and their alignment
cannot increase with depth; after O(1) gradient steps at η ≍ 1, a
two-layer network's rank-one weight update yields an outlier in the
test-data conjugate kernel, aligned with the test labels, only above a
threshold in the summed learning rate."
