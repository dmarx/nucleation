# LEAD-tmpbyvwu: Read Callard's Aspiration against the record's self-formation and value-change questions

## What it would do

Read [LIT-tmph5kpk](../literature.d/LIT-tmph5kpk.md) ([THEORY-tmpju6yf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpju6yf.md), expected), above all chapters 2
(proleptic reasons), 3 (intrinsic and extrinsic conflict) and 5 and 6
(self-creation and responsibility).

What it would settle:

- **[QUESTION-364](../questions.d/QUESTION-364.md).** It asks what makes a revision of an agent's
  will-organization the agent's own continuation rather than a replacement.
  Callard's teleological account of self-creation, "by modeling herself on
  the person she is trying to be, the aspirant brings that person into
  being" (abstract, as pasted by the owner), is a candidate answer.
- **[TERM-099](../terms.d/TERM-099.md).** Whether aspiration is self-formation in the term's sense
  (a maintained capacity produced by work on an unresolved discrepancy),
  or a distinct kind in which the discrepancy is between value standpoints.
- **[CLAIM-1036](../claims.d/CLAIM-1036.md).** It says self-changing values fix no scalar optimum.
  Proleptic reasons are Callard's way of making such change rational
  without an optimum fixed in advance. That may be an alternative to the
  claim's verdict, or a corroboration of it.
- **[QUESTION-495](../questions.d/QUESTION-495.md).** It asks about optimal learning when learning changes
  what can later be valued. Callard's "Becoming someone is a learning
  process; and what we learn are the new values" is that case, for
  persons.
