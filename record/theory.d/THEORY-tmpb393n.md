# THEORY-tmpb393n: Within levels of mechanisms, apparent top-down and bottom-up causes can be read as mechanistically mediated effects, intralevel causation among components together with the constitution of the whole by them, so that the relation between levels is constitution and not causation; Craver and Bechtel show the translation for six cases, on the assumption, not argued, that every higher-level cause has a mechanistic explanation

## Source

Craver and Bechtel (2007), [LIT-tmpgntir](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgntir.md), Biology and Philosophy 22:547–563;
read in [NOTE-tmp6uiie](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6uiie.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The definition (p. 548).** X is at a lower level than S iff X is a
  component in the mechanism for some activity ψ of S. Levels so defined
  are local to a mechanism (p. 550).
- **The argument against interlevel causation (pp. 551–554).** A whole
  and its parts share their space-time paths, so nothing is transmitted
  between them. They are not "distinct" in Lewis's sense. The
  dependence between them is simultaneous and runs both ways, while
  causation is taken to be asymmetrical. The authors grant: "None of these
  arguments is decisive" (p. 554).
- **The translations (pp. 556–561).** The general's heart attack (bottom
  up), Ignatius's hotdogs ("carried along for the ride"), Hal's glucose
  metabolism and Kane's memory ("enlisted in the ride"). In each, the
  interlevel claim becomes a causal chain among components plus a claim
  that some state of the components constitutes the whole's state. The
  general strategy is "a causal claim coupled with one or more
  constituency claims" (p. 561).
- **What could have failed.** A case could have resisted translation, with
  a change in the whole that alters a part through no chain of
  component-level causes. None of the six does. But the six were chosen
  by the authors, and the assumption that every higher-level cause is
  mechanistically explained (p. 548) guarantees that a chain exists.

## What this does not say

- **That higher-level causes are unreal.** The paper assumes they exist:
  mechanisms affect and are affected by things that their parts singly
  cannot be (p. 548).
- **That there is no downward constraint.** "Enlisted in the ride" is the
  paper's name for a whole's condition recruiting its parts' activity. It
  is a change in some components that propagates to others. The account
  relabels downward influence. It does not deny it.
- **Anything about boundaries.** Feedback "inside the mechanism" raises no
  mystery (p. 560). The paper does not ask what changes when the loop runs
  through something outside the mechanism. Who is inside is the
  definition's undefined "component" ([THEORY-tmp09e3b](THEORY-tmp09e3b.md)).
- **Anything about strong emergence,** which is set aside by definition
  (p. 551).

  criteria (predictive and interventional autonomy, closure,
