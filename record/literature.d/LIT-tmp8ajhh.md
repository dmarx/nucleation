# LIT-tmp8ajhh: Mereology

Achille Varzi (2003), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/>

## Key takeaways

- Beyond the uncontroversial core that parthood is a partial order, every further mereological principle — supplementation, extensionality, atomism, and every restricted or unrestricted composition principle — is philosophically contested, so "classical" mereology is one option among many rather than the neutral default.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry introduces mereology as the study of part–whole and part–part relations, tracing it from the Presocratics through medieval, Buddhist, Islamic and modern philosophy to Brentano, Husserl, Leśniewski and Leonard–Goodman. It separates principles that fix the meaning of "part" from substantive ones, formalises a minimal theory, then surveys decomposition principles (from whole to parts) and composition principles (from parts to whole), and ends with the question of whether parthood can be indeterminate.
