# LIT-tmpd2o1w: Précis of Responsibility and Control: A Theory of Moral Responsibility

John Martin Fischer & Mark Ravizza (2000), "Précis of *Responsibility and
Control: A Theory of Moral Responsibility*", *Philosophy and Phenomenological
Research* 61(2):441–445 — DOI-10.2307/2653660

## Key takeaways

- **Guidance, not regulative, control.** "Regulative control involves
  alternative possibilities: it is a kind of dual power of free action. In
  contrast, guidance control does not, by its nature, involve alternative
  possibilities." Moral responsibility "simply requires guidance control"
  (p. 441).
- **Two elements.** Guidance control is "understood in terms of two
  elements": the agent's "ownership" of the mechanism that actually issues
  in the behaviour, and that mechanism's "reasons-responsiveness" (p. 441).
- **Ownership is taken, not found.** "We contend that individuals make
  certain kinds of mechanisms their own by taking responsibility for them"
  (p. 441). In the ordinary case this happens through moral education, as a
  child comes to see itself as an agent and a fair target of the reactive
  attitudes (p. 442). Taking responsibility "is a matter of having certain
  (dispositional) beliefs about oneself (and having acquired those beliefs
  in appropriate ways)" (p. 443), and it works as "a kind of "standing
  policy"" for that kind of mechanism (p. 442).
- **Historical.** Two agents identical in their present properties can
  differ in responsibility, because only one took responsibility by an
  appropriate route (p. 443).
- **Moderate reasons-responsiveness.** Weak responsiveness "is too little to
  demand". A mechanism is moderately responsive "insofar as it is
  "regularly" receptive to reasons (some of which are moral), and at least
  weakly reactive to reasons" (p. 444), with receptivity forming an
  understandable pattern to a third party who knows the agent's values.
- **Semicompatibilism.** "causal determinism is compatible with moral
  responsibility, even if causal determinism rules out alternative
  possibilities" (p. 445).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as the authors' own statement of
the account the record holds as [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md), which until now rested on
encyclopedia reports. It confirms those reports on every point they make:
the mechanism rather than the agent, regular receptivity with weaker
reactivity, ownership by taking responsibility, and compatibility with
determinism.

For the owner's essay (§7.2), the précis makes the ownership test precise.
A mechanism is one's own when one has come, by an appropriate history, to
see oneself as answerable for what it produces. Whether an organization can
take responsibility for its own decision procedures in this sense is the
question the essay would have to answer; the précis does not raise it, and
the book sets groups aside ([NOTE-tmpilfmm](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpilfmm.md)).
