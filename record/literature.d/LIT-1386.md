# LIT-1386: The Evolution of Crew Resource Management Training in Commercial Aviation

Helmreich, R. L., Merritt, A. C., & Wilhelm, J. A. (1999). The evolution of Crew Resource Management training in commercial aviation. *The International Journal of Aviation Psychology*, 9(1), 19–32. doi:10.1207/s15327108ijap0901_2.

Read in full on 2026-10-22 in [NOTE-1087](../notes.d/NOTE-1087.md), from the authors'
manuscript.

## Why this is here

A25 cited it for CRM as "authority without abolishing command": keeping avenues of challenge open while preserving decisive action. The case is [CASE-127](../cases.d/CASE-127.md).

**Corrected 2026-10-22, from the reading.** Neither "authority without
abolishing command" nor keeping "avenues of challenge" open while
preserving "decisive action" is in the paper, and it makes no argument
about command. A captain's command is mentioned once, in third-generation
training for new captains on "the leadership role that accompanies
command". The idea is better sourced from the NTSB's recommendation on
United 173 ([LIT-1371](LIT-1371.md)), which asked for participative management for
captains and assertiveness training for other crew members. What the
paper does supply bears on [CLAIM-422](../claims.d/CLAIM-422.md): trained capacities decay while the
procedure (recurrent training) continues, and an assessed demonstration
of coordination is not evidence of line practice. See [NOTE-1087](../notes.d/NOTE-1087.md).
