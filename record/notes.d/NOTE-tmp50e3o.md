# NOTE-tmp50e3o: Eggert — Dirty Hands Defended

## Contribution

A defence of the possibility of dirty hands that does not rest on reactive
attitudes alone. A dirty-hands case is a conflict between two actual
duties; acting on one, permissibly, leaves the other's wrong in place, and
that remainder grounds rectificatory duties after the fact. She diagnoses
the main objections as resolving the problem's "paradoxical nature at too
high a price" (p. 15).

## Key results

- **The thesis** (p. 14): "a wrong action, such as the transgression of an
  actual, intact right, may be permissible under certain circumstances,
  without this rendering it any less wrong."
- **Remainder and repair** (p. 15): "This is why the agent's hands are
  dirty, why it would be apt for her to experience remorse, and why she
  may owe a duty to apologize".
- **Not only politics** (p. 11): "But dirty hands cases are not unique to
  political activity."
- **Conflict comes from the world** (p. 17): "Duties conflict as a result
  of things that happen, including other people's actions."
- **Policy** (p. 18): "we should work to reduce and eliminate such
  situations in public policy", attending to "what is required to change
  the circumstances, including our background institutions, within which
  the need arose to commit a moral wrong in the first place."
- **Others' wrongdoing, an aside** (p. 18): "Paradigmatic examples of dirty
  hands involve someone else creating a situation in which the agent must
  do wrong, such as in ticking bomb scenarios." Left open: "whether dirty
  hands cases are always generated by others' wrongdoing, or whether they
  may also arise from unfortunate circumstances, in the absence of any
  wrongdoing, though this is a discussion for another day." Note 60 points
  to de Wijze (1994) for the view that they arise from "immoral
  circumstances created by other persons", without adopting it.

## Assumptions

- Moral conflicts are between two actual, not prima facie, duties (p. 4).
- Rights keep their force when permissibly infringed (Thomson's
  infringement and violation, n. 37).
- Neutrality among metaethical views on which we act as though there are
  moral facts (pp. 4–5).

## Concepts

- **Problem of dirty hands**; **moral conflict** and **moral dilemma**.
- **Pro tanto wrong** and **moral remainder**: the overridden duty's wrong,
  which survives a permissible act.
- **Rectificatory duties**: apology, compensation.
- **Permissible infringement** versus violation; **political
  consequentialism**; **specificationism**.

## Bearing on the record

Filed as [THEORY-tmpqwuyi](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpqwuyi.md).

- **[CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md)** (structuring another's circumstances is a wrong of
  its own, beside the actor's). The exchange (A9) says Eggert "notes that
  such predicaments sometimes arise precisely because one person has
  wrongfully created circumstances". She does, in two sentences at the
  close, and defers the question. Nothing in the paper says that creating
  such a predicament wrongs the person placed in it, or anything of the
  arranger's responsibility: the paper is about the agent's remainder.
  What it does give the claim is the agent's side: an agent in a
  predicament still commits a wrong with a remainder, which is the claim's
  "stands beside it". But her agent acts permissibly, where the claim's is
  pressed into wrongdoing. No relation is written; the claim does not cite
  her.
- **[CLAIM-703](../claims.d/CLAIM-703.md)**: compatible ("Duties conflict as a result of things
  that happen, including other people's actions"), but she does not
  discuss manipulation or exploitation. Left open.

## Limitations

- Mostly a defence of coherence. There is little positive account of how
  rectificatory duties are fixed or to whom they are owed.
- The point the exchange cites is an aside.
- Its examples are political (Daschner, Obama, Bush) and ticking bombs.
