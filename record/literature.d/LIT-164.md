# LIT-164: Wave-Function Realism and the Scope of Physical Causal Closure

Alexander Poltorak (2026), *PhilSci-Archive preprint* — <https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/30720/>

## Key takeaways

- Causal-closure arguments for physicalism leave both their domain and their completeness relation implicit. Under wave-function realism, spacetime-restricted closure fails but theory-relative closure can hold, so quantum ontology produces no causal breach for mental causes to exploit. What it does force is that physicalists supply an independent criterion of "physical" and a bridge from dynamical to causal completeness.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Poltorak argues that "every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause" hides two parameters: the domain of closure, and whether the completeness it asserts is causal, dynamical or explanatory. Both become unstable once "physical" is indexed to rival quantum ontologies. Wave-function realism breaks closure restricted to ordinary spacetime but can keep closure relative to fundamental physics, so there is no trans-domain breach. The upshot is a dilemma for causal arguments for physicalism. A spacetime-restricted closure principle is too narrow for serious quantum ontologies, while a theory-relative one needs a non-question-begging criterion of physicality and a dynamical-to-causal bridge. The paper also argues that this gives no support to interactionist dualism, and that Hilbert space, PBR, the Born rule and Bohmian guidance cannot deliver the stronger conclusion.
