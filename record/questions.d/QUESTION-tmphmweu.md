# QUESTION-tmphmweu: Can an intervention on what an interpreter is told about a communicative situation tell the relations that constitute the act from those that only accompany it?

## The question

QUESTION-030 asks which interventions tell constitutive relations from
diagnostic ones. Most designs that propose to answer it, vignette and
rating studies among them, change what the interpreter is told: that the
speaker is a friend, has authority, or made a commitment earlier. This
question asks whether an intervention of that kind can bear on
constitution at all.

## The answer

CLAIM-130, Active, answers it: no, not by itself. In the structural model
A129 §1 writes, do(I = a) and do(S = s) act on different variables, and
nothing in the model makes their effects on the judgement equal
(THEORY-419, THEORY-138). A191 §6: "If we merely tell participants that an
utterance is a reprimand, and they subsequently classify it as a
reprimand, we have learned something about interpretation under
instruction. We have not necessarily demonstrated that the underlying
speech act changed." A do(S) needs a design in which the speaker's role
or the conversation's history actually changes.

What stays open is QUESTION-030's: which do(S) designs tell constitutive
relations apart, and the conceptual half, which needs a theory of the act.
