# CLAIM-669: An arrangement can exploit through the access it grants: a needed good offered on terms that leave the recipient with a lasting encumbrance, a reliance on the provider or an obligation hard to escape, that narrows later exit or renegotiation, so that exploitation can arise through inclusion that succeeds, not only through inclusion that fails, and widening access need not reduce it

## The claim

A9 §2, after Alicea ([LIT-2522](../literature.d/LIT-2522.md)) and Seamster and Charron-Chénier
([LIT-2525](../literature.d/LIT-2525.md)):

> An organization might not merely exploit a dependency that already
> exists.
>
> It might **produce new dependencies by offering access to a valuable
> good under terms that generate or preserve asymmetric control**.

A9 named two mechanisms. Exploitation of an existing dependency: "A party
already depends on something another controls, and that dependency is
used to obtain benefits on unjust terms." Dependency-producing
exploitation: "An arrangement initially expands access or opportunity
but, through its terms and repeated operation, creates new
vulnerabilities or undermines the participant's ability to negotiate or
exit." And: "The second mechanism deserves independent investigation. It
is not automatically implied by the first." "A particularly interesting
possibility is that exploitation can arise through **successful
inclusion**, not merely through failed inclusion. The relevant
intervention would then be different. Increasing access might not reduce
exploitation if the access arrangement itself generates the inequality."

## What the record holds beside it

- **[CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md)**: a capability lost because another reliably supplies it
  is the Black Queen's loss, and one-way supply set from outside "leaves a
  dependence that nothing in the dependent's dynamics limits and that
  withdrawal punishes". That is how supply produces dependence; it says
  nothing of the terms on which the supplier then deals. This claim adds
  the terms: the supplier that produced the reliance can set them.
- **[CLAIM-403](CLAIM-403.md)**: an organization "can widen access while every channel by
  which a less authoritative contribution could change the criteria of
  authority stays closed". That is access without authority; it does not
  say the access produced a dependence.
- **[CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md)**: the same dependence can be organized as mutual
  opportunity, competitive vulnerability or coercive leverage. This claim
  is a route into the third that runs through an offer the recipient
  had reason to accept.
- **[CLAIM-546](CLAIM-546.md)**: a stable relation is no evidence that it is cooperative.

What is new is the conjunction, in which the inclusion is the mechanism
of the exploitation and not its failure. Nothing on the record says it.

## What it does not say

- **What makes the terms exploitative.** A6 §2 gives exploitation as one
  party benefiting "through arrangements that violate a justified
  standard governing another's labor, resources, or standing", and the
  record supplies no such standard ([CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md), [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)). The claim
  is about the route, given a standard.
- **That inclusion is usually predatory**, or that the cited cases are
  representative. They are A's reports of two unread works.
- **That the colonial theatre is an instance.** Slavery's dependence
  predated the theatre; A6 §1 says so: "It would be a mistake ... to
  conclude that theatrical participation originally created enslaved
  people's dependence on colonial institutions." The case
  ([CASE-257](../cases.d/CASE-257.md)) is the other mechanism, an existing dependence used.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Seamster and Charron-Chénier, read

Read in [NOTE-1902](../notes.d/NOTE-1902.md) ([THEORY-1817](../theory.d/THEORY-1817.md)), a sense-3 case ([TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)): lenders,
colleges and households. It supports two of the claim's parts and
qualifies the third.

- **Inclusion that succeeds.** Supported. Predatory inclusion is access
  "to a good, service, or opportunity from which they have historically
  been excluded but under conditions that jeopardize the benefits of this
  access"; Black households' education debt "roughly tripled between 2001
  and 2013", and attainment does not explain the gap.
- **Widening access need not reduce it.** Supported, descriptively: access
  and the debt gap rose together, 2001–2013.
- **Producing the dependence.** Not as version 1 worded it. The need to
  borrow comes from the prior wealth gap; what the access leaves is a
  debt with which households "will potentially be permanently saddled";
  and exit is closed by law: "Student debt is legally very difficult to
  discharge, even when declaring bankruptcy". The mechanism is
  conjectured: "this is where we suspect a significant part of the
  inequality-generating action is."

The claim is narrowed in version 2 to what the reading supports, and the
judgement is [ARG-100](../arguments.d/ARG-100.md). Alicea's "predatory DEI" ([LIT-2522](../literature.d/LIT-2522.md)) is
Unavailable; only its abstract was read. No status change.
