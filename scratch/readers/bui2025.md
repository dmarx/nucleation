## Summary

The setting is retrieval. An old encoder φ_o has already embedded a gallery.
A new encoder φ_n, trained on more data, more classes or a new architecture,
should be able to query those old embeddings without re-embedding the
gallery ("backfilling"). HBCT lifts each encoder's Euclidean output z into
the Lorentz model by the exponential map at the origin, and reads the
embedding's uncertainty from its radius. It trains the new model with a
cross-entropy loss (hyperbolic MLR) plus two alignment terms. One is an
entailment-cone hinge loss that penalises a new embedding h_n for lying
outside the cone of the same item's old embedding h_o. The other is a RINCE
contrastive loss whose robustness parameter q is set to the old
embedding's uncertainty. On CIFAR100 and TinyImageNet, over four update
scenarios and one five-step sequence, HBCT gets higher compatibility
(P_com) than the Euclidean baselines (ℓ2, BCT, Hot-Refresh, AdvBCT, HOC),
and its new-model performance (P_up) stays about the same.

## Contribution

- §1: "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to leverage
  embedding geometry for maintaining consistency in updating models."
- Old-to-new as a partial order. §1: "we employ the entailment loss (Ganea
  et al., 2018) to enforce a partial-order constraint between the old and
  new models. This constraint requires the new embeddings to remain within
  the entailment cone of their older counterparts".
- Uncertainty-weighted alignment: the loss leans on old embeddings the old
  model was confident about and gives less weight to uncertain ones.

## Key results

1. **The time coordinate is read as confidence.** Abstract: "we treat time
   as a natural axis for capturing a model's confidence and evolution."
   Eq. 10 (§4.1): Uncertainty(h) = 1 − ‖h_space‖/h_time = 1 − (1/√K)
   tanh(√K‖z‖), a decreasing function of distance from the origin. This is
   the Poincaré-norm uncertainty of Atigh et al. (2022) and Franco et al.
   (2023).
2. **Unseen classes sit nearer the origin.** §4.1, Fig. 2 (a ResNet18
   trained on 50 CIFAR100 classes): images of the 50 unseen classes "exhibit
   higher uncertainty and lower norms ∥z_o∥ compared to images from the
   first 50 classes".
3. **The cone, its aperture and the loss.** §4.2: the cone "constrains the
   region where new embeddings can 'descend' from old embeddings ... the
   cone represents the set of permissible embedding adjustments as the model
   evolves." Eq. 11 gives aper(h_o) = sin⁻¹(2ε/(√K‖h_o,space‖)), with
   ε = 0.1. The loss is L_entail = max(0, ext(h_o, h_n) − aper(h_o)).
   "As h_o becomes more uncertain (lower norm), its cone 'widens', granting
   greater freedom for the evolutionary expansion".
4. **The constraint is soft.** Eq. 13: L = L_base + λ(L_entail +
   L_contrast), with λ = 0.3 (§5.1). §4.3: "the entailment cone restricts
   only the spatial coordinate changes of the embedding, without preserving
   pairwise distance relationships".
5. **Compatibility gains.** §1 and §5.2: "HBCT improves CMC@1 compatibility
   of the new model by 21.4% compared to the strongest Euclidean baseline,
   and yields a 44.8% increase in mAP compatibility." These are averages
   over the eight scenario-dataset cells of Table 1.
6. **What the cone term buys depends on the scenario.** Table 1 and §5.4:
   removing L_entail causes "a significant drop in terms of backward
   compatibility in scenarios where we update the architecture". CIFAR100
   CMC@1 P_com with and without the entailment term: New-Arch 0.937
   against −0.178; Both 0.324 against −0.487; Ext-class 0.495 against
   0.435; Ext-data 0.956 against 0.847.
7. **Sequential updates.** §5.3, Fig. 5: over five updates (20 to 100
   classes, ResNet18 then ResNet50), "HBCT demonstrates superior
   compatibility maintenance with prior models across multiple updates,
   while the compatibility of baseline methods rapidly declines." The
   matrix covers every pair of earlier and later models, so it bears on
   compatibility between non-adjacent generations.
8. **Uncertainty falls with each update.** §5.4, Fig. 6: "As the model
   improves, the uncertainty in the gallery embeddings decreases."
9. **Radius has to grow, and that is the limitation.** §4.4: the new model's
   clipping threshold is raised, ζ_n = ζ_o + 0.2, "to account for the
   evolution in the embedding space". Table 4 (App. A): with ζ_n = 1.0,
   cross-model compatibility drops. §7: "we gradually increase the clipping
   norm threshold. This may lead to instability as norms grow (e.g., ≥
   10.). A potential solution is to backfill and rescale the time dimension
   after a set number of updates".
10. **Curvature ablation.** Table 3: K ∈ [0.5, 1] is stable. A learnable K
    helps new-to-new retrieval and hurts cross retrieval.

## Assumptions

- Supervised classification training. The old model is frozen and the new
  model is trained afresh (§3, §7).
- The old and new embeddings of the same input are paired. There is no
  hierarchy of concepts: each item's cone is apexed at its own old
  embedding.
- D_new ⊇ D_old (§3). Embedding dimension 128. Hybrid Euclidean–hyperbolic
  encoder with the map at the origin only. K = 1 throughout the main
  results.
- The norm is taken as uncertainty, as Atigh et al. (2022) and Khrulkov et
  al. (2020) do. It is not calibrated against error here.

## A derivation the filer may want (the reader's, not the paper's)

Eq. 11 is THEORY-189's widest transitive family, written on the hyperboloid.
For curvature −K, √K‖h_space‖ = sinh(√K·d(O,h)). So Eq. 11 reads
sinh(√K·d) sin ψ = 2ε, a constant. For K = 1 that is THEORY-189's own form
of the family, sinh(d(O,x)) sin ψ(x) = 2K, with Ganea's K in place of ε.
Two consequences follow. Bui et al. state neither:

- If every constraint held exactly, the generations of one item would form
  a chain of nested cones, and compatibility would carry over to
  non-adjacent generations.
- Every point a cone contains lies farther out than its apex. Satisfying the
  constraint therefore forces each item's radius to grow at every update,
  so its uncertainty must fall.

Results 8 and 9 (the raised ζ_n, Table 4, and the norm growth in §7) are
what that consequence predicts. The paper presents them as empirical
tuning.

## Verdict on how the exchange characterized the work

The LIT summary says that "newer models' embeddings lie further out along a
time-like direction and older ones stay compatible". This is broadly right.
"Further out" is entailed by the cone construction and is visible in
Fig. 6 and in the raised clipping, but the paper's own wording is
"constraining updated embeddings to lie within the entailment cone of the
old ones" (abstract).

CLAIM-839 says that "Backward-compatible learning already constrains updated
embeddings to earlier cones". This is accurate as far as it goes, with four
qualifications the claim should carry:

- The constraint is a soft hinge penalty weighted 0.3. The paper never
  reports how many new embeddings actually lie inside their cones.
- The cones are per item, between the old and new embedding of the same
  input. They are not cones of concepts.
- They link two separately trained models, the old one frozen. They do not
  link checkpoints of one run.
- The order is imposed by design. It is not observed in learning.

The venue and the arXiv id are confirmed.

## Bearing on the named codes

- **CLAIM-839: supports in part, and qualifies.**
  - Supports: A33's fallback reading, temporal cone consistency "as ... a
    soft regularizer", is exactly what HBCT implements, and it works. The
    entailment term matters most when the architecture changes (result 6).
  - Qualifies in two ways. First, per item the order is a chain of nested
    cones, not a branching order. Second, narrowing is built in (see the
    derivation): the entailment term makes every embedding more "certain"
    whether or not it is correct, and correctness enters only through
    L_base. That fits CLAIM-839's "a narrower region counts as progress only
    while it stays correct". The cone term alone cannot certify progress.
  - The Blackwell side is untouched.
- **CLAIM-800: supports by example.** Occupancy of old cones is what
  L_entail pushes up, by construction. The paper rightly judges by an
  independent measure, retrieval P_com and P_up, and never reports
  occupancy.
- **THEORY-189: parallel; an application.** The same family in Lorentz
  form. Its "farther from the origin than its apex" consequence accounts for
  the norm growth the paper lists as its limitation (§7).
- **QUESTION-512: background; leaves open.** Here cones serve the evolution
  of models across updates, not the order in which training acquires
  distinctions. There is no acquisition observable.
- **QUESTION-217: background.** Generations under nested per-item cones form
  a refinement chain by construction. This is a case where a chain is
  imposed, not found.
- **ARG-153: leaves open.** There is nothing on dimension; d is fixed at
  128. The aperture depends on radius (Eq. 11), which agrees with ARG-153's
  point that cost is set by radius as well as by angle.
- **LIT-2993 (Atigh 2026): background.** Bui et al. cite Atigh et al.
  (2022), "Hyperbolic image segmentation" (CVPR), for norm-as-uncertainty.
  That is the same first author and an earlier paper, not LIT-2993.
- **THEORY-188 (hyperbolic random graphs): no bearing.**

## Limitations

- Small benchmarks (CIFAR100, TinyImageNet) and supervised learning only;
  §7 names self-supervised and multimodal settings as future work.
- One run per configuration. No error bars or seeds are reported.
- The baselines' hyperparameters were searched (10 runs each) while HBCT's
  were fixed (§5.1). That cuts in HBCT's favour on effort, but no seed
  variance is shown.
- Old models differ between the Euclidean and hyperbolic arms, so only P_com
  and P_up are comparable across them (§5.2).
- Cone satisfaction is never measured, and neither is uncertainty
  calibration.
- Norm growth is unbounded across updates (§7).
- The 21.4% and 44.8% figures are averages of relative gains and are
  sensitive to the small denominators in P_com.
