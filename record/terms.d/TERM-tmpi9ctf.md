# TERM-tmpi9ctf: consciousness, and which sense: phenomenal, access, self-consciousness or wakefulness

## Definition

A claim about consciousness says which of these it means.

1. **Phenomenal.** There is something it is like for the subject to be in
   the state (Nagel, [LIT-096](../literature.d/LIT-096.md)).
2. **Access.** Information is available to specified reasoning, control,
   decision or report processes. The distinction from the first is Block's
   ([LIT-643](../literature.d/LIT-643.md)): a deficit that removes a capacity together with
   consciousness does not show that the capacity is a function of phenomenal
   consciousness, since access is lost too ([THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md)); and phenomenal
   consciousness may overflow access ([THEORY-103](../theory.d/THEORY-103.md)).
3. **Self-consciousness.** The system represents or attends to aspects of
   itself as itself. [TERM-045](TERM-045.md)'s senses of "self" say which self.
4. **Wakefulness or arousal.** A clinical or behavioural state of
   responsiveness, distinct from any particular conscious content.

A12: "Access, reportability, self-modeling, organized interpretation, and
agency do not individually establish phenomenal consciousness by
definition. Their relationships to phenomenal consciousness require
substantive theories and evidence." That is the question of the
evidence-for-experience line, which this term does not answer.

## What it is not

- **A theory of consciousness.** A12: "*phenomenal consciousness* itself
  should not be defined through a preferred neural, computational, or
  organizational theory. Otherwise a theory proposing that consciousness is
  constituted by a particular organization becomes true by stipulation."
  The phenomenon is kept apart from its candidate explanations and from
  its indicators.
- **A phenomenal perspective.** [TERM-072](TERM-072.md)'s third sense, a perspective
  that is phenomenal, is one use of sense 1; an organizational perspective
  (its sense 1) carries no claim about experience.
- **Evidence.** A behavioural or architectural indicator of consciousness is
  not thereby a constitutive condition of it ([ADR-056](../decisions.d/ADR-056.md)'s distinction
  between criterion and indicator).
