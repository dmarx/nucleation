# QUESTION-tmpxd26n: After cued traumatic recall, do people with persistent PTSD sample disambiguating peripheral context less than matched trauma-exposed controls, and does restoring that sampling during the same exposure produce lasting change that the exposure alone does not?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-285](../theory.d/THEORY-285.md) shows, in illustrative simulation, how an overly precise prior
that one is in a harmful context can keep itself up by making exploratory
sampling look uninformative. The paper fits no data. Whether people with
persistent PTSD do this is open, and it is what [QUESTION-045](QUESTION-045.md)'s lock-in
reading of disorder would need at the individual scale.

One reading in the record leans against the sampling account in people,
on a different task. In De Kleine et al. ([THEORY-912](../theory.d/THEORY-912.md)), patients with OCD or
PTSD "sampled as often as controls and drew less from the outcomes". That
task is avoiding a safety cue, not sampling context, so it does not answer
this question.

## What would answer it

Evidence from people, not from the model. Either of two kinds would
support it. First, the authors' own prediction confirmed: after cued
traumatic recall, people with PTSD attenuate peripheral evidence about
context more than focal evidence, against matched trauma-exposed controls,
with context-sampling measured directly (for example by eye movements
toward disambiguating periphery). Second, an intervention contrast matched
on exposure: restoring context sampling during the same exposure produces
lasting change that the same exposure without it does not, while a
precision-lowering drug reduces symptoms that return on withdrawal. The
account is refuted if people with persistent PTSD sample disambiguating
context as much as resilient controls and still keep the harmful-context
belief. More simulations with other parameters cannot settle it, and nor
can symptom improvement alone.
