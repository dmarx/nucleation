# LIT-tmphbm5x: Tiktok's enshittification

Cory Doctorow (2023), "Tiktok's enshittification", *Pluralistic*, 21 January
2023 — <https://pluralistic.net/2023/01/21/potemkin-ai/>

## Key takeaways

- **The lifecycle.** "Here is how platforms die: first, they are good to
  their users; then they abuse their users to make things better for their
  business customers; finally, they abuse those business customers to claw
  back all the value for themselves. Then, they die."
- **The cause.** Doctorow calls it "a seemingly inevitable consequence" of
  how easily a platform can change how it allocates value, combined with a
  two-sided market in which the platform holds buyers and sellers "hostage
  to the other".
- **Lock-in enables each stage.** Amazon's below-cost prices, DRM and
  Prime, and Facebook's "mutual hostage-taking" among friends, make
  leaving costly; surplus then moves to suppliers and finally to
  shareholders.
- **TikTok.** Citing a Forbes report of a "heating tool", Doctorow argues
  that the For You feed is partly filled with videos chosen to win over
  creators, a lure he likens to a rigged carnival prize. He predicts the
  attention will be withdrawn once they are committed.
- **Limits on it.** Blocking interoperable clients raises "the amount of
  enshittification users can be force-fed without risking their
  departure". But the strategy "only succeeds if it is pursued in measured
  amounts": the equilibrium short of users' breaking point is fragile, and
  managers' short-termism makes it hard to hold. Monopoly, and buying out
  rivals, is why it has lasted.
- **The remedy.** Not saving platforms but lowering the cost of their
  death: end-to-end delivery between willing speakers and listeners, and
  "freedom of exit", leaving without losing one's communities, purchases
  and data.
