# QUESTION-473: When an organization has substantially contributed to the conditions that produced an agent's wrongdoing, what remains of its moral standing to blame that agent, as distinct from its authority to investigate, correct or sanction the wrongdoing?

## Why it is open

The SEP entry "Blame" ([LIT-2720](../literature.d/LIT-2720.md), read 2026-11-16; [THEORY-1912](../theory.d/THEORY-1912.md))
reports a complicity condition on standing: one who forced the wrong, or
left no reasonable alternative, is not in a position to blame the one who
committed it. The exchange's organizations are partial versions of that:
[CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md) (they rely on members' commitments to cover deficiencies),
[CLAIM-702](../claims.d/CLAIM-702.md) (they take credit while the compensation holds) and
[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md) (blame settles where failure is visible). [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md) says
the structuring party answers for the conditions; it does not say what that
does to its standing to blame.

What the question keeps apart: moral standing to blame, and the authority
to investigate, correct and sanction. An organization may have to sanction
dangerous conduct even when it is partly responsible for the conditions that
produced it.

## What would count as an answer

An account of how the complicity condition applies to organizations, which
are not persons, and how far it reaches (forced, induced, enabled); and a
showing of whether loss of standing to blame leaves disciplinary authority
intact. Distinct from [QUESTION-470](QUESTION-470.md), which asks who must repair a harm,
not who may condemn for it.

The continuation of the same exchange filed A17's inverse question as
[QUESTION-tmpvoufs](QUESTION-tmpvoufs.md): by what instruments agents can hold principals
accountable for the conditions they set. Distinct: this question asks what
the principal may still do to the agent, that one what the agent can do to
the principal.
