# QUESTION-tmp9lrtl: When does establishing a small, internally compatible subsystem make global organization more likely, and how can its effectiveness be predicted from the constraint architecture?

## Why it is a question

A's notion: an organizational nucleus is "a localized intervention or
substructure whose formation biases the subsequent evolution toward a
coherent global organization", which A marks as "not automatically a
nucleation site in the strict thermodynamic sense". Internal
compatibility can be made exact with the record's apparatus: the
subsystem's demands are a face of its compatibility complex ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)).
The question is when realizing such a face locally raises the chance
that the whole reaches a coherent organization, that is, raises reliable
emergence and not only feasibility ([CLAIM-tmp9jvpb](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp9jvpb.md)).

The readings point both ways.

- **Seeds can predict.** Stern, Pinson and Murugan ([NOTE-tmp9dvve](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9dvve.md);
  [THEORY-tmpq2qdv](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpq2qdv.md)): a folding island is "the largest subset of the pattern that
  will fold in the desired branch, if that subset is cut out and actuated
  at the chosen crease", and whether the islands of the actuated creases
  cover the sheet predicts success far better than their number
  ([CLAIM-tmp675a9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp675a9.md)). The predictor is computed from the constraint structure,
  approximately, by mountain–valley propagation.
- **Seeds can mislead.** In the same paper a badly placed seed locks in a
  distractor, there being no backtracking at zero temperature. DeLuca et
  al. ([NOTE-tmp3af4j](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp3af4j.md); [THEORY-tmpivh27](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpivh27.md)) find a persistent locally ordered precursor
  in DNA origami folding, yet forming it early "is not a perfect predictor
  of overall folding performance", and one run, best folded early, ends
  "caught in a global topological trap". The paper does not call its
  mechanism nucleation, and should not be cited as a nucleation case.

The record's nearest entries are about arguments, not organizations:
[CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md) holds that a persistent structure is available as an anchor
and [CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) that anchors entrench; [QUESTION-038](QUESTION-038.md) asks what turns a
persistent structure into an anchor. [QUESTION-090](QUESTION-090.md) asks when a population
develops self-maintaining governance, of which a nucleus would be one
route.

## What would answer it

A predictor computed from a constraint architecture, like the union of
folding islands, that says which local subsystems raise the probability
of reaching a coherent global organization, which lower it, and by how
much, with the conditions (temperature or noise, the possibility of
backtracking, the order of formation) under which it holds; tested in a
system other than the one it was built for.

## What would not answer it

A case in which some local structure formed first and global order
followed. Precedence is not bias: DeLuca et al.'s trapped run had a
precursor too.
