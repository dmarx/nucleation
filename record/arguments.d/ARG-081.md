# ARG-081: Because a false alarm's error lies in a non-factive content, Grice's analysis shows that the norm that content answers to is an intention, the designer's

## The inference, as it was made

1. [ARG-082](ARG-082.md), steps 1 to 5: a false alarm's error lies in a
   non-factive content.
2. Grice on the red light ([NOTE-735](../notes.d/NOTE-735.md), p. 385): such a sign carries "some
   sort of reference to somebody's intentions".
3. So the norm the non-factive content answers to is an intention, in a
   designed alarm the designer's.

## Critical questions

- **Does the rule apply?** No. Step 2 is not a rule but Grice's tentative
  remark on one awkward case, made where he calls his reduction of
  timeless meaning "a first shot". It is not a test that sorts
  non-factive contents by the source of their norms.
- **Are there exceptions?** Yes, and they defeat the generalization. A
  biological warning system can have a non-factive content whose norm
  comes from selection history, with no one's intention involved. A
  self-maintaining system's anticipation can be false by its own lights
  ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)). Non-factivity is common to all three sources, so it
  cannot pick out one.
- **What survives?** For the designed alarm of [CASE-243](../cases.d/CASE-243.md) the
  designer's intention is the source, but by the case's stipulation, not
  by this inference. [CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md) version 2 keeps it on that footing.

Rejected. Non-factivity shows only that the factive account is
insufficient (the review of [#483](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/483) the owner relayed on 2026-11-12).
