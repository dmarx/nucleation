# NOTE-tmpogww2: Levels of valence

## Contribution

A reconciliation of two ideas of valence that have "largely lived side by
side" (p. 1): appraisal theory's several valences and the one-dimensional
valence of core affect and common-currency theories of choice. Both are
kept, at different levels, and the paper proposes hypotheses for each path
between them.

## Key insight

Valence is not one thing. A food can be pleasant, bad for one's diet,
congruent with one's identity and morally neutral at the same moment. Each
is a separate valenced appraisal. Choice still needs one net value, and
that is a second, integrated valence. Which integration rule produces it
is an open empirical question.

## Assumptions

- The component process model's checks and their order. Pleasantness is
  a relevance check, run early; goal conduciveness is an implications
  check, run later (p. 2, citing Grandjean and Scherer 2008).
- That "there is a logical need for the integration of complex affective
  experiences into a common currency to compare, rank, and choose between
  options" (p. 4).
- That integration rules from behavioural decision theory transfer to
  affect.

## Key results

A framework, not results:

- **Five micro-valences** (pp. 2–3): pleasantness/beauty, goal
  conduciveness, power, self-congruence, moral goodness. Each is bivariate
  in itself ("more or less unpleasant and more or less pleasant").
- **Pleasantness versus goal conduciveness** (p. 2). "The degree of goal
  conduciveness is directly determined by the degree of need satisfaction
  or goal attainment. In contrast, needs may set thresholds for
  pleasantness, but cannot predict pleasantness beyond those thresholds."
  The evidence is Berridge (2003), consumer research separating hedonic
  from utilitarian attitudes, and Litt et al. (2010): "individuals strive
  harder to acquire consumer goods they like less".
- **Integration rules** (pp. 8–9): weighted sum (all information, with
  trade-offs), and tallying, lexicographic, elimination-by-aspects and
  one-clever-cue rules (some information ignored, "no trade-offs between
  micro-valences are required"). The choice of rule depends on accuracy
  versus effort, on certainty, and on ecological fit.
- **Instability** (p. 9). "The integration of strongly positive and
  negative micro-valences may be unstable because it is experienced as a
  conflict".
- **Paths over time** (pp. 9–12). Micro-valence to macro-valence;
  macro-valence back to micro-valence by misattribution; micro to micro by
  facilitation, sequence constraints, carry-over and opponent processes;
  micro-valences to emotions as attractors. No path from emotions to
  macro-valence: an emotion's valence comes from appraising it (p. 12).
- **Conscious and unconscious macro-valence can conflict** (p. 12): the
  same micro-valences can be integrated differently at the two levels.

## Concepts

- **micro-valence** — the valenced outcome of one appraisal check; it has
  an object and can coexist with opposed micro-valences.
- **macro-valence** — the one-dimensional net valence used for choice;
  always present, possibly objectless.
- **intrinsic (un)pleasantness** — a "genetically determined association
  of particular objects with (dis)pleasure", whose bounds needs can shift.
- **goal conduciveness** — the "functionality or efficiency of a situation
  to satisfy needs, achieve goals, or confirm values".

## Connections

## Bearing on the record

**Does "levels of valence" give a principled reply to [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md)?**
In part. It gives the record a principled way to concede the objection
where it is right. It does not give a way to keep "hedonic" for the regime
the record defines.

What it gives:

- **The split is the CPM's own, and grounded in Berridge.** Shuman et al.
  separate intrinsic pleasantness from goal conduciveness on the same
  evidence [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md) uses. "The sensual enjoyment of an object and the
  goal striving toward an object rely on different brain mechanisms
  (e.g., Berridge, 2003)" (p. 2). Their own example is a wanting–liking
  dissociation: people "strive harder to acquire consumer goods they like
  less" (Litt et al. 2010, whose title is "parallel counterdriving of
  wanting and liking"). So a reply of the form "the ascendancy account
  covers goal-conduciveness valence, and intrinsic pleasantness is a
  separate check with its own ordering" is not ad hoc. It is the
  taxonomy of the theory the record already draws on.
- **The record's ordering matches goal conduciveness.** [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md)'s
  ordering ranks the system's states "by their bearing on its
  maintenance", and its sign is progress against a reference course. Goal
  conduciveness is "directly determined by the degree of need
  satisfaction or goal attainment" (p. 2). Pleasantness is not: needs
  only "set thresholds" on it. The fit is with goal conduciveness, and
  the paper explains why the kind clause could not rescue the account in
  [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md)'s first attempt. Hunger's 'wanting' is about maintenance
  because it is goal-conduciveness valence, not pleasantness.
- **The anhedonia data fit the same line.** "Anhedonic individuals do not
  differ in their reported pleasure from consummatory behaviors (e.g.,
  eating dinner), but report reduced anticipatory pleasure regarding
  goal-directed activities (e.g., making dinner; Gard et al., 2007)"
  (p. 14). That is [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md)'s suggested repair, an ordering over
  consummatory states distinct from one over pursuits, in the paper's own
  words.

What it does not give:

- **Goal conduciveness is not 'wanting'.** Goal conduciveness appraises
  how far an event serves a goal; 'wanting' is the incentive salience that
  makes a cue pull. The addict who gets the drug and enjoys it less is
  high on goal conduciveness for the wanting-driven goal. That is the
  case where [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md) says the sign clause gives the wrong answer,
  and relabelling the valence does not change that.
- **It does not make pleasantness ascendant.** A reply that wants to keep
  "hedonic" needs 'liking' to be the ascendancy of a pleasantness
  ordering. The paper gives pleasantness an ordering, but no account of
  its winning anything. It appears in the macro-valence only as one
  weighted input. The paper's one candidate for a micro-valence that
  "may generally dominate" is moral goodness (p. 9), not pleasantness.
- **Pleasantness fails the kind clause.** It is an ordering over stimuli,
  fixed largely by evolution, with sugar "not a particularly sensitive
  indicator for the nutritive value of a food" (p. 2). It is not an
  ordering of the system's own states by their bearing on its
  maintenance. A pleasantness ascendancy would need [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md)'s kind
  clause rewritten.

So the candid reading is that the paper supports [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md)'s second
remedy: revise [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) and [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md) from hedonic to
goal-conduciveness (or motivational) valence, and treat intrinsic
pleasantness as a separate check with its own ordering, outside the
account. That is the owner's decision. If taken, the CPM supplies the
vocabulary.

**The paper's macro-valence is the record's common currency.** The
"integrative 'common currency' to compare alternatives for choices"
(p. 1) is [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md)'s part 2: the negotiation admitting an ordering of
the options. Two things carry over:

- **The two regimes are the paper's integration rules.** A weighted sum
  trades off; lexicographic and elimination-by-aspects rules need "no
  trade-offs between micro-valences" (p. 8). That is [CLAIM-tmp85p0g](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp85p0g.md)'s
  contrast between a currency and an ordering without one, and
  [CLAIM-tmp83ge2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp83ge2.md)'s two regimes of ascendancy. Shuman et al. reached it from
  decision theory, independently.
- **Ascendancy of one micro-valence is the paper's lexicographic case.**
  "Moral goodness may generally dominate other micro-valences" (p. 9) is a
  hypothesis that one ordering wins the integration. It is ascendancy in
  the record's sense, with priority, though the paper does not test
  persistence or resumption.

**"Perspectives".** The paper's own word for the micro-valences is
perspectives: "we focus on the qualitative difference between perspectives
with which the same object may be approached (e.g., pleasantness or goal
conduciveness of food)" (p. 13). That is a precedent for reading appraisal
as several perspectives, each with its own ranking. It is weaker than the
record's reading: the paper's perspectives are criteria of one evaluator
with weights, not parts of an organization with authority. But the
instability of integrating opposed micro-valences (p. 9), and the
evaluation that "can suppress competing evaluations" (p. 10), are the
negotiation's conflicts in the paper's terms.

**On ambivalence ([CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md), consequence 4).** The paper's
conscious–unconscious conflict, where the same micro-valences yield
different macro-valences at two levels of processing (p. 12), is a
version of the record's split ascendancy: one ordering prevailing in one
part of the system and another elsewhere. It also gives the record's
marker for it: "non-verbally avoiding and verbally approaching the
partner" (p. 12).

**For [CLAIM-tmptd5us](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptd5us.md).** The paper says integration at any level "may
occur consciously or unconsciously" (p. 7) and that the number of
micro-valences integrated and how consciously "are independent issues"
(p. 9). It has no account of when valence is felt, so it neither
supports nor rivals the self-model condition.

## Limitations

A framework with many hypotheses and no new data. The integration rules
are borrowed from choice research and none is tested on affect. The two
figures are schematic. Its use of Berridge is a citation, not an analysis;
it does not ask whether goal conduciveness is incentive salience.

## Open questions

Which integration rule governs macro-valence, and whether it changes with
state (the paper predicts more effortful integration under negative
macro-valence, p. 12). Whether a pleasantness ordering can be ascendant,
or only weighted. Whether the physiological signatures that separate
intrinsic valence from goal congruence (facial EMG versus autonomic
measures, reviewed in [NOTE-tmpmobow](NOTE-tmpmobow.md)) could serve
[QUESTION-tmpsqwu5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpsqwu5.md) as markers of which ordering prevails.
