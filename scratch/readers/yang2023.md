## Summary

Neural collapse (NC1) says last-layer features of same-label training
samples collapse to their class mean. The authors coarsen CIFAR-10 labels
(ỹ = y mod 5, five two-class super-classes) and train ResNet-18 with SGD to
zero training error. NC1 and NC2, measured on the coarse labels, go to zero
as usual. Yet within each super-class the representations form two clusters
matching the original classes: the class-distance matrix keeps a darker
diagonal, t-SNE shows separated sub-clusters, and a Cluster-and-Linear-Probe
(t-SNE, then 2-means per super-class, then a linear probe on the
reconstructed labels) reaches about 93% on the original 10-class test set.
The structure persists to 1,000 epochs, appears with DenseNet-121, on
Coarse CIFAR-100 and under random coarsening, but not with VGG-18. With
CIFAR-100's semantic super-classes, sub-classes separate only where they
are dissimilar. A one-hidden-layer theorem on a four-Gaussian mixture with
two coarse labels proves cluster-separable hidden features after gradient
descent.

## Contribution

An empirical demonstration that "collapse" as measured (NC1 → 0) is
compatible with the residual within-class variation being structured by
the input distribution, recoverable without labels; a timing observation
(input structure first, label-driven merging later); and a toy theorem.

## Key results

1. **Collapse hides structure** (Abstract): "even when representations
   apparently collapse, the small amount of remaining variation can still
   faithfully and accurately captures the intrinsic structure of input
   distribution."
2. **CLP ≈ standard accuracy** (§1, §4.3, Fig. 9): "CLP can achieve > 93%
   accuracy on the original CIFAR-10 test set, matching the standard
   accuracy of ResNet-18, even though only 5 coarse labels are provided the
   entire time." The CLP pipeline uses "t-SNE to reduce the dimensionality
   to 2 and then use k-means to find 2 clusters ... within each
   super-class"; mappings are permuted and "report the highest
   performance."
3. **NC still holds on coarse labels** (§4, Fig. 3): "we verify that Neural
   Collapse does happen, i.e. the representations converge to 5 clusters,
   and the class means form a Simplex ETF structure."
4. **Order in training** (§1; §4.1, Fig. 5): "The effect of input
   distribution emerges earlier in training, while the effect of labels
   appears at a later stage"; "at some point the representations naturally
   form 10 clusters according to the original CIFAR-10 labels ... Later in
   training (after 100% training accuracy is reached), the representations
   collapse into 5 or 20 clusters." In the distance matrix, "the central
   diagonal line – representing the samples in the same original classes –
   emerges earlier in training."
5. **Persistence** (§1, §4.2): "this phenomenon persists even after a very
   long training period (e.g. 1,000 epochs)".
6. **Fine labels** (§6, Fig. 14): with each class split at random into two
   labels, "at the early stage of training ... the last-layer
   representations are converging towards 10 clusters instead of 20. At the
   end of training, this 10-class relationship is still preserved, although
   with a lighter color."
7. **Semantics matter** (§5): on CIFAR-100's official super-classes the
   network "is not able to distinguish all sub-classes ... but can still
   produce separable representations if some sub-classes within a
   super-class are sufficiently different" ("bicycles" and "motorcycles"
   entangled; "crab" and "lobster" mixed). Random super-classes separate
   "much better". App. B.1 reproduces this in a Gaussian mixture: fine
   structure "does require sufficient dissimilarity between its
   sub-classes".
8. **Not universal** (§8; App. H): "we observe the fine-grained structure
   for ResNet and DenseNet, but not for VGG"; VGG "only displays Neural
   Collapse, and can not distinguish different original classes within one
   super-class, even in an early stage of training."
9. **Theorem 7.1** (§7): for a one-hidden-layer net with fixed all-ones
   second layer, unhinged loss, four orthogonal-mean Gaussian clusters with
   coarse labels, under stated scalings, w.h.p. "for all i1, i2 ∈ C1 and
   i3 ∈ C2, ∥h(xi1) − h(xi2)∥2 ≪ ∥h(xi1) − h(xi3)∥2."
10. **Synthetic factors** (App. B): MSDR (between-sub-class over
    within-sub-class squared distance) grows with input and hidden
    dimension; "the weight decay rate does not have an impact on MSDR in
    this setting."

## Assumptions

- Main runs: ResNet-18, SGD, lr 0.1, weight decay 5×10⁻⁴, standard lr
  decay; 3×3 lr/wd sweep in the appendix (one setting did not reach low
  training error; two reached < 0.5% but not 0). No mention of seeds, of
  repeated runs, or of data augmentation.
- Coarsening is deterministic (y mod C̃) in the main text, random in App. F.
- NC measured with NC1 = (1/C) Tr(Σ_W Σ_B†) and NC2 as distance to a
  simplex ETF, on the coarse labels.
- CLP is given the number of original classes and picks the best label
  permutation on the test set (mild optimism, 2⁵ mappings).
- Theory: orthogonal means, fixed second layer, unhinged loss, symmetric
  smoothed ReLU, d^{1/3} ≫ n; a toy setting, not the NC regime of the
  experiments.

## Concepts

Neural collapse (NC1 variability collapse, NC2 simplex ETF); Coarse/Fine
CIFAR-10; super-class and sub-class; class distance matrix; Cluster-and-
Linear-Probe (CLP); Mean Squared Distance Ratio (MSDR); unconstrained
feature / layer-peeled model (the authors' foil: it "completely ignores the
role of the input data"); Sohoni et al.'s subclass separation (contrasted:
here no performance gap between sub-classes is needed).

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

LIT-2957's summary ("Networks trained on coarse labels keep fine-grained
structure in their representations, recoverable even where neural collapse
appears complete") and CLAIM-800 §6 ("representations trained on coarse
labels keep finer structure recoverable by clustering") are **accurate**.
Two qualifications the citing entries do not carry: (i) the result is
architecture-dependent (absent in VGG-18) and semantics-dependent (weak for
similar sub-classes); (ii) "recoverable by clustering" used t-SNE plus
k-means with the number of classes known, followed by a *linear* probe, so
the fine structure is shown linearly decodable, not merely present. The
venue (ICML 2023) and authors are right.

## Bearing on named codes

- **CLAIM-800** (occupancy is not a measure of learning): **supports**, at
  §6 and §4. (a) Coarse NC1 → 0 while fine classes stay ~93% recoverable
  (Key results 1–3) is a concrete case where a collapse-type geometric
  measure rises/saturates while the information it seems to erase
  persists, the claim's reason for measuring decodability level by level.
  (b) **Qualifies the direction of §6's prediction**: under coarse
  supervision the fine (input-driven) clusters appear *before* the
  label-driven merging (Key result 4). That is an order in the distance
  geometry, not a decodability order, so it does not contradict the
  coarse-to-fine signature as defined, but it is a case in which a
  geometric order and the supervised hierarchy run opposite, which a
  filer may want beside §5. The paper measures no level-wise information
  over time (CLP is reported at the end of training in the main text;
  per-epoch only for some appendix settings), so it does not test the
  chain-rule diagnostic. It speaks to "present vs accessible to restricted
  decoders" only by showing a linear probe suffices for ResNet.
- **QUESTION-217** (refinement, relevance, acquisition orders): **background
  / leaves open**, with Key result 4 as a data point: training labels set
  the relevance order (coarse), but the representation refines by input
  structure first.
- **QUESTION-512** (entailment cones and progressive learning): background
  through CLAIM-800; the paper has no cones.
- **TERM-tmpf5ymd** ("bulk"): **background only.** The paper never uses
  "bulk" or any spectral language (one PCA plot). Read in the term's
  senses: the fine-class information sits in "the small amount of
  remaining variation", i.e. not in the variance-dominant (by-weight)
  directions, which are the coarse ETF.
- **CLAIM-tmp2r6ur** (owner's truncated band): **weak parallel, untested.**
  The top directions of the representation (the C̃−1 ETF directions of the
  coarse means) carry the coarse structure; the discriminating fine-class
  signal lives in low-variance residual variation. That fits "top = common
  / coarse, lower = discriminating" in shape, but the paper does no
  spectral decomposition, has no low-signal floor, and the "top"
  directions here are the *most* discriminating for the trained task. Not
  evidence for the claim; at most an illustration of where a finer
  distinction can live.

## Limitations

Single runs, no seeds or error bars stated; CLP uses t-SNE (nonlinear,
stochastic) and test-set selection of the label mapping; results only for
CIFAR-scale vision; architecture-dependent (VGG fails) with no explanation
("The reason why VGG is abnormal requires further exploration"); the theory
is a toy with fixed second layer, orthogonal means and unhinged loss, and
does not reach the terminal phase of training; App. H's text swaps the
labels of Fig. 4(a)/(b) (it calls the NC case 4(a)), a typo, not a
substantive error.

## Candidate THEORY

"In ResNet-18 and DenseNet-121 trained with SGD on CIFAR-10 whose labels
were merged into five two-class super-classes, last-layer features meet
neural collapse on the coarse labels (NC1 and NC2 toward zero) yet keep the
original classes as separate sub-clusters, recoverable without labels well
enough that a linear probe on the recovered labels reaches about 93% on the
original ten-class test set; these sub-clusters form before the
label-driven merging and persist to 1,000 epochs, are weaker when merged
sub-classes are semantically similar, and do not appear in VGG-18."

promote_when: an independent replication, with several seeds, on CIFAR-10
coarsened to five super-classes with a ResNet trained to zero error,
reports NC1 near zero on the coarse labels and original-class accuracy
from label-free clustering plus a linear probe within a few points of
standard training; and a VGG run reports no such sub-clusters.
