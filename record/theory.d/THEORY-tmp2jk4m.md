# THEORY-tmp2jk4m: Tarsney argues that the chance an intervention's effect is nullified acts as an exponential discount, so that under expectational utilitarianism and deliberately pessimistic inputs existential-risk reduction beats a strong global-health benchmark only if nullifying events are rare, and its robust win rests, on some views, on probabilities of order 10^-18 or less

## The account

Tarsney grants the longtermist "expectational utilitarianism": precise
probabilism, total welfarist consequentialism and expected value
maximization (p. 4). He then asks whether a $1 million longtermist
intervention, research on pandemic risk, beats a neartermist benchmark worth
10,000 QALYs (one "valon").

The intervention's effect lasts until an exogenous nullifying event arrives
at a constant rate r. That rate acts like a discount, and an exponential
discount eventually overwhelms value that grows only polynomially as
settlement spreads. So the size of the future does not by itself decide the
comparison: EV(L) is "nearly (inverse) quartic in some ranges of r, so that
an order-of-magnitude decrease in r corresponds to a four-order-of-magnitude
increase in EV(L)" (p. 25).

## Evidence

- With p = 2 × 10^-14 and settlement at 0.1c, the longtermist option wins in
  the cubic growth model only if r is below about 0.000135 per year (Table
  1); confined to Earth, only below about 1.2 × 10^-8 per year (Table 2).
- Small credences in optimistic scenarios (cubic growth, low r, fast
  settlement, Dyson-sphere values) raise the minimum EV(L) to about 1.62 ×
  10^11 valons, and uncertainty "will tend to strengthen rather than weaken
  the case for longtermism" (p. 30).
- On some views the winning component "depends on a conjunction of
  improbable assumptions with joint probability on the order of (say) 10^-18
  or less" (p. 32).
- His conclusions: "We should be 'longtermists' on the scale of thousands or
  millions or years, rather than billions or trillions of years" (p. 34),
  and the challenge is "serious" but "not fatal" (p. 34).

The inputs are guesses chosen to be conservative, the credence floors are
"unavoidably subjective" (p. 29), and only precise probabilism and
persistent-difference strategies are modelled ([NOTE-1247](../notes.d/NOTE-1247.md), Limitations).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md) names this work under one of the lines that keep it open:
"Whether longtermism can guide action. Tarsney ([LIT-1753](../literature.d/LIT-1753.md)) on how fast our
predictable influence decays". Its note of 2026-10-29 says "Tarsney calls
the epistemic challenge 'not fatal'". The reading bears on the impersonal
ground's action-guidance without answering what is owed.

The reading also offers a confound for the question's psychological test
([NOTE-1247](../notes.d/NOTE-1247.md), Bearing on the record): concern that fades with time may track a
rational discount for the hazard that an effect is undone, with no reference
to the self.
