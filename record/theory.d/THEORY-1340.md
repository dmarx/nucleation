# THEORY-1340: Juarrero reads the delayed-response studies of Churchland, Shenoy and colleagues and of Mante, Sussillo and colleagues, as she reports them, as showing that training forms a task-defined, low-dimensional subregion of motor, premotor and prefrontal population activity (a preparatory subspace whose end state predicts reaction time and movement; an axis of choice organized as a line attractor) that acts as top-down governing constraints on a monkey's delayed response while the trial's cue and evidence select within it, and suggests that the axis of choice might be considered the neural correlate of a prior intention and the selection vector, found in a simulation, that of a proximate intention

## Source

Juarrero (2023), [LIT-1995](../literature.d/LIT-1995.md), ch. 15, pp. 223–232; read in [NOTE-1532](../notes.d/NOTE-1532.md).
The chapter reports Churchland, Cunningham et al. 2012, Shenoy, Kaufman et
al. 2011, Kaufman, Churchland et al. 2014 and Mante, Sussillo et al. 2013;
none of these is held or read here.

## What was actually shown

**As the chapter reports the studies.** Single neurons in the early
preparatory period are heterogeneous and uncorrelated; dimensionality
reduction finds a low-dimensional subregion whose end state predicts
reaction time and movement, with rotational population dynamics in the same
direction across conditions (pp. 225–227); Churchland's recordings were from "motor and premotor
cortexes" (p. 224). In the color/motion task, PFC
activity lines up along an "axis of choice", a line attractor, before the
dots appear; the sensory axes are transient while the axis of choice
persists until the response; and a "selection vector" makes the same input
move the state along the attractor in one context and not the other, which
Mante et al. call "context-dependent relaxation" (pp. 227–232). The
selection mechanism is from a simulation (p. 230).

**The book's reading.** The subspace is "top-down governing constraints for
the correct response" (p. 230); its persistence "can be interpreted as the
standing cause of the response" (p. 230); the axis of choice "might be
considered the neural correlate of a prior intention" (pp. 229–230) and the
selection vector that of "a proximate intention" (p. 231); behavior "was
constrained top down, according to the relevant, task-defined contextual
cue" (p. 228).

What could have come out otherwise: the studies could have found no
low-dimensional structure, or structure that did not predict behavior. They
found both. Nothing reported could have shown the subspace not to
constrain, since nothing reported intervenes on it.

## What this does not say

- **That the pattern causes the behavior over and above its neurons.** The
  book holds that governing constraints are "not spatiotemporally other
  than" their components (p. 81), so the reading is compatible with a
  bottom-up mechanism summarized in low dimension.
- **That this is control in [CLAIM-533](../claims.d/CLAIM-533.md)'s sense.** It is confinement of
  the state to a task subregion plus context-gated integration of evidence;
  nothing about sensitivity, amplification or noise.
- **That the boundary of the system matters** in [THEORY-1314](THEORY-1314.md)'s sense;
  the studies are of task context, not of a boundary between brain and
  surroundings.
- **What the primary papers say.** Read only through the chapter.
