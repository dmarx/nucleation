# LIT-tmptq6ih: Against Good Caring? Meeting Immediate and Distant Needs Democratically

Tronto, J. C. (2014). Against Good Caring? Meeting Immediate and Distant Needs Democratically. Slides, UvH conference "A Meaningful Life in a Just Society", 30 January 2014. https://ethicsofcare.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Tronto-uvh-final.pdf

## Key takeaways

- **Democracy as allocation**: "Democracy as the allocation of caring
  responsibilities in a political community and the method of giving
  equal voice to all members of the community in making these
  allocations".
- **Two misallocations**: exclusion, "to exclude those you want to saddle
  with the most difficult tasks"; and exemption, "absent oneself from
  responsibilities because one has more important duties: protection,
  production, taking care of one's own".
- **Neoliberal care**: "Good care = personal responsibility + market";
  "If you don't have enough resources to care well, it is your own fault".

## Standing in the record

Registered as a read neighbour of Caring Democracy ([LIT-2669](LIT-2669.md)) and,
more weakly, Moral Boundaries ([LIT-2708](LIT-2708.md)). For Caring Democracy it
stands in for the political definition, in Tronto's words, and for
exemption, the mechanism by which the privileged avoid care, in outline.
For Moral Boundaries it carries only the definition of care it quotes
(Fisher and Tronto, cited as Tronto 1993, 103). Slides: claims are
telegraphic and many slides quote others.
