# LIT-2777: Attributability, Answerability, and Accountability: Toward a Wider Theory of Moral Responsibility

David Shoemaker (2011). "Attributability, Answerability, and Accountability: Toward a Wider Theory of Moral Responsibility". *Ethics* 121(3): 602–632. DOI-10.1086/659003.

## Note of 2026-11-18: the reading pass

Not read; Unavailable (status note). **Shoemaker (2019)**, [LIT-2766](LIT-2766.md),
stands in for the theory in its later form: three "radically independent"
types, attributability (quality of character), answerability (quality of
judgment, being "able in principle to answer the question, “Why did you do
that?”") and accountability (quality of regard, answered by gratitude and
anger). It confirms that answerability is Shoemaker's third type: other
pluralists, Watson's 2004 essays among them, lack "an independent notion of
answerability". It does not show the 2011 arguments.

**The exchange's characterization is partly faithful.** A110 credits the
split to "Gary Watson and David Shoemaker" jointly, which is wrong, as
already noted. Its "closer relationship to blame" holds for anger, which
belongs to accountability; but Shoemaker also allows blame "in
answerability terms", so accountability is tied to one kind of blame, not
blame as such. Shoemaker's answerability, a capacity of the agent, is not
the directed answerability of [TERM-126](../terms.d/TERM-126.md).

## Neighbour in the record

Smith (2012), [LIT-2658](LIT-2658.md), read ([NOTE-2008](../notes.d/NOTE-2008.md)), answers this paper: it argues for a unified account of the three faces this paper separates.
