# THEORY-tmp7vt9y: Syropoulos et al. report that felt responsibility to future generations is endorsed by most people in Europe and the US, is nearly unrelated to ideology and demographics and half as tied to conservatism as responsibility for climate, predicts self-reported proenvironmentalism, and was raised a little by each of five brief interventions, most by a letter to a person born today and least by a longtermist text

## The account

Responsibility to future generations (RFG) is offered as a moral reframing
of climate change: widely held, little tied to politics, normally not
salient. It correlates strongly with responsibility to reduce one's own
climate contribution (RCC), r = .58 to .76, but behaves differently, and one
manipulation moved RFG without moving RCC, which the authors take to show
"two separate mechanisms, despite their high correlation" (p. 32).

## Evidence

- Study 1 (Eurobarometers, N = 109,513): majority agreement in 33 of 34
  countries; ideology r = .03.
- Studies 2A–3B: RFG endorsed above RCC (d = 0.26), the gap driven by
  Republicans and Independents; across N = 3,558, RFG with conservatism r =
  −.21, RCC r = −.45.
- Study 3A: RFG predicted self-reported proenvironmental behaviour, β = .48
  (.41 with covariates).
- Study 4 (preregistered, N = 3,175), effects on RFG against control: letter
  b = .37 (d = .29), past generations' sacrifice .29, legacy .27, video .19,
  longtermist excerpt .18. Only the letter moved donations; the sacrifice
  task did not move RCC.

Single items carry RFG in the early studies, the two responsibilities are
never entered in one regression, and the discussion overstates two results
the results section reports more weakly ([NOTE-1293](../notes.d/NOTE-1293.md), Limitations).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md)'s note of 2026-10-29 cites the reading under "A fourth ground:
reciprocity": "Syropoulos et al. found that reflecting on past generations'
sacrifices raised felt responsibility to future generations about as much as
a legacy prompt did. A letter to one person born today raised it most, and
an impersonal longtermist text least." It adds that "Nothing in the study
measures how far the self extends, so it does not run the thesis's test."
The reading bears on what predicts concern for future generations.
