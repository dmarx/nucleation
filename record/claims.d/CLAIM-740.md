# CLAIM-740: For a party affected by a decision, explanation (understanding why it was reached), contestation (challenging it or the principles behind it) and interruption (suspending its execution while the challenge is considered) are separate entitlements, and each without the next fails in its own way: explanation without contestation is transparency without accountability, contestation without interruption a right that arrives too late, and interruption without reasons an arbitrary veto

## The claim

A23 §6 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): an
applicant may receive "a perfectly clear explanation" of a loan denial
"Yet you may still have no practical means to change the result." "So I
would distinguish three levels of agency":

| Level | What the person is entitled to do |
|---|---|
| **Explanation** | Understand why a decision was reached |
| **Contestation** | Challenge the decision or the principles behind it |
| **Interruption** | Prevent or suspend execution while the challenge is considered |

"These are not equivalent." Each pair fails differently, as the title
says, and "A robust process needs an appropriate combination."

**Two further splits A makes.**

- *Recourse is not contestation* (A19 §7). Recourse "concerns how someone
  can change their circumstances to obtain a better outcome"; contestation
  "their ability to challenge the legitimacy or correctness of the
  decision itself." Offering only recourse makes the institution's choices
  the environment: "**The institution treats its own normative choices as
  background conditions while requiring the affected person to adapt to
  them.**" Contestation in the claim includes the principles, not only the
  case.
- *Giving an account is not answering* (A23 §6). "The ability to give an
  account and the obligation to *answer to someone* are different things.
  The second involves a relationship of authority." The question is
  "**Can someone demand that the system justify itself, and what power
  does that demand have over what happens next?**"

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)** (Active) holds what a discrepancy needs to change what an
  organization does: legibility, volitional efficacy, correctability. That
  is the organization's side. This is the affected party's: explanation
  gives it legibility of the decision, contestation a demand that can have
  efficacy, interruption a hold on the deadline correctability works
  against. The claim rests on [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) for the separation of the three
  achievements and adds that, from outside, they are three entitlements
  that can be granted separately.
- **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)** names a second-order duty of answerability; the claim's
  distinction between giving an account and answering is what that duty
  requires of contestation.
- From the same exchange: [TERM-130](../terms.d/TERM-130.md) (protected contestability),
  [TERM-126](../terms.d/TERM-126.md) (answerability), [CLAIM-739](CLAIM-739.md) (answerability is directed
  and role-specific), and [CLAIM-731](CLAIM-731.md), on what contestation costs.
- The interruption right is the owner's U11 ([CLAIM-710](CLAIM-710.md)) in the form of
  an entitlement: a point at which the process stays open.
- **[CLAIM-705](CLAIM-705.md)**, from the first share of the same exchange, is distinct
  and demonstrates one of this claim's failures: when challenges arrive
  faster than they are resolved, the formal right to contest stays with
  everyone while its preventive effect cannot, which is contestation
  without interruption arriving too late. The two complement each other.
  [TERM-125](../terms.d/TERM-125.md)'s intervention opportunity is a property of a step in a process;
  the interruption here is an entitlement of the affected party, which a
  step may or may not carry. The first filing mapped A23 §6 to [CLAIM-686](CLAIM-686.md)
  and [CLAIM-410](CLAIM-410.md) and did not file it.

## What it does not say

- Not that every decision needs all three. A23 §9: "Not every
  consequential decision needs to be delayed until every conceivable
  objection has been addressed." How much interruption a decision warrants
  rises with how hard it is to reverse.
- Not that explanation is worthless without the others. It is necessary to
  informed contestation and to reasoned interruption.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Cerrato, Sahlgren and Heilmann, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1941 — new theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Cerrato, Sahlgren and Heilmann 2026, read ([NOTE-1996](../notes.d/NOTE-1996.md), [THEORY-1941](../theory.d/THEORY-1941.md)): it corroborates the claim's first failure, explanation without contestation, independently ([THEORY-1941](../theory.d/THEORY-1941.md) carries `corroborates`). In their experiments the deployer's upstream choices "have as much or more impact on the generated counterfactuals as the specifics of the generating method" (abstract), and counterfactuals "preclude consideration of whether the decision-maker ought to have acted differently". So "it may be impossible for subjects to reverse their decisions through contesting such choices absent appropriate transparency" (Implications for Recourse). This is a sharper form than the claim's: the explanation points the subject at the wrong target, their own inputs. The paper does not set recourse against contestation as rights (it folds contestation into relational recourse) and says nothing on interruption. A preprint, one domain, two methods. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Citron, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1947 — new theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Citron 2008, read ([NOTE-2003](../notes.d/NOTE-2003.md), [THEORY-1947](../theory.d/THEORY-1947.md)): it corroborates the claim independently ([THEORY-1947](../theory.d/THEORY-1947.md) carries `corroborates`). Notice (reasons, through audit trails), the hearing and process before deprivation are distinct safeguards in her account and fail differently: inadequate notice devalues hearings (p. 1254); hearings exist but are hollowed by automation bias, which "effectively turns a computer program's suggested answer into a trusted final decision" (p. 1272), and by the cost of decoding the system; and "pre-deprivation process is surely possible" because "agency officials ultimately control the administration of automated decision systems" (p. 1288), the ground for the claim's third entitlement. One difference: Citron treats explanation as a precondition of contestation rather than an entitlement that can be had alone. No status changes.
