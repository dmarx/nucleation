# LIT-1196: Self-generated validity and other effects of measurement on belief, attitude, intention, and behavior

Feldman, J. M. & Lynch, J. G. (1988). *Journal of Applied Psychology* 73(3):421–435. doi:10.1037/0021-9010.73.3.421.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-957](../notes.d/NOTE-957.md).*

- **Measurement creates cognitions.** "If the researcher asks a question
  about attitude (or belief, intention, etc.), and no such cognition
  already exists, the measure creates one" (p. 431). Evidence cited: Fazio,
  Lenn and Effrein (1984), where filling in an attitude measure made later
  evaluative answers faster, read as the formation of "retrievable
  attitudes" (p. 423).
- **The created answer is carried forward.** "Once the answer has been
  computed, it can be retrieved to answer subsequent questions" (p. 422),
  and it can drive behaviour: the survey brings "normally automatic
  behavior under conscious control" (p. 423).
- **Which earlier answer is used.** Perceived diagnosticity (asymmetric,
  itself a judgement), accessibility (time, interference, rehearsal) and
  the accessibility of more diagnostic alternatives (pp. 424–430).
  Reanalysed data: Schuman and Ludwig's order effects, 90% against 66% and
  36% against 73% (p. 425).
- **Moderators.** Direct experience, expertise and trait involvement,
  which supply formed and rehearsed judgements; experts lose the
  protection when choosing between non-comparable options (p. 430).
- **Scope.** Theoretical synthesis, "necessarily ... speculative" by its
  own account (p. 432). Mechanisms all classical. The authors hold that
  measurement is one case of a general fact: "all real-world experiences
  with an object have the potential to shape subsequent responses to it"
  (p. 431).

## Why this is here

Registered for [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md) as the empirical literature the claim
lacked. The reading confirms that the paper states the claim's diachronic
form, an earlier answer becoming input to a later one, and that the arrow
it draws runs from the measurement context into the interpreter's state,
the route [TERM-039](../terms.d/TERM-039.md) and [CLAIM-127](../claims.d/CLAIM-127.md) leave open. It also proposes a sequential,
diagnosticity-based test that would separate that route from direct
influence on the response (p. 425).

Three qualifications from the reading. The paper reports no data of its
own; its support is a synthesis of others' studies. The created state it
cares most about is one that shapes behaviour, so it does not support the
claim's exclusion of action from the loop, and it gives judgement no
special place among the experiences that shape later responses. And its
moderators are not evidence routed around the interpretation, as
[CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)'s anchor is: they are prior, rehearsed judgements, formed,
on the paper's account, through direct experience and environments with
strong outcome contingencies. That bears on [CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md) and
[QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md) as much as on [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md). It carries no instruction
for machine-learning practice.
