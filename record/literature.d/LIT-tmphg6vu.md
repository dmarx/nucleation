# LIT-tmphg6vu: Looking Like a State: Techniques of Persistent Failure in State Capability for Implementation

Pritchett, L., Woolcock, M. & Andrews, M. (2013). Looking Like a State: Techniques of Persistent Failure in State Capability for Implementation. *Journal of Development Studies* 49(1): 1–18. doi:10.1080/00220388.2012.709614. Read as CID Working Paper No. 239 (June 2012; manuscript dated 10 April 2012).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpss5e0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpss5e0.md). Page numbers are the working paper's, not the journal's.*

- **Centuries, at the historical pace.** "For the average of the countries
  in 2008 in the bottom 15 it would take 672 years" (p. 10); on ICRG data
  "the median rate of country improvement for both indicators is zero"
  (p. 11). The method assumes zero capability at independence, which can only
  overstate the historical rate: "our calculation biases the rates
  against our argument" (p. 10).
- **Mimicry.** "the ability of organizations to sustain legitimacy through
  the imitation of the forms of modern institutions but without
  functionality" (p. 14).
- **Premature load bearing.** "pushing too hard too soon, thereby creating
  a situation we call pre-mature load bearing in which stresses exceed
  capability" (p. 14).
- **Cocooning.** Parallel donor systems succeed and do not scale: "one can
  have long strings of demonstrably successful projects while a sector
  itself never improves" (p. 19).
- **What sets the equilibrium.** Whether the system will "require,
  recognize and reward novelty" (p. 16).

## Standing in the record

The companion to Andrews, Pritchett & Woolcock on PDIA ([LIT-tmptn3yr](LIT-tmptn3yr.md)),
which cites this paper for the scale of the trap. It is the batch's one
