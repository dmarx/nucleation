# CLAIM-1073: Questioning and answering are exchanged roles

## The claim

A64b §3, set against the employee and manager of A54 ([CASE-386](../cases.d/CASE-386.md)): "Within a collective, perspectives can exchange roles. One perspective proposes a question. Another challenges its presuppositions. A third develops evidence. A fourth recognizes a problem with the evaluative standard." "So we shouldn't imagine two permanent teams called Queries and Answers." "We have a governed ecology in which questioning and answering are dynamically allocated roles." The relations among them can be cooperative, competitive, investigative or about governing the process, and their differences "can be what makes the question–answer game productive".

## Why it matters

The owner's game ([CLAIM-1031](CLAIM-1031.md)) was developed in A50 as a robust interrogation game with a questioner and a respondent, and [CLAIM-1032](CLAIM-1032.md) shows when that is a game at all. [QUESTION-524](../questions.d/QUESTION-524.md) asks for the query side's payoff and the equilibrium concept where the sides are not opponents. This claim says that, for a collective, the question presupposes too much: the sides are positions a perspective occupies for a move, so a model of it needs role assignment as part of the state (A64b's governance G_t), not two fixed players with payoffs.

## Standing

Proposed, in A's voice. It does not answer [QUESTION-524](../questions.d/QUESTION-524.md) for the two-sided cases A50 had in view (a regulator and an industry), where the sides are distinct.
