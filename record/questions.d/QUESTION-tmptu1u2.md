# QUESTION-tmptu1u2: Does an inquiry need the record of its own development to know its object, as Simondon's method and its organization-studies readers hold, or only to revise its conclusions, which is what the record has shown?

## Why it is a question

Two readings filed on 2026-10-31 make a methodological demand that this
record already meets in form, for reasons it has not yet examined.

- **Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy** (LIT-tmp3mudg, p. 1366): "researchers can
  only investigate individuation processes by/while individuating into
  others", and the research process "consists in following the being in
  its genesis, in accomplishing the genesis of thought at the same time as
  the genesis of the object" (Simondon 2020, p. 14, LIT-1648, unread
  here). So researchers "should ... find ways to express our own
  individuation in relation to the individuation of others in our work".
  The authors leave open what methods would do this.
- **Bencherki and Iliadis** (LIT-tmpwspny, proposition 4, ms. pp. 21–22):
  "Knowledge of an entity ... should focus on its individuation process
  and should reflect on the way that knowledge itself has individuated."
  The first author describes his own individuation through the fieldwork
  (NOTE-tmp62e4t).

The record keeps its own development beside its findings: the workbench
journal, versions with history notes, supersession, and grammar revisions
taken by decision in the pull requests that needed them (CASE-178). It
has one demonstrated reason for doing so. Two inquiries can agree on
every current conclusion and still call for different revisions
(CLAIM-294), so the history fixes revisions that the conclusions do not
(QUESTION-289, Answered). And it has a reason pointing the other way:
a stabilized specification is stored inquiry, which later inquiry uses
without re-running the history that produced it (CLAIM-475).

The process theorists' demand is stronger. It is that knowing the object,
and not only revising what is believed about it, needs the knower's own
genesis. The record has no claim either way. If knowing the record's
objects did need the record's history, that would be a consequence of
CLAIM-476's constitutive reading that does not follow from CLAIM-469's
operational facts, which is the consequence CLAIM-476's first defeater
says has not been shown.

## What would count as an answer

- **For the strong demand.** Two inquiries with the same current
  conclusions, the same specification (TERM-095) and the same revision
  behaviour, so that CLAIM-294's difference is controlled for, that
  differ in what they can determine about their object: which questions
  are askable (TERM-092), or which distinctions among its objects are
  determinate. The record's own case would be the two-apparatus
  experiment CLAIM-476 proposes, run so that one arm keeps its history
  and the other only its current state.
- **For the narrow answer.** A showing that every task in a stated family
  that a history-keeping inquiry does better is a revision task, so that
  the history adds nothing once the current specification is fixed
  (CLAIM-475's amortization), which would leave the demand warranted
  only by revision.
- **Dissolution.** A showing that the demand is about the object's
  genesis and not the inquiry's: in Simondon an individual is a partial
  resolution of a field, so to know it is to know how it formed. That is
  history of the object. The inquiry's own history would then matter only
  where the inquiry takes part in forming its object, which for this
  record is CLAIM-476's question and not a general one.

What cannot settle it: further statements of the demand. Neither reading
argues for it beyond quotation, and the parent's own reanalysis of
someone else's video is not done in the way it asks (p. 1366: the event
"was external to us").
