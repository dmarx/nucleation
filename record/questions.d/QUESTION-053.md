# QUESTION-053: Does organizational affect need a group subject, a state of the group not reducible to its members' states, or only the synchronized convergence of members' affect?

## Why it is a question

- [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md): a group "can itself be in an emotional state that is not its
  members' states in aggregation", when its routines integrate the
  capacities an emotion requires into one action-oriented representation.
  It adds that "no actual case has yet been shown".
- [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md): a collective emotion "is the synchronous convergence of
  individuals' affective responses to one event or object", and "it needs
  no group subject".

[CLAIM-039](../claims.d/CLAIM-039.md), Deferred with its line, says affective states can be properties
of organized systems, characterized functionally. On [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md) that claim
is about convergence among members. On [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md) it is about a state of
the system. The two predict different cases.

What would count as an answer: a case where convergence holds and
integration fails, or the reverse. [CASE-050](../cases.d/CASE-050.md), the all-hands as a
synchronization mechanism, is a candidate to extend: does the event
produce only convergence, or a coordinated, action-oriented state of the
firm?

## Note of 2026-10-12: typed partial answers

[THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md) (a group subject, possibly) and [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md) (synchronized convergence, no group subject) now carry `answered_by` edges. They are the two sides this question names.

## Note of 2026-10-12: the convergence side, evidenced

[THEORY-123](../theory.d/THEORY-123.md) (Barsade) now carries an `answered_by` edge. Mood convergence is shown experimentally, and the paper calls it "a group phenomenon". That is evidence for the convergence side with no group subject. [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md), already on this question, extends it.

## Note of 2026-10-14: a third option, not the convergence side

[LIT-933](../literature.d/LIT-933.md) (Nowak et al.) was registered here as stating the synchronized-convergence option. Read in [NOTE-768](../notes.d/NOTE-768.md), it does not. Its synchronization is any binding of dynamics, including compensatory dynamics and complementary roles, not [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md)'s synchronous convergence. Its functional unit, a coordinated configuration that performs a task, is neither aggregate convergence nor a group subject. That is a third option this question does not list. The paper never asks whether a functional unit is a subject of states, and it offers no case, so it answers neither side.

## Note of 2026-10-18: integration without a subject, and a test

The exchange of 2026-10-17 bears on this question twice, without citing it.

A27 §3 took [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md)'s side for the state: "A candidate collective emotion
therefore has to be individuated through the organization's **integrated
regulatory pattern**, not by averaging people's feelings", and "The
corporation's managers might feel calm while the corporation exhibits an
increasingly threat-avoidant policy." It then denied that the state needs a
subject to belong to: "There may be **no phenomenological illusion inside the
collective at all**." That separates the two things this question's first
option joins, a state of the group and a group subject. [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md), filed
from U26–U27, answers in part on that line: the integration can be the
group's while the single owner it is attributed to is a construction.

A29's Test B, "Distributed integration versus a common alarm", is a design for
the case this question asks for: "Compare a system whose processes mutually
compensate for disturbances against one whose independent processes merely
receive the same centralized warning." Its prediction: "A measure of
organizational binding should distinguish genuine interdependence from a
common signal with no meaningful reciprocal regulation." A common alarm is
close to [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md)'s convergence on one event; mutual compensation is close
to [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md)'s integration, and to [NOTE-768](../notes.d/NOTE-768.md)'s third option, a coordinated
configuration.

## Note of 2026-10-31: differential attunement, a further option with a test of its own

Massumi ([LIT-tmp8hcfz](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8hcfz.md), read in [NOTE-tmpyf5pn](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyf5pn.md); [THEORY-tmpywwei](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpywwei.md)) gives an
answer that is neither of this question's two options. It is close to
the third option of the note of 2026-10-14 (Nowak et al.), coordination
through difference, but it is attunement to one event and not a
configuration that performs a task.

- **Not convergence.** Bodies cued by one event "will have been attuned –
  differentially – to the same interruptive commotion". "There is no
  sameness of affect. There is affective difference in the same event"
  (p. 6). He sets this against "imitation or contagion". [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md)'s
  synchronous convergence is what he denies, except where "other factors
  must have been active to pre-channel tendencies" (p. 6).
- **Not a group subject.** Affect is not "intersubjective" if that means
  starting from "subjects that are preconstituted" (p. 3), and it is
  "not a content that can be transported from one event to another" (p.
  9). The group's affect is the one event, differently taken up, and no
  subject has it.

It makes a prediction the two options do not. Members cued by one event
respond in systematically different but coordinated ways, "responding
differently together" (p. 6). Convergence predicts that their states
become alike. That is the test this question asks for, a case where the
options come apart. [THEORY-tmpywwei](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpywwei.md)'s promote_when states it. The
nearest held account is [CLAIM-437](../claims.d/CLAIM-437.md). There, one change of state is ranked
by each perspective against its own concerns, so valence differs by
perspective within one event. That is the same structure on the record's
side, an analogy and not a shared mechanism.

The parent paper does not keep to this. Its "indignation she can no
longer contain" ([LIT-tmp3mudg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3mudg.md), p. 1354) "in-forms" her colleagues
([THEORY-tmpywwei](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpywwei.md), "What this does not say"). That example is
convergence.
