# THEORY-1440: Sobczak reports, from work in the Dietz group, that 3D DNA origami folds cooperatively within a structure-specific window of about 5 °C and can be folded at a constant temperature within it, while folded objects held for two hours above the window unfold only 5–10 °C higher and within seconds, against minutes to hours for folding, and that the brick forms nothing in 48 hours 6 °C above its best folding temperature; the thesis does not pair persistence and non-formation at one temperature

## Source

Sobczak (2018), [LIT-2138](../literature.d/LIT-2138.md); read in [NOTE-1644](../notes.d/NOTE-1644.md). A stand-in for
Sobczak et al. (2012), [LIT-2131](../literature.d/LIT-2131.md), unread.

## What was actually shown

- **The window.** In a slow anneal at 1 °C every three hours, gel and
  SYBR Green assays put folding of a brick, a plate and a gear within a
  few degrees; predicted staple and domain melting temperatures are far
  broader, hence cooperativity (§3.3).
- **Constant temperature.** The brick mostly folds within 2 h at 50 °C;
  at 56 °C none folded in 48 h. A brief denaturation first is still
  needed (§3.3).
- **Hysteresis and speed.** Unfolding comes 5–10 °C above folding and
  takes seconds; folding takes minutes to hours (§§3.3, 3.5). Folded
  structures were held for two hours at jump temperatures to measure it
  (p. 22).
- **Design dependence.** Three routings of one shape differ in folding
  time by more than 100-fold and fold at different temperatures (§3.7).

Each could have come out otherwise: a broad transition, no isothermal
folding, coincident folding and melting, or routing without effect.

## What this does not say

- That a folded object persists and an unfolded one fails to form at one
  named temperature: the two are measured separately, on two-hour and
  48-hour scales.
- That the band holds for every origami: the objects are few, mostly one
  brick family, and magnesium was not varied in the sections read.
- That these numbers are the 2012 paper's. The thesis may report later
  objects; the 2012 abstract calls unfolding equilibrium, the thesis
  calls it less out of equilibrium than folding.
- That folding is trapped in particular states: the thesis's model puts
  it down to many accessible incorrect states.
