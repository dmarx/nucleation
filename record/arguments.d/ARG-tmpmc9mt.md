# ARG-tmpmc9mt: Fineman's vulnerability account shows that a whole's responsibilities to its parts grow as it integrates them

## Which sense of "whole"

[TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) sense 3. Fineman's whole is the state and its institutions, its
parts are persons. [CLAIM-tmpwn3wr](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpwn3wr.md)'s integration is sense 1, but its
consequent reaches only wholes that can bear responsibility and parts with
standing (its "Which sense" section). This argument tests the claim on
the one kind of whole the reading is about.

## The inference

1. [THEORY-tmpk4d9t](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpk4d9t.md), the reading of Fineman in [NOTE-tmpnpbzl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpnpbzl.md): "Vulnerability
   is posited as the characteristic that positions us in relation to each
   other as human beings and also suggests a relationship of
   responsibility between state and individual" (p. 255); institutions
   that "distribute significant social goods ... should be monitored by
   the state" (p. 272); the state "has an obligation not to tolerate a
   system that unduly privileges any group of citizens over others"
   (p. 274).
2. So a whole that controls the conditions its members need bears a
   special responsibility toward them, grounded in their vulnerability.
3. So, the argument runs, the whole's responsibility grows as integration
   deepens, as [CLAIM-tmpwn3wr](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpwn3wr.md) says.

## Critical questions

- **Is the evidence what the claim predicts?** Only in part. The claim
  says responsibility grows with integration, through a vulnerability that
  integration produces ([CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)'s capability loss). Fineman's
  vulnerability is universal and constant, the condition of embodiment; it
  does not grow with what the state takes over. Step 3 does not follow
  from steps 1 and 2.
- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** Yes. A
  responsibility grounded in the state's constitutive and distributive
  role, owed whatever the degree of integration, is what her text gives;
  it is expected whether or not responsibility tracks integration.
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its rival?** It favours the rival
  the claim's Admission section names: responsibility tracks the whole's
  governance of the conditions of its parts' agency, not integration as
  such. That is A12 §10's governance clause, which the reading supports.
- **Is anything in the reading closer to the claim?** Her derivative
  dependency, socially imposed on carers (p. 264), is vulnerability
  produced by an arrangement. She grounds the state's share of its burden
  in benefit (p. 265), not in integration.

## Verdict

Rejected. The reading does not support [CLAIM-tmpwn3wr](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpwn3wr.md) as titled. It is
evidence for a governance-based ground, for sense-3 wholes, and is filed
as `bears_on` [QUESTION-tmp9bbpk](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp9bbpk.md) on that footing. The claim keeps its
status, Proposed: Fineman does not meet its defeat conditions either,
since she grounds a special responsibility.
