# LIT-757: Groupware and social dynamics: eight challenges for developers

Jonathan Grudin (1994), *Communications of the ACM* 37(1):92–105 — DOI-10.1145/175222.175230

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-835](../notes.d/NOTE-835.md).*

- **Eight challenges (Table 1, p. 97).** Disparity in work and benefit;
  critical mass and prisoner's dilemma; disruption of social processes;
  exception handling; unobtrusive accessibility; difficulty of evaluation;
  failure of intuition; the adoption process.
- **The disparity.** "Groupware applications often require additional
  work from individuals who do not perceive a direct benefit". Meeting
  schedulers need everyone's calendar and help the convener; calendars
  were kept by managers, not by individual contributors, so the feature
  "is rarely used".
- **Not enough to remove it.** "Even a net benefit with equal costs and
  benefits for all users will not guarantee groupware success", because of
  critical mass and freeloading.
- **An argument record refused.** A management group dropped an
  issue-based system because "the explicit record of opposing positions
  that the application would immortalize was politically unacceptable"
  (p. 97). The case is unsourced.
- **Email as the success.** "The primary beneficiary typically does a
  little more work": the sender types, the receiver reads at leisure.
- **Advice.** Add group features to applications already in use, provide
  direct benefits for all members, and beware applications that
  selectively benefit managers.
