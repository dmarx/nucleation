# NOTE-tmpufafo: Lanier (2000) — One Half a Manifesto

## Contribution

An insider's critique of an ideology of technology, separated from enthusiasm for digital tools.

## Key results

1. The dogma: "I sometimes call it 'cybernetic totalism'."
2. "I hope that readers will not think that I've sunk into some sort of glum rejection of digital technology."
3. Real computers "intrinsically resist many of our endeavors to improve them, in large part due to legacy and lock-in".
4. "The placement of one's circle of empathy is ultimately a matter of faith."
5. "I'll worry about the future of human culture more than I'll worry about the gadgets."

## Assumptions

Subjective experience is real and morally central; engineering experience is evidence against idealized models.

## Concepts

Cybernetic totalism; circle of empathy; software legacy and lock-in.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpbypt7**: a minor point of contact. Result 3 names lock-in in software without the mechanism of adapting infrastructure, competitors and expectations; it leaves the claim open.
- **CLAIM-030**: Lanier treats attributing a direction to technology as a whole as part of the dogma; an interpretive objection to that application, with no argument against CLAIM-030's thesis about organized wholes.
- **The exchange's** "criticize specific technological arrangements without abandoning the possibilities of technology itself" (A5) fits result 2. The LIT's "digital business models" is not in the essay.

## Limitations

Essay length and polemical; the book's later themes are not here.
