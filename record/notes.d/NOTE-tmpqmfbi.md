# NOTE-tmpqmfbi: Block — Inverted Earth

## Contribution

It moves the inversion from inside the head to the world. The classic
inverted spectrum asks us to believe a subject whose visual system was
tampered with; Inverted Earth needs no tampering that the subject could
notice, so the first-person report and physical supervenience both favour
sameness of qualia, while intentional content and long-arm function invert.
It also names a confusion on both sides of the debate: treating public
contents like *looking red* as qualitative.

## Key insight

Distinguish what an experience is about from what it is like. What it is
about is fixed by what normally causes it and what it is used on, which
can change without the brain changing; what it is like supervenes on the
brain. Put a person in a world where causes and words are both inverted,
and the first changes while the second stays put.

## Assumptions

- **Qualia realism** = "there are intrinsic mental features of our
  experience" (p. 53).
- **Two contents of experience** (Shoemaker; Peacocke): intentional
  (representational) and qualitative (sensational), with "looks the same" in
  two senses (p. 54).
- **Long-arm intentional content**: intentional content is functional, with
  real things as inputs and outputs (p. 58); Harman agrees (pp. 58–59).
- **Externalist shift of content** with long embedding (Burge, Putnam;
  Davies's Margaret Thatcher analogy, p. 64).
- **Supervenience** of qualitative content on physical constitution
  (pp. 65–66), and its asymmetry (p. 68).
- **Empirical latitude**: if human physiology blocks the case, a race very
  like us with colour sensations need not (pp. 57, 64).

## Key results

- **Fallacy of intentionalizing qualia (pp. 55–57).** If inversion is rife,
  neither of us can claim our quale is *the* looking red; contents in public
  language are intentional and "Californian", not neo-Fregean. McGinn and
  Peacocke, who define 'red' by "the" quale red things produce, commit it.
- **Harman's straw man (p. 59).** Harman's inverted spectrum inverts
  intentional contents; no proponent should accept that version.
- **Intrapersonal inverted spectrum (pp. 60–61)**, in four stages; the
  crucial stage 3 testimony is attacked by Dennett (memory malfunction) and
  Rey. Block concedes "some justice" and changes tack.
- **Inverted Earth (pp. 62–67).** Intrapersonal: first day, intentional
  contents Earth's and false; after fifty years, the natives'; functional
  states follow. Interpersonal: twins with molecular-duplicate brains, one
  raised there with lenses; the colour-matching game shows the functional
  inversion (strawberry and double-decker bus). Q = F₁, Q = F₂, F₁ ≠ F₂
  contravenes transitivity of identity (pp. 64–65).
- **Objection: Twin's intentional content follows his qualia (pp. 68–69).**
  It presupposes an inverted spectrum and commits the fallacy, so neither
  Harman nor the qualia realist can use it.
- **Objection: other functional descriptions (pp. 69–71).** Brain-internal
  descriptions make the twins identical, but cannot capture intentional
  content: clones with inverters at different angles get the same neural
  signal from red, yellow and blue. A functionalist could pair long-arm roles
  for intentional content with short-arm roles for qualia; the burden is to
  say what the latter are. A physiological theory "functionalized" changes
  the subject in a debate about computational identity (p. 71).
- **Objection: inputs at the retina (pp. 71–72).** Move the inverter behind
  the retina; for a sharply bounded sensorium, run the case on a being
  without one.
- **Harman on introspection and Mary (pp. 72–75).** Introspecting a blue wall
  cannot settle whether we are aware of intrinsic properties; complex cases
  can. On Mary, Harman's reply concedes she gains intentional knowledge;
  the qualia realist says she gains two kinds, and on that Harman "is
  silent".
- **Notes 28–29.** An absent-qualia argument would also doom a short-arm
  functional theory of qualia; "thought is functional, but ... the best bet
  for a theory of qualia lies in neurophysiology".

## Concepts

- **Intentional / qualitative content** — how the experience represents the
  world, versus what it is like (p. 54).
- **Fallacy of intentionalizing qualia** — taking contents expressible in
  public language to be qualitative (p. 55).
- **Quasi-functional qualia realism** — functionalism about intentional
  content and concepts, not about qualitative content (p. 58).
- **Long-arm / short-arm roles** — functional roles reaching to things in
  the world, versus stopping at the skin (p. 58).

## Limitations

- The content shift after fifty years rests on externalist intuitions some
  readers will not share; Block offers the interpersonal case for them.
- The empirical possibility of an undetectable inverter is assumed; the
  Harrison and Hardin asymmetry objections are rebutted only in a note
  (n. 17), and Block says the paper's main claim survives them.
- Short-arm functionalism about qualia is not refuted, only challenged.

## Open questions

- What would a short-arm functional characterisation of qualia look like,
  and does Chalmers's invariance principle supply one?

  ("Mental Paint", [LIT-tmpep9zj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpep9zj.md)), settle the gradual-shift reply here?
