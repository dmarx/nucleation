# TERM-tmp383al: institutional sociopathy: the condition of an institution that makes others' circumstances legible to itself, recognizing and predicting their needs and vulnerabilities, and uses that legibility for its own objective while their justified claims gain no authority over what it does; legibility without answerability, not a failure to understand

## Definition

From [CLAIM-tmptrn75](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptrn75.md): an institution "can make others' circumstances highly
legible to itself ... and use that legibility for its own objective while
their justified claims gain no authority over what it does". Two
conditions, both needed:

- **Legibility.** It models the affected parties well: their needs,
  vulnerabilities and likely responses.
- **No answerability.** Their claims do not constrain what it does, except
  as inputs to its own objective.

## Use

- The end of A46's chain from measurement naivety ([TERM-tmpg31ao](TERM-tmpg31ao.md)) through
  greedy agency ([TERM-tmpxr1ox](TERM-tmpxr1ox.md)).
- Shareholder primacy as the owner states it ([CLAIM-tmpbucep](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbucep.md), U30) is one
  route to it: other parties' standing is known and not given authority.
- Whether such an institution shows remorse is a separate question
  ([QUESTION-265](../questions.d/QUESTION-265.md); [CLAIM-431](../claims.d/CLAIM-431.md), [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md)).

## Not to be confused with

- Incompetence or ignorance: the institution understands the harm.
- Malice: it need not aim at the harm; the harm is outside its objective.
