# CLAIM-1423: A new value is a newly consequential distinction

## The claim

A21 §3 lists transformations that are easily run together when someone
learns music: discovery, discrimination, preference change, "Valuational
differentiation | Becoming sensitive to musical qualities that previously
did not matter", interest formation, commitment formation and normative
judgment. "A model of preference change is not necessarily a model of
valuational differentiation." It separates three changes:

- "Changing the evaluation of existing distinctions."
- "Developing new evaluative distinctions."
- "Establishing new processes that maintain and act upon those
  distinctions."

A23 §9 states the second in the vocabulary of potential consequentiality
([TERM-185](../terms.d/TERM-185.md)): an evaluative distinction emerges when a difference
acquires a maintained role in evaluation; "An interest emerges when that
role becomes sufficiently organized to participate in directing
attention, activity and learning. A purpose emerges when the concern
acquires a maintained and prospective role in governing activity."

## What gives it content (the assistant's)

Any change can be redescribed as a reweighting over a large enough set of
features, and A21 §11 concedes the point: "One can avoid part of that
problem by distinguishing the theorist's language from the developing
system's own available language." The claim is substantive only against
the agent's own distinctions, and A23's construction supplies the test: a
dimension belongs to the agent's evaluation before the development if
some intervention on it, within the agent's affordable witnesses, then
changed its choices (C_i(d) held). Harmonic complexity is a new
evaluative distinction for the listener if, before, varying it with
familiarity held fixed moved nothing in what the listener chose or
attended to, and, after, it does, across circumstances. A reweighting is
a change in how much an already consequential dimension moves choice.
That is an empirical test, and A21 §8 states its form: test "whether a
developmental intervention makes new distinctions consequential to
behaviour across circumstances, rather than merely producing verbal
endorsements or temporary enthusiasm", keeping "changed valuation" apart
from "changed beliefs, capabilities, incentives, or authority".
[LEAD-285](../leads.d/LEAD-285.md) carries it as its interest-development case.

## How it stands to the record

- [CLAIM-1036](CLAIM-1036.md) shows that when what is learned changes what is valued, no
  scalar optimum is fixed without a declared evaluator; this claim says
  what kind of change of valuation can be beyond the present self's
  inquiry frontier ([TERM-092](../terms.d/TERM-092.md)), one whose dimension is not yet
  consequential to it.
- [CLAIM-169](CLAIM-169.md) gives one mechanism by which interests are produced
  (desynchronized valuation across horizons); A21 §4 asks the general
  question this claim answers in part: "Under what conditions does a
  newly consequential evaluative distinction become a maintained
  organizational process?" [CLAIM-1352](CLAIM-1352.md) is A21's mechanism.
- [CLAIM-762](CLAIM-762.md), the owner's, has collapsed equivalences become units of
  further organization; a new evaluative distinction is the evaluative
  case, a class that comes to be acted on.
- A21 §2 names the philosophy this meets: Callard on aspiration
  ([LIT-3287](../literature.d/LIT-3287.md)), Paul on transformative experience ([LIT-3304](../literature.d/LIT-3304.md)),
  Dewey on ends-in-view ([LIT-3279](../literature.d/LIT-3279.md)), all unread.

## What it does not say

- That the new value is worth having. A21 §10: "we should distinguish
  **the emergence of valuing from the emergence of value itself**"; [CLAIM-689](CLAIM-689.md)
  and [CLAIM-680](CLAIM-680.md) hold the record's separation of origin from worth.
- That autotelic agents cannot develop values. A21 §12's point is about
  goal generation under a fixed criterion, not about every learner that
  generates goals.
