  Mobahi, Farajtabar & Bartlett (2020). In ℓ2-regularized regression in
  a Hilbert space held to a fixed training-error tolerance, refitting to
  one's own predictions on the same inputs shrinks each eigencomponent
  of the labels by d_k/(c_t + d_k). The multiplier c_t must rise as the
  labels shrink. Rounds therefore amplify regularization, use
  progressively fewer basis functions (Theorem 5) and collapse to the
  zero function after finitely many. A few rounds can reduce
  overfitting and more underfit, in a spline example and, by fractions
  of a point, in ResNet and VGG runs on CIFAR.

# LIT-1895: Self-Distillation Amplifies Regularization in Hilbert Space

Mobahi, H., Farajtabar, M., & Bartlett, P. L. (2020). Self-distillation amplifies regularization in Hilbert space. arXiv:2002.05715.

Read in [NOTE-1478](../notes.d/NOTE-1478.md).

## Why this is here

A16's example that iterated self-distillation changes what is preserved round by round: inheritance by repeated projection is not neutral copying. Bears on [CLAIM-090](../claims.d/CLAIM-090.md) (serial transmission is reconstructive) and [QUESTION-361](../questions.d/QUESTION-361.md). The reading sharpens one word. The round operator here is a shrinkage, not a projection, and nothing is withheld from the student: same inputs, the teacher's exact outputs. The loss is the inheritor's own inductive bias applied again. That is the "regularization" item on A16's list of what makes iterated inheritance degrade, isolated from the others ([THEORY-1252](../theory.d/THEORY-1252.md)).
