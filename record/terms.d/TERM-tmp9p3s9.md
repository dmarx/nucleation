# TERM-tmp9p3s9: frustration, of organizationally consequential demands: one or more such demands obstructed from realization, relative to a specified bearer, collection of requirements, context, temporal horizon and set of available transformations, whether the obstruction is structural or a failure to realize an acceptable alternative

## Definition

The owner's wording, from the third review of the filing:

> *Frustration* is a condition in which one or more organizationally
> consequential demands are obstructed from realization, relative to a
> specified bearer, collection of requirements, context, temporal horizon,
> and set of available transformations.
>
> Frustration may arise because the demands cannot be jointly realized
> under the available constraints (**structural frustration**), or because
> jointly satisfactory possibilities exist but are not made effective
> (**realization failure**). Failures of representation, coordination,
> selection, and authority allocation are possible mechanisms of the
> latter.

It is a conceptual definition. It does not say how a demand is specified.
A demand is an organizationally consequential requirement. It is not a
preference, and the term is neutral about the form the requirement takes.
A preference for a better outcome is not thereby a standing requirement
that improvement occur, and the existence of a more preferred outcome does
not show that the present one is inadequate. A process whose task is to
maintain a satisfactory condition demands maintenance, not improvement. At
an optimum it is satisfied, not frustrated. The record keeps apart what a
constituent prefers, what it demands, what it will accept, what practical
authority it claims ([CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md)) and what authority the organization grants.

**What an attribution specifies.** An attribution of frustration names:

- the **bearer**: which individual, constituent, coalition or
  organization is being evaluated;
- the **requirements**: which demands count, and on what basis;
- the **opportunities**: which outcomes or transformations are available,
  under what constraints;
- the **horizon**: over what period the demands must be realized;
- the **actual organization**: which relations determine what becomes
  effective.

The demands are not just those the organization currently maintains. If
they were, an organization could make frustration disappear by definition,
by removing a constituent from practical consideration. Suppressing or
removing a demand changes the attribution. It does not show that the
original demand has been realized ([CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md)). Comparisons of relief
must keep a reference collection of requirements, or say explicitly how
demands and their bearers change across a transformation. The frustration
of a constituent and that of an encompassing organization can come apart.
An organization can realize every demand it recognizes while frustrating
a constituent whose requirements it excludes. A constituent can meet
resistance while the organization's current demands are compatible.

## Distinctions

- **Demand incompatibility.** No available outcome or policy jointly
  satisfies the specified demands. This is a modal fact, about what is
  possible.
- **Structural frustration.** Realization is obstructed by the feasible
  possibilities themselves.
- **Realization failure.** A specified demand is not realized although an
  acceptable alternative exists. This is an actual fact, about what
  happened.
- **Governance-induced frustration.** Realization failure attributable to
  the organization of effective influence, authority or selection. This is
  a causal fact. Not every realization failure is governance-induced. It
  may come from incomplete information, unsuccessful search, a poorly
  calibrated model or an unexpected event. Those may fall under a broad
  enough notion of governance, but the definition does not assume they do
  ([CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md)).
- **Regulatory strain.** The burden of maintaining or realizing activity,
  whether or not frustration occurs.
- **Distress.** A possible integrated regulatory response to frustration
  or strain, with no necessary implication of phenomenal experience.

| | question | character |
|---|---|---|
| structural obstruction | could the relevant demands be realized together? | modal: concerns possibilities |
| actual thwarting | were the relevant demands realized? | actual: concerns what happened |
| governance failure | was an available satisfactory alternative prevented from becoming effective? | causal: concerns the organization of selection and control |

**Strain is an independent dimension.** Frustration concerns thwarted
realization. Strain concerns the resource burden of keeping activity going
under a particular organization. All four combinations occur:

- a demand frustrated with little regulatory effort spent;
- a demand satisfied through substantial strain, as in the first person of
  [CASE-109](../cases.d/CASE-109.md), who keeps a commitment by repeatedly recruiting
  higher-order processes against internal resistance;
- an organization frustrated and strained at once;
- an organization neither frustrated nor strained that still fails
  non-dominating integration ([CLAIM-362](../claims.d/CLAIM-362.md)).

Constraints on available resources can alter both. A budget can turn a
realizable collection into an unrealizable one (A33 §3A: "Actual
constraints on admissible updates can change feasibility itself"), and
the budget then belongs to the opportunities.

## The mathematics underneath

Let 𝒪 be the organization, Π_𝒪 its admissible policies and Y_𝒪(π) the
outcome or history that policy π produces. For demands i ∈ D, let A_i be
the acceptable outcomes. A demand's acceptance condition belongs to the
space of outcomes or histories. The available transformations belong to
the organization. Then:

- **structural compatibility:** ∃ π ∈ Π_𝒪 with Y_𝒪(π) ∈ ⋂_{i∈D} A_i;
- **actual satisfaction:** Y_𝒪(π_actual) ∈ ⋂_{i∈D} A_i.

The first separates structural frustration from the rest. The second
separates realization failure from success. Neither collapses into the
other. Keeping demands on outcomes is what lets reference requirements
stay comparable when the organization changes its architecture, for
example through differentiation, and its policy space changes with it
([CASE-115](../cases.d/CASE-115.md), [CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md)).

How A_i is specified is the demand's business, not the term's. A demand
for strict improvement on a reference, A_i = {y : y ≻_i y₀}, is one form.
It is right only for a demand that is a demand for improvement. A
maintenance demand is a threshold or a standing condition that the
present outcome may already meet. The form A_i = {y : y ⪰_i y₀} is never
a demand by itself: by reflexivity the reference meets it, and every
collection becomes trivially compatible. The compatibility complex, the
ordinal construction and its facts are [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md). The cardinal
refinement for an improvement demand on a differentiable learner, the
residual and its dual, is [CLAIM-365](../claims.d/CLAIM-365.md).

## Frustration need not mean something has gone wrong

Some demands deserve to be frustrated. A constituent may demand authority
it should not have. A specialist may advocate a policy that would destroy
the organization's capacity for correction. An internal process may try to
remove others from influence. Appropriate governance can keep such demands
from becoming effective. An unsatisfied constituent demand does not show
that the organization should satisfy it. A34 on [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md): "a good
organization may correctly prevent an unreliable process from taking
control". A33 §2: "A conflict may be the correct consequence of optimizing
genuinely different objectives."

Non-domination ([CLAIM-362](../claims.d/CLAIM-362.md)) therefore does not require that no
constituent demand be frustrated. It requires appropriate and accountable
relations through which those demands are evaluated, negotiated,
constrained and sometimes refused.

## The division of labour

The owner: "Frustration tells us that some demand encounters an
obstruction. The geometry tells us whether that obstruction is necessary
under the available possibilities. The governance theory tells us why
available resolutions do or do not become effective. And the authenticity
theory asks what the organization's way of handling these obstructions
reveals about its internal relations."

## What it is not

- **Not disagreement, or pairwise conflict.** Two demands can disagree and
  be jointly satisfiable. Every pair can be satisfiable while the triple is
  not ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)).
- **Not low satisfaction.** [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) keeps the human case apart in the
  same way: Vansteenkiste, Ryan and Soenens treat need frustration, the
  active thwarting of a need, as distinct from a mere lack of its
  satisfaction.
- **Not regulatory effort**, which is strain (above).
- **Not an integrated affective response, and not a feeling.** Frustration
  can be present with no experienced feeling and no unified regulatory
  signal. A33 §6: it "can be organizationally consequential without
  appearing as a unified internal experience or even a unified
  representation".
- **Not [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s frustration.** There, frustration is a nonzero
  monodromy of offsets around a cycle of contexts. Here it is an
  obstruction to realizing demands over one space of outcomes, with no
  torsor and no cohomology class. They share the shape that pairs can be
  fine and a whole not. A37 warned that "Not every optimization conflict
  has a cohomology class".
- **Not Watson's total frustration** ([THEORY-232](../theory.d/THEORY-232.md)). Watson compares the
  parts' total energy at equilibrium, which is an aggregate. This term
  assigns no scalar amount of dissatisfaction to any constituent.

## History

A33's wording, the source: "Frustration: Available ways of advancing one
objective obstruct another, or the current governance cannot jointly
realize the objectives it maintains." The owner's U37 correction made it
ordinal. The first filing defined the term as "the failure of the demands
it maintains to be jointly realizable under its admissible transformations
and its governance", with acceptance sets inside the definition and strain
as the alternative to frustration. The owner's first review corrected the
acceptance sets, which by reflexivity made every collection compatible.
The third review recentred the term. It moved from joint realizability to
the obstruction of consequential demands. It replaced the binary of
structural against governance-induced with feasibility, actual
realization and the causes of failure. It made strain an independent
dimension, and moved the acceptance-set construction to [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md).
That third revision changed the definition after [TERM-060](TERM-060.md) had been
merged, so by the template's rule it is a new term, this one, which
supersedes [TERM-060](TERM-060.md). The claims stated in the frustration sense now use
this term.
