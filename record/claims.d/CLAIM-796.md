# CLAIM-796: Rich-get-richer processes and criticality reach power laws by almost opposite routes, the first by reinforcing differences and the second by removing the cutoff that had limited them

## The claim (A8 §8)

"In rich-get-richer systems, scale-free inequality arises because
advantage feeds on advantage.

In critical systems, scale-free behavior can arise because the dynamics
are balanced at a boundary between qualitatively different regimes.

Both can generate power laws, but the mechanisms are almost opposites: one
creates extreme outcomes by reinforcing existing differences; the other
permits them through the disappearance of an intrinsic cutoff.

The common mathematical result is that **the system has no single
characteristic scale that adequately describes its behavior**."

## Why A thinks it follows

In the cascade (A7, A8) no event is advantaged. A large cascade is not one
that "was initially privileged" (A3 §3); it is one the configuration let
run. In preferential attachment, the large unit is large because it was
large earlier ([CLAIM-817](CLAIM-817.md)).

## What it does not say

That the two cannot combine. A3 §4 notes that "a network created by
preferential attachment can subsequently support cascading events."

## Note of 2026-11-22: Krapivsky, Redner and Leyvraz, and Barabási, read

**A contrast** (LIT-2974, NOTE-tmp2z733; LIT-2968, NOTE-tmpnpqc8). The
objection to this claim, CLAIM-806, says linear attachment is itself
balanced at a boundary. The readings give it support in their own words:
Krapivsky, Redner and Leyvraz call γ = 1 "the borderline case" and γ > 1
"akin to gelation", and the chapter says "Π(k) needs to depend strictly
linearly on the degrees for the resulting network to have a pure power
law". Neither draws the analogy to criticality. No edge; status
unchanged.
