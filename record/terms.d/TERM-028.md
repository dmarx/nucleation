# TERM-028: fidelity, as inferential sufficiency

## Definition

A86 §3: "two notions of translation quality. **Fidelity as representational
similarity:** Does the translation resemble the original along particular
observational dimensions? **Fidelity as inferential sufficiency:** Does the
translation preserve the information needed to make the relevant distinctions
about the communicative situation? The second is much closer to the functional
character of translation." Its measure is a task-restricted Blackwell
deficiency δ_Q(V_1, V_2), "naturally directed", and A86 §7's decision distance
D_Q(U, V) = sup_q |R_q(U) − R_τ(q)(V)|. A89: "**fidelity is no longer defined as
similarity to a presumed global semantic object**. It is defined relative to the
communicative distinctions a reconstruction preserves, the decisions those
distinctions support, and the compatibility relations among the contexts in
which they are observed."

## What it is not

Not a replacement for the similarity senses ([TERM-021](TERM-021.md), [TERM-015](TERM-015.md)). The manuscript's
L = L_obs + λL_str + γL_dec keeps a similarity term, a structural term and this
one (§6). The named contrast between the two kinds of fidelity is not in the
manuscript. It is the sense [CLAIM-050](../claims.d/CLAIM-050.md) is stated in.

The crystallized argument (§24) gave the decision term its sharp form, a worst
case over tasks: D_dec = sup_(q∈Q) |R_q^o − R_τ(q)^t|. The manuscript's L_dec
"compares achievable decision risks for paired communicative tasks" (§6) and
drops the supremum, so it no longer says whether fidelity is judged on the worst
task or on an average.
