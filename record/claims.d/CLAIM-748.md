# CLAIM-748: Litigation as siege: an actor can make a legally recognized corrective objective unreachable for another party, with the merits, the law and that party's preferences unchanged, by raising what pursuing the objective costs, in spending or in time, above what that party can sustain until relief arrives

## The claim

The owner, U28 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), quoting
A59 §7's heading "The wrongdoer controls the costs of resistance":

> Let's probe this further. Time is just one resource asymmetry that can be abused here. Consider also the seemingly common strategy of a large corporate interest dragging out litigation in an effort to bankrupt their opposition or force a favorable (coercive) settlement. This is sort of the time asymmetry problem: an arbitrarily long process strains sustenance. One party "outlasting" the other in the courts can almost be interpreted as a form of siege warfare.

The owner's ideas are four: time is one of several resource asymmetries;
a party can drag out a dispute to exhaust the other or force a
settlement; a long enough process strains "sustenance", the means of
carrying on; and outlasting in court is siege. The cost-control framing
the owner quotes is A59 §7's; the litigation case, the inversion from too
fast to too slow, and sustenance are the owner's.

A61 put it as "**One actor can make the process of seeking justice last
longer than another actor can afford to sustain it.**" A64, two turns
later, opened with "The key insight is that **the cost of exercising a
right may itself be partly controlled by the actor against whom the right
is being asserted**". That is the owner's U28, told without credit; A61
had credited it ("I think the siege analogy identifies a particularly
important form of domination").

## The demonstration

[CASE-287](../cases.d/CASE-287.md) holds A's constructions, rechecked. In brief:

- **Two channels.** A's conduct u_A enters B's spending, c_B(t, u_A, π_B),
  and the time to relief, T_J(u_A, π_B, G). B's route fails when its
  exhaustion time falls below the time to relief, τ_B(u_A) < T_J(u_A).
  "**A strategy can defeat a claim by making its endurance margin negative
  without affecting the legal merits.**"
- **Leverage without a change of mind.** In the four- and seven-month game
  B's value of continuing falls from 60 to 0 while the judgment, the law
  and B's preferences stay fixed, so an offer B would refuse becomes one it
  accepts. "**The adversary can influence settlement by changing the
  conditions of refusal.**"
- **A threshold.** A cost that moves the cheapest route to J past B's
  budget removes J entirely: $5,000 against a $98,000 requirement and a
  $100,000 budget.

So the claim as titled holds by construction. A64 §15 calls the
mechanism "**Attritional foreclosure**", Candidate B, and keeps it apart
from Candidate C, the normative thesis, which "does not follow
automatically from the mathematics".

## Too fast and too slow

The owner: "This is sort of the time asymmetry problem". A61 §1 sets it
beside the fait accompli: one actor executes before the other can
intervene, the other keeps contesting until the other cannot continue,
and in both "The protected corrective capability loses practical
efficacy". A64's close: "**Temporal preemption**" and "**Attritional
exhaustion**" share the result that "A protected corrective capability
loses practical efficacy while the entitlement that justified it remains
formally unchanged." The temporal counterpart is the owner's U27,
[CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md).

**A costly wager.** A64 §16 returns to the owner's U26 (auction and game
theory meeting tort theory): the power to raise the cost of contest "can
transform a right from a protection against power into a costly wager
against power", with "an auction-like feature: the practical allocation
of authority becomes partly dependent on who can afford to keep paying
for its enforcement." A adds that it "is not necessarily an auction in the
formal sense".

**Several resources.** The owner's "Time is just one resource asymmetry"
has a sharper form in A64 §5: "An adversary need not eliminate every
resource. It may only need to exhaust whichever necessary resource cannot
be replenished in time." A74 §7 ties it to [CLAIM-585](CLAIM-585.md)'s frontier of
sufficient budgets (the note of 2026-11-17 on [CLAIM-585](CLAIM-585.md)).

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)** (Active): an intervention can make a corrective outcome
  costlier or unreachable while the standards stay the same. There the
  disputed act changes the state; here the opposing party's conduct in
  the dispute changes the cost of pursuing correction, over time.
- **[TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)** (Active) and **[CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)** (Active): correctability is
  achievement within deadline and resources. [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md) already says "the
  budget is itself partly an outcome of political and organizational
  decisions"; A61 §7 makes it an outcome of the opponent's decisions, and
  restricts the policies to the claimant's.
- **[TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md)** (Active): stock, rate and deadline are different
  constraints. The runway is a stock spent at a rate against a deadline
  the opponent can move.
- **[CLAIM-601](CLAIM-601.md)** (Active): a savings buffer is a stock of time, and below it
  a profitable delayed payoff is rightly declined. A lawsuit is such a
  payoff, and the opponent can drain the buffer.
- **[CLAIM-619](CLAIM-619.md)** (Proposed): answering a challenge by reducing the capacity
  for future challenge. A61 §7 keeps them apart: resource depletion,
  corrective foreclosure and "**Counter-corrective response:**" are three
  routes; this claim is the first.
- **[CLAIM-716](CLAIM-716.md)**: A61 §5's "**resource-mediated remedial hold-up**",
  the settlement reached once this mechanism has worked.
- **[CLAIM-731](CLAIM-731.md)** (F2): execution friction and contestation friction
  differ. A61 §10's split is related: "**Adjudicative friction** is the
  time and effort legitimately necessary" and "**Attritional friction** is
  additional burden that consumes participants' ability to continue
  without making an adequately justified contribution to those purposes."
- **[CLAIM-657](CLAIM-657.md)** (Proposed): A64 §13 adds that a reputation for costly
  resistance can keep claims from being filed at all (the note of
  2026-11-17 on [CLAIM-657](CLAIM-657.md)).
- **[CLAIM-722](CLAIM-722.md)** (F4): making a remedy unreachable gains no power over
  what is owed. This claim is the means; that one says what it does not
  achieve.

## What it does not say

- Not that the mechanism is wrongful when used. A64 §14's counterexamples
  ("**A genuinely complex dispute.**", a well-financed claimant, a
  valuable but costly inquiry, a mutually preferred settlement) show
  foreclosure without wrong and wrong without foreclosure. Whether a party
  under a justified constraint may set the cost of making it effective is
  [QUESTION-479](../questions.d/QUESTION-479.md).
- Not that the defendant is the stronger party. A64 §14: "**A poorly
  founded claim against a small defendant.**" "Our model must be
  role-symmetric."
- Not that wealth decides. A64 §2: "A smaller actor with reliable
  litigation financing may outlast a larger but financially distressed
  one."
- Not that sanctions cure it. A64 §7: "sanctions that arrive only after
  the target has already exhausted its funds may fail to preserve its
  ability to pursue the original claim. Thus deterrence and corrective
  reachability are distinct design problems."
