# NOTE-1530: Murphy et al. — The BPNSFS probably does not validly measure need frustration

## Contribution

It makes the case that the Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction and
Frustration Scales (Chen et al. 2015), "the dominant self-report measure in
the BPNT domain", cannot carry the two-dimensional theory of need
satisfaction and need frustration (Vansteenkiste & Ryan 2013) that they are
used to test. It gives an item-content argument, a factor-analytic
replication of Chen's own 2013 dissertation finding in new data, and a test
of whether the scales' different correlates track the keying of the
external measures. A second, separate finding: the two autonomy scales may
measure different things.

## Key insight

When every "frustration" item is negatively keyed and every "satisfaction"
item positively keyed, a two-factor split and a difference in correlates are
exactly what keying alone would produce, so neither can show that the two
scales measure two constructs (Kam & Meyer 2015). The one thing the
structure can show is which grouping is stronger. Theory requires the needs
(competence, relatedness, autonomy) to be the higher-order factors. In these
data the keying directions are. "Item-keying direction was an overwhelming
source of the covariance in the BPNSFS item pool, more so than the three
basic psychological needs dimensions themselves" (Discussion, Issue A).

## Assumptions

- **The construct being tested is Vansteenkiste and Ryan's (2013).** Need
  frustration is "an asymmetric configuration of (a) low need satisfaction
  alongside (b) strong, active, direct thwarting by other individuals or by
  the social context itself" (introduction). The plant analogy: lack of
  rain versus salting the soil. Items without thwarting content are judged
  against that definition.
- **The needs should be the highest-order factors** if the scale measures
  what BPNT says, since "frustration of any particular need is
  conceptualized, by definition, as inherently covarying with need
  satisfaction of that need" (introduction, after Campbell & Fiske 1959).
- **Keying and valence coincide.** In the BPNSFS, positive keying is also
  positive valence, so keying factors may carry defensiveness,
  self-esteem or social desirability (DiStefano & Motl 2009; Quilty et al.
  2007).
- **Pooling.** Items were divided by the scale maximum (5 or 7) and treated
  as continuous; the eight samples were pooled, with Sample 2's primed half
  as a cluster (Method). Seven samples are university students, one an
  online community sample; all English-language.

## Key results

### The items (introduction)

- None of the three frustration scales has "substantial active thwarting
  content". Two Competence Frustration items are reverse-worded twins of
  satisfaction items: "I have serious doubts about whether I can do things
  well" against "I feel confident that I can do things well". The other two
  concern failures ("I feel like a failure because of the mistakes I make").
- Chen (2013), the dissertation behind Chen et al. (2015), "tentatively
  concluded that the item pool did not support the creation of separate
  frustration scales", found two large keying factors in an EFA, and
  modelled the negative items as a method. The 2015 paper presented six
  scales from the same pool.
- Footnote 2 contrasts the Psychological Need Thwarting Scale (Bartholomew
  et al. 2011, [LIT-1954](../literature.d/LIT-1954.md)), which "measures being thwarted far more
  explicitly", e.g. "I feel forced to follow decisions made for me".

### Issue A: keying (Results)

- **External correlates follow the external items' keying** (all items
  scored toward well-being). Self-compassion: Satisfaction correlates .35
  with the positive SCS items and Frustration .09 (Steiger's z = 4.22);
  Frustration .67 with the negative items, Satisfaction .34 (z = 6.50).
  La Guardia need-satisfaction scale: positive items .78 vs .58 (z = 7.80);
  negative items .75 vs .45 (z = 11.16). Perceived stress (two samples):
  positive items .48 vs .53 (z = −.66), negative items .65 vs .46
  (z = 3.58). Depression (CESD): positive items .59 vs .56 (z = .49),
  negative items .62 vs .53 (z = 1.63). Self-esteem (RSE): positive .59 vs
  .57 (z = .33), negative .67 vs .57 (z = 1.90).
  So Frustration's excess with negative items holds in all five measures,
  clearly in three; Satisfaction's excess with positive items holds in two
  of five. The paper's word for it is "though not consistently".
- **EFA (Table 1, N = 3,692).** Two factors: one of all positive items, one
  of all negative items. Three factors: the first two still pure keying,
  with some relatedness items on the third. Four factors: a frustration
  factor (competence and relatedness), a satisfaction factor (autonomy and
  relatedness), a relatedness factor of both keyings, and an autonomy
  frustration factor. Parallel analysis suggested six factors, MAP four.
- **Six-factor EFA intercorrelations.** Among the frustration factors .64
  to .70; among the satisfaction factors .66 to .75; between satisfaction
  and frustration of the same need .17 (competence), .34 (relatedness), .38
  (autonomy).
- **Higher-order CFAs.** Six first-order factors under three need factors:
  RMSEA .064, CFI .924, TLI .915, BIC −28,734, with the higher-order
  correlations .94 to 1.11, "exceed[ing] 1 in most cases". Under two keying
  factors: RMSEA .052, CFI .950, TLI .944, BIC −29,631.

### Issue B: the autonomy scales (Results, Table 3)

- **Three substantive factors plus two keying factors** fit well (RMSEA
  .033, CFI .981, TLI .977). In Table 3 the four Autonomy Satisfaction
  items load .49, .34, .36, .42 on Autonomy, below the .50 threshold, and
  the four Autonomy Frustration items load .76, .62, .66, .75. Every
  relatedness and competence item but one (.48) loads above .50.
- **Four substantive factors** (autonomy split in two) fit "nearly
  equivalently" (RMSEA .033, CFI .982, TLI .978); the two autonomy factors
  correlate .82.
- **Correlates.** Autonomy Satisfaction correlates .39 with the IAF's
  Authorship/Self-Congruence (Sample 3) and .52 with presence of meaning
  (Sample 1); Autonomy Frustration −.08 and .15.

### Conclusions (Discussion)

- "The BPNSFS probably does not validly measure the dual-dimension theory of
  need frustration and need satisfaction", and "many published findings
  regarding well-being that rely on the BPNSFS may be at least partly
  driven by methodological artifact".
- Chen et al. (2015)'s nomological differences are explained by keying: need
  satisfaction tracked the Satisfaction with Life Scale (no negative items)
  and vitality, frustration the CESD (mostly negative items).
- A three-factor model (with method factors) is "better justified than a
  six-factor model"; a four-factor one is suggested and not asserted.

## Concepts

- **Item keying** — whether agreeing with an item indicates more (positive)
  or less (negative) of the construct; here also the item's valence.
- **Need frustration (as the paper uses it)** — Vansteenkiste and Ryan's:
  low satisfaction plus active thwarting by others or the context; not a
  synonym for low satisfaction, which it was before 2013.
- **Method factor ("artifactor")** — a factor shared by items because of how
  they are worded rather than what they are about.
- **Rebuttable presumption** — if a scale's two keyings each correlate
  preferentially with external items of the same valence, keying is
  presumed to drive the difference until shown otherwise.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) (need frustration as transdiagnostic).** Its "What was
  actually shown" opens "Measured directly (BPNSFS and similar scales),
  frustration predicted burnout, eating symptoms and negative affect beyond
  low satisfaction". This paper says that, for the BPNSFS, "beyond low
  satisfaction" is what keying alone would produce, and that the
  satisfaction and frustration factors of the same need correlate only .17
  to .38 after the keying is pulled apart. So the separation of frustration
  from low satisfaction, as measured by the BPNSFS, is not evidence that
  frustration is a separate dimension. Campbell et al. 2018, its one
  longitudinal study, measured need frustration with exactly these twelve
  items ([NOTE-1533](NOTE-1533.md)). The objection is distinct from the shared-content
  one [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) already notes, as its 2026-11-02 note says, and both hit
  the same evidence. Its promote_when already asks for a measure that is not
  a distress-sharing self-report; it should also exclude a frustration
  score whose separation from satisfaction is a keying split.
- **[QUESTION-131](../questions.d/QUESTION-131.md) (structural frustration and operative thwarting).** The
  paper is a measurement-level case of the question's distinction. The
  construct SDT names frustration is defined by an identified thwarting
  agent, which is the question's "operative thwarting". The BPNSFS items
  carry no agent and measure, on this reading, low satisfaction: the
  question's middle row, "non-attainment", which the question says is
  neither. The thwarting scale (footnote 2) is the instrument that names an
  agent. So the psychology already separates the three rows, and its most
  used scale collapses the third into the second.
- **[QUESTION-369](../questions.d/QUESTION-369.md) (what would show a need is a need).** The frustration
  test (frustration predicts ill-being beyond low satisfaction) is one of
  SDT's routes to need-hood. If the BPNSFS frustration score is a keyed
  satisfaction score, a candidate need's frustration "adding variance" can
  be a fact about keying; and its correlates sort by the valence of the
  outcome measure, so the test is partly the well-being measure again. This
  is the interdefinition [NOTE-439](NOTE-439.md) records, at the level of the instrument.
- **[CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md)** cites [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) as a parallel only ("keeps need frustration
  apart from low satisfaction"). That sentence is true of the theory and,
  on this reading, not established of the BPNSFS measurements. The claim's
  own case does not rest on it.
- **THEORYs filed from this reading**: [THEORY-1316](../theory.d/THEORY-1316.md) (Active), what the
  item content and keying structure show; [THEORY-1311](../theory.d/THEORY-1311.md) (Proposed), that
  the frustration scales are therefore keyed low satisfaction.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **Not dispositive, by the authors' account.** Keying and construct are
  fully confounded in the BPNSFS, so no factor analysis can separate them;
  the proposed fix, reverse-worded twins of all 24 items, was not run.
- **Secondary data.** None of the eight samples was collected to test the
  BPNSFS, and only a few had the external measures; the self-compassion,
  La Guardia and meaning analyses rest on one sample each.
- **The keying test is mixed.** Satisfaction's preference for positive
  external items appeared in two of five measures; for depression and self-
  esteem it did not. The depression and self-esteem negative-item
  differences (z = 1.63, 1.90) are marginal at best.
- **Narrow samples**: English-language, mostly undergraduates, three of the
  BPNSFS's 50-odd versions.
- **Not preregistered** (Method).
- **An internal inconsistency on the autonomy items.** Results says of the
  three-factor model that "none of the Autonomy Satisfaction items loaded
  above .50", which Table 3 bears out (.34–.49). The Discussion says
  instead that "none of the Autonomy Frustration items loaded well on an
  Autonomy factor", which Table 3 contradicts (.62–.76). Of the four-factor
  model, Results says "two of the four Autonomy Frustration items still did
  not load above .5", but in Table 3 the two items below .50 are Autonomy
  Satisfaction (.44, .46); every Autonomy Frustration item loads .62 or
  more. The introduction's report of Chen (2013) has the frustration items
  loading weakly. So the paper's own data have the satisfaction items, not
  the frustration items, as the weak ones, and the argument of Issue B,
  that the frustration items lack self-endorsement content, is supported
  by the external correlates and not by these loadings. This is a reading
  of the manuscript's table; the published version was not checked.

## Open questions

- With reverse-worded twins of every item, do satisfaction and frustration
  still separate? That is the test the authors propose.
- Does a frustration scale built from thwarting content (the PNTS, or the
  GNSF Kermavnar et al. single out) separate from satisfaction when its
  keying is balanced, and does it predict ill-being beyond low
  satisfaction?
- How much of [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md)'s evidence used the BPNSFS? Campbell et al. 2018
  did.
