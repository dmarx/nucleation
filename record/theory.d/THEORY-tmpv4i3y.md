# THEORY-tmpv4i3y: Evans, Silverberg and Santangelo show that a lattice theory of origami tessellations predicts that the soft deformations of a Miura-ori sheet are edge modes decaying from its boundaries with an analytic decay length, so that its number of degrees of freedom scales with its perimeter, not its area

## Source

Evans, Silverberg & Santangelo, "Lattice Mechanics of Origami
Tessellations", Physical Review E (2015), LIT-tmpj33xl; read in part from
arXiv 1503.05756v2 in NOTE-tmpghi05.

## What was actually shown

- **Theory.** A Bloch-wave lattice theory of vertices coupled through
  shared folds; for the Miura-ori with facet bending as extra folds,
  inhomogeneous soft modes decaying from the boundaries with an analytic
  decay length ℓ(q) (Sec. III).
- **Experiment.** An 8 × 8 paper Miura-ori, deformed with an indenter and
  with reversible pop-through defects (Fig. 6); for a defect the decay
  length "can be estimated to within 100%".
- **Conclusion (Sec. IV).** "the number of degrees of freedom scale with
  the perimeter of a finite tessellation, rather than the area."

## What this does not say

- Anything beyond linear deformations about one ground state.
- That Miura-ori cells are bistable, or that defects behave like
  vacancies, dislocations or grain boundaries.
- That the scaling was measured: it is the theory's conclusion, checked
  against decay lengths on one sheet.
