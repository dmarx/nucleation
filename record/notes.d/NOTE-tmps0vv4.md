# NOTE-tmps0vv4: Kaepernick as Confucian sage: ritual resistance

## Contribution

Walsh reads Colin Kaepernick's 2016 anthem protest — first sitting, then, after consulting veteran Nate Boyer, kneeling — through Confucian thought and phenomenology together. The Confucian account of ritual (li) and sagehood explains what the gesture means, and phenomenology describes how ritual works through the body. He argues that the gesture subverts the anthem ritual from within while remaining faithful to its ideals, and institutes a new bodily expression of patriotism compatible with both American and Confucian ideals.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 1–2): liberalism is uneasy with patriotism and its rituals, and communitarian defences say little about how protest can live inside ritual — hence the turn to Confucianism.
- §2 (p. 4): critics grant Kaepernick's right to protest yet still call it disrespectful, so rights-talk is the wrong register; the Confucian gives li an explicitly political role (Analects: keep people in line by ritual rather than coercion).
- §3 (p. 5) addresses the apparent incompatibility of Confucian ritual with democracy (Curzer: ritual instils values "under reason's radar") and develops "Confucian incivility" (Mengzi against "making obedience one's standard").
- §4 (p. 12) uses Xunzi on ritual "differentiations": the anthem ritual cultivates peerhood rather than hierarchy.
- §5 (pp. 17–23) sets out five phenomenological points: ren motivates li (ritual as grammar; Li 2007), the normativity of habit, the historical and intercorporeal body, the moral phenomenology of style, and sagehood as bodily "citational force" (via Butler and Derrida).
- §§6–7 (pp. 23–26): unlike the raised fist, kneeling embodies reverence and humility — "excorporation" within the ritual; with MacIntyre's patriot, it honours the nation as an ideal not yet lived up to.

## Open questions

- Check how "citational force" is made to do normative rather than merely descriptive work.
