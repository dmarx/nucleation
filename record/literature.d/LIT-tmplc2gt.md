# LIT-tmplc2gt: Sacrificing Humans for Insects and AI: A Critical Review of Jonathan Birch, The Edge of Sentience, Jeff Sebo, The Moral Circle, and Webb Keane, Animals, Robots, Gods

Eric Schwitzgebel, Walter Sinnott-Armstrong (2026), *Ethics, 136(3), 670–696* — DOI-10.1086/739660

## Key takeaways

- Birch, Sebo and Keane each state principles that, taken at face value, could make the aggregate interests of insects or near-future AI outweigh humanity's, yet each retreats to conservative near-term advice — and the reviewers argue the radical conditional deserves a bolder confrontation than any of them gives it.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Scientists increasingly take seriously that insects may be sentient and that AI systems may soon be. If sentience or consciousness grounds moral standing, insects in aggregate, or near-future AI systems individually or in aggregate, might matter enough that their interests outweigh humanity's. That would radically deprioritize humans in ethics. The review examines Birch's The Edge of Sentience, Sebo's The Moral Circle and Keane's Animals, Robots, Gods. It finds that all three put forward arguments with apparently radical implications but play those implications down and offer fairly conservative near-term recommendations.
