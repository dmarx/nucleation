# TERM-161: singularity, of a candidate self, in three senses that come apart: carrier singularity, one carrier at the declared level of description; organizational singularity, one overarching organizational perspective within which every perspective that makes a difference is integrated; phenomenal singularity, all its experience belonging to one subject

## Definition

A22 of the exchange "Explain First Contact RL"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b037165-3a1c-83ea-9d7c-311c7807acd3). Let a
candidate self be S = (C, P, G, H): carriers C = {c_1, …, c_n}, realized
organizational perspectives P = {p_1, …, p_m} (overlapping, nested, crossing
carriers; m ≠ n in general), governing relations G, history H.

| Sense | Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| carrier singularity | \|C\| = 1 | one carrier at the declared level of description |
| organizational singularity | a unique overarching perspective governs all relevant subordinate perspectives | one perspectival organization integrates the whole |
| phenomenal singularity | \|O\| = 1 | all relevant experience belongs to one subject |

O is the set of subjects to whom phenomenal episodes belong, "**if** such
ownership relations exist. This is not something the organizational model
establishes."

**Organizational singularity, formally** (A22 §3). Let P_eff(S) be the
perspectives that make a causal difference to S's interpretations or
actions over a declared context and horizon, and p_i ≼ p_j mean that p_i's
interpretive activity is integrated within p_j's organization of
significance, uptake and revision, "supported by causal evidence", not set
inclusion. Then

Sing_org(S) ⇔ ∃! p* ∈ P_eff(S) such that p ≼ p* for every p ∈ P_eff(S).

It "does *not* require a central executive": p* can be distributed. Nor
agreement: p* can integrate persistent disagreement.

**Unity** is not singularity. A22 §7: "Unity is a property of the relations
and dynamics that maintain coherent activity across a plurality of
processes. Singularity is the stronger claim that this unity belongs to one
encompassing perspective—or, in an entirely different sense, one subject of
experience."

## A correction to the formal definition

"Unique greatest element" says what A means only if ≼ is a partial order. A
did not say so, and integration need not be antisymmetric: two perspectives
can each be integrated within the other's organization (a pair that
mutually govern one another's uptake). If ≼ is only a preorder, a system
with two such perspectives and nothing above them has two greatest elements
and fails "∃!", though it is plausibly one organization. Two repairs:
read ≼ on equivalence classes of mutual integration (then "unique" is up to
mutual integration); or drop the uniqueness and require a greatest element.
If ≼ is a partial order, a greatest element is unique anyway, so "∃!" adds
nothing. The first repair keeps A's intent. (The assistant's; Claude, in
this session.)

## Configurations

A22 §3 draws three: a singular organization (P_1 and P_2 within one P*); a
plural organization (overlapping regimes, no encompassing one); an
integrated plurality (shared governance, no unique P*: "The enclosing
boundary in the third case represents coordination, not an additional
perspective"). "A system could be organizationally unified without being
organizationally singular." [CLAIM-1019](../claims.d/CLAIM-1019.md) states what follows.
