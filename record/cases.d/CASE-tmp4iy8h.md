# CASE-tmp4iy8h: Swapping the two persons of a dyad: nonreciprocal T/V address is changed by the swap and reciprocal address is not, with a constructed French–English dyad and a three-person teasing round

Found on 2026-10-10 by a search the owner asked for: a genuine symmetry,
acting on the situation and not on labels, with anchored identification on
both sides, and one realization the symmetry changes and one it does not,
for [CLAIM-137](../claims.d/CLAIM-137.md) and [CLAIM-tmpt76qw](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt76qw.md). [CLAIM-132](../claims.d/CLAIM-132.md)'s worked model of the swap was
built on [CASE-004](CASE-004.md), a stipulated scene. This case is **stipulated on a
documented basis**. Part A is documented. Parts B and C are constructed by
the record, and they say so.

## The case

**A. Documented: reciprocal and nonreciprocal address.** Roger Brown and
Albert Gilman, "The Pronouns of Power and Solidarity", in T. A. Sebeok
(ed.), *Style in Language* (MIT Press, 1960), pp. 253–276, read in the
transcription at https://pages.mtu.edu/~rlstrick/rsvtxt/pronoun.htm:

- p. 255: "The power semantic is similarly nonreciprocal; the superior
  says T and receives V."
- p. 258: "Solidarity is the name we give to the general relationship and
  solidarity is symmetrical. The corresponding norms of address are
  symmetrical or reciprocal".
- p. 275: the T of contempt "can, of course, also be used by a subordinate
  to a superior. As the social distance is greater, the overthrow of the
  norm is more shocking". Their example is Mosca, who says "you" to
  Volpone until he turns on him with "Bane to thy wolfish nature."

Let G = C₂ swap the two persons of a dyad. Under the solidarity semantic,
address is mutual T or mutual V, and the swap leaves the configuration
fixed: its stabilizer is C₂. Under the power semantic, the superior says T
and the subordinate V. The swap gives the subordinate saying T to the
superior, which Brown and Gilman record as the norm's "overthrow", a
different act; the stabilizer is trivial. Radcliffe-Brown draws the same
line for joking relationships, "symmetrical" (each teases the other) against
"asymmetrical" (A jokes at B's expense and B does not retaliate), in
*Structure and Function in Primitive Society* (Cohen & West, 1952), ch. IV,
p. 90.

**B. Constructed: one dyad, two realizations.** Take a French dialogue in
which a master says *tu* to a servant and the servant says *vous*. Render it
into modern English, where both say "you" (Brown and Gilman, p. 253: "Most
of us in speaking and writing English use only one pronoun of address").
Anchor the swap on the persons in both. In French, the swap moves the
address configuration. In the English rendering, it leaves the pronoun
layer fixed, unless the asymmetry is carried elsewhere (titles, "sir").
So one realization is changed by the swap on this layer and the other is
not. No attested French text and English translation were checked for
this: Molière's *Dom Juan*, with Dom Juan's *tu* to Sganarelle, is a lead
the search did not verify.

**C. Constructed: a teasing round and its mirror image.** Three friends a,
b and c sit at a round table. In one round each teases the person on their
left in turn: a→b, then b→c, then c→a. Let G = C₃ act on the persons by
r: a ↦ b ↦ c ↦ a.

- r fixes the round's configuration {a→b, b→c, c→a}, so the act is
  unchanged. It moves a one-way reprimand {a→b} to {b→c}, so that act is
  changed. The contrast of part A recurs within one medium.
- The round also has an order in time. On the clockwise round, r carries
  each turn to the next one (a→b to b→c). On the anticlockwise round,
  where each teases the person on their right (a→c, then c→b, then b→a),
  r carries each turn to the one before (a→c to b→a). Read through one
  fixed character of the cycle of turns, the clockwise round gives
  χ₁(r^k) = ω^k and the anticlockwise one χ₂(r^k) = ω^(2k), with
  ω = e^(2πi/3). These are [CLAIM-tmpt76qw](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt76qw.md)'s two nontrivial characters.
- Draw the clockwise round with an arrow from each teaser to the teased,
  and print the drawing mirror-reversed. If G is identified by a label of
  the medium, "rotate clockwise on the page", the mirror turns that
  rotation into r⁻¹ = r², and the drawing is read with χ₁∘α = χ₂, where
  α(g) = g²: it appears to show the other round. If G is identified by
  the persons (faces, names), the drawing still shows a teasing b, and the
  character is χ₁.

**A near-miss, documented.** Malinowski, *Argonauts of the Western Pacific*
(Routledge, 1922), ch. III §I (Project Gutenberg #55822): on the Kula
circuit, "In the direction of the hands of a clock, moves constantly one of
these kinds—long necklaces of red shell, called soulava ... In the opposite
direction moves the other kind—bracelets of white shell called mwali."
Reversing the orientation exchanges two different kinds of transaction,
and the participants anchor the orientation in geography, not in a label.
But rotating the ring is not a symmetry of the situation: the islands are
different communities, so it is a symmetry of the bare cycle only. And
Kula gifts are exchanges, not speech. It is the "labels, not situation"
warning in documented form.

## What it can show

That [CLAIM-132](../claims.d/CLAIM-132.md)'s distinction of stabilizers, the one-way reprimand moved by
the swap and the mutual configuration fixed, is not an artifact of
[CASE-004](CASE-004.md)'s stipulated scene. It matches a documented, cross-linguistic
distinction of address norms, and competent judgement attaches to it ("the
overthrow of the norm is more shocking"). For [CLAIM-137](../claims.d/CLAIM-137.md) that is support at
the level [CLAIM-132](../claims.d/CLAIM-132.md) allows. The invariant here is a feature of the G-set,
its stabilizer, and for C₂ the permutation character detects it: a C₂-set
with f fixed points and m free orbits has character (f + 2m, f), which
determines f and m.

Part B shows a rendering that identifies the power dyad with the solidarity
dyad on one layer: a collapse in [CLAIM-011](../claims.d/CLAIM-011.md)'s sense, where a distinction the
source kept apart is not kept apart by the target.

Part C gives [CLAIM-tmpt76qw](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt76qw.md) a group on which its point can bite. C₂ has no
nontrivial automorphism, so in parts A and B anchoring cannot fail. C₃ has
one, g ↦ g², and it swaps χ₁ and χ₂. Anchored on the persons, only the
identity survives, which is the reply in [CLAIM-137](../claims.d/CLAIM-137.md)'s note of 2026-10-10;
anchored on a label of the medium, the mirror image reads as the other
round.

## What it cannot show

- **The cross-realization condition is met only by construction.** Part A
  documents two kinds of address, one changed by the swap and one fixed.
  It does not document one act realized twice, once in a medium the swap
  changes and once in a medium it fixes. Part B builds that, and no
  attested pair of texts stands behind it.
- **The symmetry acts on possible configurations, not on any one
  situation.** A master and a servant are not interchangeable in fact. The
  swap acts on the space of address configurations, as in [CLAIM-132](../claims.d/CLAIM-132.md), and
  the "swapped" dyad is a counterfactual that Brown and Gilman attest only
  as a breach of the norm.
- **A stabilizer is not a character.** The case shows that the act's
  identity follows the stabilizer. It does not show that a character, as
  opposed to a stabilizer, carries it. For C₂ the two coincide; in
  general they do not, as [CLAIM-132](../claims.d/CLAIM-132.md)'s permutation characters show.
- **The strongest reply.** A critic pressing [CLAIM-138](../claims.d/CLAIM-138.md)'s Newman point
  would say that the anchoring "on the persons"
  is where all the work is done, and that it is supplied by the analyst,
  not by any invariant: the character tells the rounds apart only after
  the persons have been identified across the media. That is
  [CLAIM-tmpt76qw](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt76qw.md)'s own conclusion, and part C shows it rather than
  answering it.
- **Part C's characters depend on a choice.** Which round "carries ω" rests
  on a fixed character of the cycle of turns, so on time order being
  anchored in both realizations. A drawing has no time; its arrows stand in
  for it.
- **What would close the gap.** An attested source with nonreciprocal T/V
  address and an English translation that flattens it, such as a checked
  edition of *Dom Juan* with a published English version, read for whether
  the asymmetry survives in titles or elsewhere. Then a reader test,
  sketched here because the search report gives none, which could serve as
  items for the discriminating study ([CASE-tmp1uzfn](CASE-tmp1uzfn.md)): give readers of the source and of the rendering
  the original exchange and the swapped one, and ask "Is this the same
  kind of exchange, with the roles reversed, or a different kind?". The
  construction predicts that source readers call the swapped power dyad a
  different act and the swapped solidarity dyad the same one, and that
  readers of the flattened rendering cannot tell the power dyad from the
  solidarity dyad on the pronouns.
