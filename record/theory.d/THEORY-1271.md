# THEORY-1271: Mossio, Montévil and Longo argue that closure of constraints both stabilizes and propagates variation: it regenerates each constraint beyond the time scale at which it is conserved, and removes a variation only if the variation does not reach the constraints that regenerate the varied one; when it does reach them, the variation propagates and the system either loses closure and disintegrates or comes to realize a new closure, which they say a rock, a flame or a table cannot do

## Source

Mossio, Montévil and Longo (2016), [LIT-1888](../literature.d/LIT-1888.md), §5.1–5.2 (pp. 9–12) of
the authors' HAL manuscript; read in [NOTE-1461](../notes.d/NOTE-1461.md).

## What was actually shown

**Stability.** A constraint is conserved only at its own time scale τ.
"At longer time scales, they must be regenerated or repaired ... because
of closure, the maintenance of each constraint is (among other things)
dependent on the activity of other organized constraints." The result is
"a kind of 'organizational inertia'" (p. 11).

**The condition.** Conservative stabilization "would occur in those cases
in which local variation does not affect the constraints in charge of
generating the one (or set) being affected. Indeed, because of closure,
generative constraints are themselves – directly or indirectly –
dependent on the constraint undergoing variation" (p. 11).

**Propagation.** Otherwise "the variation can propagate through the
various functional constraints and two outcomes are possible. Either the
resulting system cannot realize closure anymore, in which case it is not
viable and disintegrates. Or, it does realize a new closure through
cumulative stability" (p. 12). In a rock, a flame or a table, "a local
variation cannot induce other local variations, which would result in a
global reconfiguration ... no global variation results from local
variation" (p. 12).

**Kind of support.** Argument and examples (the Na⁺/K⁺ pump, transcription
errors, non-genetic inheritance). No model, no measurement.

## What this does not say

- **That the structural condition suffices.** Even when a variation does
  not reach the generators, regeneration recovers only if the maintaining
  dynamics are stable. The record's constructions show that maintenance
  can fail at a saddle-node while the present coupling looks healthy
  ([CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md), [CLAIM-521](../claims.d/CLAIM-521.md)).
- **How stability could be observed.** The paper infers stability from
  the dependence structure; state interventions alone cannot show where
  maintenance is realized ([CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)).
- **That only organisms reach a new closure.** The paper attributes it to
  organized systems in general; Baedke's demarcation ([THEORY-514](THEORY-514.md)) uses
  it as the mark of organisms and says the restriction is asserted.
- **That non-organized systems cannot reorganize globally.** Three
  examples are not an argument; multistable dissipative structures pass
  between regimes after local perturbation, and whether that counts
  against the claim depends on what "global reconfiguration" requires.
