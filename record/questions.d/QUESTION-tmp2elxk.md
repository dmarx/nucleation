# QUESTION-tmp2elxk: What does a governed record such as Luria do to the perspectives of the collaborators who keep it: stabilize how they evolve, synchronize them, or make them converge, and can it do one without the others?

## Why it is a question

The owner's turn, U8 of the exchange "Explain First Contact RL"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b037165-3a1c-83ea-9d7c-311c7807acd3), came
after a discussion of a self as a loosely coupled organization of
perspectives:

> luria as stabilizing perspectival evolution(?) synchronization (?) and
> convergence (?) across collaborators

Each of the three words names a different effect a shared record could
have on the people who keep it:

- **stabilizing their evolution**: the claims, objections and revisions
  they produce keep their identity and history, so later work builds on
  earlier work instead of re-deriving it (CLAIM-177's failure, CLAIM-182's
  remedy);
- **synchronizing them**: they address the same objects, know which
  version is current and what a change obliges them to reconsider;
- **making them converge**: their interpretations or beliefs become alike.

What makes it open is that the three can be had separately, and the record
has reasons to want the first two without the third: CLAIM-276 says a
population can raise the coherence of a shared interpretation while
lowering its fidelity and its plurality, and CLAIM-181 says the record's
grammar can force contributions into its existing forms.

## What would count as an answer

A statement, for each pair, of whether a governed record can produce one
without the other, with a case or a study for each separation; and, for
convergence, a statement of what converges (beliefs, vocabulary,
procedure) when anything does. A study design is LEAD-tmponn7c.
