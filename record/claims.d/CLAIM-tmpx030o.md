# CLAIM-tmpx030o: Regulation governs the evolving relationship between the demands an industrial ecology can recognize and formulate and the responses it can organize, realize and maintain, so adaptive governance is the viable co-development of inquiry and response capacities, without the evaluation that joins them becoming self-confirming

## The claim

A50's payoff sets two theses side by side. "Previously": "Regulation
governs the evolving repertoire of temporal responses to demands." "Now":
"Regulation governs the evolving relationship between the demands an
industrial ecology can recognize and formulate, and the responses it can
organize, realize, and maintain."

From §8: "Adaptive governance = viable co-development of inquiry and
response capacities", whose viability criterion "asks whether the
organization can continue maintaining an adequate relationship between the
two, including the ability to revise its question language, acceptance
criteria, and response architecture." "Higher-order governance becomes the
maintenance of institutions capable of doing all three without allowing
the resulting evaluation process to become self-confirming."

And its closing formulation: "A viable organization is not one that has
already found the right answers. It is one that preserves the capacity to
encounter consequential new questions, develop adequate answers, and revise
the relationship between questioning and answering without losing the
conditions that make either activity possible."

## What it rests on

The two capacities and the asymmetry of their loss ([CLAIM-tmp11j0f](CLAIM-tmp11j0f.md)), and
the reading of a regulatory equilibrium as a stabilized question–answer
relationship that can confirm itself ([CLAIM-tmp9in65](CLAIM-tmp9in65.md)). It revises A42's
thesis ([CLAIM-tmpcag0t](CLAIM-tmpcag0t.md)) by adding the query side, and specializes A34's
"regulation governs possibility" ([CLAIM-tmptfpf7](CLAIM-tmptfpf7.md)) to that relationship.
A50 adds that it "is also not necessarily a zero-sum game".
