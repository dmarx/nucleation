# CASE-174: Paulinella chromatophora: a photosynthetic organelle in the making, from an endosymbiosis independent of the plastids', its genome reduced to about a third of its coding capacity, and hundreds of host proteins imported into it, most encoded by the host's own genes rather than transferred from the symbiont

## The case

*Paulinella chromatophora* is a thecate amoeba with photosynthetic
inclusions, chromatophores. They descend from a cyanobacterium acquired in a
primary endosymbiosis independent of, and much later than, the one that gave
plants their plastids: about 60 million years ago, by [LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md)'s estimate.

- **Reduction ([LIT-1621](../literature.d/LIT-1621.md)).** The chromatophore genome is 1.02 Mb with
  867 protein-coding genes, 26% of a free-living relative's. Pseudogenes
  show reduction still under way. It keeps the photosynthesis genes and
  lacks essential amino-acid and cofactor pathways, so it depends wholly on
  its host.
- **Transfer and import ([LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md)).** More than thirty expressed genes
  moved to the amoeba's nucleus. Three photosystem I subunits encoded by
  transferred genes are made in the host cytoplasm, imported into the
  chromatophores, apparently via the Golgi, and assembled with
  chromatophore-encoded subunits.
- **In context ([LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md)).** A 2024 review counts hundreds of imported
  proteins in such recently evolved organelles. It asks whether import
  still separates organelles from endosymbionts.

## What it can show

- **The organelle route, recent and separate.** Gene transfer plus import
  gives the host regulatory control (C) over a former organism. This is the
  route the mealybug ([CASE-171](CASE-171.md)) and aphid ([CASE-173](CASE-173.md)) cases do not
  take ([QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)).
- **A second independent run.** Since it is independent of the plastids,
  it shows which steps recur when a photosynthetic organelle is made.

Documented in works the record has not read. What it shows rests on their
abstracts until they are read.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (batch 2): the review read

Sørensen et al. ([LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md), read in [NOTE-1241](../notes.d/NOTE-1241.md)) update the figures this
case takes from its unread sources:

- **Acquisition date.** "~ 90–140 million years ago". [LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md)'s 60
  million years is an older estimate.
- **Genome.** About 1 Mbp, with "878–860" protein-coding genes and about
  two-thirds of the original coding capacity lost. The case's "26% kept"
  uses [LIT-1621](../literature.d/LIT-1621.md)'s comparator.
- **Nuclear genes.** About 60 come from the chromatophore and about 170
  from other bacteria.
- **Imported proteins.** They number "hundreds", "mostly from the host
  itself". The review says transfer played "only a circumstantial role"
  ([THEORY-1100](../theory.d/THEORY-1100.md)). So the previous title's "more than thirty genes moved
  to the host nucleus and their proteins imported back" overstated
  transfer, and the title is revised.
- **The subunits.** The review names two transferred photosystem I
  subunits shown to be imported, PsaE and PsaK. The case's "three" comes
  from [LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md)'s unread abstract and is unchecked.
- **The route.** Import runs through the Golgi, with a bipartite
  N-terminal targeting peptide on long proteins. About 20% of long imported
  proteins serve the chromatophore's DNA replication and gene expression,
  which gives the host control of both (C).
