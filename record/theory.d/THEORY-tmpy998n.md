# THEORY-tmpy998n: In visual search among up to about 36 well-spaced items, a target that alone carries one colour or one distinctive shape part is found in time almost independent of the number of items, while a target identified only by a combination of features its neighbours carry separately takes time that grows linearly with the number of items, about twice as fast when the target is absent, the pattern of a serial self-terminating scan, and practice did not remove it; that conjunctions rather than similarity cause it is the paper's reading, not its result

## Source

Treisman and Gelade (1980), [LIT-tmp9g55n](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9g55n.md), Cognitive Psychology 12(1),
read in full in [NOTE-tmpe7gfj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpe7gfj.md) from a scan of the typeset article:
Experiments I–IV (search), V–VII (texture segregation), VIII–IX
(identity and location under brief exposure) and the General
Conclusions.

## What was actually shown

**Feature against conjunction (Experiment I).** Distractors were brown Ts
and green Xs in near-equal numbers, on cards of 1, 5, 15 or 30 items.
A feature target (a blue letter, or an S) gave positive slopes of 3.8
ms/item for colour and 2.5 for shape, not linear in display size. A
conjunction target (a green T, sharing its colour with the Xs and its
shape with the Ts) gave 28.7 ms/item present and 67.1 absent, linear
(over 99% of variance), a ratio of 0.43, which the authors call "quite
close to half. These results suggest that search is serial and
self-terminating with a scanning rate of about 60 msec per item." With
one item there was no conjunction cost. Up to 13 blocks of practice
gave "no sign of a switch from serial to parallel search".

**Difficulty is not seriality (Experiments II–IV).** Making a
conjunction's colours hard to tell apart nearly tripled the slopes
(55.1/92.4 against 20.5/39.5) but kept them linear with the 1:2 ratio:
"we can no longer suppose that search becomes serial only when it is
difficult." A single-feature target hard to discriminate, an ellipse of
intermediate size among small and large ones, was slow, up to about 3 s,
but negatively accelerated: "search can be slow but need not be serial
in the sense of checking each item in turn." With letters, conjunction
sets (distractor parts that could recombine into the target) were slower
and linear; sets more confusable with the target by published confusion
matrices, but unable to form it, were faster and nonlinear.

**Absence.** Even in feature search, target-absent trials grew with
display size, 25.1 ms/item against 3.1 for present. Misses rose with
display size in conjunction search (8.2% at 30 items; 20.7% at 36 for
confusable colours).

**Conditions.** Hand-made cards of stencilled letters in a tachistoscope,
at most 30–36 well-spaced items, few and distinct colours, unlimited
viewing, accuracy stressed, six subjects per experiment. Seriality is
inferred from slopes and their ratio; no eye movements were recorded.
Features are detected in parallel only "within the limits set by
acuity, discriminability, and lateral interference". The result could
have come out the other way: conjunction slopes could have been
nonlinear, could have fallen with practice, or could have tracked
confusability rather than conjunction structure. None did.

## What this does not say

- **That similarity plays no part in search.** The paper argues that
  conjunctions, not similarity, make search item-serial, and its
  Experiments III–IV are its evidence. The rival account, that difficulty
  grows continuously with target–distractor similarity and falls with
  distractor–distractor similarity, with no sharp serial/parallel divide,
  is Duncan and Humphreys 1989 ([LIT-tmp95pcw](../literature.d/LIT-tmp95pcw.md)). The record could not obtain
  it and holds the disagreement with one side read. The slopes are the
  finding; the mechanism, feature maps and serial binding by attention,
  is Proposed at best on this record's reading.
- **That anything unique in one attribute pops out.** Which shape
  properties are features is, in the authors' words, not predictable a
  priori: an S among Ts and Xs popped out, an ellipse differing in size
  did not. Bricks of one colour that differ in stud count or length may
  be like the ellipses, not like the S.
- **That it holds at the scale of a pile.** Nothing was tested beyond 36
  items, with ten-way colour discrimination, with similar colours, or
  with occluding, overlapping items that must be moved.
- **That binning converts conjunction search into feature search.** The
  General Conclusions predict, untested, that spatially grouping the
  distractors would remove the serial scan. It is the authors'
  prediction, not their result.
- **Anything about deadlines.** Viewing was unlimited. The absent-trial
  slopes show that confirming absence costs a scan; they do not measure
  search failing under a time limit.
