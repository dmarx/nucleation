# LIT-tmpj284p: Bridging emotion theory and neurobiology through dynamic systems modeling

Marc D. Lewis (2005), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 28(2):169–194 —
DOI-10.1017/S0140525X0500004X

## Key takeaways

- **Against linear appraisal** (§2.3, p. 172). Sequential appraisal
  models, Scherer's among them, keep emotion out of the causal chain that
  forms the appraisal. On Lewis's reading, emotion components act from
  the start.
- **Self-organization** (§3.2, pp. 174–175). Positive feedback grows new
  patterns. Negative feedback stabilizes them, and through entrainment
  "individual elements or groups of elements lose their independence and
  become embedded in a larger regime" (p. 174). "The outcomes of
  self-organization are fixed or periodic (or chaotic) organizational
  patterns, called attractors" (p. 175), and for an individual "only one
  of these converges at a time" (p. 175). Jumps between them are "phase
  transitions or bifurcations" (p. 175).
- **The model** (§3.3, pp. 175–177). An emotional interpretation (EI) is
  triggered by a perturbation, followed by nucleation. "For an EI to “take
  over” the psychological system, a phase of self-amplification must
  follow" (p. 176). It then self-stabilizes: "Despite the ambiguous nature
  of the world, and even in the presence of conflicting cues,
  appraisal–emotion configurations resolve into stable patterns"
  (p. 177). A superordinate appraisal may "enslave" its constituents
  (p. 177), after Haken.
- **Neural integration** (§§5–6, pp. 185–193). Nested loops,
  neuromodulation and vertical integration. "Vertical integration
  assures that EIs have some degree of psychological momentum or
  imperturbability" and "not only maintains but also selects viable EIs"
  (p. 192). The novel prediction is that "the degree of phase synchrony
  in the theta range will correlate with measures of emotional relevance
  and subjective emotional feelings" (p. 186).
- **Timescales** (§3.3.4, §6.3). Stable EIs drive synaptic change, so
  "the accumulation of order at two very different time scales is
  considered interdependent" (p. 177).
- **Conclusion** (p. 193). "coherent appraisals are not antecedents of
  emotions, but emerging outcomes of interactions among constituent
  systems underlying appraisal and emotion."
