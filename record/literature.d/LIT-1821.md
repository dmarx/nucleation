  Nicholson (2026) reconstructs from the writings of the experimental
  biologist Paul Weiss (1898–1989) a non-formal concept of a system: a
  whole that keeps its configuration while its parts vary and turn over,
  whose parts are produced and constrained by the network they form (a
  triplet of processes each supplying conditions for the other two, no
  pair viable without the third), and which cannot be assembled stepwise
  or recovered from an inventory of its parts. He turns it against
  reductionism, genocentrism, determinism and mechanicism. The critiques
  are argued by analogy and conceptual analysis; only the one against
  determinism cites evidence, and none is presented in the paper. Weiss's
  one formula, that the whole varies far less than its parts' summed
  variances, is met by any aggregate whose whole is an average.

# LIT-1821: The Organism as a System: Insights from Paul Weiss

Nicholson, D. J. (2026). The organism as a system: Insights from Paul
Weiss. *Acta Biotheoretica*, 74(4), 34.

Read in full in [NOTE-1340](../notes.d/NOTE-1340.md).

## Why this is here

The owner's request. Weiss's system is defined by the network of
interactions generating each part. His simplest example is a triplet of
processes, each supplying conditions for the other two, which cannot be
assembled pairwise. That is the closure of constraints of [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md) and
[CLAIM-395](../claims.d/CLAIM-395.md), and it bears on [CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md) and [CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md)'s interiors that become
constraints.

The reading corrects one point. Nicholson quotes four definitions of
"system" from Weiss (1925, 1971, 1973, 1977), and each defines it by the
whole's persistence while its parts vary. The generation of each part by
the network of interactions, and the triplet, are a further property that
Nicholson states in §4 as the answer to how a system comes into being.
Weiss's members are processes supplying conditions, not constraints in
[TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s sense, and the triplet is stronger than closure: every member
depends on both others ([NOTE-1340](../notes.d/NOTE-1340.md)).
