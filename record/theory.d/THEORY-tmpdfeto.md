# THEORY-tmpdfeto: In Toner and Tu's continuum theory of flocking, the ordered phase of a rotation-invariant, number-conserving flock without Galilean invariance scales anomalously throughout the phase, not only at its transition: below four dimensions convective nonlinearities replace diffusive transport, so that the long-range order the linearized theory loses in two dimensions survives, with exponents argued to be exact there, and density fluctuations whose structure factor diverges as q → 0; an explicit preferred direction removes the anomalous fluctuations

## Source

Toner and Tu (1998), "Flocks, herds, and schools: A quantitative theory
of flocking", Physical Review E 58, 4828–4858, LIT-2970, read from the
arXiv preprint in NOTE-tmpcjw5n.

## The account

The linearized theory has an infrared divergence that "signifies the
destruction of long ranged order in the linearized model by
fluctuations, even for arbitrarily small noise ∆, in spatial dimensions
d ≤ 2" (p. 27), as at equilibrium. Below four dimensions "this slow,
diffusive transport of information is replaced by direct, convective
transport", which "suppresses the very fluctuations that give rise to it"
(p. 4), so in d = 2 "flocks exhibit a true, long-range ordered,
spontaneously broken symmetry state" (p. 2). There z = 6/5, ζ = 3/5,
χ = −1/5, by an argument (pp. 46–50) resting on all relevant vertices
being ∝ q⊥, "pseudo-Galilean invariance" at λ₁ = λρ, mass conservation,
and a flow to the type II fixed point they "consider it highly probable"
all flocks take. Density fluctuations "become infinitely bigger than
those in a fluid or an ideal gas": Cρ(q) ∝ q⊥^(−6/5) for q∥ ≪ q⊥ in
d = 2 (eqs. 1.10–1.11). The scaling belongs to "the low temperature fixed
point", not "the fixed point that controls the order-disorder
transition" (p. 64). A compass "will introduce a "mass"", after which
"there are no interesting fluctuations left at long distances and times"
(p. 63).

## What would have come out otherwise

The convective terms could have been irrelevant at d = 2, leaving the
linear theory's loss of order, as in the equilibrium XY model; the
density could have had a finite compressibility.

## What it does not say

- ΔN ∝ ⟨N⟩^φ, or "giant number fluctuations": the paper's object is the
  structure factor.
- That the exponents differ from simulation: it reports "good agreement".
- That every flocking rule is in the class: the universality is asserted,
  not derived from a rule.
- Anything certain for d > 2, which the authors leave open.
