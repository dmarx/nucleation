# NOTE-tmpatdt4: Abbott — Linked ecologies (through its French precursor)

## Contribution

Abbott revises *The System of Professions* (1988), which treated the
state, the public and the workplace as simple audiences that arbitrate
professions' jurisdictional claims. In fact "ces auditoires sont eux-mêmes
des structures interactionnelles complexes, dominées par les forces
écologiques" (these audiences are themselves complex interactional
structures, governed by ecological forces; p. 30). The precursor states
the general thesis ("nous devrions imaginer le monde social tout entier
sous forme d'écologies liées", we should imagine the whole social world as
linked ecologies; p. 30), works one case, and sketches the variables an
analysis of linked ecologies needs. The 2005 article, by its abstract,
adds universities as a third ecology, the concept of avatars, a second
licensing case (England) and four avatar cases.

## Key insight

An event in one ecology is also an event in another, and the actors in
each want it for their own reasons. "L'État ne constitue pas un auditoire
unifié, mais un système de petits groupes menant leurs propres petites
guerres pour leurs propres petites raisons" (the state is not a unified
audience but a system of small groups fighting their own small wars for
their own small reasons; p. 33). So licensing passed or lapsed according
to whether it served some political actor, not according to the merits
argued in medicine.

## Assumptions

- **An ecology is actors, locations and a mapping.** "Une écologie
  comprend trois éléments : des acteurs, des lieux et une fonction (au sens
  mathématique) associant les uns aux autres" (p. 44). In the professions:
  professions, tasks, jurisdiction. In politics: parties, officials,
  agencies and lobbies; the locations are "bundles" of political issues.
- **Between mechanism and atoms.** An ecology is less unified than a
  machine or an organism and more than the atoms of classical liberalism
  (p. 43).
- **Analytic and ontological at once.** Abbott takes "écologie" both as a
  way of seeing and as a claim about what is there, and says he will take
  both roads (p. 44).

## Key results

- **New York licensing, 1806–1907 (pp. 32–43).** Licensing was granted,
  removed and restored about eight times, with twenty or thirty failed
  bills (p. 39). Three passed bills are analysed from legislative journals.
  The 1844 repeal: "Le vote n'était pas un vote de parti" (the vote was not
  a party vote; p. 40); it split urban against rural counties, and gave
  radical Democrats an occasion for rhetoric on liberty and science, which
  Abbott quotes from the committee reports (p. 41). The 1872 act: again
  no party vote and no identifiable demographic pattern (p. 42). The 1907
  act passed unopposed when medical and Tammany elites were allied
  (pp. 42–43).
- **Asymmetry between the ecologies (pp. 45–47).** The medical ecology was
  small and its identities exclusive (an allopath was not a homeopath); the
  political one was large and its memberships overlapping. "Les acteurs du
  champ politique n'étaient donc point exclusifs" (p. 46). Licensing was a
  jurisdictional matter in medicine and a minor location in politics.
- **Tempo (pp. 47–48).** Licensing could be settled once for medicine;
  politics worked by urgency. "Cette différence de rythme entre les deux
  écologies explique que les alliés des médecins, allopathes comme
  homéopathes, n'ont pas toujours été les mêmes pendant les décennies de
  lutte sur la question du permis" (this difference of tempo explains why
  the doctors' allies were not always the same; pp. 47–48). Perhaps also
  "les médecins n'ont gagné que lorsqu'ils s'engagèrent dans des actions
  politiques d'importance générale et durable, comme la santé publique"
  (the doctors won only when they took up political causes of general and
  lasting importance, such as public health; p. 48). Note 12 adds that
  legislative arenas may look fixed only because they run "à un rythme
  beaucoup plus lent que les écologies politiques" (at a much slower
  tempo; p. 45).
- **Hinges (p. 48).** What one looks for are "les « charnières », les
  articulations entre les opérations des deux écologies" (hinges, the
  articulations between the two ecologies' operations). Licensing never
  became one: "La réglementation du permis ne devint jamais une question
  charnière parce que les acteurs intéressés ne pouvaient pas en jouer
  efficacement" (p. 48). The detailed analysis is deferred.
- **The omitted Bourdieu section (BOURD.pdf).** Ecologies differ from
  fields in having "no concept of dominance or subordination", in being
  more dynamic, and in endogenizing their actors and their own boundaries:
  "social things come from boundaries, rather than boundaries being
  produced around things or by regular oppositions". The resemblance
  between the two is "a kind of accidental resemblance".

## Concepts

- **Écologie (ecology)**: a system of many interacting, mostly independent
  elements, defined by actors, locations and the relation between them
  (pp. 43–44).
- **Écologies liées (linked ecologies)**: ecologies each of which is an
  environment for the others, so that events and alliances span them
  (pp. 30–31).
- **Charnière (hinge)**: an issue or articulation that works in both
  ecologies at once (p. 48); in the 2005 abstract, "issues or strategies
  that 'work' in both ecologies at once".
- **Bundle**: a loosely structured cluster of political issues, the
  political ecology's analogue of a jurisdiction (p. 44).

## Connections

It extends *The System of Professions* (1988) and *Department and
Discipline* (1999), and borrows from urban ecology (von Thünen, Burgess,
Hawley), Long's "ecology of games" and Laumann and Knoke's
organizational state. The Bourdieu section sets it against the field
concept and claims a pragmatist, processual lineage. Sewell's schemas and
resources (LIT-1383) and Wacquant's habitus (LIT-1377) are the
neighbouring accounts the exchange named with it; Abbott's own contrast
with Bourdieu is the closest he comes to them.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-423 (timescale and authority in a political collective).**
  The LIT says A30 called linked ecologies "even closer to the expanded
  domain" than its own "regime ecology". The precursor bears that out, and
  on the claim's own defeater. Its tempo result is the claim's link
  between speed and influence in one historical case: the political
  ecology's urgency and medicine's settle-once horizon decided who the
  doctors' allies were, and the doctors prevailed only by joining durable
  political causes. That is a case, from legislative journals, of temporal
  horizons affecting which alliances form and hold. The claim names
  Pierson and Putnam as the accounts that might already state it; Abbott
  (2003, 2005) should be added to that list. The precursor states tempo
  differences as an explanatory variable, not as an account of which
  component gains authority over another, so it does not by itself defeat
  the claim.
- **CLAIM-331 (higher levels are slower).** Note 12 is a small
  confirmation and a caution: arenas look like fixed structures from
  outside because they change more slowly, and seen close up they are
  ecologies too.
- **CLAIM-417 (selves as organizational perspectives).** The claim's
  defeater lists linked ecologies among the accounts that might explain
  the same transitions with fewer assumptions. The precursor is about
  jurisdictions and alliances, not perspectives, and has nothing on what
  a perspective is. Its one point of contact is the omitted section's
  thesis that "social things come from boundaries": actors in an ecology
  "merge, divide, and reshape", which is the carrier-level fluidity the
  claim needs and the self vocabulary lacked. It is a statement of
  orientation, not an argument.
- **CLAIM-416 (competition over tests).** The audience that judges a
  profession's claim is itself an arena of competition, and it judges for
  its own reasons. That is the claim's point that the judge of a contest
  is not neutral, from the side of the judge. The 1844 repeal is a clear
  instance: the test of medical competence was set by legislators using it
  for a contest of their own.
- **CASE-133 (party realignment).** Not touched directly. The
  precursor's New York politics (Whigs, Hunkers, Tammany, upstate
  Republicans) is the same nineteenth-century party system, and it found
  that licensing votes did not follow party lines.
- **The LIT's summary is corrected.** It said actors "seek alliances
  across ecologies ('hinges')". Hinges are not alliances: they are issues
  or strategies that work in both ecologies at once, and alliances form
  around them. The summary also lacked avatars, the article's second
  concept.
- **THEORY warranted?** None. The precursor is one case, a sketch, and not
  the work the record holds.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- Not the published article. Hinges are undeveloped here and avatars,
  universities and the English case are absent.
- One case. The vote analyses are counts by party and county from
  legislative journals, without tests, and Abbott himself offers a second
  "revisionist" reading in which licensing simply did not matter to the
  legislature (p. 39).
- The variables (size, number, exclusivity, tempo, homeomorphism) are
  listed, and Abbott says plainly that he cannot yet state which conditions
  produce which outcomes (p. 46).
- OCR of a scan: quoted passages were checked against the page images, the
  rest was read from OCR text.

## Open questions

- What makes an issue a hinge? The precursor says licensing never became
  one because no actor could play it, which is close to circular until the
  article's fuller account is read.
- Does a difference in tempo between linked ecologies predict which side
  sets the terms of an alliance? That is the form CLAIM-423 would need,
  and the precursor's case points to it without testing it.
- The published article should be read when a copy is supplied.
