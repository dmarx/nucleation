# CLAIM-873: The independent scoring that keeps a judgement–observation loop from confirming itself is scoring that reads nothing of the loop's state, so share-dependent advantage is scoring that passes through what the loop tests, and authority that reinforces itself against independent evidence too weak to overturn it does not meet the defeater of the claim that such evidence is the anchor

## The claim

**Reading (a), an alternative to [CLAIM-874](CLAIM-874.md).** [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) says what keeps a judgement–observation loop from confirming itself is evidence "whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests". Its first defeater is "A loop whose evidence is scored independently of the interpretation it tests ... shown to lock in all the same". The curation entry of 2026-11-11 (030317) asked whether authority that reinforces itself, held against independent evidence too weak to overturn it, meets that defeater. On this reading it does not, because "scored independently" in [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) is the second sense of [TERM-147](../terms.d/TERM-147.md): a rule that reads nothing of the loop's state, the authority the interpretation holds included. Share-dependent advantage reads that authority, so it is scoring that passes through what the loop tests.

Four reasons.

1. **The defeater is about the loop's scoring, not one channel in it.** It asks for "a loop whose evidence is scored independently". In [CASE-155](../cases.d/CASE-155.md) part 4 the score is ℓ′ = ℓ + h + c(2π − 1). The h channel is scored independently and the c channel is not. Lock-in when |h| < c is a loop in which an independent channel is outweighed by one that is not. [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s first "What it does not say" already covers that case: "the independent channel anchors it only as far as it outweighs the selected one". The pooled loop is not a loop whose evidence is scored independently.
2. **The record names c = 0 independent scoring.** [CASE-155](../cases.d/CASE-155.md): "Raising |h|, more diagnostic evidence, or lowering c, independent scoring, moves the threshold", and lock-in needs only "scoring that depends on who governs". [TERM-066](../terms.d/TERM-066.md), the capture independent scoring is meant to prevent, is evaluation "driven by the interpretation or party being evaluated". [CLAIM-466](CLAIM-466.md) names independent scoring as the remedy for self-scoring, which is [CASE-162](../cases.d/CASE-162.md)'s c.
3. **Share-dependent advantage is self-confirmation.** The judgement that A should govern is scored by how much A governs. Where the interpretation and authority are one state, as in [CASE-155](../cases.d/CASE-155.md) ([CLAIM-444](CLAIM-444.md)), reading the share is reading the interpretation, so even the first sense counts c as passing through it. The second sense keeps that verdict when the two are separate states, as in [CASE-312](../cases.d/CASE-312.md).
4. **The pure cases are out of scope on either reading.** [CLAIM-408](CLAIM-408.md)'s pitchfork has no evidence and no interpretation, and Arthur's adopters ([THEORY-863](../theory.d/THEORY-863.md)) choose by payoff, with no judgement scored against observation. They show entrenchment of a share, not a judgement–observation loop scored independently. And [CLAIM-408](CLAIM-408.md)'s κ, a constraint that restores equal shares when κ > α, is [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s second anchor, marginals fixed from outside. Its lock-in comes when that constraint weakens, as the claim says it should.

**What follows.** [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) stands, and its term is closed in the second sense. Its wording would then name the authority the interpretation holds beside the interpretation, for example: evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests, nor through the authority that interpretation holds. That would be a revision of a held claim, and it is the owner's.

**The cost.** On this reading, the claim's bullet "Independent scoring prevents it" rests on more than [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md). [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md) shows only that γ ≈ 0 removes the coupling-driven instability, with authority's own persistence assumed below one (δ < 1). That c = 0, or |h| ≥ c, removes share-dependent lock-in is [CASE-155](../cases.d/CASE-155.md) part 4's result, which [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) does not yet cite. Adding it to the claim's `grounds` would also be the owner's.

## What it does not say

- That share-dependent advantage is harmless or rare. It is a route to lock-in against the evidence ([CASE-155](../cases.d/CASE-155.md), [CASE-312](../cases.d/CASE-312.md)). The reading only places it on the side of the disjunction the claim already treats as failure.
- That the first sense is unused. [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md) and Regime B are stated in it, and [TERM-147](../terms.d/TERM-147.md) keeps both senses.

The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
