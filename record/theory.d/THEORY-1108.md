# THEORY-1108: On Imachi and colleagues' cultivation of Candidatus Prometheoarchaeum syntrophicum MK-D1, the first Asgard archaeon grown in the laboratory is a small, internally simple coccus with long branching protrusions that degrades amino acids only in syntrophy, handing hydrogen and formate to partners that can be exchanged, and from its genome and other Asgard genomes the authors infer that the Asgard ancestor was likewise dependent on metabolic partners; the isolate is a modern relative separated from the host lineage by about two billion years, grown only in co-culture, and the entangle–engulf–endogenize model built on it is a hypothesis the authors call not definitive

## Source

Imachi et al. (2020), *Nature* 577: 519–525, [LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1238](../notes.d/NOTE-1238.md) from the PubMed Central copy of the version of record.
"Isolation of an Asgard archaeon", "Cell biology, physiology and
metabolism", "Extant and ancestral features", "New insights into
eukaryogenesis", the Methods, the captions of Figures 1–5, and Extended
Data Figures 1–7 and Tables 1–3.

## What was actually shown

- **Syntrophy, by experiment.** Molybdate and 2-bromoethanesulfonate,
  which inhibit the sulfate-reducing and methanogenic partners,
  "significantly impaired" MK-D1's growth. High hydrogen or formate
  "completely suppressed" it. Isotope labelling showed both H₂ and
  formate production. MK-D1 grew with Methanogenium and, separately,
  with Methanobacterium sp. MO-MB1.
- **Slow growth.** Doubling in about 14–25 days, a lag of 30–60 days, no
  improvement across temperatures or fourteen substrate conditions.
- **Simple inside, elaborate outside.** No visible organelle-like
  inclusions by cryo-EM (14 images) and TEM (131). Vesicles, blebs and
  branching protrusions 80–100 nm wide.
- **Placement.** Sister to eukaryotes in ribosomal-protein trees of
  cultured organisms. 80 eukaryotic signature proteins, 23 among the 500
  most highly expressed genes.
- **The ancestor, by inference.** Amino-acid catabolism, NiFe
  hydrogenases and restricted biosynthesis are found across most Asgard
  genomes, and some catabolic genes are conserved across phyla. Hence an
  ancestor that "depended on metabolic partners, although we do not
  reject the possibility of other additional lifestyles".

What could have failed: MK-D1 could have grown alone under the
conditions tested, or kept growing with its partners inhibited. It did
neither. It could have shown internal membranes, as earlier suggestions
from genomes expected. It did not.

## What this does not say

- **Not that the host of eukaryogenesis was simple.** MK-D1 is a modern
  relative. The authors set "roughly 2 billion years" between it and the
  lineage that became LECA.
- **Not that MK-D1 depends on particular partners.** Its partners are
  interchangeable. The dependence is on a service, H₂ and formate
  removal, and the inference to "a guild, not a lineage" is mine.
- **Not that it is the closest relative of eukaryotes.** It is the
  closest among cultured organisms. Metagenome-derived genomes were left
  out of the trees.
- **Not the hydrogen hypothesis.** In E3 the host makes H₂, and the
  authors favour an interaction other than H₂ transfer as the driver of
  endosymbiosis.
- **Not a tested model.** Entanglement, engulfment by protrusions and
  endogenization are "a hypothetical model" and "not definitive".
