# LIT-233: Data-Dependent Generalization Bounds via Variable-Size Compressibility

Milad Sefidgaran, Abdellatif Zaidi (2023), *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 70(9) (2024) 6572-6595* — [ARXIV-2303.05369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05369)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-806](../notes.d/NOTE-806.md).*

- **Variable-size books.** Only the first e^{Σ_i R_{S_i,W_i}} codewords
  may cover a given block of runs (Def. 2). Then gen(S, W) ≤
  √(4σ²(R_{S,W} + log(√(2n)/δ))/(2n − 1) + ε) with probability ≥ 1 − δ
  (Theorem 1).
- **PAC-Bayes is the lossless case.** The rate log dP_{W|S}/dP(W) against
  a fixed prior meets the coding condition at ε = 0 (Theorem 2, finite
  alphabets). The bound is then the disintegrated PAC-Bayes bound. The
  rate is a Shannon code-length difference, as in Blum–Langford's
  PAC-MDL.
- **One family.** Theorems 3–5 are general change-of-measure bounds on any
  f(S, W). They recover Rivasplata et al.'s PAC-Bayes bound, Bégin et
  al.'s Rényi bound, Xu–Raginsky, and [LIT-236](LIT-236.md)'s tail bound.
- **Lossy PAC-Bayes (Proposition 1).** A surrogate within ε keeps the
  bound finite for deterministic algorithms with continuous parameters,
  where classical PAC-Bayes is infinite.
- **Trajectories (Theorem 7).** The mean generalisation error over
  iterations is at most √((RD(S, ε; π_S) + log(√(2n)M/δ))/(2n − 1) +
  4Lε). RD is the trajectory's rate-distortion function for the given
  sample. M depends on μ and absorbs I(W^T; S).
- **The experiment tests an ordering, not the bound.** On CIFAR-10, with M
  and 4Lε dropped, the estimated rate and the test gap both fall with the
  learning rate. For FCN4 the plotted term is about 3, vacuous for 0–1
  loss.
- **A hand-built surrogate gives O(1/n)** on Haghifam et al.'s example,
  where Xu–Raginsky's bound is Ω(1) (Appendix A).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 at the owner's request, from a list they grouped under
the heading *Relating K-complexity to minimum sufficient statistics*. Read
in full on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-806](../notes.d/NOTE-806.md). It is the record's source for the
variable-rate framework that joins rate-distortion and PAC-Bayes bounds,
filed as [THEORY-263](../theory.d/THEORY-263.md), and for the trajectory bound, filed with its
caveats as [THEORY-266](../theory.d/THEORY-266.md).

It generalises [LIT-236](LIT-236.md) and recovers Xu–Raginsky ([LIT-347](LIT-347.md)), so it bears on
[THEORY-028](../theory.d/THEORY-028.md), which still calls it unread. The answer to the question the
seed left, whether the variable rate is MDL in disguise, is: in the
PAC-Bayes case it is a Shannon code length against a prior, not a
Kolmogorov or two-part code, and no link to minimal sufficient statistics
is made. The same group's [LIT-245](LIT-245.md) applies the framework to
representations. No instruction for machine-learning practice.
