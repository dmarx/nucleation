# THEORY-476: If continuant persons are maximal aggregates of person-stages interrelated by the psychological relation R, then the relation of belonging to one person is R itself, so Parfit's premises do not show that identity is not what matters; the price is that persons overlap in fission and are counted by a relation weaker than identity

## Source

Lewis (1976), "Survival and Identity", in Rorty (ed.), *The Identities of
Persons*, pp. 17–40, [LIT-446](../literature.d/LIT-446.md), read in full in [NOTE-373](../notes.d/NOTE-373.md). Postscript A of the
1983 reprint was read beside it and is not the paper's text.

## What was actually shown

**Two answers, both kept.** "What matters in survival is mental continuity
and connectedness" (p. 17), and "what matters in survival is survival",
identity of the I now and the surviving I (p. 18). If they disagreed, "we
would have to prefer the platitude of common sense" (p. 18).

**Parfit's argument, with its premises granted.** (a) Identity is one-one
and not a matter of degree; (b) mental continuity and connectedness need
not be; so (c) what matters is not identity (p. 19). Lewis grants (a) and
(b) and refuses to redefine "identity" (p. 20).

**Different relata (pp. 20–23).** Continuity and connectedness hold among
"more or less momentary person-stages"; identity among "temporally extended
continuant persons" (p. 20). "Even if you survive, your present stage is
not identical to any future stage." The fair comparison is between R and
the I-relation, which holds between two stages when some one continuant
person has both (pp. 21, 23). If I is R, "something is a continuant person
if and only if it is a maximal R-interrelated aggregate of person-stages"
(p. 22). The argument that I must share identity's form "wrongly takes it
for granted that every person-stage is a stage of one and only one
continuant person" (p. 23).

**Fission and fusion as overlap (pp. 24–25).** The I-relation fails to be
transitive "if and only if there is partial overlap among continuant
persons". In fission two continuants share their pre-fission stages.

**Counting (pp. 26–29).** With overlap there are more continuants than
stages at a time, so we count stages: continuants are identical-at-t when
their stages at t are identical (p. 26). "Tensed identity is not a kind of
identity" (p. 27). Two roads that merge for a stretch are the model, and a
name for someone who will divide is harmlessly ambiguous until the
division (pp. 27–29).

**Degrees (pp. 32–36).** Naive degrees fail: an aggregate can pass the
R-test to degree 0.9 and fail maximality to degree 0.88, and so be "a
person only to degree 0.02" (p. 34). Treating each cut-off on R as a
delineation, the measure of delineations on which two stages are
I-related equals the measure on which they are R-related (pp. 35–36).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The I-relation could have been
shown to inherit identity's form whatever persons are. It does not, because
nothing forces each stage to belong to one person only.

## What this does not say

- **That persons are stages and their sums.** That is assumed (n. 4), and
  defended only in Postscript B (1983).
- **That I equals R in every case.** It holds by definition once persons are
  the maximal R-interrelated aggregates. That definition is the stipulation,
  and "at least, I cannot think of any that clearly is not" (p. 22) is its
  only support.
- **That the cost is small.** Lewis calls counting by tensed identity
  "somewhat counterintuitive" (p. 38), and on the Methuselah case continuum
  many persons share a birthday and are counted as one (pp. 29–31).
- **What a stage that will divide should fear or hope.** The paper does not
  ask. Postscript A's plural desire says what it can want, not how it should
  feel about the branch that will soon die.
- **That the answer is stable under a choice of R.** The argument works for
  any version of R (p. 20), but which aggregates are persons depends on the
  version through maximality.
- **Anything about the first-person argument from expectation.** Williams's
  case ([THEORY-531](THEORY-531.md)) has no branching, so tensed identity gives it
  nothing to do, and the paper does not discuss it.
- **That a recording played back into a new body is survival.** That is
  Postscript A (1983), from Leiber's novel, not the paper.

## Connections

- **Rival.** Parfit ([THEORY-447](THEORY-447.md)). Lewis accepts that paper's account of
  what matters and its premises about identity, and denies only that what
  matters is not identity. Olson's survey ([LIT-454](../literature.d/LIT-454.md)) files this as the
  "multiple occupancy" horn of the fission dilemma, with Perry; Lewis's
  persons are "branches" where Perry's are "lifetimes" (pp. 37–39).
- **Counting overlapping individuals.** Meincke's pregnancy
  ([THEORY-448](THEORY-448.md)) is the same problem in biology. Lewis keeps identity
  sharp and lets persons share parts; Meincke gives up substance counting
  for a count between one and two. Neither engages the other.
- **Machine instances.** The LIT entry observes that stage-sharing lets
  instances of one model that share their earlier stages be distinct
  continuants counted as one until they diverge, beside Goldstein and
  Lederman's instance agents ([LIT-206](../literature.d/LIT-206.md), [NOTE-138](../notes.d/NOTE-138.md)). That application is the
  filing reader's, not Lewis's. Shanahan's context-relative "I"
  ([THEORY-490](THEORY-490.md)) treats the same multiplicity by usage, not by counting.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

Kept Proposed by the twenty-fourth status review. The title is a
conditional in the record's voice, not a report of Lewis. Its middle
clause, "so Parfit's premises do not show that identity is not what
matters", is Lewis's interpretive conclusion against [THEORY-447](THEORY-447.md), a declared
rival. Whether it is fair to read "identity matters" as the I-relation
among stages is the point in dispute (promotion-audit decision of
2026-10-20). What is missing is the second half of the condition: a showing
that what a person wants in wanting to survive is something a stage can
want. The condition's first half asks for an argument for the antecedent.
A conditional title does not assert the antecedent, so that half asks for
more than the title ([ADR-065](../decisions.d/ADR-065.md) §1). It is left as it stands.
