# CLAIM-072: Saussure says purely differential only of signifier and signified taken apart; the sign as a whole is a positive fact, and signs stand in opposition

## The claim

Part Two, ch. IV: "dans la langue il n'y a que des différences sans termes
positifs" holds of the signified and the signifier considered separately; the
sign that unites them is "un fait positif", and signs are related by
opposition. Value, fixed by a term's relations to coexisting terms, is
distinct from signification, its counterpart relation to an idea: French
*mouton* and English *sheep* can share a signification and differ in value
([THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md)). Saussure also brackets the referent: arbitrariness is between
signifier and signified, not between sign and thing.

## What it does not say

It does not undercut the manuscript's use. Relational identity survives the
correction, and is arguably stronger for it: a term's value, not its
signification, is what the manuscript's observables track. The fix is a
sentence in §2: say value rather than significance, and drop the referent
contrast, which is not Saussure's.

## Note of 2026-10-10: the replies

The last paragraph overreaches ([CLAIM-tmpwevyr](CLAIM-tmpwevyr.md)). Value is fixed within one
language state and is a fact of *langue*, so the manuscript's observables,
facts of use, track a use-level counterpart ([CLAIM-tmpon3g9](CLAIM-tmpon3g9.md)), not Saussure's
value. The correction above stands, and the paragraph is left as written.
