# CLAIM-1279: An expectation is an operative norm rather than a prediction when a discrepancy is met by bringing the behaviour back into line with the expectation instead of revising the expectation to fit the behaviour; which response a discrepancy gets, adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or revision, is the work of normative governance

## The claim

A22 §2, on an unexpected interruption:

> **Prediction revision:** The service is less reliable than anticipated.
> Update the expectation.
>
> **Norm enforcement:** The service has failed to satisfy a maintained
> requirement. Recruit activity to restore compliance.
>
> The first changes the expectation to accommodate reality. The second
> attempts to change reality to satisfy the expectation.

So the same expectation can be held as a prediction or as a norm, and what
decides is the organization's response to a discrepancy: "An operative
norm might be distinguished partly by the organization's disposition to
preserve it against discrepancies rather than simply revise it in response
to them." This needs no represented belief about what ought to happen,
which is why A22 offers it for organizations "whose constituent processes
lack explicitly represented beliefs", extending Bicchieri's distinction
between empirical and normative expectations ([LIT-493](../literature.d/LIT-493.md), held, not read).

Neither response is better in general: "An organization that revises every
expectation immediately cannot sustain commitments. An organization that
attempts to enforce every expectation becomes rigid and potentially
coercive." Choosing among "adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or
revision" for a given discrepancy is what A22 calls normative governance.

## The record's term for it

The distinction is the record's direction of fit ([TERM-151](../terms.d/TERM-151.md)): a predicate
"written to fit what it is applied to, describing it", or one that "what it
is applied to must be made to fit". [CLAIM-942](CLAIM-942.md) uses it to tell inquiry from
engineering. Applied here, a prediction is a descriptive predicate on
another's behaviour and an operative norm a requiring one; an expectation
becomes a norm when its direction of fit turns. A22 does not name the
link; the assistant adds it (gap: the distinction is stated without the
record's term that already holds it).

## What it does not say

- Not that an operative norm is legitimate. A22 §1 separates "Operative
  norm" ("a standard against which subsequent behavior is assessed or
  regulated") from "Legitimate norm" ("adequate grounds for treating the
  standard as authoritative"), and §3's caption: "Those reasons need not
  establish moral legitimacy."
- Not that the organization formed the standard by itself. Who supplies
  the requirement is [QUESTION-128](../questions.d/QUESTION-128.md)'s open part; A22 §5 says of its own
  model that "its maintenance rule is supplied by the modeler".

The manuscript's Article 7 (§7.6, join J7.4) carries [CLAIM-946](CLAIM-946.md) over to
this claim's marker. Neither entry cites the other. [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md) (Proposed,
executed study; filer-run) committed in advance to the failure rule that
"if plain conformity passes condition 3 … the criterion fails to make the
distinction", and found that "Plain conformity restores a minority's
deviation (ρ = 0.024, against the averaging benchmark 0.25 and the
threshold 0.125)". [CLAIM-946](CLAIM-946.md): restoration "does not tell a population
whose members evaluate one another's interpretations from one whose
members copy one another's answers".

If that carries over, observed restoration of behaviour to an expectation
does not tell an enforced norm from a majority copied: members who only
copy what most others do also bring a deviant back into line, with no
disposition to preserve the expectation against discrepancy. Then the
ladder from regularity to operative norm needs a marker beyond observed
restoration. One candidate, not stated by any entry: a disposition to
restore that holds against a majority, of the kind [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md)'s evidential
condition tests in direction (there, members moved away from an
unwarranted deviation, toward the world).

The carry-over may fail, because the objects differ. [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md) restores
interpretive prototypes ([TERM-148](../terms.d/TERM-148.md)'s restorative reading), and this claim
restores behaviour against an expectation. The worry is also not the one
this claim's defeater names ("every corrective response is also a
revision of expectation"): it is that a corrective response can come from
copying, not from the expectation's direction of fit. Filed as a note, not
a question: whether it carries over is a check on this claim's marker,
answered by rerunning [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md)'s design on behaviour against an
expectation. The join has not yet been put to an independent reader
under [ADR-075](../decisions.d/ADR-075.md). No change of status. The wording is the assistant's
(Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.

**Correction of 2026-11-25 (the review of Part III, Part B).** The
review's independent reader of the joins tested J7.4 under [ADR-075](../decisions.d/ADR-075.md) and
found this note's carry-over a restatement that fails as a claim, and the
note in need of revision on three points.

- **It misses [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md)'s enforce arm.** [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md) has an arm in which "on
  disagreement a third member referees; the minority moves", with
  restoration 0.031 against conformity's 0.024, and [CLAIM-946](CLAIM-946.md)'s body
  states the comparison this note asks for: "Enforcement by a referee
  (0.031) and checking against the world (−0.030) also passed. The
  condition does not separate them." So the note's reading of [CLAIM-946](CLAIM-946.md)
  as about evaluating against copying, and its "Neither entry cites the
  other" as a gap to fill, miss the arm that is the enforced case.
- **The carry-over has a free parameter: who does the restoring.** On a
  behavioural reading of this claim's marker ("bringing the behaviour
  back into line"), the deviant's behaviour returns, and copying meets
  it. On the reading the summary quotes from A22, "Recruit activity to
  restore compliance", corrective activity by others is required, and
  that separates enforcement from copying by construction. Until the
  marker says which, no separating case can be stated.
- **The candidate marker points the wrong way.** "A disposition to
  restore that holds against a majority, of the kind [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md)'s
  evidential condition tests in direction" should be dropped. The
  evidential arm moves members toward the world, which is the descriptive
  direction of fit, the opposite of a norm's ([TERM-151](../terms.d/TERM-151.md)); [CASE-362](../cases.d/CASE-362.md)'s
  enforce arm also moves the minority; and the record's own norm model
  has a bistable norm end "by a push past its threshold" ([CLAIM-1318](CLAIM-1318.md)),
  so a genuine enforced norm can fail against a majority deviation and
  the marker would misclassify it.

The held principle that does the work is [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md) (Active): "Recovery
under interventions on state cannot show where maintenance is realized
… only interventions on the production mechanisms, relative to a
declared boundary, separate the two". The candidate test is therefore an
intervention on the corrective mechanism, knocking out others' corrective
activity and seeing whether the expectation still holds, not a test of
observed restoration ([LEAD-260](../leads.d/LEAD-260.md) step 4 cites [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md) already). The
record also holds a marker beyond restoration in a neighbouring setting,
which this note's "not stated by any entry" overlooked (the review's
Part C, §7.6): [CASE-101](../cases.d/CASE-101.md) (Active) constructs three populations with the
same turnover and aggregate statistics, one responding alike to a common
input, one transmitting its norms to its replacements, one held together
by an institution, and [CLAIM-926](CLAIM-926.md) (Proposed, demonstrated) finds that a
priced lagged block test picks out the one held by delayed transmission,
with its lag. Whether either bears on an organization's operative norms
is not decided here. No claim is filed; no change of status. The wording
is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's
call.
