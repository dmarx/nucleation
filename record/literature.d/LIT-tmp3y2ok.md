# LIT-tmp3y2ok: Contractualism

Ashford, E. & Mulgan, T. (2007; substantive revision 2018). "Contractualism". *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/contractualism/.

## Key takeaways

- Scanlon's formula, quoted with page (§1): an act is wrong if its
  performance "would be disallowed by any set of principles for the general
  regulation of behaviour that no one could reasonably reject as a basis
  for informed, unforced, general agreement" (Scanlon 1998, p. 153).
- "wrongness consists in unjustifiability" (§1); "contractualism covers
  only the realm of what we owe to one another" (§3).
- The individualist restriction (§3): only "various individuals' reasons
  for objecting to that principle and alternatives to it" count (p. 229);
  "it does not allow a number of lesser complaints to outweigh one
  person's weightier complaint."
- Rejection is comparative (§3.1): a burden gives no reason to reject a
  principle if "every alternative imposes a greater burden" on someone
  else.
