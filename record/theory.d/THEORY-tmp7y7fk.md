# THEORY-tmp7y7fk: In eight samples pooled to 3,692 respondents, the covariance of the BPNSFS items is organized more by item keying than by need: factors sharing a keying direction correlate .64 to .75 while satisfaction and frustration of the same need correlate .17 to .38, a higher-order model grouping the six scales by keying fits better than one grouping them by need, and the frustration items carry almost no thwarting content

## Source

Murphy, Watts, Baker, Don, Jolink and Algoe (2023), [LIT-tmpjcuzb](../literature.d/LIT-tmpjcuzb.md),
Psychological Assessment 35(2); read in [NOTE-tmpswlms](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpswlms.md) from the author
manuscript, PMC11554446.

## What was actually shown

**The items.** The twelve BPNSFS frustration items are all negatively keyed
and the twelve satisfaction items all positively keyed. None of the
frustration scales has "substantial active thwarting content"; two
competence items are reverse-worded twins of satisfaction items ("I have
serious doubts about whether I can do things well" / "I feel confident that
I can do things well").

**The structure, pooled N = 3,692** (eight samples, 153 to 926 each; seven
of university students; English).
- Two-factor EFA: one factor of all positive items, one of all negative
  items. Three-factor EFA: the first two still pure keying (Table 1).
- Six-factor EFA intercorrelations: frustration factors among themselves .64
  to .70; satisfaction factors .66 to .75; satisfaction with frustration of
  the same need .17, .34 and .38 (frustration items reverse-scored).
- Higher-order CFA with three need factors over the six scales: RMSEA .064,
  CFI .924, TLI .915, BIC −28,734, with need-factor correlations .94 to
  1.11. With two keying factors: RMSEA .052, CFI .950, TLI .944, BIC
  −29,631.

**External correlates sorted by keying** (all items scored toward
well-being). The frustration items correlate more with the negatively keyed
items of external well-being scales than the satisfaction items do, in all
five measures, with Steiger's z of 6.50, 11.16 and 3.58 (self-compassion, La
Guardia need satisfaction, perceived stress) and 1.63 and 1.90 (depression,
self-esteem). The satisfaction items' excess with positively keyed items
appears in two of five measures.

This could have come out otherwise. Had the needs been the stronger
grouping, the need-level higher-order model would have fitted better and
the same-need correlations would have exceeded the same-keying ones. They
did not.

## What this does not say

- **That the frustration scales measure only keying.** The authors say the
  confound between keying and construct is complete in the BPNSFS, so no
  factor analysis can decide it. That inference, and what would settle it,
  is [THEORY-tmp62iwr](THEORY-tmp62iwr.md).
- **That need frustration does not exist or does not matter.** The paper
  attacks a measure, not the construct, and points to the thwarting scale
  ([LIT-tmp6as8m](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6as8m.md)) as closer to it.
- **Anything about other languages, versions or populations.** Three of the
  scale's fifty-odd versions were used.
- **Anything about the autonomy scales that the paper's own table
  supports.** The text says at one point that the Autonomy Frustration items
  load weakly on Autonomy; its Table 3 shows the Autonomy Satisfaction items
  doing so (.34 to .49) and the frustration items loading .62 to .76
  ([NOTE-tmpswlms](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpswlms.md), Limitations).
