# THEORY-tmpm1g8e: The classical theorems on sufficient statistics need only the copy/discard structure of Markov kernels: Basu's theorem holds in every Markov category, and Fisher–Neyman and Bahadur need only strict positivity, not the existence of conditional distributions

## Source

Fritz (2020), [LIT-tmphrafc](../literature.d/LIT-tmphrafc.md), read in [NOTE-tmp23s9u](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp23s9u.md): Definitions 13.1,
13.16, 14.1–14.3, 15.1, 15.7, 16.1–16.2; Theorems 14.5, 15.8, 16.3;
Examples 13.19, 14.6, 15.4.

## What was actually shown

A Markov category is a symmetric monoidal category with a commutative
copy/discard comonoid on each object and natural discard. In one, Fritz
defines:

- a **statistic**: a deterministic morphism s : X → V for a model
  p : Θ → X;
- **sufficiency**: there is α : V → X such that, given s, the sample can
  be regenerated by α independently of θ;
- **completeness**: of the kernel sp, gf = hf implies g = h f-a.s.;
- **ancillarity**: sp does not depend on θ.

He then proves three theorems:

- **Basu** (Theorem 15.8): a complete sufficient statistic and an
  ancillary statistic are independent given θ, with no extra axiom.
- **Fisher–Neyman** (Theorem 14.5): under strict positivity, s is
  sufficient iff some α has αsp = p and sα = id sp-a.s.
- **Bahadur** (Theorem 16.3): under strict positivity, if a minimal
  sufficient statistic exists, any complete sufficient statistic is
  minimal.

Strict positivity follows from the existence of conditionals (Lemma
13.18). It also holds in Stoch directly (Example 13.19), and Stoch lacks
conditionals (Example 11.18). So none of the three theorems needs
regular conditional distributions.

The definitions could have failed to match the classical ones, and Fritz
checks them. Abstract completeness in Stoch is bounded completeness
(Example 15.4). In FinStoch the abstract Fisher–Neyman condition is the
factorisation p(x|θ) = g_θ(s(x)) h(x) (Example 14.6).

## What this does not say

- **Not that Markov categories prove anything new about classical
  statistics.** The content is about hypotheses: which structure each
  proof uses. Classical Basu already holds with bounded completeness.
- **Not that the measure-theoretic Fisher–Neyman theorem follows.** Fritz
  leaves that open (end of Section 14). In Stoch, Theorem 14.5 is a
  theorem about his notion of sufficiency, and that notion is not shown
  to agree with the classical one there.
- **Not that the abstract notions are final.** Fritz calls all his
  definitions provisional (Section 1). His a.s. equality in Stoch is
  weaker than pointwise a.s. equality on σ-algebras that are not
  countably generated (Example 13.3, after Fremlin). So "sufficient" in
  Stoch means sufficient in that weaker sense.
- **Not that positivity is dispensable.** The Fisher–Neyman and Bahadur
  proofs use it. FinStoch± (Example 11.27) satisfies the Markov axioms
  without positivity, and the paper does not say whether the theorems fail
  there.
- **Not that statistics in general needs no measure theory.** The proofs
  that particular categories (Stoch, BorelStoch) satisfy the axioms carry
  the measure-theoretic work. Fritz says so (Section 1).
