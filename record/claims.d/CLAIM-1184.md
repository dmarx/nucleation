# CLAIM-1184: Standing to contest a harm matters only as a means of preventing or remedying harm, so defeating it wrongs those affected only through the harm it lets continue or risks, and where the harm's course is fixed by causes contest could not touch, reproducing the projections that defeat it adds no wrong beyond the harm

## The objection

[CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)'s defeater names the reduction: "whatever is wrong beyond the
harm reduces to the harm's continuation". This is that reduction at its
strongest, aimed at [CLAIM-1155](CLAIM-1155.md) and through it at [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md).

1. A procedure through which those affected contest a harm is valuable
   because, and to the extent that, it prevents harm, stops it, or
   remedies it.
2. So keeping those affected from contesting it is wrong because, and to
   the extent that, it lets harm continue that contest would have
   stopped, or raises the risk of it. The second clause meets the reply
   that the actual outcome was unchanged: ex ante, a missing channel
   raises expected harm, which is [ARG-055](../arguments.d/ARG-055.md)'s own caveat ("B's missing
   procedure would raise the risk of later harm").
3. Where the harm's course, actual and expected, is fixed by causes
   contest could not touch, the defeat of standing makes no difference
   to harm, and there is nothing it is wrong by.
4. So an organization that reproduces the projections hiding its harm
   wrongs those affected no more than one that merely continues the same
   harm, once expected harm is matched.

Its best support is the practice it generalizes. Courts let an error
stand when it made no difference to the outcome, and the cost of a
procedure is weighed against the harm it averts ([CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)'s condition 5
and [CLAIM-520](CLAIM-520.md) make corrective capacity conditional on cost for that
reason).

## Where it is answered

[ARG-228](../arguments.d/ARG-228.md) concedes premise 3's case and denies
premise 1. It builds the case where expected harm is matched, and finds
that the record judges the defeat of standing a wrong there, as it already
does in [CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md) ("even where the objection would have failed") and
[THEORY-1972](../theory.d/THEORY-1972.md) ("The right to be heard does not depend upon an advance showing
that one will surely prevail at the hearing"). The harmless-error practice
does not carry premise 1: it governs whether a judgement is reversed, not
whether the party was wronged, and an error found harmless is still found
to be an error.

What would revive the objection: a showing that the record's judgements in
[CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md) and [THEORY-1972](../theory.d/THEORY-1972.md) are themselves only judgements of expected harm
under uncertainty, so that once the uncertainty is removed, as the
construction removes it, the judgement goes with it.

## Note of 2026-11-23: what holds it back

The argument that answers it, [ARG-228](../arguments.d/ARG-228.md), is Proposed, and it rests on
[CLAIM-723](CLAIM-723.md) and grounds on [THEORY-1972](../theory.d/THEORY-1972.md), both Proposed. So the objection is
neither answered by a held argument nor shown sound. Whether the matched-
outcome construction defeats premise 1, and so whether this objection goes
Rejected, is the owner's call.

## Note of 2026-11-25: a parallel instrumentalism, from the manuscript governance-of-becoming, Article 8
<!-- inactive-ok-file: ARG-228 CASE-336 CLAIM-909 CLAIM-911 CLAIM-912 CLAIM-913 CLAIM-723 — Proposed; the two constructions, their instrumentalist readings and the alternatives the owner has not ruled on, cited with their standing -->

The manuscript's Article 8 (§8.5, join J8.2) sets two constructions side
by side that the record has not linked. ARG-228's matched utilities,
which answer CLAIM-1184, fix expected harm and vary only whether those
affected can contest it. CASE-336's variants C2* and C4* (CLAIM-909),
which CLAIM-913 reads, fix what a power's holder would do and vary only
who holds a share of the power to review or revise. Both fix the
instrumental difference and vary only standing or the allocation of a
power, and CLAIM-1184 and CLAIM-913 are the instrumentalist reading of
each: what is left once the instrumental difference is matched is a
residue, "no wrong beyond the harm" in one and "a residue of protective
reasons that no longer apply" in the other.

The parallel routes; it does not bind. Standing to be heard on a harm is
not a share of a power to revise rules, so one can reject CLAIM-1184 and
accept CLAIM-913, or the reverse, without inconsistency; the stronger
form, that the two stand or fall together, fails on that reading. What
carries over is a candidate, not an entailment: a non-instrumental reason
the owner accepts in one (CLAIM-911's "personal reason of status", or
ARG-228's carried-over judgement from CLAIM-723) is a reason to test in
the other. The join has not yet been put to an independent reader under
ADR-075. No change of status; the rulings on CLAIM-1184 and on CLAIM-911
to CLAIM-913 stay the owner's. The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in
this session).
