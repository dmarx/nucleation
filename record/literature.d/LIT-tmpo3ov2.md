summary: >-
  Lush (2020), Collabra: Psychology 6(1):22. A pre-registered
  quasi-experiment on the controls of the rubber hand illusion. Twenty
  undergraduates (after 12 pre-registered exclusions) who had never taken
  part in it were shown a text and a video of the procedure and rated how
  far they expected to feel each of Botvinick & Cohen's three illusion and
  six control statements, under synchronous and asynchronous brushing.
  Expectancy was higher for illusion than control statements under
  synchrony (M 1.7 against 0.0, t(19) = 6.24, d = 1.40) and for synchronous
  than asynchronous illusion statements (1.7 against 0.4, t(19) = 4.68,
  d = 1.05), and nine who had never heard of the illusion showed the same.
  The controls therefore do not match expectations, and the author
  concludes the illusion "may be, partially or entirely, a suggestion
  effect"; he proposes controls matched on expectancy and on suggestion
  difficulty.

# LIT-tmpo3ov2: Demand Characteristics Confound the Rubber Hand Illusion

Lush, P. (2020). Demand characteristics confound the rubber hand illusion. *Collabra: Psychology* 6(1), 22. DOI 10.1525/collabra.325. Preprint: PsyArXiv, DOI 10.31234/osf.io/mpncv.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpqbo0f](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqbo0f.md).*

- **The controls rest on an untested assumption.** The illusion is
  separated from suggestion by comparing illusion with control statements,
  or synchronous with asynchronous brushing. Both comparisons assume that
  "demand characteristics (and therefore expectancies) for control and
  illusion statements are closely matched".
- **The assumption fails.** People told about the procedure, but never
  put through it, expected the illusion statements more than the control
  statements (d = 1.40), and expected them more under synchrony than
  asynchrony (d = 1.05). The expectancy profile has the shape of published
  agreement scores, with the illusion items positive and the control items
  at or below zero.
- **Naive participants expect it too.** The nine who had never heard of
  the illusion showed the same two differences (d = 2.30 and 1.68).
- **What follows, and what does not.** The standard controls "cannot
  provide evidence that the RHI is not a suggestion effect". The author is
  explicit that the results "do not provide evidence that the RHI is a
  suggestion effect, but rather that existing claims to the contrary are
  invalid".
- **Implicit measures inherit the problem.** Proprioceptive drift, skin
  conductance and imaging are tied to ownership only through their
  correlation with report. The demand characteristics of drift had, to the
  author's knowledge, never been tested.
- **A remedy is proposed, not tested.** Illusion and control statements
  should be matched on expectancy and on "suggestion difficulty", with
  Bayes factors to support claims of no difference.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request for experimental work on the
rubber hand illusion that bears on the record's four Proposed THEORYs
about it. Read in full from the author's final manuscript: **Active**.

It is the test of the gap that [NOTE-tmpt0l4z](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpt0l4z.md) named in Botvinick & Cohen
1998 ([LIT-tmpu9yys](LIT-tmpu9yys.md)): the intended answer is guessable from the set-up, and
the control items guard against blanket agreement, not against suggestion
specific to the illusion items. It shows that the guess is in fact made,
in the direction and roughly the shape of the published results.

What it does to the four THEORYs, set out in the NOTE:

- [THEORY-tmpef8lf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpef8lf.md). It measures expectations, as that THEORY's
  `promote_when` asks. But it measures them in people who were not
  stimulated, and it measures no illusion. So it does not meet the
  `promote_when` and does not trigger the refutation. What it shows is that
  the 1998 asynchronous control is not a suggestion control.
- [THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md). It warns that graded ownership ratings may track
  graded expectation.
- [THEORY-tmpom3z2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpom3z2.md). It does not touch interoception.
- [THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md). The author extends the confound to the full-body
  illusion on which that THEORY leans.

It also bears on [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md), on what report and "implicit" measures
can establish about an experience, and on [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)'s C6.
