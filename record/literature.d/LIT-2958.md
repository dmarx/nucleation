# LIT-2958: Information geometry for multiparameter models: New perspectives on the origin of simplicity

Katherine N. Quinn, Michael C. Abbott, Mark K. Transtrum, Benjamin B. Machta & James P. Sethna (2023). Review, Reports on Progress in Physics (venue as registered, not confirmed by the arXiv copy); arXiv 2111.07176.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A cites it at A17 for the sloppy-model account of why simple effective theories exist (PMC URL given). It bears on the exchange's link between coarse-graining, sloppiness and emergent simplicity. Read on 2026-11-22 ([NOTE-tmp3keyy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp3keyy.md)) and filed as [THEORY-tmpx26x7](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpx26x7.md). A17's characterization is fair, with the review's own qualifications: the ubiquity is empirical, proved only for smooth least-squares models; a hyperribbon is low-dimensional only against a demanded precision; and the review offers no single account of why simple effective theories exist, its explanations for physics and for least squares "seem completely different". It grounds [CLAIM-820](../claims.d/CLAIM-820.md) with that qualification ([ARG-tmp6xwk9](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp6xwk9.md)), and is drawn on by [TERM-135](../terms.d/TERM-135.md) and [TERM-140](../terms.d/TERM-140.md). It speculates about, and does not claim, sloppiness in large networks.
