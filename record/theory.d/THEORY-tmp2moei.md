# THEORY-tmp2moei: In animal development the organ-level outcome is reproducible while the trajectories of individual cells differ between embryos raised alike, so developmental regularity is imposed on variable cell behaviour rather than read out from a fixed sequence of cellular events (Weiss, as Nicholson reconstructs him), with invariant lineages such as that of C. elegans the conceded exception

## Source

Nicholson (2026), [LIT-tmptd6hn](../literature.d/LIT-tmptd6hn.md), §5 and nn. 30–31, reporting Weiss 1969a,
1970a, 1973 and 1974; read in [NOTE-tmpo9v9c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpo9v9c.md).

## What was actually shown

**The claim.** "there are innumerable ways to arrive to each developmental
stage, and no two embryos ever undergo cellular differentiation in exactly
the same way" (§5). Fig. 5, redrawn from Weiss 1969a, shows embryonic
regions A–E whose topological relations persist from stage S1 to S2, while
"even two very closely related embryos (top and bottom) differ drastically
in the trajectories taken by the individual cells" (caption). Weiss's
summary is "determinacy in the gross despite demonstrable indeterminacy in
the small" (Weiss 1973, quoted in §5). The river delta is the image: "one
can only tell that the water will end up in the ocean, but never what
precisely the distribution and configuration of the branches will be"
(Weiss 1970a, quoted in §5).

**The evidence offered.** Weiss's own embryological experience, as the
schematic; and four citations on the stochasticity of gene expression and
cell-fate decisions (Elowitz et al. 2002; Raj and van Oudenaarden 2008;
Losick and Desplan 2008; Balázsi et al. 2011), said to call "[t]his
deterministic understanding" into question (§5). No data are reproduced,
and no measurement compares cell-level and tissue-level variability.

**The exception conceded.** "for very simple multicellular organisms such
as the tiny nematode worm Caenorhabditis elegans we can actually track the
individual lineages of all 671 of its embryonic cells (Sulston et al.
1983). But Weiss's general point stands, as such perfectly predictable
behaviour is the exception rather than the rule" (n. 30). How rare the
exception is, the paper does not show.

**What could have failed.** Cell trajectories could have proved as
reproducible as organ outcomes across embryos, as they are in *C.
elegans*; or the outcome could have varied with the trajectories. Neither
test is reported in the paper.

## What this does not say

- **That genes or molecular mechanisms do not matter.** Nicholson says
  so: "taking systems dynamics seriously does not entail denying the
  importance of genes in development", which supply "‘boundary
  conditions’" (§5).
- **What imposes the regularity.** "imposed from the top down by the
  developmental system as a whole" (§5) names a level, not a mechanism.
  Cell–cell signalling, mechanical feedback and selection among cells would
  each produce reproducible outcomes from variable cells, and the paper
  does not choose among them.
- **That developmental programs are a myth.** A gene regulatory network
  can specify outcomes without specifying each cell's path; the
  conclusion is against microdeterminacy, not against regulation by
  genes.
- **That Weiss's variance inequality has been measured here.** The link
  §5 draws to it is by analogy ([THEORY-tmp1244l](THEORY-tmp1244l.md) on what that inequality
  can show).
