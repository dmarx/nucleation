# NOTE-tmp4hykx: Bourrat — Reproducees, reproducers, Darwinian individuals

## Contribution

Bourrat examines the concept of reproduction in evolution. He distinguishes objects that are reproduced (reproducees), objects that reproduce by virtue of some reproductive autonomy (reproducers), and Darwinian individuals, which are reproducers with a high degree of reproductive causal control. Applied to genes, viruses and cells, the distinction explains why they differ in reproductive status. It also supports Griesemer's view that reproduction requires material overlap.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 1–3): a "menagerie" of reproduction-related terms lacks conceptual bridges; the aim is one framework for comparing reproduction situations and characterising the Darwinian individual. The term "reproducee" is borrowed from Godfrey-Smith (2009, p. 88).
- §2 (pp. 3–8): sets three minimal conditions for being a reproducee, and warns against inferring from "X is reproduced" that X reproduces — the replicator concept is the prime example of this non sequitur.
- §3 (pp. 8–10): reproductive autonomy is relative and comes in degrees — the range of background conditions under which an object can produce another of its class.
- §4 (pp. 10–13): re-examines the Griesemer vs Godfrey-Smith dispute over formal reproduction (retroviruses) and sides with Griesemer for reproducers.
- §§5–6 (pp. 13–21): reproductive causal control separates mere reproducers from Darwinian individuals with adaptive capacity; "scaffolded reproducer" is rejected as self-contradictory; control can evolve through ecological scaffolding in transitions in individuality.
- §7 (pp. 21–22; Fig. 3): places the categories in a two-dimensional autonomy × control space adapted from Godfrey-Smith's Darwinian space.

## Open questions

- Refines the units-of-selection vocabulary (replicator/reproducer/interactor; [LIT-tmpjoofa](../literature.d/LIT-tmpjoofa.md), [LIT-tmpw0mon](../literature.d/LIT-tmpw0mon.md)) and biological individuality ([LIT-tmplyn8s](../literature.d/LIT-tmplyn8s.md)).
- Check how "reproductive causal control" is measured — whether it is operationalisable or only comparative.
- The treatment of genes as mere reproducees directly challenges gene's-eye views and deserves checking against Lloyd's replicator/reproducer question in [LIT-tmpjoofa](../literature.d/LIT-tmpjoofa.md).
