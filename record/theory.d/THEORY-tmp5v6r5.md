# THEORY-tmp5v6r5: Balboni, Bandiera, Burgess, Ghatak and Heil hold that ultra-poor households in rural Bangladesh face an S-shaped asset transition with an unstable threshold near the value of a cow and a small complementary asset, so that a one-off asset transfer that lifts a household above it is followed by accumulation, a shift into self-employment and higher consumption over 11 years while one that leaves it below is followed by asset loss: a poverty trap set by initial wealth and indivisible inputs, not by lower ability or effort

## Source

Balboni, Bandiera, Burgess, Ghatak & Heil (2022), [LIT-tmp6ej76](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6ej76.md); read in [NOTE-tmp6tzyt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6tzyt.md) (NBER Working Paper 29340, printed pages).

## What was actually shown

- **Non-concavity** (pp. 13–14): the four-year asset transition among
  beneficiaries "rejects the null of global concavity with p < 0.01",
  with an unstable point of "9,308.82 BDT" (504 USD) that "falls exactly
  in the low-density range of the baseline distribution of assets". The
  test could have accepted concavity, as it does in control villages,
  where the S "is absent" (p. 18).
- **Divergence** (p. 15, Table II): those who "stay below the threshold
  despite the transfer lose 14% of assets over the next four years while
  those who are pushed past the threshold grow their assets by 16%".
- **Long run** (Table IV): at year 11, above against below, productive
  assets +10,708 BDT, land +9,775, consumption +3,464, self-employed
  hours +74 a year; at year 2 consumption and hours were lower.
- **Ability** (pp. 20–21, 27): with individual thresholds and
  baseline-asset fixed effects the results "change only marginally and,
  if anything, become more pronounced", and calibrated productivity shows
  "no systematic correlation between baseline wealth and any of these
  parameters".
- **Mechanism, proposed**: indivisible complementary inputs (a cart, a
  shed) with no rental or credit markets; the median shortfall is "only
  about 300 BDT (16 USD)" (p. 15).

## What this does not say

- That the threshold was randomized. Randomization decided who got a
  transfer; the side of the threshold was set by small, non-random
  differences in baseline assets, defended by placebo and fixed effects,
  not by design.
- That indivisibility is the mechanism. Escapers "might also be healthier
  or experience less stress", and the authors "cannot know whether they
  are causes or consequences of poverty, or both" (p. 16).
- That the asset alone did it. The programme also gave 40 weeks of
  consumption support, health support and two years of training (fn. 7);
  the threshold is "a post-training threshold" (p. 17).
- That crossing guarantees escape. Among younger women, "almost half of
  those who start above the threshold end up at least retaining the value
  of the transfer in 2018 while only 30% of those below do" (p. 23).
- Anything about repeated or assured help, the Samaritan's-dilemma case of
  [CLAIM-tmpqa0qu](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpqa0qu.md): the help was one-off. It cuts against reading low
  position as low effort at household level, not against that mechanism
  where it applies; nor does it test the length-of-support part of
  [CLAIM-tmpc3x0c](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpc3x0c.md).
- Anything about states, projects or public assets.
- That the NBER text's participation figure is right: it says "above 80%
  in all wealth classes" (p. 7) where Table I shows 0.67 to 0.74.
