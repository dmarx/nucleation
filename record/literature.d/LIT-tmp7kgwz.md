# LIT-tmp7kgwz: An Introduction to QBism with an Application to the Locality of Quantum Mechanics

Fuchs, C. A., Mermin, N. D. & Schack, R. (2014). *American Journal of Physics* 82(8):749–754. doi:10.1119/1.4874855. arXiv:1311.5253.

Read in full in [NOTE-tmpfpjq8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfpjq8.md), from the arXiv v1 PDF (pages cited are
that PDF's).

## Why this is here

The closest prior art to the owner's wording, and a rival. QBism: "A
measurement does not, as the term unfortunately suggests, reveal a
pre-existing state of affairs. It is an action on the world by an agent
that results in the creation of an outcome — a new experience for that
agent. 'Intervention' might be a better term" (p. 2, verified in
[NOTE-tmpfpjq8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfpjq8.md)). A measurement that generates its outcome, as U1 has it,
but QBism makes that measurement an action on the world.
[CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) has to say why a judgement is not one (its section
"Judgement is not action").

The reading sharpens the contrast in three ways. QBism keeps judgement and
measurement apart: a judgement is a state or probability assignment, a
measurement is the action that elicits experience, and collapse is
"nothing but the updating of an agent's state assignment on the basis of
her experience" (p. 2). Its loop is judgement, action, experience,
update, which is the action–perception shape the claim rejects. The
outcome it creates is already on the agent's side, "a new experience",
and the external system is "not changed" by the intervention (p. 7); so
the difference from the claim is the route to the outcome, not where it
lands. And QBism exempts from its formalism exactly what the claim's loop
runs through: "the only phenomenon accessible to Alice which she does not
model with quantum mechanics is her own direct internal awareness of her
own private experience" (p. 2).

For [QUESTION-tmpr5tck](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpr5tck.md) it treats another agent's report as a measurement:
"When Alice elicits an answer from Bob, she treats this as she treats any
other quantum measurement" (p. 3). The report is elicited, which in this
paper is an action.
