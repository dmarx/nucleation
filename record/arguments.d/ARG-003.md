# ARG-003: In the Dual State ordinary norms governed conduct while a prerogative order held authority over whether they bound, so a constraint can govern conduct without governing its own authority

## The inference

A historical case exhibits the structure; the structure is general.

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are the cases alike?** In structure only: one governing
  order holding authority over another's applicability.
- **What transfers?** That precedence within a domain and authority over the
  domain are distinct, and that the second can be asymmetric (Section 5.8).
- **What does not?** Moral or historical character; any suggestion that
  exception is pathological as such; any model of agency. Fraenkel's subject is
  a state, not an independently warranted agent in the paper's sense, so the
  paper's own scope condition is not met by the source case.

Active, narrowed to the structural respect. The verdict, as [CLAIM-032](../claims.d/CLAIM-032.md) v1
put it: "the Dual State supplies one structural point and nothing more".
[CLAIM-032](../claims.d/CLAIM-032.md) undercuts the argument in every other respect, with the paper's
own caution: "Northstar's internal procedures are not morally or
historically analogous to the regime he described. We invoke Fraenkel for
one structural insight about institutional authority, not as a generic
model of organizations or agency."
