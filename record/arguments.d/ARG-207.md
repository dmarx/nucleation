# ARG-207: The matched member and non-member pair of the part-whole case, by contrast, together with a bare relational arrangement's not being an agent, shows that for such a whole constitution grounds no further obligation and that the bearer is the controlling agents, not the whole

## Which sense of "whole"

[TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) sense 1, a bare relational arrangement, with a part that has
standing of its own ([TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)) and, where one exists, a controlling agent
or role R, as in [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md).

## The inference

1. In a bare relational arrangement, being a constituent is standing in the
   arrangement's relations and nothing further: sense 1 is defined without
   shared practice, mutual recognition or a cooperative scheme ([TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)).
2. [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md): two persons under one arrangement, equal in vulnerability,
   interests, rights and exposure to the controlling actors, and in the
   institutional consequences; one a constituent, one not. By step 1, the only
   difference between them is standing in the relations, which by step 1 carries
   no normative content of its own.
3. So nothing that is owed moves with the variation: whatever is owed to the
   constituent is owed to the non-constituent, on grounds both share. Being a
   constituent grounds no further obligation. This is the case's second result:
   the mereological relation "help[s] identify where the obligations apply
   without independently grounding them".
4. A sense-1 arrangement is not an agent and cannot take the alternative a duty
   weighs ([CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md), Active; [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)). So it is the ground and object
   of any norm concerning the part, not its bearer.
5. Where an agent or role maintains, controls or benefits from the arrangement,
   the obligation falls there, each as far as its control reaches. This is the
   case's third result, and the bearer the line fixes elsewhere ([CLAIM-671](../claims.d/CLAIM-671.md),
   [CLAIM-690](../claims.d/CLAIM-690.md), [CLAIM-876](../claims.d/CLAIM-876.md)).

## Critical questions

- **Is everything said to be held fixed really fixed?** The pair holds fixed
  vulnerability, interests, rights, exposure and institutional consequences,
  and varies constitution. The move is sound only where constitution can be
  varied without changing one of the fixed terms. [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md) flags that in
  some arrangements being a constituent itself changes the person's
  vulnerability or the consequences; there the variation is not clean, two
  things move, and the contrast does not discriminate. The argument is
  restricted to the wholes for which a clean pair can be built, and the claim's
  `defeated_if` names the loophole — an extra owed to the constituent that is
  "not reducible to a difference the variation smuggled in".
- **Does varying constitution change anything else that bears?** In the bare
  arrangement, no: by step 1 constitution is standing in the relations, and the
  normatively relevant features are the fixed ones. If it does — if membership
  brings a shared practice or a scheme — the whole is not bare, and the case is
  read as [CLAIM-959](../claims.d/CLAIM-959.md) reads it, not here.
- **Is the case realistic, or only coherent?** Only coherent, by design, like
  the other matched pairs the line uses ([CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md) is a thought
  experiment). It shows that the outcome can be read off the shared grounds
  without the membership; it does not show how often membership and exposure
  come apart in fact.
- **Does the bearer half follow from the contrast?** No; it rests on
  [CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md). The contrast settles the ground (steps 2–3); the premise that
  a sense-1 arrangement is not an agent settles the bearer (steps 4–5). Where
  no agent controls the arrangement, step 5 names no bearer, and the inference
  reaches only the ground.

## Verdict

Proposed. The contrast supports the claim's ground half for the wholes in which
the matched pair can be built, and [CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md) supports the bearer half
wherever an agent controls the arrangement. It does not support a stronger
reading, that membership grounds nothing in any whole; the claim does not make
that reading, and [CLAIM-959](../claims.d/CLAIM-959.md) is the case read for a thick membership.
