# NOTE-tmpn7tu4: Lefèvre and others (2025) — Exploring ethical and regulatory challenges of AI integration in European Union newsrooms

## Contribution

Document analysis of 30 ethical charters, codes and AI policies, and 41 semi-structured interviews (Nov 2023–Nov 2024) with media professionals, legal experts and regulators, across 19 outlets in French-speaking Belgium, France and Spain, framed by the political economy of communication. Findings: AI use in newsrooms is experimental and cautious, mainly for efficiency (transcription, translation, tagging, headline suggestions) and for audience retention and revenue; larger groups can invest more, deepening concentration; platform dependence shapes editorial choices and visibility; in-house personalization can fragment an outlet's editorial line, so some outlets refuse it and public-service media design recommenders to promote diversity; about 60% of outlets have no public discourse on AI and about 20% state ethical commitments; regulation (AI Act, DSA, EMFA, national laws) is fragmented and not media-specific, and the AI Act's human-intervention exemption relieves media of transparency duties for AI-generated content; self-regulation is uneven and judged insufficient. Recommendations: transparency on AI's role, collaborative self-regulation, AI literacy, independent oversight.

## Key results

1. Abstract: the study finds "a limited discourse on AI use in media organisations, significant disparities in AI implementation practices, and an absence of clear, actionable regulatory guidelines".
2. Associated challenges: "The growing dependency on platform algorithms for distribution and visibility can influence editorial choices, leading to shifts in news agendas and erosion of media pluralism".
3. Associated challenges: "the relative invisibility of articles on local politics on Facebook can be explained by their low engagement rate" (citing Weber et al. 2019).
4. Results, Interviewee 29-France: "Our job is not to produce “content” but to offer keys to understanding our societies through a structured set of news analyses, seen as a whole".
5. Results, Interviewee 39-Belgium: a banner "managed automatically by an algorithm, set up by marketing in collaboration with the editors-in-chief".
6. Table 1 discussion: "Around 60% of the media sites we analysed do not have an official discourse on AI" and "only 20% express their ethical commitments".
7. Regulation: "the fact that a human intervenes in the process leading to the publication of AI-generated content is enough to exempt the media from transparency obligations towards its audience (European Union, 2024, Art. 50)."
8. Conclusion: "real-world implementation lags behind regulatory discussions."

## Assumptions

Political-economy framing (power over data, algorithms, infrastructure); three markets chosen for similar media systems (Hallin and Mancini); interviews as evidence of practice; document analysis limited to interviewed outlets.

## Concepts

Editorial autonomy and editorial line; platform dependency; news recommender systems (in-house vs platform); personalization and "atomization" of news; audience metrics, ARPU; regulation, self-regulation, co-regulation; AI Act Art. 50; EMFA; DSA; certification of methods (RSF Journalism Trust Initiative).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmpifp69](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpifp69.md)** (attention optimization in news favours clickbait and works against investigative journalism): **supports the mechanism qualitatively, at the distribution level.** Interviewees and cited work report that platform visibility rewards engagement and leaves low-engagement news (local politics) invisible, and that AI is adopted partly for audience retention and revenue. It also fits the claim's own correction that metrics do not mechanically decide: some outlets refuse personalization and public-service media design recommenders for diversity. Nothing on investigative capacity.
- **[CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md)** (governance from outside through evidence or criteria): an instance of the informational route: platforms shape which news reaches audiences and, through dependency, editorial choices, without dictating them; outlets' editorial-algorithm committees (Interviewee 40) are an internal counterweight.
- **[QUESTION-441](../questions.d/QUESTION-441.md)**: weak bearing; the paper describes where visibility criteria come from (platforms, marketing, editors) but not counter-normativity.

Searched record/ (batch ref) for the DOI, title and "Errando": not held. Related held entries: [CLAIM-tmpifp69](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpifp69.md), [CLAIM-404](../claims.d/CLAIM-404.md), [LIT-1331](../literature.d/LIT-1331.md) (recommender systems and epistemic welfare, Active).

## Limitations

Qualitative; three francophone/Spanish markets; 2023–24 snapshot; outlet names given but interview attributions anonymized; no measures of effects on content or audiences.
