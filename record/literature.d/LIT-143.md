# LIT-143: What is a Mathematical Structure of Conscious Experience?

Johannes Kleiner, Tim Ludwig (2023), *arXiv preprint; published in Synthese (online 2024-03-05)* — [ARXIV-2301.11812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11812)

## Key takeaways

- Saying that a mathematical structure "describes" experience is not enough, since it admits incompatible, arbitrarily redefined and consciousness-indifferent structures. A structure S is *of* conscious experience only if experience has an "S-aspect" — one that a variation of experience changes exactly when the variation fails to preserve S. On this test relative similarity and topology (via phenomenal unity) qualify and metric spaces do not.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Consciousness science has several promising ways of representing experience with mathematical spaces and structures (quality spaces, qualia spaces, phenomenal spaces), but no explicit definition of what a "mathematical structure of conscious experience" is. Kleiner and Ludwig propose one. It connects abstract mathematical entities with the concrete facts of experience, complements existing quality-space approaches, gives a general method for finding and studying experiential structures, and may unify approaches across fields. They hope it will ground a common formal language for studying consciousness.
