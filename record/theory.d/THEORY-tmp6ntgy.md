# THEORY-tmp6ntgy: Chang and co-authors imprint designed, anisotropic correlated disorder on nematic liquid-crystal films by photoalignment and observe branched flow of light whose first-branch distance, read from the scintillation index over about ten fabricated films, grows with anisotropy and falls with rotation of the principal axis in agreement with df ∝ ε^−2/3 A^4/3 lx (cos²α + sin²α/A²)^5/6, and at anisotropy 1/1000 channels the light along the long correlation axis with sub-diffusive growth of its velocity-angle spread

Chang et al. (2026), LIT-tmpn3i8n; read in NOTE-tmp5yuxz.

## The result

A 20 µm film of the nematic liquid crystal E7 is photoaligned with a
director pattern, digitized into 18 angles and written with a DMD and a
rotating polarizer, that sets a weak (ε ≈ 0.05) correlated potential with
chosen anisotropy A = ly/lx and rotation α for the guided extraordinary
wave. Because realizations are fabricated to the same statistics, the
scintillation index can be averaged over about ten films. Its peak, the
first-branch distance df, rises with A at α = 0 (A = 1.0, 1.8, 2.6) and
falls as α goes from 0 to π/2 at A = 1.8, in agreement with Eq. 4 and with
simulation. At A = 1/1000 the caustics stay along the principal axis for
α ≤ π/9 and leave it at π/6, and the spread of velocity angles grows as
L^s with s below 1/2. Each could have come out otherwise: df insensitive
to rotation, an elliptical rather than the predicted angular dependence,
or no channeling.

## What it does not show

- An independent measurement of the exponents: the main text shows
  agreement at a few settings, and the fit quality is in the unread
  Supplemental Material.
- A new law: Eq. 3 is Degueldre et al.'s, tested; the paper's "direct
  experimental discovery" is a confirmation.
- That sub-diffusion is general: one exponent estimate at one anisotropy.
- The isotropic scaling or the scintillation diagnostic as its own result;
  both are Patsyk et al. (2020), THEORY-tmp4wies.
- Anything about learning or computation.
