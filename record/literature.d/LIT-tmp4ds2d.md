summary: >-
  An ANU master's thesis in psychology testing Otto Rank's idea that people need to feel they will transcend death, in three modes (children, an afterlife, creative work), to which Lifton added two (nature and experiential transcendence, an expansion of the self). It reviews ethnographic, ancient and modern evidence that all five modes are expressed. Then it surveys 440 Australian adults aged 18 to 88, recruited through Christian, Yoga, Spiritualist and secular groups, using vignettes rated as imagined consolation in terminal illness. Having a mode in one's life and religious affiliation predict accepting it; education predicts accepting the modes less. The modes predict personality only weakly; Rank's links to neuroticism and to sex differences fail.
