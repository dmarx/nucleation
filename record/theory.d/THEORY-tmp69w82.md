# THEORY-tmp69w82: A singular causal claim, that C will contribute or did contribute to E in a particular setting, is warranted only by joint evidence on each step of the causal process there, its causal principle, its support factors and the absence of its derailers, which no single method supplies; a well-run RCT secures at most the study population's average effect, and often not even that rigorously, and its result is not by its method evidence about any other population

## Source

Cartwright (2021), [LIT-213](../literature.d/LIT-213.md) (*Synthese* 199:13095–13119), read in full in
[NOTE-161](../notes.d/NOTE-161.md). The full key to Fig. 1 is in Cartwright et al. (2020), not read.

## What was actually shown

**Rigour defined (§3, p. 13105).** A method is rigorous when there is a
valid argument that its results are evidence for the conclusion, and its
premises are well established or "secured by the design". The author
reconstructs this from the credibility-revolution literature, having found
no explicit definition.

**RCTs against that standard (§3).** Under (i) a potential-outcomes
equation y(u) = β(u)·x(u) + w(u) and (ii) orthogonality of β and w to x,
the difference in arm means is an unbiased estimate of the study-population
average effect, "unbiased" meaning over endless repetitions on the same
population. Randomisation secures orthogonality only at assignment;
blinding removes bias only "in the sense of prejudice". Post-assignment
differences between arms need further, non-design evidence, and premise
(i) carries metaphysics (Davidson's principles, Mackie's INUS conditions)
that no rigorous method makes credible. "External validity" is a misnomer:
nothing about the method makes the result evidence about another population
Q, unless P was randomly sampled from Q (fn. 13).

**The alternative (§4).** A causal-process-tracing theory of change has
five elements: the significant steps, the causal principle by which each
leads to the next, support factors, derailers, and safeguards. Three
evidence demands follow: that each principle operates and is triggered,
that support factors take the right values, that derailers are absent or
guarded. Evidence is gathered "by whatever method" suits each item, and
"to the extent that any have no evidence in their favor, to that extent our
case ... is weakened" (p. 13112). The World Vision Indonesia mHealth
programme illustrates it: untrained volunteer health workers make an
ability-to-use support factor salient.

**What it can do that an RCT cannot (p. 13109).** Give evidence about
individual units; predict for an unstudied setting without transport
assumptions; supply the mechanistic evidence the Russo–Williamson thesis
asks for.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Little in the paper is a test. The
What Works crime toolkit is a case where the review is rigorous about
whether alley-gating has reduced crime and says the contextual factors
"could not be tested", which are what a prediction for one place needs
(p. 13107).

## What this does not say

- **That RCTs are worthless.** Rigour should be used where available; it
  should not "dictate what methods can be used" (§5).
- **How often RCTs fail to secure the average effect.** "Often" is not
  quantified (C3, moderate).
- **That theory-of-change predictions are reliable.** "Can yield reliable
  predictions" (p. 13110) is asserted; no record of such predictions is
  reported (C6, weak).
- **How to weigh the diverse evidence.** There is no aggregation method
  (caution c), pointing to van Evera's tests and Bayesian process tracing.
- **How to transport a theory of change.** "I don't have much to offer"
  (caution e).
- **That one traced pathway settles the outcome.** Masking, dual
  capacities and other causes in w can still defeat E, and showing that C
  caused a bad E does not show that removing C prevents it (caution b).
- **That advocates who mean something weaker by rigour are answered.** A
  reading of rigour as "best available protection against selection bias"
  is not addressed.
- **That every step of every process falls under a principle.** That is the
  method's most controversial assumption, and where it fails the template
  loses its guide.
