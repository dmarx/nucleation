- **A17 (U17): *What a Description of Agency Must Preserve: Behavior,
  Governance, and the Organization of Will*.** 7,000–9,000 words.
  Outline:
  1. Introduction: The missing object of explanation.
  2. Effective descriptions and target-relative adequacy (2.1 preservation
     as factorization; 2.2 informational closure is not all-purpose
     autonomy; 2.3 adequacy changes when the explanatory target changes).
  3. Will as the organization of practical efficacy (3.1 regimes and the
     conditions of efficacy; 3.2 volition is not metacontrol; 3.3
     practical authority is not observable influence alone; 3.4 the dual
     meaning–influence model).
  4. The formal separation: behavioral closure and governance
     preservation (4.1 profiles; 4.2 Proposition 1; 4.3 Γ; 4.4
     Proposition 2; 4.5 what they establish).
  5. Distributed endorsement: how practical authority becomes organized.
  6. Persistence, resolve, and the limits of effective governance (6.1
     maintaining a policy is not maintaining the capacity to revise it;
     6.2 resolve; 6.3 frustration is not resolved by silencing a demand;
     6.4 effective governance is not authentic ownership).
  7. Discussion and conclusion: what must be preserved?

  Appendices: A, formal definitions and proofs; B, relation to closure,
  transfer entropy and Blackwell informativeness. Its closing sentence:
  "**To explain an agent's behavior is not necessarily to explain its
  will, because the organization determining which capacities become
  effective can be lost in an otherwise successful behavioral
  abstraction.**"
