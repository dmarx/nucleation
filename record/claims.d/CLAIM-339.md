# CLAIM-339: A judgement–observation loop cut off from action loses one route for evidence it did not select, and the free-energy principle's reason for minimizing surprise, that a persisting system must keep its states in bounds, needs action on the world, though a loop can confirm itself with action available too

## The claim

The free-energy principle explains why surprise matters. A system that
persists must keep its sensory states within bounds, and Friston et al.
([LIT-1181](../literature.d/LIT-1181.md)) say the active sampling that does so is "mandated by ergodic
arguments based on the very existence of adaptive agents". The enactive
account says the same of norms. Self-production gives a system only the
norm "to go on or to die", and that distinction is available to an
observer, not to the system. A graded norm the system itself has needs
adaptivity, "to detect tendencies toward the boundary of its viability and
to act so as to reverse them" ([THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)). Ashby's ultrastability
needs a channel from the essential variables, which are coupled to the
world ([THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)).

Take action out of a loop of judgement and observation, and the loop loses
the route by which evidence arrives that it did not ask for. That leaves it
more exposed to the failure the record holds as protective lock-in
([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)): a regime kept stable by behaviour that blocks its own
disconfirmation.

Kübler and Birbaumer ([LIT-1199](../literature.d/LIT-1199.md)) give the empirical form. In the
completely locked-in state no patient of theirs regained yes/no
communication, and they proposed that "the complete lack of motor control
and feedback ... might be responsible for the cessation of voluntary
cognitive activity". The judgement loop, on this view, runs down when
action is gone ([CASE-107](../cases.d/CASE-107.md)).

## Conceded

As first filed, this counter said that without action the loop has a
self-confirming minimum. That is true, but it is not specific to the
absence of action. In the active-inference model of [THEORY-285](../theory.d/THEORY-285.md) the agent
can act, and its own sampling policy "withholds the only observations that
could lower that precision" ([NOTE-766](../notes.d/NOTE-766.md)). A loop that selects its own
evidence can confirm itself with or without action ([CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)).

The reading of Friston et al. ([NOTE-960](../notes.d/NOTE-960.md)) concedes more. In the
free-energy formulation, action is not a source of evidence the loop did
not select. It is "sampling selectively what one expects to experience",
and unexpected perturbations "were explained away by action and not
perceived". The paper sees the self-confirmation and embraces it: "this
weakness becomes the strength". Its anchor is not action but existence:
"agents who do not have the right priors cannot exist". And action is
needed there by stipulation, since "the only way that the agent can change
hidden states is through action".

What survives is narrower again. The enactive account ([THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md))
and Ashby's ultrastability ([THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)) make action, or a channel through
the essential variables, what grounds a system's norms. The free-energy
formulation does not supply this counter's premise. On its own terms,
action is one more way a loop selects its own evidence.

## What it does not say

- It does not show that the world can reach the loop only through action.
  [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) names other sources: a source the agent does not select,
  and scoring fixed independently of the interpretation.
- It runs together two things that paralysis takes apart: evidence the
  loop did not select, which a paralysed person still receives through
  the senses, and contingency between intention and outcome, which they
  lose. Kübler and Birbaumer's hypothesis concerns the second. The
  anchor of [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) needs only the first ([QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md)). So
  even if the hypothesis holds, what extinguishes goal-directed thinking
  would not be the loss of an anchor.
- The extinction hypothesis is a hypothesis, stated as what "might be
  responsible", from seven patients already in CLIS when training began
  and an analogy with curarized rats ([NOTE-948](../notes.d/NOTE-948.md)). Its authors say
  passive sensory processing is intact in CLIS, and they predict that a
  contingency learned before CLIS should carry over. Chaudhary et al.'s
  patient, who communicated, had done exactly that ([NOTE-951](../notes.d/NOTE-951.md)), so he
  fits the hypothesis rather than refuting it.

## Note of 2026-10-29: filed with its concessions

[CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md), Active since the ninth status review, objects to this counter.
Its concession is in this counter's text as filed on 2026-10-16: the
section "Conceded" narrows it from a self-confirming minimum without action
to the loss of one route for unselected evidence.
