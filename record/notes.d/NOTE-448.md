# NOTE-448: Goal Striving, Need Satisfaction, and Longitudinal Well-Being: The Self-Concordance Model

## Contribution

The paper joins two of the authors' earlier findings into one causal
sequence and tests it whole:

- Sheldon and Elliot (1998): self-concordant goals are better attained
  because they get sustained effort;
- Sheldon and Kasser (1998): attaining self-concordant goals raises
  well-being more than attaining other goals.

The new link is need satisfaction. Attaining self-concordant goals is
proposed to produce daily experiences of autonomy, competence and
relatedness, whose accumulation over a semester is what raises well-being.
Study 2 tests that accumulation; Study 3 tests the whole model (Figure 1).

## Key insight

Whether a goal is "personal" is not settled by the person having chosen and
listed it. All the goals here were self-generated, "yet this does not mean
that they all belong to the self in a deeper sense" (p. 494). What matters
is the reason the goal is pursued: goals pursued from identification or
interest keep their energy when obstacles come, and their attainment feeds
the needs that well-being rests on. Goals pursued under external pressure or
introjected guilt can be attained and leave the person no better off.

## Assumptions

- **The self is the agentic "I"**: "the more-or-less integrated center of
  agentic activity" (p. 483), the subject-self of James and Mead, not the
  self-concept studied by most social psychologists.
- **SDT's continuum of perceived locus of causality** (Ryan & Connell 1989)
  is one dimension, so a single difference score (identified + intrinsic −
  introjected − external) can stand for it (pp. 483, 486).
- **Three universal needs**: competence, autonomy and relatedness are
  "qualities of experience universally required by human beings in order
  to thrive" (p. 484). Well-being judgments are assumed to be formed partly
  from recalled experiences of these kinds (pp. 484–485, citing Kahneman
  1997).
- **Self-report throughout.** Effort and attainment are the participants'
  own monthly ratings ("How hard are you trying…?", "How well are you
  doing?"), not observed outcomes.
- **Correlational inference.** The authors say causality "cannot be
  established with certainty in correlational data", and present the
  prospective design as "the next best thing" to experiments (p. 493).

## Key results

- **Study 1** (N = 169 students, ten personal strivings, one semester).
  - Self-concordance correlated with semester effort (r = .24) and
    attainment (r = .20); effort with attainment r = .76 (Table 1).
  - With both in the regression, self-concordance dropped out (β = .02,
    ns) and effort stayed (β = .76); Sobel z = 2.93 (p. 486).
  - Change in well-being: attainment β = .34; self-concordance no main
    effect; self-concordance × attainment β = .11, p < .05 (p. 487).
  - SEM (need satisfaction unmeasured): the specified model fit poorly,
    χ²(7, N = 169) = 43.8, p < .01, GFI .93, NFI .87, CFI .88. After adding
    paths from initial well-being to effort and attainment, which the
    modification indices suggested, χ²(5) = 4.95, CFI 1.00 (p. 487).
- **Study 2** (N = 152, three 24-hour activity samples over a semester).
  - At Time 1 each of competence, autonomy and relatedness predicted
    well-being uniquely.
  - Semester-long need satisfaction predicted change in well-being
    (β = .15, .21, .16 for competence, autonomy, relatedness), and the
    coefficients were "almost the same" with Time 1 need satisfaction
    controlled (pp. 488–489).
  - SEM again fit only after adding a path from initial well-being to
    semester need satisfaction: χ²(2, N = 152) = 11.02, p < .01, then
    2.83, p > .05 (p. 489, checked against the page image).
- **Study 3** (N = 73, six personal projects, eight activity samples).
  - Effort mediated again: effort β = .72, self-concordance β = .08, ns;
    Sobel z = 3.12 (p. 491).
  - Change in well-being: attainment β = .28, self-concordance β = .19
    (unpredicted main effect), interaction β = .23, p = .01.
  - Need satisfaction was predicted by attainment (β = .46),
    self-concordance (β = .24) and their interaction (β = .24).
  - Mediation of attainment's effect on well-being by need satisfaction
    was partial: need satisfaction β = .22, attainment β = .19, p = .056;
    Sobel z = 1.98 (pp. 491–492).
  - Whole-model SEM: paths .29 (self-concordance → effort), .71 (effort →
    attainment), .41 (attainment → need satisfaction), .21 (interaction →
    need satisfaction), .30 (need satisfaction → change in well-being);
    χ²(9, N = 73) = 20.3, GFI .93, NFI .89, CFI .93 (p. 492, checked
    against the page image). Adding a direct attainment → well-being path
    (β = .19, p < .07) improved fit somewhat (Δχ² = 3.7, p = .06).
  - The self-concordance → effort path held with self-efficacy,
    implementation intentions, avoidance goals or life skills added, one
    at a time; each of these except avoidance had its own effect on effort.

## Concepts

- **self-concordance**: "the degree to which stated goals express enduring
  interests and values" (p. 482); operationally, identified + intrinsic −
  introjected − external reasons. In earlier work called goal
  self-determination (n. 7).
- **conation**: "the human capacity to set and pursue personal agendas"
  (p. 482), separated from cognitive and affective processes.
- **need satisfaction**: daily, activity-based experiences of competence,
  autonomy and relatedness; the autonomy score uses the same four-reason
  method as self-concordance, applied to the day's activities (n. 5).
- **self-integrated vs. nonintegrated action** (Figure 2): intrinsic and
  identified reasons on one side, introjected and external on the other.

## Connections

- **Ryan & Deci 2000 ([LIT-559](../literature.d/LIT-559.md)) and Deci & Ryan 2000 ([LIT-558](../literature.d/LIT-558.md)).** The
  paper is the SDT goal literature these reviews later draw on, and it
  states the three needs in their terms. Its own SDT sources are Deci and
  Ryan 1985 and 1991, which the record does not hold.
- **[THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)** (autonomy as graded integration). Evidence for the
  autonomous end of the continuum, not for integration as distinct from
  identification: there is no integrated-regulation item, and identified
  and intrinsic are pooled. [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md) notes SDT studies "usually" merge
  these; this is one. The measure is the person's own report of reasons,
  which is the endorsement-based measurement [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)'s refutation clause
  worries about.
- **[THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)** (history of acquisition fixes autonomy). Not tested: no
  history is measured. C7 states the difficulty [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md) faces from inside
  SDT: the process that makes an injunction alien is also the one by which
  culture is internalized.
- **[THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md)** (need frustration as transdiagnostic). C3 is the
  well-being side of the same need theory, in a nonclinical sample; it says
  nothing about frustration or disorder.
- **[THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md)** (no attitude owns a motive by its order). The paper's
  criterion is felt ownership, "whether the person feels ownership as he or
  she pursues the goal" (p. 484), not order of endorsement. It takes the
  person's report as the measure and does not ask whether that report could
  itself be alienated, which is [THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md)'s question.
- **Authenticity ([LIT-574](../literature.d/LIT-574.md), [LIT-594](../literature.d/LIT-594.md)).** The paper speaks of
  goals that "represent … that person's authentic interests and values"
  (p. 483) and of the "true self" (pp. 485, 493), and draws on Rogers's
  congruence (pp. 485, 495). Its Kaczynski passage (p. 495) concedes that
  self-concordance is silent on the content of goals; Taylor's book is the
  philosophical statement of why that matters.
- **Schlegel et al. ([LIT-606](../literature.d/LIT-606.md)).** Sheldon is thanked in that paper's
  acknowledgements; it measures the true self-concept directly rather than
  through goal reasons.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY filed** as [THEORY-077](../theory.d/THEORY-077.md). "Goals pursued for identified or intrinsic reasons
  receive more sustained effort, which carries their better attainment"
  is well supported by this paper and by the 1998 work it recaps, and
  is filed Proposed with this paper as source. It should
  promote on a replication with an effort or attainment measure that is not
  the participant's own rating.
- **For [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md),** the paper is supporting but not decisive evidence;
  it should be cited as showing that the autonomous end predicts, not that
  integration does.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **All self-report**, including effort and attainment; shared method
  variance could carry the effort → attainment path (r = .76).
- **Small, homogeneous samples**: Rochester students, one semester each;
  Study 3 has 73 participants for a model with many paths.
- **Post hoc model revision.** Studies 1 and 2 reached acceptable fit only
  after adding paths from initial well-being that the modification indices
  suggested, and Study 3's "adequate" fit has a χ² of 20.3 on 9 df, which
  is significant at .05. The authors say the added paths are "not
  inconsistent with our conceptual model".
- **The model needs amending by its own data**: attainment's direct effect
  on well-being is not carried by need satisfaction (Study 3).
- **Integration is not measured** apart from identification (see
  Connections).
- **Content is out of scope**: goals with "manifestly evil" content are
  acknowledged and set aside (pp. 494–495).

## Open questions

- Would an observed measure of attainment (grades, gym visits) keep the
  effort mediation?
- Does integrated regulation, measured separately from identified, add
  anything to the prediction?
- Does the attainment × self-concordance interaction survive in a sample
  large enough to estimate it precisely?

## Corrections

- none to a seeded skim (there was no seed)
- **Citation.** One search summary gave the pages as 546–557; the printed
  article is pp. 482–497. The brief's citation (JPSP 76(3), 1999) is right.
- **Study 3 fit.** The paper calls χ²(9, N = 73) = 20.3 "p > .01" and the
  fit "adequate"; 20.3 on 9 df has p ≈ .016, so the model is rejected at
  .05 and passes only at .01. The printed statement is true but reads as
  more reassuring than the test is.
- **Study 2 degrees of freedom.** Both the original and the revised Study 2
  models are printed as χ²(2, N = 152) (p. 489), but the revision adds a
  free path, which should cost a degree of freedom. One of the two df
  values is a misprint, or the revision changed something else that the
  text does not report.
