# LIT-623: Bayesian Interpolation

David J. C. MacKay (1992), *Neural Computation* 4(3):415–447 — DOI-10.1162/neco.1992.4.3.415

## Key takeaways

- **The evidence is the transportable quantity.** Model comparison is a
  second level of inference, distinct from fitting. Models are ranked by
  P(D|H), "the Bayesian's transportable quantity for comparing alternative
  models", whether parametric or not. The prior over models is usually
  overwhelmed by it.
- **The Occam factor.** P(D|H) ≈ P(D|w_MP, H) · P(w_MP|H) σ_{w|D}. In k
  dimensions with posterior Hessian A, the Occam factor is
  P(w_MP|H)(2π)^{k/2} det^{-1/2}A (Eq. 6). It is "the factor by which H's
  hypothesis space collapses when the data arrive". Its log is the
  information gained about the parameters. It penalises both many
  parameters and parameters that must be finely tuned.
- **γ, the effective number of parameters.** With eigenvalues λ_a of the
  data Hessian βB, γ = k − α Trace A⁻¹ = Σ λ_a/(λ_a + α) (Eq. 23). Each term
  measures how much the data rather than the prior determine one
  direction. At the evidence maximum, χ²_W = 2αE_W = γ and
  χ²_D = 2βE_D = N − γ (Eqs. 22, 24): the noise estimate charges one unit of
  misfit per well-determined parameter.
- **Bayes cannot systematically reject the truth.** The expected log-evidence
  difference between the true model and any fixed alternative, under the
  truth, is a KL divergence and so non-negative (Gibbs' inequality). This
  also gives a test for numerical evidence code.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the model-comparison batch.
It may also belong in the anthology: its argument that evidence should set
regularisation constants, and its claim that early stopping is a symptom of
a model ill-matched to the data, are about how to train models. The
anthology's `model-stability` (regularisation) or `analysis-and-evaluation`
(theory) topics could hold it, so it carries the `anthology-candidate` flag
last ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)). The anthology does not hold it.

It is here for nucleation's question, model comparison as one line with
Fisher geometry. The Occam factor is a ratio of volumes measured by the
determinant of the posterior Hessian, which for a likelihood-dominated
posterior is the observed Fisher information. So the evidence framework
already ties model comparison to the curvature of the model at its best
fit. It also shows where that tie breaks. The Gaussian approximation
assumes a single nondegenerate peak. MacKay notes that relabelling
degeneracies need a multiplicity factor, and that multiple maxima
"complicate the analysis". Singular models, in which the Hessian is
degenerate at the optimum, are the case Watanabe's theory ([LIT-354](LIT-354.md),
[LIT-616](LIT-616.md)) handles and this paper does not.

Where it sits in the batch:

- **Wagenmakers et al. ([LIT-609](LIT-609.md))** state the same automatic Occam's
  razor (their §2.2.1), citing MacKay's 2003 textbook.

- **WBIC ([LIT-616](LIT-616.md))** replaces the Occam penalty (k/2) log n of BIC,
  which the Gaussian approximation underwrites, with λ log n for singular

- **MDL.** MacKay calls minimum description length "closely related" and
  says he "can see no advantage in MDL". The record holds MDL only in
