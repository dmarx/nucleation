# CASE-395: A proposed study: research organizations matched in initial expertise, technical resources and performance on familiar inquiries but differing in how inquiry is authorized and perspectives maintained, given opportunities graded by novelty, conceptual distance and time sensitivity; the discriminating result is a difference in success on the novel ones, and a stronger design intervenes separately on concepts, authorization and answer production

## The design

A63 §9.4: "compare research organizations possessing matched initial
expertise and technical resources but different arrangements of inquiry
authorization and perspective maintenance." One "might require centralized
approval before exploratory work. Another might allocate limited protected
resources to specialized communities while reserving consequential decisions
for broader evaluation." Investigators "introduce research opportunities
varying in their novelty, conceptual distance from established methods, and
temporal sensitivity."

Outcomes: "the cost of formulating questions, the cost of making them
legible to evaluators, the probability of authorization, time to adequate
answer, and the ability to resume initially inactive research programs."

The discriminating result: "**Two organizations with matched initial
technical competence and matched performance on familiar inquiries exhibit
different rates of success on novel inquiries because their authorization
and perspective-maintenance arrangements differ.**" That "would support the
claim that joint inquiry capacity cannot be adequately measured by present
competence alone" ([CLAIM-1190](../claims.d/CLAIM-1190.md); [CLAIM-1025](../claims.d/CLAIM-1025.md)'s organizations with the
same frontiers and different joint sets are its construction).

The stronger test "would intervene separately on conceptual resources,
authorization procedures, and technical answer-production capabilities to
determine which causal mechanism accounts for the difference", and "examine
whether successful inquiry changes the future space of askable questions"
([CLAIM-475](../claims.d/CLAIM-475.md)'s point that what can be asked is a product of what has been
realized).

## Beside the record's studies

- **[CASE-388](CASE-388.md)** (A56 §8) runs proposals through four arrangements and
  asks whether unfamiliar classes survive. This study's unit is the
  organization, and its control is matched performance on familiar work, so
  the familiar–novel gap is the measure. Its note's three gaps carry over:
  independent promise, the unit of comparison, and that staged
  authorization changes the unit of authorization ([CLAIM-1016](../claims.d/CLAIM-1016.md)) rather
  than the cost per proposal.
- **[CASE-412](CASE-412.md) and [CASE-397](CASE-397.md)**, the matched-performance model, run:
  regimes with identical output until a contingency arrives differ widely
  in readiness for it, and against a demand the specification does not name
  a reserve reaches the harm-free configuration where a redesign does not.
  That is the predicted pattern, matched on the familiar and different on the
  novel, produced by budget rules and reserves, not by authorization or
  perspective maintenance. The stronger design's separate interventions
  should add one on slack: an organization that keeps a reserve and one
  that does not, with authorization held fixed.
- **[CLAIM-1054](../claims.d/CLAIM-1054.md)**: equivalence under routine queries hides a lost
  capacity until an unasked query arrives. The study's novel opportunities
  are those queries.

## What it can show

A design, not a run. It can show that arrangements differ in what they make
of novel opportunities at matched familiar performance. Without an
independent standard of success on the novel opportunities, A56's caution
holds: an arrangement could "reward proposals that fit its own categories
and declare itself successful". The points beside the record's studies are
the assistant's (Claude, in this session).
