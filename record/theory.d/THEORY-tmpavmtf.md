# THEORY-tmpavmtf: Prediction-market prices keep much of an arbitrary price shock that no trader's information supports for weeks, rather than correcting it within a short transition phase

## Source

Rasooly and Rozzi (2025), [LIT-958](../literature.d/LIT-958.md), read in [NOTE-778](../notes.d/NOTE-778.md): the randomised field experiment (claims C1 and C2, strong), and the follow-up on 110 real-money-convertible markets (C6, weak: no control group, 6–7 days).

## What is asserted, and by whom

The thesis is that persistence of unsupported shocks is a property of prediction markets, not of Manifold's play money: a one-off shock that no information supports is still largely in the price weeks later. The authors' model, in which finitely many risk-averse traders with price impact undo a shock only partly even without learning, is one explanation; the data cannot tell it from learning from the price, and this entry does not assert the mechanism.

## What the record holds toward it

- [THEORY-267](THEORY-267.md) (Active): about 59% of a ±5-point shock still present after 60 days in unresolved Manifold markets, with fast reversion in the first week and slower after.
- The real-money follow-up, which found manipulability not much changed, is weak support only.

## What would confirm or reject it

A second randomised manipulation experiment on another platform, preferably with real money and larger markets, that again finds most of the shock after several weeks would confirm it; one that finds the shock gone within days would reject it beyond Manifold. [QUESTION-212](../questions.d/QUESTION-212.md) asks this as a question; this entry is the answer Rasooly and Rozzi propose.
