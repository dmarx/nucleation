# NOTE-tmpi27ob: Seth — Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self

## Contribution

The article applies predictive coding, until then worked out mainly for
exteroception and action, to the sense of the body's internal state. It
makes two claims under one mechanism. First, felt emotion is the content of
top-down inference about what is causing interoceptive signals, not a
bottom-up readout of the body later appraised. Second, the experience of
owning a body is the same kind of inference, run across interoceptive and
exteroceptive signals at once. This turns the rubber hand illusion from a
result about vision, touch and proprioception into one where the heartbeat
can also count. The article offers this as a framework with indirect
support. It says itself that there is no direct test yet.

## Key insight

The brain has to model the body it belongs to, inside and out, as it
models anything else. It infers which signals are "most likely to be me"
(p. 567, after Apps & Tsakiris). What is felt as emotion, and as a body
being one's own, is the current best guess of that model. Prediction error
can be reduced in two ways: by changing the guess, or by making the body
fit it. Pushing the viscera towards predicted set points is the
interoceptive counterpart of moving a limb to fulfil a proprioceptive
prediction.

## Assumptions

- **Predictive coding is the brain's general scheme.** Perception is
  "probabilistic, knowledge-driven inference on the external causes of
  sensory signals" (p. 566). Errors flow up and predictions down, weighted
  by precision. Attention is "the optimization of precision weighting"
  (p. 567). Box 1 grants that "unequivocal neural evidence for PC is still
  lacking" (p. 566).
- **The free-energy reading.** Under Gaussian assumptions, free energy is
  prediction error. Active inference is taken from Friston et al. 2010
  (ref. 21), and active motor inference from Adams et al. 2013 (ref. 29).
- **Selfhood is a "constellation concept"** (p. 565). It runs from
  homeostatic control through body ownership, perspective and agency to
  the metacognitive and narrative "I", citing Metzinger's *Being No One*
  (ref. 17). The article treats body ownership and emotion. The narrative
  levels are called "more speculatively" within reach (p. 570).
- **Interoception is not feed-forward.** The case rests on evidence of
  "substantial cross-talk" between levels, including "top-down cortical
  and behavioural influences to brainstem and spinal centres" (p. 567,
  citing Critchley & Harrison 2013).
- **The anterior insula is the comparator.** Most of the evidence "rest[s]
  on assuming a central role for the anterior insular cortex" (p. 567).

## Key results

There are no new data apart from Figure 3, which reproduces the author's
own group's study. By section:

- **Emotion as interoceptive inference (p. 567).**
  - "Interoceptive inference involves hierarchically cascading top-down
    interoceptive predictions that counterflow with bottom-up interoceptive
    prediction errors". Felt emotions "are hypothesized to depend on the
    integrated content of these predictive representations across
    multiple levels".
  - The lineage given runs from James and Lange, through "appraisal"
    theories, to Schachter and Singer's adrenaline study and their
    two-factor theory, "emotion as interpreted bodily arousal". The new
    model "generalizes" appraisal (p. 565).
- **Active interoceptive inference (p. 567, Figure 2).**
  - Interoceptive errors can be cancelled "by transcribing these
    predictions into reference points for autonomic reflexes" (after Gu et
    al. 2013). This "directly parallels" active inference in motor control.
  - Descending predictions drive reflexes "only if the corresponding error
    signals have diminished impact ... via transiently low precision
    weighting". So "active interoceptive inference depends on the
    selective attenuation of attention to interoceptive prediction
    errors".
  - Figure 2 places the comparator in a "salience network" anchored on the
    anterior insula and anterior cingulate. Autonomic outflow from them is
    "in the form of interoceptive predictions that enslave autonomic
    reflexes".
- **Evidence (pp. 567–569).** "There is not yet any direct confirmatory
  evidence for interoceptive inference", only "supportive data". These
  are:
  - fMRI of the anterior insula, which responds to anticipated pain and
    affective touch, and encodes predicted pain with pain prediction error,
    and risk with risk prediction error;
  - contagious itch (Holle et al.), read as a conflict between predicted
    and actual interoceptive state;
  - Bayesian updating of expected feelings in the ultimatum game, with
    insula activity scaling with "feeling prediction errors" (Xiang et
    al.);
  - false cardiac feedback, which enhanced emotional ratings (Valins 1966)
    and raised right-insula responses and the emotional salience of
    neutral stimuli (Gray et al. 2007).
  The verdict: "indirect support" (p. 569).
- **Box 2 (pp. 568–569).** The anterior insula is described as a "limbic
  sensory area" beside the cingulate's "limbic motor area". It has a high
  density of von Economo neurons, associated "frequently though
  circumstantially" with awareness and selfhood. Insula activity and
  morphometry predict heartbeat-detection sensitivity. Its role in
  conscious access is hedged "(though see [43,74])", where ref. 43 is
  Damasio et al. 2013 on feelings persisting after bilateral insula damage.
- **Rival models (p. 569).** Damasio, Craig and Critchley all ground the
  self in representations of the body. But "none identify emotional states
  with top-down inference of the causes of interoceptive signals"; they
  "emphasize a continuous, dynamic, but largely bottom-up interoceptive
  representational hierarchy". Singer et al.'s account of insula
  error-based learning uses "prediction error" for change detection and
  salience, not predictive coding.
- **Body ownership (p. 569).**
  - The rubber hand illusion (Botvinick & Cohen 1998) is described, with
    face (enfacement) and whole-body versions (Lenggenhager et al. 2007;
    Ehrsson 2007).
  - The standard multisensory explanation, visual capture from sensory
    correlations, is "compatible with PC". Highly precise vision and touch
    "could overcome prediction errors in a different modality
    (proprioception)". The result is a revised model "incorporating the
    fake hand as part of the self-representation".
  - "Perceptual correlations are not necessary for the RHI; the
    expectation of correlated sensory input is sufficient" (Ferri et al.
    2013).
  - Lesion and imaging data link body ownership to the anterior insula
    (Karnath & Baier 2010).
- **Interoception and the rubber hand (p. 569).**
  - "Participants with lower IS are more susceptible to the RHI"
    (Tsakiris et al. 2011), and also to shifts of self–other boundaries.
    Seth reads this as "possibly reflecting lower precision-weighting of
    interoceptive prediction errors".
  - During the illusion the real hand cools and its histamine reactivity
    rises. Cooling the real hand raises susceptibility. Threats to the
    rubber hand raise skin conductance. All are called "compatible with
    descending self-related predictions tuning reference points for
    autonomic reflexes".
- **The cardio-visual rubber hand (pp. 569–570, Figure 3).**
  - Suzuki et al. 2013 built an augmented-reality hand whose colour
    pulsed towards red over 500 ms, in or out of time with the
    heartbeat. Synchronous feedback "enhanced the experience of ownership
    of the virtual hand", both by questionnaire and by proprioceptive
    drift, with the parenthesis "these two measures do not always
    correlate".
  - The same setup replicated the tactile illusion. When finger movements
    were mapped to the virtual hand, visual–proprioceptive coherence
    "overshadowed the influence of cardiac (interoceptive) prediction
    errors". The caption: the drift effect held in the "cardiac still" but
    not the "cardiac move" condition, and control questions showed no
    effect.
  - Aspell et al. 2013 applied the same feedback to a virtual body seen
    from behind, and synchrony "increased identification with the virtual
    body", objectively and subjectively.
  - The inference drawn: "statistical correlations between interoceptive
    (e.g., cardiac) and exteroceptive (e.g., visual) signals can lead to
    updating of predictive models of self-related signals ... just as may
    happen for purely exteroceptive multisensory conflicts" (p. 570).
- **Conclusion (pp. 570–571).** "Emotion and embodied selfhood are
  grounded in active inference of those signals most likely to be 'me'".
  "Perceptual scenes that evoke interoceptive predictions will always be
  affectively coloured."
- **Box 3, psychopathology (p. 571).**
  - Alexithymia goes with failure to engage the anterior insula.
  - Low interoceptive sensitivity "provides a bridge" to disorders of body
    ownership.
  - Depersonalization and derealisation, with their "loss of 'emotional
    colour'", may come from "imprecise (as opposed to inaccurate)
    interoceptive predictions", after Seth et al. 2011.
  - The step to Cotard's delusion "may also reflect aberrant high-level
    inference".
- **Box 4 (p. 571).** The open questions include how interoceptive
  predictive models relate to "similar models of agency", and whether the
  narrative self can be accommodated.

## Concepts

- **Interoceptive inference** (equivalently here, interoceptive
  predictive coding): hierarchical top-down prediction of interoceptive
  signals, compared with bottom-up error. Felt emotion depends on its
  integrated content.
- **Active (interoceptive) inference**: cancelling prediction error by
  acting, here through autonomic reflexes whose reference points are set
  by descending predictions.
- **Precision**: the inverse variance of a prediction error, which sets
  its influence. Attention is the optimisation of precision.
- **Experience of body ownership (EBO)**: "the experience of certain
  parts of the world as belonging to one's body", for a part (a hand) or
  globally (a whole body) (glossary).
- **Interoceptive sensitivity (IS)**: a trait, "usually operationalized
  via heartbeat detection tasks" (glossary).
- **Selfhood**: "the experience of being a distinct, holistic entity,
  capable of global self-control and attention, possessing a body and a
  location in space and time", citing Blanke & Metzinger 2009 (glossary).
- **Signals "most likely to be me"**: the target of self-related
  inference, after Apps & Tsakiris.

## Connections

The article builds on predictive coding as Clark, Friston and Hohwy state
it, and on the author's earlier interoceptive model of "conscious presence"
(Seth et al. 2011, ref. 6). For emotion it builds on Gu et al. 2013.
On body ownership it builds on Botvinick & Cohen 1998, Lenggenhager et al.
2007, Blanke 2012 and Blanke & Metzinger 2009, and on Tsakiris's work on
interoceptive sensitivity. It takes the free-energy self of Apps & Tsakiris
(cited as 2013, online) as its frame for self-recognition. It sets itself
against the bottom-up interoceptive accounts of Damasio (2000; Damasio &
Carvalho 2013), Craig and Critchley. Of these, the record holds Damasio &
Carvalho ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)); Botvinick & Cohen, Lenggenhager et al., Blanke &
Metzinger and Apps & Tsakiris are being filed alongside it. The reference list
gives Suzuki et al.'s title with "interoceptive and exteroceptive"
reversed; Crossref has "exteroceptive and interoceptive".

## Bearing on the record

- **The interoception cluster and body ownership.** Before this filing the
  record held interoception as the ground of feeling ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md), [NOTE-330](NOTE-330.md);
  [LIT-628](../literature.d/LIT-628.md), [NOTE-507](NOTE-507.md)), of emotion ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), [NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md)) and of brain
  function ([LIT-996](../literature.d/LIT-996.md), [NOTE-761](NOTE-761.md)). It held body ownership only through
  Metzinger ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md)). This is the work that joins them: it
  makes ownership an inference over interoceptive and exteroceptive
  signals, and it cites evidence that the heartbeat enters it (C6, C7).
- **[THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md) (felt affect).** The account leaves open whether what is
  felt is a prediction of the body's state or a map of it. Seth takes the
  prediction side explicitly, and places Damasio's account, including
  [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md) (his ref. 14), on the bottom-up side (C1). He gives no case where
  prediction and map come apart, which is what [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)'s promotion test
  asks for. He does cite the other kind of result it names: false cardiac
  feedback that moves emotional ratings and salience (C4). That is reported
  at second hand, with emotive stimuli, so it falls inside an emotion
  episode, not outside one as the test requires. He also cites, as a
  hedge, Damasio et al. 2013 on feelings surviving insula damage, the case
  [NOTE-330](NOTE-330.md) reads for Damasio's side. A source for [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md), not a test
  of it. It bears too on [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md), through C2: on this account the
  felt state is tied to a body that the predictions act on.
- **[NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md) (Barrett).** Barrett's 2017 synthesis cites this article for
  its claim C1. The two agree on prediction. They differ on what does the
  work. For Seth it is the inferred cause of interoceptive change; for
  Barrett it is a concept that categorises. Seth's comparator is the
  anterior insula, while Barrett makes the agranular limbic cortex the
  source of predictions. The comparison is mine.
- **[LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md), [NOTE-169](NOTE-169.md) (Seth 2024).** This is the earlier statement of the
  interoceptive predictive processing on which Seth's biological
  naturalism rests ([NOTE-169](NOTE-169.md), C4). Here it is a claim about emotion and
  self, not about consciousness in general, and nothing in it says that
  only living systems could run such inference.
- **[LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md), [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) (the self-model).** Metzinger's C6, that
  body ownership can be manipulated by multisensory conflict, gets a
  mechanism here: the self-model is revised to cut precision-weighted
  error. It also gets an interoceptive channel the article does not
  have. Seth's account of the rubber hand illusion is a self-model being
  revised, which is consistent with [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md). But it is silent on
  transparency and on whether anything beyond the model is a self, so it
  neither supports nor tests [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)'s distinctive claims. Box 3's
  depersonalization proposal (C9) is the kind of case [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)'s
  promotion test names. It is offered there as a precision failure, not
  as a self-model becoming opaque, and it is speculative.
- **[LIT-1181](../literature.d/LIT-1181.md), [NOTE-960](NOTE-960.md) (Friston et al. 2010).** This is the article's
  ref. 21, the source of its active inference. Seth extends that paper's
  motor account, action fulfilling proprioceptive predictions, to
  autonomic reflexes (C2). The extension is by analogy and is not
  derived.
- **[LIT-996](../literature.d/LIT-996.md), [THEORY-291](../theory.d/THEORY-291.md) (Theriault et al.).** Figure 2's salience network,
  anchored on the anterior insula and cingulate and driving brainstem
  visceromotor targets, is an early form of the visceromotor–interoceptive
  system [THEORY-291](../theory.d/THEORY-291.md) describes. Seth gives it as a schematic, not as
  connectivity data.
- **[LIT-1009](../literature.d/LIT-1009.md), [NOTE-827](NOTE-827.md) (Haggard on agency).** Box 4 asks how
  interoceptive models relate to "similar models of agency". Haggard's
  review reports the anterior insula as the only area consistently
  associated with positive self-agency, the region Seth makes the
  interoceptive comparator. Neither paper draws the link; it is mine.
- **THEORY warranted.** No THEORY in the record states that
  body ownership draws on interoceptive as well as exteroceptive
  signals. This reading supports proposing one, and the
  sibling filings on the rubber hand and full-body illusions would be its
  other sources.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- An opinion article. Every empirical result is cited and not shown,
  except Figure 3, which reproduces the author's own study. No effect
  sizes are given in the text.
- The author says "there is not yet any direct confirmatory evidence"
  (p. 567). Most of the support depends on assuming the anterior insula is
  the comparator.
- The body-ownership account explains the standard rubber hand results
  again in predictive-coding terms. The article calls them "compatible
  with PC" and gives no result that predictive coding predicts and a
  plain multisensory account does not.
- The interoceptive-sensitivity finding rests on heartbeat detection,
  whose validity the article does not discuss, and on one study cited for
  the rubber hand illusion.
- The autonomic results (cooling, histamine, skin conductance) are said to
  be "compatible with" active inference, not predicted by it.
- Two comment letters in Trends in Cognitive Sciences (2014) are listed
  by PubMed and were not read.

## Open questions

- What result would show that felt emotion follows the predicted rather
  than the actual interoceptive state? The false-feedback studies come
  closest, but they change the signal, not the prediction.
- Is the effect of cardio-visual synchrony on ownership interoceptive, or
  just one more temporal correlation that vision can lock to? A control
  using a non-bodily periodic signal at heart rate would separate the two.
- How do interoceptive predictive models relate to "similar models of
  agency"? Box 4 asks this and leaves it open.
- Does low interoceptive sensitivity mean low precision on interoceptive
  error, as Seth reads it, or simply a worse heartbeat-counting
  performance?
