# CLAIM-155: Willed agency is a kind between agency and selfhood: a control system whose behaviour is governed by a will-organization over several regimes is more than an agent and closer to a self, in the self-governing sense, without thereby being a subject

## The claim

The owner, verbatim, on 2026-10-11: "or maybe a control system identifiable
by a will is a special kind that we should differentiate from 'agency'? This
might be the distinction I was trying to touch on with my 'self'-ness
question. we can have agency without a 'self', but a control system which
can be properly be characterized as possessing a 'will' might be worth
differentiating, at the very least insofar as it might be closer to a 'self'
than a thermostat." On the name: "willed agent works".

The tiers nest:

| tier | test | example that reaches it and no further |
|---|---|---|
| control system | feedback toward a setpoint | a bare thermostat |
| agent ([TERM-044](../terms.d/TERM-044.md)) | system, description, adaptation | a thermostat with its designers; an RL training process |
| willed agent ([TERM-048](../terms.d/TERM-048.md)) | plus a will-organization over several regimes | perhaps a firm such as Northstar ([CASE-023](../cases.d/CASE-023.md)) |
| self ([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)) | further unities, sense stated | a person, in the subject sense |

## Why this narrows the owner's earlier thesis

[CLAIM-170](CLAIM-170.md) said agents are identified by their will-organization ([TERM-023](../terms.d/TERM-023.md)). The
readings show that agency is wider. Kenton et al.'s and Orseau et al.'s
tests admit systems with no governance over several regimes. So:
- **agents in general** are identified by adaptation and description;
- **willed agents** are identified by the jurisdiction of their
  will-organization.

The factorization half of the owner's thesis, relative to a level and a
target, stands for both tiers. [CLAIM-170](CLAIM-170.md) version 2 narrows its
individuation half to willed agents.

## Why "closer to a self", and in which sense

A will-organization gives a system standards that decide among its own
regimes, and with them the persistence of that governance through changes
in what drives ([CLAIM-316](CLAIM-316.md)). That is near [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s self-governance
unity ([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md) sense 5) and supports persistence (sense 3). It gives
no point of view (sense 1). That is why the firm is the hard case: it may be
willed and self-governing without being a subject. And it is why the
thermostat is far from a self on every sense.

## The permissiveness objection, answered on its merits

The will paper's [CLAIM-010](CLAIM-010.md) said that on its account every control system has
a will. The paper set this aside by supposing its agent ([CLAIM-080](CLAIM-080.md)). On the
tiers it is simply false: control systems include agents, agents include
willed agents, and the inclusions are strict. [CLAIM-080](CLAIM-080.md) remains a sound
scoping decision for the paper. It is no longer the only answer.

## What it does not say

- **That will is required for selfhood in the subject sense.** A subject
  without governance over several regimes is not ruled out.
- **That any firm is a willed agent.** That is the test, applied.
