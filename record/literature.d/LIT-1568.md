# LIT-1568: The Black Queen Hypothesis: Evolution of Dependencies through Adaptive Gene Loss

Morris, J. J., Lenski, R. E. & Zinser, E. R. (2012). The Black Queen Hypothesis: Evolution of Dependencies through Adaptive Gene Loss. *mBio* 3(2): e00036-12. doi:10.1128/mBio.00036-12.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1212](../notes.d/NOTE-1212.md).*

- **Leaky and costly.** A function whose product leaks to neighbours is
  dispensable to any one cell while others perform it. Losing it saves a
  fraction B = (Q_A − Q_M)/Q_A of a limiting resource.
- **Self-limiting loss.** The cost of loss rises as producers thin out, so
  loss stops where B = C, leaving rare "helpers" and many dependent
  "beneficiaries" ([THEORY-1048](../theory.d/THEORY-1048.md)).
- **Not kin selection, not necessarily cheating.** No relatedness is
  needed. Beneficiaries harm helpers only when they share a limiting
  resource.
- **One case.** Prochlorococcus lacks katG and depends on its community to
  remove peroxide. The 0.2% iron saving is computed, and the other
  functions named are suggestions.
- **Scope.** It is stated for free-living microbes, and endosymbiont
  reduction is credited to drift.

## Why this is here

A1 and A2 cite it as the case where dependency arises from selection for individual gene loss, not from selection for the collective; A2 rates it "Critical". One correction from the abstract: the hypothesis is stated for free-living community members, and it attributes the reduction common in endosymbionts to drift. The exchange applies it to symbiosis generally. A reading should say whether the paper extends it to endosymbionts.
