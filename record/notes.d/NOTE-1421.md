# NOTE-1421: Lynch et al. — RefraMED, the NIHR EME monograph

## Contribution

It is the full account of the trial the record holds through its journal
paper ([LIT-1223](../literature.d/LIT-1223.md), [NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md)): the same 250 participants, the same
primary result and the same harms, with the health economics and the
mechanism analysis that the journal paper leaves out. What it adds is a
design. Within the RO DBT arm participants were randomised a second time,
to features meant to move the hypothesised mediators without being
therapy, so that the mediators' effects could be estimated by
instrumental variables free of the self-selection that biases ordinary
mediation. The authors say it is the first psychotherapy trial to do
this. It did not work: the instruments barely moved the mediators.

## Key insight

The trial knew what mediation needs and could not get it. Patients pick
how many sessions they attend, how well they bond with the therapist and
how much they learn, and whatever leads them to pick also bears on how
depressed they stay; so the authors randomised small things (a nicer
room, a feedback form, cheques handed over in session) to get exogenous
variation in those mediators. The variation was too small. The authors'
own conclusion is that to estimate mechanisms a trial has to randomise
parts of the treatment itself, more or less exposure to its core
elements, rather than additions with weak effects on the mediators.

## Assumptions

- **The mediators are process variables.** Exposure, alliance and skills
  are set to zero for usual-care participants, so there is no mediated
  path in that arm, only a direct effect (§2, "Process variables"). The
  DBT Ways of Coping Checklist is measured in both arms but treated as
  zero in usual care.
- **Timing.** The causal model takes exposure at 7 months, and alliance
  and skills "together at 7 months", as contemporaneous intermediate
  outcomes, and the HRSD outcome is also at 7 months (§2; §4,
  "Instrumental variables analysis"). Nothing in the model puts the
  mediator before the outcome.
- **Exclusion restriction.** Each instrument (room, feedback, mode of
  reimbursement, therapist) affects the HRSD only through the mediators.
  The randomised allocation itself cannot be an instrument, because its
  direct effect is one of the quantities sought.
- **Population and outcome model** as in the journal paper: refractory
  depression with the cluster B, bipolar, psychotic and substance
  presentations excluded; three-level mixed models (time within patient
  within therapist), missing at random.

## Key results

- **Efficacy (Table 8).** HRSD, RO DBT minus usual care: −5.40 (SE 1.92,
  95% CI −9.85 to −0.94, p = 0.024) at 7 months, −2.15 (−6.59 to 2.28,
  p = 0.290) at the 12-month primary end-point, −1.69 (−6.22 to 2.84,
  p = 0.424) at 18. These match the BJP paper.
- **Process outcomes (Table 8).** AAQ-II −3.37 (SE 1.30, p = 0.010),
  −4.94 (p < 0.001), −5.48 (p = 0.001); EAC +1.50 (SE 0.71, p = 0.036),
  +3.55 (p < 0.001), +2.98 (p = 0.002), at 7, 12 and 18 months. Each p
  agrees with its t = delta/SE. The BJP paper gives the same differences
  with intervals about 1.8 times wider (AAQ-II at 7 months −7.95 to 1.21,
  P = 0.096; EAC −0.84 to 3.83, P = 0.132); neither document says why.
  The report's Discussion calls the 7-month EAC difference "nearly
  significant", against its own Table 8.
- **Instrument design (§2).** Within RO DBT a 2 × 2 × 2 factorial:
  standard or enhanced therapy room; written feedback to the therapist
  after each session or not; questionnaire payment by the therapist in
  session or by post. Therapists were also randomised within centres.
  Observational instruments were planned besides (therapist style,
  pre-trial alliance, travel distance, a pre-randomisation homework task,
  conscientiousness).
- **Weak instruments (§3).** Cragg–Donald statistics no larger than 1,
  against 9.08 needed to keep bias under 10%. The IV estimates are
  "subject to severe weak-instrument bias" and, in the tables' own note,
  have no meaningful interpretation; the Discussion concludes there is as
  yet no proof of principle for IV mediation.
- **Decomposition without instruments (Table 17), HRSD at 7 months.**
  Exposure: total −3.83, direct −0.69, indirect −3.14 (−12 to 2.8).
  Skills: total −3.58, direct +11.27, indirect −14.85 (−19 to −12).
  Alliance: total −3.83, direct +15.59 (3.5 to 28), indirect −19.42 (−30
  to −7.7). The "significant" indirect effects are four to five times the
  total, with direct effects of the opposite sign; by the report's own
  rule under its Equation 3, a proportion mediated is then meaningless.
- **All-mediators model (Table 18), n = 150.** Total mediated −5.59 (−21
  to 5.9); via skills −6.46 (−12.8 to −2.04); via alliance −1.24 (−7.1 to
  1.1). The instrumented version has intervals such as −118,730 to 20.
- **Not reported.** The introduction promises cross-lagged analyses of
  depression, alliance and skills, and ecological momentary assessment of
  affect, to establish the order of change; neither appears. The
  moderators (personality disorder, invalidating childhood, reward
  sensitivity) are reported only as a passing mention that the
  PD-by-treatment interaction was not significant (§2, Bayesian model)
  and the Discussion's remark that the effect did not seem to depend on
  comorbid PD.
- **Adverse events (§3, Table 7).** 33 SAEs: 4 in usual care, 28 in RO
  DBT, 1 before randomisation. Of the 28, 12 were judged definitely not
  related, 8 unlikely, 5 possibly and 3 probably related; the 8 possibly
  or probably related are the serious adverse reactions, all in RO DBT
  (overdose with suicidal intent 1, overdose without 1, disclosure of
  thoughts of harming others 3, starting non-suicidal self-injury 2, a
  prevented suicide attempt 1). 23 of the 28 RO DBT events (82%) were
  reported by the treating therapists; GPs reported none in either arm.
  Test: "Fisher's exact test; one-tailed; p < 0.01". The DMEC's finding
  of no reason to suspect harm is from its last meeting that discussed
  SAEs, in January 2015.
- **Economics (§3).** £7050 more per RO DBT participant (95% CI £5822 to
  £8274) for 0.032 more QALYs (−0.028 to 0.093) at 12 months: £220,000
  per QALY; at 18 months usual care dominates.
- **Co-treatment.** Between 7 and 12 months 44% of usual-care
  participants had some talking therapy, against under 5% in RO DBT
  (Table 13 and §4), the authors' main explanation of usual care's late
  improvement.

## Method

As [NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md) describes for the journal paper: 15 : 8 adaptive
randomisation by the Swansea Trials Unit stratified by onset age, baseline
HRSD and cluster A or C personality disorder; masked assessors at 7, 12
and 18 months; three-level mixed models fitted in R. The mechanism
analysis fits Equations 1–2 (single mediator) and 4–7 (all mediators) by
generalized method of moments in Stata, first assuming no unobserved
confounding, then with the randomised features and therapist as
instruments, with bias-corrected bootstrap intervals.

## Concepts

- **Process variable**: a mediator defined only in the treated arm, fixed
  at zero in the control arm, so that the treatment's effect divides into
  a direct part and a part through the mediator.
- **Instrumental variable** (as used here): a randomised feature of how
  RO DBT was delivered that moves a mediator and affects the outcome only
  through it.
- **Serious adverse reaction**: a serious adverse event judged possibly,
  probably or definitely related to the intervention.

## Connections

The journal paper, [LIT-1223](../literature.d/LIT-1223.md), is the short version, published seven months
later; its efficacy numbers are this report's, its process-measure
intervals are not, and it drops the mechanism chapter. The treatment is
Lynch's, from his 2018 manual (Context Press, not registered); the
record's DOI'd statement of it is [LIT-1205](../literature.d/LIT-1205.md). The design follows Emsley,
Dunn and White's framework for mediation with randomised instruments in
trials of complex interventions (reference 130, not registered). The
standard it would have had to meet is [THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md)'s.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-292](../questions.d/QUESTION-292.md) and [THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md).** The triage expected the
  report's causal mechanism analyses to be the kind of evidence
  [THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md) asks for. They are the right kind in intent and fail it in
  fact. [THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md) counts change in the theorised process that is
  specific to it and comes before the outcome. Here the mediators are
  sessions attended, alliance and DBT skills, of which alliance and
  exposure are common factors and not RO DBT's theorised process; the
  processes RO DBT names, flexibility and emotional expression, are
  reported only as between-arm outcomes and are never entered as
  mediators; mediator and outcome are both at 7 months, so precedence is
  not established; and the planned analyses of temporal order were not
  reported. The instrumented analysis, the part designed to handle
  confounding, failed on weak instruments. So the trial meets neither leg
  of [THEORY-053](../theory.d/THEORY-053.md)'s standard. It is a worked example of the account's
  point that mechanism research has mostly failed on temporal precedence,
  and of Hofmann and Hayes's case of a model left untested. It does not
  disconfirm RO DBT's theory either: the processes did move, and whether
  the outcome moved through them was not estimated.
- **[QUESTION-045](../questions.d/QUESTION-045.md).** Its note of 2026-10-29 says the BJP paper gives
  nothing on mechanism. The report gives an attempt and no result, so the
  note's conclusion stands, with a refinement: the mediators were
  analysed here, and the analysis did not identify them. Overcontrol was
  not measured as such; the trial's proxy is a cluster A or C
  personality-disorder diagnosis (78% had at least one Axis-II disorder),
  used as a stratifier, and its interaction with treatment is reported
  only as not significant. That is the nearest the trial comes to
  [QUESTION-045](../questions.d/QUESTION-045.md)'s question, and it is no evidence either way.
- **[LIT-1223](../literature.d/LIT-1223.md) / [NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md), the P = 0.004.** The BJP paper's "one-tailed
  P = 0.004" for the eight serious adverse reactions is not in this
  report, which gives "p < 0.01". Neither reproduces. With eight
  reactions in eight different participants, all in the 162 RO DBT
  participants and none in the 88 controls, the one-tailed Fisher P is
  C(162,8)/C(250,8) = 0.029, as [NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md) found; with fewer than eight
  participants affected it is larger. Neither document says how many
  participants had the eight reactions. The test is still under 0.05 on
  either count, so the conclusion that the imbalance is significant
  survives; the stated strength does not.
- **[NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md)'s "throughout" error.** [NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md) calls the BJP abstract's
  "throughout" for the flexibility and coping gains an error against its
  Table 1. This report's Table 8 supports "throughout" (p = 0.010 and
  0.036 at 7 months) with the same differences and narrower intervals.
  So the conflict is between two analyses of the same data, not an
  abstract misreporting its own table; the BJP paper's analysis is the
  more conservative and is the one its abstract should have matched.
- **[CLAIM-349](../claims.d/CLAIM-349.md).** Its note of 2026-10-29 says the change that persisted
  was on inflexibility and emotional coping while the change on
  depression did not. The report agrees, and on its own analysis those
  changes were significant from 7 months. It adds nothing that tests the
  claim, which is about organizations.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The mechanism analysis does not identify anything**, by the authors'
  own account, and the no-confounding analysis assumes away the problem
  the design was built to solve.
- **Underpowered** at the primary end-point: 190 analysed against 245
  sought, and therapist clustering far above the assumed 0.025.
- **Unequal co-treatment and unequal harm reporting**, as in the journal
  paper; the DMEC's reassurance predates the end of recruitment (April
  2015) and all 18-month follow-up.
- **Developer and financial interest**, as [NOTE-1358](NOTE-1358.md) records: the
  developer leads the report and trained and supervised the therapists.
- **Errors and slips found.**
  - SAEs "were reported for 25 individual participants in the RO DBT
    group" (§3), but the same sentence's breakdown, 28 events of which
    seven fell to three participants, gives 28 − 7 + 3 = 24, the BJP
    paper's figure.
  - The 17 events judged definitely unrelated are split "TAU, n = 4; RO
    DBT, n = 13" in the text; Table 7 has 12 in RO DBT and the
    pre-randomisation event as the seventeenth.
  - The one-tailed Fisher test is given as p < 0.01; the counts give
    about 0.029 (above).
  - The scientific summary gives the 18-month AAQ-II interval as −8.74
    to 2.22; Table 8 has −8.74 to −2.22, which its p = 0.001 requires.
  - The text gives the HRSD cost-effectiveness ratio as £7048 per point;
    Table 16 gives £4122, which is £7048 / 1.71 points. £7048 is the cost
    difference.
  - The Discussion's "nearly significant at month 7" for EAC contradicts
    Table 8 (p = 0.036).
  - The process-measure intervals disagree with the BJP paper's for the
    same differences (above); neither explains.

## Open questions

- Which analysis of the AAQ-II and EAC contrasts is right, this report's
  or the BJP paper's? The Zenodo data and syntax (record 1442883) would
  settle it.
- Did the flexibility and coping change come before the change in
  depression? The cross-lagged analyses the report planned would answer
  it, and were not reported.
- How many participants had the eight serious adverse reactions, and
  what P did the authors compute?
