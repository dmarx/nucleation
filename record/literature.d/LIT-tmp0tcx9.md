# LIT-tmp0tcx9: The free-energy self: A predictive coding account of self-recognition

Apps, M. A. J. & Tsakiris, M. (2014). The free-energy self: a predictive coding account of self-recognition. *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 41, 85–97. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2013.01.029. Online 15 February 2013; PMC3848896.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp71fl5](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp71fl5.md).*

- **The self as the best explanation.** One's own body is the object that,
  when "touched, moved, threatened, or acted upon in any way", evokes
  events in the other senses. It is learned as a high-level multimodal
  prior, and "the notion that there is a 'self' is the most parsimonious
  and accurate explanation for sensory inputs".
- **One account of three illusions.** Synchronous visuo-tactile or
  auditory-vocal stimulation produces surprise in one sense that a
  multimodal node explains away by updating which hand, face or voice is
  "me". Earlier accounts (visual capture, updated reference frames) dealt
  with each illusion separately.
- **Ownership is a competition between models.** The illusion raises the
  probability that the rubber hand is "me" and lowers it for the real
  hand, which the authors read in reports that the real hand cools and its
  histamine rises during the illusion. The choice between the two models
  is compared to binocular rivalry.
- **Context is a prior.** A non-corporeal object, a hand of another skin
  tone or in an incongruent place, priming, and culture modulate
  ownership and self-recognition before any stimulation.
- **No self-specific circuit.** Self-stimuli engage unimodal areas that
  process all faces, bodies and voices, and multimodal areas (TPJ,
  anterior insula, IPS, IFG) that are not self-specific. The account
  needs no "self" network, only integration.
- **Untested by its own account.** "There is, as yet, little direct
  evidence that predictions and prediction errors related to self-stimuli
  are processed anywhere in the brain", and the theory is "hard to
  falsify using standard neuroscientific methods".
