# THEORY-tmpc5g6z: On Mauss's comparative account, gifts in archaic societies are voluntary in form but obligatory to give, to receive and to return. In Maori law the return is compelled by the hau of the thing given, which seeks its origin through every holder. In the Northwest-coast potlatch a gift accepted without a return, or without a larger one, costs the receiver face, rank and in the limit his freedom (debt slavery). Mauss holds that the same morality survives in modern societies, where an unreturned gift still makes its acceptor inferior and charity wounds. He argues that social insurance is right as a return owed to the worker for labour already given, beside a duty to rely on oneself.

## Source

Mauss (1925), [LIT-tmpzg9it](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzg9it.md); skimmed in [NOTE-tmpc9mii](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpc9mii.md), from the 2002
Classiques des sciences sociales edition of the 1968 PUF text. Page
numbers are that PDF's.

## What was actually shown

- **The form.** Gifts "en théorie volontaires, en réalité obligatoirement
  faits et rendus" (p. 7), and the three obligations named as such
  (p. 18).
- **The hau.** One informant's statement that a return passed on must go
  back to the first giver, on pain of harm "même la mort" (p. 15), read by
  Mauss as the hau that "veut revenir au lieu de sa naissance" (p. 16).
- **The potlatch.** Returns at 30 to 100 % a year; "On perd la « face » à
  jamais si on ne rend pas"; "La sanction de l'obligation de rendre est
  l'esclavage pour dette" (p. 55), from Boas, Hunt and Swanton at second
  hand.
- **The modern half** is argued, not shown: "Le don non rendu rend encore
  inférieur celui qui l'a accepté" (p. 90); the worker's claim on those
  he served (p. 92); and "Il faut qu'il soit forcé de compter sur soi
  plutôt que sur les autres" (p. 93).

What could have come out otherwise: the cited ethnographies, read at
first hand, might show returns that are customary but unsanctioned, or a
hau text that does not say what Mauss draws from it.

## What this does not say

- That a gift free of obligation exists. On Mauss the free gift is
  "fiction, formalisme et mensonge social" (p. 7); a gift economy that
  claims to be unconditional is not described here.
- That subordination comes from accepting. It comes from failing to
  return, or to return more; refusing is itself defeat (p. 53), and in the
  agonistic case the giver's ideal is the gift that cannot be returned
  (n. 2, p. 55).
- That reciprocity needs lost capability. The Andamanese exchange while
  self-sufficient in tools (p. 26), which bears against reading [CLAIM-tmpqa0qu](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpqa0qu.md)'s
  reciprocity as the product of capabilities lost.
- That generalized reciprocity, giving forward without return to the
  source, is Mauss's model: his circuits pass through third parties but
  close on the origin (pp. 16–17, 32).
- Anything about foreign aid, or any quantity beyond Boas's rates.
