# NOTE-tmpby7i2: Jennings — Attention as the Source of a Free Will

## Contribution

It gives an account of attention that keeps the traditional view, that a
subject directs attention, while answering the two standard objections:
the homunculus and the unspecified "resource". The subject is distributed:
a collection of interests, with no central controller (§6). It then argues
that this subject has a non-reducible causal power, through contextual
emergence, and that this power is the source of a free will (§§5–6). Its
one move not found in its sources is a condition on contextual emergence,
a boundary between the emergent phenomenon and its context (pp. 18–20; fn.
15 says this is what the paper adds to Jennings 2020).

## Key insight

"Some things only emerge in the right context" (p. 17). If the context is
neither a mere background condition nor just more parts, because a boundary
separates it from what emerges and that boundary is part of why it emerges,
then the emergent whole can constrain its parts without downward or circular
causation (fn. 14). For a brain the context is the skull and meninges, which
separate its electrical activity from the surround, and the brain stem,
which carries energy and signal in, like the container and the heating
element of Bénard cells (p. 29). The subject that emerges is the whole
collection of interests, and what it does is prioritize among them.

## Assumptions

- **Interests are the subject's parts, taken as a "ground truth" for living
  beings** (fn. 16, p. 22), restricted to "organism-level" interests that
  direct sensory and motor systems (p. 22).
- **Three kinds of interest** (pp. 23–26):
  - *fixed*, hereditary and structural (red, bright, moving, loud things);
  - *formed* by personal history, through memory and reward systems;
  - *fluid*, "neither inherited nor formed by experience", such as new
    interests and the case of equally weighted options in conflict.
- **A taxonomy of emergence by the parts' necessity and sufficiency**
  (p. 17): weak emergence, parts necessary and sufficient; strong, neither;
  supervenience, sufficient but not necessary; contextual, necessary but not
  sufficient. Attributed to Bishop and Atmanspacher 2006 (LIT-tmpo8e51) for
  the distinctness of contextual emergence; the necessity–sufficiency gloss
  is the paper's.
- **Biological systems are not deterministic** "according to the best
  science" (p. 2, citing Potter, Ellis and Mitchell 2026), and the paper
  "does not address global determinism" (p. 3).
- **Agency and free will are treated as "roughly synonymous"** (fn. 2).

## Key results

The paper is argument and review. It reports no new data.

- **Against the filter view** (§3, pp. 9–11). If attention can be a mere
  filter, nothing separates it from natural selection or Müller-cell
  filtering, so it should be tied to the subject (p. 11). Against the
  traditional view: the homunculus (pp. 11–12) and unspecified resources
  (pp. 12–13). Biased-competition models (Desimone and Duncan 1995) avoid
  both but use only weak emergence (pp. 14–15).
- **The boundary condition** (pp. 18–20). Without a functional boundary,
  contextual emergence "would collapse into either weak emergence or
  supervenience": the context's parts could be added to the base (p. 19).
  With one, "a macro-level difference between the parts of the emergent
  phenomenon and its context leads to the emergent phenomenon", and since
  "the thermal and material properties at stake here are macro-level ones,
  … contextual emergence is a better fit" (p. 20).
- **The mechanism** (pp. 25–26). Following Voigt 2022, when two options are
  equally valued, dlPFC and precuneus raise attention on one, which both
  breaks the stalemate and updates its value in memory, "so attention does
  not only help to break the stalemate but allows a fluid interest to become
  a formed interest over time" (p. 26).
- **Kane** (p. 26). Self-forming actions fit this role, but "what is missing
  from both Voigt and Kane is an explanation of how exactly the person
  manages to resolve the stalemate". If it is noise or indeterminacy, "the
  outcome arguably wouldn't be 'up to us'". The paper's answer: "attention
  by an emergent subject can resolve the conflict". Fn. 19 notes Kane's
  later mixed agent- and event-causal view.
- **The signature** (pp. 27–29). 1/f scaling and phase-amplitude coupling,
  "evidence that low frequency waves constrain high frequency waves",
  associated with attention (Szczepanski et al. 2014, LIT-tmpkq6x5). The
  analogy: a quiet flute must wait for a loud bassoon to fall silent to be
  heard (p. 28).
- **Where fluid interests enter** (pp. 30–32). Formed interests act through
  striatal oscillations and dopamine, combined with fixed ones through the
  thalamus; fluid interests need prefrontal feedback. Hence the criterion:
  "One way of determining this is through the presence or absence of
  feedback from dorsolateral prefrontal cortex" (p. 32).
- **Free will** (pp. 32–33). "The subject of attention has a power that goes
  beyond their component parts, and can exert control over those parts,
  showing how they can be such a source." The account "does not assume
  global indeterminism" but denies determinism "as a workable theory of what
  is happening at the local level for living beings" (p. 32). "Constraint, in
  a noisy environment, is what gives us power over ourselves" (p. 33).
- **Objections** (§7). Time scale (Klein): slow waves last seconds, subjects
  far longer. Reply: the subject is the collection, not a wave, and formed
  interests use memory (pp. 33–34). Weak emergence: reply by ephaptic
  coupling, which "demonstrates that brain activity cannot be described
  simply through local interactions" (p. 35; Anastassiou et al. 2011,
  LIT-tmp4kcgq). No-self views (Metzinger; Lindahl and Britton): loss of the
  sense of self is not loss of the self (pp. 36–38).

## Concepts

- **Interest** — "that which drives" a living being; for organisms like us,
  "tendencies to seek out and respond to stimuli in a certain way" (p. 22).
- **Subject** — "a collection of interests with the power to constrain those
  interests, and that power derives from the collection of interests being
  in a specific context" (p. 21).
- **Attention** — "prioritization by an emergent subject", contrasted with
  pre-attentive filtering and selective mechanisms (pp. 21–22, 32).
- **Fixed, formed and fluid interests** — as under Assumptions.
- **Contextual emergence** — parts necessary but not sufficient, with a
  functional boundary between phenomenon and context (pp. 17–19).
- **Machretic determination** — Gillett's term for the constraint an emergent
  whole places on which parts it can use (p. 20).

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmplyicq: the owner's "Frankfurt with a hat on", now checked
  against the text.** The architecture is two-level: a collection of
  interests decides which interest wins when they conflict (p. 31). The upper
  level is prioritization, a process, not a higher-order volition or an
  endorsement. The paper never mentions endorsement or identification. And
  it places itself "in between libertarianism and compatibilism" (p. 3), with
  a power it says "qualifies as a non-reductive causal power, supporting
  libertarian free will" (p. 3), and without indeterminism (p. 32). A14 reported both descriptions, and both are the
  paper's. It is not an inconsistency in A14's report.
- **CLAIM-027.** A mechanism-level candidate for its precedence relations:
  "When interests conflict, the collection will determine which of the
  interests wins. Without an emergent subject …, the winner would depend
  only on the existing strength of the interests in conflict and their local
  interactions" (p. 31). That is CLAIM-027's non-reducibility to regime
  contents, plus a claim of non-reducibility to micro-dynamics that CLAIM-027
  does not make. CLAIM-159's cells place it as "emergent from the whole"
  acting on the parts.
- **CLAIM-334.** The paper denies supervenience explicitly and says it needs
  neither downward nor circular causation (fn. 14). Its argument that the
  boundary resists redescription is that the boundary's properties "are
  macro-level ones" (p. 20). But a macro-level description of a boundary is
  still a description of micro constraints, the global supervenience the
  paper itself states and sets aside (p. 19). So the claim's premise is denied
  rather than refuted. The paper offers no intervention on the macro level
  that holds its base fixed.
- **CLAIM-tmplz9zf.** The stalemate-breaking mechanism is a causal update:
  attention on the chosen item updates its stored value (p. 25). That is the
  structure CLAIM-tmplz9zf says self-formation needs, a mixture that changes
  because of the choice. It is shown, in the paper, by citing Voigt's model,
  not by an intervention.
- **CLAIM-tmpex9rg.** The paper's reversal of the classic problem bears on
  the owner's: "while the question was once how we can be undetermined in a
  deterministic universe, the task now is to explain how we manage to find
  stability in a stochastic one" (p. 3), and "constraint, in a noisy
  environment, is what gives us power over ourselves" (p. 33). That is
  CLAIM-478's constraint read as freedom. It is also the opposite emphasis to
  Kane's chaos: for Jennings what makes the outcome the agent's is a stable
  constraint, not amplification.
- **QUESTION-364.** Fluid interests turning into formed ones are a worked
  case of a will revising itself. The paper answers whose revision it is by
  the subject's being the whole collection. It says nothing about a
  collection whose interests were installed, so QUESTION-071 and THEORY-040's
  manipulation case are untouched.
- **QUESTION-033, QUESTION-034, CLAIM-156, CLAIM-158.** "Who drives" has the
  answer "the collection, through its context"; QUESTION-034's second option,
  a change in which interests may act, is the mechanism.
- **THEORY-1296 and Metzinger.** The paper takes Metzinger's no-self view as
  an objection and answers it by separating the self from the sense of self
  (pp. 36–37). Metzinger's M-autonomy, the record's nearest neighbour, is not
  cited.
- **LIT-388 (ephaptic coupling).** The second appearance of ephaptic coupling
  as a basis of subjecthood. The paper offers it against weak emergence, but
  a field effect is another physical channel among neurons. It is non-local
  in wiring, not non-reducible.
- **The exchange's review (A5).** Its page citations check: fn. 2 on p. 2,
  the bassoon on p. 28, ephaptic coupling on p. 35, no-self on pp. 36–38. Its
  objection to the bassoon analogy is half right. The analogy describes a
  fast signal timed to a slow one's phase, which is phase-dependent gating;
  the flute's amplitude does not change with the bassoon's phase, so it is
  not phase-amplitude coupling as the paper defines it a sentence later.
  A5's "Park and colleagues (2017)" is not in the paper.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.** The paper cites the
  transformer's "attention mechanism" (p. 11) only as a bottom-up model.

## Limitations

- **The emergence argument rests on one analogy.** Bénard cells are used to
  show that a boundary does work the parts cannot. But the paper's own
  alternative description of them, global supervenience (p. 19), is not
  shown to fail; it is called a worse "explanation" (p. 19).
- **The neural evidence does not discriminate.** Phase-amplitude coupling and
  ephaptic coupling are predicted by models in which all causal work is
  micro-level. The paper says the account "would be further supported by
  evidence linking ephaptic coupling to attention" (p. 35); none is cited.
- **The criterion for attention is a proposal.** dlPFC feedback is inferred
  from the claim that maintained goals need prefrontal feedback.
- **The free-will step is asserted.** That a non-reducible power over one's
  parts makes one an ultimate source (p. 32) is not argued against the
  manipulation or luck objections, and the paper does not address them.
- **Fluid interests are defined negatively**, "neither inherited nor formed by
  experience" (p. 25), which leaves open where they come from.
- **Manuscript.** The published version may differ.

## Open questions

- Does intervening on dlPFC feedback abolish both the resolution of a
  stalemate and the later change in preference, as the account predicts and
  biased competition need not?
- Can a weakly emergent model with the same boundary conditions, encoded as
  micro constraints, fail to reproduce the macro constraint? That is what
  would separate contextual emergence from global supervenience
  (THEORY-tmp6xjgq).
- Would a subject whose interests were installed be free on this account?
