summary: >-
  Hirst, Cyr and Merck (2020). An essay joining Alexander's trauma process to the psychology of flashbulb memory. People who learned of an event at a distance keep vivid, confident memories of where they were, and these let them claim to have been "there" and to testify, like eyewitnesses, to what it was like. Such memories are community-specific and can be community-defining, so where nearly everyone holds one, carrier group and audience coincide and the trauma process can start among equals. The memories are inaccurate in detail but held with high confidence, which is what makes them count. They pass to children: in a study of children young at the time of 9/11, 89% could report a parent's flashbulb memory, and those who identified more strongly as American knew it better.
