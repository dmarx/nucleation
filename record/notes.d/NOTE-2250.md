# NOTE-2250: Song, Ahn & Yun — Does SGD really happen in tiny subspaces?

## Contribution

1. A direct test of whether training can be confined to the dominant
   Hessian subspace that gradients align with (Gur-Ari et al. 2018): it
   cannot. Dom-SGD, θ ← θ − ηP_k g, stalls; Bulk-SGD, θ ← θ − ηP_k^⊥ g,
   matches SGD.
2. An account of the alignment as spurious: noise or oscillation across a
   steep, ill-conditioned valley, not progress.
3. The same pattern for full-batch GD at the Edge of Stability, SAM,
   momentum and Adam, with an effective learning rate along the bulk that
   rises with momentum and adaptivity.

## Key insight

A gradient that points mostly up the walls of a valley says where the
landscape is steep, not where the loss goes down. The stiff directions
are already near their floor; the loss falls along the flat floor, in the
complement of the top subspace.

## Assumptions

- The Hessian is of the training loss in parameter space; "dominant" is
  the top-k eigenspace with k the number of classes (Definition 1); the
  bulk is its whole orthogonal complement, "referred to as the
  bulk subspace" (§2), every eigenvalue below the top k,
  negative ones included. It is not a set of smallest-eigenvalue
  directions.
- "Learning" is training-loss descent. Generalization is one preliminary
  appendix check; no representation or decodability is measured.
- Small subsampled data (first 5,000 of MNIST and CIFAR10, first 1,000 of
  SST2), small MLP, CNN and two-layer Transformer (VGG11 and ResNet8 in the
  appendix), categorical MSE mainly, cross-entropy in the appendix, constant
  learning rates, batch 50. The subspace is recomputed at every step.
- The switch from SGD to Dom- or Bulk-SGD comes when the moving average of
  χ_k exceeds 0.95, at "around 53%" training accuracy for the CNN and 69%
  for the Transformer (§3.2): not the end of training.

## Key results

- **Dom-SGD stalls** (§3.2): Dom-SGD "fails to further decrease the
  training loss", and "even slowly increases the loss in long run".
- **Bulk-SGD matches SGD** (§3.3): "Bulk-SGD is as effective as SGD in
  decreasing the training loss".
- **Noise causes the alignment** (§4.1): switching SGD to GD, the
  "alignment disappears as soon as we switch to GD", while GD and SGD
  trajectories track each other (appendix). Smaller batches and injected
  Gaussian noise raise χ_k.
- **A valley** (§4.3), offered as "one plausible explanation": the
  landscape is "steep along the dominant subspace and flat along the bulk
  subspace", and "there is a direction within the bulk subspace along
  which the bottom of the valley descends". A two-dimensional toy,
  L = ½(1000x² + y²) split into two noisy halves, reproduces χ₁ ≈ 0.999.
- **Edge of Stability and SAM** (§5): Dom-GD and Dom-SAM fail, Bulk-GD and
  Bulk-SAM match; at the Edge of Stability the alignment "arises from a
  self-stabilization mechanism" (Damian et al.).
- **Momentum and Adam** (§6, Table 1, SST2-1k): mean Bulk-LR 0.0100 (GD),
  0.0828 (GD with momentum), 0.4672 (Adam without momentum), 2.6639 (Adam);
  "Bulk-LR positively correlates with the training speed".
- **Sharpness** (appendix): "Dom-SGD decreases the sharpness, unlike SGD
  and Bulk-SGD", and Bulk-SGD sharpens faster than SGD.
- **Generalization**, preliminary (appendix, full MNIST): "Bulk-SGD
  generalizes as well as SGD, while Dom-SGD fails to generalize well".
- **Not low-dimensional training's subspace** (appendix): of Li et al.'s
  trajectory-PCA subspaces, "the low-dimensional subspaces they chose are
  not the dominant subspace".

## Concepts

- **Dominant and bulk subspace** (Definition 1); **χ_k(v) = ‖P_k v‖/‖v‖**.
- **Spurious alignment**: gradient alignment without progress.
- **GF and EoS regimes**; **self-stabilization**; **ill-conditioned
  valley**.
- **Dom-LR and Bulk-LR**: the effective step along each subspace.

## Limitations

- Small models and subsets, MSE mainly, constant learning rates, k fixed
  at the number of classes with no sweep in the main text.
- The valley is "one plausible explanation", supported by the toy and by
  distances moved; a theory "beyond the toy model" is left open (§7).
- A gap in the argument: the step from "Dom-SGD stalls" to "the dominant
  component is noise" assumes the stiff directions sit at their valley
  floor at the switch; the distance measurements support it for the runs
  shown, not in general.
- Generalization only preliminary; no representation measured.

## Note of 2026-11-22: a correction, at the owner's word

An earlier note here says Song, Ahn and Yun "measure only training-loss descent", and the relay behind it treated that as less than learning. The owner corrected both: "training loss falling is evidence that learning is happening. the capabilities are being measurably developed. … that's a measure of the learning that is happening, which is concretely the changes that are happening within the organization of the model's representational space". The relay also read the paper's "bulk subspace" in the owner's sense of "bulk". It is the paper's term for the many low-curvature directions, the bulk by count, where the owner's "bulk" is the dominant directions, the bulk by weight ([TERM-143](../terms.d/TERM-143.md)).

Read so, Song et al. bear on the owner's question more than the earlier note said. Projecting each update onto the dominant directions, the bulk by weight, stops training. Projecting onto the rest, the low-curvature directions that carry little of the operator's activity, trains as fast as SGD. So capability develops through the non-dominant directions. What the paper still does not do is follow one direction through training or say which distinctions it comes to carry. The note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session).
