# QUESTION-413: Do the graduation-programme results of Banerjee and colleagues' six-country trials and of Banerjee, Duflo and Sharma's West Bengal follow-up hold elsewhere: does a further randomized evaluation of a time-limited asset-and-support programme for ultra-poor households, at another site, find effects on consumption and income still significant a year or more after all support ended, with no fall in working time, and does a long-term follow-up there find effects significant at least seven years after the transfer, not shrinking after the third year, carried mainly by wage employment or migration rather than by the transferred activity?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1486](../theory.d/THEORY-1486.md) reports gains that held a year after exit across six trials,
with large differences between sites. [THEORY-1493](../theory.d/THEORY-1493.md) reports effects that
grew for seven years at the India site, the most favourable of the six.
Neither is a test at a site outside those studies, and the authors keep
open that the persistence is a poverty flat rather than an escaped trap.

## What would answer it

A randomized evaluation at another site whose effects remain significant a
year or more after support ends, with no fall in working time, would
answer the first part; a long-run follow-up there whose effects hold seven
years on, carried mainly by wage employment or migration, would answer the
second. Fade-out after exit, or controls catching up, would count against.
