# NOTE-tmpqf0zu: Killen — The origins of morality: Social equality, fairness, and justice

## Contribution

A developmental psychologist's critique of Tomasello's collaboration-first
account of morality.

## Key results

1. **Thesis.** "interdependence coexists with independent autonomous
   thinking, which is necessary for challenging group norms, authority,
   and institutional mandates" (p. 767).
2. **Tomasello's evidence.** "By ages 3–4 years, children will share the
   fruits of collaborative labor with a same-age playmate rather than take
   all the rewards for themselves" (p. 776).
3. **Not the same.** "cooperation and morality are not one and the same"
   (p. 777).
4. **Piaget revised.** "current research has revealed how the construction
   of moral concepts emerges prior to Piaget’s (1932) claims" (p. 783).
5. **Piaget's mechanism kept.** "Young children’s social conflicts reflect
   a form of disequilibrium, distinct from cooperation, which motivates
   change due to the desire to resolve conflicts (Piaget, 1932; Turiel,
   1983)" (p. 786); "Along with cooperation, conflict is essential for the
   change to occur" (p. 796).
6. **Equity late.** "It is not until 7–8 years of age that children
   consistently prioritize equity over equality in behavior, judgment, and
   reasoning (Rizzo & Killen, 2016)" (p. 794).

## Assumptions

A deontological view of morality; constructivism; social domain theory.

## Concepts

Interdependence versus autonomy; second-personal and objective morality;
disequilibrium; the ingroup deviant.

## Limitations

commitment; its charge that Tomasello equates objective morality with
conformity is contestable.
