# THEORY-1483: In Azam, Devarajan and O'Connell's model, where the cost of taxing falls with depreciating revenue-collecting experience and grant aid fills the fiscal gap, aid that ignores this learning drives a country whose initial institutions are weaker than a threshold to aid financing the whole budget and the loss of its tax capacity, while one with stronger institutions converges to a low-aid state; a planner who internalizes the learning still has a stable high-aid, low-capacity equilibrium beside the efficient saddle path, which is reached only by an up-front cut in aid

## Source

Azam, Devarajan & O'Connell (1999), [LIT-2156](../literature.d/LIT-2156.md); read in [NOTE-1682](../notes.d/NOTE-1682.md).
Page numbers are the printed ones.

## What was actually shown

A derivation, not a test.

- **The model** (pp. 4–6): surplus (Bg − g²/2) − (θr + r²/2), budget
  g = r + a, θ = 1/R with Ṙ = r − αR, and a planner maximizing discounted
  βS − a. Spending is constant on any optimal path, g = B − β⁻¹.
- **Two steady states** (p. 7, Appendix): where θ = α/r meets the ṙ = 0
  locus, roots of r² − β⁻¹r + αγ = 0, γ = δ/(α + δ), real when
  4αβ² < γ⁻¹. The low-aid point P "is a saddle point while the high-aid,
  low-tax-efficiency equilibrium M is locally stable"; any path to either
  meets the necessary conditions, and P is the optimum for low enough
  discounting. Paths to M are fully rational (p. 12).
- **The myopic threshold** (pp. 9–10): with aid at the static gap,
  a = g* − β⁻¹ + θ, initial θ above θ₁ leads to "aid finances all of public
  spending", after which "taxation capacity now gradually disappears as
  cumulative tax experience is driven to zero"; below θ₁ the economy
  converges to a low-aid state M₁.
- **Correction** (p. 11): "the optimal strategy is to reduce aid "up
  front,"" onto the saddle path, at a short-run loss to both.

## What this does not say

- That any country is in either equilibrium. The Korea, Botswana,
  Tanzania, Mali, India and Pakistan readings are illustrations.
- That aid harms capacity by incentive or accountability: the only channel
  is learning by doing in tax collection, and low revenue is a planner's
  choice.
- That the recipient wants graduation. In the model it is better off at
  the high-aid state, so moving to P needs compensation (p. 13).
- That recovery after full substitution takes longer the longer it lasted:
  with experience growing one for one with revenue and no cap of revenue
  by capacity, the model does not give that.
- That "gap-filling" aid in the benign sense, aid used only after own
  capacity, is harmless: the paper's gap-filling aid is the harmful rule.
