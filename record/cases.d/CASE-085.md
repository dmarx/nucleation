# CASE-085: The medication in the ambulance: a drug the EMT knew was stocked could not be found during a resuscitation, the paramedic changed the request, and afterwards said "If I can't find it, it isn't there"

## The case

The owner, at U16: "This reminds me of an experience I had working as an
EMT-B on an ALS unit, supporting an EMT-P. We were working a code and the
medic urgently requested a particular medication. I had checked over the
unit so I knew the medication was present in the med box, but I couldn't
remember where. after failing to find it for several minutes, the medic
changed their request. after the call, i was chastized to the effect of:
'If I can't find it, it isn't there.'"

## What it can show

That presence, knowing presence and timely deployment come apart (A17 §1:
present, yes; known present, yes; location known, not quickly enough;
deployed in time, no). A17's counterfactuals vary one thing at a time: a
labelled compartment (the environment), a memorized location (the
individual), a partner who knows (the team), more time (the deadline), a
standard layout (training), an easy-to-find drug no longer indicated
(relevance). A17 §4 added that search spends what it needs: "Knowing when
to stop searching is part of knowing how to use a resource."

## What it cannot show

A17 §9: "why retrieval failed, whether the requested intervention would
have affected the outcome, or whether the paramedic changed the request
because of the delay rather than other clinical considerations." Nor
which stage failed, cue, recall or search (A19 §3), and not that the
failure was the EMT's rather than the system's (A17 §8). The medication
and the clinical details are not recorded and should not be supplied.

## Standing

A16 and A17 proposed `documented`, "as a firsthand report". The record's
`documented` asks for sources a reader can check. The owner's report is a
source for what the owner recalls, and the event's relevant facts are
taken on it here, which is `stipulated`. The owner may judge otherwise.
