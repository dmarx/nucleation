# THEORY-tmp30jgj: Ratliff, Dong, Sekar and Fiez hold that economics, control theory and online learning each solve only part of incentive design, so that designing incentives for returning agents whose behaviour changes with the principal's own past incentives, under hidden types or hidden actions, remains open

## Source

Ratliff, Dong, Sekar and Fiez (2019), LIT-tmpsj5c6; read in NOTE-tmp5upz4
(page numbers the manuscript's).

## What was actually shown

A review, not a proof. Against one principal–agent template (§2) it finds
economics static and "very heavily model-based" (p. 4); control's adaptive
results dependent on "stable and persistently exciting dynamics—the latter
of which is very difficult to verify" (p. 18); and online learning's regret
bounds dependent on i.i.d., stationary agents, "both of which are far
removed from reality" (p. 5). From these it concludes that "it is still an
open problem to solve incentive design with repeatedly returning agents
whose decision-making processes evolve with time" (p. 24). The judgement
could be shown wrong by a framework, in any of the three literatures or
across them, that handles such agents under hidden types or actions.

## What this does not say

- That the problem cannot be solved, or that a joint framework exists. The
  paper calls for one and supplies none.
- That no special case is solved. The dynamic-contracting literature on
  the ratchet effect, which the paper does not cite, treats returning
  agents who hide their type from a principal who cannot commit (for
  example LIT-tmpbit9m).
- Anything about organizational budgets, slack or readiness; its budget is
  the principal's incentive budget.
