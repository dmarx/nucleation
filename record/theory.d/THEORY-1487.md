# THEORY-1487: Evans holds that state-society synergy combines complementarity, in which government dependably supplies inputs citizens cannot produce and citizens supply local knowledge and labour, with embeddedness, in which officials keep everyday ties with the communities they serve, and that small-scale synergy is constructable even in class-divided societies under disorganized, authoritarian governance, the binding limit being scaling up rather than prior stocks of social capital

## Source

Evans (1996), [LIT-2143](../literature.d/LIT-2143.md); read in [NOTE-1665](../notes.d/NOTE-1665.md), from the Digital
Library of the Commons copy (printed pp. 178–208).

## What was actually shown

- **Complementarity** (p. 187): per Evans, from Ostrom's production
  functions, joint production wins when the inputs are "sufficiently
  different (but equally necessary)"; "Active citizens are hamstrung
  unless their governments dependably supply them with inputs that they
  cannot produce on their own" (p. 204).
- **Embeddedness** (pp. 187–188): in the Recife sewers, per Evans,
  officials' start-up effort, and after construction "monitoring and
  maintenance required continuous face-to-face interaction". The Nepal and
  Taiwan irrigation contrast (pp. 183, 188) is the same point: substituting
  operation undercut farmers' institutions, embedded involvement did not.
- **Constructability** (pp. 191, 203–205): "prior endowments of social
  capital are not the key constraining factor"; "Small-scale successes are
  constructable even within broader contexts that are adverse. Even in
  class-divided societies suffering under disorganized, authoritarian
  governance, innovative institutional tactics can foster synergy on a
  limited scale."

All of it is argument from chosen cases; nothing was tested that could
have come out the other way.

## What this does not say

- That synergy is common, or that it works in most settings: the cases are
  positive, and the generalisations "have to be considered exploratory"
  (p. 179).
- That synergy persists when officials' contact or the public input is
  withdrawn. Evans does not measure persistence after support; Watson's
  (1995) field study, [LIT-2190](../literature.d/LIT-2190.md), is the record's evidence on that.
- That the official side is transient support. On the contrary, the
  public input is to be supplied "dependably"; this is why it qualifies
  [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) and supports [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md), not why either should change
  status.
- Anything about Ostrom's own definitions or formal conditions beyond what
  Evans reports.
