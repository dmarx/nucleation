# ARG-tmpempyl: The paperclip maximizer, by analogy, carries to the firm and the attention economy for a narrow criterion steering behaviour, not for unbounded maximization

## The inference

Source: the paperclip maximizer, an optimizer with a narrow objective and
the means to pursue it across every domain it can reach. Target: a firm
organized around financial return, and a platform optimizing engagement.
A6 §1 maps them: objective function to financial return, reward signal to
revenue and valuation, resource acquisition to capital accumulation,
instrumental power-seeking to "Market concentration and influence over
regulatory conditions", environment modification to "Advertising,
lobbying, infrastructure development". Conclusion: the owner's thesis,
that the paperclip problem is already present, in the objectives
permitted to steer behaviour.

## Critical questions

1. **Are the cases alike in the respect that matters?** Yes, if the
   respect is the owner's: a criterion narrower than the domain it acts on.
   Engagement is optimized over choices that shape "human desires and
   social relations" (A6 §3), and A6 §2's mechanism, that the optimizer
   reshapes the population whose behaviour it measures, is held for
   monitoring generally (CLAIM-1268). That an optimizer narrowly scored
   values weakening the constraints on it is CLAIM-1287, derived there in
   its own model.
2. **Do they differ in a respect that defeats the conclusion?** Two
   differences, neither defeating it:
   - *Boundedness.* A firm's pursuit meets constraints, law, competition,
     its members' interests, that the thought experiment's maximizer does
     not. A6: "Corporations have heterogeneous internal interests, face
     competing objectives and operate under legal and institutional
     constraints." This defeats an analogy of outcome (the firm converts
     everything to profit) and not one of mechanism (the criterion reaches
     beyond its adequate domain).
   - *Fixity.* The maximizer's objective is fixed; a firm's can be
     revised by its governance. That is the respect in which the owner's
     framing is a governance claim: permission is what could be revised.
3. **Is there a counter-analogy?** A firm whose objective is bounded by
   constraints it cannot weaken is the analogy's limit case, and the
   owner's thesis names it as the remedy rather than the counterexample.
4. **Does the "since the 80s" date transfer?** No: the analogy shows a
   mechanism, not when it took hold. A6's history of shareholder value is
   A's, unverified (CLAIM-tmpe432a).

Verdict: Proposed as support for the mechanism the owner names; no
support for the maximizer's unboundedness, which the thesis does not
claim.
