# THEORY-2249: Widder and Schilling show that the Dyson–Duhamel identity behind the generalized Langevin equation is proved only when PL is bounded, as for Mori's projection, and is an unsolved equation for Zwanzig's; that Mori's equation follows from Volterra equations alone and predicts nothing beyond the autocorrelation fitted to it; and that its memory term is a coupling between the kept and omitted subspaces, which vanishes when the omitted subspace is invariant

## Source

Widder and Schilling (2026), [LIT-2250](../literature.d/LIT-2250.md), read in [NOTE-2340](../notes.d/NOTE-2340.md).

## What was actually shown

Proofs in semigroup theory: the bounded perturbation theorem for bounded
PL; unboundedness of PZL for the harmonic oscillator; existence and
uniqueness of Mori's kernel and force from Volterra equations; the
vanishing of the memory term for the spectral subspaces Fω, which are
invariant.

## What this does not say

That Zwanzig-type equations are wrong, only that their derivation is not
rigorous. That memory never reflects past dependence: the vanishing is
shown for one choice of slow variables, and with other projections the
coupling term appears and may carry it.
