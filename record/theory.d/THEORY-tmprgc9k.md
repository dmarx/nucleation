# THEORY-tmprgc9k: Organization is a secondary accomplishment of change: change in organizations is continuous, as actors reweave their webs of beliefs and habits to accommodate new experience, and organization is both the attempt to stabilize that flux and the pattern that emerges from it

## Source

Tsoukas & Chia (2002), LIT-tmpls0y0, pp. 567–580; read in NOTE-tmpyz5d5.

## What was actually shown

An argument and illustrations. Stage models describe change as a
succession of states and so miss it (pp. 570–571). Institutionalized
categories cannot hold their meaning fixed, because they meet
nonprototypical cases and because actors reflect on their own
descriptions (pp. 573–575). So "organization aims at stemming change
but, in the process of doing so, it is generated by it" (p. 577).
Published ethnographies are reread to show routines and change
programmes being modified in performance (pp. 575–579). Nothing was
done that could have come out the other way.

## What this does not say

- **That organizations constantly change.** Inertia is granted, and it is
  redescribed as local change that is not institutionalized (p. 580).
- **That agency is without agents.** The change is carried by "actors
  ... reweaving their webs of beliefs" and "the ongoing agency of
  organizational members" (pp. 575, 580). Citing this paper for an
  agent-free process ontology, as LIT-tmp3mudg's "parallels" invites,
  takes from it more than it says.
- **How individual reweaving becomes organizational.** That is left open
  (p. 580).
