# THEORY-tmpegs18: The extended-mind thesis worth disputing is that external entities partly constitute a subject's mental states in virtue of the subject's sensorimotor interaction with them; so stated it can reach standing belief but not consciousness, if consciousness needs information directly available for global control, since perception makes availability indirect

## Source

Chalmers (2019), "Extended Cognition and Extended Consciousness", [LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md),
read in full in [NOTE-356](../notes.d/NOTE-356.md) from the author's copy. Chalmers (2008), the
foreword to Clark's *Supersizing the Mind*, [LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md), read in [NOTE-369](../notes.d/NOTE-369.md). Both
revise Clark and Chalmers (1998), [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md), read in [NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md).

## What was actually shown

**The 1998 thesis is too weak ([LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §1).** [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) "never quite
state[s] a single official thesis". Reconstructed, it is constitution by
external entities "when those entities play the right role in driving
cognitive processes". Following Farkas (2012), that is "near-obviously
true in extended circuit cases", such as Clark's Diva, whose damaged
division circuit is replaced by an external silicon one. The chapter says
the main opponents accept such cases. It does not quote them saying so
([NOTE-356](../notes.d/NOTE-356.md), C2).

**The proposed thesis ([LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §1).** Constitution "in virtue of the
subject's sensorimotor interaction with these entities". It counts Otto
and Tetris in, and Diva, brain-computer interfaces and intravenous
stimulation out, with telepathy borderline. Chalmers calls it
"extensionally correct" for the paradigm cases and offers it as "the
retrospectively official statement". It has no necessary-and-sufficient
test ([NOTE-356](../notes.d/NOTE-356.md), C3).

**The strongest objection, kept ([LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md); [LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §2).** If the mental
lies between perception and action, Otto's notebook is outside it, since
Otto must read and write. Both texts withdraw the 1998 reply that Otto's
access "is not perceptual at all": "there is no denying that Otto sees the
notebook and reads from it" ([LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md)). The reply Chalmers favours is
revisionism. If an extended state functions in explanation as belief does,
it is belief. Whether it counts as extended can flip with the question
asked, like a Necker cube ([LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md), C4). The objection is "not a
knockdown", and neither is the reply.

**Consciousness does not extend this way ([LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md); [LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §3).** The
foreword's reason is that no Otto-style twin case, internal duplicates who
differ in experience, can be built ([LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md), C6). It floated a bandwidth
explanation (C7). The chapter withdraws that, granting Vold (2015) that
vision is a very high-bandwidth channel. In its place it puts directness:
consciousness needs information directly available for global control,
and direct access is "a relatively short and unmediated causal pathway"
that "requires access that is not mediated by other mental states".
Standing belief needs only two-step availability, which Otto's perception
supplies. Hence "even if there is extended cognition, there is no extended
consciousness."

**What could have come out otherwise.** The reconstruction could have
found the 1998 thesis already controversial in the circuit cases, or a
twin case for consciousness could have worked. The first is settled only
by an unquoted claim about the opponents; the second by an unshown search.

## What this does not say

- **That consciousness cannot extend.** An extended circuit could carry
  consciousness, since directness is no obstacle where access is not
  perceptual ([LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md), C10), and its physical basis could extend "in some
  possible worlds" ([LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md)). The denial concerns notebooks, phones and
  screens.
- **That directness is defined.** The author has "no necessary and
  sufficient conditions for directness". Unconscious perception might
  escape the mediation argument, and a verdict there "would require more
  detailed criteria for directness than I have given here".
- **That the consciousness criterion is argued here.** It is imported from
  *The Conscious Mind* and the neural-correlate paper ([NOTE-356](../notes.d/NOTE-356.md),
  Assumptions). It is a correlate, offered to explain an independently held
  verdict ("there seem to be no plausible cases").
- **That the critics are answered.** [LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §2 gives five objections one
  sentence each. [NOTE-356](../notes.d/NOTE-356.md) rates those replies weak (C5). The
  coupling-constitution reply assumes that parity carries the argument,
  and [NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md) found parity stated in [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md), not argued.
- **Anything about the hard problem.** "Partly constituted" is read as
  partial grounding, or as nomological dependence for a dualist. The
  foreword says the thesis needs only the parity principle's "very weak
  functionalism" and is compatible with dualism.

## Connections

- **Rivals.** Adams and Aizawa's contingent intracranialism
  ([THEORY-tmpnnu03](THEORY-tmpnnu03.md)) and Rupert's embedded cognition ([THEORY-tmpgrmvp](THEORY-tmpgrmvp.md)) both
  deny that Otto's notebook is part of his cognition. Their shared logical
  point, that coupling does not by itself make parthood, is stated
  separately ([THEORY-tmpma6ht](THEORY-tmpma6ht.md)); this account accepts it and rests on parity
  instead.
- **Group consciousness.** [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) names the directness criterion as the
  most promising candidate the record holds for an architectural line
  between a society of mind and a nation. That application is the record's,
  not Chalmers's ([NOTE-356](../notes.d/NOTE-356.md), Bearing on the record).
- **AI and tools.** Direct availability for global control is kin to the
  global-workspace indicators in [LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md) and so to [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md)'s dispute. On
  this account a user's phone or assistant, reached by perception, can at
  most extend belief.
- **Implants.** A brain-computer interface falls with the extended
  circuits, which bears on Register's account of BCIs as body parts
  ([THEORY-tmpheyet](THEORY-tmpheyet.md), [LIT-138](../literature.d/LIT-138.md)).
- Clark's book ([LIT-412](../literature.d/LIT-412.md)) is filed unread.
