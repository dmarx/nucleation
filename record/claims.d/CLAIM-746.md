# CLAIM-746: Four judgments about an authority are distinct: the warrant of the obligations it is to serve, its jurisdiction over the matter, its arrangement's capacity to realize those obligations together, and its capacity to reach compliant arrangements and correct failures by permitted means; neither of the first two follows from any of the others, and of the last two only the dynamic one implies the static one, for what it reaches

## The claim

A74 §9 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), asking
whether the record's formal work helps say where legitimacy originates:

| Judgment | What establishes it? |
|---|---|
| **Normative warrant** | Reasons why a claim, obligation, or restriction should be recognized |
| **Jurisdictional legitimacy** | Reasons why a particular actor or institution may govern the matter |
| **Organizational adequacy** | Whether the governing arrangement can jointly realize its justified obligations |
| **Dynamic answerability** | Whether it can reach compliant arrangements and correct failures under appropriate constraints |

"These judgments are related, but none should be collapsed into another."

The separations, each with a case:

1. **Warrant without jurisdiction.** A71: "A restriction can be
   substantively appropriate but imposed by someone who lacks authority to
   impose it." That is [CLAIM-737](CLAIM-737.md)'s separation.
2. **Jurisdiction without adequacy.** A71: "A democratically authorized
   institution can possess legitimate authority while performing its
   obligations inadequately."
3. **Adequacy without warrant or jurisdiction.** [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md): viability does
   not establish what ought to persist. An arrangement can realize its
   obligations jointly when the obligations are unwarranted, or when the
   actor has seized the matter.
4. **Static adequacy without dynamic.** [CLAIM-560](CLAIM-560.md): a configuration can be
   feasible at a budget at which it is unreachable from the present one;
   and an arrangement able to hold a compliant configuration may lack the
   correctability ([TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)) to restore it after a failure.

One pair does not separate as A's table suggests. If an arrangement can
reach a configuration meeting all its justified obligations, that
configuration exists, so the obligations are jointly realizable: the
dynamic judgment implies the static one for the obligations reached.
"Dynamic answerability" is therefore the stronger of the two, not a fourth
independent axis. The reverse implication fails, by 4.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-737](CLAIM-737.md)** holds separation 1 for restrictions. This claim
  extends it to any obligation an authority is to serve.
- **[CLAIM-560](CLAIM-560.md)** (Active, demonstrated) holds separation 4 for budgets.
  This claim applies it to the evaluation of an authority, with
  "permitted means" restricting the paths as A74 §3's Γ_N does.
- **[QUESTION-485](../questions.d/QUESTION-485.md)**, the owner's U31 ("from whence does a
  relationship, capability, or restriction derive legitimacy and/or
  adequacy?"), asks for the grounds. This claim says only that the four
  grounds are different questions; it answers none of them.
- **[QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)** stays open. A74 §9: "I don't think compatibility and
  reachability can derive the moral authority of a norm from nothing."

"Answerability" in A74's label means reachability plus correctability,
not the directed normative relation of [TERM-126](../terms.d/TERM-126.md) and
[CLAIM-739](CLAIM-739.md). The record should not read the two as one.

## Admission

What this adds beyond its components is the ordering: of the six pairs,
five separate and one is an implication. Each separation is supported by
a case cited above, and the implication is immediate from the definitions.
The cases are sketches, not constructions, so the warrant is
interpretive.

## What it does not say

- Not that adequacy is irrelevant to legitimacy. A74 §9's next thesis,
  that legitimate authority "may carry an obligation to maintain the
  capabilities reasonably necessary to fulfill the duties that justify
  it", links them; that is a dated note on [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md), not part of this
  claim.
- Not which of the four is prior, or how they are weighed in an overall
  verdict.
