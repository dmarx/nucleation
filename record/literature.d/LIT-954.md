# LIT-954: The semantic landscape paradigm for neural networks

Shreyas Gokhale (2023), *arXiv:2307.09550*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-807](../notes.d/NOTE-807.md).*

- **A vocabulary, not a result.** Training is a trajectory over a graph of
  "heuristic models", the algorithms a network's weights implement
  (conjecture C1), with fallibility F = Σ_x D_KL(P_GT ‖ P_H) over all
  inputs as the landscape. Nothing is measured or proved.
- **Thresholds as connectivity.** Below a critical data size the graph
  disconnects the memorising model from the generalising one, so grokking
  cannot happen however long training runs; with an assumed rate
  λ_g ∝ (D − D_c), grokking time is 1/(D − D_c). Emergence with scale is
  a node appearing as N grows. Both are called percolation; no
  percolation model is specified.
- **Scaling laws as waiting times.** Bouchaud's trap model with
  exponentially distributed barriers, plus F ∝ 1/N_b, gives
  F(S) = (S₀/S)^μ, F(D) = (D₀/D)^μ and F(N) = (N₀/N)^(μ/(d_f·α)), on a
  graph assumed connected. Kaplan et al.'s data exponent gives μ ≈ 0.1.
- **Two kinds of event.** Thresholds and power laws come from different
  parts of the picture: connectivity for the first, barrier statistics
  for the second.

## Standing in the record

Filed 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for [QUESTION-039](../questions.d/QUESTION-039.md), as a
candidate for the "one kind of event" answer. Read in full on 2026-10-14
in [NOTE-807](../notes.d/NOTE-807.md), which finds it is not that candidate: it puts

thresholds and power laws as different events. It has no experiment and
no proof, so no THEORY is filed from it. For grokking's critical data

keeps the `anthology-candidate` flag: it is about training dynamics in
machine learning, which an anthology topic could hold.
