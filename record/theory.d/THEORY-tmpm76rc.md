# THEORY-tmpm76rc: Vallor holds that moral virtues are habituated and presuppose repeated practice of skills of moral analysis, deliberation and action, so that automated warfare which makes those practices redundant risks a moral deskilling of the military profession even with a human on the loop, since expertise to supervise a decision requires having practised it, and that whether a technology deskills is an empirical question of its context of use

## The account

Vallor: virtue "presupposes repeated practice and development of skills of moral analysis, deliberation and action"; automated systems can make those practices redundant, "with a resulting devaluation and/or loss of these skills", and "this deskilling remains a significant risk even with a commitment to ‘human on the loop’ protocols", because "An expert supervisor of another’s decision, in order to be worthy of the authority to override it, must have acquired expertise in making decisions of the very same or a similar kind." "Whether any automated technology produces deskilling, then, is an empirical question that depends upon the particular context of use."

## Evidence

The reading in NOTE-tmpgn1lo, results 1–8. The same structure in engineering is THEORY-1522 (Ahn on Bainbridge).

## What it does not say

That automated warfare does deskill; Vallor makes it an empirical question. Nothing on domains other than the military, which the 2015 paper adds.
