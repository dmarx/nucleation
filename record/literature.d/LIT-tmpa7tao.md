# LIT-tmpa7tao: Towards a Generalized Theory of Observers

Hatem Elshatlawy, Dean Rickles, Xerxes D. Arsiwalla (2025), *arXiv preprint; later ch. 4 ("Toward a Generalized Theory of Observers") in Quantum Gravity and Computation, eds. Rickles, Arsiwalla & Elshatlawy (Routledge, 2025-10-29), pp. 52–102* — [ARXIV-2504.16225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16225)

## Key takeaways

- The authors define a "minimal observer" as a bounded system with sensing, internal state transitions, action and feedback closure, and argue that internal/external distinctions, definite measurement outcomes, reference frames, hierarchical levels and coarse-graining are all undefined without such observers.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The paper proposes a formal framework unifying the notion of an observer across physics, computer science and philosophy. Starting from cybernetic feedback models, it gives an operational definition of minimal observers, explores their role in shaping foundational concepts, and identifies what would be left undefined without them. Drawing on quantum gravity, digital physics, second-order cybernetics, and ruliological and pregeometric approaches, it argues observers are indispensable reference points for measurement, reference frames and emergent complexity.
