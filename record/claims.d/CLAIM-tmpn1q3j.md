# CLAIM-tmpn1q3j: Governing a purpose includes being able to end it

## The claim

A16 §9: "Many organizations gradually treat their own persistence as a
purpose independent of the purposes for which they were established." A
platform for institutional alignment "would need to support not only
institutional growth, but also contraction, succession, division,
federation, transfer of capabilities, and dissolution." The qualification:
"Such a conclusion would not justify abandoning obligations to people who
have become dependent upon it. Dissolution would itself need to be
governed." A16 §11 lists it among the capabilities: "Governed termination
| Enable relinquishment of purposes and organizational dissolution", with
"Commitment continuity | Maintain legitimate obligations through
institutional change".

## How it stands to the record

- SYNTHESIS-091 separates viability from what ought to persist; A16's
  citation is apt. This claim makes the separation a capacity the
  institution must have, not only a judgement others may make of it.
- SYNTHESIS-093's duties of those who govern what others depend on, and
  CLAIM-1326's reliance that holds obligation past the original reasons,
  are the obligations governed dissolution must keep.
- CLAIM-tmp6xxuc is the general case: persistence as a purpose that
  governs its own reconsideration.
- TERM-tmpmdrmj includes "relinquish" in what developmental freedom is the
  capacity to do.

## What it does not say

That an institution should end, or that its interest in surviving is
illegitimate. The claim is about whether the finding can be reached
independently of that interest.
