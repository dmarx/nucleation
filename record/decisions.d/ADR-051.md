# ADR-051: A CASE names where the record got it: `reported_in` links it to the work, reading or account that reports it

## Context

Filing the exchange "Framework Overview Request" (workbench of 2026-10-23,
03:12:25), Bigand et al.'s dance study was read ([NOTE-1093](../notes.d/NOTE-1093.md)), filed as
a documented CASE ([CASE-149](../cases.d/CASE-149.md)), and its result stated as a THEORY
([THEORY-891](../theory.d/THEORY-891.md)). The reading agent gave the CASE `supports:
THEORY-891`, and lint refused it: a CASE's `supports` names CLAIMs
and ARGs only. No field linked a CASE to the work, reading or account it
came from, so the link was prose.

The owner: "update the nucleation config to permit linking CASEs, THEORYs,
LITs, and NOTEs (e.g. so if a reading describes a CASE we want to
reference, we know where we got it)".

Of the record's cases, 49 are documented. Each says in its summary or body
where it came from (for instance [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md), "Reported studies, read here
from their abstracts"), and none can say it in a field.

## Decision

- **CASE gains `reported_in`**, naming LIT, NOTE or THEORY entries, many,
  optional: where the record got this case. A LIT for the work that
  reports it, a NOTE for the reading that describes it, a THEORY for the
  account that states its result. A case may name several, as
  [CASE-149](../cases.d/CASE-149.md) names the paper, its reading and the THEORY.
- **LIT, NOTE and THEORY gain the converse `reports`**, written by
  `luria link --fix`.
- **Provenance, not support.** `reported_in` says where a case came from.
  It does not say the case bears on the account's truth, and it is not
  `grounds`. A case can be reported in a work the record has not read
  (a Deferred or Unavailable LIT), and the field then says what the
  case's standing rests on.
- **Scope.** New and revised cases use it. Existing documented cases are
  not backfilled in bulk; a case gains the field when it is next revised,
  or in a sweep the owner asks for.

## Alternatives

- **Widen CASE's `supports` to THEORY.** It would let the refused line
  pass, but `supports` means a case bears weight for what it points at,
  with the converse `grounds`. A paper does not rest on the case the record
  took from it.
- **Put the link on the other side only** (a THEORY or NOTE `describes`
  CASE). The CASE is where a reader asks where it came from, so the field
  that is written by hand belongs there, and the converse is generated.
- **A `source:` field like THEORY's.** THEORY's `source` is required and
  LIT-only; a case's provenance is optional and can be a reading or an
  account. A separate name avoids two meanings for one word.

## Consequences

- The CASE pages and indexes can show provenance as an edge.
- A documented CASE whose `reported_in` is only unread LITs is visibly a
  case taken on a work's word; that is the standing the prose already
  gave it.
- Nothing is required: a stipulated or fictional case, or a specimen the
  record holds first-hand, has no `reported_in`.
