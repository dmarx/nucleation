# LIT-2705: Meaningful Human Control over Autonomous Systems: A Philosophical Account

Filippo Santoni de Sio and Jeroen van den Hoven (2018). Meaningful Human Control over Autonomous Systems: A Philosophical Account. *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* 5. DOI 10.3389/frobt.2018.00015.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of 2026-11-17; the reading of 2026-11-16 is below.*

- **Presence is not control** (p. 4): "simple human presence or 'being in
  the loop' is not a sufficient condition for being in control".
- **Tracking** (p. 7) and **tracing** (p. 9), as above; tracing needs only
  "at least one human agent in the design history or use context".
- **Upstream duties** (p. 9): designers have "an obligation to design the
  system in such a way that this type of inquiry by users is not made
  impossible or unduly difficult".
- **Gaps without bad intent** (p. 11): when programmers and commanders each
  rely on the other, "no agent satisfies the tracing condition".

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read in full. The paper extends guidance control (reasons-responsiveness
and ownership, [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md)) to socio-technical systems as two design
conditions, tracking and tracing. Being in the loop "is not a sufficient
condition for being in control"; tracing is "not only meant, as it were, to
help" "finding someone to blame after an accident has occurred". Its tracing
examples carry responsibility upstream: "The captain is the choice
architect, who" sets Jim's dilemma; designers bear "their second-order
responsibility for the first-order responsibility" of operators. The
exchange's summary (A19 §10) is fair; its extension of the framework with
Tronto and Elish is A's.
