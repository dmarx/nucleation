# LIT-1420: The Contact Hypothesis Re-evaluated

Paluck, E. L., Green, S. A. and Green, D. P. (2019). The contact hypothesis re-evaluated. *Behavioural Public Policy*, 3(2), 129–158. doi:10.1017/bpp.2018.25. Preprint: SocArXiv, 24 May 2017, doi:10.31235/osf.io/w2jkf.

Read in full on 2026-10-29 (NOTE-tmpsnvbw).

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it as the check on Pettigrew and Tropp: randomized contact studies generally reduce prejudice, but the evidence for adults' racial or ethnic prejudice is limited. **Disagreement, of degree:** the abstract says not that the evidence is limited but that studies addressing adults' racial or ethnic prejudice are absent, and separately that interventions on ethnic or racial prejudice show substantially weaker effects. It also stresses the 27 studies' small number and the untested scope conditions. The exchange dated it 2019 (the issue); it was online in 2018 and posted in 2017. Bears on [CLAIM-275](../claims.d/CLAIM-275.md), as the caution on how far the contact evidence carries.
