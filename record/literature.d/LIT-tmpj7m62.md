# LIT-tmpj7m62: Regulation of choice behavior: an experiment investigating the hypothesis that people bundle sequences of expected rewards

Alexander Schuhr (2015). Doctoral thesis, School of Economics, University of Cape Town. hdl:11427/15696

## Key takeaways

- **A series raises patience in first choices.** Two or three paired
  smaller-sooner/larger-later decisions per row, with later front-end
  delays for the second and third, gave more larger-later first choices
  than the single-decision baseline.
- **Not by being forced.** Treatments that tied the paired decisions
  together lowered the mixture probability of exponential discounting by
  33 points; the free treatments carried the effect.
- **Not by doubled stakes.** The effective payout scale had no
  significant effect.
