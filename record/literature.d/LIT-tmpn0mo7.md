# LIT-tmpn0mo7: Freedom and Resentment

P. F. Strawson (1962). Freedom and Resentment. *Proceedings of the British Academy* 48: 187–211. Collected in *Freedom and Resentment and Other Essays* (Routledge, 2008), DOI 10.4324/9780203882566.

## Key takeaways

- The reactive attitudes "rest on, and reflect, an expectation of, and
  demand for, the manifestation of a certain degree of goodwill or regard"
  (p. 200).
- Pleas like "He was pushed" "do not invite us to see the agent as other
  than a fully responsible agent"; pleas like "His mind has been
  systematically perverted" "invite us to suspend our ordinary reactive
  attitudes towards the agent" (p. 193).
- Blame's "partial and temporary withdrawal of goodwill" is "the
  consequence of continuing to view him as a member of the moral
  community" (p. 207).
- The framework "neither calls for, nor permits, an external 'rational'
  justification" (p. 208).

## Note of 2026-11-18: read

Read in NOTE-tmpnfib1. A7's description, "intimately connected to these
expectations of goodwill and respect", is faithful with one word off:
Strawson's word is "regard". The link is closer than A says: "The making of
the demand is the proneness to such attitudes" (p. 207). For
CLAIM-tmpa2l21 the reading qualifies rather than supports: coercion pleas
leave the agent a participant but may make the injury one for which he was
"not fully, or at all, responsible", and systematic perversion exempts.
