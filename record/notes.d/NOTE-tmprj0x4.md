# NOTE-tmprj0x4: Shanahan — Simulacra as Conscious Exotica

## Contribution

It applies Shanahan's 2016 "conscious exotica" programme, a later-Wittgenstein
reading of consciousness language, to LLM agents understood as role-playing
simulacra. It adds a test for candidature (can we engineer an encounter?), a
verdict on current systems (disembodied chat agents fail it), and a
scenario in which virtually embodied simulacra pass it and push the
language into new shapes. For the self it adds two short claims (§3, §7.3),
not an account.

## Key insight

Ask how a word would come to be used, not what hidden fact it names. For
consciousness that means: put the entity in a world with us, let people be
with it and study its mechanism, and see what the community comes to say.
Nothing further is hidden. Disembodiment is therefore disqualifying, not
because experience needs a body but because the use of the words does.

## Assumptions

- **A Wittgensteinian method**, summarised rather than defended (§4, fn 8:
  "no attempt here to offer a definitive interpretation"). Its central step
  is the private-language conclusion that "a nothing would serve as well as
  a something about which nothing can be said" (PI §304).
- **All theories of mind-body relation are "dualistic"**, behaviourism,
  functionalism and identity theory included, because they "posit two
  metaphysical categories" (§4). This is asserted.
- **Embodiment is the home of consciousness language**: shared perception,
  joint action and purposeful movement (§6.1).
- **The role-play account of LLM agents**, taken from the 2023 paper and
  Janus (§3).
- **Scope**: LLM agents of 2024, from simple chat agents to an imagined
  virtually embodied agent trained on text, vision and action tokens (§7.1).

## Key results

- **Candidature by encounter** (§§5.2–5.3): the ability to engineer an
  encounter, "even if only in principle, establishes an exotic entity's
  candidature". Illustrated by the octopus and an imagined alien cube
  containing a simulated world and agent.
- **Inscrutability** (§5.4): no entity has a high capacity for consciousness
  and very low human-likeness; that region, the void of inscrutability, is
  empty.
- **Chat agents** (§6.1): "it is not possible to engineer an encounter with
  a simple conversational agent, even in principle", since we "cannot be with
  them in a shared world". Using consciousness words of them either bends
  the words out of shape or clings to dualism.
- **Enhanced agents** (§6.2): tools, multimodality, memory and planning
  legitimise belief and intention talk, but "we still cannot engineer an
  encounter" and "nothing has changed" for consciousness.
- **Embodied agents** (§§6.2–6.4): robots and virtually embodied agents can be
  encountered and so are candidates; edge cases (locked-in patients, the
  foetus, a phone-borne agent) are left to the community.
- **Selfhood** (§3): "There is nothing that would qualify as a self worth
  preserving for such an agent. It has no body, no personal history, and no
  autobiographical memory." Extending lifetime and persistent memory "might
  legitimise talk of needs, desires, and selfhood".
- **No person behind the mask** (§7.3): humans have "some kind of stable self
  at the core", generative agents do not; their identity is the
  distribution of roles they concurrently play.

## Method

Conceptual: a method taken from Wittgenstein, applied to a graded series of
cases (octopus, alien cube, chat agent, enhanced agent, robot, virtually
embodied agent) and one extended scenario. The paper calls its aim to
describe "without judgement, the language games of imagined communities"
(§8).

## Concepts

- **Conscious exotica**: entities very unlike any terrestrial organism that
  nonetheless belong to "the fellowship of conscious beings".
- **Engineering an encounter**: putting ourselves where we can meaningfully
  interact with an entity "given the purpose we discern in it" (§5.2).
- **Void of inscrutability**: the empty region of the space of possible
  minds where human-likeness is very low and capacity for consciousness
  above zero (§5.4).
- **Garland test**: a human ascribes consciousness to an AI while knowing it
  is an artefact (fn 16).
- **Perfect actor**: Putnam's case, here made "(almost) real" by an embodied
  simulacrum (fn 22).

## Connections

It builds on Shanahan 2016 ("Conscious exotica"), Shanahan, McDonell and
Reynolds 2023 ([LIT-tmposjpz](../literature.d/LIT-tmposjpz.md)) and Janus. It cites Putnam 1963 and 1964 as
near allies. It names Nagel ([LIT-096](../literature.d/LIT-096.md)) and Chalmers 1996 as canonical
statements of the dualist intuition. Chalmers 2023 ([LIT-411](../literature.d/LIT-411.md)) and Butlin et
al. ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md)) are named as the rival, evidential method. Seth ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md)) is
quoted on consciousness and living systems (fn 19). Birch's work on animal
sentience is cited for the octopus case, not his AI manifesto ([LIT-111](../literature.d/LIT-111.md)).

On the self, its claims meet the record's works as follows. Dennett
([LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md)) denies the stable core self that §7.3 takes for granted. Goldstein
and Lederman ([LIT-206](../literature.d/LIT-206.md)) give instances desires on behavioural grounds that §3
withholds. Minsky ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md)) puts the self in reflective self-models, which
§3's conditions do not mention. Hofstadter ([LIT-416](../literature.d/LIT-416.md), unread) is reported to
hold a conversational criterion that §6.1 rejects. None of these is cited;
the comparisons are mine.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md): a challenge to its presupposition, not evidence for or
  against it.** The account treats AI consciousness as an open question
  current evidence cannot settle. This paper says the question has no
  answer beyond an eventual consensus (C7), and that for chat agents it is
  answered now, negatively, by the grammar of the word (C3). If C1 is
  granted, [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)'s deadlock is not epistemic. The account should note
  this as a position that dissolves the dispute rather than taking a side in
  it. The promotion condition is untouched: the paper offers no evidence of
  the kind it asks for.
- **For a THEORY on LLM selfhood**: it supplies one candidate condition
  (body, personal history, autobiographical memory) and the "no core" claim
  about role players. Both are asserted, so neither could source a THEORY
  alone.
- **No ML instruction.** The ethics section warns against letting
  consciousness language "wander too far"; it is not practice.

## Limitations

- C2 does all the work and is stipulated. A reader who does not share the
  Wittgensteinian method gets no argument against pure-thinker or
  disembodied-mind views; Chalmers's is named (fn 18) and set aside.
- Calling functionalism and identity theory "dualistic" is a definition
  wide enough to make the rival positions one; it is not argued.
- The self passages are two paragraphs. The three conditions for a self
  worth preserving are listed, not defended, and §7.3's human "stable self at
  the core" contradicts the Humean and Dennettian views the author adopts a
  year later ([LIT-tmpwb2zb](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwb2zb.md), §6.1 fn 41).
- The embodied-simulacrum scenario is imagined, and the outcomes are listed,
  not weighed.

## Open questions

- Would persistent memory and a long lifetime (§3) make a chat agent a self
  without making it a candidate for consciousness? The paper allows the
  first and denies the second, so its notions of self and consciousness come
  apart; it does not say how.
- Does a phone-borne or headset agent that shares a perceptual scene with a
  user count as encounterable (§6.4)? The paper leaves it to the community.

## Corrections

- **Venue and date.** The brief's "Inquiry (2024)" is right for first
  appearance and online publication (1 December 2024). The journal issue is
  69(6), 2026, pp. 2982–3010 (Crossref).
- **The paper does not argue that LLMs lack a self from the role-play view
  alone.** §3 grounds the denial in missing body, history and memory, and
  allows that enhancements could change it.
