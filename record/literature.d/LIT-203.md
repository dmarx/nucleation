# LIT-203: Conspiracy theorists are not the problem; Conspiracy liars are

Brian L. Keeley (2024), *Inquiry, 68(8), 2744–2764* — DOI-10.1080/0020174X.2024.2375771

## Key takeaways

- Many conspiracy theories start not from errant data but from lies and fabricated facts, so the pernicious cases are bad-faith pseudo-explanations — and science has analogous value-driven false starting points.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Starting from an LA Times column arguing that Alex Jones is not a "theorist" but a liar, Keeley extends his earlier account of where conspiracy theories begin — "unaccounted-for" and "contradictory" errant data — with two further starting points that are falsehoods: lies and "fabricated facts". Conspiracy theories built on these are pernicious precisely because they are not offered in good faith as explanations. Comparing conspiratorial with scientific explanation, he finds similar erroneous starting points in science, often reflecting value-laden agendas beyond accurate explanation.
