# CLAIM-413: Grounding is not a property of an experience but a relation among an interpretation, what it purports to be about, and a process that constrains it independently; its current, developmental and epistemic senses come apart from one another, and in the current sense it comes apart from whether the interpretation occurs, so ungrounded is not unconstrained, and what makes an interpretive organization experiential need not be what makes its contents veridical

## The claim

The owner, at U1: "a necessary condition for *grounded* experience is
sensory apparatus". A1 accepted the direction and changed the terms.
Sensory apparatus "may not be sufficient for grounding", since a camera
attached to a system does not ground it, and grounding "arguably requires
an appropriately structured causal relationship between an agent and its
environment, potentially including feedback, calibration, and
opportunities for corrective action". A7 added that it is not necessary
either, in the interpreter's own body: "A system might receive reports
from another system's sensors without possessing those sensors itself."

The claim has three parts.

1. **Grounding is a relation.** A7 wrote it as \(G(I, r, \mathcal C)\): an
   interpretation, its purported referent, and the constraining
   relationships available. It is also relative to a referent. An
   envatted brain "may have a coherent experience of a tree that is
   ungrounded with respect to the tree it apparently perceives, while
   still being causally grounded in the apparatus that produces its
   signals" (A7; A2 had made the same point).
2. **Its senses come apart.** A3 separated current, developmental and
   functional grounding. A7 kept current and developmental and replaced
   functional with epistemic:
   - **current:** whether present interpretations are constrained by
     their purported external referents;
   - **developmental:** whether the organization that makes interpretation
     possible was acquired through embodied or environmental interaction;
   - **epistemic:** whether interpretations can be evaluated or corrected
     using evidence "sufficiently independent of the interpretive process
     being assessed".

   The epistemic sense is [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s. A loop is kept from confirming
   itself by evidence "whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the
   interpretation it tests". A5 drew the consequence: sensory signals
   ground "only insofar as their arrival, content, or scoring has some
   independence from the interpretive process being evaluated". It
   carried over the caveat [QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md) leaves open: "no sensory
   input arrives with an interpretation-independent *meaning*".
3. **Grounding and occurrence come apart.** A5: "Ungrounded ≠
   Unconstrained". A dream is poorly grounded in its apparent objects but
   "strongly constrained by memory, emotional organization, and the
   dynamics of the sleeping brain" (A7). Hence A7's pair of claims: "What
   makes an interpretation occur is not necessarily what makes it
   accurate", and "What makes an interpretive organization experiential is
   not necessarily what makes its contents veridical." A7's narrower form,
   which the claim takes: "current veridical grounding need not be
   constitutive of the occurrence of an experiential organization at a
   given time".

## Where it sits in the record

- It rests on [CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md) for the epistemic sense. Without it, "grounded"
  would mean only "caused by", and the claim would lose the sense in which
  grounding can be checked.
- It complements [CLAIM-412](CLAIM-412.md). That claim says what makes an
  organization experiential; this one says that grounding is a different
  question. Neither rests on the other.
- Block's Inverted Earth ([THEORY-095](../theory.d/THEORY-095.md)) is the record's reading of the
  same split for content. Phenomenal character stays fixed while
  externally individuated content and long-arm role invert. Putnam's
  brains in a vat ([LIT-1343](../literature.d/LIT-1343.md)) is A2's version: the constitution of an
  experience and "the determination of its referential content might come
  apart". A7 accepted the consequence: "distinguish the internal
  organization of experienced content from its external semantic
  reference".
- Charles Bonnet hallucinations (A3) and dreams are the natural cases.
  In both, weakened current constraint leaves rich experience. The
  record holds no reading of either.

## The objection it leaves open

The developmental objection, as the exchange stated it (A3): "the
independence of experience from current sensory grounding doesn't
establish its independence from sensorimotor grounding in general". Every
case the claim cites is a system that "acquired their interpretive
organization through embodiment" (A7). A6 separated four independence
theses: from current veridical input, from current bodily feedback, from
past sensorimotor development, and from biological realization. "The
available evidence supports these to very different degrees." The claim
asserts only the first. It is silent on the other three. A6 named the
contrast that would sharpen the dispute: "current veridical sensory
constraint" against "counterfactual sensorimotor organization". On the
brain-bound side, the exchange described Villalobos and Cea ([LIT-1350](../literature.d/LIT-1350.md))
as arguing that a disembodied brain's physiological implausibility does
not defeat a contrastive, brain-centred explanation. Their paper could not
be reached, and that description is unchecked.

## What it does not say

- It does not say that a system with no sensory history can have
  experience. That is A3's thesis C, which "does not follow" and is not
  claimed.
- It does not say that experience is invariant to grounding over time.
  A7: "Altering external constraints can reorganize interpretation over
  time."
- It does not say that internal coherence is evidence of grounding. A7's
  epistemic thesis says the opposite: "Internal coherence alone is not
  evidence of external grounding."

## Note of 2026-10-22: what the readings show about the objection

The works the exchange cited for the open objection were read on
2026-10-22. Two of them place it differently from the exchange.

- **IWMT does not require current coupling** ([NOTE-1057](../notes.d/NOTE-1057.md),
  [THEORY-876](../theory.d/THEORY-876.md)). In Safron et al., imagination is the same world model
  "decoupled from sense-data". What IWMT requires is a capacity for
  action–perception coupling and a history of it, and it allows virtual
  embodiment. So it agrees with this claim on current grounding and parts
  from it only on development and capacity, the half the claim leaves
  open.
- **Ward's objection is organicist, not developmental** ([NOTE-1065](../notes.d/NOTE-1065.md),
  [THEORY-874](../theory.d/THEORY-874.md)). The paper never discusses brains in vats, development,
  current input or grounding. Its enactivism holds that meaning, and so
  experience, depends on belonging to a living, self-maintaining system.
  That challenges the claim's split differently: it ties what makes an
  organization experiential to what keeps it alive, not to what grounds
  its contents. The claim's three senses of grounding do not answer it.
  That is the question of whether experience requires life, which
  [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md)'s self-maintaining subclass leaves open.
- **Gładziejewski** ([NOTE-1056](../notes.d/NOTE-1056.md)) separates the grounding of a modelled
  structure from the grounding of its realizer, and treats dreams as the
  off-line exception in which constraint persists without current input.
  That supports "ungrounded is not unconstrained".
- **Laukkonen et al.** ([NOTE-1063](../notes.d/NOTE-1063.md)) describe lucid dreamless sleep as
  awareness without current content. That is a case for the occurrence
  half, though it rests on report.
- **Villalobos and Cea and Putnam's "Brains in a vat"** are Unavailable
  ([LIT-1350](../literature.d/LIT-1350.md), [LIT-1343](../literature.d/LIT-1343.md)). The claim's use of each rests on the
  exchange's description.
