# THEORY-tmpopsu0: A definition of health and disease by social justice alone cannot fix what counts as disease: with justice left undefined, a majority with other values could medicalise what the definition was meant to protect, and unless medicine is fixed from outside, the category drifts until health becomes justice

## Source

Veit (2025), [LIT-098](../literature.d/LIT-098.md) (*Croatian Journal of Philosophy* 25(73)), read in full
in [NOTE-097](../notes.d/NOTE-097.md) from the PhilSci-Archive preprint. The published version was not
read, and page numbers are the preprint's.

## What was actually shown

**The target.** Kukla (2014) defines a condition as a health condition "if
and only if, given our resources and situation, it would be best for our
collective wellbeing if it were medicalized" (quoted p. 11). Veit classes this
as moral conceptual engineering: the same four-stage activity as naturalist
conceptual engineering, but with moral, political and social assessment at
the first stage (pp. 6–7).

**The first horn (pp. 13–14).** Kukla offers her neutrality about justice as a
strength. Veit calls it "a blatant weakness" (p. 14). In his thought
experiment a conservative majority uses the definition to medicalise "an
unwillingess to bear the child of one's rapist", a same-sex love (the text
says "opposite sex", apparently a slip) and opposition to the dominant
religion. The definition would then license what it was built to condemn.
It could fail even its own goal of collective wellbeing, unless there is
moral consensus.

**The second horn (pp. 14–16).** Suppose justice were agreed. The definition
still counts as health conditions "all and only those things that are
perceived to be something social justice should address" (p. 15). Medical
tools are refined for whatever they are pointed at, so medicine co-evolves
into justice promotion, and "health would simply become justice" (p. 15).
Holding medicine fixed is the only escape, and the fixing has to come from
naturalist or constructivist resources (p. 16). So "pure normativism can be
'pure' in name only" (p. 16).

**The remedy (pp. 16–17).** Pluralism, with "at least three alternative
accounts". Health needs only an empirical link to wellbeing and autonomy,
not a conceptual one, in order to matter morally (p. 17). Justice may still
properly inform medical decisions.

**What could have come out otherwise.** A definition by justice could have
been shown to fix a stable extension without outside help. The paper argues
that it cannot. It tests nothing.

## What this does not say

- **That justice has no place in medicine.** Veit endorses Kukla's aim of
  preventing medicalised oppression and keeps justice as an input to
  medical decisions. What he rejects is building it into the concept.
- **That the second horn's premise is true.** That medical tools grow toward
  whatever justice demands is asserted, with no historical case ([NOTE-097](../notes.d/NOTE-097.md),
  C3: weak–moderate). The account stands or falls with it.
- **That every normativist account fails.** A normativist account with a
  specified theory of justice escapes the first horn by construction. Veit
  grants that the first horn bites only "unless there is complete moral
  consensus" (p. 17), which makes it a pragmatic objection.
- **That Veit concedes Kukla an escape.** The seeded skim said he concedes
  medicine can be taken as independently fixed (p. 17). The full text makes
  no such concession. Holding medicine fixed is the route his second horn
  closes ([NOTE-097](../notes.d/NOTE-097.md), corrections).
- **That conceptual engineering beats conceptual analysis here.** The paper
  announces that switch and does not argue it (p. 6).

## Connections

- The pattern is general: a category defined only by the desirability of
  acting on it loses its extension. [NOTE-097](../notes.d/NOTE-097.md) notes that it could be turned
  against defining, say, "harmful content" by what it would be good to
  moderate. The paper does not make that application.
- The reading names a related case of reshaping a mental-health concept for
  its social effects, which the record holds but could not read.
