# LIT-1387: Doorways do not always cause forgetting: a multimodal investigation

McFadyen, J., Nolan, C., Pinocy, E., Buteri, D., & Baumann, O. (2021). Doorways do not always cause forgetting: a multimodal investigation. *BMC Psychology*, 9, 41. doi:10.1186/s40359-021-00536-3.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1079](../notes.d/NOTE-1079.md). The XML has no page breaks; references are
to sections.*

- **No loss of hits.** "Across this series of experiments, we observed no
  significant effect of doorways on forgetting" (Abstract).
- **Confusion under load.** With a counting task, d′ fell after a doorway
  because false alarms to recombined probes rose: "under working memory
  load, memory was more susceptible to interference after moving through
  doorways" (Abstract).
- **Conceptual, not direct.** Movement in VR was passive and rooms were
  identical; the hallway experiments used curtains and a spatial-arrangement
  task. The authors also fault prior studies for few, unbalanced trials.

## Why this is here

A37 cited it as evidence that the doorway effect "isn't perfectly robust", and A39 asked that its mixed findings be kept. The case is [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** The paper is the record's only
non-replication; it cites independent positive studies the record does not
hold (Lawrence & Peterson 2016; Seel et al. 2019). Its one positive result
fits the interference account of [LIT-1385](LIT-1385.md). See [NOTE-1079](../notes.d/NOTE-1079.md).
