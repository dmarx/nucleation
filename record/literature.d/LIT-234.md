# LIT-234: The Effects of Regularization and Data Augmentation are Class Dependent

Randall Balestriero, Leon Bottou, Yann LeCun (2022), *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 35 (NeurIPS 2022), pp. 37878–37891* — [ARXIV-2204.03632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03632)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-845](../notes.d/NOTE-845.md).*

- **Label preservation is class-dependent.** Six pretrained ImageNet
  models, tested on cropped images, keep some classes recognisable at an
  8% crop and lose others below about 50%. They agree on which classes
  are fragile (Figs. 2–3). CutOut and colour jitter behave the same.
- **The average hides per-class losses.** Over 20 ResNet-50 runs, moving
  the random-crop lower bound from 100% to 8% raises mean accuracy from
  about 70% to 76%. "Academic gown" falls from about 62% to 40% and
  "barn spider" from about 68% to 46% (Fig. 4).
- **The statistics are thin.** A one-sided Welch test per class,
  uncorrected for 1,000 classes, rejects for 4.4% at the 5% level and
  2.1% at the 1% level. The first is not above chance alone. ImageNet's
  50 validation images per class are not accounted for.
- **Weight decay too, on one figure.** Varying weight decay over 10
  runs sends selected classes' accuracies in opposite directions
  (Figs. 5, 12). No numbers are given.
- **It survives transfer.** With a frozen backbone and a linear probe on
  iNaturalist, pretraining crop strength moves selected classes by 30–60
  points while the average stays flat near 40% (Fig. 6). The abstract's
  class [#8889](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/8889) rises then falls; its 70%→30% is from moderate to strong
  cropping.
- **Theorem 1 is narrow.** Zero training error on augmentations that
  leave the target's level sets yields a model that is not f*. It says
  nothing about which classes pay.

machine-learning practice: evaluate and tune regularization per class or
worst class, not only on average. That belongs in the Anthology of the
SOTA. Its general point, that per-class risk curves under one complexity
knob need not share a minimum, is the bias–variance trade-off
illustrated, not a new account.
