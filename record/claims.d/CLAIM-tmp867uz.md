# CLAIM-tmp867uz: The manuscript's thesis and the October thesis give rival answers to what survives, which come apart in both directions on the cases of the receiver objection, yet the record links them by `complements` and scopes the October thesis to a question its fidelity clause does not stay inside

## The objection

**Two answers to one question.** [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) answers [QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md), "What
survives when an utterance is translated, retold or rendered in another
medium?": what survives is "the set of communicative distinctions and
relations a receiving system can still reconstruct and act upon".
[CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s fourth clause: "Fidelity concerns which independently
identifiable, decision-relevant relational distinctions persist through
those transformations." That is an answer to the same question. [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)
lists only [QUESTION-029](../questions.d/QUESTION-029.md) in `answers`, and [QUESTION-029](../questions.d/QUESTION-029.md) says what follows:
"A thesis that answers only the first part is metaphysics. One that
answers only the second and third is [QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md) again." [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s
fidelity clause answers the third part, under which transformations a kind
persists, which [QUESTION-029](../questions.d/QUESTION-029.md) calls "[QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md) turned round".

**The answers disagree.** [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md)'s two cases:

- **Identifiable but unusable.** A distinction the analyst's probes
  identify and a receiver with only the target text cannot recover. On
  [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) it persists. On [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) it did not survive, since the
  receiving system cannot reconstruct it.
- **Usable but unidentifiable.** A distinction a receiver with side
  information recovers and the analyst's probes cannot identify. On
  [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) it survived. On [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) it is not among the "independently
  identifiable" distinctions, so it does not count as persisting.

So for each case one thesis says the distinction survived and the other
says it did not. [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md) shows the cases and concludes only that "the
thesis now needs both conditions, or an argument for dropping one". It
does not draw the consequence for how the two standing theses are
related.

**The relation asserts the opposite.** [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) and [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) are linked by
`complements`, whose blurb in luria.yaml reads: "answers a different part
of the same question, so the two belong together rather than competing".
On the fidelity clause they answer the same part and compete. [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s
summary gives the reason for the relation: "No draft carries it, so it
complements the manuscript's thesis rather than superseding it." That is a
reason about drafts, not about content, and it is the only one given.

## What it does not say

It does not say which criterion is right, nor that [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s ontological
and epistemic clauses compete with [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md): only its fidelity clause
does. It does not say [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) must supersede [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md); two rival
theses can both stand Proposed. It says they should be related as rivals,
and that [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) answers [QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md) whether or not it lists it.
