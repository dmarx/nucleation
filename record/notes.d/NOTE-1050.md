# NOTE-1050: Espeland and Sauder — Rankings and reactivity

## Contribution

It turns reactivity, a methodological worry about measures contaminating
what they measure, into a substantive object of study, and gives a
vocabulary for it: two mechanisms that produce reactivity and three
patterns of effects. Its case, U.S. News (USN) rankings of every
accredited US law school, shows the measured changing what they do in
response to being measured, and the measure becoming more valid as a
result, not less.

## Key insight

A public measure can make itself true. "We define self-fulfilling
prophecies as processes by which reactions to social measures confirm the
expectations or predictions that are embedded in measures or which
increase the validity of the measure by encouraging behavior that
conforms to it" (p. 11). Some of the channels close through the measure's
own inputs: a school's rank moves applicants and yield, which move the
selectivity score that makes up a quarter of the rank, and past ranks
shape the reputation surveys that make up 40%. So agreement between a
measure and the world it measures can be produced by the measure.

## Assumptions

- The case is one measure in one field: USN's law-school ranking, built
  from reputation (40%), selectivity (25%), placement (20%) and faculty
  resources (15%), standardized, weighted and rescaled to the top school's
  score. USN ranks every accredited school and has no rival of comparable
  weight there, unlike business schools with "at least five high-profile
  rankings" (fn. 4). The authors chose the case for that density.
- The data are 136 open-ended interviews of about 45 minutes (two waves,
  66 and 70; 39 deans or ex-deans, 49 other administrators, 33 faculty, 9
  other staff, 6 leaders of associations), from 70 of about 190 schools
  across all four tiers, plus short interviews at an admissions fair (17
  staff, 92 prospective students), 30 business-school interviews, chat
  rooms, school materials and reanalysed fieldwork. Interviews "were coded
  for general themes rather than for precisely defined variables;
  consequently, the frequency of particular responses is only an
  approximate indication of their importance."
- The quantitative links (rank → applications and yield; past rank →
  reputation score) are from Sauder and Lancaster (2006) and Stake (2006),
  cited, not shown here.
- A mechanism is "like a causal switch that, if turned on, directs action
  in one way rather than another", and the mechanisms are patterns of
  sense making.

## Key results

- **Reactivity defined.** "individuals alter their behavior in reaction to
  being evaluated, observed, or measured." It mediates two understandings
  of a measure, a neutral depiction and a vehicle of accountability, and
  "the job of measures is to harness reactivity" on the second.
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy, four forms.** Not restricted to false
  beliefs, "requiring only that an expectation, once defined as real,
  amplify or confirm its effect."
  1. *External audiences.* Rank lists magnify "statistically
     insignificant differences"; small rank differences move applications
     and yield, which change the selectivity score "and in this way
     reinforce the original differences which were often largely a product
     of measurement noise." Two elite schools: before rankings A lost about
     two thirds of cross-admits to B; after years of ranking slightly
     higher, A reportedly takes 80–90% of them. A faculty member:
     "They create artificial lines that then have the danger of becoming
     real."
  2. *Prior ranks in the reputation survey.* Raters know little of most
     schools but their rank; Stake (2006) "finds that reputation scores
     slowly become aligned with overall rank". Footnote 14 reports the
     contrary finding of Schmalbeck (2001) for the top 50 and the
     authors' reading that the effect is slow and strongest lower down.
  3. *Budgets by rank* inside universities, which "reproduce and intensify
     the stratification they are designed to measure"; how often this
     happens "is unclear".
  4. *Realizing embedded assumptions.* Schools become "more like what
     rankings measure, which reinforces the validity of the measure":
     LSAT scores come to define merit, and an "access university" on the
     cusp of a tier weighs moving its low-LSAT admits to the part-time
     program, "which doesn't count as much toward the rankings."
- **Commensuration.** "the transformation of qualities into quantities
  that share a metric" (p. 16). It works "mainly by transforming
  cognition", against self-fulfilling prophecy, which works by changing
  expectations, so its effects can be immediate where the other's are
  gradual. Three features:
  - it simplifies, making much information irrelevant and imposing one
    form on the rest, and decontextualized numbers travel and "invite
    recontextualization" (law firms set interview thresholds by school
    rank, a use USN did not intend);
  - it unites and distinguishes, giving every school a precise relation to
    every other and to its own past, which makes "status claims not
    supported by rankings" hard to sustain;
  - it invites reflection on what numbers represent, distributed by
    position: prospective students tend to metrological realism,
    administrators who make the numbers to constructivism (Desrosières's
    four attitudes).
- **Three effects.** Maximization (merit scholarships replacing need-based
  aid; a reported over-$100,000 a year on brochures to survey raters,
  with "no evidence that it works"); redefinition of work (all 16 career
  services staff reported spending more effort on tracking graduates'
  employment); gaming, "efforts to improve ranking factors without
  improving the characteristics the factors are designed to measure"
  (29 of 177 schools in 1995 reported higher median LSATs to USN than to
  the ABA; reclassifying low scorers as part-time or probationary; spring
  leaves for faculty).
- **Variation.** Five sources: being on the cusp of a tier, position in the
  ranking, the dean's commitment, geography, and mission. Distinctive
  missions "can sometimes resist rankings pressures".
- **Conclusion.** The tension between validity and accountability is
  chronic: "We want the world to stand still for measurement and, only
  after, change in relation to it." Strathern's "When a measure becomes a
  target, it ceases to be a good measure" is quoted (p. 35). Categories
  are produced the same way: "the census produces 'Hispanics,' and the LSAT
  defines 'merit.'" Coercion is suggested as a third mechanism, since the
  ranking is relative and zero-sum.

## Concepts

- **Reactivity**: individuals alter their behaviour in reaction to being
  evaluated, observed or measured; "a form of reflexivity".
- **Mechanism of reactivity**: a pattern of sense making that directs the
  response, "like a causal switch".
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy** (widened from Merton): reactions to a
  measure that confirm its embedded expectations, or raise its validity
  by inducing conformity. No false belief is required.
- **Commensuration**: transforming qualities into quantities on a shared
  metric, so that difference becomes "a matter of more or less, rather
  than kind".
- **Gaming**: manipulating rules and numbers "in ways that are unconnected
  to, or even undermine, the motivation behind them".

## Connections

It extends Campbell's (1957) reactive measure and Merton's self-fulfilling
prophecy, follows Heimer (1985) in treating reactivity as a continuous
variable, and sets itself beside Callon's and MacKenzie's performativity,
which it declines as its frame because reactivity keeps the validity of
knowledge central. Commensuration is from Espeland and Stevens (1998)
([LIT-1317](../literature.d/LIT-1317.md), Unavailable here). Its accountability background is Power's
audit society, Strathern's audit culture, Porter (1995) and Hacking's
*Taming of Chance* (1990). It does not cite Hacking's interactive kinds or
looping effect, or Scott ([LIT-1321](../literature.d/LIT-1321.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-402](../claims.d/CLAIM-402.md).** The paper is an empirical instance of the claim's
  mechanism, and stronger than its toy in one respect: the
  validity-raising channel is part of the definition of self-fulfilling
  prophecy here. A rank that predicts applicants' choices and reputation
  scores has its success produced in part through its own use, and schools
  "become more like what rankings measure". Every school in the field is

  effect the question has to control for: a category's validity rises as
  constituencies use it. It is reported, not studied.
- **[CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md).** The reputation survey is a loop whose evidence passes
  through the interpretation it tests: raters score schools by the rank
  the scores feed.
- **[CLAIM-406](../claims.d/CLAIM-406.md).** Its loop has an institutional instance here: the

## Limitations

- **Interviews of the measured.** Most respondents are administrators who
  dislike the rankings, and the authors say that respondents would not
  admit gaming at their own schools and that "some stories may be
  apocryphal". They report only strategies corroborated by public sources
  or direct experience.
- **No counterfactual and no effect sizes.** Whether a school changed
  because of the ranking or would have anyway is inferred from accounts.
  The rank-to-yield and rank-to-reputation links rest on cited studies,
  and footnote 14 reports one that finds the second weak.
- **Selection of the case.** Chosen because USN monopolizes the field and
  ranks every school, which the authors say makes effects dense. The
  framework's reach to fields with several rankings is argued, not shown,
  and the authors' own business-school comparison (Sauder and Espeland
  2006) points the other way.
- **A framework, by its own account.** "an orienting heuristic ... rather
  than ... a general theory of reactivity." The mechanisms are named and
  illustrated, not separated empirically: the paper says commensuration
  "exacerbated" the self-fulfilling prophecies and does not apportion the
  effects between them.
- **The Strathern quotation.** The wording quoted from Strathern (1996,
  p. 4) is the familiar generalization of Goodhart's law; the paper gives
  it without Goodhart.

## Open questions

- Under what conditions does the reactive loop settle on the ranking it
  started with, and when does it amplify noise, as with the two elite
  schools? The paper gives the channels and no dynamics.
- Is the validity a measure gains through conformity evidence of anything
  about the measured beyond the measure's influence? That is
  [CLAIM-402](../claims.d/CLAIM-402.md)'s question and is not this paper's.
- Coercion as a third mechanism is suggested and not developed.
