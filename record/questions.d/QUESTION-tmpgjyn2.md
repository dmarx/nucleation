# QUESTION-tmpgjyn2: Can preserving an inquiry's justificatory history improve decisions beyond a current state that is sufficient for those decisions, and can such a state stop being sufficient when a source, the model or the decision family changes?

## The question

A8 ended by asking "under which decision families and resource
constraints does preserving justificatory history provide additional
value beyond preserving a sufficient current epistemic state?" A10 found
it mis-posed, and [QUESTION-112](QUESTION-112.md) is what it left. This question is the part
A10 answered.

## The answer

[CLAIM-300](../claims.d/CLAIM-300.md), Active, answers both halves from the definition of
sufficiency:

- **Redundancy.** No. If a state is sufficient for the whole decision
  family under a model, its history cannot improve ideal performance on
  that family. That is a negative answer, and it holds by definition.
- **Relativity.** Yes. In [CASE-087](../cases.d/CASE-087.md) two histories with the same mean are
  indistinguishable for μ until one instrument is found miscalibrated, or
  a mixture is introduced. Then the mean no longer separates them.

How sufficiency should be judged when the family itself changes with what
the inquiry finds is not answered here. It remains [QUESTION-112](QUESTION-112.md)'s.
