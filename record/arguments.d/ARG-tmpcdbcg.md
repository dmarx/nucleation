# ARG-tmpcdbcg: The manuscript's renderings, which keep the words and change the act, show that keeping the proposition is not necessary for keeping the act

## The inference

Manuscript §1: "The three utterances share a loose situation model while
differing in footing and illocutionary force. Preserving the proposition is
therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for preserving the communicative
event." CLAIM-061 in its first version grounded both halves on CASE-018 and
CASE-027, and said "necessity fails in CASE-018, read in reverse". The
necessity half is now CLAIM-486. This argument is the step from those
renderings to it.

## Critical questions

- **Is the case coherent?** Yes. Both cases are invented, and coherent as
  descriptions: one sentence said by four speakers (CASE-018), and Henley
  in the voice of an academic sociologist (CASE-027).
- **Does it separate the very things the conclusion says can come apart?**
  No. This is the question that fails. CLAIM-053 (2026-10-08): "Three
  renderings with a shared situation model and different acts show that
  sharing the proposition is not sufficient. They do not show that a
  rendering can keep the act while changing the proposition." CLAIM-229
  (2026-10-10) gives the logic: "Write P for 'the rendering keeps the
  proposition' and A for 'the rendering keeps the act'. Same words with a
  different act is a case of P without A, which refutes 'P is sufficient
  for A'. Necessity, 'A only if P', is refuted only by a case of A without
  P." Reading CASE-018 in reverse "is still the same words, so still P
  without A". CASE-027 is the sufficiency half too, as it says
  ("propositions held fixed, footing changed").

Rejected as an inference to CLAIM-486. The renderings support CLAIM-061's
sufficiency half, which is all CLAIM-061 now says, and CLAIM-229 does not
dispute that possibility.

## Other lines to the conclusion

The case against necessity lies outside the manuscript. CLAIM-053 placed it
in the exchange: A12's sentence ("you can alter many of the propositions
while preserving the speaker's attitude") and A24's compensation hypothesis
(CLAIM-091). The second route is ARG-tmpb1nnf. The documented case is
CASE-056, Phillips's "hearty handshake all round" for Paul's "holy kiss",
which CLAIM-486 grounds on directly. CASE-066's Garnett sentence bears only
weakly: "a producer's judgement of A without P, not a measured one"
(CLAIM-229's note of 2026-10-10). Neither rescues CASE-018. CASE-012's
refrains only illustrate CLAIM-061, and show "that such variation is
possible, not how readers judge it".
