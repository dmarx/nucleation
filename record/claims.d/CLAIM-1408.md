# CLAIM-1408: Extraneous load is what a context costs in attention without serving the task, for minds, models and records alike

## The claim

On [CLAIM-1360](CLAIM-1360.md), intrinsic load is a task's least peak. Extraneous load is
what presentation adds to the peak of the path actually taken. It is
material that must be read, held or filtered at some step, which no solution
needs. Split attention between a figure and text that could have been one,
redundant explanation, and irrelevant detail all raise the peak without
changing the task.

The record already prices this cost for itself, in other words.
- **[CLAIM-1403](CLAIM-1403.md):** an entry's tier is priced partly by "the attention it
  consumes in contexts that do not need it".
- **[CLAIM-1440](CLAIM-1440.md):** keeping an entry costs its retrieval, not only its
  storage. Retired entries still add to the cost of retrieval and lower
  discoverability.

Those are extraneous load, for the record's readers.

## The reader that makes it concrete

The record is read by a language model as often as by its owner. Everything
loaded into the model's context that the task does not need is extraneous
load on that reader. [THEORY-2316](../theory.d/THEORY-2316.md) (Gupta, Liu and Zou, read in full) finds
that presentation alone lowers models' accuracy with target and answer held
fixed, by up to about 37 points averaged over models. The costs grow with the
number and similarity of distractors, the cases that compete with the target
for attention. If that holds beyond their synthetic tasks, the record's
tiering question is cognitive load theory for its machine reader. Keeping a
superseded entry in view is a cost paid at every reading.

## What it does not say

That everything not needed now should be hidden. [CLAIM-1403](CLAIM-1403.md) also prices
the cost of recovering what was hidden, and germane load ([CLAIM-1450](CLAIM-1450.md)) can
make a cost now worth paying.
