# LIT-tmpry5n6: Rigid Origami Vertices: Conditions and Forcing Sets

Abel, Z., Cantarella, J., Demaine, E. D., Eppstein, D., Hull, T. C., Ku, J. S., Lang, R. J. & Tachi, T. (2016). Rigid Origami Vertices: Conditions and Forcing Sets. *Journal of Computational Geometry* 7(1): 171–184. arXiv:1507.01644.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpj2th4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpj2th4.md). Theorem numbers are the arXiv version's.*

- **Assigned vertices** (Theorem 1): "A single-vertex crease pattern (C, µ)
  can be continuously parameterized in a family of rigid origami folds if
  and only if (C, µ) contains a bird’s foot."
- **Unassigned vertices** (Theorem 2): rigidly foldable "if and only if C
  has at least four creases, all sector angles of C are strictly less than
  π, and C is not an unspecified cross."
- **Monotone in creases** (Lemma 2): "Single-vertex continuously
  parameterized rigid foldability is closed under adding creases."
- **Popping** (after Corollary 1): with tripods of both parities "it can be
  made to snap from a pops up folded state to a pops down one".
- **Open** (§5): "Finding a similar rigid-foldability result for
  multi-vertex crease patterns is an open problem."

## Standing in the record

Registered as a stand-in for Hull's chapter "Rigid Foldings"
([LIT-tmp8rseg](LIT-tmp8rseg.md)), which a paywall kept from being read. It stands in for the
chapter's "intrinsic condition" for a vertex to fold rigidly from flat, and
for nothing else there: not the reciprocal diagrams, the zero-area
theorem, infinitesimal or second-order rigidity, or the degree-4
fold-angle equations. Hull is a co-author. The pasted synthesis on
constraint architectures does not cite it. At the single-vertex level it
is what A's "chooses the intended branch" needs: popping up or down is
the two-branch choice that Chen and Santangelo and Berry et al. build on.
