# THEORY-tmpj60c9: Agency is relative to an observer: an identified system is an agent, for that observer, to the degree that a description of the function it optimises explains its behaviour better under the observer's prior than any description of its input-output mapping

## Source

Orseau, McGill & Legg (2018), "Agents and Devices: A Relative Definition of
Agency", [LIT-911](../literature.d/LIT-911.md), read in full in [NOTE-705](../notes.d/NOTE-705.md).

## Where the evidence stands

What the paper does and argues is [THEORY-624](THEORY-624.md), Active, which this entry
extends. The comparison is illustrated on seven toy gridworld trajectories,
with the prior chosen and not justified, and under a speed prior everything
may look like an agent. Kenton et al. ([THEORY-527](THEORY-527.md), [THEORY-801](THEORY-801.md)) object that a
compression test may count accidentally optimal systems as agents;
[QUESTION-228](../questions.d/QUESTION-228.md) asks for that test. The `promote_when` above is [THEORY-624](THEORY-624.md)'s condition before the twenty-fourth status review promoted it, carried here unchanged, because it asks what would make the thesis true and not what the source says.
