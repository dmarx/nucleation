# NOTE-431: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: Model, Processes and Outcomes

## Contribution

The paper sets out ACT's model of psychopathology and of change in one
place, with its philosophical root (functional contextualism) and its
basic-science root (Relational Frame Theory). It is the first review, by its
own account (p. 18), to ask whether two decades spent building that basic
science have produced "a progressive model of psychopathology and its
treatment". It answers with a review of four kinds of evidence:
correlational, experimental component, mediational, and outcome.

## Key insight

Behaviour therapy promised both validated treatments and treatments built on
basic principles. Cognitive behaviour therapy kept only the first, because
its cognitive concepts named things to change without naming the contexts a
clinician can manipulate. ACT tries to keep both. Its basic theory says
thoughts and feelings do not cause action except "as regulated by context"
(p. 5). So the clinical target is the context that links them to action, not
their content. Psychological problems are the narrowing that follows when
verbal rules and the avoidance of private experience govern behaviour more
than its actual consequences do.

## Assumptions

- **Functional contextualism (p. 5).** The goal is "the prediction and
  influence of events, with precision, scope and depth". Truth is
  workability, and causal analysis is limited to manipulable events.
- **RFT (pp. 5–6).** Human language and cognition is "the learned ability to
  arbitrarily relate events, mutually and in combination, and to change the
  functions of events based on these relations". Four implications follow
  for psychopathology (p. 6): (1) the normal verbal processes of problem
  solving underlie it and "cannot be eliminated"; (2) context controls the
  content and impact of cognitive networks; (3) networks are historical, so
  logical restriction or elimination "is generally not psychologically
  sound"; (4) direct change attempts on a node "tend to elaborate the
  network in that area and increase its functional importance".
- **Self-report measures of process.** The AAQ is the main process measure;
  the authors note it is not a pure measure of experiential avoidance but "a
  more general measure of several ACT processes" (p. 9).

## Key results

- **The inflexibility model (pp. 6–8).** Cognitive fusion is "excessive or
  improper regulation of behavior by verbal processes". It is fostered by
  contexts of literality, reason-giving and emotional control. It supports
  experiential avoidance, defined with the 1996 paper ([LIT-551](../literature.d/LIT-551.md)) as "the
  attempt to alter the form, frequency, or situational sensitivity of private
  events even when doing so causes behavioral harm". Avoidance is "due to the
  natural effects of human language", then amplified by a culture of
  "feeling good". It makes avoided events more salient, so it narrows the
  repertoire. The conceptualized past, future and self gain control, and
  values "take a backseat".
- **The six processes (pp. 7–9).** Acceptance (the alternative to
  experiential avoidance); cognitive defusion (changing a thought's
  functions, not its form, e.g. "I am having the thought that I am no good");
  being present; self as context (from deictic frames I–you, here–there,
  now–then); values ("chosen qualities of purposive action that can never
  be obtained as an object"); committed action. The first four "provide a
  workable behavioral definition of mindfulness" (p. 9).
- **Correlational evidence (pp. 9–11), in the manuscript's numbers.** 21
  studies, 4,721 participants, 52 correlations between the AAQ and outcomes.
  The overall effect is printed ".030 (95% confidence interval: 0.29 –
  0.32)", which cannot be right as printed (see Corrections). Sub-analyses:
  GHQ r = −.40, BDI −.50, SCL-90-R −.53, anxiety measures −.49. Bond and
  Bunce's two-wave panel: the AAQ predicted later GHQ and job performance,
  and not the reverse.
- **Component studies (pp. 11–13).** Defusion by word repetition lowered the
  discomfort and believability of negative self-referential thoughts more
  than distraction or thought control (N = 8, alternating treatments).
  Acceptance and defusion raised cold-pressor tolerance, though not reported
  pain (N = 32). In CO₂-inhalation studies, acceptance instructions reduced
  avoidance and raised willingness to return (Eifert & Heffner, N = 60;
  Levitt et al., N = 60, panic disorder).
- **Mediation (pp. 13–15).** Process measures met Baron–Kenny or MacKinnon
  criteria in worksite stress (AAQ, N = 90), diabetes self-management (AADQ,
  N = 81), smoking (AIS, N = 67; one-year abstinence 35% vs 15% for nicotine
  replacement), counsellor stigma and burnout (SAB, N = 93), and psychosis
  (believability, N = 29). A new reanalysis of Zettle and Hayes (1986, N =
  18): mid-treatment believability of depressive thoughts (ATQ-B) mediated
  ACT's advantage over cognitive therapy on the BDI and the Hamilton.
- **Outcomes (pp. 17–18, Table 2).** Weighted Cohen's d: within ACT 1.11 at
  post (N = 253) and 1.26 at follow-up (N = 210). Against manualized active
  treatments, .49 at post (N = 322) and .69 at follow-up (N = 218). Against
  wait list, TAU, education or placebo, .97 and 1.58. Against CT/CBT alone,
  .73 and .83, on N = 96 and 39.

## Concepts

- **psychological flexibility**: "the ability to contact the present moment
  more fully as a conscious human being, and to change or persist in behavior
  when doing so serves valued ends" (p. 7).
- **psychological inflexibility**: "an inability to persist or change in the
  service of long term valued ends" (p. 6).
- **cognitive fusion**: "excessive or improper regulation of behavior by
  verbal processes, such as rules and derived relational networks" (p. 6).
- **experiential avoidance**: as in [LIT-551](../literature.d/LIT-551.md), quoted above.
- **self as context**: a sense of self as "a locus or perspective", arising
  from deictic relations, which "is a context for verbal knowing, not the
  content of that knowing" (p. 8).
- **values**: chosen qualities of action, instantiated but never achieved;
  goals, by contrast, can be achieved (pp. 8–9).
- **third wave**: behavioural and cognitive therapies "particularly
  sensitive to the context and functions of psychological phenomena, not
  just their form" (p. 4, quoting Hayes 2004).

## Connections

- **Experiential avoidance ([LIT-551](../literature.d/LIT-551.md)), unread.** Its definition is
  quoted here, and its construct becomes one process of six. That is the
  `extends` relation on the LIT.
- **RFT ([LIT-549](../literature.d/LIT-549.md) unread; [LIT-541](../literature.d/LIT-541.md) read).** The basic theory ACT is
  "the applied extension" of (p. 5). The read 2004 paper states RFT more
  fully and gives RFT readings of three of the six processes.
- **Kashdan and Rottenberg ([LIT-553](../literature.d/LIT-553.md)), read.** They widen
  "psychological flexibility" beyond ACT and cite this paper's
  meta-analysis, giving it as 32 studies and r = .42, which is the published
  version, not this manuscript.
- **Macri and Rogge ([LIT-543](../literature.d/LIT-543.md)), unread.** A 2024 meta-analysis of the
  six processes as mechanisms. It is the test of C5 with eighteen more years
  of trials.
- **Salkovskis, Sighvatsson and Sigurdsson ([LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md))**, filed by
  another agent, argue that a treatment's success is not evidence for its
  theory, except when changing the theory's crucial factor fails to help.
  This review's mediational evidence is the kind of test that argument
  calls for.
- **Self-determination theory ([LIT-558](../literature.d/LIT-558.md), [LIT-559](../literature.d/LIT-559.md)).** Deci and
  Ryan's "rigid behavior patterns" that keep people "from dealing with their
  inner experiences" and "persist into new situations in which they are not
  needed" ([LIT-558](../literature.d/LIT-558.md), p. 250) describe experiential avoidance and
  inflexibility, but SDT traces them to thwarted psychological needs, not to
  fusion. ACT's values, "chosen qualities of purposive action", correspond to
  SDT's identified and integrated regulation ([LIT-559](../literature.d/LIT-559.md), pp. 72–73). This
  reading records no ACT distinction between values held from guilt or
  approval and values held volitionally, which is SDT's line between
  introjection and identification. The two transdiagnostic claims,
  [THEORY-050](../theory.d/THEORY-050.md) and [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md), are compatible.

## Bearing on the record

- **A candidate THEORY**: "psychological inflexibility, the dominance of
  verbal rules and experiential avoidance over direct contingencies, is a
  transdiagnostic process in psychopathology, and treatments that reduce it
  reduce distress". Sources: this paper, [LIT-553](../literature.d/LIT-553.md), and [LIT-543](../literature.d/LIT-543.md) once
  read. Status Proposed until the meta-analysis is read, since the evidence
  here is early and mostly self-report. See the batch report.
- **Against the network model ([LIT-547](../literature.d/LIT-547.md)).** This model puts one
  functional process behind many syndromes. Borsboom's network theory
  denies most disorders a common cause. The two make different predictions
  about whether reducing one process moves all symptoms. Neither paper
  addresses the other.

## Limitations

- **The manuscript, not the article.** See Corrections.
- **Allegiance.** All five authors are ACT developers or researchers, and
  most cited trials are by ACT researchers; the authors note "bias is
  possible" (p. 18).
- **Self-report processes.** The authors list the weaknesses themselves
  (p. 17): process measures without published psychometrics, measured after
  outcomes began to improve, and high in face validity.
- **Small trials.** Most outcome studies are small and short, which the
  authors put down to the programme's stage (p. 18).
- **The model is not tested as a model.** The six processes are reviewed one
  study at a time; no study tests the whole structure in Figure 1.

## Open questions

- Do the six processes separate empirically, or is there one inflexibility
  factor? The AAQ's single general factor (p. 9) leaves this open.
- Does inflexibility precede disorder, or follow it? One two-wave panel is
  the only evidence here.
- Does reducing fusion or avoidance in the laboratory produce the clinical
  changes? The authors call this the translational test the programme still
  needs (pp. 18–19).

## Corrections

- **This is an early manuscript.** It has unfilled citations: "trained
  perspective taking (xx)" and "Barnes-Holmes. Xxx 2003" (p. 6), "Sobel's
  method (19xx)" (p. 15), "Brown xxx" and "Baer xxx" (p. 11), "Herbert xxx
  2004" (p. 18). Its correlational meta-analysis has 21 studies and 4,721
  participants. Kashdan and Rottenberg, citing the published article, give
  "a meta-analysis of 32 studies" with a mean correlation of .42. The
  published numbers were not seen.
- **".030 (95% confidence interval: 0.29 – 0.32)"** (p. 10) is impossible
  as printed: the point estimate lies outside its own interval. The text
  around it calls the relation "moderate", so ".30" is the likely intended
  value. It is the manuscript's typo.
- **A copied statistic.** In the Zettle and Hayes reanalysis (p. 15), the
  mid-treatment comparisons for the BDI and the ATQ-B are both printed "F
  (1, 16) = 2.81, p = .12". The text says the first did not differ and the
  second did, so the ATQ-B statistic was evidently copied from the BDI line.
- **Repository metadata.** The GSU record lists the first author as
  "Hayes, Stephen C."; the manuscript and Crossref have Steven C. Hayes.
