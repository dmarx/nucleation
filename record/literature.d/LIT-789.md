# LIT-789: Translation Quality Assessment: Past and Present

Juliane House (2015), Abingdon and New York: Routledge, vii, 160 pp. —
DOI-10.4324/9781315752839

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read*: no lawful full text was reachable (see the history
note). What follows restates the publisher's description and the
catalogue's contents.

- The publisher's description: the book "makes explicit the grounds of
  judging the worth of a translation and emphasizes that translation is,
  at its core, a linguistic art"; it presents "a newly revised and
  presented model of translation quality assessment which, like its
  predecessor, relies on detailed textual and culturally informed
  contextual analysis and comparison", and its test cases show "two steps
  in translation evaluation: firstly analysis, description and
  explanation; secondly, judgements of value, socio-cultural relevance and
  appropriateness ... to judge is easy, to understand less so."
- Contents, from Crossref's chapter records: Translation theory and
  translation quality assessment (11–17); Different approaches to
  translation theory and translation quality assessment (18–30); The
  original House model (1977) (31–45); Implementation of the original
  model (46–63); Refining the original model on the basis of test cases
  (64–72); The revised House model (1997) (73–80); Implementation of the
  revised model: a test case (81–94); Contrastive pragmatics,
  intercultural communication and understanding, and cultural filtering
  (95–106); Globalization and cultural filtering (107–116); Corpus
  studies and the notion of genre (117–125); Cognitive translation-related
  research (126–133); Towards a new integrative model (134–153).
