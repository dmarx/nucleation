# CLAIM-315: In comparative anchoring tasks, considering an anchor shifts the later numerical estimate without adding evidence about the target, and the shift is carried by anchor-consistent knowledge made more accessible; the shift is established, the accessibility step is inferred and has not been measured

## The claim

This is the psychological form [CLAIM-298](CLAIM-298.md) cited, stated on its own so that
its warrant can be. In the comparative-question paradigm, judging whether
a quantity is higher or lower than an anchor pulls the later estimate
toward the anchor ([THEORY-337](../theory.d/THEORY-337.md)). The anchor is uninformative by design, so
the shift adds no evidence about the target. The selective-accessibility
account holds that the comparison makes anchor-consistent knowledge more
accessible and that this knowledge carries the shift ([THEORY-335](../theory.d/THEORY-335.md)).

The two parts have different standing. The shift is shown in five
preregistered experiments. The accessibility step is inferred from how
the effect varies with applicability, representativeness and
plausibility; its most direct test found nothing, with a task that also
missed direct priming, so the test failed rather than the account.

## What it does not say

- Anything about communication in general. That extension is [CLAIM-298](CLAIM-298.md)'s,
  and it is interpretive.
- That accessibility fixes the direction of the judgement. The account
  itself lets the same accessible knowledge produce contrast.
- That all anchoring runs through accessibility. Insufficient adjustment
  and response effects are rival or additional pathways.
