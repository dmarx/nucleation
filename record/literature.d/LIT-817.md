# LIT-817: On the Normal and the Pathological

Georges Canguilhem (1943; expanded 1966), *On the Normal and the
Pathological*, trans. Carolyn R. Fawcett with Robert S. Cohen, introduction
by Michel Foucault, Dordrecht: D. Reidel, 1978; reissued as *The Normal and
the Pathological*, New York: Zone Books, 1989 — DOI-10.1007/978-94-009-9853-7

## Key takeaways

*Registered from secondary description, not read.* What follows comes from
Crossref's chapter list for the English edition and from standard accounts
of the book.

- **Two essays.** The 1943 essay asks whether the pathological state is
  only a quantitative modification of the normal state (Comte's
  "Broussais's principle", Claude Bernard, René Leriche), and whether
  there are sciences of the normal and the pathological ("Norm and
  Average", "Disease, Cure, Health", "Physiology and Pathology"). The
  1963–66 reflections ("From the Social to the Vital", "On Organic Norms in
  Man", "A New Concept in Pathology: Error") extend it to social norms.
- **The normal is normative.** Its best-known claim is that health is not
  conformity to a fixed norm or an average, but the capacity to tolerate
  infractions of the usual norm and to institute new norms in new
  situations; disease is life under narrowed norms, not the absence of
  norms.
- **History of concepts.** It argues that the categories of normal and
  pathological, as nineteenth-century physiology and medicine defined
  them, were less objective than they appeared.
