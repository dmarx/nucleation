# LIT-717: Science as Social Knowledge: Values and Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry

Helen E. Longino (1990), *Science as Social Knowledge: Values and
Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry*, Princeton: Princeton University Press —
DOI-10.1515/9780691209753

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* What the record holds of its argument is what
Longino states of it in her SEP entry ([LIT-725](LIT-725.md)), §§4–5:

- The book "develops a contextual empiricism to analyze research in human
  evolution and in neuroendocrinology".
- Its starting point is an underdetermination argument: evidential
  relations are mediated by background assumptions, and the chain of
  justification ends in assumptions for which no evidence is available.
- The only check on the arbitrary dominance of preference among such
  assumptions is critical interaction among members of a community, which
  must meet norms of effectiveness to count as objective.

The four norms themselves are given in the entry; this record does not know
from the book how they were first stated or how they were revised in
[LIT-732](LIT-732.md).
