# THEORY-1466: Baird, McKenzie and Özler's structured review of cash transfers in low- and middle-income countries finds that transfers made without an employment focus leave prime-age adults' work and earnings little changed, that pensioners and, in one study, refugees do reduce work, and that transfers tied to job search or business start-up raise work and earnings, which the authors attribute chiefly to easing liquidity and risk constraints; they expect responses to differ where transfers are dependable and permanent

## Source

Baird, McKenzie & Özler (2018), [LIT-2189](../literature.d/LIT-2189.md); read as World Bank Policy
Research Working Paper 8404 in [NOTE-1684](../notes.d/NOTE-1684.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Government and charitable cash, remittances.** Banerjee et al.'s
  reanalysis of seven conditional-transfer RCTs finds no significant effect
  "either individually or pooled on employment or hours of work" (p. 7);
  GiveDirectly in Kenya, "no effects on labor supply or occupational
  choice" (p. 10); the reliable remittance studies "do not support the view
  that remittances make recipients lazy and work less" (p. 13). Any of
  these could have shown a reduction.
- **Reductions.** Pensioners: "large declines in labor force participation
  among the elderly themselves" (p. 9). Syrian refugees in Lebanon, one
  altitude-discontinuity study: adult labour supply down 13% (p. 11).
- **Employment-focused transfers.** Uganda's conditional start-up grants:
  "a 17% increase in work hours and 38% increase in earnings" (p. 15);
  Ethiopia's search subsidy, 6.7 points, significant at 10% (p. 14);
  graduation bundles, hours up 17% two years after support ended in
  Bangladesh (p. 17).
- **The authors' expectation** (p. 20): "the impacts are likely to be
  different when transfers are dependable and permanent rather than
  temporary and/or surprises".

## What this does not say

- That cash never reduces work: pensioners and the refugees do, and the
  income effect is "most apparent when transfers are large and/or
  prolonged" (p. 19).
- That the liquidity and insurance explanation is measured: it is inferred
  from the pattern, and Table 2 grades several channels as without
  evidence, scarring among them.
- That dependable, permanent, announced support leaves work unchanged:
  that is the case the base lacks, and the one [CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md) and
  [THEORY-1480](THEORY-1480.md) are about.
- Anything about states, ministries or projects: the evidence is
  household-level, and it removes the household analogy behind
  [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)'s "slack effort" without testing its state-level forms.
- That the effects persist: few studies measure after transfers stop, and
  small Kenyan grants faded.
