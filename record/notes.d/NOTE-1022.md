# NOTE-1022: Giri, Kwon and Novack — The L³-based strong Onsager theorem

## Contribution

Isett ([LIT-1255](../literature.d/LIT-1255.md)) and Buckmaster, De Lellis, Székelyhidi and Vicol
([LIT-1300](../literature.d/LIT-1300.md)) settled the negative half of Onsager's conjecture for the
total energy, in Hölder spaces. Whether rough solutions below 1/3 can
satisfy the local energy inequality, the condition that limits of suitable
Navier–Stokes solutions satisfy, was open except for β < 1/7 (De Lellis and
Kwon, Hölder). This paper settles it in the L³-based scale: for every
β < 1/3 there are weak solutions in C⁰_t(B^β_{3,∞} ∩ L^{1/(1−3β)}) with
D[u] ≥ 0 and strictly decreasing total energy. The method is new: an
intermittent convex integration measured in L³, with velocity increments
that overlap in frequency.

## Key insight

The local energy inequality brings in a "current" error, and in a
homogeneous (Hölder) scheme its Nash term caps the exponent at 1/4. Making
the building blocks intermittent, concentrated on thin pipes, weakens that
error by a factor r^{2/3} in L¹. With the right amount of intermittency
(r_q = λ_{q+n̄/2}/λ_{q+n̄}, the "Goldilocks" ratio) the cap rises to 1/3.
Keeping that ratio at every step forces each increment to reach well above
the frequency of the error it corrects. Increments of different steps then
overlap in frequency, which homogeneous schemes forbid. The paper restores
the separation in space: new pipes are bundled and placed to dodge all
recent ones. The local energy balance itself is held exact at every step,
with a fixed dissipation term −E carried along, so the limit's
Duchon–Robert measure is whatever E was at the start.

## Assumptions

- **Domain.** The periodic box T³ = [−π, π]³, a finite interval [0, T]. No
  initial-value problem: the datum is an output.
- **Notion of solution.** Distributional solutions of
  ∂_t u + (u·∇)u + ∇p = 0, div u = 0, with u ∈ C⁰_t L³ and, from the
  proof, p ∈ C⁰_t L^{3/2}.
- **Regularity class.** C⁰([0, T]; B^β_{3,∞} ∩ L^{1/(1−3β)}): L³-Besov in
  space, continuous in time, not Hölder and not shown bounded. The abstract's
  C⁰_t(W^{1/3−,3} ∩ L^{∞−}) is the same statement as β → 1/3.
- **Local energy inequality (1.6).** ∂_t(½|u|²) + div((½|u|² + p)u) =
  −D[u] ≤ 0 in distributions, D[u] the Duchon–Robert measure (1.5).
- **Exponent.** §2.1 fixes β ∈ [1/7, 1/3), Proposition 2.11 says
  β ∈ (0, 1/3); either way the proof of Theorem 1.1 runs the iteration at
  some β̄ ∈ (β, 1/3) close to 1/3, so smaller β come for free.

## Key results

- **Theorem 1.1.** For any β ∈ (0, 1/3) there are weak solutions in
  C⁰([0, T]; B^β_{3,∞} ∩ L^{1/(1−3β)}) that satisfy (1.6) and dissipate the
  total kinetic energy.
- **Proposition 2.11 (the iteration).** Given (u_q, p_q, R_q, φ_q, −π_q)
  satisfying the Euler–Reynolds system
  ∂_t u_q + div(u_q⊗u_q) + ∇p_q = div(R_q − π_q Id) and the relaxed local
  energy identity
  ∂_t(½|u_q|²) + div((½|u_q|² + p_q)u_q) = (∂_t + û_q·∇)κ_q +
  div((R_q − π_q Id)û_q) + div φ_q − E (2.8), with κ_q = ½tr(R_q − π_q Id)
  and the many inductive bounds of §§2.3–2.6, there is a next step with the
  same E. *Holds when:* n̄ and b satisfy (2.1)–(2.2) and a ≥ a*.
- **The limiting identity (Step 2 of the proof).** With the base case below,
  ∂_t(½|u|²) + div((½|u|² + p)u) = e(t)e′(t)|ρ₀|² ≤ 0, so
  D[u] = −e e′ρ₀² = τ₀⁻¹e(t)²ρ₀(x₁, x₂)². "In particular, the strict local
  energy inequality holds in the interior of supp(ρ₀), which leads to the
  total kinetic energy dissipation."
- **Base case (Step 1).** u₀ = e(t)ρ₀(x₁, x₂)e₃ with
  e(t) = c·exp(−τ₀⁻¹(t + 1)) with c a small constant built from the
  parameters (Γ_{n̄}^{−100}δ_{2n̄} times an intermittency factor); ρ₀ a pipe flow along e₃
  of thickness λ_{n̄−1}⁻¹, periodic, written as a d-fold divergence;
  R₀ trace-free with ∂_t u₀ = div R₀; p₀ = φ₀ = 0; π₀ = Σ_k Γ_kδ_{k+n̄}, a
  constant. Checked here.
- **Regularity bounds.** ‖ŵ_q‖_{C⁰B^{β′}_{3,∞}} ≤ λ_q^{−β+(b−1)/2+β′} (2.47),
  summable for β′ < β̄ − (b − 1)/2; ‖ŵ_q‖_{L^p} summable for p < p*(β̄, b)
  (2.48), which (2.46) makes larger than 1/(1 − 3β).
- **Heuristic thresholds (§1.2).** Hölder Nash error: β ≤ 1/(2b + 1).
  Homogeneous Nash current error: β ≤ 1/(3b + 1) → 1/4. Intermittent L³
  Nash current error: β ≤ (b + 3)/(3(3b + 1)) → 1/3. Recomputed here.

## Method

1. Fix β̄ ∈ (β, 1/3), n̄ ∈ 6N large and b close to 1 (2.1)–(2.2), (2.46).
   Frequencies λ_q ≈ a^{b^q}, amplitudes δ_q = λ_q^{−2β̄}, intermittency
   r_q = λ_{q+n̄/2}Γ_q/λ_{q+n̄}.
2. Start from the pipe flow u₀ = e(t)ρ₀e₃, which sets E.
3. At step q, correct only the lowest-frequency errors R_q^q, φ_q^q with
   ŵ_{q+n̄} = Σ a(∇û_q, φ_q^q) B_φ∘Φ + Σ a(∇û_q, φ_q^q, R_q^q) B_R∘Φ: the
   current part takes amplitude (−2φ_q^q)^{1/3}, the stress part a matrix
   square root built from r_q^{1/3}(−2φ_q^q)^{1/3} and the negative-definite
   R_q^q − π_q^q Id (1.18). The B are
   intermittent Mikado bundles, thin intermittent pipes at frequencies
   between λ_{q+n̄/2} and λ_{q+n̄} multiplied by a thicker "bundling pipe" at
   λ_{q+1}, transported by local flow maps Φ of û_q, cut off in space-time
   by velocity, pressure and checkerboard cutoffs.
4. Place each bundle to dodge every increment of the last n̄ steps
   (Lemmas 4.1–4.2), so cross terms with u_q − û_q vanish.
5. Sort the new stress and current errors into frequency bins
   λ_{q+1}, …, λ_{q+n̄} with a synthetic, compactly supported Littlewood–Paley
   decomposition, invert the divergence locally, and absorb the time-only
   means m(t) into the stress as (2/3)m Id.
6. Build the new intermittent pressure π_{q+1} from all the pressure
   increments, including "anticipated" rescaled copies, so that π dominates
   every error and its derivatives pointwise (Section 6).
7. In the limit R_q, π_q → 0 in C⁰L^{3/2} and φ_q → 0 in C⁰L¹, and (2.8)
   becomes (1.4) with D[u] = E.

## Concepts

- **Duchon–Robert measure** D[u]: the distribution defined by (1.5), equal
  to minus the left side of the local energy balance; a non-negative D[u] is
  the local energy inequality.
- **L³-based strong Onsager conjecture**: (a) local energy equality above 1/3
  in C⁰_tB^β_{3,∞}; (b) below 1/3, solutions in that class with the local
  inequality and dissipated total energy.
- **Relaxed local energy inequality / identity**: (1.11), (2.8), the energy
  analogue of the Euler–Reynolds system, with a current error φ_q.
- **Goldilocks intermittency**: r_q = (λ_q/λ_{q+1})^{1/2} in Novack and
  Vicol's scheme, here r_q = λ_{q+n̄/2}/λ_{q+n̄}.
- **Partial wavelet sum**: u_q = û_q + ŵ_{q+1} + … + ŵ_{q+n̄−1}, increments
  overlapping in frequency, unlike a partial Fourier sum.
- **Intermittent Mikado bundle**: a thin intermittent pipe flow multiplied by
  a thicker, mildly intermittent "bundling pipe" whose placement can be
  shifted.
- **Dodging**: choosing supports of new increments disjoint from those of
  recent ones.
- **Intermittent pressure** π_q: a positive scalar such that π_q Id − R_q is
  positive definite and that bounds all errors pointwise.

## Connections

The scheme extends Novack and Vicol's intermittent Onsager theorem (C⁰_t
H^{1/2−}, non-conservative) and the intermittent Navier–Stokes and Euler
schemes of Buckmaster, Vicol and others, with Modena and Székelyhidi's
intermittent Mikado flows. The earlier local-inequality results are De
Lellis and Székelyhidi's (admissible non-uniqueness), Isett's strong C⁰
conjecture and globally dissipative continuous flows, and De Lellis and
Kwon's Hölder β < 1/7. The positive half, local energy equality above 1/3, is
Duchon and Robert's, after Constantin, E and Titi ([LIT-1248](../literature.d/LIT-1248.md)) and Eyink.
The Hölder negative half is Isett ([LIT-1255](../literature.d/LIT-1255.md)) and Buckmaster, De Lellis,
Székelyhidi and Vicol ([LIT-1300](../literature.d/LIT-1300.md)). The technical core, Section 3, is
the authors' companion paper, "A wavelet-inspired L³-based convex
integration framework for the Euler equations" (Annals of PDE 10:19, 2024),
not held.

## Limitations

- **The theorem says less than the proof.** Theorem 1.1 states D[u] ≥ 0 and
  dissipation. That D[u] equals the prescribed E is in the proof (footnote
  6, Step 2). A reader of the statement alone would not learn where the
  dissipation is.
- **"Strict local energy inequality … in the interior of supp(ρ₀)".**
  Strict as a measure on open sets, not pointwise, since ρ₀ vanishes inside
  its support.
- **A slip in the introduction.** De Lellis and Kwon's solutions are said to
  satisfy "the strict local energy inequality (1.9)". (1.9) is the
  Navier–Stokes inequality; (1.6) is meant.
- **Not self-contained.** Section 3 quotes its results from the companion
  paper, whose errata the authors cite, and Section 4 leaves the general
  pipe direction "to the reader".
- **Scope.** Torus, finite time, not Hölder or bounded, no initial datum, no
  uniqueness or non-uniqueness for given data, nothing about Navier–Stokes,
  β = 1/3 not reached. Only one dissipation density is constructed.

## Open questions

- Hölder solutions, C^β with β < 1/3, satisfying the local inequality:
  Isett's strong C⁰ Onsager conjecture. Open, per the paper.
- Which dissipation densities E can be prescribed? Proposition 2.11 accepts
  any continuous E ≥ 0 with a starting triple. A result building such triples
  for general E, for instance concentrated on thin or fractal sets, would
  say whether the place of dissipation is as free as the total profile in
  [THEORY-776](../theory.d/THEORY-776.md).
- Is the flux one-signed at small positive scales, or only in the limit?
  Only a pointwise or scale-resolved estimate on −τ_ℓ : ∇u^ℓ would say.
- Are any such solutions limits of Navier–Stokes as ν → 0? Only a theorem
  about Navier–Stokes would settle it.
