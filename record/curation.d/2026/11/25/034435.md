LEAD-215's reading of Martin-Löf (1984), *Intuitionistic Type Theory*,
notes by Giovanni Sambin, at the owner's request. An earlier delegated
reader stopped at a usage limit and left nothing usable; this pass started
fresh, in a worker that read and filed the book itself on branch
`claude/read-martin-lof-1984`, following the read-and-file skill. The
brief asked five questions: (a) types as problems or specifications and
their elements as solutions or realizations; (b) propositions as sets;
(c) the four forms of judgement, and whether "a is an element of A" against
"A is a set" tracks TERM-152's realization against specification; (d)
universes, and what a change of type would mean for revising a question;
(e) Σ-types as witness and evidence pairs (TERM-154, TERM-153).

"(Optional) Per Martin-Lof. Intuitionistic Type Theory. (The Padova
Lectures) 1980" from Karl Crary's course "Classic Papers in Programming
Languages and Logic" (CMU 15-819, Fall 2009): a course copy, lawful under
ADR-113. It is a 100-page image scan of the typescript, with the preface
of January 1984 and the book's pagination, ix + 91, which matches W. A.
Howard's review (Journal of Symbolic Logic, DOI 10.2307/2273925, found
through OpenAlex, queried without any email address). No text layer;
OCR'd with tesseract 5.3.4 at 300 dpi. The downloaded files stay in the
scratchpad, outside git. Every quotation leaned on was checked against the
page images; two of the original's misspellings ("elemnt", p. 90;
"toghether", p. 53) are kept with [sic]. No other host was tried, and no
check, paywall or refusal was met.
