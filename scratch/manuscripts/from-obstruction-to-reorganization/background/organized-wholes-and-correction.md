Source material for the manuscript's first part. It covers the line
`distributed-agency` (SYNTHESIS-047 to SYNTHESIS-057, with SYNTHESIS-049 as
the line's own account) and the line `organizational-normativity`

Conventions. "Status" is the entry's front-matter status. For a CLAIM the

assistant's turn in a filed exchange, "U*n*" the owner's, as the record cites
them. Where a central entry's first line is not one of the two digested here
(for example CLAIM-099, CLAIM-349 and CLAIM-422 in will-organization, CLAIM-685
in dependence-and-domination), that is said.

## 1. Core concepts (TERMs)

**TERM-074, organization (Active).** "'Organization' in this record means one
of the following, and a claim should say which": (1) *relational
arrangement*, "A configuration of relations among distinguishable processes,
components, roles or constraints, whose arrangement matters to some specified
operation or outcome", the default sense and the sense of "organizational
perspective"; (2) *organizational maintenance*, "The activities that reproduce
or change an arrangement", a separate fact "because an arrangement can be
maintained from outside"; (3) *collective institution*, "a corporation, a
university, an agency, a federation", whose "persistence is not the persistence
of any one perspective or arrangement within it" (CASE-133: "A party can stay
legally continuous while its coalition and orientation change"); (4)
*organizational closure*, TERM-070's closure of constraints. SYNTHESIS-038
takes sense 1 as the default for the normative line, and CLAIM-685 declares
sense 3.

**TERM-071, persistence of an organization (Active).** Five senses, "and a
claim should say which": state (a collective variable changes slowly),
dynamical (characteristic responses continue), historical ("The organization
that produces the collective is causally transmitted across successive
populations of members"), viable (trajectories satisfying a constraint set
remain available; stability does not entail it, CLAIM-079), and reinstatable
("The capacity to reinstate an organization after inactivity, not its
continuous running (CLAIM-287). It can branch, and so is not identity").

**TERM-084, descent (Active).** Separates provenance, lineage, common ancestry
and causal descent; lineage is "TERM-071's historical persistence, and what
CLAIM-329 says persistence through total turnover consists in" (as quoted in
SYNTHESIS-047).

**TERM-072, perspective (Active; first line evidence-for-experience).**
Organizational, represented or phenomenal. Sense 1 is "a dynamically realized
regime" (CLAIM-421).

**TERM-064, legibility (Active).** "legibility, of a distinction to an
observer for a task: whether the observer, with the rules, categories and
resources it actually has, can recover the distinction from what it is given
and bring it to bear on the task; a relation among a representation, an
observer's rule class, its resources and a task, bounded above by what the
source makes possible for an ideal receiver, and directed". Its formal core is
CLAIM-199's usable information; with X = (X₁, X₂) fair and independent and
Z = X₁ ⊕ X₂, a one-coordinate receiver gets Z at accuracy 1/2 from X and
exactly from V = X₁ ⊕ X₂, so "legibility need not be monotone in informational
refinement for bounded observers". One operationalization is
L_{o,𝒬,B}(T) = −inf_{π∈Π_{o,B}} E[ℓ_𝒬(J, π(T(X)))], with the caution that
"the loss function and task family must be specified independently; otherwise
whoever controls the scoring criterion also controls what counts as legible."
The *legibility regime* is "the maintained organization of observation
channels, classifications, attention and interpretive procedures through which
distinctions become accessible and usable to specified observers", wider than
Π_{o,B}. The *direction*: "Society legible to an institution" against
"Institution legible to society", which "can vary independently". Legibility
is not truth, information preservation, observability, salience, credibility,
practical authority or transparency (A22 §8's table), and "The party
benefiting from increased legibility need not be the party paying to become
legible." Its note of 2026-10-29 adds a step after uptake: "A judgment can be
legible and taken up and still not be executed" (Cover). Its note of
2026-11-15 records that concept-erasure readings support the relational form
("A concept is 'erased' only for some class of observers") and leaves open
that "a strong enough observer may supply a distinction rather than recover
it".

**TERM-085, correctability (Active).** "the chance that the arrangement,
starting from the description of the problem it actually has, finds,
authorizes, coordinates and carries out a plan achieving the objective before
the deadline and within its resources". Its note of 2026-10-31 records that
correctability is bounded by legibility, "and it is not filed as a new claim"
(SYNTHESIS-056).

**TERM-055, operational availability (Active).** "the chance that an
agent–environment system, given the cue it actually has, deploys the resource
for the task before the deadline". It "separates the deployability of a
resource from its presence and from knowing it is present (CLAIM-296)"
(SYNTHESIS-057).

**TERM-103, conferral (Active), and TERM-101, grant (Active).** Conferral is
"any effect one process has on another's share of future influence, of which
an explicit vote is one case; negative conferral withdraws influence", keeping
apart "the authority a process holds, what it confers on others, and how far
its preferred policies are realized". A grant is conferral that "runs through
a maintained relation of granting, delegation, eligibility or support";
"Suppressing j's rivals confers influence on j and grants j nothing"
(SYNTHESIS-050).

**TERM-023, will-organization (Active; will-organization line).** "the
maintained organization of relations governing the conditions under which an
agent's available action-generating regimes acquire, retain, lose, or alter
practical efficacy."

**TERM-020, protective lock-in (Active; will-organization line).** A
disturbance activates a protective regime, which reorganizes activity,
generating conditions "that support continued activation", so that
"continued activation further reduces the practical capacity to leave or
revise it"; "Nothing in this sequence implies that the initial intervention
was mistaken."

**TERM-127, responsibility (Proposed).** Causal, attributability, culpability,
remedial and constitutive senses; "a verdict in one sense does not settle
another" (SYNTHESIS-104).

**TERM-128, normative governability (Proposed; organizational-normativity).**
"its maintained, role-appropriate capacity to recognize and respond to
justified normative considerations, including considerations that require
revising its own governing mechanisms; relational, and not corrigibility,
compliance or obedience to challengers."

**TERM-086, organizational achievement (Proposed).** The attainment or
maintenance of a target to which "an identifiable organization of component
activities makes an explanatory causal contribution", as episode, maintained
capacity or reproduction; whether revising a capacity is a fourth mode is
recorded as open (SYNTHESIS-055).

**TERM-088, senses of individuation (Active).** Descriptive, dynamical,
constitutive and organizational autonomy: "The senses are not stages, and not
degrees of existence" (SYNTHESIS-048).

## 2. Central theses (CLAIMs)

### 2.1 What an organized whole is, and its direction against its members'

- **CLAIM-030** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on none; grounds
  CASE-138, THEORY-725, THEORY-127): "An organized whole's operative intention
  is not the expectation of its members' intentions; it is produced by
  systemic mechanisms that can evolve apart from what the members intend."
  Its defeater is a collective "whose operative direction is reliably
  predicted by aggregating its members' intentions across changes in its
  decision structure"; it does not say "that the whole is an agent", which is
  QUESTION-009 (SYNTHESIS-052).
- **CLAIM-271** (Proposed; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-030): "A collective's
  reasons need not be any of its members' reasons, so aggregating members'
  conclusions, as polling does, can yield verdicts the collective's own
  reasons do not support" (SYNTHESIS-052).
- **CLAIM-332** (Proposed; interpretive): a diffuse population can have
  collective causal efficacy without collective intention, "the distinction
  between having effects as a collective and acting as a collective agent";
  its "one case does not show the third rung" (SYNTHESIS-052).
- **CLAIM-060** (Active; thesis; interpretive; grounds CASE-172): "An
  encompassing organization's success does not establish the well-being,
  viability or autonomy of its constituents — and the converse holds too."
- **CLAIM-348** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-350, CLAIM-152,
  CLAIM-060; grounds CASE-110): "a description adequate for an institution's
  performance can merge states its members' claims must distinguish, so
  adequacy is fixed by the evaluative target, and the target cannot be
  justified solely by the description that preserves it." Its body disclaims
  logical novelty: "It is CLAIM-152 and CLAIM-060 together. What the exchange
  added is the coarse-graining form and the clause against circularity"
  (SYNTHESIS-056).
- **CLAIM-418** (Active; thesis; interpretive): "Possessing, enacting or being
  shaped by an organizational perspective does not establish an independent
  agent, and perspectival, agential and institutional boundaries need not
  coincide: several agents can share a perspective, one agent can act through
  several, and a perspective can be reproduced with no agent governing it."
- **CLAIM-417** (Proposed; thesis; interpretive): what the record explained by
  "self" in its dispositional, persisting, self-governing and integrated
  senses "is better explained by organizational perspectives". It is held back
  because habitus and transposable schemas "may explain the same transitions,
  and no model in the record out-predicts them at matched complexity"; ARG-025
  (Proposed) concludes it, with the verdict "The third grade is still unmet"
  (SYNTHESIS-056).
- **CLAIM-454** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-135, CLAIM-414):
  "A latent feature or mode recovered from an organization's members' activity
  is at most a constituent of an organizational perspective, not the
  perspective", since whether modes form one perspective "depends on the
  relations of access, uptake and authority among their carriers".
- **CLAIM-327** (Proposed; interpretive): a slow collective variable is part of
  a collective's dynamics "when its members read it, condition what they do
  on it, and by doing so regenerate it"; CLAIM-331 (Active): "slowness is a
  mark of a level and not its definition" (SYNTHESIS-047).
- **CLAIM-423** (Proposed; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-331): components
  changing at different speeds differ in "which forms of influence each can
  accumulate, exercise and keep", so a state can stay continuous while "the
  organization producing its positions changes under it".

### 2.2 Authority, scoring and self-confirmation

- **CLAIM-277** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-282; grounds
  CASE-182): "trust can be the temporal regularity of grants that policies
  earn by being right often, and the same loop entrenches whatever gained
  influence early"; it gives no criterion "for when entrenched authority is
  capture and not merited trust" (SYNTHESIS-050).
- **CLAIM-297** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-277, CLAIM-275):
  "When an operator's authority is updated by how well it performs on the
  interpretation it helped make dominant, the coupled meaning and influence of
  an organization can amplify an initial interpretive advantage without any
  gain in fidelity to independent evidence."
- **CLAIM-342** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-330, CLAIM-141;
  objects_to CLAIM-339): "What keeps a judgement–observation loop from
  confirming itself is evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass
  through the interpretation it tests; action is one source of such evidence,
  not a necessary one, and a loop that selects its own evidence can lock in
  with action available."
- **CLAIM-404** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-330, CLAIM-340,
  CLAIM-342, CLAIM-348, CLAIM-410): "An organization can be governed from
  outside without any of its decisions being made for it, through the evidence
  its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh
  evidence; the two routes are distinct", and the loop "can close between the
  two institutions, so that formal independence of the evaluator's procedure
  does not make its evaluation independent."
- **CLAIM-402** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-342; first line
  pragmatic-transport): a category's predictive success warrants it as a real
  kind "only when the evidence in those contexts does not pass through the
  category's own use", since "a category that speakers or institutions act on
  can produce the regularities that confirm it".
- **CLAIM-406** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-277, CLAIM-330):
  structural pre-emptive testimonial injustice "has the form of the record's
  trust loop", but "the loop alone cannot tell perpetuated privilege from
  merited expertise".

### 2.3 Competition over tests

- **CLAIM-416** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on none; grounds
  CASE-130): "Competing perspectives compete over the tests that measure them
  as well as under them: success under an evaluative regime is evidence of
  competence relative to that regime, a fair test need not be neutral across
  competences, and a perspective that gains influence over the test can change
  what later success shows." Its three parts are relativity,
  non-neutrality and reflexivity. A40: "Success under an evaluative regime is
  evidence of competence relative to that regime. It does not by itself
  establish that the regime adequately measures what matters." A37: "Being
  effective under a test is not the same as possessing authority to determine
  what the test should measure." It extends to Challenger (CASE-138), where
  "outcomes evaluated under particular conditions can be overgeneralized into
  claims about the competence of the organization itself". It does not say
  "that tests are unfair or that rules are changed in order to entrench".

### 2.4 Legibility, uptake and the achievements of correction

- **CLAIM-349** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-025, CLAIM-027;
  first line will-organization): "An organization's access to a signal, the
  signal's authority over action, and action on it are separable, and adaptive
  regulation keeps the first while making the second conditional on evidence;
  overcontrol, which buys freedom from harmful acts by cutting access or making
  inhibition unconditional, is a maladaptive regime and not more control". Its
  ordinal form: with R(π) = {c ∈ C : π(c) ∈ A(c)}, π₁ ⪰_flex π₂ exactly when
  R(π₂) ⊆ R(π₁); "a policy that has cut the signal has made more contexts
  indistinguishable." A42: "The organization may appear disciplined while
  losing the information and flexibility necessary to remain effective." It
  "does not say less control is better".
- **CLAIM-403** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-349; grounds
  THEORY-327): "A contribution's admission to an organization's inquiry, its
  recognition in apt terms, its credibility, its uptake into judgements, and
  its contributor's part in revising the standards of uptake are separable,
  each possible without the later ones, so an organization can widen access
  while every channel by which a less authoritative contribution could change
  the criteria of authority stays closed." Its constructive point (A24):
  "how an organization maintains **channels through which less authoritative
  contributions can change the criteria of authority itself**". It does not say
  "That every contribution is owed uptake" (A26: "we must also distinguish
  *uptake* from *obedience*"), nor that the rungs are ordered stages. Notes add
  attribution as "a separate axis, not as a sixth rung" (Bencherki,
  "Usurpation is uptake without attribution") and corroboration from Arnstein
  1969 and Stasser and Titus.
- **CLAIM-455** (Active; thesis; demonstrated; grounds CASE-154): one
  transformation can make one task's distinction legible and another's
  illegible for the same bounded observer, "so legibility regimes are ordered
  only relative to a task, and a regime that improves one perspective's
  competence can disable another's".
- **CLAIM-535** (Active; thesis; demonstrated; rests_on CLAIM-350; first line
  will-organization): a policy acceptable in every circumstance exists
  "exactly when, for each observation, the circumstances it cannot tell apart
  share an acceptable action", so "which distinctions must be made legible
  depends on which acceptable action the organization commits to." CLAIM-622
  (Proposed; demonstrated) rests on it: "a difference in the distinctions two
  perspectives draw is not by itself evidence that either is deficient".
- **CLAIM-448** (Active; counter; interpretive; objects_to CLAIM-440): "where
  an organization's legibility regime selects which contests are admitted or
  recorded, an acyclic record, even a persistent potential, can be the product
  of selective contestation, so observed coherence is not evidence that the
  organization's perspectives are integrated, and an intervention that
  improves its self-governance may at first increase the disagreement
  observed."
- **CLAIM-523** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on none; uses TERM-064,
  TERM-023, TERM-085; first line will-organization): "For a normatively
  relevant discrepancy to change what an organization does, three distinct
  achievements are needed and any can fail while the others hold:
  legibility ... volitional efficacy ... and correctability ...; none
  establishes that the demand or the revision is justified". It defines
  *normative efficacy* as "the realized ability of a normatively relevant
  distinction to change an organization's behaviour or governing standards",
  and separates 𝓗_exec(O, W, Λ; B, T), the histories the agent can realize,
  from 𝓗_N, those satisfying a standard: "A nonempty intersection does not
  show the agent will choose such a history" and "An empty intersection does
  not show the agent is beyond criticism. Its earlier actions may have
  destroyed the capacities required." It is "CLAIM-349's split of access,
  authority and action, carried from an organization's own signals to
  normative demands." A note records a way the three can fail together: "the
  classification that makes a demand recognizable can also be the reason it
  is refused" (Bencherki's newsletter).
- **CLAIM-732** (Proposed; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-523; lines
  dependence-and-domination, will-organization, rightful-power): "A protected
  objection is effective only if six achievements hold together, its becoming
  formulable, legible, taken up by an authorized channel, sustainable by the
  objector, corrected and maintained", so "its chance of success is no greater
  than that of its weakest stage, and a reform that raises one stage's success
  can lower the whole". A81 §2: "**The reliability of the complete corrective
  process cannot exceed the reliability of any genuinely necessary
  component.**" Its note of 2026-11-18: "The record holds no instance of a
  reform that raised one stage's success and lowered the whole".
- **CLAIM-296** (Active; thesis; interpretive): "Possessing a resource,
  possessing information about that resource, and possessing the capability to
  deploy it are distinct conditions"; CLAIM-312 (Active; interpretive; rests_on
  CLAIM-311): "a search that fails by the deadline establishes unavailability
  outright", while absence needs a certificate "from exhaustion, from an
  invariant of the arrangement, or from a closed-world default"
  (SYNTHESIS-057).

### 2.5 Correction across institutions

- **CLAIM-462** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-422, CLAIM-099;
  uses TERM-085): "An arrangement of institutions can keep, or increase, its
  capacity for effective action while its capacity to be effectively corrected
  by others degrades, because the two are maintained by different relations,
  so the first is no evidence of the second."
- **CLAIM-464** (Active; thesis; interpretive; grounds CASE-170, CASE-175,
  CASE-176, CASE-111): "An intervention can change what it would take to
  correct it: by altering the distribution of control, access, resources or
  enforcement before it is reviewed, it can make a specified corrective
  outcome costlier or unreachable while the standards that govern that outcome
  stay the same." Its three mechanisms are physical irreversibility (CASE-170),
  enforcement-mediated entrenchment (CASE-176) and jurisdictional displacement
  (CASE-175) (SYNTHESIS-056).
- **CLAIM-463** (Active; thesis; demonstrated; grounds CASE-111): "Restoring
  the conditions under which a capacity was lost need not restore the
  capacity", since where maintaining activity reinforces itself "the same
  conditions can sustain both the capacity and its absence", and "restoring it
  can cost more than preserving it would have."
- **CLAIM-457** (Active; thesis; demonstrated; grounds CASE-175): "a true
  statement of unilateral inability is not a statement that the outcome is
  unattainable."

### 2.6 Institutional correction and learning

- **CLAIM-422** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-025; grounds
  CASE-138, CASE-135; first line will-organization): "A successful corrective
  act, an executable procedure and a maintained organizational capacity are
  three different things, so an organization can record a correction, even
  rewrite its rules, without the corrected capacity being maintained." A24:
  "A change in the archive of rules is not automatically a change in the
  collective dispositions that those rules are meant to maintain." A25:
  "Institutional learning is not complete when an organization changes its
  rules." It adds that "the record of a transformation is a fourth thing, and
  the one most easily mistaken for the third." The claim notes GAO's narrower
  finding: NASA "counted how often lessons were accessed, not how often they
  were applied".
- **CLAIM-099** (Active; thesis; interpretive; uses TERM-023, TERM-020; first
  line will-organization; concluded by ARG-007 from CLAIM-079 and CLAIM-025):
  "Maintaining a regime of effective action and maintaining the capacity to
  govern among regimes are not the same achievement." "An agent may succeed at
  the first while progressively losing the second."
- **CLAIM-025** (Active; thesis; interpretive; first line will-organization):
  "An operative regime, the governing organization among regimes, and the
  capacity to transform that organization can persist or fail separately."
  CLAIM-099, CLAIM-284, CLAIM-349, CLAIM-359, CLAIM-422, CLAIM-424 and
  CLAIM-485 all rest on it.
- **CLAIM-520** (Active; thesis; demonstrated): "The value of keeping a
  corrective capacity is conditional on objectives, uncertainty and cost": an
  audit costing C over k decisions with error rate ε is worth
  "k(0.25 − 0.5ε) − C in the exchange's model", and "where no hypothesis fits
  the world, correction gains no reward and improves only calibration".
- **CLAIM-511** (Proposed; **disclaimed**; interpretive): "A self-maintaining
  organization ought to preserve its corrigibility." ARG-042 (Rejected) found
  the record's constructions do not establish it, quoting A24: "We shouldn't
  quietly convert a proof of capacity into a proof of value" (SYNTHESIS-050,
  SYNTHESIS-091).

### 2.7 Keeping or losing the capacity to revise one's own standards

- **CLAIM-284** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-025): "A self,
  in the self-governing sense, is a historically continuous organization of
  processes that takes part in governing the conditions of its own
  persistence, differentiation and transformation, not merely one that
  persists."
- **CLAIM-485** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-025; answers
  QUESTION-092): "what endogenous governance adds over a behaviourally
  equivalent system with an external scheduler is not that it allocates
  authority among its own processes but that it can revise its own governing
  standards when they break down"; "Endogenous allocation alone: no stronger
  sense" (SYNTHESIS-054).
- **CLAIM-424** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-025): reflexive
  governance "is a capacity some organizations of perspective have and others
  lack, not what makes something a perspective; and the capacity to revise
  those standards is not the capacity to revise them well."
- **CLAIM-288** (Active; counter; interpretive): "In a population whose
  operators each maximize an objective the modeller fixed, the norms the
  organization appears to maintain are set by the modeller, not by the
  organization". CLAIM-356 (Active; first line learned-structure) objects in
  part: "exogenous origin does not disqualify a goal" (SYNTHESIS-054).
- **CLAIM-359** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-282, CLAIM-025):
  "A self-governing organization's robustness to compromised constituents is a
  property of the processes that allocate, audit and revise authority, not
  only of its constraints on harmful acts; safeguards against capture can also
  restrict the disagreement and revision that self-governance needs ... while
  others, such as audits independent of the audited that protect dissent,
  serve both". A41: "Removing a compromised process may restore immediate
  functioning while leaving the organization unable to change the governance
  arrangement that enabled the compromise"; "Security and self-governance
  therefore cannot simply be identified."
- **CLAIM-410** (Active; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-086, CLAIM-057,
  CLAIM-282): removing a constraint "changes a profile of who holds governing
  positions, among them rule-making, monitoring, enforcement, appeal and
  revision", and members' first-order options and "their share in revising
  the conditions of their action can move in opposite directions."
- **CLAIM-719** (Proposed; thesis; interpretive; uses TERM-128;
  organizational-normativity): "An institution can be reliably responsive to
  reasons within its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons
  for revising that mechanism, and because its own actions change the
  mechanism, responsiveness at the first order is no evidence of
  responsiveness at the second". ρ_N(G; 𝒮_r) = inf_{s ∈ 𝒮_r}
  Pr_G(a ∈ R_N(s) | s) is defined; "ρ^(2) is named, not defined". The mechanism
  moves: M_{t+1} = Ψ(M_t, u_t, ξ_t); "An organization can influence its future
  ability to recognize and act upon reasons that constrain it." Lack of
  second-order responsiveness is a wrong "only with CLAIM-624's duty as a
  further premise" (SYNTHESIS-104).
- **CLAIM-685** (Proposed; thesis; **empirical**; rests_on none; lines
  dependence-and-domination, organizational-normativity; TERM-074 sense 3):
  "Institutions able to recognize, authorize and carry out revisions of their
  own governing standards avoid persistent normatively significant failures
  better than institutions that only monitor outcomes against fixed standards,
  where the inherited standards are inadequate to the problems met, and not on
  routine work, where the added governance costs without benefit." It is the
  narrow survivor of "reflexive conviviality": as a thesis about freedom, that
  "asks the record to hold corrigibility as a condition of legitimacy, which
  CLAIM-511 disclaims and CLAIM-520 makes conditional". Its defeater requires
  institutions "matched on resources and on the independence of the evidence
  they receive (CLAIM-342)". It carries CLAIM-667 (Proposed; empirical;
  record-as-apparatus): "Governance raises the value of a modifiable schema on
  tasks whose inherited categories are inadequate", with CLAIM-665's limit
  (Proposed; empirical): "On routine tasks ... explicit change governance adds
  cost and no benefit".
- **CLAIM-445** (Proposed; thesis; interpretive; rests_on CLAIM-446): a
  protective regime becomes self-maintaining "when the interventions it
  authorizes shift the evidence used to judge its necessity, the authority and
  resources of those who judge it, the capacities of its constituents or the
  threat environment itself", and "it is a failure only where the regime has
  become less responsive to evidence that it is no longer needed."

### 2.8 Succession and identity over time

- **CLAIM-329** (Proposed; thesis; interpretive; grounds CASE-102, CASE-098,
  CASE-105): "A collective that replaces all its members persists, when it
  does, by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by
  resemblance: an organization of the same kind that arises independently is
  not its continuation, and a lapse in its coherence need not end it."
  "Reproducing a kind is not persisting as an individual." Held Proposed
  because without QUESTION-118 it "cannot tell continuation from causal

## 3. Formal results, simulations and documented cases

### 3.1 Demonstrated results and models

- **The coupled loop's instability (CLAIM-330, Active, demonstrated; CASE-099).**
  Linearized at symmetry, with each field stable alone and nonnegative
  couplings, symmetry is unstable "exactly when βγ > (1 − α)(1 − δ)": "only
  the loop can do it", and "Scoring operators independently of the dominant
  interpretation (Regime B, γ ≈ 0) removes the coupling-driven instability".
  CLAIM-340 (Active, demonstrated): commitment before evidence "is not by
  itself Regime B"; the coupling has the sign of κ′ − κ̄. CLAIM-400 (Active,
  demonstrated) adds a maintained legibility state; away from threshold "a
  lean in the regime can outlast the advantages it was built from"
  (SYNTHESIS-050).
- **Selective admission as a mechanism (CASE-156, Active).** With 15% alarms
  in a safe world and admission rule P(admit | y, r) = σ(c + χry), c = 0,
  χr = 2, "56.6% of the admitted record is alarms with the world and the
  sensors unchanged"; SYNTHESIS-050 reports that "lock-in needs χ above about
  9.8".
- **Replicator reading (CASE-155, Active; CLAIM-444, Active).** With
  ℓ′ = ℓ + h + c(2π − 1), there is "an unstable threshold π* = (1 − h/c)/2,
  above which a process the evidence disfavours captures the organization";
  "protective lock-in (CLAIM-446) needs no bias in any process, only scoring
  that depends on who governs". CLAIM-444: "the replicator model is the null
  model for the record's governance dynamics".
- **Scoring rule decides merit or entrenchment (CASE-182, Active).** "scored
  every round, the more accurate one holds the lead durably whatever the
  start; scored only when it acts, under sharp selection, whichever gained
  influence early keeps it."
- **Selective admission versus self-scoring (CLAIM-466, Active,
  demonstrated; CASE-162, Active).** In CASE-162's v1.3, a 32-member
  event-level population over 64 worlds, with no self-scoring: at selectivity
  s = 0.5, 0.75, 1, naive credit leaves 31, 41, 41 worlds with a wrong
  incumbent; "exact inverse-propensity weighting leaves 0, 0 and 5", the 5
  where near-zero admission probabilities break positivity. At s = 0.75
  "selection reverses the sign of the evidence: the raw cue mean is −0.193 and
  the admitted mean +0.184." "Self-scoring alone, c = 0.5 with s = 0, leaves
  39 of 64 wrong. Nothing is censored there, so the correction changes
  nothing." With both, the correction removes only selection's part (c = 0.5,
  s = 0.5: 41 to 34). Hence "correcting for how evidence was selected is not
  correcting how it is scored"; "a self-scored record is biased in what it
  counts, not in what it was shown." It does not supply "A procedure for
  unknown propensities" (the correction was an oracle's) or test "The remedy
  for self-scoring". CASE-162's v1.2 four-variable model puts the equilibrium
  fold at c ≈ 0.4247 while a declared history is trapped from c ≈ 0.4469; with
  competence growing with access, "B stays the better perspective at every
  competence the model allows, so what entrenches is authority, not merit."
- **Router equivalence (CLAIM-467, Active; CASE-158).** "Under
  full-information, independent audits, a learned allocation of influence
  among specialists is no better than a contextual router trained on the same
  audits"; ARG-036's critical question fails: "The organizational vocabulary
  adds no constraint that the conventional model lacks" (SYNTHESIS-050).
- **Hysteresis of maintenance (CASE-111, Active; CLAIM-463).** In
  ḃ = μb + κb² − b³ with κ > 0, both states are stable for −κ²/4 < μ < 0, "so
  maintenance lost past the fold is not restored by returning the parameter,
  and recovery slows to zero as the fold nears." CLAIM-521 (Active,
  demonstrated): at the fold "The coupled units are as tightly integrated at
  the edge of collapse as well inside the regime"; CLAIM-612 (Proposed,
  demonstrated): "a surviving organization's stable statistics are no evidence
  that it persists" (SYNTHESIS-048).
- **Coarse-graining merges claims (CASE-110, Active; CLAIM-348).** Total
  output "preserves an institution's performance, its survival and every
  prediction it makes, and still merges" (6, 6) with (10, 2), a member within
  the agreed cap with one far past it.
- **Policy existence under coarse observation (CLAIM-535; CLAIM-349's note).**
  A randomized policy cannot help across indistinguishable contexts with
  disjoint acceptable actions: "max over p of min(p, 1 − p) is 1/2"
  (CLAIM-349, note of 2026-11-04).
- **Influence across levels does not add (CLAIM-403's note of 2026-10-26).**
  With p = c·e and y = a·e + b·p (a = 0.3, b = 0.8, c = 0.5), the total effect
  of do(e) is 0.7, of which 0.4 runs through the team; summing member and team
  influence gives 1.1. "So attribution across the rungs is by intervention on
  specified pathways ... not by summing level-specific weights."
- **Bottleneck and composition failure (CLAIM-732).** Pr(F ∩ L ∩ A ∩ S ∩ R ∩ M)
  factorizes exactly by the chain rule; arrangement A (0.8, 0.8, 0.9, 0.8,
  0.8, 0.9) gives 0.3318, arrangement B (1.0, 0.9, 0.9, 0.35, 0.8, 0.9) gives
  0.2041: "The institution could accurately report improved complaint access
  while its actual capacity to correct justified grievances has deteriorated."
  The probabilities are stipulated.
- **Value of correction (CLAIM-520).** As in 2.6: worth k(0.25 − 0.5ε) − C.
- **Frustration's order (SYNTHESIS-051).** CLAIM-347 (Active): jointly
  satisfiable demand sets form a downward-closed complex; "relief is strict
  enlargement for the same declared demands", and "dropping or suppressing a
  demand is not relief". CLAIM-361 (Active): relief is judged "against a fixed
  reference set D₀"; A38: "Resolving a conflict, reorganizing the individual
  that bears it, and eliminating one of the parties to the conflict are
  different transformations".
- **Separating maintenance location (CLAIM-522, Active, demonstrated).** An
  outside controller supplying the production law gives the same trajectory
  under every intervention on state, "Full observation of c changes nothing";
  only interventions on production mechanisms relative to a declared boundary
  separate them (SYNTHESIS-048).

## 4. How the accounts fit together; what is open

**The line's shape.** SYNTHESIS-049 describes distributed-agency as ten part
accounts under one heading, "what an organized whole is", kept in one line
"because they are densely joined ... not because a component says they are."
Of 2,260 linked pairs among 495 components, 1,623 fall within a part; the
largest crossings are conferral with frustration (86 pairs) and with
perspectives (80). SYNTHESIS-038 describes organizational-normativity as four
parts meeting "at its root questions", QUESTION-128 and QUESTION-129; "the
line makes no claim beyond its parts", and by ADR-083's signal it "is still a
candidate to split".

**A chain from the whole to its correction.** Reading the components' own
stated links, the record holds a sequence of non-implications:

1. The whole's direction is not the members' (CLAIM-030), and its success does
   not show theirs (CLAIM-060); a description adequate for the whole can merge
   what the members' claims distinguish (CLAIM-348).
2. What the whole can see is relative to task (CLAIM-455), policy (CLAIM-535)
   and direction (TERM-064); what its record shows can be produced by what it
   admits (CLAIM-448) or by how it scores (CLAIM-297, CLAIM-342), and the two
   need different remedies (CLAIM-466; CLAIM-404 for the routes).
3. Seeing is not taking up, and taking up is not a part in revising the
   standards of uptake (CLAIM-349, CLAIM-403); taking up is not carrying out
   (CLAIM-523, with TERM-085), and carrying out is not maintaining (CLAIM-422,
   CLAIM-732's M).
4. Effective action is not correctability (CLAIM-462, resting on CLAIM-099 and
   CLAIM-422); an intervention can change what correcting it takes (CLAIM-464),
   and restoration is not reversal (CLAIM-463).
5. Responsiveness within a mechanism is not responsiveness about the mechanism
   (CLAIM-719); the stronger sense of self-governance is revising one's own
   standards (CLAIM-485), which is not revising them well (CLAIM-424), and
   whose benefit is conditional on inadequate inherited standards (CLAIM-685,
   CLAIM-667, CLAIM-665) and on cost (CLAIM-520).
6. Reproducing what keeps a justified standard from constraining the
   organization is proposed as a further wrong (CLAIM-614), bounded as a breach
   of a duty of answerability (CLAIM-624), conditional on an independently
   justified standard; three routes to becoming "normatively less informed"
   are kept apart (CLAIM-619, CLAIM-652, CLAIM-662, CLAIM-657).

The links among these are stated by the entries themselves: CLAIM-403 rests on
CLAIM-349; CLAIM-523 "extends CLAIM-349's split"; CLAIM-732 rests on CLAIM-523
and complements CLAIM-403; CLAIM-462 rests on CLAIM-422 and CLAIM-099;
CLAIM-624 rests on CLAIM-614, CLAIM-348, CLAIM-523, CLAIM-445 and CLAIM-462;
CLAIM-485, CLAIM-424, CLAIM-422, CLAIM-359 and CLAIM-349 all rest on CLAIM-025.
CASE-138 is cited by CLAIM-403, CLAIM-416, CLAIM-422 and CLAIM-624 (layer 2:
"The whole loop is documented in THEORY-140 and CASE-138, Challenger to
Columbia, reforms undone included").

**Persistence joins correction.** CLAIM-329 and CLAIM-615 make an organized
whole's identity over time a matter of transmitting "the relations that keep
its repertoire realizable"; CLAIM-421 says a perspective can be interrupted in
activation, enactment or reproduction without being lost; CLAIM-617 says
participants' verdicts can move the producers and so the persistence. SYNTHESIS-
056 tested, and did not file, the link that CLAIM-464's mechanisms produce
enactment interruptions of corrective capacity: in CASE-170 "here no remedy for
that objective exists at all", which "is not a held capacity interrupted".

**Links tested and not filed** (SYNTHESIS-056, SYNTHESIS-049, SYNTHESIS-047):
that influence over a test (CLAIM-416) and control over admitted contests

## 6. Digester's notes

*Everything in this section is the digester's reading, not the record's. Codes
mark what each point draws on; none of these inferences is filed.*

**(a) Bearing on a general theory of learning and inquiry for arbitrary,
especially collective, systems.**

1. *Learning decomposes into separately failing achievements.* The region's
   most consistent pattern is a ladder of non-implications: having, knowing
   and deploying (CLAIM-296); access, authority and action (CLAIM-349);
   admission, recognition, credibility, uptake and a part in revising standards
   (CLAIM-403); legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability (CLAIM-523);
   formulation through maintenance (CLAIM-732); act, procedure and maintained
   capacity (CLAIM-422). A general theory could take "learning" in a collective
   to be the joint realization of such a chain, with CLAIM-732's bottleneck
   bound as its quantitative form: no measure of one stage, however improved,
   certifies the whole.

2. *What makes a collective's learning its own.* CLAIM-030 and CLAIM-454 imply
   that a whole's learning cannot be read off its members' learning, nor off
   modes recovered from their activity; CLAIM-418 adds that the bearer of an
   organizational perspective need not be an agent. CLAIM-485, CLAIM-284 and
   CLAIM-288 suggest a demarcation: a system learns in the self-governing sense
   only where it can revise the standards it is scored by, not merely allocate
   authority under standards fixed outside it. CLAIM-424's caveat ("not the
   capacity to revise them well") keeps this from becoming a success criterion.

3. *Revising one's own standards of evidence and uptake.* The region holds two
   independent threats to an inquirer revising its own evidential standards:
   selective admission and self-scoring (CLAIM-404, CLAIM-466), which need
   different repairs, and competition over the test (CLAIM-416), where success
   under a regime stops being evidence about what matters once a competitor
   shapes the regime. CLAIM-342 gives the general condition the theory would
   need: evidence "whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the
   interpretation it tests". CLAIM-719's ρ^(1)/ρ^(2) distinction names the level
   at which standards are revised, though ρ^(2) is undefined; defining it
   (a case family and response set for revisions of the mechanism) looks like
   the central open formal task.

4. *Remaining correctable.* CLAIM-462, CLAIM-464, CLAIM-463 and CLAIM-099
   jointly imply that correctability is a maintained relation that can erode
   while performance improves, and that a lapse can be hysteretic (CASE-111).
   CLAIM-619, CLAIM-652 and CLAIM-662 show three distinct ways a system can
   become less informed while its measured complaint rate falls. For a general
   theory, this suggests treating correctability as a state variable with its
   own dynamics, not a by-product of accuracy, and treating "absence of
   challenge" as uninformative unless the channels are shown intact
   (CLAIM-619's test).

5. *Identity through learning.* CLAIM-615 and CLAIM-329 let a learner's
   structure change while it remains the same learner, provided the relations
   that keep its repertoire realizable are transmitted; CLAIM-421's three
   interruptions give a vocabulary for a learner that keeps a capacity it
   cannot presently exercise or pass on. QUESTION-118 and QUESTION-364 mark
   where a theory would have to say when self-revision is continuation rather
   than replacement.

6. *Normative limits.* The record keeps capacity apart from value (CLAIM-520,
   ARG-042, CLAIM-511) and causal diagnosis apart from verdict (CLAIM-624's
   fifth condition, CLAIM-075, CLAIM-363). A general theory that drew on this
   region would have to do likewise: it can say what it takes to remain
   correctable, not that remaining correctable is owed, except under the
   bounded and conditional duty CLAIM-624 proposes.

**(b) Operationalizing this in a research record kept by a human owner and
many AI assistants, with a schema revised by governed decisions.**

1. *The record is itself an organized whole in TERM-074 sense 1 (and sense 2
   for its maintenance).* Its direction can part from any assistant's or the
   owner's intention (CLAIM-030); its success (lint passing, entries filed)
   does not show the soundness of its parts (CLAIM-060). The relevant
   counterpart of CLAIM-348 is that a schema adequate for the record's
   operations can merge distinctions a contributor needs kept.

2. *CLAIM-403's ladder as an audit of contributions.* For a contribution from
   an assistant or a reading, one could trace admission (filed), recognition
   (coded in apt terms, with a fitting vocabulary word), credibility (status and
   warrant), uptake (a claim rests on it or a synthesis integrates it), and the
   top rung (it can occasion a schema-changing decision). The note on CLAIM-403
   of 2026-11-12 already records a proposed "**schema-fit disparity**" measure
   from "luria experiment design"; the reading-review and synthesis-review
   processes act at the uptake rung. Bencherki's attribution axis (CLAIM-403's
   note of 2026-10-31) has a direct counterpart in how the record credits
   voices ("A's", "the owner's").

3. *Independent scoring.* CLAIM-342 and CLAIM-466 suggest checking whether a
   claim's promotion passes through the interpretation that produced it: for
   example, whether readings that "corroborate" a claim were selected because
   the claim was already held (selection), or whether status reviews score a
   claim by the record's own syntheses (self-scoring). These need different
   remedies: registering what was searched and not found (CLAIM-312's
   certificate about the remainder), and review by a party whose scoring does
   not run through the claim.

4. *Governed schema revision as CLAIM-685 in small.* CLAIM-667 and CLAIM-665
   are already the record-scale form, and CLAIM-685 their institutional
   generalization. A record can test them on itself: compare decisions that
   revised the schema where inherited categories failed with routine
   governance overhead. CLAIM-422 warns that an ADR recorded is not a capacity
   maintained, which is what ADR-110's postmortems (per the project's own
   guidance) are positioned to check; CLAIM-732's M stage is the maintenance a
   postmortem would measure.

5. *Second-order responsiveness.* CLAIM-719 suggests distinguishing whether
   assistants apply the current schema reliably (ρ^(1)) from whether the
   record takes up justified reasons to change the schema (ρ^(2)). Candidate
   observables: how often a contributor's objection to a vocabulary or rule
   reaches a decision, and with what lag; whether objections are reclassified
   ("An objection can become feedback", CLAIM-403's note of 2026-11-13).
   CLAIM-359 adds the trade-off: rules that guard the record against a
   compromised or careless contributor can also close the channels through
   which a sound contributor revises the rules.

6. *Succession of assistants.* Each session is a change of steward in
   CLAIM-617's sense and a handoff in CASE-134's. CLAIM-329 and CLAIM-615 imply
   that the record persists by transmitting the relations that keep its
   repertoire realizable (skills, decisions, linting, the review processes),
   not by resemblance of outputs; CASE-166's reported handoff result, that
   records improve state estimation about eightfold while barely changing
   action, cautions that transmitting the record is not transmitting the
   capacity. CLAIM-421's reproductive interruption names the risk of a rule
   known to the record but no longer passed to new assistants.

7. *Standing to revise the schema.* QUESTION-481 is live for such a record:
   who may authorize changes to the governing structure, and which protections
   lie beyond revision (here, the enforced rules, such as never sending an
   email address to an outside service, and retiring by status rather than
   deletion). The record's practice of owner approval of ADRs answers "who"
   for now; the digested entries do not say whether that answer meets
   CLAIM-403's top rung for assistants' contributions.
