# TERM-tmpc9uv8: domination, of a party within an arrangement, relative to a declared standard of answerability: an agent's or role's capacity, used or not, to set or change the terms on which the party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate, where the capacity is not answerable to the party under a standard declared with the judgment

## Definition

A judgment that a party is dominated within an arrangement has four
relata, and is well-defined only when all four are stated ([CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)):

1. **The arrangement**, with its sense declared ([TERM-074](TERM-074.md)). Sense 1 is
   the default.
2. **The holder**: an agent or a role that has the capacity. It must be
   something that can choose, because domination is a capacity to set
   terms.
3. **The party dominated**, with standing ([TERM-083](TERM-083.md)).
4. **The standard of answerability**, declared with the judgment: what
   would make the holder's capacity answerable to the party. Examples are
   a channel of contest the party can use, a rule the holder cannot change
   alone, an outside enforcer, or a duty of answerability such as
   [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s.

The party is dominated, relative to that standard, when the holder has
the capacity to set or change the terms on which the party can act, exit,
contest or renegotiate, and that capacity is not answerable to the party
under the standard. The capacity counts whether or not it is used:
[CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s "used or not", and [CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md)'s channel "it never uses".

## What it is for

The record's argument about dependence needs a word for the condition in
which one party's terms are at another's discretion. [CLAIM-tmp6p3ki](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6p3ki.md) says
that this condition, and not the magnitude of dependence, sorts a
whole-part dependence in both directions. [CASE-tmpzh595](../cases.d/CASE-tmpzh595.md)'s workshop B is
domination in this sense, under the standard "P can withdraw or
renegotiate". Its workshop A is not.

## What it is not

- **Not exploitation.** Exploitation ([TERM-tmp169d8](TERM-tmp169d8.md)) is benefit on terms
  that violate a declared standard. A holder can dominate without
  benefiting, and a beneficiary can exploit without holding a capacity
  over the party's later choices.
- **Not dependence, nor integration.** Equal dependence can be dominated
  or not ([CASE-tmpzh595](../cases.d/CASE-tmpzh595.md)), and a capability given up to a supplier
  ([CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)) need not put the terms in the supplier's hands. The owner's
  review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488) asked that vulnerability to another's control be kept
  apart from integration. The term is stated in control and does not
  mention integration.
- **Not interference.** A holder that never interferes still dominates if
  it has the capacity. A party constrained by an arrangement that nobody
  controls is constrained, not dominated.
- **Not a verdict on the holder's obligations.** That a party is
  dominated says where control lies. Whether it grounds an obligation,
  and on whom, is [CLAIM-671](../claims.d/CLAIM-671.md)'s supposition and [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md)'s question. The
  term names a likely bearer, the holder, and settles nothing about the
  ground.
- **Not freedom's opposite in full.** [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s freedom of will is an
  agent's governance of its own governability. Domination is one way it
  fails, from outside. An agent can lack that freedom with no one
  dominating it.

## What would unsettle it

- A case the record needs to call domination in which no agent or role
  holds the capacity: an arrangement whose terms no one sets, which still
  dominates its parts. Then relatum 2 is too narrow, and [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)'s third
  result, constraint without a duty-bearer, would need a word of its own.
- A use in which the standard of answerability is idle, so that every
  unanswerable capacity counts whatever is declared.
- Pettit's own account ([LIT-1614](../literature.d/LIT-1614.md), Unavailable), once read, if its
  arbitrariness condition cannot be stated as a declared standard of
  answerability without loss.
