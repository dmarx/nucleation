# CLAIM-tmpbvk4j: The record's own reading finds contextuality proper in no behavioural data set, so the headline that translation changes global structure is either about contextuality, for which there is no case but a constructed one, or about inconsistent connectedness, the ordinary context dependence the line says it goes beyond

## The objection

[CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md), from A78: "Translation may preserve local meanings while
changing the global structure of meaning", "potentially the most
interesting connection we've found". Its defeat condition: "preservation of
the context-wise judgement distributions always carries preservation of
their global compatibility (overlap consistency, global extension,
contextual fraction), so that the global structure adds nothing to
predict." The manuscript keeps it as its Abstract's last sentence.

**What the record has read about behavioural data.** Dzhafarov, Zhang and
Kujala ([LIT-264](../literature.d/LIT-264.md), read in [NOTE-235](../notes.d/NOTE-235.md)) apply the Contextuality-by-Default
criterion to five kinds of behavioural data, two of them previously
published as contextual. "None is contextual." The note's key insight: a
response distribution that depends on context is inconsistent
connectedness, "a trivial sense of contextuality", and "Behavioural data
have plenty of the first and, so far, none of the second." [CLAIM-125](CLAIM-125.md), from
Wang, Sadrzadeh, Abramsky and Cervantes ([LIT-851](../literature.d/LIT-851.md)): "69 of the 90 systems
signal" and "34 signal enough that they cannot be contextual at all". The
paper tests none of the 90 for contextuality ([LIT-851](../literature.d/LIT-851.md)'s status note). Among
the line's claims, [LIT-264](../literature.d/LIT-264.md) is cited by [CLAIM-041](CLAIM-041.md) alone, against reading order
effects as nonclassical, and by none of [CLAIM-038](CLAIM-038.md), [CLAIM-044](CLAIM-044.md) or [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md).

**The dilemma.** "Global compatibility" in [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md) is one of two things.

- **Contextuality proper**: no global extension, a positive contextual
  fraction, or contextuality in the CbD sense. The data the record has read
  have none. If source and target models are both noncontextual, there is
  no contextuality to gain or lose, and "preservation of the context-wise
  judgement distributions always carries preservation" of it, which is the
  defeat condition met by default. [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md)'s own reading of the
  contextual fraction adds that it "cannot rise along free operations" and
  is "Lipschitz in the probability table" within a scenario ([THEORY-165](../theory.d/THEORY-165.md)).
  The one instance of the headline in the record is built from [CASE-037](../cases.d/CASE-037.md),
  the parity triangle, a no-signalling, strongly contextual model
  (contextual fraction 1): changed in one context it gives [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md)'s
  recovered pair, with fractions 1 and 0. [CASE-037](../cases.d/CASE-037.md) is stipulated.
- **Overlap consistency**: whether a content's distribution is the same in
  every context that measures it. A change in it is a change in
  inconsistent connectedness, which [NOTE-235](../notes.d/NOTE-235.md) calls the trivial sense, and
  which is ordinary context dependence. [CLAIM-038](CLAIM-038.md) says the theory is
  distinct "from merely saying that meanings are fuzzy, ambiguous, or
  context-dependent".

Overlap consistency is also not global structure beyond the local
distributions. It is computed from them. For a content q measured in
contexts C and C′, let d = TV(p, p′), where p and p′ are q's marginals
from the distributions in C and C′. If a transformation moves each context's
distribution by at most ε in total variation, it moves each marginal by at
most ε, since marginalizing does not increase total variation. By the
triangle inequality the new discrepancy d̃ satisfies |d̃ − d| ≤ 2ε. So
keeping the local distributions nearly fixed keeps overlap consistency
nearly fixed, and on this horn the global structure adds nothing to
predict, which is again the defeat condition.

On the first horn the headline has no case but a constructed one. On the
second it is the context dependence [CLAIM-038](CLAIM-038.md) sets the theory apart from.
The record holds both [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md)'s headline and [CLAIM-078](CLAIM-078.md)'s "it needs
neither quantum contextuality nor exact Noether laws", and the evidence it
has read favours the second.

How much contextuality a data set shows also depends on how its outcomes
are coded: [CLAIM-tmprwo1c](CLAIM-tmprwo1c.md) shows that a deterministic recoding within each
context can make a noncontextual signalling system contextual in the CbD
sense, or the reverse. So a contextual result would itself need a coding
fixed in advance.

## What it does not say

It does not say a contextual pragmatic data set cannot exist, nor that
[CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md) is false. [LIT-264](../literature.d/LIT-264.md) offers the absence of contextuality as a
working hypothesis, and the record has read one paper's five data sets and
one paper's signalling counts. It does not touch the change of scenario,
where the contextual fraction can jump ([CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md), "Only a change of
scenario can make it jump"). It says the headline, as stated over a fixed
scenario, is either unsupported or not distinctive.

## What would answer it

- A documented pragmatic data set that is contextual in the CbD sense,
  with its signalling accounted for and its coding fixed in advance, and a
  transformation that keeps its local distributions while changing that.
- Or a restatement of [CLAIM-105](CLAIM-105.md) about a change of cover or scenario, which
  the continuity results leave open.
- Or a restatement about overlap consistency alone, conceding that it is
  context dependence and saying what the theory adds to it.
