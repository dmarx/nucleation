# LIT-tmpfxss5: Reinterpreting the empathy–altruism relationship: When one into one equals oneness

Cialdini, R. B., Brown, S. L., Lewis, B. P., Luce, C. & Neuberg, S. L. (1997). *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 73(3), 481–494.

Not read. The summary is drawn from the abstract or from what the work is
standardly cited for. A reading should check it first.

## Why this is here

The empirical form of the owner's thesis that no action is selfless, only aimed at a larger self ([CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)). Its rival is Batson et al. ([LIT-tmp7qbeb](LIT-tmp7qbeb.md)), in the same issue.

## Read in its place

The work cannot be read here, so the record holds it at second hand.

The SEP entry (NOTE-tmpd1dh3, §5.3) quotes the paper's oneness index (p.
484): the IOS scale of seven increasingly overlapping circle pairs,
combined with a 7-point item on how far one would use "we" to describe the
relationship. It reports the paper's claim, from three studies, that
empathy played no causal role once oneness was accounted for. It calls
"merging" there "a label for whatever the IOS scale and the use-of-*we*
test measure". Missing still: the designs, the relationship manipulations,
the regression results, and the paper's own case that oneness is not
caring under another name.

Miyazono and Inarimori (NOTE-tmpdrfdn, pp. 3–4) report one of the paper's
studies. Students imagined a near-stranger, an acquaintance, a good friend
or their closest family member facing eviction, and chose among seven
levels of help. They rated oneness, on a seven-point item and the IOS
scale, and empathic concern, sadness and distress. With oneness
controlled, empathic concern no longer predicted helping; oneness still
did with empathy controlled. They quote the paper's conclusion that
empathy signals oneness (p. 491), that merging is "conceptual, not
physical" (p. 482), and that it undermines "the distinction between
selflessness and selfishness" (p. 481). Missing still: the other studies,
every statistic, the exact oneness items (the SEP gives a "we" item where
this paper gives a "oneness" item), and the paper's argument that oneness
is not closeness or caring under another name.
