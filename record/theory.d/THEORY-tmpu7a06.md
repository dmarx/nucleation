# THEORY-tmpu7a06: Lefebvre's two systems of ethical cognition, as Umpleby restates them: additive or multiplicative combination in a three-level model of reflexion generates two ethical systems, one in which a bad means may not serve a good end and compromise is valued, one in which the end justifies the means and confrontation is valued, which Lefebvre took to dominate the West and the USSR respectively

Umpleby (2016), LIT-tmpkuudf.

## The account

"Depending on whether the combination rule for the exponents is addition
or multiplication, two quite different ethical systems are generated"
(p. 65). "In the first ethical system, the end does not justify the
means. In the second ethical system, the end does justify the means"
(p. 65). Lefebvre "contended that the dominant ethical systems in the West
and the Soviet Union were fundamentally different" (p. 65).

## What it does not show

- **The model.** The algebra and its derivations are not reproduced; the
  means/ends gloss is Umpleby's.
- **The cultural claim.** It rests on anecdote; A's caution that these
  generalizations "should not be accepted as established psychological
  facts" is consistent with how thin the evidence is.
- **A link to manipulation.** Reflexive control appears only as a
  keyword.
