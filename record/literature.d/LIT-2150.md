# LIT-2150: Cash Transfers and Temptation Goods

Evans, D. K. & Popova, A. (2017). Cash Transfers and Temptation Goods. *Economic Development and Cultural Change* 65(2): 189–221. doi:10.1086/689575. Read as Cash Transfers and Temptation Goods: A Review of Global Evidence, World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 6886 (May 2014), doi:10.1596/1813-9450-6886.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1649](../notes.d/NOTE-1649.md). Page numbers are the working paper's.*

- **Almost no rise.** Across 44 estimates, "82% of estimates are
  negative, and just 5% of estimates are significant and positive"
  (p. 8).
- **The two positives are weak.** Both non-experimental, "not consistent
  across estimates within the same study", and small: "one is less than a
  penny, whereas the other is 21 cents" (p. 8).
- **Randomized trials.** Of "the 17 estimates from 8 randomized-control
  trials", "zero that are positive and significant" (p. 8).
- **Not the conditions.** Conditional and unconditional programs do not
  differ, "so this does not seem to be a function of conditions" (p. 14),
  and receipt lasting from 6 months to 5.5 years shows "no clear
  relationship" with the effects (pp. 9–10).
- **A vote count.** It classifies estimates; it pools none.

## Standing in the record

Filed with Baird, McKenzie & Özler (2018), [LIT-2189](LIT-2189.md), as a counterpoint
to the branch's dependency claims; a second work, not a stand-in. The
record makes no claim about temptation spending. What this bears on is the
character premise behind "slack effort" in [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) and behind the
Samaritan's logic of [CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md) and [THEORY-1480](../theory.d/THEORY-1480.md) carried down to
households: that the poor turn unconditioned help against their own
interest. At household level that is not found, and it does not grow with
length of receipt. It says nothing about states or projects, nor about
work. Filed from it: [THEORY-1494](../theory.d/THEORY-1494.md).
