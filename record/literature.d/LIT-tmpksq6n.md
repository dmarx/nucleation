# LIT-tmpksq6n: Biological organisation as closure of constraints

Maël Montévil and Matteo Mossio (2015), *Journal of Theoretical Biology*
372:179–191.

The brief's citation (J. Theor. Biol. 372, 2015) is correct.

## Key takeaways

- **Constraints versus processes.** C is a constraint on A → B at time scale
  τ iff A → B and A_C → B_C differ as far as B is concerned at τ, and the
  relevant aspects of C are conserved through the process at τ (Definition
  1). Enzymes and the vascular system are the worked examples. Constraints
  canalise flows without exchanging energy or matter with them at τ.
- **Dependence and closure.** C1 depends on C2 if C2 constrains a process
  that produces aspects of C1 relevant to its role (Definition 2), directly
  if no intervening process at τ2 does so (Definition 3). A set realises
  closure if every member depends directly on another member and is
  generative for another; strict closure if it cannot be split into two
  closed sets (Definition 4). Closure needs both slow and fast dependence.
- **Not a cycle of processes.** The hydrologic cycle is a cycle of
  transformations under external boundary conditions, not closure, because
  its entities do not act as constraints on each other (footnote 25).
- **Boundaries.** Closure is offered as "a clear-cut criterion" for drawing
  a biological entity's boundary. Ascribe it only to maximally closed
  systems. Where organisms depend on each other directly (symbiosis), the
  tendency to closure δK(V, l), a count of constraint dependencies swept by
  a growing volume, peaks inside each organism and collapses between them.
  Where cells make up an organism, cells do not individually meet the
  definition of a constraint on the organism's processes, so the two
  closures separate and nest: that is a level of organisation.
- **Not a definition of life or organism.** Whether closure is necessary
  and sufficient for biological systems "is not discussed"; an organism
  realises closure, but "a system realising closure is not necessarily an
  organism" (footnotes 4, 27), referring to Moreno & Mossio (2015).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the biological-autonomy part
of the agency batch. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no

**The paper version of Biological Autonomy.** [LIT-536](LIT-536.md), Moreno & Mossio's
book, is Deferred because no lawful copy could be read. Its first chapter,
"Constraints and Organisational Closure", states the claim this paper
states, and this paper is what the record now reads for it: closure of
constraints as the organisational regime of living systems, the
constraint/process distinction resting on time-scale symmetry, and closure
as what grounds functions. The paper itself sends the reader to the book for
what it leaves out, whether closure defines an organism. So the two are not
interchangeable. This paper covers the book's chapter 1 and touches chapters
3 (functions) and 6 (levels); chapters 2, 4, 5 and 7 (emergence, agency,
evolution, cognition) remain unread. Mossio, Saborido & Moreno (2009),
[LIT-tmp9gfzl](LIT-tmp9gfzl.md), is the earlier statement this one carries further: it used a
broader closure, a circular relation between a macroscopic pattern and its
microscopic dynamics, which a candle flame satisfies, and grounded functions
in closure plus differentiation. This paper narrows closure to mutual
dependence among constraints and states the function thesis as its first
implication, citing the 2009 paper for it.

Where it bears on what the record holds:

- **The demarcation question ([LIT-192](LIT-192.md), [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)).** Nahas & Sachs cite this
  paper as the organizational account's statement of closure and report that
  closure faces the candle-flame problem. The paper does not discuss flames.
  It excludes cycles of processes (footnote 25), and with them, on my reading
  ([NOTE-tmptk2u9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptk2u9.md)), any system with a single constraint, but it does not say
  what it would make of a flame.
- **Baedke ([LIT-167](LIT-167.md), [NOTE-148](../notes.d/NOTE-148.md))** argues that closure criteria are too
  permissive because they make the holobiont the individual and the host "a
  mere part". §6 anticipates exactly this case. Maximal closure alone would
  have that consequence, which the paper calls "a serious weakness"; the
  tendency to closure and nested separated closures are its answer. They are
  labelled preliminary, and the measure is shown on a toy diagram only.
- **Friston ([LIT-526](LIT-526.md))** draws a system's boundary at a Markov blanket. This
  paper draws it at the set of mutually dependent constraints. Raja et al.
  ([LIT-tmpumhsc](LIT-tmpumhsc.md)) argue the blanket formalism cannot represent a boundary the
  system produces, which is what closure is about. The two criteria are not
  tested against each other anywhere in the record.
- **Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde ([LIT-tmp2zh8b](LIT-tmp2zh8b.md))** define agency on an
  autonomous network of mutually enabling processes; this paper is the
  formal version of that network for constraints.
