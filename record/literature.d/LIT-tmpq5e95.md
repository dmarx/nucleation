# LIT-tmpq5e95: Life as a manifestation of the second law of thermodynamics

Eric D. Schneider and James J. Kay (1994), *Mathematical and Computer
Modelling* 19(6–8):25–48 (March 1994). The brief's citation is correct.

## Key takeaways

- **The "restated second law".** Building on the Hatsopoulos–Keenan and
  Kestin unified principle, that an isolated system reaches a unique
  equilibrium when constraints are removed, the authors propose: "as they
  are moved away from equilibrium, they will utilize all avenues available
  to counter the applied gradients. As the applied gradients increase, so
  does the system's ability to oppose further movement from equilibrium."
  They present it as avoiding nonequilibrium entropy and the extremum
  principles of Prigogine and Swenson.
- **Dissipative structures as gradient destroyers.** Their recalculation of
  Silveston's Bénard-cell data shows heat transfer, entropy production and
  exergy destruction all rising once convection begins. The governing
  principle, they say, is "not one of maximum entropy production but
  rather one of entropy production change being positive semi-definite as
  you increase the gradient." A "tornado in a bottle" drains in about 11 s
  against about 6 min without a vortex.
- **Life.** Life is "another means of dissipating the solar induced
  gradient". Living systems are "sophisticated mini-tornados, with a memory
  (its DNA), whose Aristotelian 'final cause' may be the second law". The
  restated law is "a necessary but not a sufficient condition for life",
  and "survival is equally important".
- **Ecosystems.** Succession should raise energy capture, flow, cycling,
  trophic depth, respiration and transpiration, biomass and diversity. A
  thermally stressed marsh (Crystal River, Florida) shows about 20% less
  flow, 35% less biomass and half the food-web cycles of its control.
  Airborne thermal scans of Oregon forests (Luvall & Holbo) show the
  400-year-old stand coolest and degrading the largest share of net
  incoming radiation. A satellite and SiB-model comparison (Sato and
  colleagues) puts the Amazon's evapotranspiration share highest and the
  Sahara's lowest.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as part of filling out the
record's coverage of dissipative structures and the "life as dissipation"
literature. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

[NOTE-tmpa22le](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpa22le.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the paper most often cited for "life is a gradient
dissipator", and it says more carefully than its citers do that this is a
necessary condition only. Its weak points are the ones a reader should
know before citing it. The "restated second law" is a proposal, not a
theorem. The Bénard and vortex cases show that organized structures can
raise dissipation; they do not show that systems generally do. The
ecological evidence is correlational and unreplicated.

Where it bears on what the record holds:

- **Schrödinger ([LIT-tmpncmdx](LIT-tmpncmdx.md), Deferred, unread here).** The paper
  presents itself as taking up "the task proposed by Schrödinger" and
  attributes to him an "order from disorder" programme in
  nonequilibrium thermodynamics. That attribution needs checking against
  the text.
- **The demarcation question ([LIT-192](LIT-192.md), [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)).** The paper puts life on
  "the continuum of development of natural dissipative structures from
  physical to chemical to autocatalytic to living systems". Its only
  marker of the living is "memory" (DNA), which lets the dissipative
  process continue "without having to restart" it by chance. That is a
  line drawn by heredity, not by thermodynamics. Thermodynamics, on the
  paper's own account, cannot draw it ("necessary but not sufficient").
  Its tornado and Bénard cell are the candle flame of Nahas & Sachs's
  dispute.
- **"What Lives?" ([LIT-211](LIT-211.md)).** A paradigm of the "Dissipative
  Self-Organizing Systems" cluster of definitions ([NOTE-109](../notes.d/NOTE-109.md)).
- **Self-organization.** Its autocatalysis paragraph (A catalyses B, B
  accelerates A) is the cycle structure [LIT-036](LIT-036.md) studies with non-reciprocal
  interactions, though this paper gives no model of it.
- **The rest of the batch.** Morowitz & Smith ([LIT-tmphuean](LIT-tmphuean.md)) reject
  entropy-production principles where this paper replaces them with
  gradient destruction; both read the biosphere as a channel for energy
  flow. England's and Perunov's papers ([LIT-tmp2fe4j](LIT-tmp2fe4j.md), [LIT-tmp7ay36](LIT-tmp7ay36.md)) make
  a related claim, that driven matter favours states that absorb and
  dissipate work, as statistical mechanics rather than as a law of
  development.
