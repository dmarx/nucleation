# THEORY-907: In foraging tasks, higher stress or anxiety goes consistently with less exploration, and in one task with a predator to check for, people higher in somatic anxiety checked less and did worse, a reduction associated with greater aversion to detecting the predator

## Source

Chou, Wilson & Smith, [LIT-1483](../literature.d/LIT-1483.md), read in full in [NOTE-1127](../notes.d/NOTE-1127.md) from the
PsyArXiv preprint, version 1 (24 June 2024): pp. 20–23 and 28. The
primary studies were not read.

## What was actually shown

- Acute stress (cold pressor) and chronic stress (PSS) biased foragers to
  stay too long, whatever the environment's quality (Lenow et al. 2017).
- Generalized anxiety (GAD-7) went with less exploration when leaving
  required physical effort (Bustamante et al. 2023), and with less
  adjustment of choice randomness when the environment became volatile,
  where randomness paid (Lloyd et al. 2022a).
- In a foraging task with a predator, where participants could forage,
  check for the predator or hide, "individuals with higher trait somatic
  anxiety (STICSA-trait) exhibited less checking behavior (i.e., reduced
  exploration), which led to lower task performance" (pp. 21–22), and
  "these reductions in checking behavior were associated with a greater
  aversion to detecting the predator" (p. 22; Trier et al. 2023).
- Human open-field and elevated-plus-maze studies found anxious people
  staying near the walls and in the closed arms (p. 23).

The authors' summary: "Foraging-related exploration is deterred in those
with high anxiety, perhaps due to risk aversion and/or pessimistic
expectations" (p. 28).

What could have come out otherwise: anxious foragers could have left
patches sooner, searching more, as some bandit results would suggest; and
anxious participants in the predator task could have checked more, as a
hypervigilance account predicts.

## What this does not say

- **That anxiety, not stress or effort, is the cause.** Several studies
  measured or induced stress; one turns on effort cost.
- **That checking falls in anxiety generally.** One study, one facet
  (somatic), one task. Hypervigilance in other measures may go the other
  way.
- **The mechanism.** "greater aversion to detecting the predator" is the result of
  the study's further analyses as the review reports them; the primary
  paper was not read.
