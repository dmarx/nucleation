# NOTE-tmp2ro60: List — What is it like to be a group agent?

## Contribution

The paper separates three things that the group-mind debate tends to run
together: agency, awareness and phenomenal experience. It grants
Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md)) the first two for organised groups, and locates the
whole disagreement in the third, which only a psycho-physical bridge
principle can settle. It then gives the first IIT-based case, in print,
that actual group agents have little or no phenomenal consciousness, and
connects that to a normative claim from List and Pettit's *Group Agency*
([LIT-tmp068mw](../literature.d/LIT-tmp068mw.md)): because groups are not conscious, their rights are only
derivative.

## Key insight

Schwitzgebel's marks of consciousness (information processing,
self-monitoring, goal-directed responsiveness) are all functional, and
List accepts that a group has every one of them, as awareness. A bridge
principle that ties experience to function alone will then either credit
the group or be ad hoc. IIT is the one candidate that ties experience to a
physical, hardware-neutral quantity that does not track function: Φ, the
irreducibility of the system's causal dynamics to a partition. Function
can be organised in a way that is easy to partition. A society of
self-contained units, whose steering board still works when part of it is
randomised and whose members do the heavy computing, can be a competent
agent and still score low.

## Assumptions

- **Functionalism about agency (§2).** An agent is any system with
  representational states ("beliefs"), motivational states ("desires") and
  a capacity to act on them (pp. 5–6). A thermostat qualifies "in a very
  basic sense" (p. 6).
- **Naturalistic indispensability argument for group agents (pp. 6–7).**
  Premise 1: our best social-scientific theories (the theory of the firm)
  attribute belief–desire agency to collectives. With a thin Quinean
  criterion of ontological commitment, they are committed to group agents.
  Premise 2: take that commitment at face value, defeasibly (Fine's
  "natural ontological attitude"). The conclusion is a "cautious form of
  realism about group agents" (p. 7).
- **Group agency is not joint agency (pp. 8–9).** A joint action needs
  only individual intentions suitably directed; a group agent needs a
  single "centre" of belief, desire and agency. Joint agency is "neither
  sufficient, nor … even necessary" for group agency.
- **The awareness/experience distinction (§3)**, following Chalmers 1995.
  Awareness is functional and can be studied third-personally;
  experience is not functional, as the conceivability of zombies is taken
  to show (pp. 11–12).
- **Eliminativism (type-A materialism) is set aside**, "on the grounds that
  it does not do justice to the 'what is it like' question" (p. 14).
  Type-B materialism and Chalmers's naturalistic dualism are both kept,
  and on both the question becomes which bridge principle holds (pp.
  14–16).
- **IIT is assumed as the bridge principle**, in Aaronson's simplified
  exposition of Φ (§6.1), explicitly not IIT 3.0 (n. 17). For the argument
  it suffices that a high Φ is *necessary* for a high level of phenomenal
  consciousness (p. 34).

## Key results

All are informal arguments. There is no theorem, no data, and no Φ
computed for any system.

- **Group agents have consciousness as awareness (§3, pp. 12–13).** The
  FBI is aware of information in an investigation (Goldman 2004); an
  organisation hit by scandal attends to it; group perception is mediated
  by members and procedures. Even "wakefulness and sleep" fit: List's
  university goes on vacation and "wake[s] up" only for an emergency
  (p. 13). This is offered as realism, "not just metaphorical".
- **Survey of bridge principles (§5), each with its group verdict.**
  - *1a* Agency or cognition implies consciousness: too many false
    positives (thermostats, robots, groups).
  - *1b* Agency above a complexity threshold: ad hoc, and admits groups
    that "inherit the relevant cognitive capacities from their human
    members" (p. 18).
  - *1c* Higher-order cognition: nothing bars groups, but it fails for
    non-human animals (Carruthers 2011).
  - *2a* Mammalian brains: excludes groups, except brain-simulating
    populations (Block), but cannot tell sleep and coma from waking.
  - *2b* Synchronised neural activity (Crick & Koch 1990; Clark 2009):
    implementation-specific, and consciousness is lost in synchronous
    seizures (Tononi & Koch 2015).
  - *3a* Information processing (Chalmers's double-aspect view): a
    panpsychism, too unrestricted, and silent on the unity of
    consciousness.
  - *3b* Global workspace (Baars): permits group consciousness, but only
    "asserts functionalist conditions for access consciousness and then
    stipulates" that they are phenomenal ones (p. 24).
  - *IIT*: phenomenal consciousness rises with integrated information.
  - Desiderata for any principle (pp. 19–20): capture the paradigm cases
    (humans, chimpanzees, dogs yes; tables, combustion engines no),
    non-ad hoc, revisionary only in marginal cases and then for reason.
- **Φ, as used (§6.1, pp. 26–29).** Binary components, a state-transition
  function f on Sⁿ. For a bipartition {A, B}, EI(B→A) = H(Y_A | X_A) with
  X_B uniform. Φ is the minimum over bipartitions of
  [EI(B→A) + EI(A→B)] / min(|A|, |B|).
- **Evidence for IIT (§6.2).** It accounts for cortex versus cerebellum,
  and for loss of consciousness in sleep, anaesthesia and coma via the
  perturbational complexity index (quoted from Tononi & Koch 2015). Both
  are reported, not examined.
- **Three reasons a group agent's Φ is low (§6.3, pp. 32–33).**
  - *Reason 1.* Large organisations "are decomposable into functionally
    relatively self-contained units, which are each internally more
    interdependent than they are dependent on other units". So the whole
    cannot have high Φ; "at most, some smaller sub-units might".
  - *Reason 2.* Even a board or assembly, the "cortex" analogue, "would
    still retain much of its functioning" with part of it replaced by a
    random process, because members respond rationally and procedures are
    robust. So EI across the cut stays low.
  - *Reason 3.* Much of the group's processing is done by individuals; the
    "computational contribution made by cross-member connections, while
    non-negligible, is still moderate". List says Schwitzgebel attributes
    a related point to Chalmers and quotes it.
  - Verdict: Φ is non-zero but "would not be even remotely close" to a rat's
    or mouse's (p. 33). A "biomorphic" science-fiction collective modelled
    on a neural network might score higher (p. 35).
- **Against Schwitzgebel (§6.4, pp. 34–35).** List agrees that the US is "a
  candidate" for psychological states and has non-zero Φ. He says
  Schwitzgebel's "massively complex informational integration" is the
  informal functionalist notion, which the US undeniably has, and that
  Reasons 1–3 count against high Φ in the formal sense. "Glass largely
  empty" rather than "glass partly full".
- **Exclusion (§6.5, p. 36).** Where systems nest, consciousness is at the
  Φ-maximal level only. In a group, Φ "presumably" peaks at the members,
  so their consciousness excludes the group's even if group Φ were
  substantial. List records that Schwitzgebel rejects exclusion and
  does not answer him; he says the low-Φ argument suffices without it.
- **Normative implication (§7, pp. 37–40).** List and Pettit 2011 (chs 7–8)
  asserted that groups should be held responsible but not given
  individual-grade rights, and critics (Briggs 2012) said functionalism
  alone could not justify the asymmetry. If phenomenal consciousness is
  necessary for non-derivative moral significance, and groups lack it,
  then group rights must be justified by their service to individuals.
  *Citizens United* is the worked example. The final answer: "(close to)
  nothing" (p. 40).

## Concepts

- **Group agent** — a collective that meets the general definition of an
  agent; how it comes to do so is a separate, practical question (p. 5).
- **Joint agency** — individuals acting together on shared or suitably
  directed intentions, with no group-level centre (p. 8).
- **Consciousness as awareness / as experience** — the functional,
  third-personal sense (access, attention, wakefulness) against the
  "what it is like" sense (§3).
- **Psycho-physical bridge principle** — a specification of the functional
  conditions under which a system is phenomenally conscious; a necessary
  truth for type-B materialism, a contingent law for naturalistic dualism
  (pp. 15–16).
- **Low-entropy partition** — a bipartition across which neither side's
  next state depends much on the other's; its existence means low Φ
  (p. 32).
- **Normative individualism** — only individuals (and perhaps individual
  animals) are ultimate units of moral significance (p. 37).

## Connections

- **Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md); [NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)).** The paper is a direct reply. It
  quotes the "liberal enough" conclusion and the Chalmers objection, and it
  agrees with Schwitzgebel against neural-synchrony criteria (n. 11) and on
  not ruling out aliens by stipulation (n. 10). Schwitzgebel's later book
  ([LIT-216](../literature.d/LIT-216.md); [NOTE-188](NOTE-188.md), its n. 56) answers it by attacking exclusion with the
  sleeping-citizen case, which does not touch List's main, non-exclusion
  argument.
- **Minsky ([LIT-tmpqepho](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqepho.md), [LIT-tmp4jv7c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4jv7c.md), [LIT-tmp84goj](../literature.d/LIT-tmp84goj.md); Singh,
  [LIT-tmpxp9b8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxp9b8.md)).** Not cited. Reason 1 is a near-decomposability
  argument: a whole built from self-contained specialist units is cheap to
  partition. The same reasoning bears on a society-of-agents mind. If
  Minsky's agents are as loosely coupled as his "mindless" specialists are
  usually described, IIT would give such a mind little Φ for the same
  reason it gives a corporation little. List's argument is indifferent to
  whether the parts are minded, which is what makes it a bridge between
  the decomposition and composition questions rather than an anti-nesting
  principle.
- **IIT ([LIT-185](../literature.d/LIT-185.md); [NOTE-122](NOTE-122.md)).** The paper relies on Tononi & Koch 2015 and
  Aaronson 2014, not on the IIT 4.0 that Barrett et al. assess. Their
  finding that the algorithm is undefined for non-Markovian grainings
  bears directly on applying Φ to a polity, and so on C5.
- **Butlin et al. ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md)).** List's dismissal of GWT as a bridge
  principle for experience is a reason to doubt that [LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md)-style
  indicators can settle the group case either way.
- **Collective intelligence ([LIT-046](../literature.d/LIT-046.md))** and **"Real Patterns" ([LIT-220](../literature.d/LIT-220.md)).**
  The first is a computational account of what groups can do; this paper
  explains why that leaves experience open. The second is Dennett's
  stance, mentioned for thermostats (p. 6).
- **Tomasello ([LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md)).** The joint-versus-group agency distinction of
  §2 (after Pettit & Schweikard 2006) separates the kind of "we" that
  Tomasello's account of obligation needs (joint commitment) from the
  corporate agent List is asking about.
- **Kammerer ([LIT-tmpdpmaj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdpmaj.md)).** The other contemporary materialist reply to
  Schwitzgebel. Kammerer keeps a functionalist bridge principle and adds
  an exception for wholes whose function runs through parts representing
  them. List changes the bridge principle instead. The two are
  independent: List's argument goes through if the members represent
  nothing, and Kammerer's if Φ is irrelevant.
- **Functionalism and anti-nesting.** Block's China-brain style
  population simulation is the one case List's Principle 2a might still
  count conscious (p. 21). Putnam's anti-nesting stipulation is not
  discussed. Both are being filed by another reader in this batch.
- **Not held.** Tollefsen's *Groups as Agents* (2015), Theiner's "A
  Beginner's Guide to Group Minds" (2014), Huebner, Bruno & Sarkissian
  2010 on Chinese intuitions, and Knobe & Prinz 2007. Each was searched
  for in literature.d by name.

## Bearing on the record

- **For the bridge THEORY.** This is the strongest held source for the
  claim that agency can compose without experience composing with it.
  It should be cited for the agency/awareness/experience split and for
  the conditional (on IIT) verdict. It should **not** be cited as showing
  that a group's Φ is low: that is asserted from three structural reasons,
  and nothing is computed.
- **On anti-nesting.** It is not an anti-nesting principle. It does lean on
  exclusion as a secondary route, but its main argument concerns how
  partitionable the whole is, whether or not the parts are conscious.
- **On [LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md)'s open question** (does a formal IIT calculation put a
  polity's Φ above a brain's?). This is the closest held attempt, and it
  does not compute anything.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **No Φ is computed or estimated.** Reasons 1–3 are qualitative. Reason 2
  is about a board's robustness to randomising some of its members, which
  is a claim about function, not about the conditional entropies in the
  definition.
- **The simplified Φ.** The argument uses Aaronson's exposition, not IIT 3.0
  (n. 17), and assumes binary components and a known wiring diagram. List
  himself notes that computing Φ is probably intractable (p. 31).
- **Reason 1 proves too much, or too little.** Brains are also modular. The
  paper relies on the cortex being hard to partition and does not show
  that a group's units are more separable than cortical areas are.
- **The Chalmers reply is not answered.** Reason 3 is the objection
  Schwitzgebel had already met with global-workspace and "fame" models
  ([NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)). List does not address that reply.
- **Exclusion is used, not defended.** §6.5 records Schwitzgebel's rejection
  and moves on.
- **Conditional throughout.** The author calls the argument "tentative and
  conditional" (pp. 4, 40) on IIT.

## Open questions

- What is Φ, under any version of IIT, for even a small formal model of a
  committee or firm, compared with a cortical model of similar size? This
  is the computation the paper's verdict assumes.
- Does Reason 1 apply to a Minsky-style society of mind? If the brain is
  such a society, what keeps its Φ high: dense recurrent coupling between
  agents, which Minsky's K-line and c-line memos ([LIT-tmp84goj](../literature.d/LIT-tmp84goj.md),
  [LIT-tmp4jv7c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4jv7c.md)) would have to supply?
- Is a high-Φ "biomorphic" collective (p. 35) possible with human members,
  and would exclusion then strip the members of consciousness (the case
  Schwitzgebel's one-ballot reductio targets)?
