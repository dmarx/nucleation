# ARG-tmpdf15p: The Challenger-to-Columbia history is evidence that an organization can reproduce the conditions that keep a known hazard from constraining it, reforms undone included, though it says nothing of a second-order wrong

From the second share of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6aeea2bc-d280-83e9-ac1f-f6772adf263c).

## The inference

1. THEORY-140: in both accidents, evidence that the design was not performing as expected was repeatedly reinterpreted as acceptable, with "each survived anomaly justifying the next flight", a hierarchy that kept dissent from rising, and a safety organization dependent on the program it oversaw.
2. CASE-138: NASA corrected its launch decisions and its safety organization after 1986, and the board found in 2003 that related failure mechanisms had recurred.
3. So an organization can reproduce the conditions that keep a known hazard from constraining it, and can do so after an episode was repaired: CLAIM-tmptfuky's layers 2 and 3.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** Partly. Recurrence could come from budget and schedule pressure alone, without any reproduction of the representation; the board names both, so the history does not isolate the reproductive mechanism.
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** Only on the mechanism. It does not tell CLAIM-tmptfuky from CLAIM-445, since both predict the loop; it says nothing of a second-order wrong or of a duty of answerability.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** The board's finding is a reconstruction after the fact of two cases in one agency (THEORY-140's own caveat). It shows recurrence, not the counterfactual that a different regime would have prevented it.
