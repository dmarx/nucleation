# THEORY-tmpakm3a: A dynamical model whose variables are properties of parts and operations already identified by experiment explores the dynamics of that mechanism, while one that posits its parts de novo and is checked only by fit to behaviour shows at most how a mechanism of that kind could produce the behaviour; fit can rule such models out but usually cannot decide between competing architectures that each fit, and what decides is independent evidence about the parts

## Source

Bechtel and Abrahamsen (2010), [LIT-tmppbuqq](../literature.d/LIT-tmppbuqq.md), §§2, 4–5, pp. 322–323 and
328–332; read in [NOTE-tmpbljat](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpbljat.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The definition (p. 323).** A dynamic mechanistic explanation takes
  "properties of certain parts or operations of the mechanism" into the
  model "as variables or parameters, thereby anchoring that model to the
  mechanistic account".
- **The contrast (p. 329).** "Circadian modelers begin with an existing
  mechanistic account ... The characterization of the whole mechanism, in
  detail, is a prior task achieved by other methods." For cognitive
  scientists, "the computational model, with its hypothesized
  representations and operations, is the only available account of the
  mechanism", and "the only empirical check on this is the extent to which
  the model as a whole approximates the behavior of humans".
- **The consequence, in a case (p. 330).** Rumelhart and McClelland's
  single network and Pinker and Prince's dual mechanism each answered the
  other's new phenomena by modification. "In the absence of this source of
  constraint ... the ensuing conflicts are often irresolvable. The relevant
  models are underdetermined."
- **The same limit, four more times (pp. 330–332).**
  - Beer's walking controllers, the "less hypothetical" one, being
    partly grounded in neural research.
  - McClelland et al.'s consolidation model.
  - Hinton and Shallice's deep dyslexia network.
  - van Leeuwen's lattice.

  For each the paper says the inference to the actual mechanism is
  supported "only indirectly", or holds only "if it in fact employs"
  the model's organization.
- **What could have failed.** A de novo model's fit could have singled out
  its architecture, as when no rival fits at all. In the past-tense case
  rivals did fit, after modification.

## What this does not say

- **That anchored models are right.** Anchoring makes the variables refer
  to something. The equations can still "misrepresent or leave out
  critical components" (p. 325).
- **That de novo models contribute nothing.** They demonstrate adequacy
  (C1) and explore parameters (C2), and Hinton and Shallice's offered a
  new explanation of a disorder. Their standing is how-possibly.
- **That fit can never decide.** "Usually" is the paper's word. A model
  can be ruled out, and a set of rivals can be pruned to one if the
  phenomena are rich enough. The paper gives no measure of when.
- **A general theorem of underdetermination.** That comes from the
  record's own construction ([CLAIM-519](../claims.d/CLAIM-519.md)): over a fixed interface and
  horizon, a lookup table reproduces any agent's interaction law exactly.
