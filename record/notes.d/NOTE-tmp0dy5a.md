# NOTE-tmp0dy5a: Negro — Three arguments against metaphysical structuralism

## Contribution

The paper sorts the structuralist turn in consciousness science into
three programmes and argues that only one of them is in trouble. The
metaphysical claim, that "the phenomenal character of an experience is
fully determined by its relational properties" (abstract), is attacked by
three arguments. The methodological programme (structural constraints on
neural correlates) and the epistemological one (inferring others'
experience from aligned similarity structures) are said not to need it. Its
most useful tool is the distinction between being in a structure and being
the structure.

## Key insight

Saying that experiences stand in relations is cheap and widely accepted.
Saying that an experience is nothing but its place in those relations
makes the relations prior to the experiences. It then follows that adding
an experience changes all the others, and that a system with one
experience is impossible. Both consequences look false, and a structuralist
who accepts them must explain why.

## Assumptions

- **Local experiences.** "Experience" means a local experience within a
  conscious scene, not the total state, but "my arguments would equally
  apply" to a global version (pp. 4–5).
- **The target.** Lyre 2022 is quoted as the explicit statement: "any
  phenomenal experience is fully individuated by its place in a
  Q-structure. Phenomenal content and character are relational, not
  intrinsic properties of sentient subjects" (p. 5, citing Lyre p. 6).
  Clark 2000 and Tsuchiya & Saigo 2021 are the others.
- **Structure built on appearance.** The colour structure is "built upon
  how similar colours appear to human subjects", not on objective features
  of colour. Metaphysical structuralism "should be framed in a way that is
  neutral between, say, representationalism and sense-data theory" (p. 8).
  Rosenthal's objectively built quality spaces are set aside as "not a
  direct target" (fn. 4).
- **Metaphysical means necessary.** The view is read as true in every
  possible world, so a single possible counterexample defeats it (p. 11).
- **Physicalism is not at issue.** The intrinsic property Relational Mary
  learns "does not need to be a quale in Jackson's sense, since it can be a
  property that is fully reducible to the physical" (fn. 5).

## Key results

- **Three types of structuralism (§2).** Methodological: search for neural
  correlates under the structural similarity constraint of Fink et al.
  2021, "Neural substrates of phenomenal types must share the structure
  governing the phenomenal types they are associated with" (pp. 3–4), or
  build theories with structural explanantia, as IIT does. Epistemological:
  use structure to know what others experience, as Kawakita et al. 2025
  aligned colour similarity structures across subjects without supervision
  and failed to align neurotypical with colour-blind ones (p. 4).
  Metaphysical: "the fact that an experience is the way it is entirely
  depends on the relational properties that experience has with other
  experiences" (p. 4). The truth of the epistemological view does not
  entail the metaphysical one; support would be "at best abductive"
  (p. 5).
- **Three senses of "structural" (§2.3, pp. 5–7).** An experience has an
  internal structure (like an ammonia molecule); it is in a structure (it
  has relational properties, to actual or possible experiences); or it "just
  is the structure", so that "it has only relational properties". The first
  two are "fairly uncontroversial". Only the third informs metaphysical
  structuralism, and it follows from neither of the others.
- **Relational Mary (§3.1, pp. 7–12).** Mary has seen every colour but
  orange and knows the full intersubjective similarity structure, including
  orange's place in it. P1: if metaphysical structuralism is right she knows
  everything about seeing orange without seeing it. P2: she does not know
  what it is like until she sees it. The argument is "a specific
  application of Newman's problem" (p. 7). Two replies are answered.
  - *She lacks phenomenal knowledge of the orange-relations.* Then the
    relations are activated only by "coming into phenomenal contact" with
    orange, which makes "the relata ... metaphysically prior to the
    relations". That licenses orange being in a structure, not being one
    (p. 10).
  - *She can imagine orange, so learns nothing about orange itself.*
    Metaphysical structuralism needs her to be able to do so necessarily,
    and an aphantasic Mary cannot. "Relational Charles", told the
    similarity profile of a new species, could not picture it (pp. 11–12).
- **The novel experience argument (§3.2, pp. 12–15).** A system that has
  seen red, yellow and green sees blue; its structure changes from R to R',
  so "seeing red when R' is instantiated should not feel like seeing red
  when R was instantiated". P2: "A novel experience does not change other
  experiences in a subject's phenomenal repertoire." The same instability
  across subjects would undercut the intersubjective comparisons
  epistemological structuralism relies on (pp. 12–13). Replies:
  - *Build the structure on possible experiences.* Then modify the
    system's sensorium so that it can register new frequencies; its
    possible repertoire changes and nothing suggests its old colours do
    (p. 13).
  - *Experts do report changed experiences.* Finer discrimination need
    not mean changed content; after Dennett 1988 the change may be in
    judgement. The structuralist then has two options. Either "perceptual
    judgments, together with other cognitive capacities like attention and
    memory, are fully constitutive of consciousness" (p. 14), which "aligns
    metaphysical structuralism with the eliminativist claim that there is
    nothing more to experience than cognitive capacities (Frankish, 2016)"
    (p. 15). Or a clean line between perceptual content and judgement must
    be drawn, which is "both conceptually and methodologically unclear"
    (p. 15).
- **The lonely experience argument (§3.3, pp. 15–17).** If relations are
  prior, a system needs at least one phenomenal relation and so at least two
  experiences; identity of an experience with itself will not do, since
  the structures are built on relations sensitive to difference. P2: "It is
  possible to conceive of a system having only one experience." It is said
  to be nomologically possible too: a global-workspace system with one smell
  detector is conscious of that smell when the sensor fires and of nothing
  otherwise (pp. 16–17). Footnote 10 offers brain organoids as a further
  case; footnote 11 puts the burden on the structuralist.
- **What survives (§4, pp. 17–18).** Methodological and epistemological
  structuralism need only that experiences have relational properties, "not
  that they have only relational properties". "I could correctly infer the
  travel time between two cities just by looking at their relational
  properties ... without assuming that those cities have no intrinsic
  properties whatsoever" (p. 18). The cost to the methodological programme
  is that a theory explaining consciousness only relationally "cannot be
  comprehensive" (p. 18).

## Concepts

- **Methodological structuralism**: the structural similarity constraint on
  neural correlates, or structural explanantia in theory building.
- **Epistemological structuralism**: using similarity structures to know
  what other systems experience.
- **Metaphysical structuralism**: an experience is fully determined by its
  external relational properties; it "is the structure".
- **Local and global experience**: a content within a scene, or the total
  state of consciousness. A global experience can be related only to other
  possible global experiences (p. 6).
- **Relational Mary, Relational Charles**: knowledge-argument variants in
  which all relational facts and all other experiences are known.
- **Lonely experience system**: a system able to have just one
  experience.

## Connections

The paper builds on Jackson's knowledge argument and its replies (Lewis,
Nemirow, Conee, Dennett), on Dennett's "Quining qualia" for the
judgement/content point, and on Fodor & Lepore on holism. Its targets are
Lyre 2022 (LIT-tmp3sgbg, NOTE-tmpg26n5), Clark 2000 and Tsuchiya & Saigo 2021.
It credits Kob 2023 with the point that structuralist claims about the
nature of consciousness are "largely orthogonal" to the method's
fruitfulness (p. 17). Aldé 2026 (LIT-tmpac9l2, NOTE-tmp0qd7y) cites this paper
as a critique that rests on intuition, which his own arguments are meant
to avoid. Isaac 2025 (LIT-tmpwhbpm) is not cited by Negro.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpdga80 (constitution by interpretive organization).** This is the
  objection the claim already lists, and the reading sharpens it in two
  places.
  - *Relational Mary* is the realization problem A4 drew from it. The
    claim's reply, realized and not described organization, meets it only
    in part. Negro's Mary does not merely possess a description: she has
    all the other colour experiences realized. His argument is that the
    relations to orange can be realized only once orange is, which makes
    the relatum prior to its relations. A constitutive thesis that makes
    the experience the organization owes an answer to that ordering.
  - *Making judgement constitutive.* The exchange's note said Negro
    "considers making the relational organization of judgments
    constitutive". He does: it is the first option in reply to the novel
    experience argument, and he says it aligns the view with eliminativism
    (Frankish), "at the cost of denying the existence of phenomenal
    properties as independent of cognitive capacities" (p. 15). The claim
    differs from that option in its terms (TERM-tmpgqmop: interpretation is
    organization, not judgement, and mostly not reportable) and in its
    stated realism ("It does not say that experience is unreal"). But the
    claim does make phenomenal properties not independent of the
    organization, and Negro would read that as his first option. The claim
    must either accept that experience is not independent of interpretive
    organization and argue that this is not eliminativism, or say why its
    organization is not a cognitive capacity in Negro's sense. The pressure
    is the same as THEORY-633's, from the other side.
  - *The lonely experience argument* asks whether interpretation, which
    requires "a distinction" (TERM-tmpgqmop, part 1), can occur with one
    experience. Negro's smell system has only on and off. Whether
    distinguishing presence from absence suffices is a question the claim
    has not asked.
  - *Novel experience.* The claim's relations are causal and temporal as
    well as similarities, so it need not say every new experience changes
    all others. It need not say they cannot either. Whether a change in the
    organization changes old experiences is a prediction the claim could
    make and test.
- **CLAIM-tmp1lvwf and QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.** Negro's lonely-experience system
  is a sufficiency case in reverse: a minimal organization that he takes to
  be conscious only because a workspace theory says so. It shows how a
  verdict on a minimal system can be borrowed from a theory, which
  QUESTION-tmpc4j7p says an answer must not do.
- **CLAIM-136 (relata without primitive identity).** Negro's arguments are
  about phenomenal character, not about the identity of relata, and the
  claim is about communicative acts. Two transfer by analogy. His
  three-sense distinction maps onto the claim's options: non-eliminative
  realism says that relata are in a structure; the strict version says
  their identity is given only by it; neither says that the relata are the
  structure. And his novel experience argument is the holism objection to
  the strict version: if a token's identity is only what its relations
  differentiate, adding a participant re-individuates every other token.
  CLAIM-136 does not yet say whether it accepts that. The weak version,
  numerical plurality without differentiation, is not touched, and Negro's
  reply to the first objection to Relational Mary, that relata are then
  "metaphysically prior to the relations", is close to CLAIM-134's charge
  against the weak version.
- **THEORY-577.** Negro's appeal to Dennett, that a reported change in an
  experience may be a change in judgement, is the same separation of
  judgements from what they are about that THEORY-577 uses.
- **THEORY warranted.** THEORY-tmp7uasf states the paper's thesis: the
  structuralist evidence supports "in a structure", not "is the
  structure". It is the objection to THEORY-tmpv5rmc.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- Each argument turns on an intuition or conceivability premise: what Mary
  learns, that old experiences do not change, that a lonely experience is
  conceivable. Aldé (2026) says this is why such critiques "have failed to
  convince numerous scholars". Lyre accepts the holism consequence
  outright (dichromats experience all colours differently), so the novel
  experience argument's P2 is exactly what a structuralist denies.
- The nomological case for a lonely experience assumes the global
  workspace theory, which the author himself thinks is probably
  incorrect (fn. 9).
- The eliminativism charge against making judgement constitutive is one
  paragraph long. It is not shown that every such view is eliminativist.
- The arguments target the view that an experience is its external
  relations. A view on which character is fixed by internal and external
  organization together, or by causal and not similarity relations, is
  not addressed.
- The paper does not consider whether relations to merely possible
  experiences avoid the novel-experience consequence beyond the one reply
  it answers.

## Open questions

- Is there any test of P2 of the novel experience argument? Similarity
  judgements among old colours before and after a new colour becomes
  available (as in the laser-stimulation "olo" studies Aldé cites) would
  bear on it.
- Can the line between perceptual content and judgement be drawn, as the
  second option needs?
- What would a minimal experiencing system look like on a theory not
  chosen to make it conscious?
