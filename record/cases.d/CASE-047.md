# CASE-047: Gut microbes and the host's hunger and mood: a lower-level collective that can drive the person

## The case

A person's gut hosts a microbial community that produces short-chain fatty
acids, bile-acid derivatives, indoles and proteins such as ClpB, which
mimics a host satiety peptide. These signal through gut hormones, the
immune system and the vagus nerve. In rodents:
- colonizing germ-free animals changes their stress responses;
- transplanting microbiota from depressed humans partly transfers
  depressive-like behaviour.

In humans:
- some probiotic trials reduce subjective stress in healthy volunteers;
- strains with strong animal records have failed in trials;
- delivered microbial metabolites (propionate) acutely reduce food intake.

The details and grades are in [NOTE-706](../notes.d/NOTE-706.md) and [NOTE-703](../notes.d/NOTE-703.md).

## What it can show

- **Drive from below that is not a single part.** The source is a community
  whose own drive profile ([TERM-051](../terms.d/TERM-051.md)) is likely diffuse.
- **Drive that changes with time.** Hunger and mood vary, and the
  microbiome's contribution varies with diet and state.
- **Membership in question.** The holobiont reading ([LIT-709](../literature.d/LIT-709.md)) and the
  threshold criterion ([CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)) bear on whether these microbes are
  part of the person.

As a documented case it shows only what the evidence does: routes, and
causal effects in animals. That the owner's own hunger and mood are driven
this way is the claim ([CLAIM-166](../claims.d/CLAIM-166.md)), not the case.
