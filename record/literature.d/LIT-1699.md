summary: >-
  A five-page review, by Aron's group, of research on the self-expansion model's principle of inclusion of other in the self (IOS) in long-term romantic relationships. In a close relationship each partner treats the other's resources, perspectives and identities as to some extent their own (Aron et al. 1991). The single-item IOS circle scale is the standard measure. It tracks feeling and behaving close and correlates with cognitive signs of inclusion, such as slower self-trait judgements where the partner differs and self–partner confusions in memory. Studies since 2013 link greater overlap to self-like treatment of the partner: comparisons, pain and sleep, and honour-based aggression. They also add a two-dimensional model of relational self-change and a perceived-inclusion construct. No new data.
