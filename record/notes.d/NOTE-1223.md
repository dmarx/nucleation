# NOTE-1223: Baez & Fong — A compositional framework for passive linear networks

## Contribution

It gives circuits of passive linear two-terminal components an explicitly
compositional semantics. A circuit with marked inputs and outputs is a
morphism in a category Circ, composition is gluing outputs to inputs by
pushout, and a functor sends each circuit to its external behaviour. The
authors' own account of what is new: "All we are adding is an emphasis on
symplectic geometry and an explicitly compositional framework, which
clarifies the way a larger circuit can be built from smaller pieces" (p. 3).
The main theorem is that this "black box functor" exists and preserves the
hypergraph structure (Thm 1.1, p. 7; proved as Thm 7.7, p. 47, and
identified with Thm 1.1's formula in Section 8, p. 51). Along the way it
proves that eliminating interior nodes of a Dirichlet form gives a Dirichlet
form over any field with positive elements (Thm 3.3, Lemma 3.4).

## Key insight

The external behaviour of a circuit is a subspace, not a function: the set of
potential–current pairs its terminals can jointly take. For a circuit of
resistors that subspace is the graph of the differential of the power
functional Q, the quadratic form left when the interior potentials are chosen
to minimize power: "the current at any terminal can be obtained by
differentiating Q with respect to the potential at this terminal" (p. 4).
Writing behaviours as Lagrangian subspaces rather than as quadratic forms is
what makes them compose with identities, because an ideal wire has no
quadratic form but does have a Lagrangian relation (Sec. 3.3, pp. 22–23).
Black-boxing forgets the interior, and does so functorially: "we can compute
the black boxed version of a circuit made of parts by computing the black
boxed versions of the parts and then composing them" (p. 7).

## Assumptions

- **Passive, linear, two-terminal components only.** "We only study circuits
  built from passive components … we exclude batteries and current sources.
  We only consider components that respond linearly to an applied voltage …
  we exclude components such as nonlinear resistors or diodes. Finally, we
  only consider components with one input and one output … Thus, we also
  exclude transformers" (p. 3). More general circuits are left to a companion
  paper ([3], Baez, Coya and Rebro).
- **A field with positive elements (F, F⁺)** (Def. 2.10, p. 15): F⁺ contains
  c² for every nonzero c and is closed under addition, multiplication and
  division. It follows that the field has characteristic zero. Resistances
  live in (ℝ, (0, ∞)).
- **Time dependence only through the Laplace transform**, with "(i) zero
  initial conditions (that is, f(0) = 0) and (ii) Laplace transform lying in
  the field" ℝ(s) of real rational functions (p. 16). Impedances are sL
  (inductor), 1/(sC) (capacitor) and R (resistor), in
  ℝ(s)⁺ = {Z ∈ ℝ(s) : Z ≠ 0 and s > 0 ⟹ Z(s) ≥ 0} (p. 17). The authors warn
  that this positivity "differs from the one more commonly used in circuit
  theory [11], which gives a set that is not closed under multiplication"
  (p. 17).
- **Minimization becomes stationarity over a general field.** "Although it is
  not clear what it means to minimize over the field F, we can use formal
  derivatives to formulate a version of the principle of minimum power. This
  is actually a 'variational principle'" (p. 18). Over ℝ(s) the interior is
  fixed by vanishing formal derivatives, i.e. Kirchhoff's current law at
  interior nodes (Thm 3.5, p. 22), not by a minimum.
- **Circuits are labelled graphs** with nodes N, edges E, source and target
  maps and an impedance on each edge, and an open circuit is a cospan
  X → N ← Y of finite sets with such a circuit on N (Def. 5.1, p. 29).

## Key results

### Part I: the power functional (Sections 2–3)

- **Minimum power is Kirchhoff's current law** (Prop. 2.5, p. 11): an
  extension φ of a boundary potential ψ obeys the principle of minimum power
  "if and only if the current I(e) = (1/r(e))(φ(t(e)) − φ(s(e))) obeys
  Kirchhoff's current law."
- **The power functional** Q(ψ) = min_{φ|∂N = ψ} P(φ), with
  P(φ) = ½ Σ_e (1/r(e))(φ(t(e)) − φ(s(e)))² (Def. 2.3, 2.7, pp. 11, 13).
  "We call Q the 'power functional' because Q(ψ) equals ½ times the power
  dissipated by the circuit when the boundary voltage is ψ" (p. 13).
- **Prop. 2.8 (p. 13)**: "Suppose ψ ∈ ℝ^∂N. Suppose φ is any extension of ψ
  minimizing the power. Then dQ_ψ ∈ (ℝ^∂N)* ≅ ℝ^∂N gives the boundary current
  of the current induced by the potential φ." The comment after the proof:
  "This is fundamental: the way power depends on boundary potentials
  completely characterizes the way boundary currents depend on boundary
  potentials" (p. 14).
- **Equivalence from outside** (Sec. 2.4, p. 13): "circuits with different
  power functionals have different relations between boundary potentials and
  boundary currents. So, two circuits are equivalent, as far as what can be
  observed 'from outside', if they have the same power functional."
- **Example 2.9, resistors in series (pp. 14–15).** For A —r_AB— B —r_BC— C
  with input A and output C, minimizing over φ(B) = x gives "x is the
  r-weighted average of ψ(A) and ψ(C)": x = (r_BC ψ(A) + r_AB ψ(C)) /
  (r_BC + r_AB), and Q(ψ) = ½ · (1/(r_AB + r_BC)) · (ψ(A) − ψ(C))², the power
  functional of a single resistor r_AB + r_BC, "and so these two circuits are
  equivalent."
- **Prop. 2.12 (p. 15)**: "A function Q : ℝ^∂N → ℝ is the power functional
  for some circuit if and only if it is a Dirichlet form over (ℝ, ℝ⁺)." The
  proof is a one-line appeal to the star-mesh transform, "every circuit of
  linear resistors is equivalent to some complete graph of resistors between
  its terminals", citing [29]. So circuits map onto Dirichlet forms on ∂N,
  "mapping two circuits to the same Dirichlet form if and only if they have
  the same external behavior" (p. 15).
- **Dirichlet form** (Def. 2.11, p. 15): Q(ψ) = Σ_{i,j∈S} c_ij (ψ_i − ψ_j)²
  with nonnegative c_ij. Not every nonnegative quadratic form is one:
  (ψ₁ + ψ₂)² is not.
- **Theorem 3.3 (p. 18)**, quoted: "Given a passive linear circuit
  (N, E, s, t, Z), suppose R ⊆ N and ψ ∈ F^R. Then: (i) There exists a
  potential φ ∈ F^N obeying the principle of minimum power for ψ. (ii) If φ
  and φ′ both obey the principle of minimum power for ψ, then P(φ) = P(φ′).
  (iii) If φ and φ′ both obey the principle of minimum power for ψ, then
  φ − φ′ is constant on every connected component of the circuit's
  underlying graph. (iv) There exists a unique potential f(ψ) ∈ F^N that
  obeys the principle of minimum power for ψ and vanishes on every connected
  component not containing a node in R. (v) The potential f(ψ) depends
  linearly on ψ. (vi) If P : F^N → F is the extended power functional, then
  the power functional Q : F^R → F given by Q(ψ) = P(f(ψ)) is a Dirichlet
  form on R." The proof of (i) is a block-matrix argument (im Bᵀ ⊆ im A, by
  contradiction with nonnegativity); (vi) is induction on Lemma 3.4.
- **Lemma 3.4 (p. 21)**: "Let P be a Dirichlet form on a set S and let
  s ∈ S. Then the quadratic form min_{s} P is a Dirichlet form on S \ {s}."
  The proof sets φ_s = Σ_k c_ks φ_k / Σ_k c_ks and ends with the explicit
  form min_{s} P(ψ) = Σ_{i,j} (c_ij + c_is c_js / Σ_k c_ks)(ψ_i − ψ_j)²
  (p. 22; reconstructed from the extraction, which garbles the fraction).
  This is one-node elimination of a weighted graph Laplacian, the operation
  usually called Kron reduction; the paper does not use that name or
  "Schur complement".
- **Theorem 3.5 (p. 22)**: over any (F, F⁺), φ obeys the principle of minimum
  power for ψ iff the edge currents obey Kirchhoff's current law on N \ ∂N,
  and then dQ_ψ equals the boundary current. ("The proofs are the same as
  before, with formal derivatives replacing derivatives.")
- **Section 3.3 and Prop. 3.6 (pp. 22–23): Dirichlet forms do not form a
  category.** Composition is (P ∘ Q)(α, γ) = min_T Q(α, β) + P(β, γ), "the
  power used by the entire circuit is just the sum of the power used by its
  parts"; it is well defined by Thm 3.3 and associative, but "there is
  typically no Dirichlet form 1_S ∈ D(S, S) playing the role of the
  identity." For a one-element S, an identity k(β − γ)² would need
  cα² = kc/(k + c) α² for all c, which "only holds when c = 0". In the real
  case large k gives "'approximate identities'", resistors close to zero
  resistance, but "the power functional of a purely conductive wire is
  undefined; the formula for it involves division by zero." Prop. 3.6: there
  is a semicategory with finite sets as objects and Dirichlet forms as
  morphisms.

### Part II: categories of circuits (Sections 4–5)

- **Decorated cospans** (Lemmas 4.2–4.4, pp. 27–28, from Fong [17]): a lax
  symmetric monoidal functor F : (FinSet, +) → (Set, ×) yields a hypergraph
  category FCospan of cospans whose apex carries an element of F N, composed
  by pushout; a monoidal natural transformation yields a hypergraph functor.
- **Circ** (Def. 5.3, Cor. 5.4, p. 30): the decorated cospan category of
  passive linear circuits; it is a hypergraph category. Composition "expresses
  the fact that we can connect the outputs of one circuit to the inputs of the
  next" (p. 30).
- **Thm 5.7 (p. 31)**: a strict hypergraph functor A : Circ → DirichCospan
  sending a circuit to its extended power functional P on all nodes. A is not
  faithful: two parallel resistors r₁, r₂ go to the same image as one of
  resistance 1/(1/r₁ + 1/r₂) (p. 32).
- **Lemma 5.12 (p. 34)**: Graph(dP) = {(φ, dP_φ)} is Lagrangian in
  F^N ⊕ (F^N)*, and this is a one-to-one correspondence between quadratic
  forms on N and Lagrangian subspaces with trivial intersection with
  {0} ⊕ (F^N)*.
- **LagRel** (Def. 5.15, p. 35): finite sets as objects, Lagrangian relations
  F^X ⊕ (F^X)* ⇸ F^Y ⊕ (F^Y)* as morphisms; composites of Lagrangian
  relations are Lagrangian (Lemma 5.14, cited to [43] and [18]). Its
  hypergraph structure is transferred from LagCorel through Thm 7.5.
- **Symplectification** S : FinSet → LagRel (Prop. 5.16, p. 36), which
  expresses "when two wires join, set their potentials equal and add their
  currents" (p. 35): for the map {1, 2} → {3}, S(f) = {φ₁ = φ₂ = φ₃,
  i₁ + i₂ = i₃} (Example 5.17, p. 36).
- **Thm 5.20 (p. 38)**: a strict hypergraph functor
  B : DirichCospan → LagCospan, Q ↦ Graph(dQ).

### Part III: the black box functor (Sections 6–8)

- **Corelations** (Def. 6.1, p. 39): an equivalence relation on X + Y; they
  model circuits of ideal wires, whose connected components partition the
  terminals. FinCorel is a hypergraph category (Ex. 6.6, p. 41). Decorated
  corelations (Lemmas 6.11–6.12, pp. 42–43) are cited to Fong [19].
- **Thm 7.3 (p. 45)**: a strong symmetric monoidal functor
  S : (FinCorel, +) → (LagRel, ⊕), S(e) = Φ(e) ⊕ I(e), with Φ imposing equal
  potentials on each connected component (Prop. 7.1) and I imposing
  Kirchhoff's current law (Prop. 7.2).
- **Thm 7.4 (p. 46)**: the hypergraph category LagCorel. Composition relates
  the glued states "if and only if two conditions hold: (i) φ_Y = φ′_Y and
  (ii) i_Y + i′_Y = 0." The authors: "It is remarkable that this physical
  fact is embedded so deeply in the underlying mathematics" (p. 46).
- **Thm 7.5 (p. 47)**: an isomorphism of categories C : LagCorel → LagRel
  that flips the sign of input currents.
- **Theorem 7.7 (p. 47)**, quoted: "The black box functor is a hypergraph
  functor. On objects it maps any finite set X to the symplectic vector space
  ■(X) = F^X ⊕ (F^X)*. On morphisms it maps any open circuit Γ with
  underlying cospan of finite sets X → N ← Y and passive linear circuit
  (N, E, s, t, Z) to the Lagrangian relation ■(Γ) = (1_X ⊕ 1_Y) ∘ S[i, o]^op
  ∘ Graph(dP) ⊆ F^X ⊕ (F^X)* ⊕ F^Y ⊕ (F^Y)*, where P is the extended power
  functional of Γ." (Overlines marking conjugates are lost in the
  extraction.) Proof: "it is the composite of hypergraph functors A
  (Thm. 5.7), R (Thm. 5.20), Lag (Thm. 7.4), and C (Thm. 7.5)."
- **Theorem 1.1 (p. 7)**, quoted: "There exists a hypergraph functor, the
  black box functor ■ : Circ → LagRel, mapping a finite set X to the
  symplectic vector space F^X ⊕ (F^X)* it generates, and an open circuit
  (N, E, s, t, r, i, o) to the Lagrangian relation ⋃_{v∈Graph(dQ)}
  S^t i(v) × So(v) ⊆ F^X ⊕ (F^X)* ⊕ F^Y ⊕ (F^Y)*, where Q is the circuit's
  power functional." S^t is the "twisted symplectification", flipping the
  sign of input currents (p. 6).
- **Section 8 (pp. 48–51): composition is power minimization.** DirichCorel
  (Thm 8.2, p. 49) extends Dirich along injections by minN\im(m) P (Lemma
  8.1, p. 48); the composite of two Dirichlet corelations minimizes over the
  glued nodes not in the image of the outer terminals. Lemma 8.3 (p. 50):
  S(m^op) ∘ Graph(dP) = Graph(dQ) with Q = min_{N\Y} P, i.e.
  symplectification "enacts" minimization. Thm 8.4 (p. 51): the square
  B̃ ∘ Dirich = Lag ∘ B commutes, so both routes give the same functor, and a
  three-line computation turns Thm 7.7's formula (extended power functional
  P) into Thm 1.1's (power functional Q). "We see now that Thm. 7.7 is a
  restatement of Thm. 1.1 in the Introduction" (p. 51).
- **The worked black box** (pp. 6–7): a resistor r goes to
  {(ψ₁, i, ψ₂, i) | i = (1/r)(ψ₂ − ψ₁)}, Ohm's law; a single 2-ohm resistor
  "has the same Lagrangian relation as two 1-ohm resistors in series."

## Concepts

- **Open circuit** — a cospan X → N ← Y of finite sets with a passive linear
  circuit on N; X are inputs, Y outputs, and a node may be both, or an input
  "multiple times" (pp. 5–6, Def. 5.1).
- **Extended power functional P** — ½ Σ_e (1/Z(e))(φ(t(e)) − φ(s(e)))² on all
  node potentials; **power functional Q** — P with interior potentials
  eliminated by the principle of minimum power, a function of terminal
  potentials only.
- **Dirichlet form** — Σ c_ij(ψ_i − ψ_j)² with nonnegative c_ij (Def. 2.11).
- **Lagrangian relation** — a Lagrangian subspace of V₁ ⊕ V₂ read as a
  relation V₁ ⇸ V₂ (Def. 5.13); here the set of jointly possible terminal
  potentials and currents, "the subspace of attainable states" (p. 5).
- **Black boxing** — "like encasing the circuit in an opaque box, leaving only
  its terminals accessible" (p. 7).
- **Corelation** — an equivalence relation on X + Y; a circuit of ideal wires
  (Sec. 6.1).
- **Hypergraph category** — a symmetric monoidal category with a special
  commutative Frobenius structure on every object, compatible with ⊗ (Sec.
  4.2); its operations are "placing diagrams on the same page, rearranging,
  splitting, combining and terminating wires, and then connecting these wires"
  (p. 26). A **hypergraph functor** is strong symmetric monoidal and preserves
  the Frobenius structures (p. 27).
- **Symplectification** — the functor taking a map of finite sets (or a
  corelation) to the Lagrangian relation that "'copies voltages' and 'splits
  currents'" (p. 6).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md): its static computation is the paper's Example 2.9, and its
  memory case is covered in a different form, with no time-scale condition.**
  Put k = 1/r. The claim's E = ½k₁(x₁ − x₂)² + ½k₂(x₂ − x₃)² is the extended
  power functional of two resistors in series; the paper's interior
  x = (r_BC ψ(A) + r_AB ψ(C))/(r_AB + r_BC) is the claim's
  x₂* = (k₁x₁ + k₂x₃)/(k₁ + k₂), and Q = ½ · 1/(r_AB + r_BC) · (ψ(A) − ψ(C))²
  is the claim's ½ · k₁k₂/(k₁ + k₂) · (x₁ − x₃)² (pp. 14–15). The general
  elimination is Thm 3.3(vi) with Lemma 3.4's explicit coefficients
  c_ij + c_is c_js/Σ_k c_ks (pp. 18–22). Three qualifications. (1) The
  paper's quadratic form is half the dissipated power, minimized at fixed
  terminal potentials (Kirchhoff's current law), not stored energy at
  mechanical equilibrium; Thm 3.3 is algebra on Dirichlet forms and applies
  to the spring chain as well, but the paper never discusses springs (the
  mechanical analogy is only the table on p. 3). (2) The memory case: with
  inductors and capacitors the boundary relation is a Dirichlet form over
  ℝ(s), a frequency-dependent impedance, and black-boxing is exact for it;
  nothing requires the interior to be fast. In the time domain that is a
  convolution (my reading; the paper stays in the s-domain). So in this
  setting a composite always presents its boundary with an exact relation,
  and what the claim's time-scale condition decides is only whether that
  relation is approximately instantaneous, a question the paper does not
  take up. (3) The paper assumes zero initial conditions (p. 16), so the
  claim's initial-condition term Be^{Dt}y(0) is outside it, and over ℝ(s)
  the interior is fixed by stationarity, not minimization (p. 18). The
  claim's defeater ("black-boxing says there is none for the static
  energy") is supported: circuits with the same power functional are
  "equivalent, as far as what can be observed 'from outside'" (p. 13),
  "including how it interacts when connected with other circuits" (p. 7).
- **The exchange's A23 §IV**, "Their black-box construction maps a circuit to
  its external relation between currents and potentials while abstracting
  away the circuit's internal structure. Crucially, this construction
  respects composition", **is accurate.** What is proved: a hypergraph
  functor (strong symmetric monoidal, preserving the Frobenius structure)
  from Circ, decorated cospans of finite sets, to LagRel, Lagrangian
  relations between F^X ⊕ (F^X)* and F^Y ⊕ (F^Y)* (Thm 1.1, p. 7;
  Thm 7.7, p. 47). The target is Lagrangian relations, not Dirichlet forms,
  which form only a semicategory (Prop. 3.6, p. 23). A23's "a rigorous theory
  of precisely this phenomenon" is right for passive linear two-terminal
  networks and no wider: the paper excludes sources, nonlinear components and
  transformers (p. 3) and never speaks of constraints or apparatus.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md)'s "a coupling between x₁ and x₃ that neither part has".**
  True of each part's relation, but functoriality says the composite's
  relation is the relational composite of the parts' relations (p. 7). The
  new coupling is computed from the parts' black boxes, not from anything
  else about their interiors. That is the precise sense in which composition
  here makes something new: new relative to each part, with no generative
  effect relative to the parts taken together.
- **[CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md): a demonstrated case of one specification with many
  realizations, and of the specification being realized by the interior.**
  The black box is many-to-one (2 Ω and two 1-Ω resistors in series, p. 7; A
  not faithful, p. 32), and onto for resistor circuits (every real Dirichlet
  form is some circuit's power functional, Prop. 2.12). The claim says
  "partly fixed" and is not defeated by many realizations; this supports
  that, in a setting with nothing interpretive in it. One point presses
  lightly on defeater (b), a constraint realized by nothing: the identity,
  the pure gluing condition of an ideal wire, is not the behaviour of any
  finite-impedance circuit. It is only approached ("approximate
  identities", p. 23) and is supplied by the formalism (corelations, Sec. 6).
  It is an idealization, not a constraint that individuates while realized
  by nothing, so it does not defeat (b).
- **[CLAIM-471](../claims.d/CLAIM-471.md): a theorem for its "physical gluing" in the linear passive
  case, and a nuance about "generate".** Composition is gluing by pushout
  ("the pushout of cospans gives formal precision to this gluing
  construction", p. 5), and the interconnection condition is equal
  potentials and opposite currents (Thm 7.4, p. 46), the effort–flow
  condition the claim names for port-Hamiltonian systems. Here, against
  defeater (b), the restrictions interconnection imposes are stated exactly
  as conditions on shared variables, histories included through the
  s-domain, and the composite behaviour follows from them. The nuance: in the
  paper the gluing condition at a port is not generated by the interaction.
  It is the same Kirchhoff condition for every interconnection, fixed by the
  laws (symplectification, Prop. 5.16 and Thm 7.3). What composition
  generates is the effective boundary relation, which then serves as the
  condition the next system meets. That bears on defeater (a): here the
  correspondence at each port is background structure, and interaction makes
  the composed relation, not the port condition.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md)** (separation makes constraints): the paper gives no
  support either way. Its exact frequency-domain black box needs no
  separation; separation would matter only for approximating that relation
  by an instantaneous one, which the paper does not do.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **Narrow class.** Passive, linear, two-terminal components; no sources,
  diodes or transformers (p. 3). The engineering analogies (p. 3) are cited,
  not developed.
- **Time enters only through the Laplace transform with zero initial
  conditions** and rational transforms (p. 16). Transients from a nonzero
  interior state and non-rational signals are outside it, and the paper does
  not translate its s-domain results back to the time domain.
- **Proofs partly by citation.** The hypergraph-category constructions rest
  on [17], [19] and Fong's thesis [18] (Lemmas 4.2–4.4, 6.5, 6.9, 6.11, 6.12,
  5.14); Prop. 2.12 rests on the star-mesh transform [29]. Not checked here.
- **The no-identity argument** is a computation for a one-element set; for
  larger sets the paper says "typically" (p. 23).
- **Notational slips in this version**: the functor B of Thm 5.20 is called R
  on p. 37 and in Thm 7.7's proof; Prop. 7.2 says "This functor Φ" for I;
  Thm 3.5 writes ψ ∈ ℝ^∂N in a section over general F; Section 8 cites
  "Prop. 5.12" for Lemma 5.12; references [8] and [35] carry "(See page .)".
  None affects a result.
- **Extraction losses**: the black square naming the functor, conjugation
  overlines and the Section 4.2 string diagrams. The Lemma 3.4 coefficient
  formula is reconstructed.
- The TAC published text and its page range (1158–1222 in the registration)
  were not checked.

## Open questions

- What is the time-domain form of the black box for an RLC network with
  nonzero initial interior state, and is it still functorial? The paper's
  zero-initial-condition assumption leaves this open; it is the setting of
  [CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md)'s memory formula.
- How far does the framework go beyond passive linear circuits? The authors
  point to [3] for more general circuits and to [5], [6] for Markov processes
  and reaction networks (p. 7).
- Is the approximately-instantaneous regime characterized anywhere in this
  framework, e.g. by the low-frequency behaviour of the black-boxed Dirichlet
  form over ℝ(s)? Not in this paper.
