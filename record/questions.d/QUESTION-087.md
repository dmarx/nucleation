# QUESTION-087: Can an authority structure that reproduces itself be pathological by a standard grounded in the organization's own organization, and not in goals assigned to it from outside?

## Why it is a question

A11 §6: "**Q2. What distinguishes self-maintaining authority from
authority capture?** Can an internally self-reproducing governance system be
pathological in a sense grounded in its own organization rather than
externally assigned goals?" A11 §4: "When an operator's policy repeatedly
succeeds and becomes trusted, what determines that its success is good *for
the organization*, rather than merely successful at perpetuating its own
authority?"

The last filing said this was protective lock-in and filed nothing. That was
wrong. [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) and [CASE-036](../cases.d/CASE-036.md) describe a self-justifying regime. They do not
ask whether the organization itself supplies a standard by which the regime
is a malfunction. [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) says viability alone does not, and [CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)
says the loop that builds merited trust also entrenches early advantage. So
the question is whether anything intrinsic separates the two.

The organizational account of function (Mossio, Saborido and Moreno 2009,
[LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md)) grounds norms in closure. Barandiaran 2025 ([LIT-1026](../literature.d/LIT-1026.md), unread)
criticizes its account of malfunction and proposes a "normative field" from
viability theory. An answer gives a standard, says where it comes from, and
applies it to the Northstar case, or argues that the standard must be an
observer's.

## Note of 2026-10-18: manufactured frustration and the standard for revision

The exchange of 2026-10-17 raised this question four times without naming
it.

- **Manufactured frustration** (A33 §8, A34): a common improvement is
  available and "the governance rule, restricted representation, or
  allocation of influence prevents the system from finding or using it".
  A33 gave it a test: "This is testable with an oracle or stronger solver
  for the same declared objectives and update constraints." The record now
  holds it as governance-induced frustration ([CLAIM-tmpl8vsy](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpl8vsy.md)). The test
  measures failure against declared demands. Whether the declared demands
  are the organization's own is this question.
- **A36 §1:** "slow governance is not necessarily better governance.
  Persistent commitments can sustain adaptive learning or produce
  rigidity."
- **A37 §6** made resolve need a revision policy, "To prevent rigidity from
  masquerading as resolve", and said what that requires: "an independently
  justified standard for when preservation versus revision is warranted."
  That standard is what this question asks for.
- **A42**, closing the resolve thread: the organizational analogue of
  psychological flexibility "cannot presume that an institution's
  currently maintained values are legitimate, or that successfully
  pursuing them benefits its constituents".
