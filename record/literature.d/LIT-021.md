# LIT-021: From the origin of life to pandemics: emergent phenomena in complex systems

Oriol Artime, Manlio De Domenico (2022), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A (vol. 380, issue 2227, art. 20200410; introduction to the theme issue "Emergent phenomena in complex physical and socio-technical systems: from cells to societies")* — [ARXIV-2205.11595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11595)

## Key takeaways

- This introduction to the theme issue proposes no new result. It surveys emergence from quantum matter to epidemics, adopts Chalmers/Bedau "weak emergence" as the scientifically relevant notion, and sketches an operational definition of emergence from local rules plus initial conditions. It argues that a rigorous, accepted definition and the physical conditions that favour emergence remain open problems.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

When many similar units interact locally with one another and their environment, unexpected patterns can arise at larger spatial and temporal scales. This is emergence, often tied to collective behaviour and seen in systems from synchronizing oscillators to flocks and schools. Despite abundant examples, there is no widely accepted rigorous definition and no agreed account of the conditions that favour emergence. The article gives a short overview, lists current and future challenges, and introduces and synthesizes the theme issue's contributions, from the origin of life to disease spread.
