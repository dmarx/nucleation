# QUESTION-tmpyc009: Does frustration belong primarily to the structure of incompatible demands or to the dynamics in which a consequential demand meets resistance, and how are the two senses related, given that neither non-attainment, disagreement nor effort suffices for either?

## Why it is a question

The frustration account ([TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md), [CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md)) is meant to explain how
processes meet constraints, resist one another, negotiate their
differences and develop arrangements that relieve their conflicts. Three
kinds of fact are close to it and are not the same.

| | what we know | is there an obstruction? |
|---|---|---|
| two processes' requirements cannot both be met | the requirements are structurally incompatible | yes, to joint satisfaction, relative to the constraints |
| a requirement is attainable and the organization does not meet it | an available possibility went unrealized | not established |
| a process acts to meet its requirement and another blocks or overrides it | a causal pathway to satisfaction is counteracted | yes, given a causal account |

The middle row is non-attainment. An organization can fail to realize an
attainable outcome because it never pursued it, did not recognize it,
erred, or met an unexpected event. That can become frustration, depending
on its dynamics. It is not by itself an identified obstacle. If every
unrealized requirement counts as frustration, the concept stops
explaining the developments it was introduced for.

**An obstruction needs a target.** There can be an obstruction to a
global section, to meeting all requirements jointly, to transmitting
information reliably, or to making an endorsed action effective.
"Obstruction of consequential demands" does not say which. A demand can
be unmet with no identified obstruction. An incompatibility can obstruct
joint satisfaction without singling out any demand as the obstructed one.
If A₁ = {a}, A₂ = {b}, a ≠ b, and the organization can attain either but
not both, each demand is individually realizable and the pair is not.
Which one goes unmet depends on what the organization does. The
obstruction belongs to the collection ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)'s compatibility complex),
not to a member.

**Two precedents in the record point to two senses.**

- **Structural.** In geometrically frustrated systems the interaction
  requirements cannot all be met at once. [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md) states it as the
  non-existence of a globally consistent assignment around a cycle of
  contexts. That is a legitimate obstruction, to a global realization.
- **Operative.** [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) (Vansteenkiste, Ryan and Soenens) separates
  need frustration, active thwarting, from mere low satisfaction. A need
  can be insufficiently met without being thwarted.

Both separate frustration from generic non-attainment, in different ways.

## Candidate answers on record

These are proposals, none adopted.

1. **Frustration as obstructed realization.** Drafted by the assistant in
   the owner's review conversation of 2026-10-18 and relayed by the owner
   as a recommended revision. The owner had not adopted it. "Frustration
   is a condition in which one or more organizationally consequential
   demands are obstructed from realization, relative to a specified
   bearer, collection of requirements, context, temporal horizon, and set
   of available transformations." Its cases were structural frustration
   and realization failure, with governance-induced frustration a
   cause-specific part of the latter. It was filed briefly as a term that
   superseded [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md), and withdrawn before merge. The withdrawal was
   not because obstruction is the wrong concept. It was because the
   operative criterion counted any unmet but attainable demand as
   obstructed, and because "one or more demands" placed on single
   demands an obstruction that belongs to collections.
2. **Structural and operative frustration.** From the following review,
   also assistant-drafted and relayed by the owner: "Frustration is a
   constraint-relative condition in which the realization of maintained,
   organizationally consequential demands is limited by incompatibilities
   among those demands or by identifiable constraints and countervailing
   processes. In its structural form, frustration concerns the
   impossibility of jointly satisfying specified requirements. In its
   operative form, it concerns the thwarting of an active requirement
   through the organization's actual regulatory relations. Mere
   non-attainment, disagreement, or high expenditure of effort does not
   suffice to establish frustration." The same review suggested filing it
   Proposed, not as a replacement term. The record has no Proposed status
   for terms, so it waits here.
3. **Frustration as constrained coexistence.** The same review's
   suspicion: "the most important concept is not frustration as
   non-attainment, but frustration as constrained coexistence among
   demands that retain organizational significance." An organization can
   accommodate divergent demands by differentiating niches, expanding what
   is possible or negotiating coordination. When it cannot, the demands
   stay in tension and some must be sacrificed, deferred, suppressed or
   regulated.

## What an answer must keep

Whatever is adopted, the reviews found these to hold, and they are kept
in the entries as they now stand:

- feasibility, actual satisfaction and the cause of a failure are
  different kinds of fact ([TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)'s note; [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md); [CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md));
- strain is an independent dimension ([CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md));
- maintenance is a legitimate form of demand, and a weak-preference
  acceptance set can express one ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md));
- attributions are indexed to a bearer and a reference collection of
  demands ([CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md));
- frustration need not be pathological or felt;
- the optimization diagnostics Φ and Γ ([CLAIM-365](../claims.d/CLAIM-365.md), [CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md)) are
  evidence about frustration, not findings of it.

## What would answer it

A definition of each sense such that a case of non-attainment with no
incompatibility and no countervailing process falls under neither, plus
an account of how the structural sense bears on the operative one. For
example, it might say whether operative frustration requires a
structural incompatibility among the live demands, or whether a single
demand can be thwarted with no other demand involved. A construction
that separates the two senses in each direction would settle whether
they are one concept.
