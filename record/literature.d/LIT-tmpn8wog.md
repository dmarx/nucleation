# LIT-tmpn8wog: Source Incompatibilism and Its Alternatives

Timpe, K. (2007). Source Incompatibilism and Its Alternatives. *American
Philosophical Quarterly*, 44(2), 143–155.

## Key takeaways

- Mere alternatives cannot satisfy a control condition (the luck
  objection), so any acceptable incompatibilism must appeal to source,
  ultimacy or origination.
- "Narrow" source incompatibilists (Stump, Zagzebski, Hunt) require no
  alternatives at all; "wide" ones hold that ultimacy is fundamental but
  that some alternative-possibilities condition is also true. Timpe puts
  Kane and Pereboom among those who keep an alternatives condition
  while making the source condition more fundamental (p. 147).
- Ultimacy entails indeterminism, which entails some alternative, so the
  absence of every alternative suffices for failing the ultimacy
  condition. The alternatives entailed need not be robust or voluntary.

## Why this is here

THEORY-316 states Pereboom's view that Frankfurt-style cases defeat
alternative-possibilities conditions but leave the causal-history
condition standing as the more fundamental one, from a partial reading of
*Living without Free Will* (LIT-1006, Unavailable). This paper argues that
the causal-history condition itself entails an alternatives condition.
