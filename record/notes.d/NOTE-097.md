# NOTE-097: Health and disease beyond social justice

## Contribution

Kukla (2014) argued that attempts to define health should drop naturalist and social-constructivist considerations, which fail to capture the concepts' normativity, and define health and disease purely in terms of social justice. Veit argues that this purely normativist project is self-defeating. Health and disease therefore cannot be defined by appeal to social justice alone, although justice may still properly bear on medical decisions.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (p. 2) places the debate in a philosophy of medicine that has moved from entrenched naturalist, constructivist and normativist positions toward hybrid accounts.
- §3 (pp. 5–8) sides with those who replace conceptual analysis with conceptual engineering (Haslanger). It distinguishes naturalist conceptual engineering (NCE; Carnapian explication is one form) from moral conceptual engineering (MCE), and classes Kukla's project as MCE.
- §4 (p. 8) argues that Boorse's biostatistical theory is not really an evolutionary account and may be better read as social-constructivist.
- §5 (pp. 12–14) gives the first problem: Kukla leaves social justice undefined. A conservative-majority thought experiment shows that the account could then justify the very medicalisations (of homosexuality, drapetomania) it was meant to condemn.
- §5 and §6 (pp. 13, 17) give the second problem: identifying medicine's tools and institution already requires the concepts of health and disease. Without fixing them, medicine becomes whatever promotes justice. Veit recommends pluralism: separate naturalist, constructivist and normative concepts, with justice connected to health empirically rather than conceptually.

## Open questions

- It is a worked case of the limits of ameliorative conceptual engineering, relevant wherever a concept is redefined for its social effects (compare [LIT-214](../literature.d/LIT-214.md) on blunted mental-health concepts).
- Check whether the circularity objection really bites against Kukla's institutional definition, or whether Kukla can take the institution of medicine as independently fixed, as Veit himself concedes on p. 17.
- The published version is titled differently, so check it for revisions.
