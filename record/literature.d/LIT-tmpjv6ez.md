# LIT-tmpjv6ez: Generative Entrenchment, Modularity and Evolvability: When Genic Selection Meets the Whole Organism

William C. Wimsatt, Jeffrey C. Schank (2004). "Generative Entrenchment, Modularity and Evolvability: When Genic Selection Meets the Whole Organism." In G. Schlosser and G. P. Wagner (eds.), *Modularity in Development and Evolution*, Chicago: University of Chicago Press, pp. 359-394. Read from the authors' manuscript of 2002, with proof revisions of 2003.

## Key takeaways

"We define the generative entrenchment (GE) of a node as the magnitude of this downstream dependency", on a directed graph whose nodes are "parts, processes, or events" of "the organism plus relevant aspects of its environment" (MS p. 2). More entrenched elements are more conservative. In simulations whose fitness decrements rise with downstream reach, or are simply assigned in classes, under truncation selection, strongly selected connections are kept and weak ones lost, and their loss anchors the strong ones further. Modular change needs rare conditions: small populations, very good conditions, non-lethal losses.
