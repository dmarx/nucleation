# THEORY-1198: Unlawful states of emergency, in declaration, conduct or duration, follow political turmoil far more often than natural disasters, are rarer in democracies than in autocracies, and last much longer than lawful ones

## Source

Bjørnskov, Voigt & Khesali, [LIT-1473](../literature.d/LIT-1473.md), read in full in [NOTE-1125](../notes.d/NOTE-1125.md) from ILE
Working Paper No. 51 (August 2021). What the paper reports is [THEORY-911](THEORY-911.md),
which this entry extends.

## The thesis

The abstract states its findings as facts about emergencies: of 853
declarations, 115 "are unlawful"; political turmoil is likelier than
natural disaster to be followed by an unlawful emergency, and autocracies
likelier than democracies; and unlawful emergencies last far longer.

## Why it is not held

Lawfulness is coded from English-language press and NGO reports, which
the authors gloss as "suspected to be unlawful" and expect to undercount.
Overlong emergencies with no recorded lawful extension are coded unlawful,
which builds part of the duration gap in. An independent coding from legal
sources is what would hold it.

Only the abstract was compared against the version of record, so a
discrepancy there in 97/14 or 321/115 would restore the demand for it.
