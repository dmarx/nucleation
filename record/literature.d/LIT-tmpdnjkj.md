  Gershman, Blei & Niv (2010). Recasts Redish et al.'s (2007)
  state-classification account of renewal as Bayesian inference. Each
  conditioning trial (reinforcement, cue, context) is assumed generated by
  one latent cause, drawn from a Chinese restaurant process prior that
  admits an unbounded number of causes; inference over the assignment of
  trials to causes is run online by a particle filter, and responding
  follows the predicted probability of reinforcement. Simulations
  reproduce acquisition and extinction, ABA and ABC renewal and the
  context-dependence of latent inhibition, which Redish et al.'s model
  mispredicts, and with the concentration set to zero, so that no new
  cause can be inferred, the loss of context-dependence after hippocampal
  lesions and in young rats. Qualitative fits to replotted data.

# LIT-tmpdnjkj: Context, learning, and extinction

Gershman, S. J., Blei, D. M. & Niv, Y. (2010). Context, learning, and extinction. *Psychological Review* 117(1): 197–209. doi:10.1037/a0017808.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpn30ni. Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **Extinction as a new cause.** "our model predicts ABA renewal as a
  consequence of the animal’s inference that different latent causes are
  active during conditioning and extinction" (p. 201). The conditioning
  cause's statistics are untouched by extinction trials.
- **Context is observed, causes are inferred.** Context is a feature of
  every trial; "the importance of context in our theory derives from its
  usefulness in disambiguating the latent causes of observations" (p. 201).
- **Against Redish et al.** No prediction-error gate on creating causes, so
  latent inhibition comes out context-specific, as observed (p. 204).
- **Hippocampus.** "We propose that hippocampal lesions disrupt the ability
  of the animal to infer new clusters, restricting its inference to
  already-established clusters" (p. 202), modelled by setting α to zero.
- **Qualitative only.** Simulations beside data replotted from other labs;
  no fits, no model comparison.

## Standing in the record

Cited by A's synthesis among hierarchical models of cognition. The model's
hierarchy is shallow (a CRP over causes, a Dirichlet over each cause's
feature probabilities): it is a nonparametric mixture, not a multi-level
hierarchy of knowledge. It gives a computational reason, consistent with
the inhibitory-learning view of LIT-1500, that the original association
survives extinction, and it is a worked case of inferring how many hidden
causes there are, set against the fixed pool of spare states in LIT-615
and the feature allocation of CASE-165. Filed from it: THEORY-tmp1zlbc.
