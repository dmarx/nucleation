The memos form a line by their own account. "Plain Talk" calls itself in part
a sequel to the frames memo, and K-lines complements "Plain Talk". The
filings declare that line as `extends:`, and *The Emotion Machine* extends
*The Society of Mind*.

- **"Plain Talk" does not claim to be first.** It presents the theory, then
  called "The Society of Minds", as unpublished work in progress with Papert.
  K-lines credits one principle to their 1974 lectures, which were not seen.
  Priority is left open.
- **The jokes memo is titled "Jokes and the Logic of the Cognitive
  Unconscious".** The Freud-like title in circulation is wrong.
- ***The Emotion Machine* disagrees with Damasio** ([LIT-383](../../record/literature.d/LIT-383.md) to [LIT-388](../../record/literature.d/LIT-388.md)).
  - The draft treats the body as one more resource. It takes "the opposite
    view" to the bodily account.
  - It reverses the causal direction for Damasio's patient Elliot, without
    evidence.
  - It makes feelings depend on self-models, where Damasio has feelings
    ground consciousness.
  - The two agree only that emotion-free reasoning is a myth.

Seven of the eight are unread, because the lawful full texts were not
reachable. Each `Deferred` seed records its publisher description, a contents
list somebody actually saw, and what was tried.

- **[NOTE-136](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-136.md)'s "argues nearly the opposite" is not yet supported.** As
  [NOTE-136](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-136.md) itself reports, Hofstadter's essay gives a criterion (a Turing
  test of consistent understanding suffices) and a forecast (conscious AI
  "perhaps even by 2033"). Neither says present systems are conscious. He
  plainly dissents from the editors' framing, but "opposite" needs the essay
  read. [NOTE-136](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-136.md) is left unchanged until it is.
- ***Fluid Concepts* reprints the 1992 critique as its chapter 4**, and its
  chapters 5–6 are on Copycat.
