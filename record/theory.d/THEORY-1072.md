# THEORY-1072: In Simon's analysis, whether a problem is well structured is a relation between the problem and a bounded solver, judged by practicable rather than in-principle computability, so it comes in degrees and has no sharp boundary; on that standard even theorem proving and chess fail some of the conditions, chess is well structured in the small and ill structured in the large, and problems presented by the world become well structured only in being prepared for a solver

## Source

Simon (1973), [LIT-1634](../literature.d/LIT-1634.md), §§2–2.4 and §3.6 (pp. 182–187, 197); read in
[NOTE-1232](../notes.d/NOTE-1232.md).

## What was actually shown

**The criteria are relational.** Six conditions are listed (p. 183): a
mechanizable solution test; a problem space holding all states; legal and
"considerable" moves; all acquirable knowledge representable in problem
spaces; exact correspondence with the laws of the external world; and all of
it "in the strong sense that the basic processes postulated require only
practicable amounts of computation". The criteria "generally express a
relation between characteristics of a problem domain, on the one hand, and
the characteristics and power of an implicit or explicit problem solving
mechanism, on the other" (p. 182), and their vagueness reflects "the
continuum of degrees of definiteness" (p. 183). Footnote 1 (p. 185) fixes the
standard: "effective or practicable solvability", not undecidability.

**The standard cases fail.** Theorem proving fails condition 4 "for all
problem solvers" once the solver may use metalinguistic, model-space or
analogical knowledge (pp. 184–185). Chess replaces "best move" with an
approximate evaluation, and its real moves are irrevocable, so playing a
game "involves continually redefining what the problem is … a WSP in the
small … an ISP in the large" (p. 186). This could have come out otherwise:
the two were chosen as the "extreme" and "best" examples of well
structured problems (pp. 184–185).

**The generalization.** "Definiteness of problem structure is largely an
illusion that arises when we systematically confound the idealized problem
that is presented to an idealized (and unlimitedly powerful) problem solver
with the actual problem" (p. 186). "It is not exaggerating much to say that
there are no WSPs, only ISPs that have been formalized for problem solvers"
(p. 186). The shift from computability in principle to practicable
computability is what moves the boundary (p. 197).

## What this does not say

- **That structuring is the real problem solving.** Simon calls that
  allegation refuted (by the Newell–Simon argument that a sequence of tasks
  from one environment would otherwise pose only one problem) and grants only
  that "much problem solving effort is directed at structuring problems"
  (p. 187).
- **That a problem is ill structured for every solver.** The relativity cuts
  both ways: a standard design on the architect's shelf makes house design
  not ill structured (p. 187), and the "no WSPs" sentence is hedged and about
  problems "presented … by the world" (p. 186).
- **That ill-structuredness needs new mechanisms.** That is the paper's
  further and weaker thesis ([THEORY-1084](THEORY-1084.md)).
- **Anything about questions or inquiry.** The paper's terms are problems,
  problem spaces and solvers; carrying it to what an agent can ask is the
  record's own step ([CLAIM-475](../claims.d/CLAIM-475.md)).
