summary: >-
  The SEP survey of the empirical egoism–altruism debate, revised in 2025. On the standard account, altruism needs an ultimate desire for another's well-being; biological and social-science senses of "altruism" settle nothing about it. Batson's experiments make a strong case against the aversive-arousal and social-punishment versions of egoism. Self-other merging is a different challenge: only numerical identity with the helped person would make helping egoistic, and none of the four measures used by Cialdini et al. and Batson et al. (IOS, "we", perceived similarity, trait-rating difference) tests for it. The authors report that Batson et al. (1997) avoided Cialdini's methodological problems and found empathy's role "clearly evident", and that a preregistered study (N = 680) supports the empathy–helping link. Altruism is still not shown: divine punishment, principlism, non-moral norms, and habitual or Pavlovian helping remain to be ruled out.
