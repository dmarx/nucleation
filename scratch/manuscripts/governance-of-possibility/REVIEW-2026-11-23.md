## Verdict

The draft is careful at the level of the single step. Its quotations, standings and figures are almost all exact, and it keeps the voices apart better than the first manuscript did. It also states its own gaps openly. The paper as framed does not yet hold together, for four reasons.

1. **Its model is described three different ways, and one headline result is false.** §1.2 says §9 "tests" the third claim. §8.5 says the model "finds" that output is identical across regimes. §9.3 says the identity is "built in, not found", and §9.4 says the tests "add no warrant". The abstract and §15.3 then report that "which regime is most ready depends on the deadline". In the model, II (and V, which equals it) is the most ready regime at every deadline. What depends on the deadline is the I/III-b pair and the cost ranking.
2. **The integrating thesis it leads with is not on the record.** The abstract and §15.3 present "adaptive governance is the maintained viability of a demand-appropriate response repertoire" as "an AI assistant's and Proposed". The record filed no claim for it. Both the audit and CLAIM-tmpcag0t's note judged it a recap, "'appropriate' is a free parameter".
3. **Its joins were not checked against what the accounts have already declined.** At least one (JD2) is a join that SYNTHESIS-tmp4zamw lists as "Not stated on the record". Others restate a single entry.
4. **Its disclosure of circularity is incomplete in ways that matter for standing.** Six of the Active entries its "firmer part" rests on were promoted the same day, by an assistant-run status review, and are left off the list in §14.7. The three viability distinctions it calls "developed in such exchanges" are from the owner's own essay.

None of this needs new research. All of it needs a revision, and most of the plan below is editing.

## 1. Structural problems, ranked

### 1.1 What the model is, and what it shows (abstract, §§1.1, 1.2, 8.5, 9, 15.3)

- **"Tests" against "no warrant".** §1.2 says "Section 8 develops this claim, and §9 tests it in a model." §9 computes no kernel. The separating case of §8.5 is the construction's own (the audit: "it is TERM-tmpz28l1's own construction. Nothing filed"). §9.4 itself says the model's checks "add no warrant to a claim that is already demonstrated". The divergence after a contingency follows from CLAIM-577, which the model implements. **Recommendation:** call §9 an executed illustration of demonstrated claims. Name what is new in it, which is exactly the outputs that depend on modelling choices: III-a doing worse than I, the U-shaped pL*, the step function, and JC5.
- **"Finds" against "built in".** §8.5: "Section 9's model runs this and finds output identical across every regime before the first contingency." §9.3 and analysis/README item 1: "The match is by construction: output does not depend on the budget." The skill forbids counting a measure that holds by construction as evidence. §8.5 and §1.1 should say "by construction".
- **A false headline.** Abstract: "which regime is most ready depends on the deadline". §15.3: "It also shows that which regime is most ready depends on the deadline." In results.md, "by readiness", II ranks first at every τ from 2 to 20. The dominance table has II dominating I, III-a, III-b and IV, and equal to V. The correct statement is in §6.2 and §14.5: some pairs cross (I against III-b, at t = 3), and the cheapest regime by long-run cost changes with the deadline.
- **Expectations before results.** Only the negative control states its expectation before the result ("the model predicts that it fails, and it can"). The skill asks for each expectation and its falsifier before the results that grade it. The motivating draft's stipulations (its Regime II and III outcomes, its "different means") are the natural expectations. They should be listed in §9.2 with what would falsify each, and then graded. Several of the study's findings were not anticipated, among them III-a below I and the movement of pL* with p. They should be marked as found after the fact.
- **Choices left out of §9.9.** JC5 ("a reserve serves any transition") rests on the novel route's threshold r(N) = 2.5, chosen between θ′ = 2 and the floor of 3. At r(N) ≤ 2, III-b's budget would cover it. This choice belongs in §9.9 beside the four already listed.

## 3. Scope errors

3.1 **Abstract: a reading made an identity.** "An organization's identity is kept by filtering". CLAIM-tmppqdnv: "Identity stabilization *can be read as* temporal filtering". The abstract turns a reading into an identity.

3.2 **Abstract and §15.3: the "unless" dropped from CLAIM-577.** "A budget set to last period's use forecloses any transition costlier than holding, so two regimes with the same present output can differ completely". CLAIM-577 holds "after one period spent holding it, unless the budget is raised from outside by at least the difference". The model contains that outside money (g = 0.1), which is why I's R(20) = 0.845. "Differ completely" is false of the model.

3.3 **§1.1: a sufficient mechanism made the typical one.** "a mechanism that produces the first ecosystem from the second by ordinary efficient management". CLAIM-577 is about one budget rule, and the record itself says real rules are "rarely pure spend-matching" (quoted in §9.10). The opening's ecosystem closes plants and lets suppliers go, which is consolidation (§11's route), not CLAIM-577's foreclosure by a budget rule.

3.4 **Abstract: the drill's condition dropped.** "a periodic drill is a reserve by another name" omits §9.4's "under peak accounting". CLAIM-878 says the accounting fails for "budgets in money or hours".

3.5 **§1.2: the model widened into a test.** "§9 tests it" (see 1.1).

3.6 **§1.2, abstract and §15.3: the quantifier dropped from the third claim.** GA2's qualification is missing (see 1.2).

3.7 **JC5 and §9.4: a single case generalized.** "a reserve serves any [transition]". A reserve serves transitions whose threshold is at most its budget. The finding depends on r(N) = 2.5 (see 1.1).

3.8 **JC7 (§10.2): one reading presented as the only one.** "It falls under none of CLAIM-556's six levels". On another reading, a capability reached once and lost by the budget rule is the affordability level ("Does a path fit the budget?") evaluated at the next contingency. Offer both readings, or argue for one. The reviews of CLAIM-556 cite a different counterexample (creep or fatigue).

3.9 **§15.3: half of CLAIM-tmppqdnv dropped.** "A filter … pays for its stability in lag, at a rate fixed by its own speed". This drops the claim's other half: the noise passed grows with k, and "neither the slowest nor the fastest adaptation is optimal in general".

3.10 **§9.10 item 1: a deadline-bound ratio made general.** "readiness differs by a factor of three or more at moderate deadlines". That holds for t ≤ 6 (at t = 8 it is 0.961/0.395 ≈ 2.4).

3.11 **§1.5: one exchange widened to the whole regulatory argument.** "comes from one exchange" (see 2.4).
