# NOTE-tmpj0rwa: Collins — Learning from the outsider within

## Contribution

It names the "outsider within" position and argues that it is an
epistemic resource for a discipline, not only a hardship for those who
hold it. It joins two lines: Simmel's stranger and Mannheim's marginal
intellectual, from the sociology of knowledge, and Kuhn's account of how
paradigms make facts. Black feminist thought, organized around three
themes, is offered as what the standpoint has produced.

## Key insight

The people best placed to see what a discipline's paradigms leave out are
neither its insiders nor those wholly outside it. The insiders produced the
omissions and share the assumptions that hide them. The outsiders may know
the omissions from experience but have no standing in the discipline to
press them. Someone trained into the paradigm who keeps the experience it
misdescribes has both the sight and the standing. The tension between the
two is the resource, and both ways of resolving it, leaving the discipline
or suppressing the difference, "espoliam a sociologia de sua diversidade"
(deprive sociology of its diversity).

## Assumptions

- **The sociology of knowledge premise**: thought cannot be separated from
  the material and historical conditions of those who produce it (Berger
  and Luckmann; Mannheim).
- **A shared standpoint**: Black women share a perspective on their
  experience, expressed differently by class, region, age and sexual
  orientation, whose contours may not yet be clear to Black women
  themselves.
- **Kuhn's paradigm** as "a constelação inteira de crenças, valores e
  técnicas" shared by a community, with two elements, the thought and its
  practitioners, and with facts made significant by theories.
- **Insiders by socialization**: one becomes an insider by long immersion
  until one thinks and acts by the worldview (Merton; Schutz).
- The argument is essayistic and draws on published Black feminist work
  (King, Gilkes, Dill, hooks, Rollins, Walker and others). No data are
  collected.

## Key results

- **The three themes.**
  1. *Self-definition and self-valuation*: challenging "o processo de
     validação do conhecimento político" (the process of validating
     political knowledge) that produced externally defined, controlling
     images of Black women, and replacing them. The insistence on
     self-definition moves the dialogue from the technical accuracy of an
     image to "a dinâmica do poder que fundamenta o próprio processo de
     definição em si" (the power dynamics underlying the process of
     definition itself), and questions "a credibilidade e as intenções
     daqueles que detêm o poder de definir" (the credibility and intentions
     of those who hold the power to define).
  2. *The interlocking nature of oppression*: race, gender and class
     studied as linked systems, with dichotomous oppositional difference
     (white/black, male/female, reason/emotion, fact/opinion,
     subject/object) as a shared ideological core. Oppositions are unstable
     and resolved by subordinating one half.
  3. *Black women's culture*: sisterhood, motherhood and creative
     expression as sites where self-definitions are made and transmitted;
     consciousness can diverge from outward conformity, so behaviour is not
     a reliable measure of consciousness.
- **A self-confirming status.** Among the dichotomies: denying Black women
  literacy and then claiming they lack the facts for sound judgement
  "ilustra outro caso de como se pode atribuir a um grupo um status
  inferior e depois usar esse status inferior como prova da inferioridade
  do grupo" (illustrates how a group can be assigned an inferior status and
  that status then used as proof of the group's inferiority).
- **Two kinds of anomaly.** Black women sociologists report *omission* of
  facts about Black women ("ao ler a literatura, pode-se facilmente
  desenvolver a impressão de que as mulheres negras nunca desempenharam
  nenhum papel na sociedade", Scott 1982) and *distortion* of them (the
  matriarchy thesis; explanations of poverty by low motivation). Because
  facts mean something only within a theory, correcting the facts turns
  attention to omissions and distortions in the theories of work and the
  family.
- **Who can see them.** Black women outside the academy may know these
  omissions, but "falta-lhes autoridade profissional legítima para
  desafiar as anomalias sociológicas" (they lack legitimate professional
  authority to challenge sociology's anomalies). Traditional insiders
  "certamente não estão em posição de perceber as anomalias específicas
  ... pois esses mesmos insiders sociológicos produziram essas anomalias"
  (are certainly not in a position to perceive the specific anomalies ...
  since those same insiders produced them). Black women who master the
  paradigms and stay rooted in their experience are best placed.
- **Generality.** The case is extreme, not unique: Black men, the working
  class, white women, other people of colour, religious and sexual
  minorities, and anyone uncomfortable with insiders' assumptions can be
  outsiders within.
- **The recommendation.** Keep the creative tension by "encorajando e
  institucionalizando suas perspectivas" (encouraging and
  institutionalizing their perspectives), and let intellectuals trust their
  personal and cultural biographies as sources of knowledge, rather than
  submerging them to become supposedly unbiased scientists. In the English
  abstract: "other sociologists would also benefit by placing greater trust
  in the creative potential of their own personal and cultural
  biographies."

## Concepts

- **Outsider within**: a person inside an institution or discipline who
  remains marginal to it, as Black domestic workers were inside white
  families that they "jamais pertenceriam" (would never belong to). In
  sociology, one trained into its paradigms whose experience they omit or
  distort.
- **Black feminist thought**: ideas produced by Black women that clarify a
  standpoint of and for Black women.
- **Self-definition / self-valuation**: challenging who has the power to
  define; replacing the content of the definitions.
- **Dichotomous oppositional difference**: categorizing by opposed pairs
  that gain meaning only from each other, are unstable, and are resolved by
  subordination.
- **Anomaly**: in Kuhn's sense, a fact the paradigm does not fit,
  here specified as omission or distortion.

## Connections

It builds on Simmel (1921) on the stranger, Mannheim (1954) on marginal
intellectuals, Merton (1972) on insiders and outsiders ("Insiders as
outsiders"), Schutz (1944) on the stranger and "thinking as usual", Kuhn
(1970) and Hartsock (1983) on the standpoint of the oppressed. It is a
founding paper of the standpoint tradition to which Harding's "strong
objectivity" ([LIT-tmpc03qg](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc03qg.md), not reached) belongs. Kay, Kasirzadeh and
Mohamed ([NOTE-tmpw3hh8](NOTE-tmpw3hh8.md)) cite Collins's 2000 book, not this paper.

## Limitations

- **Read in translation.** The quotations in Portuguese are the
  translator's; the English glosses are the record's. The argument's
  structure is not in doubt, but no wording here is Collins's except the
  English abstract.
- **No evidence that outsiders within detect more anomalies.** C1 and C3
  are argued from position and from examples of anomalies that Black
  feminist scholars did detect. There is no comparison with what insiders
  detected, and no case of an outsider within who missed one.
- **The standpoint premise** (a shared perspective, with contours unclear
  even to its holders) is stated, not defended, and Collins notes the
  diversity within it.

## Open questions

- Is anomaly detection in fact correlated with outsider-within position,
  holding training fixed? A history-of-science study of who first reported
  omissions and distortions later accepted would test C1 and C3.
- What happens to the outsider-within advantage as the position is
  institutionalized, as C5 recommends? If insiders' assumptions absorb the
  perspective, the advantage may dissolve.
