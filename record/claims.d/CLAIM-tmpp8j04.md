# CLAIM-tmpp8j04: Task-sensitive information preservation is established in semantic and goal-oriented communication; what is new is applying it to sheaf-structured pragmatic observations under sequential, context-dependent reconstruction

## The claim

A86 §9: "There is already an active literature on semantic and goal-oriented
communication, including semantic rate–distortion and receiver side
information. We should build on that rather than present task-sensitive
information preservation as an entirely new invention." It named a survey, Chai
et al. (rate–distortion–perception with encoder and decoder side information)
and Zhao et al. (2025), *Semantic Rate-Distortion Theory with Applications*,
which "explicitly studies ambiguity, polysemy, and distortion of conditional
semantic probability distributions. That comes close to our interest in
preserving distributions over interpretations rather than one purported
intrinsic meaning." The narrowed contribution: "**Apply information-theoretic
preservation criteria to sheaf-structured pragmatic observations, and study how
those structures change under sequential, context-dependent reconstruction.**"

## Where it went

C6 has none of these works and no concession. The manuscript's §1 disclaims
novelty for each ingredient ([CLAIM-tmp7cc3w](CLAIM-tmp7cc3w.md)), but its §5 presents task-relative
rate–distortion and side information with Shannon, Blackwell, Torgersen and
Wyner–Ziv only. Granted, because A86's own warning applies to the manuscript as
it stands. The cited works were found by web search (A86's cite tags); neither
the transcript nor the record establishes their details.
