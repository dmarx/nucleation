# THEORY-tmphw7hd: Ibrahim argues that collective capabilities, those that result from collective agency and that no individual alone could have or achieve, are intrinsically and instrumentally important for human capabilities, that their generation is good only when participation is free and voluntary and the agency empowering, and that the capability approach's evaluative space should therefore extend from the individual to the collectivity

## The account

Following Evans (2002) and Stewart (2005), Ibrahim takes collective capabilities to be those produced by the exercise of collective agency. Their benefits accrue to individuals and to the group, and no individual could have them alone. They shape individual capabilities by making choice a collective act and by forming members' conception of the good. Against Sen, who prefers "socially dependent individual capabilities", and Alkire, who warns of hidden dis-benefits and heterogeneity, she grants that not all collective capabilities are good. She states conditions: free and voluntary participation, no deliberate exclusion, empowering collective agency, preserved personal freedoms. She concludes that evaluation should cover the individual and the collectivity.

## What it does not show

- No empirical evidence in the piece itself. The self-help evidence is cited (Ibrahim 2008), not shown.
- No method for measuring a collective capability, or for telling it apart from a socially dependent individual one in a given case.
- It is a 2013 restatement. It is not the 2006 paper's framework or case studies.
