# QUESTION-tmp5fmg2: Once Qualified Competitors have used the index snapshot, the Glue and RankEmbed data and the syndication licence ordered in United States v. Google, do their results close the gap on long-tail queries, and does their search share move, compared with rivals or periods without the data?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-480 reports what the court in United States v. Google ordered: an
index snapshot, Glue and RankEmbed data shared "at least twice" under a cap
and privacy treatment, and five years of syndication. It leaves the
parameters to a Technical Committee (NOTE-004). Long-tail queries are about
90% of distinct queries. Of 3.7M unique phrases over seven days, 93% were
seen only by Google (op. pp. 89–95). Whether the sharing narrows that gap
is untested. On the EU Digital Markets Act precedent, privacy filtering
removed 99% of queries.

## What would answer it

An account of what the decree did once the data had been used. From the
old condition: "It is supported if the data measurably improved rivals'
long-tail quality and their share, and in trouble if rivals that took the
data gained nothing, or if the privacy treatment left too little of it to
use. The court's reasoning, the appellate history and the decree's terms
cannot settle it, since they say what the court expected, not what
happened."
