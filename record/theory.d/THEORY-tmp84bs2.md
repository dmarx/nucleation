# THEORY-tmp84bs2: Moats and colleagues (2026) argue, from ethnographic cases across European algorithmic deployments, that public values invoked without specification come apart from practice in five recurring ways (narrowing a broad value to a do-able measure while keeping its name, tickbox compliance that comes to redefine the value, imposing a value presumed shared, leaving conflicts between values to implementers, and vagueness that lets incompatible meanings coexist), and that these failures originate with those who state values, not only with those who implement them.

## The account

Moats and colleagues (2026) argue, from ethnographic cases across European algorithmic deployments, that public values invoked without specification come apart from practice in five recurring ways (narrowing a broad value to a do-able measure while keeping its name, tickbox compliance that comes to redefine the value, imposing a value presumed shared, leaving conflicts between values to implementers, and vagueness that lets incompatible meanings coexist), and that these failures originate with those who state values, not only with those who implement them.
