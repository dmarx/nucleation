# LIT-tmp9f0fn: Remembering: A Study in Experimental and Social Psychology

Frederic C. Bartlett (1932), Cambridge University Press, 317 pp. —
DOI-10.1017/CBO9780511759185

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read* (no lawful full text was reachable; see the history
note). From the publisher's description of the 1995 reissue
only: Bartlett's studies of memory, the best known of which had subjects read
and later retell a Native American story about ghosts, found that subjects
changed the details they could not understand from their own cultural
background. From this he argued that memory is a process of reconstruction,
organised by schemata, and that the reconstruction is in important ways a
social act.
