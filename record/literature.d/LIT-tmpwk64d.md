# LIT-tmpwk64d: From Modern to Postmodern Organizational Analysis

Chia, R. (1995). From modern to postmodern organizational analysis. *Organization Studies*, 16(4), 579–604. doi:10.1177/017084069501600406.

## Key takeaways

*From the abstract only.*

- What distinguishes the postmodern from the modern is "a 'style of
  thinking' which eschews the uncritical use of common organizational
  terms such as 'organizations', 'individuals', 'environment',
  'structure', and 'culture'".
- Modernist thought "relies on a 'strong' ontology ... of being which
  privileges thinking in terms of discrete phenomenal 'states', static
  'attributes' and sequential 'events'". Postmodern thought "privileges a
  'weak' ontology of becoming".
- The study of organization should examine "local assemblages of
  'organizings' which collectively make up social reality".

## Why this is here

Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy 2024 ([LIT-tmp3mudg](LIT-tmp3mudg.md)) take from it:

- that process perspectives reject the idea that "actions, interactions
  and the local orchestration of relationships are. . .the incidental
  epiphenomena of basic social entities such as 'individuals,'
  'actors/agents' or 'organizations'" (p. 581, quoted p. 1350);
- the "micro-practices of organizing" that "generate. . .effects of
  agency" (p. 596, quoted p. 1351), which frames the parent's literature
  review;
- Cooper and Law's "action is a happening ... it simply happens" (1995,
  p. 4), as cited in Chia 1995, p. 599 (quoted p. 1352);
- with Hernes 2014 and Chia 1996, the claim that process research "has
  not theorized what goes on in the events that are propelled by agency"
  (p. 1363).

The abstract agrees with the first two uses: individuals and
organizations are effects to be explained, and "organizings" are what is
studied. Whether the paper speaks of "effects of agency" in the words
quoted could not be checked. The abstract treats "sequential 'events'" as
a mark of the modernist ontology Chia rejects. The parent makes events
the locus of agency. The two senses of "event" may differ (the parent
takes its own from Deleuze and Massumi), but the parent does not note the
contrast.
