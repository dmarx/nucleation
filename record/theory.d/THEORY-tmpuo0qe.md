# THEORY-tmpuo0qe: Panpsychism escapes dualism's problem of mental causation only in its constitutive Russellian form, where micro-experiences constitute macro-experience; that form meets three combination problems, of subjects, qualities and structure, and every response that avoids combining (emergent, autonomous or identity panpsychism) gives back the advantage in mental causation or revised physics, while the combinatorial responses remain unworked

## Source

Chalmers (2016), "The Combination Problem for Panpsychism", in Brüntrup &
Jaskolla (eds), *Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives*, OUP, pp.
179–214, [LIT-395](../literature.d/LIT-395.md), read in full in [NOTE-358](../notes.d/NOTE-358.md) from the author's April 2015
draft. Section numbers are the draft's; the printed chapter was not seen.

## What was actually shown

**Why the constitutive form matters.** Panpsychism's attraction is a place
for consciousness in physics without dualism's problem of mental causation.
That holds only if micro-experiences are the quiddities of physical
properties and *constitute* macro-experience (constitutive Russellian
panpsychism, §2). The view must then build all of our experience from a
handful of micro-properties by necessary relations and no new laws, "a
little like trying to juggle seven balls in the air with both hands tied
behind one's back" (§9).

**Three problems, plus four (§3).** The *subject* problem, sharpest as
subject-summing: given 101 subjects, the first 100 do not seem to
necessitate the 101st. The *quality* problem, sharpest as the palette
problem: few micro-qualities, a vast array of macro-qualities. The
*structure* problem, sharpest as structural mismatch: the structure of
experience is not the structure of the brain. Then unity, boundary,
awareness and grain. A proposed solution "should indicate which problems
it is addressing, and which problems it is not".

**The arguments (§4).** Seven are set out with numbered premises.
Anti-aggregation, from James's nihilism about composites, is weak.
Subject-summing is "a significant challenge". Structural mismatch is "one
of the more powerful arguments" (§8: five premises, one of them the
structure-preservation principle).

**Non-combinatorial escapes cost the motivation (§5).** *Emergent*
panpsychism, which includes IIT read as a fundamental law, avoids
combination and inherits dualism's mental-causation problem. *Autonomous*
panpsychism, cosmopsychism among it, faces a decomposition problem "just
as hard". *Identity* panpsychism makes the macro-subject a fundamental
entity; its best form, quantum holism, faces doubt about stable
brain-scale entanglement and a structural mismatch.

**Combinatorial responses stay open (§§6–8).** Deflating the subject leaves
a "bundle combination problem". Seager's combinatorial infusion reduces to
identity or emergent panpsychism. Goff's phenomenal bonding must steer
between "a single giant subject", if bonding is transitive across
ubiquitous causal relations, and "far too many subjects" if it is not.
Functional accounts of awareness meet the conceivability objection.
Panqualityism avoids micro-subjects and leaves all three problems. For
structure, "perhaps the only viable line" is that experience mirrors
broadly physical, informational structure, and it is unclear how that
could be constitutive rather than "brute nomic principles".

**What could have come out otherwise.** A response could have avoided
combination at no cost, or a combinatorial one could have been carried
through. The case analysis finds neither, but it is an analysis of the
responses on offer, not a proof that none can work.

## What this does not say

- **That panpsychism is false.** The conclusion is that the problem may be
  unanswerable, or that answering it may point to the right theory (§9).
  The shortlist (phenomenal bonding or quantum holism, small palettes,
  informational composition, a somewhat deflationary account of
  awareness) is "not at all clear" to cohere.
- **That the arguments are independent of conceivability.** Subject-summing
  and the panpsychist-zombie argument stand or fall with the step from
  conceivability to possibility, which the paper defends only ad hominem:
  panpsychists who used it against physicalism are "not in a good
  position" to deny it.
- **That the subject-summing argument refuses group minds.** Its premise
  is James's twelve men, and it bites where a whole's experience is
  grounded in its parts' experiences. A materialist who grounds a nation's
  experience in its organisation, as Schwitzgebel does, is not its target
  ([NOTE-358](../notes.d/NOTE-358.md), Bearing).
- **That IIT's exclusion postulate is refuted.** Footnote 7 objects in one
  line that exclusion, like a view Nagel suggested, makes consciousness
  extrinsic. No case is worked through, and the objection is aimed at the
  2008-era postulate.
