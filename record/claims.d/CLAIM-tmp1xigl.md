# CLAIM-tmp1xigl: In any inquiry, anchoring is one mechanism, a fixed reference that each reconstruction is pulled toward, and what it secures is set by what the reference is tied to: tied to the target by a channel independent of the inquirer, it gives the inquiry's states content and makes a discrepancy register; tied to an earlier state of the inquiry, it gives continuity and not correctness; tied to nothing, it is bias

## The claim

The owner, 2026-11-21: "part of the issue here is that we're being myopic
about the literal luria record rather than the broader behavior of
anchoring during a general inquiry."

**Three anchorings the record holds apart.**

- **Transmission.** A restatement is held to its source, and without
  anchors restatements drift toward the collaborators' framings
  ([CLAIM-191](CLAIM-191.md)). [CLAIM-772](CLAIM-772.md) models this as what a chain of
  restatements conserves.
- **Correspondence.** A map between two systems says something only if it
  is anchored to "independently measured distinctions" and evaluated on
  tasks other than those it was fit on ([CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)). Without that it is
  vacuous or lossy.
- **Judgment.** Considering a value shifts a later estimate toward it
  "without adding evidence about the target" ([CLAIM-315](CLAIM-315.md), [THEORY-337](../theory.d/THEORY-337.md)).
  This is anchoring as a bias.

**One mechanism.** In each, a state of the inquiry (a restatement, a
fitted map, an estimate) is reconstructed under a pull toward a fixed
reference. In the simplest form, the reconstruction is a weighted blend of
the reference, the inquirer's own prior and whatever else enters, and it
settles at a point between them (Friedkin and Johnsen's step,
[THEORY-1161](../theory.d/THEORY-1161.md)). The pull is the same in all three. What differs is what
the reference is tied to:

| The reference is tied to | By | It secures | It fails as |
|---|---|---|---|
| the target | a channel independent of the inquirer: a measurement, an intervention, a specimen | content: the state says something about the target, and a mismatch with the target shows | a channel that only looked independent ([CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s uninformative correspondence) |
| an earlier state of the inquiry | storage and an address: a text, a code, a protocol | continuity: the inquiry stays the same inquiry, and its change is traceable | entrenchment, where the earlier state was wrong ([CLAIM-184](CLAIM-184.md)); staleness, where it was revised |
| nothing | salience | coordination at most | bias: the pull without the evidence ([CLAIM-315](CLAIM-315.md)) |

**So an anchor is good or bad by its tie, not by its fixity.** Every anchor
is a fixed point that reconstructions are pulled toward. Whether that pull
brings the inquiry closer to its target depends on whether the fixed point
was set by the target. "Stability is not fidelity" then splits in two:
fidelity to the source, which an anchor of the second kind secures, and
fidelity to the target, which only an anchor of the first kind can.

**Why an inquiry needs the first kind.** A discrepancy registers only
against something the inquirer cannot reinterpret at will. Without a fixed
reference, a conflicting observation can be absorbed by moving the
reference instead. Kuhn, as Kindi reads him, makes this the role of firm
commitment to a tradition: it "is what lets research recognize where the
tradition fails" ([THEORY-2096](../theory.d/THEORY-2096.md)). That is the second kind doing the
first kind's work: the commitment holds the expectation still, and the
world, through an independent channel, supplies the mismatch. An anchor is
what an obstruction is an obstruction to.

**The trade an inquiry manages.** An anchor of the second kind should be
fixed against the inquirer's own reconstructions (summarizing, rereading,
reasoning from memory) and open to the channel that ties it to the target
(new evidence, a failed prediction, an independent reader). Too open to
the first, and it drifts. Too closed to the second, and it entrenches. When
it is revised through the second, everything derived from it has to follow,
or the derived states are anchored to a version that no longer holds.

**The record as one instance.** The record's codes and verbatim reading are
anchors of the second kind. Its independent audits, fact-checks against
sources and status reviews are channels of the first. [CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)'s
nucleation, structure that can grow only on anchors, is the second kind's
contribution to an inquiry; it says nothing about whether what grows is
right. The two objections to [CLAIM-772](CLAIM-772.md) of the same day, stated in general terms, are cases of it:
the reader's prior that offsets every reading of an anchored text
([CLAIM-tmppkqzl](CLAIM-tmppkqzl.md)) is anchoring of the third kind inside one of the
second; the stale summary ([CLAIM-tmp0kqq4](CLAIM-tmp0kqq4.md)) is the second kind not
following a revision.

**Against [SYNTHESIS-066](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-066.md)'s refusal.** That review declined to file
"anchoring as the line's one condition": the join of [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)'s anchoring,
[CLAIM-342](CLAIM-342.md)'s independent evidence and [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md)'s competent uptake as one
requirement. Its reasons were that what the independent measure is differs
in each, a free parameter, and that "passes through the interpreter" was
equivocal. This claim does not make that join. Those three entries fall in
one row of the table, and the claim leaves "independent of the inquirer"
as that row's free parameter, as the review found it. What the claim adds
is a join across rows, which the review did not test: the pull that makes
an estimate biased ([CLAIM-315](CLAIM-315.md)) and the pull that keeps a restatement
faithful ([CLAIM-191](CLAIM-191.md)) are the same pull, and only the tie differs. The
admission route is the separating case: [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md) and [CLAIM-315](CLAIM-315.md) are
anchoring with the same pull and opposite effects on fidelity to the
target, and nothing on the record but the tie tells them apart.

## What it does not say

- Not that the three literatures use "anchor" for one thing. The claim is
  that one mechanism underlies the three uses, not that the word is
  univocal. Social ontology's "anchoring", what puts a kind's grounding
  conditions in place ([THEORY-487](../theory.d/THEORY-487.md)), is a further sense and is not covered.
- Not that an anchor tied to nothing is useless. It can coordinate
  inquirers on a convention, which is a gain where any shared choice beats
  none.
- Not that the blend is linear. The claim needs only that the reference
  enters each reconstruction and so enters its limit.
- Not that ties are all-or-nothing. A reference can be partly set by the
  target and partly by the inquirer, and the claim then says it secures
  content to that degree.
