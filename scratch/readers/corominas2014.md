## Summary

A sample-space-reducing (SSR) process is one whose set of reachable states
shrinks, nested, as it unfolds. The prototype: N dice with 1..N faces;
throw die N, get K, throw die K−1, and so on to 1 (equivalently, a ball
that bounces only downstairs, uniformly among lower levels). The
probability that a run visits state i is exactly 1/i (proof by induction),
so repeated runs give Zipf's law in the rank-ordered occupation
distribution. Mixing each step with probability 1−λ with an unconstrained
uniform jump over all N states gives a stationary occupation
p(i) ∝ i^(−λ), so the rank exponent is the fraction of nested steps, and
the frequency-distribution exponent is α = (1+λ)/λ ∈ [2, ∞). Examples:
word frequencies in sentence formation (λ ≈ 0.9 from The Origin of
Species), random walks on a complete directed (DAG plus return) network,
and fragmentation of a marked stick.

## Contribution

A new, self-contained route to Zipf's law and to power-law rank
distributions in general, with an interpretation of the exponent as a
degree of nestedness, offered as an alternative "without the recourse to
multiplicative, preferential, or self-organised critical processes"
(abstract, p. 1).

## Key results

- **Exact Zipf for the strict SSR** (§I, p. 2): "the probability that the
  process φ visits the particular site i in a sequence is the visiting
  probability PN(i), which can easily be shown to follow an exact Zipf's
  law, PN(i) = 1/i", Eq. (1), proved by induction on N from the recursion
  PN(i) = (1/N)(1 + Σ_{i<j≤N} P_{j−1}(i)).
- **Visiting and occupation proportional** (p. 2): because "any sequence
  generated by φ is strictly decreasing and contains any particular site i
  at most once" and ends at 1, PN(i) = pN(i)/pN(1): "an exact Zipf's law
  in the (rank ordered) occupation probabilities".
- **Nestedness is the defining property** (p. 3): Ω1 ⊂ Ω2 ⊂ ... ⊂ ΩN ⊂ Ω;
  "This nested structure of sample-space is the defining property of SSR
  processes. This type of nesting breaks the left-right symmetry of the
  iid stochastic process."
- **Noisy SSR** (p. 3, Eqs. 2-6): Φ(λ) = λφ + (1 − λ)φR, made stationary by
  restarting at N after reaching 1; the recursion p(i+1) − p(i) =
  −(λ/i) p(i+1) gives p(i)/p(1) = Π_{j<i}(1 + λ/j)^(−1) ∼ i^(−λ):
  "p(λ)(i) ∝ i^−λ", Eq. (6). Text: "an asymptotically exact power-law
  with exponent λ" (p. 4).
- **Frequency exponent** (p. 4): "if the rank distribution p is a
  power-law with exponent λ, p̃ is also a power-law with the exponent
  α = (1+λ)/λ, see e.g. [10]" (ref. 10 is Newman, LIT-2976), so noisy SSR
  covers α ∈ [2, ∞); "exponents between 2 and 3 ... relates to a mixing
  ratio of λ > 0.5".
- **Simulation** (Fig. 3a, N = 10,000, 10^6 restarts): ML fits λfit =
  0.999, 0.699, 0.499 for λ = 1, 0.7, 0.5 (Clauset-Shalizi-Newman
  estimator, ref. 24).
- **Convergence** (Fig. 3b, Eq. 7): 2-norm distance to the limit ∼ T^(−1/2)
  for both λ = 0 and λ = 1 (fitted β = 0.512, 0.463): "SSR process converge
  equally fast to their underlying limiting power-law distribution" as iid
  walks (Berry-Esseen for λ = 0).
- **Words** (p. 4, Fig. 4): Origin of Species has "a first power-law regime
  with rank exponent of γ ∼ 0.9 ... corresponds to a mixing parameter
  λ = 0.9"; caption: "about 90% of consecutive word pairs, sample-space is
  strictly reducing".
- **Networks** (p. 5, Fig. 5): node occupation is Zipf "irrespective of the
  value of pexit"; path-rank distribution is finite for the acyclic case
  and a power law for the cyclic one, matching Perkins et al. (ref. 27).
- **Fragmentation** (pp. 5-6): repeatedly breaking the fragment holding a
  mark at an endpoint "is obviously a continuous version of the SSR
  process φ"; the full fragment-size distribution is more complicated
  (ref. 29, Krapivsky and Ben-Naim).
- **Discussion** (p. 6): Zipf "emerges as a simple consequence of breaking
  a directional symmetry in stochastic processes, or, equivalently, by a
  nestedness property of the sample-space"; SSR is "a self-contained,
  independent alternative to multiplicative, preferential, self-organised
  criticality, and other mechanisms".

Checked by the reader (python3 -I, script outside the repository): the
stationary vector of Eq. (4)'s matrix at N = 2,000 gives log-log slopes
−1.0000, −0.7022, −0.5027 between i = 10 and 1,000 for λ = 1, 0.7, 0.5,
and p(1000)/p(1) equals the product Π(1 + λ/j)^(−1) to five figures
(0.0072183 at λ = 0.7), which is asymptotically Γ(1+λ)·i^(−λ) (0.0072176).
A direct simulation of the strict process (N = 100, 200,000 runs, start
by throwing die N) gives visiting frequencies 0.4997, 0.2000, 0.1001,
0.0199, 0.0103 at i = 2, 5, 10, 50, 100 against 1/i.

## Assumptions

- Jumps are **uniform** over the reduced space Ωi = {1, ..., i−1}; the
  1/i law is proved for this case only. Non-uniform jump weights are not
  treated.
- The noise is uniform over all N states and independent of the state.
- Stationarity by restart at the top (φ∞); the rank variable is the state
  label, which coincides with rank because 1/i is decreasing.
- One-dimensional, totally ordered state space; "no signs of
  multi-scaling" here, possibly in higher-dimensional versions (p. 6).

## Concepts

Sample-space-reducing (SSR) process; nestedness of sample space;
visiting vs occupation probability; directional (left-right) symmetry
breaking; mixing ratio λ as a degree of nestedness; rank vs frequency
exponent, α = 1 + 1/λ; phase-space collapse and sub-exponential growth of
the number of configurations (p. 6, refs. 21, 30-32); path-rank
distributions on cyclic networks; fragmentation as ageing.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Accurate. CLAIM-795 item 5 says "A walk that jumps uniformly to a lower
state until it reaches 1 visits state k with probability 1/k, Zipf's law",
which is Eq. (1) and Fig. 2 (right) exactly; its check (200,000 runs from
100) matches. The LIT's summary, "Processes whose set of reachable states
shrinks as they unfold ... visit states with Zipf's-law frequencies", is
the abstract's "necessarily lead to Zipf's law"; the paper proves it only
for the uniform nested case, so "necessarily" outruns the proof. Two
refinements the exchange did not carry: (a) the exponent is tunable,
i^(−λ) with noise, which A5 does not mention; (b) "history-dependent" in
the title means state-dependent: the strict SSR is a Markov chain,
P(j|i) = 1/(i−1), with no memory beyond the current state and no
reinforcement. The abstract's "exact power-laws" for the noisy case is
"asymptotically exact" in the text (p. 4); the reader's check confirms
the Γ(1+λ) prefactor and the asymptotic exponent.

## Limitations

- The 1/i law is for uniform jumps on a totally ordered set; robustness to
  other jump weights is not shown in this paper.
- The words example identifies λ = 0.9 from one book's fitted rank slope
  ("γ ∼ 0.9") with no independent measure of nestedness in sentences; it
  is a fit of the exponent, not a test of the mechanism. Ref. 26
  (arXiv:1407.4610, the same authors) is said to give the detailed model.
- Network example on a complete DAG only; generality is "conjecture" (p. 6).
- Fragmentation link is for a single marked fragment, not the size
  distribution of all fragments.
- Convergence exponents are fitted from simulation, not derived.
- Typos in the arXiv text: "pend = 0.3" for pexit (p. 5), "und" (p. 6).

## Candidate THEORY

**Sentence**: A process that from state i jumps uniformly to a state
below i, restarting at the top on reaching 1, visits state i with
probability exactly 1/i, Zipf's law in rank, and mixing each step with
probability 1 − λ with a uniform jump over all N states gives occupation
asymptotically proportional to i^(−λ) (frequency exponent 1 + 1/λ), with
no multiplicative, preferential or critical mechanism.

**promote_when**: An independent derivation of the 1/i visiting law for
the uniform nested process and of the i^(−λ) asymptotic for its noisy
mixture (the record's own check by proof or by the stationary vector
counts), and a source other than the authors placing the construction
outside multiplicative, preferential and critical mechanisms.
