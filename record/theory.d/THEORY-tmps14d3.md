# THEORY-tmps14d3: Underwood, Silbernagle, Nishimoto and Uyehara report that Hawai'i's endangered Coot, Stilt and Gallinule held stable or rising numbers from 1986 to 2007 where invasive plants, predators and water levels are actively managed, and hold that without perpetual management of those wetlands the birds would decline

## Source

Underwood, Silbernagle, Nishimoto & Uyehara (2013), LIT-tmp903lf; read in NOTE-tmpoxify.

## What was actually shown

- **Trends, 1986–2007** (Results, Table 2): statewide multiplicative trend
  per year Coot 1.0018, Stilt 1.0198, Gallinule 1.0686, from semiannual
  counts. These could have come out declining.
- **Disproportionate use** (Results): refuges, about 15 % of the wetland,
  hold on average about 36 % of Coots, 42 % of Stilts and 34 % of
  Gallinules.
- **The upkeep** (Introduction): weed control that "must be constant",
  predator barriers, and water management, and the managers' view that
  "all three management actions in concert are required".
- **One case of no management** (Discussion): at Kawainui Marsh, "one of
  the largest natural wetlands in the state provided habitat for only a
  handful of birds".

## What this does not say

- That the birds decline when management stops: no managed wetland was
  observed after management ended, and non-refuge sites lump managed with
  unmanaged.
- How fast decline would come, or whether it would be reversible: the
  return after restoration is unpublished data.
- That refuge management caused the trends: Gallinules rose faster off
  the refuges than on them.
- Anything from Scott et al. (2010), cited only for the label.
