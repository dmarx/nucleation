  Stone (1936), DOI-10.2307/1989664. The paper identifies Boolean algebras
  with Boolean rings with unit (xx = x), and generalized Boolean algebras
  with Boolean rings (Thms 1–4). Theorem 67 proves that every Boolean ring
  A is isomorphic to the algebra B(A) of classes 𝔈(a) = {prime ideals p :
  a ∉ p}, under symmetric difference and intersection. Its ideal lattice
  is isomorphic to the system of classes 𝔈(𝔞) under arbitrary unions and
  finite intersections. The existence of enough prime ideals (Thm 63) is
  proved by transfinite induction, and Theorem 70 shows that "every
  Boolean ring has an isomorphic algebra of classes" and "every ideal is
  the product of its prime divisors" are equivalent without choice.
  Theorems 68–69 classify *all* set-representations as restrictions of
  B(A) to subclasses of prime ideals. The topology, i.e. Stone spaces and
  duality, is explicitly deferred to "another paper".
