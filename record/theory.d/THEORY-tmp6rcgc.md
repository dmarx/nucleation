# THEORY-tmp6rcgc: Perception is constitutively iconic, nonconceptual and nonpropositional and cognition paradigmatically discursive, and percepts are put to cognitive use only inside a 'cognitive envelope', the global workspace read as conceptualisation, where working memory holds recoded, task-dependent abstractions rather than the percepts themselves

## Source

Block (2024), "Précis of *The Border between Seeing and Thinking*",
[LIT-649](../literature.d/LIT-649.md), read in [NOTE-498](../notes.d/NOTE-498.md). The book ([LIT-636](../literature.d/LIT-636.md)) is filed unread. This account
rests on the précis, which states the thesis and updates it; it does not
contain the arguments for the main thesis.

## What was actually shown

**The thesis (p. 273).** Perception is constitutively iconic,
nonconceptual and nonpropositional. Cognition is paradigmatically
discursive, conceptual and propositional, and can use perceptual material
inside a cognitive envelope ([NOTE-498](../notes.d/NOTE-498.md), C1: stated, argued in the book).

**The envelope (pp. 274–275, Figure 1).** Percepts compete in sensory
areas; winners trigger frontoparietal "ignition"; the reverberating
front-and-back coalition is the cognitive envelope, in which perceptual
content is held in an abstracted, partly conceptual form for reasoning.
The global neuronal workspace is reread as the mechanism of
conceptualisation, not of consciousness (C2, asserted here).

**Working memory (pp. 277–279).** Nine lines of evidence that
working-memory representations differ from perceptual ones and are
task-dependent abstractions, including Duan and Curtis (2024). Since 2022
this has increased Block's doubt that "raw" percepts are used in cognition
at all (C3, moderate). Active working memory is taken to be conscious and
maintained by broadcasting; activity-silent retention for up to 4 s is
set aside as a different phenomenon (pp. 275–276).

**Iconicity (pp. 279–280).** Three conditions, after Shepard:
representations respond to environmental differences by degree; changes in
them change what they represent by degree; relations among properties are
mirrored by relations among representations.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Working-memory representations
could have matched perceptual ones. On the evidence cited they do not;
whether the difference is one of format is left open.

## What this does not say

- **That the recoding changes format.** The nine differences "do not show a
  change in format", and a change could be within iconic representation
  (p. 279). That is the open question in `promote_when`.
- **That thought is never iconic.** Carey's parallel-individuation system
  (about 3 items in infants, 4 in adults) and entropy-based same/different
  matching are iconic, though enclosed in an envelope. Block concedes he
  "might have scanted the extent of iconic representation in thought"
  (C4).
- **That the workspace is not a mechanism of consciousness.** The rereading
  is asserted here, not argued against the consciousness reading
  ([NOTE-498](../notes.d/NOTE-498.md), Limitations).
- **That infants reason in working memory.** Hamlin's helper and hinderer
  results give "some reason" to credit 6–8-month-olds with it, and leaner
  iconic accounts are not ruled out (p. 276).
- **That the main thesis is established.** The arguments are in the book,
  unread here.

## Connections

- **Ventral and prefrontal content.** [THEORY-102](THEORY-102.md)'s finding of sustained
  ventral content and an onset-only prefrontal event is compatible with,
  and on Block's reading predicted by, the cognitive-envelope view. A
  THEORY combining the two would need the book's arguments ([NOTE-498](../notes.d/NOTE-498.md),
  Bearing on the record).
- **The workspace as consciousness.** [THEORY-096](THEORY-096.md) holds that perceptual
  consciousness is global availability through ignition. This account
  rereads the same ignition as conceptualisation. The two are compatible
  only if conceptualisation and conscious access coincide, which the
  précis does not discuss. [THEORY-103](THEORY-103.md), that perceptual consciousness
  overflows access, is the position from Block's own earlier work that
  sits behind the rereading; the précis does not restate it.
- **High-level perception.** Chalmers, French and Hofstadter
  ([THEORY-tmphgz0f](THEORY-tmphgz0f.md)) put concepts inside perception at the level where
  analogy works. Block puts the border at format, with conceptualisation
  on the cognitive side.
- **Framing without penetration.** Ivy and Mroczko-Wąsowicz ([LIT-099](../literature.d/LIT-099.md),
  [NOTE-150](../notes.d/NOTE-150.md)) cite the book for a border without cognitive penetration.
