# THEORY-485: Wave-function realism falsifies causal closure restricted to spacetime but is compatible with closure relative to fundamental physics, so quantum ontology opens no causal gap for non-physical causes; what it forces on the physicalist is a criterion of the physical and a bridge from dynamical to causal completeness

## Source

Alexander Poltorak (2026), "Wave-Function Realism and the Scope of Physical
Causal Closure", [LIT-164](../literature.d/LIT-164.md), PhilSci-Archive 30720, read in full in [NOTE-112](../notes.d/NOTE-112.md). No
journal version exists.

## What was actually shown

**Two parameters (§2.2, pp. 5–6).** C(D): every physical effect that has a cause
has a basis wholly in D that is causally sufficient, deterministically or by
fixing its chance. The domain D may be spatiotemporal (STC), the total ontology
and laws of a complete fundamental theory (FPC), or a level (LRC, which is
explanatory, not causal). The completeness relation may be dynamical, causal or
explanatory. "Dynamical completeness therefore supports causal closure only
together with a bridge principle."

**The Scope Argument (§4.1, p. 10).** Seven premises and three conclusions. C1:
wave-function realism undermines STC but not FPC. C2: there is a scope and
criterion problem, not a trans-domain breach. C3: both tacit parameters must be
made explicit. [NOTE-112](../notes.d/NOTE-112.md) rates C1 close to analytic once STC and FPC are
defined.

**Why no breach (§4.2).** The step from "the wave function is not in spacetime"
to "something non-physical causes spacetime events" needs the premise that a
structure not instantiated in spacetime is a non-physical external cause (p.
11). That premise is what wave-function realists and theory-based physicalists
deny.

**Across ontologies (§5).** Primitive ontology and Bohmian mechanics keep a
spacetime domain with nonlocal dependence, "not influence from outside the
physical". Spacetime-state realism breaks pointillist STC but may keep a
region-based one. Multi-field realism defeats the claim that realism about ψ
forces a separate high-dimensional realm. Everett adds derivative branch
structure only. [NOTE-112](../notes.d/NOTE-112.md) rates the generalization, that the finer the closure
domain the likelier a quantum ontology violates it, as weak.

**Mind (§6).** Not being in spacetime does not entail not being physical.
Stochastic dynamics leaves no "mental causal slot": a mind-modulated collapse
law is new dynamics and a rival hypothesis. Bell nonlocality with no-signalling
gives nothing for controllable mental influence.

**Limits of PBR (§3.2).** ψ-ontic means only disjoint supports over λ. The
theorem does not locate the ontology in a space or choose among readings.

**What could have come out otherwise.** A quantum ontology could have required a
cause outside the total ontology of fundamental physics. On the paper's
reading, none of the ontologies it tests does.

## What this does not say

- **That physicalism is true, or dualism false.** By its own account (§8.4) the
  paper does not establish either, defend wave-function realism, adjudicate
  interpretations, treat QFT fully or supply a theory of causation.
- **That anyone made the argument it rebuts.** The author "found no published
  argument that moves from wave-function realism to the conclusion that causal
  closure is breached" (p. 2), and concedes that sophisticated closure
  principles were never spacetime-restricted. Its target is "the picture from
  which closure borrows its intuitive force".
- **That FPC is secure.** FPC needs a non-question-begging criterion of the
  physical and a bridge from dynamical to causal completeness. The paper says
  both are required. It supplies neither, and P7 is asserted with Ney (2016)
  cited.
- **That theory-based physicalism excludes fundamental mentality.** It resolves
  the quantum case but does not rule that out (§7.2).
- **That its readings of the cited literature are accurate.** [NOTE-112](../notes.d/NOTE-112.md) did not
  check them.
