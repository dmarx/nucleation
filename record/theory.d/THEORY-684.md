# THEORY-684: Experiencing oneself as a self that was there in the further past, seeking pattern in one's life, fitting it to a story and falsifying it in the telling are separable, and fitting it to a story suffices for narrativity; on testimony that some normal people are Episodic and live good lives, neither the claim that people experience their lives as narratives nor the claim that a good life or full personhood needs it holds in general

## Source

Strawson (2004), "Against Narrativity", *Ratio* 17(4): 428–452, [LIT-452](../literature.d/LIT-452.md),
read in full in [NOTE-383](../notes.d/NOTE-383.md).

## What was actually shown

**Two theses.** The psychological Narrativity thesis is "a straightforwardly
empirical, descriptive thesis": people experience their lives as a
narrative. The ethical Narrativity thesis is that a "richly Narrative
outlook is essential to a well-lived life, to true or full personhood"
(§1). Sartre holds the first and not the second, Plutarch the reverse, the
academy both, Strawson neither.

**Diachronic and Episodic (§§2–3).** A Diachronic "naturally figures
oneself, considered as a self, as something that was there in the
(further) past and will be there in the (further) future". An Episodic
does not, while knowing perfectly well that the human being persists. It
is a matter of degree, may vary with age and health, and is called "one
normal, non-pathological form of life".

**The memory that is not mine* (§4).** Strawson remembers falling out of a
boat from the inside, visually, kinaesthetically and proprioceptively, and
it does not present as having happened to "me*", the self he now
experiences himself to be. The inference from a memory's from-the-inside
character to "happened to me*" "is simply not valid".

**The components (§§6–9).** Form-finding, a tendency to seek unity or
pattern in one's life; story-telling, fitting one's life to a recognised
genre, which entails form-finding and suffices for Narrativity; and
revision, non-conscious falsification, common but not required.
Diachronicity and form-finding are independent: all four combinations are
possible. The grid then places Dennett "[+D +F +S +R]", Schechtman
"[+D +F +S ±R]" and Strawson "[−D −F −S −R]".

**The ethical claim (§§9–11).** Every studied recall alters the memory, so
"the more you recall, retell, narrate yourself, the further you risk moving
away from accurate self-understanding". Montaigne, Episodic with a bad
memory, was "better at friendship than at anything else". Diachronicity is
not needed for morality or responsibility, and the past can shape the
present "without being present or alive as the past".

**What could have come out otherwise.** The four components could have
turned out to entail each other, so that any self-experience over time was
already a narrative. The cases for each combination show they do not.

## What this does not say

- **That the Episodic type is established.** It rests on the author's
  self-report and on classifying writers from their writings. No
  psychological measure is offered, and the reading's open question is
  whether the difference is in self-experience or in how people describe it.
- **That the remoter past did not happen to the human being.** The claim is
  about me*, the self. That it did not happen to me* "as a matter of
  metaphysical fact" is stated and referred to earlier papers (C3, weak).
- **That narration falsifies self-understanding.** Reconsolidation findings
  about recalled memories are extended to self-understanding without
  argument for the extension.
- **That temperament is genetic and spread evenly across cultures.**
  Asserted: "I take it" (C8, weak).
- **That the opponents hold what the grid says.** The placements are built
  from short quotations. Strawson flags weaker forms of Schechtman's view,
  and [NOTE-364](../notes.d/NOTE-364.md) reads Dennett's self as made more determinate, not falsified,
  so his "+R" is Strawson's attribution.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-043](../questions.d/QUESTION-043.md)** asks whether a life story, whose content changes, could
  be the coherence measure conserved when a person persists. This account
  constrains the answer: if some normal people do not experience their lives
  as narratives and persist all the same, a life story cannot be that
  measure for everyone, and the measure must differ across persons or be
  something else.
- **[THEORY-060](THEORY-060.md)** holds that a life story is "the form a person's diachronic
  identity takes". That is the psychological Narrativity thesis in
  developmental form, and the two cannot both hold in general. [THEORY-060](THEORY-060.md)
  calls universality its weakest point and cites this paper against it.
  The two are rivals.
- **Dennett** ([THEORY-691](THEORY-691.md)) is this paper's main example of the
  psychological thesis. **Gallagher** ([THEORY-458](THEORY-458.md)), as [NOTE-377](../notes.d/NOTE-377.md)
  reports, builds self-deception into the narrative self by design, which is
  the revision Strawson counts against narrative views. Gallagher also
  reports Strawson's earlier minimal self, "a bare locus of consciousness"
  lasting as long as a period of experience; the Episodic is what that view
  is like from the inside.
- **Diachronic self-governance** ([THEORY-076](THEORY-076.md)) is constituted by cross-temporal
  intention, "not by narrative unity". It is a second route by which a life
  can hang together over time without a story.
- **Parfit** ([THEORY-447](THEORY-447.md)). Strawson's Shaftesbury, content to "be twenty
  successive selfs", is close to the view that identity is not what matters.
  Strawson does not argue from Parfit; the pairing is [NOTE-383](../notes.d/NOTE-383.md)'s.
