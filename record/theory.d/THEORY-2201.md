# THEORY-2201: Takeuchi and Sano find that turbulent DSM2 clusters growing in nematic electroconvection, driven well above threshold, roughen with the 1+1-dimensional KPZ exponents and that their local-radius fluctuations follow the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution, with GOE and Gaussian ruled out by the parameter-free skewness and kurtosis, and Airy₂ correlations, which they attribute to strictly local growth, intrinsic noise and the absence of quenched disorder

## Source

Takeuchi and Sano (2010), "Universal Fluctuations of Growing Interfaces:
Evidence in Turbulent Liquid Crystals", Physical Review Letters 104,
230601, [LIT-2982](../literature.d/LIT-2982.md), read from the arXiv v3 in [NOTE-2284](../notes.d/NOTE-2284.md).

## The account

A DSM2 cluster nucleated in DSM1 turbulence at 26 V, "sufficiently larger
than the DSM1-DSM2 threshold at 20.7 V" (p. 2), grows as a circle whose
edge roughens. Over 563 runs: α = 0.50(5), β = 0.336(11), and a
Family–Vicsek collapse, so "the DSM2 interfacial growth belongs to the
(1+1)-dimensional KPZ class" (p. 2). The rescaled local radius has
skewness and kurtosis that "asymptotically converge to the values of the
GUE TW distribution, about 0.2241 for the skewness and 0.09345 for the
kurtosis", and the data "clearly rules out the GOE TW and Gaussian
distributions" (p. 3); its two-point correlation is the Airy₂ covariance
(p. 4). Curved fronts give GUE, flat ones GOE: "geometry-dependent
universality" (pp. 3–4). The authors credit the result to "strictly local
processes", intrinsic turbulent noise and no "overwhelming quenched
disorder" (p. 4), against a literature where "only two experiments among
dozens directly found the KPZ exponents" (p. 1).

## What would have come out otherwise

Exponents far from KPZ, as in most experiments; a logarithmic temporal
scaling, as Escudero proposed for droplets; or Gaussian or GOE
fluctuations. Each was open, and the data excluded it.

## What it does not say

- That the front is critical, or that nothing is tuned: the paper does
  not use either notion; that it is untuned is the record's reading of
  "well above threshold" and the stated robustness across voltages.
- That KPZ is generic in experiment: the paper's premise is that it is
  not.
- Anything about tails: the universal object is a distribution
  characterized by its cumulants, not a power law.
