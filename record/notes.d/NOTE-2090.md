# NOTE-2090: Finn and others 2017 — Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning for Fast Adaptation of Deep Networks

## Contribution

Finn, Abbeel and Levine (ICML 2017) propose MAML: train a model's initial
parameters, over a distribution of tasks p(T), so that one or a few gradient
steps on a small amount of data from a new task give good performance on
that task. The meta-objective is the loss after adaptation, not the loss at
the initialization; it is optimized by differentiating through the inner
gradient step(s). The method adds no parameters and applies to regression,
classification and policy-gradient RL.

## Key results

- **The objective is post-adaptation performance** (§2.2): the parameters
  are trained "by optimizing for the performance of fθi0 with respect to θ
  across tasks sampled from p(T )", and "the test error on sampled tasks Ti
  serves as the training error of the meta-learning process" (§2.1).
- **Trained to be adaptable, in the authors' words** (abstract): "In
  effect, our method trains the model to be easy to fine-tune." In §1:
  "our procedure optimizes for models that are easy and fast to fine-tune".
- **Read as sensitivity** (§1): "maximizing the sensitivity of the loss
  functions of new tasks with respect to the parameters".
- **Against joint pretraining** (§5.1, sinusoid regression): the model
  pretrained on all tasks "is unable to adequately adapt with so few
  datapoints without catastrophic overfitting", while MAML adapts from 5
  points and extrapolates to the unsampled half of the range. App. C.1:
  pretraining on all tasks makes the model "learn to output the average
  output for a particular input value".
- **Keeps improving past the trained horizon** (§5.1): the MAML model
  "continues to improve with additional gradient steps, despite being
  trained for maximal performance after one gradient step"; the same in RL
  (§5.3).
- **First-order approximation nearly as good** (§5.2, MiniImagenet): 48.07%
  vs 48.70% 1-shot; "most of the improvement in MAML comes from the
  gradients of the objective at the post-update parameter values".
- **RL** (§5.3): "pretraining is in some cases worse than random
  initialization".
- **Parameter averaging is not enough** (App. C, Table 2): MAML learns "a
  solution that is more sophisticated than the mean optimal parameter
  vector" (5-shot MSE 0.67 vs 2.41 for pretraining after one step).

## Assumptions

- A declared task distribution p(T) from which meta-training and meta-test
  tasks are drawn; "Generally, tasks used for meta-testing are held out
  during meta-training" (§2.1), but from the same p(T).
- A fixed, small adaptation budget (K examples, one to a few steps, a fixed
  or meta-learned step size) at meta-training time.
- A loss smooth enough in θ for gradient-based learning (§2.2).

## Concepts

- **Meta-learning / few-shot learning**: tasks as training examples.
- **Inner and outer loop**: adaptation θ′ = θ − α∇L, then meta-update of θ
  on the post-adaptation loss.
- **First-order MAML (FOMAML)**: the second-derivative terms dropped.
- **Pretraining on all tasks**: the joint-training baseline.
- **Oracle**: a model given the task identity as input, an upper bound.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-495](../questions.d/QUESTION-495.md)**: supports the candidate answer that
  meta-learning's objective already targets future adaptability: MAML's
  loss is defined after adaptation on new tasks. Qualifies it: the future
  it targets is a declared p(T) and a declared, tiny adaptation budget; it
  does not reach the question's case of benefits that cannot be specified
  in advance, nor count the cost of acquiring or keeping organization.
- **[CLAIM-758](../claims.d/CLAIM-758.md)**: supports its clause that the objective is not new, as
  written. The joint-pretraining baseline (§5.1, App. C.1) is a learner
  that minimizes its present objective over all training tasks and is
  then expensive to adapt ("catastrophic overfitting" from 5 points),
  which is an instance of the claim's signature only loosely: the paper
  calls it overfitting at fine-tuning time and does not separate the two.
  It leaves open whether overconsolidation is distinct from that.
- **[CLAIM-756](../claims.d/CLAIM-756.md)**: gives an empirical instance of a step valued by its
  effect on later acquisitions: the MAML initialization is chosen by
  post-adaptation loss, and the initialization that is best on the present
  joint objective (pretraining) is worse once adaptation is counted (§5.1,
  App. C Table 2). The later tasks are drawn from the declared p(T), the
  claim's stipulation that the later questions will come.
- **[CASE-294](../cases.d/CASE-294.md)**: supports reading adaptability as a budgeted quantity
  (K, number of steps) and suggests the case's adaptation-cost measure is
  MAML's evaluation turned around (least budget to reach a threshold rather
  than loss at a fixed budget). The finding that adaptation keeps
  improving past the trained number of steps warns that a meta-trained arm
  must be compared at several budgets, not only the one it was trained for.

## Limitations

- Benchmarks are narrow task families (sinusoids, Omniglot, MiniImagenet,
  goal velocities and directions); no shift of task family between
  meta-training and meta-test.
- Small models (a 2×40 MLP, a 4-layer CNN, a 2×100 policy); nothing on
  large pretrained models.
- Omniglot comparisons "may not be strictly comparable" (Table 1 caption).
- No measure of cost beyond gradient steps and examples; no account of
  what meta-training does to the plasticity of the model outside p(T).
