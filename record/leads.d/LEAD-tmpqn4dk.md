# LEAD-tmpqn4dk: Price the recurrence of learned accommodation: two populations meeting one recurring conflict, one settling it locally and one escalating every instance at the per-encounter price already fitted, with the break-even counted against the cost of forming the relation and a population whose conflict admits no stable accommodation as the control

## The gap

CLAIM-358 (the owner's, Proposed): "Learned local accommodation can
reduce the amortized cost of recurrent arbitration: where a recurring
conflict among an agent's processes admits a stable accommodation,
settling it among the processes can cost less over repeated encounters
than escalating each instance". The price of one escalation has been
fitted: in CASE-382, for about three-quarters of 122 participants "the
control intensity elected falls as its cost rises" (CLAIM-981). The saving
over the recurrence has not been priced, and no executed study prices it.
LEAD-128 asked only for the cost-side fit and is still Open.

## The design, fixed before any run

Taken from Article 4 §4.8.1 ("Expectation for the recurrence, stated
before any run, with no lead"), the drafter's:

1. **Two populations** of processes meet one recurring conflict that
   admits a stable accommodation, as CASE-109's two persons do. One settles
   it locally; the other escalates each instance to a mediating process.
2. **Each escalation is priced as in CASE-382**: the fitted increasing
   cost of control c(n), at the fitted parameters, per encounter.
3. **The local population pays a formation cost** to acquire the
   accommodation and a keeping cost per encounter, held apart:
   "having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it"
   (CLAIM-569, Active, demonstrated).
4. **The control**: a matched population whose recurring conflict has no
   stable accommodation.
5. **The measure**: cumulative cost by encounter, and the break-even
   encounter, as a function of the formation cost.

## What would count

- **For CLAIM-358 as conditioned**: the local population's cumulative cost
  falls below the escalating one's after a break-even that rises with the
  formation cost, and the ordering does not appear in the control.
- **CLAIM-443's guard, a necessary check**: the saving has to show up as
  later arbitration work that is not done. A persisting allocation that
  does the same work again does not count ("persistence is not
  amortization").
- **CLAIM-1139's guard**: the saving must come from learned coordination,
  not from an external rule that settles the conflict automatically; an
  arm in which a fixed rule replaces the learned accommodation separates
  the two.
- **Against reading persistence as amortization**: CASE-153's check, set
  before its run, carries over. If a habit model with matched memory
  reproduces the local population's costs encounter by encounter, the
  saving is the habit's and not an accommodation's (CASE-346).

## What it cannot show

The prices are one study's fitted effort costs for single choices, not
supervisory costs; the populations are constructed. It can show that the
owner's conditional holds in a model with an empirically fitted price, not
that people or organizations amortize this way. Whether CASE-382 meets
LEAD-128's criterion on the cost direction alone stays the owner's call
(the audit's C7).
