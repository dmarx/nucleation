# QUESTION-106: Can a textual instruction, a retrieved structured record and an activation intervention preserve the same pragmatic obligations across context shifts, judged on the decisions that matter and not on outputs or embeddings?

## Why it is a question

A3: "Can an activation intervention reproduce the task-relative
behavioral constraints induced by a textual instruction across context
shifts and conflicting instructions? For example, can either intervention
reliably preserve the distinction between describing a rejected claim and
endorsing it?" Evaluation is over task-relevant decisions, as [CLAIM-050](../claims.d/CLAIM-050.md)
requires, "not merely output embeddings or token distributions".

[CLAIM-029](../claims.d/CLAIM-029.md) treats a model's response to a context as a parameterized
pragmatic frame. A3 separated three observations its defeater runs
together: a prompt changes the output, a prompt changes an internal
representation, two interventions induce equivalent pragmatic behaviour
over a family of contexts (see the note of 2026-10-15 on [CLAIM-029](../claims.d/CLAIM-029.md)). This
question asks the third directly.

A3's counter goes with it: "Behavioral equivalence cannot establish
mechanistic equivalence." The steering papers A1 and A3 cite are machine
learning and belong to the anthology. None is registered here.
