# THEORY-tmp5lj70: Alexander holds that moral systems are in essence systems of indirect reciprocity arising from conflicts of reproductive interest, in which help seen by others pays through those observers' later choices, so that some indiscriminate altruism is social investment, idealized universal moralities misread it as self-sacrifice, and morality as practised is cohesion within groups that may be directed against others

## The account

"A moral system is essentially a society with rules", and moral problems
"arise because of conflicts of interest", ultimately reproductive. These
are softened by nepotism and by reciprocity, and "The essence of moral
systems seems to lie in patterns of indirect reciprocity": returns come
from others who watched. Because observers choose partners by what they
see, some help given regardless of the recipient pays as social
investment; idealized moralities of universal altruism misread that
investment as self-sacrifice, while practised morality is in-group
cohesion, perhaps "cooperation-to-compete" with other groups.

## Evidence

Theoretical argument from evolutionary biology, with illustrative cases;
no formal model and no data. Read through Alexander's 1985 paper, not the
1987 book.
