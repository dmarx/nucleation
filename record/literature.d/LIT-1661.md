# LIT-1661: Institutions: abstract model theory for specification and programming

Goguen, J. A., & Burstall, R. M. (1992). Institutions: Abstract model theory for specification and programming. *Journal of the ACM*, 39(1), 95–146. doi:10.1145/147508.147524.

Read in full in [NOTE-tmpyi64y](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyi64y.md), from a copy of the published article supplied by the owner.

## Why this is here

A9 §7 offered it as the formal frame for the owner's
specification–realization analogy, and for comparing record migrations
when the specification language itself changes: renaming, conservative
refinement, splitting or promoting a type, changing governing rules.
A9's correction stands with it: theory and realization "can be
mathematically related without either being ontologically prior", and
satisfaction is not production. It bears on [CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md) and
[CLAIM-476](../claims.d/CLAIM-476.md).

The paper bears out A9's correction: a specification denotes a class of
models that "Programmers construct" (p. 98), and closed theories and
closed model classes determine each other without either being defined
from the other (Proposition 3). It narrows the frame in two ways. Both
sentences and models are indexed by signatures, so the symmetry holds
over a fixed notation, not between notation and what it describes. And
of the migrations A9 listed, renaming and enrichment are signature
morphisms, while splitting a type is not a morphism from the old
signature to the new one, and changing the governing rules changes the
institution ([NOTE-tmpyi64y](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyi64y.md)). It also bears on [CLAIM-469](../claims.d/CLAIM-469.md), [CLAIM-470](../claims.d/CLAIM-470.md),
[QUESTION-130](../questions.d/QUESTION-130.md) and [CLAIM-tmpgwud2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpgwud2.md).

## Key takeaways

- The Satisfaction Condition, m′ ⊨ φe iff φm′ ⊨ e, is the whole axiom;
  sentences move with a change of notation and models move against it.
- If signatures can be glued by colimits, so can theories (Theorem 11),
  including theories with data constraints (Theorem 24).
- A translation between logics is correct when it meets the same
  condition with a model translation; given that model translation it is
  fixed up to equivalence, and with every model reached it preserves and
  reflects entailment (Propositions 33 and 35).
- The condition alone admits a translation that collapses everything to
  one model; it is relative to a model translation it does not supply,
  and it covers truth conditions only.
