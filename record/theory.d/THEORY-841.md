# THEORY-841: Knowledge of the particular circumstances of time and place is held dispersed among many people and cannot be conveyed to a central authority in statistical form, so decisions that depend on it are better taken by those who hold it, and a price system lets them fit their decisions together by conveying the relative scarcities each needs without the causes behind them

## Source

Hayek, F. A. (1945), "The Use of Knowledge in Society", *American Economic
Review* 35(4): 519–530, [LIT-1337](../literature.d/LIT-1337.md), read in full in [NOTE-1036](../notes.d/NOTE-1036.md) from
the Econlib reprint.

## What was actually shown

Three steps, each argued and none demonstrated.

- **Dispersed knowledge.** Beside scientific knowledge there is "a body of
  very important but unorganized knowledge ... the knowledge of the
  particular circumstances of time and place", in which "practically every
  individual has some advantage over all others".
- **It cannot be centralized.** It "by its nature cannot enter into
  statistics", because statistics are made "by lumping together, as
  resources of one kind, items which differ as regards location, quality,
  and other particulars, in a way which may be very significant for the
  specific decision." So decisions depending on it must be left to "the
  'man on the spot'", by "some form of decentralization".
- **Prices coordinate.** The man on the spot needs to know only "how much
  more or less difficult to procure" things have become. In the tin
  example, a scarcity spreads through substitutes and their substitutes
  "without the great majority of those instrumental in bringing about these
  substitutions knowing anything at all about the original cause". The
  price system is "a mechanism for communicating information", marked by
  "the economy of knowledge with which it operates".

What could have come out otherwise: if the knowledge that matters for most
decisions were general and stable, a body of experts could hold it, and the
case would fail. Hayek grants the point for scientific knowledge and denies
it for the rest on the premise that "economic problems arise always and
only in consequence of change". The essay does not test that premise.

## What this does not say

- **Not that prices convey all that decisions need.** The essay gives no
  measure of how close the adjustment comes, and grants that adjustments
  are "probably never 'perfect'" and less so "as prices grow more rigid".
  What prices do not carry, such as effects on third parties or knowledge
  held for advantage, is not discussed.
- **Not that removing a constraint decentralizes.** The argument is for
  competition, "decentralized planning by many separate persons", against
  one plan. It names a third arrangement, planning "delegated to organized
  industries ... in other words, monopoly", and does not say that freeing a
  market from public rules yields the first rather than the third.
- **Not a result about organizations.** It is stated for the economic
  order. That the same reasoning governs the allocation of decisions inside
  an organization is a plausible extension the essay does not make.
- **Not evidence.** The promote_when asks for a model or a measurement.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-410](../claims.d/CLAIM-410.md).** The claim treats a change of rules as a change in who
  holds each governing function. Hayek's §II puts the economic dispute the
  same way: "not ... whether planning is to be done or not ... [but]
  whether planning is to be done centrally ... or is to be divided among
  many individuals". This account is the informational reason one
  allocation of decisions can beat another. It is not a reason that every
  removal of a constraint improves the allocation.
- **[TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md).** The lumping that makes statistics usable centrally is
  what loses circumstances of time and place: a 1945 statement of what
  legibility costs.
- **[THEORY-404](THEORY-404.md).** A designed signal moves a Bayesian receiver. Hayek's
  prices are signals nobody designs, which carry what a decision needs.
  Both treat what a decision maker receives as the lever. The pairing is
  the record's.
- **[THEORY-429](THEORY-429.md)** (North) answers the same dispersal with institutions,
  constraints that economize on knowledge, rather than with prices.
