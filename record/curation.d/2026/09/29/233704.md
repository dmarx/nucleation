The close readings of the prior-art novelty map's references
(`record/curation.d/2026/09/29/230637.md`) found several places where the map
cites the wrong work or a work that could not be reached. They were recorded
as findings but not followed up. The owner asked for the follow-up. This batch
registers and reads the works the map should cite instead.

**How it was done.** Three agents shared one brief, split as follows:

- Stone 1937, Husimi, Zaslavsky's announcement and Stanley;
- the two symmetry-detection papers;
- Russo & Zou, Bennett and Earman.

Each gave a verdict on whether its work supplies what the map needs. Only
legitimate sources were used: arXiv, J-STAGE, an author-linked PDF, and the
Internet Archive's public-domain scan of *Trans. AMS* 41.

**Result.** Nine works were registered. Seven were read and two were
unreachable.

**Row 8: Stone duality and topes.**

- **Stone 1937 ([LIT-358](../../record/literature.d/LIT-358.md))** is the source of the topological duality, so
  row 8 should cite it in place of Stone 1936 ([LIT-353](../../record/literature.d/LIT-353.md)). Its Theorems 1, 2 and
  4 give the prime-ideal space as a totally disconnected, locally compact
  Hausdorff space, compact exactly when the ring has a unit. Stone calls this
  a "mathematical equivalence", not a duality, and never states it for
  morphisms. It says nothing about ultrafilters, hyperplanes or regions. The
  paper also contains Stone–Čech, Stone–Weierstrass and Banach–Stone. Stone's
  link to character-group duality (p. 393) is a conjecture in the text, not a
  result. [LIT-353](../../record/literature.d/LIT-353.md) now points at it.
- **Stanley's arrangements notes ([LIT-355](../../record/literature.d/LIT-355.md))** are the readable proof of
  Zaslavsky's theorem that the record lacked. They show that region and face
  counts depend only on the intersection poset. The notes never use "topes"
  and do not cite Zaslavsky, so they are cited *alongside* [LIT-332](../../record/literature.d/LIT-332.md), not
  instead of it.
- **Zaslavsky's announcement ([LIT-359](../../record/literature.d/LIT-359.md))** is open access at the AMS, but
  every route to it was blocked from this session. It stays `Deferred`, and
  anyone with ordinary web access can fetch it.
- **"Ultrafilters ↔ topes"** still has no source. It needs an
  oriented-matroid text plus the owner's own argument, as [NOTE-272](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-272.md) and
  [NOTE-290](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-290.md) said.

**Orthomodularity.** Husimi 1937 ([LIT-357](../../record/literature.d/LIT-357.md)) derives the Birkhoff–von
Neumann lattice from statistical premises. It *uses* the orthomodular law as
an unnamed proof step, but never states or names it. The usual credit to
Husimi is therefore a reading of his axioms. [NOTE-275](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-275.md) now carries that
correction. A source that states the law as an axiom (Kalmbach, or the Piron
line) is still missing. Husimi also raises the "meaningless proposition"
(truth-value gap) reading (p. 769) and declines it. That bears on row 5.

**Row 7: "they impose; you detect".**

- **Lenc & Vedaldi 2015 ([LIT-363](../../record/literature.d/LIT-363.md)).** They fit linear maps on a trained
  network's feature space that realise named image transformations. So
  symmetry *has* been measured in learned representations, and the group acts
  on representation space. That weakens the "where the group acts" distinction
  drawn in [NOTE-274](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-274.md), [NOTE-282](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-282.md) and [NOTE-288](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-288.md).
- **Cohen & Welling 2014 ([LIT-362](../../record/literature.d/LIT-362.md)).** They learn the irreps of a
  commutative group from pairs related by an unknown element, but the group
  class is fixed in advance. This is the "learn irreps from data" prior art.
  The map's "Cohen–Welling" is the 2016 paper, which imposes the group.
- **What survives as novel.** Both papers need paired inputs, and either a
  hypothesised transformation or an assumed group class. Neither looks at an
  operator's spectrum, so degeneracy plays no role in either. The owner's
  claim survives, narrowed to *where the group comes from*: eigenvalue
  multiplicities, with no pairs and no hypothesised group.
- **Two corrections to earlier notes.** [NOTE-282](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-282.md) said abelian symmetries give
  "no degeneracy". That is false over ℝ for SO(2) and ℤ/n with n ≥ 3, whose
  rotation irreps force pairs; that is Cohen & Welling 2014's setting and
  Engels et al.'s ([LIT-322](../../record/literature.d/LIT-322.md)). [NOTE-288](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-288.md)'s relay of Lenc & Vedaldi overstates
  that paper. Both notes now carry dated corrections.

**Row 12: the learner as a channel.** Russo & Zou ([LIT-356](../../record/literature.d/LIT-356.md)) is the
original of the bound that Xu & Raginsky ([LIT-347](../../record/literature.d/LIT-347.md)) extend, so row 12 should
cite both.

- Their Lemma 1 chains information over adaptive steps, and it is the origin
  of [LIT-347](../../record/literature.d/LIT-347.md)'s eq. (36).
- Their Lemma 2 prices one noisy query at ½·log(1 + SNR). That is the nearest
  existing result to the map's C_step = d·log₂(1 + SNR). It covers queries
  answered with added independent noise, not minibatch gradients, so it
  cannot be cited for SGD without a further argument.
- [NOTE-306](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-306.md) stated their quantity in Xu & Raginsky's notation, and now says so.

**Row 13: the heat floor.** Bennett ([LIT-360](../../record/literature.d/LIT-360.md)) settles it against the map.

- Only logically irreversible steps carry a cost, and the cost is entropy in
  the environment, which "need not" be heat.
- Bennett calls "an intrinsic cost of order kT for every elementary act of
  information processing" a misconception.
- The defensible form is "k_B T ln 2 of entropy per predicate bit erased or
  overwritten". This agrees with [NOTE-295](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-295.md) and [NOTE-296](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-296.md).
- Bennett gets the title of Landauer 1961 wrong, so cite him for the
  principle's scope and not as a substitute for [LIT-328](../../record/literature.d/LIT-328.md).

**Row 5: superselection and truth-value gaps.**

- **Earman 2008 ([LIT-361](../../record/literature.d/LIT-361.md))** is paywalled and is not on PhilSci-Archive.
  The only copy found was a draft reposted in a third party's reading-group
  folder, which was not used.
- **The truth-value-gap reading** still has no source that asserts it. Husimi
  raises and declines it, and Wick–Wightman–Wigner make cross-sector
  superpositions mixtures ([NOTE-304](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-304.md)).

**Structural realism, which needed no new reading.** The earlier curation
entry said only the unreachable Ladyman 1998 ([LIT-342](../../record/literature.d/LIT-342.md)) could stand in for
Worrall 1989 ([LIT-315](../../record/literature.d/LIT-315.md)) on ontic structural realism. That was wrong: the record
already holds ontic sources, read earlier. They are:

- [LIT-217](../../record/literature.d/LIT-217.md) (Ladyman, "Science, Metaphysics and Structural Realism");
- [LIT-219](../../record/literature.d/LIT-219.md) (Ladyman & Ross, "Patterns All the Way Up");
- [LIT-218](../../record/literature.d/LIT-218.md) (OSR applied to economics);
- [LIT-222](../../record/literature.d/LIT-222.md) and [LIT-223](../../record/literature.d/LIT-223.md) (the category-theoretic debate over radical OSR).

The map's "to be is to be an invariant of structure" should be sourced there.
