# CASE-086: The retracted finding and its descendants: an experiment fails to replicate years after three conclusions were built on it

## The case

E₁ → H₁ → H₂ → C, built into several manuscripts. Years later a
replication undermines E₁. A group that kept its conclusions and not
their dependencies "cannot immediately identify which conclusions need
reconsideration." A record that kept the chain flags H₁, H₂ and C, and
"does not automatically retract them. They may have acquired independent
support, or the original inferences may survive for different reasons."

## What it can show

A7: "it distinguishes dependency tracing from belief propagation. A
dependency can tell us what needs reconsideration without determining
what should ultimately be believed." A12 put the same thing as review,
not retraction: "Changes to epistemic grounds should propagate obligations
to review, not automatically propagate changes of belief."

## What it cannot show

That a dependency graph alone settles what to revise ([CASE-089](CASE-089.md)), or that
keeping it is cheaper than the alternatives ([CLAIM-303](../claims.d/CLAIM-303.md)).
