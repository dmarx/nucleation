# CLAIM-tmpa9usi: Communicative kinds are repeatable organizations of relations among participants, norms, commitments and possible consequences, which concrete events realize without being one another, and which kind an event realizes is not exhausted by the intrinsic properties of the expression token

## The claim

At U62 the owner asked "how would you suggest we update or modify the
theses?". A240 proposed six theses in place of one, and this is the first.
A240 T1, "Relational constitution of communicative kinds":

> **Claim:** Communicative kinds are repeatable organizations of relations
> among participants, norms, commitments, and possible interactional
> consequences. Concrete events and expressions instantiate these kinds
> without being numerically identical to one another. Their classification is
> not exhausted by intrinsic properties of expression tokens.
>
> **Status:** Philosophical thesis; proposed.
>
> **Qualification:** Competent uptake is a central practice-grounded
> criterion for identifying these kinds, but it is not presumed infallible,
> unanimous, or sufficient for every kind of institutional act.
>
> **Challenge:** Cases where an expression's intrinsic properties determine
> its communicative kind independently of the supposedly constitutive
> relations, or where the proposed relational individuation fails to
> distinguish recognized kinds without ad hoc revisions.

A232, the revised proposal of U61, states it as the first of three
propositions: "**Ontological:** Communicative kinds are repeatable relational
organizations, distinct from their individual realizations, participant
positions, and observational classifications."

It is the ontological clause of the October thesis ([CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md), restated
as [CLAIM-tmp3wo5j](CLAIM-tmp3wo5j.md)), taken out of the conjunction. [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) says a kind's
identity "is grounded in its relational organization rather than in any
expression or encoding". T1 says less: the kind is a relational organization,
and the token's intrinsic properties do not settle which kind an event
realizes. A240 places it at the top of its hierarchy as a motive, not a
premise: "The ontological and mathematical theses motivate the program; **T4
and T5 should carry its principal empirical risk**." So it rests on nothing
here, and nothing is filed as resting on it. [CLAIM-tmpattzh](CLAIM-tmpattzh.md) is the
objection to keeping it inside one thesis.

The defeat condition is A240's challenge in the record's wording, scoped to
the acts the line studies. "Without ad hoc revisions" becomes revisions made
case by case after the verdicts are known.

## What it adds and what it does not

- **The first two sentences are a definition.** They are [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)'s: "a
  repeatable organization of relations among speakers, audiences, norms,
  commitments and expected responses", which numerically distinct events
  realize. As a thesis they assert only that there are such kinds.
- **The third sentence does not tell relational constitution from its
  rival.** An intention-based account individuates an act by the utterance
  and the speaker's communicative intention ([CLAIM-tmpc25sn](CLAIM-tmpc25sn.md)). An intention
  is not an intrinsic property of the expression token, so that account also
  says the kind "is not exhausted by intrinsic properties of expression
  tokens". T1 is met by both. What separates them is [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) version 2,
  whose defeat condition holds intention fixed and varies the speaker's
  standing or the audience's role, on acts with standing conditions. T1 is
  the weaker claim, and a defeat of [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) would not defeat it.
- **Its qualification is not [CLAIM-tmpawn12](CLAIM-tmpawn12.md)'s.** [CLAIM-tmpawn12](CLAIM-tmpawn12.md) makes
  competent participants "the criterion a probe family is tested against".
  T1 makes uptake a criterion that is "not presumed infallible, unanimous, or
  sufficient for every kind of institutional act". That is the position
  [CLAIM-tmpohczg](CLAIM-tmpohczg.md) presses against [CLAIM-tmpawn12](CLAIM-tmpawn12.md). The two entries
  complement each other on what a kind is; they differ on how far uptake
  settles which kind an event realizes, and that difference is open.
- **The study cannot test it.** A232 §2.2: "Neither statistical factorization
  nor success at predicting judgments proves ontic structural realism." Its
  §11 names the "**Ontological limit:** Even the strongest empirical result
  supports a theory of effective relational kinds; it does not independently
  prove the metaphysical thesis of non-eliminative OSR." That is [CLAIM-150](CLAIM-150.md).
  The defeat condition is met by cases and by argument, not by
  [CASE-tmp1uzfn](../cases.d/CASE-tmp1uzfn.md).

## What it does not say

- It does not say an event or a participant has no identity beyond its
  relations. That is the owner's U54 position ([CLAIM-136](CLAIM-136.md)). T1 says the
  events "instantiate these kinds without being numerically identical to one
  another", which presupposes them.
- It does not say the kind is wholly constituted by relations. [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)'s
  "partly" is not contradicted, and T1 adds nothing to it.
- It does not say that teasing is relationally constituted. Its defeat
  condition names teasing among the kinds it covers, and that is the case
  [QUESTION-tmpcs4qg](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpcs4qg.md) leaves open.
- It does not say the owner holds it. It is the assistant's proposal, filed
  as a proposal.
