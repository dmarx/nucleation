# LIT-148: Computational Functionalism for the Deep Learning Era

Ezequiel López-Rubio (2018), *Minds and Machines 28(4), 667–688* — DOI-10.1007/s11023-018-9480-7

## Key takeaways

- Deep networks' success comes from extracting successively higher-level features across multiple layers. López-Rubio argues that this calls for a new version of computational functionalism, keyed to deep neural computation rather than to classic program-based functional organization.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Deep learning, machine learning in deep artificial neural networks, has repeatedly reached human-like performance on intelligent tasks, and these networks resemble biological ones in many respects. That raises the question whether the resemblance explains the performance. The paper reviews the structure and learning processes of artificial and biological networks. It names two closely linked reasons for deep learning's success: hierarchical extraction of increasingly high-level features, and multi-layer structure. After remarks on how the debate should be framed, it assesses deep networks as models of the brain from the standpoint of how similar the representations are. It ends by proposing a version of computational functionalism fitted to deep neural computation instead of classical program-based functionalism.
