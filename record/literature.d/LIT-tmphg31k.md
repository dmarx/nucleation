# LIT-tmphg31k: The Theory of Constructed Emotion: More Than a Feeling

Barrett, L. F., Atzil, S., Bliss-Moreau, E., Chanes, L., Gendron, M., Hoemann, K., Katsumi, Y., Kleckner, I. R., Lindquist, K. A., Quigley, K. S., Satpute, A. B., Sennesh, E., Shaffer, C., Theriault, J. E., Tugade, M. & Westlin, C. (2025). The Theory of Constructed Emotion: More Than a Feeling. *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 20(3): 392–420. doi:10.1177/17456916251319045.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpr1xjh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpr1xjh.md). The XML has no page breaks, so
references are to sections.*

- **Feeling and bodily regulation are one mechanism.** "Feeling cannot be
  meaningfully separated in any biologically justified manner from the
  brain's regulation and coordination of the various systems of the body."
  The hypothesis offered is that "efferent copies of the same allostatic
  signals that control and regulate the body simultaneously create feeling
  and other features of lived experience" (Error 1). It is stated, not
  tested.
- **One event, not two.** An instance of emotion is "a complex causal
  ensemble (i.e., a whole-brain event) whose simultaneous features include
  bodily regulation and the mental features of experience". Allostasis
  "does not cause separate instances of emotion but manifests them" (Error
  1).
- **The split is basic emotion theory's premise.** Separating an emotion
  into a bodily state and a feeling is "the bifurcation of nature". The
  authors say it has manufactured the correspondence problem, the poor
  covariation of physiology with reported experience (Error 1, citing Gross
  1998 among others).
- **Structured variation.** Table 2 lists studies of variation within
  emotion categories and similarity across them. "One cannot integrate a
  theory that requires unchanging prototypes with a theory that denies their
  existence" (Error 2).
- **Categories are made, and meaning is relational.** "A category is
  something you do, not something you have." Facial movements, heart rate
  and the like "have no inherent, biologically prepared emotional meaning"
  (Error 2).
- **Evolution and incommensurability.** The theory rests on the extended
  evolutionary synthesis, with cultural inheritance of emotion categories
  (Error 3), and "Our actual theory cannot be integrated with BET because
  the two are incommensurate" (Table 3).
- **Regulation appears only on the other side.** Regulation is mentioned
  only as something basic emotion theory uses to explain variation away:
  FEAR "tweaked after the fact" by "regulation strategies" (Error 3).

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange called this the closest existing formulation of the constitutive reading for affect, with cognitive behavioural therapy as its example, and named its constraint: on the theory, interpretive meaning cannot be separated from bodily regulation. The record already holds the 2017 active-inference statement of the theory, [LIT-542](LIT-542.md), and Seth's interoceptive inference, [LIT-1263](LIT-1263.md) and [THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md). Bears on [CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md), [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md).

**Corrected 2026-10-22, from the reading.** The constraint is the paper's:
meaning-making "manifests as features of allostasis, action, and lived
experience" (Table 1), and feeling is not separable from bodily regulation.
But cognitive behavioural therapy was the exchange's example, not the
paper's. The paper never mentions therapy, reappraisal or emotion
regulation, except as part of basic emotion theory's account of variation.
It is constitutive in some of its wording ("manifests") and productive in
other wording ("to create lived experience"), and it does not choose. Its
bearing on [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) is indirect, and is set out in
[NOTE-tmpr1xjh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpr1xjh.md): it puts a separate regulation process on basic emotion
theory's side, and it reads the experiment behind [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md)'s contrast
(Gross 1998) as a symptom of the split. It meets neither THEORY's promotion
test. It makes [THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md) no stronger or weaker: interoception appears only
in van Heijst et al.'s words. No THEORY was filed from it. Its substantive
claims are already held as [THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md) and [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md), and this reading
could be added as a source of [THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md).

Read with Bo et al. ([LIT-tmptaneb](LIT-tmptaneb.md)), whose finding that subcortical
responses survive reappraisal while reported affect falls is, on this
paper's terms, a limit on the constitutive reading only if one makes the
split between bodily state and feeling that the paper rejects.
