# LIT-1571: How do bacterial endosymbionts work with so few genes?

McCutcheon, J. P., Garber, A. I., Spencer, N. & Warren, J. M. (2024). How do bacterial endosymbionts work with so few genes? *PLOS Biology* 22(4): e3002577. doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3002577.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1213](../notes.d/NOTE-1213.md).*

- **Two thresholds.** Below about 400 kb, envelope and envelope-transport
  genes are lost together. Below about 200 kb, ribosomal proteins, tRNAs
  and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases erode ([THEORY-1049](../theory.d/THEORY-1049.md)).
- **Biased proteomes.** Tipping-point endosymbionts lie outside the
  amino-acid composition of nearly 99,000 proteomes, beyond extremophiles.
  *Zinderia*'s proteome is almost half lysine, isoleucine and asparagine.
  The analysis is "preliminary".
- **The host as supplier.** Three-membrane envelopes, host transporters
  at the symbiont vacuole, and single host-encoded proteins seen in aphid
  and mealybug endosymbionts suggest that the host provides what the
  genome lacks. The mechanisms are unknown.
- **Caveats.** The thresholds come from a curated set of genomes,
  including drafts. The 200 kb cutoff is "somewhat arbitrary".

## Why this is here

A2 rates it "Critical", as the complement to the 2012 review: the open mechanistic problems of extremely reduced symbionts.
