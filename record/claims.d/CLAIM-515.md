# CLAIM-515: Whether an organization has a cognitive property is four questions, not one: what information about the property can be recovered from its behaviour, what capabilities it can actually exercise, what mechanisms realize them, and whether it can maintain or transform those mechanisms; the four can come apart, and none should be silently substituted for another

## The claim

For a cognitive property X of an organization, ask separately:

1. **Recoverable.** What about X can be recovered from the organization's
   behaviour, by whom, from which queries?
2. **Exercisable.** What X-capabilities can the organization itself
   exercise, within its resources and deadlines?
3. **Realized.** Which mechanisms realize those capabilities, shown by
   interventions that discriminate implementations?
4. **Maintained and transformable.** Can the organization maintain,
   rebuild or revise those mechanisms, including after damage to them?

The record already has instances in which these come apart:
- **World models.**
  - Recoverable without exercisable: [CLAIM-513](CLAIM-513.md).
  - Exercisable without an identified mechanism: [CLAIM-519](CLAIM-519.md).
  - Their reconciliation: [CLAIM-514](CLAIM-514.md).
- **Maintenance.** Matched trajectories, even under every intervention on
  state, do not show where maintenance is realized: [CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md) and
  [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md).
- **Concepts.** Conceptual organization without conceptual corrigibility:
  [TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md).
- **Normative demands.** Legible, effective and executable are three
  achievements: [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md).
- **Organizational achievements.** These are partially ordered, not
  nested: [CLAIM-474](CLAIM-474.md).

The principle generalizes those instances. It says the four are where to
look, not that they always come apart.

## What it does not say

- **That the four always come apart.** In particular architectures they
  may coincide, and that is a finding to establish.
- **That the four exhaust the questions.** Justification, authority and
  value are further questions again ([CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md), [CLAIM-511](CLAIM-511.md)).

## Note of 2026-11-01: the "realized" question in the mechanism literature

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-tmpg57ci — Proposed; the claim filed from this reading, cited as open -->

- **Question 3, "realized".** Bechtel and Abrahamsen ([LIT-tmppbuqq](../literature.d/LIT-tmppbuqq.md),
  [THEORY-tmpakm3a](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpakm3a.md)) hold that a model posited to fit behaviour shows only
  how a mechanism of that kind could produce it, and that fit usually
  cannot decide between architectures. What decides is identifying parts
  and operations by experiment. That is this claim's "interventions that
  discriminate implementations", with a field's history behind it.
- **Question 4, "maintained".** The constitutive-relevance criterion
  answers which parts realize a capability only relative to a declared
  boundary ([CLAIM-tmpg57ci](CLAIM-tmpg57ci.md)). So the claim's fourth question, whether the
  organization can maintain its own mechanisms, presupposes an answer to
  where the organization ends. Interlevel experiments do not supply that
  answer.
