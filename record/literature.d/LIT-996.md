# LIT-996: It’s not the thought that counts: Allostasis at the core of brain function

Jordan E. Theriault, Yuta Katsumi, Henning M. Reimann, Jiahe Zhang, Philip Deming, Bradford C. Dickerson, Karen S. Quigley, Lisa Feldman Barrett (2025), *Neuron 113(24), 4107–4133*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp5s3k4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5s3k4.md).*

- **One allostatic system.** 21 seeds chosen from non-human tract-tracing
  (14 subcortical, 7 cortical visceromotor and interoceptive regions).
  Resting-state connectivity at 7 T (N = 90), replicating 3 T (N = 550),
  reproduces 100%, 94% and 96% of the monosynaptic cortico-cortical,
  subcortico-cortical and subcortico-subcortical connections.
- **It contains the named networks' hubs.** The system overlaps the
  central autonomic, skeletomotor and salience networks and a pain
  signature; its two motifs, default-mode-like and salience-like, meet
  in the rich-club hubs. The paper concludes that assigning psychological
  functions to these networks is "destined to fail".
- **A laminar gradient, read as prediction.** From sensory to limbic
  cortex neurons grow larger, sparser and more connected and layer 4
  disappears. Limbic signals are read as low-dimensional allostatic
  predictions decompressing outwards, sensory signals as errors
  compressed inwards.
- **The body shapes brain-wide signalling.** Breathing couples
  oscillations across regions; cardiac phase gates visual sampling;
  vagal stimulation affects memory and mood.
- **Stress and mood.** Stress is an anticipated large energetic outlay,
  value-neutral, felt as stressful when prediction and need mismatch.
  Mood is restated as "a low-dimensional allostatic barometer".
- **Alzheimer's disease.** Falling glucose uptake after amyloid but
  before cognitive decline may protect pH and waste clearance, so
  glucose-restoring therapies might rescue cognition while worsening the
  disease.
- **The thesis is disclaimed.** The authors aim "to justify asking
  scientific questions … in a different way", not to claim
  allostasis-first is correct.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md), as the
bodily-mechanism option: "a feature a functional organization might
lack". Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmp5s3k4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5s3k4.md), which does not bear that out.
The paper is about what the brain is for, stated in functional terms, and
says nothing about what makes a state felt or whether an organization
could lack it. Its affect claims restate Barrett's, already held in
[THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md). [THEORY-tmpxaj68](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxaj68.md), filed from the reading with Barrett (2017,
[LIT-542](LIT-542.md)), holds the anatomical core of its case at the strength it is
reported; the allostasis-first synthesis and the Alzheimer's hypothesis
are not filed.
