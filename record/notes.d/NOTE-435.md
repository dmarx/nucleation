# NOTE-435: A Network Theory of Mental Disorders

## Contribution

The network approach to psychopathology had produced statistical methods
and many exploratory studies but, by the paper's own account, had "not yet
been fully articulated as a scientific theory". This paper supplies the
theory: five principles about the structure and dynamics of symptom
networks, and from them new definitions of mental health, disorder,
resilience, vulnerability and liability, plus a classification of
treatments. It is a theory paper with no new data.

## Key insight

In general medicine a tumour is a common cause: remove it and the cough,
the chest pain and the breathlessness go together. Psychiatry has looked
for such causes and, for most disorders, not found them. The paper's
answer is that there are none to find. The symptoms cause one another, and
"connections between symptoms are often prosaic. If you do not sleep, you
get tired". If those connections are strong enough the loop sustains
itself, and the person stays ill after whatever started it has passed. A
disorder is then a place the system can get stuck, not a thing inside the
person.

## Assumptions

- **Interventionist causation** (Woodward 2003, Pearl 2000). A causal
  connection between symptoms means that intervening on one would change
  the probability distribution of the other.
- **Agnosticism about realisation.** Symptom–symptom connections may be
  biological, psychological, homeostatic or social. The theory does not say
  which.
- **Principle 2, symptom-component correspondence.** The nodes are the
  symptoms of the diagnostic manuals, at the right grain. Anything else
  (neural states, genes, processes not in the manuals) must enter by
  constituting a symptom, constituting a connection, or acting from the
  external field. The paper calls this principle "quite strict".
- **The external field.** Triggers such as life events, and also processes
  inside the person with no node in the network (inflammation, and
  possibly "abnormal brain functioning"), act on the network from outside
  it.
- **Hysteresis in realistic parameterisations.** The dynamic principle
  rests on Cramer's thesis (2013) and Cramer et al. (in press), which the
  paper says "proved the existence" of hysteresis in "realistically
  parameterized symptom networks". This reading takes that on trust.

## Key results

There are no theorems or new data. The results are the principles and the
definitions that follow from them.

- **Principles 1–4** (section Network theory):
  - P1 Complexity: disorders are best characterised by interactions among
    components of a psychopathology network.
  - P2 Symptom-component correspondence (above).
  - P3 Direct causal connections between symptoms generate the network.
  - P4 Mental disorders follow network structure: the network has a
    non-trivial topology, and its tightly connected groups are what
    present as syndromes.
- **Principle 5, hysteresis** (section The dynamics of symptom networks):
  "Mental disorders arise due to the presence of hysteresis in strongly
  connected symptom networks, which implies that symptoms continue to
  activate each other, even after the triggering cause of the disorder has
  disappeared." Development runs in four phases (Figure 3): dormant
  network; activation by an external event; spread through connections;
  self-sustaining activity after the event is gone.
- **Definitions** (Table 1). Mental health is "the stable state of a weakly
  connected network", an equilibrium the system returns to, not an absence
  of symptoms. A mental disorder is "the (alternative) stable state of a
  strongly connected network". Resilience is the disposition of a weakly
  connected network to return quickly to health. Vulnerability is the
  disposition of a strongly connected network to tip into disorder when
  perturbed. Liability differences between people are differences in
  network parameters. The table crosses connectivity with external stress:
  weak/low is resilient health, weak/high is elevated symptoms (normal
  grief), strong/low is vulnerable (possibly a remission), and strong/high
  is disorder.
- **Comorbidity is intrinsic.** Bridge symptoms such as insomnia, fatigue
  and concentration problems belong to several clusters, so activation
  crosses diagnostic borders (Figure 2).
- **The DSM-5 is not theory-neutral with respect to networks.** Its
  criteria require causal couplings between symptoms (obsession driving
  compulsion), context conditions and "negative causal specifications".
- **Treatment.** Symptom interventions (an antipsychotic suppressing a
  delusion), external-field interventions (stopping precipitating drug
  use), and network interventions that change connections (CBT, so the
  delusion "no longer has the effect of causing social isolation").
  Success will "generally require a combination of network interventions
  (in order to make the healthy state accessible) and symptom
  interventions (to knock the system into that healthy state)".

## Concepts

- **symptom network**: symptoms as nodes, with a connection wherever two
  symptoms directly activate each other.
- **external field**: conditions that act on symptoms from outside the
  network. It is "external relative to the psychopathology network, but
  not relative to the physical boundaries of the person".
- **bridge symptom**: a symptom in more than one disorder's cluster,
  through which activation and comorbidity spread.
- **hysteresis**: removing a trigger does not undo the activation it
  caused in a strongly connected network.
- **mental health**: the stable state of a weakly connected network, an
  equilibrium, not the absence of symptoms.
- **mental disorder**: the alternative stable state of a strongly connected
  network, separated from health by hysteresis.
- **network intervention**: a treatment that changes symptom–symptom
  connections rather than the symptoms or their triggers.

## Connections

- **The medical model.** The paper's opening contrast is with the
  common-cause model of general medicine. That is the biomedical model
  Engel ([LIT-548](../literature.d/LIT-548.md)) challenged in 1977, though Borsboom's objection is
  different: not that the model omits the psychological and social, but
  that psychiatry has found no common causes for it to name. Engel is not
  cited.
- **Kendler.** The premise C1 is cited to Kendler (2012) and Kendler,
  Zachar and Craver (2011), and Kendler is thanked for comments. The
  integrative ambition in the Conclusions (biological, psychological and
  sociological levels in one framework) is close to the explanatory
  pluralism of Kendler's 2005 paper ([LIT-552](../literature.d/LIT-552.md), unread here).
- **Process-based therapy ([LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md)).** Hofmann and Hayes treat
  networks as an extension of functional analysis and an "analytic
  alternative to the latent disease model". Borsboom's network
  interventions and idiographic network detection (through experience
  sampling or perceived causal relations) are the diagnostic half of what
  they want.
- **CBT's maintenance models ([LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md)).** Salkovskis and colleagues'
  maintaining "vicious circles", in which behaviour sustains the negative
  meaning that drives it, are loops of exactly this kind. The difference is
  in what the nodes are. Borsboom's nodes are manual symptoms by Principle
  2. In the cognitive model the loop runs through appraisals and
  safety-seeking behaviours, which the manuals do not list. By Principle 2
  those would have to be constituents of a symptom or of a connection.
- **Emergence ([LIT-150](../literature.d/LIT-150.md)).** Rizi treats phase transitions and order
  parameters as the mark of emergence. Borsboom borrows hysteresis from
  that physics (citing van der Maas and Molenaar's catastrophe-theory
  account of cognitive development) without an order parameter or a
  measured transition.

## Bearing on the record

- **No THEORY is indicated by this reading alone.** A candidate, "mental
  disorders are alternative stable states of strongly connected symptom
  networks, maintained by feedback after their triggers have gone", would
  be Proposed at most. Promotion would need idiographic time-series
  evidence of hysteresis or of early warning signals (ref. 15, Wichers et
  al. on critical slowing down, is cited but not read here).
- **A convergence across the batch.** This paper, the cognitive maintenance
  account of [LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md) and the process-based programme of [LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md)
  all say that disorder is maintained by self-reinforcing loops rather
  than by a single latent cause. They come from three traditions
  (psychometrics, Beckian CBT, contextual behavioural science). See the
  batch report for a proposed THEORY.
- **No ML instruction.** The network estimation methods are statistics
  read for psychiatry. Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The evidence for direct causation is indirect**, as the paper says:
  conditional associations and people's reports of causal relations among
  their symptoms.
- **Principle 2 is a stated idealisation.** If the important variables are
  not manual symptoms, the networks are drawn over the wrong nodes.
- **Slow-developing disorders are outside the clear fit**, by the author's
  own account; a "Russian doll of networks within networks" is suggested
  for them, not built.
- **No mathematical model is given here.** The paper says the theory "can
  be represented in mathematical form" and points to Epskamp et al.'s
  network psychometrics and Marsman et al.'s low-rank Ising networks for
  it.
- **The treatment taxonomy is untested.** No study is cited in which
  network interventions are distinguished from symptom interventions by
  their effect on network structure.

## Open questions

- Is hysteresis observable in individual patients' time series, and does
  its presence predict relapse after the trigger is gone?
- Does CBT in fact work by weakening connections (a network intervention),
  as the paper's example assumes, or by moving symptoms (a symptom
  intervention)? This is the mechanism question [LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md) says the
  field has not answered.
- What replaces Principle 2 if processes outside the manuals (appraisals,
  avoidance, rumination) carry the loops?
