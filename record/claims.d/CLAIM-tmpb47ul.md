# CLAIM-tmpb47ul: In an arrangement, control over the conditions of action, the practical obligation to make it succeed and blame when it fails can come to rest with different parties, upstream, downstream and where failure becomes visible; where they diverge, the conscientiousness of those downstream both keeps the arrangement running and supplies the terms in which its failure is told as theirs, credit for success goes to the arrangement, and those upstream also set the standards by which responsibility is judged

## The claim

A17's opening (exchange at
https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): "**An
institution can concentrate decision-making power upstream, concentrate
moral obligations downstream, and then assign blame at the point where the
consequences become visible.**" "Neither concept requires a conscious
mastermind."

Four parts:

- **Control and duty part.** A17 §1, on a supervisor formally responsible
  for 10,000 automated decisions a day: "**authority on paper is not the
  same as actionable control in practice**". A17 §7: "**Delegating
  responsibility to someone does not necessarily discharge your own
  responsibility for making their task feasible.**" A15 §6, after Tronto as
  A relays her: "**Power and felt obligation can become inversely
  distributed.**"
- **Conscience does double duty.** A17 §10: "First, it supplies extra effort
  that helps the institution function. Then, it supplies a moral language
  in which the institution can explain failure as a lapse in individual
  duty." The first half is the moral subsidy this claim rests on.
- **Prevention is invisible, failure is not.** A17 §6: "**The system's
  resilience is attributed to the system. The system's failure is
  attributed to the person who stopped compensating for it.**" Two
  organizations with the same policies can differ by the compensating
  labour of their staff, so "Credit and blame become distributed according
  to different principles."
- **Who sets the categories.** A17 §10: "**Power includes the ability to
  establish the categories through which responsibility is understood.**"
  Its third question: "**Who has the authority to define and enforce the
  standards by which the first two questions are judged?**" A15 §9 calls
  the power over "who receives credit, blame, and public recognition for
  moral outcomes" attributional power.

**A's later restatement.** A49 §11.1, a day later, puts the wrong at the
point of restriction: "A restriction can be wrongful because it prevents an
actor from fulfilling obligations the surrounding institution continues to
assign to them", with the principle "**Responsibility for an outcome should
be evaluated alongside responsibility for the institutional conditions
under which the outcome became possible, likely, or preventable.**" On
that reading responsibility is displaced onto the party least able to
correct the outcome.

**A test.** A17 §9's audit asks who controls the conditions, who must
ensure the outcome, who can refuse or escalate, who receives credit, who
absorbs blame and who can redesign the system. The claim's divergence is a
pattern of answers to those six.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-687](CLAIM-687.md)** raises the accountability owed by an arrangement's
  controllers with how indispensable it is; **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)** names a duty to
  keep violations knowable, contestable and correctable. This says where
  blame lands when neither is met, and why: the party that sets the terms
  of evaluation is the one the evaluation spares.
- **[CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md)** (Wells Fargo, Active) has the shape in the board's own
  findings: signals of harm "held by investigators, regional leaders and
  control functions without authority to act", and leadership that "blamed
  individual employees". **[CASE-261](../cases.d/CASE-261.md)** (a stipulated hospital AI) has
  clinicians who lose discretion.
- **[CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)** and **[CLAIM-657](CLAIM-657.md)** hold how harm and need stop being legible
  to an organization; the third part above is that mechanism applied to
  the record of who prevented what.
- From the same exchange: [CLAIM-tmp238zy](CLAIM-tmp238zy.md) (verification prompts that become
  evidence the user owned the decision), [CLAIM-tmpsk25g](CLAIM-tmpsk25g.md) and [CLAIM-tmp4o53g](CLAIM-tmp4o53g.md)
  (blame and correction kept apart). The senses of responsibility are
  [TERM-tmp8pnnr](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8pnnr.md)'s: the claim needs accountability without culpability,
  A17 §9's "A person can be required to explain their decisions and
  participate in correcting a failure without necessarily being morally
  culpable for everything that happened."
- A17 §4's Uber case (Tempe, 18 March 2018), relayed from the NTSB, is not
  filed until the report is read. A's point from it: "**A moral crumple
  zone can contain someone who is partly responsible for the failure.**"

## What it does not say

A17 §8 names three dangers, which the claim keeps:

- Not that every frontline error is systemic: "The existence of upstream
  authority doesn't itself establish a moral crumple zone."
- Not that every unequal division of duty is unjust: "Unequal duties are
  not automatically unjust duties."
- Not that structure dissolves individual accountability: "having an
  explanation does not mean lacking responsibility."

Elish 2019, read ([NOTE-tmps5u6y](../notes.d/NOTE-tmps5u6y.md), [THEORY-tmp7h69w](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7h69w.md)). The claim is built on her concept, and the reading supports its blame part: responsibility "may be misattributed to a human actor who had limited control over the behavior of an automated or autonomous system", and "the moral crumple zone protects the integrity of the technological system, at the expense of the nearest human operator" (p. 40). It also supports two further parts. Credit goes to the arrangement: regulators, engineers and managers "have created contradictory dynamics in which automation is seen as safer and superior in most instances" (p. 49). Those upstream set the standards: "Because the autopilot did not malfunction in a way recognized through its certification process, the only possible malfunction, systemically, is the human pilot" (p. 50). She also supports "can contain someone who is partly responsible": "Without a doubt, actions taken by the plant operators led to the accident" (p. 44). The claim needs the reading, so [THEORY-tmp7h69w](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7h69w.md) is proposed for its `grounds`. It does not support "conscience does double duty", which is A's (and rests on [CLAIM-tmpe5u7i](CLAIM-tmpe5u7i.md), not on Elish).

Elish marks a limit the claim's text does not state: crumple zones "are not a property of every complex and automated system" (p. 50); they "are likely to take shape in the immediate aftermath of a highly publicized event or accident", where the operator lacked "sufficient information or authority to take corrective actions" (p. 51); in the Therac-25 accidents, lawsuits went to the manufacturer. If the owner adds these conditions to the claim's "What it does not say", [THEORY-tmp7h69w](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7h69w.md) should then carry `bounds` on it ([ADR-094](../decisions.d/ADR-094.md)); it does not now, since the claim does not cite the limit.

NTSB 2019, read for responsibility ([NOTE-tmpokcvk](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpokcvk.md), [THEORY-tmp4utz4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4utz4.md), corroborates). The design "precluded activation of emergency braking for collision mitigation, relying instead on the operator's intervention"; removing the second operator "increased the task demands on the sole operator"; the company's culture contributed "specifically to the vehicle operator's extended distraction" (pp. v, 45, 46). That is the control-and-duty part in an official finding, and the operator's own failure fits the claim's carve-out. But the NTSB itself carries cause upstream, to Uber and the state, so as an allocation it sits nearer the second branch of the claim's `defeated_if` than a crumple zone. The body's line that A17 §4's Uber case "is not filed until the report is read" is now out of date: it is filed as [CASE-tmpnrxpi](../cases.d/CASE-tmpnrxpi.md) (documented, specimen), which `illustrates` this claim.

Choroszewicz 2026, read ([NOTE-tmpgwh6b](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpgwh6b.md), [THEORY-tmpmdej3](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmdej3.md), corroborates): users allocated responsibility "relative to their actual authority, control and capacity to influence these tools" disproportionately (p. 3), and vigilance as unrecognized labour (p. 9), a mild form of the conscience part. Before failure, not blame after it.

Santoni de Sio & van den Hoven 2018, read ([NOTE-tmp6l52i](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6l52i.md), [THEORY-tmpnmvsj](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpnmvsj.md), corroborates): "simple human presence or 'being in the loop' is not a sufficient condition for being in control" (p. 4); designers must not make the operator's check "impossible or unduly difficult" (p. 9).

Rubel, Castro & Pham 2021, ch. 7, read as the stand-in for their 2019 article [LIT-tmpqui4c](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqui4c.md) ([NOTE-tmpe43ee](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpe43ee.md), [THEORY-tmpwjlal](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwjlal.md), corroborates): agency laundering is the upstream half, and "humans in the loop can be a means of laundering just as well as automation itself" (p. 160). It qualifies the claim: laundering needs a misleading ascription and a failed account, and the authors leave open whether it can be inadvertent, where the claim says "Neither concept requires a conscious mastermind".

Tronto 2018 and 2014, read as stand-ins for Moral Boundaries ([LIT-tmp5epfk](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5epfk.md)) and Caring Democracy ([LIT-tmpsm285](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsm285.md)) ([NOTE-tmp0tq2n](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0tq2n.md), [THEORY-tmpna7qd](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpna7qd.md); [NOTE-tmpfpn7t](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfpn7t.md), [THEORY-tmpxw0zo](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxw0zo.md); both corroborate): "the capability of the relatively privileged to refuse to accept unpleasant or burdensome responsibilities (privileged irresponsibility)" (2018, p. 8), and exemption, "absent oneself from responsibilities because one has more important duties: protection, production, taking care of one's own" (2014). Neither the books nor any text read carries the exchange's "because other people routinely perform that work on their behalf" or "socially organized inattention"; the summary's "neither yet read here" now holds for the books only.
