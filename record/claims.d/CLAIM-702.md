# CLAIM-702: Where members' compensating effort prevents failures that an organization's conditions would otherwise produce, the prevention leaves little trace while its lapse is visible, so the organization can be credited with outcomes the compensation produced and the members blamed for failures the conditions produced: credit and blame then follow different principles

## The claim

A17 §6 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c),
"Failure is visible, prevention is invisible": a nurse who repeatedly
catches deterioration and corrects errors leaves management seeing "a
functioning ward"; when she misses one sign, her failure is visible and the
history of prevention is not.

> **The system's resilience is attributed to the system. The system's
> failure is attributed to the person who stopped compensating for it.**

So, A17 §6: "**An institution can receive credit for good outcomes
produced by extraordinary individual effort while assigning individual
blame for bad outcomes produced by structural inadequacy.** Credit and
blame become distributed according to different principles." A17's
synthesis adds the conscience: "**The same moral commitment that allows a
person to keep an inadequate system functioning can make them the person
most vulnerable to blame when it finally breaks down.**"

## Why it is filed apart

It was part of [CLAIM-707](CLAIM-707.md) until the review of 2026-11-16, which
observed that the two are separable: an institution can use a frontline
worker as a moral crumple zone without relying on unseen compensation, and
can rely heavily on compensation while assigning blame fairly. The readings
of 2026-11-16 bear on them differently: Elish ([THEORY-1928](../theory.d/THEORY-1928.md)) supports the
blame mechanism and says nothing of compensation; Liedo et al. support the
reliance on care workers' commitment ([CLAIM-713](CLAIM-713.md)) and not its
invisibility. Kept apart, each link can be looked for where it occurs.

## Where it sits

- [CLAIM-713](CLAIM-713.md), the implicit moral subsidy: the compensation this claim
  says goes unseen.
- [CLAIM-700](CLAIM-700.md): the loop in which compensation, read as adequacy, drives
  cuts toward present need. This claim is its measurement link, and the
  loop rests on it.
- [CLAIM-707](CLAIM-707.md): the crumple-zone claim this was split from.
- [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md): an organization can keep harm non-legible by the projections it
  reproduces. Unseen prevention is a non-legibility of its own dependence.
- [CASE-290](../cases.d/CASE-290.md), from the continuation of the same exchange, is a
  proposed study of a fall in complaints, to tell satisfaction from
  tolerance. It is distinct: it would observe [CLAIM-700](CLAIM-700.md)'s quiet link, not
  the visibility of compensation that this claim is about.

## What it does not say

- **That it is common.** No reading of 2026-11-16 supports it; it is A's
  hypothesis.
- **That anyone intends it.**

## Note of 2026-11-18: Cook, read: prevention seen only after it lapses
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1998 — new; the reading filed on 2026-11-18 -->

Cook 1998/2000, read ([THEORY-1998](../theory.d/THEORY-1998.md), corroborates). The claim's first half has a statement in safety science. Complex systems run "because people can make it function, despite the presence of many flaws" (point 5); "People continuously create safety", in activities that are "for the most part, part of normal operations and superficially straightforward" (point 17); and "Outsiders rarely acknowledge the duality of this role. In non-accident filled times, the production role is emphasized. After accidents, the defense against failure role is emphasized" (point 9). That is prevention leaving little trace while its lapse is seen. Cook says nothing about the organization being credited with what the compensation produced, so the claim's second half, credit and blame by different principles, is not supported. Argued, not shown by data.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-18: what holds it back

Cook ([THEORY-1998](../theory.d/THEORY-1998.md)) corroborates the first half, that prevention leaves little trace while its lapse is seen. Nothing in the record shows an organization credited with outcomes its members' compensation produced, so the second half, credit and blame by different principles, rests on A's illustration of the ward alone; a case or reading that traces the credit for a compensated outcome is what is missing.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Merton's Matthew effect, read

**A parallel** ([LIT-3002](../literature.d/LIT-3002.md), [NOTE-tmp4eogg](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp4eogg.md)). Merton documents credit
allocated by standing rather than by contribution: "eminent scientists get
disproportionately great credit for their contributions to science while
relatively unknown scientists tend to get disproportionately little credit
for comparable contributions" (p. 58), most of all in collaborations. This
claim's credit following visibility rather than the compensating effort
is the organizational form of the same misallocation. Merton's evidence is
"presumptive", laureates' testimony. No edge.
