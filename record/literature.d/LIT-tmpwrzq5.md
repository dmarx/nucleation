# LIT-tmpwrzq5: Being No One: The Self-Model Theory of Subjectivity

Thomas Metzinger (2003), *Being No One: The Self-Model Theory of
Subjectivity*, Cambridge, MA: MIT Press (A Bradford Book) — DOI
10.7551/mitpress/1551.001.0001.

**On the citation.** The brief's details are right: author, title, MIT
Press, 2003. Crossref gives the subtitle "The Self-Model Theory of
Subjectivity", carried in the title here.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description and the chapter list, not a
reading. For the theory itself, see [LIT-tmp7cy9g](LIT-tmp7cy9g.md).*

- The publisher's description, in part: "According to Thomas Metzinger,
  no such things as selves exist in the world: nobody ever had or was a
  self. All that exists are phenomenal selves, as they appear in
  conscious experience. The phenomenal self, however, is not a thing but
  an ongoing process; it is the content of a 'transparent self-model.'"
  It "draws strongly on neuroscientific research to present a
  representationalist and functional analysis of what a consciously
  experienced first-person perspective actually is", and asks "How
  exactly does strong, consciously experienced subjectivity emerge out of
  objective events in the natural world?"
- Contents (Crossref chapter DOIs, with pages): Questions (1–12); Tools I
  (13–106); The Representational Deep Structure of Phenomenal Experience
  (107–212); Neurophenomenological Case Studies I (213–264); Tools II
  (265–298); The Representational Deep Structure of the Phenomenal
  First-Person Perspective (299–428); Neurophenomenological Case Studies
  II (429–546); Preliminary Answers (547–634); References (635–662); Name
  Index (663–670); Subject Index (671–700).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request to fill out the record's
coverage of selfhood, as the book-length statement of the self-model
theory of subjectivity. No anthology topic holds it, it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice, and the anthology does not hold
it.

`Deferred` because the book was not read. The theory is in the record
through Metzinger's own short statement, "Self models" ([LIT-tmp7cy9g](LIT-tmp7cy9g.md),
read in full), which cites this book for its ten constraints on
phenomenality (ch. 3) and its analyses of altered and pathological
self-models (ch. 7). Where the book bears on the record is set out there
and in its NOTE: against Minsky's self-models ([LIT-432](LIT-432.md)), against Dennett's
narrative self ([LIT-442](LIT-442.md)), and on the question whether a machine with a
self-model has a self. `metaphysics` is carried for the book's thesis that
there are no selves, only self-models, which is an ontological claim.
