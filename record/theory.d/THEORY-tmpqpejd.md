# THEORY-tmpqpejd: Youssef and colleagues review two small parent–child studies and three of stress in pregnancy, report that Holocaust survivors had higher FKBP5 methylation and their offspring lower, and call the evidence that trauma-related methylation changes pass from parent to child accumulating but preliminary

## The account

Two parent–child studies. Rwandan widows pregnant during the genocide and
their children (25) had more PTSD and depression, higher NR3C1 methylation
and lower cortisol than women pregnant at the time but living abroad, and
their children (25). Holocaust survivors (32) and their offspring (22),
against 8 controls and their 9 offspring: FKBP5 intron 7 methylation
"significantly higher" in survivors and "significantly lower" in their
offspring, which the authors call "perplexing", suggesting "biological
accommodation" or a "compensatory mechanism". Three studies of stress in
pregnancy link it to NR3C1 methylation in newborns or adolescents.

The authors say the studies "suggest that children of parents who had
suffered from extreme trauma have methylation modifications", and that
"this PTSD risk can be passed from generation to generation", while
naming few studies, small samples, possible publication bias, unmeasured
confounds and cross-sectional designs. They recommend larger, genome-wide,
multi-centre studies that record whether trauma preceded or occurred in
pregnancy.

## Evidence

The review (NOTE-1310): PubMed to March 2018, with no stated search terms
or inclusion criteria. Each study is small and cross-sectional; the Rwanda
groups live in different countries; the Holocaust survivors' "extreme
starvation", the authors grant, "could have also contributed to the effect
seen".

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297. The Holocaust FKBP5 study, cited in the question for the
germline route, is intergenerational and finds the offspring's mark
opposite in direction to the parent's: what reaches the child, if
anything, is a response to the parent's state, not a copy of it. The
pregnancy studies belong to the womb route.
