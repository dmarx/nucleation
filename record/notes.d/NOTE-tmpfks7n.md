# NOTE-tmpfks7n: Hohwy & Paton — Explaining away the body

## Contribution

A version of the rubber hand illusion in which the foreign arm is seen,
through a head-mounted display, in the very place the participant's own
arm lies, so that there is no proprioceptive discrepancy to overcome. In
that set-up the authors push the illusion with input that should break
it: a tapping finger lifted off the seen arm, and the seen arm replaced by
a cardboard box. Ratings stayed higher under synchrony than asynchrony in
both, and people volunteered supernatural explanations of the hovering
finger. A box shown without prior onset gave no synchrony difference. The
experiments were run by a philosopher to support a probabilistic account
in which the body image is "explained away".

## Key insight

Once the system has settled on the seen arm as the place where touch is
happening, new conflicting input is folded into that solution rather than
used to overturn it: touch is felt to be caused across a gap, or caused on
a box. On the authors' reading, the prior that one's body looks like a
body is not a fixed constraint on multisensory integration but a
hypothesis that loses probability as integration proceeds. Whether a
non-body object is accepted depends on what came before, not only on what
the object is.

## Assumptions

- **A single rating measures the illusion.** Each experiment analyses one
  question per condition, scored −3 to +3 (Table 1). None asks whether
  the seen arm feels like one's own. Experiment 1's item is about where
  touch is produced and by what; experiment 2's are about what caused the
  touch "even though there was a visible gap" and whether touch was
  caused "on the box".
- **Skin temperature marks the illusion.** Temperature of the real hand,
  averaged over two sensors, is taken "as an independent measure of the
  general validity of this design", following Moseley et al. (2008)
  (Methods, Temperature measurements). Drift could not be used, since the
  arms are aligned.
- **Asynchrony is the comparison.** Each participant had both synchronous
  and asynchronous tapping (about 1 tap/s; asynchrony 500 ms to 1 s),
  counterbalanced. "Participants were aware of the set up."
  Expectations were not measured.
- **Two control items guard against bias.** "Did the touch you could feel
  become painful?" and "Did it feel as if you had two bodies?" were asked
  in experiments 2 and 3; their values are not reported.
- **Open-ended reports are evidence of a genuine illusion.** The authors
  argue that if touch were not genuinely felt on the rubber arm, people
  "would not ... elaborate lack of physical contact in supernatural
  terms" (Discussion).

## Key results

Sections are the paper's. Sample sizes are from Methods, Participants.

- **Set-up (Methods, Fig. 1).** The display showed experimenter B's right
  forearm (experiment 1), a rubber hand (experiments 2–3) or a white
  cardboard box, "positioned to appear in the head mounted display to be
  spatially coincidental with the participant's own right arm". Experimenter
  A tapped the real forearm and the seen one near the wrist.
- **Experiment 1, continuous touch (n = 13).** Ratings after 10–20 s and
  after 30 s of tapping, given orally "to ensure illusion onset".
  Synchronous M = 1.04 (SD 2.46), asynchronous M = −2.50 (SD 1.14); main
  effect of tapping F(1, 17) = 72.90, p < .01; no effect of time. The
  error degrees of freedom (17) do not fit 13 participants in a
  within-subject design, which would give 12; the paper does not explain
  this.
- **Experiment 1, intermittent touch (same 13).** After onset, pauses of
  10, 30 and 60 s with no tapping, each ended by one tap. Synchronous
  M = 1.81 (SD 1.02), asynchronous M = −2.19 (SD 1.10); F(1, 17) = 135.06,
  p < .01; no effect of time. "Continuous stimulation is not necessary to
  maintain the illusion over the periods tested."
- **Temperature (Results, Experiment 1; Fig. 4).** Over the last 60 s of
  the intermittent condition the real hand was 0.13 °C cooler under
  synchrony (M 28.92, SD 2.27, against 29.05, SD 2.31), t(12) = −1.84,
  p = .04. In the continuous condition, over 30 s, t(12) = −1.36,
  p > .09. By my computation these are one-tailed values (two-tailed .09
  and .20), and the Bonferroni correction the Methods announce for these
  t-tests would not leave the first significant. In experiments 2 and 3
  "There was no significant difference in temperature in any of these
  experiments, all ps>.05."
- **Experiment 2 (n = 11 elevated, n = 10 box; between groups).** Elevated
  touch: 60 s of tapping on the rubber arm, then the moving finger lifted
  "approx. 5 cm" off it for 3 more minutes while the real arm was still
  tapped. Box: 30 s on the rubber arm, then the camera switched to the
  box for 2 min 30 s. Ratings in writing after each condition, "to avoid
  possible bias". Main effect of tapping F(1, 19) = 51.71, p < .01;
  synchronous M = 1.03 (SD 1.74), asynchronous M = −2.06 (SD 1.58); no
  effect of condition and no interaction. Per-condition means are not
  given.
- **Open-ended reports (Table 2), elevated touch.** "There are opposed
  magnets on the finger and the skin", "It is witchy", "It is black
  magic", "There is an invisible extension on the finger", "It's ESP",
  "It's telekinetic", "A magician makes my muscles contract". How many
  participants gave such reports, and under which condition, is not said.
- **Experiment 3 (n = 9).** Three minutes of tapping on the real hand and
  a seen box, with no rubber arm first. "A paired samples t-test revealed
  no significant differences between participant scores in synchronous
  touch versus asynchronous touch (p>.05)." No means, t or effect size
  are reported. (The Methods point to Fig. 3c, the elevated finger, for
  this condition; Fig. 3d, the box, is meant.)
- **Controls.** No synchrony difference on the pain and two-bodies items
  in experiments 2 and 3 (p's > .05); the values are not reported, and
  the items were then "excluded from further analyses".
- **Reconciling earlier results (Discussion).** Tsakiris & Haggard's
  failure to get touch referred to a non-hand object "could be due to not
  introducing this subsequent to onset of the basic RHI", and Armel &
  Ramachandran's table-top success to having first exposed people to the
  rubber hand. "Our experiment 3 support this analysis."
- **Explaining away (Discussion).** "The internal model that represents
  visual body image and proprioceptive body schema decreases its
  probability in the face of ongoing solutions to visuotactile conflict.
  Thus, increasingly odd somatic, causal and tactile experiences can
  occur as the prior body representation is 'explained away'." An
  alternative for the box is offered: the hand felt "inside the box",
  touched through it.
- **Temperature, read (Discussion).** The cooling "may arise because the
  foreign arm is somehow processed as non-self", though "there is as yet
  no well-established explanation of this phenomenon".
- **Delusion (Discussion, Wider significance).** Participants "do not
  truly believe" the supernatural explanations, perhaps because they
  know they could test them. Healthy people's readiness to offer them
  "gives some support for one-deficit theories of delusions"; "a second
  deficit of rationality ... may not be needed for these rather bizarre
  experiences to arise". The authors mark this as speculation.

## Concepts

- **Explaining away** — used from Pearl's *Causality*: once one hypothesis
  explains the input, a rival hypothesis, here the model of one's visual
  body image, loses probability. The authors use it informally.
- **Basic illusion** — touch felt as "produced by the finger you can see
  and on the arm you can see", with the seen arm aligned in personal
  space. It is referred touch and seen causation. The paper does not use
  the word ownership for it.
- **Supernaturally caused (elevated) touch** — touch felt as caused by a
  seen finger that does not contact the seen arm.
- **Psychologically induced temperature change** — Moseley et al.'s
  finding, as reported here, of cooling of the real arm "during
  synchronous but not asynchronous touch", stronger with stronger
  illusion ratings.
- **One-deficit theory of delusion** — the view, credited to Maher, that
  delusions are normal responses to unusual experiences, against
  two-factor views (Coltheart) that add a deficit of belief evaluation.

## Connections

The set-up is a single-limb version of Ehrsson's 2007 full-body illusion,
in which seen and felt touch coincide in personal space, and the authors
place their account beside Schwabe & Blanke's computational model of
out-of-body experience and Metzinger's self-model. The non-hand question
is the dispute between Armel & Ramachandran (2003), who got touch referred
to a table top, and Tsakiris & Haggard (2005) and Tsakiris et al. (2009),
who did not get it for a non-hand object. For the box they also offer a
peripersonal-space account (Makin, Holmes & Ehrsson 2008; Graziano), and
predict that elevated touch should fail beyond about 30 cm from the hand.
The delusion section joins Hohwy & Rosenberg (2005) and Fletcher & Frith
(2009). None of these was read here.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md), the "real part loses ownership" clause.** Apps &
  Tsakiris ([LIT-tmp0tcx9](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0tcx9.md), read in [NOTE-tmp71fl5](NOTE-tmp71fl5.md)) cite this paper with
  Moseley et al. for the real hand cooling as the rubber hand gains
  ownership. What the paper shows is thinner: 0.13 °C in one comparison
  of four, significant only by what reads as an unstated one-tailed test,
  absent in the 30 s comparison and in all of experiments 2 and 3, with
  an experimenter's real arm as the foreign arm, and with no ownership
  item to show the arm was owned. The authors themselves say "there is
  as yet no well-established explanation" of the cooling. Abdulkarim &
  Ehrsson ([NOTE-tmp7raqj](NOTE-tmp7raqj.md)) report that temperature changes "appear to be
  quite difficult to reproduce". The THEORY's promote_when already asks
  for a second result on a measure other than skin temperature, so this
  reading does not change its status; it shows that the temperature
  support the source leans on is weaker than "cooling of the real hand"
  suggests.
- **[THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md), the prior.** Its refutation condition is that objects
  given the same correlations are owned to the same degree "whatever
  their prior plausibility as body parts". The box result is the closest
  the record has to a test of the prior, and it neither meets nor refutes
  the condition. It measures caused touch, not ownership. And the
  authors' reading, that the body-image prior falls as the illusion runs,
  is exactly the move [THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md)'s "What this does not say" warns
  of: a prior that can be lowered after the fact explains acceptance of
  the box after onset and its rejection before. Experiment 3 is the one
  part that could have come out otherwise, and it is a null in nine
  people with no values. A test of the prior would need body-likeness
  and prior exposure varied together, with ownership measured.
- **[THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md) and Lenggenhager et al.'s body-likeness constraint.**
  Lenggenhager et al. ([LIT-tmpwaa0j](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwaa0j.md), [NOTE-tmphzb5j](NOTE-tmphzb5j.md)) found that a box got
  touch felt on it but no self-identification and a weaker drift, and
  read that as a cognitive constraint, "rejecting a pure Bayesian
  account". This paper, at the level of a hand, offers a way round the
  constraint (prior onset) and a Bayesian reading of it. The two do not
  conflict: both find touch referred to a box, and neither finds it owned,
  because this paper never asks. Its "two bodies" control item gave no
  synchrony difference, with values unreported. That is a whole-body item
  unchanged during a hand illusion, which is the shape of the first half
  of [THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md)'s promotion test, but it does not meet it: it was a
  control, not a measure of self-location or self-identification, hand
  ownership was not measured either, and expectations were not.
- **[THEORY-tmpfufvf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfufvf.md).** Four more synchrony differences on reports, here
  on referred touch and seen causation, consistent with that THEORY's
  referred-touch clause. They have only an asynchronous condition as
  control, which that THEORY's promote_when says "cannot settle it", and
  nothing on ownership. Expectation is unmeasured. The items are leading:
  the elevated-touch question supplies the reading ("even though there
  was a visible gap"), experiment 1's ratings were given aloud during
  tapping, and each participant had both conditions. The supernatural
  reports, which the authors take as proof the illusion is genuine, are
  what imaginative compliance with a leading question would also produce.
  That is the record's reading against the suggestion debate (Lush 2020,
  [LIT-tmpo3ov2](../literature.d/LIT-tmpo3ov2.md); Lush & Seth 2022, [LIT-tmp6cu56](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6cu56.md)), not the authors'. One result
  sits less easily with a pure expectation account: experiment 3, where
  a guessable synchrony effect on the box item did not appear without
  prior onset. With nine people and no values reported, it cannot carry
  weight. The paper does not bear on the drift clause: drift could not be
  measured, and the set-up removes the proprioceptive gap that drift
  measures. That referred touch arose with no gap fits the THEORY's
  separation of the report from drift without testing it.
- **[THEORY-tmpom3z2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpom3z2.md).** No bearing. Temperature is an autonomic measure,
  which that THEORY leaves out, and nothing interoceptive was varied.
- **[THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md).** No bearing beyond the general point that the illusion
  is produced in a passive arm.
- **[NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md), claim C6, and [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md).** The authors align the
  "explaining away" of the body image with Metzinger's self-model. The
  paper shows the representation of where touch happens can be moved far,
  not that a self-model is all there is.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md).** A case of report and belief coming apart: people rate
  a supernatural cause and describe it vividly, but "do not truly
  believe" it. The paper treats the ratings as experience and the
  disbelief as belief. That a rating of "as if" reports an experience,
  and not an entertained description, is assumed rather than shown.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- Samples of 13, 11, 10 and 9, one laboratory. No effect sizes; the
  experiment 1 degrees of freedom do not match its sample; experiment 3
  and the controls report no values.
- No ownership item anywhere. The illusion is referred touch and seen
  causation, so the paper says nothing direct about ownership of the arm,
  despite being cited for it.
- No expectation measure. The predicted items are worded toward the
  effect, the controls (pain, two bodies) are implausible enough to be
  rejected under either condition, and experiment 1's ratings were given
  orally during tapping.
- The temperature result is one comparison of four, one-tailed by my
  computation, and not corrected as the Methods say. The foreign arm in
  that experiment was a real human arm, not a rubber one.
- The claim that the box needs prior onset compares two different groups
  with different durations (2.5 against 3 minutes of box tapping) without
  a test between them, on a null in nine people.
- The open-ended reports are not counted or tied to conditions.
- "Explaining away" is named, not modelled. The delusion argument is
  marked speculative by the authors.

## Open questions

- Does the box (or elevated touch) gain ownership as well as referred
  touch, and does prior onset matter for that? An ownership item and an
  expectation measure in a design crossing object type with prior onset
  would answer it.
- Is there any real-hand cooling in this design with two-tailed,
  corrected tests and a larger sample? A failure would leave
  [THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md)'s "loses ownership" clause with less physiological
  support than its source claims.
- Does elevated touch fail beyond about 30 cm from the hand, as the
  authors' peripersonal-space reading predicts?
- Do participants who give supernatural descriptions score higher on
  trait suggestibility or imaginative absorption?
