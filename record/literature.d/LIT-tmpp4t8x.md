# LIT-tmpp4t8x: Knowledge is not always more valuable than mere true belief

Richard Pettigrew (2025), *PhilArchive preprint (listed as "In Preparation" on the author's homepage)* — <https://philpapers.org/rec/PETKIN-2>

## Key takeaways

- Knowledge is not always more valuable than mere true belief — we do not always want it for those we care about even when it costs nothing — and veritism, on which truth is the only fundamental epistemic value, predicts exactly when it is and is not.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

If knowledge were always more valuable than mere true belief, we would want those we care about to have it whenever it is effortless to get, hope they have it when unsure, and feel relief on learning they do. Pettigrew observes that we do not always have these attitudes, nor think we should. He argues that veritism — the view that truth is the sole fundamental source of epistemic value — makes the best sense of this pattern.
