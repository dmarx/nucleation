# QUESTION-458: Is what distinguishes luria from ontology and metamodel evolution the coupling of schema change to recorded decision, migration and executable checks, and does that coupling do work that argumentation-governed or performance-gated evolution does not?

## Why it is a question

From U8 of the exchange "luria experiment design"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af35bfc-35f8-83e9-a63d-d594703eabda). The owner
had said at U7 that he was "considering writing up luria and its
application to the Nucleation project as a journal article", and A20 had
framed luria as a maintained record of claims. U8 moved the centre: "what
about luria's emphasis on schema modifiability, extendability, and coupling
those features to governance structures and mechanisms?"

The record describes the coupling but does not claim it is distinctive:

- [CLAIM-419](../claims.d/CLAIM-419.md): the grammar is fixed at any moment and amendable by decision,
  and a move that fit no scheme has repeatedly been treated as a defect of
  the grammar. [CLAIM-195](../claims.d/CLAIM-195.md): the constraint set persistence is relative to is
  fixed by an amendable decision.
- [CASE-178](../cases.d/CASE-178.md): nine grammar refinements in ten came with an ADR, against 16%
  of all pull requests, in the record's first month.
- [SYNTHESIS-012](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-012.md), [CLAIM-181](../claims.d/CLAIM-181.md) and [CLAIM-469](../claims.d/CLAIM-469.md): the grammar as an apparatus
  amended when a misfit is noticed.
- Worked instances: [CASE-131](../cases.d/CASE-131.md) and [ADR-044](../decisions.d/ADR-044.md) (corroboration split from
  dependence), [ADR-061](../decisions.d/ADR-061.md) (support judgements moved from CLAIM to ARG), and
  [ADR-084](../decisions.d/ADR-084.md) (a scheme's scope narrowed after it proved to conflate two
  things).

A22 named the comparison class the question needs, and A's point is that
it is not agent memory: "DILIGENT, Edapt and OBO Foundry are probably
essential to a credible novelty claim." Its candidate answer: "The
defensible contribution is therefore not that Luria invented governed
schema evolution", but "the particular integration of evolving
subject-matter claims, evolving representational distinctions, and
evolving governance mechanisms in one operational, inspectable research
record". A39 put the test: "The question is whether governing
representational change as a revisable epistemic and institutional
commitment improves the quality of subsequent collective inquiry." A48:
"Can rationale, explicit institutional standing, and governed migration
provide value beyond adequately designed performance-gated evolution?"

## The prior art the question is posed against

All registered unread from the same exchange; what A says of each is
unverified.

- Argumentation-governed ontology evolution: DILIGENT ([LIT-2274](../literature.d/LIT-2274.md)) and
  Apostolou et al.'s design-rationale framework for ontology change
  ([LIT-2475](../literature.d/LIT-2475.md)).
- Ontology change and coupled model migration: Flouris et al.'s survey
  ([LIT-2499](../literature.d/LIT-2499.md)), COPE, the approach behind Edapt ([LIT-2479](../literature.d/LIT-2479.md)), and KGCL,
  a change language ([LIT-2313](../literature.d/LIT-2313.md)).
- Collaborative, versioned and governed vocabularies: VoCol
  ([LIT-2507](../literature.d/LIT-2507.md)), WebProtégé ([LIT-2508](../literature.d/LIT-2508.md)) and the OBO Foundry's
  principles operationalized as checks ([LIT-2494](../literature.d/LIT-2494.md)).
- Performance-gated evolution by agents: EvoOntology ([LIT-2311](../literature.d/LIT-2311.md)).
- Self-adaptive architecture: autonomic computing's control loop
  ([LIT-2312](../literature.d/LIT-2312.md), which describes it without the later name MAPE-K) and models at run time ([LIT-2305](../literature.d/LIT-2305.md)). A50: "It would be difficult
  to argue that Luria introduces a new general architecture for
  self-monitoring and repair"; at most a "modifiable, epistemically
  structured and governed knowledge substrate" for such a loop.
- Argument and claim representation: the Argument Interchange Format
  ([LIT-2500](../literature.d/LIT-2500.md)), micropublications ([LIT-2481](../literature.d/LIT-2481.md)) and scholarly argument
  graphs ([LIT-2515](../literature.d/LIT-2515.md)); truth maintenance ([LIT-2291](../literature.d/LIT-2291.md), [LIT-2320](../literature.d/LIT-2320.md)), against
  which [CLAIM-664](../claims.d/CLAIM-664.md) states what the record does not do.
- Levels of rule: Polski and Ostrom's operational, collective-choice and
  constitutional rules ([LIT-2482](../literature.d/LIT-2482.md)), which A50 maps onto changing an
  entry, changing the schema, and changing who may change the schema.

## What would count as an answer

Two parts, which can come apart.

1. **Distinctiveness.** A capability-and-workflow comparison against the
   works above, A22's "not a claim that they cannot support accountable
   change": which of rationale, migration, executable checks and authority
   over the schema each system couples, and which it leaves to convention.
   A comparison that finds one of them couples all four answers the first
   half no.
2. **Work done.** A study that holds the information, tools and budget
   fixed and varies schema modifiability and change governance
   independently, such as [CASE-253](../cases.d/CASE-253.md), with a performance-gated arm.
   [CLAIM-667](../claims.d/CLAIM-667.md) is a proposed answer: governance raises the value of
   modifiability on tasks whose categories are inadequate. A result in
   which performance-gated evolution matches governed evolution on
   semantic correction and regressions, at lower cost, answers the second
   half no.

A22's caveat bounds both: governance in luria is "partly mechanized and
partly a human practice". "Luria does not automate epistemic authority."

## Note of 2026-11-12: the prior art read

Read on 2026-11-12 among the works of the exchange "luria experiment design"; each reading's NOTE and THEORY hold the detail.

The section above lists the prior art as registered and unread. Most of it
has now been read, from the papers; later versions of the systems were not.
For part 1, which of the four couplings each couples, as its paper
describes it:

| System, reading | Rationale or decision recorded | Migration of existing content | Executable checks on change | Authority over the schema |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DILIGENT (Tempich et al., [THEORY-1726](../theory.d/THEORY-1726.md)) | yes: issues, arguments, positions, a board's decision with status and line of reasoning | no: users "may rather reuse" new concepts | no: detection by a reasoner hoped for, not built | yes: a control board |
| OntoGov (Apostolou et al. 2005, [THEORY-1727](../theory.d/THEORY-1727.md), in place of [LIT-2475](../literature.d/LIT-2475.md)) | yes: design decisions and their reasons in a lifecycle ontology | change propagated to services through ontology links | yes: automatic identification of inconsistencies | among stakeholders; the meta-ontologies are fixed, so the grammar is not under change |
| Flouris et al.'s survey ([THEORY-1774](../theory.d/THEORY-1774.md)) | changes logged; no rationale with standing | yes: side-effects on dependants determined, a standard step by 2002 | validation of a change, not a gate on content | not addressed |
| COPE (Herrmannsdoerfer et al., [THEORY-1762](../theory.d/THEORY-1762.md)) | a replayable history of operations, no rationale | yes: each metamodel change coupled to a migration of its models | not described | not addressed |
| KGCL with Ontobot (Hegde et al., [THEORY-1756](../theory.d/THEORY-1756.md)) | a change request and a diff, not why | content changes, not schema | none described | yes: a curator approves the merge |
| EvoOntology (Chong et al., [THEORY-1733](../theory.d/THEORY-1733.md)) | a typed change with its expected effect; rejections logged | no: schema edits leave instantiated content unchanged | yes: a paired evaluation gate | only the automatic gate |
| Git4Voc (Halilaj et al., [THEORY-1742](../theory.d/THEORY-1742.md), in place of VoCol) | reasons by convention in issues and commits | diffs and releases, no migration | yes: validation hooks refuse a commit | yes: roles |
| WebProtégé (Tudorache et al., [THEORY-1768](../theory.d/THEORY-1768.md)) | discussion attached to the entity, not required | no | form-value validation only | yes: permissions by access policy |
| OBO Foundry principles (Jackson et al., [THEORY-1775](../theory.d/THEORY-1775.md)) | rule changes discussed in issues; a standing governance body; no decision record per change | no migration of members' content | yes: conformance checks, reported rather than enforced | yes: a standing governance body |
| AIF (Chesñevar et al., [THEORY-1739](../theory.d/THEORY-1739.md)) | none: the core is to evolve "as consensus changes", with no mechanism stated | not addressed | not addressed | left open |

No system read couples all four, so the readings do not answer part 1
"no". Each coupling exists somewhere: recorded decisions with reasons in
DILIGENT and OntoGov, migration in COPE and in the change process Flouris et
al. survey, checks that refuse a change in Git4Voc and EvoOntology, authority
in DILIGENT, Git4Voc, WebProtégé, KGCL and OBO. OntoGov comes nearest, with
reasons and consistency checks under change, and it keeps the grammar
fixed. What none of them shows is the decision as a typed, citable entry
that the schema change cites, changed by the same process as the content it
governs, with the migration landing in the same change. That is the narrower
form A22's candidate answer would have to take. Part 2 is untouched: none
varies governance against performance-gating, and EvoOntology is the
performance-gated comparator a study of it needs ([ARG-098](../arguments.d/ARG-098.md)).

The rest of what was read sets the comparison class or frames it:

- **Self-adaptive architecture.** Kephart and Chess ([THEORY-1796](../theory.d/THEORY-1796.md))
  couple change to regression tests and reversion and give elements
  authority relations, but what is self-managed is configuration under
  fixed human policy, and the goals are revised outside the loop. The
  paper describes a monitor, analyze, plan and execute loop over
  knowledge; it never uses the name "MAPE-K", which came later, so the
  section above now says autonomic computing's control loop. Bencomo et
  al.'s survey of models at run time ([THEORY-1788](../theory.d/THEORY-1788.md), for [LIT-2305](../literature.d/LIT-2305.md)) has
  no governance among its dimensions.
- **Argument and claim representation.** Micropublications
  ([THEORY-1758](../theory.d/THEORY-1758.md)), Song et al.'s argument graph ([THEORY-1745](../theory.d/THEORY-1745.md)) and
  ORKG ([THEORY-1783](../theory.d/THEORY-1783.md)) represent claims and support with a fixed or
  nearly absent schema and no process for changing it. Doyle's and de
  Kleer's truth maintenance ([THEORY-1753](../theory.d/THEORY-1753.md), [THEORY-1778](../theory.d/THEORY-1778.md)) maintain
  belief over a fixed vocabulary; against them [CLAIM-664](../claims.d/CLAIM-664.md), now
  narrowed, says what the record does not compute.
- **Levels of rule.** Polski and Ostrom ([THEORY-1809](../theory.d/THEORY-1809.md)) supply the frame
  A50 used, and a test the readings suggest: whether practice tracks the
  recorded decisions, their rules-in-use against rules-in-form.
- **Precedent and data.** Leitgeb and Carus on Carnap's explication
  ([THEORY-1797](../theory.d/THEORY-1797.md)) are a philosophical precedent for replacing concepts by
  decision, purpose by purpose, with rivals allowed to coexist.
  Orzechowski et al. ([THEORY-1807](../theory.d/THEORY-1807.md)) find a curated schema that
  "disagrees with its own data", and validate the ontology against a
  data-derived summary before each release, a governance step without a
  decision record. The TRACE-OU benchmark ([THEORY-1786](../theory.d/THEORY-1786.md)) scores accept,
  modify and reject decisions on proposals under written rules, with no
  rationale or migration.

Two corrections to the section above. COPE's paper never names Edapt; that
COPE became Eclipse Edapt is known from outside it. A's bare "Stojanovic" at
A22 is most likely Stojanovic et al. 2002, named by Flouris et al. and now
registered ([LIT-2514](../literature.d/LIT-2514.md), not read). The question is unchanged.
