# NOTE-tmpg5axw: Tomasello — An agency-based model of executive and metacognitive regulation

## Contribution

A compact statement of the agent architecture of The Evolution of Agency,
applied to child development: executive function and metacognition are two
analogous control systems on different tiers, and cognitive flexibility is
the internalized coordination of perspectives first learnt in shared
agency.

## Key results

1. **The model.** "The computational model for agency is cybernetic control
   systems such as thermostats and self-driving cars" (p. 2).
2. **The grades.** "Goal-directed agency evolved in the first vertebrates."
   "Intentional agency evolved in the first mammals." (p. 2).
   "Metacognitive (or rational) agency evolved in the first great apes."
   "Shared agency evolved in the early humans" (p. 3). "The hypothesis is
   that there was a gradual transition from one form of agency to another
   across species" (p. 3).
3. **Two tiers.** "executive function comprises cognitive processes that
   regulate attention and action, whereas metacognition regulates these
   executive-level cognitive processes themselves" (p. 2); "executive and
   metacognitive processes are two analogous control systems operating on
   different material from different tiers" (p. 9).
4. **Development.** "Both intentional agency and joint agency emerge at 9 to
   12 months of age" (p. 3); metacognitive and collective agency at 3–4
   years.
5. **Apes.** "The apes were in this case metacognitively assessing their
   executive decision after they had already made it" (p. 7).
6. **Normative self-regulation.** "This kind of normative self-regulation
   can be characterized as we > me normative self-regulation" (p. 7),
   citing the 2020 obligation article.

## Assumptions

Agency is feedback control; each grade adds a tier or a form of sharing;
development passes through architectures analogous to the evolutionary
ones. The phylogeny is asserted, not argued, here.

## Concepts

Feedback control; goal-directed, intentional, metacognitive (rational) and
shared agency; executive and metacognitive tiers; joint and collective
agency; we > me normative self-regulation; cognitive flexibility as
perspective coordination.

  a direct answer within one account: distinct executive and metacognitive
  tiers, each monitoring and controlling the one below. The relation
  `bears_on` is written from this reading's account.
- **[CLAIM-024](../claims.d/CLAIM-024.md) and [CLAIM-487](../claims.d/CLAIM-487.md)** (whether control theory and
  executive-control research replace the will-organization account), a
  test case: this is such an account, carried from lizards to children.
  Noted; whether it bears out [CLAIM-024](../claims.d/CLAIM-024.md)'s charge is left to the owner.
- **[CLAIM-010](../claims.d/CLAIM-010.md)** (every control system has a will), a contrast: here a
  bare control loop is only goal-directed; choosing among options needs an
  executive tier.
- **[CLAIM-155](../claims.d/CLAIM-155.md) and [CLAIM-174](../claims.d/CLAIM-174.md)**, a parallel: an independent ladder of
  grades of agency, which also leaves consciousness out.
- **[CLAIM-539](../claims.d/CLAIM-539.md)** (Jennings, a process over processes), a parallel to the
  metacognitive tier.
- **[THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)**, background: its obligation account becomes the top
  grade of the sequence.

## Limitations

A conceptual essay with no new data; the phylogeny is restated, not
argued; labels drift from the book (rational becomes metacognitive,
socially normative becomes shared); it carries none of the book's evidence
chapters.
