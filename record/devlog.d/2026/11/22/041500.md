**Going right.** Following ADR-tmpw70lb, each reader's report was relayed to the owner as it came in, with what it changed. Doing that in the chat, before any filing, put the overreaches in plain view while they were cheap to fix: CLAIM-841 grounded on a paper that never mentions criticality, ARG-141 resting tests on a paper that has none, CLAIM-795 citing Newman for all seven mechanisms when he covers four, and LIT-2983's summary placing the spike in the features rather than the weights. Each went to the filers as a named correction, and two became Rejected ARGs whose summaries keep the conclusion's standing apart from the route (ADR-136). Asking the readers to recheck the record after reading also found held entries the first search missed, around ten per work.

**Going wrong.** All three filers ran in parallel and hit the account's usage limit together, minutes in. They were resumed from their transcripts and finished, but the stop hook asked for commits while they were still editing, so the branch carries work-in-progress commits that cut across filers. That is not what ADR-tmp9sp9d's rule meant, which was to commit each worker's listed files when it finishes. The pull request was held back until all three were done, so that CI's repair would not land on the branch mid-edit.

**What to keep.** Keep relaying before filing; it is where the corrections came from. When parallel workers may be cut off, commit in progress under a plain "work in progress" message, and open the pull request only once the workers have finished.
