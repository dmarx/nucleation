# THEORY-tmpsyokc: Ocampo-Espindola and colleagues show, in a kinetic model and in nickel electrodissolution experiments, that one common open-loop periodic input designed from each unit's natural frequency and phase response curve entrains uncoupled oscillators with different responses into a prescribed phase pattern, including one-, two- and three-cluster states of four units, where a design from the mean response curve can lock them into the wrong pattern

## Source

Ocampo-Espindola, Singhal, Li and Kiss (2024), LIT-2230, read in
NOTE-tmp5rss0.

## What was actually shown

In the averaged phase model each unit's phase relative to the input is
fixed where Δωⱼ + Λᵤ,ⱼ(ϕⱼ) = 0 with negative slope; choosing the input to
meet these conditions at chosen phases with least power is a convex
quadratic program. Simulations of a two-variable electrodissolution model
and experiments with nickel electrodes realized in-, anti- and
out-of-phase pairs and cluster states of four. The comparison with a
mean-response design could have gone the other way and did not: with
strongly different responses the mean design produced the wrong pattern.

## What this does not say

That the pattern persists without the input, or that any coupling among
the units is involved. Nothing in it tells a driven pattern from a coupled
one in data where the input is not known.
