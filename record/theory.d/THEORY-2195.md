# THEORY-2195: Nachtergaele proves, by energy-entropy balance on equilibrium states, that a continuous symmetry is not spontaneously broken at finite temperature in one or two dimensions for quantum spin systems whose couplings have a finite second moment, with no need for translation invariance

## Source

Nachtergaele (2006), "The Mermin-Wagner Theorem", course notes, UC
Davis, [LIT-3050](../literature.d/LIT-3050.md), read in [NOTE-2289](../notes.d/NOTE-2289.md).

## The account

Under sup_x Σ_y |x − y|²|J(x, y)| < ∞, no continuous symmetry is broken at
β < ∞ in d ≤ 2 (Proposition 2.2); "Translation invariance is not
required; in fact the argument works for inhomogeneous systems with spins
of different magnitudes at different sites" (p. 5). The rotation is
interpolated to zero at infinity "in such a way that there is a uniform
bound on the energy involved" (p. 6).

## What it does not license

Anything out of equilibrium, or for a symmetry not of the Hamiltonian.
