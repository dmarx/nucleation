# THEORY-1338: In the Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction and Frustration Scale as Chen et al. built it, the distinction between satisfaction and frustration coincides with item direction: every satisfaction item states a positive experience and every frustration item an aversive one, so the better fit of six factors over three, which is the scale's evidence that frustration is a separate dimension, is also what a keying or valence factor would produce

## Source

Chen et al. (2015), [LIT-2000](../literature.d/LIT-2000.md), pp. 223–227 and Table 3; read in
[NOTE-1527](../notes.d/NOTE-1527.md).

## What was actually shown

**The item pool.** Of 42 drafted items, "half of the items were concerned
with satisfaction of each need and the other half with frustration" (p.
224). The 24 retained (Table 3) are four per subscale. The twelve
satisfaction items each assert a positive feeling ("I feel a sense of choice
and freedom in the things I undertake"; "I feel capable at what I do"; "I
experience a warm feeling with the people I spend time with"). The twelve
frustration items each assert an aversive one ("I feel forced to do many
things I wouldn't choose to do"; "I feel insecure about my abilities"; "I
feel excluded from the group I want to belong to"). No satisfaction item is
reverse-worded, and no frustration item is.

**The evidence for two dimensions per need.** Within-need EFAs gave two
factors, satisfaction items on one and frustration items on the other
(p. 226). In CFA the six-factor model fit better than the three-factor one:
CFI .93 against .87, ΔSBSχ²(12) = 450.79, p < .01. The final six-factor
model was metric-invariant across Belgium, China, the USA and Peru
(ΔSBSχ²(54) = 69.57, p > .05).

**Why this is not decisive.** In a scale where content and wording
direction coincide, a method factor for positively against negatively
valenced statements separates the same items into the same two groups. The
fit comparison could have favoured three factors, and it did not; but the
alternative it rules out is "one bipolar factor per need with no method
variance", not "one factor per need plus a keying factor". The paper fits
no model with method factors and no balanced-keyed version.

The check here is a reading of the items and the reported model comparison,
not a reanalysis.

## What this does not say

- **That need frustration is not a distinct dimension.** It may be; the
  instrument's own evidence is compatible with both. Murphy et al. 2023
  ([LIT-1978](../literature.d/LIT-1978.md)) argue the artefact case and are unread here.
- **That the frustration–ill-being correlations are spurious.** Keying is
  one alternative; shared content with depressive items, raised in
  [THEORY-052](THEORY-052.md), is another, and the reconstructed covariance of −.84 between
  the latent satisfaction and frustration composites (Fig. 1) is a third
  reason for caution. None is tested in the paper.
- **Anything about other frustration measures**, such as Bartholomew et
  al.'s thwarting scale ([LIT-1954](../literature.d/LIT-1954.md)).
