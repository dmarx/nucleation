# LIT-154: Criteria of Identity and Structuralist Ontology

Hannes Leitgeb, James Ladyman (2007), *Philosophia Mathematica 16(3), 388–396 (2008)* — DOI-10.1093/philmat/nkm039

## Key takeaways

- Graph theory contains structures whose nodes are not even weakly discernible, such as the two-node edgeless graph. Mathematical practice treats them as distinct objects anyway, so the identity or difference of places in a structure is fixed by the structure itself, with identity counted among its structural relations, and not by any further criterion of identity.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The debate over whether structuralist ontologies of mathematics can keep the Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles usually assumes that individuals fall under criteria of identity that account for their identity. Most of that debate concerns structures with non-trivial automorphisms. The authors present graph-theoretic cases that violate even weak versions of PII. They argue that the identity or difference of places in a structure is accounted for by nothing beyond the structure, and that mathematical practice supports this.
