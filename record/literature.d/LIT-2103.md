summary: >-
  Siemroth (SSRN 2021; Essex discussion paper 2022; IER 2024). A two-year
  model in which an agent privately observes each year's spending need and
  values spending above it at α < 1, which the principal does not value at
  all. The principal commits in advance to a roll-over share Δ of unused
  funds, to audit rules and to a punishment. Without them the agent spends
  everything. Some roll-over is weakly better than none, and works only if
  Δ > α; partial roll-over can be optimal when α and the budget are small
  and the cost of funds large, but for most parameters full roll-over is
  optimal. Audits are threshold rules (audit only large spending), except
  an interval rule in year 1 when roll-over already induces saving;
  punishment is maximal and never imposed in equilibrium; and with an
  endogenous budget, strategic underfunding can be optimal.

# LIT-2103: Ending Wasteful Year-End Spending: On Optimal Budget Rules in Organizations

Siemroth, C. (2024). Ending Wasteful Year-End Spending: On Optimal Budget Rules in Organizations. *International Economic Review* 65(3): 1163–1188. doi:10.1111/iere.12684. (SSRN preprint 2021, doi:10.2139/ssrn.3991922; Essex Economics Discussion Paper, 7 February 2022.)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of the February 2022 working paper, [NOTE-1594](../notes.d/NOTE-1594.md). Page numbers are the working paper's.*

- **The headline.** "The optimal rules are to allow the agent to roll-over
  a share of the unused funds, but not necessarily the full share, in most
  cases to audit only sufficiently large spending, and to exert maximum
  punishment if fund misuse is detected." (abstract, p. 1).
- **Full roll-over is the usual optimum.** "It can be optimal to allow
  only a partial roll-over of funds", but "for most parameter values
  allowing a full fund roll-over is optimal" (p. 2).
- **When roll-over works.** The share "must exceed α for the agent to want
  to roll-over any funds at all and stop misusing funds in year 1" (p. 18).
- **Roll-over and audits interact.** "Hence, the roll-over rule
  non-trivially interacts with the optimal audit rule." (p. 6).
- **Not precaution.** "This is not a precautionary savings motive, as the
  agent is risk neutral" (p. 19), unlike Liebman and Mahoney's model.

## Standing in the record

With Liebman and Mahoney, [LIT-2028](LIT-2028.md), the record's first works on budget
rules. Where they explain year-end waste by precautionary saving with no
misuse, Siemroth explains it by misuse under private information, and
shows roll-over to be a cheap substitute for auditing. The pasted
bibliography on emergency readiness describes the model accurately; its
remark that the model does not make unlimited roll-over always optimal is
true but hides the headline, that some roll-over is always weakly better
and full roll-over usually optimal. Read from the working paper, not the
version of record. Filed from it: [THEORY-1410](../theory.d/THEORY-1410.md). No instruction for
machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
