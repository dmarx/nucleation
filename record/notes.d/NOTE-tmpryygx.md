# NOTE-tmpryygx: Hull and Tachi — Which quadrilateral origami tessellations can self-fold

## Contribution

A self-folding sheet has actuators at its creases, and the question is
whether some setting of them folds the sheet into the intended state and no
other. In 2016 the authors gave a mathematical definition and conditions;
this paper adds a sharper criterion for one-degree-of-freedom paths and
tests the model on a whole class of crease patterns, the monohedral
quadrilateral tessellations.

## Key insight

Whether a target can be selected by crease-local actuation depends on how
many other folding modes leave the flat state near it. A pattern with too
much symmetry has many modes, and their tangents span the intended one, so
no driving force pushes only along it. Fewer modes make selection
possible.

## Assumptions

- **Rigid origami**, configuration space S ⊂ Rⁿ of folding-angle vectors.
- **Separable actuation** (§2, footnote 1): the driving force is additively
  separable by crease, "each actuator at the crease knows its own folding
  angle, but not the others".
- **Kinematic only** (§5): the model "does not take material stiffness or
  bending energy into account".

## Key results

- **Driving force** (§2): "A vector field f on S is called a driving force
  for the rigid folding ρ(t) if ρ′ (t) · f (ρ(t)) > 0 for all t ∈ [0, s]."
- **A necessary dimension count** (Theorem 2, from Tachi and Hull 2016): a
  driving force for unique self-folding exists only if the dimension m of
  the tangent space at flat exceeds the dimension n of the span of the
  valid tangents surrounding ±ρ′(0), "(I.e., we need m > n.)"
- **Grid patterns** (Theorem 3): for quadrilateral faces whose vertices
  form an a × b grid, the tangent space at flat has dimension a + b.
- **One degree of freedom** (Theorem 4, new): a driving force making ρ(t)
  uniquely self-foldable from flat exists if and only if ρ′(0) is not in
  the linear span of the valid tangents surrounding ±ρ′(0).
- **Tessellations** (Theorem 5; abstract): "the Miura-ori and Chicken Wire
  patterns are not self-foldable under our definition, but such tilings
  that are rotationally-symmetric about the midpoints of the tile are
  uniquely self-foldable". The conclusion says "in most cases".
- **Face closure** (§4, alternate proof of part (c)): for a tile to fold
  rigidly, "the product of these folding multiplier ratios around the
  polygon is 1", a known observation the authors credit to others.
- **The moral** (§5): "fewer folding modes imply easier
  self-foldability".

## Concepts

- **Configuration space S**: the folding-angle vectors of rigidly folded
  states.
- **Driving force; forward force** d(t) = ρ′(t)·f(ρ(t)), which must be
  positive along the path.
- **Uniquely self-foldable**: ρ is the only rigid folding that f drives.
- **Folding mode**: a curve of S through the flat state, a mode and its
  mountain–valley reversal identified.
- **Rotationally-symmetric tiling**: one generated by rotating the tile by
  180° about the midpoint of each side.

## Limitations

- **An abstract kinematic model**, by the authors' own account: no
  stiffness, no bending energy, no dynamics.
- **Monohedral quadrilateral tilings only.**
- **Existence, not construction**: the results say a driving force exists,
  not how robust it is to error in the actuators.
