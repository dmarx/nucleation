# THEORY-612: Natural selection cannot be both a cause and an effect of a process such as niche construction: if selection is an emergent population-level process, niche construction partly composes it, and a whole and its parts are not independently fixable and so not causally related; if selection is not emergent, it is either no cause at all or incoherently individual-level. Niche construction can still cause the product of selection

## Source

Caleb Hazelwood (2023), "An Emerging Dilemma for Reciprocal Causation",
[LIT-156](../literature.d/LIT-156.md), *Philosophy of Science* 91(3): 595–614, read in full in [NOTE-137](../notes.d/NOTE-137.md).
Page numbers are the journal's.

## What was actually shown

**The target.** RC: "the process of natural selection is both a cause and
effect of processes such as niche construction". Its textual basis is Laland
et al. 2015, "process A is a cause of process B and, subsequently, process B
is a cause of process A". Weak reciprocal causation, between organism and
environment as entities, is called "unobjectionable" (p. 597).

**Horn 1: selection is emergent (§4.1).** Emergence is Wilson's synchronic
dependence plus ontological and causal autonomy, shown by irreducibility
(individuals "do not have the capacity to evolve"), novel laws (Hardy–Weinberg,
Fisher's theorem) and multiple realizability. Realization is argued to entail
composition. Then niche construction (individual level) partly composes
selection (population level), and their counterfactual dependence is
compositional, like mean kinetic energy and temperature. RC is "conceptually
incompatible with the emergent character of natural selection" (p. 603).
Population-level niche construction does not help: variation in fitness is
still "synchronously realized, not caused" (§4.2, p. 605).

**Interventionism does not rescue RC (§4.3).** Interlevel causation is
"completely defensible under the right circumstances" (p. 606), but only between
independently fixable variables. Intervening on "is a niche constructor"
"simultaneously intervene[s] on (or perhaps introduce[s]) the emergent
variation in fitness", so the two processes are "not sufficiently distinct to
stand in a causal relationship" (pp. 607–8). The same intervention can cause a
later difference in reproductive success, so niche construction is "a bona
fide cause of evolution — the product of natural selection" (p. 608).

**Horn 2: selection is not emergent (§5).** Statisticalism denies that selection
is a cause, so RC fails a fortiori. Individual-level causalism is incoherent,
because relative fitness is a property of at least a pair (Millstein 2006;
Sober 2013).

**Conclusion (§6).** "No available metaphysical account of natural selection is
compatible with the view that selection is both a cause and effect of
developmental processes such as niche construction" (p. 610).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Niche construction and selection could
have turned out independently fixable, so that one could be set without
setting the other. The argument is that composition rules this out.

## What this does not say

- **That niche construction does not matter to evolution.** Half the
  conclusion is that it causes evolutionary outcomes, by partly composing
  selection.
- **That organisms and environments do not cause each other.** That is the weak
  sense, granted.
- **That the dilemma is airtight.** It rests on the process/product distinction
  (Millstein, Stephens, Brandon), defended by citation. An advocate who
  identifies selection with differential reproduction would not accept that
  §4.3 closes the gap. The paper says a full defence of the compositional
  reading of realization is beyond its scope (p. 601).
- **That the second horn is exhaustive.** It considers statisticalism and
  individual-level causalism. Dispositional accounts are cited but not
  discussed.
- **That a diachronic loop is excluded.** Niche construction at one time may
  change selection at a later one, which changes its frequency later still.
  The reply that the diachronic arrow reaches only the product is asserted,
  and an unrolled model could press it. Baedke and colleagues' unrolled
  organism–environment model ([THEORY-446](THEORY-446.md)) is of that kind, but it does
  not make selection a relatum.
- **That it targets Baedke et al.** [LIT-110](../literature.d/LIT-110.md) is cited twice in passing, and its
  organism–environment model falls under the weak sense ([NOTE-137](../notes.d/NOTE-137.md)).
