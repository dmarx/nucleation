# LIT-1377: A Concise Genealogy and Anatomy of Habitus

Wacquant, L. (2016). A concise genealogy and anatomy of habitus. *The Sociological Review*, 64(1), 64–72. doi:10.1111/1467-954x.12356.

Read in full on 2026-10-22 ([NOTE-1074](../notes.d/NOTE-1074.md)). The summary holds: the four
"misunderstandings" it clears are that habitus replicates one structure,
is coherent, explains only reproduction, and generates action by itself
(pp. 68–69).

## Why this is here

The replies treated habitus, as Wacquant clarifies it, as the existing account closest to a perspective carried by a person, and A40 quoted his point that dispositions can be multilayered and contradictory. Bears on [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) and [CASE-130](../cases.d/CASE-130.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** A40's point is in the text: habitus is
"multilayered" and can be "divided against themselves" (pp. 68–69). Two
cautions, set out in [NOTE-1074](../notes.d/NOTE-1074.md). [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) cites this essay together
with Swidler ([LIT-1367](LIT-1367.md)) for a perspective's dispositional repertoire,
but Swidler denies that culture works through dispositions implanted by
socialization, which is what habitus is. And [CASE-130](../cases.d/CASE-130.md)'s "different
affordances" is supported only in general: the essay names the boxing gym
as a site of secondary habitus and does not use the word or discuss
martial arts. It also bears on [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md)'s defeater, since habitus as
stated here already separates layered dispositions from the positions that
trigger them.
