# QUESTION-167: Do word learners reason about an informative speaker, or only apply the size principle, when displays are built so that the two predict different bets?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-472](../theory.d/THEORY-472.md) derives the size principle from a speaker who minimises
information loss about a point referent, with uniform literal meanings.
In one betting experiment, learners who knew the referent bet as the
inversion of that speaker predicts. Under exactly those assumptions,
though, a learner who only applies the size principle predicts the same
bets. Experiment 1 therefore cannot tell the two apart, and no competing
model was fitted.

## What would answer it

A word-learning experiment whose displays make the two learners predict
different bets. Such displays could use vague intended meanings,
non-uniform literal meanings, or a speaker the learner knows to be
uninformative. Both models and a frequency baseline would be fitted on
part of the data and tested on the rest.

The informative-speaker learner winning on held-out displays would
answer it for the informative speaker. A plain size-principle learner
doing as well where they differ would answer it against. Further
correlations with a parameter-free model, in displays where the two
coincide, cannot settle it.
