# NOTE-tmpqaps1: Sadreev — Interference traps waves in an open system

## Contribution

A review of BICs in microwave and acoustic cavities open to waveguides,
written in one formalism, the Feshbach effective Hamiltonian
H_eff = H_B − iWW†.

## Key insight

A state inside the continuum leaks only through its coupling W. Make that
coupling vanish, by symmetry or by interference, and the state stays
confined at any frequency.

## Assumptions

Linear waves; cavities coupled to directional waveguides;
energy-independent coupling; two-level truncation near a degeneracy for
Friedrich–Wintgen.

## Key results

- **Definition** (§1): a BIC "lies inside the continuum and coexists with
  extended waves, but it remains perfectly confined without any
  radiation", against the view that such a state "can only be a
  'resonance' that leaks".
- **Four mechanisms** (abstract): "orthogonality of the eigenmodes to the
  propagating modes"; "full destructive interference of outgoing resonant
  modes"; "each resonator can serve as an ideal mirror"; and accidental.
- **Friedrich–Wintgen** (§3, Eq. 5): one state "decouples from the
  continuum" while the other "acquires maximal coupling with the
  continuum", so the first is "decoupled from the continuum owing to exact
  destructive interference of leaking eigenmodes".
- **Criterion** (§3): decoupling from all channels "is a necessary and
  sufficient condition for a resonance state to be the BIC".
- **Signature** (§3): "maximal transmittance coalesces with the maximal
  reflectance (collapse of Fano resonance)"; "the BIC is a singular point".
- **Idealization** (§1, §10): "the extremely large quality factor of the
  BICs, or better to say, quasi-BICs"; a "rigorous theorem forbids BICs in
  finite dielectric structure".

## Concepts

Continuum; resonance width; effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian; coupling
W; symmetry protection; Friedrich–Wintgen interference; superradiant
partner; Fano collapse; quasi-BIC.

## Bearing on the record

By the reader's reading of Eq. 4, S = 1 − 2iW†(H_eff − E)⁻¹W, not by any
sentence of the review: W enters the scattering matrix on both sides, so
an eigenstate with zero coupling leaves no trace in S.

- **TERM-090**: a physical case of "effective, not fundamental". The
  boundary relation (S) does not record an interior degree of freedom, so
  two interiors can share one boundary relation.
- **TERM-118**: the BIC is an inaccessible state, not a masked
  disposition. The same zero coupling that stops escape stops excitation
  from outside, whereas in masking the trigger occurs and only the
  manifestation is blocked. This is the reader's inference. The quasi-BIC,
  weakly excitable and weakly leaking, is the nearer analogue.
