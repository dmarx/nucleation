# THEORY-1990: Shue argues that rights to subsistence, security and liberty are basic rights, so that subsistence rights share the highest priority with security and liberty, that defending them chiefly means answering the objection that their correlative duties ask too much of others, and that those duties are of avoidance, protection and aid

Shue, *Basic Rights* (1980; 2020), [LIT-2750](../literature.d/LIT-2750.md).

## The account

"Part I attempts to show that rights to three particular
substances—subsistence, security, and liberty—are basic rights" (p. 9);
"once some presumption has been established in favor of a right, the main
task is to answer the objection that the duties involved would ask too
much of others" (p. 9). The index gives the correlative duties as "of
avoidance, protection and aid" (p. 257).

## What it does not show

- The argument for the triad, or that every basic right needs all three
  duties: chapter 2 is unread.
- That the triad is the treaty bodies' "respect, protect and fulfil".
