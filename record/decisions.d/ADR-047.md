# ADR-047: Promotion is audited in small persisted batches, ranked by expected yield, with decisions recorded apart from status changes

## Context

The promotion audit of 2026-10-19 (curation entries of that date, PR [#165](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/165))
found a mismatch in a few entries it happened to look at. Their proved
cores were Proposed, because their promotion conditions bundled a proof
with a broader claim. The audit was not a systematic search. The backlog
it drew on is large: the pending-decisions report counts 843 undecided
documents. An earlier session had also lost progress to a timeout, so work
kept only in a conversation is at risk.

The owner proposed an incremental procedure the same day, and asked for it
to start in a new pull request. The owner then asked for "some kind of
persistent file on the PR to represent, track, and version the queue",
so that progress can be handed off if a session is interrupted.

## Decision

**Three levels of effort.** Triage ranks 20 to 30 candidates from metadata
only. An evidence audit reads at most five candidates in full, with the
records they rest on. A promotion pull request changes at most three
entries.

**The rubric orders work and decides nothing.** Each candidate is scored
out of 20: evidence already in the record (5), how clear the promotable
scope is (4), downstream reliance (4), how cheap verification is (4), and
how low the risk from known objections is (3). A high score earns an early
review, not a promotion.

**Waves.** Demonstrated CLAIMs still Proposed come first. Then come THEORYs
with verified mathematical cores, then source-backed THEORYs, then
dependency unlocks (entries resting on something just promoted), and last
broad interpretive claims. The order adapts: each batch records its yield,
and a wave whose yield falls drops in priority.

**Six dispositions.** `promote`, `narrow-and-promote`, `split`,
`retain-proposed`, `needs-correction` and `defer`. Every disposition other
than promotion names a specific blocker. "Needs more evidence" is not a
blocker; naming the missing result is.

**Persistence.**

- `record/promotion-audit/queue.yaml` is the queue. It is versioned: each
  change bumps `version`, appends to `history`, and records the record
  revision it was taken against. Its `handoff` block names the batch in
  progress, the candidate under review, and the next step, so that a fresh
  session can resume from the file alone.
- `decisions/<CODE>.yaml` is one file per reviewed candidate, with its
  `reviewed_revision`. A review that is older than the entry's last change
  is stale.
- `batches/batch-NNN.yaml` is one file per batch: what it reviewed and its
  yield.
- `check.py` fails if the queue and the decision files disagree: a
  candidate marked decided with no decision file, or a decision file whose
  disposition the queue does not show.

**Transactions.** A batch selects and persists its candidates, reviews no
more than five, writes each decision as soon as it is reached, updates the
queue, runs the check, commits, and stops. If an interruption comes, the
queue's hand-off block and the decisions already committed are the
checkpoint. A candidate gets at most two rounds of supporting retrieval per
pass before it is deferred with its blocker.

**Review is not promotion.** A decision file changes no status. Promotions
follow in their own pull requests, each citing the decision it carries
out.

## Alternatives considered

- **One large audit in a conversation.** It loses everything when the
  session is lost, which is the failure this decision exists to prevent.
- **A queue derived from the decision files alone.** This is [DP-2](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-2)'s
  preferred remedy, and it was the first design. It cannot record what is
  in progress or what to do next, which is what a hand-off needs, and the
  owner asked for the queue as a file. The decision files stay the record
  of outcomes, and `check.py` guards the property that the two agree. That
  is [DP-2](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-2)'s second remedy.
- **The curation journal.** Its entries are dated and never rewritten,
  which suits a narrative and not a queue whose states change. Each batch
  stays one file, as a journal entry would be.
- **Reviewing and promoting in one step.** It mixes the evidence decision
  with the edit. A batch can succeed with no status changed.

## What a re-derivation counts for

Added 2026-10-19, at the owner's decision on batch 007's question. Some
promotion conditions ask for a proof the record does not hold: an
independent proof, a reading of the original, or a second source. A
checked, step-by-step re-derivation recorded in the decision file meets
such a condition, and the THEORY is promoted, with the derivation cited in
its history note. That is what [THEORY-004](../theory.d/THEORY-004.md)'s promotion did. The rejected
alternatives were to file the derivation only as a demonstrated CLAIM and
leave the THEORY Proposed, and to hold the entry until the source is read.
The re-derivation must be in the decision file, step by step. A summary
that says it was checked is not enough.

## What one study counts for

Added 2026-10-19, at the owner's decision on batch 011's question
([THEORY-191](../theory.d/THEORY-191.md), [THEORY-192](../theory.d/THEORY-192.md)). An empirical THEORY can be promoted on one
study, with no replication, when all three of these hold:

- its title claims no more than that study measured, with the study's
  own hedges kept (for example, "has the signature of X, not of Y", not
  "is X");
- the NOTE on that study read it in full;
- nothing in the record contradicts it.

If its promote_when asks for a replication or a second study, that
request moves unchanged to a new QUESTION, as other out-of-scope
promote_when clauses do. A title that generalises past the study, to
other systems, scales or trained models, is narrowed to what was
measured first, or else stays Proposed. The rejected alternatives were
to require the replication that the promote_when asks for, and to put
each case to the owner.

## Where a narrowing's dropped claims go

Added 2026-10-20, at the owner's decision. Narrowing decides what an
Active entry may say. It does not decide that the rest is worthless, and
until now nothing said where the rest goes. The default had become to
drop it. Every clause a `narrow-and-promote` removes from a title, or
moves to "What this does not say", is now routed to one of four places,
and the decision file says which, clause by clause:

1. **A generalization the source or the literature asserts** becomes a new
   Proposed THEORY. It keeps the old wide wording and the same source, and
   it `extends` the narrowed Active entry, which is its evidence. It
   inherits the old promote_when. Example: a general law of neural
   collapse, beside the Active entry for the runs that measured it.
2. **The record's own extrapolation or gloss** becomes a Proposed CLAIM
   with an interpretive warrant ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md), [ADR-043](ADR-043.md)), whose `grounds` name
   the narrowed entry.
3. **A mis-statement or a clause shown false** gets a dated note only. A
   wide form that was imprecise, or that the source's own results
   contradict, is not a proposal worth keeping.
4. **Open scope that nobody asserts** becomes a QUESTION, as before.

When a clause could be read as either kind 1 or kind 3, the decision file
says why it chose. A narrowing PR files its kind-1 and kind-2 entries in
the same PR, within the limit of three status changes. A new Proposed
entry is not a status change.

**The retroactive sweep.** Every earlier `narrow-and-promote`, and as a
lower-priority check every `promote`, is sorted by this rule in triage-008
(`record/promotion-audit/triage-008.yaml`). The kind-1 and kind-2 entries
are then filed in batches of five. The Active entries are left as they
are: the sweep adds Proposed entries beside them and re-reviews nothing.

The rejected alternatives were to restore the wide titles and demote the
narrowed entries, which would undo correct reviews; to leave the dropped
claims in the narrowed entry's body, where nothing can confirm or reject
them as theses; and to file every dropped clause as a QUESTION, which
asks without proposing.

## Consequences

- Any session can resume an interrupted audit from `queue.yaml`, without
  the conversation that started it.
- The queue is hand-maintained, so it can drift. `check.py` is the guard.
  Per [DP-4](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-4) it was fired once against a deliberately broken queue before
  being relied on (record/promotion-audit/README.md says how).
- Decision files cite codes. Codes in YAML are not linked or upgraded by
  `luria repair`, so the ledger uses concretized codes, and a temporary
  code is recorded together with the entry's file name.
- Promotion pull requests take longer to arrive, since review and edit are
  separate. In exchange, each one is small enough to check.
