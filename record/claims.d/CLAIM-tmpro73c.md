# CLAIM-tmpro73c: Where an implicit relationship is ambiguous, who resolves the ambiguity, when, and at whose cost can matter more than how ambiguous it is: a party that may treat the same accommodation as discretionary when asked to compensate it and as owed when it is withheld takes advantage through the ambiguity without changing any term

## The claim

A30 §10, "Ambiguity itself can become a relational resource": ambiguity
"can preserve flexibility ... reduce the cost of premature specification
... permit cooperation among parties whose interpretations are not yet
fully reconciled. But it can also permit asymmetric invocation."

> For example, an organization might treat an employee's recurring
> accommodation as discretionary when asked to compensate it, but as
> obligatory when the employee declines to provide it.
>
> The relationship is invoked under different interpretations depending
> on which interpretation is advantageous to the controlling party.

It sorts ambiguity by function: productive under-specification, shared
uncertainty, latent disagreement, asymmetric interpretive discretion
("Lets one party determine which interpretation governs") and strategic
ambiguity ("Maintains indeterminacy because it provides leverage"). It
links this to [CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md) and to reflexive control ([CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md)): "a party can
influence another's conduct by governing which relationship is treated as
applicable rather than issuing an overt command." A32 §3 gives the framing
form: "The request *help me* acquires different practical significance
when framed as an emergency, professional obligation, reciprocal
commitment or discretionary favor."

## Where the record stands

- [CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md): the holder of residual control decides uncovered
  contingencies. This claim is different in kind: no contingency is
  uncovered; which arrangement covers the act is left open, and one party
  decides it case by case and inconsistently.
- [CLAIM-731](CLAIM-731.md): the cost of imposing and of contesting a decision come apart.
  Here the cost of contesting an interpretation, not a decision, is the
  lever.
- [CLAIM-713](CLAIM-713.md) and [CLAIM-703](CLAIM-703.md): a commitment can be exploited by whoever
  controls the circumstances that trigger it; the "obligatory when
  declined" half of the example draws on the employee's commitment.
- [QUESTION-468](../questions.d/QUESTION-468.md) asks what separates exploitation from dependence and
  domination; this is a candidate mechanism, and [TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md) still requires
  a declared standard before "exploitation" is said.

## What it does not say

That ambiguity is wrongful, or that clarifying is always in the weaker
party's interest: A30 lists productive under-specification first. An
asymmetry in who resolves the ambiguity does not by itself establish
exploitation (A30 §9: "That verdict requires the appropriate normative
standard and standing").

## Standing

Proposed, in A's voice.

A22 §13, held as CLAIM-tmp4zii5, already gave a party "disproportionate
influence over how an implicit contract is interpreted"; its dangerous
form makes the arrangement the standard for judging complaints. This
claim's mechanism is different: no standard is captured, and one party
settles which arrangement covers the act case by case and
inconsistently. A24's exploitation of accommodation (CLAIM-tmp8bimm) is a
third route, through the regulatory surplus. The three complement one
another. The owner's sidebar on productive misunderstanding draws on this
claim as the risk of leaving an ambiguity unresolved (CLAIM-tmpgx2ej). The
wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the
owner's call.
