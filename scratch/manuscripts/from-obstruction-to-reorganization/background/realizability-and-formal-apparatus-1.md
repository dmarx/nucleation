## 1. Core concepts

**Realizability and resources**

- **TERM-114**, resource-bounded constraint system (Active): "architectures,
  the configurations and costed processes each admits, costed changes of
  architecture and the observations available, with reachability indexed by
  a partially ordered budget". It is the frame the reaching claims are
  stated in (SYNTHESIS-042).
- **TERM-105**, resource constraint, by type (Active): a fixed stock, an
  instantaneous capacity, a rate limit, a cumulative expenditure, a
  replenishable reserve and a deadline, "which bound different functions of
  a path and are not one budget"; "These are not equivalent problems"
  (SYNTHESIS-042).
- **TERM-106**, reconfiguration slack (Active): "the temporary resources a
  system needs to move between two configurations it can each hold, beyond
  what holding either one requires". In CLAIM-571's example it is 0.6.
- **TERM-116**, relational profiles of a configuration (Proposed): incoming
  (cost of producing it from each other configuration), outgoing (cost of
  turning it into each), observational (what affordable tests can tell it
  from, with a minimum distinguishing budget) and maintenance ("what
  preserves or regenerates its enabling constraints, which Yoneda does not
  supply"). Its minimum distinguishing budget "is the observing counterpart
  of the transition threshold" (SYNTHESIS-040).
- **TERM-117**, productive nucleus (Active): "a partial organization whose
  establishment, relative to a declared target, context, horizon and
  comparator, causally improves the probability, timeliness, robustness or
  resource-feasible accessibility of forming the target by changing later
  interaction opportunities, constraints or transition rates; distinct from
  a structural seed and a kinetic nucleus". Its measure is interventional;
  "a high committor is not sufficient to prove causal productivity"
  (SYNTHESIS-045).
- **TERM-111**, universality, and which sense (Proposed): expressive,
  computational ("one family resource-simulates another with bounded
  overhead, an equivalence only when the simulation runs both ways") or
  scaling.
- **TERM-088**, individuation, and which sense (Active): descriptive,
  dynamical, constitutive, and organizational autonomy. The budgeted
  individuation claims say they concern the descriptive and reachable
  senses only (CLAIM-557, CLAIM-574).
- **TERM-070**, organizational closure (Active): a set of constraints each
  depending on another in the set and having another depend on it, after
  Montévil and Mossio, distinct from informational closure.

**Effective description, probes and coherence**

- **TERM-065**, effective-description regime (Active, adopted by the
  owner): ℛ = (X, 𝒬, ℐ, ℬ, ε, τ), the system, queries, interventions,
  resources, tolerance and horizon under which a description T is
  assessed, Adequate(T; ℛ). T is kept out of ℛ so "rival descriptions are
  compared under one regime"; its point is A5's: "Adequacy is relational,
  but whether the relation holds can be objective."
- **TERM-069**, observational equivalence under a probe family (Active):
  agreement on the outcome law of every probe in a declared admissible
  family, "coarser than identity unless the family is rich enough", and
  weaker on marginals than on the joint law across probes.
- **TERM-097**, coherence (Active, adopted 2026-10-29): agreement "under a
  stated correspondence structure (on overlaps, around cycles, between
  routes), distinct from fidelity to a source, from integration by
  dynamics, and from knowledge of the world"; a claim of coherence "should
  name the maps" (SYNTHESIS-074).
- **TERM-068**, closure, and which sense (Active): lists the record's senses
  so that a claim says which; informational closure (sense 1) is "Not a mark
  of a system", organizational closure (sense 6) concerns a set of
  constraints (SYNTHESIS-079).
- **TERM-062**, causal state (Active): "a class of pasts that induce the same
  conditional law over a declared target future, the coarsest prescient
  partition of the pasts"; "Not interventional" (SYNTHESIS-079).
- **TERM-058**, Granger causality, and **TERM-057**, transfer entropy (both
  Active): predictive quantities relative to a stated information set, lags
  and model class, "not by itself the effect of an intervention" and "not a
  measure of information flow" respectively (SYNTHESIS-073).

**Inquiry (the requested terms outside the accounts' membership)**

- **TERM-095**, inquiry (Active): a process that pursues a question, written
  (Σ, A, 𝓘, 𝓞, 𝓚, 𝓑): language, answer space ("which differences would
  count as an answer"), interventions, observations, criteria of
  evaluation, resources; "reflexive when any of these becomes what it is
  pursuing". It plays the role of TERM-065's ℛ "together with a fixed query
  family", and "TERM-065 takes ℛ as supplied".
- **TERM-092**, inquiry frontier (Active): for an agent with concepts K,
  budget B and horizon H, the questions some available procedure can
  formulate, "making it determinate enough to pursue", within B and H.
  Four sets differ: expressible, askable, investigable, answerable.
  Reusable specifications "move the frontier without enlarging the budget"
  and "can also make other questions harder".
- **TERM-091**, relational vocabulary (Active): the arities, domains (states
  or histories) and types of relation a class of descriptions can state,
  "and so which constraints are expressible in it at all". "Inexpressible
  is not false or unknown"; the response "is to enlarge the vocabulary, not
  to revise a value". A luria record's vocabulary is its relation types and
  fields (CLAIM-479).
- **TERM-064**, legibility (Active): whether an observer, "with the rules,
  categories and resources it actually has, can recover the distinction
  from what it is given and bring it to bear on the task", bounded above by
  what the source allows an ideal receiver, and directed. Its formal core is
  CLAIM-199: a garbling can raise a receiver's usable information "but never
  above what the source makes possible".

## 2. Central theses

Format: code, status, warrant, role; statement; what it rests on.

**The hinge and the general object**

- **CLAIM-566** (Active, interpretive, thesis): assembling, persisting over a
  specified horizon and being regenerated after turnover "are three
  properties with separate probabilities", and a structure that assembles
  and persists without producing the constraints that would regenerate it
  "does not show organizational closure". SYNTHESIS-043 calls it the line's
  hinge, linked from every part.
- **CLAIM-576** (Proposed, interpretive): what characterizes a constrained
  system is "a repertoire of realizable processes, indexed by resources and
  dependent on its architecture, rather than its set or space of states",
  changing in three ways (a budget makes a transformation affordable;
  observational or computational capability makes a possibility
  discoverable or controllable; a change of architecture alters what is
  possible). Rests on CLAIM-583. Its own note of 2026-11-06 says CLAIM-583
  does not show that the state space is not enough (SYNTHESIS-042).

  resource-bounded process structures under two-way simulation with bounded
  overhead; tested by QUESTION-385.

## 3. Formal results (demonstrated claims, with conditions)

### 3.1 Thresholds, composition, error

- **CLAIM-571** (Active). Q configurations, r(q) the holding requirement, Γ(A,
  B) admissible paths: B*(A, B) = inf over γ of max_t r(γ(t)), +∞ if Γ is
  empty. B* ≥ max(r(A), r(B)); it can exceed both: on Q = [0, 1] with
  r(q) = 0.7 + 2.4q(1 − q), B* = 1.3 while both ends cost 0.7, and at budget
  1.0 the feasible set is two components (CASE-233, now Active; ARG-077, now
  Active, concludes it). Physical instances: CASE-206 (Active), a degree-4
  origami vertex whose switch passes the flat state at about 1.866 against
  folded states of 0.75 and 0.875; CASE-204 is "The case where no budget
  helps and only the admissible-move set does". The architecture is part of
  the statement: CASE-198 lowers the threshold to 1.0 by admitting a change
  of architecture as a move. Its "What it does not say": the peak is not the
  only cost, and several resources give no scalar threshold (CLAIM-585).
- **CLAIM-563** (Active). Costs in an ordered monoid with monotone ⊗: the
  processes costing at most b form a subcategory if b ⊗ b ⪯ b, and not once
  two composable processes each cost exactly b with b ⊗ b ⋠ b; "so always
  for maximum and, for sum, only at 0 and infinity". The peak case is
  Lawvere's R_cart quantale, and its hom is CLAIM-571's B*: "The peak case is
  the record's threshold." Order-dependent (replenishable) costs need the
  reserve carried in the state.
- **CLAIM-580** (Active). If the estimated requirement is within ε
  everywhere, the threshold is within ε, "and its reconfiguration slack to
  within 2ε"; under- and overestimates move it monotonically.
- **CLAIM-585** (Active). With several capacities the sufficient budgets form
  an up-set with a Pareto frontier that "need not be convex, so no threshold
  on a weighted total of the resources describes it".
- **CLAIM-646** (Proposed). Peak thresholds do not fix cumulative costs:
  CASE-236 (Proposed), a chain and a complete graph on n + 1 configurations,
  agree on every threshold while least spend is n against 1.
- **CLAIM-577** (Active). A budget set each period to last period's peak spend
  "never rises", so any transition whose threshold exceeds the holding cost
  "is lost after one period spent holding it", unless raised from outside.
  Its summary: "CLAIM-079's stability without viability, produced by a
  budget rule".

### 3.2 Filtrations

- **CLAIM-586** (Active). The budget-indexed compatibility complex grows with
  b and never exceeds the unbudgeted complex, "So a missing face is either
  short of resources at b or structurally frustrated, and the unbudgeted
  complex tells which." CASE-212: raising the budget "leaves the frustration
  where it was".
- **CLAIM-564** (Active). Implications among demands, from the Galois
  connection restricted to affordable configurations, are antitone in b,
  "the implication counterpart of CLAIM-586's split".
- **CLAIM-560** (Active). Compatibility filtration K(b) and reachability
  filtration R(b) both grow, and "Neither fixes the other." **CLAIM-583**
  (Active): "Same complex, same move rule, same costs, different
  reachability." **CLAIM-607** (Active, conditional demonstration): the same
  "one level up, for constraint languages".
- **CLAIM-591** (Active). Under directed moves a state can share a component
  with a target and not reach it, and B*(A, B) ≠ B*(B, A) is possible, "so a
  persistence summary of sublevel sets bounds the reachability filtration
  without fixing it".
- **CLAIM-654** (Proposed). Reachability by a given process grows with the
  budget when its choices do not read the remaining budget; one that does
  "can reach a target at a small budget and miss it at a larger one".
  CASE-245 (Proposed): target edge when one step remains, trap edge
  otherwise; reaches at budget 1, never at 2 or more.
- **CLAIM-697** (Proposed). Best success over nested procedure classes is
  nondecreasing in budget; repeated sampling raises success from p to
  1 − (1 − p)^N with no parameter change; "Coverage under a sampling budget
  is reachability. Delivery at a threshold is reliable attainment."
  Generation, recognition and selection are separate capacities.

### 3.3 Complexity of navigating and diagnosing

- **CLAIM-578** (Active). Complexity belongs to a family of problems, not one
  space; "one organization's compatibility complex carries no complexity";
  finding and navigating can fall in different classes.
- **CLAIM-575** (Active), resting on it: "telling geometric disconnection from
  a capacity bottleneck can be PSPACE-hard, and knowing how far over budget
  the cheapest path is can be NP-hard."
- **CLAIM-565** (Active). With constants and single flips, positive 1-in-3
  SAT and positive NAE-3SAT have "the same clone, and navigation on opposite
  sides of the dichotomy", so the satisfiability algebra does not predict
  navigation.

### 3.4 Control and constraint

- **CLAIM-345** (Active): least quadratic effort is Δxᵀ W_T^† Δx; "Equal
  controllability rank does not fix the cost of control." **CLAIM-542**
  (Active): sensitivity fixes neither allocation nor authorship of control,
  "so only feedback that observes the state turns cheaper control into
  reliable direction". **CLAIM-589** (Active), resting on both: permitted,
  actuated and affordably achievable motions are three objects; over a
  horizon reach can exceed what either spans at an instant.
- **CLAIM-609** (Active): the force exciting a linear zero mode is (T + λI)u;
  bond stiffness never redirects it.
- **CLAIM-618** (Proposed): each locking region lies inside the
  compatibility region of the same synchronization demands without needing
  to equal it, "CLAIM-567's feasible/reliably-reached split carried over a
  space of control parameters"; ARG-061 (Proposed) finds an unstated
  premise, Q(λ) being the locked configurations.

### 3.5 Budgeted individuation

- **CLAIM-574** (Active). For nested probe subcategories P₁ ⊆ P₂, a natural
  isomorphism of restricted Yoneda profiles on P₂ restricts to one on P₁, so
  "objects told apart at a lower budget stay apart at every higher one"; "an
  equivalence a richer regime breaks was relative to its regime, not false".
  Conditions: nested families and an every-probe comparison; an averaged
  distortion is not monotone (tolerance 0.6, distortions 1 and 0, mean 0.5).
  Not that families are nested physically: a measurement that disturbs what
  a later one sees breaks it. It reads itself as "TERM-065's regime with its
  probes ordered".
- **CLAIM-581** (Active). Under peak costs on reversible moves the threshold
  d is an extended pseudo-ultrametric, d(x, z) ≤ max(d(x, y), d(y, z)), so ≈_b
  is an equivalence at every b and its classes nest into one merge tree;
  under additive costs d is an extended pseudometric and ≈_b need not be
  transitive (0.6 + 0.6). "The resource semantics is an individuation
  choice." Identity is free and holding is not: d(x, x) = 0 says nothing of
  upkeep.
- **CLAIM-570** (Active). With O_o (indistinguishable at observing budget o)
  and A_a (mutually reachable at acting budget a), S = {(o, a): O_o ⊆ A_a} is
  an up-set and T = {(o, a): A_a ⊆ O_o} a down-set; boundary of S is
  a_S(o) = max{d(x, y): x O_o y}, of T is o < min{β(x, y): x A_a y, x ≠ y}.
  Worked on CASE-199. On CASE-206 with depth and side probe costs swapped,
  the non-trivial window "disappears", so agreement "is a fact about the
  mechanism and the cost of observing it together".
- **CLAIM-592** (Active). With probes ordered by cost, the observer's chain of
  common refinements and the merge tree agree exactly on partitions in both
  chains; a merge-tree level can be brought into agreement under some cost
  order exactly when it is the common refinement of some set of probes; two
  levels from incomparable probe sets cannot both agree under one order. It
  separates "what the mechanism can become", "what can be measured at all"
  and "what is cheap to measure first".
- **CLAIM-639** (Proposed). Knowing requirements within ε fixes thresholds
  within ε "but not which of a system's own reachable classes an observer's
  partitions can agree with": merges within 2ε can swap order. Rests on
  CLAIM-580, CLAIM-592, CLAIM-581; filed at the synthesis review as passing
  the derivation route (SYNTHESIS-076).
- **CLAIM-572** (Active). One shared budget for observing and acting: the
  cheapest readiness buys a whole prefix of the probes, affordable splits can
  be disconnected, and stopping when the next probe does not pay can miss
  the optimum; a reallocation is "a transition with its own threshold, as in

## 6. Digester's notes (mine, not the record's)

*Everything in this section is the digester's reading. Codes mark what each
note leans on; the record states none of these integrations, and several
resemble candidates the record itself declined as conjunctions or labels
(section 4).*

**(a) Bearing on a general theory of learning and inquiry for arbitrary
systems.**

1. *Differentiating is budget-indexed and directional.* CLAIM-574, CLAIM-557
   and CLAIM-570 give a precise template for "a system that differentiates":
   observing resources refine a partition monotonically (under nested
   probes and every-probe comparison), acting resources under peak costs
   coarsen another, and individuation is the pair. A learner's distinctions
   are then never absolute but relative to a regime (TERM-065), and an
   equivalence later broken was "relative to its regime, not false"
   (CLAIM-574). This gives a non-sceptical account of revision: refinement
   without retraction. CLAIM-387 and CLAIM-392 mark its limit: optimal
   compressions at two budgets need not nest, so a budget-optimal learner
   can reorganize its distinctions rather than only add to them.
2. *Retaining is distinct from persisting and from conserving.* CLAIM-270,
   CLAIM-635, CLAIM-107 and CLAIM-094 separate keeping information about a
   start from staying near it and from conserving an observable; CLAIM-079,
   CLAIM-612 and CLAIM-521 separate present stability from viability. A
   theory of retention under turnover would sit on CLAIM-566's three
   probabilities and CLAIM-516's productive cycle.
3. *Enforcing is where reach and reliability split.* CLAIM-567's three
   properties and CLAIM-582's guard say an added constraint (an enforced
   distinction) can raise reliability and never reach; CLAIM-761 says a
   stabilized constraint that supplies cheaper operations is a change of
   mechanism and can move tasks under a per-task budget. The pair looks like
   the core of a theory of what learning buys: either reliability within a
   fixed repertoire or affordability that enlarges the effective repertoire
   (CLAIM-576), with CLAIM-695 and CLAIM-699 as the machine-learning case.
4. *Revising distinctions has its own threshold and complexity.* CLAIM-572
   reads reallocation between observing and acting as a threshold "in budget
   space"; CLAIM-575 and CLAIM-578 make diagnosing which obstruction holds a
   problem with its own complexity; CLAIM-470 separates revising within an
   answer space from revising the space, and TERM-091 separates a false value
   from an inexpressible constraint. A general theory would treat revision
   as a transition with a peak cost, possibly infinite under a fixed
   architecture (CLAIM-571, CASE-198's architecture-change move).
5. *Inquiry is itself resource-bounded realization.* CLAIM-475 and TERM-092
   put the question-formulating step under the same budgets as acting;
   CLAIM-474 places inquiry as a distinct achievement not implied by
   individuation or autonomy. Read with CLAIM-761 and CLAIM-756, a
   stabilized framework is a productive nucleus (TERM-117) for later
   questions, and per CLAIM-599 two such nuclei can combine into a trap.
6. *Evidence discipline for any learner.* CLAIM-011, CLAIM-266, CLAIM-322
   and CLAIM-517 converge on held-out anchoring as the condition under which
   agreement certifies anything; CLAIM-407, CLAIM-414 and CLAIM-522 say
   passive agreement never certifies interventional or maintenance
   structure; TERM-097 and CLAIM-496 keep coherence apart from fidelity.
   The record refused to join these as a universal (SYNTHESIS-072,
   SYNTHESIS-079), so a manuscript should state them as "need not certify"
   with CLAIM-517's coverage exception.

**(b) Operationalizing in a record whose schema is revised by governed
decisions.**

1. *Schema as relational vocabulary, revision as architecture change.* By
   TERM-091 and CLAIM-477, a record's types, fields and relations are its
   vocabulary, and an amendment enlarges what is expressible. CLAIM-571's
   "The architecture is part of the statement" and CASE-198 suggest
   treating a schema decision as a costed change of architecture, whose
   threshold (review, migration, re-linting) can exceed both the old and the
   new schema's holding cost (TERM-106's slack).
2. *Entrenchment is predicted, and measurable.* CLAIM-475's "If revising a
   schema is vastly more expensive than adding another record under the
   existing schema, inquiry may remain trapped" and CLAIM-761's "the
   persistence that buys it can entrench" suggest tracking, per decision,
   the cost to add under the schema against the cost to revise it, and
   CLAIM-577's ratchet warns that budgeting revision capacity to past spend
   loses transitions after a quiet period.
3. *Frontier movement as the test of a schema decision.* CLAIM-475's
   defeater (b) and TERM-092 give a criterion: a governed schema change is
   productive if it moves the askable set (in size or shape) at fixed
   resources. CLAIM-756 says to value it also by what it does to the cost of
   later decisions, not its own savings.
4. *Observing and acting budgets of the record itself.* Linting, synthesis
   reviews and status reviews are probes (CLAIM-574); filing and decisions
   are moves (CLAIM-581). CLAIM-592 says the classes the record "sees" and the
   classes it can reorganize agree only on levels common to both chains, and
   which appear depends on which checks are cheap first: a design lever for
   ordering automated checks.
5. *Reliability versus reach for rules.* CLAIM-582 implies a closed-vocabulary
   rule or a required field cannot enlarge what the record can express; it
   can only raise the reliability of how entries are filed. A decision that
   claims to enlarge capability must supply cheaper operations (CLAIM-761),
   which matches the record's own rule that a decision ships with a
   demonstration of its effects.
6. *Coherence is not correctness for the record either.* CLAIM-265 and
   TERM-097 imply that lint-clean, mutually consistent entries certify
   coherence under the declared relations, not fidelity; CLAIM-293's
   "`rests_on` and `grounds` record that support exists, not how much" and
   CLAIM-294 say the stored dependency graph is not a sufficient state for
   revision when a support fails.
