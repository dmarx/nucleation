# LIT-tmprf69i: The Ethics of Authenticity

Charles Taylor, *The Ethics of Authenticity*, Cambridge, MA: Harvard
University Press, 1991, 142 pp. (ISBN 0-674-26863-6); reissued 2018
(ISBN 978-0-674-98769-2). The publisher's digital edition carries
DOI-10.4159/9780674237117, which Crossref dates 31 December 1992; the DOI
is carried as the citable identifier, and it names the Harvard edition, not
the Canadian one.

**The two editions.** The Harvard edition's title-page verso, as Open
Library transcribes it, reads: "Originally published in Canada in 1991
under the title The malaise of modernity". *The Malaise of Modernity*
(Concord, Ont.: House of Anansi, 1991, 135 pp.) is in the series "CBC
Massey lectures, 1991"; secondary sources describe it as an extended
version of the five lectures broadcast on CBC Radio's *Ideas* in November
1991. The brief's account of the editions is right. No source gives a day
or month for either printing, so `published:` carries 1991 as the first of
January. The two are one text under two titles; this entry stands for
both, and nothing should be filed for *The Malaise of Modernity* separately.

## Key takeaways

*Not a reading. No publisher's description could be fetched (see below),
so what follows is the book's argument as it is commonly summarized,
including in the search-engine summaries I saw. It is a placeholder for a
reading and should not be cited as Taylor's text.*

- **The claim.** Contemporary individualism and subjectivism, and the loss
  of shared horizons, produce what Taylor calls malaises of modernity; yet
  the ideal behind them, authenticity, being true to oneself, is a genuine
  moral ideal to be retrieved, not dismissed as narcissism or indulged as
  relativism.
- **What authenticity needs**, on the same summaries: horizons of
  significance against which some choices matter more than others, and the
  recognition of others, because identity is formed in dialogue.
- The book is also widely summarized as naming three malaises
  (self-fulfilment that narrows lives, the primacy of instrumental reason,
  and a loss of freedom in public life). That is from my prior knowledge of
  the book, not from any source fetched here; a reading should confirm or
  correct it.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the canonical philosophical
statement of authenticity as an ethical ideal. It is moral and social
philosophy with no instruction for machine-learning practice, so no
anthology topic holds it. The Anthology of the SOTA does not hold it: a
grep of its literature.d for "Taylor" and "authenticity" found nothing by
him.

**The book rule.** The owner welcomes replacing a long book with a shorter,
lawfully readable work by the same author that does the same job. The book
is already short (142 pages, five lectures expanded). I looked for a
shorter Taylor text stating the same argument: "The Politics of Recognition"
(1992), which restates the ideal of authenticity and the dialogical
formation of identity, is the obvious candidate, but no lawful open copy
turned up; his 1992 Tanner Lecture ("Modernity and the Rise of the Public
Sphere") is about the public sphere, not authenticity. So nothing was read
in its place, and the book is filed itself, because it is the citation
anyone writing on authenticity reaches for, and the record would lose the
philosophical side of the batch without it.

**Where it would bear, once read.** The batch's psychology measures
authenticity as a trait or state in individuals: Kernis and Goldman's four
components ([LIT-tmpc6vlg](LIT-tmpc6vlg.md)), Wood and colleagues' Authenticity Scale
([LIT-tmpr2weu](LIT-tmpr2weu.md)), and the true self-concept of Schlegel and colleagues
([LIT-tmpzyy82](LIT-tmpzyy82.md)). None of those constructs includes a horizon of
significance: a person scores as authentic by acting on what he takes to
be his own values, whatever they are. Sheldon and Elliot ([LIT-tmppbyfm](LIT-tmppbyfm.md))
meet the problem from the other side: they concede that a goal can be fully
self-concordant and evil, and that a complete model must address "the
what of striving" as well as the why. That is Taylor's objection to
self-determining freedom stated by psychologists about their own construct.
SDT's integration ([THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)) is the nearest psychological analogue,
since it says some values are "not integrateable", but it grounds that in
organismic needs, not in shared horizons. A reading should say whether
Taylor's horizons are a condition on authenticity itself or on its being
worth having.

**Priority for a reading: medium.** It would give the record a first-hand
philosophical account of authenticity to set against the psychologists',
but no THEORY in the record waits on it.
