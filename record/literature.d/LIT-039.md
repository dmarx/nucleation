# LIT-039: Rigorous renormalization group

Antti Kupiainen (2023), *Nature Physics 19, 1539–1541 (2023), Comment* — DOI-10.1038/s41567-023-02233-x

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmp2pndd.*

- **From axioms to path integrals.** Constructive QFT tries to define the
  Euclidean path integral ⟨φ(x₁)…φ(x_n)⟩ = ∫φ(x₁)…φ(x_n)e^{−S(φ)/ħ}Dφ
  non-perturbatively. Lattice and box cutoffs turn this into statistical
  mechanics of continuous spins.
- **Dimension decides.** Scaling sends the φ⁴ coupling to (ℓ^{4−d}λ). It
  is suppressed at short distances below four dimensions and at long
  distances above. d < 4 needs only mass renormalization. d = 4 needs
  infinitely many counterterms.
- **One RG step is easy; iterating it is the work.** The block-spin
  integral e^{−H_{Lℓ}(ϕ′)} = ∫e^{−H_ℓ(ϕ)}δ(ϕ′ − Cϕ)Dϕ has both cutoffs, so
  it is non-critical. Its perturbation series diverges like n!. A
  polymer expansion converges where the field is small. Large fields are
  improbable and are handled separately.
- **What it proved.** Massless 4D lattice φ⁴ has Gaussian decay
  ⟨ϕ(x)ϕ(y)⟩ ∼ Z|x − y|^{−2} (Gawędzki and Kupiainen 1985; Feldman et
  al. 1987). Continuum 4D φ⁴ is Gaussian for any bare parameters
  (Aizenman and Duminil-Copin 2021). The 2D Gross–Neveu model has been
  constructed. Hall-conductivity universality has been proved.
- **Where it stops.** "A more nonperturbative formalism is still
  missing." Numerical RG shares the problem. The author sees tensor-network
  RG, which keeps actions strictly local, as more promising.

renormalization-and-learning accounts THEORY-194, THEORY-197 and
THEORY-202. What rigorous RG controls is the iterated flow of the whole
