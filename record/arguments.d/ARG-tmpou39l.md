# ARG-tmpou39l: Seamster and Charron-Chénier's predatory inclusion in education debt shows that an arrangement can exploit through the access it grants, and that widening access need not reduce it

## Which sense of "arrangement"

[TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) sense 3: lenders, colleges and the households they lend to,
persons and institutions.

## The inference

1. [THEORY-tmpi3g1u](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpi3g1u.md), the reading in [NOTE-tmpvf8za](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvf8za.md): predatory inclusion is
   access "to a good, service, or opportunity from which they have
   historically been excluded but under conditions that jeopardize the
   benefits of this access"; "Black educational debt roughly tripled
   between 2001 and 2013"; attainment does not explain the gap; student
   debt is "legally very difficult to discharge, even when declaring
   bankruptcy".
2. So households gained access to higher education and were left with a
   lasting obligation that narrows their later options, while lenders
   profited, and access and the gap rose together.
3. So an arrangement can exploit through the access it grants, and
   widening access need not reduce it ([CLAIM-tmp16mkz](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp16mkz.md), version 2).

## Critical questions

- **Does the evidence fit what the claim says?** For version 2, yes in
  outline. For version 1, no: version 1 said the arrangement produces
  the dependence through which it exploits by building reliance on the
  provider. Here the need predates the access, coming from the wealth gap
  ("Because of lower average income and wealth levels, black borrowers
  likely have to take on greater absolute and relative debt levels"),
  what the access leaves is debt, not reliance on a lender, and exit is
  closed by law, not by the lender's terms. That is why the claim was
  narrowed.
- **Is the source reliable for the step from 1 to 2?** In part. The
  pattern is descriptive survey data with one regression; the lenders'
  role is conjectured: "this is where we suspect a significant part of
  the inequality-generating action is."
- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** A debt gap
  could arise from Black students' larger need to borrow alone, with no
  exploitation in the terms; the paper rules out attainment, not that.
  So the evidence supports "can" only as far as the terms are shown to be
  exploitative, which the paper argues more than it shows.
- **Is the claim's other source read?** No. Alicea's "predatory DEI"
  ([LIT-tmp63z5g](../literature.d/LIT-tmp63z5g.md)), the organizational case, is Unavailable.

## Verdict

Proposed. Weak, descriptive support for version 2 from one market case;
no support for version 1's wording. The owner may accept or reject the
inference once Alicea is reached.
