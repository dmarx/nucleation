# CLAIM-tmphq6hp: An expectation is an operative norm rather than a prediction when a discrepancy is met by bringing the behaviour back into line with the expectation instead of revising the expectation to fit the behaviour; which response a discrepancy gets, adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or revision, is the work of normative governance

## The claim

A22 §2, on an unexpected interruption:

> **Prediction revision:** The service is less reliable than anticipated.
> Update the expectation.
>
> **Norm enforcement:** The service has failed to satisfy a maintained
> requirement. Recruit activity to restore compliance.
>
> The first changes the expectation to accommodate reality. The second
> attempts to change reality to satisfy the expectation.

So the same expectation can be held as a prediction or as a norm, and what
decides is the organization's response to a discrepancy: "An operative
norm might be distinguished partly by the organization's disposition to
preserve it against discrepancies rather than simply revise it in response
to them." This needs no represented belief about what ought to happen,
which is why A22 offers it for organizations "whose constituent processes
lack explicitly represented beliefs", extending Bicchieri's distinction
between empirical and normative expectations ([LIT-493](../literature.d/LIT-493.md), held, not read).

Neither response is better in general: "An organization that revises every
expectation immediately cannot sustain commitments. An organization that
attempts to enforce every expectation becomes rigid and potentially
coercive." Choosing among "adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or
revision" for a given discrepancy is what A22 calls normative governance.

## The record's term for it

The distinction is the record's direction of fit ([TERM-151](../terms.d/TERM-151.md)): a predicate
"written to fit what it is applied to, describing it", or one that "what it
is applied to must be made to fit". [CLAIM-942](CLAIM-942.md) uses it to tell inquiry from
engineering. Applied here, a prediction is a descriptive predicate on
another's behaviour and an operative norm a requiring one; an expectation
becomes a norm when its direction of fit turns. A22 does not name the
link; the assistant adds it (gap: the distinction is stated without the
record's term that already holds it).

## What it does not say

- Not that an operative norm is legitimate. A22 §1 separates "Operative
  norm" ("a standard against which subsequent behavior is assessed or
  regulated") from "Legitimate norm" ("adequate grounds for treating the
  standard as authoritative"), and §3's caption: "Those reasons need not
  establish moral legitimacy."
- Not that the organization formed the standard by itself. Who supplies
  the requirement is [QUESTION-128](../questions.d/QUESTION-128.md)'s open part; A22 §5 says of its own
  model that "its maintenance rule is supplied by the modeler".

## Note of 2026-11-25: restoration and a copied majority, from the manuscript governance-of-becoming, Article 7
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-946 CASE-362 TERM-148 — Proposed; the executed study, its finding and the term it tested, cited with their standing -->

The manuscript's Article 7 (§7.6, join J7.4) carries CLAIM-946 over to
this claim's marker. Neither entry cites the other. CASE-362 (Proposed,
executed study; filer-run) committed in advance to the failure rule that
"if plain conformity passes condition 3 … the criterion fails to make the
distinction", and found that "Plain conformity restores a minority's
deviation (ρ = 0.024, against the averaging benchmark 0.25 and the
threshold 0.125)". CLAIM-946: restoration "does not tell a population
whose members evaluate one another's interpretations from one whose
members copy one another's answers".

If that carries over, observed restoration of behaviour to an expectation
does not tell an enforced norm from a majority copied: members who only
copy what most others do also bring a deviant back into line, with no
disposition to preserve the expectation against discrepancy. Then the
ladder from regularity to operative norm needs a marker beyond observed
restoration. One candidate, not stated by any entry: a disposition to
restore that holds against a majority, of the kind CASE-362's evidential
condition tests in direction (there, members moved away from an
unwarranted deviation, toward the world).

The carry-over may fail, because the objects differ. CASE-362 restores
interpretive prototypes (TERM-148's restorative reading), and this claim
restores behaviour against an expectation. The worry is also not the one
this claim's defeater names ("every corrective response is also a
revision of expectation"): it is that a corrective response can come from
copying, not from the expectation's direction of fit. Filed as a note, not
a question: whether it carries over is a check on this claim's marker,
answered by rerunning CASE-362's design on behaviour against an
expectation. The join has not yet been put to an independent reader
under ADR-075. No change of status. The wording is the assistant's
(Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
