# NOTE-tmpnibvh: Ryan, Mims & Koestner — Reward contingency and interpersonal context

## Contribution

Two things. A standard vocabulary for reward contingencies, under which
studies that had seemed to conflict line up (pp. 736–744). And the first
experiment in which the same performance-contingent reward is given in two
interpersonal styles, each with a no-reward control receiving the same
feedback, so that the effect of the reward can be separated from the
effect of the information it carries and from the style it is given in
(pp. 744–747).

## Key insight

Performance-contingent rewards make both the informational and the
controlling aspect of a reward more salient, so their effect is not fixed
by the contingency: "performance-contingent rewards can be perceived as
either informational or controlling, depending on how they are
administered" (p. 742). But "like all other rewards", they "tend to lower
intrinsic motivation relative to no rewards if there is identical feedback
within the same interpersonal context" (pp. 748–749). The comparison
group is what had been confusing the literature.

## Assumptions

- **Expected rewards only.** All four reward types are announced before the
  task; unexpected rewards "are not relevant to the present discussion"
  (footnote 1, p. 736).
- **Interesting tasks only.** Dull-task conditions are excluded as "not
  germane" (p. 738).
- **Free-choice time as the measure of intrinsic motivation**: seconds
  spent on the target puzzles in a 6-minute unobserved period, out of 360
  (pp. 745–746).
- **Initial interest as covariate** (ANCOVA); sex dropped after showing no
  main effect or interaction (p. 746).

## Key results

### The review (pp. 736–744)

- **Task-non-contingent** rewards (paid for attending) do not undermine:
  three studies (p. 738).
- **Task-contingent** rewards, given without feedback, undermine relative
  to no rewards; with positive feedback added, Deci 1972b, Harackiewicz
  1979 and Swann and Pittman 1977 found no difference from no-reward,
  no-feedback groups (p. 740). The evidence that they undermine more than
  task-non-contingent rewards is "weak" (p. 740).
- **Performance-contingent vs no reward with positive feedback**: Karniol
  and Ross 1977 and Rosenfield et al. 1980, no difference; Harackiewicz
  1979, rewarded subjects less intrinsically motivated (p. 741). "The issue
  … remains unresolved" (p. 741).
- **Performance- vs task-contingent without feedback**: mixed (Greene and
  Lepper 1974 none; Luyten and Lens 1981 n.s.; Boggiano and Ruble 1979 and
  Enzle and Ross 1978 performance-contingent higher, the latter on an
  attitude measure) (p. 742).
- **Harackiewicz reinterpreted** (p. 743): her performance-contingent group
  without norms is read as controlling (mean −.96), the one with norms as
  informational (+.07), a task-contingent group −.64; the no-reward,
  no-feedback group fell between. "the results were not significant".
- **Competitively contingent** rewards undermine (Pritchard et al. 1977;
  Deci et al. 1981), and Greene and Lepper's "performance-contingent"
  good-player award is reclassified as competitive (p. 744).

### The experiment (pp. 744–748)

- **Design.** 96 introductory-psychology students, equal men and women,
  randomly assigned, n = 16 per cell. A 2 × 2 of reward ($3,
  performance-contingent, "if you do well") by feedback style
  (informational: "Do as well as you can"; controlling: "should try as
  hard as possible because I expect you to perform up to standards",
  feedback with "just as you should"), plus a no-reward, no-feedback group
  and a no-feedback, task-contingent reward group ("for doing the
  puzzles"). Hidden-figures puzzles; the free-choice observer was blind to
  condition (p. 745).
- **Table 1, free-choice seconds, mean (SD)** (p. 746):

| | no reward | performance-contingent | task-contingent |
|---|---|---|---|
| informational, feedback | 282.6 (134.7) | 229.4 (139.9) | |
| controlling, feedback | 195.8 (133.6) | 117.3 (169.3) | |
| neutral, no feedback | 164.1 (150.1) | | 113.4 (129.4) |

- **H1, reward vs same feedback without reward**: F(1, 89) = 4.34,
  p < .05. All three rewarded vs all three unrewarded: F(1, 89) = 6.36,
  p < .02.
- **H2, controlling vs informational** (the four feedback cells):
  F(1, 89) = 6.11, p < .02.
- **The two performance-contingent groups**: F(1, 89) = 4.25, p < .05;
  informational performance-contingent vs task-contingent: F(1, 89) =
  4.58, p < .04. Controlling performance-contingent and task-contingent do
  not differ (117.3 vs 113.4) (p. 747).
- **Feedback vs none**: marginal, F(1, 89) = 3.51, p = .06; informational
  feedback clearly above no feedback, controlling feedback barely (p. 747).
- **Self-report.** An 11-item interest factor correlated .42 with free
  choice (p < .001); its ANOVA "failed to reach significance, although the
  pattern of results paralleled" (p. 748).
- **Pressure–tension** (3 items): controlling 3.61 vs informational 2.91,
  F(1, 90) = 9.19, p < .005; no-feedback 3.14 between (p. 748).
- **Recomputed without the covariate**, from Table 1 (pooled SD 143.5, df
  90): H1 F ≈ 3.37 (p ≈ .07); H2 ≈ 7.69; the two performance-contingent
  groups ≈ 4.88; informational performance-contingent vs task-contingent
  ≈ 5.23; rewarded vs unrewarded ≈ 4.31; feedback vs none ≈ 4.73.
  Standardized differences: informational vs controlling
  performance-contingent d ≈ 0.78; reward vs its feedback-matched control
  d ≈ −0.37 (informational) and −0.55 (controlling). The reported Fs are
  in the same range, so the table and the tests agree; H1 depends on the
  covariate for p < .05.

## Concepts

- **Task-non-contingent reward** — given for being in the session,
  independent of the activity (p. 736).
- **Task-contingent reward** — given for doing or completing the task,
  without respect to quality (p. 737).
- **Performance-contingent reward** — given "for meeting a set criterion,
  norm, or level of competence" (p. 737).
- **Competitively contingent reward** — winners of a limited number of
  rewards outperform others (p. 737).
- **Informational vs controlling aspect** — feedback that signifies
  competence within self-determined performance, vs pressure "toward
  specified outcomes" (pp. 737–738).

## Connections

It applies Ryan 1982's informational/controlling manipulation of feedback
(the "should" locution) to rewards, and Deci and Ryan 1980's
cognitive-evaluation framework. Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999 ([LIT-557](../literature.d/LIT-557.md)) use
its contingency taxonomy; Ryan et al. 2022 ([LIT-tmpd9u4s](../literature.d/LIT-tmpd9u4s.md)) cite it as the
taxonomy's source. Cameron, Banko and Pierce ([LIT-562](../literature.d/LIT-562.md)) re-code the same
literature by what participants were told.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md): the primary study its promote_when names, read.** Its
  promote_when asks for experiments that "manipulate only the interpersonal
  framing of the same reward (informational versus controlling, as in Ryan,
  Mims and Koestner 1983) and find the predicted difference mediated by felt
  control". This study manipulates the framing and finds the difference
  (C2), and finds felt pressure higher under controlling framing (C4), but
  runs no mediation analysis, and the pressure effect is reported for the
  information–control dimension as a whole, not for the rewarded cells.
  So it is the single study the clause describes, not a mediation result.
- **[THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md)'s text, a correction.** [THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md) reports, from [LIT-557](../literature.d/LIT-557.md)
  (p. 653), that this study "found the same pattern, not significantly".
  The primary paper reports the informational and controlling
  performance-contingent groups as significantly different, F(1, 89) =
  4.25, p < .05 (p. 747). Either [LIT-557](../literature.d/LIT-557.md) computed a different contrast
  (for example, each rewarded group against its own control), or the
  record's paraphrase is wrong; [NOTE-438](NOTE-438.md) should be checked against p. 653.
- **[THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md)'s refutation clause.** The clause says the account is
  refuted "if rewards tied to a performance standard raise free choice
  against a control group given the same performance feedback". This
  experiment has exactly that control in both styles, and the rewards
  lowered free choice (229.4 vs 282.6; 117.3 vs 195.8; C3). One study of
  n = 16 per cell; it does not meet the clause and counts against the
  refuting outcome.
- **[THEORY-154](../theory.d/THEORY-154.md) (Cameron, Banko and Pierce).** The paper's review is the
  earlier, CET-side reading of the same contingency literature. Its own
  experiment's rewards were for doing "well" with no stated score or
  comparison, so it does not test [THEORY-154](../theory.d/THEORY-154.md)'s distinction between rewards
  for surpassing a score and for exceeding others.
- **[QUESTION-066](../questions.d/QUESTION-066.md).** The free-choice period measures persistence once the
  experimenter's support is removed, and initial interest is a covariate,
  so it approaches the question's "valuation held fixed", for one
  6-minute period.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **Small cells** (n = 16) and high within-cell variance (SDs 130–170 s on
  a 0–360 s measure; the controlling performance-contingent SD of 169.3
  exceeds its mean of 117.3, so the distribution is likely skewed or
  floored).
- **The covariate carries H1.** Without it the reward contrast is at
  p ≈ .07 (recomputed); the adjusted means are not reported.
- **The self-report measure did not reach significance** (p. 748).
- **Mediation is inferred, not tested.** The "salience of controlling and
  informational aspects" that "mediate the contingency effects" (abstract)
  is a reading of the means.
- **The review is narrative** and some reclassifications (Harackiewicz's
  groups, Greene and Lepper's award) are post hoc.
- College students, one task, one session.

## Open questions

- Does felt control, measured between the reward and the free-choice
  period, statistically mediate the framing effect on the same reward?
- Does the framing effect replicate at adequate power, and with rewards
  tied to a stated score, where [THEORY-154](../theory.d/THEORY-154.md) predicts no undermining?
