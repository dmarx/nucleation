# Article 6. The Organization of Ignorance

## Heuristics, reflexive control, self-confirming governance, and protected evidence

**Abstract.** An organization can be kept ignorant without being kept from information. This article argues that ignorance can be a property of how an organization's evidence, evaluation and authority are arranged, and that the arrangements come apart: each has its own mechanism, its own remedy and its own way of looking like success from inside. An organization can be governed through the evidence its procedures receive, through the criteria by which they weigh it, through the situations its sound values meet, and possibly through how its procedures form. It can stop looking for what would count against it because looking only costs it, and it can certify itself clean against a specification that never names the harm. The article shows, each time in a stipulated model, four non-equivalences: correcting how evidence was selected is not correcting how it is scored; a clean internal record is not compliance; a corrected belief is not a corrected practice; and a protected anchor is not a correct one. It then examines the general conjecture these suggest, that a system's capacity to correct itself turns on how evidence, evaluation and authority are coupled and on what it cannot rewrite or reject, and sets beside it an objection it has not answered: the same protection that enables correction can fix the system's error at the anchor's own. Familiar heuristics and biases are placed beside the mechanisms as candidates, with the tests that would decide them. None of those tests has been run on human judgement, and every executed result here holds in a model, not in any institution or mind.

---

### 6.1 Introduction

The usual picture of ignorance is subtractive. Someone lacks a fact; supply it and the ignorance ends. Organized ignorance on that picture is censorship, suppression or lies: someone withholds the fact, or replaces it with a false one. The picture is not wrong, but it misses a class of cases that matter more for organizations than for individuals. A regulator that receives every relevant fact can still decide as an interested party would wish, if what it is asked to weigh has been chosen for it. An institution can know less every year about what it owes without anyone being silenced, if finding out is costly and unrewarded. A protective practice can survive the correction of the belief that justified it. And an organization can produce, from its own monitoring, the evidence that it is doing well.

In each case the deficit is not a missing proposition. It is a feature of the arrangement: of which evidence arrives, by what standard it is scored, who has authority to act on it, and which parts of the arrangement the organization itself can rewrite. The question of this article is whether such arrangements can be told apart, so that one can say not only that an organization is ignorant but how, and what would correct it.

The argument proceeds as follows. Section 6.2 states the sense in which ignorance can be an arrangement and what makes it relative to a task. Section 6.3 sets out four routes by which one party can govern another's decisions without making them, of which the fourth is the least secure. Sections 6.4 to 6.6 take three mechanisms in turn, each in a model built to isolate it: selective admission against self-scoring, inquiry suppressed by its own score, and the persistence of a protective practice after its belief is corrected. Section 6.7 examines the conjecture that unifies them and the objection that stands against it. Section 6.8 adds a way of being ignorant that the models do not reach, the transmission of characteristic limitations without false content. Section 6.9 sets familiar biases beside the mechanisms as candidates. Section 6.10 says what the argument does not establish.

A word on the evidence. The results here are of three kinds: theorems and constructions in stipulated models, executed simulations with invented parameters, and interpretive distinctions that carry the models' structure to organizations. The first two show what can happen and why, under stated conditions. The third is where the article's claims about organizations live, and they are argued, not observed. Where a step is a conjecture or a design that has not been run, the text says so.

### 6.2 Ignorance as an arrangement

Start from the simplest of the routes. "An organization can be governed from outside without any of its decisions being made for it, through the evidence its own procedures receive or through the criteria by which they weigh evidence" (CLAIM-404). The point is structural, and it says nothing about any particular body: it "does not say that any regulator, newsroom or expert body is captured" (CLAIM-404). Two models from the literature show its halves at work. In Besley and Prat's model of news media, an incumbent can pay outlets to suppress verifiable bad news, and under capture voters, "though fully rational and not deceived, learn nothing" (Besley and Prat 2006; THEORY-837). The voters reason correctly; what they receive has been arranged. On the other side, Short, Hill and Brown argue that capture "can operate through the evaluative framework a regulator adopts rather than through its decisions" (Short, Hill and Brown 2025; THEORY-859), a reading, not yet a settled result, of the criteria route in a regulator.

The second form is ignorance with no one censored. "An organization can become normatively less informed without censoring anyone by two distinct routes, selective disappearance of the harmed from the population it observes and costly discovery that keeps it from investigating the questions that would reveal unmet obligations; neither needs deliberate suppression" (CLAIM-657). Both routes differ from the counter-corrective weakening of challenge (CLAIM-619), and they should not be conflated, the claim adds, "particularly when attributing responsibility" (CLAIM-657). This is a proposal; its first route rests on a derivation not yet adopted (CLAIM-652), and the second is the one section 6.5 models.

Third, a description can be adequate for what an organization does and still merge what its members' claims must distinguish. A coarse-graining preserves a judgment "exactly when the judgment is constant on its fibres" (CLAIM-350), a granted formal fact. What makes a description adequate is therefore not the description: adequacy "is fixed by the evaluative target, and the target cannot be justified solely by the description that preserves it" (CLAIM-348). An organization whose categories serve its performance perfectly can be blind, by those same categories, to a distinction a member's claim turns on, and nothing in its performance will show it.

This fixes the sense in which ignorance is relative. Legibility is "whether the observer, with the rules, categories and resources it actually has, can recover the distinction from what it is given and bring it to bear on the task" (TERM-064). A difference can be present in the evidence and illegible to the organization that holds it. A proposed profile of a perspective's "organized epistemic limitations", in four components, legibility, epistemic standing, practical efficacy and revisability, makes the same point from the other side: it is "Not a verdict", and a limitation counts as ignorance only relative to "an independently specified epistemic or normative task" (TERM-tmp9mag8). Ignorance in this article is always ignorance for something. That keeps the argument from the cheap move of calling any partial view ignorant, and it means each diagnosis owes a statement of the task it is measured against.

### 6.3 Four routes of governance without decision

How can one party govern another's decisions without making them? Four routes can be distinguished (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw), and they differ in what they act on.

The first is evidence. For a fixed objective, the decision moves with the evidence supplied, "even if the regulator reasons correctly on the evidence it receives" (CLAIM-404). The second is criteria: "Even perfect information about both properties does not ensure the regulator will consider the second" (CLAIM-404). The two are "distinct and each is possible without the other" (CLAIM-404). The third is circumstances: "The manipulation changes the situation the target's sound values respond to, without deceiving it or changing its criteria" (CLAIM-703), developed from Keller's account of moral blackmail (Keller 2016; LIT-2670). Stated bare, this route "is trivial, since anyone who changes the world changes what others decide" (CLAIM-703). What makes it a way of being governed is a further condition, that one party controls the trigger and another bears the cost, and that condition is itself only proposed (CLAIM-713). Whether circumstances is a third route or a case of evidence is left open (CLAIM-404).

The three routes are sometimes said to act on a decision "by a procedure that stays what it was" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw). That fits evidence and circumstances, which leave the procedure untouched and change its inputs. It fits criteria less well, since a change of criteria changes the weights the procedure applies; the more careful statement is an influence "on the standards a procedure applies while the influence lasts" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw).

The fourth route acts on formation, and it is the least secure. "A transmissible expression the target takes up can seed interpretive, evidence-selecting and authority-allocating dispositions that the target then maintains itself, so the influence outlasts its source, reaches circumstances the intervention never presented, and leaves formation provenance outside while maintenance is the target's own" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw). It builds on the first two routes (CLAIM-404) and on a conjecture that memes act as nucleation sites (CLAIM-tmp1ng5k). It is a conjecture, and the argument for it divides into two halves of very different strength (ARG-tmp2kh2e).

The possibility half is that an influence on formation can leave an organization the target maintains itself. Two constructions carry it. In one, a capacity installed by an outside pulse reaches the same maintained state (S = 2.618) as one formed by the agent's own frustration: "Upheld is told apart afterwards. Own and pulse are not: after formation they are the same organization, and only a knockout made during formation separates them" (CASE-191). That is a result of the construction, not a law about formation; stated for knockouts, "after formation no knockout tells it from a self-formed one" (TERM-100). In a thought experiment about a regulator perturbed once, "The influencing actor need not continuously control the regulator's decisions" (CASE-123), and of real regulators it shows nothing.

The carrier half is that the influence can be a transmissible cultural expression. Here nothing has been built: "Nothing built on the record has a cultural expression as the installing influence", "So the inference holds for the possibility half and is open for the carrier half" (ARG-tmp2kh2e). The nearest statement of a carrier is that a meme can carry "a portable specification of a governance or inquiry protocol" (CLAIM-tmpdq0ah), but that is a proposal, not a construction, and it leaves the verdict where it was.

The route predicts two marks: persistence after the source stops, and transfer to circumstances never presented, "the transfer by which CLAIM-532 tells a formed capacity from a temporary state" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw). The marks are evidence, not a definition. Self-formation has a sufficient condition, "as shown by transfer to discrepancies of the same class after the inducing conditions are gone and by interventions on the processes that produce the capacity while it forms" (CLAIM-532), and a sufficient condition is not what formation is. Persistence alone is a weak mark, since "Persistence and nucleating productivity in the committor sense vary independently" (CLAIM-595). Nor is the route a charge of wrongdoing: "external formation is not sufficient to establish manipulation, and endogenous maintenance is not sufficient to establish freedom" (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw).

A test of the carrier half has been designed and not run (LEAD-tmpuxizu). It would extend Weatherall, O'Connor and Bruner's model of a propagandist in an epistemic network (Weatherall, O'Connor and Bruner 2018; THEORY-855), in which a propagandist who forwards only the real results that favour the worse action can leave policy makers near-certain of a false theory. The model's lever is simple: belief depends only on the cumulative excess of successes, so "whoever chooses what the audience sees chooses that number" (THEORY-845). The extension has policy makers pass a selection rule to newcomers and removes the propagandist after a time. Its expectation is stated before any run: "If the false belief decays once the propagandist stops, the distortion was maintained from outside (CLAIM-404's route only). If it persists because the transmitted rule keeps selecting, the route of CLAIM-tmpp0ufw has a built instance with a cultural carrier" (LEAD-tmpuxizu). Knocking out the rule's transmission during and after the campaign separates the two. A question must be settled first: a rule that is only copied, with nothing selecting for it, drifts.

### 6.4 Selection is not scoring

The first two routes, evidence and criteria, are distinct as routes. Are they distinct as failures, so that the remedy for one does not touch the other? A model population answers yes, for one of the remedies.

The model is a population of 32 networked perspectives whose admission of cues depends on what each has already recorded, run with 64 world seeds per condition; the outcome counted is a wrong incumbent at the end of 1,400 updates (CASE-162). Its precursor is a legibility filter of selectively admitted evidence, built to make one point: "Selection-aware inference is the control that the pathology has to beat" (CASE-156). The run sets no criterion in advance for what would count as different remedies, so the reading that follows is drawn from the results. With no self-scoring (c = 0), the number of wrong incumbents out of 64 by selectivity s is:

| selectivity s | 0 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive credit | 0 | 31 | 41 | 41 |
| exact inverse-propensity weighting | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5 |

At s = 0.75 the raw cue mean is −0.193 and the mean of the admitted cues is +0.184: selection has reversed the sign of the available evidence. Weighting each admitted cue by the inverse of its admission probability undoes this. Self-scoring is another matter. "Self-scoring alone, c = 0.5 with s = 0, leaves 39 of 64 wrong. Nothing is censored there, so the correction changes nothing." With both present, the correction removes only the part due to selection, from 41 to 34 at c = 0.5, s = 0.5 (CASE-162).

The claim drawn from the run is that "Lock-in from selectively admitted evidence and lock-in from self-scoring need different remedies: weighting admitted evidence by known, positive admission probabilities undoes the first and leaves the second, so correcting for how evidence was selected is not correcting how it is scored" (CLAIM-466). Read against the distinction of routes, it adds that the routes' remedies are distinct too (CLAIM-466).

Three qualifications bound it. First, the result is half of "different remedies". Propensity weighting repairs selection and is idle on self-scoring, but the model does not test a remedy for self-scoring: "Independent scoring is the natural one ... the runs do not test it" (CLAIM-466). The principle such a remedy would follow is that "What keeps a judgement–observation loop from confirming itself is evidence whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests" (CLAIM-342). A simulation of a single-mind loop, not yet adopted, finds that a score or weight the loop's own authority does not set prevents lock-in, while evidence that arrives unselected "keeps it sighted but does not anchor it" (CASE-353; CLAIM-931). One candidate score, commitment to predictions before the evidence, is qualified by a theorem: such scoring "is not by itself Regime B", since in the linearized operator model "commitment removes capture only when the interpretation bends the reading of the evidence no more than it bends the predictions" (CLAIM-340). Which sense of independent scoring is meant remains open between two that have been distinguished (TERM-147).

Second, the correction is an oracle: "The correction is an oracle" (CASE-162), working "with known propensities" (CLAIM-466). What an organization can do when propensities are unknown, or zero for some evidence, the model cannot show. Third, the population's results are read from stored results that agree with the case's figures; the simulation has not been regenerated.

A further result in the same model bounds what governance adds. Even "CASE-162's run with the router's audit delay matched still had the router ahead" of the learned allocation, so "whatever governance adds over routing has to be shown where evaluation fails" (CLAIM-467). The point recurs in section 6.7: the interesting failures are failures of evaluation, not of allocation.

The same structure appears in a toy model of confirmation feedback, an accumulator whose sampling leans toward its current belief. It was first offered with a threshold at κ > 2/3. A derivation shows that in that model "the unbiased state is unstable for every κ > 0; the threshold κ > 2/3 shown with it comes from the illustration's decay factor 0.92, and a decay λ puts the threshold at κ = λ/a" (CLAIM-tmpu5cap). An accumulator that does not correct for its own selection has no threshold: a small lean grows at any coupling. A nucleation reading of confirmation bias, in which a lean must cross a threshold before it sustains itself, therefore needs a restoring term. The derivation says nothing about people; its own statement of what it does not say is "Anything about people" (CLAIM-tmpu5cap). And the name needs care: "Retrieval that depends on current belief is not a bias in itself … calling a search biased needs a standard outside the search" (CLAIM-292), while a toy's conclusion can read a parameter as the concept it stands for (CLAIM-tmpyxemn).

### 6.5 Inquiry suppressed by its own score, and self-certification

The second route of section 6.2, costly discovery, turns on a simple incentive. "For a fixed arrangement, expanding the recognized demand set cannot improve conjunctive compliance and cannot decrease the count of recognized violations; where governance penalizes newly recognized violations without rewarding their discovery, an organization may acquire an incentive not to improve its identification of its obligations" (CLAIM-662). The first half is a theorem about sets; the second is the incentive it creates under a stated governance rule. In its dynamic form, for one model institution, inquiry that only reveals requirements "never raises its score when the hidden requirements do not otherwise come to light: on every path it adds only penalized violations and spent budget, so an institution acting on that score makes no inquiry" (CLAIM-1064). Two conditions carry the result, and neither can be dropped: discovery is unrewarded, and the hidden requirements do not otherwise come to light.

Does a learner find this? A test was set before the run, with its criterion: "Less inquiry under the penalty supports CLAIM-662; none defeats it" (LEAD-181). The model institution has 30 requirements, 10 visible and 20 found only by inquiry, and a budget split among inquiry, remediation and output. It runs five regimes in four settings, with 16 learners per regime and setting (CASE-396). The results:

- "Under the penalty the institution makes no inquiry. The optimum is a = 0 in every setting, uniquely". The learners end between 0.01 and 0.36 units of 10.
- The two scorecards part. The internal count ends at 0.00 (in two settings) or 0.10 (in the other two) recognized violations, "the independent count, at 6.08 and 3.36".
- Scored by the independent count, the institution inquires at 5 to 6 units and ends with no violations.
- "removing the penalty on newly recognized violations does not by itself bring inquiry back, because inquiry still earns the institution nothing". Rewarding discovery restores some inquiry, 1 to 4 units (CASE-396).

The learners are told only their score, so the avoidance arrives "without any rule against inquiry and without suppressing anyone" (CASE-396). This is the second route of CLAIM-657 produced by learning, though the model has no exit, and so says nothing of the first route (CLAIM-657). The learners learn whether and how much to inquire, not which questions to ask.

What the run supports depends on which comparison answers the test, and two readings stand as alternatives. Against the independent count, the penalty suppresses inquiry (CLAIM-1120). Against internal scoring without the penalty, "the penalty lowers inquiry only where inquiry has some other value to the institution" (CLAIM-997), since with no penalty and no reward inquiry is idle anyway. Both readings agree on the negative result: lifting the penalty is not enough, and inquiry returns only when it is worth something to the institution that does it.

Self-certification is the complement. In a model in which a harm the specification does not name enters mid-run, "Never entering the demand gives zero violations against D0 and against the organization's own specification, in every regime, while the harm runs for all 120 periods"; "Only the true demand set ranks the two correctly. That follows from how the scorecards are built" (CASE-397). It is an instance of a general limit, that an actor's "own judgment that it has met them cannot always establish that it has" (CLAIM-727).

The result is narrower than its name might suggest. "The policy is an input. Nothing in the model makes the choice between excising and revising" (CASE-397). So the model shows self-certification given a policy of excision; it does not show ignorance that the organization's own dynamics produce. One arm comes closer. "Under the peak rule, excision happens by default": a review that needs capacity is never funded, so "The organization has not chosen to excise; its budget rule leaves it unable to revise" (CASE-397). Even there the policy given is revision, and the budget rule blocks it. The model of self-certification and the model of learned avoidance (CASE-396) are therefore two halves that no run yet joins, and the case names the other as the one "in which that choice is learned" (CASE-397). A run in which an institution learns to excise a demand and then certifies itself clean against a specification it controls has been specified and not built (LEAD-tmpv3s18). Until it is, the full mechanism of self-confirming governance, learning and certification together, is a conjecture assembled from two models. To call the result normative agnotology, "the organized prevention of relevant facts from acquiring the normative significance they warrant" (CLAIM-tmpa61rf), is to apply a proposal of its own, and that idea is not the transmitted character of section 6.8 (CLAIM-tmpa61rf).

### 6.6 A corrected belief is not a corrected practice

A protective regime can outlast the belief that justified it. The conjecture is that "Some persistent anxiety may be governance-maintained rather than belief-maintained": anticipated threat grants defensive processes authority over action, attention and evidence acquisition, and "the regime can outlast a corrected belief" (CLAIM-446). Its general form: "A protective regime, in a person, an institution or a system of states, becomes self-maintaining when the interventions it authorizes shift the evidence used to judge its necessity", and "it is a failure only where the regime has become less responsive to evidence that it is no longer needed" (CLAIM-445). The second clause matters: protection that persists while the danger persists is not a failure, and the conjecture is about protection that has stopped listening.

Human studies bear on the conjecture and none tests its distinctive part. In Goodson and colleagues' meta-analysis, reducing safety behaviours lowered symptoms (d = 0.43) and instructed increases raised them (d = 0.65), mostly in young non-clinical samples (Goodson et al. 2026; THEORY-903). In a picture-based avoidance task, patients with OCD or PTSD avoided a safety cue more than controls and, after effective avoidance, still expected the aversive picture more (THEORY-912). Mancinelli and Bach's review of 116 studies favours expectancy and inference-based accounts of avoidance (THEORY-900), while finding avoidance that persists, in some participants for hundreds of unpunished trials (THEORY-901), and avoidance responses used for stimuli never paired with the outcome (THEORY-920). A rival reading of the pattern "the belief corrected, the practice persists" is the inhibitory-learning account: extinction "does not erase the original threat association but adds a competing inhibitory one" (THEORY-914). But "None of these studies measured authority as this claim defines it, so the claim's distinctive part is still untested" (CLAIM-446), and the human tests are open questions (QUESTION-325; QUESTION-326; QUESTION-327; QUESTION-331).

What a model can do is show that the separation is possible and say what would tell it from its rivals. The design fixed its check before the run: "if a Bayesian learner with matched information costs, or a habit model with matched memory, reproduces every intervention response, the extra authority state has not earned its place. That outcome is to be reported, not tuned away" (CASE-153). The executed model is a sentry in a hidden world that is dangerous, then safe for 400 steps, then dangerous again, with 1,000 paired seeds per condition (CASE-346). Its parameters are invented. In it:

- With authority rewarded by the apparent success of protection, and the sentry restricting inspection, "no run leaves protection in 400 safe steps and 43 % of runs never even correct the belief".
- "A protected investigator corrects the belief within a median 6 steps, yet protection outlasts it in 87 % of runs". The belief is corrected in every run.
- Restriction adds needless protection under success feedback beyond what it adds under independent evaluation, an interaction of 99 [95, 103] units.
- Every regime still protects when danger returns (CASE-346).

Within the model, then, appraisal and practical correction come apart: accurate appraisal does not establish corrective governance. The same mark, persistence after the inducing context is gone, appears in the constructions of section 6.3 for a scalar capacity and a stipulated institution (CASE-191; CASE-123), and here for a sentry.

The check, however, comes out mostly against the authority state. The Bayesian with matched costs does not reproduce the responses. The habit does: under success feedback the authority update is the habit trace, and "a habit of matched memory reproduces every intervention it can receive" (CASE-346). Those interventions are belief correction, authority reset and restored evidence access. "Only interventions on the evaluation tell the two apart": an independent audit, and reinforcement made independent, under which every run leaves protection, by a median of 44 steps (CASE-346; CLAIM-924). Whether this meets the conjecture's own defeater "depends on whether an outcome-gated habit can receive reinforcement correction" (CLAIM-446), and that question is open. What the model cannot show is "That a governance account beats a habit account anywhere outside this model" (CASE-346). The honest summary is that the model separates belief from practice, and fails to separate governance from habit except by interventions on how outcomes are scored.

### 6.7 Coupling evidence, evaluation and authority

The mechanisms of sections 6.4 to 6.6 share a shape. In each, what fails is not the supply of evidence but the relation between evidence, the standard that scores it and the authority that acts on the score. This suggests a general conjecture: "A system's ability to correct itself depends not simply on having critics or feedback but on how evidence, evaluation and authority are coupled, and on what the system is prevented from rewriting or rejecting" (CLAIM-1024). For one loop the principle is already stated: evidence "whose arrival or scoring does not pass through the interpretation it tests" (CLAIM-342). The conjecture generalizes it, and it rests on four constructions, each a demonstrated result in its own model.

In a simulated sensor network, revision improves prediction "only when it answers to beacons whose position and channel the revision can neither rewrite nor refuse"; revision by the sensors' disagreement alone "restores consistency and not accuracy", with error 0.044 in 40 of 40 runs against 0.40 (CLAIM-1183). In a model of two institutions, a loop between them "can capture the evaluator's reading when each institution is stable alone, when neither has a loop inside it, and when the evaluator's conferral reads no interpretation at all", and a return channel too weak to close the loop prevents it (CLAIM-1202). Its condition is derived, "(θ + σ_E ω)(χ + σ_P ψ) > Δ_E Δ_P", and checked on 1,802 of 1,802 parameter sets: "cutting the return channel removes the instability whatever the party supplies" (CASE-401). This bears on a third part of the account of capture: where an organization evaluates another that affects its evidence or resources, the loop can close between the two, "so that formal independence of the evaluator's procedure does not make its evaluation independent" (CLAIM-404). That part would be defeated by a showing that such an evaluator cannot be destabilized when each institution's own loop is stable; in this model the showing fails, and the part, argued interpretively, has a worked model in which it is demonstrated (CLAIM-1202). In a population, grants indexed by role, with the critic role "scored by observed relevance", "leave neither belief factions nor capture", while scalar grants scored by agreement capture or split the population (CLAIM-1029); this assumes "that relevance can be observed independently of side" (CLAIM-1029). And "Where no process holds evidence on a block of tasks that does not come from the proposals themselves, a governance rule that treats its members alike cannot tell honest dissent from an attack"; the two worlds' successes sum to 1 (CLAIM-1194).

Three results qualify the components. Under role grants, shared meaning "follows where competence happens to sit and can override a weak imbalance in the evidence" (CLAIM-1150), which is the cost of the assumption that relevance is observable apart from side. Evidence of the kind the impossibility lacks breaks it: in a simulated federated learner, audit by evaluators who hold validation evidence of their own lowered attack success "to about zero" for an attack that mimics dissent (CLAIM-1007; CLAIM-1077). And safeguards against capture "can also restrict the disagreement and revision that self-governance needs" (CLAIM-359).

The step from the four models to the conjecture is interpretive. Each component is demonstrated only in its own model, and the conjecture is a reading over them that has not been adopted. Two things stand between it and an established principle.

The first is an objection it has not answered. "What a system is prevented from rewriting or rejecting can bound what it can correct as well as enable correction" (CLAIM-1185). In the same sensor network, a protected but miscalibrated beacon "drives the network's error to the beacon's own error while keeping it internally consistent": late error 0.360 against a miscalibration of 0.361 (CLAIM-1185). The mechanism is a symmetry: the miscalibration is "unidentifiable from inside the sensor network by a symmetry" (CLAIM-1125). The objection is as firm as the components it answers, a demonstrated result in the same model. Its general form beyond that model, that "an anchor a system can neither rewrite nor reject imports its own errors whole", is a proposal (CLAIM-tmpjdml5), and beside it an independent witness certifies every difference among channels "and never their common mode" (CLAIM-1143). The conjecture owes a concession to this objection, and what form it takes is open.

The second is whether the conditions are separate requirements or aspects of one. Read strictly, the conjecture "predicts separate requirements" (CLAIM-1024): removing any one condition alone should defeat correction while the others hold. Testing that needs one model that hosts all four, which has been proposed and not built (LEAD-220). The nearest design, not run, has "evidence generation, admission and scoring" as "separately lesionable" operations, with admission and scoring arms that repeat the population model of section 6.4 (CASE-186); it hosts three of the operations, not the conjecture's four components. That the conditions are separate requirements is not stated by any of the components (SYNTHESIS-111).

The sentry of section 6.6 might seem to bear on this, and it does not, in either direction. Its result for reinforcement made independent comes from the cell with success feedback and restricted inspection, "50 steps after the switch, in the success-and-restricted cell", which has no floor under inspection, and so does not belong to the protected investigator (CASE-346). Two of its results point the other way: "Under independent evaluation every cell leaves protection, by a median of 17 to 30 steps", restricted cells included; and with a switching hazard of h = 0.002 "the protected investigator ends protection in 99.5 % of runs" (CASE-346). The 87 % at the default rests on a margin: persistence needs κ ≥ 1.48 at b = 0.05, and the default κ is 1.5. Which part of the model counts as which condition is a free choice, and under different choices the case shows the conditions separating, narrowing or neither (LEAD-220; CLAIM-1024). Whether a protected investigator is protected evidence while authority's coupling fails is therefore open, and the single model would have to fix its mapping in advance.

### 6.8 Ignorance transmitted without falsehood

The models so far make an organization ignorant through its own arrangement. A further way is inheritance. "Reproducing an organizational perspective can transmit characteristic limitations on legibility, interpretation, epistemic authority, inquiry and correction without transmitting false propositions" (CLAIM-tmpjlxfy). The consequence is the one that breaks the subtractive picture of section 6.1: "An organization could become substantially better informed while remaining characteristically ignorant" (CLAIM-tmpjlxfy). What the claim adds to the routes of section 6.2 is transmission. Its corollary for memes is that a meme's "agnotological significance is therefore not intrinsic to its propositional content" (CLAIM-tmpgc8vi). And manufactured doubt can work "on the threshold of evidence for action rather than on belief" (CLAIM-tmp9k2la), moving what counts as enough evidence rather than what is believed. These are proposals. The tobacco case the last of them reads from has not been read, and its quoted memo is unverified (CLAIM-tmp9k2la).

A test is designed and not run (LEAD-tmpqucxc). Its expectation, stated before any run: transferring memes between matched populations, with evidence held fixed and its source, medium and implications varied, reproduces characteristic response profiles, blind spots included. Its falsifier is the claim's defeater: transferring the propositions the memes state, without the memes, does as well (CLAIM-tmpjlxfy). It extends a design also not run (CASE-148).

Two ignorances should be kept apart here. One is an objective that admits other parties only through their contribution to it (CLAIM-tmpa61rf); the reading of shareholder value maximization "as a kind of willful ignorance wrt legitimate standing of non-shareholder relationships" is a case of it (CLAIM-tmpbucep). The other is a transmitted character (CLAIM-tmpjlxfy). The first is an exclusion built into what an organization maximizes; the second is a limitation carried by what it reproduces. They are complements, and nothing shows them to be one.

What they share with the models is that the arrangement can produce the evidence that keeps it ignorant. Monitoring "can produce the conformity it then records as evidence of the organization's adequacy" (CLAIM-tmpefeb7). A label can be "confirmed by the response of someone who rejects it" (CASE-142). And an arrangement's "capacity for effective action" can grow "while its capacity to be effectively corrected by others degrades", "so the first is no evidence of the second" (CLAIM-462). Success, on this last point, is no evidence of correctability, which is the organizational form of the sentry's lesson.

### 6.9 Heuristics and biases as candidate manifestations

If organizations can be ignorant through arrangements, minds may be too, and the catalogue of cognitive biases is the obvious place to look. The aim here is to situate familiar biases as candidate manifestations of the mechanisms above, not to claim that they reduce to them. None of what follows has been tested on human judgement. For a single mind, what plays the part of independent scoring is an open question (QUESTION-121). For the operator model its formal half is settled: commitment before the evidence is not by itself independent scoring, and removes capture only under the condition of section 6.4 (CLAIM-340).

The programme has a condition that would let it fail. "Several conventionally distinct cognitive biases can be observable marks of one organizational constraint ... so the catalogue of biases is not a catalogue of mechanisms; the thesis earns standing only if one intervention on such a constraint, measured independently and with the information held fixed, moves several bias measures together" (CLAIM-tmp5atd3). Its rivals are Hilbert's unification of biases as noisy information processing (Hilbert 2012; LIT-tmpngp4x) and resource-rational analysis (THEORY-359). A test is designed (LEAD-tmpr4xwc). Its expectation: an intervention on the shared constraint, measured apart from the biases with the information held fixed, moves several bias measures together, and more than the rivals predict from manipulations of "noise, memory or processing cost alone". Its falsifier: the measures move one at a time, or no more than the rivals predict, or the constraint "can be identified only from the co-variation it is meant to explain". The last clause names a real danger: "a category that speakers or institutions act on can produce the regularities that confirm it, and contexts that share its use are not independent" (CLAIM-402). The design checks the reliability of the bias measures before it reads co-variation.

A second proposal classifies biases by the contrasts they mismatch. Relative to "a separately justified standard", many biases are "spurious sensitivity" or "spurious invariance" (CLAIM-tmpei0cf). Both classes are "the one difference R⁻_P − R⁻_*", and the formalization shows its own limit: a bias that departs from the standard by a constant offset "has Δ_P(g,x) = Δ_*(g,x) for every g in the family, so no response contrast shows it", and "Overconfidence measured as an excess of confidence over accuracy that is the same across the item sets compared is such a bias" (CLAIM-tmpei0cf). Separately, a perspective's character "detects the transformations it is blind to, not the states it merges" (CLAIM-tmpqbaic), a demonstrated result. Set side by side, the two limits say that a profile of transformation contrasts cannot by itself reach biases of level or ignorance at the level of merged states. Whether the classification covers the established biases is untested; a census against the two classes is specified (LEAD-tmpbtms0).

Anchoring is the case with the most human data, and the data constrain the mechanisms. "the shift is established, the accessibility step is inferred and has not been measured" (CLAIM-315). In the comparative-question paradigm, a high or low anchor pulls later estimates toward it, with large effects in five preregistered experiments (THEORY-337). But Harris and colleagues' five preregistered experiments testing the accessibility step found nothing, "with tasks that also missed direct picture priming", so the direct evidence for selective accessibility (Strack and Mussweiler 1997) "is removed, not reversed" (Harris et al. 2019; THEORY-335). Beside insufficient adjustment and selective accessibility, a third mechanism is proposed: nucleation, in which the anchor helps a response organization form that then maintains itself (CLAIM-tmpggu4v). The mechanisms part "on what follows the anchor's removal", and the proposal does not say that ordinary anchoring in a one-shot numerical estimate is nucleation (CLAIM-tmpggu4v). The planned test (LEAD-tmp3nqe0) expects that in a repeated-judgement task with the anchor withdrawn, nucleation predicts persistence, hysteresis or transfer beyond what accessibility, measured in each session, accounts for. Its falsifier is decay with further adjustment in every condition, no path dependence and no transfer, or persistence fully carried by measured accessibility (CLAIM-tmpggu4v). Persistence alone would not mark nucleation (CLAIM-595). The rival's own step is itself unmeasured (QUESTION-323), so the test would run against an account whose mechanism is open.

A third proposal concerns heuristics of a different level. "Beside heuristics that select what to do there are governance heuristics, learned priors over which perspective or procedure determines what to do; intuitive expertise and entrenched bias can then be one learned allocation of authority" (CLAIM-tmpn754o). It builds on the distinction between stored work and its amortization, whose guard holds that persistence alone does not demonstrate amortization (CLAIM-443). A governance heuristic is told from an action heuristic by a prediction: after "clear feedback in one" task, "the shift toward deliberation generalizes across the tasks that share the cue, including those where intuition is still right" (CLAIM-tmpn754o). A design needing human data is specified (LEAD-tmpotdfw). Such a heuristic can be read as "a compiled contract" (CLAIM-tmpysg95). The sentry's identification limit does not carry over to this test. There, an allocation learned on success follows a habit's law only because the protecting posture masks the outcome that would judge it: "Under protection the outcome is the same in both states" (CASE-346). The test gives clear feedback, so the limit bears only on tasks whose governing procedure masks its own outcome; and the sentry has one task and no channel for deliberation, so it says nothing of cue-generalization (CLAIM-tmpn754o).

### 6.10 Discussion: what the argument does not establish

The argument's firm part is a set of negative results, each in its own model. Selection correction does not correct self-scoring, and it needs known, positive propensities (CASE-162; CLAIM-466). Removing the penalty on newly recognized violations does not by itself bring inquiry back (CASE-396). Scorecards built on the original or the organization's own specification rank excision above revision, and that "follows from how the scorecards are built" (CASE-397). A habit of matched memory reproduces every intervention the sentry model can receive (CASE-346; CLAIM-924). Protection that enables correction can import its anchor's error (CLAIM-1185). Without evidence that does not come from the proposals, dissent and attack cannot be told apart by any rule that treats members alike, and evidence held task by task breaks the impossibility (CLAIM-1194; CLAIM-1007). A character misses merged states, and contrast signatures miss biases of level (CLAIM-tmpqbaic; CLAIM-tmpei0cf). In the confirmation toy, a threshold needs decay (CLAIM-tmpu5cap). And the formation route's carrier half rests on nothing built (ARG-tmp2kh2e). Gathered, these are four non-equivalences: correcting selection is not correcting scoring; a clean internal record is not compliance; a corrected belief is not a corrected practice; a protected anchor is not a correct one.

What the argument does not establish is larger. First, it establishes nothing about any institution or mind. Every executed study is a model with stipulated or invented parameters, and the carry from model to organization goes through interpretive distinctions (CLAIM-404; CLAIM-703; CLAIM-348) that are argued, not observed. The population model's figures, moreover, are read from stored results and have not been regenerated (CASE-162).

Second, the theory of organized ignorance it points toward is a conjecture. The unifying principle of section 6.7 (CLAIM-1024), the governance account of persistent protection (CLAIM-446), the two routes of ignorance without censorship (CLAIM-657), the formation route (CLAIM-tmpp0ufw) and all the bias proposals of section 6.9 are proposals. What is demonstrated are the negative results and the identifiability limits: that certain repairs do not reach certain failures, and that certain mechanisms cannot be told apart by certain interventions.

Third, several steps the argument would like are open. No run joins learned excision to self-certification (LEAD-tmpv3s18). No single model tests whether the conditions of correction are separate (LEAD-220). The carrier half of the formation route has a design and no build (LEAD-tmpuxizu). The remedy for self-scoring is untested in the model where the problem is shown (CLAIM-466). And no bias proposal has been tested on human judgement; the readings the argument would need, among them Hilbert's synthesis, are largely unread.

The strongest objection is the one section 6.7 left standing, and it generalizes. The argument's recurrent remedy is evidence or evaluation that the system cannot rewrite: an independent count (CASE-396), independent reinforcement (CASE-346), a protected beacon (CLAIM-1183), evaluators with their own validation evidence (CLAIM-1007). The objection holds that in each case the remedy works because the protected element is right, which is the general form proposed in CLAIM-tmpjdml5, and that when it is wrong, the same protection that made correction possible makes the error permanent and invisible from inside: the network converges on the beacon's miscalibration and stays internally consistent (CLAIM-1185), by a symmetry no internal test can break (CLAIM-1125), and an independent witness certifies everything except the common mode (CLAIM-1143). The objection does not say protected evidence is useless. It says the argument has relocated the problem rather than solved it: an organization escapes the ignorance of its own arrangement by accepting the ignorance of its anchor. A reply would have to say what keeps the anchor itself correctable without returning it to the system's control, and the components hold no such reply. Safeguards against capture can also restrict the disagreement self-governance needs (CLAIM-359), so the reply cannot simply add more protection.

A second objection targets the bias programme: that the shared constraint will be identifiable only from the co-variation it is meant to explain, and that the categories under which biases are measured help produce the regularities that confirm them (CLAIM-402). The programme concedes this as its falsifier and builds a reliability check into its design (CLAIM-tmp5atd3; LEAD-tmpr4xwc). Whether the concession is enough waits on the test.

### 6.11 Conclusion

Ignorance in an organization need not be a missing fact. It can be an arrangement: of what evidence arrives, by what standard it is scored, who acts on the score, and what the organization can rewrite. The arrangements come apart in models built to separate them. Selection and self-scoring have different remedies, and the correction of one leaves the other in place. Inquiry that only reveals what is owed is learned away under a score that penalizes what it reveals, and lifting the penalty does not bring it back. A specification that never names a harm certifies the organization clean while the harm runs. A protective practice can survive the correction of its belief, and in the one model that shows it, only an intervention on how outcomes are scored tells governance from habit. Across these, what corrects is evidence or evaluation the system cannot rewrite, which suggests a principle of coupling that the article has stated as a conjecture, because a demonstrated objection stands against it: in the sensor model the anchor that makes correction possible fixes its own error in place, and the general form of that objection is proposed.

The article's result is therefore a set of distinctions with their limits, and a programme. The distinctions say which repair reaches which failure, and the negative results say where repairs that look adequate are not. The programme, carrying these mechanisms to transmitted ignorance and to the biases of human judgement, has its tests designed and none run. What would advance it most is the single model that tests whether the conditions of correction are separate, the run that joins learned excision to self-certification, and the first human data that measures authority rather than belief.
