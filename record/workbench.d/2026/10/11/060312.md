**What the assistant contributed in chat, and the owner accepted.**
- **Agree on factorization, with a guard.** Relative to a level and a
  target, but not arbitrary: a factorization can be wrong given its target,
  or the permissiveness objection returns.
- **Will-organization, not operative will.** Agents identified by operative
  will would flicker with the drive profile. The microbiome would become an
  agent whenever it drove hunger. A will-organization is maintained, so an
  agent is its *jurisdiction*: the regimes whose efficacy it governs
  ([TERM-047](../../record/terms.d/TERM-047.md)).
- **The reversal, named.** This inverts the will paper's order of
  explanation. The paper supposed its agent ([CLAIM-080](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-080.md)) to escape [CLAIM-010](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-010.md).
  The objection is filed as [CLAIM-168](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-168.md).

**What was learned.** The strongest support came from where it was not
looked for. Kenton et al. find that the thermostat is not an agent but the
thermostat with its creation process is, and that a training process, not
the trained policy, is the agent. That locates the agent where the governing
process is, even when the governor sits outside the device. That is agent
individuated by jurisdiction, reached independently. Kenton et al. also
state the relativity: decision and utility labels are "frame-dependent". The
open question is now precise: whether the several self-consistent
factorizations the test allows are the level-relativity the owner predicted
or an indeterminacy.
