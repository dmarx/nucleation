In a random network of 10⁴ stochastic spiking neurons with a branching (absorbing-to-active) transition at λ = 1, read out by a leaky integrator of timescale T, the number of ε-discriminable input rates and a fluctuation-aware dynamic range both peak at a subcritical λ* < 1 for every finite T, at different λ* for the two measures, and λ* approaches 1 as T grows: Kinouchi and Copelli's dynamic-range maximum at criticality ([LIT-tmpcc25u](LIT-tmpcc25u.md)) holds only for infinite integration time. # LIT-2980: Finite integration time can shift optimal sensitivity away from criticality

Azizpour, Priesemann, Zierenberg & Levina (2026). Communications Physics (2026); arXiv 2602.09491.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A cites it at A15 for the finding that finite integration time moves optimal sensitivity off the critical point. It bears on the exchange's qualification of the claim that criticality is optimal for computation. Read on 2026-11-22 ([NOTE-tmporpz2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmporpz2.md)) and filed as [THEORY-tmpye6xs](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpye6xs.md). A15's characterization is accurate, with three qualifications: the shift is to the subcritical side and shrinks as the window grows; the transition is branching criticality, not the edge of chaos; and "sensitivity" is input-rate discrimination and dynamic range, not computation in general. It grounds [CLAIM-809](../claims.d/CLAIM-809.md)'s window- and task-dependence for one transition and one task family ([ARG-tmp5sgtm](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp5sgtm.md)), contrasts with [CLAIM-814](../claims.d/CLAIM-814.md), and qualifies the dynamic-range assumption [NOTE-054](../notes.d/NOTE-054.md) records. Kinouchi and Copelli ([LIT-tmpcc25u](LIT-tmpcc25u.md)) and Toyoizumi and Abbott ([LIT-tmpfn6b8](LIT-tmpfn6b8.md)) are registered from its references.
