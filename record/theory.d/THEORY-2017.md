# THEORY-2017: Carnap (1950) holds that an explication replaces an inexact concept by an exact one and is judged as more or less satisfactory, not right or wrong, after the concept to be explicated has been made practically clear by examples of uses intended and not intended; an adequate explicatum is similar enough to it (considerable differences permitted), exact, fruitful for formulating general statements, and as simple as those three permit

## The account

In *Logical Foundations of Probability*, chapter I, Carnap treats
explication as replacing an inexact explicandum by an exact explicatum.
Since the datum is inexact, a proposed explication is judged as
satisfactory or more satisfactory than another, not as right or wrong.
Before any explicatum is judged, the explicandum is made practically clear
by examples of intended and unintended uses. An adequate explicatum then
meets four requirements to a sufficient degree: similarity (usable in most
cases where the explicandum was used, with considerable differences
permitted), exactness, fruitfulness (useful for many universal
statements) and simplicity (as simple as the other three permit, in
practice a tiebreaker).

## Evidence

Stated as requirements, with the zoologists' replacement of Fish by
Piscis as the worked example of a departure from similarity justified by
fruitfulness. It is a methodological proposal, not a finding.
