# LIT-tmp7qvyd: Précis: Being No One

Thomas Metzinger (2005), "Précis: Being No One", *PSYCHE: An
Interdisciplinary Journal of Research on Consciousness* 11(5), June 2005, 31
pp. A précis of Metzinger, *Being No One* (MIT Press, 2003) —
<https://journalpsyche.org/files/0xaad5.pdf>

**On the citation.** The brief's details are right: PSYCHE 11(5), 2005, open
access. The journal assigns no DOI.

## Key takeaways

- **There are no selves, only self-models that cannot be seen as models.**
  "No such things as selves exist in the world." What we call the self is "the
  content of a self-model that cannot be recognized as a model by the system
  using it". A system that runs a transparent self-model "will by necessity"
  experience itself as a self, if the other conditions for experience hold.
  The précis's phenomenological prediction follows from this: "Were the PSM to
  become opaque in its entirety, the phenomenal property of selfhood would
  disappear."
- **Transparency is graded and attentional.** Transparency holds "if earlier
  processing stages are unavailable for attentional processing", and "the
  degree of phenomenal transparency is inversely proportional to the
  introspective degree of attentional availability of earlier processing
  stages". The bodily self-model is fully transparent, reasoning is opaque,
  and emotions such as trust move between the two. The opaque partition lets
  humans conceive that their own self-representation "might actually be a
  self-simulation".
- **Phenomenal unity is a functional cluster.** The globality constraint makes
  every conscious content part of one world-model. On the functional level it
  is "highest-order binding" into a single causal role. On the neural level
  it is "one single area of maximal causal density", in the spirit of Tononi
  and Edelman's dynamic core. "What you subjectively experience upon
  experiencing your world as coherent is the high internal correlation
  strength between a subset of physical events in your own brain."
- **A first-person perspective is a model of a relation.** The PMIR depicts
  the system as a whole directed at an object. A second-order PMIR aimed at a
  first-order one gives introspective intelligence, including awareness that
  one is an agent. One aimed at another agent's PMIR gives social
  intelligence, up to mutual acknowledgement as persons.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the read substitute for *Being
No One* ([LIT-467](LIT-467.md)). The book has no lawful full text and stays Deferred. This
précis **extends** it in the LIT scheme's sense: it is the author's sketch of
the book's three targets (consciousness, the phenomenal self, the first-person
perspective) and cites the book's sections throughout. It does not follow the
book's structure. By its own account it leaves out the analysis of qualia, the
conceptual foundations, every neurophenomenological case study and the
methodological remarks.

**Why this and not only "Self models".** The record already reads the theory
in Metzinger's encyclopedia article ([LIT-450](LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)). The précis is the
better source for three things the article only names. It sets out the
constraint machinery, six of the book's ten constraints, each on the
phenomenological, representational, functional and neural levels. It argues
that transparency is graded rather than all-or-nothing. And it gives the PMIR
and its iteration. It is also where the theory says most about the unity of
experience, so it carries `phenomenal-unity`, which [LIT-450](LIT-450.md) does not.

Where it bears on the record:

- **The minimal self.** Gallagher ([LIT-453](LIT-453.md)) splits the minimal self into a
  sense of ownership and a sense of agency. The précis treats "mineness",
  which it also calls the "sense of ownership", as the higher-order property
  that binds self-model contents into one entity. It says a self-model is
  where "the foundations for agency and autonomy are laid", because the
  organism's movements become "globally available as one's own movements".
  `agency` is carried for that claim and for the PMIR's volitional case.
- **The narrative self.** Dennett's self is a centre of narrative gravity
  ([LIT-442](LIT-442.md)). The précis's self is pre-reflexive and bodily, with "the
  invariance of bodily self-awareness and autobiographical memory" producing
  the experience of an enduring self. Both deny a substantial self. Metzinger's
  also attaches a phenomenological prediction to the denial (see the NOTE,
  C2).
- **The self as context in ACT and RFT** ([THEORY-044](../theory.d/THEORY-044.md)). That account says the
  self as perspective "cannot itself be found as content". The précis says
  the opposite thing in a nearby place. What is experienced as the self is
  content, a transparent self-model, and the perspective is itself a model,
  the PMIR. The two disagree about whether the perspective is represented.
  The NOTE says what a comparison would need.
- **Felt affect** ([THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)). The précis anchors the self-model in
  "a continuous internal source of input": the vestibular organ, the body
  schema, visceral self-representation and brain-stem nuclei that regulate
  "the internal milieu", citing Damasio. It likens the transparent PSM to
  Damasio's "core self".

No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.
