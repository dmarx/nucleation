# LIT-tmptqncy: Having to be: The possessive constitution of organization

Bencherki, N., & Cooren, F. (2011). Having to be: The possessive
constitution of organization. *Human Relations*, 64(12), 1579–1607.
doi:10.1177/0018726711424227.

## Key takeaways

*From the abstract, not a reading.*

- The question is how an organization acts: by itself, or through
  members who act on its behalf.
- Using Greimas's narratology "to make apparent in talk some of process
  philosophy's tenets", it argues that organizations act "by being
  attributed actions".
- Through "the imbrication of mandates and programs of action in a
  logic of appropriation/attribution, the organization can effectively
  act while always relying on others to do so". No essentialist
  ontology of organization is needed for it to act "itself".
- The empirical grounding is the study of meetings in a community
  organization.

## Why this is here

Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy (LIT-tmp3mudg) use it three ways. It supplies
the phrase that defines what they reject: most organizational
scholarship on agency focuses on "who or what acts" (p. 1350, citing p.
1581, "emphasis original"). It is the line of research showing that
practices of attribution move agency "from the individual to the
collective" (p. 1358). And it is named among the approaches for which
"zooming in on agents is necessary in order to move from the individual
to organizational/collective action", Cooren and Fairhurst's "scaling
up" (p. 1364).

None of this could be checked against the article. Two things are worth
checking when a copy comes. Whether p. 1581 frames "who or what acts" as
the question it answers or as the question it moves past. And whether
"possessive constitution" is an entitative account: the abstract's own
terms, attribution and appropriation in talk, and Bencherki's thesis's
grounding in the philosophy of individuation (Simondon), sit closer to
the parent paper's process view than the parent's p. 1364 grouping
suggests. Bencherki is the parent paper's first author, so the parent is
in part revising his own earlier position.
