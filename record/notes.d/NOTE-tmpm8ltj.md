# NOTE-tmpm8ltj: Reckless disregard for the truth coming from cops, doctors, and scientists

## Contribution

The post extends an earlier discussion of scientists writing false things without regarding them as lies. It sets side by side three professional settings: research papers whose titles or abstracts overstate their findings, a medical professor's paid testimony for the tobacco industry, and DHS statements accusing officials of "assault" or "lunging" that press accounts say video contradicts. Gelman adopts a commenter's term, "reckless disregard for the truth". He argues the motive is to strengthen a weak case, that the mechanism is a channel that propagates the claim (journals, courts, media), and that the pattern persists because it is accepted and goes unsanctioned.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 Scientists: the examples are a violent-video-game paper titled "long-term" that ran for 3 days, and the power-pose abstract claiming people "instantly become more powerful" without measuring power.
- §2 Doctors: Kenneth Ludmerer's 2002 testimony that he had "no opinion" on whether smoking contributes to lung cancer, given as a paid consultant for the tobacco industry.
- Interlude: the post explains why the term was chosen over "bullshit". The misstatements seem deliberate, and they are "reckless" because readers may take titles, abstracts and testimony at face value.
- §3 Cops: it quotes Ezra Klein, Philip Bump and Sam Adams on DHS saying Brad Lander was "assaulting law enforcement" and Sen. Alex Padilla "lunging", and on Bump's inability to source ICE's "413 percent increase in assaults" figure.
- §4a-c: the motive is a stronger case, the mechanism is journals, courts and media repeating the claim, and it is "standard operating procedure" because such phrases work as terms of art and critics are outsiders.

## Open questions

- For an ML anthology, §1 and §4 are the relevant parts. Overclaiming in titles and abstracts, and peer review that lets it through, is a real hazard for literature curation.
- The §3 claims rely on quoted press accounts. The underlying videos and DHS statements were not checked here.
- The original blog's comment thread, which the post itself draws on for its terminology, may add counterpoints.
