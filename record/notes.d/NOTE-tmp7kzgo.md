# NOTE-tmp7kzgo: Nowak et al. — Functional Synchronization

## Contribution

The paper proposes one formal scenario for psychology at every level: the
brain, motor action, the mind, the dyad and the group. Each function is
carried out by a functional unit: a set of lower-level elements that
synchronize for as long as a task needs them, then disassemble and may
regroup differently. It adds no new evidence. What it adds is a
vocabulary: three routes by which units assemble, two ways a unit becomes
an element one level up, and a proposal to measure units as networks of
synchronized pairs ("functional links").

## Key insight

A function is a temporary configuration, not a standing structure. The
same elements are reassembled, task by task, into different synchronized
units, and the pattern of synchronization, not the membership alone, is
what does the work. "Synchronization" is meant broadly: "binding of
dynamics … does not necessarily involve performing the same action at the
same time, but rather may involve compensatory dynamics", as in a band
where "each member plays a different instrument".

## Assumptions

- **Two readings of synchronization (p. 2).** At the system level it is
  "coordination in time among the states or dynamics of the elements". At
  the level of elements it is "mutual influence, with consistent signals
  arriving at an element from other elements".
- **Levels share one formal scenario.** Neurons, muscle movements,
  thoughts and feelings, and individuals are treated as interchangeable
  "lower-level elements". The analogy is asserted and illustrated, not
  derived. No model is written down for any level.
- **Function is presupposed.** A unit is identified by the task it
  performs. The paper does not say how to tell a functional unit from a
  set of elements that are merely in step.

## Key results

There are no theorems, simulations or new experiments. The "results" are
the scenario's parts, with the paper's own examples.

- **Three assembly routes (pp. 3–4).**
  - *Structural connections*: stable links let mutual influence produce
    synchrony, "even if the links are relatively weak". Each assembly
    strengthens the links, so the same unit tends to recur (a family
    reunites after holidays).
  - *Simultaneous salience*: elements activated together, by attention or
    by "external factors", may then synchronize. Sensory input activates
    assemblies; "if a few people stand out as the most active and
    expressive in a large group, they are likely to become coordinated".
    A positive loop is claimed: "coherent elements influence one another
    more strongly and elements that influence one another become
    increasingly synchronized".
  - *Mutual constraint*: each element's state limits the states others
    can take (multiple constraint satisfaction; affordances; footballers
    blocking opponents).
- **Assembly and disassembly mirror each other (p. 4).** Losing
  synchrony weakens momentary influence and the unit "disintegrates". If
  the elements are bound by structure, the same unit re-forms; if by
  "fast-changing bindings of dynamics", a disruption can yield "a vastly
  different functional unit".
- **Groups (pp. 8–9).** "It is not necessary for the entire group to be
  synchronized"; subsets synchronize for micro-tasks and then
  desynchronize. Emotional contagion is named as one manifestation of
  group-level synchronization. The summary of the group section: individuals
  "become synchronized, either through their mutual influence or through
  their common response to an external signal (e.g., a leader, a
  perceived threat or an opportunity)". Both count as the emergence of a
  functional unit. Momentary coherence "might be induced, for example, by
  some external signal such as music or highly salient events".
- **Two transitions between levels (p. 10).**
  - A synchronized ensemble becomes a single unit, which is then an
    element one level up (a team inside a work group). This unit "can be
    decomposed into its component lower-order units".
  - A pattern of synchronization, described by an order parameter
    (Haken), becomes the higher-level element: "it is the type of
    synchronization rather than the particular subset of elements that
    gives rise to the higher-level unit".
- **Downward influence (p. 10).** Social interaction induces thoughts and
  attention that can induce neural synchrony. The dyad is "an emergent
  whole" with "properties that cannot be reduced to minds of the
  interacting individuals" (citing Fusaroli et al. 2014), and it
  influences its members in turn.
- **Measurement (pp. 10–11).** Treat each synchronized pair as a
  functional link over a window set by the system (milliseconds for
  neurons, minutes for discussion). Analyse the resulting network's
  density, diameter and path length, and its change over time. This has
  been done for neural systems only. For minds and groups it is offered
  as a future direction.

## Concepts

- **Functional unit.** "A coherent structure that functions to accomplish
  tasks", formed by synchronizing lower-level elements, often transient.
- **Synchronization.** "The binding of dynamics (i.e., the dynamics of
  one element is dependent on the dynamics of another element)". It may be
  in-phase, anti-phase or complementary.
- **Functional link.** A pair of elements found synchronized within a
  time window, as against a structural link, which is a standing
  connection.
- **Order parameter.** "A global variable that describes patterns of
  dependency among the elements of a system". Here, a possible
  higher-level element.

## Connections

The scenario draws on Haken's synergetics and on Kelso's coordination
dynamics (the HKB line, [THEORY-069](../theory.d/THEORY-069.md)), Bernstein's synergies, the binding-
by-synchrony hypothesis (Singer and Gray), action identification theory
(Vallacher and Wegner) and Fusaroli et al.'s interpersonal synergy.
Its functional-connectivity proposal is the neural-network practice of
Friston (1994) carried up a level.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-053](../questions.d/QUESTION-053.md).** [LIT-933](../literature.d/LIT-933.md) is registered there as stating "the
  synchronized-convergence option as a theory". That is only half right,
  and it puts the paper on the wrong side.
  - Nowak's synchronization is not convergence. It includes
    "compensatory dynamics" and different roles, which is the opposite of
    [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md)'s "synchronous convergence of individuals' affective
    responses".
  - Its functional unit is a coordinated structure that performs a task.
    That is nearer [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md)'s integration into "one action-oriented
    representation" than to aggregation.
  - It also allows the higher-level element to be an order parameter, a
    pattern no member has, and calls the dyad an emergent whole that is
    not reducible to its members' minds.
  - It does not decide [QUESTION-053](../questions.d/QUESTION-053.md) either way. It never asks whether a
    functional unit is a subject of states, and affect enters only as
    "emotional contagion", one form of group synchronization.
  - Read correctly, it names a third option the question does not list:
    a coordinated configuration that is neither aggregate convergence nor
    a group subject. It offers no case.
- **[CASE-050](../cases.d/CASE-050.md).** The paper says momentary group coherence can be "induced
  … by some external signal such as music or highly salient events",
  which is [CASE-050](../cases.d/CASE-050.md)'s all-hands. It does not say what would separate
  convergence from a coordinated state there.
- **[QUESTION-063](../questions.d/QUESTION-063.md).** The paper is a direct instance of the conflation that
  question names. It counts synchrony "through their mutual influence or
  through their common response to an external signal" as the same
  functional unit. Its proposed measure, pairs found synchronized within a
  time window, would draw a functional link between two members driven by
  one cue and never coupled. So the paper's method cannot answer
  [QUESTION-063](../questions.d/QUESTION-063.md), and [THEORY-129](../theory.d/THEORY-129.md)'s coral, synchronized by a shared light
  regime, would score as a functional unit on it.
- **[CLAIM-172](../claims.d/CLAIM-172.md) and [CLAIM-167](../claims.d/CLAIM-167.md).** The paper's three assembly routes
  (structure, co-salience, mutual constraint) are three kinds of
  coupling. They are not the drive/peer distinction those claims draw.
- No THEORY is filed from it. Its claims are programmatic and rest on
  cited work, and its one sharp contribution, the broad definition of
  synchronization, is a definition, not a finding. No instruction for
  machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- The authors: "the model is in its nascent stage and thus should be
  viewed as a heuristic for research agendas".
- No model equations, no stated criterion for when a set of synchronized
  elements is a functional unit, and no prediction that could fail.
- Cross-level claims rest on analogy. The neural evidence (binding by
  gamma synchrony) is itself contested, and the paper reports it as
  settled.
- The group-level evidence is cited, not shown: emotional contagion,
  groupthink, deindividuation and a cited thesis (Ziembowicz 2015).

## Open questions

- What tells a functional unit from elements that are merely in step,
  including elements in step because one signal drives them all? A
  perturbation test (displace one member and see whether the unit's
  function survives or the others follow) would supply it.
- When the higher-level element is "the type of synchronization", what is
  it a state of? This is [QUESTION-053](../questions.d/QUESTION-053.md)'s question, and the paper raises it
  without answering it.
