# ADR-044: A `corroborates` relation between the record's own claims, for evidence one claim gives another without the second depending on it, so that a supporting claim's retirement lowers the other's credence without breaking its argument

## Context

A claim filed while porting the LEGO material ([CLAIM-310](../claims.d/CLAIM-310.md)) shows that changing
only how a collection is arranged changes the cost of retrieving from it.
That is evidence for [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md): what a system can deploy within a deadline
depends on its arrangement, not only on what it holds. It also meets
[CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md)'s defeat condition head-on, which asks for "a case in which a
change only to the arrangement of the system leaves every such capability
unchanged where this claim says it should move."

The record had three ways to say this, and each says something else.

- **`supports` / `rests_on`.** `supports` on a claim is the converse of
  `rests_on` (and of `grounds`). `rests_on` is "another of the record's
  own claims this one could not stand without — the spine of the
  argument, walked as the `argument` chain. Retired, it leaves this claim
  needing another route" ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)). Filing `supports: CLAIM-296` made
  `luria link --fix` write `rests_on: [the new claim]` onto [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md). That
  is false. [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md) was argued before the new claim existed and does not
  need it. Retiring the new claim would wrongly report [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md) as
  needing another route, and the `argument` chain would draw an edge that
  is not in the argument. The relation was removed before the merge.
- **`complements`.** "Answers a different part of the same question, so
  the two belong together rather than competing." It is symmetric. Here
  the two claims answer the same part (does arrangement change
  capability?), and one bears evidence for the other, so the relation is
  directed.
- **Prose.** It is what was done, and it is invisible to the views. Nothing
  shows a reader of [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md) that a later claim bears on it, and nothing
  tells a reader of the later claim that retiring it costs [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md)
  something.

Cases already have the distinction for themselves ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)). `supports`
(via the claim's `grounds`) is load-bearing, and `illustrates` is "a claim
this case makes vivid without bearing its weight". Claims have no
counterpart of `illustrates` for each other.

## Decision

- **A new reference field on CLAIM, `corroborates`**, scheme CLAIM, many,
  with converse `corroborated_by` written by `luria link --fix`. Its blurb:
  "a claim of the record's own that this one gives independent evidence
  for, without that claim depending on it: if this one is retired, the
  other loses support, not its footing."
- **Not part of the argument.** It is not added to the `argument` chain's
  relations, and nothing that walks `rests_on` (retraction, the chain
  views, the report of steps crossing lines) follows it. A view can list
  "corroborated by" on a claim's page, as `illustrated_by` is listed now.
- **Directed and claim-to-claim only.** Evidence from outside the record's
  argument is already `grounds` (THEORY, CASE, LIT). A case that makes a
  claim vivid is already `illustrates`.
- **The test for which relation to use.** If the target claim's argument,
  as written, uses the source claim as a step, it is `rests_on`. If the
  target stands on its own argument and the source adds evidence or
  removes a defeater, it is `corroborates`. If they answer different parts
  of one question, it is `complements`.
- **Pilot.** Accepted by the owner on 2026-10-15 ("accept ADR-044 and
  apply it"). It was applied first to the claims that prompted it: [CLAIM-310](../claims.d/CLAIM-310.md)
  corroborates [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md). [CLAIM-306](../claims.d/CLAIM-306.md), whose reading of "If I can't find it, it
  isn't there" bears on [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md)'s case, keeps `complements` until the
  pilot says which fits. Nothing else is retagged until the pilot has run
  on at least one more batch of filing.

## Alternatives considered

- **Overload `complements`.** It is free, and wrong in two ways: it is
  symmetric, and the record already uses it for claims that do not bear on
  each other's truth at all. A reader could not tell "these go together"
  from "this one is evidence for that one".
- **`supports` with a qualifier** (for example `supports: {code, weight:
  weak}`). It keeps one relation and makes every consumer of `supports`
  check the qualifier. The retraction walk is exactly what must not
  follow this edge, so it should not be the same edge.
- **`instance_of` / `instantiated_by`.** This is more precise for the
  pilot: the new claim is a special case of [CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md)'s relation (resource,
  arrangement, deadline) for one resource and one kind of arrangement. But
  a claim can corroborate another without being an instance of it, for
  example by removing a defeater or by agreeing from a different line. A
  special case is one kind of corroboration, and can be a later
  refinement if the pilot finds it common.
- **Status quo, prose only.** It costs nothing, and the views and the lint
  stay blind to a relation the record already relies on. Each time one
  claim is retired, a reader has to find by search which others lose
  evidence.

## Consequences

- One new pair of fields in `luria.yaml` under CLAIM's references. No luria
  change is needed: a declared reference with a `converse` is already
  written back by `luria link --fix`, as `complements` and `illustrated_by`
  are.
- A new judgement at filing time, between `rests_on`, `corroborates` and
  `complements`. The test above is meant to make it quick. A misfiled
  `rests_on` costs more than a misfiled `corroborates`, since only the
  first is walked.
- Credence still has to be revised by hand. The relation tells a reader
  that retiring a claim weakens another, and by how much is the reader's
  call, as with `illustrates`.
- If accepted, a follow-up: sweep the claims whose prose says one claim
  "supports" or "bears on" another without depending on it, and decide
  each. The sweep is not part of this decision.
