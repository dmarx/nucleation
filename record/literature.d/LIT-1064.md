# LIT-1064: Explaining the enigmatic anchoring effect: Mechanisms of selective accessibility

Strack, F. and Mussweiler, T. (1997). Explaining the enigmatic anchoring effect: Mechanisms of selective accessibility. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 73(3), 437–446. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.73.3.437

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-877](../notes.d/NOTE-877.md)). In three small between- and within-subjects studies (N = 32, 32 and 67), the anchor's effect on a later estimate depended on what the comparison task brought to mind, not on the number alone. With the anchor number held fixed, the effect was large when the estimate was on the dimension compared (Study 1). It reversed to contrast when the estimate was about a very different object (Study 2). After plausible anchors, the comparison took longer and the estimate came faster (Study 3). Accessibility is inferred from these moderations and from latencies. No study measures what knowledge was retrieved. The paper also keeps an adjustment-like route for implausible anchors, which anchored at least as strongly.

## Why this is here

A20 named the selective-accessibility account as the established form of what the exchange was calling retrieval-mediated anchoring: "we wouldn't be discovering retrieval-mediated anchoring from scratch." It is the psychological ground of [CLAIM-298](../claims.d/CLAIM-298.md) and the first reading for [QUESTION-109](../questions.d/QUESTION-109.md).
