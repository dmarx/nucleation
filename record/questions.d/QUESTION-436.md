# QUESTION-436: Does an independent threshold-regression test of unique against multiple equilibria, on the Japanese bombing data or another large temporary shock to cities, find a preferred multiple-equilibria model whose intercepts satisfy the ordering that larger negative shocks lead to lower equilibria?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1503](../theory.d/THEORY-1503.md) reports what Davis and Weinstein's working paper ([LIT-2201](../literature.d/LIT-2201.md), read in [NOTE-1690](../notes.d/NOTE-1690.md)) finds: on the
Allied bombing of Japanese cities, the Schwarz criterion prefers a single equilibrium for aggregate
manufacturing and for pooled city-industry output, and for population slightly prefers two equilibria whose
fit fails the intercept-ordering criterion. That is one test, by its authors, on one shock, under their own
restrictions: each city's dynamics depend on its own share alone, thresholds are common in log units, full
reversion is imposed in the threshold runs, and thresholds are searched in 5% bins. Whether the verdict
survives an independent re-run, other restrictions or another shock is open.

## What would answer it

An independent threshold test, on the same Japanese data or on another large, temporary, exogenous shock to
cities, that reports the Schwarz comparison and the intercept ordering. A preferred multiple-equilibria
model with ordered intercepts would answer it yes and refute the reading's generality; a replication of the
unique-equilibrium preference would answer it no for that setting. A test that relaxes own-size dynamics or
common thresholds bears on it more strongly than a re-run under the same restrictions.
