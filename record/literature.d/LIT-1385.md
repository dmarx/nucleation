# LIT-1385: Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Event structure or updating disruption?

Pettijohn, K. A., & Radvansky, G. A. (2016). Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Event structure or updating disruption? *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 69(11), 2119–2129. doi:10.1080/17470218.2015.1101478.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1081](../notes.d/NOTE-1081.md). Pages are the journal's.*

- **Not time, not a passing disruption.** Travel times were not longer
  through doorways, and a 3-second pause before the probe left the effect
  as it was.
- **On the carried object only**, in both experiments: "this is largely
  confined to the associated probes" (p. 2123).
- **Competition, not dropout.** Deactivation of the prior event model fits
  the object set down; "the result of central interest here is the effect
  of a shift in the associated condition, which lends itself to a
  competition-based account" (p. 2126).

## Why this is here

A37 cited it for the interference account: the effect "wasn't adequately explained by travel time or a brief transient processing disruption". The case is [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** The paper argues for interference
between event models and against the account in which a prior event model
has dropped out of working memory. The latter is closer to the activation
interruption that [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md) illustrates with the doorway effect. See
[NOTE-1081](../notes.d/NOTE-1081.md).
