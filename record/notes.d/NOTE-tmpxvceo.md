# NOTE-tmpxvceo: Fischer — The Frankfurt Cases: The Moral of the Stories

## Contribution

It restates Frankfurt-style compatibilism after the Dilemma Defense and
answers it on both horns. The new part is the deterministic horn: an
earlier argument of Fischer's assumed that Black's device by itself
removed Jones's alternatives, which the critics showed false, and here he
gives a case in which determinism and the device remove them only
together, so the device is not idle. It also states plainly what the
cases are for: a conditional, not compatibilism.

## Key insight

"If causal determinism rules out moral responsibility, it is not in
virtue of eliminating alternative possibilities" (p. 318). The cases
isolate alternatives as irrelevant; they leave every other route from
determinism to non-responsibility open, sourcehood among them.

## Assumptions

- **Robustness.** Only alternatives "sufficiently robust to ground
  attributions of moral responsibility" matter (pp. 321–322); a flicker,
  such as an involuntary sign, does not (p. 326).
- **Agnosticism, then its relaxation.** Section IV.1 assumes determinism
  but takes "no stand about whether causal determinism eliminates genuine
  metaphysical access to alternative possibilities" (p. 325), the stance of
  semicompatibilism. Section IV.2 drops it.
- **The necessary-condition conditional.** "If Jones were to choose at t2
  to vote for McCain, he would have (say) furrowed his right brow by t1"
  must be true in the case (p. 326).
- **Irrelevance is judged by intuition.** That Black's device is
  irrelevant to Jones's responsibility is taken as clear (p. 330).

## Key results

- **The case (p. 316).** Black's chip would make Jones decide for Obama if
  he showed any inclination otherwise; Jones decides for Obama on his own.
- **The Dilemma Defense (pp. 319–321).** Kane, Widerker, Ginet, Wyma, Goetz,
  Pereboom and Palmer (n. 8). Indeterministic horn: the prior sign cannot
  guarantee the choice, so an alternative remains. Deterministic horn:
  assuming determinism begs the question, and, with Goetz, it is
  determinism, not Black, that removes the alternative, so Black "drops
  out". Goetz is quoted: "if Jones is not free to choose otherwise, it is
  because of the occurrence of causal determinism in the actual sequence of
  events and not because of Black's device in the alternative sequence"
  (Goetz 2005, 85).
- **Indeterministic horn (pp. 321–323).** Buffer cases (Hunt; Pereboom,
  *Living without Free Will*, "esp. 18–28") set a necessary condition for
  choosing otherwise, such as having a certain thought by a certain time,
  that is not itself robust enough to ground responsibility, and let
  Black's device trigger on it. Footnote 15 describes Pereboom's "Tax
  Evasion": "the necessary condition for not deciding to evade taxes, that
  is, the specified level of attentiveness to the moral reasons", is one
  whose absence "at any specific time is compatible with both Joe's
  deciding to evade taxes and with his not deciding to evade taxes". "The
  jury is still out" on this horn, but it is not enough to point to some
  residual alternative (p. 323).
- **The earlier argument fails (pp. 324–325).** Fischer's previous premise,
  that Black's device in itself and apart from determinism removes
  alternatives, is false. "I accept the criticism."
- **The new argument (pp. 325–329).** (1) Assume determinism and the case.
  (2) Determinism is not assumed in itself to remove alternatives, nor is
  Black. (3) Determinism plus Black removes Jones's freedom at t2 to choose
  otherwise. (4) If Jones is not responsible, it is not because he was not
  free to choose otherwise. (5) "If causal determinism rules out Jones's
  moral responsibility for his choice at t2, it is not in virtue of its
  eliminating alternative possibilities (if in fact it does eliminate
  alternative possibilities)" (p. 328). With Black, the Republican sign
  would trigger intervention; without him nothing rules out the
  alternative (p. 329).
- **From 3 to 4 (pp. 330–331).** Claim C: that determinism and Black
  together remove Jones's alternatives is irrelevant to his
  responsibility, though determinism itself may be relevant. The analogy:
  two medicines needed together both cure a disease (through substance S)
  and raise the temperature, which does nothing for the cure. "It may be
  that causal determination also issues in lack of 'sourcehood,' in a sense
  relevant to moral responsibility" (p. 331).
- **A buffer case, or a deterministic one? (pp. 326–327).** Fischer grants
  his case may be a special case of the buffer strategy and may not need
  determinism (a point he credits to Pereboom, n. 19). A deterministic
  version is useful because some hold responsibility requires determinism,
  and because the necessary-condition conditionals are easier to defend
  under determinism. Note 20: a case with determinism in the actual
  sequence sidesteps Goetz's "drops out", and "nothing that Pereboom or Hunt
  say about the Buffer Cases can explain why this is so (I thank Derk
  Pereboom for this generous point)".
- **Without agnosticism (pp. 331–333).** Granting that determinism removes
  alternatives, the same argument runs, since nothing in it uses the
  incompatibilist assumption, and one explanation of Jones's inability
  need not crowd out another, as mechanistic and belief–desire
  explanations coexist (Dennett). Haji and McKenna's restriction of the
  cases to uncommitted audiences is "a gratuitous concession".
- **Widerker (pp. 333–335).** Widerker's IRR: "There may be circumstances in
  which a person performs some action which although they make it
  impossible for him to avoid performing that action, they in no way bring
  it about that he performs it" (Widerker 1995, 248), on which Frankfurt's
  case "depends crucially" (248–49). His dilemma: either the truth of the
  prior-sign conditional "is grounded in some fact that is causally
  sufficient ... for Jones's decision", and the case is not an IRR case,
  or it is not, and "it is hard to see how Jones's decision is
  unavoidable" (251). Fischer: the case against PAP does not need IRR,
  which would rule out every deterministic version ex ante.
- **Conclusion (pp. 335–336).** The Dilemma Defense is dead: deterministic
  Frankfurt cases need not beg the question and give Black a real role.
  Responsibility is "a matter of how I walk down the path of life, rather
  than selection from among a suite of available options."

## Concepts

- **PAP** — "An agent is morally responsible for performing a given act A
  only if he or she could have done otherwise" (p. 316).
- **Dilemma Defense** — either determinism holds in the case, and the case
  begs the question or Black drops out, or it does not, and Black cannot
  remove every alternative (pp. 319–321).
- **Robust alternative** — one whose availability could plausibly ground
  responsibility; a flicker of freedom is not one (pp. 321–322, 326).
- **Buffer case** — a case with a necessary condition for doing otherwise,
  not itself robust, on which the intervener triggers, and whose absence at
  any one time is not sufficient for the action (n. 15).
- **Semicompatibilism** — "the claim that causal determinism is compatible
  with moral responsibility, quite apart from whether causal determinism
  eliminates access to alternative possibilities" (p. 325).
- **IRR** — Widerker's thesis that circumstances can make an action
  unavoidable without bringing it about (p. 333).

## Limitations

- **The deterministic case rests on intuition** that Black's device is
  irrelevant, and on the necessary-condition conditional, which a
  libertarian may deny under indeterminism, as Fischer says (p. 327).
- **The indeterministic horn is sketched,** and the buffer cases are
  described, not defended.
- **What the paper does not claim:** it does not establish compatibilism,
  and says so (pp. 317–318).
- **Second hand for Pereboom and Widerker.** Their arguments are known here
  through Fischer's descriptions and short quotations.

## Open questions

- Does Pereboom's "Tax Evasion", read first-hand, remove every robust
  alternative without assuming determinism, as Fischer reports?
- Can a leeway incompatibilist hold that the alternative removed in the
  deterministic case is robust after all?
