# LIT-225: Minimum Description Length Induction, Bayesianism, and Kolmogorov Complexity

Paul M. B. Vitányi, Ming Li (1999), *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 46(2):446–464 (Mar 2000); first appeared as arXiv preprint (CWI Tech Report 1998)* — [arXiv:cs/9901014](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9901014)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpp6trb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpp6trb.md).*

- **The Fundamental Inequality (Theorem 3).** For recursive Pr(·|·) and
  prior P, if D is Martin-Löf random for Pr(·|H) and H is random for P,
  then K(D|H) + K(H) − α ≤ −log Pr(D|H) − log P(H) ≤ K(D|H) + K(H), with
  α = K(Pr(·|H)) + K(P). Agreement to O(1) implies both randomness
  conditions.
- **Selection (Theorem 4).** Among admissible hypotheses, those meeting
  both conditions, the ideal-MDL choice has posterior at least 2^{−α}
  times the MAP choice's. With the universal prior every hypothesis is
  random for the prior. The condition is then that the data be random for
  the chosen hypothesis (Theorem 5).
- **The slack is not small in general.** K(Pr(·|H)) ≤ K(H) + O(1), so for
  a complex hypothesis α is of the order of its whole description.
- **Where they diverge.** Data 0ⁿ are atypical for a fair coin.
  If the coin is in fact fair, Bayes with a suitable prior can select it
  while MDL selects p = 0 (Remark 3). With two hypotheses, ideal MDL ties
  where Bayes chooses (Example 3).
- **Finite sets (Theorem 1).** With uniform likelihood 1/d(H), Bayes under
  the universal prior, ideal MDL and Kolmogorov's minimal sufficient
  statistic select the same set, to the O(1) in its definition.
- **Prediction (Theorems 7–8, Corollary 2).** For μ-random sequences,
  M(y | x)/μ(y | x) → 1. The y maximising μ(y | x) is, with probability
  going to one, the one minimising Km(xy) − Km(x). The proof uses Gács's
  result that −log M and Km agree on random strings and differ unboundedly
  in general.
- **What is not shown.** Corollary 1's "with high probability" has no
  stated measure or proof. The claim that Bayes overfits where MDL does
  not is called "some evidence" and is not demonstrated. Practical codes
  are out of scope (§2.6).

## Standing in the record

Roos ([LIT-238](LIT-238.md)) leave Kolmogorov-complexity MDL to Grünwald's 2007 book. It
bears on [THEORY-087](../theory.d/THEORY-087.md) and [CLAIM-154](../claims.d/CLAIM-154.md), which read the Bayesian Occam factor as
a complexity penalty. A modeller's prior matches the description-length
penalty only for hypotheses random under it ([NOTE-tmpp6trb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpp6trb.md), Bearing on
the record).
