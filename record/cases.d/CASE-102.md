# CASE-102: A college program stays good, or bad, at a sport for decades while its whole roster turns over every four or five years

## The case

The owner, at U3: "college sports, where the team composition experiences
extremely fast churn, and yet in spite of this real world ship-of-theseus
situation, teams often maintain standing on similar timescales as
professional teams (if not longer), so we can meaningfully talk about
school's known to be 'good' or 'bad' at a particuular sport on much longer
timescales than a single student's tenure."

Held fixed: the school and the sport. Replaced: every athlete, within a
few years; coaches, administrators and facilities, more slowly.

## What it can show

A3 separated identity without stable constitution (the program is the
same historical program) from dispositional stability without stable
constitution (it keeps winning). The second is the stronger fact: it
needs a mechanism that regenerates the capacity in each new roster, and
A3 named one, a loop from success through prestige and resources to
recruiting and back. "The particular roster is an output of the program
as well as one of its temporary constituents."

It is [CLAIM-280](../claims.d/CLAIM-280.md)'s product and producer again, with the producer more
nearly observable: the roster is replaced on a schedule and the
recruiting, coaching and money that make the next one are not. It also
bears on [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md): a program's capacity may be held by an institution
that selects and trains the players, which is a boundary imposed on the
roster as much as produced by it. Bourrat's ecological scaffold ([LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md),
[NOTE-027](../notes.d/NOTE-027.md), criterion C2) is the same question asked of evolutionary
transitions.

Given its standing, it cannot show that any program's persistence is
caused by its organization rather than by money, conference or
schedule. A3's test: estimate Corr(Y_t, Y_{t+k} | roster overlap ≈ 0)
with resources, schedule strength and league structure controlled, then
repeat across coaching and administrative turnover. A5 §8 sharpened it:
compare a historically dominant program with one that bought an equally
good roster recently, and ask which reconstitutes itself after both
rosters and coaches leave. A5 also named the negative result: once
resources and recruiting are modelled explicitly, the program may add
nothing. A11 §4 added a horizon profile: different variables (roster,
recruiting, alumni, reputation) may keep their predictive information
over different lags ([CLAIM-331](../claims.d/CLAIM-331.md)).
