# THEORY-064: What is experienced as a self is the content of a self-model the system cannot recognise as a model, and nothing beyond such models is a self

## Which word this is about

This account is about the **self**: being a subject, the sense of being
someone. It is not about personhood. Metzinger does say "you become a person
by having the right kind of subpersonal self-model" ([LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), p. 25),
but that is a further step this account does not take. It is not about
phenomenal unity, which in the précis comes first and needs no self-model
([THEORY-063](THEORY-063.md)). It is not about agency, although the précis says a
self-model is where "the foundations for agency and autonomy are laid".

## Source

- Metzinger (2005), [LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), read in full in [NOTE-460](../notes.d/NOTE-460.md). Page
  references are to the PSYCHE PDF.
- Metzinger, "Self models" ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md)), read in [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md): the same theory,
  with the biconditional "a phenomenal self exists iff a system operates
  under a transparent PSM" (C2) and the body-ownership evidence (C6).

## What was actually shown

**The account.** A phenomenal self-model (PSM) is "the content of the
conscious self", "not a thing, but an integrated process" (p. 3). "We are
systems that constantly confuse themselves with the content of their PSM",
and organisms "neither have nor are a self" (p. 3). "If an organism operates
under a transparent self-model, then it possesses a phenomenal self."
Transparency is that "the representational character of the contents of
self-consciousness is not accessible to subjective experience", so selfhood
"is a function realized by a lack of information" (pp. 3–4). On the
representational level, "no such things as selves exist in the world"
(p. 24).

**Transparency is graded.** "The degree of phenomenal transparency is
inversely proportional to the introspective degree of attentional
availability of earlier processing stages" (p. 11). The bodily self-model is
fully transparent, reasoning is opaque, and emotions such as trust move
between the two ([NOTE-460](../notes.d/NOTE-460.md), C3).

**The perspective is modelled.** A first-person perspective is a phenomenal
model of the intentionality relation (PMIR), a model "of the system as
acting and experiencing", co-representing the relation itself (§4). So the
point of view is not outside the model. It is part of what is modelled.

**Why transparent.** Self-modelling is logically an infinite process, and a
transparent model is "an untranscendable object" that breaks the loop. This
is offered as "my hypothesis", the "principle of necessary self-reification"
(pp. 24–25).

**The prediction.** No wholly opaque self-representation is known.
"Were the PSM to become opaque in its entirety, the phenomenal property of
selfhood would disappear" (p. 22).

The précis states the theory and its prediction. Its evidence is cited, and
the case studies that would test it (agnosia, neglect, depersonalization)
are left to the book ([NOTE-460](../notes.d/NOTE-460.md), Limitations).

## What this does not say

- **It does not say there is nothing there.** "We are not things, but
  processes" (p. 26). The denial is of a substantial, indivisible self. A
  process view of the self is not refuted by it, and on that view the
  account is a theory of what the self is, not a denial that there is one.
- **It does not say self-models are rare.** "Any machine can do self-directed
  modeling" (p. 22). What makes a phenomenal self is a conscious,
  transparent one.
- **It does not say the prediction has been tested.** Reports of
  depersonalization meet the précis's own worry about performative
  self-contradiction ([NOTE-460](../notes.d/NOTE-460.md), Open questions).

## Connections

- **The self as context in RFT and ACT ([THEORY-044](THEORY-044.md), [THEORY-051](THEORY-051.md)).** That
  account holds that the self as context is a perspective that "cannot
  itself be found as content". This one holds that what is experienced as a
  self is content, a transparent model, and that the perspective is itself
  modelled. The two may not conflict: what cannot be found as content may
  be what is transparent, the part of the model that cannot be attended to
  as a model. They conflict if RFT means the perspective is not represented
  at all. RFT's sources do not say, so no `rivals` is declared. For the
  clinical claim ([THEORY-051](THEORY-051.md)) the précis supplies a mechanism: making
  thoughts about oneself attentionally available as thoughts is making that
  part of the self-model less transparent, which is what graded transparency
  allows.
- **The Self in IFS ([LIT-579](../literature.d/LIT-579.md), [NOTE-453](../notes.d/NOTE-453.md)).** IFS's Self is innate,
  has qualities (the eight Cs) and leads. On this account, what clients
  report when they say "that's my Self" is content of a self-model, and a
  state with qualities is a state of the model. That is the opposite of
  ACT's contentless perspective and nearer to this account's picture than
  to RFT's. The record holds no study of either.
- **Gallagher, Dennett and Hume ([LIT-453](../literature.d/LIT-453.md), [LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md), [LIT-451](../literature.d/LIT-451.md)).** The précis
  treats Gallagher's sense of ownership, "mineness", as the property that
  binds contents into the self-model. Like Dennett's centre of narrative
  gravity and Hume's bundle, it denies a substantial self. Unlike them, it
  attaches a phenomenological prediction to the denial.
- **Narrative identity ([THEORY-060](THEORY-060.md)).** That self is reflective and
  extended in time. This one is pre-reflective and present, and it
  attributes the experience of an enduring self to "the invariance of
  bodily self-awareness and autobiographical memory" (p. 26). They are
  accounts of different things and do not compete.
- **Felt affect ([THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md)).** The précis anchors the self-model in
  continuous internal input (vestibular, visceral, brain stem) and likens the
  transparent PSM to Damasio's core self. Consistent with that account; no
  evidence for it.
- **The regress ([THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)).** The précis's reason for transparency is that
  it stops a regress of self-modelling with an object that cannot be
  transcended. [THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)'s regress is of authority, not of representation:
  what makes a motive the agent's own. The shapes are alike and the
  questions differ; the précis does not touch ownership of motives.
