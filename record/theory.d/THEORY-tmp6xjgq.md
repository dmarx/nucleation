# THEORY-tmp6xjgq: Contextual emergence, on which an emergent phenomenon's parts are necessary but not sufficient, needs a functional boundary between the phenomenon and its context, without which the context could be folded into the base and the account collapse into weak emergence or global supervenience; and the subject of attention is contextually emergent in this sense, its boundary being the skull, meninges and brain stem, so that its macro-level patterns constrain micro-level activity with a power that is not reducible to the parts and needs neither downward nor circular causation

## Source

Jennings (2026), [LIT-tmpasemk](../literature.d/LIT-tmpasemk.md), pp. 15–21 and 27–29, and §7 (pp. 34–35);
read in [NOTE-tmpby7i2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpby7i2.md).

## What was actually shown

**The condition**, argued on Bénard cells. "Without a functional boundary,
contextual emergence would collapse into either weak emergence or
supervenience" (p. 19), because the heating element and container could be
counted among the parts. "In contextual emergence the context as context is
required to bring about the emergent phenomenon" (p. 19), and "the thermal
and material properties at stake here are macro-level ones, so contextual
emergence is a better fit" (p. 20). Fn. 15 says the paper "stipulates the
importance of a boundary".

**The application.** The skull and meninges "shelter and separate the
electrical activity of the brain from its surrounding environment", the
brain stem passes activity in, and "these elements constrain what can
influence the behavior of the organism, which in turn allows the
macro-level patterns to constrain micro-level ones" (p. 29). The evidence
offered is phase-amplitude coupling and 1/f scaling (pp. 27–28) and ephaptic
coupling, including across a transection (p. 35).

**What could have come out otherwise** is limited. The paper's own
alternative, global supervenience over the parts and their context (p. 19),
is set aside as a worse explanation, not shown false. Weakly emergent
models produce the neural signatures.

## What this does not say

- **That the subject has causal powers in Bishop's sense.** Bishop's 2024 SEP
  entry (THEORY-tmpnkdiy) presents contextual emergence as real constraint by
  larger-scale stability conditions, with convection as the case, and
  contrasts it with causal powers. The constraint is Bishop's; the boundary
  condition and the causal-power reading are Jennings's. (Revised on
  2026-11-03, from a gloss of Bishop and Atmanspacher as stability conditions
  for a description only.)
- **That [CLAIM-334](../claims.d/CLAIM-334.md) is wrong.** It denies its premise; it offers no
  intervention on the macro level that holds the base fixed.
- **That the subject is free.** That further step is the paper's and is not
  part of this reading.

## Note of 2026-11-03: the neural evidence, read

Weaker than the paper states. Szczepanski et al. are correlational
(THEORY-tmp9pem2); phase-amplitude coupling can be a waveform artefact
(Donoghue et al., THEORY-tmp66yzx); 1/f structure is present in every
recording (THEORY-tmpqlync); and ephaptic coupling is circuit physics with
fields applied from outside (THEORY-tmpvy87o).
