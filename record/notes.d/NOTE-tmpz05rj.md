# NOTE-tmpz05rj: Timpe — Source Incompatibilism and Its Alternatives

## Contribution

It argues that the distinction between leeway and source
incompatibilism, which Pereboom, Kane, McKenna and others draw, does not
survive reflection on what each needs. Leeway incompatibilism must appeal
to the agent's being the source of how her alternatives resolve; source
incompatibilism, in its best form, entails that some alternative exists.
It is an argument about which incompatibilism to hold, not for
incompatibilism (p. 145).

## Key insight

Ultimacy and alternatives are tied by indeterminism. If nothing outside
the agent determines her action, more than one future is compossible with
the past and the laws, so she has some alternative, however poor. The
alternative does no work in explaining her responsibility, but its absence
is enough to show she lacks ultimacy (p. 150).

## Assumptions

- **Incompatibilism is assumed throughout.** Causal determinism is taken to
  rule out responsibility; the question is only why.
- **Ultimacy requires that the agent "not be causally determined by
  anything outside of herself"** (p. 150), allowing for self-imposed
  determinism traced to earlier undetermined acts (Ekstrom's "at each
  moment", the tracing condition).
- **Robustness is set aside.** Timpe brackets the debate over how robust an
  alternative must be (p. 148) and argues the entailed alternatives matter
  whatever its outcome (p. 150).
- **Blockage cases are not refuted here.** A full case for wide over narrow
  source incompatibilism would need to argue against them; the paper takes
  the "positive route" instead (p. 148).

## Key results

- **Three claims (pp. 143–145).** The Taxonomy Claim: there are two kinds of
  incompatibilism, leeway and source. Pereboom states it: "We might call
  those incompatibilists who incline towards the view that an alternative
  possibilities condition has the more important role in explaining an
  agent's moral responsibility leeway incompatibilists, and those who are
  predisposed to maintain that an incompatibilist condition on the causal
  history of the action plays the more significant part source
  incompatibilists" (Pereboom 2003, 186). The Refutation Claim:
  Frankfurt-style counterexamples show alternatives are not required, so
  leeway incompatibilism is false (Stump 1996, 88: "Frankfurt-style
  counterexamples are successful against PAP; but ... the libertarian has
  nothing to fear from them"). The Inconsistency Claim: the ultimacy
  source incompatibilism needs is inconsistent with truths about the
  actual world. "Pereboom's *Living Without Free Will* is an exemplar":
  agent-causal libertarianism "involves no detected logical inconsistency.
  But this does not mean that it is in the clear.... The most significant
  empirical objections to agent-causal libertarianism challenge its
  capacity to accommodate our best natural scientific theories" (Pereboom
  2001, 68–69), and reconciling it with physics "involves coincidences so
  wild as to make it incredible" (Pereboom 2001, 85). If all three claims
  hold, responsibility exists only if compatibilism is true.
- **Against naive leeway incompatibilism (pp. 145–147).** In a "Fischer
  scenario", Kelvin either decides to go jogging or is smitten dead by God
  at that instant; he has an alternative, but it would be "ludicrous" to
  ground his praise in it. Mere alternatives fall to the luck objection.
  So a leeway incompatibilist must make the resolution of the leeway the
  agent's own, not determined by anything outside her, which is the
  language of source, origination and ultimacy, "the realm of Source
  Incompatibilism". Fischer 1982 is quoted for the earliest source-style
  position (pp. 31–34): Frankfurt's examples involve "alternate-sequence
  compulsion", determinism "actual-sequence compulsion".
- **Narrow and wide (pp. 147–148).** McKenna: "[t]he source incompatibilist
  agrees with Frankfurt as to the unimportance of alternative
  possibilities, but ... [determinism rules out free will] because, if
  true, the sources of an agent's actions do not originate in the agent"
  (McKenna 2003, 201–202). Some source incompatibilists still insist on an
  alternatives condition, holding the source condition "more 'fundamental'
  or 'important'": "This is true of both Robert Kane's and Derk Pereboom's
  views" (p. 147). Others (Stump, Zagzebski, Hunt) require no alternatives
  at all. Narrow source incompatibilists hold being the proper source needs
  no alternatives; wide ones hold that ultimacy is fundamental but that
  some PAP-like condition is also true.
- **Ultimacy entails alternatives (pp. 148–151).** With O'Connor: what
  Frankfurt cases show is only that freedom "is not to be identified with
  the existence of ... alternatives" (O'Connor 2005, 209), and alternatives
  matter as "indicators of the self-determination manifested by one's
  actions" (209–210). With Della Rocca: even a non-robust alternative shows
  "that the action was not determined by external factors". The alternatives
  are not "some magical element" that makes for responsibility; they are a
  necessary, not sufficient, condition of what does, namely ultimacy. If
  the agent is "the difference-maker", "there must be more than one future
  that is compossible with the conjunction of the past ... and the laws of
  nature". So "the Source Incompatibilist's emphasis on the ultimacy
  condition will entail that the agent does have alternative possibilities
  available to her, even if the explanatory weight of those alternative
  possibilities does not extend beyond merely securing the falsity of
  causal determinism", and "their absence is sufficient for the agent's not
  satisfying the ultimacy condition" (p. 150).
- **Not voluntary alternatives (pp. 150–151).** Kane (PAP-V: could have done
  otherwise "voluntarily, intentionally and rationally") and Fischer (any
  alternatives "must contain voluntary behavior", 2002, 287–288) ask too
  much. In the revised Kelvin case, Kelvin meets the ultimacy condition only
  in the actual sequence and the only alternative is being smitten; he is
  still the difference-maker, and responsible.
- **Conclusion (pp. 151–152).** "Insofar as the heart of the Source
  Incompatibilist's position is some sort of ultimacy condition, she must
  also embrace an alternative possibilities condition." Incompatibilism may
  not require PAP but requires some alternatives condition, so wide source
  incompatibilism is preferable to narrow source and to leeway
  incompatibilism, and the Taxonomy Claim should be rejected "because
  ultimacy and alternative possibilities are intrinsically related". The
  remaining alternatives are morally relevant, "even if it is only in this
  derivative way".

## Concepts

- **Leeway incompatibilism** — alternatives play the more important role in
  explaining responsibility (Pereboom 2003, 186, as quoted).
- **Source incompatibilism** — an incompatibilist condition on the causal
  history of the action plays the more significant part (ibid.).
- **Narrow source incompatibilism** — being the proper source needs no
  alternatives at all.
- **Wide source incompatibilism** — ultimacy is fundamental, and some
  alternatives condition, entailed by it, is also true.
- **Fischer scenario** — the agent dies in every alternative sequence open
  to him but one (Fischer 2002, 288, as cited).
- **Ultimacy** — the agent is not causally determined by anything outside
  herself to act as she does, allowing self-imposed determination traced to
  her own earlier undetermined acts (p. 150).

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-316, not met; it sharpens a caveat rather than confirming or
  refuting.** Its promote_when asks for a first-hand reading of Pereboom's
  Chapter 1, pp. 10–37, and a checked leeway reply. Timpe quotes Pereboom's
  book only at pp. 68–69 and 85, on agent causation, and cites p. 18 on
  blockage cases, so he supplies neither. On the account's content, Timpe
  agrees that the source condition is the fundamental one, and argues that
  it entails a weak alternatives condition. That does not touch Step 2. It
  qualifies Step 1: Frankfurt-style cases do not defeat every
  alternatives condition, since the incompatibilist must keep the one
  ultimacy entails, which is what THEORY-316's "Not that every
  alternative-possibilities condition is false" already allows. Timpe's
  argument runs from ultimacy to alternatives, not the other way, so it is
  not the refuting argument the promote_when names, that condition (3)
  matters only because it secures alternatives.
- **THEORY-119**, that the principle of alternate possibilities is false by
  Frankfurt-style cases: Timpe accepts PAP may fail and holds that a weaker
  alternatives condition, entailed by ultimacy, survives for the
  incompatibilist.

## Limitations

- **The case for wide over narrow is incomplete** by Timpe's own account:
  blockage cases, which narrow source incompatibilists rely on, are not
  answered here (p. 148, n. 31).
- **The entailment depends on an incompatibilist ultimacy condition**
  (n. 44); compatibilist accounts of ultimacy are set aside for a later
  work.
- **Second hand for Pereboom.** Timpe's classification of Pereboom as
  keeping an alternatives condition cites the book as a whole (n. 25).
- **The scan's text layer is poor.** Quotations were read from column-wise
  extraction and are reported as they read there, with spacing restored.
