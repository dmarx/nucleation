# THEORY-tmppqex3: Implicit accessibility of the true self-concept is not what self-report scales of authenticity or of need satisfaction measure, though those two self-reports are not shown to be separate constructs

## What was asked, and why the claim is narrower

The question was whether self-reported authenticity, implicit true-self
accessibility and felt autonomy are one construct. The paper that bears on
it measures all three in one sample, but not in a way that separates all
three. It separates the implicit measure from the two self-reports. It does
not separate the self-reports from each other, and its autonomy items include
an authenticity item. The title says what the evidence reaches, and leaves
the rest as the open half.

All three constructs belong to **self-governance** in its empirical form: how
far what one does is felt as one's own. The implicit measure is a measure of
a self-concept, which is why the account also carries `self`. No unity of
experience, narrative or behaviour is at issue.

## Source

- Schlegel, Hicks, Arndt & King (2009), [LIT-tmpzyy82](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzyy82.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-tmpudt8s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpudt8s.md). Table 4 was checked against the PDF text for this account.

## What was actually shown

**The measures.** True-self accessibility (TSCA) is the reversed residual of
a person's log reaction time to "me" judgments for her own true-self traits,
regressed on her speed for control traits. Authenticity is the 45-item AI-3
of Kernis and Goldman. Need satisfaction is Gagné's 21-item Basic
Psychological Needs Scale, averaged across competence, relatedness and
autonomy. Its autonomy items include "I feel like I am free to decide for
myself how to live my life" and "I feel like I can pretty much be myself in
my daily situations" ([LIT-tmpzyy82](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzyy82.md), p. 476).

**Study 4 (N = 124), Table 4.** TSCA correlated .05 with authenticity and .20
with need satisfaction. Authenticity and need satisfaction correlated .52.
All three correlated with presence of meaning in life (TSCA .30, need
satisfaction .33, authenticity .50). In a regression with nine covariates,
authenticity (β = .34) and TSCA (β = .19) each predicted meaning with the
other controlled. Studies 1 and 2 found the same for TSCA against need
satisfaction (TSCA β = .29 and .24; need satisfaction β = .37 and .32).

**The authors' reading.** True-self accessibility "resonates with previous
notions such as the awareness aspect of authenticity ... or basic need
satisfaction", and the data bear on "whether these various conceptualizations
in fact tap the same underlying construct. Results suggest that true
self-concept accessibility differs from these constructs in that each
maintained independent relationships to meaning in life. Of course, these
variables also differ methodologically" (pp. 487–488).

So the separation of the implicit measure from each self-report rests on
near-zero or small correlations and on independent prediction of a third
variable, in one sample for authenticity and three for need satisfaction.
It is confounded with method, as the authors say.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say authenticity and autonomy are distinct.** Their r = .52
  is compatible with one construct measured with error and with two
  related ones. The autonomy subscale was not analysed alone, and it
  contains an authenticity item. This is the half of the original question
  the record cannot answer.
- **It does not say the implicit measure is the better one.** TSCA predicts
  meaning with β ≈ .2–.3 in modest samples, and its priming evidence comes
  from subliminal-priming experiments of a period whose replication record
  has been widely questioned ([NOTE-tmpudt8s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpudt8s.md), Limitations; that judgement is
  the note's, not the paper's).
- **It does not say what a "true self" is.** The paper measures the
  person's avowed true self-concept, and its authors say that this "may not
  coincide with a person's 'real' true self" (p. 475).
- **It does not reach the philosophical constructs.** Authenticity in
  Taylor's sense ([LIT-tmprf69i](../literature.d/LIT-tmprf69i.md), unread) is a moral ideal, not a trait score.
