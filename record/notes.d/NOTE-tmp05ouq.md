# NOTE-tmp05ouq: Graham et al. 2013, Moral Foundations Theory

## Contribution

- A statement of MFT as four claims, any of which, if disproved, would
  sink the theory (p. 61): nativism, cultural learning, intuitionism,
  pluralism.
- Table 2.1: five foundations with adaptive challenge, original and
  current triggers, emotions and virtues.
- A review of MFT findings, framed as "pragmatic validity": the theory's
  usefulness in generating findings (section 3).
- Five criteria for foundationhood, with the evidence for each current
  foundation (Table 2.4), and a list of candidate foundations (4.1.5).

## Key insight

Pluralism is a methodological bet: if morality has several evolved
sources, a one-construct theory will be Procrustean, "cutting off phenomena
it cannot explain ... and stretching its unitary construct to fit
everything else" (p. 103). The list of foundations is provisional; the
pluralism is not.

## Assumptions

- **Nativism as preparedness.** "Innate means organized in advance of
  experience", not hard-wired (p. 61, after Marcus).
- **Modularity optional.** Foundations are thought of as sets of modules,
  but "you do not have to embrace modularity ... to embrace MFT" (p. 63).
- **Intuitionism.** The social intuitionist model (Haidt 2001,
  [LIT-tmpux6l9](../literature.d/LIT-tmpux6l9.md)) is "the prequel to MFT" (p. 66).
- **Descriptive only.** "Our project is descriptive, not normative" (p.
  57; restated at p. 105 in reply to Jost).
- **Measurement.** Almost all the evidence reviewed uses the MFQ, whose
  internet samples the chapter concedes are likely Westernized (p. 92).

## Key results

- **Table 2.1 (p. 68), the five foundations.**

| foundation | adaptive challenge | original triggers | virtues |
|---|---|---|---|
| Care/harm | protect and care for children | suffering, distress or neediness expressed by one's child | caring, kindness |
| Fairness/cheating | reap benefits of two-way partnerships | cheating, cooperation, deception | fairness, justice, trustworthiness |
| Loyalty/betrayal | form cohesive coalitions | threat or challenge to group | loyalty, patriotism, self-sacrifice |
| Authority/subversion | forge beneficial relationships within hierarchies | signs of high and low rank | obedience, deference |
| Sanctity/degradation | avoid communicable diseases | waste products, diseased people | temperance, chastity, piety, cleanliness |

- **Table 2.4 (p. 108), evolutionary model per foundation.** Care: kin
  selection (Hamilton 1964) and attachment (Bowlby 1969). Fairness:
  reciprocal altruism (Trivers 1971). Loyalty: multilevel selection and
  tribalism. Authority: rank and dominance (de Waal; Boehm). Sanctity:
  disgust and the behavioural immune system (Rozin et al.; Schaller &
  Park).
- **Table 2.4, innate preparedness.** Nonhuman primate and infant evidence
  is listed for Care, Fairness and Loyalty; Authority has primate evidence
  only; Sanctity is "not yet shown in NHP or infants". The chapter presumes
  Sanctity "is the most recently evolved foundation, perhaps coevolving with
  human religiosity" (p. 112).
- **Ideology (3.2).** In large internet samples, Care and Fairness are
  valued across the spectrum, slightly more by liberals; Loyalty, Authority
  and Sanctity rise linearly toward conservatives (Figure 2.1). Table 2.3
  reports the pattern in 12 world regions, with Authority and Sanctity
  correlating most strongly with ideology.
- **Sanctity in attitudes (p. 88).** Across 20 hot-button issues (N =
  24,739), Sanctity was the strongest predictor for most, after partialling
  ideology and demographics.
- **Pathogens (p. 93).** Country-level historical pathogen prevalence
  predicts Sanctity, and also Loyalty and Authority (Van Leeuwen et al.
  2012).
- **Candidates (pp. 104–106).** Liberty/oppression, Efficiency/waste and
  Ownership/theft (prize-winning nominations from Jost, Shulman &
  Mastronarde, and Wiessner) and Honesty/deception are under study. The
  authors' own self-criticism: fairness measures "centered on equality
  rather than equity" (Iyer), and Loyalty and Authority might be one
  foundation (p. 106).
- **Equality (p. 114).** "We do not believe that there is an 'equality'
  foundation": equality is a special case of equity, or of Liberty.

## Concepts

- **foundation** — part of the "first draft" of the moral mind: a set of
  related modules that makes it easy to learn values about one recurrent
  adaptive challenge (pp. 61–63).
- **original / current triggers** — Sperber's proper and actual domains,
  renamed (p. 67).
- **individualizing / binding** — Care and Fairness versus Loyalty,
  Authority and Sanctity (p. 106).
- **pragmatic validity** — a theory's scientific usefulness for answering
  and posing questions (p. 57).
- **criteria for foundationhood** — common in third-party judgments;
  automatic affective evaluations; culturally widespread; evidence of
  innate preparedness; an evolutionary model of adaptive advantage (4.2).

## Connections

- **Founding essay.** Haidt and Joseph 2004 ([LIT-tmpbptbc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpbptbc.md)) had four
  modules and ingroup as a footnoted fifth. This chapter promotes ingroup
  to Loyalty and renames the rest.
- **Morality-as-cooperation ([LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md), [NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md) C7).** [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) (pp. 49–50)
  says MFT has only five foundations, omits kin altruism, reciprocal
  altruism, hawkish displays and property, includes two foundations (Care
  and purity) tied to no type of cooperation, and is "avowedly ad hoc"
  (citing Haidt & Joseph 2011, not read here). Item by item against this
  chapter:
  - *Kin altruism.* Present: Care's model is kin selection (Table 2.4).
    What MFT lacks is a foundation for obligations to family as such; Care
    generalizes to "baby seals". [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) calls Care "generic" for this
    reason, which is fair.
  - *Reciprocal altruism.* Present: Fairness is Trivers (pp. 69, 114).
    [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s footnote 1 says MFT conflates reciprocity with fairness and
    the MFQ has no reciprocity items. The chapter's own report that its
    fairness measures track equality (p. 106) supports the measurement half.
  - *Hawkish displays (bravery).* Absent. Loyalty's virtues include
    "self-sacrifice"; no foundation or candidate is about courage.
  - *Property.* Named as a good candidate, Ownership/theft (p. 104), not
    measured.
  - *Care and Sanctity.* Sanctity is grounded in disease avoidance, as
    [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) says. The chapter reports Sanctity as moralized and
    predictive, which is what [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s design never measured.
  - *Ad hoc.* The chapter's criteria are the reply. They are explicit,
    but they filter candidates; they do not derive them.
- **Mutualism and dyadic harm.** Baumard et al. 2013 ([LIT-tmpof37t](../literature.d/LIT-tmpof37t.md)) is
  cited as fairness monism and Gray et al. 2012 as harm monism (pp.
  102–104); dyadic morality's 2018 statement is [LIT-tmp0aaqg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0aaqg.md). The
  chapter's argument against monism rests on disgust findings that [NOTE-132](NOTE-132.md)
  reports as disputed.
- **Relationship regulation.** Rai and Fiske 2011 ([LIT-tmpiuf8w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpiuf8w.md)) is
  treated as an alternative pluralism, compatible with MFT (pp. 106, 115).
- **Moral disciplining.** [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md) ([NOTE-132](NOTE-132.md)) cites MFQ data in which
  Loyalty and Authority predict the moralization of victimless excess as
  strongly as Purity does. With this chapter's pathogen finding (p. 93),
  that is a second sign that the binding foundations do not separate
  cleanly.
- **Obligation.** Criterion 1 (p. 109) rests the third-party character of
  moral judgment on "shared intentionality" (Tomasello et al. 2005), the
  same capacity Tomasello's account of obligation builds on ([LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md), read
  in [NOTE-185](NOTE-185.md)).
- **The MAC-Q.** Curry, Jones Chesters and Van Lissa's questionnaire,
  filed in the same batch as [LIT-tmp0moou](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0moou.md), compared itself with the MFQ
  and found MFQ purity items rated about as morally relevant as its
  loyalty and authority items.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md), C7 ("MAC is more precise and broader than MFT").** On this
  text the claim splits.
  - *More precise* holds as a claim about derivation. MAC's domains come
    from a model of cooperation; MFT's came from a reading of five books
    ([LIT-tmpbptbc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpbptbc.md)) and are now filtered by criteria.
  - *Broader* fails. Two of the four "omissions" are present in MFT's own
    table (kin selection under Care, reciprocal altruism under Fairness),
    and a third is a named candidate (Ownership). Only bravery is truly
    absent. MFT in turn contains Sanctity and care for non-kin, which MAC's
    seven leave out. Neither list contains the other.
- **[NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md)'s open question** (are purity, sex and harm moralized as
  often as cooperation?). This chapter's evidence says purity is moralized
  in Western samples and predicts attitudes, but it is self-report from
  people asked about purity, not blind coding of what a society moralizes.
  It does not answer the question [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s design left open; it shows the
  question is live.
- No THEORY document cites it. No ML instruction.

## Limitations

- The evidence is almost all MFQ self-report from English-language
  internet samples; the chapter calls them likely WEIRD (p. 92).
- Pragmatic validity is not truth: a generative theory can carve the
  domain wrongly. The authors grant the list may change (p. 57).
- The criteria are necessary conditions on candidates, with a final
  distinctness hurdle (p. 114) that has no stated test.
- The evolutionary models are borrowed and matched, not derived; the
  match for Loyalty relies on group-level selection, which the chapter
  notes is disputed (fn. 5).

## Open questions

- Is Sanctity one foundation or several? Jarudi's sexual-versus-food
  purity split (p. 106) and [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md)'s puritanical-versus-contamination
  split point the same way.
- Do bravery and property meet the five criteria? Neither has been tested
  against them.
- Do the binding foundations separate outside WEIRD samples? [LIT-tmpaywst](../literature.d/LIT-tmpaywst.md)
  reports that the individualizing–binding split is not universal.
