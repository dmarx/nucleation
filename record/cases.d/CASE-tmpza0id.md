# CASE-tmpza0id: A proposed study: whether a recommender's objective with interface friction, or a game's monetization design, raises engagement or revenue by lowering the efficacy of commitments its users keep endorsing, measured against goals and budgets elicited apart from any choice

## The case

**The recommender arm (A23 §7).** A 2 × 2 design: who sets the objective
(the user, or the platform for engagement) against the interface (neutral,
or with asymmetric friction). Participants state goals first, including how
they want their attention allocated, and use the system over repeated
sessions. Measured: whether stated objectives remain behaviourally
effective, what correction costs, whether users can say what the system
optimizes, and whether changes persist after the recommender or friction
is removed. The method extends Bogliacino and colleagues' transaction test,
in which dark patterns raised inconsistency between choices and
independently elicited preferences (LIT-tmpj7u27, unread), "from
individual transactions to trajectories of choice".

**The game arm (A25 §9).** Players set a budget and say what they want from
the game, apart from any purchase moment, then play under environments
that differ in difficulty, purchase prompts and spending controls.
Measured: spending against the budget, revision of budgets after informed
reflection, regret, whether attempts to stop or cut back work, and whether
controls hold "when monetization pressure is greatest". A further
comparison holds revenue constant and varies its concentration.

**The finding that would decide it (A25 §9).** "a design increases revenue
specifically by making players less able to act on commitments they
continue to endorse". High spending or long sessions alone would not.

## What it can show

Whether a design governs the practical efficacy of a user's preferences
rather than their content: A25's "governance over the practical efficacy of
preferences", CLAIM-404's outside governance and CLAIM-523's volitional
efficacy, in a setting where both can be measured. A23 warns against
assuming "that initial preferences are intrinsically more authentic than
later ones"; the test is whether changes "remain open to informed
reflection, revision, and comparison with alternatives".

## Limits

Not run, and the arms are A's sketches. A31 §9's caution applies: no
experiment may induce harmful spending. The method's source,
LIT-tmpj7u27, is unread.
