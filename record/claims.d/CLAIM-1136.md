# CLAIM-1136: Monitor effects do not outlast the monitor

## The claim

In 193 deferred and non-prosecution agreements with listed US firms,
2001–2019, firms required to take an on-site monitor committed about 23–27%
fewer penalised violations than agreement firms without one during the
agreement (t from −2.15 to −2.61), and −2% to −13% after it, none
distinguishable from zero ([THEORY-2304](../theory.d/THEORY-2304.md); recomputed in
scratch/studies/lead-101/results.json). The authors' measure of ethics and
compliance norms rises and falls with the monitor.

Both arms are under sanction. What the comparison isolates is the
increment of sanction a monitor adds, and that increment's effect on
conduct ends with it.

## What it does not say

- That harms stop being weighed. The outcome is penalised violations, which
  are themselves the work of enforcement; a firm can weigh harms and be
  penalised, or ignore them and not be caught.
- That any firm was changed by redesign rather than sanction, or that a
  redesigned firm would behave differently.
- That reputation or benefit was held fixed. Neither was.
- That the post-agreement effect is zero: the point estimates stay
  negative.

## Bearing

[CLAIM-432](CLAIM-432.md)'s part 5 predicts that "a corporation changed only by sanction
reverts when the sanction lifts". This is that, for a monitor's share of
the sanction. [CLAIM-431](CLAIM-431.md)'s prudence reading predicts the same, so the
finding is consistent with both and evidence between neither
([CLAIM-1169](CLAIM-1169.md)).
