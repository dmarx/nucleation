# NOTE-1033: Kleinberg and Raghavan — Algorithmic monoculture and social welfare

## Contribution

It separates two harms of everyone using one algorithm. The familiar one
is correlated failure under a shock. The new one needs no shock: shared
rankings correlate errors, so the second firm to hire finds the first
firm's mistakes are its own. The paper gives two conditions on a noise
model of rankings under which this produces a Braess paradox. A more
accurate algorithm becomes a strictly dominant strategy for both firms
and lowers both firms' utility and total welfare. It then checks which
standard ranking models satisfy the conditions.

## Key insight

The value of a ranking to someone who chooses second is not its accuracy
alone but its independence from the ranking of whoever chose first.
"Whenever decision-makers rely on identical or highly correlated
evaluations, they miss out on hidden gems." Each firm, choosing alone,
rightly takes the more accurate tool. Together they lose the
diversification that independent errors gave.

## Assumptions

- Two firms, one hire each, hiring in a uniformly random order, from n
  candidates with values x₁ > … > x_n drawn from a known distribution D.
  Offers are always accepted.
- Each ranking comes from a **noisy permutation family** F_θ
  (Definition 1): probabilities differentiable in θ; the true ranking has
  probability → 1 as θ → ∞; and the expected top candidate after any
  removal does not fall as θ rises, strictly when nothing is removed.
- Human rankings are independent draws from F_{θ_H}. The algorithm's is
  one draw from F_{θ_A}, shared by every firm that uses it.
- **Definition 2** (preference for the first position): for π, σ
  independent from F_θ, E[π₁ − π₂ | π₁ ≠ σ₁] > 0 for every θ.
- **Definition 3** (preference for weaker competition): for θ₁ > θ₂,
  σ ∼ F_{θ₁}, π, τ ∼ F_{θ₂}, E[π₁ after removing σ₁] < E[π₁ after
  removing τ₁].

## Key results

- **Identity (3), re-derived.** With U_AH the second firm's utility when it
  uses an independent ranking after a first firm using the algorithm, and
  U_AA when both use it, U_AH(θ, θ) − U_AA(θ, θ) = E[(π₁ − π₂)·1{π₁ ≠ σ₁}].
  So Definition 2 is exactly "at equal accuracy, the second mover prefers
  independence".
- **Theorem 1.** Under Definitions 1–3, for every θ_H there is θ_A > θ_H
  at which using the algorithm is strictly dominant for both firms and
  welfare is lower than if both used humans. Re-derived in
  [THEORY-835](../theory.d/THEORY-835.md).
- **Theorem 2.** Gaussian or Laplacian RUMs with three candidates meet
  both conditions for every D. Appendix B gives three-candidate noise
  models violating each condition. Fig. 2: with 15 candidates and
  Laplacian noise Definition 2 fails for a uniform D.
- **Plackett–Luce** (Gumbel RUM) has U_AH = U_AA for every θ, by
  independence of irrelevant alternatives, so it never meets Definition 2,
  and "monoculture has no effect".
- **Magnitude.** Values may be negative, so welfare at the monocultural
  equilibrium can be negative where the optimum is positive. With three
  uniform values centred at 0 and Gaussian noise, an instance loses about
  4% of welfare.
- **Theorem 3.** The Mallows model (Kendall tau, θ = φ − 1) meets both
  conditions for every D. Appendix E reduces Definition 2 to
  φ^(i−1)(1 − φ^(−n)) > 1 − φ^(−|i−j|−1), strict for some pair when
  n ≥ 3.
- **§4, k > 2 firms.** n = 4, k = 3, φ_A = 2, φ_H = 1.75, uniform values:
  all firms use the algorithm at ≈ 0.551 each against ≈ 0.552 if all used
  humans. Recomputed exactly here: 0.55111 and 0.55231, and the algorithm
  is a best response to each profile of the other two (0.55911 against
  0.54708 when one other uses it, 0.57129 against 0.55231 when neither
  does, 0.55111 against 0.54592 when both do).
- **Theorem 4.** With known sequential order and φ_H ≥ φ_A, all-human is
  optimal, uniquely if φ_H > φ_A. For φ_A > φ_H, k = 5 and n = 6, all 16
  sequences starting with A occur as optimal profiles, ordered like a
  binary counter as φ_A rises (Fig. 4), observed and not proved.

## Concepts

- **Algorithmic monoculture**: many decision-makers relying on one
  algorithm, so their errors are identical.
- **Noisy permutation family F_θ**: a θ-indexed distribution over
  rankings meeting Definition 1.
- **Preference for the first position, for weaker competition**:
  Definitions 2 and 3.
- **Braess paradox** here: adding a better option makes the unique
  equilibrium worse for everyone.

## Connections

The concern it formalizes is Citron and Pasquale's, by the agricultural
analogy (Power and Follett 1987) and its security version (Birman and
Schneider 2009). The ranking models are Thurstone's random utility models,
Mallows (1957) and Plackett–Luce (Luce 1959). The paradox is Braess's
(1968). Kleinberg's small-world paper, [LIT-1103](../literature.d/LIT-1103.md), is unrelated.

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md).** A25's "common legibility regimes and loss of
  organizational diversity" is exactly C2. When institutions share one
  rule class for reading candidates, each reads more accurately and all
  read the same errors. The cost falls on the institutions' joint
  performance and on the candidates the shared ranking misplaces, which
  is A25's point that "the party benefiting from increased legibility need
  not be the party paying to become legible", here with the institutions
  paying too. The paper's harm is to firms' utility and welfare; the harm
  to particular applicants it names and sets aside.
- **Scott's legibility ([LIT-1321](../literature.d/LIT-1321.md), unread)** and **lock-in
  ([LIT-1314](../literature.d/LIT-1314.md), Arthur, unread).** This is a static, two-firm result with
  no adoption dynamics, so it is not lock-in. Each firm's adoption is a
  dominant strategy, not a coordination effect.
- **The record's correlated-evidence condition** ([CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md), through
  [CLAIM-402](../claims.d/CLAIM-402.md)). Contexts that share a category's use are not
  independent evidence. Here, rankings that share an algorithm are not
  independent screens, and the loss from treating them as if they were
  is measurable. The record's join.

## Limitations

- **Two firms, one hire each, in random order.** The k-firm case is
  computation.
- **The conditions are model-dependent.** Plackett–Luce kills the effect,
  and Laplacian noise with 15 candidates violates Definition 2.
- **Mallows ignores values.** The authors: it "does not depend on the
  underlying candidate values, so according to this model, monoculture
  can produce arbitrarily large negative effects".
- **The magnitude is small where measured.** About 4% in the one
  Gaussian instance, and 0.551 against 0.552 for three firms.
- **No market response.** Wages, applicant behaviour and vendor choice are
  fixed. With several vendors firms might spread across algorithms, a
  case the authors leave open.

## Open questions

- The authors': Price-of-Anarchy bounds where welfare is non-negative; more
  firms and more algorithms; decomposing correlation into shared inputs
  such as standardized tests.
- Does Theorem 2 extend to Gaussian noise with more than three candidates?
- Is the binary-counter order a theorem?
