# NOTE-tmpuyt9q: Holmström and Milgrom — Multitask Principal–Agent Analyses

## Contribution

A tractable multitask agency model that explains muted incentives in
firms, fixed wages, the link between asset ownership and incentive
strength, limits on outside activities and job design as instruments
used together.

## Key insight

The incentive for one activity cannot be set by studying that activity
alone: rewarding what can be measured pulls attention from what cannot.

## Assumptions

- Linear contracts, exponential utility, normal errors.
- In the attention model, efforts are perfect substitutes in the agent's
  cost, and agents work some amount without incentive pay.
- The unmeasured task (such as an asset's value) has no performance
  indicator.
- The job-design results add strong simplifications, which the authors
  list as caveats (§5.2).

## Key results

- **Teaching** (§1): opponents argue "teachers would sacrifice such
  activities as promoting curiosity and creative thinking ... in order to
  teach the narrowly defined basic skills that are tested on standardized
  exams."
- **Direction of attention** (§1): "when there are multiple tasks,
  incentive pay serves not only to allocate risks and to motivate hard
  work, it also serves to direct the allocation of the agents' attention
  among their various duties."
- **Measurement elsewhere** (§1): "the desirability of providing incentives
  for any one activity decreases with the difficulty of measuring
  performance in any other activities that make competing demands on the
  agent's time and attention."
- **Fixed wage** (§3.2, Proposition 1): "the efficient linear compensation
  rule pays a fixed wage and contains no incentive component (a = 0), even
  if the contractor is risk neutral."
- **Ownership** (§1): "when the principal owns the returns from the asset,
  the optimal incentive contract will provide only muted incentives for
  the agent to produce output, in order to mitigate any abuse of the asset
  or any substitution of effort away from asset maintenance."
- **Constraints** (§1): "Constraints are substitutes for performance
  incentives and are extensively used when it is hard to assess the
  performance of the agent."
- **Unpaid effort** (n. 9): "In multitask models, however, the fact that
  agents supply inputs even without incentive pay can be quite
  consequential".
- **Conclusion** (§6): "one cannot make correct inferences about the proper
  incentives for an activity by studying the attributes of that activity
  alone."

## Concepts

Multitask agency; attention allocation; measured and unmeasured tasks;
fixed wages; low- and high-powered incentives; asset ownership as an
incentive instrument; limits on outside activities; job design; unity of
responsibility.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-386](../questions.d/QUESTION-386.md)** (funding by observed activity and capabilities valued
  in rare circumstances). Bears on it by supplying a mechanism: §3.3
  models the split between a measured current output and an unmeasured
  asset value, and shows strong pay on output leads the agent to neglect
  the asset. The paper concerns an agent's pay, not an organization's
  budget rule, so it does not answer the question.
- **[CASE-tmpm0rpn](../cases.d/CASE-tmpm0rpn.md)** (three hospitals). Arm B rewards a visible measure in a
  contest; the model predicts attention drawn from unmeasured duties. Note
  9 (agents supply effort without pay) is the formal room for arm C,
  though the paper does not treat moral appeals or exploitation.
- **[TERM-108](../terms.d/TERM-108.md)** (proxy objective). This is a reactivity case in that term's
  sense (the agent reallocates effort in response to the measure), beside
  [LIT-1330](../literature.d/LIT-1330.md) (Espeland and Sauder, "Rankings and Reactivity") and [CASE-023](../cases.d/CASE-023.md).

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Faithful. A53: "rewarding measurable performance can distort effort away
from less measurable tasks." A leaves out the paper's other half: the
response is weaker incentives, ownership, task restrictions and job
design.

## Limitations

Stylized (linear, exponential utility, normal errors); the attention model
excludes complementarities; the job-design model is, by the authors'
account, a caricature; empirical support is cited, not tested.
