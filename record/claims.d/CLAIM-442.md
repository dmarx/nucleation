# CLAIM-442: Frijda's emotion and the potential account's system valence sit on opposite sides of the gap between the precedence a motive claims and the authority it is granted: an emotion, action readiness claiming control precedence, is a claim, a perspective's valence is read on its claims, and the system's valence is read on what the negotiation grants, so a change in grants with the claims fixed, as mindfulness is said to make, changes the system's valence and leaves the perspectives' emotions and valences as they were

## The claim

[CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md) holds that "an operator can urgently demand control without
receiving it", so claimed precedence and endorsed authority are different
quantities and internal conflict is the gap between them. The record's
affect accounts sit on both sides of that gap without saying so.

- **Emotion is on the claim side.** For Frijda ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)) an emotion is
  a state of action readiness that claims control precedence. The claim
  is the emotion whether or not it is granted: anger held back by fear is
  still anger.
- **The system's valence is on the grant side.** The potential account
  reads valence off what wins: the ordering that the moves winning the
  negotiation's conflicts climb ([TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md), [TERM-078](../terms.d/TERM-078.md)). What a perspective
  demands and does not get leaves no mark on it.
- **A perspective's valence is on the claim side.** [CLAIM-437](CLAIM-437.md) makes
  valence relative to a perspective: each has its own ranking and its own
  progress against its own reference course. That valence exists whether
  or not the perspective wins.

So conflict, emotion and the system's valence are three readings of one
gap: conflict is the gap itself, emotion is the claim, and the system's
valence is the pattern of grants over time, of a particular kind.

## What follows

1. **Grants can change with claims fixed.** [CLAIM-156](CLAIM-156.md) holds that
   mindfulness works by "changing which of them may act, not by changing
   what they value": noticing an impulse suspends the step from its
   momentary dominance to its control of action. On this claim that
   changes the system's valence, by changing what wins, and leaves the
   impulse's claim, and so the emotion and its perspective's valence, as
   it was. The report usually given of mindful attention to affect is of
   this shape, the affect still present and no longer driving. Whether
   that report is accurate is not settled here; the mindfulness
   literature the record holds is read only as far as [CLAIM-156](CLAIM-156.md) uses it.
2. **Claims can change with grants fixed.** [CASE-112](../cases.d/CASE-112.md)'s bistable authority
   has a higher-order volition h that moves the balance of authority
   without moving the operative regime until a threshold. Read as affect,
   an emotion can grow in what it claims while the system's valence stays
   where it was, and then shift abruptly, with hysteresis, when the claim
   passes the threshold.
3. **Impulsive action is the claim outrunning the grant and governing
   anyway** ([CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)'s own example). On this reading the system's
   valence there follows the impulse's ordering for as long as it wins,
   which is the account's 'wanting' reading ([CLAIM-438](CLAIM-438.md)).
4. **Measurement must keep the sides apart.** A record of conduct
   ([QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md)) sees grants. Claims need a second record: what each
   perspective demanded, as in [CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)'s p_i, through reports, approach
   and avoidance tendencies, or physiological readiness.

## What it does not say

- Which side is "the" valence. Both are valence on the record's account;
  they are of different bearers.
- That either side is felt. That is [CLAIM-439](CLAIM-439.md)'s question.
- That the gap is always measurable. A perspective that never wins may
  leave little trace of its claim in conduct.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

Its premise CLAIM-437, which makes valence relative to a perspective, is
Proposed, and so is TERM-076, the account's valence it reads on the grant
side. Its sharp edge, that suspending a motive's hold on action leaves the
motive's emotion as it was, rests on "the report usually given" of mindful
attention, which no reading in the record measures.
