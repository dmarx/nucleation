# THEORY-tmp5748l: Testimonial injustice is an ingenuous misjudgement of a speaker's credibility caused by prejudice, not a deliberate manipulation of it, and it can be structural and pre-emptive, when a speaker's word is never sought because of a structural prejudice that is no one's fault

## Source

Fricker (2003), LIT-1049, read in full in NOTE-860, and Fricker (2017),
LIT-1048, read in full in NOTE-853. That these two papers hold the
account is THEORY-327, which this entry extends. The book, LIT-1004, where
the account is developed, has not been read.

## The claim

Fricker offers her account as an account of the wrong, not only as a
stipulation. Testimonial injustice "happens when a speaker receives the
wrong degree of credibility from his hearer owing to a certain sort of
unintended prejudice on the hearer's part" (2003). "In testimonial
injustice the absence of deliberate, conscious manipulation is
definitive, at least in my conception" (2017), and she argues for it from
the concept's point: it brings into focus a class of epistemic
misjudgements, and someone who deliberately discredits a speaker does not
misjudge her. Deliberate manipulation is "a closely related but distinct
injustice". With Anderson she accepts a structural form, which can only be
pre-emptive: when an un-refreshed list means some people's views are
never sought, "(through no one's fault, let us add)", that is testimonial
injustice before anyone hears them.

Others use the term more widely and count deliberate manipulation of
credibility (NOTE-1049). This entry is the thesis that Fricker's narrower
account marks out the wrong correctly.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, carried over unchanged, which is also the "What would
answer it" of QUESTION-322: a first-hand reading of the book's chapters on
testimonial injustice that confirms both parts as her account, with no
case that is plainly a testimonial wrong to a speaker as a knower while
deliberate and non-prejudiced. More secondary summaries cannot settle it.
