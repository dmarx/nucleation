# CLAIM-tmp887rf: A collective can want a transition and lack the readiness for it

## The claim

A5 §2: "A population can possess", in a list, "Widely shared
dissatisfaction", "Knowledge of alternative arrangements", "Formal
political rights" and "Numerical superiority over incumbent authorities",
"And nevertheless lack a viable transition to a different regime. The missing capacity may be
coordination, institutional memory, financial reserves, mutual trust,
organizational infrastructure, or the ability to survive retaliation."

"Under this interpretation, a social movement doesn't merely express a
preexisting collective will. It can **construct a collective agent where
one did not previously exist**. Trade unions are a particularly clear
example. A strike fund converts financial resources into the capacity to
sustain a refusal."

"The fund is, in Nucleation's terminology, part of the material
infrastructure of reorganization readiness. This also explains why
collective action can exhibit threshold behaviour".

## Against the record

- **Readiness.** TERM-115 is defined for an organization under its actual
  policy; a population without coordination has no single policy, so the
  claim's "lack a viable transition" is readiness of a collective that does
  not yet exist. That is the claim's point, and it is why A says the
  movement constructs the agent.
- **The fund as a stock of time.** CLAIM-601's savings buffer sets how
  many bad periods a household can absorb; a strike fund sets how long a
  refusal can be sustained. Same form, a reserve that is a deadline.
- **The threshold.** Asserted. The record's threshold baseline for
  collective uptake is LEAD-264's reading of complex contagion; whether a
  readiness threshold differs from an adoption threshold is not shown.

## What it does not say

That grievance does not matter, or that the classical literature on
collective action has missed this; A5 §5 names Hirschman, Ostrom and
others as precedent, and the claim's addition is the readiness reading,
not the observation that organization matters.
