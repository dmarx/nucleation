# NOTE-tmpyf5pn: Massumi and McKim — Of Microperception and Micropolitics

## Contribution

A compact statement of Massumi's account of affect, given to Joel McKim
on 15 August 2008 and printed in Inflexions 3 (2009). It became chapter 2
of Politics of Affect ([LIT-tmpe2cnn](../literature.d/LIT-tmpe2cnn.md)). It ties the Spinoza–Deleuze
definition of affect to microperception, to "affective attunement"
(after Daniel Stern), and to a micropolitics of modulating potential
rather than imposing programmes.

## Key insight

Affect is not a feeling someone has but the event in which a body's
capacities change, and the change is felt. "A power to affect and be
affected governs a transition, where a body passes from one state of
capacitation to a diminished or augmented state of capacitation. This
comes with the corollary that the transition is felt" (p. 1). Subjects
come out of such events; they are not their starting point.

## Assumptions

- **Process ontology.** The event comes first; categories of subject and
  object "return" through it (p. 4).
- **Spinoza via Deleuze, with Whitehead, James, Peirce and Bergson.**
  Negative prehension, the specious present and "shock" (Peirce) do the
  conceptual work.
- **No evidence is offered or needed** by the genre. Claims about the
  "half-second" kind of nonconscious priming are gestured at through
  examples (startle, alarm), not cited.

## Key results

Conceptual, not empirical.

1. **Definition as proposition.** "It's not a general definition that you
   can apply. It's not a structure you can presuppose ... Rather than a
   definition, what you have is a proposition" (p. 3). "To start in the
   middle is precisely not to perform a phenomenological reduction" (p.
   3).
2. **Not intersubjective.** "Calling affect ... intersubjective is
   misleading if intersubjective is taken to mean that we start from a
   world in which there are already subjects that are preconstituted" (p.
   3).
3. **Microperception.** Every shift of attention is a microshock, felt
   only in its effects. "In the instant of the affective hit, there is no
   content yet" (p. 5).
4. **Differential attunement.** Bodies cued together "will have been
   attuned – differentially – to the same interruptive commotion". "There
   is no sameness of affect. There is affective difference in the same
   event" (p. 6). Massumi prefers this to "imitation or contagion".
5. **Not transmitted.** Affect "is not a content that can be transported
   from one event to another. Like all event factors, it can be repeated,
   reactivated, it can rearise, but always anew ... It's an event logic,
   not a logic of transmission or communication" (p. 9).
6. **Habit forms the self.** "'I' do not contract habits. Habits contract
   to form me" (p. 10).
7. **Micropolitics.** Even the most controlled situation has "a surplus of
   unacted-out potential that is collectively felt" (p. 7). Aesthetic
   politics holds contrasting alternatives together; the aim is "to
   modulate it into a symbiosis: a cross-fertilization of capacitations"
   (p. 12), not consensus.

## Concepts

- **affect** — "an event, or a dimension of every event" (p. 1); the
  felt passage of a body's capacities.
- **capacitation** — a body's state of readiness to act, bound up with
  its lived past and tendencies (pp. 1–2).
- **microperception** — perception "of a qualitatively different kind",
  felt without registering consciously (p. 4).
- **affective attunement** — bodies primed by the same cue, responding
  differently together (p. 6).
- **ontopower** — the power that brings to be, here first as the
  "rebecoming-creative of habit" (p. 10).
- **enabling constraints** — designed constraints that make an event
  possible without fixing its outcome (p. 15).

## Connections

Builds on Spinoza and Deleuze for the definition, Peirce for shock,
Whitehead for negative prehension and contrast, James for the specious
present and the terminus, Stern for affective attunement, Stengers for
the "ecology of practices". It is the first printing of Politics of
Affect, ch. 2 ([LIT-tmpe2cnn](../literature.d/LIT-tmpe2cnn.md)). The Principle of Unrest ([LIT-tmpi0u4n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpi0u4n.md))
develops the same micropolitics as "co-patterning".

## Limitations

An interview: assertion, example and allusion, no argument that could
fail. The neuroscience it leans on (priming, nonconscious timing) is not
cited here. Its political examples (Bush-era threat, the Obama campaign)
are dated illustrations.

## Open questions

- What would distinguish differential attunement from contagion in data?
  Massumi says attunement "finds difference in unison", which predicts
  divergent responses to a shared cue. That is testable in principle, but
  he does not test it.
- How a diminished capacitation is told from an augmented one before the
  event resolves. The same question is open for the parent paper.
