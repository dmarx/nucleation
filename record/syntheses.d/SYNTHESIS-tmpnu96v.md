# SYNTHESIS-tmpnu96v: What a collective is credited with need not be what its members hold: an organized whole's operative intention is not the expectation of its members' intentions and its reasons can part from theirs; a diffuse population with no procedure can show a public stance or change an outside institution without a collective intention, whether the credit is then due to it is open, and composition and a group process are offered as routes by which its most committed or extreme members fix the stance; a person can be organized by a judgement attributed to a collective that does not hold it, governs themselves by making such a judgement's authority depend on its grounds, and that rule holds without qualification only for judgements of what already is; and whether a community of sound inquirers can settle on a belief none of them reasoned to is open, with model results whose tests in real communities are open questions

*A part account of the distributed-agency line: the part the line's map
calls collective stance, 29 entries on what is credited to a collective
as against what its members intend, judge or believe. The line's account
is built over it and its six sibling parts. Every member carries
distributed-agency as its only line. No account in force integrates any
of them; the Superseded SYNTHESIS-005 listed CASE-142.*

## The account

### An organized whole and its members

- **The thesis.** CLAIM-030 (the owner's thesis at U12, interpretive,
  Active): "An organized whole's operative intention is not the
  expectation of its members' intentions; it is produced by systemic
  mechanisms that can evolve apart from what the members intend". Its
  formal anchor is the court-agenda result (THEORY-725); its defeater is
  a collective "whose operative direction is reliably predicted by
  aggregating its members' intentions across changes in its decision
  structure". It does not say "that the whole is an agent", which is
  QUESTION-009. CASE-025 (A31's fictional Verdant Compute, Active)
  illustrates it, and shows "coherence, not frequency"; it stands in for
  the owner's CASE-007 (Superseded), a real company replaced at the
  owner's suggestion so the argument would not ride on "tangential
  reputational controversy".
- **Reasons and polling.** CLAIM-271 (the owner's thesis of 2026-10-13,
  the polling consequence drawn by the assistant, interpretive, Proposed)
  rests on CLAIM-030: "A collective's reasons need not be any of its
  members' reasons, so aggregating members' conclusions, as polling does,
  can yield verdicts the collective's own reasons do not support". Its
  step 3 rests on THEORY-131 and THEORY-139, both Proposed, which its note
  of 2026-10-29 says holds it back. It bears on QUESTION-082 (the owner's,
  Open), which keeps three answers live: the polity's decisions carry its
  members' reasons, its own, or none.
- **Attributing a mind.** QUESTION-049 (raised by the synthesis review of
  2026-10-11, Open) asks whether attributing knowledge or intention to a
  corporation tracks a mind it has (THEORY-139) or shapes it to meet the
  attribution (THEORY-137). It refines QUESTION-009.
- **Manipulation without a manipulator.** QUESTION-070 (Open) asks, from
  CLAIM-030, whether an organized whole can manipulate when no member
  intends to, and "whose agency does the wrong run through?" Its cases
  are a learning system and a state's network of channels.

### A diffuse population credited with a stance or an effect

- **The question.** QUESTION-119 (A5 §3 and A7 of the exchange of
  2026-10-16, Open) refines QUESTION-049, whose attributions are to
  corporations with procedures. It asks when a stance or effect credited
  to "a diffusely organized population with no procedure for collective
  judgment" is due to the population as organized, and when it is "a
  biased sample of what some members said or did". Its summary: CLAIM-030
  and CLAIM-271 "both assume an organization that forms them". A5:
  "publicly attributable collective orientation is weaker than collective
  intention".
- **The stance case.** CASE-104 (A2's, stipulated, Active) separates four
  readings of "the fanbase hates the owner": aggregate, shared emotional
  orientation, normative position and corporate judgment. "A fanbase can
  show 2 and 3 strongly and never reach 4." A5 §3 made it "a possible
  boundary for CLAIM-030: a public stance without a collective
  intention", filed as a question and not a claim.
- **The effect case and its claim.** CASE-100 (the owner's case, documented
  as to the sequence, Active) is the Juggernaut line. CLAIM-332 (A7's
  distributed cultural uptake, its defeater priced by the record,
  interpretive, Proposed) answers QUESTION-119 and is grounded in it: on
  A7's ladder it asserts collective causal efficacy and denies that this
  needs collective intention, "the distinction between having effects as a
  collective and acting as a collective agent". It complements CLAIM-030,
  and says the difference: CLAIM-030 "is about an organized whole's
  intention parting from its members'; this needs no intention at all".
  Its note of 2026-10-30: "Its one case does not show the third rung".
  CASE-100's note of 2026-10-29: "No one from the production says why the
  line was added."
- **Two routes by which a few fix the stance.** Both answer QUESTION-119,
  are grounded in CASE-104 and complement each other. CLAIM-490 (the
  filer's, from Giulianotti's taxonomy, THEORY-1111, interpretive,
  Proposed): the stance is "weighted towards the most committed types
  before any bias in who is sampled". CLAIM-493 (the filer's derivation
  from self-categorization theory, THEORY-1114, interpretive, Proposed):
  against an outside party the more extreme members become more
  prototypical, "so a vocal minority can fix the fanbase's public stance
  through a group process and not only through biased sampling of who
  speaks". Each says what holds it back: THEORY-1111 is unmeasured, and
  THEORY-1114 is Proposed and neither step is tested in a fanbase.
- **Who could hold a stance.** TERM-096 (Skorich's distinction, Proposed)
  separates a category of members from a group entity, and "only that is
  a candidate holder of a stance (CLAIM-490, CLAIM-493)". It "would let the
  record say precisely what CASE-104 and CASE-100 keep needing", and
  leaves whether a group entity acts as one to QUESTION-119.
- **When the stance is exposed.** QUESTION-261 (A39's "nonexistent
  consensus", Open) refines QUESTION-119 "from the members' side": whether
  a stance credited to a population, "and then built on, outlives the
  discovery that the population never held it". QUESTION-356 (the
  promotion condition moved from THEORY-242, Open) asks whether such an
  apparent consensus forms by silence at all in a real community; its body
  sets it beside QUESTION-261.

### The collective seen from inside the person credited

- **The claim.** CLAIM-415 (from the owner's U39 of 2026-10-22, the
  wording A39's and A40's, interpretive, Proposed): "A person can be
  organized by a judgement they attribute to a collective that does not
  hold it". Step 1, the imagined audience as a false unity, is
  "QUESTION-119's question ("when is the credit due to the population as
  organized?") asked from inside the person credited". Step 2, "Rejection
  does not free", is Cooley's "remoter conformity", worked in CASE-142. Its
  summary: "the attributed collective attitude itself is already held
  (List's falsely attributed common attitudes, CASE-104); what is new is
  its place in a person's own organization". Its note of 2026-10-30: the
  third clause "has no read result".
- **The term.** TERM-075 (A39's phrases gathered at the owner's request,
  Active, adopted 2026-10-29) names four senses of an attributed self:
  held, imagined, imagined audience and appropriated. "Each step of
  CLAIM-415 and its cases runs between two senses." CLAIM-415, CLAIM-425
  and CLAIM-426 use it.
- **The cases.** All three are A39's, fictional, Active, and illustrate
  CLAIM-415. CASE-140 holds the words fixed and varies only the imagined
  audience; "As stipulated it predicts nothing", and it is "QUESTION-119
  asked from inside the person credited". CASE-141, a pupil wrongly
  labelled, is "TERM-075's held attribution doing the work", and also
  illustrates CLAIM-402, outside the line. CASE-142, its variant, is "the
  judgement is confirmed by the response of someone who rejects it"; its
  note of 2026-10-29 reads Merton as naming both routes it joins and not
  joining them.
- **Self-governance.** CLAIM-425 (A39's closing thesis, interpretive,
  Proposed) rests on CLAIM-415 and CLAIM-349 (outside the line) and is
  grounded in CASE-142: a perspective governs itself with respect to
  social judgement by "making their authority over its conduct depend on
  their grounds, not on their visibility, repetition or attributed
  plurality"; "neither absorbing every judgement nor resisting every one
  meets this".
- **The objection.** CLAIM-426 (the owner's prompt of 2026-10-23,
  "hyperstition", a counter, interpretive, Proposed) objects to CLAIM-425:
  where a judgement's circulation helps bring about what it says, its
  visibility is evidence about what it will bring about, so CLAIM-425's
  rule "holds for judgements of what already is". It "narrows CLAIM-425,
  it does not reverse it". Its note of 2026-10-30: its own second defeat
  condition, whether circulation is better described as a new ground, is
  "unanswered".

### A community's settled belief

- **The question.** QUESTION-047 (filed in the fourth synthesis pass,
  Open): "Can a community of individually sound inquirers settle on a
  belief none of them reasoned their way to, as an organized whole can
  settle on a course none of its members intends". It would test CLAIM-030
  "in a second domain, through named mechanisms rather than parallels",
  and sets QUESTION-049 beside it. It lists nine readings with their
  limits; of the spiral of silence (THEORY-242): "Limit: appearance only.
  Private beliefs never change, so the community does not come to believe
  the minority view."
- **Its tests in real communities.** Four questions refine it, each the
  promotion condition moved from a reading when the reading was narrowed
  to its model: QUESTION-151 (THEORY-295, a consensus with memory),
  QUESTION-173 (THEORY-290, more than two debaters), QUESTION-184
  (THEORY-321, real groups that update by averaging) and QUESTION-212
  (THEORY-267, a price shock that traders come to believe). QUESTION-315
  (THEORY-146, industrial selection in real fields) carries no relation;
  its body calls it "one candidate answer to QUESTION-047's question". All
  are Open.

## Not stated on the record

Links across the components that no entry states, tested by ADR-075's
step 5 at this review and not filed:

- **Composition is the members' side, and only the group process the
  population's.** That of the two routes on file for a fanbase's stance,
  composition (CLAIM-490) leaves the stance predicted by the members once
  their attachment is known, and so meets the pattern of CLAIM-030's
  defeater, while the prototype shift (CLAIM-493) does not, so that only
  the second gives QUESTION-119's credit to the population as organized.
  A realistic case separates it from its premises and shows it false: a
  fanbase whose committed members are organized as supporters' groups
  that lead the chants and call the boycotts, where composition is itself
  an organization. Narrowed to weights fixed by each member's attachment
  alone, it restates CLAIM-030's defeater. CLAIM-490 leaves the placement
  to QUESTION-119.
- **A credited stance becomes the population's by being credited.** That
  a stance credited to a population with no procedure is, by CLAIM-426,
  evidence about what the population will hold, so that QUESTION-119's
  answer is indexed to a time. As "can", it is a conjunction of
  CLAIM-415, CLAIM-493 and CLAIM-426; as a general claim, QUESTION-261
  holds it as one of two open answers.
- **A public stance is not a settled belief.** Stated on both sides
  (QUESTION-047 on THEORY-242; CLAIM-415 quoting A39), above. What is not
  stated is the cross-reference: neither QUESTION-047 nor QUESTION-119
  names the other.

## What it does not say

- That any diffuse population has been shown to hold a stance, or to
  have had an effect, as a population: CLAIM-332's one case does not show
  its third rung, and CASE-104 is stipulated.
- That a corporation is an agent, or has a mind (QUESTION-009,
  QUESTION-049).
- That any of the model results in QUESTION-047's list holds of a real
  community; the refining questions hold those tests.
- Anything with standing of its own.
