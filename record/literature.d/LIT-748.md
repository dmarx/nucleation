# LIT-748: Exit, Voice, and Loyalty: Responses to Decline in Firms, Organizations, and States

Albert O. Hirschman (1970), *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty: Responses to
Decline in Firms, Organizations, and States*, Harvard University Press

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* What the record holds of it comes from Hirschman's
"Further Reflections" ([LIT-750](LIT-750.md)), which restates the book's thesis
that voice can complement and supersede exit as a recuperation mechanism,
and refers back to its arguments: competition as collusive behaviour, the
"locking in" of dissatisfied members, management's manipulation of exit
and voice, and the critique of Hotelling–Downs in chapter 6. The essay
does not reproduce the book's account of loyalty.
