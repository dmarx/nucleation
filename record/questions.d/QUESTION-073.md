# QUESTION-073: Are teasing and reproach individuated by the speaker's communicative intention, or partly by a frame the parties sustain together and by their standing?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) says which act an utterance performs is partly constituted by its
relations to participant roles, norms and expected uptake. For acts with
standing conditions, such as a judge's reprimand, the record has an account
it has read: Searle's status functions and deontic powers ([LIT-482](../literature.d/LIT-482.md)).
Teasing is the line's central case, and for it the record has no account at
all.

[CLAIM-221](../claims.d/CLAIM-221.md) names the standard rival. On the intention-based account of
illocution (Grice's speaker meaning, 1957; Strawson's "Intention and
Convention in Speech Acts", 1964; Bach and Harnish's *Linguistic
Communication and Speech Acts*, 1979), an act is individuated by the
speaker's communicative intention, an intention to be recognized as having
a certain attitude or aim. On it the friend teasing and the parent
reproaching differ in what each intends the hearer to recognize, and their
relations to the audience are evidence of the act, not part of it. The
record's own model for [CLAIM-111](../claims.d/CLAIM-111.md) is of this family: [THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md)'s listener
interprets an utterance "by inverting a model of a speaker".

The tradition has replies, and the record holds none of them read.

- **Keying.** Goffman's *Frame Analysis* (1974) treats play as a keying of
  a frame, a transformation of serious activity that the participants
  recognize and sustain together. If teasing is keyed play, whether an
  utterance teases depends on whether the frame is jointly held, not only on
  what the speaker intends. The book is not in the record; *Forms of Talk*
  ([LIT-780](../literature.d/LIT-780.md)) is held unread.
- **Uptake.** Austin held that an illocution is not performed unless uptake
  is secured ([LIT-785](../literature.d/LIT-785.md), held unread). If so, the hearer's recognition is a
  condition of the act, not only evidence of it.

[CLAIM-224](../claims.d/CLAIM-224.md) adds the translation form of the question: a translation
changes the audience and the norms, so whether teasing survives depends on
whether the target culture's norms define the same roles.

## What would count as an answer

A reading of the intention-based line and of its rivals, and a documented
or elicited case in which the speaker's intention is held fixed and the act
verdicts of informed participants change with the parties' standing, with
whether the frame is jointly held, or with uptake. Such a case would show
that teasing is partly constituted by relations, and [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) would cover
it. A body of cases in which the verdicts follow the speaker's intention
whatever the standing and uptake would show the reverse, and [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md)
would hold only for acts with standing conditions.

The works named here should be checked against the Anthology of the SOTA
before any is filed.

## Note of 2026-10-10: a documented case

[CASE-069](../cases.d/CASE-069.md), Labov's recorded sounding sessions, is the first documented
case this question asks for. Whether a pejorative remark about the hearer's
relative is a sound (ritual insult) or a personal insult follows a relation
the parties sustain together. A sound needs it to be shared knowledge before
the audience that the remark is untrue; Labov states this as his rule. The
words can be the same, and in the recorded exchanges the act changes while
the speaker's ritual aim, as Labov attributes it, stays fixed. The Crow and
Hidatsa joking-relative adds standing: the same reproof is licensed from a
*maku'tsati* and an offence from anyone else.

What it supplies: documented, non-institutional data in which teasing's
nearest documented kin, ritual insult, is individuated by a jointly held
frame. What it does not supply: the speaker's own avowal in the main
exchanges, where the aim is Labov's inference. Only the exchange in which a
researcher's hypothetical question is taken as a real sound has the
speaker's side stating its intention. The intention-based reply, that the
hearers misread a sound, is not answered by the transcripts. The case
sketches the elicited test that would answer it. No reading of the
intention-based line or of Goffman has been added, so the question stays
open.

## Note of 2026-10-13: the intention-based line, read

Grice (1957, [LIT-tmpvudnt](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvudnt.md)) and Strawson (1964, [LIT-tmpyozog](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyozog.md)) are now read.
Grice (1975, [LIT-tmpswf3g](../literature.d/LIT-tmpswf3g.md)) is read in part, and Bach and Harnish (1979,
[LIT-tmp0vfgl](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0vfgl.md)) is filed unread. Three findings bear on the question as
posed.

- **The rival makes a prediction.** For acts not essentially
  conventional, Strawson's scheme leaves no case in which the overt
  intention is fixed and the act changes. The one such case it allows is
  the institutional slip, a "redouble" let slip at bridge, which it calls
  "essentially deviant or nonstandard". A documented case of the kind this
  question asks for would therefore count against Strawson's account
  directly. [CASE-069](../cases.d/CASE-069.md) is a candidate, if Labov's attribution of the aim is
  accepted.
- **Uptake does not separate the two sides.** The question lists Austin's
  uptake as a reply to the intention-based accounts. Strawson takes uptake
  into his account as the audience's recognition of the overt intention.
  After Hart's objections he weakens it to "the aim, if not the
  achievement". On this reading uptake is common ground, not a rival.
- **The frame shows up on the Gricean side.** Grice's Cooperative
  Principle (1975, p. 45) is indexed to "the accepted purpose or direction
  of the talk exchange", which the participants recognise in common and
  which "may evolve". This is close to Goffman's jointly sustained frame.
  It is stated for implicature, not for the individuation of acts.

Teasing itself is in none of the pages read. The question stays open. Its
reading condition is now partly met: Goffman's *Frame Analysis* and Bach
and Harnish remain unread.
