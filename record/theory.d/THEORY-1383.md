# THEORY-1383: Obayashi, Aoi, Tsuchiya and Kokubu explain the small, thin, fractal-like basin of attraction of the simplest passive walking model by the saddle-type instability of its upright equilibrium: pulled back through the swing phase, the set of states that take a step is thinned along the saddle's stable manifold and folded into a V with nested slits

## Source

Obayashi, Aoi, Tsuchiya and Kokubu (2016), [LIT-2110](../literature.d/LIT-2110.md); read in
[NOTE-1586](../notes.d/NOTE-1586.md). A neighbour of McGeer (1990), [LIT-2108](../literature.d/LIT-2108.md), which was not
read.

## What was actually shown

- **The basin** (abstract; §3): computed numerically, "very small and thin,
  and it has a fractal-like shape", V-shaped with slits and stripes.
- **The construction** (§2b, p. 4): D, "the collection of initial
  conditions for which the model takes at least one step", and its inverse
  images under the step map.
- **The mechanism** (§4a–c): the upright equilibrium is a saddle-centre;
  pull-backs near its stable manifold are thinned (the λ-lemma), and
  non-uniform deformation near the centre-unstable manifold folds them into
  the V and the nested slits. A basin of another shape would have counted
  against it.

## What this does not say

- That every passive walker's basin has this shape; the authors expect the
  saddle mechanism to be shared, without showing it.
- That the mechanism is proved: it is argued from a linearisation and from
  the computed figures.
- Anything about McGeer's own walkers, results or machine.
- That control cannot enlarge the basin; that is left to future work.
