### Summary
A meta-analysis of the inter-correlations among SDT's motivation types
(amotivation, external, introjected, identified, integrated, intrinsic), from
486 independent samples (374 published, 88 unpublished articles), 4,111
correlations, >205,000 participants, 13 validated scales (SRQ, WEIMS, MAWS,
MWMS, SMS, SMS-6, SMS-II, BRSQ, EMS, BREQ, BREQ-2, BREQ-3, AMS), data
1989-Oct 2016 (pp. 14-15). Random-effects pooling, correlations corrected for
reliability (p. 17); moderators by domain, scale, country, student/employee,
school level, age, gender; then metric multidimensional scaling of the pooled
matrix. Result: the matrix "generally conforms to a simplex pattern" (p. 19);
a one-dimensional MDS solution explains 99.75% of dispersion (p. 22);
heterogeneity is very high (I2 98.13-99.75%) and unexplained by any moderator
(p. 20, 32). Integrated regulation, and the three intrinsic subtypes, are not
distinguishable from their neighbours (abstract; pp. 19, 26, 28-29).

### Contribution
First meta-analytic test of SDT's continuum (simplex) assumption across
domains and scales; recommendations on scoring (RAI weights unsupported;
autonomous/controlled dichotomy not recommended because introjection is mixed;
intrinsic subscales not recommended; integrated regulation needs either
removal from the theory or reconceptualization).

### Key results
1. Continuum supported: "Results largely supported the presence of a continuum
   ordering of motivation" (p. 23); 1-D MDS: Normalized Stress = .00246,
   DAF = .99754; 2-D adds only ".071%" (pp. 22-23).
2. **Integrated vs identified.** Pooled corrected r = .913 (k = 98, 95% CI
   .876-.940); integrated-intrinsic .818 (k = 84); identified-intrinsic .853
   (k = 384) (Tables 1-2, pp. 59-60). "The exception to this trend was
   integrated regulation which correlated with regulations in much the same
   direction and strength as identified regulation" (p. 19). "confidence
   intervals of integrated regulation almost always overlapped with
   neighboring regulations, suggesting a lack of differentiation" (p. 26).
3. "results of this meta-analysis indicate that integrated regulation may not
   have a place on the continuum ... practically indistinguishable from
   identified regulation ... Either there needs to be a revision of the theory
   to exclude this form of regulation or new ways of conceptualizing and
   operationalizing integrated regulation need to be developed" (pp. 28-29).
4. A possible second MDS dimension on which identified and integrated differ
   "somewhat" (p. 24), perhaps effort/self-control (after Sheldon et al., in
   press); "It is currently unclear whether this second dimension is
   theoretically important ... or merely represents noise" (p. 25).
5. Introjection is "relatively equidistant from both external and identified
   regulations", r = .603 with identified and .313 with intrinsic (p. 28), "a
   mix of self-determination and control" (p. 32).
6. Intrinsic subtypes (to know, accomplish, stimulation) correlate .86-.96:
   "it is recommended that intrinsic subscales are not used" (p. 29).
7. Amotivation fits the continuum, as "a negative counterpoint to intrinsic
   motivation" (p. 29).
8. RAI: "neither the results of this meta-analysis, or previous work ...
   provide evidence for the weights used in the RAI" (p. 31); a single
   self-determination score is nonetheless defensible (p. 30).
9. Heterogeneity: unexplained by domain, scale, nationality, occupation, age
   or gender (pp. 20-22, 32).

### Assumptions
- Correlational structure of self-report subscales is evidence about the
  structure of motivation ("participants perceive these factors in a manner
  consistent with the continuum", p. 24).
- Reliability correction with imputed alphas where missing (p. 17).
- Publication bias dismissed partly on a general argument that inter-factor
  correlations are not selected for significance (pp. 17-18), plus funnel
  plots and a published/unpublished comparison.
- English-language reports only (p. 15).

### Concepts
continuum of relative autonomy / self-determination; simplex and quasi-simplex
(Guttman); perceived locus of causality; internalization; external,
introjected, identified, integrated regulation; intrinsic motivation (and
three subtypes); amotivation; relative autonomy index (RAI); autonomous vs
controlled motivation; bifactor ESEM (Howard et al. 2016a); latent profile
analysis (Howard et al. 2016b); metric MDS, stress, DAF; I2 heterogeneity;
quality vs quantity of motivation.

### Verdict on the record's characterization
**Confirmed.** A2's description (THEORY-054 note of 2026-11-02, LIT-1996
summary) that "the meta-analysis does not find integrated regulation as a
distinct factor" is right, with two refinements: (a) the paper does not run a
factor analysis; it finds integrated regulation's pooled correlations and
confidence intervals indistinguishable from identified regulation's (r = .913)
and its MDS position "practically indistinguishable"; (b) it leaves open a
small second dimension on which they may differ. Ryan et al. 2022's report
(LIT-1967) that it "did not support the differentiation of integrated
regulation from identified regulation" is accurate. The "partly because too
few studies measured integration" gloss (LIT-1996 history v2) is **not** the
paper's own reason: integrated regulation has k = 84-99 samples; the paper
attributes the problem to the construct/measures ("new ways of
conceptualizing and operationalizing", p. 29), not to sample size. Citation:
authors, title and journal match; Crossref's 2017-12 date stands.

### Bearing on codes
- **THEORY-054** (autonomy graded by integration, not order): its first
  promote_when clause asks for "a measure of integrated regulation that is
  separable from identified regulation and from intrinsic motivation". This
  paper is direct, first-hand evidence that, across the 13 validated scales to
  2016, no such measure exists: r = .913 with identified, overlapping CIs. It
  supports the *graded* half (a single continuum explains 99.75% of the
  structure) while undercutting the *by integration* half as measured: the
  most-integrated rung is empirically not separate. It does not touch the "not
  by order" half. The note of 2026-11-06 can be updated: no longer second-hand.
- **THEORY-1327** (the meta-review's map): confirms its "none that separates
  integrated from identified regulation" gap from the primary meta-analysis.
- **NOTE-1500 / LIT-1967**: second-hand report confirmed; the "too few
  studies" gloss should be dropped or attributed to Ryan et al. only.
- **LEAD-127 / QUESTION-369** (needs vs enhancers under intervention): **no
  bearing.** The paper studies the structure of motivation regulations, not
  needs, their thwarting, functional deficit, or any intervention. It mentions
  need satisfaction only in background (p. 6). LEAD-127 can drop it from its
  list, or keep it only as background.
- **THEORY-120** (Frankfurt's hierarchical identification, THEORY-054's rival):
  indirect. Identified regulation ("in line with personal values", p. 5) is the
  SDT analogue of endorsement; the failure to separate integration from it
  means the self-report data cannot tell a coherence-based account from an
  endorsement-based one.

### Limitations and gaps
- Correlational and cross-sectional; structure of self-report, not of
  motivation or behaviour. "Participants perceive" (p. 24) is the most it shows.
- Correlations are disattenuated; corrected r near .9 for different subscales
  may overstate overlap if reliabilities were underestimated (imputed alphas).
- Heterogeneity near 99% is unexplained; a pooled matrix may describe no
  actual population (the authors concede this, p. 32).
- MDS is exploratory (p. 18); the 1-D vs 2-D choice is by parsimony; the second
  dimension's meaning is speculated.
- Gap: the claim that the continuum is "consistent with the continuum
  hypothesis" with integrated regulation as "the exception" (p. 24) is
  asserted without testing alternative structures (e.g. a two-cluster
  autonomous/controlled model) against the simplex.
- Gap: integration's non-separability is attributed to measures or to the
  theory alternately (pp. 28-29) without evidence that decides between them.
- Small internal inconsistencies between Tables 1 and 2 (e.g. introjected-
  amotivation -.389 vs -.370; introjected-external .600 vs .564), probably
  version drift; the filer should cite the published tables if possible.
- Manuscript, not the version of record; journal pagination not available here.

### Candidate THEORY sentence
"Howard, Gagné and Bureau's (2017) meta-analysis of 486 samples finds that SDT's
motivation regulations follow a continuum-like (simplex) ordering that one
dimension of self-determination accounts for almost entirely, but that
integrated regulation is empirically indistinguishable from identified
regulation (pooled r = .91), as are the three subtypes of intrinsic
motivation, so that the most integrated rung of the continuum is not
measured separately by any validated scale."
- promote_when: a validated measure separates integrated from identified
  regulation and predicts an outcome beyond it, or a later meta-analysis
  (e.g. Van den Broeck et al. 2021, as LIT-1967 reports) confirms the
  non-separation first-hand.
