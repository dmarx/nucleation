# QUESTION-tmpvoufs: What lets those who act for an organization hold the principals who direct them accountable for the conditions under which they are required to act, and does any principal–agent account carry accountability in that reverse direction?

## Why it is a question

The record has the downward half. CLAIM-687 raises what an arrangement's
controllers owe with how indispensable it is; CLAIM-624 names a duty to
keep violations knowable, contestable and correctable; CLAIM-tmpb47ul says
that control, practical obligation and blame can come apart, with blame
landing downstream. None says by what means those downstream can make the
upstream party answer for the conditions it set. QUESTION-461 asks when a
whole's dependence on subordinated participants becomes their authority
over it; CLAIM-tmpe5u7i gives one reason it may not, a commitment that
removes withdrawal. This asks for the instruments.

The principal–agent literature the record has read runs the other way.
THEORY-1371 (incentive design for returning agents) treats the principal
as the designer and the agent's behaviour as what is designed for. A17
§9 lists what an answer would have to supply in practice: "meaningful
authority to halt unsafe processes, transparent information about system
limitations, sufficient resources to perform oversight, and formal
channels for challenging upstream decisions."

## What would count as an answer

- **An instrument.** A mechanism (protected refusal, escalation with
  authority, a resource claim enforceable against the principal, collective
  voice) shown in a case or a model to move a principal's choice of
  conditions, not only its response to one failure.
- **A model.** A principal–agent account in which the agent's payoff
  depends on its ability to contest the principal's choice of conditions,
  with a result on when contest pays.
- **A negative.** An argument that, where the agent's effort is supplied
  from commitment rather than for terms, no such instrument can work from
  the agent's side alone, so that the accountability must come from a third
  party.
