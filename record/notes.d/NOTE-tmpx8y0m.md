# NOTE-tmpx8y0m: Castela and others (2017) — Ccl2/Ccr2 signalling recruits a distinct fetal microchimeric population that rescues delayed maternal wound healing

## Contribution

Fetal microchimeric cells (FMCs) traffic into maternal circulation and persist for decades; on maternal injury they migrate to the site and aid repair. Here, in mice, a reparative FMC subset is CD11b+ CD34+ CD31+ (fetal "MPCs") and highly expresses CCR2. Its ligand CCL2 (secreted early by wound monocytes/endothelial cells) recruits these fetal cells to maternal skin wounds, where they transdifferentiate into endothelial cells and stimulate angiogenesis via Cxcl1. Low-dose CCL2 improves delayed wound healing in pregnant and post-partum (early and 6-month) mice but never in virgins, and the effect is CCR2-dependent (shown with Ccr2KO crosses).

## Key results

- Abstract: "Ccl2 administration improves delayed maternal wound healing in pregnant and postpartum mice but never in virgin ones."
- Results: "90% of the circulating fetal cells expressed Ccr2 1 day after skin injury versus only 1.50% of those from pregnant unwounded mice."
- Results: "the presence of FMCs is mandatory to promote maternal angiogenesis and wound healing through Ccl2" (CCL2 and PBS identical in virgin mice).
- Results: "only very small numbers of fetal cells are sufficient to improve both normal and delayed maternal wound healing" (wounds contained ~6.4% fetal cells).
- Discussion: "This study lays the foundations for a new concept natural fetal stem cell therapy."
