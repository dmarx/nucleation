# QUESTION-tmpiu50n: Is an inquiry's current set of conclusions, or its logically closed belief set, enough to retain to fix its future revisions, and if not, what must a single AGM change of a closed theory have besides the theory?

## The question

QUESTION-105 asks for the least an inquiry must retain to support a family
of future revisions. Its body names the lower edge: whether current
conclusions alone are enough. This question asks that, and what the
belief-revision literature adds for one change of full belief.

## The answer

Three Active entries settle it, in the negative and then for one change:

- **CLAIM-294** answers for graded belief. Two joint distributions share
  P(H) = 0.9, and learning the same condition sends one to 1.0 and the
  other to 0.8. Keeping only the current marginal merges histories that
  need different updates.
- **THEORY-347** answers for full belief. The bases {p, q} and
  {p, p ↔ q} have one closure, yet revised by ¬p one yields q and the
  other ¬q. A closed belief set does not fix its own revision. A base,
  marking which beliefs are held in their own right, is one state that
  does.
- **THEORY-353** says what a closed theory needs instead. The
  contractions meeting the six basic AGM postulates are exactly the
  partial meet contractions, and with the two supplementary postulates
  exactly those whose selection of remainders is generated by a
  transitive relation. So a selection function or ordering must be added
  to the theory, and in the finite case distinct ones give distinct
  contractions. Revision follows by the Levi identity.

All three concern one change, not iterated revision. What the least
retained state is for a family of revisions, and whether it looks like
provenance, dependencies and defeated alternatives, remain QUESTION-105's.
