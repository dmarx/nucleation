# THEORY-1505: Bleakley and Lin find that US cities at historical portage sites, whose transport advantage ended with canals and railroads by about 1900, remain 60–100% denser than comparable sites in the same river watersheds, increasingly so relative to 1850, are neither oversupplied nor cheap in housing, infrastructure or other sunk assets conditional on density, and grow like comparably dense places from about 1930, with no effect where the upstream watershed was smallest; they read this as path dependence under strong local increasing returns, with portage a coordination device that selected the high-density equilibrium, rather than slow reversion through sunk capital, while granting that their results are not direct evidence of multiple equilibria

## Source

Bleakley & Lin (2012), [LIT-2206](../literature.d/LIT-2206.md); read in [NOTE-1697](../notes.d/NOTE-1697.md) from
Philadelphia Fed WP 11-38 (September 6, 2011) and NBER w16314 (August
2010). The QJE version of record was not read. Page numbers are WP
11-38's.

## What was actually shown

- **The advantage ended** (§3.2): the fall-line county's share of
  water-transport employment fell from 13.1% in 1880 to 2.6% in 1930.
- **Persistence** (§5): "The dummy variable for proximity predicts 60% to
  100% increases in density"; "90% lower, and insignificantly different
  from zero" at the smallest watersheds.
- **No decay** (§7): a difference of 0.456 (s.e. 0.092) between the late
  20th- and 19th-century effect, on consistent county boundaries with
  county fixed effects.
- **Conditional on lagged density** (§7, Figure 8): the coefficient "goes
  asymptotically to zero", insignificant from 1930.
- **No reversion signature** (§8.3): no differences in housing age, price
  to rent, infrastructure, crime, government and more, conditional on
  density.

What could have come out otherwise: the effect could have decayed since
1850, or portage counties could have carried old, cheap, oversupplied
housing and infrastructure, the marks of slow reversion.

## What this does not say

- **That portages sit in a second equilibrium.** "our results do not
  constitute direct evidence of multiple equilibria" (footnote 28).
- **That no sunk factor carries it.** One persistent, important enough
  factor is not ruled out (p. 33).
- **That every lost advantage leaves lock-in.** "not every historical
  agglomeration has persisted after losing its initial reason for being"
  (p. 33); the effect needs enough coordinated activity.
- **Anything about withdrawn policy support.** The advantage was natural;
  carrying the result to support withdrawn, as against the TVA
  ([THEORY-1461](THEORY-1461.md)) or [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md), is the record's analogy.
- **That the founders persisted.** The unit is a site's density, not its
  people.
