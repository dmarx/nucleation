# THEORY-tmpr8fns: Liebman and Mahoney hold that when an organization's budget expires at year-end and the value of its spending is uncertain, it holds a precautionary buffer through the year and spends it on low-value projects at the deadline, so that spending spikes and quality drops at year-end, and that letting unused funds roll over, given the budget-setter's commitment not to claw them back, removes most of the waste

## Source

Liebman and Mahoney (NBER Working Paper 19481, 2013; AER 2017),
[LIT-tmp4kmcg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4kmcg.md); read in [NOTE-tmp09iyo](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp09iyo.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The mechanism, in a model** (§2): with concave returns and uncertain
  value shocks, and a period-1 spending function concave in the shock, the
  agency saves early and "always completely exhausts its budget by the end
  of the year", including on spending below the social cost of funds.
- **The spike** (§3): "8.7 percent of spending occurs in the last week of
  the year" in federal procurement, 2004–2009, in all major agencies. This
  could have come out flat.
- **The quality drop** (§4): last-week I.T. projects have 2.2 to 5.6 times
  higher odds of a lower rating; higher end-of-year variance in volume and
  quality is set against a priority-ordering rival.
- **Commitment** (Proposition 3): without it, rollover is never used.
- **Rollover** (§5.3): DOJ's authority "reduces the volume of year-end
  spending by 9.5 percentage points"; the quality evidence rests on one
  last-week project.
- **Most of the waste** (§5.4): a CRRA calibration, in which a one-month
  grace period gives 41 percent and a four-month one 90 percent of full
  rollover's welfare gain.

## What this does not say

- That year-end spending is wasteful wherever budgets expire: the evidence
  is one government's procurement.
- That the buffer is held against emergencies in particular: the model's
  shocks are to the value of spending, of any kind.
- That rollover helps without commitment; by Proposition 3 it does not.
- That expiry has no benefit: the authors grant that the deadline may push
  out projects that would otherwise languish (p. 36).
- That agents misuse funds: the model assumes shared preferences. The
  misuse case is Siemroth's ([LIT-tmpuad7h](../literature.d/LIT-tmpuad7h.md)).
