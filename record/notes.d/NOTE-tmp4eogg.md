# NOTE-tmp4eogg: Merton, The Matthew Effect in Science, read for what the Matthew effect names and how it relates to cumulative advantage

## Contribution

A name and a functional analysis, not a model. The essay coins "the
Matthew effect" (with the "41st chair" and the "Ecclesiasticus
component"), separates its effect on careers (an inequity) from its effect
on the communication system (possibly functional), and states testable
hypotheses: citation counts for unequal-rank multiple discoveries,
readership by rank. The reading is [THEORY-tmp7rz62](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7rz62.md).

## Key results

Page numbers are Science's (PDF page n is p. 55 + n).

- **The pattern** (p. 58). Laureates "repeatedly observe that eminent
  scientists get disproportionately great credit for their contributions
  to science while relatively unknown scientists tend to get
  disproportionately little credit for comparable contributions."
- **The definition** (p. 58): "the Matthew effect consists in the
  accruing of greater increments of recognition for particular scientific
  contributions to scientists of considerable repute and the withholding
  of such recognition from scientists who have not yet made their mark."
  The evidence is "presumptive": laureates testify "not as victims" but as
  "unwitting beneficiaries".
- **Fixed places and a ratchet** (p. 57). The "41st chair" is "an artifact
  of having a fixed number of places available at the summit of
  recognition"; and a "ratchet effect", such that "once having achieved a
  particular degree of eminence, they do not later fall much below that
  level". Recognition "can be converted into an instrumental asset as
  enlarged facilities are made available", giving "differential access to
  the means of scientific production".
- **The communication hypothesis** (p. 59): "a scientific contribution
  will have greater visibility in the community of scientists when it is
  introduced by a scientist of high rank than when it is introduced by one
  who has not yet made his mark." Dysfunctional for careers, the effect
  "may operate to heighten the visibility of new scientific
  communications". The Coles' visibility scores (85 for laureates, 72 for
  Academy members, 38 and 17 for others) are called "tangential rather than
  central to the hypothesis".
- **Not merely halo** (p. 60): "This greater visibility is not merely the
  result of a halo effect"; socialization, taste for important problems
  and self-assurance account for part of it.
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy** (pp. 61 to 62). Work presented by an
  outstanding scientist "may have greater stimulus value", and "This
  principle represents a special application of the self-fulfilling
  prophecy (42)". Note 42 cites Merton's 1948 essay in the Antioch Review
  ([LIT-1398](../literature.d/LIT-1398.md)). On p. 62 the pattern is "a self-confirming process", and
  "Like other self-fulfilling prophecies, this one becomes dysfunctional
  under certain conditions".
- **Dysfunction** (p. 62): "When the Matthew effect is thus transformed
  into an idol of authority, it violates the norm of universalism"; its
  frequency among editors and referees "remains largely a matter of
  anecdote".
- **Cumulative advantage** (p. 62): "One institutional version of the
  Matthew effect ... is expressed in the principle of cumulative advantage
  that operates in many systems of social stratification to produce the
  same result: the rich get richer at a rate that makes the poor become
  relatively poorer (46)." Note 46: "Derek Price perceived this
  implication of the Matthew principle" (Nature 206, 233, 1965, not read
  here). The example: a few universities train a large share of the
  doctorates who later become laureates.

## Assumptions

- Laureates' testimony as evidence of a pattern, "presumptive".
- Recognition as the coin of science's reward system.
- That comparable contributions can be identified across authors.
- A functionalist frame: functions and dysfunctions for a social system.

## Concepts

Matthew effect; the 41st chair; the ratchet effect; the Ecclesiasticus
component (status enhancement); visibility; functional redundancy of
multiple discoveries; the self-fulfilling prophecy; universalism;
cumulative advantage.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-817](../claims.d/CLAIM-817.md)** (background for the name; qualifies its naming clause).
  It supports that "Matthew effect" is Merton's name and that he ties it
  to cumulative advantage and "the rich get richer". It does not support
  that the four names "name one feedback": the Matthew effect proper is
  credit for a given contribution allotted by rank, and the essay's own
  mechanisms include a fixed number of top places and a ratchet, neither
  of which is attachment in proportion to size. It states no proportional
  rule; the implication for growth it credits to Price. [ARG-tmpaf6km](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpaf6km.md).
- **[TERM-tmpeguzj](../terms.d/TERM-tmpeguzj.md)** (draws on it). The senses of "Matthew effect", filed
  with this reading.
- **[NOTE-tmpa7yd2](NOTE-tmpa7yd2.md)**, **[TERM-tmpg1vx2](../terms.d/TERM-tmpg1vx2.md)** (qualified the same way). Newman
  lists "Matthew effect" among the names of the Yule process; that is the
  formal sense, which the essay does not state.
- **[LIT-1398](../literature.d/LIT-1398.md)**, **[NOTE-1409](NOTE-1409.md)**, **[CASE-141](../cases.d/CASE-141.md)**, **[CLAIM-402](../claims.d/CLAIM-402.md)** (supports, a
  link the record did not draw). Merton calls the communication side of
  the Matthew effect "a special application of the self-fulfilling

- **[LIT-1330](../literature.d/LIT-1330.md)** (parallel). Status-confirming loops in institutions, there
  through rankings.
- **[CLAIM-702](../claims.d/CLAIM-702.md)**, **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**, **[THEORY-1928](../theory.d/THEORY-1928.md)** (parallel and contrast).
  Credit and blame allocated by position or visibility rather than
  contribution; Merton's credit-by-rank is the mirror of blame falling on

- **[CLAIM-830](../claims.d/CLAIM-830.md)**, **[CLAIM-803](../claims.d/CLAIM-803.md)**, **[CLAIM-841](../claims.d/CLAIM-841.md)**, **[ARG-127](../arguments.d/ARG-127.md)**,

  formal sense. Merton's university example is an instance of
