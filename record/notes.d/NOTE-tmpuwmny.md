# NOTE-tmpuwmny: Halperin, read for what the Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem covers and does not, as a stand-in for Mermin and Wagner

## Contribution

What the theorem proved, on what model and condition, and what it does
not imply, by a physicist who knew its authors; and a new rigorous
extension to itinerant electrons with SU(2) symmetry. The reading is
THEORY-tmpqma2p.

## Key results

1. **What Mermin and Wagner proved** (§I): "a similar proof of the
   absence of long-range order in one and two dimensions for a
   Heisenberg ferromagnet or antiferromagnet, with SU(2) symmetry, or a
   magnetic system with U(1) symmetry and an order parameter
   perpendicular to the symmetry axis"; "Mermin and Wagner discussed
   spins on a lattice, whereas Hohenberg was concerned with bosons or
   fermions in the continuum". Submitted a week before Hohenberg's and
   printed "some six months before Pierre's", so the proofs "should be
   properly referred to collectively as the "Hohenberg-Mermin-Wagner
   theorem"".
2. **Range** (§II): "The argument for the non-existence of long-range
   order, as given in the Mermin-Wagner paper requires that these second
   moments be finite"; for ferromagnetic couplings falling "slower than
   1/r⁴" long-range order "should indeed be possible in two dimensions".
3. **What it does not say** (§II): "The HMW theorem sheds no light on the
   question of whether quasi-long-range order can exist in any particular
   system." The reading that it rules out long-range order in any 2D
   system breaking a continuous symmetry "is not completely correct":
   bond-orientational order in a 2D crystal can be long-range, because
   "the generator of these rotations cannot be written as an unweighted
   sum of local operators". And "The HMW theorem has nothing to say about
   the existence or non-existence of long-range order in a quantum
   mechanical system at T = 0."
4. **The heuristic** (§II, eqs. 2–3): a stiffness ρ_s gives
   ⟨[θ(r) − θ(0)]²⟩ ∼ (T/πρ_s) log r and correlations ∼ r^(−T/2πρ_s); for
   Heisenberg spins correlations "decay exponentially with distance at
   any non-zero temperature".
5. **Equilibrium** (§III, eq. 12): the inequality used "follows directly
   from the quantum version of the fluctuation dissipation theorem".
6. **Membranes** (§II): Gaussian fluctuations of the normal diverge, but
   coupling to in-plane strain renormalizes the bending constant, "which
   diverges for k → 0, and which removes the divergence of fluctuations",
   so long-range orientational order is predicted to survive.

## Assumptions

As stated for the theorem: a continuous symmetry generated by a sum of
local operators, finite second moments, a Gibbs state, T > 0.

## Concepts

Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem; quasi-long-range order; second moment
of the coupling; bond-orientational (hexatic) order; tethered membrane;
fluctuation-dissipation theorem.

## Bearing on the record

- **LIT-2985**: stands in for the paper's scope (ADR-114); restates
  items 1–2 and 5 of the 1966 paper, not its proof. It shows the
  exchange's gloss ("no continuous symmetry breaking in two dimensions")
  to overstate it.
- **CLAIM-842**: **qualifies.** The rigorous result needs (i) a
  continuous symmetry whose generator is a sum of local generators,
  which CLAIM-842's first caveat already finds missing for a trained
  representation; (ii) finite second moments, so long edges on an
  interaction graph could restore order; (iii) equilibrium, through the
  fluctuation-dissipation theorem; and (iv) the membrane shows a Gaussian
  divergence removed by nonlinear coupling, order surviving where the
  Gaussian criterion predicts disorder (ARG-tmpx5evn). THEORY-tmpqma2p
  `bounds` CLAIM-842.
- **CLAIM-841**: **supports the Goldstone and XY rows' no-tuning point,
  and scopes a citation.** The divergence that kills order holds at every
  T > 0; item 4 gives the XY exponent the claim rechecked. But item 3
  means the XY row cannot rest on Mermin–Wagner: it rests on Kosterlitz
  and Thouless (LIT-2969) and Kosterlitz (1974), LIT-tmph3tia.
- **CLAIM-846**: **parallel**; item 4 is the Gaussian spin-wave
  computation the claim checks numerically.

## Limitations

A five-page retrospective; it does not reproduce Mermin and Wagner's
proof, and its exceptions are argued with references, not proved here.
