# LIT-2189: The effects of cash transfers on adult labor market outcomes

Baird, S., McKenzie, D. & Özler, B. (2018). The effects of cash transfers on adult labor market outcomes. *IZA Journal of Development and Migration* 8(1): 22. doi:10.1186/s40176-018-0131-9. Read as World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 8404 (April 2018), doi:10.1596/1813-9450-8404.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1684](../notes.d/NOTE-1684.md). Page numbers are the working paper's.*

- **The prediction fails for prime-age adults.** The labour-leisure
  model says recipients "should work less and earn less" (p. 3); in fact
  "prime age adults tend to see very little change in either the amount
  they work, or the amount they earn" under conditional and unconditional
  transfers or charitable grants (p. 19).
- **Who does work less.** Pensioners: "large declines in labor force
  participation among the elderly themselves" (p. 9). And Syrian refugees
  given winter cash in Lebanon, in one discontinuity study (p. 11).
- **Transfers for job search and start-up raise work.** In Uganda,
  conditional start-up grants gave "a 17% increase in work hours and 38%
  increase in earnings" (p. 15); the authors read the general pattern as
  easing liquidity and insurance constraints.
- **Remittances.** "the more reliable studies in the literature do not
  support the view that remittances make recipients lazy and work less"
  (p. 13).
- **The income effect is real but narrow.** It "appears most apparent
  when transfers are large and/or prolonged (as with lottery winnings and
  pensions)" (p. 19).
- **The untested case.** "the impacts are likely to be different when
  transfers are dependable and permanent rather than temporary and/or
  surprises" (p. 20).

## Standing in the record

Filed as a counterpoint to the branch's dependency claims. It is about
households and individuals, not states or projects, so it does not test
the record's state- and project-level aid evidence. What it removes is the
household analogy behind them: assured cash does not, as a rule, make poor
adults work less, so "slack effort" in [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) and the Samaritan's
logic of [CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md) and [THEORY-1480](../theory.d/THEORY-1480.md) cannot borrow support from
that intuition. What it supports is the distinction [CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md) draws:
cash that relaxes a constraint while the recipient does the work raises
effort. It is the household evidence's own limit that the case the
record's claims are about, dependable and permanent support, is the one it
leaves untested. Filed from it: [THEORY-1466](../theory.d/THEORY-1466.md).
