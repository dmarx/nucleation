# CLAIM-194: Gap test cannot tell made from imposed boundaries

## The claim

[CLAIM-157](CLAIM-157.md) makes a combined form one system, relative to a target, when its
members form a nearly decomposable block whose gap beats the Occam factor.
[CLAIM-174](CLAIM-174.md) takes "one system there" as its first condition for agency.
Both read the boundary off the strength of interaction inside a candidate
block against interaction across its edge.

[THEORY-073](../theory.d/THEORY-073.md) says of the Markov blanket that "the formalism also gives the
same description to a membrane the cell makes and a beam nobody's states
make". [THEORY-066](../theory.d/THEORY-066.md) gives the formal reason: the partition "takes the
internal set as an input" and "never outputs the internal set". The gap
criterion is better off than the blanket on the second point, since it
compares candidate partitions and does not take one as given. It is not
better off on the first. Two pendulums joined by a beam clear the threshold
for a target such as the next swing, and so does a cell. The criterion has
no term for who made the coupling.

The record holds two accounts that put the line there:

- [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md): an organism differs from a candle flame by "several distinct
  constraints that produce one another at different time scales". That is
  production, not coupling strength.
- [THEORY-1119](../theory.d/THEORY-1119.md): minimal agency needs individuality in which "the system
  distinguishes itself from its environment, and an observer does not do it
  for the system".

So the block test can find a system in the sense of a unit of description,
but the individuality [THEORY-1119](../theory.d/THEORY-1119.md) requires is a further condition. When
[CLAIM-174](CLAIM-174.md) uses the block test as its first condition, it admits jointly
coupled forms whose boundary is maintained from outside.

## What it does not say

- **That the block criterion is wrong as an answer to [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md).** As a
  test of when a description should name a unit, the counter leaves it
  standing. It bears on [CLAIM-174](CLAIM-174.md)'s use of it as the individuality
  condition of agency.
- **That observer-relativity is the problem.** [CLAIM-174](CLAIM-174.md) already concedes
  that its tests are relative to level and target. This counter holds even
  with the target fixed.
- **That production is measurable where coupling is.** [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md) is
  Proposed, and its own promote_when asks for a flame and a protocell to be
  analysed the same way. Neither has been done.

## What would answer it

Either a case showing that, for a natural target, imposed boundaries
systematically fail the gap test while produced ones pass it, so the
criterion tracks production after all. Or a reply that [CLAIM-174](CLAIM-174.md)'s later
conditions (adaptation of the policy, governance over regimes) already
exclude externally maintained blocks, so the first condition need not.

## Note of 2026-10-21: regulation of exchange

A9 §3 of the exchange of 2026-10-20 separated predictive closure from
organizational closure and proposed that "A living or agentive boundary
may be distinguished by its regulation of exchange, rather than by the
absence of exchange". That is [THEORY-1119](../theory.d/THEORY-1119.md)'s interactional asymmetry, which
A9 does not cite. A8 §1 adds the informational side of this claim's point:
since agents are coupled to their environments, exact closure "cannot
straightforwardly serve as a necessary condition for agency", and since
noise is closed, it is not sufficient either. So neither a coupling
criterion (this claim) nor a closure criterion ([THEORY-820](../theory.d/THEORY-820.md)) tells a
produced boundary from an imposed one. The two closures are separated in
[TERM-068](../terms.d/TERM-068.md) and [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md).

## Note of 2026-11-01: the mechanists' discriminator is a coupling notion too


Craver and Bechtel ([LIT-1928](../literature.d/LIT-1928.md), [NOTE-1491](../notes.d/NOTE-1491.md)) concede that interlevel
experiments show only prima facie that an item is a component. They say
the difference from a background condition is whether it is
"interactively integrated into the system" (p. 553, n. 10). That is a
coupling notion, so it inherits this claim's point that it has no term
for who made the coupling. An outside controller coupled to the system
in both directions passes their four experiments exactly as an internal
producer does ([THEORY-1287](../theory.d/THEORY-1287.md), [CLAIM-530](CLAIM-530.md)). Same structure, not
shown same mechanism: this claim is about observational statistics, and
theirs is about interventions. Their framework draws a mechanism's
boundary by its parthood condition, which takes the boundary as an input,
as this claim says a gap criterion must.

No status, title or warrant changes.

## Note of 2026-11-22: an instance in population form

[CASE-347](../cases.d/CASE-347.md) runs [CASE-101](../cases.d/CASE-101.md)'s populations through the priced block test,
lagged over windows of 1 to 16 steps ([LEAD-026](../leads.d/LEAD-026.md)). Two of the populations
are matched in turnover and aggregate statistics:

- in one, the members respond to a common driver;
- in the other, an institution trains and selects the members and does not
  answer to them.

They get the same verdict at every window: each set of members passes only
with its external variable. Every information measure computed agrees to
within a few per cent. An institution that does answer to its members
differs only in the direction of that feedback, and the price removes it
beyond four steps. This is the claim's beam and membrane, with the imposed
boundary indistinguishable from a common input. [CLAIM-926](CLAIM-926.md)
corroborates this claim. The claim is unchanged.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Bourrat 2022 and Bourrat et al. 2024, read

Read for [LEAD-176](../leads.d/LEAD-176.md): Bourrat's endogenization paper ([LIT-1927](../literature.d/LIT-1927.md), [NOTE-2361](../notes.d/NOTE-2361.md))
and the scaffolding group's 2024 statement ([LIT-3137](../literature.d/LIT-3137.md), [NOTE-2362](../notes.d/NOTE-2362.md)),
the second standing in for Black, Bourrat and Rainey ([LIT-1940](../literature.d/LIT-1940.md), Unfetched).
A parallel, not evidence for the claim's argument; no edge.

- **The same distinction, drawn in biology.** Bourrat's "Darwinian-like"
  collectives get their properties from "external (that is, ecological)
  individuating mechanisms"; "Darwinian" ones from internal mechanisms
  (§2, Table 1). Black et al.'s patch collectives have real interaction
  among their members, through the patch's resources, and still have
  their boundary and bottleneck "purely external to the collective"
  (§4). A criterion that read interaction strength would pass them, as
  the claim says a gap criterion passes the beam.
- **Bourrat tells them apart by an intervention, not a coupling
  measure**: lift the scaffold and see whether the collective's
  properties survive. That is the kind of test [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md) says separates
  the two. In his model it lifts one part of the scaffold only, the
  migration ban, relative to a candidate (the patch) declared in advance.
- **The 2024 paper complicates the dichotomy.** Its constructive route
  has cells make the scaffold, which first acts on them as an imposed
  boundary and is later taken into the collective, and it holds that "a
  boundary shift between what is considered internal and external
  occurs during the emergence of new levels of individuality". A
  produced boundary can act as an imposed one before it is endogenized.
  The claim's distinction stands, but which side a boundary is on can
  change over a transition.

No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity', A42 §7, a miscitation

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-1308 — Proposed; filed from A42 of the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity' -->

A42 §7 cites this claim as distinguishing "a boundary produced and
maintained by an organization from a boundary merely identified or imposed
by an observer". That misreads it. The claim is that a gap or coupling
criterion cannot tell a boundary a system produces from one imposed on it
by an outside coupling, a beam between two pendulums; no observer is
involved, and the claim says a criterion fails, not that it draws the
distinction. What A42 builds on, "A meaningful organizational boundary may
involve the capacity to regulate exchange across it, including
informational exchange", is [THEORY-1119](../theory.d/THEORY-1119.md)'s interactional asymmetry, which
this claim's note of 2026-10-21 names; the informational extension is A42's
own and is filed in [CLAIM-1308](CLAIM-1308.md). Bearing: miscitation; the claim is
unchanged. The wording of this note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session), reporting A's (ChatGPT's) text; adopting what it reports is the owner's call.
