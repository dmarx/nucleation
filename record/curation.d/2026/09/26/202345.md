The owner asked for James Ladyman and Don Ross's accounts of **ontic
structural realism (OSR)** and **rainforest realism** to be on the record,
ideally from their book *Every Thing Must Go: Metaphysics Naturalized* (OUP
2007, with Spurrett and Collier).

## What could not be obtained

Only legitimate sources were used: publishers, repositories, the authors' own
pages and the Wayback Machine. Shadow libraries were ruled out.

- ***Every Thing Must Go*.** Not open access, and OUP returned 403. No free
  sample was found. An archive.org upload labelled "Public Domain" is an
  unauthorised copy and was not used.
- **Ladyman 1998, "What is structural realism?"** (SHPS), the founding OSR
  paper. Paywalled, with no repository copy.
- **Ross 2000, "Rainforest Realism: a Dennettian theory of existence"** (in
  *Dennett's Philosophy*, MIT). Closed access.
- **Berenstain & Ladyman 2012, "Ontic Structural Realism and Modality".**
  On PhilArchive, which blocked this session, as did the Wayback Machine.

## What was read instead

Four freely available works, each read in full and `Active`:

| work | what it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Ladyman 2001, *Science, Metaphysics and Structural Realism* ([LIT-tmp32dfc](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp32dfc.md)) | the earliest free statement of OSR by its founder |
| Ladyman, *Patterns All the Way Up* (2024 preprint, MIT 2026) ([LIT-tmpazqjv](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpazqjv.md)) | the core of rainforest realism, and his current second thoughts |
| Ross 2008, *Ontic Structural Realism and Economics* ([LIT-tmp9hl55](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp9hl55.md)) | OSR carried beyond physics |
| Dennett 1991, *Real Patterns* ([LIT-tmpeniq7](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpeniq7.md)) | the criterion that rainforest realism generalises. Read because Ross 2000 was unreachable. |

**What each supplies:**

- **Ladyman 2001:**
  - the quantum non-individuality argument, framed as an underdetermination
    between individuals and non-individuals, not a proof;
  - entanglement against Humean supervenience;
  - theories describing "the modal structure of the phenomena";
  - a non-eliminative line: "the phenomena have structure but they are not
    structure".
- ***Patterns All the Way Up*:**
  - "to be is to be a real pattern";
  - scale-relative, effective ontology;
  - no single hierarchy of levels;
  - agnosticism, not denial, about a fundamental level (fn. 9);
  - the Principle of Naturalistic Closure quoted from the book (p. 37), then
    called "problematic" by Ladyman himself.
- **Ross 2008:** individuals are "book-keeping devices", not denied. That is a
  deflationary third position, which Chakravartty's dilemma ([LIT-196](../../record/literature.d/LIT-196.md)) does not
  address.
- **Dennett 1991:** a pattern is real iff some description beats the bit map,
  "whether or not anyone can concoct it". Rival real patterns can coexist.

## How far the two ideas are covered

- **OSR:** covered by the new works together with [LIT-045](../../record/literature.d/LIT-045.md) (the SEP entry, by
  Ladyman) and [LIT-196](../../record/literature.d/LIT-196.md). The explicit eliminative/non-eliminative choice still
  rests on those two held notes: no new text by the authors stakes it out.
- **Rainforest realism:** covered for real patterns, scale relativity, the
  rejection of a fundamental level of individuals, and the PNC.

**Gaps:**

- The **Primacy of Physics Constraint** is stated in none of the four.
- The **book's own real-patterns definition** (Ross 2000 and *Every Thing
  Must Go*), which adds projectibility to Dennett's compression, is referred
  to but not quoted.

The book, Ross 2000, or Berenstain & Ladyman would close both. Berenstain &
Ladyman is the one to try first, from a session that can reach PhilArchive.

## Corrections the readings raise

These are recorded here, not written into the older notes, because each is
about what the *new* sources say.

- **[NOTE-038](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-038.md) and [NOTE-129](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-129.md)** credit the projectibility and perspective clauses
  to "real patterns" in general. Dennett's own criterion is plain lossless
  compression.
- **[NOTE-129](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-129.md) has Ladyman and Ross "deny" a fundamental level.** *Patterns All
  the Way Up* fn. 9 says they are agnostic about one. That may be a later
  retreat, or a misreading; the book would settle which.
- **The record's two accounts of real patterns disagree** on whether the
  fundamental level is itself a pattern: Wallace ([LIT-151](../../record/literature.d/LIT-151.md)) against Ladyman &
  Lorenzetti ([LIT-184](../../record/literature.d/LIT-184.md)). *Patterns All the Way Up* states the disagreement.
