# LIT-1940: Ecological scaffolding and the evolution of individuality

Andrew J. Black, Pierrick Bourrat, Paul B. Rainey (2020). "Ecological scaffolding and the evolution of individuality." *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 4(3): 426–436. DOI-10.1038/s41559-019-1086-9.

## Key takeaways

*Seeded from citing papers, not read.* An externally imposed population structure (discrete resource patches with dispersal between them) can give collectives of cells variation, differential reproduction and heredity at the collective level before the collectives have any mechanism of their own. Griesemer and Shavit ([LIT-188](LIT-188.md), §2) cite it for a "scaffolded imposition of a linkage between cell growth rate and dispersion" and fault its "classical island migration" setting for neglecting Wright's shifting-balance tradition.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-01 from the re-reading of [LIT-188](LIT-188.md) ([NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md)). [NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md) notes that [LIT-005](LIT-005.md) names it as a test case. It is the constructed case in which collective boundaries are imposed from outside before any are produced from inside, which is [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s distinction and [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)'s, posed as an evolutionary model.

On 2026-11-22 ([LEAD-176](../leads.d/LEAD-176.md)) its lawful copies were located and could not be fetched; see the status note. Two read works restate it. [LIT-1927](LIT-1927.md) §4 ([NOTE-tmp1shj8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp1shj8.md)) gives its setting and verdict: patches, resource depletion before the patch generation T, single-cell founding of new patches, slow growers winning when T is long, and collectives that are Darwinian-like, their bottleneck and patch boundary "purely external to the collective". [LIT-tmp6nn9d](LIT-tmp6nn9d.md) ([NOTE-tmpculbo](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpculbo.md)) restates the setting and the framing. Neither gives its equations, the parameter regimes, or its result on reproductive division of labour, which the preprint's abstract (from bioRxiv's API) reports: "When this timescale is sufficiently long and new collectives are founded by single cells, collectives experience conditions that favour evolution of a reproductive division of labour." Those stay unverified until the paper is read.
