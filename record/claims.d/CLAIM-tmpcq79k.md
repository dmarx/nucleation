# CLAIM-tmpcq79k: Under partial relational constitution, a translation always changes the participants, audience and norms that the relational-constitution thesis makes constituents of the act, so whether the act survives turns on a correspondence of relations the structure cannot supply from inside

## The objection

[CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md): which act an utterance performs "is partly constituted by its
relations to participants, norms and expected uptake, so a transformation
that changes those relations can change the act and not only its
realization". [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s ontological clause rests on it.

A translation changes those relations by definition. Its audience is the
target language's readers, not the source's. It is read under the target
culture's norms and genres. Usually it has a second speaker, the
translator. And it changes the words too, which are the intrinsic part
that "partly" leaves the act. So in a translation neither the relational
constituents nor the intrinsic ones are held fixed. On [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md), whether
the act survives cannot be read off either structure. It depends on
whether the changed relations count as the same relations.

The record's statements of survival all say this, in one word each. A191
§1, in [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md): "Changing u while preserving the appropriate
relationships may conserve the act's identity." [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md), quoting A218 §1:
source and translation "may realize corresponding relational
organizations". "Appropriate" and "corresponding" name a map from the
source's relations to the target's. Nothing inside the source's
organization, or inside the target's, says which map that is. This is
[CLAIM-138](CLAIM-138.md)'s point, that bare structure cannot pick out the relations that
count, met at the level of [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) by the move every translation makes.
[CLAIM-138](CLAIM-138.md) spares [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) ("'Partly constituted' already grants a role to
what is not bare structure"), and rightly for a single utterance. Under
translation the non-structural part changes as well, so "partly" no longer
helps.

Two consequences follow.

- **[QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) changes kind.** It asks how to operationalize
  "corresponding communicative environments and responses" without
  building the preferred notion of fidelity into the measurement. Under
  [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) the correspondence is part of what the act is, so the question
  is about the act, not only about measuring it.
- **The record's model of translation does not cover the case.** [CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md)
  models translation as (u, s, a, k, g) → (u′, s, a, k′, g′), where a is
  the audience ([TERM-031](../terms.d/TERM-031.md)). The audience is held fixed. The case [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)
  makes hard, a new audience, is outside the model.

[CLAIM-004](CLAIM-004.md), which [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) rests on, already lacks the criterion this
needs. It says the manuscript "has no way to tell (ii) from (iv) in a given
pair", representation-changing equivariance from structural
reorganization, and that a restated criterion would turn on "what the
change of target context requires". That is again a correspondence of
contexts. [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s four speakers change one relation at a time with the
words fixed. They do not show what happens when every relation changes at
once.

## What it does not say

It does not say that acts never survive translation, nor that [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) is
false. A teasing remark can plainly be translated as a teasing remark. It
says that on [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) such survival is a fact about a correspondence
between two relational organizations, and that the record has no account of
which correspondences preserve an act. It does not touch [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) for
transformations that keep the audience and the norms, such as [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s
reattributions.

## What would answer it

- An account of which correspondences of participants, audiences and norms
  preserve an act, fixed independently of judged fidelity. [QUESTION-030](../questions.d/QUESTION-030.md)
  asks the nearest question, which interventions tell constitutive
  relations from accompanying ones.
- Or a restatement of [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) in which the constituents are relations to
  roles (addressee, authority, co-participant), not to particular people.
  A new audience in the same role would then keep the constituent, and the
  open part would narrow to whether the target culture's norms define the
  same roles.
