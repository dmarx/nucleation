# LIT-tmpjbd64: Friction-In-Design Regulation as 21st Century Time, Place, and Manner Restriction

Brett Frischmann and Susan Benesch (2023). *Friction-In-Design Regulation as 21st Century Time, Place, and Manner Restriction*. Yale Journal of Law and Technology 25: 376–447. Working paper: SSRN, doi:10.2139/ssrn.4178647 (2022).

## Key takeaways

- Friction-in-design is "friction with a functional purpose" built into
  an architecture; friction-in-design regulation is state action that
  requires it, or requires outcomes best reached through it (§II.B,
  pp. 416–17).
- Six parameters describe any friction measure: the type of friction,
  its direct effect on subjects, its architectural design, its purpose,
  its scope of application, and its governance (who decides) (§II.A,
  pp. 399–403).
- Content-neutral friction regulation is a modern time, place and manner
  restriction and gets intermediate scrutiny (§III); a trigger based on
  content (TikTok's sensitive-content warning) does not qualify (p. 443
  n. 190).
- A speed bump makes a driver internalize a cost; a checkpoint or a
  CAPTCHA stops passage until a condition is shown, and only the second
  creates a "governance seam" (pp. 404–05 and n. 89, citing Frischmann
  and Ohm).
- No evidence of its own: the article aims "to open the regulatory
  possibilities and frame and motivate systematic empirical research"
  (p. 434).
