# CLAIM-229: The footing, compensation and constitution theses ground on invented cases whose readings the assistant assigned, and the case filed for the proposition's non-necessity holds the words fixed, so it shows only insufficiency

## The objection

**Fictional cases as evidence.** [ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md) separates a CASE that
`illustrates` a claim, which "bears no weight", from a CASE a claim
`grounds` on, which is "evidence from outside the record's own argument".
Four theses of the line put fictional cases in `grounds`:

- [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md) grounds on [CASE-027](../cases.d/CASE-027.md) and [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md);
- [CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md) grounds on [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md);
- [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) grounds on [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md);
- [CLAIM-084](CLAIM-084.md) grounds on [CASE-030](../cases.d/CASE-030.md) and [CASE-032](../cases.d/CASE-032.md).

All five are `standing: fictional`, and their bodies say what they can
bear. [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md): "Invented, so it shows a possibility; whether readers'
judgements shift as described is what [CASE-021](../cases.d/CASE-021.md) would test." [CASE-012](../cases.d/CASE-012.md), the
refrains that illustrate [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md): they "are the assistant's inventions,
so they show that such variation is possible, not how readers judge it".
[CASE-027](../cases.d/CASE-027.md): "As a thought experiment it relies on the reader's judgement that
something fundamental changed." [CASE-030](../cases.d/CASE-030.md): "the retention of complicity is
the author's reading, not a measurement. It illustrates [CLAIM-084](CLAIM-084.md) and can
test nothing." [CLAIM-084](CLAIM-084.md) says the same of itself: "They illustrate it, and
their pragmatic retention was asserted, not measured."

The possibility is not in dispute. That the same words can perform
different acts is the part [CLAIM-023](CLAIM-023.md) disclaims as not the contribution ("not in discovering that translation is pragmatic
or that speech acts change contexts"). The theses need more than the
possibility, and the test that would supply it, [CASE-021](../cases.d/CASE-021.md), is among the
October outline's drops (workbench entry for U57–U60, 2026-10-10).

[CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)'s defeat condition carries the same confusion into a test. It is
met if a rival account "would predict the [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md) variation as well". The
[CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md) variation is A12's, A18's and A24's description of four imagined
speakers. It is not an observation, so there is nothing yet for a rival to
predict.

**The necessity half.** [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md) says the proposition is "neither
necessary nor sufficient" for the communicative event, and that
"necessity fails in [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md), read in reverse". [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s "What it can
show": "The necessity half of [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md): the same words, and so the same
proposition, do not fix the act."

That is the sufficiency half. Write P for "the rendering keeps the
proposition" and A for "the rendering keeps the act". Same words with a
different act is a case of P without A, which refutes "P is sufficient for
A". Necessity, "A only if P", is refuted only by a case of A without P: the
act kept while the proposition changes. Reading [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md) in reverse, from
the reprimand back to the tease, is still the same words, so still P
without A. [CASE-027](../cases.d/CASE-027.md) is the sufficiency half too, as it says ("propositions
held fixed, footing changed").

[CLAIM-053](CLAIM-053.md) already grants that the manuscript argues only insufficiency, and
locates the missing half in the exchange: A12's sentence ("you can alter
many of the propositions while preserving the speaker's attitude") and
"A24's compensation hypothesis ([CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md)) gives the mechanism". But
[CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md)'s thesis is that "lexical infidelity can be required for
pragmatic fidelity", which is about words. A change of wording can keep the
proposition, and [CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md) does not say its compensations change it.
[CLAIM-053](CLAIM-053.md) itself notes that the Abstract's "change almost every word" "is
about words, not propositions"; the same holds of [CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md).

No case in the record is filed for A without P. The documented literary
cases are about wording and form: [CASE-010](../cases.d/CASE-010.md) shows "that a celebrated
translation can keep almost none of the source's wording and still be taken
as Villon". The nearest is [CASE-030](../cases.d/CASE-030.md)'s stable chain, whose final text drops
the hustle and the bet and is said to keep the friend's complicity, and
that retention is "the author's reading". So the non-necessity of the
proposition rests on A12's one sentence. [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md), which rests on
[CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md), then has support only for "not the proposition alone".

## What it does not say

It does not say the theses are false, nor that invented cases are useless:
they show what is possible, and the record files them correctly as
fictional. It says that, as filed, these theses have no evidence beyond
possibility for their footing and constitution claims, and that the
non-necessity half has no case at all. It does not say [CLAIM-053](CLAIM-053.md) is wrong.
It says [CLAIM-053](CLAIM-053.md) credits the exchange with more than [CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md) supplies.

## Note of 2026-10-10: a documented case

[CASE-066](../cases.d/CASE-066.md), found by a search the owner asked for, gives
[CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md) a documented case to set beside the invented [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md): three
published English translations of Dolly's slip into ты in *Anna Karenina*
I.4, one keeping the Russian word with a gloss (Dole 1886) and two adding
words not in the source to carry the relation (Garnett 1901, "Stiva"; the
Maudes 1918, "our children"), with a 2025 translator's stated reasoning for
the companion passage I.5. It answers this objection's point about
[CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md) at the level of existence: the mechanism is attested, not only
imagined.

It does not answer the objection's deeper point, that the theses need more
than possibility. The case's evidence about what the renderings convey is
the translators' and Tolstoy's, not readers'. Whether readers recover the
relation better from the compensating versions is untested, and the case
files a reader test for it rather than a result. On the necessity half it
bears only weakly: Garnett's narrator sentence changes its proposition
while, on the translator's judgement, keeping its act, which is a
producer's judgement of A without P, not a measured one.

## Note of 2026-10-10: a documented case for the necessity half

[CASE-056](../cases.d/CASE-056.md), found by a search the owner asked for, is the first case
filed for A without P that is neither invented nor a matter of wording. J. B.
Phillips renders the "holy kiss" of Rom 16:16 and four parallels as a
handshake all round, which has different compliance conditions. Nida (1964,
p. 160) judged that the rendering keeps the greeting, and Munday (2016)
calls it the field's "notorious example". It answers this objection's point
that "the non-necessity half has no case at all". The necessity half of
[CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md) no longer rests on A12's one sentence.

It does not answer the deeper point, that the theses need more than
possibility. The evidence that the act survived is expert judgement, and the
experts divide: the UBS handbook prefers a rendering that keeps the kiss,
and Gayle (2013) argues that the footing among the addressees changes. No
reader evidence exists. And the defender of necessity can reply that the
proposition kept is the coarser "greet one another warmly". The case forces
that defender to individuate propositions by function. It does not refute
the defender.
