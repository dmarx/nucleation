# LIT-2677: Wrongness and Blame Judgments and Their Dynamics: Toward a Three-Input Processing Model of Moral Judgment

Aurore Gaboriaud, Flora Gautheron, Jean-Charles Quinton and Annique Smeding (2024). Wrongness and Blame Judgments and Their Dynamics: Toward a Three-Input Processing Model of Moral Judgment. *International Review of Social Psychology* 37(1): 19, 1–14. DOI 10.5334/irsp.868.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of 2026-11-17; the reading of 2026-11-16 is below.*

- Intent, outcome and causality each affect both wrongness and blame
  judgments; intent most (b = 2.46, d = 1.68).
- "the causality effect was stronger (+ 0.16) in the case of blame
  judgments (b = 0.98) compared to wrongness judgments (b = 0.82)"; the
  predicted intent and outcome differences were not found.
- The findings "partially support the proposed three-input processing
  model of moral judgments, while also suggesting that the distinction
  between types of judgment (particularly wrongness versus blame) must not
  be overestimated".

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read from the Europe PMC full text. The experiment combines Cushman's and Malle's accounts into a "three-input processing model" and tests it on wrongness and blame. Intent, outcome and causality each had main effects. The agent was judged more harshly when it "directly caused the outcome (M = 3.72, SE = 0.07) compared to when the outcome was caused by another source (M = 2.82, SE = 0.07)". The only supported type difference was causality's larger weight in blame (d = 0.05). The authors: "this study does not reveal clear differential weight for intent and outcome inputs in blame versus wrongness judgments". A5 §5 reports this accurately but leans on it for a fourfold distinction of responsibility the paper does not study. The registration's link to [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md) does not hold: no scenario has one party structuring another's circumstances. See [NOTE-2013](../notes.d/NOTE-2013.md).
