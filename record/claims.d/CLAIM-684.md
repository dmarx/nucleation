# CLAIM-684: When a choice about a technology or an institution could substantially and durably narrow the possibilities later open to those it affects, the burden of justifying it should rise with the size and the irreversibility of the narrowing

## The claim

A13 §VIII, "The reversibility principle":

> One particularly useful addition would be a preference for reversible
> arrangements under conditions of significant uncertainty.

and its formulation:

> When a technological choice could substantially and durably narrow
> future human possibilities, the burden of justification should rise
> with the magnitude and irreversibility of the change.

A adds the counterweight in the same section: "Not every innovation can be
fully tested before implementation. And excessive caution can preserve
harmful arrangements." The claim generalizes "technological" to choices
about institutions, which A's examples (education, employment, healthcare,
political participation) already include.

## Why irreversibility, and where the reason runs out

The reason the claim needs is uncertainty with learning. [CLAIM-559](CLAIM-559.md) shows
that where reassessment is informative, activating a capability
provisionally and cancelling it later can both be optimal under one
objective; an irreversible choice forecloses the second step. Where nothing
will be learned, irreversibility adds no reason of its own, and the
defeater says so. The claim is therefore a rule for choices made before
the evidence is in, which is the situation of [CLAIM-677](CLAIM-677.md)'s ratchet,
whose point of no return is where restoring the alternatives costs more
than any participant can bear.

## What it does not say

- Not a presumption against novelty. A13 §X: restraint "should not become
  a universal presumption against novelty".
- Not who bears the burden or who judges it. A pairs the principle with
  "pilot programs, independent evaluation, meaningful appeals, and
  opportunities for revision"; which institutions those are is left open.

## Note of 2026-11-13: the readings of 2026-11-13

- **Postman** ([THEORY-1840](../theory.d/THEORY-1840.md)): the consequences of technological change are "always vast, often unpredictable and largely irreversible", and he counsels caution. Corroborates the premise; he states no burden rule.
- **Moen** ([THEORY-1867](../theory.d/THEORY-1867.md)): control of technological progress faces "both an epistemic problem and a coordination problem", the uncertainty with later learning this claim's reason needs. Corroborates the premise.
- **Rosa** ([THEORY-1835](../theory.d/THEORY-1835.md)): a modern society can be slowed "but only at the cost of its institutions getting in crises", the cost of reversing a structural ratchet at the scale of the whole. A point of contact, no relation.

## Note of 2026-11-13: Marcuse and Illich, read

- **Marcuse, One-Dimensional Man** ([THEORY-1853](../theory.d/THEORY-1853.md), [NOTE-1924](../notes.d/NOTE-1924.md)).
  Complements the premise in part: it gives a reason irreversibility
  matters, since a project once operative "tends to become exclusive" and
  its initial choice "precludes alternative possibilities incompatible
  with it", and chapter 3 speaks of "the degree of its irreversibility"
  of change brought by technical progress. But Marcuse states no burden
  of justification; his remedy is "planning for the whole against these
  interests" (ch. 10), which "presupposes the repression of much that is
  now free". A point of contact, no relation.
- **Illich, a two-page excerpt** ([THEORY-1861](../theory.d/THEORY-1861.md), [NOTE-1921](../notes.d/NOTE-1921.md)). Leaves it
  open: controls over tools should be "governed by political process
  rather than by decisions by experts", which bears on who carries the
  burden, not on how it scales. Nothing on irreversibility. No relation.

## Note of 2026-11-13: stand-ins, read

- **Winner, "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"** ([THEORY-1859](../theory.d/THEORY-1859.md), [NOTE-1941](../notes.d/NOTE-1941.md)), read in full as a partial stand-in for Mumford. Corroborates the premise and the occasion, more strongly than Postman or Moen; argued in [ARG-103](../arguments.d/ARG-103.md). "By far the greatest latitude of choice exists the very first time a particular instrument, system, or technique is introduced", and "the original flexibility vanishes for all practical purposes once the initial commitments are made"; so innovations "are similar to legislative acts or political foundings that establish a framework for public order that will endure over many generations" and deserve the attention given to politics. Winner states no rule that the burden scales with the size and irreversibility of the narrowing; that remains the claim's own. Status unchanged.
- **Illich, Energy and Equity** ([THEORY-1863](../theory.d/THEORY-1863.md)): qualifies it from the side of who decides. Limits come through "juridical and, above all, to political process", by "provisional" measures, and the threshold "can be pinpointed by social analysis", which favours deciding before lock-in. No burden rule. No relation.
- **Vallor, CyCon 2013** ([THEORY-1847](../theory.d/THEORY-1847.md)): support in spirit; moral skill lost when practice stops is hard to restore, a reason for restraint before deployment. No relation.
- **Vallor's Noema essay** ([THEORY-1856](../theory.d/THEORY-1856.md)) and **Lanier's excerpt** ([THEORY-1846](../theory.d/THEORY-1846.md)): leave it open. "I don’t think it is too late" assumes reversibility; "I hope will not be locked in" presupposes the irreversibility the claim prices.

## Note of 2026-11-13: [CLAIM-605](CLAIM-605.md)'s model and the reading "go gradually" (the assistant's)

A filer's idea, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13, was that [CLAIM-605](CLAIM-605.md)
(Active, demonstrated) cuts against reading this claim as "go gradually".
Checked in [CLAIM-605](CLAIM-605.md)'s model (its note of this date gives the numbers and
scratch/formal/capability_trap_reversibility.py asserts them), for support
that does work through which a capacity is kept:

- **Exit.** A slow taper of substitutive support is the irreversible path;
  an abrupt cut leaves a recoverable shortfall. This claim, which prices
  irreversibility and not speed, therefore favours the abrupt cut there.
  The tension is with the gloss, not with the claim.
- **Entry.** A gradual phase-in stays reversible much longer (8.52 time
  units against 3.10 in the model), so gradualism is right on adoption.
- **Pilots.** A13 §VIII pairs this claim with "pilot programs, independent
  evaluation, meaningful appeals". In the model a pilot of substitutive
  support is reversible only until a deadline, and the room for a gentle
  exit shrinks as it runs. A pilot needs a clock as well as an evaluation:
  one evaluated after its deadline has tested a choice already
  irreversible.
- **Size and irreversibility.** Below a share of the work of about 0.585,
  substitution in the model has no deadline at all; above it, any duration
  long enough is irreversible. The two factors this claim scales by need
  not be independent.

This bears on the claim's application, not its truth; the claim's
`defeated_if` is not met. Status unchanged. The note is the assistant's
(Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
