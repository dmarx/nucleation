# dv-tom REPORT (2026-11-20)

## Answers in brief (each sourced below)
- Explicit first-order false belief: from around age 4 (Rakoczy 2022, the "4 year revolution"; Arslan et al. 2017 intro: "around the age of four").
- Second-order ("A thinks that B thinks"): Arslan et al. 2017, citing Perner & Wimmer 1985, Sullivan et al. 1994 and Miller 2009/2012: a further one to 3 years after first-order, so about 5 to 7. In their own Dutch sample (5.0-6.8 y), 7 of 79 passed both second-order questions; among the other 72, 17% of second-order answers were correct. Rakoczy 2022: higher-order recursive ToM "develops gradually over childhood, adolescence and adulthood". 
- Common-knowledge reasoning: NONE of the three works gives an age. Not established by this reading. (Arslan et al. fn 8: a ToM game needing reasoning about the computer's belief about the child's own decision was "too hard" for 5-6-year-olds.) Grueneisen, Wyman & Tomasello 2015 (second-order false belief in peer coordination, 6-year-olds) is the obvious source but no lawful full text was reached.
- Implicit false belief: as of Rakoczy 2022, the looking-time/anticipatory/interactive findings are contested: many non-replications, including large direct replications and a self-replication of Southgate et al. 2007; no convergent validity across implicit tasks; one camp (Baillargeon, Buttelmann & Southgate 2018) holds the originals, another (Poulin-Dubois et al. 2018) says it is unclear; a ManyBabies adversarial consortium was under way. I did not read its outcome (post-2022).
- Minimal ToM (Butterfill & Apperly): tracks goals, encounterings (object in an agent's field) and registrations (agent-object-location relation, the most recent encounter), enough for many location false-belief tasks; it cannot track beliefs involving identity or quantification, and registration lacks representational content and "the possibility of embedding". So it cannot represent nested beliefs. The paper says NOTHING about coordination, common knowledge, joint action or salience. Whether it supports coordination on a salient option without nested beliefs is not answered by the work; see the labelled inference below.

## Work 1: Rakoczy 2022 (review)
Provenance: https://www.psych.uni-goettingen.de/de/development/pdfs/rakoczy_tom_nature_review_psychology_2022.pdf . Kind: the author's own institute (Göttingen developmental psychology group) page, lawful. Format: publisher-typeset PDF proof ("Published online xx xx xxxx"), 23 PDF pages, no printed page numbers. Read in full (main text, Tables 1-2, Figs 1-3, Box 1); reference list scanned for the cited replication and higher-order refs.
Other copies: nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00037-z (publisher, paywalled per search result; not fetched). Göttingen also hosts a Routledge-handbook chapter on implicit ToM (rakoczy_implicit_tom_routledge-handbook...pdf), not read.
Bibliographic: Hannes Rakoczy, "Foundations of theory of mind and its development in early childhood", Nature Reviews Psychology 1 (2022), DOI 10.1038/s44159-022-00037-z (DOI and © 2022 in the text). Volume/issue/pages 1(4):223-235 come from a search-engine listing, NOT from the copy.
Why chosen: recent (2022) peer-reviewed integrative review, by a replication-programme author, that reports the implicit-task replication problems; lawful author-hosted copy. Passed over: Wellman, Cross & Watson 2001 (2001, pre-replication debate; Rakoczy cites it for convergence).
Summary: ToM's core is meta-representation, with four signatures (difference, incompatibility, mis-representation, aspectuality). Perception-goal psychology appears around 9 months (only the "difference" signature) and grounds joint attention and joint intention; full belief-desire meta-representation appears around 4 (false belief, appearance-reality, level-II perspective, aspectual tasks, passed together and correlated); recursive higher-order ToM develops gradually into adulthood. Executive function, language, and social input (siblings, mental-state talk) are determinants. Implicit ToM findings face replication and validity failures.
Contribution: a current consensus map of the explicit timeline, and an even-handed status report of the implicit-ToM crisis.
Key results (PDF page of the proof copy):
- PDF p.10 (Summary): "Major changes in ToM capacity occur at around 9 months and 4 years of age."
- PDF p.3 (Emergence of basic ToM): "perception–goal psychology involves only the signature of difference"
- PDF p.3: infants who understand goals "form joint intentions to act together towards a shared goal" (quoted: "they form joint intentions to act together towards a shared goal")
- PDF p.4 (The 4 year revolution, standard false belief): "Children younger than four systematically answer incorrectly"
- PDF p.5: tasks are passed together: "Children come to master all these tasks"
- PDF p.6 (Continuing development): "higher-order recursive meta-representation (for example, the ability to represent and understand ‘A thinks that B thinks that C desires that…’) develops gradually over childhood, adolescence and adulthood" (cites Liddle & Nettle 2006; O'Grady et al. 2015)
- PDF p.8 (Table 2, anticipatory looking): "The basic findings could largely not be replicated in direct large-scale replication studies"
- PDF p.9 (Replicability and validity): many non-replications "are direct replications with the original stimuli, methods and procedures, but with much larger samples than the original studies"
- PDF p.10: "no systematic correlations have been found between the different types of implicit tasks"
- PDF p.10 (Status of implicit ToM): the cautious camp argues "it is unclear (without more systematic future experimentation) whether implicit ToM exists"
- PDF p.10 (Box 1): "This shared intentionality provides the basis for cooperation, culture and communication" (which, via language and discourse, grounds full meta-representation)
Also (p.10): a possible split: implicit tasks needing real meta-representation may not replicate, while those solvable by level-I perspective-taking may, which would fit a dual-process (type I / type II) account.
Assumptions: meta-representation is the criterion for "full" ToM; explicit-task convergence is evidence of a single competence.
Concepts: meta-representation; perception-goal vs belief-desire psychology; 9-month and 4-year revolutions; level I/II perspective-taking; implicit (VoE, anticipatory looking, interactive, altercentric) vs explicit; dual-process (type I/II); ManyBabies.
Bearing:
- THEORY-495 (Tomasello obligation) / NOTE-185 (sharing at 3): supports a timeline where 3-year-olds have joint intention and joint attention (from ~9 months) but not yet explicit false belief (~4) or second-order (later). Qualifies any account of equal splitting at 3 that needs belief attribution.
- Schelling THEORY-tmpqxwyk: contrast. A mutual-expectation analysis needs recursive ToM, which Rakoczy places well after age 4.
- Sugden THEORY-1630 / LIT-1698 (team reasoning): parallel. Rakoczy treats "we"-perspectives and shared intentionality as resting on perception-goal psychology, not on meta-representation, which leaves room for non-recursive coordination.
- NOTE-tmpe9epc (Tomasello 2016 joint intentionality): parallel; Box 1 adopts the shared-intentionality grounding.
Limitations: review, not new data; proof copy without page numbers; nothing on common knowledge or coordination; predates any ManyBabies outcome.
Candidate THEORY: "Rakoczy reviews theory-of-mind development as a perception–goal psychology from about 9 months, which supports joint attention and joint intention, followed by explicit belief–desire meta-representation from about age 4 and a gradual later development of recursive higher-order mindreading, and reports that infant implicit false-belief findings have repeatedly failed direct replication and lack convergent validity."
promote_when: "A later integrative review or meta-analysis confirms the 9-month and 4-year transitions and reports the outcome of large multi-lab replications of implicit false-belief tasks."

## Work 2: Butterfill & Apperly 2013
Provenance: https://www.butterfill.com/pdf/minimal_theory_of_mind.pdf . Kind: first author's own site, lawful. Format: author's manuscript dated "July 11, 2012", pp. 1-28 with own pagination (NOT the journal's 606-637). Read in full (sections 1-7, footnotes); references skimmed. Text extraction drops "th"/"tt" ligatures; quotes chosen to avoid affected words.
Other copies: warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/philosophy/people/butterfill/minimal_theory_of_mind/ (Warwick page, abstract/citation); wrap.warwick.ac.uk/50726/ (says request a copy); research.birmingham.ac.uk (record). Commentaries (Rakoczy, Spaulding, Zawidzki) at philosophyofbrains.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/*.pdf, not read.
Bibliographic: Stephen A. Butterfill & Ian A. Apperly, "How to Construct a Minimal Theory of Mind", Mind & Language 28(5), 606-637, 2013, DOI 10.1111/mila.12036 (journal details from butterfill.com page; manuscript gives title/authors only). NB the brief's "Mind & Language" is right; Rakoczy's ref list gives the same volume/pages.
Why chosen: named in the task; the standard non-recursive alternative; author's open copy.
Summary: A computational-level construction of what a creature could represent to track perceptions and beliefs (including false beliefs) without representing propositional attitudes. Four principles: goal-directed action (teleological function of movements); encountering (object in agent's field) as condition on acting on it; registration (agent-object-location, set by the most recent encounter) with correct registration as a success condition; registration as a cause of action (act as if the object is where registered). Signature limits: no identity- or quantification-involving false beliefs. Motivated by automatic vs non-automatic belief tracking in adults, infant findings, and animal findings.
Contribution: shows systematic success on many location false-belief tasks is possible with relational, non-representational proxies; gives testable limits.
Key results (manuscript page):
- p.1 (abstract): "Minimal theory of mind is rich enough to explain systematic success on tasks held to be acid tests"
- p.5 (§2): propositional attitudes "have arbitrarily nestable contents" (part of why full ToM is demanding)
- p.13 (§4.3): "an individual registers an object at a location if and only if she most recently encountered it at that location"
- p.16 (§4.4): "the agent will act as if the object were in the location she registers it in"
- p.19 (§5): "Lucky should have no problem predicting actions based on false beliefs about the locations of objects" (but should fail on identity mistakes)
- p.27 (§7): registration differs from belief in "lacking representational content and the possibility of embedding"
- p.27 (§7): "we do not assume that minimal theory of mind develops into full-blown theory of mind in humans"
Assumptions: Marr computational level; principles need not be explicitly represented; teleological functions are trackable; data cited are pre-2012 (i.e. before the replication failures Rakoczy reports).
Concepts: ToM ability vs ToM cognition; tracking; encountering; field; registration; signature limits (identity, quantification); behavioural strategies; altercentric interference.
What it can and cannot support (from the text): CAN: predicting goal-directed action, who has encountered what, success/failure from correct/incorrect registration, location false-belief prediction, informing pointing (infant pointing to restore correct registration, §4.3). CANNOT: identity/quantification false beliefs; embedding (so no registration of a registration, i.e. no nested beliefs). The paper does NOT discuss coordination, joint action, common knowledge or salience.
Inference (mine, NOT the authors'): since registrations are relations to objects, a minimal mindreader could track that a partner has encountered or registered the same salient object it has, but not that the partner registers its own registration; any coordination on a salient option resting on minimal ToM would have to be first-order (each tracks the other's relation to the object), not mutual expectation.
Bearing:
- Schelling THEORY-tmpqxwyk: contrast. Mutual expectation needs embedding that registration lacks.
- THEORY-1630 / LIT-1698 (team reasoning): parallel only by implication: both offer non-recursive routes; B&A never mention coordination.
- THEORY-495 / NOTE-185: background. Supplies a candidate cognitive basis available to 3-year-olds, but the authors make no claim about sharing.
- THEORY-1779 (Clark & Brennan, common ground as mutual belief): contrast, as for Schelling.
Limitations: theoretical construction, explicitly not a claim about what any creature does; written before the implicit-ToM replication crisis, so its empirical motivation is weaker now (Rakoczy 2022); silent on social coordination.
Candidate THEORY: "Butterfill and Apperly construct a minimal theory of mind, in which an agent's relations to objects (encountering, and registration set by the most recent encounter) stand in for perception and belief, and argue that it suffices for systematic success on false-belief tasks about object location but cannot track beliefs involving identity or quantification, because registration lacks representational content and embedding."
promote_when: "A study finds subjects who succeed on location false-belief tasks but fail matched identity false-belief tasks, as the construction predicts."

## Work 3: Arslan, Taatgen & Verbrugge 2017 (stand-in for item 3)
Provenance: Europe PMC full-text XML, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5329038/fullTextXML (PMC5329038). Kind: PMC/Europe PMC, lawful; licence CC BY 4.0 (stated in the text). Read in full (all sections, tables, footnotes; not the reference list or supplement). No printed pages (article number).
Bibliographic: Burcu Arslan, Niels A. Taatgen, Rineke Verbrugge, "Five-Year-Olds' Systematic Errors in Second-Order False Belief Tasks Are Due to First-Order Theory of Mind Strategy Selection: A Computational Modeling Study", Frontiers in Psychology 8:275 (2017), DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00275.
Why chosen: the preferred item-3 sources were not reachable lawfully (see Copies). Perner & Wimmer 1985 and Miller 2009 (Psychol Bull 135(5):749-773, abstract only seen) had no open copy found; Osterhaus & Bosacki 2022 (Dev Rev 64:101021) no open copy found. Arslan et al. is open, recent, states the age norm with those citations, and adds data on 5-6-year-olds. How far it stands in: for the age norm it is a secondary citation of Perner & Wimmer / Sullivan / Miller; on coordination it offers only footnote 8.

- Europe PMC: not open access.
Summary: Two ACT-R models of how 5-year-olds pick a ToM strategy for second-order questions; the instance-based model predicts that failures will be first-order answers. Test with 72 Dutch children aged 5.0-6.8: 17% of answers correct; of wrong answers, 65% first-order, 29% zero-order. The authors say failure comes from lack of experience with second-order strategies, which feedback can revise.
Key results (section):
- Introduction: "While children start to pass verbal first-order ToM tasks around the age of four, it takes them a further one to 3 years to pass second-order ToM tasks" (Perner & Wimmer 1985; Sullivan et al. 1994; Miller 2009, 2012)
- Participants: of 79, "seven children who gave correct answers for both of the second-order false belief questions were excluded"
- Results: "Overall, 17% of the second-order false belief answers were correct and 83% of them were wrong."
- Results: "65% of the wrong answers were based on a first-order ToM strategy"
- Footnote 8 (ToM game requiring reasoning about the computer's belief about the child's decision): "However, the task was too hard for our sample."
- Discussion: "5-year-old children’s failure is due to lack of experience in using a second-order ToM strategy"
Assumptions: 5-year-olds already have efficient first-order strategies; second-order competence is strategy selection plus experience; ACT-R defaults. The sample is upper-middle-class from an "Excellence school".
Concepts: zero/first/second-order ToM; reality bias; second-order false belief; strategy selection; instance-based vs reinforcement learning; serial-processing bottleneck.
Bearing:
- Schelling THEORY-tmpqxwyk: contrast. Second-order belief is mostly failed at 5-6, so a mutual-expectation story of focal points does not fit 3-year-olds.
- THEORY-495 / NOTE-185: qualifies. Any account of 3-year-olds' equal splitting that relies on "I expect you to expect" is ruled out by these norms.
- THEORY-1630 / LIT-1698: background. Gives the age gap that a non-recursive (team-reasoning) account would fill.
- THEORY-172 (Frank & Goodman, recursive pragmatics) and level-k/cognitive-hierarchy accounts: parallel. Its "as simple as possible" incremental-level account cites Camerer et al. 2004.
Limitations: an exclusion-selected sample (the passers were dropped), so 17% is not a population pass rate; one school; no common-knowledge task reported; age norm second-hand.
Candidate THEORY: "Arslan, Taatgen and Verbrugge report that children pass verbal second-order false-belief tasks one to three years after first-order ones, that 5- to 6-year-olds who fail them mostly answer at first order rather than from reality, and model this as failure to select a second-order strategy for lack of experience."
promote_when: "An independent sample of 5- to 6-year-olds, not selected by second-order failure, again gives mostly first-order wrong answers on second-order false-belief questions."
