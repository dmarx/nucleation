# NOTE-761: Theriault et al. — Allostasis at the core of brain function

## Contribution

The paper gathers the Barrett group's anatomical, imaging and
physiological work into one argument for an "allostasis-first" view: the
organ's job is to coordinate and regulate the body's systems, and mental
activity is "a means to the allostatic end". It adds an applied example:
a hypothesis that falling glucose uptake before cognitive decline in
Alzheimer's disease protects waste clearance and pH at the cost of
cognition. It offers a definition of stress as anticipated metabolic
outlay, and a position on whether allostasis can be measured (Box 3).

## Key insight

Read the brain's anatomy before its psychology. The regions that issue
visceromotor commands and receive interoceptive signals form one
connected system, sit at the top of the cortex's laminar gradient, and
include the hubs that imaging studies have labelled with psychological
functions. On that reading, the default-mode and salience networks are
the allostatic system under other names, and thought, feeling and
perception are ways of meeting the body's predicted needs.

## Assumptions

- **Efficient metabolic regulation is "a fundamental selection
  pressure"**, so an allostatic architecture should be "evident in the
  core of the brain". The argument is from expected design to found
  anatomy.
- **Predictive processing as the reading of cortical signal flow.**
  Centrifugal signals are predictions, centripetal ones prediction
  errors. The laminar anatomy is cited; the computational reading is
  hypothesis.
- **"Resting-state" BOLD largely reflects interoceptive signalling**,
  with exteroceptive input reduced, not psychological activity such as
  self-referential thought. This is asserted from cited work.
- **Allostasis is not a measurable quantity.** "It is not reducible to
  any single measure or small set of measures"; it is "a conceptual tool
  to frame more focused, tractable questions" (Box 3).
- **The scope is stated as modest.** "Our goal in this paper was not to
  claim our 'allostasis-first' hypothesis is correct, but more modestly
  to justify asking scientific questions about the links between brain,
  body, and mind in a different way" (Summary and Conclusion).

## Key results

All results are reviewed, mostly from the group's earlier papers.

- **An intrinsic allostatic system (Figs. 1–2).** 21 seeds: 14
  allostatically relevant subcortical regions and the 7 cortical regions
  receiving their monosynaptic projections in non-human tract-tracing
  (aMCC, pACC, sgACC, ventral anterior insula, dorsal mid and posterior
  insula, hippocampus). Resting-state connectivity at 7 T (N = 90),
  replicating 3 T (N = 550), "replicated 100% of the monosynaptic
  cortico-cortical connections, 94% of the monosynaptic subcortico-cortical
  connections, and 96% of the monosynaptic subcortico-subcortical
  connections". The system extends to regions not seeded, including
  primary motor cortex and cerebellum.
- **Overlap with named networks (Fig. 2).** The system overlaps the
  central autonomic network, the skeletomotor network, the
  salience/cingulo-opercular network and a heat-pain signature. k-means
  on the 21 seed maps gives two motifs, "default-mode-like" and
  "salience/cingulo-opercular-like", which overlap in the connectome's
  "rich club" hubs. Hence: "Scientists have been studying the allostatic
  system all along but under different names", and efforts to assign
  psychological functions to these networks "are destined to fail".
- **The limbic-to-sensory gradient (Fig. 3).** From sensory to limbic
  cortex, layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons become larger, sparser and more
  connected, and layer 4 thins to agranular. Read as dimensionality
  reduction: sensory signals are "increasingly compressed into low
  dimensional, multimodal summaries" towards the limbic edge, and
  limbic signals decompress outwards. Predictions flow from deep layers
  of less laminated to superficial layers of more laminated cortex, and
  errors the reverse. NEFH, PV and SST expression follow the gradient.
  Interoception, olfaction and taste arrive already compressed, which
  places them "higher on the predictive hierarchy than the distance
  senses".
- **Two-way centrifugal flow (Fig. 4).** Limbic control signals go down
  to the vagus and spinal cord as visceromotor commands and out to
  sensory cortex as their "efferent copies", which act as sensory
  predictions. The ACC projects directly to V1.
- **Interoception shapes brain-wide signalling.** Respiration couples
  oscillations across many regions and modulates hippocampal
  sharp-wave ripples; vagal stimulation enhances memory consolidation
  and mood; people saccade more in systole and fixate more in diastole,
  and visual search is slower and less accurate in systole. Visceral and
  heartbeat-evoked potentials track detection and attention (Box 2).
- **Stress redefined (Box 1).** "Stress is a brain-body state associated
  with an anticipated allostatic perturbation involving a significant
  increase in energy utilization", value-neutral. It is felt as
  stressful when predicted and actual outlay mismatch, or the system is
  depleted. The box restates that "affective experience, or mood,
  functions as a low-dimensional allostatic barometer", and that an
  illness disrupting the brain's model of the body "will involve
  affective symptoms … [and] some dysfunction in energy regulation".
- **Alzheimer's as allostatic trade-off.** Anaerobic glucose metabolism
  is ~15× less glucose-efficient and acidifies tissue; as vascular waste
  clearance declines with age and amyloid, lowering glucose uptake may
  protect pH. Cited support: GLUT1 and GLUT3 density and glucose uptake
  "decline following amyloid accumulation, but before the appearance of
  cognitive decline". Hence glucose-restoring therapies might rescue
  cognition while "worsening the more fundamental disease process". The
  brain's "core function is not to think, or to feel, or even to sense".
- **Concepts and mental causation (Box 3).** Concepts are "emergent,
  lower dimensional features that describe and causally constrain" the
  allostatic network; all animals have concepts, and "concepts do not
  necessarily need to be conscious". Psychological interventions act on
  the body through them (CBT and cardiovascular risk, conditioned immune
  responses, emotion granularity and cardiorespiratory differentiation).

## Concepts

- **Allostasis.** Predictive regulation that balances the demands of
  many bodily systems, "stability through change"; distinguished from
  homeostasis, reactive return to a set point.
- **Interoception.** The brain's predictive modelling of the body's
  sensory conditions, part of allostasis because it supports
  visceromotor control as somatosensation supports skeletomotor control.
- **Allostatic system.** The 21-seed network and the regions intrinsically
  connected with it.
- **Centrifugal / centripetal signals.** Limbic-to-sensory (prediction,
  decompression) / sensory-to-limbic (prediction error, compression).
- **Stress.** Anticipated large energetic outlay, value-neutral.
- **Allostatic load.** Rejected as "an unhelpful abstraction".

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md) (Deferred).** The LIT was filed as "the bodily-mechanism
  option: a feature a functional organization might lack". The reading
  does not support that use.
  - The paper is about what the brain is *for*, not about what makes a
    state felt. Its only claims about feeling are restatements:
    affect as "a low-dimensional allostatic barometer" (Box 1) and
    stress becoming "psychologically 'stressful'" on mismatch.
  - It does not say an organization lacks the mechanism, or that feeling
    needs a body. Its terms are functional: prediction, regulation,
    compression. A functionalist could take every claim in it as a
    description of a role.
  - Its one remark on consciousness is that matrix thalamic inputs to
    upper cortical layers are "considered to be causally important to
    levels of consciousness", cited and not developed.
  It is relevant only as the newest statement of the allostatic frame
  in which [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)'s Barrett half is set.
- **[THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md).** Consistent, and adds nothing to the felt-affect claim.
  It strengthens the "prediction" side of [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)'s open "prediction or
  map" question only by restating it. It also puts allostatic generation
  in limbic cortex *and* brainstem nuclei together (Fig. 1), which neither
  settles nor sharpens the disagreement [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md) records with Damasio's
  brainstem-only generation.
- **[THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md).** Box 3's account of concepts as low-dimensional
  constraints on allostasis, and the claim that networks were named for
  psychological functions they do not specifically serve, are the
  constructionist view extended from emotion to all mental categories.
- **[THEORY-093](../theory.d/THEORY-093.md), [THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md).** Its subcortical list (PAG, PBN, NTS,
  hypothalamus, monoamine nuclei) overlaps the substrates those accounts
  name, but the paper makes no claim about where feeling arises.
- **[THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md).** Veit's "coordinated regulatory state" and this paper's
  allostasis are close in content; the paper does not identify feeling
  with regulation, so it is not a second source for [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md).
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
  The Alzheimer's section is a hypothesis for clinical research, not a
  practice recommendation for ML.

## Limitations

- **A review, mostly of the authors' own work.** The decisive evidence
  for C1 and C2 is in cited papers not read here.
- **The central thesis is disclaimed.** The authors do not claim
  "allostasis-first" is correct, and give no result that would refute it.
  Allostasis is said to be unmeasurable by any single variable.
- **Overlap is not function.** That the allostatic system overlaps the
  default-mode and salience networks shows shared topography; that the
  networks' psychological labels are "destined to fail" goes beyond it.
- **The Alzheimer's account is speculative.** It rests on the order of
  GLUT changes and cognitive decline, and on mouse work, and predicts
  harm from glucose-restoring therapies without data.
- **No claim about experience.** Nothing in it bears on whether, or why,
  allostatic states are felt.

## Open questions

- What finding would count against allostasis-first? A brain region or
  network whose activity is reliably dissociated from any bodily
  regulatory demand while carrying a psychological function would.
- Does the default-mode network's activity in tasks track interoceptive
  or metabolic variables once task demands are matched?
- Does lowering glucose uptake in early Alzheimer's disease protect pH
  and clearance, and do glucose-restoring therapies worsen long-run
  outcomes as predicted?
