# NOTE-tmpc4m2u: Cayley (2021) — Illich as Revolutionary

## Contribution

A biographer's close account of Illich's argument, quoting him.

## Key results

1. "A radical monopoly exists where all alternatives have been rendered either inaccessible or unimaginable."
2. The key issue is "whether one can still find an alternative or decently do without the monopolized commodity".
3. "An obvious example is an environment in which one simply can't live without a car."
4. Illich, quoted, on burial: "Now legislation is being passed to make the mortician's ministrations compulsory."
5. Conviviality: "individual freedom realized in personal interdependence."

## Assumptions

Illich's criterion is autonomous action: whether a tool serves one's own ends or reshapes them.

## Concepts

Radical monopoly; second watershed; counterproductivity; conviviality; autonomous action.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpbypt7**: corroborates its measure and two mechanisms. Result 2 is the claim's measure, what remains open to a non-adopter; result 3 its infrastructural mechanism; alternatives made "unimaginable" its cultural one. It **qualifies** the title: Illich's burial example (result 4) ends in law, so a de facto monopoly can later be made compulsory by rule. "Without any rule requiring it" says how compulsion can arise, not that it must stay unwritten.
- **CLAIM-tmpkjv7u**: Illich's harm, the paralysis of autonomous action, needs no discretion-holder; see ARG-tmpsh7he.
- **CLAIM-598** uses "counterproductive" in a different sense (assembly steps), not Illich's.

## Limitations

A secondary account; the book's list of threats and its political programme are not covered.
