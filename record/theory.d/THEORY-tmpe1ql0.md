# THEORY-tmpe1ql0: A corporation that forms its judgments through authorised avowal and corrects the inconsistency any mechanical aggregation of its members' views produces has a mind of its own, and as a conversable agent it meets the conditions for being fit to be held responsible: a significant choice, the capacity to judge its options normatively, and control over acting on that judgment

## Source

Pettit (2017), [LIT-tmp486vh](../literature.d/LIT-tmp486vh.md), read in [NOTE-tmpc4hkd](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpc4hkd.md). The 2007 paper it
restates, [LIT-tmpoyd9z](../literature.d/LIT-tmpoyd9z.md), is unread.

## What was actually shown

Pettit argues it in the chapter ([LIT-tmp486vh](../literature.d/LIT-tmp486vh.md)), in two premises.

**Corporations are conversable agents.** They meet a functional standard of
agency: they hold purposes, represent their circumstances reliably, and act
on them. Through spokespersons whom members have authorised, they avow
attitudes and make promises, forgoing excuses the way a person does who
says "I'll be there". Majority voting on connected propositions can be
inconsistent, so a conversable corporation must check and correct its
aggregate. The judgments it ends up with are its own, not borrowed from a
leader or a rule. That is the judgment-aggregation result of [THEORY-127](THEORY-127.md),
carried from belief to commitment.

**Conversable agents are fit to be held responsible.** Fitness needs a
significant choice, a position to make a normative judgment about the
options, and the capacity to choose as that judgment requires because it
requires it. Holding responsible is a regulative practice addressed to an
agent that responds to reasons, so contra-causal freedom is not needed.

**Corporations meet the conditions.** A corporation cannot coherently deny
that it can judge right and wrong. If it lacks the means to act on its
judgments, it can give itself those means, so the excuse expires.

## What this does not say

- **Not that individuals are off the hook.** Both corporation and members
  can be responsible in their domains of control.
- **Not that corporations are moral persons.** "Persons" is functional, an
  agent that speaks for itself. Fitness to be held responsible is not
  moral standing, and it is not the corporation's being good or
  legitimate.
- **Not shown empirically.** That real corporations correct their
  aggregates and act on the result is asserted from how incorporation
  works, not documented.
- **It sets aside deception.** A corporation masking its real attitudes is
  excluded "for the sake of simplicity".
