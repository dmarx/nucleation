# LIT-tmphg6wn: Gender and Race: (What) Are They? (What) Do We Want Them To Be?

Sally Haslanger (2000), *Noûs* 34(1):31–55 — DOI-10.1111/0029-4624.00201

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the works filling out
the record's coverage of social ontology under the topic [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added; [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md)
names Haslanger among the authors whose absence showed the gap.

[NOTE-tmpqk6e0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqk6e0.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the record's first work on social kinds as distinct from
social groups that act, and its first statement of the ameliorative method:
the question "what is gender?" is answered by deciding what concept a critical
theory needs, constrained by a semantic condition (the term keeps its central
functions) and a political one (left general). The definitions place gender
and race in social position, not in bodies or psychology: a woman is someone
subordinated because she is marked as female, and someone marked for
subordination as male is neither a woman nor, in that context, functioning as
a man.

Read it as a proposal. Haslanger says she is "less committed to saying that
this is what gender is and what race is, than to saying that these are
important categories that a feminist antiracist theory needs" (p. 52). The
empirical premises about race (no racial genes, classification differs between
Brazil and the US) are given as "matters of established fact" without
citation (p. 43).

**Bearing on what the record holds.**

- **Ritchie ([LIT-tmppeiud](LIT-tmppeiud.md)).** Ritchie's structuralism reinterprets these
  definitions: the social group *women* becomes a node in a social structure
  whose relations are subordination and privilege, with the bodily marker as
  a further requirement on occupiers. Ritchie quotes this paper's p. 39 and
  p. 38 correctly. On Ritchie's taxonomy, the groups defined here are
  *feature* social groups, kinds rather than organised wholes.
- **Ásta ([LIT-tmprri7i](LIT-tmprri7i.md)).** Ásta's conferralism is the main rival account of the
  same categories, filed unread beside this one. Haslanger thanks "Ásta
  Sveinsdóttir" for comments (n. 1).
- **The group-agency strand ([LIT-391](LIT-391.md), [LIT-401](LIT-401.md), [LIT-159](LIT-159.md)).** No direct
  connection. The groups this paper defines are social positions, not
  organisations, and nothing in it treats women or a race as an agent.
- **Natural kinds ([LIT-244](LIT-244.md)).** The paper's descriptive project asks whether
  race and gender terms track "social kinds" (p. 33), the social counterpart
  of the natural-kind question that SEP entry surveys.

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary: what gender and race are and what
makes someone a member. `philosophy-of-language` holds the method, whose
subject is revising a concept and when a term may be appropriated
(pp. 34–35, 46–48). `ethics` holds the normative aim and the argument that
an account's marginalising some individuals is answered by the values guiding
it (p. 46). `metaphysics` holds the reality claim of the conclusion. No word in
the vocabulary names gender, race or feminist philosophy as such; the work's
primary subject is held by `social-ontology`, so the lack does not block
filing; whether a word for it is wanted is a question for the vocabulary.
