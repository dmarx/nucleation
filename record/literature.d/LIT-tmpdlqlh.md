# LIT-tmpdlqlh: Emotion in the Workplace: A Reappraisal

Blake E. Ashforth and Ronald H. Humphrey (1995), *Human Relations* 48(2):97–125 — DOI-10.1177/001872679504800201

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's abstract, not read. What follows is what
the abstract says.*

- Work is "saturated with emotion", but research has neglected everyday
  emotions in organisational life. Scholars and practitioners often treat
  emotionality as "the antithesis of rationality", and so take a
  pejorative view of emotion.
- That view has produced four institutionalised mechanisms for regulating
  the experience and expression of emotion at work: (1) neutralizing, (2)
  buffering, (3) prescribing and (4) normalizing.
- Against it, the authors argue that emotionality and rationality are
  "interpenetrated", that emotions are "an integral and inseparable part of
  organizational life", and that they are often functional for the
  organisation. Motivation, leadership and group dynamics are the
  applications.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-04 at the owner's request, with four works on emotion in
groups and organisations. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice. `Deferred` because it was not
read.

Its place in the batch is as the field-founding argument that emotion in
organisations is worth studying at all. Barsade ([LIT-tmpojrfm](LIT-tmpojrfm.md)) cites it for
using "emotion" as a broad label for subjective feeling, and her paper is
one answer to its call to study everyday emotions at work. Von Scheve &
Ismer ([LIT-tmp0fwb0](LIT-tmp0fwb0.md)) do not cite it. Their "culture and shared knowledge"
mechanisms, feeling and display rules after Hochschild, are the family the
four regulating mechanisms belong to: an organisation's norms making its
members' emotions converge, or keeping them out of view.

Two links to the record's emotion holdings can be stated from the abstract
alone, and a reading would test both.

- **The record's regulation literature.** Gross ([LIT-554](LIT-554.md)) treats emotion
  regulation as something individuals do to their own emotions, and Frijda
  ([LIT-540](LIT-540.md)) argues it is mostly one emotion checking another. The four
  mechanisms here are regulation done *by an institution* to its members'
  emotions, a level neither of those addresses.
- **Emotion and rationality.** The claim that the two are "interpenetrated"
  is the organisational version of a claim the record holds in Damasio
  ([LIT-384](LIT-384.md)) and Frijda ([LIT-540](LIT-540.md)): emotion is part of practical reason, not
  its opponent.

**Priority for a reading: low to medium.** It is a review and position
paper, and its central claims are in the abstract. A reading would add what
each regulating mechanism is, and the group-dynamics section, which is
where it would bear on collective emotion.
