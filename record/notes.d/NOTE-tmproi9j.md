# NOTE-tmproi9j: Fanning & Raworth (2025) — Doughnut of social and planetary boundaries monitors a world out of balance

## Contribution

An updated, time-series dashboard of social shortfall and ecological overshoot, each indicator judged against its own threshold, with a cross-scale comparable disaggregation by country cluster.

## Key results

- Abstract: "Here we present a renewed Doughnut framework with a revised set of 35 indicators that monitor trends in social deprivation and ecological overshoot over the 2000–2022 period."
- Abstract: "the richest 20% of nations, with 15% of the global population, contribute more than 40% of annual ecological overshoot, whereas the poorest 40% of countries, with 42% of the global population, experience more than 60% of the social shortfall."
- Main: aim to "explore inequalities that tend to be masked by such global aggregates by comparing the performance of countries clustered by income level".
- Twenty-first century trends: "The median share of humanity experiencing social shortfall declines from 47% in 2000 to 35% in 2022".
- Same section: "The median level of overshoot beyond the ecological ceiling increases from 75% in 2000 to 96% in 2022".
- Country clusters: the ecological indicators "are related to Earth-system processes that cannot be disaggregated to a national scale in a directly comparable manner"; consumption-based per capita footprints are used instead.
- Redefining progress: the Doughnut is "a coherent, visual dashboard of 35 indicators", monitoring well-being and planetary health "in their own terms".
- Same: "A disadvantage is that there is no directly traceable link between the social and ecological domains in the Doughnut".

## Assumptions

The planetary boundaries framework (2023) and its thresholds, not assessed here; minimum social standards as share of population below a threshold; OLS linear trends; linear scenario paths to zero shortfall by 2030 and zero overshoot by 2050; country clusters by mean income per capita; per capita equality-based downscaled boundaries.

## Concepts

Social foundation / ecological ceiling; shortfall vs overshoot; dashboard vs single index; disaggregation and masking by global aggregates; downscaled (per capita) boundaries; consumption-based footprints.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** Accurate. "35 indicators", "2000 to 2022" and "inequalities hidden by global aggregates" all match. It should add that the inequality analysis is for 2017 only and by country clusters, not within countries, and that the framework is deliberately a dashboard, each indicator against its own threshold, while the summary statistics across indicators are medians.

- **[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)** (frustration is ordinal before quantitative): **supports, as an illustration from outside**. The Doughnut judges each demand against its own acceptance threshold and declines a common scale between the social and ecological domains ("no directly traceable link"). It **qualifies** the claim's spirit in one respect: the paper still summarises across indicators with medians and interquartile ranges, a cross-indicator aggregate that the claim would treat as a declared measure, not as frustration itself.
- **[QUESTION-tmpuftum](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpuftum.md)** (who counts as a claimant, and how demands are inferred): **bears on it, leaves it open**. Disaggregation changes the verdict: the global aggregate hides that the clusters causing overshoot and those bearing shortfall are different. The paper also shows the unit of claimant constrains measurement (ecological boundaries cannot be disaggregated comparably; footprints are a constructed substitute). It does not address how the set of demands is defensibly established; the 35 indicators are taken from SDG data and planetary boundaries science.

- Not held under another LIT (no DOI, "Raworth", "planetary boundar" hits; one unrelated "Doughnut" in [NOTE-1651](NOTE-1651.md)).
- Further held entries it bears on: **[CLAIM-371](../claims.d/CLAIM-371.md)** (incomparable profiles; positive weightings rank them either way) — the dashboard is the refusal to weight; **[CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md)** (no coarse-graining normatively adequate because it preserves aggregate performance) — the masking-by-aggregates finding is an instance; **[CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md)** (aggregation as an independent obstruction).

## Limitations

Inequality analysis for one year (2017) and three coarse clusters; trend lines linear; data gaps for several indicators; authors are the framework's originators and advocates (they state a scholar-activist aim).
