# NOTE-tmppxn3n: Chotikul — the Soviet theory of reflexive control

## Contribution

The report says what Western analysts knew of reflexive control in 1986,
mostly in Lefebvre's words. It covers the definitions, the
reflection-and-control split, rank of reflection, the list of methods, and
the history from Lefebvre's 1963–64 proposals to Panov's classified report
of 1968 and Druzhinin and Kontorov in 1972–76 (pp. 89–91). Its own
contribution is a "psychohistorical framework" (abstract): the claim that
reflexive control grew out of Russian history and character. These are the
swaddling hypothesis, projection, the idealized leader, *vranyo* and
*maskirovka* (pp. 39–77). By its own account the study is "basically
exploratory and descriptive as opposed to scientific in nature" (preface).
For the record its value is second-hand access to Lefebvre and Smolyan's
*Algebra of Conflict* (1968) and Lefebvre and Lefebvre's 1984 report, which
the record cannot reach.

## Key insight

In the source's own statement, the target is not overpowered. It is
supplied "with the grounds by which he is able to logically derive his own
decision, but one that is predetermined by the other side" (Lefebvre,
quoted p. 83). What the controller supplies is a component of the target's
decision: its picture of the situation, its goal, its doctrine, or the
decision itself (p. 82). Two things make it work: an accurate model of how
the target reasons, and concealment of the influence. Neither depends on
the target reasoning well (p. 79).

## Assumptions

The account is not formal. These are the conditions it says reflexive
control needs, as stated.

- **An accurate model of the target.** "an accurate image of the
  opponent's image of the situation and of how the opponent applies a
  particular "doctrine"" (Lefebvre, p. 78). It also needs knowledge of the
  target's "politics, ideology, military doctrine, objectives, ... psychology,
  the personal qualities of his executive personnel, his mutual relations,
  and emotional state" (Druzhinin and Kontorov, p. 79). And it needs a high
  enough estimate of the target: "avoidance of an underestimation of the
  enemy's abilities and of his rank of reflection" (Chotikul, p. 83).
- **Concealment.** "at the same time conceal from him the fact that one
  "is" influencing him" (Lefebvre, p. 78). And "The main condition for
  success in this propagandistic influence is masking the very fact of
  influence" (Lefebvre, 1984 report pp. 125–126, quoted p. 10). Chotikul:
  "the appearance of non-threat and non-influence is an important
  component, in fact probably the principle element, of reflexive control
  theory" (p. 98).
- **Plausibility, fitted to the target's existing beliefs.** Of
  *maskirovka*, "which is also crucial to their perception of reflexive
  control": "whatever is done must appear as highly plausible to the enemy,
  and conform to both his perspective on Soviet doctrine and to his
  strategic assumptions" (Chotikul, p. 70). Of *vranyo*: "reflexive control,
  like vranyo, must have some grounding in reality so that it can enter and
  effect the target's cognitive arena without setting off his alarm
  systems" (Chotikul, p. 68).
- **A target whose reasoning can be imitated.** Reflexive control "can be
  successfully executed independent of the "quality" of the opponent's
  reasoning so long as this reasoning is imitated thoroughly and accurately
  enough" (Chotikul, attributing it to "the Soviets", p. 79).
- **The setting.** The setting is Cold War conflict: military command and
  control, diplomacy, propaganda aimed at Western publics, and the Soviet
  state's control of its own population ("allies", p. 45). No individual
  case is analysed in detail. The cases are historical anecdotes (Yalta,
  detente, the Korean War prisoners of Appendix A(3)).

## Key results

The report has no theorems or experiments. Its main statements are listed
here, with whose they are.

- **Definition (Lefebvre, 1984 report p. 4, quoted p. 5 and in the
  abstract).** "conveying to a partner or an opponent specially prepared
  information to incline him to voluntarily make the predetermined
  decision." Footnote 4 credits the 1984 report to Lefebvre alone. The
  references list it as Lefebvre and Lefebvre, Science Applications, Inc.,
  1984.
- **Definition (Lefebvre, *Algebra of Conflict* p. 45, quoted p. 78).**
  "One gains an advantage in conflict if one has an accurate image of the
  opponent's image of the situation and of how the opponent applies a
  particular "doctrine" in an attempt to solve the problem as "he" sees it;
  above all if one is able to influence the opponent's perception of the
  situation or his goals or his doctrine and at the same time conceal from
  him the fact that one "is" influencing him." Chotikul glosses it: "the
  first part expresses in simple form the purpose of reflexive control, the
  latter part the method."
- **Chotikul's gloss (p. 78).** "Reflexive control is thus an
  identification of the reasoner's intellect with that of his opponent and
  an emulation of this reasoning with the objective of ultimately
  disrupting and controlling the opponent's 'decision algorithm.'" And on
  p. 79: it is "accomplished by understanding his cognitive map thoroughly
  enough to shape his perceptions of the situation without evoking his
  awareness that his thoughts are not his own."
- **The formalism, as Chotikul reports it (p. 79).** "Reflexive
  interaction can be expressed by the polynomial: Ω = T + Ωx + Ωy (where T
  represents the situation)." The decision to execute reflexive control is
  drawn as a figure, an agent beside the sum [real stage] + [reflection of
  opponent's goal] + [problem solving doctrines]. Ωx and Ωy are not
  defined. The images of self and other appear in words only, as Lefebvre's
  "multiple-tier awareness" (Algebra of Conscience p. 76, quoted p. 59): "a
  person's direct awareness of his own "inner world," his awareness of his
  awareness of his inner world, coupled with an image of someone else's
  inner world, an image of someone else's image of his inner world, etc."
  Figure 13, "derived from" the 1984 report pp. 35–37, adds: "In order to
  correctly interpret the actions of another, one must take into account
  these reflexive constructions" (p. 59). Figure 11 (p. 48) draws the target
  as nested boxes, doctrine ⊃ goal ⊃ "perceptual arena", with the "agent
  of reflexive control" acting on each.
- **Rank of reflection (p. 80).** Chotikul: "Awareness of the existence of
  reflection in social interaction and of its importance will obviously
  result in a higher rank of reflection, and, in the Soviet view, the
  advantage in a conflict situation lies on the side having the highest
  rank of reflection." Lefebvre, in a personal interview of 12 September
  1984 (fn. 145), as Chotikul reports it: the United States has "a rank of
  reflection of zero (0)--that is, Americans tend to pay attention and give
  validity only to the real situation as they perceive it, excluding the
  importance of the situation from the opponent's perspective". The Soviet
  Union has "a rank of reflection of one (1), which is being raised rapidly
  as a result of conscious educational efforts." No measurement is given.
- **No feedback (p. 81, Chotikul, uncited).** "It was demonstrated that
  reflexive control may be conducted effectively without a chain of
  feedback, which, prior to this realization, was believed to be a
  necessary part of any type of control. Feedback is useful in terms of
  measuring the effectiveness of the control and correcting mistakes, but in
  cases where it would be difficult to organize or prohibitively expensive,
  it can be omitted without adverse results." No experiment is described.
  The conclusion repeats it as "the closed cybernetic control loop (without
  the necessity of feedback) at the heart of reflexive control theory"
  (p. 94). Figure 15 (p. 87), from Lifshits 1984, is a loop *with* a
  FEEDBACK arrow, and marks the command channel "(reflexive control would be
  exercised here)".
- **Two modes, two types (p. 81; fn. 146, the 1984 report pp. 144–145).**
  The modes: "1) reflexive control through transformation of the enemy's
  information processing (cognitive), and 2) reflexive control by selecting
  the messages (informational)." The types: "1) constructive reflexive
  control in which the enemy is influenced to voluntarily make a decision
  favorable to the controlling side, or 2) destructive reflexive control in
  which means are employed to destroy, paralyze, or neutralize the
  procedures and algorithms of the enemy's decision making processes." This
  is Chotikul's paraphrase, not marked as a quotation.
- **The methods (p. 82).** Chotikul's outline, introduced as "each of
  which are described in detail in Lefebvre's book on Reflexive Control".
  The list carries no quotation marks and no footnote. The symbols are as
  printed and are not explained in the report:
  1. "transferring false information about the real situation" (π_yx → π_y)
  2. "creating a goal for the opponent" (μ_yx → μ_y)
  3. "creating the doctrine for the opponent" (Δ_yx → Δ_y)
  4. "transferring a decision" (P_yx → P_y)
  5. "transferring an image of the stage" (μ_yx → π_yx → π_y → μ_y)
  6. "the transformation" π_yxy → π_yx
  7. "the transformation" μ_yxy → μ_yx
  8. "the transformation" Δ_yxy → Δ_yx
  9. "the chain" μ_yxy → π_yxy → π_yx → μ_yx
  10. "neutralization of an opponent's deductions"

  A bracket against items 5–10 reads: "When the rank of reflection
  (reflexive control ability/skill) is raised, these more complicated
  transformation chains can be used." From Druzhinin and Kontorov
  (paraphrased): "the main types of reflexive control over an opponent are
  camouflage, disinformation, demonstration ... and stimulation". The last
  "is synonymous with conveying information to cause an adversary to act in
  a predetermined manner". Chotikul adds that "The enemy's perceptions of
  the situation and his goals appear to be most susceptible" (pp. 82–83).
- **Summary definition (Lefebvre, *Algebra of Conflict* p. 34, quoted
  pp. 83–84).** "Control of an opponent's decision, which in the end is a
  forcing of a certain behavioral strategy on him through reflexive
  interaction, is not achieved directly, not by blatant force, but by means
  of providing him with the grounds by which he is able to logically derive
  his own decision, but one that is predetermined by the other side. The
  transfer of grounds is a switching of X into the process of reflection of
  the situation by Y; it is by this that X begins to control the decision
  making process. ... The process of transferring grounds for making
  decisions from one opponent to the other we call reflexive control. Any
  deceitful movements (provocation, intrigues, disguises, deceit in general)
  are achievements of reflexive control."
- **The controller's own guard (p. 83, Chotikul).** "It is also important
  to apply non-repeating techniques to prevent the opponent from deducing
  what means and methods are being applied and thus allowing him the
  opportunity to develop appropriate countermeasures." Compare p. 80, "means
  of eliminating patterns and vulnerabilities and establishing randomness of
  actions", and Ashby's requisite variety (p. 75). Lefebvre (*Algebra of
  Conflict* p. 18, quoted p. 80): "In contrast to a scholarly debate, the
  most inventive liar wins in conflict."
- **Origin (pp. 89–90, Chotikul's paraphrase of the 1984 report
  pp. 5–31).** In 1963 Lefebvre proposed a "'modeling system'" of three
  units: one to simulate one's own decisions, one to simulate the
  adversary's, and a decision unit. He did so against objections that
  "decisions must be independent of the decisions of the adversary". He set
  out "the concept of influencing the enemy's channels of information ... by
  influencing the adversary to make a false 'optimal' decision--optimal in
  fact to the opposing side." In 1964 he proposed a "positional indexation"
  of goal, doctrine, map and decision. Chotikul: "Lefebvre's ideas were
  followed by experiments which proved their efficacy". The experiments are
  not described.
- **The psychocultural hypotheses, and the author's verdict.** The four
  hypotheses are on pp. 5–6. On the two psychocultural ones: "Due to the
  impossibility of scientifically validating these hypotheses, it cannot be
  said that they have been proved as presented here" (p. 93). On the
  direction of cause: "it is of utmost difficulty to conclusively determine
  whether reflexive control is a cause or an effect of the Soviet social
  environment" (p. 64). The swaddling hypothesis is called "less
  scientifically accepted" (p. 53).

## Concepts

- **Reflexive control.** As defined in C1 and C2. The report uses it both
  for a formal theory (Lefebvre's) and for any purposeful shaping of
  perception, including a state's management of its own people's "cognitive
  map" (p. 47) and propaganda, *maskirovka* and *vranyo*.
- **Reflection.** In the Marxist–Leninist sense, cognition as "the
  reflection of the material world in the human mind" (p. 43). Reflexive
  control is "a natural extension and refinement of the concept of
  reflection" (p. 45).
- **Multiple-tier awareness.** Lefebvre's term: images of one's own inner
  world, of another's, and of the other's image of one's own, "etc."
  (p. 59).
- **Rank of reflection.** The level of awareness of reflection a person or
  society operates at (p. 80). The report gives it as an integer and says
  the higher rank wins.
- **Constructive and destructive reflexive control.** Constructive leads
  the target to a decision. Destructive breaks the target's
  decision-making ("destroy, paralyze, or neutralize the procedures and
  algorithms", p. 81).
- **Cognitive and informational modes.** Cognitive transforms the target's
  information processing. Informational selects the messages (p. 81).
- **Targets of reflexive control (Figure 10, p. 48).** These are "Decision
  Process or System of Decision Making", "Individuals Responsible for
  Decision Making" and "Cultural Complex Within Which Decision is Embedded".
- ***Vranyo*.** "the dissemination of untruths which have some grounding in
  reality" (p. 66), as distinct from *lozh*, outright lies.
- ***Maskirovka*.** "camouflage, concealment, and deception" (p. 69).

## Connections

**Whose words.** Most of the theory in the report is Lefebvre's, at second
hand, from three of his works. The rest is Chotikul's. Her conclusion,
§IV, is about the psychocultural thesis.

- *The 1984 report (Lefebvre and Lefebvre; Lefebvre alone in fn. 4)*: the
  one-line definition (p. 5); the masking passage (p. 10); the
  cognitive/informational and constructive/destructive distinctions (p. 81,
  paraphrased); Figure 13 (p. 59) and Figure 17 (p. 91); the history
  (pp. 84–91, paraphrased). The ten methods (p. 82) are presented as an
  outline of "Lefebvre's book on Reflexive Control", uncited and not
  marked as quotation.
- *Algebra of Conflict (Lefebvre and Smolyan, 1968; JPRS 52700, 1971)*: the
  long definitions (pp. 78, 83–84), "the most inventive liar wins" (p. 80)
  and the leader passage (p. 62). Footnotes 142, 144 and 147 credit it to
  Lefebvre alone. Footnote 112 and the references give Lefebvre and Smolyan.
- *Algebra of Conscience (1982)*: the two ethical systems (pp. 23–25, 55)
  and multiple-tier awareness (p. 59).
- *Lefebvre in person*: the ranks of reflection (interview, p. 80), the
  claim that the theory was classified (pp. 6, 90), the children's guessing
  games (seminar, p. 93), and a class handout (Figure 3).
- *Chotikul's own*: the glosses on pp. 78–79, the no-feedback statement
  (p. 81, uncited), the maskirovka and vranyo conditions (pp. 68, 70), and
  all of the psychocultural argument.

Other sources are Druzhinin and Kontorov (pp. 46, 79, 82), Ashby (pp. 75,
85), Hopple on strategic assumptions (p. 70), and a large Cold War
secondary literature (Hingley, Gorer, Mehnert, Beaumont, Raven and
Kruglanski, Festinger by way of Brehm and Cohen).

**The psychocultural argument.** It claims that reflexive control,
formalized only in the 1960s, had been practised informally throughout
Russian history. It traces this to the harsh steppe and the peasant
commune (pp. 39–40). It adds the swaddling hypothesis and the projection it
is said to train (pp. 51–53), dissonance reduced by projection onto
external enemies (pp. 53–58), dependence on an idealized leader (pp. 60–64),
a culture of *vranyo* and secrecy (pp. 64–68), and risk aversion with
redundancy (pp. 72–76). The support is citation of secondary sources,
national-character studies, journalism and two émigré interviews. There is
no data bearing on reflexive control itself. The links from trait to
theory are stated ("It appears that ...", "It stands to reason ...") rather
than shown. The author grants that the hypotheses are unproved (p. 93) and
that cause and effect cannot be told apart (p. 64). The Cold War polemic is
open (the Soviet Union is "the most militarized and militant nation on
earth", p. 16). The closing appeal to Soviet psychic research as "a
refinement and application of reflexive control at a highly developed
level" (p. 97) does not help its credibility. As evidence on reflexive
control the argument carries no weight. It does document how the concept
was received in US defence analysis in 1986.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmpa2zsl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2zsl.md), the channel.** The reading supports the core of the
  claim, in its sources' words.
  - The definition is about the evidence channel: "conveying ... specially
    prepared information" (p. 5).
  - The informational mode is literally "selecting the messages" (p. 81).
  - Chotikul says it of a state's own population: "control over a human
    being would best be exercised by purposefully influencing the inputs he
    receives from his environment (i.e., through purposeful manipulation and
    management of perceptions)" (p. 45).
  - The claim's phrase "an image of the target's image of the situation"
    is Lefebvre's own, in *Algebra of Conflict* (p. 78).
  - Concealment is a defining condition (pp. 10, 78, 98). That supports
    the claim's contrast with Bayesian persuasion ([THEORY-404](../theory.d/THEORY-404.md)), where the
    design is open.
- **[CLAIM-tmpa2zsl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2zsl.md), the ρ/b split. It does not match the methods.** The
  list on p. 82 is indexed by *which component of the target's decision is
  replaced*: situation image π, goal μ, doctrine Δ, decision P. It is also
  indexed by *depth*: yx for the target's own element, yxy for the target's
  image of the controller's image. It is not indexed by whether the signal
  tracks the target's current lean. Read against e = e₀ + ρm + b (my
  observations):
  - **b fits only method 1.** Method 1, "transferring false information
    about the real situation", is an evidence term. It is constructive and
    steers toward an outcome fixed in advance. So it is b, and it matches
    the claim's "The second is closer to reflexive control as usually
    described."
  - **Methods 2–4 act on something other than evidence.** They supply the
    goal, the doctrine or the decision. In [CASE-106](../cases.d/CASE-106.md)'s model these act on
    what the evidence is scored *for* and *by*, not on e. So does the
    cognitive mode, "transformation of the enemy's information
    processing", which in the model's terms changes κ′ itself.
  - **Destructive control is neither ρ nor b.** It aims to "destroy,
    paralyze, or neutralize" the target's procedures (p. 81) and "to keep
    the adversary confused, uncertain, and lacking in knowledge" (p. 68).
    That is noise or a broken loop. A linearized mean model has no term for
    it.
  - **ρ appears only as a condition on b.** The source has nothing like ρ
    as a separate mode of control. The nearest thing is Chotikul's
    condition that deception "conform to ... his strategic assumptions"
    (p. 70) and have "some grounding in reality" so as not to set off
    "alarm systems" (p. 68). That is a signal that agrees with the target's
    present lean, used so that the push b is accepted. Hopple, quoted on the
    same page, gives the consequence the claim derives for ρ > 0:
    assumptions "reinforced by the other side's active deception", and
    "unwillingness to look at evidence in the light of alternative beliefs
    leads to warning disasters". So the source agrees that tracking the
    lean locks the target in, but treats it as stealth for steering, not as
    a second kind of control.
  - **No feedback means open-loop control.** If reflexive control "may be
    conducted effectively without a chain of feedback" (p. 81), the
    controller does not observe the target's current lean. A ρ term needs
    either feedback or a forecast of m from the controller's prior model.
    What the source describes is selection fixed in advance by a model of
    the target, "imitated thoroughly and accurately enough" (p. 79). That is
    exactly [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)'s added clause, "nor through anyone's model of it",
    and it sits on the b side. The no-feedback statement is uncited and
    contradicts the report's own Figure 15, so it is weak support.
- **[CLAIM-tmpa2zsl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2zsl.md), whether the target's reasoning is left intact.** For
  constructive control, yes. The target "is able to logically derive his
  own decision" (Lefebvre, p. 83), with no awareness "that his thoughts are
  not his own" (p. 79). But the source's scope is wider than the claim's
  "reasoning for itself". Methods 2–3 supply the goal and the doctrine,
  which are the premises the inference runs on. Destructive control aims
  to break the inference. The claim's account fits constructive control by
  false situation information. It should say that the source also counts
  the supply of goals and decision rules, and the disabling of the loop.
- **[CLAIM-tmpa2zsl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2zsl.md), Defences (commitment).** The source sharpens the claim
  against commitment fixed in advance. A doctrine fixed in advance is what
  reflexive control models: "how the opponent applies a particular
  "doctrine"" (p. 78). It is also something reflexive control can supply:
  "creating the doctrine for the opponent" (p. 82). A committed rule makes
  the target easier to imitate, which open-loop control needs. My
  observation: commitment defends only if the committed rule does not take
  the reachable channel as input, and if the commitment was not itself
  supplied.
- **[MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md), "Depth is an arms race, not a defence".** The source
  confirms that its tradition offers depth as both weapon and defence, and
  never considers escalation.
  - "the advantage in a conflict situation lies on the side having the
    highest rank of reflection" (p. 80).
  - Deeper chains, methods 5–9, need a higher rank (p. 82).
  - The controller must not underestimate the target's rank (p. 83).
  - Lefebvre's remedy for the United States is to raise its awareness,
    that is, its rank (p. 80).
  - The epigraph's defence is vigilance, "to be continually aware of one's
    vulnerabilities as a target" (Moose, p. 5). So is Targ and Harary's,
    learning "to discriminate our own psychic impressions from externally
    induced suggestions" (p. 97).

  This is [MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md)'s §IV defence, depth, with nothing on a fixed point
  of escalation. On the claim's refutation route, a bound on useful depth,
  the report offers only Lefebvre's assertion that whole societies run at
  ranks 0 and 1. That is unmeasured, though consistent with low level-k.
  None of [MOM-CLM-132](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-132.md)'s three structural defences appears. One more is
  implicit, on the controller's side: "non-repeating techniques" and
  "randomness of actions" (pp. 80, 83) deny the other side a model to
  reflect. My observation: unpredictability is a structural defence that
  needs no depth. It is a fourth candidate beside restricting the channel,
  costly signals and commitment, and it works against commitment.

## Limitations

- It is a secondary source. The formalism appears as a single uninterpreted
  polynomial and a list of unexplained index chains. Nothing of Lefebvre's
  algebra of images is reconstructed.
- The no-feedback result, the "experiments which proved their efficacy"
  (p. 89) and the claim that the theory was classified are reported without
  evidence.
- Attribution is loose. The 1984 report and *Algebra of Conflict* are
  credited to Lefebvre alone in the footnotes and to two authors in the
  references.
- The psychocultural argument is unproved by the author's own account, and
  it is written as Cold War advocacy.
- The OCR loses most figures. Figures 1–9, 12, 14, 16 and 17 were not
  checked against page images.

- Whether the no-feedback claim is Lefebvre's, and on what evidence.
