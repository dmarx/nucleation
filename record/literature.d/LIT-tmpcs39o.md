# LIT-tmpcs39o: A multifaceted program causes lasting progress for the very poor: Evidence from six countries

Banerjee, A., Duflo, E., Goldberg, N., Karlan, D., Osei, R., Parienté, W., Shapiro, J., Thuysbaert, B. & Udry, C. (2015). A multifaceted program causes lasting progress for the very poor: Evidence from six countries. *Science* 348(6236): 1260799. doi:10.1126/science.1260799.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpl3nvb](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl3nvb.md). Pages are the article's, 1260799-n.*

- **A time-limited push.** Consumption support lasts "between 4 and 13
  months", partly "to reduce incentives to sell (or eat up) the productive
  assets"; coaching visits are "to provide accountability" (p. -2).
- **Gains hold after exit.** At endline 2, one year after all support
  ended: "It is striking that there is no evidence of mean reversion in the
  per capita consumption, food security, or assets" (p. -8). Pooled
  consumption +0.122 SD at endline 1 and +0.120 SD at endline 2.
- **More work, not less.** "Adult labor supply increases by 17.5 min per
  adult per day" at endline 1 "and 11.2 min (6.1% increase) at endline 2"
  (p. -8); savings stayed up though "continued savings was not required
  after the program".
- **Where it failed.** In Honduras "a large fraction of the chickens died
  due to illness" and households "had lost most of their productive asset"
  (p. -10).
- **Trap or flat.** "The fact that the effects of the program seem durable
  supports the interpretation that the program unlocked a poverty trap.
  Nevertheless, the average effects are not very large" (p. -14); a
  "poverty flat" fits too.
- **Open in 2015.** "How long will these results persist? This will not be
  known until some participants are followed for a longer period of time"
  (p. -15). Answered for India by [LIT-tmpit9mn](LIT-tmpit9mn.md).

## Standing in the record

Registered as a counterpoint to the branch's dependency claims, which lean
on support producing low effort or lost capacity in the recipient
([CLAIM-tmpqa0qu](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpqa0qu.md), the slack-effort route of [CLAIM-tmpc3x0c](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpc3x0c.md), [THEORY-tmph35ra](../theory.d/THEORY-tmph35ra.md)'s
Samaritan's dilemma). For poor households given time-limited support, the
evidence runs the other way: effort and capacity rose during support and
stayed up after it. The study does not test the dilemma itself, whose
premise is help the recipient can count on; this help was announced as
temporary. It is evidence about households, not about states or donor
projects. Filed from it: [THEORY-tmpmcnzn](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmcnzn.md).
