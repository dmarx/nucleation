# CLAIM-738: Rights, demands and commitments are distinct and none establishes another: a right does not depend on being demanded or on anyone's commitment to it, a demand's being asserted, maintained or recognized gives it no warrant, and a commitment is neither an obligation nor an authorization, while an obligation persists without commitment; each passage between them, a right generating a demand or a valid undertaking generating a right, needs its own warrant

## The claim

The owner, at U41 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):

> rights vs demands vs commitments

A95 answers with three definitions:

> **A right establishes a normative position: what someone is entitled to do, receive, control, or resist.**
>
> **A demand specifies a condition that someone or something requires an arrangement to satisfy.**
>
> **A commitment organizes an agent's or institution's conduct around maintaining or fulfilling some condition, policy, or obligation over time.**

"A right can generate a demand. A commitment can fulfill a demand. A
promise or contract can sometimes create a right. But none of these
transformations is automatic. That is the crucial point."

The non-implications, each with A's case:

- **Right and demand.** Right_N(B, r) ⇏ DemandExpressed(B, r): an easement
  "can remain legally valid" though its holder never uses it, does not
  know of it, or is "too intimidated to demand access". The converse fails
  too: "Someone may demand access to a private residence without
  possessing any corresponding entitlement." (A95 §1.)
- **Demand and warrant.** A95 §2 separates asserted, operational and
  normatively warranted demands; A97 IV adds that a demand "can be
  *institutionally recognized* without actually being justified" and
  that "**model inclusion must not imply normative endorsement**".
- **Commitment and obligation.** Committed(A, φ) ⇏ ∃B ClaimRight(B, A, φ):
  "I can abandon a private writing project without necessarily wronging
  anyone." And A97 V: Committed(A, φ) ⇏ Owes(A, B, φ) and
  Owes(A, B, φ) ⇏ Committed(A, φ), since a promisee's claim survives the
  promisor's loss of commitment, and a commitment can persist after the
  promisee releases the promisor.
- **Commitment and authorization.** A95 §3 writes psychological or
  organizational commitment ≠ normative authorization. "An actor might be
  committed to excluding a population from a common resource while
  lacking legitimate authority to do so."

The passages that do occur are conditional (A95 §4): rights generate
demands, commitments implement demands, valid agreements generate rights,
rights constrain commitments, demands challenge commitments, and
legitimate revision transforms commitments. "The important feature is
that each transition requires its own explanation." A97 XII states two
of them as propositions: "Normative non-conversion" and
"Commitment-generated obligations are conditional on normative authority".

"Commitment" has three senses, which must be kept apart (A95 §3, A97 V,
A106 §5): practical (an agent's organized pursuit), promissory or
normative (a valid undertaking that can create obligations) and
institutional (an organization's maintained policies). A106 §5: "A
promisor can owe performance without remaining psychologically committed
to it." A102 §7 adds a fourth term, efficacy, separating "Normative
validity", "Demand specification", "Operative commitment" and "Normative
efficacy": "**The moral authority of a standard is distinct from the
organization of agency through which that standard becomes practically
authoritative.**"

## How it sits with the record

- **[TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)** (Active) represents each demand by an acceptance set and
  says "A demand is a requirement". It is the record's term for
  frustration, not a definition of demand, though A95 §2 calls it one; a
  dated note on [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md) records that. The demand in this claim is
  [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)'s, and the claim adds that nothing in an acceptance set
  carries a warrant.
- **[CLAIM-344](CLAIM-344.md)** (Active): resolve as a maintained organization of
  precedence, commitment and revision. That is the practical sense of
  commitment. This claim says it neither creates nor tracks obligations.
- **[CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)** (Active): legibility, volitional efficacy and
  correctability, of which "none establishes that the demand or the
  revision is justified". A102 §7's efficacy is [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)'s three
  achievements; this claim adds the rights and commitment terms around
  them.
- **[CLAIM-361](CLAIM-361.md)** (Active) and **[CLAIM-662](CLAIM-662.md)** (Proposed): the recognized
  demand set is not the warranted one. A97 IV's D_recognized against
  D_warranted,N is that distinction.
- **[CLAIM-725](CLAIM-725.md)** files the demand side in its sharpest form, from A106
  §4's provenance tuple: a demand's satisfaction condition does not carry
  its warrant. A97 IV first proposed the tuple as a "provenance record",
  d = (source, bearer, addressee, A_d, scope, warrant). That "provenance"
  is not any of [TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md)'s senses (formation, maintenance, source of a
  capacity): it includes a warrant, which no process supplies.
- **[CLAIM-743](CLAIM-743.md)** files the one passage A97 X singles out: a valid
  release changes what is owed, and an institution's ceasing to count a
  demand does not.
- [TERM-080](../terms.d/TERM-080.md) (Proposed) and [TERM-109](../terms.d/TERM-109.md) (Active) use "commitment" in passing
  and fix none of these senses.

The aquifer, [CASE-282](../cases.d/CASE-282.md), puts all three to work in one arrangement.

## What it does not say

- Not that warranted demands must derive from rights. A100 §12:
  "Justified demands may derive from rights, but they may also derive from
  other contractualistically justified obligations."
- Not which rights exist or which demands are warranted.
- Not that Hohfeld's "claim-right" is the record's CLAIM. A95 §9: "Hohfeldian
  *claim-right* is not the same as an ordinary asserted *claim*."

## Note of 2026-11-17: Darwall, through Stern: an asserted demand has no warrant of its own, but obligation is legitimate demandability
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1975 LIT-2756 — new or Unavailable; the reading filed on 2026-11-18 and the unread book it stands in for -->

Stern 2014, read ([NOTE-2065](../notes.d/NOTE-2065.md), [THEORY-1975](../theory.d/THEORY-1975.md)), standing in for Darwall 2006 ([LIT-2756](../literature.d/LIT-2756.md)). The THEORY now `corroborates` this claim, which does not rest on it.

**What it corroborates.** On Darwall's account "whether or not an obligation obtains doesn’t depend on the actual demands an individual or a community of individuals might make: if they could legitimately make such demands, then an obligation holds" (p. 3). An actual demand, asserted or not, settles nothing; legitimacy does. That is the claim's "a demand's being asserted, maintained or recognized gives it no warrant", reached independently.

**Where it parts.** Darwall makes obligation itself consist in legitimate demandability: obligation "involves the exercise of legitimate authority over the person obliged" (p. 2). The claim keeps rights and obligations apart from demands altogether and treats each passage between them as needing its own warrant; on Darwall's view the passage from obligation to legitimate demand is constitutive, not a further step. Stern's objection (p. 12), that a sufferer's need obliges without any second-personal demand, is the case where the claim's separation and Darwall's identification give different verdicts, and it sides with the claim.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Hohfeld, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1984 — new reading and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Hohfeld 1913, read ([NOTE-2071](../notes.d/NOTE-2071.md), [THEORY-1984](../theory.d/THEORY-1984.md)): corroboration for legal relations, which the THEORY carries. Hohfeld's positions are logically independent, as the claim says rights, demands and commitments are: "X's right that Y should not eat the food persists even though X's own privilege of doing so has been extinguished" (pp. 36–37), and whether a privilege should carry protective claims "is ultimately a question of justice and policy" (p. 36); he warns that "the term 'rights' tends to be used indiscriminately to cover what in a given case may be a privilege, a power, or an immunity" (p. 30). He does not discuss demands or commitments, so the corroboration is for the general point that normative positions do not entail one another, not for the claim's three terms; the claim's own body already warns that a Hohfeldian claim-right is not the record's CLAIM. No status changes.
