# THEORY-tmps7et3: Single judges agree moderately on how pragmatically similar two spoken utterances are, mean pairwise correlation 0.45 to 0.72 by session, less when the utterances differ in words, so a mean over six to nine judges is much more reliable than any one judge

## Source

Ward and Marco (2024), LIT-tmphdoda, §§6–8, Tables 2–3, pp. 157–160 of the
LREC-COLING 2024 proceedings; read in NOTE-tmpmkero.

## What was actually shown

Judges rated each pair of a dialog utterance and its re-enactment on a 1–5
slider, for similarity "in terms of the overall feeling, tone, and intent"
(Fig. 1). Session 1: 220 English pairs, 9 judges; Session 2: 238 English
pairs, 9 judges; Session 3: 235 Spanish pairs, 6 judges. Mean pairwise
Pearson correlation between judges: 0.45, 0.72, 0.66 (Table 2). In Session
1 the pairwise values ran 0.18 to 0.80 (Table 3; mean recomputed as 0.447).
Leaving out the lexically different pairs (Lexically-Distinct and
Context-Only re-enactments) raised Session 2 to 0.80 (p. 160).

Treating judges as exchangeable, the Spearman–Brown reliability of the
panel mean is 0.88, 0.96 and 0.92 by session. That is my computation, not
the paper's.

What could have failed: judges could have agreed near zero, as some pilot
judges' lack of confidence suggested (p. 156). They did not, even in the
first session.

## What this does not say

- **That pragmatic similarity is rated at 0.45.** That is the first session
  and a single-judge figure. The second English session, the Spanish one
  and any panel mean are higher.
- **That the agreement would hold for strangers rating alone.** Judges
  discussed their ratings during the session (p. 158), were socially
  connected, and included the authors (p. 157).
- **Anything across languages.** Every pair is within English or within
  Spanish.
- **That a judge is self-consistent.** No second pass was run.
- **What the judges attend to.** They reported different cues (p. 156), and
  the instruction mixes static tone with likely listener response (Fig. 1).
  The agreement is on a blend, not on one construct.
