**Lookups.** Jaksland 2024 is open access (CC BY) at Cambridge Core and
was read from the publisher's PDF. Faye and Jaksland 2021 is open access
(CC BY), but the Springer PDF, the Copenhagen repository copy and the
PhilPapers preprint all returned a bot challenge to this client; it was
read from the publisher's HTML full text, rendered in a headless browser,
which has no page numbers. Barad 2003 has no open copy: Unpaywall reports it
closed, and OpenAlex lists no open location and no repository full text.
Its two most-quoted passages (pp. 814–815) are read at second hand in both
notes.

**What the readings found, against what the record filed this morning.**

- **The exchange's accounts held up.** A4 and A6 reported both papers
  accurately: the three criticisms of Faye and Jaksland, Jaksland's
  bracketing of the quantum question, his reconstruction of Barad as
  favouring features that resist approximation, and his leaving that open.
- **[CLAIM-476](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-476.md) compares on the template conception, which Barad
  rejects.** Jaksland shows Barad holds agential realism as naturalized
  metaphysics applied once, and rejects both analogy and a template other
  domains instantiate ([THEORY-1083](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-1083.md)). The luria comparison is the
  template reading. Its argument never leaned on Barad's quantum
  credentials, which Faye and Jaksland show quantum mechanics does not
  supply ([THEORY-1071](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-1071.md)), so it stands, with a dated note saying which
  conception it uses.
- **[CLAIM-477](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-477.md) is an unrestricted template too.** Its route to
  unrestricted scope is the template's, needing a warrant in each domain,
  not Barad's, which needs features that admit no approximation
  ([THEORY-1086](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-1086.md)). Its non-eliminative physical precedent is Bohr as Faye
  and Jaksland read him ([THEORY-1076](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-1076.md)), not Barad.
- **[QUESTION-280](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-280.md) asked the easier half.** Jaksland finds
  individualism "immediately more agreeable" as approximable; the dualisms
  are the open case. He adds a third answer: independence that is real but
  contingent, on the size of Planck's constant.
- **[TERM-087](../../record/terms.d/TERM-087.md) is corrected in place.** It had kept the Heisenberg cut
  apart. Jaksland glosses the agential cut as "more commonly known as a
  Heisenberg cut", and Faye and Jaksland note that a movable cut is
  Heisenberg's view against Bohr's. The TERM now has Barad's definition of
  an apparatus at second hand (2007, 148, as Faye and Jaksland quote it).
- **[CLAIM-136](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-136.md) and [CLAIM-472](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-472.md).** Barad's view is eliminative at the
  fundamental level ("relations without preexisting relata"); the first
  half of [CLAIM-472](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-472.md) has Barad's own statement of exclusion (2010, 253,
  as Jaksland quotes it).
