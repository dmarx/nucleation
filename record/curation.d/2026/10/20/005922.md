The owner accepted the UC Davis copy of Botvinick & Cohen ([LIT-1277](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1277.md)'s
history now says so). The owner then asked for experimental work, in the
spirit of experimental philosophy, that builds on the rubber hand
illusion. A search found almost no x-phi in the strict sense of folk-
intuition surveys about body ownership. What does build on the illusion is
experimental work aimed at the questions the THEORYs of the entry of 00:10
left open. Nine works were filed in three groups, and each was read in full
from a lawful copy.

- **Suggestion.**
  - Lush 2020 ([LIT-1276](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1276.md)), from the PsyArXiv final manuscript.
  - Lush et al. 2020 ([LIT-1271](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1271.md)), with its supplement and peer-review
    file.
  - Ehrsson et al. 2022 ([LIT-1264](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1264.md)) and Lush & Seth 2022 ([LIT-1267](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1267.md)),
    filed as rivals.
  - Roseboom & Lush 2022 ([LIT-1280](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1280.md)), from the accepted manuscript and
    the authors' OSF notebooks.
- **Drift.** Rohde, Di Luca & Ernst 2011 ([LIT-1281](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1281.md)) and Abdulkarim &
  Ehrsson 2016 ([LIT-1268](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1268.md)).
- **Structure.** Longo et al. 2008 ([LIT-1275](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1275.md)), from the published PDF
  on the first author's lab site, and Kalckert & Ehrsson 2012
  ([LIT-1273](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1273.md)).

**What changed.** [THEORY-765](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-765.md) had named its own refutation: drift
toward the rubber hand "as strongly under asynchrony". Rohde et al. found
exactly that, with asynchronous brushing interrupted every 10 s, and found
as much drift with the hand only seen. The THEORY is Rejected, and
[THEORY-766](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-766.md) corrects it. The new THEORY claims a difference in reports.
It ranks the ownership report as weaker than referred touch, and it says
drift is not a measure of either report. It leaves open whether the
stimulation or expectation produces the difference.

The old promotion test also had a flaw that the 2022 exchange exposed. The
test accepted "trait suggestibility measured and the synchrony effect
survives it". Ehrsson et al. met that clause on 353 participants. Lush &
Seth then showed that uniform but unequal expectations predict the same
result. The new test requires expectations to be matched in the
participants who were actually stimulated.

[THEORY-769](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-769.md), [THEORY-767](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-767.md) and [THEORY-768](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-768.md) keep their claims. They
take version 2 with tighter promotion tests: expectations measured or
matched, drift not standing in for ownership, and, for the interoceptive
one, the sensitivity relation surviving trait suggestibility. None moves
status.

**Who is on which side.** The record's two predictive accounts of ownership
have source authors on opposite sides of the 2022 exchange. Tsakiris, of
Apps & Tsakiris, signs Ehrsson et al. Seth, of Seth 2013, signs the reply.
Longo signs Ehrsson et al. and is first author of Longo et al. 2008.
