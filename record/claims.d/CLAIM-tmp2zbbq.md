# CLAIM-tmp2zbbq: Self-presentations that differ by audience do no harm while the audiences are kept apart; people keep them apart and script the occasions where they must meet, and broadcast and networked media bring them together unchosen, which is when the presentations collide

## The claim

This is the observation [CLAIM-196](CLAIM-196.md) draws on, stated on its own so that its
warrant can be. A person presents differently to different audiences
([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md), the presented self), and the difference is not a defect while
the audiences are kept apart. Three findings carry it, all from [LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md).

- **Segregation.** Different styles for different groups do no harm while
  the groups are kept apart. Carmichael "was able to maintain distinct
  styles as long as he was able to segment social groups, he ran into
  trouble when electronic media collapsed those social groups" ([LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md),
  pp. 36–37, after Meyrowitz).
- **Scripting.** Weddings, "in which context collisions are common, are
  frequently scripted to make everyone comfortable" ([LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md), p. 38).
- **Collapse.** Broadcast and networked media bring the audiences together,
  making occasions on which they meet that the person did not choose.

[CASE-064](../cases.d/CASE-064.md) is a documented collapse: a photograph posted for friends who knew
a running joke reached strangers four weeks later, who read it as contempt
for the war dead, and Stone lost her job.

boyd makes the point that keeping contexts apart is not deception:
distinct contexts are how people "properly locate their performances".

## What it does not say

- Anything about sections, covers or gluing. That reading, and the claim
  that role conflict can only be where audiences meet, are [CLAIM-196](CLAIM-196.md)'s, and
  interpretive; [CLAIM-196](CLAIM-196.md) rests on this claim.
- That role conflict is rare. For networked publics boyd describes the
  meeting of audiences as constant.
- That the evidence is measured. boyd's account of collapse is an
  ethnography of US teenagers in 2004–2008, documented with interviews and
  examples, and the Carmichael case is Meyrowitz's, which the record has not
  read.
