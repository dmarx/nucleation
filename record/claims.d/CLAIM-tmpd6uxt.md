# CLAIM-tmpd6uxt: One party can wrongfully bring about circumstances in which another incurs a genuine obligation, so that the second does right in discharging it and is still wronged, and the manipulation works through the target's sound moral reasoning rather than a defect in it; the obligation so created is no larger than the circumstances support, whatever the manipulator claims

## The claim

The exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c). The
owner, at U7: "double click on normative manipulation". A13 opens:

> The most striking thing about **normative manipulation** is that it can
> work *because the target's moral reasoning is sound*, not because it's
> defective.

A13 §1's case. Bob, who takes animal welfare seriously, declines to look
after Alice's dog; Alice leaves town anyway, "abandoning the dog at Bob's
house without arranging another caretaker". Bob "may genuinely have a
moral obligation to care for the animal", because "Alice has deliberately
created a situation in which fulfilling his moral duties requires doing
what Alice wants". A13 attributes the structure to Keller (2016), who
"argues that someone can wrongfully place another person under a real
moral obligation and exploit that obligation for their own ends", and
draws it out:

> *Bob ought to care for the dog. But Alice ought not to have made caring
> for the dog Bob's responsibility.*

"In other words, **an obligation can be genuine while the circumstances
that produced it are unjust**." Bob has done right and "has a legitimate
complaint against Alice".

The limit, A13: "**An exploitative attempt to create an obligation does
not establish that the obligation is unlimited—or even that it exists in
the form the manipulator claims.**" Bob may be permitted to call a
shelter; appeals to identity ("*A conscientious doctor wouldn't walk
away.*") try "to transform a broad moral value into an absolute obligation
to perform some specific act", and "The inference is frequently invalid."

## Where it sits

- [CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md) (Active): an organization can be governed from
  outside through the evidence its procedures receive or the criteria by
  which they weigh it. Normative manipulation in A13's sense is neither:
  Bob's evidence is accurate and his criteria are his own and sound. What
  the manipulator sets is the situation those criteria are applied to. A11
  and A13 call the evidence route "epistemic" manipulation; this is a third
  route, through circumstances.
- [THEORY-235](../theory.d/THEORY-235.md), the record's reading of covert
  influence, places the harm of manipulation in making the target "opaque
  to herself". Here the target can see the whole manoeuvre and still be
  bound, so whatever wrong is done is not that one. The claim does not deny
  [THEORY-235](../theory.d/THEORY-235.md), which is about covert influence; it marks a case the reading
  does not reach.
- The same exchange's [CLAIM-tmpnw7ez](CLAIM-tmpnw7ez.md) (responsibility for the conditions of
  another's conduct): there the structured party does wrong, here it does
  right. Both say the structuring party answers for the conditions.

## What it does not say

- **That Bob may refuse.** The dog's welfare still matters: "Alice's
  wrongdoing doesn't magically erase Bob's reasons to prevent suffering."
- **What Keller says.** The paper ([LIT-tmp55sg8](../literature.d/LIT-tmp55sg8.md)) is registered and unread; the
  attribution and the case's closeness to Keller's own are A's report.
