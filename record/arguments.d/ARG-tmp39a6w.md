# ARG-tmp39a6w: An intervention that disables a production pathway inside a declared boundary, or interrupts an outside controller, locates the producer of a maintained organization, because the pathway causes the capacities with a delay and can be intervened on surgically, so the fat-handedness of interventions on wholes does not reach the inference

## The inference

[CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md) gives two systems with the same trajectory under every
intervention on state. In A, the capacities produce each other inside a
declared boundary. In B, an outside controller supplies the same
production. Disabling a production pathway inside the boundary ends A's
maintenance and leaves B's alone; interrupting the controller does the
reverse. So the systems differ in one factor, where the producer sits, and
an intervention on that factor gives different outcomes. That is the
controlled contrast [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md) concludes: only interventions on the
production mechanisms, relative to a declared boundary, separate the two.

## Critical questions

- **Is everything said to be equal equal?** Yes, by construction.
  Substituting u_ext = P(c) gives the same differential equation for every
  state and every damage schedule ([CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)), so no intervention on state
  differs between them.
- **Is the factor the thing that differs?** Yes. The two systems differ only
  in whether P is computed by pathways inside the boundary or by a
  controller outside it.
- **Is the intervention surgical with respect to what it is said to
  reveal?** This is the doubt the constitution debate raises. Baumgartner
  and Gebharter, and Baumgartner and Casini, show that if a whole's
  behaviour supervenes on its parts' and is not caused by them, every cause
  of the whole is also a cause of some part along another path, so
  manipulations of whole and part never show what the whole consists in
  ([THEORY-tmp3pqnm](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp3pqnm.md)). If [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)'s conclusion were a claim of that kind,
  the knockout would be fat-handed and the contrast would show nothing.
  It is not of that kind. Removing the c₂ → c₁ production changes ċ₁ at
  once and c₁ only afterwards, so the pathway is a cause of the later
  capacities. That holds even by the debate's own temporal criterion for
  causation (Baumgartner and Gebharter's M**, [NOTE-tmpeljd8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeljd8.md) p. 17). A
  bottom-up intervention on a cause can be surgical, which the debate
  grants (their footnote 8). The answer is yes. [CLAIM-tmp1qybv](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1qybv.md) states the
  distinction.
- **Does the contrast supply the boundary?** No, and the inference does not
  claim to. That pathway X is "inside" is declared. The debate's one
  positive result, a single intervention that is surgical within each
  level, needs parthood and the level hierarchy as inputs as well
  ([THEORY-tmpv5g3z](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpv5g3z.md)). So intervention data locate a producer relative to a
  boundary and do not fix the boundary. That is [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)'s own qualifier,
  and [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s point. This is the inference's weakest step. It is a
  limit on scope, not a defect, because the conclusion is relativized to
  the declared boundary.

Active. The inference holds as [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md) states it. It would fail for a
stronger conclusion, that the maintained organization consists in the
internal production network at a time. [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md) does not draw that
conclusion, and on the debate's results interventions could not show it.
