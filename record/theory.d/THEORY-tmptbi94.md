# THEORY-tmptbi94: Stark and Hester hold that coercive control, coercion by force or threat combined with control by deprivation, exploitation and command, is not a type of violence but a condition of unfreedom experienced as entrapment, and review evidence that the level of control, with or without violence, predicts harms including post-separation violence

## Source

Stark and Hester (2019), Violence Against Women 25(1): 81-104 (LIT-tmpxwlgx), the stand-in for Stark (2007), LIT-tmp77bag;
read in NOTE-tmp4yi9s (draft).

## What was actually shown

A review, not a study. It quotes Stark's definitions (coercion, "the use of force or threats to compel or dispel a particular" response; control, "structural forms of deprivation, exploitation, and" command; their joint result, a
"condition of unfreedom") and states "Coercive control is not a type of violence." It reports studies in which
entrapment occurs "without any notable incidents of" violence in perhaps a third of cases and in which coercive
control predicts post-separation harm "far better than relationship violence".

## What this does not say

- That responsibility for a relationship goes with control; the account is of harm and its mechanism.
- That coercive control is gender-neutral: Stark ties it in heterosexual relationships to sexual inequality and holds
  the evidence on same-sex relationships to be thin.
- The 2007 book's own arguments beyond these definitions and claims.
