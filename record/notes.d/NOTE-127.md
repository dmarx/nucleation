# NOTE-127: Holik et al. — Quasi-set theory for a quantum ontology

## Contribution

Earlier work by the authors (Lombardi, da Costa and others) proposed an ontology of properties for quantum mechanics, in which quantum systems are bundles of properties that lack any principle of individuality. This paper argues that quasi-set theory, built to handle collections of items that are not individuals, is a suitable meta-language for that ontology and its structure.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (pp. 2–6): formalisms are not ontologically neutral. Peano versus Russell on numbers is the example: the Hilbert-space formalism suggests individuals with priority over their properties, while the algebraic formalism (observables first) suggests an ontology of properties. Observables are paired with universal type-properties and their instances, and eigenvalues with case-properties.
- §3 (pp. 6–10): the property ontology dissolves contextuality, non-separability and indistinguishability. The Indistinguishability Principle falls out of the requirement that only symmetric observables are allowed (Messiah & Greenberg), so it no longer needs to be postulated ad hoc.
- §4 (pp. 10–11): any language with individual constants or variables fails for a domain of non-individual bundles. They note French's (2020) observation that their view is close to OSR, and French and Ladyman's complaint that logic and set theory are inadequate.
- §§5–6 (pp. 11–22): quasi-set theory (French & Krause 2006) is introduced. Collections of indistinguishable property-instances become qsets, a bundle is built by the Axiom of Choice (one element from each qset), and qsets of bundles have a definite quasi-cardinality without individuality.
- §7 (pp. 22–23): the particle is recovered as a bundle, and the indistinguishability of "elementary particles" is inherited from the indistinguishability of the bundles' components.

## Open questions

- A worked formal answer to the complaint, which Wallace ([LIT-151](../literature.d/LIT-151.md)) also addresses, that structuralist and non-individual ontologies cannot be stated in standard logic. Compare quasi-set theory with Wallace's math-first route.
- Relevant to identity and individuality (the Identity of Indiscernibles). Compare with Leitgeb & Ladyman ([LIT-154](../literature.d/LIT-154.md)) on criteria of identity in structuralist ontology.
- Check whether using the Axiom of Choice to form bundles quietly reintroduces a selection, and so a form of individuation.
