The owner asked to file Floridi, "AI as Agency without Intelligence: On
Artificial Intelligence as a New Form of Artificial Agency and the Multiple
Realisability of Agency Thesis" (Philosophy & Technology 38:30, 2025), and
the line of work it builds on. Six works were filed. Three could be read
from lawful open copies and were. For the other three, the target among
them, no lawful full text could be reached, and they were registered unread
and Deferred, each with its access attempts recorded. The owner then
supplied the published version of the target, and it was read the same day
(see the last section). Four are read, and two remain unread.

## What entered

Read, each with a NOTE (the target was read later the same day, from a copy
the owner supplied; see the last section):

- **Floridi 2023, "AI as Agency Without Intelligence: on ChatGPT, Large
  Language Models, and Other Generative Models"** ([LIT-896](../../record/literature.d/LIT-896.md),
  doi:10.1007/s13347-023-00621-y), read in full from the publisher's
  open-access PDF ([NOTE-697](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-697.md)). It is the first statement of the slogan,
  and an editorial rather than an argument: no criteria for agency are given.
- **Floridi & Sanders 2004, "On the Morality of Artificial Agents"**
  ([LIT-898](../../record/literature.d/LIT-898.md), doi:10.1023/B:MIND.0000035461.63578.9d), read from the
  University of Hertfordshire preprint, in full, from page images, because
  the PDF has no text layer ([NOTE-701](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-701.md)). It is where the line
  starts: agenthood as interactivity, autonomy and adaptability at a stated
  level of abstraction, and moral agency split from responsibility.
- **Floridi 2008, "The Method of Levels of Abstraction"** ([LIT-900](../../record/literature.d/LIT-900.md),
  doi:10.1007/s11023-008-9113-7), read from the author's preprint in the
  University of Hertfordshire repository, in full ([NOTE-700](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-700.md)). It is a
  method set out in definitions, not a result: observables, levels and
  gradients of abstraction, with an epistemological levelism in place of an
  ontological one. Its one formal claim, that disjoint and nested gradients
  convert into each other, fails under its own definition of nesting. It
  never analyses agency.

- **Floridi 2025, the target** ([LIT-897](../../record/literature.d/LIT-897.md),
  doi:10.1007/s13347-025-00858-9). It was first registered unread, because
  no open copy could be reached. It was then read in full from the
  published version the owner supplied ([NOTE-702](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-702.md)), and it is now
  Active. It names the Multiple Realisability of Agency thesis and sets it
  against the Artificial Realisability of Intelligence. It is a taxonomy of
  kinds of agency, not an argument for that thesis. The thesis is assumed
  (p. 2). The three criteria of 2004 come back, loosened. AI's lack of
  intelligence is asserted, and no level of abstraction is stated.

Registered unread, Deferred, seeded from the abstract:

- **Floridi 2013, "Distributed Morality in an Information Society"**
  ([LIT-901](../../record/literature.d/LIT-901.md), doi:10.1007/s11948-012-9413-4). Its own abstract was not
  seen; the entry is seeded from search-result descriptions of it.
- **Floridi 2016, "Faultless responsibility"** ([LIT-899](../../record/literature.d/LIT-899.md),
  doi:10.1098/rsta.2016.0112), seeded from the published abstract, which
  Crossref carries.

Each identifier and publication date was checked against Crossref on
2026-10-10.

The relations, as filed: F2025 `extends` F2023; F2013 `extends` FS2004;
F2016 `extends` F2013. The two on unread works rest on the abstracts and

F2025 cites all five others.

## How deeply

Three read and three not when the batch was filed, and four read and two
not by the end of the day. [ADR-001](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-001.md) leaves the depth of each filing to the
curation log, and this is the statement for this batch. The owner has said
that in this record registering a work means reading it (the curation entry
of 2026-10-09, 00:41). The three unread works are an exception forced by
access, not chosen: each was looked for on 2026-10-10 and none could be
reached lawfully.

- **F2025.** OpenAlex lists it as green open access, but the open copy is
  the submitted version on PhilArchive (philpapers.org/archive/FLOAAA-5.pdf),
  which returned a Cloudflare challenge (403) and refused headless Chromium.
  SSRN 5135645 returned 403, and "Content Blocked" in a browser. The
  Springer version of record is not open access. ResearchGate was not tried,
  since it refuses automated access. The owner later supplied the published
  version, and it has been read.
- **F2013.** PhilArchive (philpapers.org/archive/FLODMI.pdf) returned a
  Cloudflare 403. The Hertfordshire record (uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/20632)
  has no file. Springer is not open access, and PubMed 23197312 has no free
  text.
- **F2016.** PhilArchive (philpapers.org/archive/FLOFRO.pdf) returned a
  Cloudflare 403. The Royal Society PDF returned a Cloudflare challenge and
  refused a browser too. PubMed 28336791 has no free text.

F2013 and F2016 each say in their status notes that they stay Deferred
until read, not on merit. Reading either would move it to Active with a
NOTE. A reader with a browser session past PhilArchive's challenge could
reach both submitted versions.

## The boundary

None of the six is held in the Anthology of the SOTA. A grep of a clone of
its record/ (commit d8b5ba5, 2026-10-09, possibly stale) for "Floridi" and
for each DOI found nothing. They are philosophy of agency and ethics, which
no anthology topic holds and none of which carries an instruction for
machine-learning practice, so they belong here and
[ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md) does not apply. None carries
`anthology-candidate`. They are tagged `agency` first.

## Found along the way

- **Where the line meets the record.** The batch bears on the record's
  distributed-agency line ([CLAIM-030](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-030.md), [CLAIM-045](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-045.md)) and on [QUESTION-009](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-009.md), "When
  does a pattern of coordination constitute an additional agent?". Floridi
  2013 and 2016 allocate moral standing across a network of human,
  artificial and hybrid agents. Floridi 2025's agency without mental states
  could say what the network's nodes are, but the paper does not make that
  connection. It cites distributed morality in one sentence (p. 6). It
  names three collective kinds of agency (ant colonies, corporations,
  Agentic AI) without a criterion for when a group is one, so
  [QUESTION-009](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-009.md) stays open. It is compatible with [CLAIM-030](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) and gives no
  evidence for it. On [ADR-024](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-024.md), Floridi 2025 keeps agency apart from
  intelligence and personhood, as that decision does. But the human kind
  of agency in its taxonomy absorbs identity, narrative self-understanding
  and a sense of self (pp. 14–15).
- **Permissiveness again.** The criteria of Floridi & Sanders 2004 meet the
  objection the record holds as [CLAIM-010](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-010.md), that on such an account every
  control system is an agent; the reading of that paper finds the objection
  answered by assertion. Floridi 2025 does not do better. It accepts the
  objection's conclusion: interactivity alone makes a river a natural agent
  (p. 9), and a thermostat is an artefactual agent (pp. 4, 13). It restricts
  no level of abstraction. It sorts agency into kinds, and the kinds are
  told apart by origin, mechanism and goal source, not by the three
  criteria. Floridi & Sanders' "for most entities there is no LoA at which
  they can be considered an agent" is not repeated.
- **Already held, cited by the target.** Frankfurt 1971 ([LIT-692](../../record/literature.d/LIT-692.md), read),
  cited only for the moral dimension of human agency (p. 15); Prigogine &
  Stengers 1984 ([LIT-534](../../record/literature.d/LIT-534.md), unread).
  Floridi's levels of abstraction were already used here, for structural
  realism ([LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md)) and personal identity ([LIT-136](../../record/literature.d/LIT-136.md)).
- **Cited by the target, not held, not registered.** List 2021 on group
  agency and AI (doi:10.1007/s13347-021-00454-7), Dung 2024 on understanding
  artificial agency (doi:10.1093/pq/pqae010), Popa 2021
  (doi:10.1007/s13347-021-00483-2), van Lier 2023
  (doi:10.1007/s11229-023-04083-9), Himma 2009
  (doi:10.1007/s10676-008-9167-5), Bandura 2006 (the record holds only
  Bandura 1977, [LIT-545](../../record/literature.d/LIT-545.md)), Pearl & Mackenzie 2018 (the record holds Pearl's
  *Causality*, [LIT-894](../../record/literature.d/LIT-894.md)), Floridi & Chiriatti 2020 on GPT-3, Floridi &
  Nobre 2024, and Floridi's *The Ethics of Artificial Intelligence* (2023).
  List 2021 and Dung 2024 would be the first to file if the agency line
  wants rivals to Floridi's account.
- **Distributed knowledge, then common knowledge.** Floridi 2013's abstract
  borrows distributed knowledge from epistemic logic; Floridi 2016's borrows
  common knowledge. Both LITs ask a reading to say why.

## 2026-10-10: the target read

After this entry was written, the owner supplied the published version of
record of Floridi 2025 (Springer PDF, 27 pp., an Editor Letter). It was read
in full the same day ([NOTE-702](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-702.md)), with the text extracted by
`pdftotext -layout`. The PDF is not in the repository. The paper's own
outline replaced the reconstructed abstract, and the LIT is now Active.
It was retagged `agency`, `cognition`, `social-ontology`,
`society-and-governance`. It `extends` F2023 and FS2004, and it `rivals`
the organisational account of minimal agency ([LIT-566](../../record/literature.d/LIT-566.md)) and the account
of large models as social technologies ([LIT-848](../../record/literature.d/LIT-848.md)). The headline is that
it gives a taxonomy, not an argument. The thesis named in the title is
assumed. Of the four supports it announces, only the literature is used,
as a survey. Floridi & Sanders' criteria return, loosened, and they
separate only natural agency from the rest. AI's lack of intelligence is
asserted. No level of abstraction is specified. Against what the 2004 and
2023 readings found missing, it supplies criteria, though graded ones that
do not sort the kinds. It sets no limit on choosing a level of
abstraction beyond keeping one stable and ranking levels by purpose, and
it does not keep to that itself. It answers the permissiveness objection
by accepting it. Following the record's practice for comparable accounts
of agency ([THEORY-071](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-071.md), [THEORY-173](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-173.md)), the account is filed Proposed as
[THEORY-208](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-208.md), with those two as rivals.

## 2026-10-10, later: the two Deferred works read

The owner then supplied the published versions of the other two, and both
were read in full.

- **Floridi 2013** ([LIT-901](../../record/literature.d/LIT-901.md), [NOTE-698](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-698.md)), Deferred to Active. It
  defines distributed morality and introduces infraethics, the framework of
  moral enablers such as trust, privacy and transparency. Its analogy with
  distributed knowledge is looser than it claims: every example sums
  contributions of one kind past a threshold left unspecified, and there is
  no aggregation rule for actions. Its supra-agents are stipulated, and no
  Floridi & Sanders criterion is applied to them. Responsibility is left to
  the 2016 paper. `extends` [LIT-898](../../record/literature.d/LIT-898.md) is confirmed. No THEORY was filed.
- **Floridi 2016** ([LIT-899](../../record/literature.d/LIT-899.md), [NOTE-699](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-699.md)), Deferred to Active. It
  allocates responsibility for distributed moral actions by default,
  overridably and in full, to every causally accountable node. The reading
  finds that its "responsibility" is Floridi & Sanders 2004's
  accountability, used as a corrective signal to agents that learn. It also
  finds that back-propagation, the model it borrows, assigns credit in
  proportion to contribution, not equally, and that its own case of the
  Dutch boats is settled by control, not causation. It `extends` [LIT-901](../../record/literature.d/LIT-901.md)
  and [LIT-898](../../record/literature.d/LIT-898.md). Following the record's practice for French and Pettit
  ([THEORY-131](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-131.md), [THEORY-139](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-139.md)), the account is filed Proposed as
  [THEORY-207](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-207.md).

All six works in the batch are now read.
