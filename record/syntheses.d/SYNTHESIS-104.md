# SYNTHESIS-104: Accountability without blame and normative governability: blame and accountability are proposed to come apart, so that correction does not wait on culpability and an organization may owe repair where no one is to blame, blame traced upstream supplies the provenance of an incapacity, an incapacity an institution brought about in breach of a duty does not excuse it, blame and repair do not substitute for each other, a complete record of who approved an outcome does not settle whether it can fairly be attributed to them, and an institution responsive within its governing mechanism need not be responsive to reasons for revising it

*A part account of the organizational-normativity line, on accountability without blame and on normative governability. It was filed at the interim synthesis review of 2026-11-17, over ten components from the continuation's filing of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (turns U1 to U48, filed 2026-11-17). Version 2, at the follow-up review of 2026-11-18, integrates four entries the first share's filing made (turns U1 to U17, pull request 510, filed 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16), which were in no account, and folds in the readings of the exchange's works (pull request 514). The first filing's entries here come from A's turns and from reviews of that filing which the owner forwarded on 2026-11-16, whose voice is ChatGPT's (the first filing's curation entry, "Review of 2026-11-16"). None of the nine entries reconciled into the first filing's codes is in this part; they are in dependence-and-domination's parts. Most components also carry distributed-agency. Its sibling on rights, demands and commitments is [SYNTHESIS-102](SYNTHESIS-102.md); the organizational wrong beyond harm and [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s duty, which several components here cite, are mapped in [SYNTHESIS-094](SYNTHESIS-094.md).*

**Repair without blame, the question.** [QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md) (A's, from A7 §2 of
the first share, "Someone can owe another person recognition, sympathy,
or efforts at repair without deserving blame for what happened", put as a
question for organizations by the third review of 2026-11-16, ChatGPT's,
forwarded by the owner; filed by the first filing; Open) stores no
relation: under what conditions does an organization's responsibility to
repair a harm survive the absence, uncertainty or dispersion of
blameworthiness among those who contributed, so that causal
responsibility, culpability, accountability and remedial responsibility
come apart? Its body names [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md), [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md), [CLAIM-671](../claims.d/CLAIM-671.md), [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)
and [QUESTION-068](../questions.d/QUESTION-068.md) as neighbours, not answers, and Young ([LIT-2246](../literature.d/LIT-2246.md),
Deferred) and Williams ([LIT-2714](../literature.d/LIT-2714.md), Unfetched; read through Nelkin,
[THEORY-1942](../theory.d/THEORY-1942.md)) as readings, and says an answer would be a principle "from
benefit, from control over the arrangement that produced the harm, or
from capacity to repair", or an argument that the four never come apart.
[CLAIM-742](../claims.d/CLAIM-742.md), [CLAIM-733](../claims.d/CLAIM-733.md) and [CLAIM-717](../claims.d/CLAIM-717.md) bear on it, and [QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md) refines
it (below). No reading carries a relation to it.

**Blame is not accountability.** [CLAIM-742](../claims.d/CLAIM-742.md) (the owner's, U47; A110's
four questions, usage note, amendment of "unintentionally" to "unwitting"
and three assessments are A's and credited in its body; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; also distributed-agency) uses [TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md) and bears
on [QUESTION-068](../questions.d/QUESTION-068.md) and [QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md): an agent's diminished culpability for
the reasons it acts on "does not diminish the wrongfulness of what it
does, the claims of those it harms, or the need for correction, because
the grounds of correction are not exhausted by the grounds of blame". Its
body reads "accountable" in [TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md)'s remedial sense, says the claim
leaves open who owes the correction, and says that if the grounds of
correction are not exhausted by those of blame, "the need for correction
can survive diminished culpability, which is one condition" [QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md)
asks for.

  correction of A110, that the three-way split of attributability,
  answerability and accountability is Shoemaker's, not Watson's and
  Shoemaker's jointly; King 2013 for Watson ([LIT-2718](../literature.d/LIT-2718.md); [NOTE-2053](../notes.d/NOTE-2053.md)) gives
  Watson's accountability a fairness condition, which supports reading

  relation to the claim.

**Self-induced incapacity.** [CLAIM-714](../claims.d/CLAIM-714.md) (A's, A108 §5 and A110 §7;

**Blame and repair do not substitute.** [CLAIM-717](../claims.d/CLAIM-717.md) (A's, A110 §8; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; also distributed-agency) rests on [CLAIM-742](../claims.d/CLAIM-742.md),
uses [TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md) and bears on [QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md): assigning blame for a harmful
condition does not discharge present remedial duties, and assigning
remedial duties does not show blame, so treating one guilty official as a
repair is "blame displacement" and loading repair on whoever is nearest
is "remedial scapegoating". Its body names [CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md) as a documented
instance of blame displacement, [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)'s moral crumple zone as the
mechanism of scapegoating, and [QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md) as the question it bears on
most directly.

  second half; the claim does not rest on it. Miller assigns remedial

**Blameless culture.** [QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md) (the owner's suggestion, U48, the
exchange's last turn, unanswered; the wording is the filer's; Open; also
distributed-agency) refines [QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md): does blameless post-incident
practice in engineering keep accountability while giving up blame, and
does it keep or lose the upstream provenance blame supplies? Its body
puts it to [CLAIM-742](../claims.d/CLAIM-742.md) and [CLAIM-733](../claims.d/CLAIM-733.md), and names [CLAIM-717](../claims.d/CLAIM-717.md)'s blame
displacement "turned round" as one possible answer. The candidate sources
it names are the filer's, not the exchange's.

- *Readings.* Three THEORYs bear on it, and its dated notes say how.
  Shoemaker 2019 ([THEORY-1974](../theory.d/THEORY-1974.md), [NOTE-2048](../notes.d/NOTE-2048.md), skimmed): corporations
  "blameworthy and blamable in answerability terms, but not ... in
  accountability terms", a worked model of a practice that keeps one kind
  of blame and gives up another; the fit is partial, since blameless
  engineering withholds anger from persons who have the capacities it
  needs, and Shoemaker's accountability is not the remedial sense; it
  says nothing on provenance. The NTSB's Tempe report ([THEORY-1932](../theory.d/THEORY-1932.md),
  [NOTE-2034](../notes.d/NOTE-2034.md)): a statutory investigation that "does not assign fault or
  blame" still ranks cause and carries it upstream to the company's
  safety culture, which "contributed ... specifically to the vehicle
  operator's extended distraction"; so one blameless practice keeps the
  provenance of an incapacity, by naming contributing factors; "Whether
  this holds of blameless engineering post-mortems ... is not shown".
  Elish ([THEORY-1928](../theory.d/THEORY-1928.md), [NOTE-2019](../notes.d/NOTE-2019.md)): where no blameless practice intervenes,
  blame lands on the nearest operator. [CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md), the Tempe crash filed
  from the report (distributed-agency only, in no account of this line),
  bears on the question too.

**Traceability is not fair attribution.** [CLAIM-712](../claims.d/CLAIM-712.md) (A's, A19 §9 of the
first share: "Traceability and fair attribution are different
achievements"; left unfiled on 2026-11-15 for want of a separating case

exactly which agent approved an outcome while leaving open whether that
agent had the time, information, authority and alternatives that
responsibility for it requires, so two arrangements with equally complete
audit trails can warrant very different attributions. Its body says a

records [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md)'s first kind of responsibility and not the second, and

**Standing to blame.** [QUESTION-473](../questions.d/QUESTION-473.md) (A's, raised by the third
review of 2026-11-16, ChatGPT's, forwarded by the owner; first filing;
Open; line organizational-normativity) asks what remains of an
organization's moral standing to blame an agent whose wrongdoing its
conditions substantially produced, as distinct from its authority to
investigate, correct or sanction. Since the owner's decision of
2026-11-18 it is integrated here, in its own line's part; it was mapped
before in dependence-and-domination's [SYNTHESIS-099](SYNTHESIS-099.md), beside [CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md),
[CLAIM-702](../claims.d/CLAIM-702.md), [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md) and [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md), which its body names and which stay
there. It belongs with this part because it asks where blame ends and
the other senses of responsibility ([TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md)) go on: the just-culture
carve-outs, where blame resumes inside a blameless practice, are the
boundary it asks about, drawn by the organization whose conditions it
asks about. Its body says it is distinct from
[QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md), which asks "who must repair a harm, not who may condemn
for it". It stores no relation to any component here.

**Normative governability.** [CLAIM-719](../claims.d/CLAIM-719.md) (A's, A108 §6, A110 §9 and §10,
A106 §18; thesis, interpretive, Proposed) uses [TERM-128](../terms.d/TERM-128.md): an institution
can be reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and
unresponsive to justified reasons for revising it, and first-order
responsiveness "is no evidence of responsiveness at the second". Its body
says ρ^(2) "is named, not defined", and that the lack of second-order
responsiveness is a wrong only with [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s duty as a further
premise. [TERM-128](../terms.d/TERM-128.md) (A's term, A110 §13's version of four; the link to
reasons-responsiveness is the owner's, U46; Proposed) draws on [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md)
and [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md): the maintained, role-appropriate capacity to recognize and
respond to justified considerations, "including considerations that
require revising its own governing mechanisms"; relational, and not
corrigibility, compliance or obedience. Its body says the second-order
clause is [CLAIM-719](../claims.d/CLAIM-719.md)'s responsiveness, that A110 §13's last paragraph
("Deficiencies in normative governability may explain wrongful conduct
without excusing every duty arising from it") ties the term to [CLAIM-742](../claims.d/CLAIM-742.md)
