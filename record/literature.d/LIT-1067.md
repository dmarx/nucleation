# LIT-1067: Failures to replicate a key result of the selective accessibility theory of anchoring

Harris, A. J. L., Blower, F. B. N., Rodgers, S. A., Lagator, S., Page, E., Burton, A., Urlichich, D. and Speekenbrink, M. (2019). Failures to replicate a key result of the selective accessibility theory of anchoring. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 148(9), e30–e50. DOI 10.1037/xge0000644

Read in full (NOTE-876). Five preregistered experiments of 128 people each repeated the lexical-decision test of selective accessibility (Mussweiler and Strack 2000a). Anchoring was large in all five and the accessibility effect absent in all five, with an exploratory Bayes factor of about 21 to 1 for the null. The same tasks also missed direct picture priming, so the authors conclude that the method cannot test the account, not that the account is false.

## Why this is here

[CLAIM-298](../claims.d/CLAIM-298.md) grounds its psychological form in the selective
accessibility account of anchoring ([LIT-1064](LIT-1064.md), [LIT-1078](LIT-1078.md)). This paper
shows that the most direct published measure of the accessibility step
does not replicate, and that the measure could not detect priming at all
([NOTE-876](../notes.d/NOTE-876.md)). The account survives as theory; its retrieval step is
inferred, not measured.
