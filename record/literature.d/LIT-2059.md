# LIT-2059: Economies-of-Scale in Many-Server Queueing Systems: Tutorial and Partial Review of the QED Halfin–Whitt Heavy-Traffic Regime

van Leeuwaarden, J. S. H., Mathijsen, B. W. J. & Zwart, B. (2019). Economies-of-Scale in Many-Server Queueing Systems: Tutorial and Partial Review of the QED Halfin–Whitt Heavy-Traffic Regime. *SIAM Review* 61(3): 403–440. doi:10.1137/17m1133944. arXiv:1706.05397.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1577](../notes.d/NOTE-1577.md). Page numbers are the arXiv PDF's.*

- **The square-root rule** s = λ + β√λ, "which prescribes how to size
  capacity as a function of the offered load" (§1, p. 3).
- **Halfin and Whitt's theorem** (Prop. 2.1, p. 7): the delay probability
  has the limit g(β) = [1 + βΦ(β)/φ(β)]⁻¹ if and only if (1 − ρ)√s → β.
- **Economies of scale** (§3, p. 14): utilization tends to one, the delay
  probability to a constant, and "the mean delay vanishes at rate" 1/√s.
- **Three regimes, not one** (§1, p. 4): efficiency-driven, where "Systems
  scaled according to this rule value server efficiency over QoS",
  quality-driven, with much idle time, and QED between them.
- **Dimensioning** (§4): the QED staffing level and the exact optimum
  differ by at most one server in the authors' numerical examples.
