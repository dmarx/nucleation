# NOTE-1716: Kiczales, Lamping, Mendhekar, Maeda, Lopes, Loingtier and Irwin — Aspect-oriented programming

## Contribution

An analysis of why some design decisions are hard to capture in procedural
or object-oriented code: the properties they address, which the authors
call aspects, "cross-cut the system's basic functionality" (abstract), so
code implementing them is scattered and tangled. The proposal is to write
components and aspects in separate languages and combine them with an
aspect weaver (§3, §5).

## Key results

- **What a reflective system is** (§8.2): "A reflective system provides a
  base language and (one or more) meta-languages that provide control over
  the base language's semantics and implementation." The meta-languages
  "provide views of the computation that no one base language component
  could ever see".
- **Reflection as tool** (§8.2): "AOP is a goal, for which reflection is one
  powerful tool." "When prototyping AOP systems we often start by
  developing simple metaobject protocols for the component language".
- **Power needs bounding** (§6.2): "But this kind of reflective access is so
  powerful that it can be dangerous or difficult to use." The authors
  provide instead "a higher-level aspect language".

## Assumptions

- That a program's functional decomposition is fixed by its component
  language, and that the aspects can be separated into languages of their
  own and woven back without loss.
- The examples are the authors' own systems; no comparative evaluation was
  read.

## Concepts

Aspect; component; cross-cutting; tangling; aspect weaver; reflective
system; base language and meta-language; metaobject protocol.

## Bearing on the record

- **As a stand-in for [LIT-2335](../literature.d/LIT-2335.md).** Weak. It restates the general idea of
  a meta-level controlling a language's own semantics and implementation,
  and cites the book's metaobject protocol as an instance. It does not
  restate that protocol, its explicit restrictions on users and
  implementors, its dependent-maintenance protocol, or the book's argument
  for protocol design. What A cited the book for in the exchange "luria
  experiment design" (A50 §12), a system that exposes its own behaviour to
  modification through a meta-level interface, is covered only by §8.2's
  definition.
- **[QUESTION-388](../questions.d/QUESTION-388.md)** (what distinguishes a programmable system from one that
  governs its own reconfiguration). One point, by analogy: the authors
  found unbounded reflective access dangerous and bounded it with a
  narrower language that they designed. The more a meta-level can change,
  the more someone must fix what it may change, and here that someone is
  the language's designer, outside the system. The paper does not ask who
  should govern reconfiguration. Dated note on the question.
- **[CLAIM-469](../claims.d/CLAIM-469.md).** Leaves it open; nothing here concerns records or revision
  through use.

## Limitations

Skimmed; the paper's main subject, weaving cross-cutting concerns, is not
what the record wanted the book for. One paragraph bears on metaobject
protocols.
