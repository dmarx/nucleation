# THEORY-tmppcqht: Mulligan and colleagues report that, among Syrian refugee families, grandchildren of women pregnant during the 1980 Hama violence differed at 14 DNA methylation sites from unexposed families, that directly exposed people differed at 21 other sites and prenatally exposed people at none, and read the first as a germline epigenetic signature of violence

## The account

Mulligan, Quinn, Hamadmad, Dutton, Nevell, Binder, Panter-Brick and
Dajani (2025), LIT-tmpglael. 48 families, 131 people, with buccal DNA on
the EPIC array. There are three exposure categories and controls:

- germline: the 1980 group's grandchildren, 18;
- prenatal: the 1980 group's mothers and the 2011 group's younger
  children, 28;
- direct: the 2011 group's mothers and older children, plus one
  grandmother, 43;
- controls: families in Jordan before 1980, 42.

Two models, robust regression and GEE clustered by family, both at
Bonferroni p < 6.5 × 10⁻⁸. That gives 14 germline DMPs and 21 direct ones,
with no prenatal DMPs and no overlap. 32 of the 35 shift the same way
across all three exposures. The authors read "a common epigenetic
response to violence" and "an intergenerational epigenetic signature of
trauma in humans".

## Evidence

An EWAS with an unexposed comparison group, and a sensitivity analysis
restricted to child controls, in which 8 of the 14 germline sites hold.
There is a dose–response plot against the ancestor's count of traumatic
events. The result could have been null. It was null for prenatal
exposure.

## What it does not show

- **That the route is the germline.** The germline children were carried
  by prenatally exposed mothers, raised by them in families headed by
  survivors, and live in a community whose history is the 1980 violence.
  Each of QUESTION-297's other three routes runs from the exposure to these
  children (NOTE-tmpxwx4b).
- **Any effect on the children.** No phenotype was measured, and gene
  ontology found no enrichment.
- **Transgenerational inheritance**, in Heard and Martienssen's sense
  (THEORY-1576): the grandchild's germ cell was present at the exposure.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297's germline route, as the strongest human design for trauma
found, and a ground of CLAIM-tmp3qsdg (which leans that it does not
separate the routes) and of CLAIM-tmpgv8bs (which reads it as a partial
separation).
