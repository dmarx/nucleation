# THEORY-tmpcq8bx: Mind-wandering is a recurring loss of M-autonomy, the veto-centred ability to suspend or end one's own ongoing mental processes; on the figures Metzinger uses, it takes about half of conscious time, not the two thirds he states

## Source

Metzinger (2013), [LIT-tmp23d7b](../literature.d/LIT-tmp23d7b.md), pp. 2–7 and Box 1, read in [NOTE-tmp97akw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp97akw.md);
Metzinger (2015), [LIT-tmp0w298](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0w298.md), pp. 276–280 and 292–295, read in
[NOTE-tmprpztl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmprpztl.md).

## What was actually shown

**The concept.** M-autonomy is "the ability to control the conscious
contents of one's mind in a goal-directed way, by means of attentional or
[cognitive agency]". "What is crucial is the 'veto component': Being
mentally autonomous means that all currently ongoing processes can in
principle be suspended or terminated ... the ability, the functional
potential, is given and that the person has knowledge of this fact"
(2013, p. 4). In 2015 it is the capacity for second-order mental action, of
which veto is "the central semantic element" (pp. 276–278).

**The identification.** Unintentional mind-wandering is "an involuntary
form of mental activity"; "As long as it is going on, we lack the ability
to terminate or suspend it" (2013, p. 3). So it is a loss of M-autonomy by
definition, once one grants that an episode cannot be stopped from inside
it. Deliberate daydreaming, or mind-wandering while knowing one could stop
("tuning out"), keeps M-autonomy and does not count (2013, p. 3, note 4;
2015, p. 280).

**The share.** Both papers use the same minutes (2013, p. 6; 2015, pp.
292–294): waking 960 min, of which 40% mind-wandering, 384 min; REM 105 min
× 81.9% reporting mentation, 86 min; NREM 375 min × 43%, 161 min. Conscious
time is 1207 min, "about 84%" of the day, and "healthy adults are only
M-autonomous for 9.6 h (576 min; or 40% of an average day)". Both conclude
"roughly two thirds" of conscious life without M-autonomy.

**Recomputed.** Non-autonomous conscious minutes are 384 + 86 + 161 = 631,
and 631/1207 = 0.52. The 40% is 576 of all 1440 minutes in a day, not of
conscious time. Two thirds would need the exclusions the papers name and
do not count: childhood, old age, illness, intoxication, mind-blanking and
insomnia. The figure also treats every waking minute not spent
mind-wandering as M-autonomous, which is generous in the other direction.

**What could have failed.** Nothing in the papers could have: the
identification is by definition and the share by arithmetic on others'
figures.

## What this does not say

- **That half or two thirds of conscious time is non-agentive in any
  measured sense.** The 30–50% figures (Kane et al. 2007; Killingsworth and
  Gilbert 2010; Schooler et al. 2011, none read) count task-unrelated
  thought caught by probes. They do not separate intentional from
  unintentional episodes, so they can overcount losses of M-autonomy.
- **That mind-wandering is dysfunctional.** The papers say it may be
  adaptive, maintaining the autobiographical self-model (2013, p. 5) or
  supplying candidate thoughts ([LIT-tmppbt0s](../literature.d/LIT-tmppbt0s.md)).
- **That the ability itself is lost.** The 2015 paper's "moderate"
  reading is that its representation is lost ([THEORY-tmptbvmq](THEORY-tmptbvmq.md)).
- **Anything about overt action.** M-autonomy is control of mental
  processes; a person mind-wandering may be acting skilfully in the world.
