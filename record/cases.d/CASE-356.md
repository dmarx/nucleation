# CASE-356: NIMA tolerance: own-maternal vs donor cells

## Why this case

[QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md) asks whether two systems with matching behaviour and persistence
can be told apart by whether their own processes generate and maintain the
constraints that keep them going, and by what intervention. [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md) says
that an internal producer and an outside controller supplying the same
production law meet mutual manipulability's two manipulability conditions
identically, so what puts one inside and the other outside is parthood, a
boundary the experiments presuppose rather than find.

The note of 2026-11-12 on [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md) (the assistant's, accepted by the
owner) proposed maternal microchimerism as a natural instance of exactly this
pair, and the note of the same date on [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md) recorded the pointer. Having
read Peng et al. ([LIT-2462](../literature.d/LIT-2462.md), [NOTE-2337](../notes.d/NOTE-2337.md)), this case turns that pointer into a
specified design, using the paper's own handles. It is a construction of the
record's argument, not a study anyone has run.

## The pair

The maintained capacity ψ is NIMA-specific tolerance, measured by the paper's
two readouts: expansion of FOXP3+ regulatory T cells with NIMA specificity
(I-Ab:2W1S tetramer enrichment), and resiliency against *Listeria*-induced
fetal wastage in a NIMA-matched allogeneic next-generation pregnancy. The
producer X is the tolerogenic cell population.

- **Arm A (internal producer).** An offspring whose NIMA tolerance is
  sustained by its own LysM+ CD11c+ Vav1+ maternal microchimeric cells, as in
  Peng et al. These cells arose in the host developmentally and, per the paper,
  persist in the host independently of the tolerance they confer.
- **Arm B (external supply).** An offspring in which the same tolerogenic
  function is supplied from outside: matched tolerogenic cells (the same
  LysM+ CD11c+ lineage, from a donor of the maternal genotype expressing the
  same surrogate NIMA) transferred later in life, into a host that either
  lacks its own tolerogenic MMc (born to a dam without them) or has had them
  depleted first. Arm B must be matched to Arm A on the supplied cells'
  identity, antigen and dose, so the two differ only in provenance: the
  host's own persisting lineage versus a donor transfer.

Confounds to control before any conclusion: that transferred donor cells
actually engraft, traffic to the right tissues (liver and secondary lymphoid
organs, where the paper localizes the cells and the Tregs), and reach
comparable numbers. A failure of Arm B to sustain tolerance that is traceable
to engraftment, trafficking or dose is not evidence about parthood.

## The interventions, applied identically to each arm

1. **Disable** the producer: deplete the tolerogenic cells (diphtheria toxin
   in Arm A; a matched depletion handle on the donor cells in Arm B). Measure
   whether ψ collapses.
2. **Restore** the producer: allow natural renewal (Arm A) or re-transfer
   donor cells (Arm B). Measure whether ψ recovers.
3. **Intervene on the maintained capacity** ψ itself: the inflammatory
   next-generation-pregnancy challenge, which the paper shows draws on ongoing
   MMc stimulation. Measure whether the activity/turnover of X changes
   (top-down).
4. **Withdraw external support / block renewal** (the maintenance-provenance
   probe, [TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md)): stop any re-supply and prevent the supplied cells from
   being renewed, and ask whether ψ decays at a different rate in the two arms.
   In Arm A the host's own processes keep and renew the lineage; in Arm B,
   once-supplied donor cells are renewed by the host only if they engraft and
   self-maintain.

## What would count

This case follows the owner-accepted note of 2026-11-12 on [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md), whose
logic is: *if depletion and stimulation affect the capacity identically in
both arms, the interventions cannot tell an internal producer from an outside
controller.*

- **Supports [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md) (the parthood clause).** If interventions 1-3 move ψ
  identically in both arms -- disabling either producer collapses tolerance,
  restoring either recovers it, and challenging the tolerance changes the
  activity of either -- then mutual manipulability's two manipulability
  conditions hold alike for the host's own cells and the donor's. Nothing in
  the manipulation experiments then separates them; only the stipulated
  boundary (own persisting lineage = inside, donor transfer = outside) does.
  That is [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s reading applied to a single cell population: the
  experiments presuppose the boundary and do not find it.
- **A candidate answer to [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md) ("by what intervention").** If 1-3 are
  identical but intervention 4 separates the arms -- Arm A sustains ψ through
  cell turnover because the host renews the lineage, while Arm B's ψ decays as
  unrenewed donor cells are displaced (the paper notes MMc are displaced over
  time, independently of adaptive immunity) -- then maintenance provenance
  ([TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md)), not any manipulability condition, is the discriminating
  intervention short of removing the cells. This would answer [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md)
  positively for this instance while leaving [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s manipulability point
  standing.
- **The strongest support for [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md), and a negative answer to
  [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md).** If even intervention 4 fails to separate them -- the host
  renews donor cells as it renews its own, so ψ is sustained identically and
  decays identically -- then no intervention short of removal distinguishes
  self-maintained from externally supplied here, and the inside/outside
  distinction rests wholly on the declared boundary.
- **Against [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s pair being the right model.** If matched, engrafted,
  correctly-trafficked donor cells at comparable dose cannot sustain tolerance
  at all, so that the capacity genuinely requires the host's own cells, then
  the two arms are not a manipulability-equivalent pair and [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s
  construction does not apply to this instance. (Note the tension flagged
  below: [LEAD-051](../leads.d/LEAD-051.md)'s one-line summary reads this last outcome, not identical
  response, as the one that "supports" the parthood clause.)

## A tension to resolve

[LEAD-051](../leads.d/LEAD-051.md)'s "What would count" says: "Tolerance that needs the offspring's own
maternal cells supports [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s parthood clause; donor cells that do as
well count against it." Read literally this is the opposite of the
owner-accepted [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md) note, on which [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s own content turns:
[CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md) holds that the manipulability experiments *cannot* separate inside
from outside, so donor cells doing as well is what *supports* it, and donor
cells being required is what would show the pair disanalogous. This case is
built on the note and on [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)'s text. Which reading of the lead's
one-liner is intended is left to the owner; it does not change the design,
only which outcome is labelled support.

## Standing

Stipulated: no such comparison has been run. Arm A is Peng et al.'s actual
system; Arm B (donor supply of a matched tolerogenic subset, with a depletion
handle) is a proposed extension. The case serves [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md) and [QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md)
whatever its standing; being unrun, it illustrates [CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md) rather than
grounding it.
