# CLAIM-1212: Repetition and reliance can turn a discretionary contribution into an operative obligation, whose withdrawal is treated as a breach, with no agreement ever made; but being expected, being relied on, being committed and owing are distinct, and reliance makes something owed only under a further principle, such as one about reliance knowingly induced

## The claim

Two parts, the first descriptive and the second normative.

**1. Normalization into an operative obligation** (A22 §12). The same
contribution is read differently as it recurs:

| Stage | A22's reading |
|---|---|
| Initial | "An exceptional accommodation" |
| Repeated | "A helpful regularity" |
| Expected | "A predictable contribution" |
| Relied upon | "A necessary component of normal operation" |
| Normalized | "Something the participant is supposed to provide" |
| Enforced | "Its withdrawal is treated as a violation" |

"There may never have been an explicit moment of agreement. The
recipient's expectations may gradually harden into asserted normative
claims." A22 §6's case: "The employee's discretionary contribution has
been transformed into an organizational dependency without necessarily
becoming a mutually recognized commitment." The end state is an operative
norm in [CLAIM-1279](CLAIM-1279.md)'s sense, a requirement whose violation recruits
correction, reached by the route of the owner's U16 ([CLAIM-1210](CLAIM-1210.md)).

**2. What that does not make owed.** A22 §12 separates Expected(A, φ),
ReliedUpon(A, φ), Committed(A, φ) and Owes(A, B, φ): "The first three can
contribute to the circumstances under which a duty arises. None
establishes that duty automatically." A20 §7 had drawn the same line from
the provider's side, "the contract as expected by the recipient, the
contract as enacted by the provider, and the relationship as maintained by
the encompassing organization", with a fifth row, "Warranted service: What,
if anything, is genuinely owed?" A24 §11 names the gap between the two
parts as C^op, "the actual contractual organization", against C^just, "an
arrangement satisfying the relevant justified standards": "There is no
reason to presume that these coincide."

This rests on [CLAIM-738](CLAIM-738.md): a demand's being asserted, maintained or
recognized gives it no warrant, and a commitment is neither an obligation
nor an authorization. The normalization ladder is the route by which an
unwarranted demand comes to be maintained.

## The condition A names, and the gap

A does not leave reliance normatively idle. A22 §12: "Reliance might
generate justified claims in some circumstances—for example, where another
party has knowingly induced reasonable reliance." A23 §8 lists what would
have to be investigated: "whether the reliance was reasonably induced,
whether it was knowingly encouraged, whether alternatives remain available,
what commitments were undertaken, and what competing claims exist."

The principle that would turn these into a verdict is not given (gap: a
condition asserted without the principle that makes it one). The record
holds one candidate as a supposition, [CLAIM-671](CLAIM-671.md)'s, where an agent's
discretionary power over what a party has come to depend on grounds
responsibilities under a principle that vulnerability grounds special
obligations. Whether induced reliance is a separate ground, and what
"reasonable" and "knowingly" add, is [QUESTION-538](../questions.d/QUESTION-538.md), filed by the
assistant.

## What it does not say

- Not that a normalized contribution is exploited. A22 §10: "That is not
  necessarily exploitation; it could be a fair division of labor."
  Whether it is turns on a declared standard ([TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md)), and on the
  distribution of what the regularity saves ([CLAIM-1244](CLAIM-1244.md)).
- Not that the party who normalized the contribution acted wrongly. The
  ladder needs no one's intent; the recipient's expectations "may
  gradually harden".

## Relation to existing work

Rousseau's distinction between psychological contracts, perceived
reciprocal obligations, and implied contracts at the level of the
relationship (A20 §7; Rousseau 1989, [LIT-3259](../literature.d/LIT-3259.md), not read) is the
organizational-psychology form of part 2's first gap, between what one
party takes to be owed and what the relationship carries.
