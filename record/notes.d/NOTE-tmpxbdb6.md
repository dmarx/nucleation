# NOTE-tmpxbdb6: Turner — Ultrafilters as Propositional Theories

## Contribution

Turner gives a philosophically friendly explanation of filters and ultrafilters from mathematics and model theory. A filter is treated as a propositional theory: a consistent set of propositions closed under finite conjunction and under implication. An ultrafilter is a filter that is also negation-complete. The central ultrafilter theorem is shown to be a propositional analogue of Lindenbaum's lemma. Products and ultraproducts are introduced and Łoś's theorem is proved, with compactness as an application.

- §1 (pp. 2–6): W-propositions are subsets of a set of worlds W, each world agreeing with some classical model. Filters are defined as propositional theories closed under conjunction and implication, and ultrafilters as negation-complete filters.
- §2 (pp. 6–9): states and proves the central theorem on ultrafilters, which the published abstract calls a propositional variant of Lindenbaum's lemma.
- §3 (pp. 9–18): "intensional spaces", covering models and intensions, variable assignments, and what a propositional theory "says".
- §4 (pp. 18–21): proves Łoś's theorem, then compactness as an application, by building a filter from the finite conjunctions of Γ.
