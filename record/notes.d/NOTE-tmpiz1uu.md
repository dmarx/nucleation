# NOTE-tmpiz1uu: Hazelwood — Emerging dilemma for reciprocal causation

## Contribution

In debates over the extended evolutionary synthesis, "reciprocal causation" is the view that adaptive evolution runs both ways, with organisms and environments acting on each other through niche construction and natural selection. Hazelwood argues this is incompatible with treating natural selection as a metaphysically emergent causal process. That emergent character creates a dilemma neither horn of which saves reciprocal causation, so EES proponents should give up the claim that selection takes part in cycles of reciprocal causation.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1: sets two claims against each other — (RC) selection is both a cause and an effect of processes such as niche construction; (EC) natural selection is an emergent causal process.
- §3: motivates EC from the population-level causalist literature (Millstein, Sober, Reisman & Forber): the causal relata of selection are properties of the aggregate.
- §4.1: a Kimian exclusion problem — the counterfactual dependence of selection on niche construction reflects composition, not causation.
- §4.3: considers and rejects a Woodwardian interventionist defence of interlevel causation.
- §5: denying EC means statisticalism, which denies that selection is causal and so rejects RC, or an individual-level account, which Millstein shows is no account of selection.
- §6: concludes the EES can still give niche construction a central explanatory role without claiming it and selection are causally interdependent.
