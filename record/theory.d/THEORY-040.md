# THEORY-040: A condition on free agency stated only in present psychological structure is met by a manipulated agent, so the account must count that agent free or add a historical condition, and no historical condition in the record's readings is shown to escape

## Source

- McKenna & Coates, SEP *Compatibilism*, [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §§4.2.2, 4.3 and supplement §A, as read in [NOTE-270](../notes.d/NOTE-270.md).
- Dryden, IEP *Autonomy* (2010), [LIT-297](../literature.d/LIT-297.md), §2.a.ii, as read in [NOTE-266](../notes.d/NOTE-266.md).
- Buss & Westlund, SEP *Personal Autonomy* (2018 revision), [LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md), §3, as read in [NOTE-267](../notes.d/NOTE-267.md).
- Pettigrew (2026), [LIT-202](../literature.d/LIT-202.md), as read in [NOTE-144](../notes.d/NOTE-144.md). This is a worked historical condition, in a neighbouring question.

## What was actually shown

A "mesh" or "snapshot" account makes free agency a relation among the agent's present attitudes. Frankfurt's is one: the effective desire issues from the will the agent wants. So is Watson's valuational account, and so are reasons-responsive mechanisms described without their history. Such a relation says nothing about how the attitudes came to be there. A manipulator who installs the right mesh therefore produces an agent the account must call free.

[LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md) argues that this holds for every mesh compatibilism it surveys: Frankfurt, Wolf, Fischer & Ravizza, Haji, Wallace, Bok and Mele (C12). Each must either say what is wrong with manipulation cases or "bite the bullet".

Frankfurt bites it. [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md) quotes him from 2002 (p. 27, "Reply to Fischer"): a manipulator "may succeed … in providing a person … with a new character. That person is then morally responsible". Whether the causes are natural or "the deliberate manipulative designs of other human agents" is "irrelevant". This quotation is **second-hand**. It is the only verbatim Frankfurt passage in the entry apart from one phrase.

[LIT-297](../literature.d/LIT-297.md) states the same objection against hierarchical autonomy (the hypnotist case, citing Mele 2001). It reports Christman's historical model (1991) as the standard repair, and reports that the repair mishandles freely chosen loss of autonomy.

[LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md) argues (§3) that coherence is not sufficient for minimal autonomy because the endorsement itself can be manipulated. It adds, in one sentence, that historical supplements have trouble telling autonomy-conferring reasoning from autonomy-undermining reasoning "without implicitly appealing to the very phenomenon one is trying to explain".

[LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md) reports a further step: manipulation "from a temporal distance" can load in a historical requirement too (Pereboom; Mele's zygote argument). On this account Bratman's externalist view is also exposed.

[LIT-202](../literature.d/LIT-202.md) shows what a historical condition looks like in use. Whether a preference grounds effective consent depends on "the space and resources" its formation mechanism left "for the free exercise of agency". Its sorting criterion uses free agency without analysing it ([NOTE-144](../notes.d/NOTE-144.md), C6). That is the circularity [LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md) names.

The first half of the claim follows from the form of a snapshot condition, and all three surveys agree on it. The second half, that no historical condition in the record escapes, is the record's synthesis. It rests on reports and one-sentence arguments.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say manipulated agents are unfree.** That is the premise the manipulation argument needs, and Frankfurt denies it. The claim is a forced choice. It does not settle which option is right.
- **It does not say historical conditions fail.** [LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md) asserts the circularity worry and does not argue it. Pereboom's and Mele's arguments are known only through [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md). Christman's counterfactual non-repudiation test is named and not assessed.
- **It is not the regress objection.** The regress ([THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)) asks what gives a present attitude authority. This objection grants the authority and asks about its origin. An account could answer one and fail the other.
- **[LIT-202](../literature.d/LIT-202.md) is not about free will.** It is used only as an example of a historical, mechanism-based condition. Its "Frankfurt-style" point concerns the 1969 alternate-possibilities cases, not the hierarchy.

## Connections

- [LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md) (Robb): the 1969 Frankfurt cases leave identification as one of two compatibilist accounts of responsibility. That makes this objection's reach wider than the hierarchy.
- The curation entry of 2026-09-29 on Frankfurt records that the resigned-addict amendment of 1994 is reported second-hand and is contradicted in part by Frankfurt's own 2004 text ([LIT-293](../literature.d/LIT-293.md), p. 173).
- Self-determination theory supplies a historical condition with
  experimental support: the same regulation is introjected when taken in
  under control and can be integrated under autonomy support (Deci et al.
  1994, as reported in [LIT-559](../literature.d/LIT-559.md), pp. 73–74, and [LIT-558](../literature.d/LIT-558.md),
  pp. 237–238; [THEORY-047](THEORY-047.md)). Ryan and Deci's own test of autonomy is
  counterfactual reflective endorsement ([LIT-560](../literature.d/LIT-560.md), p. 1572), close to
  Christman's. They do not apply either to manipulation, and their sorting
  of histories by "autonomy support" uses the notion of autonomy it serves,
  which is the circularity this account records from [LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md).
