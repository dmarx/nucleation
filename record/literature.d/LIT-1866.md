# LIT-1866: Potential Games

Apt, K. R. (2013). *Potential Games*. Lecture slides, CWI Amsterdam and
University of Amsterdam.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1427](../notes.d/NOTE-1427.md).*

- **Definitions (slides 6, 10, 12, 14).** Exact potential: every
  unilateral payoff change equals the potential's change. Ordinal: the
  payoff rises if and only if the potential does. Generalized ordinal: a
  rise in payoff implies a rise in potential. FIP: every improvement path
  is finite.
- **The theorem (slide 15).** "Every finite game has a generalized
  ordinal potential iff it has the FIP", credited to "Monderer and Shapley
  '96". The forward direction is proved; the converse is a sketch that
  counts, for each profile, the improvement sequences ending there.
- **Ordinal is stronger (slide 13).** A two-by-two game has the FIP but
  no ordinal potential, because one player's indifference forces two
  profiles to equal potential that a chain of strict improvements orders.
- **Congestion games (slides 17–26)** are potential games (Rosenthal
  1973), so they have pure equilibria.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-30 for [THEORY-896](../theory.d/THEORY-896.md). It confirms, second-hand, that the
class matched to the FIP is the generalized ordinal one. Biggar and
Shames's weaker restatement, which [THEORY-896](../theory.d/THEORY-896.md) records, says only that
ordinal potential games have the FIP; the slides' two-by-two game shows
that the converse fails for ordinal potentials, so an equivalence can
hold only for the generalized class. Its sketch agrees with the record's
proof. It cannot confirm the lemma's wording,
number or page in Monderer and Shapley.
