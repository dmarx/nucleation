# NOTE-tmpbwyy5: de Haan et al., the split-brain review, read as a stand-in for Pinto et al. 2017 on whether access and experience divide together

## Contribution

A "collective review paper" that "aims to summarize the empirical common
ground, to delineate the different interpretations, and to identify the
remaining questions" (abstract), written after Pinto et al. 2017 reopened
the debate. Its authors include Pinto, Lamme and de Haan, who hold that
consciousness stays unified, and Volz and Corballis, who press other
explanations.

## Key results

- **Unity defined** (Introduction): "subject unity" after Bayne: "consciousness in a split-brain is split if each cortical hemisphere houses an independent conscious agent."
- **Detection and localisation unified** (Common Ground): "split-brain patients can accurately report the presence and location of stimuli for any position in the whole visual field, with either hand, and even verbally." So earlier patients who said they saw nothing "may have meant that they could see it but that they could not identify or retrieve the name of the object."
- **Identity split, by field and not by effector**: "Perception appears to be more split, while responding remains largely unified." Restating Pinto et al. 2017a: the patient matched pictures to sample better for left-field stimuli and matched them to verbal labels better for right-field ones, and "Crucially, response type did not play any role."
- **Cross-midline comparison**: most patients cannot say whether two stimuli
  in opposite fields are the same, though good continuation and apparent
  motion are integrated across the midline.
- **Mechanisms** (Interpretations): subcortical transfer, ipsilateral motor
  control and cross-cueing, "not-mutually exclusive".
- **The open question**: "Unconscious information processing is almost certainly split across hemispheres in a split-brain. However, this does not prove that consciousness is split or unified." And "The current consensus is that the body of evidence is insufficient to answer this question".
- **Proposed tests**: covert parallel responding, each hemisphere choosing
  by imagery decoded "in parallel from each hemisphere using neuroimaging
  techniques"; and ERPs "as markers of concurrent conscious processing".
- **Theories**: GNW "seems to endorse the split consciousness idea, given that each hemisphere has its own prefrontal hub"; IIT predicts split; recurrent processing allows unity without integration.

## Assumptions

- That unity of response selection is evidence about unity of the subject,
  which the authors treat as open rather than assume.
- Subject unity in Bayne's sense as what "unified" means.

## Concepts

- **Subject unity**: all experiences in a system belong to one subject.
- **Cross-cueing**: one hemisphere signalling to the other by behaviour.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-061** (bears; the nearest case found). The identity of a
  left-field stimulus is available to picture matching, whatever the hand
  or voice, and much less to verbal labelling, and mostly not to
  comparison with a right-field stimulus; its presence and location are
  available to every response. So one content is available to some
  consumers and not to others, divided by operation rather than by output
  channel. Limits: it is a disconnected brain, not an intact one; the
  evidence is behavioural, not the recording the question asks for; and
  differential post-perceptual cognition is not removed, so it is a
  compartment of access, not shown to be one of experience.
- **CLAIM-003** (supports, narrowed; ARG-tmpymket). The claim's brain half
  says access can be compartmented within one agent. Split-brain behaviour
  is a case of that, and the authors' own unresolved question, whether the
  agent is one, is the claim's "within one agent" condition. It supports
  the access half in a lesioned brain only, and it says nothing either way
  on the claim's experiential half, which the authors call undecided.
- **QUESTION-048** (deficit kind; confirms the limit). The deficit divides
  access and leaves experience undetermined, by the authors' consensus.
  That is THEORY-091's pattern: the deficit tells us about access and not
  about experience. The tests it proposes, parallel covert choices and ERP
  markers, would each need a marker validated without report, which is
  THEORY-298's problem.
- **THEORY-096** (contrast). The review reads GNW as predicting a split
  subject, since each hemisphere has a prefrontal hub. If subjects stay
  unified while access splits, a workspace account that ties the subject to
  one broadcast is under pressure; the record's account states uniform
  broadcast, and this is a case where broadcast is not uniform.
- **CLAIM-039** (background). Response selection unified over split
  perception is a functional unity with experience undetermined, the kind
  of separation the claim needs, but in a brain and for action control,
  not affect. No edge.

## Limitations

- A review: Pinto et al. 2017 is restated, not reproduced; the stand-in
  covers its results as summarised, not its methods, sample (two patients)
  or statistics.
- Small and varied patient group; the authors stress individual
  differences.
- Gap in the argument: "consciousness remains unified" (the recurrent
  processing reading) and two agents who synchronise their output are both
  offered as
  consistent with the same data, and nothing in the review discriminates
  them; it says so.
