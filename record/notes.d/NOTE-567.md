# NOTE-567: Network Epistemology: Communication in Epistemic Communities

## Contribution

A short review of economics and philosophy results on how a group's
internal communication structure affects what it comes to know, organised
by type of learning problem.

## Key insight

There is no epistemically best network. A group cannot be criticised for
communicating too little, or too hierarchically, until one knows what kind
of learning problem it faces.

## Assumptions

Varies by model, and the survey is explicit about it. The main switches are
whether information can be passed on second-hand, whether links cost
anything, whether evidence arrives independently of what agents do, and
whether agents learn new facts during deliberation.

## Key results

- **Transmission** (§2): with lossless second-hand transmission and costly
  links, minimally connected networks are optimal; with distance loss,
  stars. Optimal networks are usually stable but may not be reached.
- **Pooling** (§3.1): under DeGroot averaging, regular networks are optimal
  in the long run; more connected ones converge faster.
- **Specialised knowledge** (§3.2): a few heavily contacted individuals
  emerge naturally, so prominence is not evidence of sociological
  distortion.
- **Active learning** (§4): with a truth-pull and connectivity, structure is
  irrelevant to the limit; in bandit problems minimally connected networks
  did best in small simulated groups.

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Which network is best depends on the learning problem | strong | the surveyed results, each proved or simulated in its source |
| C2 | Unequal centrality is not by itself evidence of an epistemic pathology | moderate | follows from star and specialised-knowledge results under their assumptions |
| C3 | In bandit problems less communication is better even when free | moderate | Zollman's simulations; later narrowed by [LIT-733](../literature.d/LIT-733.md) |

## Concepts

- **one way / two way, one pays / both pay** — three cost models for a
  communication link.
- **truth pull** — an idealised small attraction of some agents' beliefs
  towards the truth during averaging.

## Connections

Surveys Bala & Goyal, Jackson & Wolinsky, DeGroot, Hegselmann & Krause,
Anderson, and the author's own work ([LIT-724](../literature.d/LIT-724.md), [LIT-735](../literature.d/LIT-735.md)).

## Bearing on the record

Supports [THEORY-143](../theory.d/THEORY-143.md) by placing it in a wider landscape where
denser is sometimes better. It is the best single citation for the owner's
sentence that research on epistemic networks "complicates any simple claim
that more communication or tighter integration necessarily improves
collective judgment". It also bears on the essay's corporate setting: §1
names corporations as group believers.

## Limitations

A survey of idealised models. Utilitarian optimality only; fairness and
robustness to link failure are noted as outside most models.

## Open questions

How groups find their way to optimal structures, and models of agents
sharing reasons rather than attitudes (Betz 2012 is the one example cited).
