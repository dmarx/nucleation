# CLAIM-tmp817r3: Translation is not the transmission of an invariant semantic object but the selective preservation of decision-relevant information across changing observational and interpretive structures

## The claim

A86: "**Translation is not the transmission of an invariant semantic object. It
is the selective preservation of decision-relevant information across changing
observational and interpretive structures.**" And "its dynamical complement":
"**What survives each act of communication depends not only on what was
transmitted, but on what each successive interpreter is capable of observing,
inferring, and reconstructing.**" "I think this is strong enough to justify
reorganizing the manuscript around a unified theory of **contextual pragmatic
information transport**."

The division of labour behind it, A86: "Sheaf theory tells us how observations
fit together; information theory tells us which distinctions survive
transmission; communication theory tells us how to design a channel that
preserves the distinctions that matter."

## Why it was replaced

U32 ("make it so") produced C6, *What Survives Translation? Contextual Pragmatic
Information Transport*. Its abstract fuses this with [CLAIM-tmpzxah5](CLAIM-tmpzxah5.md): "Communicative
identity is characterized by partially overlapping families of observations
rather than a privileged global semantic vector." The manuscript's last sentence
([CLAIM-tmpww3q0](CLAIM-tmpww3q0.md)) keeps both halves: the distinctions "that a receiving system can
still reconstruct and act upon". Intermediate versions after C6 are outside this
chunk.
