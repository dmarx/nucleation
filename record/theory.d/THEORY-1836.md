# THEORY-1836: Wolf argues that meaningfulness is a dimension of a good life distinct from happiness and morality, arising when a person loves objects worthy of love and engages with them actively, so that neither fulfillment alone nor contribution alone suffices and the concept needs a standard of value that is not radically subjective, of which she offers no theory

## The account

Wolf argues that meaningfulness is a third dimension of a good life, beside happiness and morality. It arises when a person loves objects worthy of love and engages with them actively ("fitting fulfillment"). Fulfillment without worth (Sisyphus Fulfilled) and worth without engagement each fall short. So the concept needs a standard of value that is not radically subjective; she admits she has no theory of one.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-1949](../notes.d/NOTE-1949.md), key results 1–6, quoted with their lectures. The book ([LIT-2609](../literature.d/LIT-2609.md)) revises these lectures; its additions were not read.

## What it does not say

Nothing about technology or society; Wolf disclaims practical use. The exchange's use of her for a critique of technological civilization is the exchange's.
