# LIT-tmpxqwjm: Just culture: "Evidence", power and algorithms

Sidney W. A. Dekker and James M. Nyce (2013). "Just culture: "Evidence", power and algorithms". *Journal of Hospital Administration* 2(3): 73–77. DOI-10.5430/jha.v2n3p73.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading (NOTE-tmp0gu2z).*

- Just-culture algorithms take "the reality of someone's behavior, as well
  as the intentions behind it, as unambiguously and objectively available
  to others" (p. 74).
- Whether a response is just "depends on where one is located in the
  organization and medical competence hierarchy, and who gets to say"
  (p. 75).
- In a survey of almost 2,000 staff at 12 US facilities, which they relay,
  accountability was rated worst, and "The most powerful group (doctors)
  was most likely to see responses as just" (p. 75).
- Just culture "risks becoming one more rhetoric or metric by which
  dominant groups can judge others" (p. 75).
