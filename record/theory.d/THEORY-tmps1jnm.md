# THEORY-tmps1jnm: Bird argues that mammals actively erase epigenetic marks between generations, that no validated mechanism lets the environment instruct a heritable epigenome, and that the human cases most cited, the Swedish famine studies and the Holocaust FKBP5 study, are weak

## The account

Reprogramming is a function, not a barrier the environment sometimes
crosses: "rather than eagerly harnessing environmental inputs from the
life experiences of their ancestors via epigenetic mechanisms, organisms
strive to prevent contamination of a new generation with the accumulated
epigenetic baggage of the previous one" (p. 3). Marks persist in mammals
where the eraser is broken (Dppa2 mutants) or a mutation keeps re-creating
them. Environmentally induced methylation changes are usually downstream
of programmed responses, in which "transcription factors are the prime
movers, with epigenetic marks behaving as their clients" (p. 2). "There is
as yet no validated mechanism by which the external environment can
directly communicate with the epigenome" (p. 3).

On the human cases: the Överkalix studies and their like "ignored the
potential effects of cultural transmission of behaviours within families",
a larger cohort failed to reproduce the paternal-grandfather effects on
diabetes and heart disease, and multiple testing is likely (pp. 3–4). The
Holocaust study "made headline news, despite numerous technical weakness
(e.g., a sample of only 32 people, limited controls and unclear
methodology/interpretation)" (p. 4). Bird grants "some evidence that
severe environmental insults can impact the succeeding generation", doubts
it is adaptive, and closes: "Of course, the answer may be no." (p. 5).

## Evidence

The article (NOTE-1264): arguments from known mechanisms and from worm,
yeast and mouse mutant studies, with new negative results (the Agouti diet
effect failing to validate, a largely negative screen). The cultural
objection to Överkalix is argued by analogy to Leningrad siege survivors'
descendants. The Holocaust critique names sample size and controls and
does not analyse the paper.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297, the germline route. It names what is wrong with the two
human cases the question cited, and its chief confound, families passing
on habits, is itself one of the question's routes. Its account of the
Swedish replication is one-sided, as the question's note of 2026-10-29
records.
