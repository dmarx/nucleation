# LIT-tmpfjacn: Emotions are emergent processes: they require a dynamic computational architecture

Klaus R. Scherer (2009), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society
B* 364(1535):3459–3474 — DOI-10.1098/rstb.2009.0141

## Key takeaways

- **Definition** (§1). Emotion is "a bounded episode in the life of a
  system that is characterized as an emergent pattern of component
  synchronization, preparing adaptive action tendencies to relevant
  events, as defined by their behavioural meaning and aiming at
  establishing control precedence over behaviour." Control precedence is
  Frijda's, glossed as "claiming (not always successfully) priority in the
  control of behaviour and experience".
- **Coherence as criterion** (§2c). Of the three theory traditions, only
  the component process model insists on synchronization and
  desynchronization of components, "to the point of making the degree of
  coherence a central criterion for the existence of an emotion".
- **Sequential checks** (§3a, §3c). Four appraisal objectives (relevance,
  implications, coping potential, normative significance) are met by
  stimulus evaluation checks (SECs). "The CPM claims that the SECs are
  processed in sequence, following a fixed order". All run in parallel,
  but each reaches "preliminary closure" only after its predecessor, and
  "relevance detection is considered to be a first selective filter".
- **Feeling monitors** (§3e). "The CPM assigns a special status to the
  feeling component in the emotion process, as it monitors and regulates
  the component process". It "needs to integrate and centrally represent
  all information about the continuous patterns of change and their
  coherence in all other components." Of the three circles in the Venn
  model, the unconscious representation, the conscious part (qualia) and
  the verbal report, only the last can be measured.
- **Synchronization and consciousness** (§3e). "It is hypothesized that
  it is the degree of synchronization of the components (which might in
  turn be determined by the pertinence of the event as appraised by the
  organism) that will generate conscious experience."
- **Nonlinear dynamics** (§4). Components should show "lagged
  covariation, nonlinearity, differential damping and many other aspects
  of complex synchronization". Emotions are attractor basins of coupled
  oscillators, and an episode ends "when the specific attractor loses its
  force owing to changing appraisal results". Hysteresis in the
  frustration–anger relation, and a cusp catastrophe over goal
  conduciveness and coping power, give abrupt jumps between emotions.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the record's proposal that hedonic valence is
ascendancy of one ordering ([CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md), [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md)). It bears on
two questions, set out in [NOTE-tmpsx1uk](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpsx1uk.md): whether
synchronization is a measurable analogue of ascendancy, and whether the
feeling component is a version of the proposal that what is felt is the
system's model of its own ascendancy. In short:

- synchronization is a correlate ascendancy would produce, not the
  ascendancy itself;
- the monitoring component is close to that proposal, and Scherer also
  makes a stronger, separate hypothesis;
- the paper gives no operational measure of synchronization.

It also uses Frijda's control precedence, which the record's term reads
ascendancy through ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)).

The paper is addressed to computational modellers of emotion in agents.
It is not an instruction for machine-learning practice, so nothing here
belongs in the anthology.
