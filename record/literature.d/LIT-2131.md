# LIT-2131: Rapid Folding of DNA into Nanoscale Shapes at Constant Temperature

Sobczak, J.-P. J., Martin, T. G., Gerling, T. & Dietz, H. (2012). Rapid Folding of DNA into Nanoscale Shapes at Constant Temperature. *Science* 338(6113): 1458–1461. doi:10.1126/science.1229919.

## Key takeaways

*From the PubMed abstract only; unverified until the paper is read.*

- **Constant temperature.** Staples fold the scaffold "within minutes
  into complex nanoscale objects" without an anneal.
- **Out of equilibrium.** "Folding occurred out of equilibrium along
  nucleation-driven pathways", at "temperatures that could be influenced
  by the choice of sequences, strand lengths, and chain topology".
- **Hysteresis.** "Unfolding occurred in apparent equilibrium at higher
  temperatures than those for folding".
- **Yield.** Optimized constant temperatures gave "yields that approached
  100%".

## Standing in the record
