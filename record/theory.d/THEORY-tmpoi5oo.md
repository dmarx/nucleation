# THEORY-tmpoi5oo: Banks' agentic A.I. should be governed by intent-anchored accountability: an agent acts only for an approved business intent within delegated authority, accountability stays with identified human owners, human approval counts as oversight only where the approver sees the substance and has the authority, time and practical ability to reject or stop, and an agent's speed is limited to what that oversight can review

Hong Kong Monetary Authority (2026), [LIT-tmpn5g57](../literature.d/LIT-tmpn5g57.md).

## The proposal

"The Discussion Paper is centred on intent-anchored accountability" (p. 5).
"A formal approval step is not, by itself, meaningful human oversight"
(p. 37); speed is to be limited by "the demonstrated effectiveness of
oversight arrangements" (p. 19); and a stop mechanism must work "in
substance, regardless of how effective the mechanism appears on paper"
(p. 38). Accountability "cannot be passed to customers" (p. 27).

## What it does not show

- **That it is required.** The paper "does not itself introduce binding
  supervisory requirements".
- **That it works.** It sets out what banks should be able to show, with
  no evidence of effect.
- **A right of the affected to suspend.** Customers may request review;
  interruption stays with the bank.
