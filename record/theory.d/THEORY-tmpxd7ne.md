# THEORY-tmpxd7ne: Hardesty, Crossman, Haselschwerdt, Raffaelli, Ogolsky and Johnson find, in 190 divorcing mothers, that coercive control scored by the frequency of tactics rather than their number yields stable high- and low-control groups, and that violence with high control differs from violence with low control in fear, threat and harassment when the frequency and severity of violence are held constant

Hardesty et al. (2015), LIT-tmpjl3bp.

## The result

Cluster analyses of the PMWI Dominance–Isolation subscale scored by
frequency agree across methods (4.2% of cases change cluster) where scoring
by count does not (21.5%). With high control at 19 or more and any CTS2
violence, four groups result. The two violent groups differ as the typology
predicts, and "The results were identical" once the frequency and severity
of violence are controlled, "suggesting that the group differences reflect
the control context rather than differences in violence."

## What it does not show

- **Generality.** One county, divorcing mothers, mothers' reports, a 24%
  response rate.
- **Who controls whom in a couple with violence on both sides.** Only the
  mother's partner's control is measured.
- **Violent resistance or mutual violent control.** Neither is examined.
