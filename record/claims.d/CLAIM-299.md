# CLAIM-299: Decision sufficiency, historical accountability and correctness are three properties of a record of inquiry, and each can hold while the other two fail

## The claim

A8: "Decision-relative sufficiency ≠ historical accountability ≠
epistemic correctness. These are three distinct predicates on
representations of an inquiry. We can now exhibit concrete models where
they come apart."

- **Sufficient without accountable.** [CASE-082](../cases.d/CASE-082.md): a Bayesian agent keeping
  only p(Θ | E₁:t) updates as well as one keeping everything, and cannot
  say how it came to believe what it does.
- **Accountable without correct.** [CASE-084](../cases.d/CASE-084.md): a community whose record
  is impeccable and whose vocabulary excludes the right explanation.
- **Correct without sufficient.** [CASE-093](../cases.d/CASE-093.md): a marginal that is accurate now
  and cannot make the next update.

A5 drew the moral that matters for this record: "A representation may
preserve the history of an argument without enabling good future
decisions, and it may enable good future decisions without preserving a
human-inspectable argument history. That observation prevents us from
assuming the answer to our own research question."

## Why it is Active

Each separation is an existence result from a coherent construction. A
non-implication needs nothing more.

## What it bears on

- [CLAIM-189](CLAIM-189.md) says fidelity is the wrong measure of a developing argument,
  and what a record can secure is traceability. This says traceability
  is not sufficiency either.
- [CLAIM-190](CLAIM-190.md) said the record's own anchoring measures cannot certify drift
fell; it is Superseded by [ARG-028](../arguments.d/ARG-028.md), which carries that point. [CASE-084](../cases.d/CASE-084.md) is the same point about correctness, and A6 called it "a
  direct companion" to [CLAIM-181](CLAIM-181.md) and [CLAIM-190](CLAIM-190.md).
- [CLAIM-250](CLAIM-250.md) is about accountability. A note of 2026-10-15 there records
  which of the three it concerns.

## What it does not say

That any of the three is unimportant. A6 on [CASE-084](../cases.d/CASE-084.md): "An accountable record
may help identify errors once the excluded possibility is made
available."

## Note of 2026-11-12: Lebo, Sahoo and McGuinness (eds.), read

Read on 2026-11-12 among the works of the exchange "luria experiment design"; each reading's NOTE and THEORY hold the detail.

- **Lebo, Sahoo and McGuinness (eds.)** ([THEORY-1806](../theory.d/THEORY-1806.md)): **supports by illustration**, not by argument. A complete PROV-O description of a record secures the historical-accountability property (who produced what from what, and revised it how) while saying nothing about the other two: the vocabulary has no term for whether a derived statement is supported or correct. That a mature standard for provenance carries no support or standing vocabulary is consistent with treating accountability as a separable predicate. It does not exhibit the separations itself.

The claim is unchanged.
