# LEAD-tmphby6s: Measure, in one institution with a documented history of a governing rule and of the practice it governs, how fast the practice settles after the rule changes against how often the rule is revised, to test whether the rule is a slow variable in the sense of rate differences

## The idea

[CLAIM-730](../claims.d/CLAIM-730.md) is supposed because it reads a governing rule as a slow variable
in [CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md)'s sense, and its defeater is "that a governing rule is not a
slow variable in [CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md)'s sense, because what it constrains does not
settle relative to it". That is measurable in an institution that keeps
dated versions of a rule and data on the practice under it: a technical
standard with versioned revisions and conformance data, or a filing rule
with filing behaviour by year.

Measures:

- the rule's revision interval, and its spread;
- the practice's settling time after each revision: how long its
  distribution takes to come within a stated tolerance of its new level;
- the spread of settling times within the practice.

[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md) needs the practice to settle faster than the rule changes, by
more than the rates vary within each level; how large that ratio must be
is [QUESTION-500](../questions.d/QUESTION-500.md)'s. The record's own rates ([CASE-180](../cases.d/CASE-180.md)) are a model of the
measurement in a non-industrial case.

## Checks the record can run

- The defeater is met if the practice's settling time is not shorter than
  the rule's revision interval, or if revisions follow practice (the rule
  tracks what is done) rather than practice settling to the rule.
- A null: a rule that is never applied should show no settling, since
  "Slowness makes a constraint available", not effective.

The institution and the data are open; that is why this is at stage idea.
