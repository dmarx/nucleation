summary: >-
  Lin & Lu (J. Comput. Phys. 2021). Writes data-driven model reduction for an invertible map with an invariant measure in Koopman-operator terms, derives the discrete Mori–Zwanzig equation from the Dyson formula, and introduces the Wiener projection: orthogonal projection onto the span of the entire past of chosen functions Ψ of the resolved variables. Under it the memory term vanishes, the reduced equation is the causal Wiener filter x_{n+1} = Σ_k Ψ(x_{n−k})h_k + ξ_{n+1}, and the residual is orthogonal to all of Ψ's past. A rational approximation of the filter's transfer function, called "uncontrolled" by the authors, turns it into a NARMAX-type recursion. That recursion is fitted by nonlinear least squares with a stability constraint and tested on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky and a stochastically forced Burgers equation. The Wiener projection is proved; the NARMAX derivation is heuristic and no error is bounded.

# LIT-1307: Data-driven model reduction, Wiener projections, and the Koopman-Mori-Zwanzig formalism

Lin, K. K. & Lu, F. (2021). Data-driven model reduction, Wiener projections,
and the Koopman-Mori-Zwanzig formalism. *Journal of Computational Physics*
424: 109864. doi:10.1016/j.jcp.2020.109864. arXiv:1908.07725.

Read in full in [NOTE-1029](../notes.d/NOTE-1029.md).

## Key takeaways

- The discrete Mori–Zwanzig equation is an exact identity for any
  projection, obtained by applying the Dyson formula to the observation
  function. It reduces nothing by itself.
- Projecting onto the whole past of the resolved observables, rather than
  onto their present, removes the memory term: all memory moves into the
  projection, and the residual is orthogonal to every past observable. The
  reduced equation is then the causal Wiener filter, built only from the
  coarse variable's own history ([THEORY-819](../theory.d/THEORY-819.md)).
- NARMAX is reached from there by approximating the filter's transfer
  function by a ratio of polynomials. In general that step is uncontrolled.
  In the linear Gaussian VAR(1) class it is exact ([NOTE-1029](../notes.d/NOTE-1029.md)).
- On Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (5 of 108 modes, step 0.1) and on a stochastic
  Burgers equation (9 of 128 modes), the fitted models reproduce spectra,
  autocorrelations and marginals and forecast well. That is numerical
  evidence for two systems, with orders chosen by trial.

## Standing in the record

Filed for [QUESTION-222](../questions.d/QUESTION-222.md). It supplies what Chorin and Lu ([LIT-1304](LIT-1304.md)) left
as analogy: an exact statement linking Mori–Zwanzig to a reduced model
whose memory is carried by the coarse variable's own past. It does not
supply an error bound for a finite augmentation. The reading adds, for the
linear class, how the Wiener filter and Mori's kernels relate, and why
their memory lengths differ.
