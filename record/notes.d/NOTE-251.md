# NOTE-251: Contextuality, Cohomology and Paradox

## Contribution

AMB 2011 ([LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)) introduced a Čech obstruction γ(s) that certifies, when non-zero, that a support section s does not extend. It showed on examples that the obstruction sometimes works and sometimes does not. This paper adds three things.

- **A class on which it always works.** Suppose the outcomes carry a ring structure R and the model's support satisfies R-linear equations with no global solution: an "All-vs-Nothing" argument, AvN_R. Then the obstruction, with coefficients in R or in ℤ, is non-zero on every section (Theorem 21).
- **A large quantum source of such models.** Every state stabilised by the group generated by an "AvN triple" of n-qubit Pauli operators is one (Theorem 4). For independent triples these states form a 2ⁿ⁻³-dimensional subspace.
- **A more abstract definition.** γ is re-defined as the connecting homomorphism of the snake lemma. AMB had only pointed to this form in a Remark.

The paper also offers a short, informal reading of Liar cycles as contextual models, with the PR box as the length-4 Liar cycle.

## Key insight

AvN arguments are linear-algebraic, and so is cohomology with free-module coefficients. Take the free R-module F_R S on the support presheaf.

- *The families are affine.* A compatible family of formal combinations must have the same coefficient sum in every context, since all of them restrict to the empty context. So if it passes through 1·s₀, it consists of affine combinations.
- *The counit evaluates them.* The counit of the free–forgetful adjunction, F_R U E → E, turns formal affine combinations into actual affine combinations of outcome vectors. It therefore sends a compatible family in F_R S to a compatible family in the affine closure Aff S.
- *The consequence.* A vanishing obstruction yields a global section of Aff S. But the R-linear theory of S equals that of Aff S, so a global section of Aff S would satisfy every equation of the theory, which is exactly what an AvN argument forbids.

The Hardy-style "false positives" of AMB are exactly what the affine closure can glue. Cohomology over R sees the model only up to its affine closure.

## Assumptions

- **Scenario (§3).**
  - *The measurement cover.* ⟨X, M, O⟩, with X a finite set of measurements. M is a cover of X, and an antichain: it lists the maximal contexts.
  - *Outcomes.* One outcome set O for all measurements.
  - *The event sheaf.* ℰ(U) = O^U.
- **The model is the support presheaf only (Def 1).** A model is a subpresheaf S ⊆ ℰ with three conditions.
  - *(E1)* S(C) ≠ ∅ for every context C.
  - *(E2) Flasque beneath the cover.* Restrictions S(U′) → S(U) are surjective for U ⊆ U′ ⊆ C. By [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md) this "amounts to" no-signalling.
  - *(E3)* Compatible families induce a (unique) global section in S(X).
  - *No probabilities.* Probabilities are discarded throughout: "we shall only work with the associated support presheaf" (p. 7).
- **Contextuality (Def 2).**
  - *LC(S, s).* s belongs to no compatible family.
  - *LC(S).* LC(S, s) holds for some s.
  - *SC(S).* LC(S, s) holds for all s, equivalently S(X) = ∅.
- **Rings (§4.2, footnote 3).** R is commutative with unit. The AvN_R results need the scenario ⟨X, M, R⟩: the outcomes are identified with R, so each ℰ(U) = R^U is an R-module. For binary models this means embedding {0, 1} in R, as box 25 does with ℤ/3.
- **The cover must be connected, although Theorem 21 does not say so.**
  - *Where the paper says it.* Prop 16 (γ(r₀) = 0 iff r₀ extends to a compatible family) is stated for connected M. Footnote 5 says "we are only interested in connected covers".
  - *Where it is used.* The coefficient-sum remark at the end of §5.3, which the proof of Theorem 21 uses, needs compatibility over the empty intersection. A Čech cocycle guarantees that only on a connected cover.
- **Stabiliser setting (§4.1).**
  - *The group.* The Pauli n-group Pₙ: n-fold tensor products of {I, X, Y, Z} with global phase in {±1, ±i}.
  - *Qubits only.* Eigenvalues ±1 are relabelled as ℤ₂ = {0, 1}, so the construction is n-qubit only. There are no qudits and no other Pauli dimension anywhere in the paper.
  - *Measurements.* Each party measures a local Pauli. A joint context is one Pauli per site, and the "measurement h" of the proof is the corresponding product.
- **Paradox section (§2).** It takes over, as argued "extensively and rather compellingly" in the cited literature (Wen 2001; Cook 2004; Walicki 2009), that a Liar cycle's content is preserved by replacing reference and truth predicates with Boolean equations. That premise is not argued in this paper.

## Key results

- **Definition 3 (AvN triple).** ⟨e, f, g⟩ in Pₙ, with global phases +1 and pairwise commuting, such that:
  - *(A1)* at each site at least two of eᵢ, fᵢ, gᵢ are equal;
  - *(A2)* the number of sites with eᵢ = gᵢ ≠ fᵢ, all distinct from I, is odd.
- **Theorem 4.** Let S be the subgroup generated by an AvN triple, and V_S its stabilised subspace. Every |ψ⟩ ∈ V_S, under Pauli measurements, "admits an All-vs-Nothing argument": the parity equations its support satisfies are inconsistent.
  - *Proof.* Complete. By (A2), efg = −h with h ∈ Pₙ of phase +1, which forces odd parity for h. By (A1), any global assignment gives h even parity.
  - *Size of the class.* The remark after the theorem says that for linearly independent check vectors, dim V_S = 2ⁿ⁻³ (cited to Nielsen–Chuang and Caves).
  - *My check of V_S ≠ ∅.* V_S is never empty. −I ∈ S would need efg = −I, but (A2) forces efg to be non-identity at the (A2) sites. The paper does not say this.
  - *My numerical check.* I sampled 40 commuting AvN triples for n = 3 and 12 for n = 4. For each, a random state in V_S under local X/Y/Z measurements gave a strongly contextual model that is AvN over ℤ₂, with no failures.
- **GHZ (§4.1).** The four Mermin parity equations, with the corrected right-hand sides of v2, sum to 0 = 1. This is a complete hand proof of strong contextuality for GHZ(3). It fills the n = 3 case that [NOTE-016](NOTE-016.md) recorded as only sketched in [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md) ([LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md) had a machine check).
  - *My extension.* By brute force, the GHZ(n) model with local X/Y measurements is AvN over ℤ₂ for n = 3, 4, 5, 6. The paper does not claim this for general n.
- **Box 25 (§4.2).** The paper says Pironio–Bancal–Scarani's 'box 25' "admits no parity AvN argument" but satisfies six equations mod 3.
  - *My check of the six equations.* Each variable's coefficients sum to 3, and the right-hand sides sum to 8 ≡ 2 (mod 3), so they are jointly inconsistent.
  - *Unverified.* Whether box 25's support satisfies them, and whether it has no parity argument. I did not obtain the box.
- **Definitions 5–6.** An R-linear equation is φ = ⟨C, a, b⟩, satisfied by s when Σ_{m∈C} a(m)s(m) = b. The model's theory is T_R(S) = ⋃_C T_R(S(C)). **AvN_R(S)** means T_R(S) has no global solution g: X → R.
- **Proposition 7.** AvN_R(S) ⇒ SC(S). The proof is complete and one line.
- **Affine closure (§4.3).**
  - *Solution sets are affine.* The Galois connection S ⊆ M(Γ) ⟺ T(S) ⊇ Γ gives aff ≤ M∘T, with equality when R is a field.
  - *Definition 8.* (Aff S)(C) := aff(S(C)). Aff is natural under restriction (eq. 1), so Aff S is flasque beneath the cover. Aff S(X) can be non-empty when S(X) is empty.
- **Proposition 9.** AvN_R(S) ⇒ SC(Aff S), with the converse when R is a field. Both directions are proved.
  - *A slip.* The statement writes "AvN(S)" without the subscript.
- **Cohomology (§5).**
  - *Definitions 10–14.* The Čech complex as in AMB. The coboundary's face range is now correctly 0 ≤ j ≤ q + 1, fixing the typo [NOTE-250](NOTE-250.md) found.
  - *Relative presheaves.* F|_U and F_Ū are defined as in AMB, with the exact sequence 0 → F_Ū → F → F|_U.
  - *Exactness.* The paper argues that the cochain map C⁰(M, F) → C⁰(M, F|_U) is surjective by flasqueness beneath the cover. This answers the exactness question [NOTE-250](NOTE-250.md) raised.
  - *The snake lemma.* It gives a connecting map Ȟ⁰(M, F|_{C₀}) ≅ F(C₀) → Ȟ¹(M, F_{C̄₀}).
  - *A minor overstatement.* The text calls both rows "short exact", but the bottom row's right-hand surjectivity is not argued. It is also not needed for the exactness used.
- **Definition 15.** γ(r₀) is the image of r₀ under that connecting homomorphism.
  - *My check.* Unwinding the snake construction (lift r₀'s restrictions to a 0-cochain of F, apply δ⁰, read the result in Z¹(F_{C̄₀})) gives exactly [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s concrete γ(s₁) = [δ⁰(s₁, …, sₙ)]. The two definitions agree, which the paper does not state.
- **Proposition 16.** For connected M, γ(r₀) = 0 iff there is a compatible family {r_C ∈ F(C)} with r_{C₀} = r₀.
  - *Where the proof is.* v2 says "For a proof … see [2]". The proof is in v1 (Prop 5.7): exactness at Ȟ⁰(F|_{C₀}), so ker γ = im(Ȟ⁰(F) → Ȟ⁰(F|_{C₀})).
- **Definition 18.** CLC_R(S, s₀) means γ_{F_R S}(s₀) ≠ 0. CSC_R means γ ≠ 0 for all sections.
- **Proposition 19.** CLC_R ⇒ LC and CSC_R ⇒ SC. This is [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s Prop 4.3, generalised to any ring; the proof is complete.
- **Proposition 20.** For a ring homomorphism R′ → R, γ_{R′}(s) = 0 ⇒ γ_R(s) = 0, so CSC_R ⇒ CSC_{R′}. Since ℤ maps to every ring, CSC_R ⇒ CSC_ℤ.
  - *A slip.* The proof's first line swaps the direction ("If h: R → R′"). The statement is right.
- **The coefficient-sum remark (end of §5.3).** A compatible family has equal coefficient sums in every context. So a family through 1·s₀ consists of affine combinations.
- **Theorem 21 (main).** AvN_R(S) ⇒ SC(Aff S) ⇒ CSC_R(S) ⇒ CSC_ℤ(S) ⇒ SC(S).
  - *The proof.* The new step is the second. By Prop 16, a vanishing γ gives a compatible family of formal affine combinations, and the counit ε pushes it to a compatible family of Aff S. The proof is complete, given connectedness.
- **The logical analogue (p. 16), sketched.** AvN_R(e, s₀) ⇒ LC(Aff S, s₀) ⇒ CLC_R(S, s₀) ⇒ CLC_ℤ(S, s₀) ⇒ LC(S, s₀), where AvN_R(e, s₀) means no solution of the theory extends s₀.
  - *Its support.* "Essentially the same strategy". My check: each step does follow by the same argument, with (E3) for Aff S giving the global section in the first step.
- **Discussion claims.** The first implication of Theorem 21 reverses for general rings under "a more abstract notion of equational consistency, in terms of quotient modules". That is asserted, not shown.
- **Liar cycles (§2).**
  - *The encoding.* The cycle S₁: S₂ is true, …, S_N: S₁ is false becomes x₁ = x₂, …, x_{N−1} = x_N, x_N = ¬x₁, each equation fibred over its two variables.
  - *The claims.* Any N − 1 of the equations are consistent, and all N are not. "Up to rearrangement, the Liar cycle of length 4 corresponds exactly to the PR box" via x₁ ∼ a₂, x₂ ∼ b₁, x₃ ∼ a₁, x₄ ∼ b₂. The usual Liar reasoning "corresponds precisely" to searching for a univocal path in the PR bundle.
  - *My check of the PR identification.* It is exact at the level of supports: three correlated edges {00, 11} and one anti-correlated edge {01, 10}.
  - *My computation.* I encoded the Liar cycle as a support model, with contexts {xᵢ, xᵢ₊₁} and support {00, 11}, or {01, 10} on the closing edge. For N = 3, …, 8 it is a valid model (both single-variable marginals are {0, 1}, so it is no-signalling), strongly contextual and AvN over ℤ₂. γ ≠ 0 for all 2N sections over both ℤ and ℤ₂. By Theorem 21 that holds for every N ≥ 3. The paper does not state or apply this: it defers "the cohomology of paradox".
  - *Where the encoding fails.* It breaks down at N = 1 and N = 2, including the classic Liar the paper names. For N = 1 the only "context" {x₁} has empty support (x₁ = ¬x₁), violating (E1). For N = 2 both equations sit over the same set {x₁, x₂}, violating the antichain condition, and merging them gives empty support. The paper does not note this.
- **The completeness claim (p. 17).** "all known quantum examples of strong contextuality are of AvN type, hence all such examples are captured by cohomology". This is an assertion with no citation or survey.

**What it does to [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s documented gaps ([NOTE-250](NOTE-250.md)).**

- **The Hardy gap is not closed or narrowed.**
  - *Why no AvN argument exists.* Hardy is not strongly contextual, so only the logical chain could apply, and it cannot. Every Hardy context's support contains three affinely independent points of R², so (Aff S)(C) = R² = ℰ(C) at every context, over every ring. Then T_R(S) is trivial, and AvN_R(e, s₀) fails for every R and every s₀.
  - *My computation.* It agrees: Hardy is not AvN over ℤ₂ or ℤ/3, and γ = 0 on all 13 sections over ℤ and ℤ₂.
  - *What the paper does with the case.* v1 still named Hardy as a false positive (§5.3). v2 deletes the sentence and does not discuss the case at all.
- **The strongly contextual Kochen–Specker cover with γ = 0 on 9/15 sections is explained but not narrowed.**
  - *The cover.* [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s §8 cover is {A,B,C},{B,D,E},{C,D,E},{A,D,F},{A,E,G}.
  - *What Theorem 21 implies.* Read contrapositively: γ_ℤ = 0 on some section means not CSC_ℤ, hence not CSC_R, hence not SC(Aff S), hence not AvN_R. That holds for every ring R.
  - *What follows.* The cover is a strongly contextual model with no All-vs-Nothing argument over any ring. It shows that the class Theorem 21 covers is strictly smaller than strong contextuality.
  - *My computation.* It agrees: not AvN over ℤ₂ or ℤ/3, and γ ≠ 0 on exactly 6 of the 15 sections over ℤ and ℤ₂.
  - *What the paper says.* It does not mention the example.
- **[LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s positive results are all AvN over ℤ₂, so Theorem 21 recovers them.**
  - *The examples.* PR box (the Liar-4 cycle). GHZ. Peres–Mermin (row parities 1, column parities 0, summing to 3 ≡ 1 against 0). The triangle (x + y = 1 three times). The 18-vector set (Σ_{m∈C} x_m = 1 over 9 contexts, with each vector in 2 contexts, gives 0 ≡ 9).
  - *Checks.* I confirmed PR, GHZ, Peres–Mermin and the triangle by computation, and the 18-vector case by the parity count.
  - *[LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s Prop 6.2 too, on connected covers.* A Kochen–Specker model failing the GCD condition is AvN over ℤ/g with g = gcd{d_m}. Summing Σ_{m∈C} x_m = 1 over the contexts gives Σ_m d_m x_m ≡ 0 against |𝒰| ≢ 0 (mod g).
  - *What the paper says.* It asserts the subsumption ("These results will be subsumed and greatly generalised in §6", p. 15) but does not show it example by example. The derivations above are mine.

## Method

- **Linearise the support.**
  - *The theory.* For each context take all R-linear equations its support satisfies. The union is the model's theory T_R(S).
  - *AvN.* The model is AvN_R when that theory has no global solution.
  - *Aff S.* Equivalently for fields, Aff S is strongly contextual. Aff S is the model whose context supports are the affine hulls.
- **Define the obstruction abstractly.** Free the support presheaf over R, lift 0 → F_{C̄₀} → F → F|_{C₀} to cochains (right-exact by flasqueness), and take the snake-lemma connecting map.
- **Compare the two.** The coefficient-sum argument makes any compatible family through 1·s₀ affine. The counit of Free ⊣ Forget evaluates it into Aff S, so vanishing cohomology produces a global section of Aff S, contradicting AvN.
- **Produce quantum instances.** Stabiliser algebra, conditions (A1) and (A2), turns three commuting Paulis into an odd-parity constraint on their product that no global assignment can meet.

## Concepts

- **Empirical model (this paper)** — the support subpresheaf S ⊆ ℰ satisfying (E1)–(E3), not the probability table. [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md)'s "empirical model" is the table.
- **Logically contextual at s, LC(S, s)** — s is in no compatible family. This is [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s "possibilistic non-extendability" under the later name.
- **Strongly contextual, SC** — LC at every section, i.e. S(X) = ∅.
- **Univocal path** — in the bundle diagram, a closed path through every fibre exactly once, i.e. a global assignment.
- **AvN triple** — Def 3 above.
- **R-linear equation, theory T_R(S), AvN_R** — Defs 5–6.
- **Affine closure Aff S** — per-context affine hull, flasque below the cover.
- **CLC_R, CSC_R** — "cohomologically" logically or strongly contextual: γ_{F_R S} ≠ 0 at some or at every section.
- **Cohomological obstruction γ(r₀)** — the image of r₀ ∈ F(C₀) ≅ Ȟ⁰(M, F|_{C₀}) under the connecting map into Ȟ¹(M, F_{C̄₀}).
- **Liar cycle of length N** — the equations x₁ = x₂, …, x_N = ¬x₁, each fibred over its variables.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-012](../theory.d/THEORY-012.md) (Active).** This paper supports it and sharpens its new "does not say" line. It contradicts nothing.
  - *What the line gets right.* The cohomological witness is sufficient, not necessary, and nothing here claims otherwise.
  - *What it now leaves out.* The witness is not merely incomplete: it is complete on a named class. Every All-vs-Nothing model, over any coefficient ring, has a non-vanishing obstruction on every section (Theorem 21). ## Limitations

- **Completeness on AvN, not a characterisation.** Theorem 21 is one-directional. Whether every cohomologically strongly contextual model is AvN_R for some R is not addressed; the paper lists it as future work.
  - *Where the known failures sit.* All of [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s failures lie outside the AvN class. Hardy is not strongly contextual. The §8 cover is strongly contextual but, by Theorem 21, has no AvN argument over any ring.
- **Only supports are used.** It is blind to probabilistic contextuality, and to anything below CF = 1.
- **Qubit stabiliser only.** Theorem 4 needs ±1-eigenvalue Paulis relabelled into ℤ₂. No qudit or other-ring quantum family is given. The only non-ℤ₂ example (box 25, mod 3) is a no-signalling box, not a quantum model, and its details are unverified.
- **Unsupported headline sentences.**
  - *"all known quantum examples of strong contextuality are of AvN type" (C10).* Uncited.
  - *"the other All-vs-Nothing arguments … come down to exhibiting AvN triples" (C8).* Unshown.
  - *The field-free converse (C15).* Asserted only.
- **Paradox.** It is an analogy with one exact case.
  - *What is borrowed.* The reduction of truth and reference to Boolean equations is taken from the literature, not argued.
  - *Where it breaks.* The encoding does not produce a valid model for N = 1 (the Liar itself) or N = 2.
  - *What is not given.* No general correspondence theorem.
- **Proof availability.**
  - *Prop 16.* The published text (v2 = LIPIcs) proves Theorem 21 using Prop 16, whose proof it defers to "[2]", the arXiv version, which v2 itself now replaces. The proof is in v1 only.
  - *Connectedness.* The theorem silently assumes a connected cover.
- **Slips.**
  - *Prop 20.* The proof's first line reverses h's direction.
  - *Prop 9.* It drops the subscript R.
  - *The snake diagrams.* The text calls both rows "short exact" without arguing the bottom row's surjectivity. It is not needed.
  - *The abstract.* "contexuality".
  - *v1's GHZ equations.* They had the wrong right-hand sides for the stated state; corrected in v2.

## Open questions

- **The converse.** Does CSC_ℤ imply AvN_R for some R? The §8 cover shows that SC does not imply AvN; whether CSC does is the question. A counterexample, or a proof, would settle "what cohomology detects".
- **Conjecture 8.1.** [LIT-277](../literature.d/LIT-277.md)'s completeness conjecture, under symmetry and connectedness, is dropped here without comment. Per its abstract, Carù 1701.00656 disproves it (unverified).
- **Quantum strong contextuality beyond AvN.** Is there a quantum-realisable strongly contextual model with no AvN argument over any ring? That would refute C10. The §8 cover is not claimed to be quantum-realisable.
- **Qudits.** Do stabiliser AvN triples generalise to qudit Paulis with ℤ_d outcomes? The ring-general Theorem 21 would then apply at once. The paper does not say.
- **Cohomology of paradox.** Can the promised treatment handle the paradoxes that are not cycles (Yablo-type infinite chains, which would need an infinite cover) and the N = 1, 2 cases the support encoding cannot express?
