# LIT-tmph8p41: Exponential growth and selection in self-replicating materials from DNA origami rafts

He, X., Sha, R., Zhuo, R., Mi, Y., Chaikin, P. M. & Seeman, N. C. (2017). Exponential growth and selection in self-replicating materials from DNA origami rafts. *Nature Materials* 16(10): 993–997. doi:10.1038/nmat4986.

## Key takeaways

*Not from the paper, which was not read: from how Zhou et al. (2021),
[LIT-tmpb87sq](LIT-tmpb87sq.md), and Zhang, Sha and Chaikin (2025) describe it. Unverified
until read.*

- **Exponential growth and selection, by their account.** "A system of
  DNA origami dimers has been shown to exhibit exponential growth and
  selection" (Zhou et al. 2021, abstract).
- **Doubling, at most.** The one-sided dimer design "doubles in each
  cycle" (Zhou et al. 2021); Zhang et al. (2025) give this paper
  "~7,000,000-fold amplification after 24 cycles within about 730 h",
  which is about 22.7 doublings in 24 cycles, a mean of about 1.93 per
  cycle and about 30 h a cycle (the reader's arithmetic, not a figure of
  either paper).
- **Unseeded dimers are sterile.** "any dimers that are formed are sterile
  and do not replicate (19)", ref. 19 being this paper (Zhou et al. 2021).
- **Enzyme-free by design.** "That system purposefully avoided the use of
  enzymes to get a hint of how self-replication might have evolved in a
  prebiotic world by using CNVK and UV light" (Zhang et al. 2025,
  abstract).
- **Error-free by design.** "the initial efforts were to make the system as
  error-free as possible, and in fact the experiments showed only copies of
  the seeded patterns" (Zhou et al. 2021).
