# LIT-261: A Generalized Representer Theorem

Bernhard Schölkopf, Ralf Herbrich, Alex J. Smola (2001), *Computational Learning Theory (COLT/EuroCOLT 2001), Lecture Notes in Artificial Intelligence 2111, Springer, pp. 416–426* — DOI-10.1007/3-540-44581-1_27

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpe7ywm](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpe7ywm.md).*

- **The theorem (Theorem 1).** X is any nonempty set, k a real
  positive-definite kernel, g strictly increasing, and c any cost on the
  training triples, coupled, non-convex or ∞-valued. Every minimizer of
  c + g(‖f‖) over the countable kernel expansions of finite norm (eq. 13)
  is f = Σ_{i=1}^m α_i k(·, x_i).
- **The proof (eqs. 18–24), checked.** The component of f orthogonal to
  span{k(·, x_i)} does not change any f(x_j), by the reproducing
  property, and strictly raises g(‖f‖), by Pythagoras. Nothing else is
  used.
- **No Riesz.** The RKHS is built from the kernel (§1.2), so
  ⟨k(·, x), f⟩ = f(x) holds by construction (eq. 11). The word "Riesz"
  does not occur in the paper.
- **Hypotheses.** Strictness of g is what forces every minimizer into the
  span; with mere monotonicity some minimizer still is (p. 420, asserted).
  Convexity matters only for local minima of the reduced m-dimensional
  problem (eq. 30).
- **Not shown.** Existence of a minimizer; that the class F is the whole
  completed RKHS; the semiparametric Theorem 2, which is stated without
  proof.
- **Examples.** SV regression and classification, a strictly monotone
  regularizer taken from a risk bound, MAP estimation under a Gaussian
  process prior, and kernel PCA as a unit-variance hard constraint
  (eq. 31).

the needed structure is had without the Riesz theorem; the reading

to an eigenspace is not made here.
