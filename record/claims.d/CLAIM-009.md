# CLAIM-009: Pragmatic judgements may be formally contextual: their context-relative distributions may admit no global extension once direct context effects are accounted for

## The claim

A24 §6: "The context-dependent judgments through which communicative identity
is constituted may exhibit structures that cannot be reconciled within a
noncontextual probabilistic model." A30's H2: "The structure of those operations
and their observable statistics cannot be represented by a common classical
joint model satisfying explicitly specified compatibility and noncontextuality
assumptions. This requires additional empirical constraints."

A24 set it as the third of three layers: a pragmatic thesis ("well motivated",
now [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md)), a theory of translation ("developing proposal", the ancestor of
[CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)), and this. "I would resist collapsing these into one thesis. The first
doesn't establish the second, and neither establishes the third." That
discipline is the manuscript's §11.

## What it does not say

That meaning, the text or the reader is quantum. A21: "This is not evidence that
the poem or brain is physically quantum." Manuscript §9: "Quantum-like models
may be useful in constrained cases, but are optional realizations"; §11: "not a
presumption that pragmatic context effects are quantum". The manuscript
supposes it only so as to test it: §3 says "Whether pragmatic observables
exhibit such patterns requires repeated, controlled measurement", and §10 says
to "report context effects separately from possible contextuality". [THEORY-013](../theory.d/THEORY-013.md)
counts against it for the data published so far ([CLAIM-041](CLAIM-041.md)).

## The nearest test so far

Wang et al. ([LIT-tmp9rgb4](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9rgb4.md)) test lexical selection in ambiguous phrases, not
pragmatic judgement. They report two contextual systems, and both fail the
paper's own parametric bootstrap. So the paper shows that the test can be
run on language data. It is not evidence for this claim.
