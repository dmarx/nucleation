# THEORY-498: A mathematical structure whose domains are aspects of experience and whose axioms hold there is not thereby a structure of experience, because that test passes incompatible topologies on one set and every rescaling of a metric without consulting experience; requiring, for each relation, an aspect that changes exactly when a variation of experience breaks the relation removes those automatic passes, and has so far been met only by relative similarity and a visual topology, each on an assumed premise

## Source

Kleiner & Ludwig (2023), "What is a Mathematical Structure of Conscious
Experience?", [LIT-143](../literature.d/LIT-143.md), read in full in [NOTE-135](../notes.d/NOTE-135.md) in the Synthese version of
record (2024, 203:89), compared against arXiv v1. Section and page numbers
are the Synthese version's.

## What was actually shown

**The implicit criterion and its failure (§1).** The authors reconstruct
the standard in use as (MDC): a structure is of conscious experience if
its domains are sets of experiential aspects (D1) and its axioms hold
(D2). As a sufficient condition it fails three ways, each by explicit
construction:

- *Incompatible structures* (p. 4): any aspect set X carries both the
  discrete topology and {∅, A, A^⊥, X}, so (MDC) certifies both.
- *Arbitrary re-definitions* (pp. 4–5): if (M, d) passes, so does
  (M, C·d) for every C > 0, and (f(a)+f(b))·d(a,b) passes under modified
  axioms, which can break the triangle inequality.
- *Indifference* (p. 5): the first two need no input about experience
  beyond "some set of aspects".

**The replacement (pp. 10–12).** A variation is a change e → e′ of
experience with a partial map between their aspects. (MSC): S is a
structure of experience iff its domains are aspects (S1) and every
relation or function S_j has an S_j-aspect (S2): an aspect that, for all
variations and all relata, changes exactly when the variation fails to
preserve S_j. (MSC) implies (MDC), so every structure *of* experience
also *describes* it.

**The two cases that pass.** Relative similarity to a reference colour is
a strict partial order satisfying (MSC), if transitivity holds, which the
authors assume and call "ultimately, an empirical question" (p. 14). The
regions of visual experience form a topology satisfying (MSC), given the
subsumptive unity thesis of Bayne and Chalmers, restricted to sets
"experienced as unified" (§4, fn. 12), and closure properties that "seem
to" hold (p. 18).

**How (MSC) meets the three problems (§5).** No aspect can be both an
S-aspect and an S′-aspect of incompatible structures, since one variation
cannot both change and not change it. A rescaled C·d has different
relata, so whether it has an aspect is a question about experience, not
automatic. And (MSC) cannot be applied without examining experience.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The constructions in §1 are
proofs; they could not have failed. The positive cases could have failed
had the similarity aspect or the unity aspects not tracked the relations.
[NOTE-135](../notes.d/NOTE-135.md) records that the relative-similarity proof is near-definitional,
since < is defined from the experienced aspect.

## What this does not say

- **That metric spaces cannot model experience.** That argument (from the
  construction of ℝ, and from doubt that two qualities are experienced as
  "a specific number apart") is in arXiv v1 §4 only. The published paper
  removes it and concludes for relative similarity and topology alone. A
  citation for "metric structure is not a structure of experience" is a
  citation of a withdrawn preprint argument.
- **That incompatible structures cannot both pass.** The §5 argument shows
  only that no single aspect serves both. Two incompatible structures
  could each pass through different aspects. It also assumes that a
  mathematical automorphism is realised as a variation of some actual
  experience, which is not argued ([NOTE-135](../notes.d/NOTE-135.md), C5).
- **That any S-aspect can be certified.** (MSC) quantifies over all
  variations of all experiences in E. Any real application checks a finite
  sample, and the paper does not say how to certify an aspect from finite
  data.
- **That rescaled metrics are excluded.** §5 shows that they no longer pass
  automatically, not that they fail.
- **Which account of experience is right.** The definition is neutral
  between qualia, qualities and IIT's phenomenal distinctions (fn. 3). It
  presupposes that experiences have well-defined sets of aspects, so an
  illusionist would read the aspects as quasi-phenomenal properties
  ([THEORY-633](THEORY-633.md)).
