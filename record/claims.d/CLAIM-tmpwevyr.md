# CLAIM-tmpwevyr: Read as the Saussure correction has it, the structuralist premise argues against transport: value is internal to one language state, so what can cross between languages is signification, the aspect the premise demotes

## The objection

[CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md) is the manuscript's structuralist premise, and [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) rests on
it: "A sign's communicative significance is fixed by its contrasts within a
system rather than by correspondence to a referent." [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md), granted,
corrects how it states Saussure. Value, "fixed by a term's relations to
coexisting terms, is distinct from signification, its counterpart relation
to an idea", and the fix is to "say value rather than significance, and
drop the referent contrast, which is not Saussure's". Then: "It does not
undercut the manuscript's use. Relational identity survives the
correction, and is arguably stronger for it: a term's value, not its
signification, is what the manuscript's observables track."

The correction turns the premise the other way.

- **Value does not cross.** [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md): "The account concerns the
  relations inside a language state". Its first example: "French *mouton*
  may share a signification with English *sheep* but not its value,
  because English also has *mutton* for the meat." A translation moves a
  text into another language state, so every value is fixed afresh by the
  target's coexisting terms. Where the two fields differ, as they do for
  *mouton* and *sheep*, for the Sanskrit and French plurals and for French
  *louer* against German *mieten* and *vermieten* (all [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md)'s), the
  translation cannot keep the value. If the observables track value, as
  [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md) says, then the premise predicts that what they track is what a
  change of language changes.
- **What can cross is signification, or something from outside both
  systems.** In Saussure's example the thing *mouton* and *sheep* share is
  signification. Otherwise a correspondence between the two systems must
  be supplied from outside them. [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s own qualification from
  relative representations ([THEORY-175](../theory.d/THEORY-175.md)) says so: "Within one system
  relations suffice; for transport between systems they work only once
  something ties the anchor in one to the anchor in the other." Neither is
  value in Saussure's sense, which is the only relational notion the
  premise draws from him.
- **The premise is about another object.** Value is a fact of the language
  system, the *langue*. [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s observables, "stance, footing,
  alignment, expected uptake", are facts of utterances in use. Nothing in
  the line argues that a relational account of the language system carries
  over to utterances. A search of the claims on 2026-10-10 finds *langue*
  only inside [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md)'s quotation of Saussure, and *parole* nowhere.

[CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s title still contrasts contrast with "correspondence to a
referent", which [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md) says is not Saussure's and [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md) confirms
("it says nothing about how terms relate to things"). [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) rests on
the uncorrected title.

## What it does not say

It does not say that relational identity is false, nor that [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)
needs Saussure. [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md)'s body calls it "a motivating premise, not a
result the manuscript relies on formally". It
says that the premise, read as [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md) says it must be, motivates the
opposite of transport for the language system, and says nothing about
utterances. It does not dispute [CLAIM-072](CLAIM-072.md)'s correction, only its last
paragraph.

## What would answer it

- An argument that values in two language states can correspond without
  passing through signification: for instance a correspondence of contrast
  systems, fixed independently of the fidelity it is used to measure. That
  correspondence is the τ of [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md), and it would have to be argued,
  not assumed.
- Or a restatement of [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md) at the level of use, where stance and
  footing live, with a reading that supports a relational account there.
  Saussure would then be precedent, not premise.
