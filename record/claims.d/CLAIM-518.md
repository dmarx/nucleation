# CLAIM-518: Normative judgments about an organization's continuity, reform or obligations can depend on diachronic relations that descriptions of present states or terminal outcomes do not show, so specifying the relevant relations and histories can be necessary to apply them adequately; which continuations ought to follow is a further normative question, not a prerequisite of every normative judgment

## The claim

Separate two things a normative standard N can do with a history γ of an
organization:

- **J_N(γ), evaluating the history.** For example: was an agreement kept,
  was a commitment honoured, was harm done?
- **𝒜_N(γ), fixing its admissible continuations or remedies.** For
  example: should the organization reform, compensate, dissolve or keep
  what it has?

The first can be determinate while the second is not. "This institution
violated its agreement" is a complete normative judgment that prescribes
nothing.

The claim is about the first. For judgments about an organization's
continuity, reform or obligations, J_N can depend on diachronic relations
that a description of the present state or the outcome does not contain.

- Two histories can end in the same state, one keeping a cap on working
  hours throughout and the other breaching it and then compensating (A27
  §1). A norm about the breach is not a function of the end state. This
  is [CLAIM-350](CLAIM-350.md)'s factorization condition applied to histories: J_N
  factors through a description only if the description separates the
  histories J_N separates.
- A reform can keep one capacity of an organization by changing another
  of its structures. "The judgment favors persistence of one
  organizational capacity through transformation of another
  organizational structure" (A26). Judging it needs the relations it
  keeps and the ones it changes, not only the result.

So specifying the relevant relations and histories can be necessary for
applying such judgments adequately. That is the owner's insight, beside
[CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md)'s standpoint, standard, scale and context.

**Prescription is a further question.** Which continuations ought to
follow, 𝒜_N(γ), is a separate normative problem. It is not a
prerequisite for J_N(γ). This is also where the owner's U26 wording sat:
on "whether some organization should persist or change". A26 found that
wording regressive if read as needing a prior verdict. The narrowed claim
does not need one.

## What it does not say

- **That every normative judgment concerns organizations.** Judgments
  about experiences, such as that suffering is bad, may need no
  diachronic relatum. This is [CLAIM-512](CLAIM-512.md)'s narrow horn, which the
  narrowed claim no longer contests.
- **That persistence is privileged.** Which continuation is warranted is
  the standard's question. The whole's persistence cannot outweigh its
  constituents' claims by being the whole's ([CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md)).
- **That the organization judged is the one whose identity persists.**
  Obligations can survive an organization's dissolution, and an
  organization can persist while its constitutive commitments lapse (A27
  §6). Normative continuity is not organizational identity.
- **That specifying the history justifies the verdict.** Viability does not
  establish what ought to persist ([CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md)).
- **That a judgment no one recognizes is false.** "An injustice does not
  cease to be wrong because the institution responsible lacks the
  categories needed to recognize it" (A26).

## Note of 2026-10-31: two cases with one end state and opposite verdicts

Bencherki (2017; [LIT-1901](../literature.d/LIT-1901.md), read in [NOTE-1482](../notes.d/NOTE-1482.md)) gives a pair of
cases of the kind this claim needs.

- **The checklist** (pp. 789–790). An army officer presents a
  community-made checklist as a service "we offer". He also says it was
  made by the "community": "action is shared between the two". Bencherki
  classes this as positive co-individuation.
- **The daycare** (pp. 791–792). The same officer presents the daycare as
  "one of the services we offer". It runs only because spouses volunteer
  ("If I volunteered, my daughter could go to daycare for free"), and
  their work "goes unacknowledged, unrecorded, and unpaid". Bencherki
  classes this as usurpation.

The end state is the same in both: the army offers a service built on
the community's work. The verdicts are opposite, and the difference is
who did the work and whether that is acknowledged. "Usurpation" is a
judgment of the J_N(γ) kind. It is not a function of the end state, as
this claim says. It also prescribes nothing: Bencherki does not say what
the army should do.

Two limits. Part of the difference lies in the present attribution, the
officer's words, as well as in the history. And the cases are excerpts
from a documentary whose framing Bencherki follows (p. 785). The parent
paper ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), p. 1366) then does what this claim separates. It
makes responsibility the prescription of continuations, "the collective
duty to foster each being-in-becoming". That is 𝒜_N, offered as the
content of a judgment and not as a further question. [CLAIM-512](CLAIM-512.md) meets it.
