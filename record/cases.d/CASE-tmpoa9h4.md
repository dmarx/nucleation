# CASE-tmpoa9h4: Aphids and Buchnera: an obligate nutritional symbiosis in which neither partner reproduces alone, and the symbiont's genome reduced to a seventh of E. coli's

## The case

Almost every aphid species has 60 to 80 bacteriocytes holding *Buchnera*,
a close relative of *E. coli*, transmitted maternally to eggs and embryos.
The abstract of the genome paper (LIT-tmp98l8f) says the mutualism "is so
obligate that neither can reproduce independently". The 640,681-bp
chromosome is a seventh of *E. coli*'s. It keeps genes for the amino acids
the host cannot make and lacks those for the ones it can, as well as genes
for cell-surface components, regulators and defence. The authors read
*Buchnera* as viable only in the bacteriocyte.

Its genes evolve faster than free-living relatives', with unusually many
replacement substitutions and A+T-rich changes. Moran (LIT-1573) explains
this by Muller's ratchet in small asexual populations, with mutational bias,
so much of the reduction is not selected.

In the record, NOTE-114 reports Godfrey-Smith's placement of aphid–*Buchnera*
as both an organism and a Darwinian individual. The entry it reads, Wilson
and Barker, misplaces *Buchnera* in a human gut.

## What it can show

- **Dependence through degradation.** The symbiont's dependence was
  produced largely by drift, by the abstracts' account. That separates the
  route to dependence from any selection for the collective (QUESTION-277).
- **Constituent autonomy lost while the whole thrives.** *Buchnera* cannot
  live outside the host. The partnership is one of the most successful in
  insects. It illustrates CLAIM-060: the whole's success does not preserve a
  constituent's viability on its own.
- **High cohesion by transmission.** Vertical transmission couples the
  partners' reproduction. The abstracts report the two genomes as
  complementary, and report no gene transfer to the host. Whether any
  occurred is for a reading to check. Compare the mealybug (CASE-tmpir76a)
  and *Paulinella* (CASE-tmpowpq1).

The case is documented in works the record has not read. What it shows
rests on their abstracts until they are read.
