# THEORY-090: A basic, affective form of consciousness is realised subcortically, in the upper brainstem and thalamus, before and without cortex, and its first function is alarm: survival behaviour, care for the body and generalised one-shot learning, with no choice between options

## Source

- Newen & Montemayor (2025), [LIT-626](../literature.d/LIT-626.md), read in [NOTE-489](../notes.d/NOTE-489.md):
  "Two framework observations" (a), "The various functions of phenomenal
  consciousness", Table 1 and the Conclusion.
- Damasio & Carvalho (2013), [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md), read in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md): "The neural
  substrates of feeling" and its claims C4–C5, for the subcortical clause
  only.

## The claim, in two clauses

**Basic arousal is realised without cortex.** Newen and Montemayor, in
[LIT-626](../literature.d/LIT-626.md), set "as a criterion of adequacy that basic arousal can be
realized without the necessary involvement of cortical areas". Basic
arousal is "a sensitivity for pain, pleasure and other basic affective
states without focused attention", which they identify with Denton's
primordial emotions. Their evidence is three cited findings. Merker's
hydranencephalic children, born without cortex, "most plausibly have very
basic affective experiences of comfort and distress". Cats with the cortex
removed in infancy still orient by vision and touch and solve a visual
discrimination in a T-maze. In Redinbaugh et al. (2020), stimulation of the
central lateral thalamus woke anaesthetised macaques, which fell back when
it stopped; while stimulated they "reacted to pain stimuli and perceptual
stimuli" but could not do tasks that need focused attention. The neural
correlates of basic arousal, they conclude, can lie in the upper brainstem
and thalamus, "but this seems to be only the case for basic arousal".
They reject Merker's stronger claim, that no consciousness depends on
cortex.

Damasio and Carvalho, in [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md), reach the same place from the anatomy of
feeling, without the word consciousness doing the work. The upper
brainstem (parabrachial nucleus, periaqueductal grey, deep superior
colliculus, with the nucleus of the solitary tract) is where interoceptive
signals first form a whole-body map, and they place the generation of
feeling there (C5 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)). Damage to the posterior half of the upper
brainstem gives coma or a vegetative state, in which "feelings and
sentience are abolished"; damage to the ventral half gives locked-in
syndrome, with feeling spared. Their patient B, whose insulae were both
destroyed, still felt pain, pleasure, hunger and compassion, so the insula
is not needed (C4). They also cite decorticate children. Two independent
lines, one from a theory of consciousness and one from a theory of
feeling, put a felt, affective state below the cortex.

**Its first function is alarm, with no choice.** The function clause is
ALARM's alone. In [LIT-626](../literature.d/LIT-626.md), slow homeostatic updating cannot secure
survival under a sudden, extreme challenge, so the incoming signal is
given "100% weight", triggering "an immediate survival reaction". A reflex
could do that much. What felt pain adds, they argue, is three things: the
animal keeps away from the hot stove afterwards, it rests and protects the
injury while the pain lasts, and it learns to avoid anything that causes
pain "even for the first time … without any multi-case associative
learning". Those are basic arousal's "three specific functional roles":
"(i) triggering a survival behaviour, (ii) caring for the body, and (iii)
initiating a generalized learning". The first "conscious manager" relies
"on rather rigid behavioural programmes without choices", and choice
"emerge[s]" only with general alertness, which is cortical: "the kind of
cost avoidance involved in basic arousal does not involve a choice".
Later functions work only while the older one is not overriding them.

## How this bears on the record's accounts

**It extends [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md).** [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md) holds that felt affect is the
experience of the body's regulatory state, valenced by what that state
means for staying alive, and it leaves open whether what is felt is a map
of the body or a prediction of it. This account builds on that base and
widens it in two directions [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md) explicitly declines: it says that
such felt affect is a form of consciousness, and the first one, and it
says what it was for. ALARM's alarm is [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md)'s regulatory state in
the case where a variable leaves its tolerance range. The subcortical
clause also sides with Damasio and Carvalho ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)) on the location half
of [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md)'s open question, the upper brainstem rather than Barrett's
agranular limbic cortex. It does not settle that question: ALARM's one
sentence against prediction, that pain alarms "in ways that mere
predictions … cannot capture", is asserted, not argued, and the evidence
here bears on where feeling is generated, not on whether it is a map or a
forecast.

**It rivals [THEORY-101](THEORY-101.md).** Veit's pathological complexity thesis, in
[LIT-105](../literature.d/LIT-105.md), holds that consciousness began as hedonic valence serving as a
common currency for trade-offs in action selection, so that motivational
trade-offs are his criterion for consciousness. ALARM's first
consciousness is the opposite: a signal whose job is to remove the
trade-off by taking all the weight. Both accounts are affect-first and
adaptationist, and both place the oldest consciousness in subcortical
evaluative structures (Veit reads the bilaterally integrated
SN–SC–PAG "selection triangle" as "strong evidence here that the
evaluative side of experience came first"). So they agree on the
subcortical clause and exclude each other on the function clause. A first
consciousness whose job is to arbitrate between options and one whose job
is to override them cannot both be the original function. They also split
on learning: ALARM ties basic arousal to a new, generalised kind of
learning close to unlimited associative learning, which Veit holds
explains later features of consciousness, not its origin.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say that more complex consciousness needs cortex.** ALARM
  claims that general alertness needs cortical processing, but this
  account leaves that clause out. Maldarelli and Güntürkün's review
  ([LIT-650](../literature.d/LIT-650.md)), filed as [THEORY-100](THEORY-100.md), reports report-tracking
  neurons in the crow's nuclear, non-laminar pallium, which is evidence
  against reading "cortex" there as laminar cortex. That review also reads
  global workspace theory as a connectome property a bird can meet, where
  ALARM reads it as prefrontal.
- **It does not say that the subcortical state is felt because it is
  subcortical.** The evidence is that some state survives without cortex
  and that behaviour suggests affect. Whether a decorticate cat's
  orienting, or a stimulated macaque's response to pain, is felt is the
  question at issue, and both sources fall back on behaviour "consistent
  with" feeling ([NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md), Assumptions; [NOTE-489](../notes.d/NOTE-489.md), Limitations).
- **The function clause is argued, not tested.** No comparative or
  phylogenetic data are offered for it (C2 in [NOTE-489](../notes.d/NOTE-489.md), weak to
  moderate). Its argument from felt pain's advantages presupposes that
  the pain is felt.
- **[LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md) does not support the function clause.** Damasio and Carvalho
  hold that felt experience was added to body mapping because it makes
  regulation more flexible, forcing attention and supporting learning and
  prediction (C3 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md), a hypothesis). That agrees with ALARM on
  learning and sits uneasily with its "without choices". Damasio &
  Damasio ([LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md), unread) state the affect-first origin more directly.
- **It takes no side on IIT, global workspace or higher-order theory as
  theories of general alertness.** ALARM's complaint is that each makes
  neocortex essential and so omits basic arousal; it says it stays
  "neutral" among them for the rest.
- **It is not a claim about phenomenal character.** Like [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md), it
  says where a felt affective state is realised and what it does, not why
  it is felt at all. ALARM sets epiphenomenalism aside without argument.
