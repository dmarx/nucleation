## 7. Departures from A51 (and A49)

- **Order.** A51 leads with CASE-325, and so does the article's argument (§§4.3–4.4), after the vocabulary (§4.2), which the comparison needs.
- **CASE-325's strength.** A51 calls the study "considerably better evidence than a demonstration that a relational model can reproduce ordinary behavior". That is accurate about the design: it has a comparison, held-out interventions and worlds in which it can fail. It overstates what the record has done with the result: the run now has a second session's check, but CLAIM-889 is Proposed.
- **The interacting world.** A51 leaves it out. It shows that the study's headline measure, evidence on the training arrangements, cannot tell a right composition from a wrong one; only the held-out combinations do (§4.3).
- **Recursive grants.** A51 names SYNTHESIS-087 as "especially relevant". A51's phrasing, "forbidding direct self-grants is insufficient to prevent some forms of authority amplification", is CLAIM-282's first clause, correctly scoped.
- **Directed tolerances.** A49 made a directed tolerance, Q_{i→j} = 1[d_i ≤ τ_{i→j}], its formal centre. The record holds directedness (CLAIM-552), derives a tolerance from a threshold (CLAIM-550), and gives compatibility (CLAIM-538, CLAIM-537), but with symmetric bounds on an edge (§4.6).
- **The affective sketch.** A51's has four elements: "frustration, appraisal, action readiness and the difference between a concern's claimed precedence and its practical efficacy". Three are Active (CLAIM-368 and CLAIM-347; TERM-094; CLAIM-283 with CLAIM-548) and one Proposed (appraisal, CLAIM-440). A51's "The record's work on potentials", which "supplies a way to distinguish circumstances in which a unified evaluative representation is available from circumstances in which one cannot simply be presumed", is §4.8.4.
- **Corporate remorse.** A51 §3 corrects its own earlier suggestion: "SYNTHESIS-018 and CLAIM-432 already distinguish harm that becomes an organizational concern from harm that becomes consequential only as an external threat." A51 assigns the argument to "the center of the institutional-sociopathy article", its Article 12. The article states the argument's firmness, which A51 does not. A51's "Actual remaining work" names "A philosophical argument concerning functional affect … particularly the objection concerning felt valence recorded in SYNTHESIS-018"; that objection is THEORY-101, and the answer is open work, not a reported result (§4.9).
- **Accommodation.** A49 Article 4's abstract says "Implicit contracts and learned tolerances are shown to provide a potential means of amortizing recurrent regulatory negotiation". That overstates CLAIM-358: it is argued, not shown. A51's abstract says the claim correctly ("can reduce"). A51's abstract: "low conflict and fluent performance do not establish the adequacy of accommodation" is §4.10.4 (JR4.1).
- **Flow.** A51's final section is "learned accommodation, organizational flow, competing demands and nonintegrable evaluations" (§§4.10–4.12).
- **The closing claim.** A51 ends: "This paper establishes why an organizational individual may require an architecture of differentiated authority rather than a single scalar objective." The record supports less (§4.12; J4.2), and the article says so in §4.12 and §4.14.1.
