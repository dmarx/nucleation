# NOTE-054: Brain states across spacetime via whole-brain models

## Contribution

Adaptive behaviour depends on whole-brain activity patterns that balance stability (order) with enough instability for flexibility, and this balance over space and time defines distinct brain states. Depression shows overly rigid, ordered dynamics, while psychedelics produce more disordered and sometimes over-flexible states. The review surveys neuroimaging and whole-brain modelling work that uses dynamical-systems principles to characterize these states. It suggests that the resulting spatiotemporal descriptions may eventually inform treatments that rebalance brain states in disease.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Neuroimaging (§2, Fig. 2): the review moves from static to dynamic functional connectivity, where substates are described by fractional occupancy, dwell time and transition probabilities. Connectome harmonics (graph-Laplacian eigenmodes of the structural connectome) serve as building blocks of activity.
- Whole-brain models (§3, Fig. 3): regions are coupled nonlinear units (mean-field, Kuramoto, Hopf) wired by the structural connectome. The best fit to data comes from small-world topology, weak coupling, and local dynamics poised at the edge of instability, which is what produces metastability.
- Theory (§5): the entropic brain hypothesis and REBUS (psychedelics relax high-level priors, in a predictive-processing framing).
- Evidence (§6): psychedelics broaden the repertoire of harmonic and connectivity states, raise LZ complexity, and decrease within-network and increase between-network connectivity. Depression shows reduced visits to a frontoparietal/default-mode/salience state and more global synchrony and temporal stability.
- Outlook (§6c–7): an "activity landscape" picture (depression = deep attractors, psychedelics = a flattened landscape). Causal whole-brain models with receptor maps, such as 5-HT2A, are proposed to explore transitions between states.

## Open questions

- A compact review of the Deco/Kringelbach whole-brain modelling programme. Relevant to neuroscience and complex-systems notes on criticality and metastability.
- The evidence it reviews is largely small-sample neuroimaging. A deeper reading should look at the primary studies behind the depression and psychedelic claims before treating the landscape picture as established.
