**What the reading found.** [MOM-CLM-088](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-088.md)'s point is that "tall" for people
and for trees cannot fail to glue, because the contexts do not overlap.
The record already had the general point, and none of its own gluing
claims made the mistake: [CLAIM-143](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-143.md), [CLAIM-131](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-131.md), [CLAIM-144](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-144.md) and [CASE-039](../../record/cases.d/CASE-039.md) all
separate disagreement from obstruction. So the value was not a
correction. It was a consequence for the will-organization line.

**The consequence.** Ainslie's interests take turns over time. If their
windows do not overlap, they glue, and a reversal of preference is not a
division of the self. Division needs a choice both interests bear on at
once, and on Ainslie's account the overlap is made by recursive
self-prediction. That gives [CLAIM-163](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)'s list of missing parts its first
item, a coupling, together with where it acts. The order parameter and
the transitions are still missing, so the objection stays open, answered
in part. Memory plays the same part for the film joke in [CASE-048](../../record/cases.d/CASE-048.md).

**What to watch.** Both new claims leave the cover unstated: which
contexts of choice count, and how finely. That is the piece [MOM-CLM-088](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/claims.d/CLM-088.md) says
decides everything, and it is open here as in MOM. A claim that says a
self "fails to glue" should be asked for its overlap first.
