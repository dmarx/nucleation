# CASE-tmpfh2in: The presidential two-term tradition

## The case

A36 §4: "George Washington established a precedent by leaving office
after two terms. The tradition became a durable expectation, but it was
not originally a constitutional requirement. Franklin Roosevelt
subsequently won four presidential elections, and the Twenty-Second
Amendment, ratified in 1951, converted the two-term tradition into an
explicit constitutional limitation on election to the presidency."

These facts agree with the standard history as the assistant knows it,
with one qualification: Washington's retirement in 1797 set the example,
but historians commonly credit Jefferson's refusal of a third term (1807)
with making two terms a principle rather than one man's choice. A36's
"limitation on election" is exact: the Amendment limits how often a
person may be elected, not how long one may serve.

## The reading (A's)

A36 sets four stages: "Perspectival invitation" (1796–1797), "Norm
stabilization", "Exposure of under-specification" (1940 and 1944,
elections "which the Constitution then permitted") and "Formal
specification" (1951). "The informal relationship was comparatively
inexpensive to maintain while the relevant cultural expectations
remained effective. Its breakdown revealed that the formal specification
did not itself guarantee the expected behavior. A subsequent
constitutional change relocated the constraint from cultural maintenance
into legal architecture." And its limit: "This does not establish that
formalization is always desirable. It establishes that different
mechanisms of norm maintenance possess different counterfactual
reliability profiles."

## Bearing

It illustrates [CLAIM-tmpzas6q](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpzas6q.md) on one point only: a restraint that law did
not guarantee was relied on and then broken lawfully. It does not show
the other point, that institutions had given up alternatives to the
restraint; it shows the reverse response, formalization. It is the
record's ladder of [DP-003](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-3), walked by a polity: a norm kept as convention,
walked up to guarantee after it failed. [LEAD-tmps9jkd](../leads.d/LEAD-tmps9jkd.md) lists it among six
cases for comparative study.
