# THEORY-1068: In the founding statement of relational quantum mechanics the states and values of systems are relative to other systems, but the systems are not: the world is assumed decomposed, possibly in several ways, into systems each characterized by the questions that can be asked of it and a finite information capacity, so the relata precede the relations, as an assumption and without an ontology of events

## Source

Rovelli (1996), [LIT-1635](../literature.d/LIT-1635.md), arXiv quant-ph/9609002v2; read in
[NOTE-1227](../notes.d/NOTE-1227.md). Page numbers are the preprint's.

## What was actually shown

**What is relational.** "The quantum state of a system is always a state of
that system with respect to a certain other system" and "In quantum
mechanics all physical variables are relational, as is velocity" (§II.C, p.
6). "There is neither an absolute state of the system, nor absolute
properties that the system has at a certain time" (p. 7).

**What is not.** The reconstruction begins: "Assume that the world can be
decomposed (possibly in a variety of ways) in a collection of systems, each
of which can be equivalently considered as an observing system or as an
observed system" (§III.A, p. 10). A system "is characterized by the family
of yes/no questions that can be asked to it", W(S), whose structure gives
"the general kinematical features of S" (pp. 10–11); by Postulate 1 "each
system is characterized by a number N", its information capacity (p. 11);
and W(S) "includes all Heisenberg observables at all times", so it fixes
the Hamiltonian (§III.D, p. 15). The summary repeats it: "A physical system
is characterized by the structure on the set W(S) of questions that can be
asked to the system" (§IV.A, p. 16). None of these is relative to an
observer. A state exists "only if some system O … has interacted with S"
(p. 6): the interaction is between systems already there.

**What is absent.** The 1996 text has no ontology of events or facts. Its
primitives are systems, questions and information (correlation); "the state
of the system" is replaced by "the information that a system has about
another system" (p. 11). The "sparse ontology of (relational) quantum
events" that Faye and Jaksland quote ([LIT-1672](../literature.d/LIT-1672.md), §5) is from later
writing.

The reading could have come out otherwise: the paper could have made a
system's identity relative too, or taken interactions as primitive with
systems derived from them. It does neither.

## What this does not say

- That the priority is argued. It is an assumption ("Assume"), made for the
  reconstruction, and the paper declines to discuss it philosophically
  (footnote ‡‡, p. 19).
- That the systems are uniquely given. The decomposition is "possibly in a
  variety of ways", and how different decompositions relate is not
  discussed.
- That a system is individuated. W(S) and N fix what kind of system it is
  (a lattice of subspaces of a k-dimensional Hilbert space). Nothing in the
  paper says what makes two systems of one kind two, which is [CLAIM-136](../claims.d/CLAIM-136.md)'s
  question, not answered here.
- That RQM in every later form keeps this structure. That is for the later
  texts ([LIT-177](../literature.d/LIT-177.md) and those Faye and Jaksland quote).
- That the questions constitute the system. "Characterized" is Rovelli's
  word; [CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md)'s stronger "partly fixed by the constraints" is not
  his.
