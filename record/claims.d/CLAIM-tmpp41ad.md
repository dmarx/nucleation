# CLAIM-tmpp41ad: Read as description, the manuscript's thesis is close to true by definition; read as a criterion, it is a schema until the decision family Q is fixed, and its descriptive defeat condition can test neither reading

## The objection

[CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) says what survives translation is "the set of communicative
distinctions and relations a receiving system can still reconstruct and act
upon, not the wording or the proposition". There are two ways to read it.

**As description.** "What survives" is what in fact gets through to a
receiver. Then the positive clause is close to true by definition: a
distinction that no receiving system can recover has not survived for
anyone, and one that a receiver can recover and use has survived for that
receiver. What has content is the negative clause, "not the wording or the
proposition", and that is a separate claim, [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md)'s, whose "necessary"
half [CLAIM-053](CLAIM-053.md) already finds unargued in the manuscript.

**As a criterion.** The thesis says which distinctions a translation ought to
keep. Then it depends on which decisions the receiver is to make. [CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md):
"the manuscript restricts this to a family Q of tasks — tell teasing from
reproach, recognize a position, choose a reply". [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) leaves Q open. Its
summary: "the choice of observables and decision tasks is stated to be
normative and contestable (§11)". Its "What it does not say": "It does not say
the observables are fixed in advance". Until Q is fixed the thesis is a
schema, a form that yields a claim for each choice of Q.

**The defeat condition fits neither reading.** It fixes observables, "(stance,
footing, alignment, expected uptake)", which the thesis declines to fix. And
it is descriptive: it asks whether "Human judgements of communicative
fidelity" are predicted by one measure better than another. What it tests is
a narrower empirical claim, that these four observables predict fidelity
judgements better than propositional or embedding similarity. That claim can
be true while the thesis, read as description, adds nothing to it, and false
while the thesis, read as a criterion, is right about what translations ought
to keep.

A descriptive test cannot settle a normative criterion. [CLAIM-085](CLAIM-085.md) makes the
point for language-model judges: "what a translation ought to preserve needs
criteria the model cannot supply". Human judgements of fidelity are also a
description of interpreters, so the point carries to them.

[CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) `uses` no TERM. Its "receiving system" and "act upon" are bound to
none of the record's senses of fidelity, such as [TERM-034](../terms.d/TERM-034.md)'s, which is
"relative to an interpreter". So nothing in the record fixes which receiver
the thesis means.

One consequence bears on [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md), which says the October thesis ([CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md))
"replaces what a receiver can reconstruct with what the analyst's probe system
can identify". On the criterion reading, [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s receiver was already an
idealized interpreter facing the manuscript's Q, not a named audience. The
contrast [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md) draws is then one of degree.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the thesis is empty. A near-analytic positive clause can
  still organize an account, and the negative clause is a substantive claim.
- It does not say [CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md) is wrong. The October thesis does drop the
  receiver from its wording; this says the receiver in [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) was already
  the analyst's.

## What would answer it

Either of these:

- fix Q, or the receiver population and its tasks, in the thesis, so that it
  becomes a substantive empirical claim the present defeat condition fits;
- restate the thesis as a normative proposal, with a normative warrant (an
  argument about what a translation owes its readers) and a defeat condition
  of the matching kind, such as a case in which keeping the named distinctions
  is plainly the wrong thing for a translation to do.
