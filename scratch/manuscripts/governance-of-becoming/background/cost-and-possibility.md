## 3. Claims, by Article 3's outline

Each entry gives status, warrant and role, then premises, then a quotation.

### 3a. §1–2: organization as stored work; the general cost model

- **CLAIM-761** (Active; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-303, CLAIM-475, CLAIM-582; member, Art. 3 abstract and §1; also in governance-of-possibility and cost-structure-of-possibility). **The owner's thesis, in A's wording** (U17 of "Topological protection explained"). "A stabilized constraint can be stored work: by replacing later work with cheaper operations it changes which activities are realizable within a fixed per-task budget, so amortization becomes capability expansion without any added degrees of freedom or resources; the expansion is conditional on the costs of constructing, keeping, retrieving, verifying and revising the constraint …, and the persistence that buys it can entrench." Its worked step: a task costing 12 fresh and 3 with a framework, at a per-task budget of 5, "This is the point where amortization becomes capability expansion". Its guards: CLAIM-582 (a cost-lowering constraint "has changed the mechanism") and CLAIM-443 ("Persistence is not amortization"). The stronger reading it "invites and does not support" is that "every emergent organization is such an investment".
- **CLAIM-303** (Active; interpretive; thesis; grounds THEORY-386, THEORY-389; **not a member**: in cost-structure-of-possibility only, though A49 anchors it). A's, at the owner's request. "Filing an argument moves work across time: it is paid for now … against reconstruction later, so it is worth its cost only when expected reuse covers it". Its note of 2026-11-24 flags A34 §7's "directly captured by … CLAIM-303" as a miscitation: "this claim is about filing an argument in the record, not about automation".
- **CLAIM-475** (Active; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-470; member). The owner's. "a stabilized specification is past inquiry stored so that later questions become cheaper to ask, and it can make other questions harder".
- **CLAIM-582** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; not a member). Under a fixed dynamical model an added hard constraint "cannot enlarge what some admissible path reaches or lower the least cost of reaching it".
- **CLAIM-443** (Active; interpretive; not a member). "persistence is not amortization, which is shown only when earlier work replaces later computation, communication or recruitment".
- **CLAIM-569** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-571; member, Art. 3 abstract and §2). A's proposal; the construction is the filer's. "A form has at least three resource requirements, to construct it, to maintain it, and to move into or out of it, and none fixes another: a capability can be cheap to keep and costly to acquire". Its numbers: "24 to build and 2 a year to keep, against one costing 6 and 4, under a surplus of 3 a year". Note of 2026-11-19: "the same investment can lower the first while raising the second" (A43: "inexpensive to operate while expensive to revise"). Note of 2026-11-24, on the owner's U21: "a contract read as a stabilized relational constraint has a construction (formation), a maintenance (persistence) and a transition (modification and exit) requirement"; "F(C) ≠ R(C → C′), and with TERM-116 the revision cost is directed. The exchange supports and applies the claim; it adds nothing to it."
- **CLAIM-891** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-303, 761, 760, 307; grounds CASE-326, CASE-094, CASE-088, THEORY-351; not a member). The assistant's, LEAD-108's result. "One cost model prices a crew's speed bugs, a filed argument and a LEGO bin with the same terms, once work is priced by the moment it is done and checked against the budget for each demand, not only summed". The four terms QUESTION-095's note listed "alone do not: the cockpit is carried by the price of work at the moment it is done". Defeated by a case "whose verdict on feasibility, on lifetime cost or on who pays turns on a quantity that none of the model's terms carries".
- **CLAIM-892** (Proposed; demonstrated; thesis; grounds CASE-326; not a member). The assistant's. Erosion costs, per demand, "εc[(1 − π) + π(σδ + φ)]": "it weighs little where demand is predictable … as with a cockpit's speed bugs, and most where reuse cannot be forecast or checking re-derives, as with a filed argument".
- **CLAIM-755** (Proposed; demonstrated; not a member) and **CLAIM-756** (Proposed; demonstrated; rests on CLAIM-303). Both A's. Economic and dynamical separation of time scales "come apart both ways". "An acquisition can be worth making for what it does to the cost of other acquisitions".

### 3b. §3: contracts as stateful stored work

- **CLAIM-tmpsf9hp** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-761, CLAIM-569, CLAIM-1156; member). A's (A30, A32), at the owner's U21 and U22; "relational invocation" is the owner's term per the curation. "Invoking a relationship differs from reusing a stored result in that the invocation can change the relationship it invokes … a stored-work cost model that holds the store fixed between uses prices a contract's lifecycle but not its invocation, which returns an act, a distribution of costs over the parties and the relationship's next state." A30: "A cached result normally substitutes for a computation without intentionally changing the underlying problem. Contracts can change the problem." The formal objects are Invoke(q, C_t) → (a_t, C_{t+1}) and K_C(q) = Law(a, τ, κ, C′, S′ | do(q), C, S), with reproductive, formative and transformative invocation. Admitted by a discriminating prediction: matched relationships "part in their later costs, response and exit profiles when one's invocations change it and the other's do not". A30's own hedge: "I suspect the latter [richer state variables] will cover most of the cases."
- **CLAIM-tmp80ny9** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; member). A's, at the owner's U21. There are three kinds of ambiguous invocation, "epistemic ambiguity … resolved by information; relational disagreement … resolved by negotiation or arbitration; and constitutive indeterminacy … resolved only by forming one".
- **CLAIM-1156** (Proposed; interpretive; member). A's. "a response kernel that includes the organization's state after answering separates what an answer benchmark treats as equivalent".

- **CLAIM-tmpro73c** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-tmp80ny9; member). A's (A30 §10). "who resolves the ambiguity, when, and at whose cost can matter more than how ambiguous it is".

### 3d. §5: timescale separation and readiness

- **CLAIM-921** (Proposed; demonstrated; thesis; grounds CASE-343; member, Art. 3 §5 and the Art. 10 table). The assistant's, found in running LEAD-036. "a budget that follows observed use has a state in which the capability is never used or funded again whenever the base budget alone sustains competence below that level … at the same expected funding a rarer capability is lost sooner, while maintenance funded apart from use … has no such state". In the study the coupled rule lost the capability "within thirty years in every run at one occasion in two and a half years and in 39 % at four a year, and the decoupled rule never did".
- **CLAIM-1060** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-475, CLAIM-761; member). A's (A52 §5). "Organizational governance is partly the intertemporal allocation of scarce resources among inquiry, response and the upkeep of the structures that make both possible, slow organizational modes arise partly as ways of amortizing inquiry and coordination costs". The step to slow modes is ARG-238 (Proposed), "which CLAIM-755 narrows to the economic sense of slow".
- **CLAIM-358** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-261; member, Art. 4). The owner's (U45, 2026-10-17); A49 Art. 3 anchors it. "Learned local accommodation can reduce the amortized cost of recurrent arbitration".
- Held beside these and not members: CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-679 (Active; demonstrated). A budget set to last period's peak "never rises", and cutting toward the holding cost "removes exactly the transitions whose threshold" lies between.

### 3e. §6: reachability and transition asymmetry

- **CLAIM-571** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; grounds CASE-233; member, Art. 3 abstract and §6). A's. "the resource threshold for reaching a target configuration need not equal the resource requirement of the target: it is the least, over admissible transition paths, of the peak requirement along the path, and it is infinite when no admissible path joins the two". It is checked on r(q) = 0.7 + 2.4q(1 − q) (endpoints 0.7, threshold 1.3). Note of 2026-11-17: under a normative path set Γ_N ⊆ Γ, "b*_N ≥ b*". Note of 2026-11-24, A30 §8: "The new contract would be cheaper to maintain, but the organization cannot afford the transition. This produces contractual lock-in without requiring any preference for the existing arrangement"; "An application that supports the claim's reading; no change."
- Not members: **CLAIM-567** (Active; demonstrated), "feasible … reachable … reliably reaches it are three properties", and **CLAIM-591** (Active; demonstrated), "the least budget for going from one configuration to another can differ from that for coming back". CLAIM-591 is the record's statement of transition asymmetry.

## 4. Cases

- **CASE-326** (Proposed; 2026-11-22; LEAD-108's run, the assistant's; code in scratch/studies/lead-108/). **What was run.** One model, two routes (compute each time, or store, retrieve, fall back), with terms fixed before pricing: F, λ_F, U, π, ρ, miss, v, δ, σ, L, R, ε, spill, c, λ, B and party. A served demand costs "ρ + v + σ[δ(c(1+ε) + R + σ_A L_A) + (1 − δ)L]". Three verdicts are reported apart: feasibility, lifetime, distribution. The model has six tests: "it reduces to CLAIM-303 and reproduces CASE-088's 45 against 34". **What was found** (values mostly illustrative). The cockpit's bugs "lose at 1 and 1.5 and win from 2" in the load ratio. Filing staleness priced "turns a saving of 11 into a loss of 1 at three uses". A LEGO bin for one copy among 200 "pays at its first use", while for a brick that stands out "a bin never pays". On the owner's alternatives, counting the owner's review at four times the session's price moves break-even from 1.7 to 3.3. Three results do not depend on the values: F < 4(c − ρ − v) for the flat cockpit; erosion weighted by "(1 − π) + πσδ"; and a construction at a quiet moment and one at the moment of demand being "the same term, λ_F F". **What it cannot show**: "Any measured cost"; "That the terms are complete"; "Anything about the record's own filings". Its choices: "ε is a fixed fraction, not a function of how long the store has been relied on". Note of 2026-11-24 (A30): the contract reading "A qualification of scope, not of the case: for a store whose use changes it, σ and ε are not fixed."
- **CASE-343** (Proposed). The maintenance-funding loop run coupled and decoupled; it grounds CLAIM-921.

## 7. The digester's own reading (not the record's)

**What Article 3 can rest on (Active, demonstrated).** The reachability spine is CLAIM-571 (threshold as least peak, possibly infinite), CLAIM-569 (construct, maintain and move are three requirements), CLAIM-591 (going and coming back differ), CLAIM-567 (feasible, reachable, reliably reached) and CLAIM-582 (an added constraint enlarges nothing). Together they carry the abstract's "the affordability of an organizational state does not establish the affordability of reaching it" and §6. CLAIM-761 and CLAIM-760 are Active but interpretive. They carry §1 and §4 ("local efficiency need not establish systemic savings"). TERM-114 is the Active frame; TERM-116 is Proposed.

**What is only Proposed.** The cost model itself (CASE-326, CLAIM-891, CLAIM-892) is Proposed, illustrative, and not in the work. A49's "Extend the existing … machinery" is "standing told as settled" (curation). All of §3 (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, ARG-tmpa5ped, CLAIM-tmp80ny9, CLAIM-tmpro73c) is Proposed. So are §7's predicates (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6, CLAIM-tmp2jhc1, CLAIM-1016) and §5's CLAIM-921 (demonstrated within its model) and CLAIM-1060. CLAIM-731 and CLAIM-1117 on who pays are Proposed.

**What is missing.**
- R_i(S; B, H, W), the article's formal centre, is defined nowhere. It is LEAD-tmpeo8wq, idea stage. The four predicates sit in three places and no one object holds them: Θ is a note on TERM-114, not in its definition; Perm_N is in CLAIM-tmpdu9v6; affordability is TERM-114's ⪯ b.
- No entry yet prices invocation that changes the store; LEAD-tmp14n26 is unrun. The article's §3 is therefore a claim with a proposed test, not a result.
- No entry defines a directed revision cost R(C → C′). It is a phrase in CLAIM-569's note.
- The comparison A49 asks for (transaction-cost economics, incomplete-contract theory, dynamic optimization, resource-bounded planning) is barely supplied. The record holds Grossman–Hart and Hart readings (LIT-2745, LIT-2776, THEORY-1979 Proposed) behind CLAIM-728 (Proposed, not in this work), and North's "Institutions" (LIT-489). A quick search found no Williamson or Coase reading.
- §4's "work … embodied in acquired capabilities" (the fourth category beside eliminated, transferred and deferred) has no entry. CLAIM-760 has three achievements, and "deferred" is folded into "transferred … to an earlier time".
- SYNTHESIS-106 predates CASE-326, CLAIM-891 and 892, and the contract entries. No account maps the cost model as it now stands.

**Tensions between entries.**
- CLAIM-569's note says the contract exchange "adds nothing to it". CLAIM-tmpsf9hp and ARG-tmpa5ped say the model misprices contracts whose use changes them. These are consistent (lifecycle against invocation), but the article should state which of the two it is claiming.
- CLAIM-tmpsf9hp's own source hedges toward the null: A30 suspects richer state "will cover most of the cases", and LEAD-tmp14n26's null is "richer state". If that holds, "relational invocation" is a reparametrization of CASE-326. It is listed first among A49's contributions, where the curation says it goes without credit to the owner's U21.
- CLAIM-tmp2jhc1's A24 §4 model already shows joint production rising while B's independent capacity falls. That is the shape LEAD-tmpeo8wq asks for, in values, not in reach. It is unpriced ("Maintaining fallback capacity is not priced"). The lead's construction would be the reachability version. The article should not present the shape as new.
- CLAIM-tmpdu9v6 says an obligation is a bar, not a cost. CLAIM-571's note says a normative path set can only raise the threshold (b*_N ≥ b*). These agree, but CLAIM-tmpdu9v6 itself says defeasible obligations "would need rules of priority", which neither supplies.
- Membership is out of step with the anchors. CLAIM-303, CLAIM-891, CLAIM-892, CLAIM-1016 and CASE-326 carry the article's model, and none is a member. CLAIM-1016 appears in the curation's Art. 3 row without `works`, and CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 is a member mapped to Art. 9 only.
