# CLAIM-tmpgetbr: Predictor and criterion in the central defeat tests are both human pragmatic judgements of the same renderings while the baseline is a machine score, so the theory's measures can win by shared method variance alone

## The objection

In the central defeat tests three measurements are compared.

- **The predictor** is the theory's measure. [CLAIM-005](CLAIM-005.md)'s is "the match
  between distributions of context-indexed communicative judgements".
  [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s is "measured preservation of pragmatic observables (stance,
  footing, alignment, expected uptake)", and in the designs these are
  measured by human judgement: [CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md)'s interpretive judgements are of
  "tone, stance, solidarity, and authority". [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s "relational
  profiles (pragmatic, interactional, intervention-response)" are judgements
  in part.
- **The criterion** is human judgements of communicative fidelity, or of
  shared kind, for the same renderings.
- **The baseline** is a machine score: propositional, embedding, topic or
  surface similarity.

Two human judgements of one rendering share sources of variance that an
embedding score shares with neither: the rater's construal of the passage,
scale use, a general impression that colours every rating (a rater who
finds a rendering wrong in one respect tends to rate it lower in all), and
the context and order of elicitation. So the predictor correlates with the criterion partly
by method alone, and the comparison with a machine baseline is not like for
like. The theory's measures can beat the baseline without carrying any
information the baseline lacks about what the renderings preserve.

The one guard the record names is the manuscript's. [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md): "The
manuscript preregisters the cover and splits annotators (§10)". Splitting
annotators removes the shared rater. It does not remove a shared construal
of the stimulus, or a response style common to raters drawn from one
population. No entry in claims, questions, cases or terms mentions method
variance.

It rests on [CLAIM-tmphn6za](CLAIM-tmphn6za.md): that objection says the baseline is one no rival
holds, and this one says the comparison with it is confounded as well.

## What it does not say

- It does not say judgement-based predictors are illegitimate. Pragmatic
  observables may have no other measure.
- It does not say the theory's advantage would vanish once method is
  controlled, only that the stated tests cannot show it survives.
- It does not reach a predictor measured by behaviour rather than by rating.

## What would answer it

Any of these:

- a design in which predictor and criterion are measured by different
  methods, such as a predictor from behavioural uptake ([CASE-040](../cases.d/CASE-040.md)'s "expected
  uptake (choices among continuations)") against a criterion from fidelity
  ratings, or the two from different raters on different tasks;
- a baseline of the same kind as the predictor, such as human ratings of
  propositional sameness of the same renderings, so that method variance is
  matched;
- a multitrait–multimethod analysis that estimates the method share and
  shows the theory's advantage exceeds it.
