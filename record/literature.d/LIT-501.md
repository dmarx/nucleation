# LIT-501: Five Rules for the Evolution of Cooperation

Martin A. Nowak (2006), *Science 314(5805): 1560–1563 (Review), 8 December 2006* — DOI-10.1126/science.1133755

## Key takeaways

- With the donation game (a cooperator pays c so another gets b), defectors win in a well-mixed population; five mechanisms change that, each with a threshold on b/c: r > c/b, w > c/b, q > c/b, b/c > k, b/c > 1 + n/m.
- Each mechanism can be written as a 2×2 payoff matrix between cooperators and defectors, so all five can be compared by the same criteria: evolutionary stability, risk dominance and advantageousness (fixation probability above 1/N under weak selection).
- Punishment "is not a mechanism for the evolution of cooperation"; every model of it rests on one of the five.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the evolutionary
sources of morality-as-cooperation. It is not in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md)'s lineage
paragraph. Curry 2016 ([LIT-503](LIT-503.md)) lists it in Table 3 among previous
reviews of cooperation that are "incomplete": it covers kin and exchange
but none of the conflict-resolution domains.

[NOTE-409](../notes.d/NOTE-409.md) is the reading of 2026-10-02, main text only, and it placed
the work: **Active**, as the compact map of mechanisms. For
morality-as-cooperation it grounds the kin domain (via Hamilton,
[LIT-497](LIT-497.md)) and the exchange domain in two forms, direct reciprocity
(after Trivers and Axelrod, [LIT-510](LIT-510.md), [LIT-498](LIT-498.md)) and indirect
reciprocity by reputation. Network reciprocity and group selection are
population-structure mechanisms for altruism and do not map cleanly onto
MAC's group domain, which is mutualism and coordination. Because every
mechanism is modelled with the donation game, coordination, contests,
division and possession are absent.

Its one sentence on morality, that indirect reciprocity "leads to the
evolution of morality (30) and social norms", cites Alexander's *The
Biology of Moral Systems* ([LIT-495](LIT-495.md)).

One tension with the record. This paper presents kin selection theory
"based on the Price equation" as general enough to compare all
mechanisms. Luque's analysis of the Price equation ([LIT-205](LIT-205.md)) answers
critics who later include Nowak & Highfield (2011). The disagreement is
between Nowak's later work and this paper, and is not adjudicated here.
