# CLAIM-tmp6vck6: Inalienability is a limit on normative powers, not on exercise: a person may restrict the exercise of a right or delegate limited authority over decisions, and no delegation, contract, collective decision or success in imposing an arrangement thereby confers authority to determine whether that person keeps the standing from which claims against the arrangement arise, because the holder lacks the power to confer it

## The claim

A85 §2 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), taking
up the owner's proposal at U37: "An inalienable right is not necessarily a
right whose exercise can never be restricted. Nor does inalienability
necessarily mean that every possible waiver, delegation, or voluntary
commitment is invalid."

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Exercise restriction** | A person agrees or is required not to exercise a particular capability under specified conditions |
| **Delegation of authority** | A person or community grants another agent limited power to make or enforce decisions |
| **Alienation of standing** | A person purportedly loses the underlying normative status from which legitimate claims against power arise |

The first two are ordinary; the third is what inalienability rules out.
The reason, after Locke's Second Treatise §§22–23 and 135 as A85 relays
it, is that "a person cannot convey to another an unlimited arbitrary
power that the person did not legitimately possess in the first place."

A85 §4 stated the limit as an axiom on all agents, ∀a,
¬Authorized(a, ExtinguishStanding(i, r)), and as a Hohfeldian immunity.
A87 §4 corrects this: "A Hohfeldian immunity means that **a specified
other party lacks a particular normative power**", while an unwaivable
right raises another question: "does B itself possess the authority to
surrender, transfer, or extinguish the right?" With P_N(r) the valid
operations on a right r under a standard N, Waive, Transfer and Extinguish
"need not all belong to" it. The claim is filed in that form: the limit
falls first on the holder's own power to confer, and the immunity against
others follows, since what the holder cannot give no recipient can have
been given.

A97 IX puts it most compactly: "**Inalienability is therefore a claim
about the limits of certain normative transformations, not simply a
demand for a permanently fixed outcome.**"

The contract consequence, A85 §4: "the right to contract cannot
automatically include the unrestricted right to contract oneself into
domination". A marks this "a proposed moral argument, not a claim that
every broad waiver or arbitration agreement is legally invalid".

## How it sits with the record

Nothing on the record mentions inalienability, Hohfeld's incidents or
waiver. The nearest entries:

- **[CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md)** (Active): a person gives a friend the password and is
  later refused it, against the same restriction imposed. It shows a
  delegation can be the person's own and hard to revoke. This claim
  marks where such self-binding stops: it can restrict exercise, and it
  cannot transfer the standing to object to the arrangement itself.
- **[QUESTION-454](../questions.d/QUESTION-454.md)** (Open): what makes authority over another's body
  legitimate when the other cannot decide. This claim limits what any
  answer can license.
- **[TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)** (Proposed): moral standing. "Standing" here is narrower:
  standing to make claims against an arrangement that governs one, which
  presupposes moral standing.
- **[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)** (Proposed): domination as an unanswerable capacity to set
  another's terms. An arrangement that purports to own the delegator's
  standing is that capacity made total by consent; this claim says the
  consent cannot make it valid.

[CASE-tmpgp2qo](../cases.d/CASE-tmpgp2qo.md) is A85 §11's test of the claim: two informed, voluntary
delegations, one limited and one purporting to be unlimited.

## What it does not say

- Not which rights are inalienable, nor how to justify them; that is
  [QUESTION-tmptinzk](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmptinzk.md). A85 §12: "We still need to justify the rights
  themselves, distinguish genuinely inalienable standing from rights that
  may legitimately be waived or restricted".
- Not that inalienable means absolute. A87 §4 separates inalienable,
  nonforfeitable and absolute: "a right can be inalienable yet permit a
  justified, temporary restriction in an emergency."
- Not a legal finding. Calabresi and Melamed's inalienability rules (1972)
  are the law-and-economics counterpart, which A does not cite; the
  record has not read them.
- Not that a collective may override it. A85 §8: "A majority's legitimate
  authority to reform government cannot by itself justify extinguishing
  the underlying rights of a minority" ([QUESTION-tmpgeqju](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpgeqju.md)).
