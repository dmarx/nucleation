## Summary

The paper characterizes the outlier ("spike") eigenvalues and eigenvectors
of a nonlinear spiked covariance model K = GᵀG, whose rows have covariance
Σ with finitely many bounded spikes and arbitrary nonlinear dependence
across coordinates, and shows a "Gaussian equivalence" with the linear
spiked model of Bai and Yao: an outlier exists iff z′(−1/λᵢ) > 0, it sits
at z(−1/λᵢ), and its eigenvector's squared overlap is φ(−1/λᵢ) (Theorem 6,
formal Theorem 12). Two applications: (i) in a deep fully-connected network
with random Gaussian weights at linear width, a spike in the input Gram
matrix propagates layer to layer through a recursion with a BBP-type test
at each layer; spikes that fail it are absorbed into the bulk, and the
number of spikes and their alignment with the input signal never increase
with depth (Theorem 3, Corollary 4): a "curse of depth" at random
initialization. (ii) After O(1) GD steps with η ≍ 1 on a two-layer network,
the first-layer weights are approximately W₀ plus a rank-one term; the
test-data CK then has a spike, above a threshold, whose eigenvector aligns
with the test labels (Theorem 5), proving Conjecture 4 of Ba and colleagues
(LIT-tmpnwvnx).

## Contribution

1. First-order limits and the BBP transition for spikes of nonlinear
   spiked covariance models, not only weak convergence of the bulk.
2. A deterministic equivalent of the resolvent for spectral arguments on
   the positive real line outside the support, and a "no outliers" theorem
   (Theorem 9), extending Chouard and Schröder and colleagues.
3. Spike propagation through depth at random weights (Theorem 3).
4. The test-data CK spike and its label alignment after a few GD steps
   (Theorem 5), verifying [BES+22, Conjecture 4].

## Key results (section, verbatim)

- **Motivation** (§1): spikes "are often the primary spectral features
  (signal) of interest, because they pertain to low-rank structure of the
  underlying learning problem (e.g., class labels or the direction of the
  target function)".
- **Per-layer phase transition** (§2.1, after (2.7)): "The condition
  zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) > 0 describes the 'phase transition' phenomenon for spike
  eigenvalues in this model, where spikes i ∈ Iℓ−1 with zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) > 0
  induce spike eigenvalues in the CK matrix Kℓ of the next layer, while
  spikes with zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) ≤ 0 are absorbed into the bulk spectrum of Kℓ."
- **Theorem 3(c)**: the squared overlap of layer ℓ's spike eigenvector with
  the input spike eigenvector tends to ∏ₖ φₖ(si,k−1), a product of factors.
- **Corollary 4** (GMM-type signal-plus-noise input X = Σθᵢaᵢbᵢᵀ + Z): Kℓ
  has a spike for θᵢ "if and only if θᵢ > γ0^{1/4} and i ∈ Iℓ".
- **Curse of depth** (§2.1, numerical illustration): "IL ⊆ · · · ⊆ I0 and
  φℓ(si,ℓ−1) ∈ (0, 1), so the number of spike eigenvalues of Kℓ induced by
  K0 and the alignment of the spike eigenvectors of Kℓ with the true class
  label vectors {bi} are both non-increasing in the network depth ... In
  other words, at random initialization, the input signal diminishes as the
  depth of the NN increases."
- **Remedies, empirical** (Fig. 2): larger width preserves alignment over
  more layers; "gradient descent training on the weight matrices also
  restores and even amplifies the informative signal in the CK matrix of
  each layer; specifically, after 50 steps of GD training (yellow curve),
  the alignment between the class labels and the leading eigenvector of Kℓ
  may increase through depth." This is simulation (µP, η = 0.1), not a
  theorem.
- **Activation conditions** (§2.1, on (2.5)): "if bσ = 0, then spiked
  eigenstructure does not propagate across the NN layers in our studied
  regime of bounded spike magnitudes"; E[σ″(ξ)] = 0 is assumed because
  otherwise "Kℓ may have spike eigenvalues even when the input K0 has no
  spiked structure" ([BP22]); "We assume E[σ″(ξ)] = 0 for clarity, to avoid
  characterizing also such uninformative spikes across layers."
- **Trained weights** (§2.2, from [BES+22]): W_trained ≈ W₀ + (ηbσ/n)Xᵀya⊤,
  a change "O(1) in operator norm and approximately rank-1".
  Proposition 35 (App. F): "The largest singular value smax(W) falls outside
  the limit of its empirical singular value distribution if and only if
  θ1 > γ0^{1/4}".
- **Theorem 5**: the test-data CK "has a spike eigenvalue if and only if
  θ1 > γ0^{1/4} and z′(−1/λ1) > 0", with label alignment (2.14) > 0.
  Fig. 3(b): "above a phase transition threshold, the alignment ⟨û, ỹ⟩²
  ... increases with the learning rate or number of GD steps; in addition,
  alignment also increases with the training set size n."
- **Gaussian equivalence** (§3): "an exact asymptotic equivalence between
  spiked spectral phenomena in a nonlinear spiked covariance model with
  those of a linearly defined (possibly Gaussian) model."

## Assumptions

- Proportional limit n/dℓ → γℓ; fixed depth L; i.i.d. N(0,1) weights
  independent of X (§2.1). Input τn-orthonormal with τn·n^{1/3} → 0, a
  fixed number r of spikes of bounded size outside the bulk support.
- σ twice differentiable, bounded σ′, σ″; Eσ(ξ) = 0, Eσ² = 1; bσ ≠ 0 and
  E σ″(ξ) = 0 (e.g. odd σ such as tanh).
- §2.2: isotropic Gaussian inputs, single-index labels σ*(β*ᵀx) + noise,
  second layer frozen, fixed T and η_t ≍ 1 (not the maximal-update
  η = Θ(d) of Dandi and colleagues), independent test data.
- General model: independent rows, concentration of quadratic forms.

## Concepts

Conjugate kernel (CK); nonlinear spiked covariance model; BBP phase
transition per layer (z′ > 0); spike absorbed into the bulk; spike
propagation and its product of overlap factors; "curse of depth" at random
initialization; Gaussian equivalence; deterministic equivalent and
generalized resolvent; "master equation" for spikes; uninformative spikes
from non-odd activations ([BP22]); pre-trained (test-data) CK.
"Bulk" is used by count throughout ("spectral bulk", "bulk spectrum").
