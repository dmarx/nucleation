# CASE-tmps7vk0: The imagined owner

## The case

A27 §10, Experiment C: "Build two computational systems with equivalent
distributed control and similar behavior. In one, a higher-level self-model
represents the actual division of labor. In the other, it represents the
system as governed by one centralized inner agent."

Held fixed: the distributed control and the behaviour it produces. Varied:
only the content of the self-model, accurate or centralized.

Measured, in A27's words: the systems' "judgments of authorship,
explanations of mistakes, capacity to identify which component caused an
outcome, and ability to revise governance after an error."

A29's Test C is the same design, applied to distress: "Give otherwise
similar systems accurate or overcentralized self-models. Test whether a
unified report of distress predicts real regulatory integration, merely
coordinated communication, or neither." Its second appearance is why it has
a code.

## What each outcome shows

- **The centralized model misattributes control, and revises governance
  worse after errors.** A27: "we would have a concrete model of **illusory
  authorship or subject-attribution**", "a specific representational error
  and the conditions that produce it". The error belongs to the system
  that holds the self-model, as [CLAIM-tmpk7n2q](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpk7n2q.md) says it must.
- **No difference on any measure.** The owner content is idle for these
  functions. The self-model narrates and does not govern, against A26's
  "self-representations may themselves be parts of the real causal
  organization they represent" and A27 §12's loop from self-attribution
  back to governance.
- **The centralized model does as well or better.** One owner is a useful
  compression and misdescribes nothing that these measures reach. That is
  A27's "Neither representation must be false", and Drayson's reading
  ([THEORY-646](../theory.d/THEORY-646.md)): the represented owner has a truthmaker. It counts against
  [CLAIM-tmpk7n2q](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpk7n2q.md) for this system.

## The main threat to inference

The two self-models differ in more than whether they present an owner. An
accurate model of the division of labour carries more information about
the system than a centralized one. Worse attribution by the centralized
system may then show only that a model with less information answers
questions about components less well, which is true of any compression.
The comparison needs the two self-models matched in capacity, with the
centralized one wrong about control rather than merely coarser. A second
threat follows from A27 §12: if the self-model feeds back into governance,
the two systems' control does not stay equivalent, and what was held fixed
drifts.

## What it cannot show

- That a distributed agent would come to represent a single owner. The
  modeller writes both self-models, so the content is the modeller's
  ([CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md)). The case tests what such a content does once present.
- Anything about phenomenal properties. A27: "We would not thereby have
  demonstrated illusionism about phenomenal properties."
- What access each component has to the integrated state. That is A27's
  Experiment B, binding by restricted access, noted on [QUESTION-061](../questions.d/QUESTION-061.md).
