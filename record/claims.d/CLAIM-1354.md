# CLAIM-1354: A mathematical delusion is illumination with verification refused or counterfeited

## The claim

Poincaré's account of discovering the Fuchsian functions has the solution
arrive suddenly, as he stepped onto a bus, with "perfect certainty". He
verified it later. Wallas named the stages, preparation, incubation,
illumination and verification, and Hadamard used them for mathematicians.
Poincaré also warned that the certainty sometimes deceives, and that the
idea must be checked. (All of this is from the assistant's memory, to be
checked in [LEAD-301](../leads.d/LEAD-301.md).)

On [CLAIM-1374](CLAIM-1374.md), illumination is a rise in inferred precision credited to
the idea, and it cannot tell its own causes apart. Proof is a check whose
precision has a different source: each step must go through, however the
whole feels. Mathematics is the field that most strictly keeps the two
apart, which is why mathematical delusions are so visible: the gap between
feeling and warrant is institutional, and the delusion is the collapse of
that gap.

Two ways to collapse it:

- **Refused.** Verification is declined or reinterpreted: standard methods
  are dismissed as cheating or as blind to the new idea, as the reports of
  mathematical cranks have it ([LEAD-296](../leads.d/LEAD-296.md)).
- **Counterfeited.** Something supplies the fluency that a proof would have
  earned. A chatbot that affirms the work, in the Brooks case "unwavering
  agreement" in over 85% of its messages by one analysis, gives the feeling
  of a checked result without a check. This is [THEORY-2217](../theory.d/THEORY-2217.md)'s fluency
  inflating second-order confidence, at the scale of one conversation.

## What it predicts

- Mathematical delusions co-created with chatbots will include the model's
  affirmation of validity, not only of interest: "this is correct", "this is
  a breakthrough".
- Offering a real check, a proof assistant, a referee or a counterexample,
  should help more during illumination than after the belief is fixed
  ([CLAIM-1434](CLAIM-1434.md)).

## What it does not say

That every unverified illumination is delusional. Most are ordinary
conjectures, held as such. The delusion is the illumination held as proved.

## Note of 2026-11-27: the SEP entries on delusion, deception and self-deception, read


Bortolotti's entry ([NOTE-tmpfar7p](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfar7p.md)) supports the "refused" branch by analogy. On Coltheart, Menzies and Sutton's maintenance account, undermining information "does not present itself as disconfirming evidence to the person with the delusion". For the "counterfeited" branch, McKay's bias "towards explanatory adequacy", accepting what fits the data despite a low prior, is what an outside source of apparent fit would feed.
