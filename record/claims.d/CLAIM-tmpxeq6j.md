# CLAIM-tmpxeq6j: Traceability does not settle fair attribution: a record can identify exactly which agent approved an outcome while leaving open whether that agent had the time, information, authority and alternatives that responsibility for it requires, so two arrangements with equally complete audit trails can warrant very different attributions

## The claim

A19 §9 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c):

> **A technology can improve the traceability of decisions while worsening
> the fairness of the responsibility assigned for those decisions.**

"An automated system might record precisely which clinician approved a
recommendation. That tells us something useful. But it doesn't tell us
whether the clinician had enough time, information, or authority to
evaluate it responsibly. Traceability and fair attribution are different
achievements."

Traceability locates an act causally and procedurally: who did what, on
what input, when. Fair attribution needs more: the agent's authority over
the act, what it knew and could know, the resources and time it had, and
what refusing would have cost it. The second is not read off the first.

## Admission

By a separating case ([ADR-075](../decisions.d/ADR-075.md)), [CASE-tmplgw46](../cases.d/CASE-tmplgw46.md): two hospitals with equally
complete and accurate audit trails of the same approval, in one of which
the clinician could meaningfully evaluate and refuse and in the other could
not. Argued in [ARG-tmpf8gmh](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpf8gmh.md).

## Where it sits

- [CLAIM-tmpnfjln](CLAIM-tmpnfjln.md): blame settles where failure becomes visible. A complete
  trail can make the last human approval the most visible thing about a
  failure while the conditions that shaped it stay out of the record.
- [CLAIM-tmpnw7ez](CLAIM-tmpnw7ez.md): responsibility for an act and for its conditions are
  distinct. Traceability records the first.
- [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md): legibility of a discrepancy is not its efficacy. Here the act
  is legible and the conditions are not.
- The HKMA paper's "A formal approval step is not, by itself, meaningful
  human oversight" ([THEORY-tmpeq8z1](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpeq8z1.md)), and Zeiser's attributability gap
  ([THEORY-tmp4z0a7](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4z0a7.md)), are readings in the same neighbourhood.

## What it does not say

- **What fair attribution is.** The case shows the two come apart, not how
  attribution should be done.
- **That traceability is worthless or harmful.** It is a precondition of
  much accountability; the claim is that it is not sufficient.
