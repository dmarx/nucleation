# CLAIM-271: A collective's reasons need not be any of its members' reasons, so aggregating members' conclusions, as polling does, can yield verdicts the collective's own reasons do not support

## The claim

The owner, 2026-10-13: "the good/will of a higher level agent need not
align with the respective independent goods/wills of the parts that
comprise it. this feels like it likely has implications for how modern
politics leans so heavily on polling data." Then: "maybe rather than
'good', I meant 'reasons'?"

The argument, drawn by the assistant:

1. [CLAIM-030](CLAIM-030.md): a whole's operative intention is produced by its organization,
   not by taking an expectation over its members' intentions.
2. [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md) (List and Pettit) makes this exact for reasons. A group that
   judges interconnected propositions, premises and the conclusion they
   imply, cannot always form each judgement from its members' judgements
   on that proposition alone. Majorities on each premise can imply a
   conclusion that a majority rejects. The group must aggregate reasons or
   conclusions, and the two can come apart.
3. [THEORY-131](../theory.d/THEORY-131.md) (French) and [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) (Pettit) place a collective's reasons
   in its decision structure: its stations and recognition rules, or its
   authorised avowal and its correction of the inconsistencies aggregation
   produces. On both, the collective's reasons are made by procedure and
   need not be any member's.
4. A poll asks members for conclusions ("do you support X?"), one question
   at a time. A politics steered by polls therefore aggregates by
   conclusion. By 2, its verdicts can be ones that its members' reasons,
   taken together, do not support. By 3, it may hold no reasons of its own
   that support them either.

An illustration, invented, not a case the record holds: 60% oppose a
policy, half because it goes too far and half because it does not go far
enough. The poll reports a majority against it. No majority holds either
reason, and a government that acts on the majority acts on neither.

## What it does not say

- It does not say polling is illegitimate or should be ignored. [CLAIM-060](CLAIM-060.md)
  and [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) cut the other way: a whole's success or persistence does
  not establish its members' flourishing or what ought to persist, which is
  a case for polls as a constraint on the whole.
- It does not say the collective knows better than its members. Its
  reasons differ from theirs because they are made by procedure, and
  procedure can produce rationalization as easily as reasons. [THEORY-137](../theory.d/THEORY-137.md)
  warns that attributing a mind to an institution partly shapes the
  institution to fit.
- It does not say that the discursive dilemma arises often in actual
  polities. [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md) shows it is possible. How often it happens is the
  empirical question [QUESTION-082](../questions.d/QUESTION-082.md) asks, and the record holds no evidence
  on it yet.

## Note of 2026-10-18: a second appearance, as A21's Candidate 3

A21 §18 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 proposed as a new claim: "The
judgments and actions of an organized whole need not be recoverable through
independent aggregation of corresponding constituent judgments, because
maintained rules of precedence, admissibility, and authority can be
indispensable to the macro-level result." It argued it from List and Pettit
(THEORY-127): "an organized whole's judgment is not generally recoverable by
independently aggregating each corresponding judgment of its constituents".

That is this claim, without the polling consequence, and it was not filed
again. A21's addition, that maintained rules of precedence and
admissibility do the work, is THEORY-139's (Pettit: the corporation
corrects the inconsistency aggregation produces) and CLAIM-027's. A21 cited
THEORY-127 and CLAIM-027 and not this claim. A21 §16 and A22's Construction
C pair it with a whole whose description merges its members' claims
(CLAIM-tmp679hm, CASE-tmpfusdm), where a collective judgment rule is the
optional extension A22 left unbuilt. The status is unchanged.
