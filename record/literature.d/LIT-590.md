## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request. The record now holds two of the
essays' themes at second hand. Bratman's 2018 essay ([LIT-567](LIT-567.md)) cites
"Reflection, Planning, and Temporally Extended Agency" for the claim that
plans induce "psychological connectedness and continuity of intention and
plan that are in the spirit of broadly Lockean models of personal identity"
(pp. 28–33). That is why the book carries `personhood`. It cites "Three
Theories" for self-governing policies, and "Temptation Revisited" for an
earlier view of willpower that it now thinks will not "apply in a
sufficiently general way". Korsgaard ([LIT-587](LIT-587.md)) cites "Reflection,
Planning" and "Two Problems about Human Agency" for Bratman's Lockean view of
identity, and measures her normative account against it. The IEP survey
([LIT-297](LIT-297.md)) cites the book for the claim that plans partly constitute
identity.

`Deferred` because it was not read. The owner allows a book to be replaced by
a shorter lawful work by the same author. For this book that work is the
2018 essay, which states where these essays led. "Three Theories" is filed
separately by another reader. The two essays most worth reading next are
"Reflection, Planning, and Temporally Extended Agency", for the Lockean
claim, and "Autonomy and Hierarchy", for [THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md).
