# LIT-250: Duality of Bures and Shape Distances with Implications for Comparing Neural Representations

Sarah E. Harvey, Brett W. Larsen, Alex H. Williams (2023), *Proceedings of UniReps: the First Workshop on Unifying Representations in Neural Models (UniReps 2023), PMLR 243:11–26 (2024); first appeared as arXiv preprint* — [ARXIV-2311.11436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11436)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-753](../notes.d/NOTE-753.md).*

- **The duality (Thm 1).** For centred linear kernels K_X = CXXᵀC,
  B(K_X, K_Y) = P(X, Y) and NBS(K_X, K_Y) = cos θ*(X, Y).
  - B is the Bures distance and P the Procrustes distance
    min_Q ‖CX − CYQ‖_F.
  - Both rest on Lemma 2, F(K_X, K_Y) = Tr[(K_X^½K_YK_X^½)^½] = ‖XᵀCY‖_*,
    proved by an eigenvalue argument (App. A.2).
- **Unequal widths.** Written as ‖Σ_XY‖_* over traces, shape distances
  are defined for N_x ≠ N_y, and they stay metrics because B is one.
- **Uhlmann (eq. 25).** The fidelity is the largest overlap |Tr XᵀY| over
  all representations X, Y consistent with the two kernels. Such
  representations differ by orthogonal maps, a fact the paper states
  without proof.
- **NBS is a fidelity of trace-normalized kernels.** NBS(K_X, K_Y) =
  F(K_X/Tr K_X, K_Y/Tr K_Y). App. A.3 treats K/Tr K as a unit-trace PSD
  matrix, a density matrix in all but name.
- **CKA is a different geometry.** CKA is the Euclidean cosine of the
  kernels, and NBS the Bures one. They are tied only by
  CKA/√(r_X r_Y) ≤ NBS² ≤ min(r_X, r_Y)·CKA (eq. 24) and by eqs. 27–28
  for the square-root kernels. On random matrices they differ two- to
  three-fold.
- **Limits (§4).** As stimuli M → ∞ or neurons N → ∞ the normalized
  distances converge, by a law-of-large-numbers sketch. The joint limit,
  a Bures distance between covariance operators, is asserted with
  citations. The paper never mentions RKHS or GNS.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 while pursuing, at the owner's request, the connection
*GNS, unitary equivalence and the convergence of representations* (see the
curation entry of that date). Read in full on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-753](../notes.d/NOTE-753.md).

It is held as the finite-dimensional, linear statement that the kernel is
the invariant and a representation one realization of it. Distance between
kernels in the Bures geometry is distance between representations after
optimal orthogonal alignment. It uses quantum-information language
(fidelity, Uhlmann's theorem) to say so. [THEORY-004](../theory.d/THEORY-004.md) rests on it, and
[NOTE-753](../notes.d/NOTE-753.md) finds [THEORY-004](../theory.d/THEORY-004.md) sound with three corrections of detail:
- the NBS–shape-cosine equality is Theorem 1, not Lemma 2;
- a centred kernel fixes the representation up to translation as well as
  an orthogonal map;
- the "equal kernels iff zero Procrustes distance" statement is made in
  §2.2 for RDMs.

[THEORY-002](../theory.d/THEORY-002.md) cites it for the kernel-side metric, which is accurate for
linear kernels. Its CKA results bear on [THEORY-002](../theory.d/THEORY-002.md)'s promote_when, which
says a CKA result does not count by itself, since the two bound each other
only within a rank-dependent factor (§5, eq. 24). That envelope is filed as
[THEORY-233](../theory.d/THEORY-233.md). The GNS reading the strand
wants comes from [LIT-259](LIT-259.md); this paper supplies none of it.
