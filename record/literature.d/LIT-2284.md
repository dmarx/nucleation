summary: >-
  Zhao, Liu et al. (2025), When Tools Hurt LLM Reasoning: State-Dependent
  Belief Revision under External Evidence. Tool use (Python, Wikipedia) is
  often assumed to help when relevant and be harmless otherwise. The paper
  shows the effect depends on the model's belief before the tool: tools help
  when the model's pre-tool probability on the correct answer is low and can
  flip correct answers to wrong when it is high, even when the tool's output
  is itself correct. The authors model revision as logarithmic pooling of
  the pre-tool belief P and a tool-conditioned belief Q with an "authority"
  weight λ, derive that under a logistic risk the optimal λ is zero whenever
  the internal log-odds already exceed the expected support from the tool
  channel, and test three predictions: harm concentrates where the internal
  belief exceeds the tool-conditioned support; failed tool-assisted traces
  often look supported and confident; and the same misleading clue does more
  or less damage depending on how the prompt frames its authority. They
  propose CASE, a label-free controller choosing between no-tool and tool
  trajectories by answer-state certainty. Read for the exchange "luria
  experiment design".
