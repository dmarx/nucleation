# CLAIM-tmp1bwsn: What fundamentally distinguishes variations of a translation is the frame from which it is delivered

## The claim

U6: "do you think it's fair to summarize one of our observations here as 'the
main thing that fundamentally distinguishes variations of a translation is the
frame from which it is being delivered'?"

## What happened to it

- **A11** agreed, recast it as the interpretive frame ([TERM-tmp36e3d](../terms.d/TERM-tmp36e3d.md)), and qualified
  it: "I wouldn't say that framing is *the only* fundamental distinction. Two
  translators can adopt essentially the same interpretive frame yet produce
  substantially different translations because of differences in linguistic
  skill, aesthetic judgment, rhythmic intuitions, or the constraints they're
  trying to satisfy." The owner never answered this.
- **U7** restored delivery; **A12**: "What most fundamentally distinguishes
  translations is the frame from which the original is reinterpreted and
  delivered" ([TERM-tmpx0l4b](../terms.d/TERM-tmpx0l4b.md)).
- **A14**, in speech-act terms: "**Perhaps the most fundamental differences
  between translations are pragmatic rather than semantic**: they arise from
  the communicative footing, speaker stance, and illocutionary force through
  which substantially similar propositional content is delivered." "The main
  thing" had become "perhaps".
- **A18** objected that some differences are not of frame at all ([CLAIM-tmp0talu](CLAIM-tmp0talu.md)).

The manuscript keeps the contrast and drops the ranking: [CLAIM-tmphg89g](CLAIM-tmphg89g.md).
