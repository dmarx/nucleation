# THEORY-tmptv9vu: Horsthemke argues that human studies of germline epigenetic inheritance can exclude genetic inheritance but not ecological or cultural inheritance, proposes a three-step standard of proof that he grants can hardly be met in humans, and classifies the Dutch Hunger Winter effects as fetal programming

## The account

Offspring inherit genes, an environment and a culture, the last two
partly built by earlier generations, so shared marks between parent and
child are expected without any germline transmission: "it is extremely
difficult to divide out whether these features have been transmitted
through the germline or established anew in each generation by the action
of shared genes and shared environments". Familial epimutations are often
secondary to a nearby mutation, which is genetic inheritance.

The roadmap: rule out genetic, ecological and cultural inheritance;
identify the factor in purified germ cells; show by removal and addition
that it causes the effect. In humans "it is impossible to rule out
ecological and cultural inheritance ... but genetic effects should and can
be excluded", and "all of these experiments can hardly be done in
humans". The Dutch Hunger Winter's effects on the exposed fetuses are "not
due to the transmission of epigenetic information through the maternal
germline, but a direct consequence of the exposure in utero". Grandparental
food-supply findings await replication. Cultural transmission "by
communication, imitation, teaching and learning surpasses the effects of
epigenetic inheritance and our ability to detect this phenomenon". "For
now, I remain skeptical."

## Evidence

The Comment (NOTE-1265): an argument from design, three cited family
studies of secondary epimutations, and two rodent examples. The claim that
reported effects are almost all deleterious is open to the reporting bias
the author grants. The comparison with cultural transmission is opinion.
Holocaust offspring, FKBP5 and the Dias and Ressler odour study are not
discussed.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297's test on the routes. It is the reading behind the
question's note that the first test is, for now, the second answer the
question allows: no clean separation is achievable in humans. Adoptee and
paternal-only designs weaken the confounds without removing them.
