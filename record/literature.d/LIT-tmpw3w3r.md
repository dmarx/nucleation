# LIT-tmpw3w3r: Collective Intentions and Collective Intentionality

Zaibert, L. A. (2003). Collective Intentions and Collective Intentionality.
*American Journal of Economics and Sociology*, 62(1), 209–232.
doi:10.1111/1536-7150.t01-1-00008.

## Key takeaways

- Searle's "Collective Intentions and Actions" (LIT-490) does not show
  that collective intentionality is irreducible. On its own words, as
  Zaibert quotes them, its arguments only "suggest that our intuition is
  right", and "I have not demonstrated that no such analysis could ever
  succeed" (CIA 401, 406).
- The analysis Searle favours there, "i.a. collective B by means of
  singular A", has the same structure as the analysis of one person firing
  a gun by pulling a trigger, so it states a complex individual intention,
  not a collective one.
- Searle's rejection of Tuomela and Miller rests on one counterexample,
  which Zaibert finds doubtful (n. 16).
- Intentions have only actions as their conditions of satisfaction, and
  one cannot intend what is beyond one's control. Zaibert argues that this
  is why groups cannot have irreducible we-intentions, though they may
  have collective desires or beliefs.

## Why this is here

THEORY-625's promote_when waits on an argument that collective
intentionality is irreducible, which Searle's 2006 paper defers to his
1990 paper (LIT-490), and that paper is Unavailable. This is a published
report of the 1990 paper, with page references, by a critic.
