# NOTE-2312: Metzinger (2021) — Artificial Suffering

## Contribution

The moratorium with its revision mechanism; a four-condition analysis of
suffering; two design routes to conscious AI that cannot suffer (opaque
self-models, and systems identifying only with minimal phenomenal
experience); and an argument that artificial suffering could interact with
the risk of artificial moral agents.

## Key results

1. **The thesis (p. 46).** "As we presently have no good theory of
   consciousness and no good, hardware-independent theory about what
   'suffering' really is, the ENP risk is currently incalculable. It is
   unethical to run incalculable risks of this magnitude. Therefore, until
   2050, there should be a global ban on all research that directly aims at
   or indirectly and knowingly risks the emergence of synthetic
   phenomenology."
2. **The ethics behind it (p. 44, footnote a; p. 45).** The "Principle of
   Pathocentrism": "All and only sentient beings have moral standing,
   because only sentient individuals have rights and/or interests that must
   be considered"; and "a priority for the reduction of suffering".
3. **Negative phenomenology (p. 45).** "any kind of conscious experience a
   conscious system would avoid or rather not go through if it had a
   choice."
4. **The self (pp. 48–51).** "Only beings with conscious experience and a
   PSM can suffer"; "Suffering presupposes egoic self-awareness"; "the
   essence of suffering lies in the fact that a conscious system is forced
   to identify with a state of negative valence and is unable to break this
   identification". By precaution, "every representational system that is
   able to activate a PSM, however rudimentary" should be treated "as a
   moral object", and creating artificial phenomenal selves "should be a red
   line".
5. **Valence (p. 51).** "it suffices if it does not want to undergo this
   current conscious experience, that it wants it to end"; a self-conscious
   entity without preferences would "abide in a form of 'choiceless
   awareness'".
6. **Transparency (p. 54).** With an opaque self-model "There would be a
   system model, but not a self-model."
7. **Stock-taking (p. 55).** "It is not yet clear whether the relevant class
   of systems have a welfare that we should care about for their own sake,
   if they are genuine moral patients", but "a pro tanto case can be made
   that any system satisfying all of these conceptual constraints should be
   treated as an object of ethical consideration"; "a conscious system
   without a coherent PSM is unable to suffer".
8. **Metric and design (pp. 56–57).** The unit of suffering is a "negative
   self-model moment": "Can there be conscious AI without a single NSM?"
   Systems that "identify only with the phenomenal character of awareness
   itself" lack an egoic self-model, so "MPE architectures may be a viable
   path for ethics by design", though they "could still instantiate a
   non-egoic form of self-awareness and identify with it".
9. **Risk interaction (pp. 58–62).** Artificial suffering might give rise to
   artificial moral agents with "Kantian" self-models and moral injury;
   "self-models do not have to be veridical in order to cause conscious
   suffering and dangerous behavior".
10. **Conclusion (pp. 62–63).** "Every entity that is capable of
    self-conscious suffering automatically becomes an object of ethical
    consideration"; "we should have a global moratorium on synthetic
    phenomenology until 2050 -- or until we know what we are doing."

## Assumptions

- Pathocentrism and a priority for reducing suffering, cited (Singer,
  Mayerfeld, Vinding), not argued.
- The self-model theory of subjectivity and a placeholder theory of
  consciousness; hardware independence of the relevant properties.
- That machine consciousness, once it arises, brings preferences and a
  self-model, asserted.
- A precautionary rule for "incalculable" risks.

## Concepts

Synthetic phenomenology; explosion of negative phenomenology (ENP);
negative phenomenology; pathocentrism; the C, PSM, NV and T conditions;
minimal phenomenal experience (MPE); existence bias; negative self-model
moment (NSM); unit of identification; MPE architecture; ethics by design;
artificial moral agent; Kantian self-model; moral injury.

  restatement's "until we know ... what we are doing" is the paper's
  alternative ending. The licence is CC BY 4.0. And the paper confirms what
  "Standing in the record" inferred from the restatement: a self, not
  consciousness as such, is necessary for suffering (p. 55).

## Limitations

- The four conditions are stipulated as necessary, "deliberately"
  coarse-grained (p. 48), on the author's self-model theory.
- The valence condition is not cleanly separated from "frustrated
  preferences ... that involve no conscious suffering" (p. 52).
- Identification: MPE systems "identify with" non-egoic awareness (p. 57)
  while "the phenomenology of ownership and identification would
  disappear"; the paper does not say why such identification cannot carry
  negative valence.
- The moratorium's scope ("knowingly risks") is not operationalized, and
  2050 is not justified.
- Precaution runs one way; the costs of a ban are not weighed.
- Pathocentrism makes sentience the only ground of standing, yet p. 55
  leaves moral patiency open; the two are not reconciled.
- The section on artificial moral agents is avowedly speculative.
