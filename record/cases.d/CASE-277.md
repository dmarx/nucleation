# CASE-277: Two hospitals, same AI and audit trail

## The case

Two hospitals use the same AI diagnostic system. In both, the system
recommends a treatment, a clinician approves it, and the patient has a bad
outcome. Both keep complete audit trails: the records establish precisely
which recommendation was issued, which clinician approved it and when, and
they are equally accurate.

- **Hospital A.** The clinician has enough time, independent information,
  authority to override the recommendation and a protected route of
  escalation. The logged approval records an act the clinician could
  meaningfully evaluate and refuse.
- **Hospital B.** The clinician faces overwhelming volume, too little
  information and penalties for overriding. The identical logged approval
  records an act whose practical conditions were largely set upstream.

What is held fixed: the system, the recommendation, the approval, the
outcome and the record. What varies: time, information, authority to refuse
and the cost of refusing.

## What it can show

That a complete and accurate record of who approved an outcome does not
settle whether responsibility for it can fairly be attributed to the
approver: the records are the same and the grounds for attribution are not
([CLAIM-712](../claims.d/CLAIM-712.md)). Hospital B is the moral crumple zone of [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)
with a perfect paper trail.

## What it cannot show

- **How responsibility should be divided** in Hospital B, or that the
  clinician bears none.
- **That real hospitals are like B.** It is stipulated.
