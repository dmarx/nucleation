# LIT-168: Biological Individuals

Robert A. Wilson, Matthew J. Barker (2007), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/biology-individual/>

## Key takeaways

- Biological individuality cannot be settled by equating individuals with organisms: the space of biological individuals is better mapped along the evolutionary (Darwinian) vs physiological (living-agent) distinction, with organisms, some parts of organisms and some groups of organisms each filling distinct regions — genes are Darwinian individuals without being agents, honey-bee colonies arguably likewise.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry starts from the question of what biological individuals are and shows how it quickly runs into puzzling cases. It argues for laying out the conceptual space around the question, using the distinction between evolutionary and physiological individuals, and considers groups such as superorganisms and species that have been proposed as individuals. It links individuality to life, reproduction and agency, and closes with recent work on how evolutionary individuals are created and regulated and on the evolution of individuality.
