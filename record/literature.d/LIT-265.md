# LIT-265: The contextual fraction as a measure of contextuality

Samson Abramsky, Rui Soares Barbosa, Shane Mansfield (2017), *Physical Review Letters 119, 050504 (2017)* — [ARXIV-1705.07918](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07918)

## Key takeaways

- **Definition.** For an empirical model e on ⟨X, M, O⟩, with marginals
  agreeing on overlaps, NCF(e) is the largest λ in e = λe^NC + (1 − λ)e′
  with e^NC noncontextual and e′ an empirical model. Equivalently, it is
  the largest weight of a subprobability b on global assignments with
  b|_C ≤ e_C. CF = 1 − NCF. Every model splits as NCF·e^NC + CF·e^SC with
  e^SC strongly contextual, not uniquely: the supplement's Table II gives
  two such splits of one (3,2,2) model. CF = 1 is strong contextuality
  ([LIT-016](LIT-016.md)).
- **Linear programming and Bell inequalities** (Eqs. 3–4, Theorem 1).
  NCF = max 1·b subject to Mb ≤ v_e, b ≥ 0. With y* solving the dual
  (min y·v_e, Mᵀy ≥ 1, y ≥ 0), a* = |M|⁻¹1 − y* defines a Bell inequality
  with bound 0 and algebraic bound 1. Its violation by e is exactly CF(e),
  and no Bell inequality's normalised violation exceeds CF(e). It is tight
  at e^NC and maximally violated by e^SC in every optimal decomposition.
- **Monotonicity** (Theorem 2). CF is invariant under relabelling and
  non-increasing under translation of measurements (including restriction
  to fewer measurements) and coarse-graining of outcomes. It is convex
  under mixing. CF(e & e′) = max{CF(e), CF(e′)}, and
  NCF(e ⊗ e′) = NCF(e)·NCF(e′). The mixing bullet of the printed theorem
  has a typo (CF(e) for CF(e′)); the proof is for the correct statement.
- **Computational advantage** (Theorems 3–4). An l2-MBQC computing f with
  average failure p̄_F has p̄_F ≥ NCF(e)·ν̃(f), where ν̃(f) is f's average
  distance to the nearest Z2-linear function. A valid strategy for a
  k-consistent system of n formulae has p_F ≥ NCF·(n − k)/n.
- **Examples** (supplement B). On |Φ⁺⟩ with equatorial measurements, CF
  peaks at the Tsirelson models, not at the textbook CHSH angles, and is
  never 1. On GHZ(n), CF = 1 at a family of angle pairs that are
  relabellings of Mermin's X/Y model (Proposition 4).
