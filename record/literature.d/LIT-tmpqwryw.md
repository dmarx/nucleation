# LIT-tmpqwryw: The stance triangle

John W. Du Bois (2007), in Robert Englebretson (ed.), *Stancetaking in
Discourse: Subjectivity, evaluation, interaction*, Pragmatics & Beyond New
Series 164, John Benjamins, pp. 139–182 — DOI-10.1075/pbns.164.07du

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-tmp7fqen](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7fqen.md)).

- **One act, three aspects.** Evaluation (*that's horrible*), positioning
  (*I'm glad*, *I know*) and alignment (*I agree*) are not stance types but
  facets of every stance act. The definition: "a public act by a social
  actor, achieved dialogically through overt communicative means, of
  simultaneously evaluating objects, positioning subjects (self and
  others), and aligning with other subjects, with respect to any salient
  dimension of the sociocultural field."
- **The triangle.** Nodes: first subject, second subject, shared stance
  object. Two sides are evaluative vectors from each subject to the object;
  the third is alignment between the subjects; positioning reflects back
  on each subject. Knowing part of the triangle lets participants infer
  the rest: agreeing implies the prior evaluation, repeating an evaluation
  implies convergence.
- **No stance stands alone.** "I'm glad", "I know" and "I agree" are
  grammatically complete and incomplete as stances; their object and the
  stance they answer usually come from a co-participant's earlier turn,
  sometimes dozens of lines back. "Subjectivity takes an object."
- **Marking the follow.** When a second stance resonates with a first
  (*I don't like those* / *I don't either*), the follow must carry *too*
  or *either* and the lead cannot; differences inside a parallel follow
  (a rising *Yeah?*, *want* for *love*) calibrate alignment, which is
  scalar, convergent or divergent by degrees.
- **Evidence.** Qualitative analysis of short excerpts from the Santa
  Barbara and Longman spoken corpora; the *too*/*either* claim rests on a
  separate study (Du Bois 2004) whose figures are not given.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-09 at the owner's request, as one of the works in the
reference list of the owner's working manuscript (October 2026) that the
record did not yet hold. See the curation entry of that day. Read the same

As first filed, this note described the triangle as one subject evaluating
an object and thereby aligning "or disaligning" with another. The chapter
gives the triangle two evaluative sides and one alignment side, with
positioning reflexive at each subject, and treats alignment as a scale
from convergent to divergent rather than a binary.

The chapter sets aside, in its note 13, the case where the stance subject
is not the speaker, and cites Goffman's *Forms of Talk* ([LIT-tmpsg9d2](LIT-tmpsg9d2.md)) for
it; the two filings meet there. No THEORY is filed from it; the NOTE gives
a candidate on the obligatory marking of stance follows.
