# THEORY-1981: Repeat players make the haves come out ahead

Galanter (1974), [LIT-2728](../literature.d/LIT-2728.md).

## The account

The repeat player is "a unit which has had and anticipates repeated
litigation, which has low stakes in the outcome of any one case, and which
has the resources to pursue its long-run interests" (p. 98). "RPs can play
for rules as well as immediate gains" (p. 100). Courts marked by
"passivity and overload" (p. 119) press claimants to settle by delay and
by "raising costs (of keeping the case alive)", and "overload tends to
protect the possessor" (pp. 121–22).

## What it does not show

- A threshold or model of when a claimant gives up; the essay is
  qualitative and "speculative".
- That repeat players are the haves: "it is not suggested that RPs are to
  be equated with 'haves'" (p. 103), and the two are ends of a continuum.
- Anything normative about what is owed once a right cannot be
  vindicated.
