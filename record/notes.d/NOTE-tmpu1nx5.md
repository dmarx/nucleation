# NOTE-tmpu1nx5: Council on Ethics, Norwegian Government Pension Fund Global — Recommendation to remove Siemens AG from the watch list

## Contribution

The Council had recommended excluding Siemens from the fund in 2007 for the
risk of gross corruption; the Ministry put it under observation in 2009.
This recommendation, of 15 June 2012, ends the observation after annual
meetings with Siemens's compliance officers and with "the independent
monitor appointed by U.S. authorities, Dr. Theo Waigel".

## Key results

- **The system.** A compliance budget of "approximately EUR 100 million,
  comprising 600 full-time compliance positions"; business staff rotate
  through compliance for three to five years; "responsibility for
  compliance cannot be shifted to the compliance officers, but must be
  assumed by everyone" (Section 4.3).
- **A costly refusal (2011).** Three employees in Kuwait agreed to pay a
  EUR 1.25 million bribe for a EUR 180 million contract; an internal
  investigation "prevented the payment of the bribe", three managers were
  dismissed, the SEC and the Munich prosecutor were notified, and "The
  incident resulted in Siemens losing the tender competition" (Section
  4.2).
- **The reasons given.** The compliance leaders' message was that
  violations "may lead to external investigations" under the FCPA or the UK
  Bribery Act and to exclusion from development-bank projects, "which will
  have major financial consequences for the company" (Section 4.3).
- **The verdict.** Risk of corruption not "higher ... than in other
  comparable companies" (Section 1).

## Assumptions

That a compliance system's design, management's message and the handling
of recent cases are evidence of its working; the Council's sources include
the company and its monitor.

## Concepts

Watch list; observation; compliance as prevent, detect and respond.

## Bearing on the record

- **CASE-tmphvbov.** The outside assessment on which Siemens is coded as a
  redesign (R): a procedure with authority to stop a payment, kept and
  staffed.
- **CLAIM-432 and CLAIM-431 (illustration, both).** The Kuwait refusal is
  harm weighed at a cost to the firm, during the sanction and in public.
  The compliance leaders' own reasons for the system are threats:
  prosecution and exclusion. A procedure that did what CLAIM-432 calls
  remorse was explained to staff as CLAIM-431 says it would be. Neither
  claim is settled by it: the weighing happened while the monitor was there.

## Limitations

A watch-list decision, not an audit. Redacted. It dates from the last
year of the monitorship, so it says nothing of conduct after it ended.
