# CLAIM-tmpuiaop: Automation reduces procedural friction, yet procedural friction is where an opportunity to intervene in a process is exposed and perhaps what such an opportunity consists in, so removing friction can remove the points at which an unfolding decision can still be interrupted

## The claim

The owner, U11 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c,
2026-11-15):

> we can view automation as the effort to reduce procedural friction, but
> paradoxically procedural friction exposes (or perhaps even characterizes)
> opportunity for intervention.

Two readings, both the owner's:

- **Exposes.** Friction (a handoff, a wait, a review, an approval that
  someone must give) is where a decision in progress becomes visible to
  someone other than whoever is making it, while it can still be changed.
  Removing the friction removes the visibility at that point.
- **Characterizes.** An opportunity to intervene is a place in a process
  where it can be stopped or redirected before its consequences settle,
  and that is what a piece of procedural friction is. On this reading,
  friction-free and interruptible are in tension by definition, not only
  as it happens.

The paradox is that the property automation is built to remove is one of
the properties through which others can act on the process.

## Where it sits in the record

- [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) (Active) names what an objection needs in
  order to change what an organization does: legibility, volitional
  efficacy and correctability. This claim is about where in a process the
  occasion for an objection lies. A30 §2 of the exchange made the
  connection: "procedural friction is one possible institutional mechanism
  for maintaining the three achievements across time", and "not every
  interruption provides all three". The point of intervention this claim
  locates is the occasion; [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) says what the occasion
  must carry to work.
- [CLAIM-462](CLAIM-462.md) (Active): an arrangement's capacity for
  effective action can grow while its capacity to be corrected degrades.
  Removing friction raises the first. This claim names one way the second
  falls with it.
- [CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md) (Active), the owner's from 2026-10-26: an
  intervention can make its own correction costlier before review. With
  friction removed there is less time between action and the point after
  which that happens.
- [CASE-003](../cases.d/CASE-003.md), Northstar-A and Northstar-B, is the
  record's controlled contrast for one kind of friction: an authorized
  objection that suspends deployment, against one that does not.

## A's developments, mapped, not adopted

The exchange's A21 and A23 (ChatGPT's) build on U11. They are A's voice;
adopting any is the owner's call.

- A21 strengthens "exposes" to "constitutes": friction "actually
  **constitutes the opportunity for intervention**, because it creates the
  temporal, informational, and organizational space in which someone can
  notice a problem, exercise judgment, contest a decision, or refuse to
  participate". That is the owner's
  "characterizes", which the owner hedged.
- A21 §3: friction is distributed, not only removed. "What looks like a
  reduction in friction from the organization's perspective may actually
  be a transfer of discretion from one group of people to another." A
  benefits system that decides at once and appeals that take weeks have
  low execution friction and high contestation friction. A21 §9's
  "strongest proposition": "The distribution of friction partly
  constitutes the distribution of political power."
- A21 §4: "extractive friction" (it costs those subject to a process time
  and effort and gives them no influence) against "deliberative friction"
  (it lets reasons, exceptions and objections in). The claim is about the
  second.
- A26 §VI: "countervailing friction", "procedural mechanisms that
  constrain consequential exercises of institutional authority while
  minimizing unnecessary burdens on those subject to it".

## What it does not say

- **That friction is good.** The owner's claim is about where intervention
  opportunities are, not that more friction is better. Extractive friction
  offers none. A's own caution, A26 §XI: "friction isn't inherently
  valuable."
- **That only friction can supply them.** A33 §8 and A35 §7 argue that
  slowly set standing constraints with fast enforcement can keep a process
  interruptible without delaying it. That is filed as an objection to the
  "characterizes" reading,

## Note of 2026-11-16: Hong Kong Monetary Authority, read

A regulator's paper supports the "exposes" reading in two ways. First, its
answer to agents that outrun oversight includes restoring delay: velocity
limits commensurate "with the demonstrated effectiveness of oversight" (§4.3,
p. 19). Second, "The last opportunity for human intervention may occur"
several steps before harm is visible (p. 18). Against an overly strong
"characterizes" reading, it also shows friction without opportunity: an
approval step that remains in place but is perfunctory under volume (§4.4,
p. 20). Relation: none.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Natali, read

Natali, Naiseh, Cabitza and Frischmann (LIT-tmphus2f) were registered against this claim from A21 §2 of the exchange. Their poster is about a different friction: "deliberate inefﬁciencies" placed "at critical points" of one user's interaction with an AI, against automation bias. That is cognitive friction, as A23 §7 itself classes it, not the procedural friction of handoffs, reviews and approvals between parties that this claim is about. It gives the claim no support and reports no evidence. It does show a case the claim's gloss of "exposes" (visible "to someone other than whoever is making it") does not settle. If the executor is the AI pipeline, a pause before its output shows a human the machine's proposal before it becomes the decision, which is exposure in the owner's sense. If the executor is the human user, the friction exposes the decision to no one but its maker, and what it opens is the executor's own correction. Whether such friction counts depends on who executes the process. The claim may want to say so.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Alfrink, read

Alfrink, Keller, Kortuem and Doorn (read as a stand-in for Harid, LIT-tmp7ftjn, which could not be fetched) synthesize the contestable-AI literature. Two of their findings bear here. One feature they gather is a second system that every automated decision is run through: "If disagreement between both systems occurs, decision can be flagged for human review." That is a designed point at which a decision in progress is shown to someone other than its maker before it takes effect, the claim's "exposes" reading built in. And for subjects: "Requests for human intervention are necessarily post-hoc, since they happen in response to discrete decisions." Absent such a designed point, the subject's opportunity to intervene comes after the decision. The paper supports the claim's concern as a design consensus, not as a finding: its sources are mostly "not based on empirically validated interventions".
