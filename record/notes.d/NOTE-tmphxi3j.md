# NOTE-tmphxi3j: Leitgeb & Ladyman — Criteria of identity and structuralism

## Contribution

The debate over whether structuralist ontologies of mathematics can keep the Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles usually assumes that individuals fall under criteria of identity that account for their identity. Most of that debate concerns structures with non-trivial automorphisms. The authors present graph-theoretic cases that violate even weak versions of PII. They argue that the identity or difference of places in a structure is accounted for by nothing beyond the structure, and that mathematical practice supports this.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 1–2): places the note in the PII/structuralism debate (Ladyman 2005; MacBride; Button; Shapiro; Ketland; Saunders and Hawley for physics). Examples are complex conjugation, the fermion singlet and Black's spheres. The method is to follow mathematical practice.
- §2 (pp. 2–5): the two-node graph G (one edge) has weakly discernible nodes. Deleting the edge gives G′, which has a non-trivial automorphism and no irreflexive relation, yet graph theorists count two unlabelled two-node graphs (Pólya enumeration). The identity relation belongs to the structure, like successor in the natural numbers.
- §3 (pp. 5–6): irreducible identity facts do not imply haecceitist Platonism, because permuting the nodes of G′ yields the same graph. They stay neutral on the weaker two-category Platonism and reply to Button's epistemic and metaphysical objections to primitive identity facts.
- Closing (p. 6): graph theorists treat nodes as objects proper. Physics may differ, since fermions have irreflexive relations and Saunders denies objecthood to bosons, which marks a possible disanalogy between mathematical and physical objects.
