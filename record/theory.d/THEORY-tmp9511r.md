# THEORY-tmp9511r: Jawaid and Mansuy argue that in rodents parental trauma, stress and drugs pass behavioural phenotypes to offspring through the germline, with sperm RNA the best-supported carrier, while granting that how effects pass beyond the first generation is unclear and that germline dependence is not established in humans

## The account

In rodents, unpredictable maternal separation and stress applied to pups
(MSUS) yields depressive-like symptoms, risk-taking and antisocial
behaviour "up to the third or even the fourth generation", through fathers
and mothers; adult social instability, odour conditioning, drugs and
in-utero insults pass effects too. Paternal enrichment passes benefits,
and enrichment of MSUS fathers "preserves their progeny from being
affected". Germline dependence is shown by artificial insemination, IVF
and sperm-RNA injection: RNA from the sperm of traumatized males injected
into control oocytes "recapitulated trauma-induced behavioral and metabolic
symptoms in the resulting progeny" (p. 99). Of DNA methylation, histone
modifications and non-coding RNAs, "ncRNAs are the best described
candidates today".

The open problems are stated plainly: the link from stimulus to germ-cell
signature "remains unknown", and "it is not clear how the phenotypes
associated with transgenerational inheritance are further passed on beyond
the first generation" (p. 99). In humans, descendants of genocide and war
show more psychopathology; germline dependence "is not established in
humans but initial results indicate that it is likely the case".

## Evidence

The review ([NOTE-1296](../notes.d/NOTE-1296.md)): a survey of rodent studies, with Table 1 giving
generations affected but no effect sizes or replication record, and a few
causal injection experiments, three on behaviour or stress hormones. The
human evidence in Table 2 is, by the table's own title, intergenerational,
and nothing in it separates the germline from rearing; the one sperm study
(n = 28) has no offspring.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md), the germline route. With the sceptical reviews, both sides
now agree on an intergenerational effect in mice and on the gaps: the
bridge from experience to sperm, transmission past the first generation,
and any human germline test. Its FKBP5 annotation records methylation
higher in survivors and lower in their offspring.
