# THEORY-1593: Heijmans and colleagues report that people conceived during the Dutch Hunger Winter had, about six decades later, slightly lower methylation of the imprinted IGF2 gene in blood than their same-sex siblings, while people exposed only late in gestation showed no difference

## The account

Sixty people whose mother's last menstrual period fell during the Hunger
Winter of 1944–45 (official rations averaging 667 kcal a day) had, about
58 years later, average methylation at five CpG sites of the IGF2
differentially methylated region of 0.488, against 0.515 in their unexposed
same-sex siblings: −5.2%, P = 5.9 × 10⁻⁵, lower in the exposed sibling in
43 of 60 pairs. Sixty-two people exposed only late in gestation were born
296 g lighter than reference births but showed no methylation difference
(P = 0.64), and the timing interaction was significant (P = 4.7 × 10⁻³).
Birth weight did not track methylation, so birth weight is "not
necessarily sufficient to test the involvement of epigenetics" (p. 63).
The authors read this as evidence that transient conditions early in
human gestation "can be recorded as persistent changes in epigenetic
information".

## Evidence

The paper ([NOTE-1248](../notes.d/NOTE-1248.md)): an observational sibling comparison, with a
technical re-run of the assay on 46 of the same pairs, not an independent
replication. One blood sample per person, so persistence since early life
is inferred from the authors' earlier twin study. Siblings differ from the
exposed in birth order and mother's age as well as famine. That famine,
not cold or emotional stress, is the cause is argued from similarity to
rat diet studies.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md), the womb route. The paper shows a lasting mark, not a
lasting harm: no expression, disease or behaviour outcome is measured. The
exposure is undernutrition, the exposed person's own, and the sensitive
window starts at the mother's last menstrual period, so it takes in the
egg's final maturation as well as the early embryo. Nothing in it needs
the exposed person to know of the famine.
