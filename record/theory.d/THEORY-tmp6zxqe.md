# THEORY-tmp6zxqe: Scheffler argues that the survival of other people after one's death is a condition of much of what matters to us mattering, and matters to us in its own right, and reads this as our dependence on future people rather than altruism toward them

## The account

Our own deaths do not drain our projects of point; humanity's imminent
end would. So the survival of others after we die is "a condition of
other things mattering to us" (Lecture I, part 3), and humanity's
continuation "also matters to us in its own right". Hence "there is a
very specific sense in which our own survival is less important to us
than the survival of the human race" (Lecture II, part 5).

Scheffler reads this as dependence: "The point has not been that we are
more highly motivated to advance the interests of future generations,
but rather that we are in certain ways more dependent on them" (Lecture
II, part 6). The surprise shows less that "we have been blinded to our
own altruism than ... that we have overestimated our independence and
self-sufficiency". Much individual valuing presupposes that "Humanity
itself as an ongoing historical project provides the implicit frame of
reference" (Lecture II, part 2). So we have reasons to attend to future
generations "simply because they matter so much to us", beyond justice
and responsibility.

## Evidence

- Imagined reactions, granted to be conjectural and stipulated as "the
  afterlife conjecture" (Lecture I, part 9).
- The infertile world, where no one dies early, would still be a
  catastrophe, so the concern is not only for loved ones (Lecture I,
  parts 7–8).
- Caregiver robots would not remove the catastrophe, so it is not egoism
  after all (Lecture I, part 9).
- Exceptions: relief from pain, probably friendship, and games (Lecture
  II, part 1).

## What it bears on

QUESTION-301 holds Scheffler's reading, dependence on a future we will
not see, as one of its three, reworded to match this reading, and its
note of 2026-10-29 says "Scheffler is less hard a case than filed
(NOTE-1298; NOTE-1306)".

QUESTION-299 holds the ground that "Scheffler grounds concern for the
future in what the living value". The lectures state that ground:
reasons concerning future generations that come from what matters to us,
not only from obligations to them.
