# QUESTION-063: When a collective outcome such as synchrony, or the fall of a regulated quantity, can come from a shared external drive, from coupling among the members or from different internal routes, what measurement tells which architecture produced it?

## The question

The record reads a system's architecture from its coupling. Mutual coupling makes peers, one-way drive makes a hierarchy ([CLAIM-172](../claims.d/CLAIM-172.md), [CLAIM-167](../claims.d/CLAIM-167.md)). Often only an outcome is observed: units in phase, or a shared quantity falling. The question is what must be measured to tell common drive from coupling. A second part asks how to tell one route to an outcome from another.

## Why the record needs it

A timing test that sees only phase alignment would pass units that are synchronized by a common cause and never coupled. [CLAIM-172](../claims.d/CLAIM-172.md)'s criterion requires that "each side's timing shifts with the other's". That calls for an intervention on one member, which the record has not specified. [QUESTION-033](QUESTION-033.md) wants drive apportioned among members. An outcome reached by several routes cannot apportion drive until the members are measured separately. These are direct cases of multi-unit biological systems, the kind [QUESTION-035](QUESTION-035.md) must handle. They are not analogies.

## What the record holds

- [THEORY-129](../theory.d/THEORY-129.md): in coral spawning, colonies spawn together because each responds to "the natural night-light regime". Displacing it "delays or abolishes" release. It "shows synchrony by a shared cue, not coupling between colonies". Limit: one species, small groups, and coupling is untested, not excluded.
- [THEORY-130](../theory.d/THEORY-130.md): coral bleaching is "reached by more than one route". The same fall in symbiont density came from fewer symbionts per host cell in one species and from the loss of host cells in another. "Only measuring host and symbiont together showed it." Limit: reviews and one octocoral study. It says the exchange is regulated from more than one side, and "nothing about agency follows".
- [THEORY-275](../theory.d/THEORY-275.md) ([LIT-947](../literature.d/LIT-947.md), [NOTE-788](../notes.d/NOTE-788.md)): between two symmetrically coupled colloids, directional information measures follow the path of a recorded drive, not the symmetry of the coupling. Under strong drive of one unit a reciprocal coupling reads as one-way flow. It answers the question in part, negatively: an observational test of direction can misreport the coupling. Limit: the measures follow a recorded drive on one unit. No shared unrecorded drive is tested, which is this question's case.
- [THEORY-276](../theory.d/THEORY-276.md) ([LIT-960](../literature.d/LIT-960.md), [NOTE-775](../notes.d/NOTE-775.md)): in a network of identical units, the orbits of the graph's symmetry group are the synchronization clusters, and one cluster can lose synchrony while the others keep it. Limit: the paper never mentions external drive. Reading such a cluster pattern as a coupling signature that a shared drive does not predict is the record's inference, valid only for near-identical units on a known graph.
- [LIT-933](../literature.d/LIT-933.md) (Nowak et al., [NOTE-768](../notes.d/NOTE-768.md)) is an instance of the conflation this question names. It counts synchrony "through their mutual influence or through their common response to an external signal" as the same functional unit.

## What would answer it

An intervention design for the record's candidate systems that separates the possibilities. Displace the shared drive for one member only and see whether the others follow. Perturb one member and measure the others. With that, a statement of which of [QUESTION-035](QUESTION-035.md)'s tests can and cannot see the difference.
