## 2. The argument: arguments that drift, records that anchor, inquiries that respecify

This section reports the account of inquiry that the record holds, the account the predecessor manuscript began. It is not a new argument. Each step cites the entry that holds it, with that entry's standing: *Active* means the record accepts it, and *Proposed* means it is filed and argued but not adopted.

**Provenance.** Much of this account was developed in exchanges between the owner and ChatGPT, which the owner then filed. Several steps were proposed by the assistant while it was reading luria and this record:

- the inquiry tuple, from turns A15 and A16 of the exchange of 27 October (TERM-095);
- the reading of luria's two loops, also A16 (CLAIM-470);
- the CEGAR analogy.

CLAIM-470 itself names this record's grammar change, as measured in CASE-178, as its instance. The account is therefore not independent of the system the paper studies. We use it as an **interpretive framework**: a vocabulary for describing what happened, not a theory the case tests. Section 8 says what the case can and cannot bear on, and §10 lists this among the threats to validity.

### 2.1 What fails: an argument loses its identity, not its structure

An argument developed across many sessions with collaborators who keep no memory between them does not typically fail for lack of structure. It fails by losing the identity of what was said. Concepts are redefined silently, objections come back as though new, and the thesis changes with no turn arguing for the change. CLAIM-177 (Active, empirical) records this across six exchanges between one owner and one family of assistants. Its generalisation beyond that population, CLAIM-483, is Proposed.

CLAIM-324 (Active) is the documented instance. A manuscript drafted straight from an argument's matured state changed the thesis "with no turn arguing the move" and dropped 24 of the argument's items, none on request.

Two refinements matter below:

- **Maturing drops things legitimately** (TERM-052). An absent item is either dissolution or loss, and only a record can tell which.
- **An inquiry can drift in its question** as well as its concepts (TERM-056): a provisional answer can become part of what is being asked.

CLAIM-081 (Active) adds a third. Forgetting the origin is not drifting: a chain of restatements can converge on something stable and still be far from where it started, so stability is not preservation.

The record holds three Proposed mechanisms for the drift (CLAIM-180, CLAIM-191, CLAIM-192), not yet discriminated by any test. This paper depends on none of them.

### 2.2 What helps: anchors

The predecessor's metaphor is crystal nucleation: later structure grows only on structure that persists. CLAIM-185 (Proposed) states the analogue for arguments. Three further steps say what persistence requires:

- **It is relative to stipulated identity.** What counts as "the same claim persisting" is fixed by an amendable decision, not discovered (CLAIM-195, Active).
- **Addressability, not storage, makes an anchor** (CLAIM-182, Proposed, empirical). A transcript stores everything and anchors nothing. Content becomes an anchor once its parts can be pointed at.
- **A trigger is needed.** The record's *second-occurrence rule* codes a thing when it turns up a second time, catching drift the first time it could occur without flooding the record (CLAIM-176, Proposed; ADR-032, later confined to the record's process by ADR-072).

Two limits qualify anchoring from the start. First, **persistence is not productivity** (CLAIM-595, Active, demonstrated). Two intermediate states of an assembly can have the same expected residence time and different chances of leading to the target, and a state can persist while making the target less likely. A productive anchor is judged against a declared target, horizon and comparator (TERM-117), and the record declares none for an argument. As the record puts it, "a persisting trap is still built on".

Second, **an anchor is not shared knowledge.** A shared record can show who proposed something and what standing it has. It cannot show what collaborators believe, or that each knows its current standing (CLAIM-668, Proposed). For memoryless collaborators, access to the record is a precondition of coordinating on it, not evidence that they have.

### 2.3 What anchoring costs

- **Entrenchment.** An early anchor can lock in its framing (CLAIM-184, Proposed). Retirement by status is a remedy only if superseded anchors stop attracting new structure. A count on 6 November found that, on this record, they do.
- **Who pays.** Design-rationale capture was largely abandoned because its cost fell on one person and its benefit on another (LIT-757). When a memoryless agent files, capture and benefit fall to the same party (CLAIM-179, Proposed). "The disparity does not disappear. It moves" to the owner's review, which is why flooding becomes the failure mode.
- **When filing pays.** Filing moves work across time, so it is worth its cost only when expected reuse covers it (CLAIM-303, Active). Where reuse cannot be forecast, a break-even rule is within a factor of two of the hindsight optimum (CLAIM-308, Active, demonstrated). That rule coincides with the second-occurrence rule only while filing costs between one and two reconstructions' overpayment. Beyond that, the two justifications part, and the cost rule prices none of the drift in between (CLAIM-630, Proposed, demonstrated).

### 2.4 What a record of inquiry can secure

If maturing legitimately drops things, a record cannot aim at preservation. What it can secure is *traceability*: change that is announced and located (CLAIM-189, Proposed, filed as a counter to an earlier claim). Traceability is defined over the argument's *accountability structure*: what was asserted, why, how it was challenged and what justified its revision (CLAIM-250, Proposed).

Demonstrated results fix what such a record is not (SYNTHESIS-017, Proposed, gathers them). Current conclusions do not fix how to revise (CLAIM-294), nor does the topology of the support graph (CLAIM-293). A sufficient state stops being sufficient when a source is discredited or the decisions change (CLAIM-300). And sufficiency, accountability and correctness each come apart from the other two (CLAIM-299). CASE-084 (Active) is the vivid case: a community whose record traces every commitment perfectly, and whose vocabulary excludes the right explanation.

A kept record of justifications therefore buys, at most, a wider family of decisions and decisions a bounded agent could not otherwise afford (CLAIM-295, Proposed). Two limits follow for the record studied here:

- **Retiring an entry decides nothing about what rests on it.** The record reports the dependants and leaves each to a judgment. It computes no withdrawals (CLAIM-664, Active). Traceability stops short of truth maintenance.
- **A manuscript is a view over the record, not its only copy** (CLAIM-178, Proposed). This paper is meant to be such a view.

### 2.5 Inquiry as specification

The record's most general account is the owner's: "inquiry is a form of specification" (CLAIM-470, Active). A question fixes which differences would count as an answer. An inquiry adds operations that make those differences accessible. Its findings can stabilize into specifications that constrain later inquiry. The difference from specification is that **an inquiry can revise what an answer could be**.

TERM-095 writes an inquiry as a tuple with six components: a language, an answer space, interventions, observations, criteria and resources (Σ, 𝒜, 𝓘, 𝓞, 𝓚, 𝓑). An inquiry is *reflexive* when one of these becomes its object.

CLAIM-470 itself distinguishes two ways an inquiry can change its regime, and this paper keeps them apart:

- **Refinement** adds distinctions within an existing language and answer space. Counterexample-guided abstraction refinement is the formal case: an abstraction is split exactly where a spurious counterexample shows it too coarse (LIT-1652).
- **Revision** changes what counts as an answer, a criterion, or a unit of inquiry.

CLAIM-470's own proposed test compares a fixed, a refinable and a revisable regime (§9.4). The claim's distinctive clause concerns revision.

CLAIM-419's notes record two disanalogies with CEGAR:

- **CEGAR's misfit is certified.** A spurious counterexample can be checked against the concrete model, so every merge that matters surfaces. A record has no concrete model to check against, so a move it forced silently leaves no trace. That is the source of the selection problem in §5.
- **CEGAR only splits.** The record's grammar has also grown by adding types beside old ones, and once by withdrawing one.

Two Active claims bound this account:

- **Specifying is itself resource-bounded** (CLAIM-475). The questions an agent can pose are a product of what it has already built, its *inquiry frontier* (TERM-092). A stabilized specification is stored inquiry that makes some questions cheaper and others harder. That is entrenchment reached through cost.
- **A vocabulary can make a constraint inexpressible**, which is different from false or unknown (TERM-091). Composition can force this. Eliminating a system's interior can create a coupling its parts never needed (CLAIM-480, Active; QUESTION-283, Open).

The record names Dewey (LIT-1645) and Collingwood (LIT-1651) as the nearest predecessors. It has read neither.

### 2.6 The record as its own apparatus

The predecessor's own objection, CLAIM-181 (Proposed, filed as an objection), was adapted from Shipman and Marshall's "Formality Considered Harmful" (LIT-756). A record's closed grammar formalizes early, so a move that fits no type or relation "is forced into one or lost until the misfit is noticed and the grammar amended to hold it."

CLAIM-419 (Active, empirical) records what happened in practice. Noticed misfits were repeatedly treated as defects of the grammar and answered by amending it. Its own caveat is that whether every misfit is caught "is not shown".

Two further claims constrain how the grammar works:

- **A typed relation is more rigid than either of its ends** (CLAIM-479, Active, demonstrated). Its rigidity is a fact about the grammar, not evidence that the dependency it records is sound. CASE-131 is the instance: a fixed converse made the record assert an inference nobody had made.
- **A migration is faithful when it preserves the accountability structure, not the old graph** (CLAIM-666, Proposed). Where the old grammar forced a relation nobody asserted, a faithful migration removes it (ARG-096).

TERM-089 defines an *apparatus* operationally, by four functions that can come apart: it differentiates, retains, enforces and revises distinctions. CLAIM-469 (Active) holds that luria is such an apparatus for the record's objects. The stronger, constitutive reading (CLAIM-476) is Proposed, and nothing here depends on it. TERM-089's gloss is the caution this paper carries: "A self-modifying apparatus is not necessarily an epistemically self-correcting apparatus."

### 2.7 The framework this paper uses

The predecessor asked what makes an argument developed with memoryless collaborators settle, and what a project memory contributes (QUESTION-037). Its answer was anchors. We join the steps above into four statements. They are this paper's integrations. The record holds their parts, not the statements themselves.

- **F1. Anchors are written in a grammar, and the grammar is part of the inquiry.** What can persist, be addressed and be traced is fixed by the record's types, fields, vocabularies and relations. In TERM-095's terms, the grammar realizes the language and much of the answer space and criteria.
- **F2. Changing the grammar is the inquiry acting on its own regime.** It is refinement when it adds distinctions and revision when it changes what counts as an answer, a criterion or a unit of inquiry.
- **F3. Traceability should reach the grammar.** If a record secures announced and located change for its claims, it should do so for its grammar. Otherwise drift recurs one level up, as a word that silently changes sense. CLAIM-666 supplies the criterion for a faithful migration.
- **F4. The costs of anchoring apply to the grammar.** A grammar change costs something to build, to migrate to and to maintain (CLAIM-569). A stabilized grammar can entrench (CLAIM-475, CLAIM-184), and can persist without being productive (CLAIM-595).

F1 and F2 are definitional: they say how we describe the record. F3 is normative. F4 is a claim about costs, part of which §8 can test. The empirical questions the paper does test are stated in §3.6, each with what would count against it.
