# QUESTION-365: When a conflict among an agent's processes is settled neither by local accommodation nor by escalation, can it be settled by another organization taking up the demand the agent's arbitration leaves ungranted, and what would tell that apart from suppression of the demand or a change in the agent's own arbitration?

## Why it is a question

The record has two routes by which an agent settles a recurring conflict
among its processes. They settle it among themselves, or they escalate it
to a process that mediates or overrides ([CLAIM-358](../claims.d/CLAIM-358.md)). Either way the
conflict is settled inside the agent, and [CLAIM-080](../claims.d/CLAIM-080.md) keeps the will
account there: it asks how a given agent's efficacy is organized.

The readings filed on 2026-10-31 describe a third route, outward.

- **The parent's example.** "Feeling a sudden urge to do something about
  the climate crisis while having never been an activist can raise the
  question: 'How can I go on as the person I know to be "myself" now that
  I feel this urge?'" To resolve it, "the person may enlist friends with
  complementary skills and start a local nonprofit", incorporating the
  urge "into a broader individuation process" ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), p. 1356).
- **The mechanism.** Agency occurs when one process takes up "the excess
  action of another ... (the feelings you cannot deal with, unchanneled
  energy, skills in search of an outlet, etc.)" (p. 1351;
  [THEORY-1280](../theory.d/THEORY-1280.md)).
- **Its source.** Bencherki (2017, [LIT-1901](../literature.d/LIT-1901.md), p. 781) quotes Simondon:
  an individual "does not exhaust the tensions that allowed its
  constitution", and from that load "man (sic) seeks his kindred to
  constitute a group" ([THEORY-1285](../theory.d/THEORY-1285.md)).

In the record's terms, "feelings you cannot deal with" and "skills in
search of an outlet" are readiness claiming more precedence than it is
granted: [CLAIM-484](../claims.d/CLAIM-484.md)'s gap. The readings' route grants the claim elsewhere,
in an organization the agent joins or forms, and the agent's own
arbitration need not change. The record's nearest case runs the other
way. [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md) places authority over a demand in another person, the
friend who refuses the password; here the demand itself is met in
another organization.

The readings give no criterion for telling this route from two others
that look the same from outside:

- **suppression:** the claim falls, and nothing grants it;
- **internal re-grant:** the agent's own will-organization changes so
  that the demand now has authority there, and joining the collective
  is how it acts.

## What would count as an answer

A case in which the marks of an agent's conflict ([CLAIM-484](../claims.d/CLAIM-484.md):
interruption, oscillation, a costly override) fall after the demand gains
authority in another organization in which the agent takes part. In it,
the demand's claim stays as it was and the agent's own grants are
recorded as unchanged. A contrasting case would be one in which the
marks fall because the claim did, or because the agent's grants moved.
Or an argument that the third route always reduces to one of the other
two once the collective is counted as part of the agent. That would
dissolve the question and bear on where [CLAIM-080](../claims.d/CLAIM-080.md)'s agent ends.

It borders [QUESTION-364](QUESTION-364.md). If the person in the parent's example goes on
as "myself" by relocating the urge rather than revising their
will-organization, it is not yet clear whether that counts as
continuation.
