# NOTE-1609: Vipond — Multiparameter persistence landscapes

## Contribution

A stable, computable and interpretable vectorization of the rank invariant
of multiparameter persistence modules, extending Bubenik's one-parameter
landscape: "In this paper we generalise the persistence landscape to
multiparameter persistence modules providing a stable representation of
the rank invariant" (abstract). Being functions in a Banach space,
landscapes can be averaged, tested and fed to kernels.

## Key insight

Restrict to lines. "the multiparameter landscape of a persistence module M
indexed over Rn is characterized by the fact that it restricts to the
single parameter landscape function of the restriction of M to every line
parallel to the main diagonal vector" (§1). One-parameter tools then apply
line by line, at the price of seeing only what the rank invariant sees.

## Assumptions

- Modules indexed by Rⁿ with the product order, pointwise
  finite-dimensional, finitely presented for injectivity; field
  coefficients.
- The ∞-norm ball and a choice of rescaling w; only the rank invariant
  enters.
- For the statistics, i.i.d. samples with finite moments in a separable
  Banach space.

## Key results

- **The difficulty** (§1): "unlike the single parameter case where we may
  associate a persistence diagram to a module, there is not an analogous
  complete discrete invariant in the multiparameter setting", and "The PH
  theory becomes wildly more complicated when the family of topological
  spaces on our data set is indexed by multiple parameters."
- **Sublevel-set modules** (Example 1): for f : X → Rⁿ, "Xa = {x ∈ X :
  f (x)i < ai ∀ i = 1, ..., n}".
- **The landscape** (Definition 19): "The multiparameter persistence
  landscape considers the maximal radius over which k features persist in
  every (positive) direction through x in the parameter space".
- **Stability** (Theorem 30): "the ∞-landscape distance of the
  multiparameter persistence landscapes is bounded by the interleaving
  distance dI"; for sublevel-set modules, d∞ ≤ ‖f − g‖∞ (Corollary 31).
- **The interleaving distance** (§2): "This pseudo-metric has been shown to
  be the most discriminating, stable metric on multiparameter persistence
  modules", and "The interleaving distance has been shown to be NP-hard to
  compute".
- **Injectivity** (§4.2, Proposition 36): "the collection of weighted
  landscapes associated to a module preserves almost all the information
  contained in the rank invariant of finitely presented persistence
  modules."
- **The paper's proofs, repaired** (read 2026-11-06; second check by the
  status review's coordinator). Theorem 30's own proof is complete. Three
  slips elsewhere, none affecting a stated result:
  1. *Proposition 28.* Its last line, λw(M)(k, x) = λ1(M ∘ ϕw)(k, w⁻¹x),
     with ϕw(x) = (wᵢxᵢ)ᵢ (Definition 27), is false: on Example 3 it gives
     λw(N)(1, (5, 1.5)) = 0.5, where the paper reports 0.1. From Definition
     25, ‖h‖w∞ = ‖(wᵢhᵢ)ᵢ‖∞; with h = w⁻¹ ⊙ t the points x ± h become
     w⁻¹ ⊙ (w ⊙ x ± t), so the identity is
     λw(M)(k, x) = λ1(M ∘ ϕ_{w⁻¹})(k, w ⊙ x).
  2. *Corollary 33.* Its proof says "(ϕw )∗ (M ), (ϕw )∗ (N ) are also
     ε-interleaved". That fails for M ∘ ϕw in general but holds for
     M ∘ ϕ_{w⁻¹}, the module the corrected identity uses, since ε·maxᵢ wᵢ
     = ε. The corollary also follows directly by Theorem 30's argument,
     since ‖h + ε1‖w∞ ≤ ‖h‖w∞ + ε.
  3. *Proposition 36.* Its proof ("For all a < b there is some unit
     rescaling vector w such that the pair of parameter values (ϕw(a),
     ϕw(b)) lie on a line of slope 1") needs a and b to differ in every
     coordinate. For the rest: a finitely presented module has finitely
     many generator and relation grades, each copy Pn(−g) supported on
     {x ≥ g}, so M_x → M_{x+δ1} is an isomorphism for small δ > 0, and
     β^{a,b} = β^{a,b+δ1}. The same step is used silently in Proposition
     24's converse.

  With these repairs Theorem 30, Corollary 33 and Proposition 36 hold as
  stated (scratch/formal/vipond_checks.py).
- **The limit** (§7): "We have restricted our invariant to the
  discriminating power of the rank invariant", with features "born at
  incomparable" parameter values named as beyond it.

## Concepts

- **Multiparameter persistence module**: an Rⁿ-graded module over the
  monoid ring of ([0, ∞)ⁿ, +), equivalently a functor Rⁿ → Vect
  (Defs. 1–2).
- **Fibered barcode**: the barcodes of restrictions to positively sloped
  lines (Def. 3).
- **Rank invariant** β^{a,b} = dim Im(M_a → M_b) (Def. 16).
- **Interleaving distance** (Appendix A.2); interval-decomposable modules,
  bottleneck and persistence-weighted Wasserstein distances (Defs. 4–9).
- **Landscape** (Def. 19) and weighted landscape λ_w.

## Connections

Extends Bubenik's one-parameter persistence landscape; computes with RIVET
on two-parameter (scale, density or codensity) bifiltrations. Botnan &
Lesnick's survey ([LIT-2022](../literature.d/LIT-2022.md), [NOTE-1568](NOTE-1568.md)) places it as one
vectorization among several and states the classification difficulty more
fully.

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization** (Response 3, §5). A: "Multiparameter persistent
  homology is a developed mathematical field, with invariants and stability
  results, although its classification is more complicated than that of
  one-parameter persistence." A's sentence, not the paper's. Right, and
  understated: the paper says no complete discrete invariant exists, and
  Botnan & Lesnick that the modules are of wild representation type, so a
  full classification is regarded as hopeless; the interleaving distance,
  the natural metric, is NP-hard to compute. A says nothing about
  computation.
- **A's module** b ⟼ H_k(Q_z(b)) is the paper's Example 1 with f the vector
  of resource requirements (the paper uses strict inequality, A uses ≤; an
  inessential difference).
- **What the invariants see.** A's sentence leaves out that the standard
  invariants are incomplete. *The reader's interpretation, stated in
  neither paper:* for [CLAIM-585](../claims.d/CLAIM-585.md)'s two paths with peaks (3, 1) and
  (1, 3), the endpoint components merge at two incomparable budgets. The
  rank invariant registers each merge, along comparable pairs such as a
  budget below both and (3, 1); but the frontier {(3, 1), (1, 3)} as one
  object is the kind of structure that has no barcode-like summary in
  general.
- **[LIT-2047](../literature.d/LIT-2047.md), [NOTE-1616](NOTE-1616.md), [THEORY-1420](../theory.d/THEORY-1420.md)** (sublevel-set persistence
  of energy landscapes, one parameter): supports and extends. [LIT-2047](../literature.d/LIT-2047.md)'s
  status note records multiparameter persistence as mentioned once and not
  treated; this is the multiparameter version of that construction, with
  stability.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md)** (one capacity's threshold as a merge level): supports;
  the one-parameter, zero-dimensional case of Example 1.
- **[CLAIM-585](../claims.d/CLAIM-585.md)** (a Pareto frontier of sufficient budgets): supports and
  qualifies. The multiparameter sublevel module is the right object, and
  its merges need not reduce to one threshold, as the claim says; but the
  standard summaries are incomplete, so none is guaranteed to recover the
  frontier.
- **[CLAIM-555](../claims.d/CLAIM-555.md)** (the compatibility complex as a nerve of sublevel sets in
  learning): related; a multiparameter filtration by demand, to which
  Corollary 31's stability would apply.
- **Direction.** A: "Topology records what is connected, not necessarily
  what can be computationally or dynamically traversed." Consistent with
  the paper, which is about the topology of sublevel sets and says nothing
  about paths, direction or effort.
- Filed: [THEORY-1430](../theory.d/THEORY-1430.md). A method paper in a machine-learning venue, but it
  carries no instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- **The author's** (§7). Only the rank invariant is seen; how the landscape
  distance relates to the interleaving distance, beyond the bound, is open;
  the weighting w is a hyperparameter; whether applications need
  multiparameter complexity is open; computation relies on RIVET and
  discretization.
- **Scope.** No treatment of direction, paths or the cost of traversal.
- **This reading.** §6 and Appendix B not read; neither bears on
  THEORY-1430's title. The repairs above are the reader's, checked by the
  coordinator, not the paper's.
