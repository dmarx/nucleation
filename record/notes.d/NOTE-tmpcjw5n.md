# NOTE-tmpcjw5n: Toner and Tu 1998, read for long-range order in two-dimensional flocks, the argument for exact exponents, and scaling through the ordered phase

## Contribution

The full derivation behind the 1995 letter (LIT-1670): the λ₂ and λ₃
terms missed in 1995, which leave d = 2 intact but leave d > 2
undetermined (p. 11); a new, symmetry-based argument for the d = 2
exponents; an anisotropic model with exact exponents for 2 ≤ d ≤ 4; and
a list of measurements. The reading is THEORY-tmpdfeto.

## Key results

1. **Order in d = 2** (abstract, p. 2): "flocks exhibit a true,
   long-range ordered, spontaneously broken symmetry state, in contrast
   to equilibrium systems, which cannot spontaneously break a continuous
   symmetry in d = 2 (the "Mermin-Wagner" theorem)"; and (p. 4) "this
   result does not, in fact, violate the Mermin-Wagner theorem, since
   flocks are a non-equilibrium dynamical system."
2. **The linear theory is the equilibrium case** (§IV, p. 27): the
   infrared divergence "signifies the destruction of long ranged order in
   the linearized model by fluctuations, even for arbitrarily small noise
   ∆, in spatial dimensions d ≤ 2", the integral diverging logarithmically
   in d = 2; it is "very reminiscent of the familiar
   Mermin-Wagner-Hohenberg" theorem. Power counting gives χ = 1 − d/2
   (eq. 5.4) and λ₁, λ₂, σ₂ relevant below d = 4.
3. **The mechanism** (p. 4): below d = 4 "this slow, diffusive transport
   of information is replaced by direct, convective transport … There is
   a sort of "negative feedback," in that this improved transport
   actually suppresses the very fluctuations that give rise to it".
4. **Exponents** (eq. 1.20, p. 10): z = 6/5, ζ = 3/5, χ = −1/5 in d = 2,
   "exact and universal for all flocks with the simple symmetries we
   discussed". For d > 2, "we do not know which of the above
   possibilities holds" (p. 12).
5. **How d = 2 is made exact** (§V.C, pp. 46–50). Every relevant vertex
   is ∝ q⊥ (the L–T cross terms dropped as irrelevant, the L–L term a
   total derivative), so ∆, D∥ and Dρ are not renormalized; at λ₁ = λρ
   the equations have "an exact symmetry which we call pseudo-Galilean
   invariance" (eqs. 5.76–5.77), so λ₁ is not renormalized; with exact
   mass conservation this gives χ = 1 − z (eq. 5.79) at a "type II" fixed
   point. That every flock flows there: "We consider it highly probable
   that this is, in fact, the case" (p. 48); then "So all models in d = 2
   must have the canonical exponents" (p. 50).
6. **Density fluctuations** (pp. 7–8, eqs. 1.10–1.11): they "become
   infinitely bigger than those in a fluid or an ideal gas"; Cρ(q)
   diverges as q → 0, as q⊥^(−6/5) for q∥ ≪ q⊥ in d = 2, unlike any
   equilibrium system's, "which goes to a finite constant (the
   compressibility)". The paper writes no ΔN ∝ ⟨N⟩^φ, no exponent φ, and
   not the phrase "giant number fluctuations" (searched).
7. **Goldstone modes, by name** (abstract): the ""Goldstone modes""
   of the broken rotational symmetry "mix with modes associated with
   conservation of bird number to produce propagating sound modes".
8. **Damping** (p. 9): z < 2 for all d < 4, so "sound modes are much more
   heavily damped at long wavelengths in flocks than in most [7]
   equilibrium condensed matter systems".
9. **Ordered phase, not transition** (§VII, p. 64): keep ∆ "well below
   the critical value"; otherwise, within the correlation length, "the
   scaling properties of the flock will be controlled by the fixed point
   that controls the order-disorder transition, not the low temperature
   fixed point we have studied here."
10. **A compass ends it** (p. 63): it "will introduce a "mass" that makes
    any fluctuation away from the pre-picked direction of flock motion
    decay rapidly", so "there are no interesting fluctuations left at
    long distances and times."
11. **A possible fixed line** (§V.D, p. 52): continuously varying
    exponents, "somewhat reminiscent of that of the d = 2 equilibrium
    X − Y model", though "not associated in any way with the absence of
    true long ranged orientational order"; one of three possibilities.
12. **No Galilean invariance** (pp. 16–17): "birds move through a
    resistive medium, which provides a special Galilean reference
    frame"; "In contrast to the Navier-Stokes equation, there is no
    conservation of momentum".
13. **Tests** (pp. 64, 70): Vicsek et al. "probably never explored" the
    regime where the nonlinearities matter; of later simulations, "good
    agreement with our prediction has been reached" (ref. [12], not
    read).

## Assumptions

Rotation invariance the only symmetry; bird number conserved;
short-ranged interactions and short-correlated Gaussian noise; lowest
order in gradients; coefficients taken constant; deep in the ordered
phase and far from boundaries. The equations are posited from symmetry,
not coarse-grained from a rule ("This approach allows us to bury our
ignorance in a few phenomenological parameters", p. 18). The d = 2
exactness rests on item 5's steps.

## Concepts

Spontaneously broken rotational symmetry; Goldstone modes; breakdown of
linearized hydrodynamics below d = 4; dynamical RG; exponents χ, ζ, z;
pseudo-Galilean invariance; anisotropic sound; divergent density
structure factor; hyperdiffusion; fixed line.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-841** (ordered active matter): **supports the row, not its
  formula.** Anomalous scaling holds through the ordered phase, at "the
  low temperature fixed point", apart from the transition's (item 9).
  But the row's ΔN ∝ ⟨N⟩^φ is a later restatement, not this paper's,
  which predicts a diverging structure factor (item 6); and A's "some
  initially predicted exponents differ from those obtained in
  large-scale simulations" is not in it, which reports "good agreement"
  (item 13) (ARG-tmpdc2qe). Also supports the folded-in "explicitly
  breaking that symmetry can introduce a characteristic scale" (item
  10), and qualifies the XY row: a fixed line need not mean
  quasi-long-range order (item 11, one possibility of three).
- **THEORY-1095**: **qualifies.** Its two unproved steps (a cubic vertex
  staying irrelevant; z < 2 "on physical grounds") are the 1995
  letter's. The 1998 argument (item 5) replaces them with others: the
  L–T cross terms negligible, only λ₁, λ₂ and σ₂ among the
  nonlinearities, and the "highly probable" flow to type II. The
  reader's observation, not the paper's: eq. (5.8) gives
  γₙ = n + (1 − n)d/2 = 1 for every n in d = 2, so every pressure term
  σₙ(δρ)ⁿ is relevant there by power counting; they are ⊥ gradients, so
  the non-renormalization of ∆ and D∥ survives, but the type I/II
  analysis treats σ₂ alone.
- **THEORY-1085**: **supports and corrects.** The slaving of δv∥
  (eqs. 3.1–3.2, p. 19) is its "eliminated degree of freedom". Its "What
  this does not say" holds that the paper does not use "Goldstone mode";
  the 1998 paper does (item 7).
- **CLAIM-842**: **qualifies its scope.** The linear theory has CLAIM-842's
  logarithmic divergence in d = 2 (item 2), and the nonlinear,
  non-equilibrium theory removes it (items 1, 3). So the d ≤ 2 criterion
  is a theorem of the Gaussian, equilibrium premise, not of the
  geometry alone: a worked case where relaxing CLAIM-842's own
  equilibrium caveat reverses the conclusion. The mechanism (convection
  changing who interacts with whom) has no stated analogue in training.
- **QUESTION-508**: **a partial answer for flocks**: symmetry plus
  conservation. Rotation invariance forbids a mass for v⊥ (a compass
  restores one, item 10), number conservation makes δρ slow, and the
  fixed point is reached without tuning deep in the ordered phase
  (item 9).
- **CLAIM-tmpvq33w**: one of its three cases, through items 5 and 10.
- **CLAIM-478**: **supports**, through the slaving of δv∥ (p. 19), as
  THEORY-1085 records for 1995.
- **CLAIM-846**: **parallel.** The flock's scale-free content is in
  correlation functions and structure factors, not in a distribution.
- **TERM-139**: background; the terms are used in its sense (pp. 18, 29).
- **QUESTION-515**: background; it names what an active-matter model must

## Limitations

The equations are posited, not derived from any rule; the bridge is the
universality hypothesis. The d = 2 exactness rests on argued steps, and
d > 2 is undetermined by the authors' own account. The simulations cited
are not shown. The scaling functions are not calculated. Bulk only. The
reader skimmed §V.B's algebra and §VI.
