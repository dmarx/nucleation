# LIT-tmp2r8ig: Foundations for a Social Ontology

Amie L. Thomasson (2003), *ProtoSociology* 18/19 (Understanding the Social II – Philosophy of Sociality), pp. 269–290 — DOI-10.5840/protosociology200318/199

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the works filling out
the record's coverage of social ontology under the topic [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added.

`Deferred` because no lawful full text was reachable, so it is not read and no

**The abstract**, verbatim from the Philosophy Documentation Center: "The
existence of a social world raises both the metaphysical puzzle: how can there
be a 'reality' of facts and objects that are genuinely created by human
intentionality? and the epistemological puzzle: how can such a product of
human intentionality include objective facts available for investigation and
discovery by the social sciences? I argue that Searle's story about the
creation of social facts in *The Construction of Social Reality* is too narrow
to fully solve either side of the puzzle. By acknowledging different forms of
rules for constructing social reality paralleling rules for creating
'make-believe' truths, we can build a more comprehensive social ontology and
allow for a more appropriate range of discovery for the social sciences.
Nonetheless, I argue that despite the parallels between methods for
constructing make-believe and social facts, it would be mistaken to treat talk
about social reality as involving a mere pretense to refer."

**Where it sits.** Its target is Searle's social ontology (*The Construction of
Social Reality*, [LIT-tmpnf4e6](LIT-tmpnf4e6.md), filed unread in the same batch; its 2006
restatement, [LIT-tmpkai94](LIT-tmpkai94.md), is read). That is a
different Searle subject from the Chinese Room the record already holds
([LIT-400](LIT-400.md)). The epistemological puzzle is Durkheim's methodological question
([LIT-tmpl1nlb](LIT-tmpl1nlb.md)) in a later vocabulary: how social facts, made by people, can be
objects of discovery. Ritchie ([LIT-tmppeiud](LIT-tmppeiud.md)) cites later Thomasson papers
("Social Entities", 2009, and "The Ontology of Social Groups") and thanks
Thomasson for the amendment that norm-bound members count as members (its
n. 21); she does not cite this one. Nothing seen bears on the group-agency or
group-mind works.

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary. `metaphysics` holds the reality
puzzle, `epistemology` and `philosophy-of-science` hold the question of
discovery in the social sciences.
