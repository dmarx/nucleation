# CLAIM-tmp6yhlh: Two forms of assistance that deliver the same outcome can leave the person different participation in worthwhile activity, by removing drudgery, by opening an activity of worth to them, or by displacing participation they have reason to value, so assistance, a technology's above all, is assessed by the participation it enables or displaces as well as by its outcomes

## The claim

A9 §IV, after Wolf and Rosa:

> **The value of technological assistance should be assessed not only by
> the quality of the outcomes it produces, but also by the forms of human
> participation it enables or displaces.**

"The philosophical distinction is between meaningful participation and mere possession of an outcome." A9 §XI gives three functions of automation:

- "**Liberating automation:** Removes drudgery, danger, or barriers while preserving or expanding opportunities for worthwhile engagement."
- "**Enabling automation:** Makes previously inaccessible valuable activities possible, even if the person exercises some capabilities through technological assistance."
- "**Displacing automation:** Replaces forms of human participation that people have reason to value without providing comparable opportunities elsewhere."

"And none is automatically decisive. Even displacing automation may be justified by major benefits in health, safety, or justice."

## Evidence

- **Vallor, CyCon 2013** (THEORY-tmpm76rc, NOTE-tmpgn1lo): a loss of moral skill "would not be rendered insignificant by the emergence of machines that produce equivalent, or arguably even better outcomes", since the intrinsic value of moral skill is "not only paralleled but dwarfed" that of artisanal skill. Equal or better outcomes do not settle the assessment. Corroborates.
- **Vallor, Noema 2024** (THEORY-tmpt34ma, NOTE-tmpl6ia5): "we asked AI developers to help us with the most meaningless tasks in our lives, the ones that drain our energy for everything else that matters", rather than replacing "humane vocations". That is liberating against displacing automation, in her words. Corroborates.
- **Illich, Tools for Conviviality** (THEORY-tmpww16o): "People need new tools to work with rather than tools that “work” for them." The same distinction, between tools that leave the person's activity and tools that replace it. Already corroborates CLAIM-tmp6wuql; here it is a point of contact, no new relation.

## Beside the capability trajectory

CLAIM-tmp6wuql shows that two supports equal in present capability can differ later, where capacity decays with disuse. This claim needs no dynamics: two forms of assistance equal in outcome can differ now, in whether the person takes part in an activity of worth. The two complement each other; neither rests on the other. CLAIM-603, that a capability given up for reliable supply can be a division of labour, is the liberating case.

## What it does not say

- Not that manual work is more meaningful. A9 §IV: participation can be "directing, interpreting, collaborating, appreciating, or caring", not only performing every step.
- Not which participation people have reason to value; that judgment is the person's and is fallible (CLAIM-tmpfyxes).
