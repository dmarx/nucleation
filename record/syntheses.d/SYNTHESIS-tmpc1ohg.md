# SYNTHESIS-tmpc1ohg: What a whole and its parts can owe and claim: the ground of an obligation is kept apart from its bearer, vulnerability and control from integration; control over the terms, not the magnitude of a dependence, is proposed to sort whether a whole's need of a part becomes the part's hold, exploitation and domination are defined relative to a declared standard, and a test is proposed for when dependence is presumptively domination by those who control it

*A part account of the organizational-normativity line, filed at the synthesis review of the line on 2026-11-15, at the owner's word of that day ("file the organizational-normativity summary"). The line's account, SYNTHESIS-038, is built over it and three sibling parts. Every component is new to any account. Four separations the owner asked the record to keep run through it, and the account keeps each component on its own side of them: "organization" in TERM-074's sense 1, a relational arrangement, by default, and not an institution unless a component says so; the ground of an obligation apart from its bearer ("A whole can be the ground or object of a normative judgment without necessarily being the bearer of the corresponding obligation", the owner's review of #488 on 2026-11-13); vulnerability and control apart from integration ("deeper integration need not monotonically increase vulnerability", the same review); and the party with standing apart from parts that can be used but not wronged (TERM-083).*

## The account

### What a whole owes its parts: ground and bearer

- **The question.** QUESTION-460 (the owner's, U5 of "About Plantation
  Paradise"; Open) refines QUESTION-128 and asks whether an organized whole
  grounds norms concerning its own parts and, if it does, who bears the
  obligations: "the whole, where it is the kind of thing that can owe, or
  the agents and roles that maintain, control or benefit from the
  arrangement", and on what ground. Since its version 2 the whole is
  TERM-074's sense 1 by default, and the question has a prior part:
  "whether, and in which sense of obligation, a whole that is not an agent
  can bear obligations at all". Since version 3 the ground and the bearer
  are two inquiries, (a) and (b). Its notes place each reading by sense:
  Lovett and Riedener, Thompson, Fineman, Kittay and Goodin are about
  sense-3 wholes and "do not transfer" to sense 1; Wardrope's biotic
  community is closer to sense 1, and there "the bearer of the obligation
  is the members, as agents, not the whole"; Marcuse answers the bearer
  half for hypostatized wholes and "gives no account of the ground". The
  prior part is "untouched by all of them".
- **The test.** CASE-256 (the owner's, from the review of #488;
  thought experiment, stipulated, Proposed) bears on QUESTION-460: hold a
  person's vulnerability, the controlling actors' capacities and the
  institutional consequences fixed, and vary only whether the person is a
  constituent. Its three results: obligations from membership itself;
  obligations from vulnerability or control regardless of membership; or
  an arrangement that constrains without being a duty-bearer, the
  obligation falling on agents or roles. Its summary: "The first two
  results answer the ground; the third answers the bearer."
- **Vulnerability, not integration.** CLAIM-671 (the owner's proposition,
  stated in the review of #488, replacing A's "The whole's
  responsibilities could therefore grow as its integration deepens";
  supposed, interpretive, Proposed; also transient-support) bears on
  QUESTION-460: where a transformation increases a constituent's
  vulnerability to the discretionary choices of a responsible agent, and
  that vulnerability grounds special obligations under an independently
  justified principle, the transformation can strengthen that agent's
  responsibilities. Its body: integration "is one route by which a
  transformation can increase vulnerability, not the mechanism"; the
  arrangement transformed "can be any sense", and "the bearer must be
  something that can owe, an agent or a role". ARG-099 (Rejected) is the
  tempting reading of Fineman (THEORY-1818), rejected for both titles: her
  vulnerability is universal and constant, and her whole is sense 3, the
  state.

### When a whole's need of a part becomes the part's authority

- **The question.** QUESTION-461 (A's, A6 §8 and A9 §1 of "About Plantation
  Paradise"; Open; also distributed-agency) asks when an organization's
  dependence on participants it subordinates increases their effective
  authority, and when it reproduces the arrangements that keep their
  contributions from becoming effective claims on it. Its four candidate
  answers are A's: bargaining power, a governing coalition's control over
  alternatives and rules, exceptional positions that change nothing
  general, and intervention from outside. Its notes place Prest and the
  H-France Forum (sense 3, the theatre) on the first and third answers,
  Kittay (sense 3, society) on the fourth, and Marcuse on the second
  branch.
- **The case.** CASE-257 (A's report of Powers and Prest at filing;
  specimen, documented as reported, Proposed; also distributed-agency)
  bears on QUESTION-461 and illustrates CLAIM-546, CLAIM-403 and CLAIM-348:
  enslaved musicians, supernumeraries and builders, and a few free
  performers of colour, are documented as contributing to the public
  theatres of colonial Saint-Domingue and as excluded from standing in
  them. Since the owner's review of #488 it separates the documented
  allocation of standing from the mechanism that kept it, which it leaves
  "as an interpretation for QUESTION-461 to test". Its summary: "Its
  dependence is slavery's, which predated the theatre; it is not an
  instance of CLAIM-669." It declares no sense; the readings filed on
  QUESTION-461 treat the theatre as sense 3.
- **What makes the terms unjust.** QUESTION-463 (raised from A's
  "constitutive exploitation", A23 §4 of "Analyze technological
  civilization trap"; Open) refines QUESTION-461: what makes the terms on
  which an organization's sustaining activities are organized unjust, so
  that reproducing them exploits and does not only subordinate. It says
  the record holds candidate standards (CLAIM-669, CLAIM-670, CLAIM-428,
  CLAIM-624 with TERM-083, CLAIM-682) and "none is yet stated as one".

### Exploitation: two routes and a definition

- **Through access.** CLAIM-669 (A's, A9 §2, narrowed at version 2 after
  Seamster and Charron-Chénier were read; thesis, interpretive, Proposed;
  also transient-support) bears on QUESTION-461 and complements CLAIM-670:
  an arrangement can exploit through the access it grants, on terms that
  leave a lasting encumbrance that narrows later exit, so "widening access
  need not reduce it". ARG-100 (Proposed) concludes it from that reading
  (THEORY-1817), a sense-3 case, as weak descriptive support.
- **By producing the dependence.** CLAIM-670 (A's original sense, A9 §2,
  filed at the owner's review of #488; thesis, interpretive, Proposed;
  also transient-support) rests on CLAIM-603, bears on QUESTION-461 and
  complements CLAIM-669: reliable supply can create a reliance the party
  did not have, which the provider can use to set terms the party could
  earlier have refused. The review asked that the two mechanisms, a new
  encumbrance within an existing dependence (CLAIM-669) and the production
  of the dependence (CLAIM-670), be kept apart; CLAIM-669's history,
  version 3, says so. CLAIM-670's summary: "No reading supports it yet."
- **The predicate.** TERM-123 (the assistant's, after A's A6 §2; Proposed):
  exploitation is one party's benefiting from an arrangement on terms
  that, as they bear on another's labour, resources or standing, violate a
  standard declared with the judgment. It applies CLAIM-363's relata and
  "supplies no standard"; it is "Not dependence, not domination
  (TERM-124), and not the bearer of the remedy".
- **The question of identification.** QUESTION-468 (the assistant's, at
  step 12 of the filing of #488, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13; Open)
  refines QUESTION-129 in three parts: what separates exploitation from
  dependence and from domination, which declared standard supplies the
  verdict, and who, if anyone, answers for it when the arrangement is not
  an agent. Its section "Kept apart" holds the ground (part 2) apart from
  the bearer (part 3), and control apart from integration.

### Control over the terms, and domination

- **Control sorts a dependence.** CLAIM-690 (the assistant's, step 12 of
  #488, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13; thesis, interpretive,
  Proposed; also distributed-agency) rests on CLAIM-428, grounds in
  CASE-264, uses TERM-124, and bears on QUESTION-461, QUESTION-460 and
  QUESTION-468: whichever way a whole and its part depend on each other,
  "what sorts it is who controls the terms on which it is met", while
  "the magnitude and the integration that produced it set only how much
  rides on it". With the part able to withdraw or renegotiate, the whole's
  need of it is the part's hold; with the terms at an agent's discretion,
  the part's dependence is vulnerability to that agent, on whom, under a
  principle grounding obligations in such vulnerability, "the strengthened
  responsibility falls". Its summary: "control fixes on whom a
  strengthened responsibility falls, not whether the principle that
  grounds it holds." Sense 1 by default.
- **The contrast.** CASE-264 (the assistant's; thought experiment,
  fictional, Proposed; also distributed-agency) bears on QUESTION-461,
  QUESTION-460 and QUESTION-468: two sense-1 workshops alike in dependence,
  integration and membership, different in who controls the terms;
  "Fictional, to hold fixed what no real case does". ARG-110 (Proposed)
  rests on CLAIM-428 and CLAIM-671, grounds in CASE-264, uses TERM-124 and
  concludes CLAIM-690; it says "that magnitude never sorts a dependence is
  not shown".
- **The predicate.** TERM-124 (the assistant's, after CLAIM-545 and A's
  A6 §2; Proposed): domination is an agent's or role's capacity, used or
  not, to set or change the terms on which a party can act, exit, contest
  or renegotiate, not answerable to the party under a standard declared
  with the judgment. "Its holder must be an agent or role; an arrangement
  with no controller can constrain but does not dominate in this sense."
- **The threshold.** CLAIM-682 (A's, A13 §II of "Analyze technological
  civilization trap"; thesis, interpretive, Proposed, version 3) bears on
  QUESTION-461 and complements CLAIM-428 and CLAIM-669: a dependence is
  presumptively dominating by those who control it when participation in
  an important sphere rests on it, its controllers hold consequential
  discretion, and the dependent lacks effective means of protection,
  influence, challenge or justified withdrawal; since version 2 it is "a
  test for domination by an arrangement's controllers, not for every
  unfreedom". Its arrangement is sense 1 by default, and "the arrangement
  itself is not assumed to owe or to dominate anything, as QUESTION-460
  keeps the bearer apart from the ground". Its body says its second and
  third conditions are CLAIM-545's domination, "unchecked". ARG-109
  (Proposed) concludes it from Han, Benjamin and Marcuse, which describe
  constraint with no one holding present discretion and so bound the
  claim.
- **The third condition in the record's terms.** CLAIM-686 (the
  assistant's, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13; thesis, demonstrated,
  Proposed; also will-organization) rests on CLAIM-523, uses TERM-064,
  TERM-023 and TERM-085, bears on QUESTION-461 and complements CLAIM-682:
  a failure of legibility, volitional efficacy or correctability for a
  dependent's claims at the controllers means the dependent lacks
  effective influence and challenge, and at every party able to constrain
  them, effective protection; "justified withdrawal lies outside those
  three achievements", so the third condition is met by such failures
  "only together with a failure of exit". It uses only CLAIM-523's
  necessity.
- **A case for both.** CASE-261 (A's, A23 §9; thought experiment,
  stipulated, Proposed) bears on QUESTION-461 and illustrates CLAIM-686,
  CLAIM-523 and CLAIM-060: a hospital network whose aggregate gains settle
  none of six relationships, and a test of whether one justified objection
  becomes effective when raised by clinicians and when raised by patients
  or low-status workers. It is "the voice half of CLAIM-686". It declares
  no sense.

## Where the components place the bearer

The account maps; it does not add. Each component that names a bearer
names an agent or a role, and none names a sense-1 arrangement as one:
CLAIM-671 "a responsible agent"; CLAIM-690 the agent with discretion over
the terms; TERM-124 "an agent or role"; CLAIM-682 "those who control it";
CASE-256's third result "agents or roles maintaining the arrangement".
QUESTION-460's prior part, whether a whole that is not an agent can bear
an obligation at all, is untouched by every component, as its notes say.

## Not stated on the record

- That every bearer is an agent or a role is a feature of the components
  as filed; no component states it as a thesis, and QUESTION-460 leaves
  open that it might not be so.
- How CLAIM-690 and CLAIM-682 relate. They were filed in parallel (#492
  and #489); neither cites the other, and TERM-124 does not cite CLAIM-682. Both
  relate to CLAIM-545: TERM-124 joins CLAIM-545's clause, and CLAIM-682's
  body says its second and third conditions are CLAIM-545's domination
  "unchecked". Whether CLAIM-682's first condition, essentiality, only sets
  how much rides on a dependence in CLAIM-690's sense, or also bears on
  whether withdrawal is available, is stated by neither; the review of
  2026-11-15 tested the first reading as a claim and it failed (the
  curation entry of that day).
- Which standard of unjust terms the record adopts (QUESTION-463,
  QUESTION-468 part 2), and where the duty falls when the arrangement is
  not an agent (QUESTION-468 part 3).
- Whether CASE-256's test has been run on any case.

## What it does not say

- That any whole, in any sense, owes its parts anything: QUESTION-460 is
  Open, and CLAIM-671 is supposed.
- That the Saint-Domingue theatres' exclusions were kept by the mechanism
  CASE-257 leaves open.
- That magnitude of dependence never sorts it (ARG-110), or that domination
  needs a discretion-holder on every account (CLAIM-682 since version 2).
- That integration increases vulnerability (CLAIM-671's body denies it is
  the mechanism).
- Anything with standing of its own.
