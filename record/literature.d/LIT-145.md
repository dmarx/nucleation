# LIT-145: Ultrafilters as Propositional Theories

Jason Turner (2025), *Philosophy Compass 20(7), e70047* — DOI-10.1111/phc3.70047

## Key takeaways

- Filters and ultrafilters, and with them Łoś's theorem and compactness, become accessible to philosophers once read as propositional theories: sets of possible-worlds propositions that are consistent and closed under conjunction and implication, and, for an ultrafilter, negation-complete.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Turner gives a philosophically friendly explanation of filters and ultrafilters from mathematics and model theory. A filter is treated as a propositional theory: a consistent set of propositions closed under finite conjunction and under implication. An ultrafilter is a filter that is also negation-complete. The central ultrafilter theorem is shown to be a propositional analogue of Lindenbaum's lemma. Products and ultraproducts are introduced and Łoś's theorem is proved, with compactness as an application.
