# NOTE-tmplmlej: Sen (1999) — Development as Freedom (Introduction and Chapter 1)

## Contribution

A statement, for a general audience, that development should be judged by substantive freedoms, joined to the instrumental thesis that freedoms of different kinds reinforce one another.

## Key results

1. Introduction, p. 3: development as "a process of expanding the real freedoms that people enjoy" [OCR-normalized], against identifying it with GNP growth, industrialization "or with technological advance" [OCR-normalized].
2. Introduction, "A concluding remark": freedoms "are not only the primary ends of development, they are also among its principal means" [OCR-normalized]; "Freedoms of different kinds can strengthen one another" [OCR-normalized].
3. Introduction: "economic unfreedom can breed social unfreedom" [OCR-normalized].
4. Chapter 1: the distinction between "the process aspect and the opportunity aspect of freedom" [OCR-normalized].
5. Chapter 1: freedom is "a principal determinant of individual initiative and social effectiveness" [OCR-normalized].
6. Chapter 1, on unemployment: it is "a source of far-reaching debilitating effects on individual freedom, initiative, and skills" [OCR-normalized], which income transfers do not make up.
7. Chapter 1: where tradition and modernity conflict, "the people directly involved" [OCR-normalized] must be able to take part in deciding.

## Assumptions

- Freedoms are diverse and need explicit valuation, without a complete ordering of all states ("complete ordering" is disclaimed in the concluding remarks).
- The empirical claims rest on cited survival and famine data, not reproduced.

## Concepts

Substantive freedom; constitutive and instrumental roles of freedom; five instrumental freedoms; process and opportunity aspects; agency aspect; capability deprivation; culmination outcomes.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmp6wuql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6wuql.md)**: supports its premise. Result 6 is Sen's own case of support that replaces income but not activity, while skills and initiative erode, the claim's contrast between support that does the work and support that covers a shortfall. Results 2 and 5 show Sen counts present freedoms as instruments of later ones, but at the level of society and outside the evaluative space; nothing read argues that the capability set already includes later sets, the claim's named defeater. Corroborates (the claim does not rest on it).
- **[CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md)**: result 4 is first-hand confirmation of the two aspects the claim divides.
- **[CLAIM-tmpbypt7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md)**: leaves open. Sen counts denial of access to markets as an unfreedom; he does not discuss compulsion by others' adoption.
- **[CLAIM-tmpkjv7u](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpkjv7u.md)**: a point of contact only. Result 7 and the famine argument concern accountability, the claim's third condition, not framed as domination.
- **The exchange's attributions.** A10's "development as the expansion of substantive freedoms" is accurate. "Fasting against starving" and "agency freedom against well-being freedom" are not in the chapters read; they are in [LIT-tmpc3h2w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc3h2w.md).
