# CLAIM-364: Processes conditioned never to object coordinate without override at the same behaviour and supervisory cost as processes that negotiate, so the two part only under intervention, when a constituent dissents, or in their history

## The objection

The owner's criterion ([CLAIM-362](CLAIM-362.md)) marks authenticity by higher-level
processes not needing to impose themselves, and the owner tied that to a
saving in effort ([CLAIM-358](CLAIM-358.md)). A45, at U45 of the exchange of
2026-10-17, added *contestability*, for this reason:

> Otherwise, a system whose subordinate processes have been conditioned
> never to object might exhibit perfect local harmony.
>
> The supervisor would rarely intervene, not because its constituents had
> developed autonomous coordination, but because the possibility of
> meaningful disagreement had already been eliminated.

"So non-domination must concern **the effective distribution of
authority, including counterfactual situations in which a constituent
dissents**. A quiet subordinate process isn't necessarily a satisfied
one." And: "non-domination should be evaluated historically and
counterfactually, not inferred from presently low intervention costs." A45
closed on it: "**low strain alone cannot establish authenticity**: a
dominated organization can be efficient."

This is the manipulation objection ([THEORY-tmpk6bx2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpk6bx2.md)) in a new place. A condition stated only in
present structure is met by an agent whose structure was installed. Low
supervisory cost is a present-structure mark. [QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md) asks the
general form: can a will-organization be manipulated without anything in
its present structure showing it?

## What would tell them apart

A45's tests, negotiated coordination against conditioned conformity:

- "Introduce a novel but legitimate constituent demand": local processes
  "can reopen negotiation", or the demand "is ignored or suppressed".
- "Alter the formerly dominant process's preferred policy": others "retain
  independent evaluative influence", or "continue following the dominant
  process".
- "Reveal evidence that existing coordination is mistaken": processes "can
  revise their agreement", or the consensus "remains disproportionately
  entrenched".
- "Perturb the local coupling mechanism": behaviour "degrades in a
  mechanistically predictable way", or depends "on fixed compliance
  rules".

A45 flagged that one test does not discriminate: "Remove the higher-level
supervisor temporarily". Compliance may persist too, "so this test alone
is insufficient". A46 added a test of the same kind: whether a specialist
can "recover effective influence" when its conditions return.

Every discriminating test is an intervention or a history. None reads
the difference from cost. That is the objection.

The organizational form is March's ([THEORY-142](../theory.d/THEORY-142.md)): a code that learns only
from members who conform loses what deviation would have taught it, and
fast socialization makes the organization worse. A conditioned process is
a member socialized too fast.

## What it does not say

- **That the criterion is wrong.** Non-domination stated counterfactually,
  as A45 and A46 state it, survives the objection. What fails is reading
  it from the economy.
- **That every quiet process is suppressed.** Agreement in value
  ([CLAIM-162](CLAIM-162.md)) is also quiet.
- **That history settles it.** [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md) notes that no historical
  condition in the record is shown to escape its own objection, and
  [CLAIM-016](CLAIM-016.md)'s manipulation defeater is still outstanding.

## Note of 2026-10-22: cases for the quiet subordinate

At U18 of the exchange "Graph Learning Applications" the owner quoted A17,
"What if the subordinate processes are quiet because they've been
conditioned never to object?", and asked for cases. A18 wrote six. Four
restate this claim's tests or what follows from them: agreement after
deliberation against learned silence (A), the satisfied against the
conditioned subordinate (D), the specialist who participates rarely but
meaningfully (E: "a constituent's meaningful participation need not
involve frequent participation"), and perfect imitation (F, which is
[QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md)). Their shared form, alike in ordinary operation and parting
under an intervention, is [CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md)'s. They are not filed.

Two are, because they add to the claim:

- **The alarm that learned and the alarm that was disabled**
  ([CASE-129](../cases.d/CASE-129.md)): a quiet signal may be calibration or suppression, and
  only "response to changed evidence" tells them apart.
- **The voluntary delegation** ([CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md)): a restriction on a
  constituent the person authorized, and can reconsider under stated
  conditions, is self-governance and not domination. So whether a
  constituent is dominated cannot be read from whether it is overridden.

A18's summary of the mistake all six guard against: "The common mistake
is to infer something about the **distribution of practical authority**
from the **absence of overt struggle**."

## Note of 2026-10-28: split under [ADR-061](../decisions.d/ADR-061.md)

The old title's first clause, "Low supervisory cost cannot show
non-domination", was a verdict on support. It is now [ARG-030](../arguments.d/ARG-030.md), from
[CLAIM-358](CLAIM-358.md) to [CLAIM-362](CLAIM-362.md), form evidence, Proposed. This claim keeps what is
about the subject matter: conditioned and negotiating processes match in
behaviour and cost and part only under intervention or in their history,
with the defeat condition it already had. It undercuts the argument and no
longer objects to [CLAIM-362](CLAIM-362.md), which it never said fails.
