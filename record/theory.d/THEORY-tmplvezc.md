# THEORY-tmplvezc: Bovens proposes that accountability, in a narrow analytical sense, is a social relation in which an actor is obliged to explain and justify its conduct to a forum that can question and judge it, with the possibility of consequences for the actor, and that whether a practice is accountability is an either/or question kept apart from classifying it by forum, actor, conduct and obligation and from evaluating it from democratic, constitutional and learning perspectives

Bovens (2007), [LIT-2729](../literature.d/LIT-2729.md).

## The account

"Accountability is a relationship between an actor and a forum, in which
the actor has an obligation to explain and to justify his or her conduct,
the forum can pose questions and pass judgement, and the actor may face
consequences" (p. 450). Mapping it "is a dichotomous exercise that follows
the logic of either/or" (p. 460); assessing it rests on "a theory, often
implicit" (p. 467).

## What it does not show

- When an actor owes an account: the framework is descriptive, and its
  "why" sorts obligations by source.
- That answerability is wider than accountability: Bovens treats it as a
  stage within it.
