# NOTE-1732: Geddes (2022) — Pregnancy, Parthood and Proper Overlap: A Critique of Kingma

## Contribution

A mereological correction: four possibilities (parthood either way, proper overlap, disjointness) must all be addressed; criteria for organismality or co-parthood are not criteria for one organism's being part of another.

## Key results

- §I: the four possibilities "are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive: Either x is part of y, or y is part of x, or x and y properly overlap (roughly: share some but not all of their parts), or x and y are disjoint".
- §I: "Kingma runs together ∼(1) and (4), equating rejection of (1) with acceptance of (4) and vice versa."
- §II: the first three criteria give "a test for organismality, or for constituting an organism, not for organismic parthood."
- §III.1 (immunological tolerance): "For the criterion points to proper overlap, and not to parthood."
- §III.2 (continuity), problem of defeasibility: any such criterion "will simply be blind to the difference between proper overlap and parthood".
- §IV: "we have no good grounds, at present, to rule out the possibility of proper overlap."
- §V: "We must, for the moment, suspend judgment."

## Assumptions

Classical mereology with parthood reflexive, anti-symmetric and transitive (fn. 5; transitivity is used in §III.1); Kingma's criteria taken as defeasible; for the sake of argument, the broad criteria are treated as reasonable tests for organismality; focus on later stages of pregnancy.

## Concepts

Proper overlap; disjointness; parthood vs co-parthood (underlap); organismality vs organismic parthood vs organismic constitution; problems of "form" (symmetric criteria) and "defeasibility" (false positives under overlap); gravida/foster.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** Mostly accurate, with one overstatement. Geddes argues that Kingma's case fails and that proper overlap is a live, better-supported option; he does not assert that the two organisms do properly overlap ("I stop short of endorsing the conclusion"). "Two organisms can properly overlap" should read "proper overlap cannot be ruled out, and one criterion favours it".

- **[THEORY-448](../theory.d/THEORY-448.md)** (Meincke: substance ontology vs process ontology on parthood): **qualifies**. Meincke adopts Kingma's four criteria and keeps parthood; Geddes argues those criteria do not support parthood over proper overlap, which removes a premise Meincke treats as conceded. It is also a third option her dichotomy (parthood under substance or process ontology) does not consider, though it does not touch her internal critique of substance ontology. [THEORY-448](../theory.d/THEORY-448.md)'s `promote_when` already says the four criteria only bear on mere containment; Geddes supports that remark and sharpens it.
- **[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)** (when a combined form is one system, where its boundary lies): Geddes is directly on the boundary question and gives the condition that tests of "one system" (organismality) do not settle part–whole relations between the systems inside it. Leaves it open.

- Not held under another LIT (no DOI, "Geddes" or "proper overlap" hits on main or the branch). Kingma (2019) itself is cited in held notes ([NOTE-115](NOTE-115.md) etc.) but no Kingma LIT was found by title search.
- Further held entries: **[CLAIM-530](../claims.d/CLAIM-530.md)** (where maintenance is realized depends on the parthood condition) — Geddes shows parthood conditions taken from organismality criteria do not discriminate parthood from overlap; **[LIT-1227](../literature.d/LIT-1227.md)** (Pradeu, *The Limits of the Self*, Unavailable) — Geddes's §III.1 is a reading of Pradeu's criterion of inclusion and argues it implies overlap in pregnancy.

## Limitations

Conceptual paper; no new empirical evidence; sets aside early pregnancy stages and the "gravida part of foster" option; draws on an unpublished manuscript of his own for the Pradeu critique.
