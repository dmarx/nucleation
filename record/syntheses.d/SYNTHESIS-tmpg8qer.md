# SYNTHESIS-tmpg8qer: Saussure's value, as distinct from signification, is fixed by contrast with the coexisting terms of one language state and is not set against reference; whether it supports transport across languages is open, its use-level counterpart is offered as an utterance's contrast with the alternatives its speaker could have used, and the stronger reading, that contrast replaces reference, is rejected

*A part account of the pragmatic-transport line: the structuralist premise
of the manuscript "What survives translation" (§2), as the record corrected
it on 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10 and the status reviews of 2026-10-28 and
2026-10-29 filed it. The line's account is built over it and the accounts
of its sibling parts. The mechanical map put these entries in one part
with the entries on renderings that keep the proposition and change the
act (the act part); the two share one pair, against thirteen within this
part and sixty-two within the other, so each has its own account. Every
component carries pragmatic-transport only.*

## The account

- **The corrected premise.** CLAIM-082 (the manuscript's §2, narrowed at
  the sixth status review; thesis, interpretive, Proposed): "A term's
  value, as distinct from its signification, is fixed by its contrasts
  with the coexisting terms of its system". It is "a motivating premise,
  not a result the manuscript relies on formally". Its body adds
  qualifications from Bergen, Goodman and Levy ("contrast together with
  reference, not contrast instead of it"), from relative representations
  ("for transport between systems they work only once something ties the
  anchor in one to the anchor in the other") and from closed-form word
  embeddings. Its note of 2026-10-30: it "waits on a study that adds,
  loses or merges a term in a language's inventory". CASE-019 (A4's
  "Hug from Doug", documented, Active) illustrates it: "what a rendering
  preserves can be a position in a structure rather than a token".
- **The correction.** ARG-034 (the manuscript's citation of Saussure,
  source, Rejected) judges the first title: "Saussure says purely
  differential only of signifier and signified taken apart, distinguishes
  value from signification, and brackets the referent". The fix is "to
  say value rather than significance and drop the referent contrast".
- **The stronger reading, rejected.** CLAIM-489 (CLAIM-082's first title,
  kept as its own entry; thesis, interpretive, Rejected): "A sign's
  communicative significance is fixed by its contrasts within a system
  rather than by correspondence to a referent". Rejected on its own
  condition at the thirteenth status review: in Bergen, Goodman and Levy's
  Experiment 1, "Contrast did its work through the correspondence the
  title says it replaces."
- **From value to transport, open.** ARG-033 (CLAIM-072's inference,
  CLAIM-254's objection, source, Proposed): the premise "supports
  transport of relational identity across languages". CLAIM-254 objected
  that value "is a fact of the language system, the *langue*", while the
  observables are facts of use, and that "If the observables track value,
  as CLAIM-072 says, then the premise predicts that what they track is
  what a change of language changes." ARG-033 leaves it Proposed: "whether
  that use-level counterpart is the structuralist premise restated or a
  replacement for it is arguable."
- **The use-level counterpart.** CLAIM-242 (the record's reply of
  2026-10-10; thesis, interpretive, Proposed): "The use-level counterpart
  of Saussure's value is an utterance's contrast with the alternatives its
  speaker could have used in context; values do not cross between
  languages, which is why fidelity is graded transport through an anchored
  correspondence rather than identity of value". It grants the objection's
  premises and denies its conclusion, and "Saussure becomes precedent, not
  premise". Its note of 2026-10-29: "No comparison in the record tests
  whether renderings that keep a source's contrast with its alternatives
  keep its judged stance or footing more often than renderings that change
  it".
- **A second line, independent of Saussure.** ARG-040 (filed at the sixth
  status review at the owner's direction; analogy, Proposed) argues from
  CLAIM-242 that "what a translation must let a receiver reconstruct is
  those contrasts, not the wording or the proposition". "The mechanism,
  interpretation by contrast with alternatives, transfers. The alternative
  sets do not". It is Proposed because "Whether the target reader can
  reconstruct the source's contrasts, rather than the target language's
  own, is what the analogy does not settle."

## Not stated on the record

Tested by ADR-075's step 5 at this review and not filed:

- **That CLAIM-242's contrast is the mechanism of the act part's
  dissociation.** That renderings which keep the proposition change the
  act (CLAIM-061) by changing the speaker's contrast with alternatives.
  Whose alternatives is a free parameter, which ARG-040 names and leaves
  open; read as the source speaker's, CASE-056's literal kiss in a culture
  where it reads as amorous keeps the proposition and the source's
  contrasts and changes the act, a realistic case that shows the sentence
  false as a general mechanism without meeting CLAIM-242's `defeated_if`,
  which is comparative.
- **That ARG-033 and ARG-040 now conclude nothing that carries.** That,
  since CLAIM-220 drops the rest on CLAIM-082 and compares predictors,
  the part motivates the line's central thesis without supporting it. The
  first half is CLAIM-220's own statement ("motivates the thesis and does
  not carry it"); the rest relabels it.

## What it does not say

- That relational identity is false, or that the line needs Saussure
  (ARG-033: "Nothing here says relational identity is false").
- That contrast with alternatives is value (CLAIM-242: "it says contrast
  with alternatives plays value's role at the level of use, not that it
  is value").
- Anything with standing of its own.
