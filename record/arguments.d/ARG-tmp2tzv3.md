# ARG-tmp2tzv3: Marcuse's false needs and Illich's needs that productivity cannot satisfy corroborate the claim that satisfying preferences more fully need not leave lives going better, but by criteria the claim does not adopt, Marcuse's origin in particular interests and Illich's level of conviviality, so they support its separation of satisfaction from how lives go, not a measure of worth

## The inference

1. **Marcuse** ([NOTE-tmpf1tpw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpf1tpw.md), ch. 1): false needs are "superimposed upon the individual by particular social interests in his repression: the needs which perpetuate toil, aggressiveness, misery, and injustice." "Their satisfaction might be most gratifying to the individual", yet "The result then is euphoria in unhappiness." Satisfaction and how a life goes come apart, and an arrangement produces the needs it satisfies: [CLAIM-tmp58u4k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp58u4k.md)'s separation, by another route than Wolf's.
2. **Illich** ([NOTE-tmp8ceuj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8ceuj.md)): below a level of conviviality "no amount of industrial productivity can effectively satisfy the needs it creates among society’s members", and institutions that "hallow industrial productivity at the expense of convivial effectiveness, are a major factor in the amorphousness and meaninglessness that plague contemporary society." More output, more satisfaction of produced needs, and less meaning: the claim's separation again, at the scale of a society.

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** Yes. Both passages state their author's thesis; Marcuse's is the definition on which chapter 1 turns, Illich's the opening of the excerpt.
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** For the separation, yes. For what makes a life go better, no: Marcuse's standard is the vital needs and needs that do not perpetuate toil and misery, judged by individuals "only in the last analysis", and part of his criterion is origin, which [CLAIM-tmpx60mo](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpx60mo.md) argues cannot decide alone. Illich's standard is conviviality, "individual freedom realized in personal interdependence", not Wolf's worth.
- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** If satisfying people's preferences more fully always left their lives going better, Marcuse's "euphoria in unhappiness" and Illich's needs that no amount of productivity satisfies could not occur. Both describe them as actual. They are diagnoses, argued from cases and theory, not demonstrations, so they corroborate and do not establish.

## Verdict

Proposed. Both corroborate [CLAIM-tmp58u4k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp58u4k.md)'s separation of satisfaction from how lives go; neither supplies its standard of worth, which rests on Wolf ([ARG-tmpl8zwp](ARG-tmpl8zwp.md)). The relation is `corroborates` from each THEORY.
