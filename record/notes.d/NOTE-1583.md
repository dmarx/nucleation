# NOTE-1583: Mukhopadhyay et al. — Review of incident prediction, resource allocation and dispatch models

## Contribution

A review of approaches to emergency response management in four parts:
incident prediction, incident detection, resource allocation and
computer-aided dispatch. The part read, §6.1, sorts the static coverage
models used to site stations into deterministic and probabilistic ones and
summarizes each in a line, with Table 1 listing their objectives.

## Key insight

The deterministic models assume a station can always serve the demand it
covers: "Resources at a facility are considered to be always available"
(§6.1). Covering a point twice reduces the cost of that assumption but does
not remove it, since both covering stations may be busy, and "Accounting
for such situations requires modeling facility availability explicitly"
(§6.1). The probabilistic models do so, at first with crude assumptions
that later models relax.

## Assumptions

As the review attributes them (§6.1): LSCP and MCLP assume that stations
act independently, response is deterministic, at most one ambulance is at
each facility and there is one type of ambulance. MEXCLP inherits many of
MCLP's assumptions, gives every facility the same busy probability, and
assumes facilities act independently.

## Key results

A review, with no results of its own in §6.1. What it states:

- **The deterministic models.** LSCP, MCLP and many of their extensions
  "assume deterministic system behavior in regards to response" (§6.1).
- **MEXCLP.** "MEXCLP was introduced by Daskin (Daskin, 1983) and extends
  MCLP, modifying the optimization function to account for station
  availability." "It assumes that each facility has the same probability of
  being busy, which simplifies computation but does not accurately
  represent the real world where facilities near incident hot spots are
  unavailable for a greater proportion of the time." (§6.1)
- **MALP.** "MALP, proposed by Revelle and Hogan (ReVelle and Hogan, 1989),
  maximizes the demand covered by facilities with some exogenously
  specified probability." The first version assumes equal busy
  probabilities; "MALP-II (ReVelle and Hogan, 1989), however, removes this
  assumption. The proportion of time that facilities are busy is computed
  as a ratio between the total demand generated by demand points and the
  availability of facilities covering them." (§6.1)
- **Extensions.** TIMEXCLP adds temporal variation in travel times; AMEXCLP
  "relaxes MEXCLP's assumption that facilities are independent by treating
  them as servers in a hypercube queuing system (Larson, 1974) with equal
  busy factions" ("factions" sic); QPLSCP computes each facility's busy
  fraction with a queueing model and feeds it into MALP-II (§6.1,
  Table 1).

## Concepts

- **Coverage** — a demand point is covered if a facility lies within the
  distance or time standard.
- **Station availability** — the chance a covering facility is not busy.
- **Multiple coverage** — covering a point by more than one station, as in
  the double standard model, to hedge against one being busy.

  framing, which remains A's.

  from congestion alone.
- **[CLAIM-296](../claims.d/CLAIM-296.md) and [CLAIM-312](../claims.d/CLAIM-312.md): qualify.** A busy covering station is present
  but not deployable.
