# CASE-tmp0b3lc: Franklin's borrowed book: Benjamin Franklin, opposed by a new member of the Pennsylvania Assembly when he stood again as its clerk, asked to borrow a rare book from him rather than courting him, returned it with thanks, and found the man civil and later a friend

## The case

Franklin, in the Autobiography (LIT-{FRANKLIN}), on his re-election as
clerk of the General Assembly: "a new member made a long speech against
me, in order to favour some other candidate." Franklin "did not, however,
aim at gaining his favour by paying any servile respect to him". Instead:
"Having heard that he had in his library a certain very scarce and
curious book, I wrote a note to him, expressing my desire of perusing that
book, and requesting he would do me the favour of lending it to me for a
few days. He sent it immediately, and I return'd it in about a week with
another note, expressing strongly my sense of the favour. When we next met
in the House, he spoke to me (which he had never done before), and with
great civility; and he ever after manifested a readiness to serve me on
all occasions, so that we became great friends, and our friendship
continued to his death." Franklin's maxim: "He that has once done you a
kindness will be more ready to do you another, than he whom you yourself
have obliged."

The experimental descendant is Jecker and Landy (1969, LIT-{JL}): as A1
reported it, participants who returned their winnings at the
experimenter's personal request then rated the experimenter more
favourably. A1 added that its direct replication record is limited.

## What it shows

- **The order of act and attitude.** The help came before the liking. The
  conventional reading runs the other way (I like you, so I help you), and
  CLAIM-275 holds that an act can come before the interpretation it makes
  available.
- **A role, not a reason.** The request gave the rival something to do
  for Franklin, not an argument about Franklin. CLAIM-tmprti7p reads that
  as an invitation to enact a relation the adversarial arrangement did
  not hold.
- **Three explanations it does not decide between.** Dissonance (the
  helper reconciles act and attitude), self-perception (the helper infers
  his attitude from his act), and the relational reading (the request
  signals trust and opens a cooperative exchange). A1 listed all three and
  said they "aren't mutually exclusive".

## What it does not show

- That the effect is reliable. One anecdote, and an experimental record
  A1 called limited.
- Which mechanism worked. The account records the outcome, not the rival's
  reasons, and Franklin wrote it as a lesson.
