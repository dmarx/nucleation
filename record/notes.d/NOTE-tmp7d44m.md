# NOTE-tmp7d44m: Pattee — the physics of symbols

## Contribution

A retrospective synthesis of Pattee's answer to Karl Pearson's question,
what physically distinguishes the living from the lifeless. The answer:
life entails an epistemic cut between rate-independent symbolic
constraints and the rate-dependent dynamics they control, and this cut
cannot be seen in microscopic laws. It names one necessary physical
condition for bridging the cut, non-integrable constraints, and adds
conditions specific to heteropolymers (search, reliable replication,
smooth variation). There is no new formal result; the argument rests on
von Neumann's measurement regress and on Pattee's 1969 statement of
non-holonomic constraints.

## Key insight

Measurement and control are one bridge crossed in two directions:
"measurement transforms physical states to symbols in memory, while
memory-stored controls transform symbols to physical states" (§5). Both
are performed by a physical constraint that is on the same formal footing
as the laws, but whose function as measurer or controller disappears if
it is described by the laws alone: "if you choose to do so you will lose
the function of M as a measuring device" (§9). The cut is where a subject
makes "a distinction or classification between physical states that is
not made by the laws themselves" (§10).

## Assumptions

- **Dynamical description has three epistemic conditions** (§6, restated
  for all dynamical laws in §7): laws and initial conditions crisply
  separated; initial conditions fixed by measurement; and "this form of
  description can claim to be objective only if the act of measurement
  does not influence the form of the laws, and if the laws do not
  influence the act of measurement."
- **Memory needs energy degeneracy.** "memory storage requires
  alternative states of the same energy (energy degeneracy)" (§8).
- **Symbols exist only with life.** "a symbol can only exist in the
  context of a living organism or its artifacts" (note 6).
- **Von Neumann's argument is taken as decisive** for the irreducibility
  of measurement (§9).
- **Classical language for results.** "the results of all measurements of
  both relativistic and quantum mechanical systems must be expressed in
  this classical language" (§7).

## Key results

- **The epistemic cut.** Abstract: "Evolution requires the
  genotype-phenotype distinction, a primeval epistemic cut that separates
  energy-degenerate, rate-independent genetic symbols from the
  rate-dependent dynamics of construction that they control." It is the
  general problem of "how to bridge the separation between the observer
  and the observed, the controller and the controlled, the knower and the
  known, and even the mind and the brain" (§5).
- **Measurement and control are converses.** Abstract: "The converse of
  control is measurement in which a rate-dependent dynamical state is
  coded into quiescent symbols." §5: "measurement and control are inverse
  processes".
- **Non-integrable constraints bridge the cut (§8).** Quoting Pattee
  1969b: memory or heredity needs "alternative pathways", so "there must
  be more degrees of freedom available for the description of the total
  system than for following its actual motion. . . Such constraints are
  called non-holonomic." They "bridge all epistemic cuts between the
  controller and the controlled, the classifier and the classified, the
  observer and the observed": latches, escapements, gates, switches,
  enzymes, channel proteins.
- **Irreducibility (§9).** From von Neumann: describing measuring device M
  together with system S needs a further device M′, an infinite regress;
  "laws cannot describe the pragmatic function of measurement even if they
  can correctly and completely describe the detailed dynamics of the
  measuring constraints." The same holds for control: in (S + C) "the
  control function simply disappears into the dynamics."
- **Epistemic, not ontological (§§9–10).** "This epistemic irreducibility
  does not imply any ontological dualism." "The cut itself is an
  epistemic necessity, not an ontological condition." Its placement is
  "arbitrary to a large extent", following von Neumann.
- **The subject side (§10).** "in order to perform a measurement the
  subject must have control of the construction of the measuring device.
  Only the subject side of the cut can measure or control."
- **Control came first (§10, note 6).** "The concept of an epistemic cut
  must first arise at the genotype-phenotype control interface." The
  first new distinction in a cell occurs where the gene's degeneracy is
  partly removed, in protein folding; transcription, translation and
  copying "make no new distinctions". Note 6: "There are innumerable
  symbol functions at many hierarchical levels, but control of
  construction came first."
- **Semantic closure (§10).** Genes control protein synthesis only by
  relying on "previously synthesized and organized enzymes and RNAs";
  "This additional self-referent condition for being the subject-part of
  an epistemic cut I have called semantic (or semiotic) closure".
- **Pearson answered (§11).** "It is not possible to distinguish the
  living from the lifeless by the most detailed "motion of inorganic
  corpuscles" alone", because life entails an epistemic cut not
  distinguishable by microscopic laws.
- **Laws as compression (§7).** The law–initial-condition cut is "an
  extreme case of the distinction made, even by the first cells, between
  stimuli that cannot be correlated and stimuli that can be correlated";
  laws are compressible records, initial conditions incompressible ones.
- **Quantum (§8).** Non-integrable constraints in quantum theory restrict
  the wave function "as if some measurement is being made (Eden 1951)";
  Pattee repeats his 1967 speculation that enzymes "execute a quantum
  measurement and rate control function".
- **Physical conditions for evolvability (§13).** Functional redundancy
  of sequences eases search; replication balances reliability against
  variation; landscapes balance steep folding against smooth tuning.
  Qualitative, with citations, no numbers.

## Concepts

- **Epistemic cut.** The necessary separation of subject and object,
  symbol and referent, syntax and pragmatics (§9), made "just in order to
  speak of knowledge as being "about" something" (§10).
- **Non-integrable (non-holonomic) constraint.** A constraint whose
  equations cannot be solved separately from the dynamics, leaving more
  configurational coordinates than dynamical degrees of freedom, so that
  some alternatives remain to "read" memory (§8).
- **Measurement.** Coding of a rate-dependent dynamical state into
  quiescent symbols (abstract); determining initial conditions (§6).
- **Control.** The converse: memory-stored symbols constraining dynamics.
- **Symbol.** A rate-independent structure within a semiotic system of
  symbol vehicles, interpreting constraints and an organism in which they
  function (note 6).
- **Semantic closure.** The self-referent condition that the symbol
  reader is itself built from the symbols (§10).

## Connections

- **[NOTE-121](NOTE-121.md) (Elshatlawy et al., observers).** That chapter's key insight,
  that observation and control are "the same loop, run in opposite
  directions", is Pattee's measurement–control converse, stated here two
  decades earlier. Pattee gives the boundary the physical content
  [NOTE-121](NOTE-121.md) finds missing from its B: an energy-degenerate memory read by
  non-integrable constraints. That answers [NOTE-121](NOTE-121.md)'s open question, "Can
  B be formalised?", in part, and as constraint mechanics rather than a
  Markov blanket. Both deny observer status to elementary systems:
  Pattee finds a subject–object distinction before life "entirely
  gratuitous" (§10).
- **[NOTE-174](NOTE-174.md) (Rovelli, RQM).** RQM lets any physical system be an
  observer. Pattee denies it for the reason just given, and requires
  function, memory and control. They agree, after von Neumann, that the
  cut's placement is largely conventional.
- **[NOTE-249](NOTE-249.md) (measurement as sheafification).** Both start from Bohr's
  premise that results must be stated in classical language (Pattee, §7).
  Ghose's account of measurement is logical (presheaf to sheaf), Pattee's
  is physical (state to record through a constraint). Neither offers a
  model of a measurement; Pattee at least names the physical class of
  device.
- **QBism ([LIT-tmp7kgwz](../literature.d/LIT-tmp7kgwz.md), read in [NOTE-tmpfpjq8](NOTE-tmpfpjq8.md)).** Opposed on instruments.
  QBism calls instruments "the Copenhagen surrogate for experience"; for
  Pattee the measuring constraint is the bridge itself and the subject is
  defined by its control of such constraints. Both cite Born and Bohr on
  communicating private experience through symbols or language (Pattee §5;
  QBism p. 3).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) and the LIT's "Why this is here".** The LIT calls
  Pattee "measurement-first prior art" in which measurement, "not
  action", separates a living system from its dynamics. The reading does
  not bear that out.
  - *Measurement is half of a bridge whose other half is control.* The
    paper treats measurement and control as converses, executed by the
    same class of constraint (abstract, §5, §8). That is the shape of the
    action–perception cycle the claim rejects, not of a loop without
    action.
  - *Control came first.* The primeval cut is at "the genotype-phenotype
    control interface", and "control of construction came first" (§10,
    note 6). If anything is first in Pattee, it is control.
  - *Measurement presupposes control.* "in order to perform a measurement
    the subject must have control of the construction of the measuring
    device" (§10).
  - *Where the claim can use him.* The first new distinction in a cell is
    made where the system reads its own memory: degeneracy "is partially
    removed" in protein folding (§10). That primeval epistemic event is an
    operation of the system on its own records, not on the world. And
    semantic closure, in which the reader of the symbols is built from the
    symbols (§10), is structurally the claim's diachronic loop: what is
    made from one reading becomes the apparatus of the next (my
    observation). But Pattee's control is construction, a change in the
    system's own physical make-up, not action on an environment; so he
    does not separate the claim's two kinds of operation, on the self and
    on the world. He neither supports nor refutes the claim's exclusion of
    action on the world.
- **[CLAIM-tmpt568z](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt568z.md) (the anchor).** Pattee's third epistemic condition is
  close to the claim's anchor: description is objective "only if the act
  of measurement does not influence the form of the laws, and if the laws
  do not influence the act of measurement" (§6). It is a condition on
  description, not on a loop, but it states the same independence of
  measuring from what it tests.
- **[CLAIM-tmp9cjyo](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp9cjyo.md) (one blanket inward).** Pattee's view that the cut's
  placement is "arbitrary to a large extent" (§§9–10), with any constraint
  describable as dynamics if one gives up its function, supports the
  counter's point that where the boundary goes is a modelling choice.
- **[CLAIM-tmpki5gp](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpki5gp.md), [QUESTION-tmpr5tck](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpr5tck.md), [CASE-tmpxgcq2](../cases.d/CASE-tmpxgcq2.md).** No direct bearing.
  The paper is about molecular control and evolution; on cognition it says
  only that a dynamical account of the brain would not describe the genome
  (§3).
- **For the anthology.** No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- A retrospective and programmatic essay. The physical claims (C1, C3)
  are argued by citation to von Neumann and to Pattee 1969; nothing is
  derived here.
- The "real-life conditions" (§13) are qualitative.
- Read from the author's posted text, without page numbers and not
  compared with the typeset version.

## Open questions

- Whether a constraint that changes the system's own reading apparatus
  (semantic closure) without acting on an environment is control in
  Pattee's sense or a third kind of crossing; the paper does not separate
  them.
- Whether the quantum speculation about enzymes (§8) has been tested; the
  paper cites only Pattee 1967.
