The owner asked the assistant to "proceed systematically filling all of
those identified gaps". Two of the gaps go here. One is whether
persistence after a shock or after support ends is self-sustaining or
slow reversion. The other is whose persistence counts, people's or
places'.

- **Read.**
  - Davis and Weinstein 2002 ([LIT-tmpntaj3](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpntaj3.md)), from NBER w8517. The AER
    version, which adds at least one analysis, was not read.
  - Their search for multiple equilibria ([LIT-tmpgvz2v](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpgvz2v.md)), Skimmed.
  - Bleakley and Lin 2012 ([LIT-tmppaj4l](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmppaj4l.md)), from the Philadelphia Fed and
    NBER working papers.
  - Chetty, Hendren and Katz 2016 ([LIT-tmp2zxnh](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp2zxnh.md)), from NBER w21156.
- **Dates.** The filer dated Davis and Weinstein 2002 and Bleakley and Lin
  from versions other than the working papers read. The lead set both to
  the working papers' months, as the earlier filings did.
- **What changed.**
  - The record now has both poles of the slow-reversion question:
    Japanese cities reverted, portage cities locked in, and the TVA sits
    between them.
  - Moving to Opportunity mirrors urban renewal: the gain went with
    children who moved young, not with the places.
  - The reader corrected a framing in the brief: the voucher was standing
    support, not transient.
