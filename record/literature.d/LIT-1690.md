summary: >-
  A short review of intergroup emotions theory by its founders. When people self-categorize as group members, the group becomes an aspect of the self and events are appraised for what they mean to the group; the resulting emotions are felt on the group's behalf. Which emotions are felt shifts with the salient membership; members converge on a group norm for them; they need no personal involvement (guilt for others' misdeeds); and identification moderates them, the highly identified sometimes reappraising to avoid negative group emotions. Such emotions predict collective action and treatment of out-groups (contempt more than anger predicts extreme violence) and of the in-group, and they can be regulated, which suggests interventions in intractable conflicts.
