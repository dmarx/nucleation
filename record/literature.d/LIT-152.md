# LIT-152: A defence of informational structural realism

Luciano Floridi (2007), *Synthese 161(2), 219–253 (online 2007; March 2008 issue)* — DOI-10.1007/s11229-007-9163-z

## Key takeaways

- Epistemic and ontic structural realism can be reconciled through the method of levels of abstraction, and the structural objects that ontic structural realism needs can be read as informational objects, clusters of mind-independent differences, which gives "informational structural realism".

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

This is the revised text of a 2003 keynote at the first Australian Computing and Philosophy Conference. The first part defends an informational version of structural realism in three steps. First, epistemic and ontic structural realism are compatible. Second, not every relatum is logically prior to every relation. Third, structural objects can be spelled out as informational objects, which applies to observables and unobservables alike. The upshot is a world made up of interacting informational objects. The second part answers ten objections raised by colleagues.
