summary: >-
  Christie, Marine, French & Blouin (2012). A pedigree of 12,700 Hood River
  winter steelhead at eight microsatellite loci over 15 run-years tests two
  predictions that domestication selection makes and relaxed selection does
  not. Both hold. First-generation hatchery fish "had nearly double the
  lifetime reproductive success" of wild fish when both were spawned in
  the hatchery; and among wild broodstock, those most successful in the
  hatchery left offspring least successful in the wild (0.62 against 2.05
  returning offspring), in 7 of 8 run-years. Inbreeding cannot explain it.
  The one exception, a release year with few broodstock, few smolts reared
  at low density and released larger, "points to crowding as a possible
  selection pressure". The authors conclude that less than one generation
  in captivity can produce "a rapid, genetically based fitness reduction in
  the wild".

# LIT-tmpt7aa3: Genetic adaptation to captivity can occur in a single generation

Christie, M. R., Marine, M. L., French, R. A. & Blouin, M. S. (2012). Genetic adaptation to captivity can occur in a single generation. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 109(1): 238–242. doi:10.1073/pnas.1111073109.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmp52jvk. Main text only; the SI was not read.*

- **A hatchery advantage.** First-generation hatchery fish "had nearly
  double the lifetime reproductive success ... when spawned in captivity
  compared with wild fish" (Abstract), "In 4 of 5 run-years".
- **A tradeoff.** Offspring of broodstock families with five or more
  returning offspring "averaged 71% lower per capita reproductive success
  in the wild" than offspring of smaller families (Results), "In 7 of the
  8 F1 run-years".
- **Not disuse.** Relaxed selection predicts no tradeoff, "a flat slope";
  inbreeding simulated at "11 lethal equivalents" cannot explain it.
- **A dose.** The exception year released "4,600 vs. a mean of 52,700"
  smolts, reared at low density, "which points to crowding as a possible
  selection pressure". One year.

## Standing in the record

Read (main text), and Active. It stands in for Araki, Cooper & Blouin
(2007), LIT-tmpcmwya, on mechanism only: it carries that the loss is
adaptation to captivity, not relaxed selection or inbreeding, and that it
arises within one generation. It does not carry that paper's estimates.
It cites the 2007 paper for two-wild-parent fish averaging 85% of wild
fish's success, a figure not reconciled with the 0.70 and 0.88 of the
2008 review's table (LIT-tmpzkw55). Filed from it: THEORY-tmp5qpsj.
