# LIT-tmpgoyge: Contractualism and Intuitionism

T. M. Scanlon (2025). "Contractualism and Intuitionism". Paper for the NYU School of Law Colloquium in Legal, Political, and Social Philosophy, session of 9 October 2025. Unpublished draft, 62 pp.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading (NOTE-tmpjth7b).*

- The 1998 formulation has "some serious problems, but I don't regret
  offering it" (p. 1); its account of interpersonal aggregation "now seems
  to me mistaken in several respects" (p. 41).
- The facts of morality "are facts about the sufficiency of various reasons
  for rejecting a principle" (pp. 6–7).
- Not complying with an established, needed practice is wrong (p. 21); where
  a needed norm is missing, "failing to do one's part in getting such a norm
  established" is wrong too (p. 22).
- Relevance is "broadly speaking a moral question", which "weakens the
  explanatory power of a reductive view" (pp. 53–54).
