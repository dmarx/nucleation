# NOTE-tmpss5e0: Pritchett, Woolcock & Andrews — Looking like a state: isomorphic mimicry and premature load bearing

## Contribution

It documents that state implementation capability in many countries is
both low and rising very slowly or falling, and asks how, not why, states
and the agencies that support them keep up "the logics of “development”"
for decades while acquiring little capability. Its answer is two
techniques of persistent failure, isomorphic mimicry and premature load
bearing, set in a framework of agents, organizations and systems.

## Key insight

Where an organization's legitimacy rests on resembling organizations
judged successful elsewhere, rather than on demonstrated accomplishment,
its agents follow rules rather than innovate. Which source of legitimacy
holds is set by the system around it: whether it will "require, recognize
and reward novelty" (p. 16). Development, on this account, is moving that
equilibrium, not supplying better forms.

## Assumptions

- **Method (p. 10).** Each indicator is rescaled so that Somalia is 1 and
  Singapore 10. A country's current gap from zero capability, divided by
  its years since independence, gives an upper bound on its historical
  rate, since it may have started above zero: "our calculation biases the
  rates against our argument". The time to Singapore's level is the
  remaining gap at that rate.
- **Indicators.** WGI government effectiveness, the Bertelsmann index's
  resource efficiency and the Failed State Index's public-services
  component for the first method (2008 values); ICRG bureaucratic quality
  and corruption, 1985–2009, for the second. All are perception-based.
- **Agents respond to the legitimacy their organization draws on.**
  "If their organization's legitimacy stems from accomplishment, agents
  will face incentives that reward innovation and 'bureaucratic
  entrepreneurial' behavior; if from mimicry, they will just follow the
  rules" (p. 15).
- **Transplant fits some tasks.** Problems with a universal technical
  answer, such as vaccines or hyperinflation, should be solved by
  transfer; the argument is about tasks with many discretionary
  face-to-face transactions (pp. 6, 16).

## Key results

- **Abstract (p. 1).** "countries like Haiti or Liberia will take many
  decades to reach even a moderate capability country like India, and
  millennia to reach the capability of Singapore."
- **Vignettes (pp. 7–9).** India's education programme raised enrolment
  while PROBE's resurvey found teacher absence unchanged: "Their (still
  very preliminary) finding on teachers absence rates: 48 percent" (p. 8).
  In Mozambique "PEFA indicators reflecting de jure changes in form average
  a B while PEFA dimensions reflecting de facto implementation and
  functional adjustment average a C" (p. 8). In the Solomon Islands a new
  courthouse and jail were built and "the courthouse has been used twice
  in its first year" (p. 9).
- **Table 1 (pp. 10, 12).** "For the average of the countries in 2008 in
  the bottom 15 it would take 672 years" (p. 10); those ranked 15th to
  30th, 209 years. Selected three-indicator averages: Haiti 1,768,
  Afghanistan 1,422, Myanmar 517, Nigeria 198.
- **Table 2 (pp. 11–13).** On ICRG data "the median rate of country
  improvement for both indicators is zero" (p. 11); most of the bottom 30
  have negative rates, "hence the estimated time is infinity" (p. 11); at
  the all-country average pace bureaucratic quality takes 314 to 503
  years, and for corruption the average pace is itself negative.
- **Mimicry (p. 14).** "the ability of organizations to sustain legitimacy
  through the imitation of the forms of modern institutions but without
  functionality".
- **Premature load bearing (pp. 1, 3, 14).** External engagement pushing
  "too hard too soon, thereby creating a situation we call pre-mature load
  bearing in which stresses exceed capability" (p. 14); "These carbon-copy
  organizations are then asked to perform tasks that are too complex and
  too burdensome, too soon and too often" (p. 3).
- **The "good bad" responses to failure (pp. 18–19).** Adopt a better policy;
  build individual capacity, which may not move a stressed organization off
  its low equilibrium; cocoon the project in parallel systems, which
  succeed and do not scale, so that "one can have long strings of
  demonstrably successful projects while a sector itself never improves"
  (p. 19); and add resources.
- **Donors' measures (p. 20).** "It is much more attractive for donors to
  measure their success as either inputs provided, training sessions held,
  or “reforms” undertaken" and in process compliance.
- **Cash on Delivery (p. 20).** Paying against third-party-verified
  progress on an agreed system-level target is "a bold attempt to stand
  isomorphic mimicry on its head", whose success "remains to be seen".
- **Conclusion (p. 21).** "many countries will take centuries (if not
  millennia) to teach high levels of state capability" (the source's typo
  for "reach").

## Concepts

- **Isomorphic mimicry** (DiMaggio and Powell 1983; "institutional
  monocropping" in Evans 2004): imitating the forms of functional
  organizations to camouflage a lack of function.
- **Capability trap**: a near-zero or negative rate of improvement in
  state capability.
- **Premature load bearing**: loading fledgling organizations with more
  than they can yet bear, so that failure undercuts their learning and
  legitimacy.
- **Cocooning**: protecting a donor project in parallel implementation
  systems.
- **Ideal against robust capability**: what an organization could do if
  all worked to capacity, against what it does under stress (p. 18).
- **Big and Small Development**: transplanted best practice at scale
  against local projects whose test is sustainability, "the imperative to
  be able to continue functioning once external support is withdrawn"
  (p. 6), and which rarely scale.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-597 (supplied maintenance).** Qualifies its setting. The
  capability that supply is meant to build accumulates, where it
  accumulates at all, on a timescale of centuries, far beyond any aid
  programme, and cocooning is supplied maintenance that keeps a project
  working while the host's own capability does not grow. Nothing here
  measures what happens when supply stops.
- **QUESTION-398 (independence from the founding intervention).** Bears
  on it without answering it: Small Development's test, functioning once
  external support is withdrawn, is the question's condition; the paper
  says such projects meet it locally and rarely scale.
- **QUESTION-386 (funding by observed activity).** Supports its premise:
  donors measure inputs, training and reforms undertaken, which can show
  success with "zero actual improvement" (p. 20). Cash on Delivery is the
  proposed alternative, untested.
- **CLAIM-566 (assemble, persist, regenerate).** Supports the distinction
  with cases: the Solomon Islands courthouse and Mozambique's de jure B
  against de facto C are forms assembled without function.
- **TERM-117 (productive nucleus).** Premature load bearing is the
  failure of a nucleus loaded before it is established; the paper defines
  it and gives no measure.
- **CLAIM-423 (components changing at different speeds).** The slow
  rates here are a case of one slow component, administrative capability.
- **THEORY-859 (capture through the evaluative framework).** A parallel:
  which signals the system rewards fixes what organizations become.

  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- The estimates are linear extrapolations of perception-based, ordinal
  indicators rescaled to Somalia–Singapore units; "infinity" follows
  mechanically from a negative rate.
- The zero-at-independence assumption bounds the historical rate and does
  not estimate it.
- The vignettes are from the authors' own experience; the framework is
  conceptual.
- Premature load bearing is named and defined but not developed into its
  own section in this version, and a reference to "Figure 2" for capacity
  frontiers (pp. 18–19) does not match the paper's Figure 2.
- Read in the working-paper version; the journal text may differ.
