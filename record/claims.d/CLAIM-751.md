# CLAIM-751: Provenance can be carried without blame

## The claim

The owner's U47 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse": ""blame" gives
us provenance for holding upstream actors accountable as well"
([CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md)). The owner's U48 then proposed blameless culture as a case.
Blameless practice tries to keep what the owner's sentence credits to
blame, the trace upstream, by causal tracing alone: "contributing causes",
in the SRE chapter's words, "without indicting any individual or team".
If that works anywhere, blame is one carrier of provenance and not its
source.

It works in one documented case. The NTSB's report on the Tempe crash
([CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md), read in [NOTE-2034](../notes.d/NOTE-2034.md)):

- **No blame, by rule.** "The NTSB does not assign fault or blame for an
  accident or incident"; its investigations "are not conducted for the
  purpose of determining the rights, liabilities, or blame of any person or
  entity" (49 CFR 831.4(c)).
- **No liability, by statute.** "No part of a report of the Board ... may
  be admitted into evidence or used in a civil action for damages
  resulting from a matter mentioned in the report" (49 U.S.C. § 1154(b),
  read in [NOTE-2081](../notes.d/NOTE-2081.md)).
- **Upstream all the same.** The probable cause is the operator's
  distraction; contributing are Uber ATG's risk assessment, oversight of
  operators and lack of countermeasures to automation complacency, "all a
  consequence of its inadequate safety culture", and the Arizona
  Department of Transportation's "insufficient oversight". The culture
  contributed "specifically to the vehicle operator's extended
  distraction" (pp. v, 46).
- **Repair upstream.** Its recommendations (p. 60) go to NHTSA, the state
  of Arizona, the American Association of Motor Vehicle Administrators and
  Uber ATG ("Complete the implementation of a safety management system").
  None goes to the operator.

So the provenance of the operator's incapacity, in [TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md)'s causal and
constitutive senses, was traced to the company and the state, and the
remedial question, who should act, was answered upward, by a body that
could blame no one. [ARG-118](../arguments.d/ARG-118.md) sets out the inference and its critical
questions.

## Its relation to [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md)

Proposed as a qualification of [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md), it turned out not to deny
anything [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md) states. [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md)'s own "What it does not say" already
has "Not that provenance is only a matter of blame. Formation and
maintenance can be traced descriptively". What this claim adds is a
documented instance at the level of an institution, with the conditions
that made it work: an investigator independent of the parties, forbidden
to blame, and fenced off from liability. Hence `complements`, not
`objects_to`. [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md) says what blame can carry; this says what can
carry it without blame.

Cook ([THEORY-1998](../theory.d/THEORY-1998.md)) gives a reason to expect the two to differ in
practice. Root-cause attribution, he argues, reflects "the social, cultural
need to blame specific, localized forces or events", so blame tends to
stop near the event. That is no evidence against [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md), which is about
blame traced upstream, but it is a reason to doubt that blame is usually
the carrier that reaches there.

## What it does not say

- **That [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md) holds only where no independent investigator exists.**
  That was the stronger form proposed. One specimen shows that blame is not
  the only carrier. It does not show that, where such an investigator
  exists, blame adds nothing: blame may still be what makes an upstream
  actor act on the trace, which is this claim's second defeater.
- **That internal blameless reviews reach upstream.** The NTSB is outside
  the organizations it investigates. Whether a review commissioned by the
  organization reaches above its own commissioners is [CLAIM-750](CLAIM-750.md)'s
  question, and the claims complement each other.
- **That no blame fell on the operator.** The company suspended her ([NOTE-2034](../notes.d/NOTE-2034.md),
  p. 27); prosecution and press are outside the report and unread.
- **That the NTSB's language is neutral.** "Inadequate" and "insufficient"
  are evaluations. What the Board withholds is the assignment of fault and
  liability, not judgement of practices.

## Note of 2026-11-22: CSB reports alongside the Tempe report

Five of them reach corporate governance or oversight, funding, or regulatory gaps, and 9 address recommendations above the team. One, PEMEX Deer Park, stops at site procedures. That is more instances of this claim's existence statement, not a test of its defeater, which needs blame-based processes on the same failures. The arms differ in domain and severity (software outages against chemical accidents) as well as in who reviews.

## Note of 2026-11-22: the Tempe prosecution, read: the blame-based process on the same failure stopped at the operator
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-1005 ARG-232 — new; filed by the lead run on 2026-11-22 -->

Read for [LEAD-189](../leads.d/LEAD-189.md) on 2026-11-22: the AP's report of the plea, with NPR's reports of the declination and the charge ([THEORY-2289](../theory.d/THEORY-2289.md); [NOTE-2389](../notes.d/NOTE-2389.md), [NOTE-2397](../notes.d/NOTE-2397.md), [NOTE-2398](../notes.d/NOTE-2398.md)). The theory now `corroborates` this claim. This claim's "What it does not say" had left "prosecution and press" unread.

- **What happened.** The Yavapai County Attorney found "no basis for criminal liability for the Uber corporation" (March 2019). The operator was charged with negligent homicide (September 2020) and pleaded guilty to endangerment, an undesignated felony, with three years of supervised probation (July 2023). No one else was charged in the reports read.
- **What it does for this claim.** It is not needed for the existence statement, which rests on the NTSB report. It turns the first defeater the other way on this failure: the defeater asks whether blame-based processes on the same failures reach upstream actors that no-blame investigations do not, and here the criminal process reached less far. [CLAIM-1005](CLAIM-1005.md) files the contrast, with [ARG-232](../arguments.d/ARG-232.md). One failure is an instance, not a rule.
- **The objection.** [CLAIM-1033](CLAIM-1033.md): the victim's family settled with Uber and the governor suspended its testing, so liability and sanction did reach the company outside the criminal law. It concedes that neither traced which of the company's decisions failed, which leaves this claim's point standing: the provenance, as distinct from the consequences, was carried by the investigation that could blame no one.
- **[CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md).** The criminal law, one practice of blame, carried no provenance upstream here. That bears on whether blame usually does, which [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md) does not assert.

No status changes.
