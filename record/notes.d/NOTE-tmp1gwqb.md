# NOTE-tmp1gwqb: Mossio, Montévil & Longo — organization as a theoretical principle

## Contribution

A programmatic paper in a special issue that proposes principles for a
theory of organisms. It argues against "genocentrism", the view that
genetic information determines biological organization (§1), and for
organicism. It surveys the history of the idea of organization from Kant
to Kauffman (§3), restates closure of constraints as the paper LIT-582
gave it (§4), and draws two implications for the relation between
stability and variation (§5). Its novelty relative to LIT-582 is the
status it gives closure: not a model or a criterion for drawing
boundaries, but a *theoretical principle*.

## Key insight

"All biological organisms ... meet two general principles without
exceptions: they are organized, and their organization undergoes
variation" (p. 2). The principles are "overarching hypotheses that frame
the intelligibility of the objects within the biological domain",
analogous to space and time, or to "positing a priori the phase space",
in physics (p. 2). So the claim is not that closure has been found in
organisms by observation. It is that biology should take it as
constitutive of its object.

## Assumptions

- **Thermodynamic openness.** "Only thermodynamically open systems can
  possibly comply with the organization principle, although not any
  thermodynamically open system does" (p. 6).
- **The process/constraint distinction (§4.1).** Given a process A ⇒ B,
  C is a constraint on it at time scale τ iff (I) the process with C
  differs from the process without it at τ, and (II) the aspects of C
  that matter are conserved through the process at τ. "A given entity
  cannot be qualified as a constraint in abstracto" (p. 7). Conditions I
  and II must hold at the same τ.
- **Direct dependence (footnote 15).** C₁ depends on C₂ when C₂
  constrains, at τ₂, a process that produces C₁, and no other process
  constrained by C₂ intervenes in time. Otherwise "the concept of
  dependence would include many biologically irrelevant situations".
- **Generative, not modulatory (footnote 17).** Only dependences that
  produce the relevant aspects of a constraint count towards closure.

## Key results

These are definitions and arguments, not theorems.

- **Closure (§4.2, p. 8).** A set C of constraints realizes closure if
  every Cᵢ in it depends directly on at least one other member and has at
  least one other member depending on it. "The principle of organization
  states that biological systems realize a closure of constraints"
  (p. 7). Further specifications are deferred to Montévil and Mossio 2015
  (footnote 16). The abstract diagram: of C₁ to C₅, C₁–C₄ are dependent
  and C₂–C₅ generative, so {C₂, C₃, C₄} realizes closure. C₅ is an
  external constraint the closure is "always realized with respect to".
- **Functions (p. 9).** "Constraints subject to closure define biological
  functions"; "constraints do not exert functions when taken in
  isolation".
- **Stability (§5.1, pp. 9–11).** Each constraint is conserved only at its
  own scale τ and must be regenerated beyond it. Because of closure, its
  regeneration depends on other constraints, so closure maintains the
  constraints beyond their time scales. This is "a kind of
  'organizational inertia'". Conservative stabilization removes a local
  variation only "in those cases in which local variation does not affect
  the constraints in charge of generating the one ... being affected"
  (p. 11). DNA's stability is "a consequence of the biological activity
  of the whole organization" (p. 11), not the source of it.
- **Variation (§5.2, p. 12).** When a variation does feed back into its
  generators, it propagates, and "two outcomes are possible. Either the
  resulting system cannot realize closure anymore, in which case it is not
  viable and disintegrates. Or, it does realize a new closure through
  cumulative stability". By contrast, in a rock, a flame or a table "a
  local variation cannot induce other local variations, which would
  result in a global reconfiguration ... no global variation results from
  local variation" (p. 12). This is offered as a reading of Darwin's
  "correlated variations".
- **Scope.** Biology "is the science of systems meeting the principles of
  organization and variation, organisms being a specific, particularly
  relevant, class of biological systems"; ecosystems might qualify
  (p. 2).

## Concepts

- **Constraint** — an entity that acts on a process at time scale τ while
  its relevant aspects are conserved at τ (§4.1).
- **Dependence** — C₁ is produced by a process constrained by C₂;
  C₂ is *generative* for C₁ (§4.2).
- **Closure** — mutual dependence in the sense above; the constraints in
  it are *constitutive*, the rest external (footnotes 17, 21).
- **Theoretical principle** — an overarching hypothesis framing the
  intelligibility of a domain's objects (§1).
- **Conservative / cumulative stability** — removing variation versus
  integrating it into a new closure (§5).

## Connections

- LIT-582 (Montévil and Mossio 2015, read) is the formal source; this
  paper adds no formal content to its definitions and drops its
  "tendency to closure" measure. TERM-070 holds the definition.
- The companion on variation, Montévil et al. 2016 in the same issue, is
  not held. Soto et al. 2016's default state (proliferation with
  variation and motility) is discussed in the conclusion as connected to
  organization, not proposed here as a third principle.
- Rosen's closure to efficient causation, Kauffman's work–constraint
  cycles, Piaget, autopoiesis (THEORY-666), and Ruiz-Mirazo and Moreno's
  basic autonomy are surveyed (§3). Footnote 9 uses the hydrological
  cycle as a mutual dependence that is not biological organization,
  because the paper requires constraints, not just processes.

## Bearing on the record

- **The registration's summary needs a correction.** It says the paper
  proposes organization "alongside variation and the default state of
  proliferation with variation". The paper proposes two principles,
  organization and variation; the default state is Soto et al.'s, and
  the conclusion only relates organization to it.
- **TERM-070.** Consistent; the same definition with the same direct and
  generative qualifications (footnotes 15, 17). The paper adds the
  explicit statement that a closure is always realized relative to
  external constraints (C₅), which TERM-070 could note.
- **CLAIM-tmpczfpk.** The paper supports two parts of the claim. A
  dependence cycle is needed for anything to persist beyond its own time
  scale. And a cycle of processes alone, like the hydrological cycle, is
  not closure. It says nothing quantitative.
- **CLAIM-tmppht9k and CLAIM-tmpr8p2x.** The paper's stability argument
  is exactly the inference these claims caution against: it reads
  stability off the network of dependences without a dynamical model.
  Its own condition for conservative stabilization (that the variation not
  reach the generators) is a structural condition. Whether the
  regeneration recovers is a separate dynamical question that CASE-tmpwxags
  shows can fail at a saddle-node while the coupling looks healthy.
- **THEORY-514** (Baedke): the "new closure" outcome of §5.2 is the
  positive mark Baedke proposes for organisms. Mossio et al. state it as
  a feature of all organized systems, with no claim that only organisms
  achieve it. Their scope (biology covers any system meeting the
  principles, ecosystems possibly included) agrees with Baedke's negative
  half.
- **THEORY-072.** Supports its demarcation from the flame (pp. 6, 12) but
  adds no argument beyond LIT-582.
- No instruction for ML practice.

## Limitations

- No model, measurement or proof; the stability and variation claims are
  argued from examples.
- The principle's a priori status is asserted by analogy with physics;
  what would count against it is not said.
- "Without exceptions" (p. 2) is a definitional claim once biology is
  defined as the science of systems meeting the principles, so it cannot
  be tested by finding an unorganized organism.
- The contrast with rocks, flames and tables is three examples.

## Open questions

- What dynamical condition on a closure network makes conservative
  stabilization succeed? The structural condition is necessary, not
  sufficient.
- Can the claim that only closed systems turn local into global
  reorganization be stated so that it could fail, for example for
  coupled non-closed dissipative structures?
