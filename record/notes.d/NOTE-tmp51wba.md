# NOTE-tmp51wba: Keller — Sprachwandel

## Contribution

The invisible-hand theory of language change: explaining structure as the
unintended causal result of many intentional acts, without either design
or nature.

## Key results

1. **Convention.** A language is a complex system of conventional
   rules: "Eine Sprache ist ein komplexes System konventioneller Regeln."
   (lecture, p. 4).
2. **Not the world.** Changes in the world are "weder notwendig noch
   hinreichend" (neither necessary nor sufficient) for language change (p.
   7).
3. **The third kind.** Paths trodden across a lawn are made by people
   but neither willed nor known: "Trampelpfade sind von Menschen gemacht,
   aber weder willentlich noch wissentlich." (p. 8). "So genannte
   natürliche Sprachen sind Phänomene der dritten Art." (So-called natural
   languages are phenomena of the third kind, p. 9), quoting Ferguson:
   "the result of human action, but not the execution of any human design"
   (p. 9).
4. **Levels.** The explanation links the micro level of individual
   action and the macro level of the structure it produces (p. 9); only
   systematic breaches become the new rules: "Nur für die systematischen
   Regelverletzungen gilt, dass sie die neuen Regeln von morgen sind." (p.
   10).
5. **Example.** The pejoration of words for women as the unintended
   consequence of many people trying to be especially polite (p. 11).
6. **Prediction.** From the Klett excerpt: the theory has the three
   stages of the ideal-type model (motives, cumulative process,
   structure), and it is not fit for predicting the development of a
   particular meaning (excerpt, pp. 1–2).

## Assumptions

Speakers act for local goals under partly shared maxims (save effort, be
understood, be noticed, be polite); a language is a system of conventions;
the three-stage model fits culture generally.

## Concepts

Phenomenon of the third kind; invisible-hand explanation; micro and macro
level; maxims; systematic violation; pejoration; structural prediction.
