# NOTE-1476: Dal Cengio, Lecomte & Polettini — Geometry of nonequilibrium reaction networks

## Contribution

For networks in which each reaction turns one species into another, the
Hill–Schnakenberg theory reads off cycles from a spanning tree and uses them
to identify the forces that keep a system out of equilibrium. Chemical
networks with many-to-many reactions are hypergraphs and have no spanning
tree. The paper replaces the tree by Gauss–Jordan reduction of the
stoichiometric matrix, −GS = [[0, 0], [1_M, T]] (eq. 22). The same matrix T
then gives a basis of cycles c_α = (−T, 1)ᵀ for ker S and of cocycles
c_γ = (1, Tᵀ)ᵀ for im Sᵀ, and G gives an integrator that recovers potentials
from conservative forces (eqs. 26–27). On this geometry it builds a
Helmholtz–Hodge-like decomposition of affinities and currents (§IV A), a
hidden spectral symmetry in linear response (§V A 3), and an algorithm for
thermodynamically feasible reconstruction of metabolic fluxes (§V B).

## Key insight

The cycles of a reaction network are the sets of reactions whose combined
effect leaves every species as it was (S c = 0). They are where steady
currents live and where non-conservative force is measured: the circulation
of the affinity around a cycle, A^e_α = c_α · A, does not depend on the
concentrations and is the drive (eq. 30). Everything orthogonal to the
cycles, the cocycles, is relaxation. So a network can be held from
equilibrium only through its cycles, and in an open network the relevant
ones are the emergent cycles that run from one chemostat to another.

## Assumptions

- **Dilute, well-stirred mass-action networks with every reaction strictly
  reversible**, k^±_ρ > 0 (§I C). "The only stringent assumption being that
  reactions ... are reversible, so as for Eq. (6) to be well-defined" (p. 5).
- **Net stoichiometry.** S_ρ = ν^{−ρ} − ν^{+ρ}, so a species that is both
  reactant and product of a reaction, a catalyst, has entry zero (eq. 5 and
  following).
- **Chemostats as exchange reactions** ∅ ⇌ Y_i with rates not bound by the
  Wegscheider condition (eq. 42), so that the network stays a graph-like
  object when opened (§IV C).
- **Linear regime** for §V only: affinities small, near an equilibrium.
- The results being "of topological nature", the authors say they extend to
  any kinetics with "conjugate currents and forces such that J > 0 if and
  only if A > 0" (p. 24).

## Key results

- **Cycles and cocycles (§III A).** dim ker S = R − M cycles, M = rank S
  cocycles, N − M conservation laws; c_α · c_γ = 0. For graphs these reduce
  to the Hill–Schnakenberg ones; for hypergraphs entries of T can be
  fractional (Fig. 6: c = (1/2, 1/2, 0, 1)ᵀ).
- **Islands (§III B 4).** Each cocycle bounds an "island" z_γ = (G_M x)_γ
  with d_t z_γ = J · c_γ (eq. 28). If the cocycle's current is cancelled,
  z_γ is conserved: "the vector v^γ can be seen as a new conservation law
  which emerges when removing c_γ" (p. 12). Numerically, suppressing a
  cocycle reaction by ε = 10⁻¹ to 10⁻³ produces a plateau, "a new timescale
  controlled by the inhibitor", while suppressing a non-cocycle reaction
  changes little (Fig. 9).
- **Kirchhoff laws (§IV A).** Conservative affinity iff c_α · A = 0 for every
  cycle (eq. 31, KVL); stationarity iff S J = 0 (eq. 35, KCL), so steady
  currents are combinations of cycles and "the currents J^e_γ ... are
  transient and they vanish at steady state" (p. 14). σ = J · A splits into a
  cocycle part that vanishes at steady state and a cycle part that vanishes
  for reversible dynamics (eq. 36).
- **Wegscheider ⇔ reversibility (§IV B, App. B).** Seven conditions are
  equivalent, among them the product of rate-constant ratios around every
  cycle being 1, the existence of standard chemical potentials, of an
  equilibrium cancelling all currents, and microscopic detailed balance of
  the population process.
- **At least two chemostats (§IV C).** With one chemostat the affinity stays
  in im Sᵀ, "the dynamics still reversible", with equilibrium μ_eq = a ℓ₀
  (eq. 47). Driving needs two chemostats at different a, which open an
  emergent cycle; # emergent cycles = dim ker S_res − dim ker S (eq. 49).
- **Gordan (footnote 6, p. 15).** Either a positive conservation law exists
  or some j has S j > 0, never both; the second "implies that the set of
  reactions can create matter". The authors note that "chemostatted
  autocatalytic reactions fall in case (ii)".
- **Linear response (§V A).** Transient relaxation is governed by an M × M
  Onsager matrix L_Q = Λ_M + TΛ_{R−M}Tᵀ, steady response by an (R − M) ×
  (R − M) matrix L_P = Λ⁻¹_{R−M} + TᵀΛ⁻¹_M T (eqs. 69–70). After the rescaling
  Ĵ = Λ^{−1/2}J, Â = Λ^{1/2}A they share all eigenvalues other than 1
  (eq. 80), "which generalizes to network topologies the 1D Einstein relation".
- **Metabolic reconstruction (§V B).** The cocycle currents are fixed
  linearly by the external currents, valid "arbitrarily far from the linear
  regime" (eq. 82–83). Only the chord (cycle) currents carry the nonlinear
  KVL constraint, and a linear-regime formula (eq. 96) spans the feasible
  ones by varying positive λ's. Feasible regions are shown for one example
  (Fig. 12).

## Concepts

- **Chemical cycle** — a vector in ker S: a combination of reactions that
  leaves every species unchanged (§II B, §III A).
- **Cocycle** — a vector in im Sᵀ, the boundary of an island; carries
  transient flux and conservative force.
- **Island** — a weighted set of species, v^γ, whose total changes only by
  the current across its cocycle.
- **Emergent cycle** — a cycle of the network with its chemostatting
  reactions that is not a cycle of the closed network (eq. 49).

## Connections

It generalises Polettini's earlier graph cocycles (their ref. 17) and the
Polettini–Esposito emergent-cycle bookkeeping that Rao and Esposito use
([LIT-1889](../literature.d/LIT-1889.md), [NOTE-1464](NOTE-1464.md)). Liang, De Los Rios and Busiello
([LIT-1886](../literature.d/LIT-1886.md), [NOTE-1463](NOTE-1463.md)) build their bounds on the same cycle structure,
as elementary flux modes. The Schnakenberg decomposition for Markov chains
is in Seifert's review, [LIT-523](../literature.d/LIT-523.md) ([NOTE-420](NOTE-420.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **A13 §4, "A productive cycle need not be a stoichiometric cycle": right,
  and stronger than A13 put it** ([THEORY-1248](../theory.d/THEORY-1248.md)). The paper's cycles are
  vectors in ker S built from net stoichiometry, from which a catalyst
  cancels. In my rewriting of [CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md)'s first construction (c₂
  catalysing S → c₁, c₁ catalysing S → c₂, both decaying to W), the internal
  matrix has kernel spanned by (1, 0, 1, 0) and (0, 1, 0, 1): fuel → c₁ →
  waste and fuel → c₂ → waste. The closed network's one cycle is
  (−1, 1, −1, 1), a futile loop of zero affinity. The mutual production of
  c₁ and c₂, the cycle [CLAIM-516](../claims.d/CLAIM-516.md) proves necessary, is none of these. It
  lives in the rates, through the exponents of the mass-action law, and the
  paper's geometry, thermodynamics included, does not see it. Net production
  from the reservoirs, (1, 1, 0, 0) ↦ S_X j = (1, 1) > 0, is Gordan's case
  (ii), which footnote 6 says only an open network can have. So the three
  kinds of cycle A13 separated are three different objects: a production
  cycle in the catalytic influence graph; a stoichiometric or emergent
  cycle in ker S; and a sheaf's kernel, on which this paper says nothing.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md): a chemical instance of the first half, by inhibition.**
  Suppressing the reactions of a cocycle by a factor ε makes the island
  total z_γ conserved over the window the suppression opens, with a plateau
  whose length is set by ε (Fig. 9, §III B 4). That is the claim's
  "relation they settle into is a constraint ... over the window between the
  two time scales", with the slow reaction, not a slow variable, making the
  constraint. The authors tie it to "the control of chemical kinetics
  through catalysts and inhibitors" (p. 23). The claim's list of mechanisms
  should carry it: a slowed reaction channel makes an approximate
  conservation law.
- **[CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md) and the record's toy models in general.** The authors note
  that in "phenomenological models of evolutionary games, gene regulatory
  networks or theoretical ecology ... effective reactions are typically
  irreversible" (p. 15), and that for biological species "no notion of
  thermodynamic feasibility imposes that the affinities of internal cycles
  have to be zero" (p. 24). The record's constructions are of that kind. The
  cycle–cocycle decomposition applies to them, but no thermodynamic
  conclusion follows until they are given reversible elementary chemistry.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **Mostly steady state and relaxation.** Multistability, oscillations and
  chaos are named in the Outlook as open; the linear-regime reconstruction
  "is beyond the reach" of transitions between attractors (p. 23).
- **Fig. 9's time-scale claim rests on one example**; the exact statement is
  only eq. 28.
- **The reconstruction is linear-regime.** Whether physiological networks
  are in it is open (p. 24).
- **Appendices skimmed**; I rely on their statements.

## Open questions

- Is there a graph-free characterisation of which reactions belong to every
  cocycle basis, and so which inhibitions always create a conservation law?
- Does the isospectrality survive away from equilibrium in any form?
