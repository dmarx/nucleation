# CLAIM-1309: Exploitation can reveal tacit expectations

## The claim

The owner's words, at U17, as in [CLAIM-1209](CLAIM-1209.md). **Alternative (b) of
two**: "expose" as *make visible*.

An implicit contract is, by [CLAIM-1210](CLAIM-1210.md), unspoken: its terms live in
what the parties expect and assume. While it runs smoothly nobody needs to
state them. Exploitation, an asymmetric use of the reliance, is one of the
events that brings them out: the exploited party finds out what it had been
relying on, and that the reliance gave another party something to use.

A22 §6's example: "an employee who routinely volunteers to repair failures
may discover that their employer has ceased maintaining the resources
needed to prevent those failures. What the employee experienced as
occasional assistance has become part of the institution's operating
model." The discovery is the revelation; before it, the expectation was
tacit on both sides.

## What it does not say

- Not that exploitation is the only thing that reveals an implicit
  contract. A20 §9 gives breach in general ("An implicit contract may become
  legible only when its expected regularity fails"); exploitation is the
  case where the failure, or the asymmetry, is someone's use of the
  reliance.
- Not that revelation is reliable. [CLAIM-700](CLAIM-700.md) holds the opposite tendency:
  where conscientious compensation covers a shortfall, the quiet it buys is
  read as adequacy, so a successful exploitation of reliance can hide the
  expectation as well as reveal it.

## Standing

Proposed, the owner's, filed as one of two readings of U17; the other is
[CLAIM-1209](CLAIM-1209.md). The owner chooses which U17 meant, or keeps both.
