# THEORY-tmpp1ev4: Once context-dependent marginals are separated from contextuality, the behavioural, social and word-meaning data published as contextual show none

## Source

Dzhafarov, Zhang & Kujala (2015), [LIT-tmp3ue9r](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3ue9r.md), §§1–8. Supporting: Budroni et al. ([LIT-tmp0el4c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0el4c.md), §IV.C.5) on the same criterion in physics.

## What was actually shown

Contextuality-by-Default gives each object a separate random variable in each context and calls a system noncontextual when some coupling makes those variables coincide as often as each pair's own marginals allow (§1, Eq. 7). With consistent marginals its cyclic criterion reduces to the Leggett–Garg, CHSH and KCBS inequalities (§2). Applied to 73 poll question-order pairs (§3), Schröder-staircase judgements (§4), the Aerts–Gabora–Sozzo animal/sound choices (§5), 23 primed ambiguous word combinations from Bruza et al. (§6) and psychophysical matching (§7), the criterion finds no contextuality, apart from one poll pair the authors discount for multiple comparisons. The reported CHSH violation in §5 assumed consistent marginals; once inconsistent marginals are accounted for, it disappears. The Hilbert-space model of question-order effects predicts noncontextuality (§3). In physics the same criterion still finds KCBS data contextual ([LIT-tmp0el4c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0el4c.md) §IV.C.5).

## What this does not say

- That behavioural systems are never contextual; that is the authors' working hypothesis, and later Contextuality-by-Default work is reported to find behavioural contextuality (not held, not read).
- That quantum-like models of cognition or retrieval (van Rijsbergen, [LIT-262](../literature.d/LIT-262.md)) are wrong; the paper says such a model predicts the absence of contextuality.
- Anything about Kochen–Specker or Spekkens contextuality in physics.
