# QUESTION-059: Does a state that meets a functional characterization of affect thereby have to be felt, or does feeling need a mechanism, such as a body acting on the neurons that map it, that an organization lacks?

## The question

[CLAIM-039](../claims.d/CLAIM-039.md) says an organized system can have affective states, characterized functionally, without subjective experience. That needs the functional state and the felt state to come apart. The question is whether they can. Either the functional role is what makes a state felt, so that meeting it is feeling. Or feeling needs a further mechanism, and then the question is which one, and whether an organization has it.

## Why the record needs it

[CLAIM-039](../claims.d/CLAIM-039.md)'s own note names [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md) as the objection to answer first if the line is taken up. [QUESTION-053](QUESTION-053.md) asks whether organizational affect needs a group subject. If functional affect is felt affect, that question collapses into whether the group is a subject. If feeling needs a bodily mechanism, [CLAIM-039](../claims.d/CLAIM-039.md) can stand as a claim about function alone.

## What the record holds

- [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md) answers on the identity side: to feel bad is "to occupy a coordinated regulatory state", and function and phenomenality "are identical". Limit: a sketch, for hedonic valence in animals, not Frijda's action readiness.
- [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md) names the mechanism side: felt affect is "the experience of the body's regulatory state". Limit: it does not say why the state is felt.
- [THEORY-093](../theory.d/THEORY-093.md) names a candidate mechanism: feelings are "hybrid", because interoceptive pathways "let the body act directly on the neurons that map it". An organization has no such pathway. Limit: an interpretation of anatomy, read Skimmed.
- [THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md) puts felt affect in a specific subcortical substrate, not in a role. Limit: no marker separates a felt alarm from an unfelt one.
- [THEORY-095](../theory.d/THEORY-095.md) shows that long-arm functional role does not fix phenomenal character. Action readiness is a relation to the world, so meeting it does not fix what the state is like. Limit: about character, not about whether there is any.

## What would answer it

A case where the regulatory role is met and feeling is absent, or where feeling goes with the bodily mechanism when the role is held fixed. Another argument from the definition of affect would not answer it.

organization* to *felt valence*. Our framework can help articulate that
dispute, but does not yet decide it."
