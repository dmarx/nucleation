# CLAIM-002: A text's identity across retellings is a family of relations, namely identity of the artifact, continuity of transmission and similarity of communicative function, which is why a distant retelling can be recognizably descended from an original it no longer resembles

## The claim

A50 §7: "His many translations aren't alternative perfect realizations of a
single invariant structure. They occupy different regions of a multidimensional
space of partial preservation ... not only can different translations preserve
different structures, but **the act of translation changes the conditions for
subsequent translation**. That may be the most consequential point. The object
being transported is itself undergoing reconstruction, and each reconstruction
becomes the source for the next."

A52 §13.5: "Develop the philosophical idea that a text's identity across
realizations may be characterized by a structured family of preservation and
distortion relations. Distinguish identity of an artifact, continuity of
transmission, and similarity of communicative function. Consider how these
different notions jointly explain why a distant retelling may remain
recognizably descended from an original despite substantial semantic or
pragmatic change."

It bears on [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md)'s second horn without answering it: it gives a
reason that horn needed, that descent is not similarity, but does not argue
against a frame-independent act.

## Where it went

The manuscript's conclusion asks "which differences and relationships can be
recognized across a specified family of transformations", which keeps the
family and loses the three relations and the point about descent. Nothing argued
against it.

Proposal v5 restated it once more (A82 §9.4, under U30): "Propose that
continuity across transformations can be characterized by a family of directed
preservation relations. Distinguish historical descent, pragmatic similarity,
and exact equivalence. Revisit Hofstadter's analogical conception of identity."
C6 dropped it; its two uses of "descent" are gradient descent.

## Note of 2026-10-31: communication as transduction is continuity of transmission taken alone

<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmp5ux44 THEORY-tmpywwei — Proposed; readings filed on 2026-10-31 and set beside this claim -->

Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy (LIT-tmp3mudg, read in THEORY-tmp5ux44)
define communication as "the continuity of action through beings who are
in a perpetual state of becoming" (p. 1364). It is a transduction in which
"any communication also implies a transformation" (p. 1356). Bencherki and
Iliadis (LIT-tmpwspny, NOTE-tmp62e4t) give the mechanism as Simondon's
propagation "from kin to kin", in which each structured region serves as
the principle of the next (ms. pp. 13–14).

In this claim's terms, that is the second of its three relations,
continuity of transmission, taken alone. Two things follow.

- **The chain is the same.** A50 §7's "each reconstruction becomes the
  source for the next" and kin-to-kin propagation describe the same
  structure: a chain in which what is passed on is reworked at each step.
  The parallel is of structure, not of mechanism (ADR-032). The record's
  chains are retellings judged by receivers. Transduction is any causal
  continuation.
- **This claim locates the readings' vacuity.** The paper's examples
  include a piston that "communicates movement" (p. 1364). The record's
  reading found that "communication" then covers every causal continuity
  (THEORY-tmp5ux44, "What this does not say"). On this claim, continuity
  of transmission without similarity of communicative function gives
  descent, not identity or fidelity. A distant retelling is recognizably
  descended because the relations come apart. An account that keeps only
  continuity cannot say whether a descendant still does what its original
  did, and that is the discrimination the reading found missing.

Massumi's "not a logic of transmission or communication" (LIT-tmp8hcfz,
p. 9; THEORY-tmpywwei) does not conflict with continuity of transmission
here. He denies that a content is carried unchanged from one event to
another, and he grants that event factors "can be repeated, reactivated".
This claim's continuity is causal descent, not conservation of a content,
which CLAIM-112 already disowns.
