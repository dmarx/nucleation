# THEORY-2210: Withholding avoidance didn't change expectancy

## Source

Sridhar, Biagi & Wake (2026), [LIT-3076](../literature.d/LIT-3076.md), read in [NOTE-2299](../notes.d/NOTE-2299.md).

## The account

In a four-phase avoidance conditioning task, an instruction given before
avoidance extinction, "Please press the non-avoidance button to test your
expectancy of whether the noise and electrical stimulus will occur", is
compared with a pause instruction. The authors conclude that the
instruction "reduced avoidance behaviour among individuals with higher
social anxiety" and "did not attenuate conditioned threat expectancies",
"a potential dissociation between behavioural responses and cognitive and
physiological components of fear".

The reported tests support the second half and not the first: no
significant effect of instruction on US expectancy (max F = 0.48) or skin
conductance, and no significant effect on avoidance overall (p = .230) or
in interaction with social anxiety (p = .288), with a difference at +1 SD
of social anxiety of p = .066 that the authors call exploratory.

## What it would take

A confirmatory test of the avoidance effect, with expectancy measured
after the instruction. For the record's purposes ([CLAIM-446](../claims.d/CLAIM-446.md)) more is
needed: threat belief held fixed by design rather than found unchanged,
and a manipulation that changes what governs action without being a
request the participant obeys.
