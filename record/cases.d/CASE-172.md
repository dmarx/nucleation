# CASE-172: Federal science and education in the United States, 2025–2026: grants cancelled, frozen and in part reinstated, staff departures, advisory committees ended and research programs suspended, so that funding, organization and scientific capacity could be restored, or not, separately

## The case

The owner, at U4: "another case work examining here is interrogating the
full breadth of the harms incurred by the actions of the DOGE project,
and the trump administration's broader assault on the scientific research
and education establishment."

In 2025 the administration and its Department of Government Efficiency
(DOGE) cancelled or froze federal research grants, cut agency staff and
contracts, and suspended research programs; courts ordered some grants
reinstated and Congress rejected many proposed cuts. The particulars are
A4's, from reports this filing has not read, and are kept as the
exchange's:

- A *Nature* investigation of January 2026 counted more than 7,800 NIH
  and NSF grant cancellations or freezes in 2025, some later reversed, and
  more than 25,000 departures from federal science agencies; the totals
  are the administration's, not DOGE's alone.
- A 2026 Department of Education inspector-general review found a
  workforce cut of at least 1,579, 129 contracts ($1.3 billion) and 90
  grants ($504 million) terminated, with the review's access restricted.
- GAO found that about half the Office for Civil Rights' staff had been
  placed on leave, later rescinded, and that about 90% of complaints
  resolved in one 2025 period were dismissed; and in August 2026 that it
  could not verify the method behind 96% of DOGE's reported grant
  savings, and that some reported contract savings had not occurred.
- The Institute of Education Sciences lost or suspended multiyear studies;
  by March 2026 a rebuilding was under way without the original workforce
  or programs.
- A 2026 SciOPS survey: 70% of academic scientists reported less ability
  to recruit international students or postdocs, 56% less funding, and
  more than half had changed keywords in proposals (self-report).
- A 2026 *Nature* investigation counted more than 100 science advisory
  committees eliminated.

## What it shows

- **Restoring the resource is not restoring the organization.** A4: "Even
  if the funding returns a year later, the missing observations may be
  unrecoverable", and "If a doctoral student leaves research because a
  laboratory closes, reinstating a grant later does not necessarily bring
  the student back." Fiscal, organizational and scientific restoration are
  three outcomes. [CASE-111](CASE-111.md) is the formal possibility, and [CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md) the claim
  drawn from it; A4: "The empirical
  challenge is to establish whether particular systems exhibit such path
  dependence, rather than merely labeling any slow recovery as hysteresis",
  which is [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md)'s caution.
- **Reproduction, not only enactment** ([CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md)). A lab that cannot run
  experiments for a while can resume; one that loses its team must
  rebuild; a program whose cohorts stop coming may lose a local research
  tradition. A4: the damage is to "the process by which the scientific
  community generates its successors".
- **Capacity lost without output falling.** A lab can keep publishing from
  projects under way while losing the people who would start new ones; the
  reachable set of future work contracts while present output holds
  ([CLAIM-352](../claims.d/CLAIM-352.md)'s opportunity aspect). A4: the sets need not be nested, and
  "Lost reachable possibilities are not automatically harms."
- **Damage to the critical organization of inquiry.** On Longino's account
  ([THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)), ending advisory committees and narrowing fundable questions
  can weaken a community's capacity for criticism and correction without
  any researcher becoming less competent. A4: "The final step requires
  empirical investigation."
- **Success described at one level** ([CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md)). Savings reported as an
  efficiency gain and losses borne by the programs, students and
  communities are measured by different descriptions; [CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md) says the
  one does not settle the other.

## What it does not show

- **The size of any loss.** A4: "The resulting damage cannot be inferred
  simply by summing every announced cancellation or proposed budget
  reduction." The counts are announcements and self-reports, some
  reversed.
- **That DOGE did it.** A4 kept DOGE's own actions apart from the wider
  administration's, and so does this case.
- **That every lost relation was worth keeping.** A4: "Some organizational
  relations should be dismantled"; some programs may need reform. What
  counts as harm needs a normative target the structural description does
  not supply ([CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md)).
- **Standing.** That grants were cancelled and partly reinstated in 2025 is
  documented; the figures above are the exchange's and unread here. A4
  advised filing with "a clearly dated evidence window" and legal
  reversals kept apart from functional recovery, which this case does.

## Note of 2026-10-29: three of A8's works, read

A8 offered three works for this case. Two are now read and one is read in
its introduction.

- **Jasanoff, "Technologies of humility"**, [LIT-1594](../literature.d/LIT-1594.md) ([NOTE-tmp4y156](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp4y156.md)),
  offered for the breadth of harms that are hard to quantify in advance.
  That fits, with one adjustment: she writes about assessing a technology
  before it is adopted, not about counting the harm of a policy after it.
  Her first limit is this case's "success described at one level": a
  saving reported by a predictive, accounting frame overstates what falls
  within its field of vision and leaves out what falls outside. Her
  vulnerability point, that "history, place, and social connectedness"
  fall out of population-level indicators, names what a reinstated grant
  does not restore: the cohort and the local research tradition. She gives
  no method for measuring either, so she supports "What it shows" and not
  any size of loss.
- **Fiss, "The forms of justice"**, [LIT-1600](../literature.d/LIT-1600.md) ([NOTE-tmpafym3](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpafym3.md)). The formula
  "Reversal ≠ Repair ≠ Structural reform" is A8's, not Fiss's, but the
  distinction is in the paper: the dispute-resolution remedy corrects or
  prevents "a discrete event", while the structural remedy removes "a
  condition" by reorganizing an ongoing institution over many cycles. Fiss
  does not discuss restoring an earlier state; his structural suit
  presumes the existing arrangement is what threatens the value, which is
  this case's point that some relations should be dismantled.
- **Herd and Moynihan, *Administrative Burden***, [LIT-1609](../literature.d/LIT-1609.md), of which only
  the introduction is read ([NOTE-tmpkbyme](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpkbyme.md)). It says burdens can restrict
  access to a benefit or a right with no change to formal eligibility, so
  that what is lost is the practical ability to use it. That is [TERM-055](../terms.d/TERM-055.md)'s
  operational availability carried from a resource to a right. Whether
  the chapters give a measure of that loss is not known; take-up is the
  introduction's only one.

The case is unchanged.
