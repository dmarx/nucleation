# THEORY-1497: St John holds that Burning Man's gifting carries no obligation on the recipient to reciprocate, yet sustains circulation through gratitude that recipients return to the community in later labour, a generalized rather than bilateral reciprocity, and that perhaps the most pressing threat to it is non-participation, which the organization answered in part by reforming how theme camps are registered and placed

## Source

St John (2017), [LIT-2188](../literature.d/LIT-2188.md); read in [NOTE-1662](../notes.d/NOTE-1662.md).

## What was actually shown

An interpretive argument from the organization's statements, the
Philosophical Center's essays, press and the author's fieldwork, not a
measurement.

- **No obligation** (p. 4): the Gifting principle, quoted, "does not
  contemplate a return"; and (p. 5) "an absence of an obligation on the
  part of the recipient to reciprocate".
- **Circulation by gratitude** (p. 5): gifts "inspire gratitude and thus a
  response that keeps the gift in motion", likened to Sahlins's
  "generalized reciprocity", set beside the balanced reciprocity of
  swapped trinkets (p. 4).
- **The return goes to the community** (p. 7): "through countless labors in
  subsequent events a gratitude for untold gifts bestowed unto them", the
  recipients "identifying with the gift itself".
- **Non-participation as the threat** (pp. 3–4, 7–8): of the event's
  crises, "perhaps none more pressing than the growing population of
  non-participants"; the 2014 "Plug-n-Play" camps; among the responses,
  "reform of theme camp registration criteria and placement policy".

## What this does not say

- That Burning Man's gifts in fact carry no obligation: "While one can
  observe such relationships at Burning Man" (p. 7), St John says of
  Mauss's gift that obliges.
- That gifts are returned at any measured rate, or more to third parties
  than to givers: nothing is counted.
- That the placement reform reduced non-participation.
- That receiving gifts erodes or builds any capability of the recipient.
  Self-provision ("pack in all their supplies", p. 3) is stated beside
  gifting; how the two trade off is not examined. It bears on
  [CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)'s reciprocity only by analogy.
