# THEORY-023: Current evidence cannot settle whether an AI system is conscious: mimicry undercuts behavioural evidence and architectural indicators presuppose the disputed computational functionalism, so the dispute is over what counts as evidence as well as the answer

## Source

- Birch (2025), *AI Consciousness: A Centrist Manifesto*, [LIT-111](../literature.d/LIT-111.md), §§10–12, as read in [NOTE-183](../notes.d/NOTE-183.md).
- Schwitzgebel (2025), *AI and Consciousness*, [LIT-191](../literature.d/LIT-191.md), chs. 6–10, as read in [NOTE-104](../notes.d/NOTE-104.md).
- Seth (2024), *Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism*, [LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md), §§3, 5, as read in [NOTE-169](../notes.d/NOTE-169.md).
- Butlin et al. (2023), [LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md), §§1.2, 2, 3, as read in [NOTE-052](../notes.d/NOTE-052.md).

## What was actually shown

**Behaviour.** Four readings independently deny that humanlike behaviour is evidence of consciousness in a system trained on human output:

- Birch's gaming problem (§10): a system incentivised to reproduce our markers empties them of evidential value.
- Schwitzgebel's Mimicry Argument (ch. 7): a known mimic's resemblance to the model no longer licenses the inference to the underlying feature. It *undercuts* and does not *rebut* (p. 55).
- Seth's note on the Turing test (fn. 41).
- Butlin et al. §1.2.3.

**Architecture.** Here the readings diverge on the answer while agreeing on the structure:

- Butlin et al. derive 14 indicators on the explicit assumption of computational functionalism. They claim only that more indicators raise the probability.
- Seth (§§3.9, 5.2) makes conscious AI require functionalism *and* silicon substrate flexibility, and doubts both. For him the indicator route is conditional on what he rejects.
- Birch names this the Janus problem (§11). A global workspace found in an LLM would be read by a functionalist as evidence for consciousness, and by a biological naturalist as evidence that workspace theory is insufficient. "Both responses are reasonable" (p. 22). So the deadlock is at two levels: over the answer, and over what counts as evidence.
- Schwitzgebel adds that global workspace, higher-order and recurrence theories face *minimal instantiation*: trivial machines meet their minimal criteria (ch. 8). All of them rest on a narrow vertebrate evidence base (ch. 9).

The claim is the record's synthesis across the four. Birch and Seth state its two halves most directly, and [NOTE-169](../notes.d/NOTE-169.md) records that Birch's Janus sceptic "is Seth's position in effect".

## What this does not say

- **It does not say AI is or is not conscious, nor which way the prior should lean.** The readings contest exactly this:
  - Seth: unlikely on current trajectories, and false positives are the likelier error. He supports this weakly and concedes it is "not watertight".
  - Birch: flicker and shoggoth hypotheses cannot be ruled out for current LLMs.
  - Butlin et al.: no current system is a strong candidate, though their abstract's "no current AI systems are conscious" outruns the body.
  - Schwitzgebel: we "will not know" before mass deployment.
- **It does not say the deadlock is permanent.** Birch argues it can be broken over the long run by comparative animal studies (§12, Fig. 4). Schwitzgebel's iterative natural-kind strategy may converge, but on a timescale longer than AI development. Both are proposals, argued informally.
- **Mimicry is a defeater, not evidence of absence.** Schwitzgebel allows the output to remain weak evidence if one credits "the likeliest way to be an effective mimic is to actually be conscious" (fn. 95). How much post-training erodes mimic status is unanalysed ([NOTE-104](../notes.d/NOTE-104.md), C6).
- **Biological naturalism is not shown to be testable or untestable here.** Birch holds that a brute metabolism claim would be untestable, and that an explanatory one is testable. Seth frames his as explanatory, but names no property that separates conscious from non-conscious life.
