# THEORY-1942: Agent-regret fits faultless harm

## Source

The SEP entry "Moral Luck" by Dana K. Nelkin (2004, rev. 2025), skimmed in
[NOTE-2023](../notes.d/NOTE-2023.md) as the stand-in for Williams's "Moral Luck" ([LIT-2714](../literature.d/LIT-2714.md)), which
was not read.

## What was actually shown

Nothing first-hand about Williams. The entry quotes the lorry-driver
passage: "we feel sorry for the driver, but that sentiment co-exists with,
indeed presupposes, that there is something special about his relation to
this happening, something which cannot merely be eliminated by the
consideration that it was not his fault" (1976 [1993b, 43]). It describes
agent-regret as first-personal and expressed partly in "the willingness to
compensate a person who was harmed by one’s actions". It reports that the
1993 Postscript draws "a distinction between morality and ethics that
allows him to deny the existence of moral luck", at the cost of morality
no longer being a "shelter against luck" of "supreme value".

## What this does not say

- That agent-regret is blame or self-blame. Nelkin files it under
  appropriate feeling, not differential moral assessment.
- Anything about moral dilemmas or "moral residue". That is Williams's
  "Ethical Consistency" (1965), not this paper.
- That Williams's 1976 paper affirms moral luck outright. The entry calls
  extracting his position "a notoriously difficult task".
