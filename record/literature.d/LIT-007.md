# LIT-007: Quasi-quantization: classical statistical theories with an epistemic restriction

Robert W. Spekkens (2014), *arXiv preprint; later a chapter in "Quantum Theory: Informational Foundations and Foils" (G. Chiribella & R. W. Spekkens, eds.), Fundamental Theories of Physics, Springer (online 2015-12, DOI 10.1007/978-94-017-7303-4_4)* — [ARXIV-1409.5041](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5041)

## Key takeaways

- Imposing a single "classical complementarity" restriction (you may only know jointly the values of Poisson-commuting quadrature variables) on a classical statistical theory reproduces a large part of quantum phenomenology, so any phenomenon arising in such an "epistricted" theory should be counted only weakly nonclassical.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The paper argues that much of quantum theory follows from one departure from classical physics: a fundamental limit on which probability distributions over physical states can be prepared, i.e. on what any observer can know. It surveys such "epistricted" theories for continuous and discrete degrees of freedom and shows that one restriction, classical complementarity, defined via the symplectic structure of phase space, begins to unify them. Because it applies to any classical degree of freedom it acts as a "quasi-quantization" scheme, yielding only a subset of quantum theory (and, for bits, a distorted one). The author proposes classifying quantum phenomena as weakly or strongly nonclassical according to whether they can arise in an epistricted theory.
