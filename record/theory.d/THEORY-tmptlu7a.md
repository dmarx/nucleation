# THEORY-tmptlu7a: Tessman argues that oppression burdens the moral goodness of the oppressed both by preventing some virtues from developing and by requiring others, burdened virtues, that enable survival of or resistance to oppression while detracting from their bearer's own flourishing, so that eudaimonism must allow virtues disjoined from their bearer's well-being

## Source

Bell's review in NDPR (LIT-tmppxwo6), read as a stand-in for Tessman (2005), LIT-tmpovdz8, which is Unavailable. The reading is NOTE-tmp8mutd.

## What was actually shown

Bell reports, quoting Tessman, that burdened virtues contribute to flourishing "only because they enable survival of or resistance to oppression" and "in other ways ... detract from their bearer's well-being" (p. 95), and that they "could only actualize their potential to be partly constitutive of a flourishing life if the background conditions were different" (p. 160). Anger is the worked example. This is an argument in virtue ethics, not evidence.

## What this does not say

- That oppression makes the oppressed more likely to wrong others (A7's use).
- Who answers for the burdens: the account concerns their cost to the bearer, not responsibility for them.
- Anything Bell does not report; the book itself was not read.
