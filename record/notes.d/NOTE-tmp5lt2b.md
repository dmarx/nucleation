# NOTE-tmp5lt2b: Wacquant — A concise genealogy and anatomy of habitus

## Contribution

A compact restatement of habitus by Bourdieu's closest expositor. It adds
nothing new to the concept. It lists its philosophical ancestry (Aristotle,
Aquinas, Durkheim, Mauss, Weber, Veblen, Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Elias),
gives a five-part anatomy, and corrects four "recurrent misunderstandings"
(p. 68). Its use to this record is that it states, with Wacquant's
authority, that habitus is layered, can be split, can misfire, and is
inert without a field that triggers it.

## Key insight

Habitus is a disposition that needs a situation: "like a spring, it needs
an external trigger and so it cannot be considered in isolation from the
definite social worlds (and eventually fields) within which it operates"
(p. 69). So an analysis of dispositions is always paired with "the mapping
of the system of positions that alternately excite, suppress, or redirect
the inclinations of the agent" (p. 69). Practice comes from the meeting of
the two, which Wacquant calls "the microdialectic of dispositions and
positions" (p. 69).

## Assumptions

- **Dispositions are acquired socially, in the body.** Habitus is "the
  ways in which the sociosymbolic structures of society become deposited
  inside persons in the form of lasting dispositions" (p. 65).
- **It operates below consciousness.** "a practical competency, acquired in
  and for action, that operates beneath the level of consciousness" (p. 66).
- **Earlier layers filter later ones.** Each layer "operates as a prism
  through which later experiences are filtered" (p. 67), so infancy weighs
  most.
- **The essay is Bourdieu's frame throughout.** Rival accounts of action
  (rational choice, symbolic interactionism) are characterized briefly and
  dismissed as "spontaneist, instantaneist, and intellectualist" (p. 68).

## Key results

There are no data. By section:

- **Genealogy (pp. 64–65).** From hexis, "an acquired yet entrenched state
  of moral character that orients our feelings and desires", through
  Aquinas, to the classical sociologists and phenomenology. Bourdieu
  reworked it "to transcend the opposition between objectivism and
  subjectivism" (p. 65).
- **First use was for discordance, not reproduction (pp. 65–66).** In
  Béarn and Algeria Bourdieu used habitus "to capture the discordance
  between the culturally given capacities and proclivities of people and
  the requirements of the emerging social system". Uprooted Algerian
  peasants carried a "split habitus" (p. 66).
- **The anatomy (pp. 66–67).** Unlike Chomsky's grammar, habitus is (i)
  social and so variable "across distributions of power"; (ii)
  transferable across domains; (iii) "enduring but not static or eternal";
  (iv) "endowed with built-in inertia"; and (v) it "introduces a lag, and
  sometimes a hiatus, between the past determinations that produced it and
  the current determinations that interpellate it".
- **Sociation and individuation (p. 67).** Shared conditions give "a
  masculine habitus, a bourgeois habitus, a national habitus" and
  institutional ones ("a juridical habitus"); at the same time each person
  "internalizes a matchless combination of such schemata". Practices are
  "orchestrated without being the product of the organizing activity of a
  conductor" (Bourdieu, quoted).
- **Misreading 1: one structure (p. 68).** Habitus is "a multilayered and
  dynamic set of schemata that records, stores, and prolongs the influence
  of the diverse environments successively traversed". A secondary habitus
  (school, "a boxing gym, a painter's studio, a religious sect, a political
  party") is grafted onto the primary one, giving "a compromise formation".
- **Misreading 2: coherence (pp. 68–69).** "habitus is not necessarily
  coherent and unified". Congruent institutions make a cohesive habitus;
  divergent ones make "unstable systems of dispositions divided against
  themselves and wont to generate irregular and inconsistent lines of
  action".
- **Misreading 3: only reproduction (p. 69).** Habitus "does not
  necessarily agree with the social world in which it evolves", and its
  misfiring "constitutes a major spring of personal resistance, social
  innovation and structural transformation".
- **Misreading 4: self-sufficiency (p. 69).** "the same habitus will yield
  different lines of conduct when called out by different strategic
  opportunities".
- **A research posture (p. 70).** Habitus is "first and foremost a
  stenographic manner of designating a research posture"; "the proof of
  the theoretical pudding of habitus must consist in its empirical eating".
- **Note 7 (p. 70).** There is "no need to 'supplement' the theory of
  habitus" for creative action (Joas), "to capture the multiplicity of
  temporalities and structures (Sewell, 2005: ch. 4)", or for the acting
  subject (Ortner).

## Concepts

- **Habitus**: "lasting dispositions, or trained capacities and patterned
  propensities to think, feel and act in determinate ways" (p. 65),
  socially acquired and embodied.
- **Primary and secondary habitus**: the first acquired in the family in
  early childhood; the second "grafted later onto the latter by the
  specialized pedagogical labour of the school and other didactic
  institutions" (p. 68).
- **Split, broken or cleft habitus**: dispositions formed by incompatible
  worlds and divided against themselves (pp. 66, 69, n. 6).
- **Hysteresis (not named so)**: the "lag, and sometimes a hiatus" between
  the conditions that formed a habitus and those that now call on it
  (p. 67).

## Connections

A gloss on Bourdieu's Outline (1972), Logic of Practice (1980a) and
Pascalian Meditations (1997), against two foils: Bourdieu read as a
"reproduction theory" (p. 66) and the rational and symbolic actors of
economics and interactionism (p. 68). It names its critics in note 7: Joas,
Sewell and Ortner. Sewell's 1992 essay, [LIT-1383](../literature.d/LIT-1383.md), read in the same batch, is
the earlier form of the critique the note dismisses: there Bourdieu's
habitus is "agent-proof" and "unrealistically unified and totalized". The
two readings of habitus disagree, and Wacquant answers by assertion, not
argument.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) (selves as organizational perspectives).** The claim
  maps [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)'s dispositional sense onto "a perspective's dispositional
  repertoire", habitus in the sociology it leaned on. The reading supports
  the borrowing, and sharpens its defeater. Habitus, as Wacquant states it,
  already does three things the claim credits to the perspective
  vocabulary: it is layered and can be split (C1, C2); it is shared as a
  kind and unique as a token (C5); and it is distinct from the positions
  that call it out (C4). The last is close to the claim's separation of a
  perspective from its carrier's situation. So the defeater "habitus …
  explain[s] the same transitions as well with fewer assumptions" is live,
  not hypothetical. What habitus does not supply is the claim's lineage
  and its distributed realization: habitus is always "deposited inside
  persons" (p. 65), and its sharing is a matter of similar conditioning,
  not of one organization spanning several people.
- **Habitus and repertoire are cited together for something they
  disagree about.** [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) cites this paper and Swidler ([LIT-1367](../literature.d/LIT-1367.md))
  side by side for sense 2. Swidler says culture does not work "via
  enduring psychological proclivities implanted in individuals by their
  socialization" (her p. 283); Wacquant's habitus is exactly such
  dispositions. The claim should say which it means.
- **[CASE-130](../cases.d/CASE-130.md) (Gracie against luta livre).** The case says trained
  practitioners "may perceive different affordances in the same movement,
  which Wacquant's account of habitus … and his boxing ethnography already
  hold." This essay supports the general point, a secondary habitus
  grafted by "a boxing gym" (p. 68) that works as "a matrix of
  perceptions, appreciations, and actions" (Bourdieu, quoted p. 66). It
  does not use the word affordance, and it gives no martial-arts
  material. The boxing ethnography itself was not read.
- **A40's point, as the LIT records it, is in the text.** Dispositions can
  be "multilayered" and "divided against themselves" (pp. 68–69).
- **[TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md) (perspective).** Wacquant's sociation and individuation
  (C5) is the token and kind distinction the term's vocabulary note draws
  between a realization and a kind, solved inside one concept: "a
  bourgeois habitus" is a kind, each person's habitus a token. It has no
  lineage object and no distributed realization.
- **[CLAIM-416](../claims.d/CLAIM-416.md) (competition over tests).** Indirect. C4 says the same
  habitus yields different conduct "when called out by different strategic
  opportunities", which is the claim's relativity of competence to a
  regime, stated for dispositions. Nothing on who sets the test.
- **THEORY warranted?** None filed. The essay is exegesis, and its claims
  are Bourdieu's, asserted rather than shown.

## Limitations

- Seven pages of exposition. The empirical cases (Béarn, Algeria, the
  hyperghetto, Manet) are named and cited, not presented.
- It is a defence of Bourdieu by his collaborator; critics appear only as
  misreadings to be cleared, and note 7 dismisses Sewell, Joas and Ortner
  in one sentence.
- "Not necessarily coherent" and "can misfire" make habitus fit both
  stability and change. The essay does not say what observation would show
  a habitus account wrong, beyond the closing call for "empirical eating".

## Open questions

- How is a split habitus told apart from two habitus in one person, or
  from a person moving between positions that call out different parts of
  one? The essay gives no criterion, and the same question is [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md)'s
  for a carrier with several perspectives.
- Is a shared habitus ever more than similar conditioning? Bourdieu's
  "orchestrated without … a conductor" suggests coordination without
  organization; whether that is a distributed realization in [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md)'s
  sense, or only a kind, is not addressed.
