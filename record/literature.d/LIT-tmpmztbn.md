# LIT-tmpmztbn: The Need for a New Medical Model: A Challenge for Biomedicine

George L. Engel, *Science* 196(4286):129–136, 8 April 1977 — DOI-10.1126/science.847460

The brief's citation (Engel 1977, Science 196(4286)) is correct. Crossref
gives the full date and the pages. The article has been reprinted at least
five times under the same title (Crossref): *Dimensions of Behavior*
(Butterworth, 1978, pp. 3–21), *Holistic Medicine* 4(1):37–53 (1989),
*Family Systems Medicine* 10(3):317–331 (1992), *The Health Psychology
Reader* (Sage, 2002, pp. 50–65), and *Psychodynamic Psychiatry*
40(3):377–396 (2012). The Science DOI is carried as the first and
canonical printing.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the PubMed abstract, not a reading. What follows is what
the abstract says, and nothing more.*

- The dominant model of disease is biomedical, and it "leaves no room within
  its framework for the social, psychological, and behavioral dimensions of
  illness" (abstract; PubMed prints "tis" for "its").
- A biopsychosocial model is proposed as a blueprint for research, a
  framework for teaching and a design for action in health care.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the founding statement of
the medical model the new `psychopathology-and-treatment` topic names
first in its blurb. No anthology topic holds it and it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice; the Anthology of the SOTA does
not hold it (a grep of its literature.d, notes.d and theory.d for
"Engel (1977", "biopsychosocial" and the DOI found nothing).

It is filed as a seed, not as a reading, because it is the canonical
citation the rest of this batch's models are measured against, and the
record would lose that anchor without it. The three works of the batch
that were read each take a position on the medical model it criticised,
and none of them cites it:

- Hofmann and Hayes ([LIT-tmpeg79d](LIT-tmpeg79d.md)) attack the "latent disease model" of
  DSM psychiatry and the NIMH's "mental illnesses are brain disorders",
  and ask what "core biopsychosocial processes" a therapy should target.
  They use Engel's adjective without his name.
- Borsboom ([LIT-tmpmi1g3](LIT-tmpmi1g3.md)) replaces the common-cause disease model with
  causally connected symptoms, and says a theory of mental disorders must
  integrate the biological, psychological and sociological levels.
- Kendler ([LIT-tmpsxzb4](LIT-tmpsxzb4.md)), also unread here, argues for explanatory
  pluralism against biological reductionism, which is the same quarrel
  in a philosopher's terms.

Whether Engel's own model is a theory, a stance or a list of levels is the
question a reading would have to answer. This entry does not try to
rebuild it from what others say.

**Priority for a reading: high**, for the reason above.
