# LIT-264: Is there contextuality in behavioral and social systems?

Ehtibar N. Dzhafarov, Ru Zhang, Janne Kujala (2016), *Philosophical
Transactions of the Royal Society A* 374(2058):20150099 (arXiv v1 28 April
2015) — [ARXIV-1504.07422](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07422), DOI-10.1098/rsta.2015.0099

## Key takeaways

- **The distinction.** Each measurement is indexed by what it measures
  and by its context, and measurements in different contexts have no
  joint distribution. A system is noncontextual if some coupling of all
  contexts makes each object's measurements coincide as often as their
  own marginals allow (m = m′). If an answer's distribution merely
  depends on the context, that is "inconsistent connectedness", and the
  authors call reading it as contextuality "a trivial sense of
  contextuality".
- **The criterion** (Eq. 7, proved in the cited papers, not here): a
  binary cyclic system of rank n is noncontextual iff
  ΔC = s₁(⟨V₁W₂⟩, …, ⟨VₙW₁⟩) − (n − 2) − Σ|⟨Vᵢ⟩ − ⟨Wᵢ⟩| ≤ 0. With
  consistent marginals it is Leggett–Garg (n = 3), CHSH (n = 4) and KCBS
  (n = 5), all violated by some quantum systems.
- **Question order (§3, S1).** The QQ equality ⟨V₁W₂⟩ = ⟨V₂W₁⟩ makes
  ΔC ≤ 0 automatic. Of 73 pairs from Wang et al.'s collection, 67 have
  ΔC < 0. The six positive values are all small (≤ 0.063), and in five
  of them the QQ equality cannot be rejected. Over the 72 pairs other
  than Rose–Jackson, the pooled χ² test of the QQ equality gives p ≈ 0.35
  (72 df). Rose–Jackson violates QQ (p < 10⁻⁷) and is still
  noncontextual, so QQ is sufficient for noncontextuality, not necessary.
- **The other data (§§4–7).** Schröder staircases: ΔC = −1.233. Aerts,
  Gabora and Sozzo's animal–sound choices: ΔC = −3.357; the published CHSH
  "violation" assumed equal marginals, which the data do not have.
  Bruza et al.'s 23 primed word combinations: ΔC from −2.882 to −0.418
  (rechecked here from S2). Psychophysical matching: no positive ΔC in any
  of between 3,024 and 11,663,568 dichotomisations per design.
- **The conclusion is a working hypothesis**: behavioural and social
  systems are noncontextual. The authors add that this does not make
  quantum formalisms inapplicable, since the quantum question-order
  model is what predicts noncontextuality here.
