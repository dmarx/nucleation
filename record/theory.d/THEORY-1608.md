# THEORY-1608: Spears argues, reviewing the self-categorization account of social influence, that when a group is internalized as a level of self, influence from in-group members is private acceptance and partly self-influence, bounded by shared identity and stronger from more prototypical members, and that crowd members switch to a group identity rather than lose the self

## The account

The account's "fundamental insight" is that "the group is not only an
external entity but (for high identifiers, at least) becomes
internalized as a level of self. Thus, social influence is partly
self-influence, saved from circularity by the fact that other in-group
members form its source" (p. 373). Influence from in-group members is
referent informational influence, private acceptance rather than
compliance. Fit decides which identity carries it. The prototype is the
position that maximizes similarity within and difference without, and "a
group effectively becomes salient and operative only in contrast to a
(relevant) out-group" (p. 379). Crowd members "actually switch to a
group identity" rather than lose the self (p. 375). The review also
names a "realm of the other" with "few concerns about creating or
protecting the self" (p. 370).

## Evidence

From [NOTE-1268](../notes.d/NOTE-1268.md), all at second hand: Lea et al. (2007), in which
nationality or sex carried influence by the topic's fit; Barreto and
Hogg's meta-analysis of 20 studies, more prototypical leaders more
influential (p. 379); Postmes and Spears's 1998 meta-analysis, in which
immersion and anonymity "enhanced depersonalization and thus
conformity to these local group norms"; Levine and Crowther, bystanders
help "primarily when they share a social identity" with the victim
(p. 375). No effect sizes, and much of the work is the author's own.

## What it bears on

- [CLAIM-493](../claims.d/CLAIM-493.md) says a fanbase's extreme members gain prototypicality
  against an outside party, and that the step from prototypicality to
  influence "rests on Turner 2005, unread, reported only by Hornsey"
  (its note of 2026-10-30). The review reports that step from another
  source, Barreto and Hogg's meta-analysis, and states the
  intergroup-contrast condition. It corroborates the claim; the claim
  does not rest on it.
- [QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) asks whether any motivation aims at another's good and
  at no self's. The review treats the group-influenced member as acting
  on a group self, not selflessly, and names a "realm of the other"
  where no self is in play, for information rather than motive.
