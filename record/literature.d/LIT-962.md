# LIT-962: Bisimulation Metrics are Optimal Transport Distances, and Can be Computed Efficiently

Sergio Calo, Anders Jonsson, Gergely Neu, Ludovic Schwartz and Javier Segovia-Aguas (2024), *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37* (NeurIPS 2024).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp0pkqj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0pkqj.md).*

- **Bisimulation metrics are bicausal transport costs.** In a bicausal
  coupling, given the joint past, each chain's next state keeps its own
  law. For finite action-free chains with real labels, the bisimulation
  metric is the unique solution of
  U(xy) = (1−γ)|r(x)−r(y)| + γ inf over couplings p of P_X(·|x) and
  P_Y(·|y) of Σ p U. That is the Bellman equation of transport over
  bicausal couplings with that ground cost, so the two coincide (§2.2).
  The step is a comparison of two fixed points. The metric's equality
  with its logical definition is van Breugel and Worrell's theorem,
  cited.
- **A finite LP (Lemma 1, Theorem 1).** The discounted frequencies of
  state-pair transitions under a coupling are characterised exactly by a
  flow constraint and two coherence constraints. The transport cost is
  1/(1−γ) times the minimum of ⟨µ, c⟩ over that polytope, with |X|²|Y|²
  variables.
- **Sinkhorn Value Iteration.** Mirror descent with a conditional-entropy
  regulariser, projecting alternately onto the two coherence sets, gets
  within ε after 324‖c‖²∞ log(|X||Y|)/((1−γ)⁵ε²) iterations (Theorem 2).
  That holds only if each inner soft-Bellman fixed point is solved
  exactly. With one inner step it still converges in experiments, and is
  up to ten times faster than earlier solvers, mostly through warm
  starts.
- **Limits.** There are no actions, γ < 1, and the kernels are known.
  The only statement relating bisimulation to trace distance is Bian and
  Abate's, cited: near-bisimilar implies close finite-trace
  distributions, and the converse is not shown.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for [QUESTION-008](../questions.d/QUESTION-008.md), and read
on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmp0pkqj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0pkqj.md). The reading confirms the claim it was filed
for, that bisimulation metrics are optimal-transport distances between
Markov chains, and narrows it. The chains have real-valued labels and no
actions, so this is the formal object of approximate bisimulation for
action-free processes. A communicative system driven by utterances is
not covered. The paper also gives no judgement that observational
equivalence ([TERM-033](../terms.d/TERM-033.md)) gets wrong and bisimulation gets right, which is
what [QUESTION-008](../questions.d/QUESTION-008.md) says an answer must name. The identification and the LP
are filed as [THEORY-tmp7x3wf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7x3wf.md), with no `answers:` relation, since the
question is Deferred. Its sequel, [LIT-948](LIT-948.md), makes the same quantity
estimable from samples. The `anthology-candidate` flag stays: the paper's
motivation is representation learning in reinforcement learning, though
the reading carries no instruction for practice.
