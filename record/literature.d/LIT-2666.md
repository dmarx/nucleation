# LIT-2666: Owning Decisions: AI Decision-Support and the Attributability-Gap

Jannik Zeiser (2024). Owning Decisions: AI Decision-Support and the Attributability-Gap. *Science and Engineering Ethics* 30(4). DOI 10.1007/s11948-024-00485-1.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of 2026-11-17; the reading of 2026-11-16 is below.*

- **The gap**: a decision-tool that "presupposes values that have a
  significant influence on the outcome of the decision and that they
  haven't endorsed as their own values"; "Despite being technically made by
  a human the decision doesn't reflect characteristics of Susan's
  judgement."
- **Distinct from accountability**: "the right person to blame didn't
  actually make the decision, such as when a manager takes the blame for
  her employee".
- **Degrees**: "attributability might not be a binary property, but a
  gradual one."

## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read in full. The paper is conceptual. It separates "who is to blame?",
"who made the decision?" and "who is in a position justify this
decision?" and argues decision support threatens the second:
"Attributability-gaps occur when a human decision-maker uses a
decision-tool that presupposes values that have a significant influence on
the outcome of the decision and that they haven’t endorsed as their own
values." Descriptive tools are not exempt, since "descriptive AI systems
also don’t provide neutral, value-free information". The exchange (A19 §2)
reports the distinction accurately; its doctor example and its tie to
responsibility for structuring others' conditions are A's own.

## Reading of 2026-11-17

Read a second time on 2026-11-17 by the continuation's readings and reconciled with the reading above on 2026-11-18: [NOTE-2007](../notes.d/NOTE-2007.md) now carries both readings and [THEORY-1915](../theory.d/THEORY-1915.md) both accounts. What that reading recorded here:

**The exchange's characterization: faithful.** A19's "related to but
distinct from accountability" and its gloss, "whose *value judgment* the
decision actually expresses", are the paper's. A's doctor example is A's
(Zeiser uses a recruiter and therapy-prediction algorithms) and makes the
same point. A's follow-on, about someone else determining "the evaluative
framework", goes slightly beyond the paper, which concerns the tool's
embedded values, not an institution's framework.
