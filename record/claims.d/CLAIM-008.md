# CLAIM-008: The abstract similarity between renderings is similarity of higher-order latents, which can survive large differences in lower-order ones

## The claim

U27: "use higher order latents to characterize the more abstract similarity we
need to invoke here". A73: "similarity in the highest-order latents can survive
substantial differences in the lower-order latents", with
D_prag = d_Z(Z(u, c_u), Z(v, c_v)), a distributional version, and a dynamical
one, D_dyn = E_(a∼π) d_Z(F_a(E_φ(u)), F_τ(a)(E_φ(v))). Its thesis: "Pragmatic
fidelity consists in graded preservation of higher-order relational and
dynamical structure across successive communicative reconstructions." And: "The
deepest form of communicative drift is not necessarily a change in what is
being talked about, but a change in the relationship between the speaker, the
audience, and what is being said."

A73's identifiability caveat, citing Locatello et al. (2019): there is "no
uniquely recoverable decomposition ... The solution is to define the latent
variables through independently testable communicative contrasts."

## Why it was replaced

U28 ([CLAIM-042](CLAIM-042.md)): "of course, this isn't a strict hierarchy". Latents were demoted to
"compressed representations of recurring patterns across these observational
constraints. They are not the ontological foundation" (A75), which is where the
manuscript leaves them (§2; [CLAIM-046](CLAIM-046.md)). The relationship-change line survives
in the Abstract ("retain its topic while reversing the speaker's stance").
