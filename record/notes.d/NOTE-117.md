# NOTE-117: Drayson — What motivates mental fictionalism?

## Contribution

Mental fictionalism holds that we should keep engaging in truth-apt talk about the mind while believing that talk lacks truthmakers. Drayson examines the two arguments Adam Toon's Mind As Metaphor (2023) offers for it. Against the negative argument — that there are no internal states with intentional and causal properties, so folk psychology has no truthmakers — she argues that committing to internal mental states does not commit one to the representational theory of mind. Against the positive argument — that treating the discourse as make-believe best explains the mind–behaviour relation — she argues it undermines fictionalism by ontologically committing Toon to the very mental states he denies.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1: motivating any fictionalism needs a negative step (the discourse lacks truthmakers) and a positive step (keeping the discourse beats eliminating or reinterpreting it); mental fictionalists have mostly defended coherence rather than motivation.
- §2: reconstructs Toon's negative argument as three premises — Cartesian folk psychology posits internal, intentional, causal states; that commitment entails the representational theory of mind (RTM); RTM is incoherent — and targets premise 2.
- §2: Lewis/Putnam/Loar-style non-representational functionalism individuates attitudes as functional wholes that are internal, intentional and causally efficacious without distinct mental representations, so it satisfies the Cartesian truth conditions without RTM; redefining "Cartesian" to require RTM would make Toon's positive claim harder to establish.
- §3: fictionalism is a semantic thesis, so Toon must show it beats eliminativism and anti-Cartesian readings (logical behaviourism, dispositionalism); Drayson argues his account of what folk-psychological metaphors "pick out" commits him to causal "overall mental states" (fn. 18–20).
- §4: agrees with Toon's scepticism about an innate, language-like RTM, but concludes that forms of realism other than RTM may address his concerns better than fictionalism does.
