# QUESTION-041: When an organized whole's operative course moves apart from what its members intend, what channels from the members keep that course correctable, and is losing them the loss of the whole's capacity to transform its own organization?

## The question

[CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) holds that a whole's operative intention is produced by systemic mechanisms that can evolve apart from its members' intentions. This question asks what lets the members bring that course back. The candidates are feedback channels. Members' signals, such as voice, exit, monitoring and sanctions, and a rule for allocating accountability, reach the organization that governs the whole and can change it. The question also asks whether losing those channels is the failure [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md) and [CLAIM-099](../claims.d/CLAIM-099.md) describe: the operative regime and its governance persist while the capacity to transform them degrades.

## Why the record needs it

[CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) says the course can drift. It does not say what stops the drift. [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md) and [CLAIM-099](../claims.d/CLAIM-099.md) separate the capacity to transform from the operative regime, but name no mechanism by which that capacity is kept or lost. If member-side channels are that mechanism, the two lines meet: the drift [CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) records is what follows when those channels fall below a floor.

## What the record holds

- [THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md): users govern a commons durably through "user-made and user-monitored rules, graduated sanctions" and nested layers, without central control. Limit: these are correlates of survival, and "Not shown for firms."
- [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md): exit and voice are "both recuperation mechanisms", and an organization "needs floor levels of both for feedback". Managers may suppress whichever channel threatens them. Limit: argued from examples, untested.
- [THEORY-207](../theory.d/THEORY-207.md): a rule that allocates responsibility to every causally accountable node, "made common knowledge, works as a corrective". Limit: it is a proposed design with no test, and what it allocates is accountability, not blame.

## What would answer it

An organization in which members' signals were cut or restored while policy and attitudes stayed about the same, with the change in its capacity to change course measured. Northstar is the record's candidate. More examples of organizations that drifted would not answer it.

## Note of 2026-10-21: whether keeping failures legible is constitutive of self-governance

A23 §13 of the exchange of 2026-10-20 named as its "most important
unresolved conceptual choice" whether "the maintenance of appropriate
legibility [is] itself a constitutive aspect of certain kinds of
self-governance", for an organization "capable of making its own failures,
dependencies, suppressed alternatives, and revision opportunities legible
to the processes authorized to respond". It proposed keeping that
"stronger position as Proposed". A24 §6 asked the same of "channels
through which less authoritative contributions can change the criteria of
authority itself". It is this question's second half, whether losing the
channels is losing the capacity to transform, asked of recognition rather
than of intention. A23 did not cite it. [QUESTION-089](QUESTION-089.md) is its companion.
[CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md), filed from A24, bears on it without answering it. No
answer is added.
