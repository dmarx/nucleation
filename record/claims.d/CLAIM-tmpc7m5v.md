# CLAIM-tmpc7m5v: Ambiguity and contextuality are different phenomena: an ambiguous utterance admits several compatible global assignments, a contextual one admits none

## The claim

A81 §3.4: "Show how ambiguous communicative meaning can correspond to multiple
compatible assignments, while incompatible constraints produce a different
phenomenon." A82 §8.7, for chains: "Distinguish increased ambiguity from an
increase in formal contextuality." A78 had said the theory differs from "merely
saying that meanings are fuzzy, ambiguous, or context-dependent" ([CLAIM-tmpbbjw4](CLAIM-tmpbbjw4.md)); this
says how.

## Where it went

C6 has no occurrence of "ambigu". The manuscript separates ordinary context
dependence from contextuality ([CLAIM-tmpaviuz](CLAIM-tmpaviuz.md)), which is a different contrast. It treats
an object as "generally a region, not a point" (§2, from A75), which is
ambiguity, and gives the parity case ([CASE-tmpzeh33](../cases.d/CASE-tmpzeh33.md)), which is incompatibility. It
never says that the two are opposite failures of a unique global reading. That
contrast is what would let a chain be described as becoming more ambiguous
without becoming more contextual ([CLAIM-tmpukbg3](CLAIM-tmpukbg3.md)).
