# THEORY-tmpopaql: Phenomenal consciousness is the coherent functioning of an embodied agent's generative model of its sensorium, coherent in space, time and cause and run either coupled to sense data (perception) or decoupled from it (imagination); embodied grounding, the model's capacity to inform and be informed by action–perception cycles at behavioural timescales and the history of such coupling by which it was learned, is necessary, so that integrated information or a functional global workspace without it does not suffice

## Source

Safron, Klimaj & Sheikhbahaee (2026), [LIT-tmp5k0u3](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5k0u3.md), read in full in
[NOTE-tmp9lg9m](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9lg9m.md) from the publisher's PDF: the abstract (p. 345) and the
sections on coherent modelling, embodiment and machine consciousness
(pp. 346–349). The earlier IWMT papers were not read.

## What was actually shown

Nothing is shown in this paper; the theory is stated.

- **The identity.** Phenomenal consciousness is what it feels like to be
  "the spatiotemporally and causally coherent functioning of a
  probabilistic generative world model for an embodied agent" (p. 345),
  "entailing either perception or imagination when respectively coupled to
  or decoupled from sense-data" (p. 346).
- **The necessity of embodiment.** "Critically, IWMT requires (body-)world
  models to be capable of both informing and being informed by
  action-perception cycles at behaviorally relevant timescales" (p. 345).
  Against IIT, systems could have "arbitrarily large amounts of integrated
  information and still not be conscious without embodied grounding";
  against GNWT, they "could instantiate functional global workspaces
  without entailing any kind of subjective experience" (p. 346).
- **Historicity.** A feedforward workspace "would be divorced from the
  environmental couplings and historicity by which adaptive predictive
  (world-)models are learned" (p. 349).
- **What could have come out otherwise.** The paper proposes that IWMT
  could be tested by building systems that meet it and "examining whether
  various conscious functions are realized" (p. 349). That tests conscious
  functions, not experience.

## What this does not say

- **It does not require current sensory input.** Imagination is the model
  decoupled from sense data, and it counts. The requirement is a capacity
  and a history.
- **It does not set a threshold.** Embodiment comes in "sufficient degrees
  (and kinds)" (p. 345), and "virtual" embodiment is allowed (p. 346).
- **It does not settle substrate.** A simulation "would recapitulate the
  phenomenology of wetness within the simulated world" (p. 349), yet
  realisation "may practically (and perhaps also in principle) require"
  physical recurrence (p. 349). The paper holds both.
- **It is not argued here.** The paper restates and aligns; the case for
  the theory is in work not read.
