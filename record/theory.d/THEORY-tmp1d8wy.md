# THEORY-tmp1d8wy: Report-based evidence cannot test whether posterior cortex suffices for experience, because report itself needs anterior activity, and no marker that bypasses report has a validation resting neither on introspection nor on a contested theory

## Source

Seth & Bayne (2022), [LIT-982](../literature.d/LIT-982.md), the section "Evaluating theories of
consciousness" and Box 3 ("The measurement problem"), as read in
[NOTE-tmpw4eom](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpw4eom.md).

## What was actually shown

An argument, in two steps.

**The report channel.** IIT and reentry theories predict that posterior
cortical activity can support experience "without contribution from
anterior areas". But "we lack reliable methods to verify such claims,
since verification relies on subjective report (or at least, executive
control in some form), which in turn requires anterior cortical
activity". The evidence channel carries the very activity whose
necessity is in dispute. The review locates the front-versus-back debate
here: "at its heart is a disagreement about the relationship between
consciousness and cognitive access".

**The bypass.** No-report markers (PCI, optokinetic nystagmus,
bifurcation dynamics) avoid overt report, but "any proposed indicator …
must be validated". Validation by introspection inherits its
unreliability and is unavailable for infants, patients and animals;
validation by theory "seems unlikely" to give intersubjective validation
"because theories of consciousness are themselves contentious". Of the
proposals for a third route, "none is uncontroversial".

What could have failed: the argument would fail if some validated
measure of conscious content existed that used neither route; the review
surveys the candidates and finds none accepted.

## What this does not say

- **Not that posterior theories are wrong or untestable for ever.** The
  limit is "at present"; the review expects methodological advances (for
  example, optogenetic tests of IIT's inactive-neuron prediction).
- **Not that no-report studies show nothing.** They show diminished
  prefrontal engagement when report is removed; the limit is on what that
  can establish about experience, which is [THEORY-094](THEORY-094.md)'s point from
  another side.
- **Not a proof.** It is a two-author review's argument; it has not been
  tested against a reply.
- **Not specific to cortex in its second half.** The validation problem
  applies to any system, including animals, organoids and AI.
