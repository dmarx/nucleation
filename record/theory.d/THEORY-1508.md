# THEORY-1508: Chetty, Hendren and Katz find, in the Moving to Opportunity experiment, that a housing voucher requiring a family in high-poverty public housing to move to a low-poverty tract for its first year raised the adult earnings of children who moved before age 13 by about 31% at ages 24 and over, that the gain fell by about $364 a year for each year of age at the move and was absent or negative for those who moved as teenagers, and that their parents’ earnings were unchanged however long they lived in lower-poverty areas; the authors conclude that the effect runs through the amount of childhood exposure, not through family income or exposure in adulthood

## Source

Chetty, Hendren & Katz (2016), [LIT-2193](../literature.d/LIT-2193.md); read in [NOTE-1695](../notes.d/NOTE-1695.md) from
NBER w21156, revised September 2015. The AER version of record and the
online appendix were not read. Page numbers are the working paper's.

## What was actually shown

- **Design (pp. 6–7).** Random assignment of 4,604 families, 1994–98, to
  an experimental voucher restricted for its first year to tracts under
  10% poverty, a Section 8 voucher, or control; 7,340 children linked to
  tax records through 2012.
- **Young children (p. 18).** TOT "$3,477. This is a 31% increase relative
  to the control group mean"; also college, marriage and adult
  neighbourhood.
- **Older children (p. 19).** About −$2,000, not distinguishable from 0;
  young–old difference p = 0.02.
- **Gradient (pp. 28–29).** $4,823 at birth falling $364 a year, zero at
  13.25; "There is little evidence of a “critical age”".
- **Adults (pp. 31–32).** Earnings effect "consistently close to zero",
  "with no evidence of the increasing pattern" as exposure accumulated.
- **Income ruled out (p. 32)** as the channel for children's gains.

What could have come out otherwise: the earlier evaluations' nulls could
have held at older ages of measurement, or adults could have gained with
years of exposure.

## What this does not say

- **That transient support persisted.** The voucher was standing support;
  the authors call the neighbourhood change "an on-going treatment
  throughout childhood rather than a one-time treatment" (p. 40). What
  ended was childhood.
- **That each year of exposure causes $364.** "one cannot necessarily
  interpret the interaction effects" causally; age at move was not
  randomized (p. 29).
- **That the gains last a lifetime.** Observed to about 28; the lifetime
  figures assume they persist.
- **That there is a threshold age** ([CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md)): the dose is roughly
  linear; the crossover at 13 is a benefit meeting a cost.
- **Which part of the neighbourhood works.** Tract poverty indexes a
  bundle.
- **That the policy has no costs elsewhere.** Spillovers on destination
  residents are not measured.
