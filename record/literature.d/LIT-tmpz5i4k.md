# LIT-tmpz5i4k: Good Sewers Cheap? Agency-Customer Interactions in Low-Cost Urban Sanitation in Brazil

Watson, G. (1995). *Good Sewers Cheap? Agency-Customer Interactions in Low-Cost Urban Sanitation in Brazil.* Washington, DC: The World Bank, Water and Sanitation Division. http://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/888621468004795413/pdf/multi-page.pdf.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpckznv, which skimmed it: Part IV and the
conclusion only. Page numbers are PDF pages; the text is OCR.*

- **Devolved upkeep failed.** Feeder upkeep handed to residents "has not
  worked well even in the best cases"; "increased resident responsibility
  requires increased interactian [sic] with agencies, not less" (p. 33).
- **The void.** "By retreating from feeder portions of the network, the
  agencies studied created a void of service provision" (p. 33).
- **Blocks did not self-organise.** In Natal "Only four percent of
  residents, however, knew who the representative was ten years later"
  (p. 34); one Recife association's line-clearing campaign "was the only
  example of common blockage removal encountered" (p. 37).
- **Refusal as sabotage.** "Non-maintenance can be a form of passive
  sabotage" (p. 39); in Recife the state water company had not accepted
  80 percent of the city's condominial systems (Table 7, p. 38).
- **What worked.** Staff continuity from construction to operation, a
  specialised maintenance crew, face-to-face contact and ongoing
  monitoring, repair and education (p. 39), and "continued agency
  involvement with feeder line maintenance" (p. 47).
