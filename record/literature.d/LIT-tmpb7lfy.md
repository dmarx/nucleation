# LIT-tmpb7lfy: Philosophy of Fame and Celebrity

Catherine M. Robb, Alfred Archer, Matthew J. Dennis (eds.) (2024), *Bloomsbury Academic (edited volume; ISBN 9781350401297)* — DOI-10.5040/9781350401327

## Key takeaways

- The first introductory collection on the philosophy of fame and celebrity, arguing that work on admiration, achievement, epistemic authority, virtue and moral psychology can be brought to bear on what fame is and how it shapes how we live.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Against the background of cancel culture, digital identities and parasocial relationships, the editors note that philosophy has said little about celebrity, even though work on admiration, achievement, talent, epistemic authority, virtue and moral psychology bears on it directly. The volume offers an introductory overview in four parts: conceptual differences between fame and celebrity, the experience of fame, celebrities' interactions with the public and the motives for seeking fame; fame and virtue and the ethics of fame; and fame in contemporary online culture.
