# CLAIM-173: The threshold criterion measures coupling strength, and coupling is not constitution: a metronome driving a musician, or a nerve driving a muscle, forms a strongly coupled block that clears the threshold without being one system

## The claim

Simon's criterion compares the size of interactions inside a group with the
size of interactions across its edge. A metronome that a musician follows
closely has a large coupling into the musician and none back. Group the two,
and the block can be nearly decomposable from the room with a wide gap. The
same holds for Adams & Aizawa's nerve and muscle ([LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)), their case of
coupled but not part. [CLAIM-157](CLAIM-157.md) then counts both as one system. The
record's entrainment claim ([CLAIM-172](CLAIM-172.md)) counts neither, because it
requires each side to adjust the other. A pacemaker that "entrains the
rest" is not thereby one with them ([NOTE-414](../notes.d/NOTE-414.md)).

## Why it was rejected

The owner asked: "and why shouldn't they?" [CLAIM-167](CLAIM-167.md) gives the
answer:
- **The criterion never claims parthood.** It says nerve and muscle *form* a
  system, not that one is part of the other. [CLAIM-172](CLAIM-172.md) had already
  used that move to sidestep the coupling-constitution objection.
- **The intuitions were about agents.** The sense that "they are not one
  system" was a sense that they are not one agent or self.
- **Inside or input is a choice.** Whether a one-way driver is inside the
  block or an input to it depends on the target.

The reply below, version 1's, is the one this objection anticipated. It is
kept as history. It would have excluded the dancing couple.

## The reply version 1 anticipated

There is a direct reply, and it is not yet filed. Require the block to clear
the threshold in *both* off-diagonal directions inside it: the musician must
drive the metronome as well as the reverse. That imports entrainment's
mutuality as a condition on the matrix. It would make the criterion
directional, and it would answer this case. Whether it over-excludes is
untested. A notebook, a tool and a leader-follower pair are all asymmetric,
and the owner's dancing couple has a lead and a follow. Until a directional
version is stated and tried on those cases, the objection stands.
