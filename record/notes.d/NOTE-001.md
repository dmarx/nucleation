# NOTE-001: Is the United States Still a Liberal Democracy?

## Contribution

Writing about three weeks into the second Trump administration, Drezner asks whether the US will still be a liberal constitutional democracy in January 2029. He reviews earlier warnings from political scientists: Barbara Walter on the US Polity score falling to "anocracy" levels for 2020, Vanessa Williamson (Brookings) on election manipulation and executive overreach, and Polity's current statement that the US "lies at the cusp of autocracy". He weighs counterpoints, including continued compliance with court orders and a law professor's view that there is no constitutional crisis yet. He then endorses Levitsky and Way's forecast of competitive authoritarianism, in which elections continue but the incumbent tilts the playing field.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Opening: the Churchill quotation about Americans doing the right thing is flagged as unverified, and the post frames its central question about 2029.
- Evidence review: it quotes Walter (2022) on the Polity score falling to +5 and Williamson (2023) on the forms of democratic erosion. It notes that Polity's own site says the US is no longer considered a democracy, while acknowledging that the score recovered by 2024 and that the coding may be "wonky".
- Counterpoints: the executive branch is still complying with court freeze orders, with a footnote conceding the compliance is imperfect, and Kristin Hickman's view is quoted that there is no constitutional crisis yet.
- Core: long quotations from Levitsky and Way. Breakdown would take the form of competitive authoritarianism rather than classic dictatorship. They give examples (Fujimori's Peru, Chávez's Venezuela, Hungary, Turkey) and warn about the "invisible" loss of opposition that never forms.
- Conclusion: the author says elite and civil-society actors appear to be appeasing out of fear, that a public rebuke has not materialized, and that the durable condition will be democracy's "precarity". A correction notes that How Democracies Die was co-authored with Ziblatt, not Way.

## Open questions

- It is a commentary and synthesis piece. The load-bearing sources are Levitsky and Way (Foreign Affairs, February 2025) and the Polity/CSP coding, which should be read directly.
- The measurement question is worth checking: how the Polity, Freedom House and V-Dem indices coded the US in 2025-2026, and how sensitive they are to coding rules.
- It is an opinion piece by a political scientist, and its forecasts should be checked against later events.
