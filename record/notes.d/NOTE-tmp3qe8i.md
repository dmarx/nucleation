# NOTE-tmp3qe8i: Gaboriaud, Gautheron, Quinton and Smeding — Wrongness and Blame Judgments and Their Dynamics

## Contribution

A three-input processing model of moral judgment, joining Malle's
typology of judgments (wrongness about the act, blame about the agent) with
Cushman's inputs (intent, and causal information split into outcome and
causality), and a first test with many scenarios, a within-participant
design, mixed models and mouse-tracking. The predicted differences between
wrongness and blame were mostly not found.

## Key results

- **Wrongness and blame can part** (Introduction): the tax-cheat example
  "illustrates that wrongness judgments do not necessarily overlap with
  blame judgments".
- **All three inputs matter, intent most** (Results, step 2): intent
  b = 2.46, d = 1.68; outcome b = 0.78, d = 0.53; causality b = 0.90,
  d = 0.61.
- **Causality weighs more in blame** (Results, step 2): "the causality
  effect was stronger (+ 0.16) in the case of blame judgments (b = 0.98)
  compared to wrongness judgments (b = 0.82)", with d = 0.05.
- **The other predictions failed** (Results): "we did not find the
  expected interaction between intent and type of judgment"; the
  outcome-by-type interaction was not found either.
- **Unexpected** (Results, step 2): "the effect of outcome was stronger in
  non-intentional (b = 0.85) compared to intentional scenarios (b = 0.71)".
- **Against Cushman's separation** (Discussion): "for both types of
  judgment (i.e., wrongness and blame), intentional as well as causal
  features play a role (and a preponderant one) all along the decision
  process."
- **Conclusion**: the findings "partially support the proposed three-input
  processing model of moral judgments, while also suggesting that the
  distinction between types of judgment (particularly wrongness versus
  blame) must not be overestimated."
- **Small** (Limitations): "The current observed differences on final
  judgments as well as on mouse trajectories remain low in size."

## Assumptions

Mouse trajectories trace the time course of judgment; intent is the
belief component only; outcome and causality are coded as one Helmert
factor; information comes in a fixed order, intent first.

## Concepts

- **Wrongness judgment** (of the act) and **blame judgment** (of the
  agent), after Malle.
- **Intent, outcome, causality**; causality as whether the agent or
  "another independent source" brought about the outcome.
- **Moral luck effect** as the outcome effect.

## Bearing on the record

- **The exchange's characterization** (A5: "All three influenced
  participants' assessments, although the proposed differences between
  wrongness and blame judgments were only partly supported"): faithful.
  The one difference that held is causality's, and it is tiny (d = 0.05).
- **[CLAIM-tmpciy9q](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpciy9q.md)**: observers' blame rises with the agent's intent, the
  harm and the agent's own causal part, which are what a party who
  arranges another's real, knowing wrong would set up. The study says
  nothing about provocation, arranged conditions, or how observers weigh
  an upstream arranger, so it does not test the claim's audience move. No
  relation is written.
- **[CLAIM-tmpa2l21](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpa2l21.md)**: the scenarios vary whether the agent or "another
  independent source" caused the outcome, not whether a source structured
  the agent's choice.
- **Moral luck**: the outcome effect is the experimental form of moral
  luck; Williams and Nagel's symposium ([LIT-tmpswc1p](../literature.d/LIT-tmpswc1p.md)) is unread.

## Limitations

The authors': a WEIRD student sample, 131 of 145 women; care/harm
scenarios only; a within-participant design that may blur the two
questions; intent "blurred" in some scenarios; small effects; a fixed
order of information.
