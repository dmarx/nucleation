# THEORY-610: What survives radical theory change in a mature science, and so what its predictive success warrants belief in, is mathematical structure, equations carried over intact or recovered as limiting cases, and not the ontology that interpreted them, as Fresnel's reflection and refraction equations survive into Maxwell's theory while the elastic-solid ether does not

## Source

Worrall (1989), [LIT-315](../literature.d/LIT-315.md) (*Dialectica* 43(1–2), 99–124), read in full in
[NOTE-297](../notes.d/NOTE-297.md).

## What was actually shown

**The setting (pp. 101–111).** Two old arguments pull apart. The
no-miracles argument says predictive success would be a miracle unless the
theory were essentially correct; Worrall weakens it to "a prima facie
plausibility argument" (p. 102). The argument from scientific revolutions
says successful theories have been overturned at the theoretical level.
Convergent realism (Boyd, Putnam) denies the history and fails on Fresnel's
ether, which was "entirely overthrown" (p. 116). Instrumentalism and
Popperian conjectural realism give up no-miracles.

**Maturity (pp. 113–115).** A science is mature once it has a theory that
predicted new *types* of phenomena not written into it. This removes some
of Laudan's counterexamples (Hartley's and LeSage's ethers). Fresnel's
ether remains the sharpest case.

**The case (p. 119).** Fresnel's four equations for reflected and refracted
amplitude ratios, tan(i−r)/tan(i+r) and its companions, are "directly and
fully entailed by Maxwell's theory" once the amplitudes are read as those of
the electric vector. Fresnel was wrong about what vibrates and right that
the phenomena "depend on something or other that undergoes periodic change
at right angles to the light" (p. 120). The reader checked the four ratios
against the standard Fresnel coefficients. They match under the modern
reading of "polarised in the plane of incidence", and Maxwell's theory
yields exactly these forms only for media of equal magnetic permeability,
a condition the paper does not state.

**The general pattern (pp. 120–121).** Usually old equations reappear as
limiting cases of new ones (Newton from general relativity) while the
mechanism does not: there is "no clear sense" in which action at a distance
approximates space-time curvature. So the correspondence principle is "not
evidence for full-blown realism – but instead only for structural
realism" (p. 121).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Fresnel's equations could have been
lost in Maxwell's theory, or his ether kept. The general claim could fail
case by case, but no survey was run.

## What this does not say

- **That the world is structure.** The view is epistemic. It is "a mistake
  to think that we can ever 'understand' the nature of the basic furniture
  of the universe" (p. 122), and Poincaré's "real objects which Nature will
  hide for ever" is quoted with approval. The ontic turn is Ladyman's
  ([LIT-342](../literature.d/LIT-342.md), [LIT-217](../literature.d/LIT-217.md)).
- **Invariance under a symmetry group.** "Structure" here is equations
  preserved across theory change, identically or in a limit. Group-theoretic
  structure does not appear ([NOTE-297](../notes.d/NOTE-297.md)).
- **Anything about Ramsey sentences or the Newman objection.** Those belong
  to Worrall & Zahar 2001 and later, as the SEP entry ([LIT-045](../literature.d/LIT-045.md)) reports.
- **That retention is the general pattern, shown.** One detailed case,
  called unrepresentative by its author, and one example of limiting-case
  recovery. "The only available account" (p. 121) claims more than the
  elimination argument shows.
- **What structural approximation is.** "Much clarificatory work needs to be
  done … concerning the notion of one theory's structure approximating that
  of another" (p. 121). Worrall's own objection to approximate truth, that it
  is intransitive, is not shown to spare structural approximation.
- **That empirical content always accumulates.** That premise is asserted
  and defended elsewhere.

## Connections

- French ([THEORY-594](THEORY-594.md)) and De Haro and Butterfield ([THEORY-690](THEORY-690.md))
  concern ontic structural realism and its physics. This is the epistemic
  position those later debates start from.
- [THEORY-034](THEORY-034.md) places the ontic and informational structural realisms against
  Chakravartty's dilemma. Worrall's view is not of the kind that dilemma
  targets: it makes no claim about relata.
- [THEORY-017](THEORY-017.md) holds that in a Hilbert-space model only unitarily invariant
  structure is intrinsic and a basis is extra data. [NOTE-297](../notes.d/NOTE-297.md) calls the link
  an analogy: a basis plays the role Worrall gives the interpretation of the
  terms, but his structure is equations up to limits, not invariants of a
  group.
