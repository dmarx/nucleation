# Article 8. What We Owe Through Cooperation

## Rights, demands, commitments, and the legitimate governance of relational becoming

**Abstract.** Cooperation changes its participants. It creates capacities, dependencies and relations of authority that alter the conditions on which they act next. This article sets out what the Nucleation record holds about the normative side of those changes, and with what standing. The frame is held. Rights, demands and commitments are distinct, and none establishes another (CLAIM-738, Active). An institution's maintained requirements cannot certify their own authority (CLAIM-725, Active). Viability does not establish what ought to persist (CLAIM-075, Active). Each was made Active under rulings the owner delegated, or carries no recorded promotion. The article's centre is not held. Whether a right that protects a choice constrains a policy and not a history is demonstrated for two stipulated arrangements (CLAIM-741, Proposed). That defeating a party's standing to contest is a wrong even where the objection would have failed is the owner's criterion (CLAIM-723, Proposed). A matched-outcome argument extends it from timing to representation (ARG-228, CLAIM-1155, both Proposed), and the record files the instrumentalist objection beside it (CLAIM-1184, Proposed); whether that objection is answered is the owner's call. Answerability is proposed as a directed relation owed on several grounds (CLAIM-739, Proposed). It is bounded by three held or proposed non-implications: influence does not ground answerability (CLAIM-749, Active), standing does not require direct authority (the owner's CLAIM-tmpsh3og, Proposed), and responsibility can grow without authority over the dependent (a note on CLAIM-671). Membership as a ground of obligation is worked into two alternatives for the owner (CLAIM-960, CLAIM-959, Proposed). The second depends on an associative-obligation principle that the record has not read. Repair is proposed not to wait on culpability, which is the owner's distinction (CLAIM-742, Proposed). One pilot, not independent, measures where blameless reviews stop their trace (CASE-335, Proposed). Last, morality is proposed to evaluate the terms of cooperation (CLAIM-tmpreipd, Proposed). The record has rejected the step that set this against morality-as-cooperation (ARG-tmpn6xou, Rejected). A51 says this article's remaining work is "philosophical argument". The record holds much of that argument already, in Proposed form. What remains is mostly the owner's rulings and three readings.

## 8.2 Rights, demands and commitments

The owner's contrast at U41 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" was "rights vs demands vs commitments". A's definitions and non-implications fill it in (A95, A97), and the record holds the result as CLAIM-738 (Active, interpretive): "Rights, demands and commitments are distinct and none establishes another: a right does not depend on being demanded or on anyone's commitment to it, a demand's being asserted, maintained or recognized gives it no warrant, and a commitment is neither an obligation nor an authorization, while an obligation persists without commitment; each passage between them, a right generating a demand or a valid undertaking generating a right, needs its own warrant." The defeater does not count "cases where one gives rise to another through a valid act (a promise creating a claim, a right grounding a demand)". The claim denies that any of the three establishes another by itself. It allows passages that have warrants of their own.

A's companions sort what may pass between the three:

- **A demand's ending.** A demand owed to a party "can cease to apply legitimately only by the exercise of a valid normative power over it" (CLAIM-743, Proposed, interpretive). An institution's ceasing to count a demand "changes only the set it recognizes".
- **What a holder can confer.** "no delegation, contract, collective decision or success in imposing an arrangement thereby confers authority to determine whether that person keeps the standing from which claims against the arrangement arise" (CLAIM-720, Proposed, interpretive; A85 at the owner's U37, QUESTION-486). ARG-117 narrows its support: Locke's text "supports the claim's form and not its scope". SYNTHESIS-102 leaves under "Not stated" whether standing can be forfeited, as Locke allows.
- **Rights in later justification.** "rights can be prior in organization to later exercises of authority even where principles are prior to them in justification" (CLAIM-735, Proposed, interpretive).
- **Duties to institutions.** The duties to comply, to support and to help establish are distinct, and the third "is bounded by whether the needed arrangement can be reached from the present one by permissible, resource-bounded transitions" (CLAIM-729, Proposed, interpretive).

The step A51 needs most is held. A51's abstract says that "successful cooperation cannot establish the legitimacy of its own terms". The record's form of this is CLAIM-725 (Active, interpretive): "the joint satisfiability of demands cannot certify that any deserves fulfilment, and an institution's maintained requirements cannot certify their own authority". Beside it are CLAIM-075 (Active, disclaimed), "Viability does not establish what ought to persist", whose summary adds "An unjust institution may be highly viable", and CLAIM-060 (Active, interpretive), the owner's thread: "An encompassing organization's success does not establish the well-being, viability or autonomy of its constituents". Each holds in its own scope: demands and maintained requirements, viability, and success at one scale against well-being at another. A51's sentence restates them taken together (JR8.1). It adds no inference of its own.

Article 3 builds on CLAIM-738 for reliance: being expected, relied on, committed and owing are distinct, and "reliance makes something owed only under a further principle" (CLAIM-tmp0i6c0, Proposed, interpretive). That is the same structure, a passage that needs its own warrant, applied to implicit contracts.

## 8.3 A right as a constraint on policy

A51 opens with a distinction it calls sharper than A49's: "a right protecting a choice may constrain the policy determining how a decision is made, rather than merely the realized outcome." The record holds this as CLAIM-741 (Proposed, demonstrated; A's, A74 to A106): "Whether an arrangement respects a right that protects a choice is a property of its policy over the protected choices, not of any realized history … so an evaluation over endpoints or realized histories cannot represent such rights, which constrain policies and transition rules."

Its ground is CASE-283 (Proposed, construction, stipulated; A's). The case is stated "assuming a competent patient, an applicable right of refusal, and no justified exception". Arrangement A treats on consent and refrains on refusal. Arrangement B treats either way. CASE-283's check: on consent "both produce the history (c, a) = (1, 1)", and "Any evaluation that is a function of the realized history gives the same verdict to both". A respects the right at both values of c, and B fails it at c = 0. "If the patient always consents, no observed history ever separates them." The check is the filer's, recorded "on filing". No other session's check is recorded.

Its scope is narrow, and the entries state the limits:

- **Only rights that protect choices.** The claim is "Not defeated by rights that are outcome requirements (a fishing quota, a water allocation), which the claim does not cover." Which rights protect choices is not said: "What it cannot show: which rights protect choices rather than outcomes, or that any actual institution behaves like B."
- **Observation runs one way.** "An observed refusal overridden would show B's policy; consent observed, however often, cannot show A's" (CASE-283).
- **A formal point.** "Demonstrated" is about two stipulated policies. It is not a finding about any institution.

CLAIM-741 has no dependants by front-matter relation. Two accounts integrate it, SYNTHESIS-102 and SYNTHESIS-100. Its defeater names "a right of refusal (or of appeal, or an easement)". So a right of appeal, which is a kind of standing to contest, is inside its scope as filed. §8.4 uses that.

## 8.4 Defeated standing

### 8.4.1 The owner's criterion

At U27 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" the owner wrote: "would it help if we formalized the notion of consent violation? maybe something like acting before a recognized opportunity for intervention is able to take effect?" The record holds the criterion as CLAIM-723 (Proposed, interpretive): "Acting before a recognized opportunity for intervention can take effect is a wrong of its own, apart from acting without consent: where a party has standing to authorize, suspend or contest an act, an actor who without adequate justification makes the act's consequences irreversible before that standing can be exercised defeats it, even where the objection would have failed." The four conditions are A59's, and the defeater lists them: protected standing; "the act made that standing ineffective before it could be exercised"; attributability to "the actor's conduct, timing, withholding of notice or control over execution"; and "nothing justified proceeding without it". A wrong needs all four. Irreversibility alone does not make one.

The causal half is held. CLAIM-464 (Active, interpretive) says "An intervention can change what it would take to correct it", and it "says nothing about intent". What CLAIM-723 adds is the wrong. Its legal ground is Fuentes v. Shevin (THEORY-1972, Proposed): the right to be heard "does not depend upon an advance showing that one will surely prevail". The reading's promote_when is unmet, and it adds "Whether the decision remains good law is not asserted." CLAIM-723's dated note says the opinion "supports the claim as a legal analogue, not as a moral principle".

### 8.4.2 Executed work on the timing, which the record has not linked

A51 says "Another simulation is unlikely to settle those questions". That is right about the norm. But the record holds an executed model of the mechanism behind CLAIM-723's second condition, and neither entry cites the other. CASE-316 (Proposed, executed study, documented; the assistant's, for LEAD-068) is a queueing model run over 20 seeds. In it the overseer keeps "the same authority, information and reviewers at every speed". CLAIM-879 (Proposed, demonstrated) states its finding: "at ten times the speed the share of objectionable actions stopped before irreversibility fell from 0.994 to 0.396, already 0.711 at five times". With volume multiplied as well, the share fell to 0.043.

The model counts how often the act becomes irreversible before the objection can take effect. That is CLAIM-723's second condition, for objectionable actions only. It does not count the other three conditions, and it does not count objections that would have failed. So it measures how often the occasion of the wrong arises, not the wrong. Linking the two entries is a join (J8.3). It is a candidate dated note, not a claim.

### 8.4.3 From timing to representation

The owner's CLAIM-614 (Proposed, interpretive) holds that an organization which harms and also "reproduces the representational projections that make its harm non-legible, commits a counter-normativity distinct from the harm". Its note of 2026-11-18 says what it lacked: an argument that reproducing the projections wrongs those affected beyond the harm's continuation. A's answer, the duty side, is CLAIM-624 (Proposed, interpretive). It calls the wrong a breach of "a second-order duty of answerability", and "a verdict only given an independently justified standard and a feasible alternative". CLAIM-624 is the region's most relied-on Proposed entry, with 32 dependants by front-matter relation.

For LEAD-097 the assistant filed the claimant's side as CLAIM-1155 (Proposed, interpretive). Its title: "Where those an organization harms have standing to contest the harm with it, reproducing the projections that keep their claims from registering wrongs them beyond the harm, in that standing … so it holds where contesting would not have changed the harm's course". It keeps three things apart: **H**, the harm; **C**, "The further harm that follows because the harm goes unrecognized and so uncorrected"; and **D**, the defeat of standing. It holds only under three conditions:

1. Those affected have standing, which the claim "takes … as a condition. It does not derive it from the harm's being a wrong, since CLAIM-738 says a right does not by itself generate a warranted demand".
2. The organization reproduces the projection as part of its self-maintenance.
3. A feasible alternative representation exists. Where none does, "the claim says nothing".

The inference is ARG-228 (Proposed; rests on CLAIM-348, CLAIM-523 and CLAIM-723; grounds THEORY-1972). It builds two water utilities. Both contaminated a town's supply in an incident that is over, and a regulator testing "on its own schedule and not on complaints" triggers a compensation scheme that pays "whatever either utility does". O₁'s complaint taxonomy registers contamination-related illness. O₂'s "merges contamination-related illness with pre-existing conditions, so the same complaint is filed as a non-incident". H and C are matched, and so is expected C: "Here the match holds ex ante." That closes the gap left by A's earlier construction CASE-229 (Proposed, construction, stipulated), where "B's missing procedure would raise the risk of later harm" (ARG-055). D differs. The argument's step 4 then carries over the record's judgement from CLAIM-723 and Fuentes. CLAIM-1155 says the new element is the mechanism: "the objection arrives and has no category to arrive in."

Two limits are written into the claim. "If D is a harm", being unheard counted as a setback of its own, then CLAIM-614 still survives, because "its defeater is the reduction to the harm's continuation, not to harm in general". And who bears the wrong, an arrangement's controllers or an institution that is an agent, is left to CLAIM-876 and CLAIM-877 (Proposed alternatives; LEAD-131).

A51 calls the wrong a question still to be settled, and so does the record. The precursor draft, A's "Becoming Through One Another" §10.1, says that "defeating standing can be a distinct wrong, including where a successful objection would not have changed the final outcome (CLAIM-1155)". That tells it too strongly. CLAIM-1155 is Proposed and stands under an objection the owner has not ruled on.

### 8.4.4 Why the policy test bears here, and how far

A51 says the policy distinction "permits a rigorous treatment of why defeated standing can matter even where its proper exercise would not have changed the result." No entry states that step. Neither CLAIM-723, CLAIM-1155 nor ARG-228 cites CLAIM-741. This article makes the link as a join (J8.1), in a limited form:

- CLAIM-741's defeater counts a right of appeal among those it covers. Standing to contest is of that kind.
- In CLAIM-723's case where "the objection would have failed", and in ARG-228's matched utilities, the arrangement that respects the standing and the one that defeats it reach the same outcome. CLAIM-741's second separation says that an evaluation over realized outcomes "cannot represent" such a right.
- So the phrase "even where the objection would have failed" is not a paradox. It is what a policy-level right looks like when the realized outcome is fixed.

The join stops there. CLAIM-741 is a claim about representation: what an evaluation over histories can and cannot see. It does not say that failing a policy-level constraint wrongs anyone beyond expected harm. An instrumentalist can accept it and still evaluate policies by the harm they are expected to cause. That is CLAIM-1184's second premise (§8.5). The policy test explains why the defeat is invisible in outcomes. It does not settle whether the defeat is a wrong in its own right.

## 8.5 The instrumentalist objection

A51's "Actual remaining work" is the objection "in CLAIM-1184 to the independent wrong of defeated standing", which "deserves sustained treatment". The assistant filed it for LEAD-097 at its strongest. CLAIM-1184 (Proposed, interpretive, counter; objects to CLAIM-1155 and CLAIM-614): "Standing to contest a harm matters only as a means of preventing or remedying harm, so defeating it wrongs those affected only through the harm it lets continue or risks, and where the harm's course is fixed by causes contest could not touch, reproducing the projections that defeat it adds no wrong beyond the harm." Its premises run in four steps. Premise 2 already meets the reply that the outcome was unchanged: "ex ante, a missing channel raises expected harm". Its best support is harmless-error practice.

The record's reply is ARG-228. CLAIM-1184's body records how it answers: it "concedes premise 3's case and denies premise 1", and "The harmless-error practice does not carry premise 1: it governs whether a judgement is reversed, not whether the party was wronged". The objection also names what would revive it: "a showing that the record's judgements in CLAIM-723 and THEORY-1972 are themselves only judgements of expected harm under uncertainty, so that once the uncertainty is removed, as the construction removes it, the judgement goes with it."

The weak point is the one the revival condition aims at. ARG-228 does not argue that D is a wrong from premises the instrumentalist shares. Its step 4 carries over the record's existing judgement. That judgement rests on CLAIM-723 (Proposed) and on one Proposed reading of one narrow decision. CLAIM-1184's note of 2026-11-23 states the position exactly: "the objection is neither answered by a held argument nor shown sound … whether this objection goes Rejected, is the owner's call." A51's §7 row says "The instrumentalist objection remains substantive." That is a fair summary of the record's state.

The same structure appears a second time, in a construction for a different question, and the record has not linked the two. For LEAD-033, CASE-336 (Proposed, construction, stipulated) has variants C2* and C4*. In them "a decider known to be error-free, a sole reviser known to enact exactly what the subjects would vote for" fix what a power's holder would do, so "only the allocation differs" (CLAIM-909, Proposed, interpretive). Reading (a), CLAIM-913 (Proposed, interpretive), is instrumentalist at that level: the subjects' lacking a share "is no less legitimate", and "the sense that it is defective is a residue of protective reasons that no longer apply". Reading (b), CLAIM-911, is the assistant's lean. It finds "a personal reason of status, not to live under another's sole say". Reading (c), CLAIM-912, finds that the verdict comes only "by importing a principle of standing".

Both constructions fix the instrumental difference and vary only standing or the allocation of a power. That makes them parallel. It does not make them one position. Standing to be heard on a harm is not a share of the power to review or revise rules. One could reject CLAIM-1184 and accept CLAIM-913 without inconsistency. So the parallel is a join that routes, not one that binds (J8.2). A reason the owner accepts in one, such as CLAIM-911's reason of status, is a candidate in the other. It is not entailed there.

## 8.6 Standing and answerability as directed relations

A51's abstract asks for "an account of standing and answerability as directed relationships, with separate requirements concerning recognition, authority, accessibility and maintenance." Most of it is held, in two layers.

**What is Active.** These are the separable requirements:

- **Three achievements.** "For a normatively relevant discrepancy to change what an organization does, three distinct achievements are needed and any can fail while the others hold: legibility …; volitional efficacy …; and correctability", and "none establishes that the demand or the revision is justified" (CLAIM-523, Active, interpretive; the owner's synthesis).
- **Applied to domination.** CLAIM-686 (Active, demonstrated; the assistant's) applies the three to domination's third condition. They are "needed, not … sufficient", and they meet the condition "only together with a failure of exit".
- **Three entitlements.** For an affected party, explanation, contestation and interruption "are separate entitlements": "contestation without interruption a right that arrives too late" (CLAIM-740, Active, interpretive).
- **No self-certifying authority.** "Authority over a decision does not carry authority over the conditions on which it may be challenged" (CLAIM-727, Active, interpretive).
- **Influence is not enough.** "Influence over others does not by itself ground a duty to answer to them" (CLAIM-749, Active, interpretive, counter). CLAIM-687 (Active), whose accountability "should rise with how indispensable the arrangement has become", concedes it.

**What is Proposed.** A's answer to the owner's QUESTION-480 ("when is answerability required of an authority or governing capability? under what conditions is justification owed, to whom, and why?") is CLAIM-739 (Proposed, interpretive). "Answerability is directed and role-specific: an actor owes an answer to a party with a claim concerning the matter, on one of several grounds that need not presuppose the actor's legitimate authority, among them interference with a protected interest, entrusted authority, collective rule, consequential dependence and control over recourse". And "what is owed is indexed to the claim, not to how much the party is affected". The terms are A's and Proposed: answerability (TERM-126), and protected contestability (TERM-130), "not a guarantee of success, a veto or costless adjudication". CLAIM-732 (Proposed) adds six achievements of an effective objection. SYNTHESIS-103 leaves the root under "Not stated": "Why anyone is owed an answer at all: QUESTION-480 and CLAIM-739 leave it open."

**Three bounds on authority.** A51 ends its abstract with "the legitimate governance of the power that interdependent individuals acquire over one another through cooperation." The record bounds that power from three sides, each with its own standing:

- *Influence without answerability.* CLAIM-749 (Active), above.
- *Responsibility without authority.* A71's rider in CLAIM-671's note of 2026-11-17 reads: "Creating a dependency can generate or strengthen responsibilities without granting legitimate authority over the dependent." The rider is A's and interpretive. The claim itself is Proposed and supposed.
- *Standing without direct authority.* This is the owner's, under the dialectic regime on 2026-11-24: "the structural situation you describe can create the conditions necessary for institutional sociopathy to arise, but an institution can still respect the standing and claims of its constituents without giving them direct authority to effect change within the institution (e.g. effective representative governance vs. captured)". The record holds it as CLAIM-tmpsh3og (Proposed, interpretive). It objects to A's CLAIM-tmptrn75, whose "their justified claims gain no authority over what it does" the claim says "runs direct authority and answerability together".

The third bound changes how this article can speak of institutions. Asymmetric legibility, where the institution makes those it affects legible to itself while they hold no direct authority, is "a structural condition that makes institutional sociopathy possible and does not constitute it" (TERM-tmp383al, Proposed). The vice is a separate term. An institution is in that condition when the claims of those it affects "carry no effective weight in what it does by any route, direct or representative; typically arising where legibility is asymmetric and the processes meant to carry the claims are captured" (TERM-tmp72sq3, Proposed). The vice belongs to Article 12. What this article takes from the owner's correction is narrower. Respecting someone's standing does not require giving them authority over what the institution does. Representation that works can carry their claims. Representation that is captured cannot. So "they have no direct say" does not by itself show a defect in an arrangement of power. Whether the claims carry weight by some route is what has to be shown. CLAIM-tmpsh3og's defeater makes this testable: it fails if "'effective weight' cannot be told apart from direct authority by any observation".

## 8.9 Morality and cooperation

At U18 of "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity" the owner said: "i think this significantly helps clarify the relationship between morality and cooperation" (CLAIM-tmp1fj5p, Proposed, interpretive). The owner did not say how. Its defeater is a showing that the account adds nothing to what morality-as-cooperation (THEORY-468), Tomasello's shared-agency account (THEORY-2109), Killen's reply (THEORY-2110), CLAIM-546 and CLAIM-075 already say, "so that it relabels them".

A's version is CLAIM-tmpreipd (Proposed, interpretive; A23, A24): "morality evaluates the terms on which cooperation is formed, maintained and transformed". The claim sorts its own five steps by strength. The assistant rejected the step by which A24 set the account against Curry. A "sequence of cooperation problems is still cooperation problems", and the step "passes from where moral dispositions come from to what makes verdicts valid" (ARG-tmpn6xou, Rejected). So: "The claim therefore stands, if it does, on steps 3 and 4, with step 2 as an important case and step 5 as a research direction." The steps are these:

- step 3: "Cooperation is not necessarily morally good, and noncooperation is not necessarily morally bad";
- step 4: "moral consideration cannot be restricted to cooperative participants";
- step 2: exploitation within successful cooperation;
- step 5: the inverse question, how cooperation creates relationships that make morality necessary.

Curry is not refuted. ARG-tmpn6xou says only step 4 "does tell against THEORY-468's closing clause that behaviour unrelated to cooperation is morally neutral". The article's contrast with morality-as-cooperation therefore has to run through wrongful cooperation, justified refusal and consideration owed to those who do not or cannot cooperate. It cannot run through "cooperation creates new problems".

Two held results keep cooperation apart from its moral assessment, each at its own level:

- **Coexistence is not cooperation.** CLAIM-546 (Active, demonstrated): in Hawk–Dove with C > V the types coexist "with Hawks still taking the whole resource from Doves", so "whether types persist together and whether they cooperate are separate questions".
- **Three achievements that do not secure one another.** CLAIM-547 (Active, interpretive): "cooperation local to a group, the persistence of several groups whose interests are opposed, and concern for those outside one's group are three achievements, and none of them secures the next".

Of the readings, only THEORY-505 is Active. It finds that morality-as-cooperation's items form seven factors "when rated for relevance … but not when rated as agreement", which is "evidence about the instrument's structure, not about what morality contains". THEORY-2110 (Proposed) is Killen's: "cooperation and interdependence are a basis for morality but not sufficient for it".

**A51's closing sentence.** A51 ends: "Morality is situated within the legitimate governance of the power that interdependent individuals acquire over one another through cooperation." Read literally, this confines morality to power acquired through cooperation. CLAIM-tmpreipd's step 4 says the opposite, that moral consideration is not confined to cooperators. CLAIM-547's third achievement, "concern for those outside one's group", points the same way. So this article does not adopt A51's sentence as a thesis (J8.5). The record's version is weaker and goes the other way: morality evaluates the terms of cooperation, among other things.

**Contractualism.** The owner's QUESTION-483 (U44: "our theory might provide a response to Scanlon's approach?") asks whether the account only supplements contractualism. LEAD-033 (Open, idea) is "Done" by its note of 2026-11-22. It was to count as an answer if there were "A construction it cannot explain". The result: as stated, CASE-336's four constructions are explained "without a principle of who holds a power" (CLAIM-908, Proposed). The variants discriminate (CLAIM-909), and their verdict is the three readings of §8.5, for the owner. Scanlon's book (LIT-2721) is Unavailable. The record reads contractualism through Scanlon's replies and the encyclopedia entry.
