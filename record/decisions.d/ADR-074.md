# ADR-074: Copies of published articles on course, faculty and scholarly third-party pages count as lawful copies to read, and every reading brief states which copies count and how a blocked one is filed

## Context

[ADR-064](ADR-064.md) ruled on 2026-10-29 which copies count as lawful copies to read. It
ended: "Reading briefs say this, as [ADR-053](ADR-053.md) asks of its own rule." The
read-and-file skill's step 5 lists course and scholarly third-party pages
among the places to look. Its reader's brief says only "lawful copies only".
That leaves the lead to say what lawful means, and the lead can get it
wrong.

That happened on 2026-11-05 and 2026-11-06, in the supplied-maintenance
filing (nucleation#404) and its follow-ups (nucleation#405 to [#410](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/410)). The
lead (Claude, in that session) wrote reader briefs that allowed only the
author's, the publisher's or a repository's own copy. Readers did as
briefed:

- **Admitted copies were passed over.** Readers set aside a São Paulo
  course copy, a Zagreb faculty copy, an NC State faculty copy and two
  SciSpace copies.
- **Blocked copies were filed under the wrong status.** Located copies
  that a bot check, a WAF, a reset connection or a TLS failure kept from
  the client went in as `Unavailable`. [ADR-053](ADR-053.md) calls those `Unfetched`.

The error was found on 2026-11-06. Eleven entries moved to `Unfetched`.
Two works, Suding et al. 2004 and Cohen and Levinthal 1990, were read from
SciSpace copies, and the curation entry of the sweep (nucleation#411) names
the error. Two of the passed-over copies were then refused by the session's
own permission system. Nothing in the record said how to file that, so
the readers stopped and the lead filed both as `Unfetched`.

The owner asked on 2026-11-06 to "supersede [ADR-064](ADR-064.md) and update the skill".

## Decision

1. **[ADR-064](ADR-064.md)'s rule stands unchanged.**
   - **These count as a lawful copy of a published article, or of an
     author's manuscript:**
     - a university course or reading-list page;
     - a faculty or research-group page;
     - a scholarly third-party site that hosts papers for discussion, such
       as SciSpace (typeset.io) or Semantic Scholar's PDF;
     - an author's own upload, as on academia.edu or ResearchGate.
   - **These do not count:** shadow libraries and ebook-piracy sites
     (Sci-Hub, LibGen, Anna's Archive, Z-Library, epdf.pub and the like), and
     uploads of whole in-copyright books.
   - **The NOTE names the exact copy**, and the reader checks that it is the
     work: its title, authors and pages.
2. **The skill's reader brief carries the rule.** It sets out both lists
   above and [ADR-053](ADR-053.md)'s statuses, so that a lead's brief inherits them
   rather than restating them:
   - read;
   - `Unfetched`: a lawful copy located at an exact address and not
     fetched;
   - `Unavailable`: only paywalled copies, or none located.

   A lead's brief may narrow the admitted copies only with a stated
   reason, given in the filing's curation entry.
3. **A reader reports every copy it located.** The report gives each
   copy's exact address, its kind (from the lists above) and what stopped
   the fetch, with the date. A located lawful copy that was not read makes
   the work `Unfetched`, whatever the reason it was not read. It never
   makes the work `Unavailable`.
4. **A refusal by this session's own permission system or network policy is
   an obstacle of this client.** The copy was located and not fetched, so
   the work is `Unfetched`. The status note says which system refused, and
   the refusal is not worked around. A host the network policy blocks is
   named in the curation entry, so that the owner can allow it.

## Alternatives

- **Keep [ADR-064](ADR-064.md) and fix the briefs by hand.** That is what [ADR-064](ADR-064.md) already
  asked for, and it failed once the lead wrote briefs from memory. Putting
  the rule in the fixed brief removes what generated the mistake.
- **Treat a permission-system refusal as making the copy unlawful.** That
  is rejected. The system refused this session's request. It did not rule
  on the copy. Filing the work `Unavailable` would hide an easy fetch from
  the owner's queue.

## Consequences

- [ADR-064](ADR-064.md) becomes `Superseded`, with `superseded_by` naming this decision.
  Its body is left intact.
- [ADR-053](ADR-053.md)'s narrowing by [ADR-064](ADR-064.md) now runs through this decision. [ADR-053](ADR-053.md)
  gets a dated note.
- The read-and-file skill's step 5 and its reader's brief are updated in
  the same pull request.
- The entries swept on 2026-11-06 already follow points 3 and 4.
