# THEORY-tmp50zaf: Locke argues in the Second Treatise that because no one has an absolute arbitrary power over his own life no one can by compact or consent give another such power, except where life is forfeited by a fault deserving death, so that the legislative, holding only the joint power its members could transfer, cannot be absolutely arbitrary over their lives and fortunes and holds a fiduciary power the people may resume when the trust is breached

Locke, *Second Treatise*, [LIT-2738](../literature.d/LIT-2738.md).

## The argument

"a man, not having the power of his own life, cannot, by compact, or his
own consent, enslave himself to any one" (§23), unless "by his fault" he
has "forfeited his own life". "no body can transfer to another more power
than he has in himself; and no body has an absolute arbitrary power over
himself" (§135). "the legislative being only a fiduciary power to act for
certain ends", the people keep "a right to preserve, what they have not a
power to part with" (§149).

## What it does not show

- A limit grounded in standing in general: Locke's ground is theological
  and concerns life.
- That nothing inalienable can be lost: forfeiture by fault is allowed.
