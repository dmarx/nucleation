# NOTE-134: Carroll — Reality as a vector in Hilbert space

## Contribution

Carroll defends the self-described extreme view ("Mad-Dog Everettianism" / Hilbert space fundamentalism) that the world is fundamentally just a vector in Hilbert space obeying the Schrödinger equation. On this view the laws of physics are fixed entirely by the energy eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian. Everything recognisable — space, and the fields in it — is to be recovered as a higher-level emergent description. He sketches a route to that recovery while conceding much remains to be done.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Framing (pp. 1–3): positions the view against wave-function realism on configuration space (Albert) and Wallace's objection that configuration space is only one representation; opts for the Everettian minimal starting point.
- Theory specification (p. 4): Hilbert space has no preferred basis, so a quantum theory is fixed by its dimension plus the Hamiltonian's eigenvalue list — with the caveat that non-separable spaces need an algebra of observables.
- Finiteness (p. 5): holographic/black-hole entropy bounds suggest the Hilbert-space factor for our observable universe is finite-dimensional (dim ≲ e^(e^123)).
- Quantum mereology (pp. 7–8): classical subsystems are to be picked out as the tensor factorisation H = H_S ⊗ H_E under which the Hamiltonian yields quasi-classical, decohering dynamics with robust pointer states.
- Emergent space (pp. 10–11): spatial geometry is reconstructed from mutual information between factors in near-vacuum states; time is taken as fundamental, with an effective-Hamiltonian route suggested for Wheeler–DeWitt settings; emergent spacetime and fields are "equally real as tables and chairs".

## Open questions

- A clear statement of an extreme structuralist/emergentist ontology; pairs naturally with Rovelli's relational view ([LIT-177](../literature.d/LIT-177.md)) as opposite poles on the reality of ψ.
- Check the uniqueness claims for the quasi-classical factorisation (Carroll & Singh 2019) against the known "preferred factorisation" objections (e.g. Stoica's arguments that spectrum-only structure underdetermines physics).
- Check whether "equally real" emergent structure amounts to a substantive metaphysics of emergence or a pragmatic one.
