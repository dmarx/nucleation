# LIT-403: Intrinsic content, active memory and the extended mind

Andy Clark (2005), *Analysis* 65(1), 1–11 — DOI-10.1093/analys/65.1.1

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, with Chalmers's extended-mind
programme. [NOTE-363](../notes.d/NOTE-363.md) is the close reading of the same day, and it
placed the work: **Active**.

**It carries [LIT-097](LIT-097.md) further.** It restates the 1998 argument as a
challenge ("show us why the case of Otto and his notebook ... is not simply
that of an unusual realizer"), gives its §2 sentence the name "the Parity
principle", and adds new parity cases in its defence: Venn-diagram
imagery, Martians who store bit-mapped images of text, and a world in which
our memory for faces is not reconstructive. It also restates the thesis as
one about vehicles, not contents (footnote 1), which [LIT-097](LIT-097.md) did not do.
It quotes the parity sentence with "(for that time)" where [LIT-097](LIT-097.md), as
[NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md) read it, has "(so we claim)"; see the NOTE.

**The critics it answers.** Adams and Aizawa ([LIT-438](LIT-438.md)) on intrinsic
content; and O'Brien, Dartnall and Weiskopf, none held here, on "active
dispositional memory". Its strongest move is to quote Adams and Aizawa's
p. 50 concession and say it "removes the entire force of the appeal to
intrinsic content", since "it was no part of EM to claim that one could
build an entire cognizer out of Otto-style notebooks". It does not mention
Rupert's paper ([LIT-422](LIT-422.md)), published the same year, whose memory
arguments are of the active-memory kind.

**What it concedes.** "It was never our claim that Otto is behaviourally
indistinguishable from Inga", and "the notebook's lack of inbuilt links and
pathways makes it unlike biological memory". The claim is now that
Otto-and-the-notebook "exhibit enough of the central features and dynamics"
to warrant the belief ascription. That is weaker than the parity of
[LIT-097](LIT-097.md) and closer to the explanatory-purpose view Chalmers gave in his
foreword ([LIT-421](LIT-421.md)).

**On persons, consciousness and [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md).** The paper closes on
persons. Brains are "open-ended controllers" practising "ecological
control". Persons are best identified with a "rational point of view"
(Rovane), and the identification of persons with biological structures is
a Lockean "forensic matter". That is the extended-self claim of [LIT-097](LIT-097.md) §5
given a direction, and it sits beside [LIT-136](LIT-136.md) and [LIT-138](LIT-138.md). Rovane's book,
not held here, is usually cited for allowing group persons; Clark does not
draw that out, so the bearing on [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) is at most on its agency
half, and the paper says nothing about consciousness. It carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.
