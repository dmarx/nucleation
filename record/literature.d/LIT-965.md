# LIT-965: Evolution of consciousness: Phylogeny, ontogeny, and emergence from general anesthesia

George A. Mashour and Michael T. Alkire (2013), *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 110 (Suppl. 2): 10357–10364.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-797](../notes.d/NOTE-797.md).*

- **A proposed model, not a study.** Emergence from anaesthesia runs from
  breathing to arousal to following a command to higher cognition, and
  can be watched "in real time over the course of hours". The authors
  propose it as a model of the evolution of consciousness.
- **The cited core.** In Långsjö et al. (2012), the return of a response to
  command correlated "primarily with activity of the brainstem (locus
  coeruleus), hypothalamus, thalamus, and anterior cingulate", with
  "limited neocortical involvement". The authors grant that this may
  reflect "an arousal-related response to the test stimulus itself".
- **Access contaminates the model.** The model needs phenomenal and access
  consciousness "closely juxtaposed or collapsed" at emergence, and the
  authors concede "the contamination of access consciousness, because
  language is involved in assessing return".
- **The step to animals is by homology.** Birds and mammals share
  brainstem, thalamus and a pallium or cortex "capable of recurrent
  processing". So the authors "postulate that early mammals and birds ...
  are capable of phenomenal consciousness", along "two independent
  lineages" from the amniote radiation about 315 million years ago.
- **Human richness.** It is put down to frontal interconnectivity and a
  dominant anterior-to-posterior information flow. The authors call the
  role of that flow "unclear at this time".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md), and read on 2026-10-14 in
[NOTE-797](../notes.d/NOTE-797.md). For that question it is a worked example of recovery
evidence pressed into service for experience. Its marker is an access
marker, and it assumes access and experience collapse at emergence. That
is the limit [THEORY-091](../theory.d/THEORY-091.md) states for deficits, here conceded in the authors'
own words. Its transfer to animals without a human cortex rests on
anatomy and behaviour, not on anaesthesia data from them. That is the
architectural route [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) questions, and [THEORY-100](../theory.d/THEORY-100.md) holds the bird
evidence more directly. The cited emergence data fit [THEORY-090](../theory.d/THEORY-090.md)'s
subcortical clause, but the paper keeps limited recurrent cortex in the
core and uses command-following as its marker, so it is not a second
source for "without cortex". No THEORY was filed. The paper has no data
of its own, and its primary source, Långsjö et al. (2012), is not held.
