# THEORY-tmpyot08: Illich holds that beyond a socially critical threshold of energy per person motorized transport reduces equity and establishes a radical monopoly, the dominance of an industry as the means of satisfying a need that formerly occasioned a personal response, imposed not by scarcity or by eliminating competitors but by its acquired ability to create and shape the need it alone can satisfy, and that the encroachment of such a monopoly can be pinpointed by social analysis while its limit is a matter of politics

## The account

Illich: "Past a certain threshold of energy consumption, the transportation industry dictates the configuration of social space"; "Beyond a certain point, more energy means less equity"; "Because of its hidden, entrenched, and structuring nature, I call this a radical monopoly", which arises "by virtue of its acquired ability to create and shape the need which it alone can satisfy"; "The magnitude of voluntary limits is a matter of politics; the encroachment of radical monopoly can be pinpointed by social analysis."

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmp0cqdl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0cqdl.md), results 2–8.

## What it does not say

That the exclusion of the non-industrial alternative is always unenforced: in the shoe and school cases teachers, officials and laws enforce it. Nor the burial case or "unimaginable", which are Tools for Conviviality's as Cayley reports them.
