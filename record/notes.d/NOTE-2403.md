# NOTE-2403: Knight — Slammed for Bribery, Siemens Continued to Ignore Red Flags

## Contribution

An account of Siemens's compliance with its monitor's recommendations on
third-party resellers in China, from the four monitoring reports
(2009–2012), released heavily redacted after 100Reporters sued under the
Freedom of Information Act, and from former insiders' documents.

## Key results

- **The redesign was real in scale.** After 2008 Siemens "increased its
  number of compliance officers to over 500 worldwide and introduced
  stringent new internal policies".
- **A signal exempted (2010).** A June 2010 memo by two senior compliance
  officers said the business-partner red-flag tool "does not apply to
  Models A, B, and D", three of four reseller models, and footnoted "We
  cannot exclude the possibility that the [import/export company] and the
  end-customer abuse the structure and make other dealings under the
  table." A compliance employee who flagged price gaps was let go; his
  summary: "compliance gravity shifted from ethics to revenue".
- **The monitor's verdict, to the letter.** In October 2011 the monitor
  found Siemens had "fully implemented all of his Year One
  recommendations"; the reporter's reading is that the recommended review
  was done and its intent sidestepped.
- **After.** "in the same year that Waigel concluded his monitorship, a
  Siemens business manager offered to pay a hospital president" ¥2 million,
  per a Chinese verdict against the hospital president; a 2013 email by the
  most senior sales manager at Siemens China, himself sacked over alleged
  corruption, raised the "huge gap between biddings and contracts".
- **Siemens's position.** Its system was "adequately conceived"; "Whenever
  we find misconduct by distributors, we take action".

## Assumptions

That the documents are what the sources say; the reporter could not see
the redacted parts.

## Concepts

Third-party reseller risk; red flags; business-partner tool.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-405](../cases.d/CASE-405.md).** The post-monitorship evidence on Siemens: of the
  same kind of harm, by report and allegation, not by a finding against
  Siemens.
- **[CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md) (illustration).** A procedure built to weigh the harm (the
  red-flag tool) was exempted where it would have bitten: a signal kept
  and denied precedence inside a redesigned firm. That is the claim's
  distinction appearing within the "redesigned" arm, which complicates
  coding any firm as wholly redesigned.

## Limitations

Reporting, partly from interested insiders. It says the 2013 email came
"Two years after Waigel's monitorship ended", which does not square with a
monitorship ending in 2012; the email's year is taken as given. The 2012
offer's timing against the monitorship's end is not stated.
