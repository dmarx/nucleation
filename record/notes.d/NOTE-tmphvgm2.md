  Review of what is proposed about how Ophiocordyceps unilateralis s.l.
  infects carpenter ants and changes their behaviour (loss of daily rhythm,
  reduced communication with nestmates, hyperactivity, staggering,
  summiting, biting at about solar noon, death), compared with other
  behaviour-manipulating parasites (Entomophthora in flies, baculovirus in
  caterpillars, and others). Two hypothesised mechanism families: secreted
  fungal effectors (enterotoxins, an aflatrem-like alkaloid cluster, protein
  tyrosine phosphatases, juvenile-hormone modulation, small secreted
  proteins predicted to bind ant GPCRs), and disruption and resetting of the
  host's biological clock. Similar phenotypes and some genes across
  unrelated systems suggest convergent evolution.

# NOTE-tmphvgm2: van Roosmalen & de Bekker (2024) — Mechanisms Underlying Ophiocordyceps Infection and Behavioral Manipulation of Ants: Unique or Ubiquitous?

## Contribution

An integrative map of candidate mechanisms for one of the best-studied behavioural-manipulation systems, with the question of convergence posed across taxa.

## Key results

- §1: manipulated behaviours "comprise behaviors that the host would normally not express, or only within a certain context (e.g., certain life stage), and are adaptive to the survival and fitness of the parasite", distinguished from general sickness behaviours.
- §3: "ant nervous tissue remains intact during Ophiocordyceps infection and manipulation while clear degradation and hypercontraction of muscle tissue in the head were observed".
- §3: Ophiocordyceps "might be protecting the brain from inflammation by upregulating and synthesizing neuro- and cytoprotectants".
- §3.2: on staggering, "this assigning of a putative adaptive function to aflatrem-induced staggering is based only on deductive reasoning".
- §3.4 (baculovirus summiting): "This suggests that the virus hijacks an existing mechanism already present in the host."
- §4.1: "Ophiocordyceps is slowly disrupting and then resetting the biological clock of the ant to its advantage."
- §5: "this work has remained necessarily correlative and, therefore, descriptive."
- §5: "manipulation mechanisms have likely convergently evolved."

## Assumptions

Parasite-extended-phenotype framing (manipulated behaviour as parasite adaptation); transcriptomic co-regulation and machine-learning interaction predictions as evidence of candidate function; cross-taxon analogy as hypothesis generation.

## Concepts

Extended phenotype; manipulation phenome vs manipulation genome; effectors; summit disease; death grip; sickness behaviour vs manipulation; zeitgebers and clock hijacking; convergent evolution.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** Accurate but thin. It should add the two mechanism families, the nervous-system-intact/muscle-degraded finding, and the authors' own caution that the mechanistic evidence is correlative and some adaptive attributions rest on deduction.

- **[CASE-tmp10ykm](../cases.d/CASE-tmp10ykm.md)**: **supports and refines** A19's report. The review confirms that the ant's nervous tissue stays intact while head muscle degrades, and adds that the fungus may protect the brain; the route is effectors and clock disruption acting on the host's own machinery. It also supports the case's "what is held fixed" (execution by the host) and adds a further route the case does not name: timing (the host clock).
- **[QUESTION-tmpgciew](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpgciew.md)** (can one organism impose its goals on another): **bears on it, leaves it open**. The review's sense of "manipulation" is the selected-for sense (adaptive to the parasite), separated from sickness behaviour, which matches the question's own requirement that serving the parasite is not enough. Its caveats (correlative evidence; some adaptive attributions by deduction only) count against reading the mechanisms as settled. Nothing in it speaks to represented ends.

## Limitations

A review by one lab's authors, centred on their own system; most mechanistic claims are hypotheses from transcriptomics and predictions; final published version not compared with the Review in Advance read.
