# LIT-tmp1wuw5: Darwall on Second-Personal Ethics

Stern, R. (2014). Darwall on Second-Personal Ethics. *European Journal of Philosophy* 22(2): 321–333. doi:10.1111/ejop.12090.

## Key takeaways

- Darwall's thesis (p. 2): obligation "involves the exercise of legitimate
  authority over the person obliged" and is "essentially second personal".
- Not actual demands (p. 3): "whether or not an obligation obtains doesn’t
  depend on the actual demands an individual or a community of individuals
  might make".
- Against Raz (p. 7): "meeting the standards of the normal justification
  thesis is not in fact sufficient to establish that one person has
  authority over another"; the cooking expert cannot "insist that you
  comply ... in a second-personal manner".
- Stern's triad (p. 12): "(1) A person’s suffering does not give a
  second-personal reason to act (2) Obligations require second-personal
  reasons (3) The suffering of others can give us obligations".
