# THEORY-132: Stability, a system's speed of return to equilibrium after disturbance, and resilience, its capacity to absorb change and persist within its domain of attraction, are distinct properties that can trade off, so a system can be highly unstable and highly resilient, and managing it for constancy can erode its resilience

## Source

Holling (1973), [LIT-741](../literature.d/LIT-741.md), read in [NOTE-572](../notes.d/NOTE-572.md).

## What was actually shown

Holling ([LIT-741](../literature.d/LIT-741.md)) argues from model behaviour and from case histories.

- **Models.** Adding realistic features to predator–prey models (lags,
  nonlinear responses, contagious attack, a reproduction threshold) turns
  globally stable or unstable behaviour into bounded domains of attraction.
  Within a domain, trajectories return; outside it, a population goes
  extinct. Stability describes behaviour near the equilibrium, and
  persistence depends on the boundary.
- **Cases.** The spruce budworm forest cycles through outbreaks and is "highly
  unstable", and that instability renews the forest and keeps the system
  going. Forest insects in extreme climates fluctuate widely yet absorb
  extremes. Great Lakes fisheries were constant and collapsed under
  exploitation. A lake kept one state for millennia, then flipped after a
  small hydrological change.
- **Definitions.** Resilience is the ability "to absorb changes of state
  variables, driving variables, and parameters, and still persist".
  Stability is "the ability of a system to return to an equilibrium state
  after a temporary disturbance".

The distinction could have failed if persistence had tracked closeness of
return. The budworm and insect cases show it does not.

## What this does not say

- **Not that instability causes resilience in general.** The cases are
  chosen to show the two can separate, and Holling says his sample "is
  inevitably biased".
- **Not that maximum-yield management always erodes resilience.** That is
  argued from the framework and supported by examples, not tested.
- **Not Aubin's viability.** Resilience is about staying within a domain of
  attraction of the system's own dynamics. Viability ([THEORY-134](THEORY-134.md)) is
  about staying within a constraint set given apart from the dynamics. A
  regime can be resilient, hard to dislodge, while violating what it needs.
