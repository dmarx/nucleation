# THEORY-1165: Kahl proposes that agency in the sense that bears on responsibility needs a standpoint that settles and carries forward revised evaluative standards when its inherited ones break down, which is absent where revision is imposed from outside, escaped by rollback or chosen by a fixed higher-order rule; that allocating authority among a system's own components coordinates execution without authoring its governing frame; and that planning, autonomy, memory and coordination do not supply that standpoint

## Source

Kahl (2026), [LIT-1033](../literature.d/LIT-1033.md), read in full in [NOTE-723](../notes.d/NOTE-723.md): §3 (the presupposition
argument), §4 (continuity pressure), §5 (the test and its cases, with the
group-agency reply in §5.3 and the borderline cases in §5.5).

## The proposal

**Two senses of agency.** Operational autonomy is task execution with
little prompting. Attributive agency is agency "in the sense relevant to
responsibility, authority, and governance". The proposal is a necessary
condition for the second.

**The condition.** Continuity pressure "begins where an inherited
evaluative frame no longer settles how future operation should proceed."
Authorship attaches when the system settles a revised frame and later
assessment proceeds through it. It is absent "where revision is
externally imposed, where rollback avoids carrying forward the
consequences of revision, or where the system merely selects among
revision paths already ranked by a fixed higher-order criterion."

**Allocation is not authorship.** A workflow's "voting rules, ranking
functions, confidence thresholds" coordinate execution and "do not
generate authorship of the governing frame". What separates a workflow
from a court is "whether revision of that rule under breakdown is internal
or external to the candidate group agent", not whether the rule was
designed.

**The cases.** Tool-using agents fail by resetability, reinforcement
learners by an "antecedent admissibility structure" over every update,
multi-agent workflows because "unity of action is not unity of
authorship", and institutional systems because their standards are
"authored elsewhere". Planning, autonomy, memory and coordination measure
"what a system can do", not "what is at stake for the system in doing it".

These are the distinction [CLAIM-485](../claims.d/CLAIM-485.md) carries over to the self-governing
sense.

## What this does not say

It does not say the proposal is true. The paper argues from cases, gives
no positive case, and does not make "settled under breakdown" precise:
every revision has some prior structure, and how much antecedent ranking
disqualifies one is not said. That test is [THEORY-219](THEORY-219.md)'s `promote_when`,
and [THEORY-219](THEORY-219.md) stays Proposed until the record holds it. It is a necessary
condition, not a theory of agency, and it is not minimal agency in
[THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md)'s sense.
