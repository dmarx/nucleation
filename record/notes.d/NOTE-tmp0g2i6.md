# NOTE-tmp0g2i6: Ince and others (2024) — Problematic pornography use and novel patterns of escalating use: A cross-sectional network analysis with two independent samples

## Contribution

First quantitative study of binges, edging and tab-jumping alongside time- and genre-escalation in relation to PPU, separating quantitative (time) from qualitative (content) escalation.

## Key results

- Abstract: "quantitative tolerance was centrally placed within the overall network".
- Results, Model 2: "quantitative escalation was the most central node in Model 2" (both samples; CS-EI = 0.75).
- Results, Model 2: the strongest links with PPU severity were "quantitative tolerance (edge weight= 0.46 in both samples) and binge frequency" (0.06, 0.14).
- Qualitative (genre) escalation was measured by a single item: "I gradually watched more “extreme” pornography, because the porn I watched before was less satisfying" (Methods, Measures), on a 1-7 scale; Table 2 means 2.46 (SD 1.68) in Sample 1 and 2.58 (1.69) in Sample 2.
- Its main link is to quantitative tolerance, not to PPU directly: edge "between the qualitative and quantitative tolerance (Sample 1 edge weight: 0.39; Sample 2 edge weight: 0.41)" (Results, Network structure).
- Discussion: binge frequency "was primarily (but not exclusively) linked to PPU through tolerance".
- Limitations: "our cross-sectional design precludes any causal implications"; "we also recognise that escalating behaviours can reflect non-pathological mechanisms such as curiosity, exploration, and skill mastery".

## Assumptions

Self-report validly measures desensitisation-driven escalation; group-level partial correlations are informative about individual dynamics (the authors flag this mismatch); BPS covers PPU adequately (they flag it does not cover salience, withdrawal); male-only, crowdsourced, non-clinical samples.

## Concepts

Problematic pornography use (PPU); quantitative tolerance vs qualitative escalation; "intensity indicators" (binges, edging, tab-jumping); network centrality (expected influence) and bridge nodes; CSBD (ICD-11).

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** The LIT summary (A27 §2) is accurate on samples (1,356 and 944, male), the four patterns, and cross-sectionality. Two refinements: "genre seeking" is one self-report item whose own association with PPU runs mainly through time-tolerance; and the study says nothing about what users are offered or about platforms. The summary also has a grammatical slip ("it two samples").

- **CLAIM-tmpcv1pg** (heavy users' habituation drives the mainstream toward extreme content): **leaves open, slightly qualifies the escalation half.** It shows, cross-sectionally and individually, that self-reported escalation in time and (more weakly) toward "more extreme" content co-occurs with control difficulties. It does not show that heavy users drive what is produced or offered, nor any time trend; the authors warn escalation can be non-pathological curiosity. Endorsement of genre escalation is low on average (about 2.5 of 7).
- **ARG-tmpshmi5** (found on recheck): **supports** its verdict that the escalation evidence is "small, observational and self-reported": this study is cross-sectional, single-item for genre escalation, and self-report.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional; male-only; crowdsourced US/UK; single-item genre measure; no platform or content data.
