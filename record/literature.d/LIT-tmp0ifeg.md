# LIT-tmp0ifeg: Causation and Manipulability

James Woodward, "Causation and Manipulability", *The Stanford Encyclopedia
of Philosophy* (first published 17 August 2001; substantive revision 6 May
2023), ISSN 1095-5054

## Key takeaways

- **Causes are handles** (§1). If C causes E, manipulating C in the right
  way should be a way of changing E.
- **Agency theories fail** (§3). A correlation that persists when A is
  brought about by a free act need not be causal: the placebo case.
- **An intervention is surgical** (§§4–5). Woodward's conditions M1–M4,
  relative to a target Y: the intervention I is the only cause of X; I
  does not cause Y by a route avoiding X; I is not caused by anything that
  affects Y by such a route; I leaves Y's other causes, off the path from I
  through X, unchanged.
- **Interventionist counterfactuals** (§5). Whether X causes Y is what Y
  would do under an intervention on X. Setting X differs from observing it.
- **Non-reductive, not viciously circular** (§§5–6). An intervention is a
  causal notion, so there is no reduction, but defining X's causing Y does
  not presuppose causal facts about X and Y themselves.
- **No human needed** (§8). A natural process with the right causal
  history counts as an intervention: a natural experiment.
- **Variables** (§12). A cause must be representable as a variable whose
  values the same system can take. Where no well-defined intervention
  exists, as with "being a raven", there is no bona fide cause on this
  account.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a supplement to the essay on
organizational will. It supplies the account of causation that List and
Menzies ([LIT-tmp6nylr](LIT-tmp6nylr.md)) build on. For §5.3 it gives the general form of a
test for an organization-level cause: an intervention on the organizational
variable that changes the outcome while meeting M1–M4. The entry does not
apply it to organizations. The account is [THEORY-tmpax4ff](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpax4ff.md). The book
