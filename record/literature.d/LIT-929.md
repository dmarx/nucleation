# LIT-929: Generalized Stability Theory. Part II: Nonautonomous Operators

Brian F. Farrell and Petros J. Ioannou (1996), *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences* 53(14), 2041–2053 — `doi:10.1175/1520-0469(1996)053<2041:gstpin>2.0.co;2`

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-842](../notes.d/NOTE-842.md).*

- **No modes, but the theory carries over.** For du/dt = A(t)u the
  temporal eigenmodes "are not defined". The first Lyapunov exponent λ =
  lim sup ln‖Φ(t)‖/t replaces the spectral abscissa. The first Lyapunov
  vector depends only on the current time, and its optimal excitation
  only on the initial time.
- **Non-normality is necessary for destabilization by time variation.**
  λ lies between the long-run averages of the extreme eigenvalues of
  (A + A†)/2 (eq. 12), so an operator normal and stable at every instant
  cannot be destabilized.
- **And with it, time variation destabilizes.** A frequency-switched
  oscillator neutral at every instant, and random rotations of a neutral
  2 × 2 matrix, have positive exponents. "Transient growth instigated at
  the starting time is continued with further growth when the switch to
  the second operator takes place." In both, the exponent vanishes as the
  switching interval goes to zero.
- **Multiplicative noise destabilizes generically.** Cited to
  Has'minskii and Colonius & Kliemann: for dimension ≥ 2 and noise
  matrices neither skew-symmetric nor commuting with the mean operator,
  noise of sufficient size gives a positive exponent. More non-normality
  lowers the threshold; more damping raises it (Figs. 8–9).
- **Lorenz and the atmosphere.** Along the Lorenz trajectory the mean
  numerical abscissa is about 4.2 against a first exponent of 0.88. A
  three-layer baroclinic model gains a positive exponent at wind
  fluctuations of about 10%, which the authors take to suggest that
  long-range forecast error comes from the flow's time dependence.

## Standing in the record

Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-842](../notes.d/NOTE-842.md). [THEORY-325](../theory.d/THEORY-325.md) is filed from it:
time dependence can destabilize a non-normal system stable at every
instant; non-normality is necessary; random variation destabilizes
generically, needing less the more non-normal and less damped the mean
operator.

[CLAIM-186](../claims.d/CLAIM-186.md) cites it for the remark that an argument under development is
the nonautonomous case, and this entry, when filed, asked what carries
over to an operator that changes as fast as the state does. The reading answers
more than the remark asked. The autonomous comfort of Part I ([LIT-931](LIT-931.md)),
that a stable non-normal system settles after its burst, does not carry
over: a structure stable at every snapshot can fail to settle if it
keeps changing. The answer to the question is conditional. In the
deterministic examples change much faster than the transient growth
destabilizes nothing, and change on the growth timescale is what
destabilizes. Under white noise the fast limit stays unstable. Damping
stabilizes in all cases. Which of these anchoring corresponds to is the
reading across, and the paper says nothing about arguments.
