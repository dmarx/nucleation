# LIT-260: Kernel Mean Embedding of Distributions: A Review and Beyond

Krikamol Muandet, Kenji Fukumizu, Bharath Sriperumbudur, Bernhard Schölkopf (2016), *Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning 10(1–2):1–141 (2017); first appeared as arXiv preprint* — [ARXIV-1605.09522](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09522)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpgvy79](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpgvy79.md).*

- **Riesz makes the kernel.** An RKHS is defined by bounded evaluation
  (Def. 2.4), Riesz is stated (Thm 2.4), and the representer k_x with
  f(x) = ⟨k_x, f⟩ follows (Prop. 2.1). Aronszajn's theorem (Thm 2.5) runs
  the other way, from a positive-definite k to its unique RKHS.
- **Riesz makes the mean embedding.** If E_P√k(X,X) < ∞, then
  |E_P f| ≤ E√k(X,X)·‖f‖, so Riesz gives μ_P ∈ H with E_P f = ⟨f, μ_P⟩,
  and μ_P = ∫k(·,x)dP(x) (Lemma 3.1, proved, after Smola et al. 2007).
  The review also defines μ_P as a Bochner integral, a second route to the
  same object under the same condition.
- **MMD and HSIC are geometry of embeddings.** MMD over the RKHS unit ball
  is ‖μ_P − μ_Q‖ (eq. 3.29), a metric on distributions iff k is
  characteristic (Def. 3.2). HSIC is the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of
  the centred cross-covariance, zero iff independence when the product
  kernel is characteristic (§3.6).
- **Operators.** C_YX is the embedding of the joint law, C_XX is positive
  and self-adjoint, and C_XX on L²(P_X) has the Mercer operator's
  eigenvalues (§3.2). The conditional mean embedding C_YX C_XX⁻¹ needs
  E[g(Y)|X = ·] ∈ H, which fails for Gaussian kernels in simple cases, so
  a regularized form is used (§4.1, Remark 4.1).
- **What it does not cover.** No result for learned kernels or feature
  maps. Kernel choice is "an ultimate problem" (§6.1).
