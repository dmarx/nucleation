# LIT-tmpsf1ce: Explaining the Brain: Mechanisms and the Mosaic Unity of Neuroscience

Carl F. Craver (2007), *Explaining the Brain: Mechanisms and the Mosaic
Unity of Neuroscience*, Oxford: Oxford University Press —
DOI-10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199299317.001.0001

## Key takeaways

*Registered from secondary description, not read.* What follows comes from
Crossref's chapter list and from reviews of the book.

- **Explanation is of mechanisms.** The book asks what distinguishes good
  explanations in neuroscience from bad, and answers, descriptively and
  normatively, that good ones describe mechanisms. Chapters: "Explanation
  and Causal Relevance", "Causal Relevance and Manipulation", "The Norms of
  Mechanistic Explanation", "A Field-Guide to Levels", "Nonfundamental
  Explanation", "The Mosaic Unity of Neuroscience".
- **Two kinds of relevance.** Causal relevance, between events, is given
  a manipulationist account. Constitutive relevance, between a mechanism's
  behaviour and its components, is a part–whole relation: a component is
  relevant when it is part of the mechanism and the two are mutually
  manipulable, so that intervening on either changes the other. The
  criterion has been much criticised since.
- **Levels of mechanisms and the mosaic.** Levels are levels of mechanisms
  nested in mechanisms. Neuroscience is unified not by reduction but as a
  mosaic: fields and techniques each constrain a shared mechanism, and
  independent methods triangulate on it. The worked cases are the
  Hodgkin–Huxley model of the action potential and the link between
  long-term potentiation and memory.
