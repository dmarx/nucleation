# LIT-664: Generalized Linear Mode Connectivity for Transformers

Alexander Theus, Alessandro Cabodi, Sotiris Anagnostidis, Antonio Orvieto,
Sidak Pal Singh and Valentina Boeva (2025), 39th Conference on Neural
Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2025) — [ARXIV-2506.22712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22712)

## Key takeaways

- **Four symmetry classes, nested.** Permutations (elementwise activations
  such as GELU), semi-permutations (sparse stochastic mixing, for
  positively homogeneous activations, ReLU and attention heads), orthogonal
  maps (RMSNorm) and invertible maps (linear layers, the QK and OV circuits)
  each preserve the function of the component they fit (Table 1).
- **Where they sit in a transformer.** Feed-forward hidden units take a
  permutation. Attention heads take a semi-permutation, because heads are
  summed and each head's OV circuit splits linearly. Each head's W_Q W_Kᵀ and
  W_V W_O are invariant under invertible maps, so the circuits themselves
  are matched. The residual stream takes one orthogonal map O, once
  LayerNorm is rewritten as RMSNorm with its centring and scale absorbed
  into neighbouring layers (§3.2).
- **Weight matching** solves a layerwise assignment for feed-forward and head
  permutations and a Procrustes problem for O, iterated, with no data
  (§4.1). **Learned matching** starts there and trains the latent
  permutation and orthogonal matrices with the task loss at
  λθ_A + (1 − λ)π(θ_B), λ ∼ U(0.4, 0.6), using straight-through gradients
  for permutations (Algorithm 1).
- **Barriers fall to zero (Table 2, test loss).** Vanilla averaging gives
  1.69, 2.46 and 2.84 on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, and 2.02 and
  4.34 on Tiny Shakespeare and BookCorpus. Weight matching gives 0.36, 0.69,
  0.47, 0.34 and 1.56. Learned matching gives 0.00, 0.00, 0.00, 0.02 and
  0.42. Learned matching restricted to permutations gives 0.45, 0.53, 0.29,
  0.63 and 1.60.
- **The gap is the residual rotation.** Learned matching leaves weight
  matching's permutations essentially unchanged and makes small corrections
  to O (Fig. 5). Iterating orthogonal weight matching keeps lowering the
  barrier; iterating permutation matching does not (Fig. 7).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 with the owner's mode-connectivity batch ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). It
is the batch's transformer case, and its strongest evidence that what looks
like a barrier between two transformers is mostly a choice of coordinates.

It extends Git Re-Basin ([LIT-661](LIT-661.md)). Its weight matching is Git
Re-Basin's sum-of-bilinear-assignments objective, adapted to carry an
orthogonal map on the residual stream. Its learned matching is Git
Re-Basin's straight-through estimator, widened from permutations to
orthogonal maps. Its permutation-only learned-matching row in Table 2 is, in
the paper's words, "akin to the STE-based approach in Git Rebasin", and
that row does not reach zero. The anthology holds Git Re-Basin as
[ANTH-LIT-333](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-333.md). The conjecture its results bear on is Entezari et al.'s
([LIT-652](LIT-652.md)), which its related work restates: once symmetries are
resolved, SGD solutions lie in one basin.

Across the boundary it bears on two Proposed anthology documents.
[ANTH-THEORY-010](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/theory.d/THEORY-010.md) (the barrier is mostly permutation) names "the account
demonstrated on a transformer" as a condition for promotion. This paper
demonstrates it, but with the symmetry group widened beyond permutations,
and with permutations alone falling short. So it supports the account's
spirit and corrects its letter. [ANTH-SOTA-217](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/practices.d/SOTA-217.md) (align the hidden-unit
permutation before averaging) would, for transformers, need the orthogonal
residual alignment as well.

Like Zhou et al. ([LIT-674](LIT-674.md)), whom it cites for layerwise linearity, it
reads mode connectivity as a fact about coordinates. Unlike Bo Zhao et al.
([LIT-655](LIT-655.md)), it uses the invertible symmetries of attention products to
align, not to show that the straight line can fail.

It is flagged for the anthology: it carries a procedure for merging
transformers.
