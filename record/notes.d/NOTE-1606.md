# NOTE-1606: Foschi, Hull and Ku — Closed-form kinematics of degree-4 origami vertices

## Contribution

Equations for the folding angles of a degree-4 vertex were known in closed
form for flat-foldable vertices, and in part for others through Huffman's
relations. This paper derives algebraic equations for completely general
degree-4 vertices, Euclidean (developable) and non-Euclidean, that trace
the whole configuration space, and uses them on two designs.

## Key insight

Parameterized by tan(ρ/2), the configuration space of a degree-4 vertex is
cut out by two algebraic relations. Solving the adjacent-angle relation for
one angle gives two branches of a square root, which the paper calls the
two folding modes. In a developable vertex they cross at the flat state. A
non-developable vertex has no state with every folding angle zero, so its
modes cannot meet there.

## Assumptions

- **A single degree-4 vertex** with rigid faces, 0 < α_i < π.
- **Folding angles in [−π, π].** Self-intersecting solutions are drawn
  faded, not excluded: "Faded parts of curves correspond to
  self-intersections of the material or folding angles going beyond ±π"
  (Fig. 2 caption).
- **Sector-angle classes**: developable (sum 2π), elliptic (sum below 2π)
  and hyperbolic (sum above 2π).
- **The closure condition** ∏R(e_i, ρ_i) = I is stated as a necessary
  condition, cited to [18]. Appendix A handles a non-developable vertex by
  splitting crease e3 and inserting an angle θ.

## Key results

- **Closure** (§II): "a necessary condition for an origami vertex to be
  rigidly foldable with folding angles ρi is ∏R(ei,ρi)=I where I is the
  identity matrix [18]", [18] being belcastro and Hull (2002).
- **Flat-foldable degree 4** (§II, Theorem 1, "For a proof, see [17] or
  [19]", Origametry and Tachi and Hull): opposite folding angles equal up
  to sign, and "adjacent folding angles have a linear relationship when
  parameterized by the tangent half-angle".
- **Theorem 2** (§III): one equation for opposite folding angles (Eq. 4)
  and one for adjacent folding angles (Eq. 5), in tan(ρ/2), for "a general
  degree-4 rigid origami vertex". "Proofs of these equations can be found
  in Appendix A." Appendix A derives Eq. 5 by requiring the images of e2
  and e3 to stay at angle α2, then making the Weierstrass substitution:
  "This proves Theorem 2."
- **Two modes** (Corollary 1): "Degree-4 rigid-foldable vertices have
  exactly two folding modes, meaning that their configuration space
  consists of two curves in R4", from the two roots of Eq. 6, "(which is
  already generally known …)".
- **Theorem 3** (§IV, proof outlined in Appendix B): tangent-form
  equations for general developable vertices. They reduce to Huffman's
  Eq. 3 and, for flat-foldable vertices, to Theorem 1.
- **Disconnection** (§III, Example 1): "in both the elliptic and
  hyperbolic cases the configuration spaces are disconnected, whereas in
  the developable vertex they are connected", since "it is impossible to
  unfold the vertex so that all the creases have folding angles of zero at
  the same time". §V B generalizes it: "non-developable vertices cannot
  fold to a state where all the folding angles are zero, and therefore, as
  seen in Example 1 of Section III, the configuration spaces for the two
  folding modes are disconnected."
- **Square-twist pouch** (§V A): "the rigid origami configuration space of
  the square twist pouch determined by sector angle θ is discrete". It has
  the unfolded state and the two states at ±(π − arccos(cot θ)), a proof of
  the bistability felt as a snap.
- **Folding table** (§V B): a hyperbolic vertex (5π/8, 5π/8, π/2, 3π/4)
  with Eq. 10 and one non-self-intersecting mode. "Thus ρ4 = 0 is a
  discontinuity for the ρ1 (and ρ3) folding angle."
- **Care with roots** (Remark 2): isolating a mode from the equations
  needs tracking "which branch of the square root is needed to preserve
  the folding mode".
- **Bird's feet** (Appendix C): equations the authors call new "for
  non-developable bird's foot-type vertices".

## The check

**The equations.** Folding angles were measured on vertices built
independently of the paper, 40 random vertices of each type. The scripts
are scratch/formal/foschi_deg4_check.py, foschi_eq4_search.py and
foschi_closure_check.py, which the coordinator second-checked on
2026-11-06.

- **Hold to rounding error:** Eq. 5 for all four i; Eq. 6; Theorems 1 and 3;
  Eq. 3; Eq. 10; (B1); the pouch's fixed points; and closure on developable
  states, where the rotations do not commute.
- **Printed Eq. 4 fails**, missing by about 2. It holds with its numerator
  read as (1+T)cos(α_{i−1}+α_i) − T cos(α_{i+1}−α_{i+2}) − cos(α_{i+1}+α_{i+2}).
  The cosine rule on the diagonal gives the same form.
- **(A3)** holds with sin α3 sin α4 in place of sin α2 sin α4.
- **(A6)** holds with its x² and y² coefficients exchanged; it is then
  Eq. 5 at i = 4.

**The topology.** The pair (ρ1, ρ4) fixes the whole state, so the topology
was checked on the (ρ1, ρ4) torus. The grid script
scratch/formal/foschi_deg4_topology.py was second-checked. The grid-free
counts in foschi_deg4_monodromy.py and the branch joins in
foschi_deg4_turning.py are not second-checked.

- **Developable vertices.** Every one tested crosses its two modes at the
  flat state.
- **Example 1, elliptic perturbation.** The popped-up and popped-down
  pieces are separate while every folding angle stays inside (−π, π). They
  join into one loop once creases may pass ±π.
- **Example 1, hyperbolic perturbation.** Two disjoint loops even then.
- **The table vertex and about half of 80 random non-Euclidean vertices**
  are single loops on the torus.

## Concepts

- **Developable / elliptic / hyperbolic vertex**: sector angles summing to
  2π, less, or more.
- **Folding mode**: a branch of the square root in Eq. 6.
- **Valley / mountain**: ρ_i > 0 / ρ_i < 0.

## Connections

- **The original it stands in for.** It restates the flat-foldable degree-4
  fold-angle equations of the "Rigid Foldings" chapter of Hull's Origametry
  ([LIT-2039](../literature.d/LIT-2039.md)) as its Theorem 1, citing the book for the proof. The
  publisher's summary describes them as "linear when parameterized by the
  tangent of half the folding angles, also known as the Weierstrass
  transformation". It does not restate the chapter's reciprocal diagrams,
  its zero-area theorem, or its infinitesimal and second-order rigidity.
- **Also belcastro and Hull (2002)** ([LIT-2067](../literature.d/LIT-2067.md)). It states their
  single-vertex identity-product condition as necessary, citing them, but
  not the global or affine form; for that see Akitaya et al.,
  [LIT-2071](../literature.d/LIT-2071.md).
- **Agreement.** It finds from exact equations the disconnection that
  Berry, Lee-Trimble and Santangelo ([LIT-2024](../literature.d/LIT-2024.md)) find near flat, and
  for degree 4 on both sides of 2π, as they do.
- **Neighbouring entries.** The bird's-foot equations bear on
  THEORY-1393. The pouch's rigid discreteness contrasts with the
  square twist that must bend (THEORY-1399).

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis.** A does not cite this paper. Its square-twist
  pouch is an exact bistable example for A's point that bistability is "a
  physical implementation of a form of memory". But the bistability is
  kinematic, a discrete configuration space, not a double-well energy as

## Limitations

- **Degree 4 only.**
- **Three misprints in the arXiv copy**: Eq. 4, (A3) and (A6). The
  corrected forms are under The check. Whether the journal version repeats
  them was not seen.
- **The disconnection is argued from Example 1** ("This makes intuitive
  sense"), not proved. Whether it holds depends on whether a crease may
  pass ±π.
- **Self-intersecting parts of the curves** are only faded in the plots.
