# LIT-910: Effortless Attention: A New Perspective in the Cognitive Science of Attention and Action

Brian Bruya (ed.) (2010), *Effortless Attention: A New Perspective in the Cognitive Science of Attention and Action*, Cambridge, MA: MIT Press — [doi:10.7551/mitpress/9780262013840.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/9780262013840.001.0001)

## Key takeaways

*Unread. From the title, the introduction's title ("Toward a Theory of Attention That Includes Effortless Attention and Action") and the publisher's description.*

- **The problem it poses.** Focused attention is usually modelled as effortful, but in flow and in expert action people report high focus with little or no effort. A theory of attention has to include that case.
- **Why the record wants it.** [THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md) makes control costly, with an intrinsic cost that rises with intensity. Effortless attention is the phenomenon that account has to meet. The record's own reading ([CLAIM-162](../claims.d/CLAIM-162.md)) is that the cost is the cost of overriding a competing regime, so it vanishes when nothing has to be overridden.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-11 with the flow cluster. `cognition` comes first because attention is its subject; `agency` is for effortless action; `phenomenology` is for its first-person reports. Not held in the anthology.
