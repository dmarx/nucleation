# CLAIM-tmp9jg87: Possessing, enacting or being shaped by an organizational perspective does not establish an independent agent, and perspectival, agential and institutional boundaries need not coincide: several agents can share a perspective, one agent can act through several, and a perspective can be reproduced with no agent governing it; agency is exercised through perspectives, and its exercise can transform them

## The claim

The owner, at U38 of the branch: "what's the relationship between
perspective and agency?" A38: "I think the relationship is close, but we
shouldn't identify them." A perspective asks "**What can matter, and how
can it matter?**"; agency asks "**What can this system do, under which
conditions, and in service of what?**"

The claim has three parts.

1. **A perspective is not thereby an agent.** A martial tradition
   organizes how practitioners see openings and judge technique, but "may
   lack any single organization capable of deciding how it will respond to
   a rival school". A38 set it as a display formula: perspectival
   organization does not imply independent collective agency. Having
   causal influence is not exercising agency.
2. **The boundaries need not coincide.** A38's four arrangements: one
   agent with several perspectives, several agents with one shared
   perspective, a perspective with no governing agent, and an agent
   governing several perspectives. "**perspectival boundaries, agency
   boundaries, and institutional boundaries need not coincide**." Two
   fighters of one tradition share a perspective and oppose each other.
   [CLAIM-388](CLAIM-388.md) is the record's formal case: a dynamically closed collective
   variable need not be a bounded subsystem, and closure does not by itself
   individuate an agent.
3. **The relation runs both ways.** "**agency is exercised through
   perspectives, while the exercise of agency can itself produce and
   transform perspectives**." [CLAIM-275](CLAIM-275.md) already says an intervention can
   come before the interpretation it makes available; a fighter can find an
   opening by moving, not only see it first.

A39 asked the record to state the distinction so that the perspective
account does not assume "that an organizational perspective must itself
be an individuated agent, or must be confined within one already
individuated agent, in order to be real and causally consequential".
That second clause is [CLAIM-tmp8lheg](CLAIM-tmp8lheg.md)'s distributed realization; this
claim keeps it from being read as a distributed agent.

## Which agent

The record holds two criteria of agency and has not chosen. [TERM-044](../terms.d/TERM-044.md)
(Active) makes an agent a system for which a goal-directed description
earns its keep and whose policy would adapt. [TERM-063](../terms.d/TERM-063.md) (Proposed), from
Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde ([LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md)), requires individuality,
interactional asymmetry and norms the system's own organization sets.
A38: "there may be no theory-neutral answer to the question *Does
perspective imply agency?*" The claim holds under both: neither makes
every organization of significance an agent. [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md) sense 1 was
defined without self-maintenance so that this could stay a question and
not a definition. Participatory sense-making (De Jaegher and Di Paolo,
[LIT-571](../literature.d/LIT-571.md)) gives an interaction's own dynamics without settling whether it
is a further agent, which is the same separation.

## What it does not say

- **Not that agency needs no perspective.** A38 proposed that "Every
  sufficiently rich exercise of agency involves an organization of
  practical significance", and qualified it at once: defensible if
  significance is read broadly, "controversial if perspective is
  understood as conscious, conceptual, or explicitly represented". That
  half is not claimed; it is part of [QUESTION-tmpmzijj](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpmzijj.md).
- **Not phenomenal perspective.** Nothing here bears on [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md) sense 3.
- **Not that governing a perspective is ordinary agency.** A38 separated
  first-order agency, perspectival regulation and reflexive governance;
  the last is [CLAIM-tmpqktfo](CLAIM-tmpqktfo.md), and A38's Challenger contrast, "operational
  agency is substantial while perspectival self-governance is deficient in
  a particular domain", is [CASE-tmpv2pte](../cases.d/CASE-tmpv2pte.md) read through it.
