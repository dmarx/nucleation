# LIT-952: Neural Networks Learn Statistics of Increasing Complexity

Nora Belrose, Quintin Pope, Lucia Quirke, Alex Mallen, Brennan Dury and
Xiaoli Fern (2024), *Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on
Machine Learning*, PMLR 235; *arXiv:2402.04362*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-777](../notes.d/NOTE-777.md).*

- **Grafted statistics fool early networks.** ConvNeXt V2, Swin V2 and
  RegNet-Y are trained on CIFAR-10 and four other small image sets.
  - Between roughly 2^4 and 2^12 steps they classify images whose
    pixel marginals were quantile-normalised to another class as that
    class.
  - ConvNeXt exceeds 40% against the target class on images given
    another class's mean and covariance, near 2^10 steps; chance is
    10%.
- **First order peaks before second, then both fall.**
  - Accuracy on maximum-entropy samples that match only the class
    marginals peaks earlier and lower than on samples that match means
    and covariances.
  - Later, both fall, often below chance. MNIST and Fashion-MNIST barely
    fall. On SVHN, first-order samples never beat chance.
  - Model scale changes little.
- **The same order in language.** Pythia, 14M to 12B, reaches its lowest
  loss on unigram-sampled text before bigram-sampled text. The loss then
  rises and falls again. The second fall is attributed, on correlational
  evidence, to in-context learning.
- **Theory is light.**
  - The n-gram/moment equivalence (Thms. 2.1–2.2) holds because a product
    of one-hot indicators is a joint probability.
  - The ordering is motivated by a Taylor expansion of the loss in
    central moments, with an informal argument that its terms become
    relevant in order.
  - Third-order samples are generated, but no ordered result beyond
    second order is stated.
