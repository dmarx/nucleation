# LIT-tmp2u6df: Freedom Within Reason

Susan Wolf, *Freedom Within Reason*, New York: Oxford University Press,
1990 (print ISBN 9780195056167; online ISBN 9780197730430) —
DOI-10.1093/oso/9780195056167.001.0001.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's abstract, the chapter list and openings
Crossref carries, and second-hand reports. Not a reading of the book.*

- **The thesis**, from the publisher's abstract: freedom of the will
  "cannot be understood as a purely metaphysical notion". You enjoy it
  "only if you are in a position that enables you, not only to govern your
  actions on the basis of your own values, but also to develop and alter
  your values on the basis of the right data and by the right means". The
  metaphysical conditions of freedom "vary in accordance with the value of
  the action in question", which is the asymmetry of [LIT-tmpbc48b](LIT-tmpbc48b.md).
- **The structure**, from Crossref's chapter records: 1 The Dilemma of
  Autonomy (3–22); 2 The Real Self View (23–45); 3 The Autonomy View
  (46–66); 4 The Reason View (67–93); 5 Ability and Possibility (94–116);
  6 The True and the Good (117–147); Notes (149–153).
- **Two conditions, not one.** Chapter 6 opens (as Crossref gives it): a
  responsible agent's status rests "not only on her ability to make her
  behavior conform to her deepest values but also on her ability to form,
  assess, and revise those values on the basis of a recognition and
  appreciation of what I have called the True and the Good", so that "an
  agent must be in a position that allows her reasons to be governed by
  what reasons there are". The first condition is the Real Self View's;
  the second is what Wolf adds.
- **Why autonomy is not needed.** Chapter 4 opens by separating the ability
  to act autonomously from the ability to act in accordance with Reason:
  among agents who share the latter, autonomy adds only "the ability to act
  in discordance with Reason", which "at best seems irrelevant" to
  responsibility. And "dogs and psychopaths might conceivably be
  autonomous", so autonomy without Reason does not suffice either.
- **The autonomy requirement**, as Holton's MIT handout quotes p. 10: the
  agent's control must be "ultimate—her will must be determined by her
  self, and her self must not, in turn be determined by anything external
  itself". Wolf calls this, after Kant, the requirement of autonomy, and
  the book's first chapter argues it is at once necessary-seeming and
  impossible.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as a Deferred seed. Moral
philosophy with no instruction for machine-learning practice; no anthology

**Why the book is filed, not only the papers.** The record's rule is to
replace a book with shorter lawful works by the same author and to seed the
book only if the record would lose something without it. Here it would:

- **The label the record already cites.** McKenna and Coates's SEP entry
  ([LIT-291](LIT-291.md), read in [NOTE-270](../notes.d/NOTE-270.md)) categorised Frankfurt's view as a "*Real Self
  Theory* (Wolf, 1990, p. 29)" in its 2004–2015 revisions, and [NOTE-270](../notes.d/NOTE-270.md)
  records that the remark now dangles in the supplement. Page 29 is in this
  book's chapter 2. Without this entry, that citation points nowhere in the
  record.
- **The three-way frame.** The papers state the asymmetry ([LIT-tmpbc48b](LIT-tmpbc48b.md))
  and the sanity condition ([LIT-tmpooua4](LIT-tmpooua4.md)). The book adds the dilemma that
  organises them, the rejection of the Autonomy View alongside the Real
  Self View, and chapter 5's defence of the ability blame needs. The SEP
  ([LIT-291](LIT-291.md), §4.3) cites pp. 79–81 for the asymmetry, so the book's
  statement of it is the one the encyclopaedia leans on.
- **The papers are unread too.** The substitution the rule allows only
  works if the shorter works can be read, and neither could.

**The relation.** It `extends` both papers. McKenna and Coates ([LIT-291](LIT-291.md),
§4.3) report that Wolf develops the Reason View "in *Freedom within Reason*
(1990), as well as in several provocative papers (1980, 1987)", and cite
the book and [LIT-tmpbc48b](LIT-tmpbc48b.md) together for the asymmetry. Holton's handout
says the book and [LIT-tmpooua4](LIT-tmpooua4.md) "take a broadly similar line", with the
book's Real Self View starting from Hume where the essay's exemplars are
Frankfurt, Watson and Taylor. The book is the later, fuller statement of
the account both papers give, so it builds on them; that is a bibliographic
fact the two lawful reports establish, not a reading of the argument.

**How it bears on the record.** McKenna and Coates ([LIT-291](LIT-291.md), §4.3) say the
Reason View is exposed to the manipulation objection as Frankfurt's is,
and must either show what is defective in manipulation cases or bite the
bullet; that is the forced choice of [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md). The second condition, the
ability to revise one's values by "what reasons there are", is the place a
reading should look for Wolf's answer: a manipulator who leaves that
ability intact has not made the agent unfree on her view, and one who
removes it has. Robb's PAP map ([LIT-294](LIT-294.md), [NOTE-269](../notes.d/NOTE-269.md)) names Frankfurt and
Fischer and Ravizza as the two compatibilisms that replaced PAP; the Reason
View is a third, keeping PAP for blame alone.

**Priority for a reading: medium.** The two papers come first; chapters 2
and 4 are what a reading of the book adds.
