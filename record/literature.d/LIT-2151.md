# LIT-2151: Sustaining Development: A Synthesis of Results from a Four-Country Study of Sustainability and Exit Strategies among Development Food Assistance Projects

Rogers, B. L. & Coates, J. (2015). *Sustaining Development: A Synthesis of Results from a Four-Country Study of Sustainability and Exit Strategies among Development Food Assistance Projects.* Washington, DC: FHI 360 / Food and Nutrition Technical Assistance III Project (FANTA). The December 2015 date and the USAID address are from the file name and search listings, unverified.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. What follows comes from the two country summaries that stand in for it, and is not the synthesis's own wording.*

- **Twelve projects, four countries.** Food for Peace development
  projects in Bolivia, Honduras, India and Kenya, whose funding ended in
  2008–09, followed with qualitative rounds and a replicated endline
  survey 2–3 years after exit.
- **Four factors and the exit process.** Resources, capacity, motivation
  and linkages, with gradual exit and a period of independent operation
  before closure, as the study's frame.
- **Exit impact did not predict sustainability**, in the Kenya summary's
  words; whether the synthesis says so across all four countries is
  unverified.

## Standing in the record

Unfetched. Two executive summaries of country studies from the same
study stand in for it, and only so far:

- **Kenya**, Coates, Kegode, Galante & Blau (2016), [LIT-2154](LIT-2154.md), read in
  [NOTE-1650](../notes.d/NOTE-1650.md). Coates is the synthesis's second author.
- **India**, Rogers, Klemeyer, Bondre & Fierstein (2017), [LIT-2148](LIT-2148.md),
  read in [NOTE-1655](../notes.d/NOTE-1655.md). Rogers is its first author; India is the study's
  case of phase-over to government.

They carry the study's design, its four factors, the gradual-exit finding
and sector results for Kenya and India. They do not stand in for Bolivia
or Honduras, for any cross-country tally of what was sustained, for any
pooled figure, or for the synthesis's own wording. Nothing should be cited
from the synthesis as read until its text is obtained. It bears on
[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (independence from the founder, never from the feed), on
[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) (when a partial organization becomes independent of the
intervention that established it) and on [CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md) (assembling and
persisting come apart).
