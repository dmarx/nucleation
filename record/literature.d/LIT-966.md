# LIT-966: Cooperative Dilemmas in Rational Debate

Toby Handfield, Julián Garcia, Christian Hilbe and Shang Long Yeo (2025), *arXiv:2504.06485*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-795](../notes.d/NOTE-795.md).*

- **The model.** Positions are truth assignments, and valid arguments
  eliminate them. A refuted debater moves minimally. Each debater picks
  one of 16 premise × conclusion rules (self or other, accept or reject),
  or Exit. The payoff is u = w·v − d.
- **Bold argument is the public good.** Strategies like S+S− (premises
  I accept, conclusions I reject) refute their user's own position and
  are truth-maximising when no argument is invalid. Their cost falls on
  whoever is refuted.
- **Free-riding.** At moderate truth weight or in longer debates, the
  best reply to a bold partner is a compromise strategy that forces
  revision only where the two disagree. This gives an asymmetric
  snowdrift equilibrium with a modest loss of collective accuracy.
- **Exit.** (Exit, Exit) is an equilibrium everywhere and is selected at
  low truth weights, an "agreement to disagree". With invalid arguments
  allowed (α = 0.1, 0.3), Exit spreads over much of parameter space.
- **Non-monotonicity.** Where only mixed equilibria exist, a debater who
  cares more about truth faces an opponent who must frustrate it more
  often, so collective accuracy dips as truth weight rises. The dip is
  small and local, and survives a utilitarian selection rule.
- **Scope.** Two players, n = 3, memoryless strategies, independent
  atomic propositions. The authors' links to confirmation bias,
  polarisation and mistaken expert consensus are suggestions, not
  models.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-047](../questions.d/QUESTION-047.md) and read on 2026-10-14 in
[NOTE-795](../notes.d/NOTE-795.md). What it shows is held as [THEORY-290](../theory.d/THEORY-290.md): a mechanism,
the private cost and shared benefit of being refuted, by which
individually rational reasoners produce a worse collective epistemic
state. It does not show a group settling on a belief no member reasoned
to. The seeded body said it did, and that was the record's reading, not
the paper's.
