# NOTE-856: De Marco and Cyr — Manipulation Cases, Part 1

## Contribution

A survey that sorts the manipulation literature by case type and by kind
of reply. For the record it supplies Pereboom's Case 1 in his words
(2024 wording), a description of all four cases and of how his argument
concludes, and the case types: mid-life, original design, natural force
and parallel. Each type is matched to the reply it defeats.

## Key insight

The manipulation argument is two premises. No-FR: the manipulated agent
is not responsible. No-Diff: she does not differ relevantly from an
ordinary determined agent. Each soft-line condition that tries to deny
No-Diff is met by some type of case. A bypassing condition is met by
original design, where nothing developed is bypassed. A manipulator
condition is met by a natural force, where there is no manipulator. Each
hard line has to swallow No-FR's intuition, or explain it away.

## Assumptions

- **Direct responsibility only.** The epistemic condition is "not
  typically in question", so a victim's lack of responsibility is put
  down to the freedom condition (§3).
- **Determinism in the cases.** The classic cases are "set in
  deterministic universes" (§2), except the parallel cases of §6.

## Key results

- **Case 1, quoted (§2, from Fischer et al. 2024, 98).** "A team of
  neuroscientists has the ability to manipulate Professor Plum's neural
  states at any time by remote optogenetic neural stimulation. In this
  particular case, they do so by pressing a button just before he begins
  to reason about his situation, which they know will produce in him a
  neural state that realizes a strongly egoistic reasoning process, which
  they know will deterministically result in his decision to kill White.
  Plum would not have killed White had the neuroscientists not
  intervened." He is "stipulated to satisfy various compatibilist
  conditions on responsibility", for example "a process that is
  appropriately responsive to reasons, and conforms to higher-order
  attitudes". Endnote 2 lists versions in Pereboom 1995, 2001 (pp.
  112–113), 2014 (pp. 76–77), 2017 and 2021.
- **Cases 2 to 4, described (§5).** In Case 2 Plum is "pre-programmed to
  behave in the way that his manipulators intend", an original-design
  case like Ernie (n. 4). In Case 3 he "grows up in an egoistic society
  that instills in him the very same tendencies". In Case 4, "an ordinary
  deterministic world", he "comes to have the same tendencies and to
  perform the same action without any manipulators in the picture".
  "Pereboom begins with Case 1 and attempts to carry the No-FR judgment
  over to Case 4 by arguing that there is no relevant difference between
  any two adjacent cases."
- **Pereboom's conclusion is abductive (n. 23).** "Having presented the
  four cases, and argued that there is no relevant difference between
  cases 1–4, Pereboom argues that the best explanation of the Plums' lack
  of responsibility for their decisions is that the decisions were
  causally determined by factors outside of the Plums' control."
- **Mele's cases (§2).** In Chuck and Sweet Sally, Sally's values are
  replaced overnight with Chuck's, and many judge Chuck responsible and
  Sally not. In Ernie, Diana makes a zygote so that Ernie does A thirty
  years on. Ernie is "covert non-constraining control" in Kane's sense
  (n. 5).
- **The schema and its variants (§3).** No-FR, No-Diff, therefore
  compatibilism is false. A stronger conclusion, that determinism
  *explains* why, needs further premises (n. 8). Weaker ones target
  existing views only.
- **The soft-line worry (§4).** "For any soft-line view one offers … the
  proponent of a manipulation argument will simply be able to produce a
  new case in which Victim does meet the proposed necessary condition."
  With Ernie, "bypassing views cannot account for a difference … since
  there is no difference in terms of whether their capacities, once
  developed, were bypassed".
- **Hard lines (§5).** Frankfurt: freedom does "not depend on the origin
  of the conditions". Fischer: intuitions track autonomy, not
  responsibility. Natalie, a natural-force case, answers the worry that
  reactions to a manipulator distort judgements about the victim.
  McKenna reverses the argument: start agnostic at Case 4 and carry
  agnosticism back to Case 1. McKenna 2004: manipulated agents are
  responsible for fewer things, not for the character they act from.
  Cyr, and Montminy and Tinney: blame is mitigated.
- **Parallel cases (§6).** Paru is manipulated in an indeterministic
  world where she could have done otherwise. If she is still not
  responsible, libertarians are pressed too. Only one original-design
  parallel case is known (Cyr 2020b).
- **Dilemmas (§7).** King: the libertarian needs a contentious
  sourcehood thesis. Kearns: either manipulation itself undermines (so
  No-Diff fails), or it is idle. Vihvelin: the "tool" explanation either
  justifies a difference or discredits the intuition.
- **Todd (§8).** The modified argument needs only that Ernie's blame be
  mitigated. A second argument asks why God lacks standing to blame
  Ernie.

## Concepts

- **No-FR and No-Diff.** The two premises above.
- **Mid-life and original-design manipulation.** Intervention on an
  existing agent, against design before the agent exists.
- **Natural-force case.** The same change produced with no manipulator.
- **Parallel case.** A manipulation case in which the victim meets
  libertarian conditions.
- **Hard-line and soft-line.** Relative to a case, not to a theorist (§4).

## Connections

It is Part 1 of three. Parts 2 and 3, on manipulator-focused and
bypassing views, were not read. It builds on McKenna 2008 for the
dialectic and on Mele 2006 and 2019 for the cases. Fischer and Ravizza
1998, chapters 7–8, are listed among bypassing views (n. 15), which is
where their taking-responsibility condition belongs. SEP *Compatibilism*
([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md)) and Caruso's SEP entry ([NOTE-862](NOTE-862.md)) cover the same ground
from the compatibilist and the skeptic's side.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md).** Two points, the first stronger.
  - *First half* (a structural condition is met by a manipulated agent).
    Case 1 meets reasons-responsiveness and "conforms to higher-order
    attitudes" by stipulation. Frankfurt's hard line ("do not depend on
    the origin of the conditions", 1988, 45–6) is a second first-hand
    quotation, cited in a survey, of the bullet [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md) reports him
    biting.
  - *Second half* (no historical condition escapes). Ernie defeats any
    bypassing condition, since nothing he has developed is bypassed.
    Fischer and Ravizza's condition is listed as a bypassing view (n.
    15). That is the strongest reason the record now holds for the second
    half, though it is reported, not read in Pereboom or Mele.
  - The promote_when's "first-hand reading of Pereboom's four-case
    argument or Mele's zygote argument" is not met. Mele's Ernie is
    quoted (2006: 188–9), but not his argument.
- **[THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md).** Its refuter, a four-case version that "manipulates the
  taking of responsibility", is not described here. The Ernie point bears
  on it. If taking responsibility is a bypassing-type condition, an
  original-design agent takes responsibility in the ordinary way, and the
  account must either count Ernie responsible (a hard line) or find
  another difference.
- **[QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md).** Two things here bear on it.
  - Its description of Pereboom's cases is now confirmed in outline, and
    its qualification holds: Case 4 has no manipulator. Case 3, an
    egoistic society, is between the two. Someone else produces the
    character, but by upbringing, not intervention.
  - Part 2 asks what history would tell a manipulated will from one that
    is merely effective. Original design is the hard case. A history
    that records only how capacities were formed, or whether they were
    bypassed, records nothing that separates Ernie from an ordinary
    agent.

## Limitations

- A survey. It quotes one of Pereboom's four cases, in the 2024 wording
  with optogenetics, which differs from the 2001 text.
- It does not report a version of the cases built to defeat Fischer and
  Ravizza's taking-responsibility condition.
- Parts 2 and 3, where soft-line views are assessed, were not read.

## Open questions

- Whether any soft-line condition survives both original-design and
  natural-force cases. Parts 2 and 3 take this up.
- Whether Case 3, manipulation by upbringing, belongs with the
  manipulated cases or with ordinary determination. Most of the
  soft-line debate turns on it.
