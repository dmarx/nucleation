# ARG-008: A language model's response to its context is functionally a parameterized frame, so context-conditioned models give a controlled setting in which to test the theory

## The inference

U17: an LLM "and how it responds to a context, i.e. functionally a
parameterized frame" can serve as a motivating example, "maybe even just for
notational purposes". A43: the LLM is "a concrete computational realization of
a context-dependent communicative system", and lets us "**separate changes in
communicative context from changes in the underlying interpretive system**". So
experiments on models can test what the theory says about frames ([CLAIM-058](../claims.d/CLAIM-058.md)).

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are the cases alike?** Both condition their responses on a
  context, and appending an utterance is a context transition, T_u(c) = c‖u,
  "a very straightforward realization of context-change semantics" (A43 §2).
- **What transfers?** Control. Frames, orders and adaptation can be manipulated
  exactly, and the context-versus-interpreter distinction becomes measurable
  ([CLAIM-118](../claims.d/CLAIM-118.md)).
- **What does not?** A48 §3.3: "a prompt is not equivalent to a real social
  situation, but can encode evidence and instructions concerning one". A48 §4.6:
  "An LLM's context-conditioning behavior need not correspond to human
  inference over communicative situations." Order effects in a model may be
  string concatenation or positional artifacts (A43 §2; [TERM-024](../terms.d/TERM-024.md)). And the
  circularity of A45 §9 ([CLAIM-085](../claims.d/CLAIM-085.md)).
- **Is that the respect the conclusion needs?** Control is, but only with
  the generator and the evaluator separated. A45 §9 warns against a design that
  generates translations with an LLM, asks the same LLM whether they
  preserve pragmatic meaning, and declares its preferred translations
  more faithful: "That would primarily characterize the model's own
  preferences." The remedy it gives is "different models for generation
  and evaluation, independently constructed framing conditions,
  calibrated response measurements, and held-out human assessments".
- **What relevant difference is there?** Training data. Agreement with
  humans in direction is weak evidence. "Matching direction is weak evidence that
  the model tests anything about the theory's claims on human
  interpretation" ([CLAIM-248](../claims.d/CLAIM-248.md) v3): a model trained on human writing about
  teasing and reproach would agree in direction whatever its mechanism.
  And the founding stimuli and commentary on them are likely in training
  data, while what a context does to one model need not be what it does
  to another ([CLAIM-248](../claims.d/CLAIM-248.md)). [CLAIM-058](../claims.d/CLAIM-058.md) v2 answers this in its defeat
  condition: the pattern of effects across conditions, across model
  families and sizes, on stimuli written after the training cut-offs.

Active: the inference holds as stated, to a testing ground. It does not extend
to evidence about human pragmatics. Since version 2 it is narrowed further, to
a testing ground with separate generator and evaluator, stimuli held out from
training, and agreement required across model families.
