# THEORY-tmpz2zb6: Blackiston, Silva Casey and Weiss report that tobacco hornworm caterpillars conditioned in the last larval instar to avoid an odour still avoided it as moths, that residue on the pupal case does not explain the aversion, and that an aversion learned two instars earlier survived larval molts but not metamorphosis

## The account

Tobacco hornworm larvae trained to pair ethyl acetate with mild shock
avoided the odour the next day and, four to five weeks and a
metamorphosis later, as moths. Four controls, backward pairing among
them, showed nothing. The authors take this to "challenge a broadly-held
popular view of lepidopteran metamorphosis: that the caterpillar is
essentially broken down entirely, and its components reorganized into a
butterfly or moth" (p. 5).

What survives depends on when it was learned. An aversion learned in
early third instar lasted through two larval molts and was not expressed
by the adult. By analogy with Drosophila, the authors place early memory
in a mushroom-body lobe pruned in pupation and late memory in one that
keeps its projections. They say their results are "consistent with, but
do not provide conclusive support for" surviving synaptic connections
(p. 5).

## Evidence

- Larvae: forward pairing 78%, N = 41, p < 0.001; untreated, shock-only,
  odour-only and backward-paired groups at chance (p. 2).
- Adults, 28 to 35 days after training: forward-paired 77%, N = 27, p =
  0.005; controls at chance. Of forward-paired adults choosing ambient
  air, 80% had chosen it as larvae (N = 15, p = 0.035) (pp. 2–4).
- Chemical legacy: odour gel on naive pupae gave no aversion (N = 30, p
  = 0.20); washing trained pupal cases did not remove it (N = 31, p =
  0.438, a test the text does not name) (p. 4). Internal residue is
  argued from the design, not measured.
- Third-instar training: 81% avoidance in late fifth instar (N = 32);
  adults at chance (N = 15, p = 0.5), with no direct comparison to the
  fifth-instar adults (pp. 2, 4).
- One species, one odour, one lab; NOTE-1249 lists caption and text
  discrepancies in sample sizes.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-302 names this paper as its example of a memory surviving
metamorphosis, and its note of 2026-10-29 reports the results above. The
paper excludes residue on the pupa as the carrier, and its timing result
places the carrier in something the larval molts keep and metamorphosis
replaces. On NOTE-1249's reading, the moth is the same organism as the
caterpillar with a thin, timing-dependent psychological connection to
it.
