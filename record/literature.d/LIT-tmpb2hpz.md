# LIT-tmpb2hpz: The Homological Nature of Entropy

Pierre Baudot, Daniel Bennequin (2015), *Entropy 17(5):3253–3318 (MDPI)* — DOI-10.3390/e17053253

## Key takeaways

- Shannon entropy is the (first) universal cohomology class of an "information structure" built from a family of observables and a family of probability laws. The same holds for quantum probabilities and for dynamic observation trees, which gives a topology of information that recovers multivariate mutual informations and KL divergence.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The authors propose that entropy is a universal cohomological class in a theory attached to a family of observable quantities and a family of probability distributions. They develop three cases: classical probabilities with random variables, quantum probabilities with observable operators, and dynamic probabilities with observation trees. The result is a new kind of topology for information processes. It accounts for entropy, mutual informations of all orders and Kullback–Leibler divergence, and generalizes them. The article has two independently readable parts. The first is an overview of results, open questions, future directions and application to complex data. The second gives complete definitions and proofs of the main theorems (A, C, E), showing that entropy is the first homological invariant of an information structure in four settings: static classical or quantum probability, and dynamics of classical or quantum observation strategies.
