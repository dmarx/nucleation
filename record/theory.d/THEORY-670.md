# THEORY-670: Ordinary dispositions can be modelled as restricted possibilities individuated by their manifestation alone: 'fragile' as manifestation in a sufficient proportion of cases over maximally varied external circumstances, 'breakable' as plain possibility; grading by closeness to an ideal world fails for any choice of ideal

## Source

Barbara Vetter (2014), "Dispositions without Conditionals", [LIT-109](../literature.d/LIT-109.md), *Mind*
123(489): 129–156, read in full in [NOTE-125](../notes.d/NOTE-125.md) from the penultimate draft (28
pp., all 27 footnotes). Page numbers are the draft's.

## What was actually shown

**The two conceptions (§2.1, pp. 6–7).** The standard conception individuates a
disposition by stimulus and manifestation, "a disposition to M when C", and
gives it a counterfactual modality. The possibility conception individuates it
by manifestation alone, "a disposition to M, fullstop", and gives it the
modality of possibility, "x can M". The relevant worlds hold the bearer's
intrinsic make-up fixed and vary its surroundings, the device Lewis (1976)
used for 'can'.

**Against closeness (§2.2, pp. 9–11).** Suppose "easily possible" means true in
some relevant world close enough to an ideal world. The ideal cannot be
actuality, or anything that actually breaks would be maximally fragile. For
any other ideal, stipulate a block with an Achilles' heel that breaks only in
that world. It then counts as fragile, and as more fragile than a champagne
glass. "One world is not enough." The recipe is general across ideals, and
rests on the intuition that the block is not fragile.

**Proportion instead (§§2.2–2.3, pp. 11–14).** x is more fragile than y iff x
breaks in more of the relevant cases. A case is a world, a time and an object,
because the irascible get angry many times in one world. To avoid counting
non-denumerable sets, count over a finite subset of cases with the greatest
variation in causally relevant external circumstances. The needed proportion
is small, near "at least one".

**Plain possibility (§2.4, pp. 14–16).** For 'breakable', 'soluble' and
'transmissible', one actual manifestation suffices, so the modality is
possibility simpliciter; even the Achilles'-heel block is breakable. Across
terms the variation runs from "at least one" to "a few", never toward
necessity.

**Discrediting the contrary data (§3.1, pp. 16–20).** In COCA, 341 hits for
"disposed to" left 226 relevant uses. 221 had agents as subjects and 5
abstracta; only 5 interacted with 'if' and 1 with 'when'. None ascribed a
permanent intrinsic tendency to a concrete inanimate subject. So "disposed to"
is a theoretical term, and intuitions about it encode the conditional theory.

**Stimuli recast (§3.2).** Where a stimulus seems to matter, it becomes part of
a causally complex manifestation (claustrophobia: being caused by enclosed
rooms to feel anxiety), or a fact about the typical causes of that kind of
process.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Corpus uses of "disposed to" could have
ascribed standing tendencies to objects, and closeness grading could have
survived some choice of ideal. Neither happened.

## What this does not say

- **That the conditional analysis is wrong.** The paper will "not … address the
  question which conception is to be preferred" (p. 6) and leaves "the argument
  for another day" (p. 26). The comparative case is deferred to "Multi-Track
  Dispositions".
- **That degrees of disposition are defined.** Counting cases in a non-unique
  finite subset is figurative. The cardinality problem is sidestepped, and
  vagueness is offered as the reason no canonical subset is needed.
- **That finks, masks and antidotes are solved.** The proposal "is not going to
  solve" them (p. 5); they arise on both conceptions (p. 7).
- **That it covers the dispositions of physics.** Natural properties that ground
  laws, such as charge, need nomological necessity. The clash with
  dispositional essentialism is called "merely nominal", which restricts the
  view's scope rather than answering the essentialist (pp. 24–25).
- **That model capabilities are dispositions of this kind.** [NOTE-125](../notes.d/NOTE-125.md) notes the
  analogy between "can do X" (one elicitation) and "reliably does X" (robust
  across varied prompts). The paper does not draw it, and the record has not
  tested it.
- **That it holds beyond English.** The linguistic evidence is English-centred,
  with a cross-linguistic assertion (fn. 2), and the corpus count is small and was
  coded by hand with no inter-rater check reported.
