Three pre-registered, placebo-controlled field experiments (230 canvassers, 6,869 voters reached, 7 US locations) on attitudes toward unauthorized immigrants and transgender people. In Experiment 1 (2018), a door-to-door conversation with arguments alone had no detectable effect (pooled d = 0.02, p = 0.27), while the otherwise identical, twice as long conversation that added a non-judgmental exchange of narratives reduced exclusionary attitudes (pooled d = 0.08 over surveys from 4 days to about 4.5 months; difference 0.06, p < 0.01). # LIT-1407: Reducing Exclusionary Attitudes through Interpersonal Conversation: Evidence from Three Field Experiments

Kalla, J. L. and Broockman, D. E. (2020). Reducing exclusionary attitudes through interpersonal conversation: Evidence from three field experiments. *American Political Science Review*, 114(2), 410–425. doi:10.1017/s0003055419000923.

Read in full on 2026-10-29 ([NOTE-tmpwypf3](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwypf3.md)), from the accepted manuscript.

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it for the claim that a non-judgmental exchange of narratives reduced exclusionary attitudes durably, where arguments alone did not. The abstract agrees, with qualifications the exchange left out: "durably" means at least four months; the arguments-only comparison is from Experiment 1 alone; and the effects are small (d = 0.08 and 0.04). For [CLAIM-275](../claims.d/CLAIM-275.md), the narrative exchange is an invitation to take another's perspective that comes before any change of view, as the favour does.
