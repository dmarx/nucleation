# NOTE-547: Stories and the Meaning of Life

## Contribution

Two things. First, a compact, first-hand list of the elements of Fischer's
framework for moral responsibility, with his statement of which of them he
regards as essential and which as revisable details (§I). Second, an
account of why acting freely matters: its value is the value of artistic
self-expression, because free action makes a life a story with narrative
value. That revises his 1999 view that it is self-expression of an
unspecified kind (note 6). The product of that activity, a life, is
evaluated mainly in moral and prudential terms, and Fischer argues that
this mismatch is what is special about free action (§§II–III).

## Key insight

Fischer's responsibility framework has a fixed core and adjustable parts.
The core is that responsibility needs guidance control and not regulative
control, that it is historical, and that it is compatible with
determinism. The adjustable parts are the specific accounts of ownership
(the "subjective" condition) and of reactivity ("all-of-a piece"). His
answer to "why care about guidance control?" is that acting from one's own
reasons-responsive mechanism is writing one's life.

## Assumptions

- A Strawsonian working hypothesis about the *concept* of moral
  responsibility, aptness for the reactive attitudes, while taking "no
  official stand" on its analysis (p. 3).
- Aristotle's two conditions: an epistemic condition and a
  freedom-relevant condition. The paper concerns only the second (p. 3).
- Velleman's view that what turns a chronicle into a story is a distinctive
  kind of understanding it provides, accepted in "bare bones" (p. 5).
- That evaluation is purpose-relative, so the mode of evaluating a product
  is not fixed by the kind of activity that produced it (pp. 6, 8–9).

## Key results

The paper argues by stating, not by proof, and its §I is a summary of
arguments made elsewhere.

- **§I, the framework.** Its elements: the concept/conditions distinction;
  regulative versus guidance control; guidance control as the
  freedom-relevant condition; guidance control as "mechanism ownership and
  moderate reasons-responsiveness", so that an agent "exhibits guidance
  control of an action insofar as the action issues from the agent's own,
  'moderately reasons-responsive' mechanism"; its compatibility with
  causal determinism; and its value as self-expression (pp. 3–4). The
  supporting arguments are in the works of note 1, chiefly Fischer and
  Ravizza 1998 ([LIT-684](../literature.d/LIT-684.md)), and are not given here.
- **§I, Frankfurt cases.** Frankfurt-style examples, cases of "pre-emptive
  overdetermination", imply that the Principle of Alternative
  Possibilities is false. Their moral is that acting freely and being
  responsible depend on the actual sequence. Fischer adds that there are
  other routes to the same conclusion (citing Wallace 1994 and Dennett
  2003 in note 2), so the conclusion does not depend on accepting the
  examples (p. 4).
- **§I, what is negotiable.** He can give up "strong subjectivity" in
  ownership and the thesis that "reactivity-is-all-of-a piece" and keep
  the three core claims. He cites his 2005 and 2006 replies for this (note
  3) (p. 4).
- **§II, the value.** Free action is "a sentence in the story of my life"
  (p. 5). It gives the life a narrative dimension of value, along which
  momentary well-being is not simply summed. Since a narrative is "by
  definition a work of art", acting freely is artistic self-expression
  (p. 5). The product, the life, is typically and primarily evaluated
  prudentially and morally, so free action is a sui generis artistic
  context: the activity is aesthetic and its product's main evaluation is
  not (p. 7). The two components of the value, self-expression and moral
  and prudential assessment, are matched to the two components of
  guidance control, ownership and reasons-responsiveness, though "the
  relationships here are perhaps less tight than one might like" (p. 7).
- **§III.** (a) Family or regional histories are narratives without being
  works of art, but they do not themselves have narrative value, so the
  objection (credited to Schwitzgebel) does not reach lives (pp. 7–8). (b)
  "The Aesthetic Fallacy" is inferring that lives should be judged
  primarily aesthetically from the aesthetic nature of free activity
  (pp. 8–9). (c) Since others interpret our behaviour too, acting freely
  does not write a fixed story but constrains the plausible stories,
  which parallels the "humbler" guidance control against the "Total
  Control" of "metaphysical megalomania" (pp. 9–10). (d) Lives are
  vehicles of narrative content, as sentences are of propositions, and
  some lives are like poems in their economy of expression (pp. 10–11).
- **§IV.** The meaning of life lies "at the intersection of the aesthetic,
  moral, and prudential", and creative self-expression, after Richard
  Taylor, is what makes us images of God, "even if pale images" (p. 12).

## Concepts

- **regulative control**: control involving access to alternative
  possibilities, freedom to do otherwise.
- **guidance control**: actual-sequence control; acting from one's own,
  moderately reasons-responsive mechanism.
- **mechanism ownership**: the first component of guidance control; the
  paper says only that it "involves a certain sort of 'subjective'
  condition" and that the strong form is revisable.
- **narrative value**: a dimension of a life's value along which momentary
  welfare is not added up and events take meaning from their relations to
  other events. Lives, not narratives, have it (note 8).
- **the Aesthetic Fallacy**: inferring the primary mode of evaluation of a
  product from the aesthetic nature of the activity that produced it.

## Connections

- **Fischer and Ravizza, *Responsibility and Control* ([LIT-684](../literature.d/LIT-684.md)).** §I
  is a summary of it. The paper confirms first-hand the book's guidance and
  regulative distinction, the two components of guidance control,
  historicity and compatibility with determinism. It does not state the
  receptivity and reactivity analysis, or taking responsibility, beyond
  the word "subjective".
- **McKenna and Coates ([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), [NOTE-270](NOTE-270.md)).** Their §4.4 and supplement §B
  report the same framework at second hand, in more detail. Nothing here
  contradicts them. Fischer's remark that the subjective ownership
  condition is revisable bears on their report (supplement §B.2) that the
  condition draws the objection that an agent could escape responsibility
  by failing to take it.
- **Robb's PAP map ([LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md), [NOTE-269](NOTE-269.md)).** Fischer's "other dialectical
  routes" put him on the side of Robb's map that treats Frankfurt-style
  cases as one argument among several against PAP.
- **Galen Strawson, "Against Narrativity" ([LIT-452](../literature.d/LIT-452.md)).** Note 10 quotes
  Strawson's statement of the psychological Narrativity thesis and calls it
  "entirely orthogonal" to the paper's claims, which are about
  interpretation, not experience.
- **Narrative identity ([THEORY-060](../theory.d/THEORY-060.md)).** That account makes a constructed life
  story the form of a person's diachronic identity. Fischer's narrative is
  a dimension of value that free action confers, and he says our stories
  are partly told by others, so the two do not meet directly.

## Bearing on the record

This is the first first-hand text by Fischer in the record. For the THEORY
on Fischer and Ravizza filed in the same batch, it is direct evidence that
Fischer holds the claim: responsibility requires guidance control and not
regulative control, guidance control is moderate reasons-responsiveness of
one's own mechanism, and it is compatible with determinism. It is not
evidence for the analysis of moderate reasons-responsiveness, which it does
not give. It supports [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md)'s report that Fischer's condition is
historical. It does not bear on whether that history escapes manipulation.

## Limitations

- A lecture-length paper whose framework section summarises; every element
  of §I is argued elsewhere.
- Its thesis about value rests on an analogy with art that Fischer himself
  calls loose ("less tight than one might like").
- The volume and date are from the repository's record (Philosophic
  Exchange, 2009). The PDF carries no date of its own.
