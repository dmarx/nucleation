## Verdicts on the questions

- **(a)** Yes, for what the type is taken as: a synthesized type is "a
  fact", a checked one "a requirement" (§4.4), and inference "finds all
  behaviours, not only the intended behaviours" (§9.1). No, if the split
  is read as informative against performative update: the term is an
  input in both modes (§1) and a check "returns a boolean" (§9.1).
- **(b)** Subsumption sits at the change from synthesis to checking and
  is a test, "A <: B becomes A ⊃ B" (§4.6); it moves nothing. The one
  change to a term the survey treats is the programmer's annotation
  (§3.2, §4.5.3), which under type erasure changes no behaviour (§2).
  Elaboration and coercion are only cited ("automatically insert
  implicit operations", §1). The record's claim that they are the
  performative half is left open, for LEAD-tmp7mkn0.
- **(c)** Not the same distinction. Mode-correctness is "about a
  particular strategy for applying the rule" (§3.1), and the modes can
  be reversed ("works precisely as well if you did it backwards",
  §6.5), combined (§6.4) or solved from the term (§5.1.3, §10), while the
  term always answers for a mismatch. In the Pfenning recipe they
  coincide.
