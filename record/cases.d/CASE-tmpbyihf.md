# CASE-tmpbyihf: Perspective transplantation: two populations matched in behaviour exchange different combinations of learned interpretive rules, specialist parameters, training histories, institutional rules and shared memory, and are then set where the transferred parts disagree with their new surroundings

## The design

A4: "Construct two populations whose behavior is initially matched.
Transfer among them different combinations of learned interpretive rules,
specialist parameters, training histories, institutional rules, and
shared memory. Then expose the populations to situations in which the
transferred components disagree with their new environment."

## What is measured

Which transferred combination carries the source population's
characteristic responses into the new one, and for how long; which needs
the old environment to keep being enacted; and which is reabsorbed.

## What the outcomes would show, and what they would not

- **What is transmitted.** A4: the payoff is "a causal account of **what
  is transmitted when a perspective persists across carriers**". That is
  CLAIM-329's causal transmission, broken into its parts, and QUESTION-118
  asked as an experiment.
- **Interruptions apart.** A transfer that keeps a disposition and loses
  the conditions to enact it separates CLAIM-421's enactment interruption
  from its reproduction interruption.
- **Not identity.** The causal story it can give does not settle which
  population is the same as which.
- **Its standing evidence.** Jacobs and Campbell's laboratory microculture
  (QUESTION-118) shows a norm surviving the replacement of everyone who
  set it; this design asks which parts of what survives did the work.
