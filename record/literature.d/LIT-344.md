# LIT-344: Formal Concept Analysis: Mathematical Foundations

Bernhard Ganter, Rudolf Wille (1999), *Springer (book; transl. C. Franzke)* — DOI-10.1007/978-3-642-59830-2

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read*: no lawful full text was reachable (see the history
note). What follows restates the book's own preface and contents, which
Springer serves freely, labelled as such.

- **What it is, by its preface.** Formal Concept Analysis is "a field of
  applied mathematics based on the mathematization of concept and
  conceptual hierarchy", treated here as chiefly "a branch of applied
  lattice theory". A formal context is a cross table of objects and
  attributes; its formal concepts, ordered by hierarchy, form the concept
  lattice. Many-valued contexts are reduced to that basic type by
  conceptual scaling.
- **What it does not claim.** The preface says FCA "does not strive to
  explain conceptual thinking": using it on human conceptual thinking
  "would be an application of the mathematical method and a matter for the
  experts in the respective science, for example psychology". The word
  "formal" is meant to delimit the theory in exactly this way.
- **Contents.** Order-theoretic foundations (ordered sets, complete
  lattices, closure operators, Galois connections); concept lattices of
  contexts; determining all concepts, diagrams, attribute implications;
  parts and factors; decompositions and constructions of concept lattices;
  distributivity, modularity and dimension; morphisms, bonds and scale
  measures.
