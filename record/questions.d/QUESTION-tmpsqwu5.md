# QUESTION-tmpsqwu5: What record of an organization's or an animal's conduct shows that one ordering dominates the negotiation among its perspectives, which conflicts it wins, for how long, and whether it returns after interruption, and does dominance measured on conflicts actually won give the same verdicts as dominance read as a disposition?

## Why it is a question

TERM-tmp8gbrt makes valence a regime of dominance and reads dominance
through Frijda's marks of control precedence (THEORY-055). Every
consequence CLAIM-tmpz8lli draws, and every defeater it names, needs
dominance to be measurable: which ranking wins the pairs on which the
perspectives disagree, whether it keeps winning, and whether it returns
after a perturbation.

Three candidate measures are in reach.
- **Conflict record.** For an organization, its decisions on the pairs
  where its perspectives disagree, with the perspective each decision
  followed (for Wells Fargo, CASE-151, where the sales model's ranking
  repeatedly overrode risk and HR). For an animal, choices in conflict
  tasks.
- **Out-prediction.** A ranking dominates when it predicts the system's
  next moves better than its rivals, the evidential grade CLAIM-427 asks
  for.
- **The coupled meaning–influence model.** Past the instability
  threshold of CLAIM-330, one direction grows and mixes meaning with
  authority: an ordering coming to dominate, in the model's own terms.

Two measures from emotion research are near but not the thing. Scherer's
synchronization of response components (LIT-tmpfjacn, NOTE-tmpsx1uk) and
Lewis's predicted theta phase synchrony (LIT-tmpj284p, NOTE-tmpz7wab) are
correlates that dominance would produce. Neither is signed, neither says
which ranking is winning, and neither is given as a measure per episode.

The second half of the question is the eraser. Priority, persistence and
resumption are dispositional. If dominance is read from conflicts
actually won over an interval, a clamp that never acts cannot change it;
if it is read as a disposition, THEORY-252's argument applies to it.

**When to read it.** Kahneman, Wakker and Sarin (LIT-tmpd3ww1,
NOTE-tmpj69i0) separate decision utility from experienced utility and
show later choices following remembered utility, which neglects duration
and weights peaks and ends: "In many other situations the sovereign
masters that determine what people do are not pleasure and pain, but
memories of pleasure and pain" (p. 385). Dominance during an episode
must therefore be read from the conflicts won during it, not from later
choices about it, which measure a different ordering, the remembered one.

## What would count as an answer

A procedure that, applied to a record of conduct, returns which ranking
dominates and how strongly, with a case where it separates ambivalence
(no dominant ranking, or split dominance) from neutrality (dominance with
no movement), and a statement of whether it measures actual or
dispositional dominance.

## What would not answer it

A measure of how strongly a system pursues one goal, without the
conflicts it wins against the others.
