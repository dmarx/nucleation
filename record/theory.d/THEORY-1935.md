# THEORY-1935: Gaboriaud and colleagues find, in a preregistered mouse-tracking experiment, that intent, outcome and the agent's causal role each make wrongness and blame judgments harsher, but that the two judgment types differ little: only causality weighed more in blame, and by a very small amount

## Source

[LIT-2677](../literature.d/LIT-2677.md), read in [NOTE-2013](../notes.d/NOTE-2013.md) (Europe PMC full text, PMC12372680).

## What was actually shown

In 145 participants judging 64 scenarios, intent had a large effect, and harmful outcome and the agent's own causation had moderate effects, on both judgment types. Of the predicted differences between wrongness and blame, only one held: "the causality effect was stronger (+ 0.16) in the case of blame judgments (b = 0.98) compared to wrongness judgments (b = 0.82)". The authors: "this study does not reveal clear differential weight for intent and outcome inputs in blame versus wrongness judgments", and the distinction "must not be overestimated".

## What this does not say

- Anything about responsibility for patterns, for conditions others act under, or for prevention: none was manipulated.
- That the three-input model is confirmed: the authors call the support partial.
- Anything beyond student vignette judgments in a within-participant design.

## From the second reading (2026-11-17)

The second reading gives the main effects: intent d = 1.68, outcome
d = 0.53, causality d = 0.61; the causality difference between the
judgments is d = 0.05. It adds one limit:

- **How observers weigh a third party who structured the agent's choice.**
  Causality is the agent's against "another independent source", so the
  study does not test [CLAIM-726](../claims.d/CLAIM-726.md)'s audience move. No relation.
