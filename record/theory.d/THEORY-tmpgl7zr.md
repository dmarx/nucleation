# THEORY-tmpgl7zr: Anodal transcranial direct current stimulation over the left frontal lobe raises how much task-unrelated thought people report during a monotonous task, which its authors take as evidence that the frontal lobes, and executive control, play a causal part in mind-wandering

## Source

Axelrod, Rees, Lavidor and Bar (2015), LIT-tmpquab4, read in NOTE-tmp7gplv:
"stimulation of the frontal lobes [anode electrode at the left dorsolateral
prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), cathode electrode at the right supraorbital
area], but not of the occipital cortex or sham stimulation, increased the
propensity to mind-wander" (Abstract).

## What was actually shown

In a 40-minute SART with 24 thought probes rated 1–4, 20 minutes of 1 mA
anodal tDCS at F3 raised mean task-unrelated-thought ratings against sham
within subjects (2.39 against 2.05, P = 0.021, n = 14) and against sham and
occipital stimulation between subjects (2.93 against 2.12 and 2.17, P =
0.007, n = 31). Target detection did not fall. The outcome could have gone
the other way: the occipital control could have raised reports as much,
and in the second experiment it did not.

## What this does not say

- **That the frontal lobes generate mind-wandering.** The authors: "our
  findings do not imply that the frontal lobes are the only region of the
  brain causally involved", and the montage cannot resolve the DLPFC from
  medial frontal or frontopolar cortex.
- **That the stimulation removed or blocked anything.** The authors read
  it as adding executive capacity. Nothing measured the agent's
  meta-awareness, its sense of control, or when episodes began or ended.
  So it does not bear on whether blocking the agent's model of its own
  control starts mind-wandering (Metzinger's H1, THEORY-1300), though it
  was cited for that.
- **That the effect is robust.** It rests on two small samples; see the
  rival account THEORY-tmpseev1.
