# THEORY-tmpfugkf: On Millikan's biosemantics, a sign has content because a producer and a consumer were shaped by selection or learning to cooperate through it: its content is the affair it must map onto, by a rule, for the consumer to perform its functions in the way that explains that shaping, and it can be true or false only by reference to that history, which natural signs lack

## Source

Ruth Garrett Millikan (2009; pre-print 2008), "Biosemantics", *Oxford
Handbook of Philosophy of Mind*, pp. 394-406, LIT-tmpu3jv7, read in full
in NOTE-tmpqexrv from the author's pre-print. Page numbers are the
pre-print's.

## What was actually shown

The chapter states the theory and answers three objections. It argues
two structural points:

1. **Teleology is a shell** (pp. 2-4). What teleological theories share
   is "only a view about how falseness in representations is possible":
   a system selected to make representations sometimes makes them
   wrongly. A theory of what representing is must be added, and the
   selection requirement constrains it: producing representations must
   benefit the organism, so they must be used. Hence the consumer.
2. **Producer, consumer, rule** (pp. 5-6). The content of a descriptive
   icon is "a condition or state of affairs that must obtain if the
   consumer is to perform its tasks ... in the normal way", where the
   mapping rules are those "instantiated in the past when the consumer
   and producer or their ancestors have succeeded in performing their
   cooperative function(s)".
3. **Truth needs history** (p. 9). Picturing and carrying natural
   information are "not a matter determined by a history of selection",
   but "attributions of truth and falsity ... make sense only by
   reference to function, hence by reference to a history of
   selection", and "do not apply to natural signs".

Selection includes learning: producer and consumer "maintain their
current settings (selection through learning)" (p. 5).

## What it does not show

- The account of function is cited (Millikan 1984, 1993, 2002), not
  given.
- Artefacts are not discussed; whether a designed signal's content is
  fixed by its designer is not in this chapter.
- The objections the SEP survey reports (LIT-tmpppa43, NOTE-tmp60j0q):
  indeterminacy, Swampman and liberalism are answered there only in
  summary, and the survey reads her as consumer-based where she says she
  is not.
