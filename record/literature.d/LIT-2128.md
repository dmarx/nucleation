# LIT-2128: Quantifying quality in DNA self-assembly

Wagenbauer, K. F., Wachauf, C. H. & Dietz, H. (2014). Quantifying quality in DNA self-assembly. *Nature Communications* 5: 3691. doi:10.1038/ncomms4691.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1637](../notes.d/NOTE-1637.md).*

- **Routine probes merge it.** Under TEM averaging "no difference could
  be discerned between the pseudo-defective variants and the putative
  non-defective variant"; "All variants might be judged as high-quality
  assembly products".
- **A cheap probe splits it.** The label "does not affect the
  electrophoretic mobility", yet "the defect label did capture the
  differences in composition".
- **Complete is not complete.** Remaining defects "ranged from 44 to 263
  unpaired bases (in a background of 7,560 planned dsDNA bases per
  object)", quality "above 99% for the 42-helix bundle and 96.5% for the
  8-helix bundle".
- **Costlier is not finer everywhere.** Cryo-EM "might be able to resolve
  site-specific defects, but it will be blind to stochastically
  distributed defects".
- **The schedule, not the time.** Hours at 52 °C did not reach the 16 h
  ramp; a 1.5 h three-step protocol gave quality "comparable to that
  obtained from 16-h long annealing".

## Standing in the record

Registered at the owner's request to connect the realizability line to
DNA origami, mainly for its observing side: a worked chain of probes
whose partitions of the same samples differ, and are not nested by cost.
On the acting side, it shows that a temperature path, not a total time
or a single temperature, decides how completely a target is reached, and
that two routings of one shape have different optima. It adds to the
held DeLuca et al. reading ([THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)) that a correct shape under TEM
does not certify completeness. Filed from it: [THEORY-1443](../theory.d/THEORY-1443.md). No
instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
