# TERM-tmp8pnnr: responsibility, and which sense: causal (whose conduct contributed), attributability (whose action or process it was), culpability (who is to blame), remedial (who owes prevention, relief or repair) or constitutive (who established or maintained the conditions that produced it); a verdict in one sense does not settle another

## Definition

A110 §2 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): "I would now
distinguish at least five forms of responsibility. They answer different
questions and may attach to different parties."

| Sense | A110 §2's question | Needs blameworthiness? |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal** | "Whose conduct contributed to the outcome?" | No |
| **Attributability** | "Whose action, judgment, or institutional process was it?" | No |
| **Culpability** | "Who could appropriately have understood or avoided the wrong, and is blame warranted?" | Yes, by definition |
| **Remedial** | "Who owes prevention, relief, repair, or compensation?" | Not necessarily |
| **Constitutive** | "Who was responsible for establishing or maintaining the conditions that produced the outcome?" | Not necessarily |

The rule the term carries: a finding in one sense does not settle another.
A110 §2: "Those relationships should not be collapsed into a single
judgment about who is *to blame*." A110 §14 compresses three of them as
"Responsibility for the act", "Responsibility for the capacity governing
the act" and "Responsibility for correcting the consequences": "They may
attach to different people and institutions. And none can be inferred
solely from the others."

## The same senses earlier in the exchange

A used them from the start, never in one list:

- **A5 §5** (2026-11-15), "Four kinds of responsibility worth keeping
  separate": causal responsibility; "Responsibility for a particular
  wrong"; "**Responsibility for the overall pattern:** Did
  they create, sustain, or exploit the coercive relationship?"; and "**Responsibility
  for preventing further harm:** Who has the power and capacity to change
  the circumstances going forward?" The particular wrong is attributability
  with culpability; the overall pattern is constitutive; preventing further
  harm is remedial.
- **A9 §5**: "**Attribution:** Does the action reflect the person's own
  choices, intentions, or character?" against "**Accountability:** Given
  their capacities and circumstances, is it fair to condemn or sanction
  them for it?", after Watson. A9's "accountability" is this term's
  culpability.
- **A17 §9**: "A person can be required to explain their decisions and
  participate in correcting a failure without necessarily being morally
  culpable for everything that happened." That "accountability" is this
  term's remedial sense, with a duty to explain.

## "Accountability" is ambiguous, so the term does not use it

In Watson's "Two Faces of Responsibility" (1996) there are two faces,
attributability and accountability, and accountability is the face tied to
blame and the reactive attitudes. Shoemaker's "Attributability,
Answerability, and Accountability" (2011) adds answerability as a third.
A110 attributes the three-way split to "Gary Watson and David Shoemaker"
jointly; the third member is Shoemaker's. [THEORY-147](../theory.d/THEORY-147.md) (Wolf's 2013 lecture)
holds attributability and accountability as independent kinds, in the
Watsonian sense.

The owner's U47 uses "accountability" for what survives diminished blame,
which is this term's remedial sense. A110's introduction proposes
"**remedial and institutional accountability**" for it. The record says
"remedial responsibility", and reserves "culpability" for blame, so that
neither reading of "accountability" is assumed.

## Sources of the senses

- **Remedial**: A relays David Miller's remedial responsibility, with
  grounds in causal involvement, moral responsibility, relationships and
  capacity to remedy (Miller, "Distributing Responsibilities", 2001, not
  registered), and Iris Marion Young's forward-looking political
  responsibility for unjust structures ([LIT-2246](../literature.d/LIT-2246.md), *Responsibility for
  Justice*, Deferred). [THEORY-207](../theory.d/THEORY-207.md) (Floridi) allocates it without fault.
- **Culpability and attributability**: [THEORY-147](../theory.d/THEORY-147.md) and [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md).
- **Constitutive**: A's. "It distinguishes the person who acts through a
  defective mechanism from the actors who designed, authorized, installed,
  funded, maintained, or knowingly failed to correct that mechanism."

## What it is not

- **Not [TERM-100](TERM-100.md)'s provenance.** [TERM-100](TERM-100.md) says whose process formed or
  maintains a capacity, descriptively. Constitutive responsibility asks
  who is responsible for having done so, which needs a duty or a fault
  besides the provenance.
- **Not answerability in TERM-tmp4zjar's sense** (filed from the part
  before), which is owed to someone through a forum. Remedial
  responsibility can include a duty to explain, and need not.
- **Not [CLAIM-299](../claims.d/CLAIM-299.md)'s triad** (decision sufficiency, historical
  accountability, correctness), which sorts what a decision record must
  show, not who is responsible.
