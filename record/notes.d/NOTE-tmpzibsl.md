# NOTE-tmpzibsl: Graziano — Illusionism big and small

## Contribution

The paper presents illusionism as a framework rather than a single theory,
and shows that existing theories can be fitted into it. Its device is a
four-item chain from the visual neuroscience of report: Item 1, the thing
modelled; Item 2, an automatic model of it; Item 3, higher cognition; Item 4,
speech. Applied to the claim "I have a subjective experience", the question
becomes what Item 1 is. Six answers are given: a magical essence, nothing,
integrated information, the global workspace, attention, and the deep
selective processing that the last three share. The positive proposals are
the author's attention schema theory and a "deep illusionism" that absorbs
IIT and the workspace into it.

## Key insight

Whatever consciousness is, a person can believe and say they have it only
if their brain encodes information that they have it. That encoding is a
model, and models in the brain are simplifications. So what people report,
an intangible what-it-is-like essence, is a model's depiction and need not
be a property of what is depicted. A real process can be described as an
ethereal essence, as a paper map describes New York. Hence "caricature"
rather than "illusion" (p. 2).

## Assumptions

- **Every claim needs an encoding.** "if Bob believes he has conscious
  experience, if he says he has it, then whatever conscious experience is
  or is not, Bob must have a neural embedding that represents it" (p. 4).
- **All brain models are inaccurate.** "The brain's internal models are
  never fully accurate, nothing is exactly as the brain represents it"
  (abstract). This is offered as something "all neuroscientists should
  know" (p. 7).
- **Causal closure of report.** A feeling can explain a report only if it
  physically affects neurons that encode it (Arrow B, p. 4). The physics
  of the brain is taken to be complete without it.
- **Models track useful real things.** Evolution "shapes the brain to
  construct models that can monitor and predict real items in the world"
  (p. 6). This is the argument against hard illusionism.
- **The control rationale.** "any controller performs better if it has a
  model of the thing it controls" (p. 8). This motivates a model of the
  workspace, or of attention.

## Key results

There are no new data. By section:

- **From vision to speech (pp. 2–3, Figure 1).** An apple (Item 1) is
  encoded in a visual model (Item 2) that informs higher cognition (Item 3)
  and speech (Item 4). The visual model is automatic, and "necessarily a
  simplification": the apple has a reflectance spectrum, not a colour
  (p. 3).
- **From self-model to speech (p. 4, Figure 2).** Bob's "I have a
  subjective experience of it" needs an Item 2 that "encodes the presence
  of subjective experience". Graziano places it at the temporoparietal
  junction for reasons from his lab and says the location is "not
  important to the present argument".
- **Hypothesis 1, the magical mind, and Arrow B (pp. 4–6, Figures 3–4).**
  Arrow A is how neurons produce a feeling. Arrow B is how the feeling, once
  produced, "cause[s] a specific information embedding" (p. 4), which
  would need neurons that respond to "the emergent feeling itself". Without
  Arrow B, "the conscious feeling that emerges from the brain cannot be the
  consciousness that we say we have" (p. 5). Even with it, what Bob reports
  "is entirely dependent on the information embedding", and there is "no
  reason to suppose that the model is accurate". So "Even the traditional
  account, when held to a basic standard of logic, turns into an illusionist
  account" (p. 6). The blood-flow analogy: the brain has a blood pattern
  but no model of it, so no one reports it (p. 5).
- **Hypothesis 2, hard illusionism (pp. 6–7).** No Item 1: the model
  depicts nothing, perhaps learned culturally. Graziano finds it unlikely,
  because evolution would not build costly models of nothing and "the
  sheer scale and consistency of the bamboozlement seems improbable".
  Against it, "Subtle illusionism is necessarily true of all models in the
  brain and must be true of consciousness as well" (p. 7).
- **Hypothesis 3, illusionist IIT (p. 7).** Item 1 is the brain's level of
  integrated information, detected and modelled. Then "consciousness
  resembles integrated information because consciousness is a distorted
  representation of integrated information". The hard problem "is
  dissolved", and panpsychism goes, since integration without a model "would
  have no relationship to consciousness". The open problems are whether
  neurons can detect integration and what the model is for.
- **Hypothesis 4, illusionist workspace (pp. 7–8).** Item 1 is the global
  workspace, and the brain models its own workspace. Item 3 is also the
  workspace, so "the global workspace contains simplified information about
  itself". The model "depicts me as a magical essence inhabiting my body,
  capable of subjective, phenomenal experience and of a conscious will"
  (p. 8). People do not become conscious of the model: "the model contains
  information" that drives belief and report.
- **Hypothesis 5, the attention schema (pp. 8–10).** How to identify Item 1:
  by analogy with the body schema, find something that can affect neurons,
  resembles the reported consciousness in outline, and "should covary with
  Bob's reported consciousness almost all of the time" (p. 9). Attention
  is offered as meeting all three. It covaries with reports (inattentional
  blindness), though it can be dissociated from them in the laboratory,
  which the paper reports as "difficult, though possible" (p. 10, citing
  blindsight and attention without awareness). AST is called an
  "illusionized" higher-order theory: the model of attention is a
  higher-order representation, but automatic (p. 10).
- **Hypothesis 6, deep illusionism (pp. 11–12).** Attention, the workspace
  and integration are "different descriptions of the same underlying
  object—the deep, selective processing of information in the cerebral
  cortex" (p. 11). Treisman's feature integration is cited for the link
  between attention and integration. The theory also explains the field:
  each school reads off a different feature of the same "schematic,
  internal model" (p. 11). Where consciousness resides is a "poorly
  formulated" question: "One cannot point to a part of that framework and
  say, 'That's where the consciousness essence lives'" (pp. 11–12).
- **Why it matters (p. 12).** "there are now only two broad classes of
  explanation for consciousness: magic and illusionism". The framework
  "could have applied to artificial as well as biological consciousness".

## Concepts

- **Item 1–4**: the thing modelled, its automatic model, higher cognition,
  speech output.
- **Arrow A / Arrow B**: the production of a feeling by neurons; the
  feeling's effect on neurons that encodes it for report.
- **Magical mind theory**: any theory that takes reported phenomenal
  experience to exist as reported and supplies Arrow A without Arrow B.
  IIT and the workspace theory in their usual forms are counted as such.
- **Hard illusionism**: no Item 1; the model depicts nothing.
- **Subtle illusionism**: an Item 1 exists, and the model depicts it
  schematically and inaccurately.
- **Caricature**: Graziano's preferred word: a real subject distorted, not
  a fake.
- **Attention schema**: the brain's automatic model of its own attention,
  which depicts it as "an amorphous, mental essence, a mind vividly
  possessing something in the moment" (p. 10).
- **Deep illusionism**: Item 1 is the deep selective processing of
  information, of which attention, workspace and integration are aspects.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) (real binding, constructed owner).** The exchange cited the
  paper "to show that illusionism need not make consciousness wholly
  fictional", with an illusionist layer on a workspace or integration
  account "so that real organization is misdescribed by a simplified
  self-model". That matches Hypotheses 3, 4 and 6. Read against the claim:
  - *It supports* the structure, at the level of one brain. The workspace
    is real, and the model depicts it as "a magical essence inhabiting my
    body" (p. 8): a real organization, and an owner that is the model's
    content. The paper also says which system holds the representation
    (Item 2, read by Item 3), which [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) requires of an illusion.
  - *It goes further than the claim.* What it calls the misdescription is
    not only the single owner but the phenomenal character itself: "The
    subjective, phenomenal experience that Bob describes is a distorted
    construct" (p. 7). [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) explicitly leaves open whether experience
    is real, and Graziano's "small" illusionism closes it on the
    illusionist side. So the paper is a source for the stronger option
    [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) sets aside, not for [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) itself.
  - *It complicates* [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)'s test, a misdescription shown. Graziano's
    arm analogy (pp. 8–9) is a model that is schematic and mostly right.
    By that standard, a self-model of attention that presents an owner may
    be a caricature with true outlines, which is Drayson's point.
- **[THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md) (Frankish's strong illusionism).** The exchange read the
  paper as showing that illusionism need not make consciousness wholly
  fictional. That is right only if "consciousness" means the process
  modelled. Graziano's "small" or subtle illusionism is not Frankish's
  weak illusionism: it does not keep real phenomenal properties that are
  merely misdescribed. It keeps a real Item 1, deep selective processing,
  and denies that the reported "intangible essence" is a property of it.
  That is Frankish's strong illusionism (quasi-phenomenal properties
  misrepresented as phenomenal) with Item 1 identified. Graziano's "hard"
  illusionism, a model of nothing, is more extreme than anything [THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md)
  holds. So "big and small" divides illusionists by whether the model has
  a target, while Frankish's strong and weak divide them by whether
  phenomenal properties survive. The comparison is the record's; the paper
  does not draw it. It does supply what [THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md) says Frankish lacked, a
  mechanism for the representation, though as a hypothesis.
- **[THEORY-577](../theory.d/THEORY-577.md) (Chalmers's meta-problem challenge).** Arrow B is the same
  constraint, stated in 2013 and here without citing Chalmers: a theory
  that names a basis of consciousness must say how that basis produces the
  reports. Graziano draws the conclusion Chalmers resists, that the gap is
  fatal to "magical" theories. His i-IIT answers Chalmers's challenge to
  IIT by making integration the target of a model. Chalmers's reply, that
  a zero-Φ duplicate would report alike, does not touch i-IIT, which needs
  the model and not Φ.
- **[CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md) (experience constituted by interpretive
  organization).** The paper cuts against the claim's bridge. On deep
  illusionism, the organization (deep selective processing) is real, and
  what is called experience is its representation ([TERM-tmpe36gg](../terms.d/TERM-tmpe36gg.md) sense 2).
  No further fact of a phenomenal perspective (sense 3) needs
  constituting, and the question where it resides is "poorly formulated".
  This is the strong-illusionist objection [CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md) lists, now with
  a mechanism. It also restates the claim's open sufficiency condition as
  a question with an answer: an interpretation is reported as conscious
  when the attention schema models it.
- **[CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md) (most interpretation is unconscious).** AST supplies a
  candidate for the further organization the counter-claim asks for, and
  it is a represented, not organizational, condition. Processing is
  claimed as experienced when it is attended and modelled as attended. The
  paper itself reports the dissociations that test this: attention without
  awareness in blindsight (Kentridge et al. 1999), which it calls
  "difficult, though possible" to produce (p. 10). That fits the theory
  if awareness reports track the schema and not attention itself. But it
  makes the attention schema, not attention, the separating condition. It
  meets [CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md)'s defeater only if being modelled by the attention
  schema can be stated without appeal to report, which the paper does not
  do.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md).** Every criterion here is covariation with report, as
  the method requires: illusionism takes reports as the explanandum. It
  answers the question by changing it. There is no experience beyond
  access to explain.
- **[THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) (Metzinger's self-model).** Graziano's workspace model
  "depicts me as a magical essence", a self-model content the system cannot
  see through. The two agree on the self, and Graziano goes on to the
  phenomenal.
- **[THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) (unity).** "Everyone knows that consciousness is both rich
  and at the same time unified" (p. 11) is read as a feature of the model,
  the source of IIT's appeal. It gives no account of unity.
- **THEORY warranted.** One is filed, [THEORY-tmpncy2z](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpncy2z.md): deep illusionism,
  with Arrow B as its argument and attention as its proposed target.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice. The remarks on artificial
  consciousness are speculative and explicitly set aside (p. 12).

## Limitations

- An essay. The empirical support is cited, and only for attention and
  report covarying.
- The argument slides between two senses of illusion. "Nothing is
  exactly as the brain represents it" makes any model inaccurate, which is
  trivially true and says nothing about phenomenal properties. The
  conclusion, that the intangible essence is a distortion, needs the
  stronger claim that the depicted property is absent from the target.
  That is assumed, on causal-closure grounds.
- Arrow B assumes the feeling is distinct from, and must act on, the
  neural process. A view on which the feeling is the process, or is the
  model's activity, needs no extra receptor. The paper treats every
  realist theory as positing a further essence.
- Deep illusionism's unification of attention, workspace and integration
  is asserted from their overlap. Treisman's feature integration is cited
  for the link to integration, which is a different notion from IIT's.
- The tone is polemical ("a Trojan horse for the entry of overt
  spiritualism", p. 6), and the alternatives are named "magic".
- The temporoparietal location of the model is set aside as unimportant.

## Open questions

- What would separate subtle illusionism from realism about a schematic
  but true self-description? Graziano's arm schema is mostly right, so a
  mostly-right attention schema would make "illusion" a misnomer.
- Can being modelled by the attention schema be stated without report, so that
  blindsight-type dissociations test it rather than define it?
- Does the paper's claim that integration and attention are one process
  survive the formal difference between Treisman's integration and IIT's?
