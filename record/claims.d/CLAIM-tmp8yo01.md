# CLAIM-tmp8yo01: Communicative acts and their participants exist as relata but have no identity beyond what their relations differentiate: non-eliminative structural realism without primitive identity

## The claim

U54: "Individuated objects exist, but only in so far as they are
differentiated by their relata." A194 restated it: "**Objects exist, but they
do not possess a primitive identity independently of their structural
relationships.**" It placed the view "close to the non-eliminative OSR
developed by Esfeld and Lam" ([LIT-tmpme4lk](../literature.d/LIT-tmpme4lk.md)), on which objects and relations
are on the same ontological footing and objects are characterized only by the
relations they stand in. The record's survey reading records Esfeld's
moderate structural realism as "there are objects, but all their properties
are relational" ([LIT-045](../literature.d/LIT-045.md), [NOTE-038](../notes.d/NOTE-038.md)).

The owner wrote "objects" without restriction. This claim is filed for the
relata of this line: communicative acts, their tokens and their participants.
Nothing in the exchange argues the general version, and A203 §35 itself
scopes its ontological thesis to "Communicative acts and other structured
phenomena".

**The reading adopted.** "Relata" in U54 is read as relations: an object is
differentiated by the relations it stands in. The literal reading,
differentiated by the things it is related to, needs those things to be
individuated first. A194 §5 says as much of its own relational profile: "this
expression uses already individuated y's."

It uses [TERM-tmp7encb](../terms.d/TERM-tmp7encb.md)'s split of kind, position and token. The claim is about
tokens and positions. The kind is [CLAIM-tmpaedgb](CLAIM-tmpaedgb.md)'s.

## Two versions

- **Strict**, the owner's wording. A relatum exists as an individual only in
  so far as its relations differentiate it.
- **Weak**, the assistant's. Numerical plurality may outrun differentiation,
  but nothing intrinsic grounds the plurality. A194 §5: "**Two distinct
  objects can occupy structurally indistinguishable positions without
  becoming numerically identical.**" "Their numerical plurality is real;
  their distinction is not grounded in independent intrinsic identities."
  A198 §3: "**Relata are numerically real, while their qualitative identity
  and role are determined by relational structure.**" A203 §2: "It is safer
  to say that **qualitative identity and role are relationally grounded**,
  while numerical plurality need not correspond to a unique structural
  label."

The weak version has a held precedent, for mathematics only. Leitgeb and
Ladyman's edgeless two-node graph has two nodes that no relation discerns,
not even weakly, and they take the identity and difference of places as
primitive and internal to the structure ([LIT-154](../literature.d/LIT-154.md), read in [NOTE-133](../notes.d/NOTE-133.md)). They
explicitly withhold the move for physics.

A194 presented the weak version as the owner's own ("So your formulation can
consistently maintain … Numerical distinctness does not require an intrinsic
identifying label"). It is a different thesis. The owner has not chosen, and
the objections below bite the two differently.

## Why character theory does not carry it

A group character classifies a representation up to isomorphism. It does not
individuate the vectors of the representation, nor the isomorphic copies of V
inside V ⊕ V, which all have the character χ_V and differ only by how they are
embedded ([CLAIM-tmp3sn40](CLAIM-tmp3sn40.md)). A194 §6 says so: "A group character classifies the
*representation* up to equivalence. It does not generally individuate each
vector." So the owner's character analogy supports the kind claim
([CLAIM-tmpaedgb](CLAIM-tmpaedgb.md)) and not this one. This claim needs another ground, which A194
§5 supplies: positions in a structure, and orbits of its automorphism group.

The Yoneda lemma is no help either way. It fixes an object up to isomorphism
among objects the category already has, so it presupposes the relata
([THEORY-032](../theory.d/THEORY-032.md)). That suits the non-eliminative choice and gives no account of
what makes two isomorphic objects two.

## What it does not say

- That relations exist without relata. It is non-eliminative.
- That it is a theorem of anything. A194 §8: "our proposed interpretation,
  not a theorem of character theory".
- That it holds for physics, or for objects in general. A191 §2: "It does not
  require accepting OSR as the fundamental ontology of physics."
- That communicative cases contain relata no relation differentiates. Whether
  two act tokens can be relationally undifferentiated is open.

It faces two objections. Chakravartty's dilemma reaches it, and the weak
version concedes a non-relational ground of identity ([CLAIM-tmp4xhdn](CLAIM-tmp4xhdn.md)). And the
Newman objection presses on it if its relations are read extensionally
([CLAIM-tmpayhau](CLAIM-tmpayhau.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-10: Esfeld & Lam read

Esfeld & Lam ([LIT-tmpme4lk](../literature.d/LIT-tmpme4lk.md)) were read in full on 2026-10-10 ([NOTE-tmpmrrjk](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpmrrjk.md)).
The reading corrects two things this entry says about them.

- **Esfeld & Lam hold the weak version, not the strict one.** Relations
  provide identity conditions wherever intrinsic properties could. Where
  nothing discerns the relata, as with entangled quantum systems of one kind
  and points of a symmetric space-time, "one can simply accept a numerical
  distinction (diversity) … as primitive" (p. 33). So when A194 §5, A198 §3
  and A203 §2 moved from "only in so far as differentiated" to numerical
  plurality without differentiation, they moved *toward* the view A194 called
  "close", not away from it. The strict version is stronger than anything
  Esfeld & Lam assert. The summary's "placed it 'close to' Esfeld and Lam …
  then weakened it" should be read in that light.
- **"The weak version has a held precedent, for mathematics only" is no
  longer true.** Esfeld & Lam make the same move for physical objects (pp.
  33–34), and they credit it to Pooley (2005). They deny that primitive
  numerical diversity is a primitive thisness, "for it does not establish an
  identity in time—or any other sort of an identity" (p. 33). Leitgeb &
  Ladyman remain the mathematical precedent. Neither text extends the move
  to communicative acts or persons.

The claim's own content is unchanged. The owner still has to choose between
the two versions.
