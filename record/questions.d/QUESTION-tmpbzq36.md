# QUESTION-tmpbzq36: Can a party's ability to activate someone's genuine moral obligations give it control over the terms of that person's dependence, even without direct authority over their choices, so that the moral cost of withdrawing does the work that control over exit does elsewhere?

## Why it is a question

CLAIM-690 sorts a dependence by who controls the terms on which it is met:
exit, renegotiation, the conditions of supply. Its controls are external:
a contract, an outside option, an enforcer. A30 §4: "Suppose a hospital is
critically dependent on a particular nurse. That dependence might give the
nurse considerable bargaining power." "Or it might leave the nurse vulnerable to
exploitation if the employer knows the nurse will not abandon patients and
the nurse has no effective way to withdraw without violating their
commitments." Nothing external has changed between the two; what differs
is a commitment of hers that someone else can call on.

TERM-124 defines domination as a capacity to set or change the terms on
which a party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate. If the ability to
trigger an obligation is such a capacity, then a party with no authority
over the person can dominate them through their own conscience;
CLAIM-tmp76x3e says how, and CLAIM-tmpe5u7i says it can happen without
intent. If it is not, those are exploitation without domination, and the
record's two terms come apart there.

## What would count as an answer

- **Yes, with a condition.** An account of when control over the
  circumstances that trigger an obligation is control over terms in
  TERM-124's sense: A15 §7's condition (the party controls the trigger and
  avoids the cost) is a candidate.
- **No.** An argument that a capacity to set terms must act on the
  person's options and not on their reasons, so that a triggered obligation
  is a cost the person bears and not a term another sets.
- **A case** in which two people of equal indispensability differ only in a
  commitment to those served, and their leverage over their terms is
  compared.
