summary: >-
  Buckmaster, De Lellis, Székelyhidi & Vicol (2017; CPAM 2019). For every β < 1/3 and every smooth strictly positive e : [0, T] → R there is a weak solution of the 3D incompressible Euler equations on T³ × [0, T], in C^β in space and time, with ∫|v(x, t)|² dx = e(t) for every t. With e strictly decreasing these are dissipative (admissible in the sense of a non-increasing total energy), which Isett's solutions were not. An h-principle (Theorem 1.3) adds that any smooth strict subsolution is a weak-* limit of such solutions. The proof modifies Isett's: a gluing step whose Reynolds stress stays trace-free, then Mikado-flow perturbations placed in disjoint "squiggling" space-time regions that cover every time, so energy can be pumped in even where the stress vanishes.

# LIT-1300: Onsager's conjecture for admissible weak solutions

Buckmaster, T., De Lellis, C., Székelyhidi, L., Jr. & Vicol, V. (2019). Onsager's conjecture for admissible weak solutions. *Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics* 72(2): 229–274. doi:10.1002/cpa.21781. arXiv:1701.08678 (30 January 2017).

Read in full in [NOTE-1013](../notes.d/NOTE-1013.md), from the arXiv copy. The theorem, and what it
implies for the coarse-grained energy flux, is filed as [THEORY-776](../theory.d/THEORY-776.md).

## Why this is here

Named by the reading of Isett ([NOTE-974](../notes.d/NOTE-974.md), [THEORY-763](../theory.d/THEORY-763.md)) as the one-way version
of the negative half of Onsager's conjecture. Isett's solutions start from
rest and return to it, so the energy flux they carry across vanishing scales
runs in both directions. [QUESTION-219](../questions.d/QUESTION-219.md) needed to know whether a rough Euler
solution can carry a flux that runs only into the small scales, the
dissipative cascade A3 had in mind. This paper gives one, for the energy of
the whole box: any smooth decreasing energy profile is attained exactly.
