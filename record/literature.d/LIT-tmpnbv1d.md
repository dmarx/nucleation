# LIT-tmpnbv1d: Full-body illusions and minimal phenomenal selfhood

Blanke, O. & Metzinger, T. (2009). Full-body illusions and minimal phenomenal selfhood. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 13(1), 7–13. DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2008.10.003. PMID 19058991.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmplqqpe](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplqqpe.md).*

- **A target phenomenon.** Minimal phenomenal selfhood is "the
  experience of being a distinct, holistic entity capable of global
  self-control and attention, possessing a body and a location in space
  and time". The authors propose isolating it first, as "the simplest form
  of the target phenomenon".
- **Three dimensions.** Self-identification with the body as a whole,
  spatiotemporal self-location, and a weak first-person perspective, a
  purely geometrical point of projection. They are claimed necessary and
  sufficient for MPS, and the weak 1PP is part of MPS, while MPS is
  necessary for the strong (subjective) and cognitive (conceptual) 1PP.
- **Partial against global ownership.** The rubber-hand illusion and
  somatoparaphrenia change ownership of a part. Because those studies do
  not manipulate self-location, self-identification or the weak 1PP, the
  authors hold that they do not reach MPS.
  Body-part ownership and global self-identification are proposed to rest
  on different neurons: fingertip and hand fields against the large
  fields of area 5 and VIP, with the vestibular system.
- **Neurology and experiment vary MPS along its dimensions.** Autoscopic
  hallucination leaves all three dimensions normal; heautoscopy and
  out-of-body experience disturb all three, with lesions mostly at the
  temporo-parietal junction. Full-body illusions in virtual reality shift
  self-identification and self-location towards wherever the stroking is
  seen, and are abolished by asynchronous stroking.
- **Agency is not constitutive.** A "passive, multisensory and globalized
  experience of 'owning' a body is sufficient for minimal conscious
  selfhood". Agency belongs to the causal background, and MPS could in
  principle occur in "a disembodied, non-neural, artificial system
  possessing no effectors".

## Standing in the record

Filed and read on 2026-10-19 at the owner's request, to fill the gap
around the rubber-hand illusion and bodily self-consciousness. Until now
the record held body ownership only through Metzinger's "Self models"
([LIT-450](LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)), whose claim C6 cites this paper and Lenggenhager et
al. (2007) for the manipulation of body ownership by multisensory
conflict. This paper is where that claim is argued, and it narrows the
claim table's wording: what is "the simplest form" of selfhood is not
body ownership in general but *global* ownership, and the rubber hand,
which manipulates part ownership, does not reach it.

It is the empirical arm of the self-model theory the record holds as
[THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md) (from [LIT-568](LIT-568.md) and [LIT-450](LIT-450.md)): it ties transparency and opacity of
the body model to how strongly self-identification is expressed, and it
reads out-of-body experience as an error through misidentification. That
is misidentification of the body one identifies with, so it does not
contradict the immunity clause of [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md) (Gallagher, [LIT-453](LIT-453.md)), which
allows "I" as object to misidentify. Its
thesis that agency is enabling but not constitutive sits against
Gallagher's pairing of ownership and agency within the minimal self, and
agrees with Haggard's point ([LIT-1009](LIT-1009.md), [NOTE-827](../notes.d/NOTE-827.md)) that involuntary
movement brings ownership without agency. That thesis is the paper's
weakest: the evidence is from adults whose agency was intact, and
[NOTE-tmplqqpe](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplqqpe.md) says what it does and does not show.
It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice; its remark on
artificial systems is a conceptual possibility, not a practice.
