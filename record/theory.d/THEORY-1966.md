# THEORY-1966: The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure forbid papers and discovery requests presented for an improper purpose such as to harass, cause unnecessary delay or needlessly increase the cost of litigation, limit discovery to what is proportional to the needs of the case with the parties' resources among the factors, and back these limits with sanctions up to dismissal or default

Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rules 11, 26 and 37, [LIT-2748](../literature.d/LIT-2748.md).

## The rules

Rule 11(b)(1) and Rule 26(g) certify that a paper or request is not
presented "for any improper purpose, such as to harass, cause unnecessary
delay, or needlessly increase the cost of litigation". Rule 26(b)(1) limits
discovery to what is "proportional to the needs of the case", the parties'
resources one of six factors. Rule 37(b)(2)(A) allows sanctions up to
"rendering a default judgment against the disobedient party".

## What it does not show

- That the guard works: the texts say nothing of how often sanctions are
  sought or imposed.
- Any limit on delay or cost imposed by means that are themselves proper
  under the rules.
- Anything outside US federal civil procedure.
