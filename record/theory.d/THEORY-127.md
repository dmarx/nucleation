# THEORY-127: On the court agenda, and on any agenda in which every proposition reaches every other by conditional entailment and some minimally inconsistent triple stays consistent with two of its members negated, a group whose judgments are robustly rational under universal domain and respect unanimity cannot form its judgment on each proposition from its members' judgments on that proposition alone unless one member dictates; its judgments must supervene on the members' whole sets of judgments, and so can depart from what the members judge on the same proposition while being wholly fixed by them

## Source

- List & Pettit (2006), [LIT-705](../literature.d/LIT-705.md), read in full in [NOTE-550](../notes.d/NOTE-550.md), from
  the authors' revised version of 11 January 2006. Page numbers are the
  manuscript's. Read as the lawful substitute for Group Agency ([LIT-391](../literature.d/LIT-391.md)),
  which stays Deferred.
- List (2014), [LIT-700](../literature.d/LIT-700.md), read in full in [NOTE-554](../notes.d/NOTE-554.md).

## What was actually shown

**The setting.** A group's constitution is a function from its members'
judgments to the group's judgments on an agenda of propositions closed
under negation. List and Pettit, [LIT-705](../literature.d/LIT-705.md), take the court example: P, Q,
R and "R iff (P and Q)". The constitution has universal domain, accepting
every profile of complete, consistent and deductively closed individual
judgments. The group is *robustly rational* if its judgments are complete,
consistent and deductively closed for every such profile (p. 9). Because
the constitution is itself members' dispositions, the group's judgments
supervene on what the members judge and do, and nothing non-individual is
added (p. 6).

**The impossibility (pp. 9–12).**

- Proposition 1: robust group rationality is inconsistent with majoritarian
  supervenience, the group judging each proposition as the majority does.
  The paper sketches the proof with the discursive dilemma: three
  individuals, each consistent, whose majorities accept P, Q and the
  biconditional and reject R.
- Proposition 2: it is inconsistent with uniform proposition-wise
  supervenience, the same function, for every proposition, of the members'
  judgments on that proposition. Proved by Pauly and van Hees (2003); the
  proof is omitted.
- Proposition 3: it is inconsistent with proposition-wise supervenience at
  all, even if the function differs by proposition, provided it depends on
  more than one individual and respects unanimity. Proved by Dietrich and
  List (2005); the proof is omitted. "The group's judgment on a particular
  proposition cannot generally be a function of the group members'
  individual judgments on that proposition" (p. 12).

The results are stated for the court agenda and said to hold for wider
classes (n. 3): Proposition 1 for agendas with a minimal inconsistent subset
of three or more propositions, 2 for "minimally connected" and 3 for
"strongly connected" agendas.

**The possibility (pp. 13–17).** Under the premise-based procedure, majority
votes on the premises and the conclusion derived by the logical constraint,
the group is robustly rational and its whole set of judgments is a function
of the members' whole sets: *set-wise supervenience* (Proposition 4, whose
proof is given). Members' judgments on the conclusion are then insufficient
to fix the group's ("weak discontinuity") and not even needed ("strong
discontinuity") (p. 14). Under the distributed premise-based procedure each
premise is settled by its own subgroup, so each member need contribute "a
single judgment on a single proposition" and no member's judgment on the
conclusion enters at all (Proposition 5, pp. 16–17, with details in List
2005). The group's judgment is still wholly fixed by its members'. So a
group agent is "nothing mysterious" and yet "may hold judgments that are
not directly continuous with its members' corresponding individual
judgments" (abstract).

**The extension to kinds of attitude.** List, [LIT-700](../literature.d/LIT-700.md), divides
collective attitudes by their supervenience base. Aggregate attitudes,
summaries such as a poll, supervene proposition-wise and are rational only
contingently. Common attitudes, held by all with common awareness,
supervene on an enriched proposition-wise base and are consistent if the
members are. Corporate attitudes, a group agent's, satisfy *holistic
supervenience*: "the collective (here corporate) attitude on any
proposition p supervenes on the individual attitudes across a web of
interconnected propositions and/or other non-attitudinal contributions by
the individuals" (§4.4). He cites the 2006 paper and the book for why:
"Except in special cases, no aggregation rule can robustly secure
consistent and complete corporate attitudes on a set of interconnected
propositions and also make the collective attitude on every proposition a
function of the individual attitudes on the same proposition". The kinds
are almost logically independent: a corporate attitude need be neither
any member's nor commonly known (§5).

## What this does not say

- **It does not hold without its conditions.** Proposition-wise rules
  survive if the domain is restricted, completeness or closure relaxed, a
  dictator or a constant rule used, or the agenda shrunk (n. 9). Real groups
  with shared priors may restrict their own domain. The account is a claim
  about robustness across every rational profile, not about what a given
  group does on a given day.
- **It does not say a group's judgments are anything over and above its
  members'.** Supervenience is kept throughout. What fails is
  proposition-by-proposition dependence, not dependence. List calls the
  result "supervenience without reducibility" by analogy with
  non-reductive physicalism; what more than holistic supervenience
  "not reducible" amounts to, the paper does not say ([NOTE-554](../notes.d/NOTE-554.md),
  Limitations).
- **It does not reach attitudes other than categorical judgments by
  proof.** The theorems concern binary judgments; List and Pettit say little
  generality is lost for plans (pp. 4–5), and List names "aggregate fears
  and hopes, common fears and hopes, and corporate fears and hopes" (§1),
  but neither paper proves the impossibility for degrees of belief, desires
  or emotions. That is why the claim is stated for judgments.
- **It does not say group agency can arise without members who sustain it.**
  List and Pettit doubt it can: their §6 argues that no constitution could
  guarantee robust rationality whatever members' reasons, without central
  direction and deference, and concludes that "at this margin of
  excitement, alas, the intelligibility runs out" (p. 21). That is an
  argument from the absence of a theory, offered as such, and it is not
  part of this account.
- **It does not say the non-aggregativity is emergence.** One reading the
  record has offered is that the failure of proposition-wise supervenience
  is non-aggregativity in Wimsatt's sense, and so emergence compatible with
  reduction: List and Pettit's homogeneity condition, invariance of the
  group's judgments under permuting the members (p. 15), is an
  invariance-under-rearrangement condition, and proposition-wise
  supervenience is close to invariance under decomposing the agenda and
  reaggregating. That reading is the filing agent's conjecture, not either
  author's. Wimsatt's paper ([LIT-707](../literature.d/LIT-707.md)) is unread, his conditions are
  known here only from his later summary, and whether they apply to a
  mapping from judgment profiles to judgments at all is unchecked. It is
  kept out of the claim.

## Connections

- **The authors' replies ([LIT-730](../literature.d/LIT-730.md), [NOTE-582](../notes.d/NOTE-582.md); [THEORY-536](THEORY-536.md)).** List and
  Pettit say that weakening universal domain or completeness complicates
  the formal arguments, but that rules meeting proposition-wise
  supervenience stay "extremely limited" and exclude "some of the most
  natural methods of aggregation" (p. 4). That supports this account beyond
  the strict conditions its title states, on the authors' word and not by
  proof, so this account does not lean on it. The replies also confirm that the
  premise-based procedure is one illustration, not a recommendation.
  [THEORY-536](THEORY-536.md), their account of group attitudes as real and
  non-redundant, is compatible with this supervenience.
- **Genuinely collective emotion.** Huebner's test for a group's own
  emotion, a state "that could not be exhaustively characterized in terms
  of intentional states of individuals in aggregation", is the informal
  counterpart of the failure of proposition-wise supervenience, and his
  navigation crew, routines each sensitive to one constraint and integrated
  on a chart, has the shape of the distributed premise-based procedure.
  [THEORY-125](THEORY-125.md) states his account. No relation is declared: this is a
  theorem about rational judgments, and it is not shown to carry over to
  emotions, whose coherence conditions are not deductive closure.
- **[THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)** (minds of minded agents) finds the readings agree that
  agency survives composition in a thin, functionalist sense, with List
  ([LIT-401](../literature.d/LIT-401.md)) granting group agency. This account supplies, for judgment, a
  principled structural condition on that agency: a collective of minded
  members holds robustly rational judgments only if its organisation routes
  them holistically. It says nothing about experience, which is where that
  account's dispute lies, so it does not meet its `promote_when`.
- **Korsgaard ([THEORY-065](THEORY-065.md))** grants collective agents agency in the same
  sense as individuals, because a group can achieve the unity of agency by
  conforming to its constitutive norms. This account says what such
  conformity costs in judgment: the group must sometimes judge against the
  majority of its members on a proposition to stay consistent across its
  agenda. The two are compatible.
- **Social choice.** Sen's impossibility, [THEORY-027](THEORY-027.md), is the nearest result
  in the record: a different aggregation problem, over preferences and
  rights rather than logically connected judgments, with the same form of
  conclusion that conditions each reasonable alone cannot all hold.

## Note of 2026-10-19: the court-agenda results filed apart

On the court agenda, majority voting fails robust rationality (Proposition
1), the premise-based procedure secures it with set-wise supervenience
(Proposition 4), and the members' judgments on the conclusion neither fix
the group's nor need to enter (weak discontinuity, Proposition 5). These are
now [THEORY-725](THEORY-725.md), Active, with the proofs written out. What keeps this
THEORY Proposed is the general claim its title states: that no
proposition-wise rule, uniform or not, can do what the premise-based
procedure does (Propositions 2–3). The source cites that claim without
proving it.

## Note of 2026-10-19: Proposition 3 re-derived, and the title narrowed

The record now proves Proposition 3, which [LIT-705](../literature.d/LIT-705.md) cites from Dietrich
and List (2005) without proof. The full steps are in
record/promotion-audit/decisions/THEORY-127.yaml (batch 023, [ADR-047](../decisions.d/ADR-047.md) v2).
In outline, with independence, unanimity, universal domain and robust
rationality:

1. For each literal a, let W_a be the family of coalitions C such that
   the group accepts a whenever exactly C does. By independence this is
   well defined. C ∈ W_a exactly when N∖C ∉ W_¬a.
2. If a minimally inconsistent set contains a and ¬b, then
   W_a ⊆ W_b. Let C hold a together with the rest of the set, let
   everyone else hold ¬b together with the rest, and apply unanimity and
   consistency.
3. The court agenda {P, Q, R, R↔(P∧Q)} has six minimally inconsistent
   sets: {¬P, R, B}, {¬Q, R, B}, {¬P, ¬R, ¬B}, {¬Q, ¬R, ¬B},
   {P, Q, ¬R, B} and {P, Q, R, ¬B}. Through them every literal reaches
   R and R reaches every literal, so all eight W_a are one family W.
4. The triple {¬P, R, B} stays consistent with two members negated,
   {P, ¬R, B}. A profile built on it shows that W is closed under
   intersection.
5. W is then an ultrafilter on a finite set, so it is generated by one
   member, who dictates.

The uniform case, Proposition 2, follows. A uniform rule must be
self-dual. It must also respect unanimity, because the unanimous profile
{P, Q, R, B} would otherwise yield the inconsistent {¬P, ¬Q, ¬R, ¬B}.

The title used to say "interconnected propositions" without conditions,
and that is false. On {p, q, p↔q} the judgment sets form an affine
subspace over GF(2). There the issue-wise parity rule x₁ ⊕ x₂ ⊕ x₃ is
independent, unanimous and robustly rational, and it depends on three
members. So the title now names the court agenda and the two conditions
the proof uses:
- every literal reaches every other by conditional entailment;
- some minimally inconsistent triple stays consistent with two of its
  members negated.

Whether these coincide with [LIT-705](../literature.d/LIT-705.md) n. 3's "strongly connected" agendas
is not claimed. The extension to corporate attitudes beyond judgment,
which [LIT-700](../literature.d/LIT-700.md) proposes, is [QUESTION-152](../questions.d/QUESTION-152.md).
