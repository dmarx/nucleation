# CASE-288: Three hospitals with the same output, budget, staff competences and commitments, differing only in how extra work is obtained: negotiated cooperation, a competition for scarce advancement that all entrants pay for, and an appeal to professional duty; the second and third both make withholding labour individually costly while the institution takes the benefit, by incentive in one and by conscience in the other

## The case

A53 §7 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b).
Three hospitals produce the same output on the same nominal compensation
budget. "The employees possess identical competencies, preferences, and
professional commitments. What differs is the mechanism governing
discretionary labor." All three give equally good care over the period
observed.

- **Organization A: Negotiated cooperation**. "Workers and management
  jointly establish terms for emergency coverage. The institution
  maintains staffing reserves, compensates additional work, and provides
  genuine opportunities to refuse or renegotiate."
- **Organization B: Competitive sacrifice**. "Workers compete for scarce
  career opportunities through displays of exceptional availability.
  Management benefits from the resulting labor and retains unilateral
  authority over future expectations."
- **Organization C: Moralized obligation**. "Management avoids explicit
  competition but appeals to professional duty whenever additional work
  is required. Workers experience refusal as a moral failure because
  vulnerable people depend on their continued service."

Stipulated: A is constructively organized, B and C exploit, in different
ways.

**Arm B's mechanism** (A53 §3B): an all-pay contest, in which every
entrant pays its effort and one wins. A's illustration: three employees
spend 8, 7 and 6 units of extra effort for one promotion and one wins, 21
units in all (8 + 7 + 6 = 21, checked). A: "Hypothetical costs, not an
equilibrium prediction." The employer may take the benefit of the losers'
work too. "**the allocation of the prize and the allocation of the costs
of competing for it are distinct normative questions**."

## What it can show

**What B and C share**, A53 §7:

> **Both arrangements can make it individually costly to withhold labor while permitting the institution to benefit from the resulting willingness to provide it.**

"But the mechanisms differ. B operates through strategic incentives. C
operates through normative commitments." And so "the corrective
intervention may be different in each case": compensation for all
productive effort, or fair terms of contest, for B; staffing reserves and
a right to refuse that does not fall on patients, for C.

- **For [CLAIM-713](../claims.d/CLAIM-713.md)** (F2, the moral subsidy loop): arm C is that
  claim's mechanism, conscientious workers absorbing what the arrangement
  leaves unmet. Arm B shows the same extraction with no appeal to
  conscience, so the loop is one of at least two routes.
- **For [CLAIM-703](../claims.d/CLAIM-703.md)** (F2, normative manipulation): arm C changes the
  world so that workers' sound values do the institution's work.
- **For [QUESTION-463](../questions.d/QUESTION-463.md) and [QUESTION-468](../questions.d/QUESTION-468.md)**: a stipulated triple in which
  output, pay budget and the workers' own dispositions are held fixed,
  so that what makes B and C exploitative ([TERM-123](../terms.d/TERM-123.md), relative to a
  declared standard) has to lie in the mechanism and in who holds
  authority over expectations (B: "retains unilateral authority over
  future expectations"). That is [CLAIM-728](../claims.d/CLAIM-728.md)'s residual control in a
  workplace.

## What it cannot show

- **That the verdicts are right.** They are stipulated, to find what
  would make them true; the case cannot establish them.
- **That competition exploits.** A53 §3B: "The mere presence of
  competition does not establish exploitation." The arm needs the losers'
  costs taken without return and the employer's unilateral control of the
  rules.
- **That all-pay models fit promotions.** A gives the auction literature
  without a citation the record can identify; the all-pay framing is an
  analogy until a model is specified.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Keller read: arm C's mechanism in a published case
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1917 — new; the reading filed on 2026-11-18 -->

Keller 2016, read ([NOTE-2005](../notes.d/NOTE-2005.md), [THEORY-1917](../theory.d/THEORY-1917.md)). Arm C, "Moralized obligation", has the mechanism of Keller's third-party moral blackmail. His law firm: "I can increase your workload without having to increase your pay and without losing clients for the firm" (p. 24), relying on lawyers' obligations to their clients. His strike asymmetry is the arm's individually costly withholding: "It really is more difficult to go on strike if you are a nurse or a teacher than if you are a policy analyst or a software developer" (p. 25). Keller also gives the case's stipulated verdict on C a published argument: the institution's act is a wrong ("moral blackmail") and the obligation it exploits is genuine.

Two differences. Keller's cases are deliberate; the case's arm C "appeals to professional duty" without saying whether management intends the effect. And he does not compare C with a competitive arm like B.

Proposed, for the owner: CASE-288 `draws_on` [THEORY-1917](../theory.d/THEORY-1917.md), as a check its arm C is set against ([ADR-095](../decisions.d/ADR-095.md)). The case is the filer's to change only with the owner's or lead's agreement, so it is not written here.
