# ADR-018: moral-psychology joins the topics

## Context

The owner asked how the record covers morality-as-cooperation. It held
the theory's main empirical test, Curry et al. 2019 ([LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)), read with
its commentaries, and Fitouchi, André & Baumard's "Moral disciplining"
([LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md)), which answers the commentaries' sharpest objection without the
record saying so. Almost none of the theory's own programme, its rivals
(Moral Foundations Theory, dyadic morality, relationship regulation) or its
evolutionary sources was filed.

Curry et al. carried `ethics` and `social-science`. But `ethics` is "moral
philosophy — what is owed, permitted or good". Morality-as-cooperation is
a descriptive theory of what morality is and where it came from, and says
nothing about what is owed. "Moral disciplining" carried no moral word at
all, and Tomasello's "The moral psychology of obligation" ([LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md)) carried
`ethics` for a paper about how children come to feel obligated.

## Decision

Add `moral-psychology` to `topics`, in the `philosophy` group, after
`social-ontology`. Its blurb: "morality as a natural phenomenon — moral
judgement, emotion and norms, and their evolution, development and
variation across cultures; descriptive, where ethics is normative".

It sits in the `philosophy` group because moral psychology is argued by
philosophers and psychologists in the same literature; the group is a
label over words, not a claim about who writes the papers ([ADR-003](ADR-003.md)). A work
that also argues what is owed keeps `ethics` beside it.

**Retagged.** No tag was removed.

- **First tag:** [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) (Curry et al., morality-as-cooperation in 60
  societies), [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md) (Fitouchi, André & Baumard, moral disciplining) and
  [LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md) (Tomasello, the moral psychology of obligation).
- **Second tag:** [LIT-493](../literature.d/LIT-493.md) (Bicchieri, *The Grammar of Society*), whose
  social norms are a subject of both this word and `social-ontology`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Widen `ethics`.** The record would then have one word for "what is
  owed" and "why people believe things are owed". Morality-as-cooperation
  and Moral Foundations Theory are disputes about the second, and a reader
  of moral philosophy would meet them as if they were arguments about the
  first.
- **Leave it to `social-science`.** That holds the empirical method, not
  the subject. Tomasello's developmental account and the evolutionary
  game theory behind morality-as-cooperation would share a page with
  housing markets and inequality.
- **A narrower word, `evolution-of-cooperation`.** It names the lineage of
  one theory. Moral Foundations Theory and dyadic morality are its rivals
  precisely because they deny that morality is all about cooperation, so a
  word that presupposes it would beg the question the batch is filed to
  ask.

## Consequences

The filings in the same contribution lead with the new word. The
cooperation sources (Hamilton, Trivers, Axelrod, Nowak) take it only where
they are read as accounts of morality; read as biology, they belong to
`natural-sciences` and `complex-systems`.
