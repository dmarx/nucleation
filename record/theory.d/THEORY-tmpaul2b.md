# THEORY-tmpaul2b: Mazinani and O'Connor hold that, when meta-analytic risk markers for physical intimate partner violence are organized by Bartholomew and Cobb's dyadic model, the strongest and most consistent fall at the relationship-context level, and that a dyadic framing implies neither symmetric control nor shared responsibility

Mazinani and O'Connor (2026), [LIT-tmpxr3yx](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxr3yx.md).

## The account

Eight meta-analyses (2000–2024) supply risk markers for physical IPV,
mapped by the first author onto the model's levels. The relationship
context carries "the most consistent risk markers", "particularly prior
partner aggression, emotional abuse, and relationship dissatisfaction";
individual markers are more numerous and weaker. The authors bound their
terms: bidirectional violence "does not imply equivalence in motive,
coercive control, severity, consequences, or responsibility" (§2.3).

## What it does not show

- **The model's pathways.** "the present synthesis does not constitute
  empirical validation of the model’s integrated temporal or causal
  pathways."
- **Reproducibility.** A selective, unregistered review with one coder.
- **Who controls whom in a given couple**, or how often violence by both
  partners coincides with one partner's control.
