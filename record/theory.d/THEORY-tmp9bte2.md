# THEORY-tmp9bte2: Organizational categories are radially structured, so applying them to nonprototypical cases extends and changes them; this is one source of continuous change in routine organizational action

## Source

Tsoukas & Chia (2002), [LIT-tmpls0y0](../literature.d/LIT-tmpls0y0.md), pp. 573–575; read in [NOTE-tmpyz5d5](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyz5d5.md).

## What was actually shown

An argument from prototype theory. Categories have a stable core of
prototypical members and an unstable periphery (Rosch, Lakoff, Johnson).
Applying a category at the periphery is "an imaginative extension" that
extends "the radius of application of the concept, thus transforming
it" (p. 574). It is also a normative act. The illustrations:

- Hart's statute banning wheeled vehicles in parks;
- Tsoukas and Vladimirou's call-centre operators deciding what counts as
  good service;
- Feldman's move-in routine, extended to traffic, vendors and a football
  game.

The paper's second source of change, human reflexivity, works
independently of this one (p. 575).

## What this does not say

- **That every application changes a category.** Prototypical cases
  leave it stable, and modification can be "minimal" (p. 577).
- **That the extension is arbitrary.** "The indeterminacy of extension
  does not indicate arbitrariness" (p. 574).
- **That an extension, once made, becomes organizational.** That needs
  institutionalization, which may not happen (p. 580).
