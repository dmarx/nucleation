# ARG-tmp67frz: The NTSB's Tempe investigation, barred from assigning blame or liability, still traced the operator's distraction to the company and the state and directed its recommendations to them, which shows that upstream provenance can be carried without blame

## The inference

1. The NTSB investigated the Tempe crash and does not assign fault or
   blame; by regulation its investigations are not conducted "for the
   purpose of determining the rights, liabilities, or blame of any person
   or entity", and by statute its report cannot be used in a civil action
   for damages ([THEORY-tmpq12rp](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpq12rp.md), read in [NOTE-tmp45cjg](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp45cjg.md)).
2. Its report found the company's safety culture and the state's
   oversight contributing, and the culture contributing "specifically to
   the vehicle operator's extended distraction" ([CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md), [THEORY-1932](../theory.d/THEORY-1932.md)).
3. Its recommendations went to NHTSA, Arizona, the motor vehicle
   administrators' association and Uber ATG, none to the operator (p. 60,
   rechecked 2026-11-18).
4. So a trace of a failure upstream, to those who set the conditions of
   the operator's incapacity, and a direction of repair to them, occurred
   without blame being assigned.

## Critical questions

**Is the instance documented?** Yes. [CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md) is filed from the final
report, read ([NOTE-2034](../notes.d/NOTE-2034.md)), and the recommendations and the notice page were
rechecked against the NTSB's PDF on 2026-11-18. The statute and
regulations were read at the LII.

**Does it separate the very things the conclusion says come apart?**
Mostly. The trace upstream is plain. "Without blame" holds in the sense the
regulation gives: no assignment of fault, liability or blame to any
person or entity. It does not hold in the sense of neutral language: the
report calls the company's procedures "inadequate" and the operator's
distraction "a clear example of deliberate engagement in visual
distraction" (p. 43, as [NOTE-2034](../notes.d/NOTE-2034.md) records it). A reader may say that is
blame in all but name. The claim is stated for the institutional sense,
and says so.

## Verdict

Proposed. One documented instance shows the separation is possible, which
is all the claim asserts. Whether such investigations usually reach
upstream, and whether their findings are acted on without blame or
liability following, are the claim's defeaters and are not shown.
