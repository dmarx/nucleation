# CLAIM-tmpki5gp: A judgement–observation loop cut off from action loses one route for evidence it did not select, and the free-energy principle's reason for minimizing surprise, that a persisting system must keep its states in bounds, needs action on the world, though a loop can confirm itself with action available too

## The claim

The free-energy principle explains why surprise matters. A system that
persists must keep its sensory states within bounds, and Friston et al.
([LIT-tmp670rc](../literature.d/LIT-tmp670rc.md)) say the active sampling that does so is "mandated by ergodic
arguments based on the very existence of adaptive agents". The enactive
account says the same of norms. Self-production gives a system only the
norm "to go on or to die", and that distinction is available to an
observer, not to the system. A graded norm the system itself has needs
adaptivity, "to detect tendencies toward the boundary of its viability and
to act so as to reverse them" ([THEORY-tmprt7jm](../theory.d/THEORY-tmprt7jm.md)). Ashby's ultrastability
needs a channel from the essential variables, which are coupled to the
world ([THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)).

Take action out of a loop of judgement and observation, and the loop loses
the route by which evidence arrives that it did not ask for. That leaves it
more exposed to the failure the record holds as protective lock-in
([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)): a regime kept stable by behaviour that blocks its own
disconfirmation.

Kübler and Birbaumer ([LIT-tmpxcw33](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxcw33.md)) give the empirical form. In the
completely locked-in state no patient of theirs regained yes/no
communication, and they proposed that "the complete lack of motor control
and feedback ... might be responsible for the cessation of voluntary
cognitive activity". The judgement loop, on this view, runs down when
action is gone ([CASE-tmpxgcq2](../cases.d/CASE-tmpxgcq2.md)).

## Conceded

As first filed, this counter said that without action the loop has a
self-confirming minimum. That is true, but it is not specific to the
absence of action. In the active-inference model of [THEORY-285](../theory.d/THEORY-285.md) the agent
can act, and its own sampling policy "withholds the only observations that
could lower that precision" ([NOTE-766](../notes.d/NOTE-766.md)). A loop that selects its own
evidence can confirm itself with or without action ([CLAIM-tmpt568z](CLAIM-tmpt568z.md)). What
survives is the narrower claim: action is one source of evidence the loop
did not select, and the free-energy principle and the enactive account
both make it the source that grounds the system's norms.

## What it does not say

- It does not show that the world can reach the loop only through action.
  [CLAIM-tmpt568z](CLAIM-tmpt568z.md) names other sources: a source the agent does not select,
  and scoring fixed independently of the interpretation.
- The extinction hypothesis is a hypothesis. Chaudhary et al. report a CLIS
  patient who communicated ([LIT-tmpzuemc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzuemc.md)), with cautions the case states.
