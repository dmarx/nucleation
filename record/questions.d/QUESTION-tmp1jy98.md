# QUESTION-tmp1jy98: Have the DNA-origami folding-pathway and thermodynamic results that the record holds from single groups been reproduced independently: the order in which staple domains bind and the compacted intermediate, where folding nucleates under changed staple concentrations, the design aggregates that order defectivity, the asymmetric hysteresis and the nucleation barrier of domain-level models, and single-fold stability and blocking by excess staple?

## Why it is a question

Each of these readings is Active as a report of one group's study or
model, read in full, with its hedges kept. None has been reproduced by
another group in anything the record holds. A reproduction, or a failure
to reproduce, of any one of them answers the question for that reading;
the conditions they carried, word for word, say what would count.

## The conditions it holds

### THEORY-1441

> An independent measurement, on this brick or another multilayer DNA
> origami folded at constant temperature, finds (a) a domain binding
> order that correlates between two designs sharing connectivity but not
> sequence and correlates weakly with hybridization free energy, (b)
> that the two termini of a staple bind independently, and (c) that
> removing the earliest-binding domains abolishes an early compacted
> intermediate and delays the folded state without preventing it.

### THEORY-1442

> An independent single-molecule or bulk study of a 2D DNA origami
> reproduces the move of the nucleation site by changing staple
> concentrations alone, with sequences and ramp held fixed, and finds
> the site where a melting-temperature account of the staples places it.

### THEORY-1446

> An independent study of several designs of one DNA origami shape,
> under its own anneal conditions and quality measure, finds defectivity
> or yield ordered by the skew of the first-fold loop-entropy penalty
> distribution (or an equivalent fold-distance metric) together with
> melt temperature, and finds a design that adds short folds at the
> expense of medium ones performing worse.

### THEORY-1447

> An independent experiment or model on a DNA origami shows (a) that
> raising staple-staple cooperativity, by stacking or by loop closure,
> widens the gap between melting and annealing temperatures, and (b)
> that increasing the cooling rate or lowering staple concentration
> shifts the annealing temperature by several times more than it shifts
> the melting temperature.

### THEORY-1453

> An experiment shows the predicted design dependence: origami that
> differ only in staples per crossover column or in breakpoint stacking
> strength (for example three-domain against two-domain maximum-crossover
> designs, or monodisperse against disparate staple energies)
> show a nucleation signature, such as a lag in isothermal folding just
> below the melting temperature or ramp hysteresis, only in the high-stacking
> design, and the signature disappears a few degrees below the
> melting temperature; or an independent model, such as oxDNA or a
> kinetic domain-level model, reproduces the same presence and absence
> of the barrier and its control by stacking rather than hybridization
> strength.

### THEORY-1454

> A reading of the paper's supplement confirms the affine correction and
> the fits as described, and an independent measurement of a single
> staple fold on a DNA origami scaffold, by another group or another
> readout (calorimetry on the full scaffold, or fluorescence followed on
> the anneal as well as the melt), reproduces both that the anchored
> fold melts higher than bimolecular binding of the same domain and that
> its melting temperature falls with fold distance, by several degrees
> from short to long folds.

### THEORY-1457

> An independent measurement of single-fold yield against staple excess,
> by a second lab or with a reporter that separates the blocked from the
> unbound scaffold, reproduces the turnover and the decline at high
> excess, and its independence of anneal rate and staple motif. The
> proposed whole-origami escape is not part of the claim and needs no
> test for promotion.
