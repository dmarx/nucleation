# NOTE-2181: Arslan et al. — Five-Year-Olds' Systematic Errors in Second-Order False Belief Tasks Are Due to First-Order Theory of Mind Strategy Selection: A Computational Modeling Study

## Contribution

A computational model of second-order false-belief errors, with an age
timeline from the literature.

## Key results

1. **Timeline.** "While children start to pass verbal first-order ToM
   tasks around the age of four, it takes them a further one to 3 years to
   pass second-order ToM tasks" (Introduction).
2. **Errors.** "Overall, 17% of the second-order false belief answers were
   correct and 83% of them were wrong."; "65% of the wrong answers were
   based on a first-order ToM strategy" (Results).
3. **Too hard.** On a game needing reasoning about the computer's belief
   about the child's decision: "However, the task was too hard for our
   sample." (footnote 8).

## Assumptions

Second-order competence is strategy selection plus experience; the sample
was selected by second-order failure.

## Concepts

First- and second-order theory of mind; strategy selection; ACT-R.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md) and the owner's question**, a qualification: an account of
  three-year-olds' equal splitting that needs "I expect you to expect" is
  ruled out by these ages.

## Limitations

The age figure is cited from earlier work; the sample was selected by
failure.
