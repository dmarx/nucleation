# NOTE-tmpyzsg9: Blanke and Metzinger — Full-body illusions and minimal phenomenal selfhood

## Contribution

It names and dissects a target that work on the bodily self had left in
pieces. That work studied body parts (the rubber hand, somatoparaphrenia)
and action (agency). This paper defines "minimal phenomenal selfhood" as
the global experience of being a bodily self, and breaks it into three
parts that can be told apart: self-identification, self-location and the
weak first-person perspective. It then shows that the clinical autoscopic
phenomena and the first two full-body illusion experiments can be sorted
by which part each displaces. It closes with a philosophical conclusion
drawn from the experiments: agency is not part of the minimal self.

## Key insight

Selfhood at its simplest is a place and an identification. The self is
located where a transparent whole-body model puts it, and it sees from that
model's centre of projection. Since each of these can be displaced on its
own, by lesions or by stroking seen on video, each is a separate dimension.
So the minimal self can be studied by displacing them one at a time,
without first solving agency, concepts or language.

## Assumptions

- **Metzinger's self-model theory.** MPS is the content of a conscious body
  model that is "phenomenally transparent because it cannot introspectively
  be recognized as a model" (p. 8). The paper cites *Being No One* ([LIT-467](../literature.d/LIT-467.md))
  and the précis ([LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md)) for this, and does not argue it here.
- **Phenomenal reports index MPS dimensions.** Patients' descriptions and
  questionnaire scores are read directly as states of self-location,
  self-identification and perspective.
- **Global properties need global representations.** MPS belongs to the
  whole system, and so does the body representation behind it. Cells in
  area 5 and VIP, with whole-body receptive fields, "are of importance for
  self-identification", while fingertip and hand cells serve partial
  ownership (p. 9). This is offered as argument, with no data of its own.
- **A strategic premise.** "An optimal strategy is likely to be the
  (initial) isolation of the simplest form of the target phenomenon"
  (p. 7).

## Key results

The paper reports no new data. Its results are conceptual distinctions and
readings of earlier data.

- **MPS and its parts (pp. 7–8).** MPS is "the experience of being a
  distinct, holistic entity capable of global self-control and attention,
  possessing a body and a location in space and time" (p. 7). Its parts
  are (i) "a globalized form of identification with the body as a whole",
  (ii) spatiotemporal self-location and (iii) the weak 1PP. The weak 1PP is
  part of MPS, so it is not a condition MPS must meet. MPS is necessary for
  the strong 1PP. The essence of MPS is that it signals "an integrated
  functional state, which for the first time makes the body available for
  attention and global control" (p. 8).
- **Box 1, the logic.** "We claim that spatiotemporal self-location,
  self-identification and weak 1PP are necessary and sufficient for MPS.
  If there are further necessary conditions, then our claim is false."
  A few lines later: "Weak 1PP, self location and self identification are
  jointly sufficient for MPS. Perhaps one or more of them are not
  individually necessary" (p. 8). The two statements conflict, and the box
  does not choose between them.
- **Part against whole (p. 9).** Somatoparaphrenia and the rubber hand
  "are only associated with changes in partial ownership and do not enable
  the investigation of MPS as the most fundamental sense of selfhood".
  MPS "is not altered in somatoparaphrenic patients and not manipulated in
  healthy subjects during the RHI".
- **Autoscopic phenomena (Box 2, Figs. 1–2, pp. 9–11).** In autoscopic
  hallucination all three parts are normal, and insight is kept. In
  heautoscopy all three are abnormal, may alternate between bodies,
  sometimes bilocate, and "being split in two selves" is reported, the
  only case of "a brief double instantiation of MPS" (p. 11). In
  out-of-body experience all three sit at the illusory body, with
  disembodiment, and the strong 1PP is also disturbed. The lesion overlap
  is right parieto-occipital or temporo-occipital for autoscopic
  hallucination (7 patients), right temporo-parietal for out-of-body
  experience (n = 12) and left temporo-parietal for heautoscopy. The
  heautoscopy count prints as "n= 1028" in the publisher's PDF, presumably
  10 with a reference marker; reference 28 there is Ehrsson's Brevia, not a
  lesion study.
- **The experiments, as described (pp. 11–12).** For Lenggenhager et al.,
  the paper says the "full-body illusion was abolished during asynchronous
  stroking", that both measures "were also observed for a fake body, but
  not (or less so) for a rectangular object", and (Fig. 3 caption)
  "self-identification only with the virtual body". *Checked against
  [NOTE-tmp9c2fc](NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md):* in Study II the own-body drift did not differ between
  synchronous and asynchronous stroking. The mannequin's
  self-identification score was 0.6 on a −3 to +3 scale. The box's drift,
  14.3 cm, was not clearly below the own body's 17.1 cm. Study I's
  asynchronous drift was 12.5 cm, half the synchronous drift, and not
  significant against zero. "Abolished" and "observed for a fake body" are
  stronger than the data. Both protocols produced "a state that is
  comparable to heautoscopy", and "neither of them managed to manipulate
  ... the strong 1PP" (p. 12). That matches the source.
- **Agency (pp. 12–13).** "The reviewed clinical and experimental data
  show how a passive, multisensory and globalized experience of 'owning' a
  body is sufficient for minimal conscious selfhood" (p. 13). Then: "If,
  as we propose, 'global owning' ... is the simplest form of
  self-consciousness, then it follows that MPS is constituted by something
  'less' than agency." The conclusion: "bodily agency is a causally
  enabling, but not a constitutive condition, for phenomenal selfhood".

## Concepts

- **MPS (minimal phenomenal selfhood)**: the conscious experience of being
  a bodily self, made of global self-identification, self-location and a
  weak 1PP.
- **Weak 1PP**: the geometrical origin of an egocentric, usually visual,
  model of reality, its centre of projection.
- **Strong 1PP**: the whole organism, given through MPS, represented as
  directed at an object. Metzinger's PMIR. It needs MPS.
- **Cognitive 1PP**: having the concept of a subject of experience and
  applying it to oneself.
- **Self-identification**: "the degree to which an organism identifies
  with the content of a global body representation" (glossary).
- **Self-location**: a volume in space, normally within the represented
  body, that contains the origin of the weak 1PP (the "embedding
  principle").
- **Global against partial ownership**: ownership of the body as a whole,
  against ownership of a hand, finger or hemibody.
- **Global control**: control of properties only the whole system has,
  such as the whole body's surface or its translation.
- **Causally enabling condition**: one that helps bring a phenomenon about
  without being necessary to it or part of what constitutes it. Box 1's
  example is striking a match.

## Connections

This is Metzinger's self-model theory ([LIT-467](../literature.d/LIT-467.md), [LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), [LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md)) given a
minimal target and an experimental handle. "Self models" ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md)),
written at the same time, states its last claim in brief: global body
ownership is "the most simple form of self-awareness" ([NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md), C6). Its
evidence is Lenggenhager et al. ([LIT-tmpeggnf](../literature.d/LIT-tmpeggnf.md)), Ehrsson's Brevia, Blanke's
clinical series, and Stratton's 1899 mirror experiment. It cites the
Science e-letters (Meyer; its own authors' reply) for the dissociation of
self-location from perspective. It does not cite Ehrsson and Petkova's
e-letter in the same thread, which disputes that Lenggenhager's protocol
induced ownership at all.

It sets itself against accounts that make agency necessary for the minimal
self (Pacherie; Roessler), and close to Gallagher's distinction between
ownership and agency ([LIT-453](../literature.d/LIT-453.md), [NOTE-377](NOTE-377.md)). Gallagher's minimal self has both
parts. This paper keeps ownership and drops agency.

Petkova and Ehrsson 2008 (filed in this batch) comes from the group whose
e-letter argued that ownership needs a first-person view, and swaps bodies
from one. Apps and Tsakiris's free-energy self (also in this batch) is, by
its title, a predictive-coding account. If so, it would supply what this
paper lacks, a mechanism for what it calls "visual capture". I have not
read either, so their readings should say how they bear.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-372](NOTE-372.md), C6.** The row reads "Body ownership can be manipulated by
  multisensory conflict and is the simplest form of self-awareness", with
  "asserted for 'simplest'". At source, three corrections:
  1. *Simplest* is a claim about **global** ownership only. The paper says
     body-part ownership, the rubber hand, "do[es] not enable the
     investigation of MPS". The row's two halves are about different
     things.
  2. *Simplest* is a **proposal**, "if, as we propose", made as a research
     strategy and true by how MPS is defined. "Asserted" understates its
     kind and overstates its warrant: it is a definition, not a finding.
  3. The manipulation the paper cites for global ownership is
     Lenggenhager et al., which it describes more strongly than the data
     ([NOTE-tmp9c2fc](NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md)).
- **Metzinger ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md), [LIT-568](../literature.d/LIT-568.md), [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)).** The paper is the place to
  cite for MPS and the weak/strong/cognitive 1PP split. The strong 1PP is
  [THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)'s PMIR. It adds no test of transparency. Its one reference to
  opacity ("if parts of the conscious body-model become phenomenally
  opaque ... self-identification ... can be weakened", p. 8) gives
  autoscopic insight as an example, with no data.
- **Agency and active inference ([LIT-1181](../literature.d/LIT-1181.md), [NOTE-960](NOTE-960.md)).** The conclusion that
  agency is not constitutive of the minimal self cuts against an
  active-inference reading on which the bodily self is the model that
  action fulfils. But the argument does not reach that far. Every subject
  in its evidence is an agent. The illusions show that MPS can be moved
  passively, not that a system without action could have it. The paper
  concedes that agency enables MPS "in practically all humans", and its own
  definition of MPS includes being "capable of global self-control". That
  reading is mine. On Friston et al.'s terms, passive stroking would be
  perception updating a model that action built.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) (machine consciousness).** Box 1 says what constitutes MPS
  "could also take place in a disembodied, non-neural, artificial system
  possessing no effectors". That is a claim about possibility, from
  definitions. It adds nothing to the question [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) leaves open.
- **[LIT-1186](../literature.d/LIT-1186.md) (attempted out-of-body experiences).** The paper says the
  experimental protocols induce heautoscopy-like states, not out-of-body
  experiences. Nothing here supports perception from outside the body.
- Filed as [THEORY-tmp6ib7r](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp6ib7r.md).

## Limitations

- An Opinion piece with no new data. Its empirical base is two
  experiments, one described more strongly than its data, and clinical
  series of small size.
- Box 1 states two different logical relations between the three parts
  and MPS.
- The definition of MPS includes being "capable of global self-control",
  while the conclusion makes global motor control unnecessary. The paper
  does not separate being represented as capable of control from
  exercising it, which would reconcile the two.
- "Self-identification" in the experiments is a questionnaire item, and
  "self-location" is a walking error. The paper treats both as direct
  readings of the phenomenal dimensions.
- The rival reading in the same e-letter thread (Ehrsson and Petkova:
  self-recognition on video, not ownership) is not mentioned.

## Open questions

- Is agency constitutive? What would test it is a case the paper does not
  have: MPS assessed in someone with no history of global motor control,
  or MPS that survives when ongoing efferent and reafferent signals are
  removed. More passive illusions in healthy adults cannot settle it.
- Are the three parts individually necessary? A dissociation would settle
  it: reported bodily selfhood intact with one part absent, or absent with
  all three intact. Box 2's autoscopic hallucination has all three normal
  and MPS normal, so it does not test this.
- Can an experiment reach the strong 1PP? The paper predicts that multiple
  perspectives and incongruent body positions might.
