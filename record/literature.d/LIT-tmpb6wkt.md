summary: >-
  A short review of the minimal group paradigm (MGP) after 45 years. Its defining features are a novel, arbitrary and anonymous categorization in which no personal self-interest is served by the allocations. Tajfel's first studies found that fairness guided allocations strongly, but in-group favouritism appeared reliably, and maximizing the in-group's lead over the out-group sometimes beat maximizing its gain. The effect replicated with revised matrices. Critics say it may come from reciprocity expectations or from projecting a positive self onto the new group. It is largely confined to positive resources. When participants can also pay themselves, they take the most for themselves and still favour the in-group with what is left. The paradigm is now used mostly to study other effects of mere categorization: implicit attitudes, face processing, trust, communication.
