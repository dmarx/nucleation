# THEORY-tmpuq37u: Fridman reports that, in a registry sample, women who survived the Holocaust as young children and lost both parents showed more dissociation, less life satisfaction and higher cortisol than matched women six decades later, while their daughters did not differ on any psychological, health or cortisol measure

## The account

Thirty-two Israeli women who survived the Holocaust as young children and
lost both parents, now about 77, reported more dissociation (7.13 against
5.03, p = .03) and less life satisfaction (p = .001) than 33 matched women
who left Europe with their parents before the war, scored lower on
cognition, and had higher diurnal cortisol (η² = .10). Their 79 daughters
did not differ from comparison daughters on any psychological, cognitive,
health or cortisol measure, nor in their response to a laboratory
stressor. Only an exploratory median split found lower cortisol in
daughters of more dissociative survivors (n = 17 and 10, one-tailed
p = .04).

The thesis reads the daughters' adjustment as the survivors' achievement:
"they were unable to integrate their past and present challenges, but they
nevertheless successfully managed to keep their children emotionally
unharmed" (p. 40). It restates its promotors' meta-analyses: "no evidence
for secondary traumatization in studies with non-select recruitment and
non-clinical samples" (32 samples, 4,418 children; p. 26), and, for 13
non-clinical third-generation samples (N = 1012), "Again, there was no
evidence for intergenerational transmission" (p. 67). Among the
explanations offered is that survivors "acquired a new sense of meaning,
goals, and pride in building the new state of Israel".

## Evidence

The thesis (NOTE-1291): four studies of one registry-drawn sample first
studied 11 years earlier. Self-report, one day of cortisol sampling, a
null for the daughters at power the authors call sufficient for medium
effects. Attrition was selective. The ADRA2B moderation is exploratory by
the authors' description. The meta-analytic results are reported at second
hand.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297, the parenting route: in a non-clinical sample, the trauma
marks the survivors and largely stops there. The comparison families
shared the survivors' national memory, so the design tests family routes
with the identity route held fixed. The thesis's appeal to meaning found
in building a state is one of the record's suggestions that a group's
story buffers as well as carries.
