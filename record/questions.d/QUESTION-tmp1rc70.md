# QUESTION-tmp1rc70: To what extent can an utterance's pragmatic identity be reconstructed from a globally consistent collection of context-relative judgements, and what fails when it cannot?

## Why it is a question

A24: "**To what extent can the pragmatic identities of utterances be
reconstructed from a globally consistent collection of context-relative
judgments—and what precisely fails when that reconstruction is impossible?**"
It replaced the question "Is linguistic meaning quantum?", and A24 called it "a
more mature descendant of our original observation about delivery frames".

It carries A21's narrower question: whether illocutionary force, footing,
solidarity, irony and condemnation "exhibit something stronger than ordinary
context sensitivity: **an empirically identifiable incompatibility
structure**".

## What would count as an answer

Two halves. The first is a measure of how well a rendering preserves the pattern
of context-relative judgements ([CLAIM-tmp0wmy3](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp0wmy3.md)); the manuscript's Propositions 1 and 2
(§6) say when transport preserves overlap consistency and a global extension.
The second is whether real pragmatic judgements are formally contextual at all
([CLAIM-tmp1ycte](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1ycte.md)), which needs the experiment [CASE-tmpn2q0k](../cases.d/CASE-tmpn2q0k.md) or its §10 successors. Neither half
has data.
