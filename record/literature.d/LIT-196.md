# LIT-196: Ontological Priority: The Conceptual Basis of Non-eliminative, Ontic Structural Realism

Anjan Chakravartty (2011), *in E. M. Landry & D. P. Rickles (eds.), Structural Realism: Structure, Object, and Causality, Western Ontario Series in Philosophy of Science 77, Springer (2012), ch. 10, pp. 187–206* — DOI-10.1007/978-94-007-2579-9_10

## Key takeaways

- Non-eliminative OSR's core thesis, that relations are ontologically prior to their relata because they determine the relata's identity, fails. Every workable theory of objects leaves relata with intrinsic features that fix identity, so OSR collapses back into eliminativism, and even quantum entanglement leaves particles with identity-fixing intrinsic properties (mass, charge).

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Chakravartty examines non-eliminative ontic structural realism, which keeps objects but gives relations ontological priority over them. He argues that this priority thesis is as problematic as the eliminative OSR it was meant to improve on. The result is a dilemma for anyone hoping OSR, in either form, will give realism a way forward. The argument is mainly conceptual, but he closes by showing that the physics cited for the view does not escape it.
