# CLAIM-tmpwpvbz: A process with no procedural friction can still be interruptible: standing constraints set slowly in advance and enforced by mechanisms faster than the process can foreclose alternatives keep it open to intervention without delaying it, so an opportunity to intervene does not consist in friction

## The claim

A33 §8 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c):
an AI agent processes transactions rapidly, but "the agent's permissions,
maximum transaction size, spending budget, and categories of authorized
action can be established beforehand". "The distinction is between
**slow review after action** and **slowly established rules that
constrain action before it happens**." So "procedural friction can be
selectively *compiled into enforceable operating constraints*", on a
condition: "the slower governing process must itself remain open to
legitimate revision."

A35 §7 restates it as two architectures, reactive oversight (fast action,
then consequences, then slow review) against prospectively constrained
execution, and draws the condition: "The slow authority must have fast
enough *actuators*, even if its deliberative processes remain slow."

## Why it objects

The owner's claim (CLAIM-tmpuiaop) has two readings. On "exposes", friction is where an
intervention opportunity becomes visible, and removing friction can remove
it. On "characterizes", an opportunity to intervene is a piece of
friction. The standing-constraint architecture is a process in which an
exception halts before crossing a consequential boundary and no step waits
on anyone, so it is interruptible without friction. If that is coherent,
the "characterizes" reading is false and the "exposes" reading is not
touched: friction is one place opportunities are exposed, and there are
others.

What carries the opportunity, on this account, is a relation of speeds: the
enforcing mechanism must act before the process forecloses the
alternatives, whatever the speed of the deliberation that set the limit.
That is TERM-085's deadline applied to the
actuator, not the deliberation, and
CLAIM-478's point that what settles slowly can act as a
constraint on what moves fast.

## What it does not say

- **That standing constraints suffice.** They interrupt only the
  transitions anticipated when they were set. A novel failure still needs
  a channel through which someone can stop the process, and that channel
  may need the friction the owner's claim describes. A35 §5 makes the same
  point about specification lag.
- **That the constraints are legitimate.** A35 §7: "The second design isn't
  automatically legitimate, of course. Its constraints may be poorly
  chosen, unjust, or ineffective."
