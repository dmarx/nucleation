# THEORY-908: Between tests, uncorrected for publication bias, implementation intentions in the contingent if-then format had larger effects than plans that schedule when, where and what without linking cue to response (d = .43 against .29); tests in which the plan was rehearsed had larger effects (.45 against .33, with no gain beyond one rehearsal), tests in which it was recorded had smaller ones (.31 against .44), and adding how or for how long to a time and place went with about half the effect (.24 against .46)

## Source

Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, [LIT-1488](../literature.d/LIT-1488.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1131](../notes.d/NOTE-1131.md): pp. 165–166, 173–175 and 179–180, with Tables 3 and 4.

## What was actually shown

- **Format (Table 3, p. 173).** Contingent plans d = .43, schedule plans
  .29, Q(1) = 20.81, p < .001. The authors conclude "that contingent or
  if-then planning leads to superior impact on outcomes" (p. 180).
- **Formation (Table 3).** Any rehearsal .45 against none .33, with no
  gain from more than one rehearsal; recording .31 against .44;
  visualisation no difference.
- **Recording (p. 180).** "when participants had the opportunity to
  revisit their plans (e.g., because they retained copies), those plans
  were less effective."
- **Elaboration (Table 4).** Time and place .46; time, place and how or
  for how long .24.

What could have come out otherwise: scheduling specifies the same when,
where and what, and could have done as well as the link; a written copy
could have helped as a reminder, as the authors thought possible (p. 165).

## What this does not say

- **That format causes the difference.** Contingent and schedule plans
  come from different studies, outcomes and settings; no within-study
  comparison is pooled.
- **Why recording hurts.** Deliberation undermining automaticity is the
  authors' reading; it was not measured.
- **That the moderator estimates are bias-corrected.** They are not.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-443](../claims.d/CLAIM-443.md).** The claim's mechanism is learned relations, not
  fixed behaviour. That the conditional link outperforms a schedule of the
  same behaviour is consistent with the link being what matters, though
  the link here runs from cue to action. That revisiting the plan weakens
  it is consistent with the plan's value being deliberation removed from
  the moment: the amortization side of the claim, shown here only as an
  outcome difference, not as computation saved.
- **[THEORY-913](THEORY-913.md)** holds the automaticity evidence the authors'
  reading leans on.
