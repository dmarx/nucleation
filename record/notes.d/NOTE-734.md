# NOTE-734: Grice — Logic and Conversation

## Contribution

The passage read states the Cooperative Principle and the four categories
of maxim that Grice's theory of conversational implicature rests on. The
theory itself, how an implicature is worked out from them, falls outside
the pages read.

## Skim

*pp. 45–47 of 41–58, read in full. Not enough to state the account of
implicature exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- **The principle (p. 45).** Talk exchanges are "characteristically, to
  some degree at least, cooperative efforts", with "a common purpose or
  set of purposes, or at least a mutually accepted direction". That
  direction "may be fixed from the start … or it may evolve during the
  exchange". "At each stage, SOME possible conversational moves would be
  excluded as conversationally unsuitable." Hence the Cooperative
  Principle, which participants "will be expected (ceteris paribus) to
  observe".
- **The categories (pp. 45–46).** "Echoing Kant, I call these categories
  Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner."
  - Quantity: "Make your contribution as informative as is required (for
    the current purposes of the exchange)" and "Do not make your
    contribution more informative than is required". The second is
    "disputable", and "its effect will be secured by a later maxim, which
    concerns relevance".
  - Quality: the supermaxim "Try to make your contribution one that is
    true", with "Do not say what you believe to be false" and "Do not say
    that for which you lack adequate evidence".
  - Relation: "Be relevant." Grice says its formulation "conceals a number
    of problems that exercise me a good deal" and hopes "to revert to them
    in a later work".
  - Manner relates "not … to what is said but, rather, to HOW what is
    said is to be said": "Be perspicuous", then obscurity, ambiguity,
    brevity and order. "And one might need others."
- **Ranking (p. 46–47).** Quality's first maxim may be a precondition of
  the others, but "so far as the generation of implicatures is concerned"
  it plays "a role not totally different from the other maxims".
- **Other maxims (p. 47).** "There are, of course, all sorts of other
  maxims (aesthetic, social, or moral in character), such as 'Be polite',
  that are also normally observed by participants in talk exchanges, and
  these may also generate nonconventional implicatures."
- **Too narrow (p. 47).** The maxims are stated "as if this purpose were a
  maximally effective exchange of information; this specification is, of
  course, too narrow, and the scheme needs to be generalized to allow for
  such general purposes as influencing or directing the actions of
  others".

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-221](../claims.d/CLAIM-221.md) and [QUESTION-073](../questions.d/QUESTION-073.md).** The pages read hold no account of
  illocution and nothing on teasing. One feature bears on the dispute.
  The Cooperative Principle is indexed to "the accepted purpose or
  direction of the talk exchange", a purpose the participants "recognize"
  in common, which "may evolve". That is a jointly held frame, not a
  speaker-internal state. Grice's implicature therefore depends on a
  relation among the parties, even though his speaker meaning (1957,
  [LIT-1042](../literature.d/LIT-1042.md)) is speaker-internal. Whether the full chapter keeps the
  two apart cannot be said from these pages.
- **"Be polite".** Grice allows social maxims to generate nonconventional
  implicatures, but excludes them from the conversational ones. A teasing
  or reproaching implicature may fall on that side of his line, where
  this chapter, as far as read, gives no account.
- **[THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md).** Rational-speech-act models formalise Quantity as
  informativeness. Grice's own doubt about the second Quantity maxim, and
  his remark that its effect may come from Relation, are the points where
  that formalisation could be compared with its source. The pages
  defining implicature would be needed to do so.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
