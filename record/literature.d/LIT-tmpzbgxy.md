# LIT-tmpzbgxy: Mapping the Two Faces of R&D: Productivity Growth in a Panel of OECD Industries

Griffith, R., Redding, S. & Van Reenen, J. (2004). Mapping the Two Faces of R&D: Productivity Growth in a Panel of OECD Industries. *The Review of Economics and Statistics* 86(4): 883–895. doi:10.1162/0034653043125194.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp7qron](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7qron.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **The mechanism, asserted.** Tacit knowledge is "hard to acquire without
  direct investigation. By actively engaging in R&D in a particular
  intellectual or technological field, one acquires such tacit knowledge
  and can more easily understand and assimilate the discoveries of others."
  (§I, p. 883).
- **The estimate.** The R&D-by-distance interaction is 0.596 (s.e. 0.333)
  in the base specification, "positive and statistically significant at
  the 10% level", and 0.815 (0.350) with human capital added (Table 2,
  p. 889).
- **Absorption is large far from the frontier.** Technology transfer is
  54% of R&D's total effect for Finland, 48% Norway, 40% Italy, 23%
  Germany, 14% the United States (Table 3, p. 891).
- **The conclusion.** "R&D stimulates growth directly through innovation
  and also indirectly through technology transfer." (§VI).

## Standing in the record

Here as its own work and as the stand-in, for the core claim only, for
Cohen and Levinthal (1990), [LIT-tmpwa0ib](LIT-tmpwa0ib.md). It bears on [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) and
[CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md): outside knowledge stays available, yet how much of it a party
can use scales with its own work in the field, so an available flux is not
enough without the party's own doing. No R&D is ever withdrawn in the
sample, so it says nothing on what happens to the ability to absorb once
own work stops. Filed from it: [THEORY-tmpwegr1](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwegr1.md).
