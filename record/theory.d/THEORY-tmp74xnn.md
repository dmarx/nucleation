# THEORY-tmp74xnn: On Sørensen and colleagues' review, a bacterial endosymbiont becomes genetically integrated, the authors' mark of an organelle, when host proteins imported into it replace genes it has lost, and this does not require gene transfer from the endosymbiont: in the Paulinella chromatophore and the nitroplast, the two systems with hundreds of imported proteins, the replacing genes are mostly the host's own or come from other bacteria

## Source

Sørensen, Stiller, Kröninger & Nowack (2025), *The FEBS Journal*
292(12): 2992–3013, LIT-1560, read in full in NOTE-tmpl32gd from the
PubMed Central copy. The introduction's definition of genetic
integration; the sections on *Paulinella* and on the nitroplast; the
concluding scenario's paragraph on EGT.

## What was actually shown

The review reports, from cited primary studies:

- **The chromatophore.** Proteomics showed "hundreds of proteins that
  derive from EGTs and HGTs, but mostly from the host itself are
  imported and compensate for functions lost from the chromatophore
  genome or add novel functionality". About 60 EGT-derived and 170
  HGT-derived nuclear genes contribute. Two EGT-derived photosystem I
  subunits were shown by radiolabelling to be made on host ribosomes and
  assembled in the chromatophore.
- **The nitroplast.** "Evidence for EGT has not been found in B.
  bigelowii". Proteomics of isolated UCYN-A found "> 350 nucleus‐encoded
  proteins, many of which compensate for functions lost from the
  cyanobacterial genome and complete metabolic pathways". 62% carry a
  common C-terminal extension, taken to be the import signal.
- **The authors' conclusion.** "EGT is not a precondition for the
  genetic integration of an endosymbiont. In Paulinella, EGT played only
  a circumstantial role ..., whereas in Braarudosphaera no EGT at all was
  required."

What could have failed: the imported proteins could have turned out to
be mostly products of transferred endosymbiont genes, as in the classic
picture of mitochondria and plastids, and UCYN-A could have shown EGT.

## What this does not say

- **Not how many imported proteins come from each source.** "Mostly from
  the host itself" is a summary. No count is given in the review.
- **Not that every imported protein replaces a lost gene.** Some "add
  novel functionality", and some may be effectors.
- **Not how import works.** The *Paulinella* route goes through the
  Golgi; the inner-membrane step is "currently unclear". The nitroplast
  mechanism is unknown.
- **Not that EGT is unimportant for old organelles.** Mitochondria and
  plastids moved "hundreds of endosymbiont genes" to the nucleus. The
  claim is that transfer is not needed to start.
- **Not that gene loss followed import in time.** The order is the
  authors' scenario (THEORY-tmpkvjih), not observed.

  describes the route as genes moving to the nucleus and their products
  returning. On this account the route is import, and the genes doing
  the replacing come mostly from the host's own lineage and from third
  parties. That joins this route to the question's host-compensation
  route, which also uses genes from third lineages. The joining is mine.
- **CASE-174 and CASE-167.** The two systems behind the claim. CASE-174's
