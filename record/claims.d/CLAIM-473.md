# CLAIM-473: Coupling locally described systems necessarily induces a separation of time scales between their collective behaviour and their relative behaviour

## The claim

The owner, at U20, after [CLAIM-471](CLAIM-471.md)'s first sentence: "and coupling inertial
frames necessarily induces scale separation (way less confident about this one
than the former assertion)."

Read with [CLAIM-471](CLAIM-471.md)'s restatement, it says: when locally described systems
are coupled, some modes of the whole (the collective ones) come to evolve on a
time scale well separated from others (the relative ones).

## Why it is Rejected

The necessity fails, and every reply that took it up said so.

- A20 §3: "the strength of the result comes from the specific dynamics, not
  from coupling alone."
- A21 §3: "**Does coupling split the system into distinct normal modes?** Often,
  yes. **Does it create a hierarchy in which relative modes rapidly disappear
  while collective modes persist?** Only under additional dynamical
  conditions."
- A23 §V.3: "This was the least secure part of your initial conjecture, and it
  remains conditional."

[CLAIM-481](CLAIM-481.md) is the counterexample, with the condition under which the claim
does hold.

The status is set on filing because the defeater is a computation, not because
the owner's question is closed. What remains open is which couplings separate
time scales and why. That is [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)'s second half, that organization
makes separations, and [CLAIM-481](CLAIM-481.md)'s condition is a mechanism for it.

## What survived

A23 §V.3: "**Interconnection can produce a hierarchy of collective and relative
modes. Dissipation, spectral separation, stability, and the relevant
observation horizon determine whether this hierarchy supports an effective
higher-level description.**" A22 §2 added a route the owner's sentence points
toward: in a chain of n locally coupled units the slowest relaxation time
grows as n². "**By linking many components, it can create new long-wavelength
modes that evolve much more slowly than local disturbances.**" That is size,
not coupling strength, making the separation. A23 also listed sources of
separation that involve no coupling at all: conservation laws, metastability,
critical slowing and imposed time scales.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (evening): the readings of the U15–U23 works

Spivak ([NOTE-1225](../notes.d/NOTE-1225.md); [THEORY-1054](../theory.d/THEORY-1054.md)) supplies a family of counterexamples
beside [CLAIM-481](CLAIM-481.md)'s. Every harmonic network under his phase-space
semantics is coupled and its step is symplectic (Prop. 6.6.1), so relative
modes never decay. The status stands.
