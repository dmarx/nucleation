# ARG-022: The 2016 Contextuality-by-Default paper grounds the criterion the manuscript's general observational covers need

## The inference

[CLAIM-037](../claims.d/CLAIM-037.md) holds that formal contextuality is a failure of global extension,
distinct from ordinary context dependence, and needs Contextuality-by-Default
"when marginals shift with context". It grounds on the 2016 paper,
[LIT-777](../literature.d/LIT-777.md) (read in [NOTE-600](../notes.d/NOTE-600.md)). The manuscript applies the criterion to the
general scenario of its §3.

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** Yes, for the 2016
  criterion. But the paper's own footnotes 1, 7 and 10 point to the
  authors' later "multimaximal" version (arXiv 1604.04799, 1604.08412).
  [CLAIM-069](../claims.d/CLAIM-069.md) (2026-10-09): "That version changes which systems count as
  contextual when a content appears in more than two contexts. The authors
  say the cyclic binary case, the consistently connected case and the
  measure theorems are unchanged."
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** No. This is the question
  that fails. [CLAIM-069](../claims.d/CLAIM-069.md): "The manuscript cites the 2016 paper; its general
  observational covers need the later version." The point bites on the
  general scenario of §3 and on Case II's overlapping elicitation contexts,
  where an observable may be asked alongside several others. CbD 2.0
  ([LIT-831](../literature.d/LIT-831.md)) and its companion ([LIT-836](../literature.d/LIT-836.md)) confirm it: verdicts change only
  for contents measured in three or more contexts.
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than the reading holds?**
  Not for the manuscript's parity example: each of S, M and H appears in
  exactly two contexts, a cyclic binary system, so the 2016 criterion
  suffices there. The canonical-systems paper ([LIT-790](../literature.d/LIT-790.md), [THEORY-168](../theory.d/THEORY-168.md)) adds a
  qualification: whether a fixed set of measurements counts as contextual
  depends on how the system is represented. "The parity example is
  unaffected" assumes S, M and H are binary. If any is multi-valued and its
  coarsenings matter, even a single observable can come out contextual.

Proposed. The owner ruled [CLAIM-069](../claims.d/CLAIM-069.md) Proposed on 2026-10-27 rather than
narrowing it. Its second clause, that the change matters for observables in
more than two contexts, is the part [THEORY-168](../theory.d/THEORY-168.md) keeps Proposed: [LIT-831](../literature.d/LIT-831.md)
asserts it with no worked example, and [THEORY-722](../theory.d/THEORY-722.md) shows only that the
change can bite in three or more contexts, not that it does. This
inference goes Rejected on [THEORY-168](../theory.d/THEORY-168.md)'s own promote_when: a worked system
that is noncontextual under CbD 1.0 and contextual under 2.0. [THEORY-168](../theory.d/THEORY-168.md)
already holds the reading, and gains nothing.

Filed 2026-10-28 by the [ADR-061](../decisions.d/ADR-061.md) migration, from [CLAIM-069](../claims.d/CLAIM-069.md).

## Note of 2026-10-29: rejected on its stated condition

[THEORY-168](../theory.d/THEORY-168.md)'s note of this date works the case this argument named: a binary
content measured in four contexts, inconsistently connected, that is
noncontextual under CbD 1.0's maximal couplings and contextual under CbD
2.0's multimaximal ones, with a subsystem that is contextual under 1.0. So
the 2016 criterion and its replacement disagree on a content in more than
two contexts, which the manuscript's general observational covers allow,
and the 2016 paper does not ground what those covers need.
