# TERM-tmp0evbm: higher-order volition: an attitude that a given first-order motive or regime should be the one that moves to action, Frankfurt's second-order volition, the reflective sense of "endorse", kept apart from endorsement between processes, from the machinery that makes a motive effective, and from identification

## Definition

A **higher-order volition** is an attitude that a given first-order
motive, or a regime among motives, should be the one that moves to
action.

Frankfurt's 1971 paper is the source (LIT-692, read; THEORY-120). A
person's will is "an effective desire—one that moves (or will or would
move) a person all the way to action" (p. 8). A second-order volition is
wanting a desire "to be his will" (p. 10).

**The job.** It is a disambiguation. Where an exchange, a reading or a
claim says "endorse" for this attitude, the record writes "higher-order
volition" and cites this term. "Endorsement" stays TERM-053's.

**Bearer.** In Frankfurt the bearer is the person. The record also uses
the term for a process's attitude about which regime should govern
(CASE-112's h). A14 §5: "An internal process wanting another process to
govern is structurally analogous to higher-order volition, but it is not
automatically the person's own Frankfurtian endorsement."
A14 §5: "That attribution is precisely the ownership problem we haven't
settled."

## What it is not

- **Not endorsement between processes.** TERM-053 is an effect, the
  change an intervention of one process makes in another's share of
  future influence. A higher-order volition is an attitude. A14 §2 put
  it as wanting a desire to govern ≠ causing that desire to govern.
- **Not efficacy.** Frankfurt's unwilling addict has the volition and is
  moved by the craving anyway (CASE-181). In CASE-112 a sincere volition
  can move the balance of authority without moving the regime. The
  volition may still be a positive TERM-053 endorsement that falls short.
- **Not metacontrol.** CLAIM-122's note of 2026-10-18: "Higher-order
  volition is an attitude concerning what should be effective.
  Metacontrol is the causal machinery capable of altering what becomes
  effective." CLAIM-539 reads Jennings as putting metacontrol where
  Frankfurt puts the volition.
- **Not identification or ownership.** Whether a higher-order volition
  makes a motive the agent's own is THEORY-120's claim. THEORY-029 rivals
  it. This term takes no side.
- **Not a tolerance criterion.** A perspective's acceptance condition for
  another's activity (CLAIM-tmpgjdby) can supply conditions for a
  volition but does not constitute one. A14 §5: "The former can supply
  conditions for the latter, but it does not constitute it."
- **Not caring.** CLAIM-492 reads "cares about" as what the agent is
  moved to promote, not as "reflective endorsement".
