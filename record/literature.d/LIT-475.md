**Where it would sit.** The title's question is the one the record's
social-construction filings answer in different ways: Haslanger
([LIT-480](LIT-480.md)) constructs gender and race as social positions, Ásta
([LIT-491](LIT-491.md)) as conferred statuses, and Ritchie ([LIT-486](LIT-486.md)) treats social
kinds as nodes in social structures. Ritchie cites a different Hacking work
("The Looping Effects of Human Kinds", 1995) for kinds that change the people
they classify; whether chapter 5 develops that idea is to be checked. The SEP
entry on natural kinds ([LIT-244](LIT-244.md), [NOTE-217](../notes.d/NOTE-217.md)) records Hacking's later advice to
delete talk of natural kinds; that, too, is a different work. Berger &
Luckmann ([LIT-474](LIT-474.md)), filed unread beside this, is the earlier book with
"social construction" in its title.

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary. `philosophy-of-science` holds its
chapters on the natural sciences, weapons research and rocks, and
`metaphysics` holds the realism question those chapters raise.

No instruction for machine-learning practice appears in what was seen, so
nothing here points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.**

- What distinctions chapter 1 draws among construction claims, and whether they
  sort Haslanger's and Ásta's accounts.
- Whether chapter 5's "kind-making" is the looping-kinds account Ritchie cites
  from 1995, and how it relates to Ritchie's social kinds as nodes.
- Whether the book engages Berger & Luckmann.
