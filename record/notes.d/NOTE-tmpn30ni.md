# NOTE-tmpn30ni: Gershman, Blei & Niv — Context, learning, and extinction

## Contribution

Redish et al. (2007) explained renewal with temporal-difference learning
plus a state-classification rule that creates a new state when
observations stop matching existing ones, gated by tonically negative
prediction errors. This paper replaces that heuristic with Bayesian
inference over an unbounded set of latent causes (a Dirichlet process
mixture), reproduces the phenomena the earlier model got right and one it
got wrong (the context-dependence of latent inhibition), and adds a
one-parameter account (α = 0) of hippocampal-lesion and developmental
effects.

## Key insight

"The central claim of our account is that, to adaptively predict events in
their environment, animals attempt to partition observations into separate
groups on the basis of their properties" (p. 198). Extinction does not
unlearn conditioning; it is assigned to a different cause, and what comes
back at test depends on which cause the test trials look like they came
from. Context matters because it helps tell causes apart.

## Assumptions

- **One cause per trial** (a mixture, not a product of co-present causes),
  defended against Rescorla–Wagner-style product models (pp. 204–205).
- **Conditionally independent, discrete features** given the cause;
  real-valued features gave "essentially the same results" (fn. 3).
- **A static, exchangeable mixture**: no temporal dynamics and no
  transitions between causes. Of Fuhs & Touretzky: "In contrast with our
  static mixture model, they used a dynamic mixture model" (p. 205).
- **Uniform Dirichlet priors; α fixed at .1**; learning α did not change
  the results substantially (fn. 6).
- **Response proportional to the predicted probability of reinforcement**
  given cue and context.
- **The particle filter (3,000 particles) as approximate inference only**,
  not as an algorithmic claim (p. 206).
- **Lesion and immaturity both as α = 0**, one hypothesised mechanism.

## Key results

- **Abstract (p. 197).** The authors "show that online Bayesian inference
  within a model that assumes an unbounded number of latent causes can
  characterize a diverse set of behavioral results from such
  manipulations, some of which pose problems for the model of Redish et
  al."
- **The prior (p. 199).** "In this model, the animal prefers a small number
  of causes but can, at any time, infer the occurrence of a new latent
  cause when the data support its existence".
- **Renewal (p. 201).** "our model predicts ABA renewal as a consequence of
  the animal’s inference that different latent causes are active during
  conditioning and extinction." No renewal when tested in the extinction
  context.
- **Context (p. 201).** "Thus, the importance of context in our theory
  derives from its usefulness in disambiguating the latent causes of
  observations".
- **ABC renewal (p. 202).** "ABC renewal will be observed to the extent that
  the prior expectation of reinforcement in a new cluster is greater than
  zero." The simulation reported shows another route, C split between the
  conditioning and extinction clusters; the paper lists three.
- **Latent inhibition (p. 202).** With pre-exposure and conditioning in
  different contexts, "the reinforcement statistics learned from the
  conditioning trials are segregated from the reinforcement statistics of
  the pre-exposure trials".
- **Against Redish et al. (p. 204).** "the dependence of new state inference
  on negative prediction errors, which prevented Redish et al.’s model from
  explaining the context specificity of latent inhibition, is wholly absent
  from our account."
- **Hippocampus (pp. 202–203).** "We propose that hippocampal lesions
  disrupt the ability of the animal to infer new clusters, restricting its
  inference to already-established clusters", implemented by "setting
  alpha to zero at the time of the lesion". The same manipulation models
  rats younger than about postnatal day 22; dentate gyrus–CA3 pattern
  separation is proposed as the substrate.

## Concepts

- **Latent cause**: the hidden discrete variable assumed to generate all of
  a trial's features. "Cluster" names its inference-side counterpart: "The
  clusters inferred by the animal may not be identical to the true causes
  of its observations." (fn. 4).
- **Generative model and recognition model**: the animal's prior beliefs
  about how observations arise, and its posterior over the hidden
  variables (fn. 2).
- **Infinite-capacity mixture**: a Chinese restaurant process (Dirichlet
  process) mixture (fn. 5).
- **Concentration α**: the prior on how many causes there are; α = 0 gives
  one cause, α → ∞ a cause per observation.
- **Four axes** (Discussion): generative against discriminative, mixture
  against product, finite against infinite capacity, batch against
  incremental inference.

## Connections

A reply to Redish, Jensen, Johnson & Kurth-Nelson (2007), not held, which
it reformulates; it builds on Courville's (2006) latent-cause work and
contrasts Fuhs & Touretzky's (2007) dynamic mixture. The record holds none
of these, nor Bouton's renewal work. The CRP used here is what the nested
CRP of LIT-1542 (Blei a co-author there too) generalises to trees.

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization.** A has the paradigm right (renewal of an
  extinguished response), the Bayesian latent-cause framework with an
  expanding number of causes, the non-overwriting of the conditioning
  memory, and coverage of renewal, latent inhibition and phenomena that
  troubled Redish et al.'s model. Corrections:
  - **"infers a transition to a different latent cause"** is imprecise. The
    model has no transitions: it is a static mixture, set by the authors
    against a dynamic one. Extinction trials are assigned to another
    cluster.
  - **"Context as something inferred rather than supplied"** is wrong as
    worded. Context is an observed feature on every trial; the latent cause
    is what is inferred, and context matters because it disambiguates
    causes.
  - **"accounted for multiple established conditioning effects"** is
    overstated: qualitative simulations beside replotted data, no fitted
    parameters, and the comparison with Redish et al. argued, not
    simulated.
  - **Missing**: the hippocampal and developmental account (α = 0), the
    stated limitations, and that the hierarchy is shallow, a
    nonparametric mixture rather than a multi-level hierarchy of knowledge.
  - **The sentence A puts in quotation marks is A's paraphrase**, not the
    paper's.
- **LIT-1500 / NOTE-1137 (inhibitory learning in exposure; renewal shows
  the original learning survives).** Supports: a computational reason the
  original association survives extinction.
- **THEORY-1193 and QUESTION-333 (fear decline within or across sessions
  does not predict follow-up fear).** Supports as a mechanism: in this
  model the fall in responding during extinction is learning about a new
  cause and says nothing about the old one. The paper does not model
  follow-up fear; that step is the record's.
- **LIT-983 / NOTE-766 (impaired context disambiguation in post-traumatic
  psychopathology).** Shares the idea that context matters by
  disambiguating hidden causes, in a different formalism; a precursor
  NOTE-766 could cite.
- **LIT-615 / NOTE-485 and CLAIM-337 (active-inference structure learning
  by spare hidden-state slots).** Qualifies: a rival formalisation of
  inferring that a new hidden state exists, with an unbounded CRP prior in
  place of a fixed pool of spare slots.
- **CLAIM-446 and QUESTION-325 (anxiety maintained although safety is
  experienced), with LIT-1477 / NOTE-1134.** Qualifies tangentially: a
  belief-level route by which repeated safety leaves the threat estimate
  standing (safety assigned to a new cause), a rival to CLAIM-446's
  governance-maintained route. The paper models no avoidance.
- **QUESTION-276 and CASE-165 (identifying perspectives without fixing
  their number; a latent-feature prior).** Qualifies: a worked case of
  inferring an unbounded number of causes, but a mixture allows one cause
  per observation, so it cannot represent overlapping perspectives; the
  product-style allocation CASE-165 uses is what the paper sets aside
  (pp. 204–205).
- **LIT-1384 / NOTE-1082 (event models segmented at boundaries).** Related:
  a human-memory relative of context as a disambiguator of causes; not
  cited by the paper.
- Filed: THEORY-tmp1zlbc. No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **The authors'.** The partial-reinforcement extinction effect: "Our
  model, without further assumptions, cannot demonstrate this effect"
  (p. 207). No representation of time, so no spontaneous recovery: "We
  leave development of a temporally sophisticated mixture model to future
  work". On the algorithm: "it would be premature to commit to the
  particle filter as an algorithmic-level description of the conditioning
  data that we model" (p. 206). The hippocampal role is not offered as a
  general account of the hippocampus; prefrontal maturation is an
  unexamined alternative; the link to dopamine is left open.
- **Qualitative fits only**, to data replotted from other studies, with
  noise-free identical trials within a phase.
- **The lesion and developmental accounts rest on one manipulation**
  (α = 0) chosen to produce them.
- **ABC renewal depends on parameterisation**: the paper lists three routes
  to it.
- **One cause per trial** excludes compound causes.
