# CLAIM-1179: Collective identity may be a sloppy mess of governed perspectives, that is, a pseudo-self, playing a question–answer game

## The claim

The owner's words, at U33b: "collective identity as a (sloppy?) mess of (governed) perspectives (i.e. a pseudo-self?) playing a question-answer game".

What is new beside the owner's earlier turns is the join. U6 made a self "a literal sloppy mess of connected perspectives" ([CLAIM-1196](CLAIM-1196.md)). U10 made a team a pseudo-self ([TERM-166](../terms.d/TERM-166.md)). U26 proposed a game between queries and targets ([CLAIM-1031](CLAIM-1031.md)). U33b carries the first to a collective, names it with the second, says its perspectives are governed, and says what holds the mess together: the question–answer game it plays.

## The question marks

- **"(sloppy?)"** asks whether the word fits. The record holds two readings of it from U6, filed as alternatives: colloquial ([CLAIM-1196](CLAIM-1196.md)) and technical ([CLAIM-1174](CLAIM-1174.md)). The owner's choice there carries here. A64b §1 gives it a third, precise meaning of its own: "The decomposition into perspectives is not necessarily unique, stable, or equally resolvable under every observational regime." That is the regime-relative decomposition the record holds as [CLAIM-1044](CLAIM-1044.md) and, for teams, [CLAIM-1039](CLAIM-1039.md).
- **"(i.e. a pseudo-self?)"** asks whether a collective identity is a pseudo-self. A64b §6 answers with five criteria for a stronger candidate ([CLAIM-1048](CLAIM-1048.md)), and wonders whether the name "undersells the possibility".

## Standing

Proposed, the owner's. A64b develops it without objecting ([CLAIM-1165](CLAIM-1165.md)), and so the claim stays open on the record's own pseudo-self question, [QUESTION-521](../questions.d/QUESTION-521.md): a collective that plays such a game may still be an ecosystem of pseudo-selves and not one.
