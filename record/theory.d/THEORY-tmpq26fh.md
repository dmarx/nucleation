## The account

Bao et al. (2026) report that when 6,749 authors of recent preprints rated hypotheses that 26 LLMs generated from their own papers, non-reasoning models produced tightly clustered ideas, every model proposed null hypotheses less often than the authors had, and LLM judges, novelty metrics and existing reward models agreed with the authors' pairwise preferences at about chance, while a reward model trained on those ratings exceeded the agreement rate between human peer reviewers.
