# LIT-tmpmkmjq: Early processing of traumatic material and contextual information in posttraumatic stress disorder and its relation to memory impairments

Zidda, Steiger-White, Winkelmann, Ruttorf, Andoh, Nees and Flor (2025),
*Scientific Reports* 15: 16362. DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-00322-w.

## Key takeaways

- With trauma-specific cues embedded in neutral contexts and free viewing,
  "the PTSD group had a significantly longer latency for looking to the
  context than the NPTSD group", with no group difference for the cue
  (Results, eye-tracking).
- The delay predicted poorer recognition of rearranged cue–context pairs,
  beside the earliest visual potential (C1), whose polarity differed
  between the groups.
- The only gaze measure is the time to the first fixation. How long or how
  widely the context was then looked at is not reported.

## Standing in the record

Read for LEAD-106 as the nearest study to the first kind of evidence
QUESTION-303 asks for: context sampling in PTSD against trauma-exposed
controls, measured by eye movements. It lacks the cued-recall
manipulation, and its "context" is a neutral background, not periphery
that disambiguates a harmful from a harmless context.
