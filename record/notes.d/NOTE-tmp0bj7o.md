# NOTE-tmp0bj7o: Supporters, Followers, Fans, and Flaneurs: A Taxonomy of Spectator Identities in Football

## Contribution

A vocabulary that is still standard. It splits "fan" into four ideal
types by two independent axes, against the older binary of traditional
supporter versus consumer (Taylor; Critcher, after Raymond Williams's
members, customers and consumers). It adds a historical thesis: the
commodification of elite football since the late 1980s is a change in
kind ("hypercommodification") that pushes spectators from hot,
traditional identification towards the cool consumer.

## Key insight

How much a club matters to someone (hot or cool) and on what basis it
matters (place and inheritance, or the market) are different questions.
A fan can be hot and still a consumer, loving a club through its shirts
and its stars at a distance. A follower can be cool and still
traditional, tracking a friendly club through ties to their own. Talk of
"the fans" usually means only one of these.

## Assumptions

- **Ideal types, Weberian.** The categories "allow for degrees of
  empirical variation" (§ "Principles"). No spectator is claimed to be
  pure.
- **Bryan Turner's hot/cool and thick/thin solidarity** (1999, on body
  marks), with McLuhan's and Baudrillard's hot/cool media. Hot loyalty
  goes with thick solidarity, cool with thin.
- **A political economy**: Lash and Urry's "disorganized capitalism";
  commodification as "that process by which an object or social practice
  acquires an exchange value or market-centered meaning".
- **Privately owned clubs** in Western Europe are the principal
  domain; member-owned clubs (Spain, Latin America, Germany) are
  partial exceptions (Introduction).

## Key results

The four types (§§ on each), each with an identification, a motive and a
relation to place:

- **Supporter (traditional/hot).** A long-term, obligatory, kin-like tie
  ("mothers" and "sons" in South America). Switching to a rival is
  "impossible"; supporters are "culturally contracted to their clubs".
  The club is a totem of its community, and match rituals are "a
  ceremony, through which the supporters worship themselves". The
  relation to the ground is *topophilic*, the ground is "home".
  Subcultural capital accrues to those who kept attending in bad years,
  and cannot be bought.
- **Follower (traditional/cool).** Follows clubs and football people
  through mediated, vicarious communion, often via ties to their own club
  (shared players, ideology such as St. Pauli or Barcelona, ultras'
  friendships). Holds "nested identities" (Cohen 1978) with no fixed
  ranking. Knows which combinations are illegitimate: "Only flâneurs
  would declare a penchant for both Liverpool and Manchester United".
  Place matters only as a stage.
- **Fan (consumer/hot).** Intense but "inordinately unidirectional"
  affection for a club or its stars, authenticated by consumption
  (merchandise, shares, subscriptions): Thompson's "non-reciprocal
  relations of intimacy with distant others". Thicker forms can
  "collectivize and agitate to unseat the incumbent controllers" (sack
  the board, form shareholder associations). Most fans are "politically
  passive". Under celebrity commodification fans "learn to cool their
  affections".
- **Flâneur (consumer/cool).** A bourgeois, cosmopolitan window-shopper of
  clubs, players and even national teams, engaged through television and
  the internet. Wears club signifiers "as if adopting a temporary
  tattoo". Belongs to "a virtual community of strollers". At home in
  Augé's non-places. Switches affiliation "like television channels".
- **Trend and paradox** (final §). The historical drift is from supporter
  to flâneur, with follower and fan as "intermediary retreats". But the
  two poles depend on each other. Clubs court flâneurs for revenue, and
  flâneurs consume a spectacle that supporters create: "if supporters
  become flâneurs, then the spectacle that is created by the spectators
  themselves will be threatened" (after Walzer: the cosmopolitan "is
  parasitic on people who are not cosmopolitans").

## Concepts

- **hypercommodification**: the intensified commodification of football
  since the late 1980s, driven by satellite and pay-TV, telecoms,
  merchandise transnationals and stock markets.
- **hot / cool**: the degree to which the club is central to the
  individual's project of self-formation.
- **traditional / consumer**: a long, local, popular-cultural basis of
  investment, versus a market-centred one.
- **thick / thin solidarity** (after Turner).
- **topophilia**: love of place (note 7).
- **subcultural capital** (after Thornton, from Bourdieu).
- **nested identities** (Cohen 1978): an unranked set of allegiances
  integrated by the self.

## Bearing on the record

- **Source of [THEORY-tmp800bi](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp800bi.md)** (Proposed): the four-type taxonomy, and
  its historical trend claim, filed with a promote_when that asks for
  the trend to be measured.
- **[QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md) and [CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md).** The taxonomy answers part of "who is
  'the fanbase'?". Supporters, followers, fans and flâneurs relate to a
  club in different ways, and a public stance such as "the fans hate the
  owner" is likely voiced by the hot types, chiefly supporters. Flâneurs,
  by construction, do not take part. A fanbase's credited stance is
  therefore weighted by type before any sampling bias. That is an
  inference; the paper does not discuss stances. Giulianotti's "fans"
  forming shareholder associations to unseat boards is a route to the
  case's fourth reading (corporate judgment).
- **"Identification is not agency."** The record filed this work as a
  check against reading identification as corporate agency. Read, it
  supports the check: three of four types are passive or market
  relations, and collective action appears only in the thick forms.

## Limitations

- **No data.** Ideal types and a trend claim, illustrated by examples,
  mostly British.
- **The text read is the pre-review original.** The published version
  may differ.
- **Normative weight.** The analysis plainly favours supporters, and the
  flâneur is drawn as a caricature. Giulianotti acknowledges this in
  note 17.
- **The axes may not be independent** in practice, since market-centred
  consumption can deepen as well as cool attachment. The paper allows
  thicker and thinner variants within each type but does not measure
  them.

## Open questions

- Can the four types be recovered empirically, for example by clustering
  survey responses on attachment, consumption and place? Several later
  studies (Biel cites Grau et al. 2016; García & Llopis-Goig 2020) may
  have tried; none is held.
- Has the share of supporters fallen since 2002, as C3 predicts?
  Longitudinal attendance and membership data would answer it.
