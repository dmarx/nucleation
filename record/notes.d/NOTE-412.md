# NOTE-412: Curry, Jones Chesters & Van Lissa 2019, the MAC-Q

## Contribution

The paper ([LIT-496](../literature.d/LIT-496.md)) gives morality-as-cooperation its own instrument. It copies the
MFQ's two-part format: a Relevance scale ("when you decide whether something
is right or wrong, to what extent are the following considerations
relevant") and a Judgement scale (agreement with moral statements). It
writes items for the seven domains, and in three online samples tests
whether seven factors emerge and whether the MFQ's five do. It also states
the theory's structural prediction precisely. Each type of cooperation gives
a distinct factor, and "behaviour not tied to a specific type of cooperation
will not constitute a distinct moral domain" (p. 9). Care and purity are
named as the domains expected to fail (pp. 10–11).

## Key insight

The paper converts a claim about content (cooperation is moralized) into a
claim about structure (endorsement of the seven varies independently). The
structural claim is the one a questionnaire can test, and the one that
distinguishes MAC from MFT on the domains they share (group, deference,
fairness). The content claim, that nothing else is moralized, needs items
the MAC-Q does not contain.

## Assumptions

- **Items from the theory.** The authors wrote 57 Relevance and 97
  Judgement items "based on a comprehensive review of the game-theoretic …
  literature" and focused on "the principal moral value in each of the seven
  hypothesised domains" (p. 18). They then kept three items per subscale,
  chosen for theoretical consistency and item-total correlation within the
  subscale (p. 19). A seven-factor solution is partly built into this
  selection.
- **Virtue-term wording.** The items describe the behaviours as virtues or
  their violations: "acted heroically", "showed love for their family",
  "kept their promise". This is the same feature [NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md) found in [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s
  codebook.
- **Samples.** UK and US online panels, paid £0.5 and $0.75. Exclusions were
  heavy. In Study 1, 2,396 people opened the survey and 1,392 were kept;
  the listed exclusions (544 inattentive, 151 incomplete, 305 spoiled) sum to
  1,000, not 1,004. In Study 3a, 688 of 1,157 were excluded, 537 of them for
  inattention.
- **Fit criteria.** RMSEA below .05 good, CFI above .90 acceptable, SRMR
  below .08 good (endnote x).

## Key results

- **Study 1 (UK, n = 1,392, September 2015).**
  - The EFA gives seven factors on both scales, all items loading highest on
    their own factor.
  - CFA, Relevance: RMSEA .03, CFI .98, SRMR .03.
  - CFA, Judgement: RMSEA .06, CFI .87, SRMR .07. The authors' own criteria
    rate that CFI "unacceptable".
  - Combined (Table 9): the seven-domain "Simple Domains" model has CFI .73
    and RMSEA .07. The "Different but Related" model, with fourteen
    correlated latent variables (seven per scale), has CFI .93 and RMSEA .04.
    The multi-trait multi-method model has CFI .90.
  - Alphas: .76–.86 on Relevance, .53–.83 on Judgement (Property .53).
- **Study 2 (MFQ, UK, n = 1,042, pilot data 2013–2014).**
  - Relevance: parallel analysis gives three factors (Care/Fairness,
    Ingroup/Authority, and a single-item Disgust factor).
  - Judgement: five factors, but not MFT's five (Care/Fairness, Purity,
    Patriotic Authority, Family, Gender Roles).
  - No five-factor CFA fits on all criteria.
  - Relevance means on the 1–6 scale: Care 4.63, Fairness 4.46, Authority
    3.89, Purity 3.80, Ingroup 3.77 (Table 12).
- **Study 3a (US, n = 469, June–July 2016).**
  - MAC Relevance gives seven factors.
  - MAC Judgement gives five by parallel analysis (Family,
    Group/Reciprocity, Martial Virtues, Fairness, Property), and seven only
    when seven are forced.
  - MFQ Relevance gives two factors (Care/Fairness and
    Ingroup/Authority/Purity).
  - MFQ purity Relevance mean 4.19, against Ingroup and Authority at 4.31
    (Table 28). MFQ purity Judgement α = .75, the highest of the MFQ
    Judgement subscales.
  - Criterion validity is mixed. On MAC Relevance only Family and Deference
    were the best predictors of their own criterion scales, and Property did
    not correlate with its criterion (p. 35).
  - Incremental validity: the MAC-Q predicts better for family, group,
    heroism and property; the MFQ for deference and fairness. For
    self-reported cooperative actions, the MAC-Q was better in 10 cases,
    equal in 3 and worse in 1.
- **Study 3b (retest after one month, n = 137).** MAC-Q .79–.89 on
  Relevance and .66–.87 on Judgement; MFQ .70–.80 and .46–.75.
- **Data.** Not shareable, because the consent form "inadvertently
  precluded data sharing" (endnote vii). Materials and syntax are on OSF
  (osf.io/w5ad8).

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Each of the seven types of cooperation is regarded as morally relevant | moderate | Relevance means run from "somewhat" to "very" relevant in both samples. With virtue-worded items, a low rating was unlikely |
| C2 | Each type of cooperation is a distinct moral domain | moderate for Relevance; weak for Judgement and for the combined scale | seven factors on Relevance in two samples. Judgement gives five in the US sample, and the combined seven-domain model fails (CFI .73) |
| C3 | Behaviour not tied to a type of cooperation (care, purity) does not form a distinct domain | weak | shown only with the MFQ's items, whose low reliability the paper itself documents. Purity is a distinct Judgement factor in Study 2, and its α is the MFQ's best in Study 3a |
| C4 | Cooperative behaviour is "the" content of morality; purity's relevance "remains to be explained" | conceded against | MFQ purity items are rated relevant at the level of loyalty and authority; the authors say this "appear[s] to contradict" MAC (p. 27) |
| C5 | The MAC-Q's coverage, reliability, structure and fit are superior to the MFQ's | moderate | true of alphas, retest and Relevance fit; criterion and incremental validity are "broadly comparable" by the authors' own summary (p. 38) |
| C6 | MAC "identif[ies] for the first time" moral domains of family, reciprocity, heroism and property | moderate as a measurement result; weak as discovery | the domains were written into the item pool, so their recovery is a check on the items, not an independent finding |

## Concepts

- **Relevance vs Judgement.** The MFQ's two item formats, kept here. The
  paper's strongest methodological finding is that they should not be pooled
  without modelling their difference.
- **Simple Domains / Different but Related / MTMM.** Three ways to combine
  the two formats: seven factors; fourteen correlated factors; or seven
  traits plus two method factors.
- **Theory of dyadic morality, side-taking theory.** Rivals the paper
  reviews (pp. 12–13), with relational models, before testing only against
  MFT.

## Connections

- **The sixty-society test ([LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)).** [NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md)'s verdict, that the test is
  weakly diagnostic, is not changed by this paper. It is reinforced at one
  point and confirmed at another.
  - Reinforced: the items, like the codebook, are phrased in virtue terms.
  - Confirmed: the paper has no non-cooperative items, so it is no answer to
    the missing comparison domain. Where non-cooperative items do appear (the
    MFQ's purity items), they are moralized.
- **The founding statement ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md)).** That chapter predicted that
  non-cooperative behaviour would be morally neutral. Studies 2 and 3a are
  the only place in the programme's own work where that prediction meets
  data, through the MFQ's purity items, and the authors report it failing.
- **Purity ([LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md)).** The paper's reply is that purity is not a coherent
  domain, and that disgust may be a proximate mechanism serving several
  cooperation problems (p. 40, endnote xv). Endnote xv points to the
  sixty-society Reply's sketch of a cooperative sexual morality. Fitouchi,
  André and Baumard are the worked version of this reply, at least for
  puritanical norms. They exclude contamination purity by definition. They
  also reduce what remains to harm and fairness concerns, while this paper
  argues that harm is not a domain. Their account rescues "morality is about
  cooperation". It does not rescue "morality is these seven domains".
- **Moral molecules ([LIT-509](../literature.d/LIT-509.md)).** The limitations section announces the
  21 second-order "moral molecules" (pp. 40–41) that the later paper
  develops.
- **The Turkish validation ([LIT-513](../literature.d/LIT-513.md)).** An independent team reproduced
  the main result: seven factors fit Relevance and fail on Judgement and
  the full form.
- **Not held here.** The MFQ (Graham et al. 2011), relational models, dyadic
  morality and side-taking theory are being filed in parallel by other
  contributions.

## Bearing on the record

No ML practice, and the anthology does not hold the paper. For the
morality-as-cooperation thread it is the evidence for MAC's structure. It is
not evidence for MAC's scope. Citing it for "morality is about cooperation"
overstates it, since it never asks about anything else. It is also the
programme's own admission that purity is moralized.

## Limitations

- **Self-built items.** The domains were written into the items, which were
  then selected by within-domain fit, so recovering the seven is partly a
  check on construction.
- **No non-cooperative items**, so the content claim is untested.
- **Judgement scale.** Fit is unacceptable by the paper's own CFI criterion
  in both samples, and five factors emerge in the US sample. The Property
  Judgement items are all reverse-coded, which the authors flag as a
  possible valence confound (p. 42). Equality is the only fairness measured.
- **Two WEIRD samples** with heavy exclusions.
- **Data cannot be shared**, so no reanalysis is possible.
- **Overstatement.** The Study 1 discussion calls the seven-factor model "a
  good fit for both scales" (p. 23), but the Judgement CFI is .87.

## Open questions

- With harm, purity, honesty and religious-observance items added to the
  pool, do they load on MAC's factors, form their own, or scatter?
- Does the seven-factor Relevance structure hold with items written by
  people outside the programme?
- Why do Relevance and Judgement diverge? The paper leaves it open (p. 40).

## Corrections

- none to a seeded skim (there was no seed)
- **The citation.** The owner's citation is correct: Curry, Jones Chesters
  and Van Lissa (2019), *Journal of Research in Personality* 78:106–124. The
  published title is hyphenated ("morality-as-cooperation"). The PsyArXiv
  manuscript's is not ("morality as cooperation").
- **What the MFQ comparison shows.** It is often summarised as the MAC-Q
  beating the MFQ. On fit and reliability it does. On criterion and
  incremental validity the authors call the two "broadly comparable"
  (p. 38).
