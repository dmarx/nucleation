# SYNTHESIS-102: Rights, demands and commitments are distinct

*A part account of the organizational-normativity line, on rights, demands and commitments. It was filed at the interim synthesis review of 2026-11-17, after the filing of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" the same day, from which every component but one comes; none was in any account before. Version 2, at the follow-up review of 2026-11-18, folds in the readings of the exchange's works (pull request 514) and integrates [ARG-117](../arguments.d/ARG-117.md), the readings' judgement on [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md). Every component here was filed by the continuation's filing (turns U1 to U48); the first share's filing (pull request 510, turns U1 to U17) filed nothing in this part, and none of the nine entries reconciled into the first filing's codes is here. Several components also carry dependence-and-domination, and two cases and the argument carry it and not this line (below). Its sibling on accountability without blame and normative governability is [SYNTHESIS-104](SYNTHESIS-104.md).*

## The account

Each link is stated by the entry named first, in its front matter unless
its body is cited. Every component is Proposed, or Open for a question.
Readings are not components: each is cited as the support a component
has, by the THEORY that carries the relation (`corroborates`, `bears_on`,
or the component's own `grounds`) and the NOTE that reads the work, and a
reading that qualifies a component without a relation is cited from the
component's dated note. Every reading named is Proposed; none changed a
status.

**The contrast.** [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md) (the owner's contrast, U41, "rights vs
demands vs commitments"; the definitions and non-implications are A's,
A95 and A97; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; also will-organization):
rights, demands and commitments are distinct, none establishes another,
and each passage between them "needs its own warrant". It uses [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)
and complements [CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md), [CLAIM-743](../claims.d/CLAIM-743.md) and [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md). Its body says
[CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md) files the demand side "in its sharpest form" and [CLAIM-743](../claims.d/CLAIM-743.md) "the
one passage A97 X singles out", and that the aquifer, [CASE-282](../cases.d/CASE-282.md), "puts all
three to work in one arrangement".

- *Readings.* Two THEORYs corroborate it; it rests on neither. Hohfeld
  ([THEORY-1984](../theory.d/THEORY-1984.md), [NOTE-2071](../notes.d/NOTE-2071.md)): his eight positions are logically
  independent, and its dated note limits the corroboration to "the
  general point that normative positions do not entail one another, not
  ... the claim's three terms", since Hohfeld discusses neither demands
  nor commitments. Stern, standing in for Darwall's unavailable book
  [LIT-2756](../literature.d/LIT-2756.md) ([THEORY-1975](../theory.d/THEORY-1975.md), [NOTE-2065](../notes.d/NOTE-2065.md)): on Darwall an actual demand settles
  nothing and legitimacy does, which the note reads as the claim's "a
  demand's being asserted, maintained or recognized gives it no warrant",
  reached independently. The note records where Darwall parts from the
  claim: he makes obligation consist in legitimate demandability, so the
  passage from obligation to legitimate demand is constitutive, not a
  further step; and Stern's sufferer who makes no demand is the case
  where the two give different verdicts, siding with the claim.

**A demand carries no warrant.** [CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md) (A's, A106 §4; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed) stores no relation. Its body: a demand's
acceptance set fixes "what meets it, not its source, who bears it, to
whom it is addressed or why it binds", so the compatibility problem
"cannot establish that each deserves fulfillment"; it calls itself the
formal reason for [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md)'s point, says [CLAIM-741](../claims.d/CLAIM-741.md) holds the other half
of A's two kinds of compatibility, and says [CLAIM-745](../claims.d/CLAIM-745.md) applies it. No
reading carries a relation to [CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md); the clause of [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md) that
Stern's Darwall corroborates is the nearest any reading comes.

[CLAIM-745](../claims.d/CLAIM-745.md) (A's mechanism and wording, A108 §1 and §4, at the owner's
U46, whose specimen it is; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; also
dependence-and-domination) rests on [CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md) and grounds in [CASE-289](../cases.d/CASE-289.md) and
[THEORY-1952](../theory.d/THEORY-1952.md): a categorical administrative gate can make evidence the
governing standard requires to count unable to change a decision, and
that the criterion is imposed and complied with "establishes neither its
legal authority nor its justification". It makes no finding on motive.

- *Readings.* The opinion in CLINIC v. Rubio, read ([THEORY-1952](../theory.d/THEORY-1952.md),
  [NOTE-2054](../notes.d/NOTE-2054.md)), is now among its grounds. Its dated note: the court found
  the consular officers' eligibility determinations "ultimately
  irrelevant. The outcome is predetermined", and held that the Policy did
  not displace the individualized public-charge assessment, so the
  required evidence was still assessed and could not change the decision,
  "unable to change a decision", not "not considered", as the claim words
  it. The court's holding that the Policy "displaces consular officers
  from the role Congress assigned them" bears out the claim's legal half;
  the opinion "says nothing on the Policy's moral justification, the
  claim's second half".

[CASE-289](../cases.d/CASE-289.md) (the owner's specimen; documented, Proposed; also
dependence-and-domination) illustrates [CLAIM-725](../claims.d/CLAIM-725.md): the 75-country
immigrant-visa pause of January 2026 and its vacatur on 2026-08-21. Its
body records the scope of closure and the motive as the owner's
characterization, with A108 §1's judgement that "all" avenues is too
broad. Its dated note, from the opinion ([THEORY-1952](../theory.d/THEORY-1952.md)), confirms the
dates, the court and the vacatur, and sharpens three things: the Cable
ordered refusal under § 221(g), not a pause; the nationality-
discrimination holding is the court's own, not only reported; and the
court rejected two of the challengers' grounds (the public-charge
provision and notice-and-comment), which the case had left out.

**Rights that protect a choice.** [CLAIM-741](../claims.d/CLAIM-741.md) (A's, A74, A85, A90, A97
III, A102 §4; thesis, demonstrated, Proposed; also will-organization)
grounds in [CASE-283](../cases.d/CASE-283.md), bears on [QUESTION-486](../questions.d/QUESTION-486.md), and complements [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md),
[CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md), [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md) and [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md): whether an arrangement respects a
right that protects a choice "is a property of its policy over the
protected choices, not of any realized history". [CASE-283](../cases.d/CASE-283.md) (A's
construction, checked on filing; stipulated, Proposed; also
will-organization) bears on [QUESTION-454](../questions.d/QUESTION-454.md): under a right of refusal, two
arrangements produce identical histories whenever the patient consents,
and only one respects the right. No reading bears on either; the claim's
warrant is the construction.

**Release, not suppression.** [CLAIM-743](../claims.d/CLAIM-743.md) (A's, A97 X; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; first line distributed-agency) rests on
[CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md), bears on [QUESTION-456](../questions.d/QUESTION-456.md), and complements [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md), [CLAIM-722](../claims.d/CLAIM-722.md)
and [CLAIM-662](../claims.d/CLAIM-662.md): a demand owed to a party ceases legitimately only by a
valid normative power, which changes the reference set; an institution's
ceasing to count it changes only the set it recognizes. Its body says
some rights "may not be releasable at all", citing [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md).

- *Readings.* Scanlon's "Reply to Leif Wenar" ([THEORY-1967](../theory.d/THEORY-1967.md), [NOTE-2075](../notes.d/NOTE-2075.md))
  corroborates it for promises: contractualism "explains why a promisee
  has the power to release the promisor", so an obligation owed to
  someone ceases legitimately by her release. The dated note says the
  reply "says nothing about an institution's ceasing to count a demand",
  the contrast the claim draws, and that Hohfeld ([NOTE-2071](../notes.d/NOTE-2071.md)) gives the
  general form.

**Inalienability.** [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md) (A's, A85 §2 and §4 as corrected by A87
§4, after Locke's Second Treatise, at the owner's U37; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; first line dependence-and-domination) uses
[TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md), bears on [QUESTION-486](../questions.d/QUESTION-486.md), [QUESTION-481](../questions.d/QUESTION-481.md) and [QUESTION-454](../questions.d/QUESTION-454.md), and
complements [CLAIM-722](../claims.d/CLAIM-722.md) and [CLAIM-727](../claims.d/CLAIM-727.md): no delegation, contract, collective
decision or success in imposing an arrangement confers authority over
whether a person keeps the standing from which claims against it arise,
"because the holder lacks the power to confer it".

- *Readings.* Locke, skimmed on the sections the claim cites
  ([THEORY-1959](../theory.d/THEORY-1959.md), [NOTE-2077](../notes.d/NOTE-2077.md)), corroborates its form: "No body can give more
  power than he has himself" (§23). [ARG-117](../arguments.d/ARG-117.md) (Proposed; form source; the
  verdict is the assistant's, Claude, in the readings' session; line
  dependence-and-domination only) grounds in [THEORY-1959](../theory.d/THEORY-1959.md) and concludes
  [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md), and narrows it: Locke's ground is the absence of power over
  one's own life, a theological premise (§6), not standing in general,
  and Locke admits forfeiture "by his fault" (§23), which the claim's
  list and defeater do not address. Its verdict: "The source supports the
  claim's form and not its scope. The claim needs a ground of its own, or
  a narrower statement, and a word on forfeiture." Two readings qualify
  the claim in its dated notes without a relation: Calabresi and Melamed
  ([THEORY-1962](../theory.d/THEORY-1962.md), [NOTE-2070](../notes.d/NOTE-2070.md)), the source of the exchange's "inalienability
  rules", make inalienability a limit on transfer justified
  instrumentally, possibly partial, and compatible with compensation;
  and Hohfeld ([THEORY-1984](../theory.d/THEORY-1984.md), [NOTE-2071](../notes.d/NOTE-2071.md)) bears out A87 §4's correction, the
  holder's inability being expressible as a disability "but its grounding
  is not his".

[CASE-286](../cases.d/CASE-286.md) (A's, A85 §11 Case A; stipulated, Proposed; in
dependence-and-domination only) illustrates [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md) and bears on
[QUESTION-486](../questions.d/QUESTION-486.md) and [QUESTION-454](../questions.d/QUESTION-454.md): two informed, voluntary delegations, one
limited and one purporting to be unlimited. No reading bears on it.

[QUESTION-486](../questions.d/QUESTION-486.md) (the owner's, U37; Open; also dependence-and-domination)
refines [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md): can rights no institution creates or can extinguish
serve as the institution-independent criteria the record's judgments
need? Four readings bear on it: Nickel and Etinson ([THEORY-1964](../theory.d/THEORY-1964.md)), who
characterize human rights as held independently of local recognition but
deny that inalienability belongs to the concept; the Declaration of
Independence ([THEORY-1994](../theory.d/THEORY-1994.md)), on "unalienable rights" that governments are
instituted to secure; the Ninth Amendment ([THEORY-1954](../theory.d/THEORY-1954.md)), that an
enumeration of rights does not exhaust those retained; and the SEP entry
"Rights" ([THEORY-1992](../theory.d/THEORY-1992.md)), on rights as complexes of Hohfeldian incidents
whose function is contested.

[QUESTION-481](../questions.d/QUESTION-481.md) (A's, A85 §8 and A90 XII; Open; first line
dependence-and-domination) stores no relation: who has standing to
change an arrangement's governing structure, and which protections lie
beyond revision. Its body says [CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md) bears on the limit half ("no
reconstitution by those who delegated can either"), and that
[QUESTION-486](../questions.d/QUESTION-486.md) asks whether rights of that kind can be had. Five readings
bear on it: Locke ([THEORY-1959](../theory.d/THEORY-1959.md)), on a fiduciary legislative power the
people may resume when the trust is breached; the Virginia Declaration of
Rights ([THEORY-1970](../theory.d/THEORY-1970.md)), on a majority's inalienable right to reform; the
Declaration of Independence ([THEORY-1994](../theory.d/THEORY-1994.md)), on the people's right to alter
or abolish; Schlager and Ostrom ([THEORY-1957](../theory.d/THEORY-1957.md)), on collective-choice
rights as authority to define future operational rights; and Waldron and
Penner ([THEORY-1965](../theory.d/THEORY-1965.md)), on private property as decisional authority over
resources, with title analysed as powers to license and transfer.

**Rights in later justification, and the reply to Scanlon.**
[QUESTION-483](../questions.d/QUESTION-483.md) (the owner's, U44; the alternatives and the test are A's;
Open) stores no relation: does the record's account only supplement
contractualism, or show an allocation of normative powers reasonable
rejection cannot explain without importing principles of standing or
authority? Its body names [CLAIM-741](../claims.d/CLAIM-741.md), [CLAIM-735](../claims.d/CLAIM-735.md), [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md), [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md) and
[CLAIM-747](../claims.d/CLAIM-747.md) as pieces a reply would use, and [QUESTION-484](../questions.d/QUESTION-484.md) as narrower.

- *Readings.* Four bear on it, and its dated note says what each does.
  Wenar ([THEORY-1988](../theory.d/THEORY-1988.md), [NOTE-2038](../notes.d/NOTE-2038.md)) is "a published form of the second
  option": contractualism "must be a multi-level theory". Scanlon's reply
  ([THEORY-1967](../theory.d/THEORY-1967.md), [NOTE-2075](../notes.d/NOTE-2075.md)) answers it, explaining powers, the promisee's
  among them, by reasonable rejection; "Neither shows the second level
  for institutional powers." Scanlon's 2025 paper ([THEORY-1977](../theory.d/THEORY-1977.md), [NOTE-2051](../notes.d/NOTE-2051.md))
  concedes that relevance depends on "the relation we stand to one
  another", where relations do work in his own account. The SEP entry
  "Contractualism" ([THEORY-1971](../theory.d/THEORY-1971.md), [NOTE-2043](../notes.d/NOTE-2043.md)), read as the neighbour of the
  unavailable book [LIT-2721](../literature.d/LIT-2721.md), gives the scope and the circularity answer
  that a "supplement only" reading relies on.

[CLAIM-735](../claims.d/CLAIM-735.md) (A's, A102 §3 and A106 §19, at the owner's U44; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed) grounds in [THEORY-1967](../theory.d/THEORY-1967.md): once justified, rights and
powers become "constitutive elements of the structure within which
further justification occurs", so they can be prior in organization where
principles are prior in justification. Its body relates it to [CLAIM-086](../claims.d/CLAIM-086.md),
to [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md) ("This claim says what a right does once the passage is
made") and to [CLAIM-741](../claims.d/CLAIM-741.md), and says it is compatible with [QUESTION-483](../questions.d/QUESTION-483.md)'s
supplement answer.

- *Readings.* Scanlon's reply ([THEORY-1967](../theory.d/THEORY-1967.md)), now a ground, carries the
  claim's premise in his words (contractualism explains claims, powers and
  liberties without taking rights as basic) and not its further step: he
  treats the promisee's power as justified once, "and stops there". Two
  THEORYs corroborate it: Wenar ([THEORY-1988](../theory.d/THEORY-1988.md)) separates first-order rights
  from second-order rights that "assign authority to change requirements
  on conduct", the distinction the claim needs, without the claim's
  further step; and the SEP entry "Rights" ([THEORY-1992](../theory.d/THEORY-1992.md), [NOTE-2035](../notes.d/NOTE-2035.md))
  corroborates its structural half, powers that fix which later acts
  change what is owed, and "says nothing about justificatory priority".
  The further step is in no reading.

**Duties to establish, and feasibility.** [CLAIM-729](../claims.d/CLAIM-729.md) (A's, A102 §5, after
A's report of Scanlon's "Contractualism and Intuitionism"; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed) rests on [CLAIM-560](../claims.d/CLAIM-560.md): the duties to comply with a
justified rule, to support a justified institution and to help establish
a needed one are distinct, and the third is bounded by reachability by
permissible, resource-bounded transitions and by whose part it is. Its
body says [CASE-282](../cases.d/CASE-282.md) illustrates the gap the bound names, that [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s
duty is A's third illustration, and that it leaves [QUESTION-481](../questions.d/QUESTION-481.md) open.

- *Readings.* Scanlon's paper, now read ([THEORY-1977](../theory.d/THEORY-1977.md), [NOTE-2051](../notes.d/NOTE-2051.md)),
  corroborates it; the claim does not rest on it. Scanlon separates the
  wrong of not complying with a needed practice from "failing to do one's
  part in getting such a norm established", and cites Rawls's natural
  duty "to further just arrangements not yet established, at least when
  this can be done without too much cost to ourselves": the three duties,
  with a cost bound on the third. The dated note says the paper "does not
  state its reachability terms or "whose part it is"", and that A's report
  understated Scanlon, who calls the third failure wrong.

[CASE-282](../cases.d/CASE-282.md) (A's construction, every number rechecked; stipulated, Proposed;
lines dependence-and-domination and realizability) illustrates [CLAIM-586](../claims.d/CLAIM-586.md),
[CLAIM-560](../claims.d/CLAIM-560.md), [CLAIM-690](../claims.d/CLAIM-690.md) and [CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md) and bears on [QUESTION-484](../questions.d/QUESTION-484.md): two

the SEP entry "Contractualism" ([THEORY-1971](../theory.d/THEORY-1971.md)) bear on it.

**The test run, and three readings.** LEAD-033 wrote out A102 §12's three
matched constructions, with the one QUESTION-481 asks for, on one
stipulated courier platform that varies only the capacity to formulate
objections, control of review, the erosion of alternatives or the power
to revise the rules (CASE-336), and applied the contractualist test. As
stated, each construction is explained by reasonable rejection on
personal reasons of protection, against undetectable error, self-serving
review, engineered dependence and unilateral change, with no principle of
who holds a power imported (CLAIM-908, argued by ARG-203), so as stated
they support QUESTION-483's supplement answer. They do not discriminate,
because each explanation runs through what a holder might do with the
power. Variants that fix the holder's use, a decider known to be
error-free and a sole reviser known to amend as the subjects would, do
