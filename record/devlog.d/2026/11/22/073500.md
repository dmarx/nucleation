**What happened.** CLAIM-778 (filed 2026-11-20) stated its defeater as a count the record could run on itself: cited claims revised no less often, at matched age, than uncited ones. LEAD-199 specified the count the same day. Run on 2026-11-22 (CASE-tmpdcoyb), it met the defeater: cited claims are revised about 1.45 times as often.

**Why it went right.** Three things made the result usable rather than arguable. The defeater was written before the data, in terms a script could check. LEAD-199 fixed the design (population, what counts as substantive, exposure, age matching) before anyone looked, so the analyst's choices were few and stated. And the synthesis review's admission of 2026-11-21 had already named the confound that would produce this result (scrutiny follows citation) and the narrower sentence that would survive it, so the two readings (CLAIM-tmpylhdj, CLAIM-tmpdsiv5) were filed from text the record already held, not improvised after the fact.

**What to keep.** State defeaters as checks the record can run on itself, and specify the lead's design when the claim is filed. Keep admissions that say in advance how a claim could fail: they turn a refutation into a choice between readings instead of a dispute.
