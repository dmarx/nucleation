# THEORY-1955: Administrative burden is often a political choice

Moynihan, Herd & Harvey (2015), [LIT-2769](../literature.d/LIT-2769.md).

## The account

Burden "is conceptualized as a function of learning, psychological, and
compliance costs that citizens experience in their interactions with
government" (p. 43). Its level and its split between state and individual
"will often be a function of deliberate political choice" (p. 43), and
because changes to burdens "may be presented as technical fixes" (p. 44)
they suit hidden politics. The read part reviews US take-up evidence,
strongest for compliance costs (p. 51).

## What it does not show

- **The political-choice half from the part read.** Its evidence is the
  Wisconsin Medicaid case study, which was not read.
- **That burdens are illegitimate.** The authors assume they "will often
  serve legitimate purposes" (p. 47).
- **Anything about the cost of contesting a decision**, as distinct from
  the cost of obtaining an entitlement.
