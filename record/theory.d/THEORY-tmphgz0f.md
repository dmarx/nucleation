# THEORY-tmphgz0f: A model of discovery or analogy that is given representations built with the answer in view has been given most of the answer, as BACON was with Kepler's data and SME with its solar system and atom, and representation-building cannot be postponed to a separate module, because which representation is right depends on the task and on the analogy being made

## Source

Chalmers, French & Hofstadter (1992), "High-level perception,
representation, and analogy", [LIT-414](../literature.d/LIT-414.md), read in full in [NOTE-359](../notes.d/NOTE-359.md) from the
1991 preprint (page numbers are the preprint's). Burns (1995), a review of
*Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies*, [LIT-457](../literature.d/LIT-457.md), read in [NOTE-375](../notes.d/NOTE-375.md).

## What was actually shown

**The hidden work ([LIT-414](../literature.d/LIT-414.md) pp. 4–5).** Hand-built representations hide
*high-level perception*: deciding which data are relevant (the "problem of
relevance") and organising them into structure (the "problem of
organization").

**BACON (pp. 8–10).** Its rediscovery of Kepler's third law used only mean
distances and periods, "precisely the data required to derive the law",
plus a bias toward algebraic laws. Kepler took thirteen years among
Platonic solids, circles and theology. Qin and Simon's finding that
students given BACON's data rediscover the laws within an hour is read as a
"reductio ad absurdum of the BACON methodology" (p. 10). [NOTE-359](../notes.d/NOTE-359.md) rates
this strong as a critique of the claim made for BACON (C2). Burns agrees:
finding the data was the discovery ([LIT-457](../literature.d/LIT-457.md)).

**SME (pp. 13–15).** In the atom and solar-system example the
representations contain almost exactly the relations the analogy needs:
"attracts", "revolves around", "gravity", "opposite-sign", "greater",
"cause". Arity matching and the object/attribute/relation split force
arbitrary choices to which the program is "highly sensitive" (C3,
moderate; no alternative encoding is run).

**No representation module (pp. 17–19).** First, perception depends on
analogy (Saddam Hussein as Hitler or as Robin Hood), so analogy-making
cannot be split into "first perception, then mapping". Second, a module
would have to supply one context-independent representation. Either it is
too narrow for some tasks (DNA as a zipper or as source code), or it is
all-encompassing, and then selecting from it is "tantamount to high-level
perception all over again" (C5, informal, the paper's strongest).

**Copycat (pp. 21–29).** Offered as an architecture that builds
representations and mappings together, by stochastic codelets biased by a
concept network. It is described, with one worked problem; the comparison
with people is one sentence citing other work (C6–C7).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The BACON and SME results could have
survived re-encoding by someone who did not know the answer. The paper does
not try it.

## What this does not say

- **That Copycat escapes the charge.** Its codelets and Slipnet are hand-
  coded, and the Slipnet's concepts (successor, sameness, leftmost) are the
  domain's relevant relations, so the hindsight charge is moved, not
  answered ([NOTE-359](../notes.d/NOTE-359.md), Limitations). Burns: SME is faulted because it would
  run the same with *water* and *ice* replaced by A and B, yet the same
  objection to Copycat is dismissed because an observer would work out that
  SIGMA means successor; "the symbol grounding problem is not so easily
  solved" ([LIT-457](../literature.d/LIT-457.md), C4).
- **That microdomains are innocent.** Burns grants the defence that
  "real-world" programs also restrict their domains, but microdomains "can
  contain hidden assumptions", and it is "not clear to what extent the
  programs ... succeed because they avoid representational issues"
  ([LIT-457](../literature.d/LIT-457.md), C3).
- **That "endemic" is shown.** BACON and SME are the case studies; ACME and
  four other programs get a sentence each (C1, moderate).
- **Anything about learned representations.** The paper names
  connectionist, context-dependent representations "a step in the right
  direction" (p. 7) and says no more. Whether a representation learned by
  training on many tasks is the flexible kind it asks for or another fixed
  module is open ([NOTE-359](../notes.d/NOTE-359.md), Open questions).
- **That the module argument is a proof.** It assumes that working memory
  is small and that selecting from a large representation is as hard as
  perceiving afresh; both are asserted.

## Connections

- **Frames.** Minsky's frames ([LIT-410](../literature.d/LIT-410.md)) are among the formats the paper
  names. Its charge is not that frames are the wrong format but that a
  format leaves the filling unexplained ([THEORY-tmp8bxd9](THEORY-tmp8bxd9.md) for the memo's own
  account of defaults).
- **Perception and cognition.** Block's border between seeing and thinking
  ([THEORY-tmp6rcgc](THEORY-tmp6rcgc.md)) separates them by format. This paper assumes, in one
  sentence ("Few would dispute"), that Fodor's and Pylyshyn's encapsulation
  arguments apply mostly to low-level perception, and needs top-down
  influence at the conceptual level.
- **Machine understanding.** The paper's "meaning barrier" frames the
  question [LIT-131](../literature.d/LIT-131.md) and Hofstadter's 2026 essay ([LIT-416](../literature.d/LIT-416.md)) put to learned
  systems.
- **Emergence.** Copycat is an engineered case of "higher-level
  understanding emerges" from local parallel processes ([LIT-141](../literature.d/LIT-141.md), [LIT-150](../literature.d/LIT-150.md));
  the paper offers no account of emergence beyond the phrase.
- The Copycat paper ([LIT-435](../literature.d/LIT-435.md)) and the reviewed book ([LIT-428](../literature.d/LIT-428.md)) are filed
  unread.
