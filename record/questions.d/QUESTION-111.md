# QUESTION-111: Which communicative distinctions remain recoverable by a bounded interpreter after a rendering, and how does the cost of recovering them change under framing?

## Why it is a question

A14 §1: "A distinction may survive a transformation in principle while
becoming too computationally expensive for the receiver to recover." The
second and third failure modes "matter even when the transformation is
mathematically invertible." [CLAIM-304](../claims.d/CLAIM-304.md) shows the gap exists. This asks where
it falls for communicative distinctions and real renderings.

A15 §4 added a contrast with [CLAIM-117](../claims.d/CLAIM-117.md). That claim asks whether a
translation preserves how interpretation changes under later framings.
"Complexity introduces a further question: even if the transformed
interpretive dynamics correspond mathematically, can a bounded receiver
actually recover or enact that correspondence?"

An answer measures, for named receivers and budgets, the bounded risk on a
communicative decision before and after a rendering, Δ_q(r, T) in A15's
notation, and shows how it varies with r.
