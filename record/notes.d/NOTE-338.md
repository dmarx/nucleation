# NOTE-338: Where Am I?

## Contribution

The essay separates three things that ordinarily coincide: where a
person's body is, where the brain is, and where the person takes himself
to be. It does so with a scenario vivid enough that the separation sticks.
It then adds a fourth thing that ordinarily goes unnoticed, the brain's
uniqueness, and separates that too with a functional duplicate. What it
leaves behind is a set of named cases, Yorick, Hamlet, Hubert and
Fortinbras, that the literature on personal identity and location still
uses.

## Key insight

Where a person is follows the point of view, not the hardware, and the
point of view follows the causal links. Cut the links to the body and the
narrator is "disembodied in Houston" at the speed of light, with no change
of mass. So "where am I?" has no answer deeper than where the links and the
point of view put you, and when there are two sets of links there can be
two of you.

## Assumptions

- **Radio links preserve everything.** "No information would be lost, all
  the connectivity would be preserved", "a mere stretching of the nerves".
  Only the speed of light intrudes, as coordination lags over distance.
- **A program can duplicate a brain.** Hubert reproduces "both the
  complete information-processing structure and the computational speed"
  of the brain. Matching outputs over weeks "did not prove" the copy, the
  narrator says, but was "greatly encouraging".
- **Physicalism.** The narrator is "a philosopher of firm physicalist
  conviction", and the puzzles are posed inside physicalism.
- **Brain-transplant intuitions are taken as data,** that one would rather
  be "the donor not the recipient".

## Key results

The essay offers no theorems. Its moves:

- **Thinking "here" of the vat fails.** "Here am I, Daniel Dennett,
  suspended in a bubbling fluid, being stared at by my own eyes" cannot be
  thought with conviction. "There"s can be aimed anywhere; "here in the vat
  always seemed merely an unmeant mental mouthing". So either the narrator
  thinks "systematically false indexical thoughts", or "where a person is
  (and hence where his thoughts are tokened for purposes of semantic
  analysis) is not necessarily where his brain … resides".
- **Three principles tried.**
  1. "Where Hamlet goes there goes Dennett" is refuted by brain-transplant
     cases.
  2. "Where Yorick goes there goes Dennett" is unappealing from the inside,
     and the bank-robbery jurisdiction case (California or Texas?) makes it
     seem a matter for lawyers.
  3. "Dennett is wherever he thinks he is": a person's location is the
     location of his point of view, "determined internally by the content of
     the point of view". This threatens "unlikely infallibility" about
     location, and point of view is "itself an unclear notion": the
     Cinerama viewer shifts it by illusion, and the teleoperator shifts it
     without being fooled.
- **Two places at once.** On reflection the narrator is "in two places,
  clearly", "one of those scattered individuals". The more obviously true
  that seems, "the less important the question to which it could be the
  true answer seemed". A residual question, "in some sense I and not merely
  most of me was descending into the earth", is left.
- **Disembodiment.** When the links fail, the narrator "changed location
  from Tulsa to Houston at the speed of light" with no gain in mass. He
  calls this, ironically, "an impressive demonstration of the immateriality
  of the soul based upon physicalist principles and premises". The
  alternative, "banishing all talk of persons", is called as unconvincing as
  "non sum".
- **Hubert.** Brain and program run in parallel. A master switch hands the
  new body to either "without a pause or hitch", mid-sentence. Which pairing
  would be "the true Dennett" if each drove a body turns on legal
  analogies (causal priority, "squatter's rights") that are "a bit too
  redolent of the arbitrariness of consanguinity and legal possession, to be
  convincing at the metaphysical level". The narrator's "strongest
  intuition" is that he survives while either brain–body couple does.
- **Drift.** "Our two brains drifted just a bit out of synch", the
  difference "snowballed", and the other brain lost control of the body
  without anyone knowing it existed. One body now houses two persons, one a
  passenger "like being possessed".

## Concepts

- **Yorick, Hamlet, Hubert, Fortinbras** — the brain in the vat, the
  original body, the program duplicate of the brain, the replacement body.
- **point of view** — where the person takes himself to be, "determined
  internally by the content of the point of view"; explicitly not the
  content of his beliefs.
- **scattered individual** — a person whose parts are far apart, here brain
  and body.

## Connections

- **The extended mind** ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md), [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md)). Clark and Chalmers argue from
  coupling and parity that cognitive vehicles can lie outside the skull, and
  infer in one paragraph that the self extends too (their C14). This essay
  stretches the vehicles from the other side: the brain itself is moved out
  of the body and linked by radio, "a mere stretching of the nerves". It
  agrees with them that where the vehicles are does not fix where the person
  is. It differs on what does: for Clark and Chalmers, what is reliably
  coupled and endorsed; for Dennett, the point of view. The two tests can
  come apart. Otto's notebook is coupled but is not where Otto takes himself
  to be. Neither paper cites the other, as far as the two readings show.
  Chalmers's later papers on extended cognition and consciousness, being
  filed in parallel, are where that question is taken up.
- **"The Self as a Center of Narrative Gravity"** ([LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md), [NOTE-364](NOTE-364.md)).
  That essay says what a self is: an abstractum with only grossly defined
  location. This essay's three candidate locations, and its conclusion that
  the location question loses interest once the answer is "two places", fit
  that view. The narrative essay was written later and does not cite this
  one.
- **Functional duplicates.** Hubert is the case that Chalmers's
  fading-qualia and dancing-qualia arguments, being filed in parallel, turn
  into an argument. Dennett switches between brain and program and finds
  "no trace of a difference". Chalmers argues that a switch between
  functionally identical systems that changed experience would have to go
  unnoticed by the subject, which he holds implausible. Dennett's essay
  assumes the conclusion inside the story. It does not argue it.
- **Dennett's intentional-stance and consciousness papers,** being filed in
  parallel, give the theory of point of view and of the narrator's
  "here"-thoughts that this essay only dramatises. *The Mind's I*, also being
  filed, reprints this essay with a reflection by its editors.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) (the bridge): an individuation criterion by coupling.**
  Two brains in lockstep make one person; out of step they make two. That
  makes coupling and synchrony, not material or place, decide how many
  persons there are. It is the person-scale form of Levin's criterion, on
  which parts that share information form one Self and parts that stop
  sharing it fall back into many ([LIT-439](../literature.d/LIT-439.md), [NOTE-341](NOTE-341.md), C3). The
  "scattered individual" is the person-scale form of Schwitzgebel's claim
  that spatial contiguity is not required ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md), [NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md), C2). The essay
  also bears against Clark's high-bandwidth-synchrony requirement as a
  necessary condition, which Schwitzgebel answers in §6 ([NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)). The
  narrator stays one person across Houston and Tulsa, with lags so long he
  cannot track a moving object. These pairings are mine. The essay is about
  persons, not groups.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) (AI consciousness): a scenario, not evidence.** Hubert is a
  program with the brain's "complete information-processing structure",
  and the story treats it as carrying the person. That is the
  computational functionalism [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) says is the disputed premise. The
  essay presupposes it in the fiction and argues for nothing.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **It is a story.** The essay stages intuitions and reports the
  narrator's. Its official answer, C3, is left with the objections it
  raises unresolved.
- **The duplication is stipulated.** Hubert's adequacy is assumed. The
  narrator notes that matching outputs over weeks "did not prove" it.
- **The ending is a reversal, not a result.** The drift scene shows that
  the earlier "nothing happened" was compatible with a second person
  suffering unnoticed. It is the essay's sharpest point. It is not developed.
- **No sources.** The copy read has no notes or citations. The essay names
  Locke only as someone a legal argument "might have appealed to".

## Open questions

- What fixes the content of a point of view, if not the content of beliefs?
  The essay says it is "determined internally" and leaves the rest open.
- At what degree of drift do two synchronised systems become two persons?
  The essay shows the endpoints, not the threshold.
