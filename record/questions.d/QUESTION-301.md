# QUESTION-301: Is acting for one's legacy, for how one is remembered or for people who will live after one's death, self-interest on a self extended in time, as collectivism on the owner's thesis is self-interest on a self extended across people?

## Why it is a question

The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md)) says apparently selfless action serves a
self enlarged to include others. Legacy is the same move in time. Johnston's
*Surviving Death* ([LIT-1759](../literature.d/LIT-1759.md)) made it explicit: the good person, who
identifies with humanity, lives on in its onward rush. Johnston has since
given that account of the self up ([NOTE-1257](../notes.d/NOTE-1257.md)). The record already holds
the owner's own version, "immortality" through influence that reinforces
itself across generations, as [CLAIM-420](../claims.d/CLAIM-420.md).

Four bodies of work bear on it.

- **The self over time, measured.** Hershfield ([LIT-1702](../literature.d/LIT-1702.md)) measures
  continuity with one's future self with overlapping circles adapted from
  Aron's scale, and finds that people who feel less continuous treat their
  future self like another person. That makes prudence an instance of the
  thesis's shape, and links it to Parfit ([THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md)).
- **Legacy motivation.**
  - Generativity, care for the next generation, includes a desire for
    symbolic immortality (McAdams and de St. Aubin, [LIT-1777](../literature.d/LIT-1777.md)).
  - Legacy motivation predicts, and when primed increases, beneficence
    toward future generations (Wade-Benzoni, [LIT-1716](../literature.d/LIT-1716.md); Wade-Benzoni et
    al., [LIT-1760](../literature.d/LIT-1760.md); Zaval et al., [LIT-1724](../literature.d/LIT-1724.md)).
- **Death and the group.** Terror management theory (Burke et al.,
  [LIT-1687](../literature.d/LIT-1687.md)) draws on Lifton's symbolic immortality ([LIT-1714](../literature.d/LIT-1714.md)). On
  it, reminders of death make people identify more with a group they see
  as lasting (Castano et al., [LIT-1719](../literature.d/LIT-1719.md)). The temporal and social
  extensions of the self then meet.
- **Three philosophical readings of concern for what comes after us.**
  - **An extended self.** The thesis's reading: what one cares about after
    death is part of oneself.
  - **Interests that outlast death.** On Pitcher ([LIT-1740](../literature.d/LIT-1740.md)) the living
    person can be harmed by what happens after their death. So acting for
    one's legacy can be self-interested in the ordinary sense, with no
    extended self.
  - **Dependence on a future we will not see.** On Scheffler
    ([LIT-1754](../literature.d/LIT-1754.md)) much of what we value depends on our confidence that
    humanity will go on after we die. He reads this as dependence on
    future people, not altruism toward them: their going on keeps our
    valuing secure ([NOTE-1298](../notes.d/NOTE-1298.md)). It was filed as the hardest case for
    the thesis in time, and the reading makes it less hard.

## What would count as an answer

- **A test that separates the readings.** Measure felt continuity with
  one's legacy or with future generations, and test whether concern for
  them follows that measure or follows caring alone. That is
  [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)'s test applied to time.
- **A case for one reading.** An argument that concern for people one will
  never know cannot be self-interest on any self, or a model showing that
  it can be without triviality.
- **Evidence from the legacy experiments.** If legacy priming raises
  beneficence only through the wish to be remembered, the thesis's
  reading, or Pitcher's, gains. If it raises beneficence also where no one
  will know, Scheffler's gains.

## The wider question

What the living owe the future, as distinct from why they care about their
own legacy, is [QUESTION-299](QUESTION-299.md): longtermism, person-affecting views, the
non-identity problem and Scheffler's grounding of concern in what the
living value.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the legacy works, read

The originals are Unavailable. Works read in their place, and the two read
themselves, change the question in five ways.

- **Scheffler is less hard a case than filed** ([NOTE-1298](../notes.d/NOTE-1298.md);
  [NOTE-1306](../notes.d/NOTE-1306.md)). The Tanner Lectures claim that our valuing depends on
  confidence that others will live after us. Scheffler says the surprise
  shows "less that we have been blinded to our own altruism than ... that
  we have overestimated our independence and self-sufficiency". He
  explains even his preference to die sooner so that humanity survives by
  the limits of individualism, not of egoism. For the thesis, the self
  served would be one's standing as a valuer: a third sense of
  "self-interest", after welfare and after a self-concept kept true. The
  triviality objection applies to it.
- **Pitcher's reading depends on the theory of welfare** (Luper, SEP,
  [NOTE-1294](../notes.d/NOTE-1294.md)). Posthumous harm is the thwarting of desires held while
  alive, so legacy-seeking is self-interest with no extended self only on
  a desire-based theory of welfare. If any satisfied desire counts as
  one's good, it is self-interest by definition.
- **Terror management is weaker support** (Many Labs 4, [NOTE-1322](../notes.d/NOTE-1322.md);
  Chen et al., [NOTE-1271](../notes.d/NOTE-1271.md)). Seventeen labs (N = 1,550) found no
  mortality-salience effect: g = 0.07, 95% CI [−0.03, 0.17]. Corrected for
  publication bias, the meta-analytic effect falls from about r = .35 to
  about .18, or to nothing. Castano et al. ([NOTE-1297](../notes.d/NOTE-1297.md)) state the join
  of the social and temporal self as a conjecture: a lasting group lets
  the self "project themselves in space and time, beyond their personal
  death". Their one experiment (N = 48) builds on that contested effect
  and measures no self.
- **Legacy motivation is real but not yet separated from caring.**
  - Generativity includes a need for symbolic immortality (Kruse and
    Schmitt, [NOTE-1261](../notes.d/NOTE-1261.md)). A recent validation separates a three-item
    "Legacy" factor from generative action (Sandoval-Obando et al.,
    [NOTE-1260](../notes.d/NOTE-1260.md)), but its items are not printed.
  - Wade-Benzoni's experiments ([NOTE-1273](../notes.d/NOTE-1273.md)) never measure legacy
    concern and generosity in the same study. Two of their p-values do
    not match their statistics.
  - Hurlstone et al.'s legacy messages ([NOTE-1279](../notes.d/NOTE-1279.md)) bundle
    identification with generations, a moral appeal and one's own death,
    so they cannot tell the thesis's reading from Pitcher's.
  - Symbolic immortality, as Sturesteps measured it (skimmed,
    [NOTE-1312](../notes.d/NOTE-1312.md)), is consolation that goes with accepting death as
    final, not a motive to act for the future.
- **The self over time is measured as the thesis needs** (Hershfield,
  [NOTE-1259](../notes.d/NOTE-1259.md)). Felt similarity to one's future self predicts patience
  where directly reported caring did not, but that caring was near
  ceiling. Hershfield guesses that the future self may continue in one's
  offspring, which is the untested bridge from prudence to legacy.

The question's third philosophical reading is reworded to match Scheffler:
dependence on a future we will not see, not care for a future that does
nothing for us.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Zaval's legacy measures, read at second hand

Wickersham et al. ([NOTE-1316](../notes.d/NOTE-1316.md)), read in place of Zaval et al.
([LIT-1724](../literature.d/LIT-1724.md)), reuse its legacy writing task and Legacy Motivation Scale
with 988 adults.

- **The effect replicates.** The task raised pro-environmental intentions
  and hypothetical donations at every age, and the scale partly mediated
  it.
- **The scale does not isolate the wish to be remembered.** Of its eight
  items, two are about being remembered, four about self-regard, and two
  about responsibility and connection to future generations. Only the
  total is analysed.
- **A parallel route.** The task also raised love of nature, which
  carried more of the donation effect than legacy motivation did (0.138
  against 0.097, in the supplement's two-mediator model).
- **The question's test is still untried.** No condition varies whether
  anyone will know what one did.
