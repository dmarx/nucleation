# QUESTION-531: Do contract formation, persistence and modification need terms of their own in the record's cost model of stored work, or are they already there under other names, ambiguous invocation of a relationship among them?

The owner's words, at U21: "let's integrate contract formation,
persistence, and modification in our cost model. It might actually already
be in there already under other names like ambiguous invocation of
relationship".

## What the record held

The owner's suspicion was checked on 2026-11-24 by searching the record and
the scratch tree for "ambiguous invocation", "invocation of a
relationship", "relational invocation" and their variants, and for
ambiguity near relation, role, address and frame.

- **Formation, persistence and modification: held, for any form.**
  [CLAIM-569](../claims.d/CLAIM-569.md) separates the resources to construct a form, to maintain it
  and to move into or out of it, none fixing another. [CLAIM-761](../claims.d/CLAIM-761.md) makes a
  stabilized constraint stored work, conditional on "the costs of
  constructing, keeping, retrieving, verifying and revising" it. [CASE-326](../cases.d/CASE-326.md)
  and [CLAIM-891](../claims.d/CLAIM-891.md) price these as F, U, ρ, v, σ, R and ε; [CLAIM-892](../claims.d/CLAIM-892.md) prices
  erosion. [TERM-116](../terms.d/TERM-116.md)'s incoming and outgoing profiles make revision cost
  directed, and [CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md) shows a transition can cost more than either
  endpoint. A contract read as a stabilized relational constraint inherits
  all of this. On this half the suspicion is right: A30 §1 says "we
  probably do not need an entirely separate contract cost model".
- **Ambiguous invocation of a relationship: not held.** No entry uses the
  phrase or a synonym, and A30 §3 and A32 §21 report the same ("I still
  did not locate your exact phrase"). The nearest entries are about
  something else: [CLAIM-044](../claims.d/CLAIM-044.md) (ambiguity against contextuality, for
  utterances), [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) and [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md) (a communicative act constituted by
  its relations to roles and norms), [TERM-030](../terms.d/TERM-030.md) (the frame), [CLAIM-201](../claims.d/CLAIM-201.md)'s
  example of a rendering deciding "who is addressed and in what
  relationship", [CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md) (an interaction inviting a new way of relating),
  [TERM-165](../terms.d/TERM-165.md) (dispositional address) and [CLAIM-728](../claims.d/CLAIM-728.md) (residual control over
  uncovered contingencies). None prices which relationship an act invokes.
- **What the stored-work model leaves out.** Its store is fixed between
  uses: staleness σ and erosion ε are exogenous ([CASE-326](../cases.d/CASE-326.md): "ε is a fixed
  fraction"). A contract's invocation can change the contract.

## What would count as an answer

- **Already there**, with each contractual cost shown to be one of
  [CASE-326](../cases.d/CASE-326.md)'s terms under a richer state, including the cost of resolving
  which relationship is invoked (a retrieval or verification cost) and the
  change an invocation makes (a revision).
- **Terms of their own**, with a case the existing terms misprice: two
  relationships of matched per-use cost and output whose later costs part
  because one invocation changed the relationship and the other did not.

A30's answer is between the two: [CLAIM-1319](../claims.d/CLAIM-1319.md) (invocation is stateful,
and the model needs it to return the next state), [CLAIM-1243](../claims.d/CLAIM-1243.md) (three
kinds of ambiguity about which relationship applies) and [CLAIM-1317](../claims.d/CLAIM-1317.md)
(control over resolving the ambiguity). [ARG-246](../arguments.d/ARG-246.md) says what [CASE-326](../cases.d/CASE-326.md)
does and does not price, and [LEAD-237](../leads.d/LEAD-237.md) is the test that would settle
it.
