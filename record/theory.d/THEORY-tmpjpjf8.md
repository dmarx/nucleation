# THEORY-tmpjpjf8: Moral judgment is usually caused by quick affective intuitions, and moral reasoning is usually a post-hoc search for supporting reasons that changes judgments mainly by triggering intuitions in other people

## Source

Haidt (2001), [LIT-511](../literature.d/LIT-511.md) (*Psychological Review* 108(4)), read in [NOTE-403](../notes.d/NOTE-403.md).

## What was actually shown

**The model (pp. 7–8).** Six links: intuitive judgment, post-hoc reasoning,
reasoned persuasion of others, social persuasion, reasoned judgment and
private reflection. Moral judgment consists "primarily of links 1 through 4".
Reasoning overriding intuition (link 5) is rare. Private reflection works
mostly by role-taking, which triggers a competing intuition (link 6).

**Four problems for rationalism.** Affective evaluation is fast and pervasive.
Reasoning is motivated: people are "intuitive lawyers". People confabulate
reasons for their behaviour (Nisbett & Wilson). Moral reasoning predicts
moral action weakly once IQ is partialled out. Most of this evidence comes
from outside morality, as the paper says.

**Harmless wrongs (p. 5).** In Haidt, Koller & Dias (1993), Americans and
Brazilians condemned harmless offensive acts, and affect predicted the
judgments better than claims of harm. Haidt & Hersh found the same for
sexual morality, with "moral dumbfounding".

**Manipulated intuition (p. 17).** Wheatley & Haidt: a hypnotically induced
pang of disgust attached to a word raised condemnation of stories containing
it, in a small sample of highly hypnotizable participants.

**What could have come out otherwise.** People could have withdrawn
condemnation once they agreed that no harm was done. They did not.

## What this does not say

- **That this is established.** The author calls the model "a plausible
  alternative ... not ... an established fact" (pp. 2–3).
- **That disgust drives moral condemnation.** The hypnosis study is small,
  and later work finds disgust does not amplify condemnation (Landy &
  Goodwin's meta-analysis and replications, reviewed in [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md), [NOTE-132](../notes.d/NOTE-132.md)).
- **That reasoning never changes judgments.** Links 5 and 6 allow it. Because
  they do, any such case fits the model, which only says they are rare.
- **That moral action is driven by emotion rather than reasoning.** The
  argument is from correlations and clinical cases (psychopathy, VMPFC
  damage), not experiments.
- **Anything about why morality has the content it has.** It is a proximate
  account and is compatible with a cooperative ultimate function, which it
  invokes (pp. 18–19).
