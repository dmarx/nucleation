# NOTE-1543: Lucas & Griffiths — Learning the form of causal relationships with hierarchical Bayesian models

## Contribution

Earlier work showed that prior knowledge of how causes combine (their
functional form: disjunctive, conjunctive, noisy) shapes causal
induction. This paper gives an account of how that knowledge is itself
learned and carried to new variables: inference over abstract theories in
a hierarchical Bayesian model. In the authors' words, "This account of
how knowledge of functional form can be acquired is the main novel
contribution of our approach" (§4.2). Five experiments test its two
qualitative predictions: that people make inferences appropriate to more
than one functional form, and that evidence about one set of variables
changes inferences about a disjoint set.

## Key insight

Functional form lives in the parameterization of a causal graphical
model, so it can be lifted out of any one set of objects and held as a
theory t that defines P(h|t) and P(d|h,t). Learning P(t|d(1)) from one
data set then changes judgments about a second data set with entirely
new objects, which no model without the theory level can predict,
"because the events in d (1) do not involve any of the prospective
causes in d (2)" (§5).

## Assumptions

- **Setting (§4.1).** Variables come in two known classes, prospective
  causes and effects; only cause-to-effect links; events i.i.d. given the
  structure; cause presence independent of the structure.
- **The theory space (§5).** Blickets are exchangeable: activation
  probability is a sigmoid in the number n of blickets present,
  1/(1+exp{−γ(n−b)}), with bias b and gain γ. The family is monotone, so a
  form in which causes act independently but not jointly cannot be
  expressed.
- **Priors.** P(h|t) uniform over structures; exponential priors on b and
  γ with two hyperparameters (λb = 4.329, λg = 0.299), chosen to minimize
  squared error against the same ratings the model is scored on.
- **Stance.** Rational analysis: judgments are compared with the optimal
  solution at the computational level.

## Key results

- **Abstract.** "The Bayesian model accurately predicted human judgments
  and outperformed several alternative models."
- **Experiment 1 (§6.2; N = 57, structure given in training).** Model
  "mean-squared error was 0.38, and the linear correlation between the
  ratings and participants’ judgments was 0.99". ΔP and causal power give
  "overall mean-squared error per rating of 5.83 and a correlation of 0.82
  with human ratings"; the best training-blind predictions would "yield a
  mean-squared error of 3.97 and a linear correlation of 0.83". The
  conjunctive condition's rating of D was above 5 (t[16] = 3.15), which
  the authors call "inconsistent with all linear and noisy-disjunctive
  interpretations".
- **Experiment 2 (§7.2; N = 102, structure inferred too).** "Contrary to
  our predictions, the mean rating given to object E was not higher in the
  noisy-disjunctive condition than in the conjunctive condition". Model
  MSE 0.29, r = 0.99; but "the best-possible training-blind predictions
  were better than in Experiment 1, giving an MSE of 0.63 and a
  correlation of 0.97".
- **Experiment 3 (§8.2; N = 41, noise stated or shown).** "The correlation
  between the ratings of participants and the predictions of the model was
  0.98, and the mean-squared error was 0.55." The aggregated D < E effect
  is marginal (p = 0.049); the event-based condition alone is a trend.
- **Experiment 4 (§9.2; N = 41, failure rates).** "The mean rating for D
  was higher in the high-noise condition (3.8, SD 2.4) than in the
  low-noise condition (1.8, SD 2.4)"; r = 0.98, MSE 0.52.
- **Experiment 5 (§10.2; N = 60, survey).** The ANOVA "revealed a main
  effect of changing domains between the training and test blocks"
  (F[1,56] = 12.8): transfer is larger within a domain.
- **Robustness (§11.1).** "it is important that the mean of the prior on
  the bias be low (1/kb > 0.35), but the mean of the gain distribution is
  not especially important" (kb is λb in the extraction): the fits need a
  prior that favours disjunctive forms.
- **A generic prior in the same hierarchy (§11.3.1).** With the
  necessary-and-sufficient prior of Lu et al., "this model failed to make
  the key prediction that people would infer that two blickets were
  necessary to activate the meter in the Experiment 2 conditions" (MSE
  0.71, about twice the sigmoid model's).
- **Individuals (§11.2).** Rating distributions generated by
  probability-matching samples of hypotheses match the individual (D, E)
  rating pairs, answering the worry that fits to means are "artifacts of
  averaging".
- **Scope (§5).** "Our goal was thus not to define a general purpose
  model that could explain all instances of learning of functional form".

## Concepts

- **Functional form**: "the nature of the relationship between causes and
  effects—and specifically on how causes interact in producing their
  effects" (§1), carried by the likelihood of a causal graphical model.
- **Theory**: abstract knowledge that identifies "types of objects, the
  plausibility of causal relationships, and the form of those
  relationships" (§4.2); formally, t defines P(h|t) and P(d|h,t).
- **Disjunctive and conjunctive** (note 2): disjunctive, the effect's
  probability rises at most linearly in the number of causes; conjunctive,
  it rises sharply once some number above one is exceeded. Noisy-OR and
  noisy-AND are special cases.
- **Bias and gain**: b is the number of blickets at which activation is as
  likely as not; γ its steepness. b = 0.5 with large γ is deterministic OR,
  b = 1.5 with large γ deterministic AND.

## Connections

It builds on causal graphical models and their parameterizations (Cheng's
causal power, ΔP, noisy-OR), on the blicket-detector paradigm of Gopnik
and colleagues, and on hierarchical Bayesian models of abstract knowledge
(Tenenbaum, Griffiths, Kemp). The authors call Kemp, Goodman and
Tenenbaum's causal schemata "complementary" (§11.5): that work learns
which objects belong to which causal types, this one how causes of a type
combine. Jiang & Lucas (2024), [LIT-2014](../literature.d/LIT-2014.md), read the same day, takes
this paper's sigmoid theory space into active learning. For individual
responses it adopts probability matching (Vul & Pashler 2008), the
response rule [NOTE-599](NOTE-599.md) also argues for. Its rational-analysis framing is
shared with [LIT-1071](../literature.d/LIT-1071.md) (Lieder & Griffiths) and [LIT-1056](../literature.d/LIT-1056.md) (Gershman &
Goodman), as background only.

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization.** A's "tested learning about different
  functional forms of causal relationships in five experiments. Its model
  successfully predicted judgments and outperformed several alternatives"
  is A's paraphrase, close to the abstract, not a sentence of the paper.
  It is accurate as far as it goes, and placing the work beside Kemp,
  Goodman & Tenenbaum is fair. Corrections:
  - **Authors.** A writes "Lucas and colleagues"; there are two authors,
    Lucas and Griffiths.
  - **The alternatives are weak.** They are ΔP and causal power, which by
    construction cannot vary between conditions; the best training-blind
    constant predictions; and an HBM with a generic prior. In Experiment 2
    the training-blind baseline came close (MSE 0.63 against 0.29). The
    fits are in-sample, with two hyperparameters tuned on all the mean
    ratings.
  - **Left out.** The domain-specificity of transfer (Experiment 5); the
    reversed prediction of Experiment 2; the need for a
    disjunction-favouring prior; and the authors' own statement that the
    sigmoid model is not a general account.
- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md)** (identifying latent higher-level perspectives from
  members' activity): unrelated in subject; a structural analogue at most,
  inference to an abstract level shared across data sets with disjoint
  variables. Neither supports nor qualifies it.

## Limitations

- **The authors' own (§11.6).** The sigmoid theory space is narrow and
  monotone, not intended as a general account; priors over richer function
  classes are future work; generality across cover stories was only partly
  tested (Experiment 5).
- **Samples.** 17–28 per condition, Berkeley undergraduates throughout;
  live demonstrations by an experimenter working the meter with a foot
  switch; one test block shared across conditions.
- **Fits.** To condition means, with hyperparameters tuned on the data
  evaluated; no held-out test. The rivals mostly cannot predict any
  condition effect. The authors argue that the question is which region of
  parameter space works, not overfitting (§11.1).
- **Mixed results.** One predicted contrast reversed (Experiment 2), later
  explained by a preference for deterministic forms (Experiment 3); one
  key effect marginal (Experiment 3, p = 0.049).
- **An alternative reading.** A noisy-OR with a near-universal blicket base
  rate could mimic part of the D/E pattern; the authors rule it out using
  object E (note 6).
