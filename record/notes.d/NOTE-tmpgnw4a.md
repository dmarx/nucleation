# NOTE-tmpgnw4a: Feenberg (2002) — Transforming Technology (Preface and Chapter 6)

## Contribution

A critical theory of technology against both neutrality and determinism.

## Key results

1. Preface, p. v: the issue is "the variety of possible technologies and paths of progress among which we must choose".
2. Preface, p. ix: "Computer design is now political design."
3. p. 141: "The idea that industrial technology is irredeemable is essentially determinist."
4. p. 143: "Technological development is a scene of social struggle".
5. p. 152: the capitalist economic code "rewards deskilling".

## Assumptions

Design choices reflect the values of dominant groups and can be contested.

## Concepts

Technical code; ambivalence; democratic rationalization (not in the pages read); overdetermination.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmp6wuql](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6wuql.md)**: corroborates its premise at the level of economic codes: a code that rewards deskilling chooses designs that shrink workers' capacities, and one that counted skill would choose otherwise (result 5).
- **[CLAIM-tmpu2cwc](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpu2cwc.md)**: supports its premise that governing standards (the technical code) are contingent and revisable; no comparative evidence.
- **[CLAIM-tmpbypt7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md)**: consistent with it and shifts the emphasis: lock-in depends on the prevailing code, not on the technology.
- **The LIT** is the second, revised edition of Critical Theory of Technology (1991); corrected there.

## Limitations

Two parts of the book; chapter 7, where democratic rationalization is concluded, not read.
