# THEORY-tmpts7sl: The restrictions on syntax reaching into words that a separate word-formation component was credited with are restrictions on heads versus phrases and on what individual affixes select, and phrases feed word formation, so a single combinatorial component can build both words and phrases

## Source

Bruening (2018), [LIT-071](../literature.d/LIT-071.md) (*Language* 94(1), 1–42), read in full in
[NOTE-065](../notes.d/NOTE-065.md), with the abstract and opening of Müller's reply (2018,
*Language* 94(1):e54–e66).

## What was actually shown

**The target.** The lexicalist hypothesis in one sense only: a separate,
encapsulated word component that feeds syntax one way (p. 1). Not
"lexicalist" in the sense of rich lexical entries, of which the paper has
"absolutely nothing to say" (p. 2). Words are granted for the argument
(fn. 2).

**Wrong, on three counts.**
- *Phrases feed word formation (§2).* Phrasal compounds (*a don't-you-dare!
  look*), zero-derivation from phrases (*know-it-all*, *Bonnie and Clyded*)
  and overt derivation (*do-it-yourselfer*, *unputdownable*) are
  productive, obey phrasal syntax inside, and stay open to anaphora ((12)).
  The "quotation" defence fails, because the encapsulation it gives is
  strict cyclicity, which holds of every stem ((15)–(18)).
- *Phrasal operations reach sub-word units (§3).* Word-part ellipsis
  (*pre- and post-revolutionary*) respects morpheme boundaries ((24)–(29))
  and is the process seen in right-node raising. Focus targets morphemes
  (*a-moral, not im-moral*).
- *The claimed differences in principle fail (§4).* Locality (*self-*),
  adjunct scope (Venda causative, Japanese *-sugiro*), headedness (*enrage*,
  *two years ago*) and idiosyncrasy (multiword expressions at least as
  numerous as words, fn. 17) do not separate words from phrases.

**Superfluous (§5).** A formal X0, "a segment of a syntactic node that
includes nothing but a single bundle of morphosyntactic features" ((62),
p. 25), makes complex heads and phrase-plus-null-N0 both X0s. Then:
extraction targets only XPs, which rules out word parts and also whole
words ((55)–(58)); apparent X0 coordination is phrasal (the *but did not*
test); inbound anaphoric islands follow from pro-forms' lack of content;
the no-phrase constraint is affix-specific selection, with complement
preservation tracking it ((89)–(95)). The resulting typology "makes no
reference to the notion of a word" (p. 29). Parsimony then favours one
component, "assuming that they are equivalent in their empirical
coverage" (p. 1).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Phrasal input to word formation
could have been confined to listed or quotative cases, and word-part
ellipsis could have ignored morpheme boundaries. In English, neither
happened.

## What this does not say

- **That the case is made beyond English.** Other languages are cited from
  secondary literature, often in a sentence. Müller's reply presses
  exactly this, and the paper expects "there may be languages where
  extraction can target an X0" (p. 26).
- **That every sub-argument stands.** The German resultative nominalization
  is "not decisive" (p. 12), derived nouns' structure "requires more
  research" (p. 31), the analysis of *-ness* may have "a better analysis"
  (p. 35), and fn. 9 leaves non-quotative phrasal compounds open. Müller
  disputes X0 extraction, phrasal coordination and the resultatives.
- **That every lexicalist framework is refuted.** "Superfluous" is shown
  case by case for Bresnan and Mchombo, Williams, Newmeyer and Chomsky
  1970. The author says the criticisms "may not apply to certain other
  approaches" (p. 2).
- **That one component is simpler overall.** The X0/XP replacement needs
  null heads, segment-based adjunction and extraction blind to X0s. That
  it is cheaper than a lexical component is argued, not measured.
- **That there is no cross-linguistic word.** That is Haspelmath's claim
  ([THEORY-tmp9vso8](THEORY-tmp9vso8.md)). Bruening sets it aside, so this account survives if
  Haspelmath is wrong, and the reverse.
- **That lexical items are not form–meaning–syntax triads.** Bruening
  declines that sense of lexicalism. The second assumption Krauska and Lau
  reject ([THEORY-tmpn6aoc](THEORY-tmpn6aoc.md)) gets no support here.

- Haspelmath ([THEORY-tmp9vso8](THEORY-tmp9vso8.md)) reaches the same verdict on lexical
  integrity from typology, and calls it partly circular (his §8). The two
  use the same diagnostics: word-part coordination, Georgian inbound
  anaphora and extraction.
- Krauska and Lau ([THEORY-tmpn6aoc](THEORY-tmpn6aoc.md)) take this account's conclusion, one
  component for words and phrases, as a premise for a claim about
