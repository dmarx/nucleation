# CLAIM-652: An organization that weighs a harmful practice by the failures its present users report can expand the practice with no act against complaint: as the harmed leave, the recognized cost of the practice falls with their share, so the score favours more of it, and the failures it records grow more slowly than the harm it does

## The claim

Let a practice x harm a group whose members complain and whose exit rises
with x. If the organization sets x by a score that subtracts a penalty for
the complaints of users still present, the penalty's slope is proportional
to the harmed group's present share s. Two decreasing maps then compose
into an increasing one: x*(s) falls with s, and s(x) falls with x through
exit. So x ↦ x*(s(x)) is increasing, and a myopic organization that starts
at x*(s0) climbs.

Two things follow:

1. **The practice expands with no counter-corrective act.** Complaints
   are counted honestly, and no channel is narrowed. This is a route to
   [QUESTION-441](../questions.d/QUESTION-441.md)'s non-legibility that does not need [CLAIM-619](CLAIM-619.md)'s
   disposition or [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md)'s reproduction of representations by any act
   beyond allocation.
2. **The record understates the harm by a growing factor.** Recognized
   failures need not fall. In [CASE-246](../cases.d/CASE-246.md) they more than double. But the
   fraction of the harmed who remain to report shrinks with x, so the
   failures recorded grow more slowly than the harm done.

## Evidence

[CASE-246](../cases.d/CASE-246.md): from x = 0.05 to x = 0.223, the harmed share falling from
0.30 to 0.185, with a control that does not see exit staying at 0.05. The
monotonicity argument above shows the drift does not depend on its
parameters, given exit rising with x and an interior optimum.

## What it does not say

That any organization does this, or that it is a wrong. Whether keeping
such a score breaches a duty is [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)'s question, and it depends on
the duty. That the drift is unbounded: it stops at a fixed point.
