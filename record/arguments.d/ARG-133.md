# ARG-133: Transtrum et al. → sloppiness is not singularity

## The inference

[CLAIM-813](../claims.d/CLAIM-813.md): sloppiness (a spread of nonzero Fisher eigenvalues in a
regular model) and singularity (rank loss on a stratum) are different
properties. Its defeater: "A model in which a spread of nonzero Fisher
eigenvalues forces rank loss somewhere in the parameter space". The
readings give:

- The hyperribbon reading the claim cites: geodesic widths falling
  geometrically, tracking √λ in natural units ([THEORY-2155](../theory.d/THEORY-2155.md),
  [THEORY-2156](../theory.d/THEORY-2156.md)).
- Linear polynomial fitting, with its Hilbert-matrix Hessian, among the
  2011 paper's sloppy examples (Fig. 2b), and its manifold a hyper-ellipse
  when the coefficients are confined to a ball: the claim's "sloppy
  everywhere, singular nowhere".
- And: "Model boundaries correspond to points on the manifold where the
  metric is singular."

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** The last
  item is the one that could defeat it: if a sloppy model's manifold must
  be bounded and its boundaries are singular points, sloppiness would
  force rank loss. It does not, for three reasons in the papers
  themselves: "Typical boundaries occur when parameters are near their
  extreme values (such as ±∞ or zero)", outside the parameter space
  proper ("boundaries typically do not exist (i.e. they lie at parameter
  values equal to ∞)"); finite singular points are generic only when
  M < 2N; and the linear model has no boundary at all unless one is
  imposed (the paper's Σθ² ≤ 1). So the claim's defeater is not met.
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its rival?** The rival, "sloppiness
  is singularity", is refuted by the linear case the papers themselves
  call sloppy. The other rival, "unrelated", is refuted by the edges, which
  the claim's own "What it does not say" already grants.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** The claim's "regular"
  is a property at the point of evaluation, in the interior; the papers'
  singular points are at the edges. The two descriptions agree once that
  is said: a sloppy model is regular in its interior, and its manifold's
  edges, where present, are where the metric degenerates.

## Verdict

Supports the claim, with the qualification that sloppiness and metric
singularity are tied at the boundary of a bounded sloppy manifold, mostly
at infinity in parameter space and at finite folds in some models. The
THEORYs replace the unread LITs in the claim's grounds. Proposed.

## Note of 2026-11-21: Transtrum & Qiu (2014), read

Supports, with the method's boundary lists ([NOTE-2253](../notes.d/NOTE-2253.md)): the Ising
chain's boundaries are all at Jμ → ±∞, two of the three kinds for a sum of
exponentials at a rate going to 0 or ∞, and only the merging of two terms
at finite values. The reduced EGFR model has "lack of small eigenvalues":
the method runs from a sloppy regular interior to a degenerate boundary
and ends regular again, which fits sloppiness being the interior's and
singularity the boundary's.
