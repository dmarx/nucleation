# CASE-036: Northstar emergency governance that sustains itself

## The case

Deployments frozen, production access restricted, review suspended. Deferred
maintenance and workarounds accumulate, the system becomes harder to read,
and the anomalous signals it emits satisfy the emergency criteria.

## What it can show

That the operative regime and its governing organization can persist while the
capacity to transform them degrades. It is the premise of [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md).

Not shown: that the anomalies come from the regime rather than from the
underlying failure. The first draft's test for that ("contributes materially")
did not survive the rewrite.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the emergency that controls its own evidence

At U22 of the exchange "Graph Learning Applications" the owner asked to
revisit Northstar, and A22 called this "the most philosophically fertile
original variant". It sharpened what the case shows: "**A system can lose
the ability to correct a protective policy because the policy controls
access to the evidence required for its correction.**" Deferred
maintenance and restricted access, adopted for the emergency, produce the
anomalies that extend it. That is [CLAIM-349](../claims.d/CLAIM-349.md)'s overcontrol, which buys
freedom from harmful acts by cutting access, turned on the evidence for
lifting the control itself.

A24 named the missing capacity: flexibility depends on how readily a
regime acquires authority and "also on **the conditions under which they
relinquish it**". [TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md) names suspension and not release. The
documented counterpart is the mayday at Brookhollow ([CASE-tmpqq6jz](CASE-tmpqq6jz.md)), where
the exchange reported, unverified, that no clear announcement ended the
mayday phase.
