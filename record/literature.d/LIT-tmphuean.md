# LIT-tmphuean: Energy flow and the organization of life

Harold Morowitz and D. Eric Smith (2007), *Complexity* 13(1):51–59
(September 2007; online 8 October 2007). First issued as Santa Fe
Institute Working Paper 2006-08-029 (dated 7 August 2006), the version
read.

The brief's citation (Complexity 13(1):51–59, 2007) is correct. The brief
offered either this paper or Morowitz's *Energy Flow in Biology* (Academic
Press, 1968). I chose the paper because the book could not be read: the
Internet Archive holds four scans of it (1968 and 1979 printings), all
lending-only, and no lawful copy was found. The book is not filed.

## Key takeaways

- **The thesis.** "The continuous generation of sources of free energy by
  abiotic processes may have forced life into existence as a means to
  alleviate the buildup of free energy stresses." If so, life "had to
  emerge on the earth", and its early steps would recur on any similar
  planet.
- **The analogy.** Lightning and hurricanes are non-equilibrium channels
  that carry current (charge, heat) between reservoirs much faster than
  diffusion does. Metabolism is such a channel "in the chemical domain".
- **The evidence from metabolism.** A universal anabolic core of fewer than
  500 small molecules; the reductive citric-acid cycle at its root, which
  is network-autocatalytic for carbon from CO₂; a cascade from it to amino
  acids, sugars and fatty acids that "leaves no energetic waste products".
  Read as a relaxation channel for redox and phosphate energy.
- **The framework.** Jaynes's maximum entropy with entropies that depend
  on currents, which can predict that currents form. This is set against
  entropy-production principles, which "ultimately fail as general
  principles". Positive feedback gives thresholds that are phase
  transitions "equivalent in all statistical respects to equilibrium phase
  transitions".
- **The "collapse to life".** The abiotic earth was metastable under
  continuous geochemical free-energy production; the emergence of life was
  its collapse to a more stable state, more like phase separation in an
  alloy than a new "state". Major transitions (photosynthesis,
  endosymbiosis, multicellularity) are further, irreversible collapses.
- **Metabolism first.** Selective advantage came from interaction with
  metabolites before replication was coordinated, which the authors note
  is "directly opposite" to the RNA-world premise. Cells and individuality
  are "a by-product of cellular form".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as part of filling out the
record's coverage of the "life as dissipation" literature. No anthology
topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for machine-learning
practice.

[NOTE-tmpsa53y](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpsa53y.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is a programmatic essay. Its value is the specific picture
of core metabolism as a geochemical channel and the explicit rejection of
entropy-production principles in favour of Jaynesian entropy. It proves
nothing new; the formal results it leans on are Smith's earlier papers,
which the record does not hold.

Where it bears on what the record holds:

- **The demarcation question ([LIT-192](LIT-192.md), [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)).** The paper's aim is "an
  essential continuity between physics, chemistry, and biology". Life is a
  lightning bolt "through the graph of geochemical reactions". The only
  distinguishing feature it allows is that life's chemical substrate has
  "a complex structure and informational richness exceeding that of other
  physical systems". It draws no principled line, and it treats the
  individuality the demarcation debate turns on as a late by-product.
- **Schrödinger ([LIT-tmpncmdx](LIT-tmpncmdx.md), Deferred).** Cited for the "paradox" that
  biomass has lower equilibrium entropy than its abiotic matter. The paper
  says the paradox comes from using equilibrium entropy on a driven earth.
- **The rest of the batch.** Like Schneider & Kay ([LIT-tmpq5e95](LIT-tmpq5e95.md)), it reads
  the biosphere as a channel for energy flow. Both reject
  extremum principles on entropy production, but they replace them
  differently: Schneider & Kay with a "restated second law" about gradients,
  this paper with Jaynesian entropy maximization over states that include
  currents. Both use the hurricane as the non-living analogue.
- **Self-organization in the record.** The reductive citric-acid cycle as
  a network-autocatalytic engine is the metabolic counterpart of the
  primitive catalytic cycles in [LIT-036](LIT-036.md), which are self-organized
  spatially rather than chemically.
- **"What Lives?" ([LIT-211](LIT-211.md)).** Fits the "Dissipative Self-Organizing
  Systems" cluster ([NOTE-109](../notes.d/NOTE-109.md)), with a metabolism-first emphasis.
