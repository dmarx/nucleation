# ADR-055: endosymbiosis joins the topics

## Context

The exchange of 2026-10-27, "Repository coverage assessment", found that
the record had no treatment of reductive genome evolution or of the
endosymbiont-to-organelle transition. Eighteen works it cited were
registered the same day (PR [#337](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/337), curation entry of 2026-10-27). They were
tagged with the nearest words the list had:

- `ecology` for the symbioses, under ADR-028's "symbiosis and host–microbe
  partnerships";
- `natural-sciences` for everything else: genome reduction, organelle
  import and loss, eukaryogenesis.

The exchange had warned that `ecology` and `individuation` "would otherwise
obscure their shared mechanism". The mechanism is how a partner loses
autonomy, by gene loss, drift, gene transfer or host control, and the
filing showed the problem. The Black Queen paper and *Monocercomonoides*
landed under different words, though both are about what losing functions
does to an organization. The eukaryogenesis papers sat under
`natural-sciences` with physics and astronomy. ADR-028 had anticipated
this: "Split it when the holdings warrant it." On 2026-10-27 the owner
asked for the topic, and a further seventeen works came with it.

## Decision

Add **`endosymbiosis`** (label: *Endosymbiosis and reductive evolution*).
It covers how one organism comes to live inside another and depend on it,
and how that ends:

- reductive genome evolution and its causes, whether drift, deletional
  bias or adaptive loss. This includes free-living microbes that come to
  depend on a community (the Black Queen Hypothesis);
- complementary gene loss across partners, nested symbioses, and symbiont
  lineage splitting and replacement;
- endosymbiotic gene transfer, protein import and the host's control of
  symbiont division and transmission;
- organellogenesis, including organelles in the making, and organelle loss;
- eukaryogenesis as the founding case.

Tags:

- Works whose subject this is take `endosymbiosis` first. That covers the
  thirty-five works of 2026-10-27, apart from the four adjacent ones on
  cooperation, branching processes and transmission conflict, which take it
  second or not at all.
- QUESTION-277 and the six CASEs filed with this decision take it.
- Other documents are retagged as they are next touched, as ADR-028
  arranged. NOTE-114, NOTE-159, LIT-210 and LIT-528 mention endosymbiosis
  in passing and are not retagged.

`ecology`'s blurb now points here, so it is clear which word a work goes
under. The ecology of a partnership is `ecology`, and its evolution into
dependence is `endosymbiosis`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep `ecology` plus `natural-sciences`.** This is what the first filing
  did, and it split one mechanism across two words.
- **`reductive-evolution` and `organelle-evolution` as two words.** The
  exchange offered both names. Its thesis, though, is that reduction and
  integration are one subject whose dimensions come apart, so two words
  would file one argument twice. Split it when the holdings warrant it.
- **`symbiosis`.** This is broader. Ecological mutualisms and holobionts
  are already `ecology`, and the new word is for the intimate,
  dependency-forming end.
