# LIT-tmpxug14: Ketamine promotes strategic and efficient approach-avoidance conflict resolution

Vestring et al. (2026). Communications Medicine 6, 605. DOI
10.1038/s43856-026-01998-5.

## Key takeaways

- Both ketamine (β = 0.31, p = 0.007) and lorazepam (β = 0.48,
  p = 0.001) raised approach rates against the drug-free session.
- Under ketamine approach declined less steeply with threat probability
  (β = 0.13, p = 0.005) and more steeply with loss magnitude
  (β = −0.17, p = 0.001); lorazepam flattened both slopes.
- Participants "were not told the specific probabilities and had to learn
  them from experience", and no rating of the represented threat was
  taken.

## Standing in the record

Filed by [LEAD-047](../leads.d/LEAD-047.md)'s search as the nearest design of a second kind: an
intervention on the arbitration of an approach-avoidance conflict with the
objective threat set by the experimenter. It does not measure or fix the
represented threat, so it cannot separate authority from belief for
[CLAIM-446](../claims.d/CLAIM-446.md).
