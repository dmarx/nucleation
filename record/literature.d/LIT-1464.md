# LIT-1464: The Nature and Dynamics of Relevance and Valence Appraisals: Theoretical Advances and Recent Evidence

Klaus R. Scherer (2013), *Emotion Review* 5(2):150–162 —
DOI-10.1177/1754073912468166

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* From the abstract:

- "In this contribution three issues have been chosen for discussion: (a)
  varieties of relevance detection, (b) varieties of valence appraisal,
  and (c) sequential-cumulative effects of appraisal results."
- New theoretical proposals and an update on the sequence of appraisal
  processes. "Special emphasis is placed on nonverbal signatures of
  appraisal processes."

The varieties of valence are read in Shuman, Sander and Scherer
([LIT-1458](LIT-1458.md), [NOTE-1108](../notes.d/NOTE-1108.md)): intrinsic pleasantness,
goal conduciveness, power, self-congruence and moral goodness, each a
separate valenced check. The sequence evidence, intrinsic pleasantness
before goal conduciveness, is read in Grandjean, Sander and Scherer
([NOTE-1115](../notes.d/NOTE-1115.md)) and Scherer and Moors
([NOTE-1106](../notes.d/NOTE-1106.md)).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the objection that the record's potential account
tracks 'wanting', not 'liking' ([CLAIM-438](../claims.d/CLAIM-438.md), against [CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md)).
What this paper would add over its readings elsewhere is Scherer's own
statement of why intrinsic pleasantness is a relevance check and goal
conduciveness an implications check. If the record answers the objection
by assigning its account to goal-conduciveness valence, this is the paper
to cite for the division.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
