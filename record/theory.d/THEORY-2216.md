# THEORY-2216: Corporate agency must be moralised collectivism

## The account

Mulgan (2018) argues that the only account of corporate agency that is
temporally robust is moralised extreme collectivism. Moral agency belongs
only to corporate groups reliably disposed to respond appropriately to moral
reasons; full moral personhood is open in principle to the sophisticated
among them; and a group whose structure has no mechanism for recognising
and responding to moral reasons is "irredeemably psychopathic" and has "no
moral standing at all" (p. 910). Robustness means giving people in credible
broken and digital futures the concepts to resist domination by corporate
groups.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-2306](../notes.d/NOTE-2306.md); its key results are quoted there with
locations. The argument is from predicted future belief, which Mulgan
grants "may not persuade realists" (p. 914), and the reliability condition
is named, not operationalised. It reports Velasquez ([LIT-1429](../literature.d/LIT-1429.md), [LIT-1432](../literature.d/LIT-1432.md))
by citation and paraphrase only.

## Bearing

On [QUESTION-265](../questions.d/QUESTION-265.md), as a third position beside [CLAIM-431](../claims.d/CLAIM-431.md) and [CLAIM-432](../claims.d/CLAIM-432.md):
"psychopathic" said of a corporate group as the structural absence of a
mechanism for moral reasons, which removes moral agency rather than
grounding blame.
