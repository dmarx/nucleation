# NOTE-2064: Shaw-Young — Global Institutions and Human Rights (on Miller's connection theory)

## Contribution

For the record, the chapter's report of Miller: outcome responsibility
(whose action produced a situation) and remedial responsibility (who must
put it right) come apart, and remedial responsibility is distributed by a
plurality of connections. Shaw-Young's own thesis, that only moral and
outcome responsibility ground duties of justice while causal
responsibility, benefit and capacity rest on beneficence, is not used.

## Key results

Miller (2007), as quoted:

- **The connections** (thesis p. 20, citing Miller 2007 pp. 100–104):
  "moral responsibility, outcome responsibility, causal responsibility,
  benefit, capacity, and community".
- **Moral responsibility** (p. 20, Miller p. 100): "In order to be morally
  responsible for P's condition, A must have acted in a way that displays
  moral fault".
- **Coercion** (p. 25, Miller p. 102): "Or consider someone who under
  coercion injures another. If the coercer himself disappears from the
  scene, causal responsibility falls on to the person who has been made to
  inflict the injury, and with it the responsibility to care for the
  injured party."
- **Capacity** (p. 29, Miller p. 103): "if I am the only person walking
  along a river bank when a child falls in, then it is my responsibility
  to rescue the child".
- **Strength** (p. 26, Shaw-Young's paraphrase): "with more direct
  connections generating stronger duties".

## Assumptions

The thesis takes Miller's 2007 book as the statement of his view.

## Concepts

- **Outcome responsibility** and **remedial responsibility**.
- **The connection theory**: six connections that make an agent
  remedially responsible.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-717](../claims.d/CLAIM-717.md)** (blame and repair do not substitute): corroborates
  the half "assigning present remedial duties does not show that those
  who bear them were to blame": the capacity connection puts the rescue on
  a bystander who did nothing wrong.
- **[CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md)** (second-order wrongdoing): in tension, not conflict.
  In Miller's coercion case the coerced agent takes on the duty of repair
  when the coercer "disappears from the scene". The claim says the
  coercer's wrong stands beside the actor's culpability. Miller speaks of
  repair, not blame, so the two are compatible; but repair can fall on the
  structured party.
- **[CLAIM-714](../claims.d/CLAIM-714.md)** (self-induced incapacity): nothing reached addresses
  it.
- **[TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md)**: its remedial sense is Miller's term, and its causal
  sense matches Miller's causal responsibility; Miller's outcome
  responsibility has no slot among the term's five senses.
- **[CASE-284](../cases.d/CASE-284.md)**: the capacity ground the case says it has not read is
  here in Miller's words, as reported.
- **The exchange's characterization** (A110: "causal involvement, moral
  responsibility, relevant relationships, and capacity to remedy"): all
  four are among Miller's six in 2007, with outcome responsibility and
  benefit besides. Whether the 2001 article lists these is unchecked.
