# ARG-130: The finite-width computation for a rank power-law spectrum supports the participation-ratio regimes as asymptotic statements and qualifies their use: just either side of an exponent of 1 they separate only at widths no network has, so they cannot be read from width scaling

## The inference

[CLAIM-847](../claims.d/CLAIM-847.md), from A30 §5: D_PR grows as D for α < 1/2, as D/log D at
1/2, as D^(2(1−α)) between 1/2 and 1, as (log D)² at 1, and tends to a
constant above 1. A30 states them as "Asymptotic behavior", "assuming an
ideal rank-power-law spectrum". A30 §9 then applies them: train at several
embedding dimensions D and ask whether the participation ratio grows with
D, to tell which regime a learned representation is in.

[CLAIM-848](../claims.d/CLAIM-848.md) supplies the finite-D behaviour. For α > 1 the share of
the limit reached is about 1 − D^(−(α−1)), so the bounded regime is within
a fixed fraction of its limit only at D ≈ e^(c/(α−1)). At Stringer's
smoothness bound α = 1 + 2/m, for a pure power law from rank 1 (the limit
of smooth codes: the bound is strict and constrains only the tail,
ARG-tmpbad4y), width 768 gives D_PR between 13 and 29 for m from 10 to 100, and half the limit needs width 1.2 × 10⁵ at m = 20 and
1.2 × 10¹³ at m = 50.

So the asymptotic table is correct and its application in A30 §9 is not
available near α = 1: no measured growth of D_PR with width at real widths
places α either side of 1. The exponent has to be fitted from the spectrum
directly, as [LEAD-210](../leads.d/LEAD-210.md) now specifies.

## Critical questions

- **Does the mapping premise hold?** The computation is for the same
  object as A30's table, a pure rank power law, so the mapping is exact.
  It was done with Hurwitz zeta partial sums and checked against direct
  summation ([CLAIM-848](../claims.d/CLAIM-848.md)).
- **Are the model's assumptions met in the new domain?** The new domain
  is the widths at which networks are trained, 10² to 10⁴. The
  assumption the asymptotics need, D large enough that the partial sums
  have reached their leading terms, is not met there for α within about
  0.1 of 1, which is where the limit of Stringer's bound lies for
  manifolds of intrinsic dimension 20 or more. Smooth codes sit strictly
  above that limit in the tail, and their heads are free, so a real code's
  D_PR can sit well away from the table (ARG-tmpbad4y). Far from α = 1 the regimes
  do separate at real widths (at α = 2, D_PR is 2.22 at width 10 and 2.47
  at width 100 against its limit of 2.50; computed with `python3 -I`),
  so the qualification is specific to the neighbourhood of α = 1. A
  spectrum that is not a pure power law falls outside both claims and
  needs its own computation.

## Verdict

[CLAIM-848](../claims.d/CLAIM-848.md) supports [CLAIM-847](../claims.d/CLAIM-847.md) as an asymptotic statement and
qualifies its use: near α = 1 the regimes separate only at astronomical
widths, so width scaling of the participation ratio cannot identify them.
Proposed.

*Changed 2026-11-22, version 2:* Stringer's bound is called the limit of
smooth codes, strict and on the tail, as the reading gives it.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Stringer and colleagues, read

Stringer and colleagues (LIT-2972) were read (NOTE-tmp923nm,
THEORY-tmpwtoad). They **support** the computation's premise in corrected
form (ARG-tmpbad4y): Supplementary Theorem 5 gives λ_n = o(n^(−1−2/d)) for
a code with finite expected derivative, an asymptotic statement about the
tail. The spot-checks above were recomputed with `python3 -I` and stand:
local slopes between widths 10⁴ and 10⁶ of 0.287, 0.167 and 0.087 at
α = 0.9, 1 and 1.1; at α = 2, D_PR is 2.22 at width 10 and 2.47 at width
100. The reading also makes this argument's point in its own domain: "The
actual value of N is unimportant if α > 1 ... However, we observe a rapid
increase in total variance when α becomes close to 1" (SI §3.5).
