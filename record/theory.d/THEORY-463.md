# THEORY-463: In classical statistical mechanics a strictly irreversible approach to equilibrium cannot come from the isolated Hamiltonian dynamics alone: the fine-grained Gibbs entropy is constant and bounded systems recur, so each account surveyed either weakens the target to typical or long-run behaviour or adds something from outside the dynamics

## Source

Frigg & Werndl (2023), [LIT-114](../literature.d/LIT-114.md), the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
entry "Philosophy of Statistical Mechanics", read in full in [NOTE-103](../notes.d/NOTE-103.md).

## What was actually shown

**The obstacles.** A system is a dynamical system whose flow is deterministic
and measure-preserving, as Liouville's theorem gives for Hamiltonian systems
(§3). Loschmidt's reversibility objection (1876) and Zermelo's recurrence
objection (1896), the latter from Poincaré recurrence in bounded
measure-preserving systems, block a strict approach to equilibrium (§4.3). In
the Gibbsian framework the fine-grained entropy S_G = −k∫ρ log ρ dx is a
constant of motion, and a distribution that is not stationary can never
become stationary (§6.3). These are stated as mathematical fact.

**The Boltzmannian accounts (§§4.4–4.7) weaken the target.** Ergodicity gives
time-fractions equal to measure-fractions for almost all initial conditions,
but many paradigm systems are not ergodic, the ideal gas among them.
Typicality makes the approach overwhelmingly likely, and needs a dynamical
assumption to do it. The Mentaculus conditionalizes on the Past-Hypothesis,
a low-entropy initial condition. The authors' long-run residence account
defines equilibrium as the macro-state the system occupies most of the time.

**The Boltzmann equation (§5).** Lanford's theorem recovers a statistical
H-theorem only up to about two-fifths of the mean free time, "in the order of
microseconds" for air. Where its irreversibility comes from is disputed.

**The Gibbsian repairs (§§6.4–6.6) add something.** Coarse-graining adds a
grid, needs mixing and reaches quasi-equilibrium only as t → ∞.
Interventionism adds an environment, has no theorem that an arbitrary
environment drives equilibrium, and faces a regress up to the universe. The
epistemic account adds the observer's knowledge, and its most fundamental
problem is that kettles do not boil because of what we know.

**What could have come out otherwise.** An account could have derived strict,
monotonic irreversibility from the isolated dynamics. The formal facts rule
that out, and no account in the survey claims it.

## What this does not say

- **Which account is right.** Each repair is reported with its standing
  objections. Several verdicts are reported rather than adjudicated
  ([NOTE-103](../notes.d/NOTE-103.md), Limitations).
- **That the classification is proved.** That every Boltzmannian account
  drops strict irreversibility is stated account by account, not argued as a
  general result ([NOTE-103](../notes.d/NOTE-103.md), C4). An account outside the survey would need its
  own check.
- **That the authors' own account is established.** The long-run residence
  definition and its existence theorem are described sympathetically,
  without a formal statement, and the theorem is cited as forthcoming (C5).
- **That statistical mechanics reduces thermodynamics.** The entry declines to
  frame its aim as reduction (§1, §7.5), and the phase-transition debate is
  left open.
- **Anything about quantum statistical mechanics or the direction of time.**
  The first is flagged as understudied (§4.8); the second has its own entry.
- **That coarse-graining or maximum entropy are refuted.** Coarse-graining's
  indistinguishability premise is contested by the spin-echo experiment, and
  there is "no consensus" on the significance or "even cogency" of the
  maximum-entropy account. Contested is not refuted.
