# THEORY-tmpmju6r: Kane and Lubensky show that in isostatic lattices the number of zero modes in a region equals a local count of sites and bonds plus a topological count fixed by winding numbers of the bulk equilibrium matrix, so that gapped phases of different topological polarization carry protected floppy modes or states of self-stress at their boundaries and interfaces even where degrees of freedom and constraints locally balance

## Source

Kane and Lubensky (2014; arXiv v1 2013-08-02), LIT-tmpldjyt; read in NOTE-tmpp5rni.

## What was actually shown

- **The theorem** (index-theorem section; supplement): ν^S = ν_L^S +
  ν_T^S, where ν_L^S "is a local count of sites and bonds in S" and ν_T^S
  "is a topological count, which depends on the topological structure of
  the gapped phases on the boundary of S". Proved.
- **Two origins**: zero modes from a mismatch of sites and bonds, or, "at
  a domain wall", "in a location where there is no local mismatch."
- **The models**: a 1D rotor-spring chain (the SSH model), with an
  interface carrying "a state of self-stress localized to the middle three
  bonds, in addition to floppy modes localized at either end"; and a
  deformed kagome lattice whose edge and domain-wall counts agree with the
  theorem. These could have disagreed.

## What this does not say

- That every boundary mode is topological: the local term contributes too.
- That interfaces always create freedom: the opposite orientation binds
  states of self-stress (THEORY-tmp2e4h8 finds the same in kirigami).
- That the zero modes survive beyond infinitesimal distortions.
- That the count is defined without a bulk gap and a gauge choice of unit
  cell.
