# LIT-tmpuh84t: A General Feedback Theory of Human Behavior: Part II

Powers, W. T., Clark, R. K. & McFarland, R. L. (1960). *Perceptual and Motor Skills* 11(3):309–323. doi:10.2466/pms.1960.11.3.309. Reprinted in *Living Control Systems: Selected Papers of William T. Powers* (Control Systems Group, 1989), pp. 25–45.

Read in full in NOTE-tmplcbcz, from the reprint. Page numbers are the
reprint's.

## Key takeaways

- **Perception gives form to behaviour.** "it is perception which gives
  form to behavior. Behavior will make sense to E only if E knows what
  perceptual variables the behavior is maintaining at some reference-level"
  (p. 26).
- **Six orders**, each a self-evident class of perception: first-order
  spinal loops, elementary sensations, configurations, sequences,
  relationships, and (vaguely) system concepts (pp. 28–35). "Eliminating
  higher-order perceptions leaves the lower; blocking the lower partially
  or totally eliminates the higher" (p. 27).
- **Reorganization reshapes perception, not content.** The N-system "never
  adds new information to the system ... It merely alters the properties of
  a system, thus changing the transformations applied to existing
  information" (p. 37). Insight is "a new organization of perception" (p.
  36). Its intrinsic variables may include "average stimulus input rate, or
  mean error signal in the hierarchy" (p. 35).
- **Consciousness** "is the state of the feedback-function in a subsystem in
  the hierarchy which is being affected by the output of the N-system" (p.
  37).
- **Conflict.** Two higher systems demanding contradictory settings of a
  shared lower one leave it at a "virtual reference-level" (p. 40). One way
  out is "switching one system entirely to the imagination-connection [the
  mechanism (See Part I) of fantasy, wish-fulfillment, and closure]" (p.
  41).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the reading of Part I (LIT-tmpgo33i, NOTE-tmpf65in), because Part
I's question for the record, whether anything in the theory reshapes the
perceptual functions, is answered mainly here. It does: the N-system alters
the transformations that perceptual functions apply. But what drives it is
error in inherited intrinsic variables, not a judgement: "insights which
are not useful are quickly discarded because they create conflict or tend
to increase intrinsic errors" (p. 36). So for CLAIM-tmpq9do8 it is a rival with the claim's shape (the
observer reshaped) and a different engine. For QUESTION-tmpr5tck it offers
"mean error signal in the hierarchy" as an intrinsic variable, a scoring
signal about how the hierarchy is doing that the hierarchy does not set.
It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.
