# CLAIM-tmp76x3e: An agent can be governed through its own sound values, without being deceived and without any change in its criteria, by a party that changes the situation those values respond to, so that the agent has a genuine reason, even a duty, to do what the party wants; a commitment becomes exploitable in this way when the party controls the circumstances that trigger it while avoiding the costs of fulfilling it

## The claim

A13 opens: "The most striking thing about **normative manipulation** is
that it can work *because the target's moral reasoning is sound*, not
because it's defective." "You might recognize every relevant fact, reason
flawlessly, and make the morally correct decision. **You've still been
manipulated.**" (exchange at
https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b)

A13 §2 separates three mechanisms: "Manipulating beliefs about duty",
"Creating genuine moral obligations" and "Exploiting a conflict between
genuine obligations". The second is the one the claim is about: "correcting
the person's beliefs doesn't solve the problem. They may understand
perfectly well that they're being exploited, but their duty still exists."
The third, a "moral trap", is its sharpest form. A13 §6 states the
difference from persuasion: "Normative manipulation may instead alter the
world so that new reasons exist". A13 §1, on Alice leaving her dog with
Bob, who had declined to mind it: "**an obligation can be genuine while
the circumstances that produced it are unjust**". The dog example is A's
account of Simon Keller's "moral blackmail"; the attribution is unchecked
until the work is read.

**The condition.** A15 §7: "A moral commitment becomes a strategic
vulnerability when another actor can control the circumstances that
trigger it while avoiding the costs of its fulfillment." Its bargaining
case: an organization that will never abandon beneficiaries negotiates with
one that will suspend operations, and "**B's ethical commitment gives A an
option that B doesn't possess**". A15 §2 lists what a commitment offers to
whoever controls its trigger: predictability, credibility, motivational
force and legitimacy.

**In the record's terms.** CLAIM-404's evidence route moves a*(e) =
argmax_a E[U(a, ω) | e] by moving the evidence e with U fixed; its criteria
route moves U. Here neither moves: the manipulator changes ω, the target's
evidence of it is accurate, and a* moves because ω did. Stated that way the
route is trivial, since anyone who changes the world changes what others
decide. What makes it a way of being governed, and a wrong, is A15's
condition: one party controls when the commitment is triggered and another
bears what fulfilling it costs.

## How it sits with the record

- **CLAIM-404** (Active, the owner's) is the claim this adds a route to, so
  the claim rests on it.
- **THEORY-225** reads Noggle: manipulation has no single mechanism. This
  is one mechanism, and A's boundary below keeps it from becoming a
  definition.
- **CLAIM-690** puts the weight of a dependence on who controls its terms,
  exit among them. A commitment that makes withdrawal a moral failure
  removes exit from inside the agent; QUESTION-tmpbzq36 asks whether
  activating it is control over the terms.
- **QUESTION-071** asks what tells a manipulated will from an effective
  one. Here nothing in the target's present structure is defective, which
  is the hardest case for any present-structure test.
- **CLAIM-660** (parasites acting through a host's own machinery) is the
  biological analogue.
- CLAIM-tmpe5u7i is the institutional, unintended form: an arrangement that
  comes to rely on members' commitments to supply what it does not fund.

## What it does not say

- Not that appealing to someone's commitments is wrong as such. A13 §5:
  "**Exploiting someone's moral commitments isn't inherently wrongful.**"
  Holding a government to its declared norms "May be legitimate accountability". A13 §9 gives four questions to tell the cases apart:
  whether the predicament was created unjustly, whether costs are unfairly
  transferred, whether the target is respected as an independent agent,
  and whether a genuine wrong is being held to account.
- Not that the obligation is what the manipulator says it is. A13 §7: "**An
  exploitative attempt to create an obligation does not establish that the
  obligation is unlimited—or even that it exists in the form the
  manipulator claims.**"
- Not that the remedy is less conscientiousness. A15's close: "**The
  challenge isn't to make people less conscientious.**"
