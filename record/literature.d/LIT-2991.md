# LIT-2991: Real-Time Computation at the Edge of Chaos in Recurrent Neural Networks

Bertschinger & Natschläger (2004). Neural Computation 16(7), 1413–1436. DOI 10.1162/089976604323057443.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A cites it at A15 for computation at the edge of chaos in recurrent networks, naming Bertschinger, Natschläger and Legenstein. It bears on the exchange's question whether scale-free (critical) systems have a distinctive computational capacity. Not read: Unavailable since 2026-11-22 (paywalled; the hosts that might hold an author copy are listed in the status note). The abstract was read.

A neighbour stands in for it (ADR-114), and how far it reaches matters:

- **Natschläger, Bertschinger and Legenstein (2004), LIT-tmpdsowv** (NOTE-tmpi5t3r, THEORY-tmps8t83): the same programme, a mean-field critical line for driven threshold networks and readout performance peaking near it, yes; it says "A similar analysis for a restricted case (zero mean of synaptic weights) of the network model considered in this paper can be found in [4]", [4] being this work, and cites it for the experimental details. This work's own zero-mean model, its figures and caveats, no. Its NM-separation measure and synaptic-scaling rule are the companion's, not this work's.

Its authors are the three A named: A ran the two 2004 papers together (CLAIM-809, version 3). A later RBN-reservoir study, Calvet, Reulet and Rouat (LIT-tmpahs2x), presents its finding that "the edge of chaos, or the critical region, does not consistently align with the peak performance region" as refining this work. CLAIM-809 now grounds its strand 1 on the companion's reading (ARG-tmp2zz6k), keeping this entry beside it.
