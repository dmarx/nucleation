# QUESTION-tmp07sug: Across many artworks of one crowd-built canvas, do placements follow revisions of each artwork's design made or approved by a small identifiable set of accounts, rather than the designs recording what placers had already done, and does the coordination record assign to other groups placements that a behaviour-only method gives to one?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-218](../theory.d/THEORY-218.md) reads Litherland and Mørch's case study of the 2017 Mona Lisa.
In the threads matched to snapshots, changes discussed in the forum then
appeared on the canvas, and a few central members admitted or refused
additions. That is one artwork, and the authors call the link from forum
power to placements "unclear": most placers never posted, and how many
followed the designs was not measured. Whether design generally leads
placement, and whether a coordination record changes what a behaviour-only
method would conclude, is what [QUESTION-084](QUESTION-084.md) needs beyond that one case.

## What would answer it

A study across many artworks of one crowd-built canvas that pairs the
placement log with time-stamped coordination records (forum or chat
decisions, template or overlay revisions) and shows that (a) changes in an
artwork's placements follow revisions of its design, with a lag, far more
often than the reverse, (b) those revisions are made or approved by a
small, identifiable set of accounts, and (c) at least some placement
patterns that a behaviour-only method assigns to one group belong, by the
coordination record, to others. It is answered no if designs mostly follow
what placers have already done, so that the forum records rather than
directs, or if decision-making is spread across the forum with no core.
What cannot settle it: more excerpts from single artworks, or centrality
scores that participants never saw, unless they are tied to what was
placed.
