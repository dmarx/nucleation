# NOTE-1390: Scheppele — Autocratic legalism

## Contribution

It gives a name and a profile to a kind of democratic decline that is
carried out by law: elected leaders who use their mandates to change the
rules that would hold them to account. Its distinctive claims are that the
tactics form a shared, borrowed "script", that the autocrats exploit the
pluralism of legitimate constitutional forms by combining features that
are tolerable separately and toxic together, and that they avoid the
twentieth-century signals of tyranny on purpose.

## Key insight

The autocrats operate "in the world of legalism", not liberalism: a law
meets legalism's test if it "follows the rules laid down, regardless of the
content", while constitutionalism requires substantive compliance with a
liberal order. Because the procedural form is kept, observers who look for
tanks, emergencies and mass arrests see a normal state; the institutions
"survive in the same buildings, but their liberal souls have been killed".

## Assumptions

- **Liberal constitutionalism** is taken as a normative standard: protected
  rights, checked power, a self-sustaining democracy whose institutional
  prerequisites (plural media, independent judiciary, neutral election
  officials, a loyal opposition) are protected even from majorities
  (pp. 557–559).
- **The "democratic decline" camp has the better case** (p. 547); the
  essay does not argue this.
- **Intent.** The essay presumes "deliberate" design, "constitutional
  malice" (p. 547), and reads it from the pattern and the leaders'
  statements.

## Key results

- **Recognition** (Part I). "One should first suspect a democratically
  elected leader of autocratic legalism when he launches a concerted and
  sustained attack on institutions whose job it is to check his actions."
  Hungary since 2010 is the archetype: a two-thirds majority on about 53
  percent of the vote, a new constitution, the constitutional court
  captured in three years, judges' retirement age lowered to open court
  presidencies.
- **Borrowing.** Orbán's "vertical of power" from Putin; court enlargement
  plus new jurisdiction from Erdoğan, so packing looked like support;
  Poland's attack on its tribunal and court presidents from Hungary;
  constituent assemblies from Chávez to Correa.
- **Exploiting pluralism** (Part II). Gerrymandering (from the US) plus 15
  percent variation in district size (from Germany) "can steal an
  election"; "the Frankenstate".
- **Tactics** (Part III). Avoid the Hitler and Stalin scenarios; no early
  emergency, no mass violation of traditional rights; "repurpose rather
  than abolish" institutions (Hungary's National Judicial Office, one
  person, replacing the National Judicial Council); leave a weakened
  opposition and a few critical outlets; punish opponents through job
  loss, benefits, inspections and buy-outs, which no court will treat as
  rights violations; rig elections in ways observers cannot see.
- **Precondition.** Following Tocqueville, a liberal order toppled quickly
  "must have been ill long before" — polarization, party-system failure,
  economic shocks, politicized courts (pp. 569–570, 579–580).
- **Remedy** (Part IV). Scholars must document the new signs; "civil
  defense requires citizens to be empowered with law".

## Concepts

- **Autocratic legalism**: "When electoral mandates plus constitutional and
  legal change are used in the service of an illiberal agenda". Taken from
  Javier Corrales on Chávez, but stressing the deliberate creation of new
  law over Corrales's "use, abuse and non-use" (n. 7).
- **Legalism**: the formal test of enactment by the rules, as against
  constitutionalism's substantive test.
- **Frankenstate**: a state assembled from parts of good systems that is
  monstrous as a whole (Scheppele 2013).
- **Constitutional capture** (Müller): systematically weakening checks and
  balances.

## Connections

It builds on Landau's "abusive constitutionalism" (2013), Corrales (2015),
Huq and Ginsburg on retrogression, Tushnet's "constitutional hardball",
and Müller on constitutional capture, all cited. It uses Scheppele's own
earlier work on Hungary.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md).** The claim is stated so as not to choose between itself and
  Scheppele's strategic explanation, and the reading supports keeping them
  apart, for a reason the claim does not give: the two mechanisms differ in
  what they change. [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md) changes the facts on the ground so that a
  correction costs more "while the standards that govern that outcome stay
  the same". Scheppele's autocrats change the standards and the correctors:
  they amend constitutions "to make what was once unconstitutional into
  something constitutional", disable constitutional courts first, and
  pack the courts that would review them. One of her tactics is [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md)'s
  mechanism, though: enlarging a court and its jurisdiction at once so that
  a flood of minor cases needs the new judges (Hungary, Turkey) raises the
  cost of review without any change of standard. Her intent claim is
  presumed from the pattern, which bears out A8's warning that rising costs
  do not show obstruction. And her Tocquevillean precondition, a liberal
  order hollowed out before the leader arrives, is a non-strategic process
  inside her own strategic account, so the two explanations are not as
  exclusive as the claim's edge treats them.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **An essay in a symposium.** The cases are narratives from secondary
  sources and the author's commentary; there is no case selection, no
  comparison set, and no test of the borrowing claim.
- **The remedy** promised in the abstract ("how the legalistic autocrats
  may be stopped") is a page on civic education.
- **The intent of the leaders** is presumed, not shown.
- **Errors and slips.**
  - Note 73 dates Harris v. Arizona Independent Redistricting Commission,
    136 S. Ct. 1301, to 2014; it was decided in 2016.
  - A sentence on p. 573 is printed twice ("The bite-sized takeaway lessons
    from the two signature authoritarianisms of the twentieth century
    constitute the modern repertoire of signals…").
  - Note 54 credits Dietrich Bonhoeffer with coining "Führer Princip", a
    doubtful attribution: the Nazi party's Führerprinzip was in use before
    his broadcast of 1 February 1933, which the note itself describes.

## Open questions

- Is borrowing traceable (advisers, model laws, cited precedents), or only
  parallel? A test would distinguish diffusion from common response to
  similar constitutions.
