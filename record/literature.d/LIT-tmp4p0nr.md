# LIT-tmp4p0nr: Philosophy of Mathematics and Natural Science

Hermann Weyl (1949), *Princeton University Press (1949; reissued 2009 with an introduction by Frank Wilczek)* — DOI-10.1515/9781400833337

## Key takeaways

- Philosophy of mathematics and philosophy of physics are intertwined, with advances in philosophy led by scientific discovery. Weyl's two-part treatment (mathematics, then natural science), drawing on Descartes, Galileo, Hume, Kant, Leibniz and Newton, is a classic mathematician-physicist's statement of that view.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Weyl, a mathematician, wrote this philosophy book despite "conflicts of conscience" about how the objectivity of science fits the uncertainty of philosophy. He argues that the two are already bound together, and that philosophical progress has followed scientific discovery. The book has two parts, one on mathematics and one on the physical sciences. It works through the ideas of Descartes, Galileo, Hume, Kant, Leibniz and Newton as a guide to understanding science philosophically.
