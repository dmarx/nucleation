# LIT-100: Quantum States for Primitive Ontologists: A Case Study

Gordon Belot (2011), *European Journal for Philosophy of Science 2(1), 67–83 (2012)* — DOI-10.1007/s13194-011-0024-8

## Key takeaways

- Primitive-ontology versions of Bohmian mechanics are dualistic, and none of the natural ways of placing the quantum state in a familiar category (field on configuration space, multi-field, law, or a history of dispositional properties) is free of serious costs. The multi-field view is the most plausible by orthodox standards, but Belot prefers to keep the alternatives open as a matter of van Fraassen-style interpretation.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

On primitive-ontology approaches, such as some Bohmian and spontaneous-collapse theories, describing a quantum system's history attributes properties to regions of spacetime. This promises an easy link to ordinary macroscopic bodies. But these approaches are dualistic, positing a quantum state alongside the material degrees of freedom. Belot sets out and compares the primitive ontologist's options for understanding that quantum state.
