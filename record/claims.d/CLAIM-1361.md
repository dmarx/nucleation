# CLAIM-1361: Most new entries are short-lived, by hypothesis

## The hypothesis

A29 §3: generational collectors "exploit the empirical observation that
many newly allocated computational objects die relatively quickly. They
collect young objects frequently while avoiding the cost of repeatedly
inspecting long-lived ones." For the record: "Many newly formulated
distinctions are exploratory. A smaller proportion survive criticism,
acquire dependencies, become reusable abstractions, and contribute to
sustained development."

A reads it against the record's filing convention ([CLAIM-176](CLAIM-176.md), coding a
thing the second time it turns up): new claims "could be cheap to
articulate and provisionally preserve without immediately imposing the
maintenance burden associated with mature intellectual structure", and
promotion "could be justified by independent corroboration, substantive
downstream use, repeated successful application, or deliberately assigned
developmental importance". The record's Proposed status already does part
of this.

## Generation is not temperature

A29 §3:

| Dimension | What determines it |
|---|---|
| Generation | Developmental history |
| Temperature | Present accessibility requirements |
| Standing | Epistemic judgment |
| Fidelity | Required representational detail |

This widens [CLAIM-1365](CLAIM-1365.md) by one dimension.

## Held evidence nearby

[CLAIM-863](CLAIM-863.md) and [CLAIM-864](CLAIM-864.md) are about revision, not use: whether a claim with
inbound edges is revised less or more per audit. They are the same kind of
measure from the same history. A29 VII's "retention survival curves"
from the record's git history, substantive use against age beside the share
still maintained, are the test; [LEAD-305](../leads.d/LEAD-305.md)'s event log would supply the
events and [LEAD-294](../leads.d/LEAD-294.md)'s replay the analysis, with recent entries
right-censored. A's chart of such curves is labelled "Hypothetical curves
illustrating the empirical question, not repository data".
