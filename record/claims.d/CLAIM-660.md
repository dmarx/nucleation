# CLAIM-660: Behaviour-manipulating parasites show one organism's selected adaptations controlling another's behaviour through the host's own machinery, not goals the parasite represents and imposes: evolutionary goal-directedness and causal control come apart from intentional agency, and that an action is executed by the host's own mechanisms does not show that the host's governance of it is independent

## The claim

A19 §1: "Crucially, the parasite need not consciously intend anything for
this to occur. Evolution can produce highly specific mechanisms whose
effects promote parasite transmission without the parasite representing a
goal or deliberating about how to achieve it." Three claims are separated:

1. evolutionary goal-directedness: adaptations selected because of their
   effects on the host;
2. causal behavioural control: those adaptations change the host's
   behaviour in the parasite's favour;
3. intentional agency: the parasite represents goals and directs the host.

"The first two have substantial empirical support in several parasite–host
systems. The third is a much stronger and generally unsupported
interpretation."

The second half (A19 §3): "An action can be generated by the host's own
mechanisms while its causal organization has been selectively altered by
another organism." The routes are input (what the host senses), evaluation
(how it weighs it) and execution (what it can do).

So the answer to the owner's question is: yes in senses 1 and 2, no evidence
in sense 3. The microbiome case the owner set aside sits further down the
same scale (A17's table: microbial influence on motivation substantial,
goal formation "Not established").

## Where it sits

- [CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md) holds that an organization can be governed from outside, with
  none of its decisions made for it, through its evidence or its criteria.
  This claim carries that across an organism boundary and adds a third
  route, execution. A19 calls parasitic manipulation "a biological
  realization of precisely that structure". The case illustrates the claim;
  it is not a premise of it.
- [CLAIM-167](CLAIM-167.md) (coupled components can be one system relative to a target
  without being one self): parasite and host can be one causal system with
  conflicting interests.
- [CLAIM-362](CLAIM-362.md) (non-domination among constituents as a candidate part of
  authenticity): A19 §4 notes the hostile influence need not be a
  constituent at all, and separates internal integration from
  cross-organism capture.
- [THEORY-413](../theory.d/THEORY-413.md) (interactor, reproducer, manifestor of adaptation,
  beneficiary): the beneficiary of a manipulated behaviour is the parasite
  while the host executes it, as A19 §6's extended-phenotype reading
  ("the boundary of an organism is not necessarily the boundary of the
  phenotypic effects of its genes") has it.

## What it does not say

That parasites are morally culpable (A37 §7: "causal capture is not
sufficient for culpable capture"), or that anything here transfers to human
preferences (the Mendelian-randomization null A19 §7 reports).

## Note of 2026-11-12: Sullivan et al., read

From the reading in [NOTE-1775](../notes.d/NOTE-1775.md) ([LIT-2345](../literature.d/LIT-2345.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **qualifies, by supplying the rival that has to be
ruled out**. The challenges are sterile, so every behavioural change here is
generated by the *host's* own detection and response programmes, none by a
pathogen acting. Yet the changes are pathogen-specific, and STAg, a
*Toxoplasma* preparation, increased social interaction. So pathogen-specific
behaviour change, even one that might favour a parasite's spread, is not by
itself evidence of manipulation (sense 2 of the claim): host sickness
programmes produce it too, and the claim's step 2 needs evidence that the
change serves the parasite and is caused by its adaptations rather than the
host's. This supports the claim's caution at its first step and complicates
inferences in [CASE-244](../cases.d/CASE-244.md). The paper itself does not discuss manipulation;
the link is the record's inference. Other held entries it bears on (found on

## Note of 2026-11-12: Hafer-Hahmann (2026), The Evolution and Ecology of Host Manipulation in Helminth Parasites, read

From the reading in [NOTE-1730](../notes.d/NOTE-1730.md) ([LIT-2402](../literature.d/LIT-2402.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **supports the causal-control-by-adaptation half.**
Stage dependence and the dominance of mature parasites in mixed infections
are what selection for transmission predicts. It says nothing on intentional
agency or on mechanism ("To fully understand host manipulation, we must link
its evolutionary dynamics with the underlying mechanisms"). Its "extended
phenotype" framing is the record's "selected adaptations controlling
another's behaviour" in Dawkins's terms.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Cuny and Poelman (2022), Evolution of koinobiont parasitoid host regulation and consequences for indirect plant defence, read

From the reading in [NOTE-1756](../notes.d/NOTE-1756.md) ([LIT-2464](../literature.d/LIT-2464.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **supports by extension**: control by selected
adaptations through the host's own machinery (its hormones), with plasticity
in direction that is what adaptation, not pathology, predicts.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Sullivan and Hafer-Hahmann weighed together

[ARG-094](../arguments.d/ARG-094.md) weighs the two readings. Sullivan, Dulac and colleagues show
that a pathogen-specific behaviour change, a pro-social one after a
Toxoplasma antigen included, is produced by the host's own sickness
programmes with no pathogen acting, so behaviour alone is no evidence of
manipulation. Hafer-Hahmann shows the evidence that does discriminate,
mature helminths altering their hosts and immature ones not, but for
trophically transmitted helminths only. The causal-control half is supported
for that group; for hosts whose change could be their own response,
Toxoplasma first, it is not yet. Hafer-Hahmann's reading replaces its LIT in
the grounds (version 2). No status change.
