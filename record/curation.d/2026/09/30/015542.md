The owner asked whether signal detection theory (SDT) is covered in this
record. It was not. The only related entries were Van Trees 1968 ([LIT-351](../../record/literature.d/LIT-351.md), the
owner's prior-art map cites it for row 14's detection model, P(flip) =
Q(γ/σ) with a Cramér–Rao bound), Rao 1945 ([LIT-349](../../record/literature.d/LIT-349.md)) and Cramér 1946 ([LIT-311](../../record/literature.d/LIT-311.md)).
All three are `Deferred` and unreachable. This batch registers the founding
and standard SDT sources and reads the ones that could be reached.

**How it was done.** Two agents shared one brief.

- **One took the statistics and engineering line:** Neyman & Pearson 1933,
  and Peterson & Birdsall.
- **The other took the psychophysics line:** Tanner & Swets 1954, Green &
  Swets 1966, and an open tutorial on the measures.

Each also judged whether its work could stand in for Van Trees on row 14. The
sources used were a public-domain Internet Archive scan of the November 1954
*Psychological Review*, the publisher's own free PDF, and DTIC scans mirrored
in the Internet Archive's US-government documents collection.

**Result.** Five works registered: three read and two unreachable.

- **Peterson & Birdsall 1953 ([LIT-364](../../record/literature.d/LIT-364.md)), Skimmed.**
  - **What was read.** The University of Michigan technical report, Parts I
    and II, registered as the report. The 1954 IRE paper with Fox is its
    journal version, given in the venue. Fox is thanked in the report, and
    becomes an author only in 1954.
  - **Findings.**
    - One receiver that outputs the likelihood ratio is optimal under the
      payoff criterion, the Neyman–Pearson criterion and the posterior
      approach alike.
    - It introduces the ROC and proves that its slope equals the threshold.
    - In white Gaussian noise the log-likelihood ratio is equal-variance
      normal with detection index d = 2E/N₀, and the optimal receiver is a
      matched filter.
    - Its index d is the square of the later d′. It never uses d′ or "ideal
      observer".
- **Tanner & Swets 1954 ([LIT-368](../../record/literature.d/LIT-368.md)), Read.** The founding psychophysics
  citation.
  - It names d′ (as √d, after Peterson & Birdsall), separates sensitivity
    from criterion, and draws the ROC.
  - It gives the optimal criterion β = prior odds × payoff ratio.
  - With three observers it predicts four-alternative accuracy from yes-no
    d′, and rejects the "false alarms are guesses" threshold model. The paper
    says "four" observers once, but only three appear in the data.
- **Stanislaw & Todorov 1999 ([LIT-367](../../record/literature.d/LIT-367.md)), Read.** The compact source for the
  formulas: d′, β, c, A′, B″, A_z, and 2AFC accuracy Φ(d′/√2), each with its
  assumptions. The reader checked the worked example by hand. It gives no d′
  formula for m-alternative choice with m > 2.
- **Neyman & Pearson 1933 ([LIT-365](../../record/literature.d/LIT-365.md)), unreachable.** OpenAlex marks it
  open access, but every Royal Society URL returned a challenge page, and the
  Wayback copy was blocked by this session's egress policy. The only other
  copies were reposts, which were not used. A session with ordinary web access
  should be able to read it.
- **Green & Swets 1966 ([LIT-366](../../record/literature.d/LIT-366.md)), unreachable.** In copyright, and the
  only copy is controlled lending. Its identifier is an Open Library URL,
  since the book has no DOI.

**What the record now says about SDT.** The core of SDT now rests on sources
read first-hand: the likelihood-ratio decision rule, the ROC, the
equal-variance Gaussian model, and the split between sensitivity (d′) and
criterion (β, c).

- The engineering statement is Peterson & Birdsall.
- The psychophysical application is Tanner & Swets.
- The measures are Stanislaw & Todorov.

The likelihood-ratio lemma itself is still second-hand until Neyman &
Pearson is read. The only thematic link to earlier holdings is [LIT-099](../../record/literature.d/LIT-099.md) on
framing effects in perception.

**Row 14 of the map.**

- **The detection half can now cite Peterson & Birdsall instead of Van
  Trees.** The equal-prior error Q(√d/2) = Q(d′/2) follows in one line from
  its eqs. 4.3–4.8, but it is never printed. So it is cited as derived, not
  quoted. [LIT-351](../../record/literature.d/LIT-351.md) now says so.
- **Tanner & Swets and Stanislaw & Todorov supply the tail-at-criterion
  form.** Error rates are Gaussian tails at a criterion measured in noise SDs,
  which is the Q(γ/σ) form, and Stanislaw & Todorov print the 2AFC result.
- **The Cramér–Rao half still has no reachable source.** None of the three
  has estimation theory.

**Traps.** Royal Society and DTIC pages sit behind challenges from here, even
for open-access items. The Internet Archive's government-documents mirror of
DTIC scans is the legitimate route for pre-1960 US government technical
reports.
