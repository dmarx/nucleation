# THEORY-429: Institutions are the humanly devised formal and informal constraints on interaction; with technology they fix the costs of transacting, organisations arise to exploit the opportunities they offer, and an economy grows when its framework rewards productive rather than redistributive activity, while increasing returns to the institutional matrix make frameworks path dependent, so that economies with different frameworks diverge and need not converge

## Source

Douglass C. North (1991), "Institutions", *Journal of Economic
Perspectives* 5(1):97–112, [LIT-489](../literature.d/LIT-489.md), read in full in [NOTE-392](../notes.d/NOTE-392.md). The essay is
"based in part on" his 1990 book, which the record does not hold.

## What was actually shown

**The definition (p. 97).** "Institutions are the humanly devised
constraints that structure political, economic and social interaction.
They consist of both informal constraints (sanctions, taboos, customs,
traditions, and codes of conduct), and formal rules (constitutions, laws,
property rights)." Institutions are the rules; organisations (firms,
guilds, states) are the players.

**Costs (p. 98).** Institutions and enforcement, with technology,
determine the cost of transacting. "Effective institutions raise the
benefits of cooperative solutions or the costs of defection." Cooperation
is easy with repeated play, full information and few players, and
impersonal exchange needs third-party enforcement (p. 97, stated, not
modelled).

**Static and evolving exchange (pp. 102–108).** Tribal exchange rests on a
dense network in which deviance threatens survival; the Suq has high
measurement costs and no legal underpinning; the caravan trade ran on
personal protection. None evolved from within. Early modern European
trade did, through innovations at three margins (negotiable bills and
accounting; printed prices and merchant manuals; marine insurance and the
commenda), with causation running both ways between trade volume and
cheaper enforcement, and with merchant law absorbed by a state that had to
be bound against confiscation.

**Path dependence (pp. 109–111).** The institutional matrix has increasing
returns through set-up costs, learning, coordination and adaptive
expectations, so frameworks lock in. England's and Spain's colonies
diverged and have not converged; the same constitution exported to Latin
America did not reproduce the United States.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Little in the essay could. Its
cases illustrate the account; none is set up so that it might have failed.

## What this does not say

- **Why some constraints are followed.** Compliance is a matter of
  enforcement cost, and the essay's closing questions ("What is the
  relationship between formal and informal constraints?") are left open.
  That is the gap Hindriks and Guala name in rules accounts.
- **What makes a constraint an institution** rather than, say, a physical
  obstacle, beyond its being humanly devised.
- **That the staged history happened.** North says the sequence from
  village to long-distance trade need not match real historical sequence
  (n. 1).
- **That path dependence in institutions is shown.** It is asserted to have
  "equal explanatory power" by analogy with technology (n. 10).
- **Anything about group minds or agency.** Organisations appear only as
  players; [NOTE-392](../notes.d/NOTE-392.md) finds no bearing on [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md).
- **The transaction sector's growth as a result.** The figures (25% of US
  GNP in 1870, 45% in 1970) are cited from Wallis and North (1986), not
  derived.
