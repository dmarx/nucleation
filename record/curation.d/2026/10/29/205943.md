## How the works were judged

The test was what reading a work would change in the record's own
argument. Its QUESTIONs come first: 281 were Open on the day, across the
nine lines in `luria.yaml`. Its CLAIMs, CASEs and THEORYs come next. Four
readers each took some of the lists' groups. For each work they checked
three things:

- whether the record already holds it, by grepping literature.d for the
  surname, the title and the identifier;
- which NOTE or entry named it, and why;
- which Open QUESTION or open entry turns on it. They read that entry in
  full, including its answer criteria, promote_when and dated notes.

Three tiers:

- **File:** reading the work would settle, test or correct something the
  record holds open. That means it answers or tests a QUESTION, meets or
  defeats a promote_when, resolves a flag, an attribution or a "register
  and read" request, or is the primary source for something the record now
  states second-hand and leans on.
- **Consider:** it bears on a live line, but no open item turns on it.
- **Skip:** it is cited in passing, is ML practice (which belongs to the
  anthology under ADR-048), is not findable, or is already held.

"Named in a NOTE" was not a reason to file.

**Reconciled.** Two works were triaged twice.

- **Laycock 1990** (103 Harv. L. Rev. 687) was rated File in one group and
  Consider in the other. It is Consider. Of the record's entries, only
  CLAIM-464 names Laycock (LIT-1610), which is Proposed, and that claim
  makes one passing contrast. The other group wrote that CASE-176 and
  CASE-170 rest on the book, but neither cites it.
- **Merton 1949** appeared in both lists. It is counted once, as Skip: the

Not every work had a NOTE or entry that named it, and these could not be
