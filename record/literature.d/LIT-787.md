# LIT-787: Design for a Brain: The Origin of Adaptive Behaviour

W. Ross Ashby (1952), *Design for a Brain*, London: Chapman & Hall; second
edition, revised, 1960, as *Design for a Brain: The Origin of Adaptive
Behaviour* — DOI-10.1007/978-94-015-1320-3

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-851](../notes.d/NOTE-851.md).*

- **Adaptation is stability.** Behaviour is adaptive if it keeps the
  essential variables within limits, which is stability of the whole
  organism–environment system in that region (Ss. 5/3, 5/8). Stability
  belongs to the whole, never to a part (S. 4/18).
- **The second feedback.** Against an environment it does not know, a
  system can adapt only by trial and error, so it must have a channel from
  its essential variables to the parameters of its reacting part:
  unsettled when they are out of limits, at rest when in (Ss. 7/7–7/8).
  By the veto theorem every equilibrium it reaches is then adapted
  (S. 7/11). The new parameter values may be random (S. 7/25).
- **The Homeostat.** Four magnets, weights set by uniselectors wired from
  random-number tables; it recovers from reversed connections by finding a
  compensating reversal (Ch. 8).
- **The time problem.** If all successes must come at once, the trials
  grow as (1/p)ᴺ: for 100 essential variables, about 10²² years. Kept one
  by one, roughly N/p. "Partial successes go for nothing" in the first
  case; crystallisation works by succession (Ss. 11/2–11/8).
- **Retention needs separation.** Accumulating adaptations requires that
  the system "not be fully joined"; constancies cut it into temporarily
  independent parts (Ss. 11/10, 12/17). The Homeostat is "too well
  integrated" and forgets everything (S. 10/5).
- **Interference.** Separate situations need separate stores; similar
  ones share parts, and then new learning destroys old (Ss. 10/9, 16/14).
- **Stability falls with size.** Random linear systems sampled up to 4×4
  were stable with probability near 1/2ⁿ (S. 20/10).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-07 at the owner's request, because the owner's essay "The
Organization of Will" cites the 1960 edition for ultrastability. Read on
2026-10-14 in [NOTE-851](../notes.d/NOTE-851.md), from the scan the Ashby estate's archive
links.

The essay's reading, regulation against alteration of the parameters of
regulation, is Ashby's (S. 7/26), with one qualification: in Ashby the
alteration is blind, new values drawn at random and kept if not vetoed,
not a choice of better parameters.

Two accounts are filed from the reading: [THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md), the second
feedback as necessary and sufficient for adaptation by trial and error,
and [THEORY-307](../theory.d/THEORY-307.md), the retention of partial adaptations as the
condition for adaptation in practicable time. The second bears on the
nucleating-argument line: [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)'s anchors, with the crystal as
Ashby's own example, and the condition that a retained part be cut off
from what is still changing.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
