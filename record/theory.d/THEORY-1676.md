# THEORY-1676: Close and colleagues, pooling six open-access surveys of 7,771 loot-box purchasers, find that self-reported loot-box spending is heavily concentrated, the top 5% of spenders (over $100 a month) accounting for over half of it, and that spending correlates with problem-gambling severity (ρ = .33) but not with earnings (ρ = .02), so that the high spenders the industry calls whales are disproportionately at-risk gamblers rather than wealthy players

## The account

Close and colleagues, pooling six open-access surveys of 7,771 loot-box purchasers, find that self-reported loot-box spending is heavily concentrated, the top 5% of spenders (over $100 a month) accounting for over half of it, and that spending correlates with problem-gambling severity (ρ = .33) but not with earnings (ρ = .02), so that the high spenders the industry calls whales are disproportionately at-risk gamblers rather than wealthy players.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-1784](../notes.d/NOTE-1784.md); its key results are quoted there with locations.
