# CLAIM-216: A thesis that conjoins relational constitution, statistical recovery, transport and receivers' recovery is defeated whole by any one of its empirical findings, though its clauses can hold or fail apart, so the line is better put as separate theses, each with its own defeat condition, resting on one another only where one is a premise of another

## The objection

A240 opens its reply to U62 ("how would you suggest we update or modify the
theses?") with it: "I would make one substantial change to the architecture
of the theses: **stop asking a single overarching thesis to carry the
ontology, the theory of translation, the account of fidelity, and the
empirical prediction.**" Its reason: "PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) has shown that these claims can
succeed or fail independently."

A240 then sets out six theses (T1 to T6) in a dependency diagram, and says
how to read it: "I would treat this diagram as an argumentative sequence,
**not a strict logical derivation**. T4 could be empirically supported even if
T1's metaphysics remains disputed. T5 could fail while T4 succeeds. And the
mathematical claims in T2 and T3 can remain useful independently of either
empirical outcome." And for the record's graph: "use `rests_on` only when a
claim genuinely requires a premise."

**Against [CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md).** The restatement keeps [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s four clauses,
ontological, epistemic, transport and fidelity, in one sentence. Its defeat
condition opens "Any of three findings", and all three concern same-kind
verdicts: whether relational profiles predict them better than rivals,
whether a model of the response profiles does as well, and whether the
relational signatures transfer between languages. It rests on seven claims,
from [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md) to [CLAIM-077](CLAIM-077.md).

A conjunction is false when any conjunct is, so the logic is not at fault.
What the form loses is information. Any one of the three findings defeats the
whole entry, including the fidelity clause, which none of them tests
(receivers' recovery is tested in [CLAIM-220](CLAIM-220.md)), and the ontological
clause, which the entry itself says "stays untested". The retired thesis
then leaves its surviving clauses with no entry that states them. The same
holds of retiring any one of its seven premises.

**Against [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md).** The same form: four clauses, a defeat condition of
three disjuncts on human same-kind judgements, and five premises.

**Why the clauses can come apart.** The record's own claims already separate
the fidelity clause from the ontological one. Fidelity belongs to a
transport, and sharing a kind gives none ([CLAIM-210](CLAIM-210.md)); a transformation
that changes an act's relations can change the act ([CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)). A232 §5
says the same: "a transformation can be a genuine transport even if it fails
to preserve that kind." So what survives a transport is not defined as the
kind persisting, and the fidelity clause does not need the ontological one as
a premise.

**What the record had already done.** A240's "PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) has shown" is right of
two replies. [CLAIM-220](CLAIM-220.md) dropped [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)'s rest on [CLAIM-082](CLAIM-082.md): "The
structuralist premise motivates the thesis and does not carry it."
[CLAIM-202](CLAIM-202.md) is a separate thesis with its own test. It is not right of
[CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md), which joins the clauses again. A240's table row for
[CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md), "Supersede with a narrower integrative thesis, drawing on
[CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md)", gives this objection as its reason, so it does not endorse
[CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md) as filed.

**The separate theses, as the record holds them.**

- T1, relational constitution: [CLAIM-215](CLAIM-215.md), filed with this entry.
- T2, restricted recoverability: [CLAIM-209](CLAIM-209.md), filed with this entry,
  answering [QUESTION-027](../questions.d/QUESTION-027.md).
- T3, admissible transport: held in pieces, each with its own code:
  directed, stochastic, non-invertible transport ([TERM-008](../terms.d/TERM-008.md)), anchored
  correspondence on held-out cases ([CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md)), designated distinctions
  kept apart ([CLAIM-077](CLAIM-077.md)), a class-level kernel family ([CLAIM-228](CLAIM-228.md)),
  kind-sharing is not transport ([CLAIM-210](CLAIM-210.md)), and transport can change the
  act ([CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)). A further entry would say what their relations already
  say.
- T4, receiver-relative survival: [CLAIM-220](CLAIM-220.md).
- T5, incremental transport structure: [CLAIM-202](CLAIM-202.md).
- T6, changing-cover effects: [CLAIM-257](CLAIM-257.md) and [CLAIM-201](CLAIM-201.md).

No one of the six rests on another, except inside T3, where
[CLAIM-228](CLAIM-228.md) rests on [CLAIM-011](CLAIM-011.md). Each thesis among them has its own
defeat condition, and [CLAIM-220](CLAIM-220.md), [CLAIM-202](CLAIM-202.md) and [CLAIM-201](CLAIM-201.md)
state theirs on the discriminating study ([CASE-055](../cases.d/CASE-055.md)).

**The relations A240 asks for.** It advises: "Otherwise prefer typed
relationships such as `complements`, `operationalizes`, or `tests`, wherever
the record schema permits them." The schema has no `operationalizes` and no
`tests`. A claim can `complement` another, be `grounded` in a case, or be
`illustrated_by` one, and a thesis is tied to the study that tests it by
naming the study in its defeat condition, as the record's replies do.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the clauses are unrelated. They answer one question
  ([QUESTION-029](../questions.d/QUESTION-029.md)) and belong together as complements.
- It does not say an integrative statement is useless. A manuscript may want
  one sentence for its thesis.
- It does not say [CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md) or [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) should lose their `rests_on`
  now, and neither is edited.
- It does not adopt A240's hierarchy, or its recommendation to supersede
  [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md). Those are the owner's to take.

## What would answer it

- An argument that the fidelity clause needs the ontological one: that what
  persists through a transport must be the kind, against [CLAIM-210](CLAIM-210.md) and
  [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md). Then the conjunction would be a chain of premises, not a list.
- Or the owner's choice to keep an integrative statement for the manuscript,
  with the separate theses carrying the defeat conditions and the integrative
  one marked as a summary of them.

## Where it stands

Open. The separate theses are filed as proposals beside [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) and
[CLAIM-204](CLAIM-204.md), and the owner has decided neither this objection nor
A240's recommendations.

## Note of 2026-10-30: promoted

The status review of this date promoted it. CLAIM-204 now records the
concession, this objection's second answer: it is the integrative
statement, and the separate theses carry the defeat conditions of the
clauses its own condition does not test. A240's hierarchy and its other
recommendations are not adopted by this.
