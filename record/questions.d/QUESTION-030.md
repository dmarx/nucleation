# QUESTION-030: Which interventions tell the relations that constitute a communicative act from those that only accompany or diagnose it?

## Why it is a question

A191 §6: "Causal interventions can help distinguish features that merely
accompany that act from relations whose alteration changes the act itself."
But A191 gives no criterion. §11: "Causal stability alone does not
establish constitution." A constitutive claim "must be grounded in a
substantive theory of the act and its normative conditions, not only its
measured classification." A191 §13 proposed the question for the record:
"Which interventions distinguish constitutive relations from diagnostic
correlates?"

A218 asks it again in two parts.

- **The empirical half.** RQ5: "Which candidate relations predict
  responses to independently specified interventions, and which are merely
  correlated with surface or thematic features?"
- **The conceptual half.** A218 §2: "some observed relations are merely
  diagnostic, some causally influential, and others constitutive". Its
  Chapter 10 lists "Diagnostic relationships versus constitutive
  candidates" (10.7), and its core claim concedes that the tests stop short:
  "Causal tests can help evaluate the significance of recovered
  relationships without automatically establishing their ontological
  constitution."

The three kinds of relation are [TERM-038](../terms.d/TERM-038.md). The question sharpens
[QUESTION-021](QUESTION-021.md): if an act's identity is constituted by relations, which
relations, and how would one find out?

## What would count as an answer

For the empirical half, a design that intervenes on a candidate relation
itself, not on what the interpreter is told about it, and finds that act
classifications and their consequences change while the wording is held
fixed. [CLAIM-130](../claims.d/CLAIM-130.md) answers part of this: only a do(S) bears on it, and a
do(I) bears on interpretation under instruction.

For the conceptual half, a theory of the act that says which relations are
its conditions, against which the empirical result is read. Searle's
account of constitutive rules is one such theory. The record holds the
book unread ([LIT-485](../literature.d/LIT-485.md)) and reads the theory through Searle's 2006
restatement ([LIT-482](../literature.d/LIT-482.md)). Neither has been brought to this question.

An answer would not be a further finding that a cue changes a judgement.
That is evidence for the empirical half only when the cue is an
intervention on the relation and not a description of it.

## Note of 2026-10-14: readings that bear on each half

None of these speaks of constitution; each bears on one half.

- **The empirical half.** [THEORY-282](../theory.d/THEORY-282.md) (from [LIT-968](../literature.d/LIT-968.md)): a subspace
  intervention can reproduce the output effect of changing a variable
  without localizing it, through a direction that cannot reach the output
  on the data. An intervention that produces the predicted output is not
  yet evidence that the intervened variable mediates. [THEORY-287](../theory.d/THEORY-287.md)
  (from [LIT-987](../literature.d/LIT-987.md)): variables located by interchange intervention predict
  behaviour under shifts that keep the mechanism better than features
  chosen otherwise. That separates mediating from accompanying features in
  a weaker form, and says nothing about constitution.
- **The conceptual half.** [THEORY-243](../theory.d/THEORY-243.md) (from [LIT-961](../literature.d/LIT-961.md) and [LIT-968](../literature.d/LIT-968.md)):
  interventions on a fixed set of variables can be satisfied equally by
  alignments related by a change of basis, so which direction mediates is
  fixed only relative to a chosen decomposition into units. The tests
  alone do not supply that choice. Shown exactly for one constructed
  network.

## Note of 2026-10-28: the instruction part split off

The status review of this date split this question under [ADR-049](../decisions.d/ADR-049.md).
[CLAIM-130](../claims.d/CLAIM-130.md), now Active, settles one part of the empirical half: an
intervention on what the interpreter is told, do(I), cannot by itself
tell constitutive relations apart, since it acts on a different variable
from the situation. That part is [QUESTION-296](QUESTION-296.md), Answered. This
question keeps what is still open: which interventions on the situation
itself, do(S), do tell them apart, and the conceptual half, which needs a
theory of the act. The title already asked only that, so it is unchanged.
