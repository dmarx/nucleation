# QUESTION-tmpz8xkx: What should a governed record cool, condense or erase, and how can what it keeps within easy reach be governed apart from what it endorses?

The owner, at U15: retired and superseded content "still impacts retrieval
cost and discoverability, as do proposed/active items that don't contribute
to further development", and "developed-upon content which contains
redundant information could potentially even be condensed or removed", while
luria has no "hot vs warm vs cold storage distinction, just present or not"
(CLAIM-tmpu1fef).

## What makes it open

The record settles whether an entry is in force (its status) and whether it
was worth filing (CLAIM-303, CLAIM-308). It does not settle four things.

1. **What to cool.** Which entries should leave default retrieval while
   staying addressable, and by what evidence. Standing is not the answer:
   a rejected argument that keeps recurring may need to be met more often
   than an Active lemma a synthesis already carries (A27 §2,
   CLAIM-tmp5zt7f). Use is not the answer by itself either, since the
   record's own retrieval decides what gets used (CLAIM-tmp6tc1j).
2. **What to condense.** Which entries, or parts of entries such as
   accreted dated notes, can be replaced in ordinary use by a smaller
   representation of the same judgment (TERM-tmp3t5jq), and how to show
   that nothing a later correction needs was lost. CLAIM-tmpnf6q4 gives
   the condition for a declared family of corrections; QUESTION-105 asks
   for the least retained state for revisions in general.
3. **What to erase.** Whether anything should leave the tree. The record's
   rule is retirement by status, never deletion, and git keeps what a file
   said; erasure in a git record is a separate operation from removal from
   the tree.
4. **How to govern availability.** Who may cool or condense, on what
   grounds, how the decision is reversed, and how a cooled entry is found
   again when it matters (TERM-tmp96b3g's reactivation triggers,
   CLAIM-tmpdkwbk's visible dependencies).

## What would answer it

An answer would be a rule, or a family of them, that the record can apply
and check: for each kind of entry, which tier it belongs in and what
representation it keeps, with what reverses the decision; and a test, on
the record's own tasks, that the rule lowers the cost of reaching what a
task needs without losing what corrections need (LEAD-tmpeqy94).
CLAIM-314's non-independence means the rule cannot be
per entry alone: whether an entry is in the way depends on what else is
in default retrieval.
