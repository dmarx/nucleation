# LIT-838: The Semantics of Definite and Indefinite Noun Phrases

Irene Heim (1982), Ph.D. dissertation, University of Massachusetts
Amherst; published New York: Garland, 1988 (Outstanding Dissertations in
Linguistics), xi + 413 pp. — https://semanticsarchive.net/Archive/Tk0ZmYyY/

## Key takeaways

- **Indefinites do not quantify.** "A cat" contributes an open formula
  cat(xᵢ); its apparent force comes from the operator that binds it:
  *always*, *usually*, *every* (whose restrictor it sits in), a modal, or
  the unpronounced necessity operator of a bare conditional, or else
  existential closure. "Every man who owns a donkey beats it" is
  universal over man–donkey pairs, and "if a man owns a donkey he beats
  it" is necessity restricted by the if-clause, after Lewis on adverbs of
  quantification and Kratzer on modals (ch. II).
- **Against the rivals.** Geach-style binding gives truth conditions only
  to whole texts; Grice–Kripke–Lewis salience cannot explain why
  truth-conditionally equivalent sentences differ in what they let a
  later pronoun pick up ("I dropped ten marbles and found all of them,
  except for one. It is probably under the sofa" versus "…found only
  nine of them. It…"); Evans–Cooper–Parsons E-type pronouns predict
  uniqueness implications that conditionals refute ("If someone is in
  Athens, he is not in Rhodes") (ch. I).
- **Files.** A file is a common ground with structure: a domain of card
  numbers and a set of satisfying sequences. Each logical form has a
  file change potential; conjunction is successive update, and
  quantifiers and negation work through tentative auxiliary files. A
  file is true if some sequence satisfies it, which is where existential
  force comes from, so existential closure and the indexing rules can be
  dropped from grammar (ch. III, §§1–4).
- **Definiteness is familiarity.** The Extended Novelty-Familiarity
  Condition: an indefinite's index must be new to the file; a
  definite's must be in it, with its descriptive content entailed.
  Deixis and anaphora are one phenomenon (both need a familiar card);
  definites that are novel are licensed by accommodation, which must
  "bridge" the new card to old ones, and which yields narrow-scope
  definites ("Every man hates his father") and paycheck pronouns
  (ch. III, §5).

Read on 2026-10-09 ([NOTE-608](../notes.d/NOTE-608.md)). With Veltman's and Krifka's
([LIT-835](LIT-835.md)) readings of the same day it is a source of [THEORY-162](../theory.d/THEORY-162.md),
that what an utterance does to a context is part of its meaning and is
not fixed by its truth conditions. `linguistics` is primary: it is a
theory of English noun phrases argued from grammatical judgements;
`philosophy-of-language` holds the account of reference, anaphora and
the common ground; `logic` holds the file change semantics. Chapter III
reads Stalnaker's "Assertion" closely and quotes its account of the
common ground and of assertion's essential effect, so it is also where
this record has read that essay's words.
