# CASE-tmproiqs: East Potomac Golf Links at Hains Point, 2026: about 430 trees cut with less than a day's notice while the government told the court no final renovation plan existed, then a preliminary injunction finding the plan final and its environmental review likely segmented, and requiring notice before any irreversible change

## The case

The owner, U13 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c),
quoting A23's "The speed at which an institution can act imposes an upper
bound on the practical effectiveness of anyone else's right to intervene":

> this is a concise characterization of how the executive branch under
> trump has attempted to bypass oversight and regulation, e.g. third
> country deportations, the eastwing/ballroom, haines point...

East Potomac Golf Links occupies East Potomac Park, which runs to Hains
Point in Washington, DC. As reported (checked 2026-11-15):

- **May 2026.** In a suit by the DC Preservation League and two golfers,
  Judge Ana Reyes (D.D.C.) declined to block the course's closure, saying
  reports of renovation plans were not enough, and ordered the government
  to give notice before cutting more than ten trees. She said "bulldozers
  show up, we're not doing a whole briefing schedule." (Sinclair wire
  report, 4 May 2026: abc6onyourside.com, "Judge allows Trump admin to
  close golf course for WH renovation".)
- **October 2026.** Contractors cut about 430 trees on the course with less
  than a day's notice to the court. Reyes halted further cutting for two
  weeks. (OPB, 14 October 2026; NPR, 27 October 2026.)
- **26 October 2026.** A preliminary injunction: no cutting, substantial
  pruning or other damage to trees without a court order, and at least ten
  business days' written notice before closing the park or making any
  irreversible modification. Reyes found that a renovation plan existed and
  was final "well before October 15, 2026", although officials had
  testified, hours after the President posted that work would begin
  "immediately", that they knew of no final plan; called the government's
  explanation that the clearing carried out a 2019 Park Service report
  "pretextual"; and found the Interior Department had likely violated NEPA
  "by impermissibly segmenting its review of the Plan's environmental
  effects". The President said construction would begin on 15 November and
  the park stay closed for fifteen months, and that the department would
  appeal. (NPR, 27 October 2026,
  https://www.kunc.org/npr-news/2026-10-27/a-judge-halted-d-c-golf-course-renovations-trump-says-theyll-still-start-next-month.)

A25 of the exchange also reported that on 14 November Reyes temporarily
blocked a closure planned for 15 November. That was not found in the
reports checked on 2026-11-15 and is not part of the case.

## What it shows

- **A fourth mechanism for [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md).** [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md)
  names physical irreversibility, enforcement-mediated entrenchment and
  jurisdictional displacement. Here the cutting happened while the
  government's position was that no final decision existed, and review of
  agency action ordinarily needs one. A25: "if an institution can
  physically alter the world while insisting that it has not yet made a
  reviewable decision, the distinction between planning and execution
  becomes consequential." The court's finding that the plan was final
  answered that position after 430 trees were down.
- **The counter-instance.** The May order's notice requirement was the
  point at which the case could be stopped, and the October cutting is
  reported to have come with less than a day of it. The October injunction
  restores a longer one, ten business days before any irreversible change.
  A25: "oversight has not simply ceased to function." It shows the owner's
  procedural-friction claim ([CLAIM-tmpuiaop](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpuiaop.md)) from the other side: a court can impose the
  friction that keeps a point of intervention open.

## What it does not show

- **Intent.** The owner's U13 says "attempted to bypass"; the case records
  what was done and found, not why. A25: "A documented strategic intent to
  constrain oversight would require more evidence."
- **That the cutting was unlawful.** The findings are at the
  preliminary-injunction stage, and the government said it would appeal.
- **What happened on or after 15 November 2026.**

## Note of 2026-11-16: Cuéllar, read

Cuéllar's essay ([LIT-tmpryo91](../literature.d/LIT-tmpryo91.md)) predates the case, but the case fits his
account. A presidential announcement that work would begin "immediately" at a
particular site is the President driving a specific agency matter: his
"presidential adjudication". The court's finding that the stated rationale
was "pretextual" is his Part III.A route to setting aside the presumption of
regularity. The case's added mechanism, acting while denying that a
reviewable decision exists, falls in a gap he leaves himself. His "harder
look" would follow only "the sort of final agency action that tends to be
necessary for judicial review," and so "casts aside plenty of words or deeds
of a more preliminary nature." (p. 645). Read with the case, his remedy
reaches no further than the finality the government denied, and here the
court had to find finality before it could act. Relation: none formal (the
case is not reported in the essay); a specimen of his account.
