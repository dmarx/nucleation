# THEORY-tmpqnfk9: A group learning by trying needs diversity in what its members pursue that lasts long enough to survive misleading early evidence and then gives way to consensus; limited communication and stubborn priors each supply it, and together they make it permanent, so the group never settles

## Source

Zollman (2010), [LIT-tmpkqfje](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkqfje.md), read in [NOTE-tmp8fh62](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8fh62.md); Rosenstock, Bruner &
O'Connor (2017), [LIT-tmphw2gz](../literature.d/LIT-tmphw2gz.md), read in [NOTE-tmp09jwk](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp09jwk.md), for its robustness.

## What was actually shown

Zollman's 2010 model ([LIT-tmpkqfje](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkqfje.md)) gives agents beta priors whose strength
can be varied, and two methods with success rates 0.5 and 0.499.

- With weak priors, sparse networks again beat dense ones.
- As priors become extreme, the order reverses: dogmatic agents in a
  complete network do best. In sparse networks of dogmatic agents the
  group often fails to agree within 10,000 rounds.
- Zollman's conclusion: "At the heart of these models is one single virtue,
  transient diversity." Limited information and extreme priors are two
  ways to it, but "both together make the diversity too stable".

Rosenstock et al. ([LIT-tmphw2gz](../literature.d/LIT-tmphw2gz.md)) find the sparse-network advantage fragile.
They report this observation, that transient diversity is necessary,
"holds for all the models discussed".

## What this does not say

- **Not that more diversity is better.** The account is about duration:
  too little and the group drops the better option, too much and it never
  converges.
- **Not a measure.** Nothing here says how long is long enough in a real
  community, or how to tell transient from entrenched diversity before the
  outcome is known.
- **Not historical proof.** The peptic-ulcer history illustrates the model.
  It does not test it.

## Connections

- **Carries [THEORY-tmpgo526](THEORY-tmpgo526.md) further.** Sparse communication is one source
  of the diversity this account says is the real benefit.
- **Lock-in.** The owner's essay (§5.4) calls it "protective lock-in"
  when a protective regime, once running, reproduces the conditions that
  justify it and resists a return to normal operation. This account has a
  similar shape in an epistemic setting: what protects a minority option
  is, past a point, what stops the group from settling. The parallel is the
  record's, and no relation is declared.
