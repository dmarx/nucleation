# LIT-tmpz3v2y: Brief Online Training in Mindfulness Meditation Momentarily Reduces Some Impulsive Behaviors: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Chunlei Ge, Yuhong Xie, Meijuan Sun, Fanfan Fan, Yiemin Zhang and Lei Liu (2024), "Brief Online Training in Mindfulness Meditation Momentarily Reduces Some Impulsive Behaviors: A Randomized Controlled Trial", *Mindfulness* 16: 448–464 — [doi:10.1007/s12671-024-02479-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-024-02479-3)

## Key takeaways

*From the title and a search-result summary of the abstract; the paper is unread, and which impulsive behaviours improved is unchecked.*

- **Design.** Brief online mindfulness training against a monitor group and a control group; impulsivity measured by Stroop, Go/No-Go and delay-discounting tasks, at post-test and follow-up.
- **Result, as summarised.** Relative to the monitor and control groups, the mindfulness group did not show significantly improved impulsive decision-making (delay discounting) at post-test. The title reports that some impulsive behaviours were reduced momentarily. No effect was sustained at follow-up.
- **Why the record wants it.** If the reduction was in response inhibition while discounting stayed put, that is the dissociation [CLAIM-tmp7lzto](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7lzto.md) predicts: which regime acts changes without the valuations changing. Unread, it cannot carry that weight; it is filed so that the check is one reading away.
