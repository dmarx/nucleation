# NOTE-580: The Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge

## Contribution

An encyclopedia survey, written by one of the positions it surveys. For this
record it is the first-hand statement of Longino's view, standing in for
her two books, and a map of the alternatives.

## Key insight

Objectivity is not a property of a method that an individual can apply
alone. It is a property of how a community handles criticism. A community
whose criticism has no venue, no uptake, hidden standards or excluded
voices is not objective, however capable its members.

## Assumptions

- The underdetermination argument: a "semantic gap" between data and
  hypothesis that only background assumptions bridge, and assumptions at
  the end of the chain for which no evidence is available (§5).
- "There is no higher authority or transcendent aperspectival position from
  which it is possible to adjudicate among foundational assumptions" (§5).

## Key results

Conceptual, not formal:

- **Four norms** (§5): (1) venues for critical interaction; (2) uptake,
  "demonstrated in change of belief distribution in the community over time
  in a way that is sensitive to the critical discourse"; (3) public
  accessibility of the standards that regulate discourse; (4) tempered
  equality of intellectual authority, a prima facie standing that can be
  lost "owing to failure to engage or to respond to criticism".
- **2002 extension**: observation and reasoning are themselves social, so
  the norms govern research in progress, not only finished work.
- **Contrast with Solomon**: Solomon locates rationality in the distribution
  of decision vectors and the acceptance of empirically successful
  theories, "regardless of the interactions among community members";
  Longino attends to deliberation.

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Background assumptions mediate all evidential relations, so no individual method secures objectivity | moderate | the underdetermination argument, stated not defended at length in the entry |
| C2 | Criticism makes a community objective only under the four norms | moderate | argued in the books; stated here |
| C3 | Zollman "proves" that sparse networks are more reliable | weak | the entry's wording; the source is a simulation study ([LIT-724](../literature.d/LIT-724.md)) |
| C4 | Zollman's effect disappears for larger groups or more distinct alternatives | strong | reports [LIT-733](../literature.d/LIT-733.md), read separately and consistent |

## Concepts

- **critical contextual empiricism** — empiricism in which evidential
  relations are fixed relative to background assumptions, and the
  legitimacy of those assumptions comes from community criticism under
  norms.
- **uptake** — criticism changing what the community believes over time, in
  response to the criticism.
- **tempered equality** — equal prima facie standing for perspectives,
  forfeitable by not engaging.

## Connections

The entry places Longino with Mill, Peirce and Popper on criticism and
supplies "conditions for the efficacy" of what they advocated. It sets her
against Solomon's social empiricism, Kitcher's credit models and
Goldman's veritistic social epistemology.

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md). The owner's essay (§8.2) applies Longino
to corporate self-governance: an organization of conscientious employees
may lack "the institutional capacity to make their criticism consequential".
That is norm (2), uptake, and the essay's list of what the capacity depends
on (information pathways, evaluative practices, standing rights to
challenge, revision procedures) maps onto norms (1), (3) and (4). The essay
does not name the norms; naming them would give §8.2 a test instead of a
suggestion.

Longino's norms are about a community's knowledge, not about its action or
will. Carrying them from knowing to governing is an extension the essay
makes, and should argue.

## Limitations

Written by a party to the debate. The entry states Longino's view and its
rivals; it does not adjudicate them, and the objections to tempered
equality are mentioned ("perhaps the most controversial") but not given.

## Open questions

Whether the norms can be measured in an actual institution, and whether an
institution meeting them in fact tracks truth better. Neither is addressed
here.
