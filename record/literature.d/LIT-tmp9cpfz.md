# LIT-tmp9cpfz: Increasing propensity to mind-wander by transcranial direct current stimulation? A registered report

Boayue, N. M., Csifcsák, G., Aslaksen, P., Turi, Z., Antal, A., Groot, J.,
Hawkins, G. E., Forstmann, B., Opitz, A., Thielscher, A. & Mittner, M.
(2020). Increasing propensity to mind-wander by transcranial direct current
stimulation? A registered report. *European Journal of Neuroscience* 51(3):
755–780. DOI 10.1111/ejn.14347. Read as the authors' preprint,
osf.io/dfex3.

Read in NOTE-tmps966y.

## Key takeaways

- **No effect.** "Contrary to our hypothesis and the findings from Axelrod
  et al. (2015), we found that the participants receiving anodal
  stimulation were numerically less likely to respond being off-task"
  (§4); BF for the null against a positive effect 10.65.
- **Replication failed.** "there was extreme evidence (BFreplication =
  0.002) that the original study was not replicated" (§4).
- **The montage is diffuse.** Electric-field models of 18 heads show the
  F3–supraorbital montage reaching medial and lateral cortex in both
  hemispheres, at about 0.1–0.2 V/m in the target, highly variable
  between people (§4, Fig. 6).

## Standing in the record

Registered for LEAD-141. With LIT-tmpg3vlm it leaves the tDCS effect on
mind-wandering contested; a later meta-analysis by two of its authors
(Nawani, Mittner and Csifcsák 2023, NeuroImage, DOI
10.1016/j.neuroimage.2023.120051, abstract only, not registered) finds "no
reliable evidence for tDCS influencing MW in the healthy".
