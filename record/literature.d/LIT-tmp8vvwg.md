# LIT-tmp8vvwg: On the psychology of poverty

Haushofer, J. & Fehr, E. (2014). On the psychology of poverty. *Science* 344(6186), 862–867. doi:10.1126/science.1232491.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpv5r2w](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpv5r2w.md). Page numbers are the Science print's.*

- **Not blame.** "we are not suggesting that the poor bear blame for
  their poverty. Rather, an environment of poverty into which one happens
  to have been born can trigger processes that reinforce poverty." (p. 862)
- **Link 1, poverty to affect, has causal support.** Of 25 causal
  studies, "18 studies show a positive effect of poverty alleviation on
  psychological well-being or stress" (p. 864). In the Kenya transfer
  trial, cortisol "was only reduced in those who received large
  transfers".
- **Link 2, affect to choice, is lab-only.** "the weakest link" is the
  effect of stress and negative affect on economic choice, and "the
  evidence on this link" "is currently restricted to laboratory studies"
  (pp. 865–866).
- **No trap shown.** A trap needs "a strong nonlinearity"; studies of
  income and psychological outcomes "show no strong signs of being
  nonlinear" (p. 866).
- **Persistence unknown.** "Little is known about whether poverty
  alleviation leads to a permanent or only a temporary increase in
  psychological well-being." (p. 866)
