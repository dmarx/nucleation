# NOTE-571: Catastrophic regime shifts in ecosystems: linking theory to observation

## Contribution

A standard of evidence. It separates what would be seen if a system had
alternative attractors from what would also be seen without them, and says
which observations can discriminate.

## Key insight

A sudden change, a bimodal distribution, or a state that persists are all
what alternative stable states predict, and all are also predicted by
simpler causes. What discriminates is reversibility. The system returns
when conditions are restored past the original point, or a temporary push
leaves a permanent change, and the return time is long enough to exclude
mere slowness.

## Assumptions

- The control factor is external to the system, or much slower than it.
- Recovery is not blocked by isolation (for example, no source of
  recolonists).

## Key results

- Field indicators (Figure 3): jumps in time series, multimodal frequency
  distributions, dual relationships to a control factor. Each has
  alternative explanations.
- Experimental indicators (Figure 4): initial-state dependence (Elodea and
  Lemna), lasting shifts after temporary fish removal in shallow lakes,
  hysteresis in charophytes in Lake Veluwe.
- Traps: Lake Zwemlust stayed clear for six years after biomanipulation and
  then slipped back. Hysteresis-like loops appear whenever the response is
  slow relative to the driver.
- Positive feedbacks "only lead to alternative attractors if they are
  sufficiently strong".

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Field patterns alone cannot establish alternative stable states | strong | each indicator shown to have alternative explanations |
| C2 | Experiments can come close to proof | moderate | worked examples, each with stated caveats |
| C3 | Slow erosion of resilience is neglected in favour of obvious triggers | moderate | argued, with examples |
| C4 | Positive feedback is necessary but not sufficient | strong | model results |

## Concepts

- **regime shift** — an abrupt change to a contrasting regime, a word
  preferred to "stable state" because regimes fluctuate.
- **hysteresis** — different paths for forward and backward change in a
  control factor.
- **resilience** — here, the size of the basin of attraction.

## Limitations

Ecological examples only; spatial scale and environmental noise are noted
as poorly explored.

## Open questions

How to apply the experimental tests to systems too large or slow to
manipulate, which is the problem for organizations as for oceans.
