summary: >-
  A laboratory climate game: 180 people in 30 groups of six, each with $40 to spend, decide over ten rounds whether to put $0, $2 or $4 into a climate account. If the group reaches $120, each member's $45 endowment is saved; if not, it is lost with 90% probability. When the saved endowments were paid to the players the next day, 9 of 10 groups reached the target. When they went instead to planting trees for future generations, 2 of 10 did. When players in the tree-planting game also read three short messages meant to activate the legacy motive (death awareness, the powerlessness of future generations, and paying forward a past generation's sacrifice), 7 of 10 did, and group investment rose from $77.20 to $105.20. All three messages raised investment over the matched rounds of the plain tree-planting game. Legacy induction made fair shares, not extra giving, the norm. There were no manipulation checks and no control message. The authors say people invest in legacies "for largely—although not entirely—selfish reasons".
