# CLAIM-680: That some ways of living are more worthwhile than others, and that this can be judged fallibly, licenses institutions to cultivate the conditions in which people can engage with what is worthwhile, not to impose a conception of it: a reasoned judgment of worth is not by itself a warrant for coercion

## The claim

A9 §VII, "fallibilist value pluralism", in three parts: not all activities are equally worthwhile because someone prefers them; judgments of worth are "imperfect, historically situated, and open to rational disagreement"; and "Third, believing that some values are genuine does not entitle any particular institution to impose its conception of the good life on everyone else." Its slogan: "**The existence of objective value does not imply the existence of an authority with infallible access to it.**" A9 §VIII draws the boundary:

> **Institutions can cultivate the conditions for meaningful engagement,
> but they cannot legitimately guarantee meaning by imposing particular
> commitments.**

A10 restates it with Sen: "**The political obligation is primarily to expand the capability for meaningful engagement, not to compel its realization.**" (§III), and "Our synthesis needs a distinction between **philosophical evaluation and political legitimacy**." (§IX).

## What grounds it, and what is A's

Wolf supplies the first two parts. Asked "Who's to say?" what is worthwhile, her answer is "no one in particular", with fallibilism and pooled judgment, and she calls an account of value's objectivity "an unsolved problem in philosophy" ([THEORY-1836](../theory.d/THEORY-1836.md), [NOTE-1949](../notes.d/NOTE-1949.md) results 5–6). The third part, the political boundary, is A's. Wolf disclaims practical use, so the inference from her fallibilism to a limit on institutions is A's, argued in [ARG-106](../arguments.d/ARG-106.md). Sen's public reasoning about weights ([THEORY-411](../theory.d/THEORY-411.md)) is a neighbour: it leaves the weighting of functionings to public reasoning, which is a procedure for disagreement, not a ban on imposition. Marcuse's "no tribunal can justly arrogate to itself the right to decide which needs should be developed and satisfied" ([THEORY-1853](../theory.d/THEORY-1853.md)) agrees in words and differs in reason: he defers to individuals only "if and when they are free to give their own answer".

## What it does not say

- Not neutrality. Institutions may judge some ways of living better and act on it by cultivating conditions: education that introduces people to fields of worth, support for valuable activities markets neglect, limits on exploitative practices (A9 §VIII, A10 §IX).
- Not that coercion is never justified. The claim denies only that the worth of a commitment, by itself, justifies imposing it.
- Not how to tell cultivation from imposition in hard cases. Nudges and defaults sit between them; [CLAIM-683](CLAIM-683.md) says what goes wrong when an institution measures meaning.

## Note of 2026-11-17: permitted is not authorized, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-737 — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17, Proposed -->

A90 II of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b, 2026-11-17) separates Can(A, x | s, G, K), feasible action, from Permitted_N(A, x), permissible action under N, and from Authorized_N(A, B, x), "A's legitimate authority to make a consequential decision in relation to B", with Can ⇏ Permitted and Permitted ⇏ Authorized: "A may be morally permitted to pursue a beneficial outcome while lacking authority to compel B's participation. This is one of the strongest explanations of why well-intentioned paternalism can still be wrongful."

This claim is an instance of the second non-implication. A reasoned judgment of worth can make it permissible for an institution to cultivate the conditions of worthwhile engagement; it does not authorize the institution to impose a conception of worth on those it governs. The general separation of grounds from authority, first in A71 §3 of the same exchange, is filed as [CLAIM-737](CLAIM-737.md).

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-18: what holds it back

The step from Wolf's fallibilism about worth to a limit on what institutions may impose is A's, and the body asserts it rather than argues it; [ARG-106](../arguments.d/ARG-106.md) records the step as A's without arguing it. The general separation it instances, that what may be pursued need not be imposed, is [CLAIM-737](CLAIM-737.md), Proposed; once that is held, the claim can rest on it.
