# LEAD-tmpbtms0: Take a census of established cognitive biases against the two classes of spurious sensitivity and spurious invariance, each bias with its transformation and its standard declared, counting how many fall in each class, in both, and in neither, status quo bias and motivated skepticism among them

## The gap

CLAIM-tmpei0cf (A11, Proposed): relative to a separately justified
standard, "many cognitive biases are spurious sensitivity, a response
that varies under a transformation of the situation that the standard
treats as irrelevant, or spurious invariance, a response that does not
vary under one the standard treats as relevant". "Many" is untested. The
assistant's note of 2026-11-24 on it shows "both classes are one
quantity", and that "a uniform bias escapes both", so a census needs a
third column.

## What it would do

1. **The list**: a published catalogue of established biases, fixed
   before coding (a standard review's list, named in advance), with status
   quo bias and motivated skepticism added if it lacks them.
2. **For each bias, declare**: the transformation of the situation (a
   reframing, an anchor, a change of base rate, a change of source); the
   standard that says whether the transformation is relevant (expected
   value, Bayesian updating, a declared norm); and the response measured.
3. **Code each** as spurious sensitivity, spurious invariance, both (one
   quantity read two ways, per the note), or neither (a uniform bias, or
   one with no transformation to declare).
4. **Status quo bias and motivated skepticism** get full rows: status quo
   bias as a choice that moves with which option is labelled the default,
   where the standard says the label is irrelevant (spurious sensitivity),
   or as a choice that stays put when the options' values change enough
   that the standard says it should move (spurious invariance); the
   census records which reading the source's measure supports. Motivated
   skepticism as an evidence threshold that varies with whether the
   conclusion is wanted, where the standard says it should not. Each row
   also says whether CLAIM-tmpysg95's compiled-contract reading applies.

## What would count

- **For CLAIM-tmpei0cf**: most established biases fall in one of the two
  classes once transformation and standard are declared.
- **Against it**: a large share falls in neither, or the coding of a bias
  flips between classes with the choice of standard, so the classes
  describe the standard and not the bias.
- **For LEAD-tmpr4xwc**: the census's rows are the bias measures that
  CLAIM-tmp5atd3's co-variation test would move together; which share a
  transformation is a prediction it can use.
