# NOTE-tmpe9j5q: A Phase Transition in Diffusion Models

## Contribution

A reason, in a solvable model, why noising a datum and denoising it back
changes its class abruptly at some noise level while keeping many of its
low-level parts. For hierarchical data generated by a random grammar, the
optimal denoiser is belief propagation on the generating tree, and the
paper shows (by an annealed mean-field calculation, confirmed numerically)
that the reconstruction of the root has a phase transition in the noise in
the large-depth limit when the grammar is sparse, whereas the
reconstruction of leaves is smooth. It then quantifies the corresponding
effect for a real diffusion model on ImageNet through the hidden layers of
pretrained classifiers, and contrasts it with a two-mode Gaussian mixture,
which has a class transition but no hierarchy.

## Key insight

In a sparse grammar most strings are not grammatical, so an error at a
low level is detectable and correctable from its neighbours: whether the
evidence about the class grows or shrinks on the way up the tree is
decided by one number, s f (branching times the fraction of tuples in
use). Below a critical noise the evidence is amplified at each level and
the class survives; above it the evidence decays at each level, and over
many levels it reaches chance. The leaves need no such amplification, so
they degrade gradually. Class and parts therefore come apart in noise:
the whole is lost all at once, the parts one by one.

## Assumptions

- **Data.** The RHM of [LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md), with v classes, every level's vocabulary
  of size v, branching s, depth L, d = s^L leaves, and m rules per symbol
  drawn uniformly without replacement from the v^s tuples, no tuple
  shared between parents. Main text: f = m/v^(s−1); SI uses
  f = (mv − 1)/(v^s − 1), the same to leading order.
- **Known rules, uniform prior.** The denoiser knows the grammar and has a
  uniform prior over classes (unconditional diffusion). It is the Bayes
  posterior, i.e. a diffusion model with perfect generalisation, which the
  authors call "a strong assumption".
- **Noise.** Either Gaussian diffusion of each one-hot leaf in ℝ^v, whose
  posterior per leaf is a softmax of x(t)·√α_t/(1 − α_t) (Eq. 4, SI 1), or
  the simplified ε-process: belief 1 − ε on the true symbol, ε uniform
  (Eq. 6). The two are connected empirically through an effective ε(t)
  (SI 3, Fig. S7).
- **Annealed mean field.** Messages are replaced by their average over
  realisations of the rules; correlations between messages and the
  fluctuations of the random rules are neglected, and the downward pass
  substitutes average upward messages (Eq. 54).
- **Large depth.** The transition is a statement about L → ∞; at finite L
  it is a crossover that sharpens with L (Fig. 5, S4).

## Key results

- **Eq. 4 / SI Eq. 12.** p(x_i(0) = e_γ | x_i(t)) = exp(x_{i,γ}(t)/Δ_t)/Z,
  Δ_t = (1 − α_t)/√α_t: leaf initialisation for BP.
- **Eqs. 5, 13–16.** BP upward and downward updates over factor nodes
  ψ(y, x_1, …, x_s) = 1 iff y → (x_1, …, x_s) is a rule.
- **Fig. 5.** Exact BP, v = 32, m = 8, s = 2, L = 10, versus diffusion
  time: the class's probability of being unchanged drops sharply at a
  characteristic t; lower levels decline smoothly.
- **Eq. 8 / SI Eqs. 35–38.** Annealed upward map
  F(p) = (p^s + f (m−1)/(mv−1)(1 − p^s)) / (p^s + f(1 − p^s)), with
  p = 1 − ε + ε/v. Fixed points p = 1 and p = 1/v.
- **SI Eqs. 39–42.** F′(1) = f s m(v−1)/(mv−1), so p = 1 is attractive iff
  s f < 1 + (1 − 1/m)/(v − 1), i.e. s f < 1 + O(1/v). Then a repulsive
  fixed point p* in (1/v, 1) appears and the class is recoverable iff
  ε < ε*(s f); for s f > 1 only p = 1/v attracts and the class is never
  recoverable at depth. *Holds in:* annealed approximation, L → ∞.
- **Fig. 6 / S2.** Phase diagram in (s f, ε) from upward BP on one
  realisation (v = 32, s = 2, L = 10) agrees with ε* from the map.
  "Inference" there means the class's probability exceeds the leaves'
  initial belief.
- **Eqs. 54–59, Fig. 7a, S4–S6.** Combining upward and downward maps gives
  per-level marginals p^(ℓ); they match exact BP for leaves and class, and
  the curves for top and bottom levels cross at the transition. Downward
  messages fluctuate strongly at small ε (synonyms leave a child
  underdetermined by its parent).
- **Fig. 2, S8.** Unconditional ImageNet DDPM (Dhariwal and Nichol,
  T = 1000): cosine similarity of standardised ConvNeXt-Base activations,
  original versus forward–backward sample, drops sharply for the logits
  near t ≈ T/2 while early layers decline smoothly, and the layer curves
  invert around the drop; ResNet-50, Wide ResNet-50 and ResNet-152 agree.
- **Fig. 7b.** A 7-layer CNN trained to 99.2% on an RHM (L = 7, s = 2,
  v = 16, m = 4; s f = 0.5) with BP-sampled reconstructions shows the same
  inversion versus ε.
- **SI 5, Fig. S9.** A two-mode Gaussian mixture in d = 1024 with exact
  score: the classifier's logits drop at t ≈ 0.6 T, but hidden layers move
  with the logits; no inversion.

## Method

Exact belief propagation on the RHM's factor tree, run numerically on
single draws of the rules from leaf beliefs set either by Gaussian
diffusion or by the ε-process; then an annealed average of one BP step
over rule draws (multivariate hypergeometric counts of tuples at each
Hamming distance from the true one), iterated level by level as a scalar
map whose fixed points give the phase diagram. Empirically, the
forward–backward protocol of Ho et al. on ImageNet, measured through the
standardised activations of pretrained classifiers at every depth.

## Concepts

- **forward–backward experiment** — noise a datum to time t by the forward
  process, then sample the reverse process from there; compare the result
  with the original.
- **f** — the fraction of the v^s possible s-tuples a level's rules use;
  f = 1 makes every string grammatical.
- **ε-process** — leaf beliefs 1 − ε on the true symbol and ε uniform over
  the alphabet; a stand-in for Gaussian noise.
- **phase transition** — here, the change at ε* in the L → ∞ fixed point of
  the annealed map, between a phase where the class is recoverable and one
  where it is at chance.
- **inversion** — the crossing, at the transition, of the "unchanged"
  curves of top and bottom levels (or of logits and early layers).

## Connections

It applies [LIT-877](../literature.d/LIT-877.md)'s grammar to generative modelling. Ho et al. introduced
the forward–backward protocol and the remark this paper half-contradicts.
Biroli and Mézard's and Raya and Ambrogioni's speciation or
symmetry-breaking times for mixtures are the nearest theory; SI 5 is the
paper's case that they miss the hierarchy. The phase transition is a tree
reconstruction problem in the sense of Mossel (cited for BP), with an
annealed criterion: stability of the perfectly informed fixed point, which
is my reading of Eq. 40, not a bound the paper proves. Mei's "U-Nets as
belief propagation" (arXiv:2404.18444) is cited as the follow-up showing
U-Nets can approximate the BP denoiser. Kadkhodaie et al.'s multiscale
wavelet models are the other hierarchical account of images it cites, and
it notes that their graph of latents is not tree-like.

## Limitations

- **The transition is not proved.** It is the fixed-point structure of an
  annealed scalar map; agreement with quenched BP is shown numerically for
  one or two parameter sets (v = 32, s = 2, L = 10; m = 8 for the
  marginals). The downward map is a further approximation and fluctuates
  at small ε.
- **Ideal, not trained, denoiser.** The theory assumes the rules are known.
  Nothing shows that the ImageNet DDPM, or any trained network, computes
  BP; the link is the shape of the curves.
- **Proxies.** Latent features of images are not observed; classifier
  activations at each depth stand in for them, with the classifier's own
  hierarchy taken on trust.
- **Small inconsistencies.** The related-work section says results are
  averaged over 10^5 ImageNet samples, the Fig. 2 caption over 10,000
  images; the caption's statistic, "the maximum of their empirical
  distribution", is not defined further. Main text and SI define f
  slightly differently.
- **Uniform grammar, s = 2.** All numerics use s = 2 and equiprobable
  rules.
- **The contrast case is one mixture.** That only hierarchical data invert
  is supported by one Gaussian mixture against one RHM.

## Open questions

- A rigorous threshold for class reconstruction on the RHM with quenched
  random rules, and its relation to known tree-reconstruction thresholds
  (Kesten–Stigum and beyond).
- What a trained diffusion model on RHM data does with finite training
  sets, and whether its threshold moves with the number of examples (the
  regime of [THEORY-195](../theory.d/THEORY-195.md)).
- Whether hierarchical segmentations of real images, as the authors
  propose, show their latent variables changing at the predicted times.
