# CLAIM-tmp4zii5: An asymmetric arrangement can come to supply the standard by which complaints against it are judged, so that a party objecting to its terms is counted as the party violating them; this normative capture is self-sealing, since the objection is heard only in the terms it contests

## The claim

An implicit contract has no text, so what it requires is whatever the
organization takes it to require. A22 §13: a party "may acquire
disproportionate influence over how an implicit contract is interpreted,
including what counts as compliance, violation, reasonable expectation and
legitimate revision." Two forms: the organization "might come to treat one
participant's withdrawal as a breach while regarding another participant's
withdrawal as ordinary discretion", or it "might evaluate both participants
using nominally identical standards whose practical consequences are highly
asymmetric."

The form with content of its own is the last step: "transforming an
asymmetric arrangement into the very standard by which complaints against
that arrangement are evaluated. The process that objects can then be
characterized as the process violating the norm, even where the objection
concerns the unjust organization of the norm itself."

It is self-sealing because of how the operative norm works
([CLAIM-tmphq6hp](CLAIM-tmphq6hp.md)): a discrepancy with an operative norm recruits correction
of the behaviour, not revision of the norm. When the discrepancy is an
objection to the norm, the correction is aimed at the objector. And by the
normalization route ([CLAIM-tmp0i6c0](CLAIM-tmp0i6c0.md)), the norm need never have been agreed
to, so there is no moment of agreement to appeal to against it.

A22 §13's taxonomy of what can be captured, which A offers as working
labels: interpretive (what the relationship requires), evidential (what
counts as proof of performance), affective (which discrepancies feel
urgent), normative (which expectations count as obligations) and
constitutional (who can revise these).

## Relation to the record

- Interpretive capture ([TERM-066](../terms.d/TERM-066.md)) is the evaluation driven by the party
  evaluated rather than by independent evidence. Normative capture is the
  case where what is captured is the standard itself, not only the reading
  of evidence against it.
- [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md) holds the wrong of reproducing representations and allocations
  of authority that keep a justified standard from constraining an
  organization. Normative capture is one mechanism by which that happens:
  the arrangement's operative standard takes the justified one's place in
  hearing complaints.
- [CLAIM-448](CLAIM-448.md) holds that observed coherence can be the product of selective
  contestation. A captured standard is one way contests get excluded.
- Argyris's self-sealing theories-in-use ([THEORY-145](../theory.d/THEORY-145.md)) are a neighbour: tests
  kept from testing the premises they rest on.

## What it does not say

- Not that every objector is right. A24 §19: "dissent is not intrinsically
  correct."
- Not that "normative capture" should be a term. A offers it as an
  extension, not a settled term; the record holds the claim and leaves the
  label in A's voice.
