# LIT-130: Personal Identity and Ethics

Annette Dufner (2025), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-ethics/>

## Key takeaways

- Which theory of persistence is true (psychological or biological), and whether identity or only the relations composing it is what matters, jointly settle a family of ethical questions — prudence, responsibility, abortion, advance directives, distributive justice — and the priority between metaphysics and ethics is itself unsettled.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry surveys how our being temporally extended and distinct individuals bears on ethics. It sets out the psychological and biological accounts of personal identity and Parfit's challenge that identity itself is not what matters, then works through the practical consequences: special prudential concern for one's future, responsibility for past acts, the beginning and end of life (abortion, death, dementia and advance directives), and the separateness of persons in normative ethics and distributive justice. It ends by noting that it is unclear whether a theory of what matters should be fixed first or developed in reflective equilibrium with ethics.
