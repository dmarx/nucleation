# THEORY-tmpgrb17: Levin and Shomrat report, in an announcement without data, that planarians trained to recognise a laser-etched chamber recognised it after head removal and regeneration given a brief refresher, and infer that memory can be stored outside the brain and imprinted on the regenerated one

## The account

Worms trained to a chamber with a "specifically rough (laser-etched)
surface" recognised it, by eating liver there sooner than naive worms,
after "complete head removal and regeneration", given a brief refresher
(paragraph 6). The authors read this as showing that "not only can
memories be stored somewhere outside of the brain, but ... they can be
imprinted on a naïve regenerated brain" (paragraph 7), and suggest that
memory "could be globally distributed throughout the body" in non-neural
cells linked by gap junctions (paragraph 8). "At the moment, we do not
know where and how the memory is encoded in the body."

## Evidence

- The result is stated in words only; the worms "need a brief refresher
  after head regeneration in order to show good recall" (paragraph 6).
- The post does not say whether the refresher alone gives untrained
  worms the same result.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-302](../questions.d/QUESTION-302.md) names the planarian result and its reminder, and its note
of 2026-10-29 records that "The planarian result is weaker than filed
(Levin and Shomrat's post, [NOTE-1283](../notes.d/NOTE-1283.md))": until the refresher control is
reported, the case shows that something outlasts the head, not yet that
a memory does. The reading holds the authors' answer to what carries it
across, something outside the head, which they say they cannot yet
locate.
