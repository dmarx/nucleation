# NOTE-1596: Hopkins — Flexure motion stages via Freedom, Actuation and Constraint Topologies (PhD thesis)

## Contribution

As the abstract states it: (1) fifty freedom and constraint space types
for parallel and serial synthesis; (2) intermediate freedom spaces, for
avoiding or using underconstraint; (3) the twist–wrench stiffness matrix
(TWSM); (4) twenty-six actuation spaces; (5) a theory of the force and
displacement an actuator must supply for a desired motion. A serial
lead-screw flexure is designed, and a parallel stage is built to test the
actuation theory.

## Key insight

What a flexure permits, how it is constrained, and where it must be
driven are three different things. The freedom space is fixed by the
constraint space ("Equation 1.9 suggests that a parallel flexure system's
freedom space is the null space of that system's constraint space.",
§1.2.5, p. 26), but freedoms a parallel system cannot have come from
stacking bodies in series, and the lines along which the freedoms can be
driven cleanly depend on stiffness, which the freedom space does not
fix.

## Assumptions

- **Small motions** (first-order twists); flexible constraints modelled as
  pure-force wrenches (§1.2.3).
- **Actuation chapter**: parallel systems only, linear actuators, and
  "quasi-static actuation scenarios where the dynamic behavior associated
  with stage inertia may be ignored" (§4.1, p. 115).
- **Serial synthesis is kinematic.** Dynamics enters qualitatively, as the
  cost of underconstraint, and is not modelled.

## Key results

- **Fifty types.** Abstract, p. 3: "the fifty freedom and constraint space
  types were found". §2.1, p. 29: "There are only 50 freedom and constraint
  space pairs called types." Columns of 0–6 DOF hold 1, 3, 10, 22, 10, 3
  and 1.
- **Duality (§1.2.5, p. 26).** The freedom space is the null space of the
  constraint space; "Intuitively this means that DOFs are the motions that
  produce the least resistance from the constraints."
- **Redundant constraints (§1.2.4, pp. 24–25).** "Constraint space is the
  geometric shape that visually represents the regions where redundant
  constraints could be placed without altering a system's DOFs." Adding
  them "would affect the system's elastomechanics, dynamics, and load
  capacity without changing the system DOFs".
- **The parallel pyramid (§2.1, p. 30; §1.1.1, p. 18).** "Parallel flexure
  systems may only possess freedom spaces that lie within the parallel
  pyramid." Three pure translations are "only possible for a serial flexure
  system", because "the moment a single flexible constraint is connected
  directly from a stage to ground, the stage looses one of its three
  translations along the constraint's axis".
- **Underconstraint (§2.2.2, pp. 33–34).** "When a serial flexure system
  possesses one or more redundant DOFs, the system is underconstrained."
  It buys stroke, but "Unfortunately, underconstrained flexure systems
  generally have poor dynamic characteristics."
- **Kinematic equivalence (§2.2.4, p. 36).** "Although kinematically
  equivalent flexure elements may be substituted without changing a
  system's DOFs, the system's elastomechanics, dynamics, and
  manufacturability may be altered to satisfy desired design
  requirements."
- **Where the ideal picture fails (§1.1.2, p. 21).** Real flexures stretch
  along their axes, giving parasitic error: "These deformations cannot be
  predicted via constraint-based design principles."
- **Actuation space (§4.2, p. 118).** "An actuation space is a shape that
  represents the actuator locations and orientations that must be used to
  minimize parasitic errors when the number of actuators is to equal the
  number of DOFs to be actuated." It is computed from the TWSM, which holds
  "the orientation, location, geometry and material properties of each
  flexible constraint" (§4.2.1). Off it, "coupled actuators not in the
  actuation space do the job of a single actuator in that space with much
  more complexity and cost" (p. 119).
- **An actuatability test (§4.3.2, p. 122).** "All flexure systems that may
  be fully actuated with linear actuators will possess one of the
  twenty-six actuation spaces"; otherwise "the designer knows that this
  system cannot be actuated with linear actuators alone".
- **Experiment (§4.7, p. 134).** "According to the measured data, the
  minimum error occurred when the actuation force was applied around 17.5
  cm below the tip of the probe.", against a prediction of 20 cm. The
  measured errors were much larger than predicted, which the author puts
  down to large-deformation kinematics.

## Concepts

- **Freedom space**: "the geometric shape that visually represents a
  system's kinematics, i.e. all the twists the flexure permits" (§1.2.2).
- **Constraint space**: the region where redundant constraints can be
  placed without altering the DOFs.
- **FACT chart**: the 50 types; the practical chart keeps those with
  usable pure-force constraint spaces, bounded by the parallel pyramid.
- **Intermediate freedom spaces**: the freedom spaces of the stacked
  parallel modules, which sum to the serial system's freedom space.
- **Underconstraint**: a serial system with redundant DOFs, intermediate
  bodies left free when stage and ground are fixed.
- **Kinematic equivalence**: having the same freedom space.
- **Twist–wrench stiffness matrix**: W = [K_TW]·T, the wrench that
  produces a motion.
- **Actuation space**: the lines along which a minimal set of linear
  actuators must act to drive the DOFs without parasitic error.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures.** A's quoted gloss,
  that the thesis "expands the framework to Freedom, Actuation and
  Constraint Topologies, retaining the acronym FACT", is A's paraphrase;
  the sentence is not in the thesis, though the name is its title's. It is
  right, and leaves out what the expansion is: serial synthesis and the
  50-type chart with the parallel pyramid, underconstraint, the TWSM and
  actuation spaces. A gives no title; the title names the subject as
  flexure-based motion stages for mechatronic systems.
- **Freedom, constraint, actuation as three things.** A's "A mechanism may
  be free to move in a direction without possessing the means to drive
  itself in that direction" fits closely, with one correction: the thesis
  is about external actuators a designer places, and nothing in it is
  about a mechanism driving itself.
- **[CLAIM-345](../claims.d/CLAIM-345.md) (equal controllability rank does not fix the cost of
  control).** Supports A's proposed extension, that equal nominal freedom
  spaces do not entail equal effort or precision, in a mechanical
  setting: kinematically equivalent designs differ in elastomechanics and
  dynamics, the error-free actuator placement depends on the TWSM and not
  on the freedom space, and underconstrained systems with the same DOFs
  have poor dynamics. An analogy only: the thesis is quasi-static and
  computes no effort integrals or Gramians.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md) (composing interactions turns an interior into a
  constraint).** Qualifies, and is the closest held entry. FACT's serial
  stacking is the kinematic counterpart, the stage seeing the sum of the
  intermediate freedom spaces. Underconstraint is the case where the
  interior, an intermediate body, is not fixed by the boundary, and the
  thesis says those systems have poor dynamics: a mechanical instance of
  the condition under which eliminating the interior stops describing the
  system well.
- **[CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md) (matched ordinary trajectories can conceal differences).**
  Supports, by analogy: kinematically equivalent flexures with the same
  freedom space differ in stiffness, dynamics and actuation, differences
  hidden by the matched nominal motion and exposed by loading or driving.
- **[CASE-194](../cases.d/CASE-194.md) (the same failure from an actuator setting, an observation or
  an authority rule).** Qualifies: the thesis separates the freedom space
  from where actuation must act, and shows that an actuation space outside
  the 26 cannot be met by linear actuators, a mechanical case of actuation
  as a constraint of its own.
- **[THEORY-1335](../theory.d/THEORY-1335.md) (enabling constraints) and A's framing that constraints
  make useful freedom possible.** Qualified. **In FACT, new freedom comes
  from stacking bodies in series, not from adding constraints**; constraints
  in parallel only intersect freedom spaces. What constraints add is
  stiffness and precision.
- **[THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md) (viability kernel) and [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md).** Unrelated: no
  invariance or viability notion here.
- Filed: [THEORY-1390](../theory.d/THEORY-1390.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The author's own (ch. 5).** No large-deformation synthesis; no
  actuator placement for serial systems; no dynamic actuation; no
  synthesis for dynamic characteristics; no sensitivity analysis for
  fabrication, orientation or location errors.
- **One experiment**: a single stage with one actuator line varied, and
  measured error much larger than predicted; only the location of the
  minimum is compared.
- **Completeness of the 50 types** rests on App. A and the master's thesis,
  not checked here.
- **A thesis**, not peer reviewed.
