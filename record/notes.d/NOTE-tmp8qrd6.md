# NOTE-tmp8qrd6: Zeiser (2024) — Owning Decisions: AI Decision-Support and the Attributability-Gap

## Contribution

Shifts the AI-responsibility question from agent-like systems and blame to decision support and authorship, using Watson's and Shoemaker's three faces of responsibility, and states a positive criterion for responsibility-enabling tools.

## Key results

1. Abstract: the problem is "primarily a problem of attributability rather than of accountability".
2. "The Attributability-Gap": "Attributability-gaps occur when a human decision-maker uses a decision-tool that presupposes values that have a significant influence on the outcome of the decision and that they haven’t endorsed as their own values." Of the recruiter: "Despite being technically made by a human the decision doesn’t reflect characteristics of Susan’s judgement."
3. The criterion: "A decision-support tool should enable a human operator to make decisions in accordance with their goals and values (attributability), in a way that allows them to normatively justify and defend their decision (answerability)."
4. "The Faces of Responsibility": the faces diverge; "Sometimes, for example, the right person to blame didn’t actually make the decision".
5. "Descriptive Decision-Support": "descriptive AI systems also don’t provide neutral, value-free information"; attributability "might not be a binary property, but a gradual one".
6. Meaningful human control (LIT-tmpqyqtw) "does not specifically focus on attributability".
7. Conclusion: where blame and liability are the aim, "we could still aim for responsibility without attributability".

## Assumptions

Watson's self-disclosure view, approximated by value-judgements; endorsement as the test of ownership; a single final human decision-maker; the value-ladenness of evidence-production (Rudner, Douglas, Biddle).

## Concepts

Accountability, attributability, answerability; attributability gap; decision ownership; prescriptive and descriptive decision support; endorsement; value-flexible AI; blank check responsibility.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-068**: evidence that ownership and accountability come apart in both directions, in ordinary cases: a manager who takes the blame for an employee's decision, a negligent black-box user blameworthy without answerability, and "responsibility without attributability" as a design option.
- **THEORY-147**: the same distinction, from Watson and Shoemaker rather than Wolf, with decision-support cases of divergence.
- **The exchange** (A19 §2) reports the distinction accurately. Its doctor example is A's, and its link to responsibility for structuring others' conditions (CLAIM-tmpnw7ez) is A's: Zeiser treats the user's authorship, not the tool-maker's answerability.

## Limitations

Conceptual; no evidence on whether users endorse tool values. The endorsement test is assumed rather than defended, and "a detailed analysis of their ethical impact is beyond the scope of this article".
