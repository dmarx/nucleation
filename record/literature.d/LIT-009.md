# LIT-009: An 800 Myr-old Impact Shower on the Terrestrial Planets from the Breakup of the Eulalia Parent Body

William F. Bottke, David Vokrouhlický, Melissa Dykhuis, Nicolle Zellner (2026), *The Planetary Science Journal (vol. 7, no. 7, art. 171)* — [ARXIV-2606.05036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05036)

## Key takeaways

- The collisional breakup of the carbonaceous Eulalia parent body near the Jupiter 3:1 resonance, about 800–865 Ma, can plausibly explain the spike in large lunar craters (including Copernicus) and impact-glass ages near 800 Ma. About three-quarters of the family's fragments drained into the J3:1 over about 150 Myr.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Several lines of evidence point to a surge of large lunar impacts about 800 Ma: the model ages of big craters such as the 93 km Copernicus, and clustering in the ages of Apollo impact glasses. The authors look for a source and point to the Eulalia asteroid family, whose primitive, carbonaceous-chondrite-like parent was disrupted near the J3:1 mean-motion resonance. Their collisional and dynamical simulations send about three-quarters of the fragments into the resonance within about 150 Myr, some directly and some by Yarkovsky drift. From there the fragments reach planet-crossing orbits and raise the bombardment rate on the Moon and the terrestrial planets. The authors speculate that the same shower may connect to biosphere changes on Earth and to a pulse of Martian volcanism.
