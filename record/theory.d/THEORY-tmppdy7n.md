# THEORY-tmppdy7n: Organizational closure is a circular organization of efficient causes by which a system produces the conditions of its own existence, distinct from feedback, self-organization and catalytic cycles; systems whose loops do not regenerate the constraints that make them possible approximate closure without instantiating it

## Source

Rubin, Roli, Stano & Damiano (2026), [LIT-tmpedw28](../literature.d/LIT-tmpedw28.md), read in [NOTE-tmp88qyc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp88qyc.md):
§3 (closure against feedback), §4 (order against organization), §5 note
17 (regulation as observer-relative), §6 (Rosen, Table 1, the halting
argument), §7 (convergence, Table 3, the AI verdict, note 35, conclusions
a–d).

## What was actually shown

The paper is a conceptual genealogy. It measures nothing and models
nothing.

**What it documents.** The notions of closure have distinct origins and
close different things: Ashby information, Maturana and Varela operations,
Rosen efficient causes, Montévil and Mossio constraints, Kauffman
catalysts. First-order cybernetics equated circular causality with
feedback. The BCL and Rosen each rejected that equation, one through
self-reference and material self-production, the other through the
(M, R)-system's internally produced replication map. The paper reports
that for Maturana and Varela regulation "depends entirely on the observer".
Catalytic closure is "not the organizational closure found in autopoietic
or (M, R)-systems", since its catalysts are not produced by a closed
network of efficient causes.

**What it proposes.** That the common problem is "How can a living system
become the cause of the conditions required for its own persistence?", and
that the coherent answer is a circular organization of efficient causes,
"the circular entailment of efficient causes that fabricate and regenerate
one another while maintaining their own boundary conditions". So read,
closure "cannot be exhaustively characterized as a state-determined
syntactic procedure". The authors say this is not a claim that living
systems cannot be simulated in some respects.

**What it concludes about AI.** In-context learning, agentic loops,
persistent memory and RLHF add "operational circularity and adaptive
regulation". But "Their feedback loops do not regenerate the constraints
that make those loops possible, nor do they fabricate the conditions of
their own continued existence." So they are "engineered approximations of
closure rather than instances of organizational closure itself".

## What this does not say

- **It does not show closure is non-computable.** The halting-problem
  argument against a computable expression of Rosen's closure makes the
  verdict depend on requiring computations to halt, and the authors say
  so: Rosen's claim "holds true if and only if computability is defined by
  halting algorithms". They call the conclusions provisional and decline to
  assess Rosen.
- **It does not analyse any AI system.** The verdict follows from the
  definition. No architecture is put through Table 3's criteria.
- **It gives no test.** The paper says understanding closure "must
  ultimately rely on the ability to detect when closure occurs", and note 35
  says verdicts change when "the analysis is shifted to descriptive levels
  other than those initially specified by the model". It supplies no
  detection method. It does not answer [QUESTION-tmpgjngk](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpgjngk.md).
- **It does not settle the flame.** [THEORY-072](THEORY-072.md), following [LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md), grants
  a flame closure and draws the line at differentiation. This account's
  closure would deny the flame closure, but it analyses no flame and does
  not mention differentiation. The two are different criteria for the same
  line, and neither has been applied to both sides.
- **It is not about self-governance or a will.** Its "autonomy" is
  constitutive autonomy, as in [THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md). Following Maturana it equates
  life with mind, which is a claim about cognition, not about a will;
  [CLAIM-116](../claims.d/CLAIM-116.md)'s disclaimer is untouched.
- **It is not ML practice.** It says what current systems are, not how to
  build them.
