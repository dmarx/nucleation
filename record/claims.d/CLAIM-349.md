# CLAIM-349: An organization's access to a signal, the signal's authority over action, and action on it are separable, and adaptive regulation keeps the first while making the second conditional on evidence; overcontrol, which buys freedom from harmful acts by cutting access or making inhibition unconditional, is a maladaptive regime and not more control, so flexibility is a context-sensitive property of maintained influence relations, not of how much is inhibited

## The claim

A41: "An organization could become extremely resistant to internal changes
while losing its capacity to learn, correct errors, or act at all." The
owner, at U42: "it feels like there might be some useful connections to the
ACT and DBT frameworks here, which provide us with accounts/ontologies of
maladaption and productive flexibility."

A42's opening: "**the ability to regulate a threatening or conflicting
process is not the same as the ability to suppress it, and neither is the
same as knowing when suppression is warranted.**" It separates "**Recognizing
a signal**, **granting it authority**, and **acting on it**." A warning
from a threat monitor admits three responses: suppress it, obey it and halt
everything, or "Preserve the warning, investigate its evidence, and condition
its influence on the findings." The third "preserves both **access to the
signal** and **the organization's authority to evaluate it**."

## What each framework supplies

- **ACT: salience is not authority.** [CLAIM-156](CLAIM-156.md) says mindfulness changes
  which interests may act, not what they value. A42 wrote it as
  representational salience ⇏ practical authority, with ACT's defusion as
  a mechanism: "A
  thought may continue to occur while its capacity to govern behavior
  changes." The record's readings are [THEORY-051](../theory.d/THEORY-051.md) (self as context and
  defusion) and [THEORY-050](../theory.d/THEORY-050.md) (inflexibility: fusion supports experiential
  avoidance, which narrows behaviour). ACT's flexibility is not frequent
  change: "**sometimes persistence is the flexible response**". Kashdan and
  Rottenberg, read in [NOTE-436](../notes.d/NOTE-436.md) and one of [THEORY-050](../theory.d/THEORY-050.md)'s sources, make the
  general form: flexible regulation beats any fixed strategy.
- **DBT: stabilize, then correct.** A42: "**An organization may need to
  remain operational in the presence of unresolved frustration long enough
  to identify a better response, rather than immediately eliminate the
  signal or enter an irreversible defensive regime.**" Its four stages are
  immediate regulation, diagnostic preservation, adaptive correction and
  governance revision. The last is [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md): the regime, the governing
  organization and the capacity to transform it fail separately. The
  pattern is "**bounded stabilization followed by evidence-responsive
  correction**". The source framework is Linehan's DBT, not held in the
  record.
- **RO-DBT: control can be the disorder.** Lynch's RO-DBT, in A42's table,
  sets "Adaptive self-control versus excessive inhibition, rigidity, and
  restricted social signaling".
  A42's organizational translation: tighter monitoring, centralized
  authorization and costly challenge may reduce unauthorized acts and also
  produce "Less disclosure of near misses", "Fewer challenges to mistaken
  decisions" and "Lower visibility into emerging risks". "The organization
  may appear disciplined while losing the information and flexibility
  necessary to remain effective." That is protective lock-in ([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)): a
  protective regime that "generates the conditions that justify its own
  continuation and reduces the capacity to leave it". [QUESTION-045](../questions.d/QUESTION-045.md) asks
  whether a disorder is the same mechanism at the individual scale.

A42's four classes, "proposed organizational classifications, not clinical
diagnostic categories": undercontrol, overcontrol, authority capture and
adaptive governance. "An organization could exhibit both overcontrol and
authority capture." A43 kept them as "analytic proposals, not clinical
diagnoses of institutions".

## The ordinal form

A42 §6 states flexibility as an inclusion order. For contexts C and
acceptable responses A(c) fixed independently of the policy,
R(π) = {c ∈ C : π(c) ∈ A(c)}, and π₁ ⪰_flex π₂ exactly when
R(π₂) ⊆ R(π₁). It is a preorder and leaves policies that succeed in
different contexts incomparable. Unconditional inhibition succeeds in the
threat contexts and fails in false alarms and warranted change; a
permissive policy does the reverse. A policy that inhibits on evidence of
threat succeeds in both sets wherever the evidence tells the contexts apart,
and so dominates both there. Where two contexts with disjoint acceptable sets
produce the same evidence, no policy can succeed in both, and the order
leaves the candidates incomparable. That is why the claim needs access: a
policy that has cut the signal has made more contexts indistinguishable. It
is the inclusion construction of the compatibility complex ([CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md))
applied to contexts.

## Where it stands

A42 found Guida, Minutola and Presti (2026), "Propagation, not
aggregation", and said "I would add both works to the record before claiming
novelty": on their account organizational flexibility is not an aggregate
of flexible individuals but "must be **retained and propagated through the
organization's pattern of consequences and authority**". Their antecedent is
Bond's organizational flexibility model, which A42 dated 2018 and which
could not be identified uniquely. Neither is held yet. The organizational
transfer of ACT is theirs before it is the record's; what the record can
add is the influence model of how flexibility propagates or entrenches.

A42 listed [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) among "The existing ACT records". It is a reading of
self-determination theory (Vansteenkiste, Ryan and Soenens; Deci and Ryan),
which the record treats as a partner of the inflexibility account, not part
of ACT.

## What it does not say

- It does not say less control is better. A42: "The prediction is not that
  maximal openness wins. A system without effective threat containment may
  fail catastrophically."
- It does not say organizations feel anything. A42: "The organizational
  analogue concerns causal access and authority, not a literal
  institutional experience of anxiety, acceptance, or mindfulness."
- It does not say the clinical evidence carries over. A42: RO-DBT's evidence
  is "promising but not definitive"; Hatoum and Burton (2024), the first
  systematic review, and the RefraMED trial (Lynch et al. 2020) are A42's
  sources for that, and the trial's longer-term and economic findings "were
  less conclusive".
- It does not say an organization's current values are legitimate. A42:
  "Its organizational analogue cannot presume that an institution's
  currently maintained values are legitimate, or that successfully pursuing
  them benefits its constituents." That standard is [QUESTION-087](../questions.d/QUESTION-087.md)'s open
  problem.
