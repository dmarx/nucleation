# CLAIM-tmp7z7si: No public statement found by the league's reported count, the club or its supporters counts the 2005 Washington Nationals as the original Washington Senators continued: the franchise is counted through the Montreal Expos, and what the club and supporters claim of the Senators is Washington's baseball history

## The claim

CASE-105 left open how the league, the club and supporters count the
2005 Nationals, and CLAIM-329's defeater names it as the test. Read from
public pages on 2026-11-23 and coded by rules written first
(CASE-tmp44mzc):

- **League.** Not reached in its own voice (mlb.com refused the fetch).
  The pages that state the standard count all count the franchise through
  Montreal: the Hall of Fame, "the Expos/Nationals franchise", with Walter
  Johnson on the Nationals' page as "star of the old Washington Senators
  (now the Minnesota Twins)"; the statistical reference, "Team Names:
  Washington Nationals, Montreal Expos", 1969 on, with the original club's
  seasons on the Twins' page; the AP in 2019, "the first title in franchise
  history".
- **Club.** At its founding its president, Tony Tavares, as quoted in
  2020: "a page of records from the old Senators, a page of records from
  the Expos" "and a blank page writing our own records in Washington",
  and "This is a new team." Its Ring of Honor (2010) honours Hall of Famers
  of the Nationals, both Senators clubs, the Homestead Grays and the Expos,
  and since 2016 anyone who contributed "to the game of baseball in
  Washington, D.C."
- **Supporters.** On a fan board at the move (2004): "Original Senators
  records are part of Twins history, and 60's/70's Senators records are
  now part of the Rangers"; the posters divided over the Expos' records. A
  supporter-writer (2023): "The legacy of the Washington Nationals goes
  back not to Canada, but to the rich baseball history" of Washington, its
  "tradition that has existed in Washington since 1901", with no claim to
  the Senators' records.

So no party read counts the Nationals the Senators continued, although
the club took the original club's official name of 1905 to 1956. What the
club and supporters claim is the city's baseball, a unit in which the
Homestead Grays stand beside the Senators; the franchise they trace
through Montreal.

## Limits

The league's own voice was not reached. Two supporter pages show usage,
not how supporters divide. The club's own history pages were seen only
as search titles.
