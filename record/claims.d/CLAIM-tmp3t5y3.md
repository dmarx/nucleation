# CLAIM-tmp3t5y3: In a simulated federated learner with two of seven evaluators compromised, revisable safeguards relocated the trade-off between attack and dissent rather than moving it: they lowered the success of an attack that mimics dissent only by suspending most of the honest majority and a third of the dissenters, and only through a monitor that, compromised, suspended every dissenter and no attacker; counting honest members suspended for honest error as suppressed, no architecture of the study lowered attack success under compromised evaluators without suppressing honest members

## The claim

The numbers are CLAIM-tmpf0b31's; the reading differs in what counts as
suppressing honest participation. A safeguard that suspends 72% of the
honest majority and 36% of the dissenters at some point, on the word of
one monitor, has put the members' capacity to propose under the
monitor's judgement, and CLAIM-359's concern is exactly the capacity of
constituents to influence and revise. The arm stops the attack by
spending that capacity, and the spending is controlled by a process
whose compromise turns it against the dissenters (100% suspended) and
for the attackers (none). Under compromised evaluators the three arms
that lowered attack success did so by suppressing honest members
(validation-weighting, adaptive) or did not lower it (role-limited).

## What it does not say

- It does not say the adaptive arm does nothing. Its numbers are better
  than role-limited audit's on both scores while its monitor is honest.
- It does not deny that the cooperatives are wrong on the minority's
  tasks. It says the organization's warrant for treating them as wrong
  is one monitor's evidence, and that is the judgement in question.
