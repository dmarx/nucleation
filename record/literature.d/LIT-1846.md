# LIT-1846: Experimental reductions of delay discounting and impulsive choice: A systematic review and meta-analysis

Rung, J. M. & Madden, G. J. (2018). *Journal of Experimental Psychology:
General*, 147(9), 1349–1381. doi:10.1037/xge0000462. PMC6112163.

## Key takeaways

- Delay discounting can be reduced by many environmental manipulations,
  with effect sizes that differ widely by kind.
- Learning-based approaches gave the most reliable and largest
  reductions (B = 0.62, z = 7.43). Reward bundling, the subcategory the
  record's will-organization line cares about, reduced impulsive choice
  in humans and rats (z = 4.57).
- Clinical interventions gave small reductions (B = 0.23). Mindfulness
  and ACT were not significant overall (g = 0.32, z = 1.89, p = .059),
  from two studies.
- Mediation is almost never tested, so the processes by which any
  manipulation lowers discounting are open.

## Why this is here

[CLAIM-156](../claims.d/CLAIM-156.md) says mindfulness integrates competing interests without
needing to change the discount curves. Its defeat condition is that the
effect is fully mediated by measured discounting. This review gathers the
experimental literature on changing discounting, including the
mindfulness studies published before 2016.
