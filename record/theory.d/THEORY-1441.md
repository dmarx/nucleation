# THEORY-1441: Schneider, Möritz and Dietz show that, in isothermal folding at 50 °C of one multilayer DNA origami brick, staple domains bind in a defined order set more by the scaffold's topology of connectivity than by their binding strength, the two termini of a staple bind independently, and a set of early-binding nucleating domains forms a compacted intermediate without which the object still folds but hours later by a slower pathway

## Source

Schneider, Möritz and Dietz (2019), [LIT-2122](../literature.d/LIT-2122.md); read in [NOTE-1641](../notes.d/NOTE-1641.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Order** (Figs 2-3): per-staple completion from about 20 to 200 min,
  ranked by mean folding time across 140 beacon and 12,688 FRET reactions.
- **Termini** (Fig. 3B-D): a clear early-to-late gradient for 5′ termini,
  none for 3′; the authors conclude the termini bind independently.
- **Strength against connectivity** (Fig. 4): little correlation of
  hybridization free energy with folding time; strongest and weakest
  segment truncations act alike; a circular permutation with new sequences
  and the same connectivity gives a correlated order and the same knockout
  effect.
- **Nucleus** (Fig. 3F): without the 20 fastest 5′ segments no compacted
  intermediate forms and the folded state appears with an hour-long delay;
  without the 20 slowest the intermediate persists.
- **Could have come out otherwise**: the permutation could have scrambled
  the order, and strength could have predicted it; neither happened.

## What this does not say

- That topology alone sets the order: local sequence is also named a
  factor, and correlations are described, not given here.
- That the slower pathway reaches the same yield: progress is normalized,
  and no yields are reported.
- Anything about other designs, temperatures or annealing ramps.
- That the compacted intermediate is locally ordered: gels show mobility,
  and the compaction curve is computed from the mean times.
