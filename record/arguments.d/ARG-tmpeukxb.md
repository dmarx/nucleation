# ARG-tmpeukxb: The partial order of organizational achievements, by analogy, would show that the dimensions of memetic success form no hierarchy and so support the memetic nucleation hypothesis

## The inference

CLAIM-474: "From interconnection to individuation, the relationships are
distinct achievements that form a partial order, not a nested sequence:
none implies the next". A4 §6 lists nine properties of memetic success
(fidelity, fecundity, longevity, nucleation capacity, generativity,
structural reach, entrenchment, organizational dispensability,
corrigibility), says "These properties should not be treated as a
hierarchy", and cites CLAIM-474 for it. A4 §10 then lists CLAIM-474 among
what its hypothesis rests on.

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are they alike, and is that the respect the
  conclusion needs?** CLAIM-474's achievements are stages an organization
  may reach (compatibility, dynamics, individuation, autonomy, inquiry),
  ordered by what each requires of the others. A4's properties are
  measures of a transmitted item's effects. That one set is partially
  ordered says nothing about whether the other is: independence of
  properties is shown by cases that separate them, not by analogy to
  another set's order.
- **What relevant difference is there?** CLAIM-474's conclusion is that
  "none implies the next". A4's examples do that work directly ("A highly
  transmissible meme may organize very little. A rarely transmitted meme
  may induce profound organization."), which is CLAIM-tmp4uj40's claim and
  needs data, not CLAIM-474.
- **What transfers, and what does not?** Nothing the hypothesis uses.
  CLAIM-tmpgvsgg's content, that a transient structure can start an
  organization and that three persistences differ, rests on CLAIM-463,
  CLAIM-280, CLAIM-329 and CLAIM-420. Its being true would not make
  CLAIM-474 more or less credible, nor the reverse.

## Verdict

Rejected. CLAIM-474 is cited accurately for what it says, and does not
bear on either conclusion. It is left out of CLAIM-tmpgvsgg's `rests_on`.
