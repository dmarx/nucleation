# LIT-202: Consent and the Formation of Preferences

Richard Pettigrew (2026), *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research, 113(2), 251–261* — DOI-10.1111/phpr.70131

## Key takeaways

- Whether consent is morally effective depends on the mechanism by which the underlying preference was formed — unjustly imposed sanctions or manipulated evidence defeat it, while preferences formed through (even coerced) attention do not — which leaves us pervasively uncertain whether the consent we receive changes the moral facts.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Under ideal conditions, consent turns something impermissible into something permissible, but coerced consent does not. Pettigrew argues that consent also fails when the preferences behind it were formed in certain ways under pressure from unjust social forces. He catalogues mechanisms of preference formation, sorts them into those that can ground effective consent and those that cannot, and explains the division. He ends with a puzzle: the relevant mechanisms are usually opaque to the person consenting and to the person receiving consent.
