# NOTE-tmp11fci: Syropoulos, Law, Coleman and Young — A Future Beyond Ourselves

## Contribution

A joint study of self- and other-oriented prospection, proposing that
future self-continuity may lead to concern for future people through an
"expansive sense of identity that transcends the self and includes future
others in one's self-concept" (Potential Mechanisms).

## Key results

1. **Internal meta-analysis (Table 5).** CFC–RFG r = .54; FSC–RFG r = .21;
   FSC–identification .42; identification–RFG .54; efficacy–RFG .58.
2. **Study 1 (pre-registered).** CFC and FSC each correlate with RFG
   (r = .49 and .30) and are unique predictors.
3. **Study 2 (cross-sectional mediation).** "FSC had a non-significant
   indirect effect via efficacy ... but a significant indirect effect via
   identification"; the authors add that "no arguments for a causal
   mechanism can be drawn".
4. **Study 3a (pre-registered experiment).** The FSC writing task raised FSC
   and RFG (d = 0.24) but not identification with future generations
   (p = .133, d = 0.09).
5. **Study 3b (direct replication).** FSC rose again, RFG did not (p = .288,
   d = 0.06); an identification manipulation moved nothing.
6. **The authors' verdict (3b Discussion; Limitations).** "we cannot
   confidently claim that experimentally manipulating any of the target
   predictors will result in increases in responsibility for protecting
   future people"; the reverse direction "is equally plausible".
7. **Measures (Table 2; Study 2 Materials).** Identification with future
   generations: "To what extent do you feel connected to future generations
   of people?", "It is easy for me to put myself in the shoes of future
   generations of people.", and an overlapping-circles item; Table 2
   glosses it as whether a person feels "empathy towards future generations
   of people". RFG: four items from Syropoulos et al. 2020, e.g. "People
   living today have an obligation to protect future generations, even if
   it means tightening our belts now", plus an item valuing far-future lives
   "equally with the lives of people today".

## Assumptions

- Construal level theory: temporal and social distance share one
  architecture, so self-oriented prospection can transfer to others.
- Self-report scales capture the constructs, and a writing task can induce
  them.
- Identification with future generations is self-extension.

## Concepts

Self- versus other-oriented prospection; future self-continuity;
consideration of future consequences; responsibility for future
generations; intergenerational identification; intergenerational
efficacy; construal level theory; present bias.

## Bearing on the record

- **On the LIT's characterization.** Accurate to the abstract, but it
  overstates by omission: the "consistent connection" is correlational,
  and the two pre-registered experiments give a small effect and then a
  null. Identification with future generations is **not** separated from
  caring: its items include perspective-taking and its stated focus is
  empathy. The authors are Syropoulos and Law as joint first authors,
  Syropoulos first, then Coleman and Young. The version read is the
  2024-05-21 manuscript. Sample totals disagree in the text (11,261 in the
  abstract and Table 1; 9570 in the limitations).
- **LEAD-062 / CASE-tmp413kq**: not the test the lead asks for; supplies an
  off-the-shelf circles item for future generations, and two manipulations
  that failed as identification inductions.
- **CLAIM-492**: an instance of the problem it names.
- **QUESTION-301**, **QUESTION-299**, **QUESTION-300**: dated notes on each.
- **LIT-1713 / NOTE-1293 / THEORY-1579**: the same group's RFG work; the RFG
  scale here is Syropoulos et al. 2020 (the gratitude and stewardship
  paper), which the record does not hold; it is not LIT-1741.
- **LIT-1702 / NOTE-1259** (Hershfield): the source of the FSC construct.
- **NOTE-1274**: its doubt about what the overlapping-circles measure
  measures bears on this paper's identification item.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- All but two studies are cross-sectional; the mediation is
  cross-sectional, and the authors disclaim causal inference.
- The identification circles item copies the FSC item's format, which may
  inflate their correlation.
- Identification is measured partly by empathy and perspective-taking.
- Weak manipulations (d about .1 to .4), and 3a's effect did not replicate.
- US online samples; attitudes only, no behaviour.
- The manuscript cites some studies by stale numbers and gives two sample
  totals; study numbers are less stable than section headings.
- The step from future self-continuity to an identity that "transcends the
  self" is proposed, not shown: the FSC manipulations did not move
  identification.
