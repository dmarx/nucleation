# CLAIM-1132: Possibility is shaped by affordable legibility

## The claim

A56's last line: "An organization's possibility space is shaped not only by
what its members know how to do, but by what they can afford to make
intelligible to those empowered to let them do it."

Its four steps are the claims it rests on: authorization is a costly
second-order query with its own deadline ([CLAIM-996](CLAIM-996.md)); who pays to make a
proposal legible steers what is pursued ([CLAIM-1117](CLAIM-1117.md)); evidence and
permission can depend on each other in a circle ([CLAIM-1140](CLAIM-1140.md)); and
alternative perspectives are alternative regimes in which a question can be
made intelligible more cheaply ([CLAIM-1119](CLAIM-1119.md)). What it adds is the design
conclusion: pluralism of perspectives is not enough without "maintained
pathways through which initially local forms of intelligibility can acquire
evidence, resources, and consequential authority."

## What it does not say

That authorization should be lax, or that every local perspective's
conclusions should be authorized. The pathways are for evidence to be
produced and reach evaluation.
