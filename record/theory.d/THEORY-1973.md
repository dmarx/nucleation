# THEORY-1973: Strawson holds that holding people morally responsible consists in the reactive attitudes, which rest on a demand for goodwill or regard; that pleas of excuse leave the agent a full participant while pleas of exemption suspend the attitudes toward the agent; and that a general thesis of determinism neither does nor could rationally undermine the framework of these attitudes

Strawson (1962), [LIT-2679](../literature.d/LIT-2679.md).

## The account

The personal reactive attitudes "rest on, and reflect, an expectation of,
and demand for, the manifestation of a certain degree of goodwill or
regard" (p. 200). Pleas such as "He was pushed" "do not invite us to see
the agent as other than a fully responsible agent"; pleas such as "His mind
has been systematically perverted" "invite us to suspend our ordinary
reactive attitudes towards the agent" (p. 193). Neither kind is produced by
a general thesis of determinism, and the framework "neither calls for, nor
permits, an external 'rational' justification" (p. 208).

## What it does not show

- **A criterion for exemption.** The examples are a schema; where
  conditioning becomes "systematically perverted" is not fixed.
- **Anything about the wrong of the one who pushed.** The lecture concerns
  the agent's standing and the attitudes toward the agent.
- **That the attitudes are universal.** Strawson allows they may reflect
  "local and temporary features of our own culture".
