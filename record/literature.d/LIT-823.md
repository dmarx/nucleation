# LIT-823: Semantic Communication: A Survey of Its Theoretical Development

Gangtao Xin, Pingyi Fan and Khaled B. Letaief (2024), *Entropy* 26(2):102 —
DOI-10.3390/e26020102

## Key takeaways

- **The frame** (Section 1). Weaver's three levels: technical (are the
  symbols received?), semantic (is the intended meaning conveyed?),
  effectiveness (does it change conduct as desired?). Semantic
  communication is the second; Shannon's theory serves the first and
  ignores truth, task relevance and timeliness.
- **Semantic entropy has no agreed definition** (Section 3). Carnap and
  Bar-Hillel's H_s(e) = −log m(e), with m(e) a logical probability, gives
  a contradiction infinite information; Floridi and D'Alfonso measure
  distance from truth instead; Bao et al. weight models of a sentence by
  their statistical probability. Later variants are task-oriented (the
  least expected number of queries needed to solve a task),
  knowledge-based (entropy relative to a knowledge base) or context-based
  (outcomes weighted by their utility for a goal).
- **Semantic rate–distortion is the most developed part** (Section 4).
  In Liu, Zhang and Poor's framework the source is a pair (S, X): S the
  unobservable intrinsic state (the "semantic" aspect), X the observation;
  the encoder sees only X, and the rate is traded against a semantic
  distortion on S and an appearance distortion on X. Extensions add side
  information at the decoder (Guo et al.) and Hamming distortion (Stavrou
  and Kountouris).
- **Semantic capacity can exceed channel capacity, by definition**
  (Section 5). Bao et al.'s semantic channel coding theorem adds a term for
  the receiver's inferential power and subtracts one for encoding
  ambiguity; Ma et al. divide Shannon capacity by the fraction of messages
  that carry distinct meanings. Either way, a system may tolerate bit
  errors without semantic error. The authors' own view is that a general
  semantic capacity must be conditioned on task, shared and private
  knowledge, goals and time.
- **Tools, not theory** (Section 6): age of information and its
  semantics-aware variants, the information bottleneck, deep joint
  source–channel coding (DeepSC and its successors), and LLMs as knowledge
  bases.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-09 from the manuscript bibliography of 2026-10-09 (work
`what-survives-translation`) at the owner's request. It is the survey the
exchange named when it conceded that task-sensitive information
preservation is established in semantic and goal-oriented communication
([CLAIM-088](../claims.d/CLAIM-088.md)); the manuscript did not keep it. It is read here on its
own merits. See the curation entry of that day.

The reading finds that the survey supports the concession in general
terms (task-relative and goal-oriented formulations are a recognised line
of work) but does not cover the rate–distortion–perception results the
exchange named alongside it, Chai et al. ([LIT-812](LIT-812.md)) and Zhao et al.
([LIT-793](LIT-793.md)). Its section on the information bottleneck cites
Tishby, Pereira and Bialek's bottleneck, [LIT-338](LIT-338.md), and Zaslavsky, Kemp,
Regier and Tishby's short paper on human-like semantic representations
(arXiv 1808.03353), not the colour-naming paper held here as
[LIT-810](LIT-810.md), whose finding it reports. The record's account of what the semantic
rate–distortion frameworks formalise is [THEORY-161](../theory.d/THEORY-161.md).
