# LIT-tmpy1y1q: Debunking Corporate Moral Responsibility

Manuel Velasquez (2003), *Business Ethics Quarterly* 13(4):531–562 —
DOI-10.5840/beq200313436

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read beyond the publisher's abstract.* By the abstract:

- A "core argument" that the corporate organization is a separate moral
  agent "is based on an unnoticed but elementary mistake deriving from the
  fallacy of division".
- Collectivists misunderstand "the nature of intentional causality and of
  “as-if” intentionality".
- Velasquez's two theses: "individual members of an organization are always
  causally responsible for any corporate act", and "attributions of
  intentions to corporations are always either descriptive or prescriptive
  attributions of “as if” intentionality".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 at the owner's request, as the developed form of the
1983 objection (LIT-tmp7dpgf). Its second thesis bears directly on the
corporate-psychopathy thesis: if a corporation's intentions are only "as
if", so would be any callousness or lack of remorse ascribed to it. The
accounts it opposes are THEORY-131 (French) and THEORY-139 (Pettit).
