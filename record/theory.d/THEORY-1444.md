# THEORY-1444: Douglas and colleagues report that custom multilayer DNA origami on a honeycomb lattice folds in one pot at 7 to 44 % of scaffold in monomer, many of the designs only with week-long thermal ramps at about 16 mM MgCl2 and low NaCl, short ramps, low Mg2+ or high Na+ giving slow-migrating, grossly misshapen products, whereas flat origami and simpler nanotube structures fold in 72-minute ramps

## Source

Douglas, Dietz, Liedl, Högberg, Graf and Shih (2009), [LIT-2130](../literature.d/LIT-2130.md); read
in [NOTE-1629](../notes.d/NOTE-1629.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Yield**: "varied from 7% to 44% for these targets as estimated by
  ethidium-bromide fluorescence intensity".
- **Ramp length** (Fig. 3b): ramps from 1.2 h to 173 h over the same
  temperatures; short ramps "yielded a slowly migrating species" and
  "grossly misshapen objects", week-long ramps a fast-migrating,
  well-folded species. A short ramp could have sufficed; it did not.
- **Cations** (Fig. 3c–f): Mg2+ varied "from 0 to 30 mM", low Mg2+ giving
  gross defects; "Excess NaCl inhibits proper folding".
- **Simpler designs**: "the Rothemund flat origami and these simpler
  nanotube structures could be folded with 72 minute ramps".

## What this does not say

- Which designs needed the week-long ramp, or the six-helix-bundle data:
  these are in the unread Supplementary Information.
- Why multilayer folding is slow: the kinetic traps are "Presumably", with
  no trap identified.
- How yield scales with complexity: the comparison is categorical.
- What the cation effects are mechanically: offered as "may" and "might".
- Anything about folding temperatures, hysteresis or isothermal folding,
  none of which is measured; Sobczak's later work ([THEORY-1440](THEORY-1440.md)) and the
  held simulation ([THEORY-1398](THEORY-1398.md)) take these up.
