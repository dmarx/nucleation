# THEORY-tmpv5g3z: Given that every change in a phenomenon is simultaneously realized by some constituent on every lower level, that simultaneous changes are not causally related, that co-occurrence is transitive, and that the parts and their levels are already known, a single intervention that changes a part and the phenomenon simultaneously while directly changing at most one behaviour on each lower level shows that the part is a constituent; the condition presupposes the boundary and the level hierarchy it is applied to and cannot supply them

## Source

Baumgartner, Casini and Krickel (2020), LIT-tmp130h8, §2 (pp. 3–6), §3
(pp. 6–9) and §4 (pp. 9–12) of the penultimate draft; read in
NOTE-tmpz7pqv.

## What was actually shown

**Principles.** (UC): every change in a non-fundamental phenomenon Ψ is
"necessarily and simultaneously realized" by a change in some constituent
on every lower level. (SIM): "Simultaneous changes cannot be causally
related." (TR): co-occurrence is transitive. Parthood relations and the
local level hierarchy (direct proper parthood relative to Ψ) are assumed
known (p. 5).

**The restricted intervention (H, p. 9).** I is horizontally surgical on
a part Φᵏᵢ w.r.t. Ψ iff it causes Φᵏᵢ, any changes it makes to both Φᵏᵢ and
Ψ are simultaneous, and it directly causes at most one behaviour on every
level below Ψ.

**Sufficiency (SUF, pp. 9–11).** If a horizontally surgical intervention
changes both Φᵏᵢ and Ψ, Φᵏᵢ is a constituent. Suppose not. By UC, some
constituent Φʰⱼ on Φᵏᵢ's level realizes Ψ's change at the same time. By TR,
Φᵏᵢ and Φʰⱼ change together; by SIM, neither causes the other; so both are
direct effects of I, two on one level, against H(iii). Checked: valid.

**Why weaker notions fail (pp. 7–8).** Admitting common causes whose
effects stand in supervenience presupposes the supervenience base, that
is, the constituents. Admitting all common causes lets in sterile effects
and detached parts.

**What could have failed.** The proof could have needed a premise about
the particular mechanism. It needs only the three principles and settled
parthood.

## What this does not say

- **That horizontally surgical interventions exist** anywhere. The paper
  notes they need ideal interventions on a constituent with respect to
  the non-overlapping parts of the phenomenon, whose existence is as
  doubtful as in causal contexts (p. 12).
- **That the condition is necessary.** hFAT, which would make it so, is
  "tempting" and "beyond the scope" (pp. 3, 11).
- **Where the boundary is, or what the levels are.** These are inputs.
  The condition sorts parts already known to be parts. Leuridan's
  endosymbiosis case (LIT-tmpgs9lv, unread) is a reason to doubt that
  spatiotemporal inclusion settles parthood.
- **That simultaneity is sharp.** H(ii) and SIM draw the line at zero
  delay. Where a part's response takes time, as for an interior that
  relaxes at a finite rate, whether it counts as simultaneous depends on
  the time resolution of the description (CLAIM-480; CLAIM-tmp1qybv part
  3). Krickel's temporal-phase reading of mutual manipulability
  (LIT-tmp5bf7n, footnote 3, unread) points the same way.
