# ARG-203: Courier constructions → reasonable rejection

## The inference

The test, as [THEORY-1971](../theory.d/THEORY-1971.md) reports it: an act or arrangement is wrong when
every set of principles no one could reasonably reject disallows it,
judged by individuals' reasons for objecting to a principle and its
alternatives, and only by reasons about what happens to individuals. The
constructions are C1 to C4 of [CASE-336](../cases.d/CASE-336.md). For each pair, the inference
names the principle permitting the worse member, the objector's reason,
the other side's reason, and whether a principle permitting the better
member survives; [CLAIM-908](../claims.d/CLAIM-908.md) sets out each verdict.

| Pair | Principle rejected | Objector's reason | Other side's reason | Allocation presupposed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | deciding on grounds withheld from the person decided against | cannot detect or contest error | slight cost of disclosing logs held anyway | none in question |
| C2 | the decider controls its own review | review may not reverse wrong decisions | slight cost of an independent panel | no: two allocations ranked |
| C3 | narrowing others' options, then citing their narrowness | loss of control over one's working life; vulnerability | competitive advantage | no: the sphere is itself fixed by the test |
| C4 | one party amends at will rules others plan around | assurance against adverse or sudden change | adaptability, met by consent requirement | no: two allocations ranked |

## Critical questions (formal application)

- **Does the mapping premise hold?** The test applies to principles, and
  each pair is mapped to a pair of principles for the platform that
  differ only in the feature the pair varies. This holds because each
  construction fixes the rules, decisions and recorded history, so the
  only difference between the two principles is the feature. One risk is
  that the recorded history, on which both worlds come out the same,
  might be taken as the input; it is not, since principles are judged
  ex ante under the uncertainty they govern, and the protective reasons
  are reasons under that uncertainty.
- **Are the test's assumptions met?** The test admits only reasons about
  what happens to individuals. Each objector's reason here is: the risk
  of an undetected or unreversed error, of a narrowed working life, of an
  adverse rule change, are each things that happen to a courier. No
  reason depends on the objector's already being owed a share of a
  power. The assumption that the class of those to whom justification is
  owed is given (moral standing) is made, as it is by any theory, and
  [CLAIM-908](../claims.d/CLAIM-908.md) says why it is not counted as an import.

## Scope

The inference stops where its second answer stops. In C2* and C4* the
protective reasons are stipulated away, and whether what remains is an
admissible reason is the question [CLAIM-909](../claims.d/CLAIM-909.md) sets and three
alternatives answer. This argument supports nothing about them.
