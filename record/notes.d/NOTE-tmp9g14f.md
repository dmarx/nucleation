# NOTE-tmp9g14f: Caliari and Petri — Irrational Random Utility Models

## Contribution

The random utility model (RUM) treats aggregate stochastic choice as generated by a population of preference maximizers. The authors show that RUM data can equally be generated by a population of decision-makers none of whom maximizes a preference, if and only if the rational population's preferences are "sufficiently uncorrelated" (their correlation bounds, a strengthening of lower Fréchet bounds). Consequences: the rational interpretation of RUMs is hard to falsify; individual rationality cannot be inferred from aggregate data without prior knowledge; welfare conclusions depend on which representation the analyst adopts.

## Key results

1. Abstract: "if (and only if) preferences are sufficiently uncorrelated, RUM choices can also be generated by a population of decision makers who do not maximize any preference."
2. Introduction: "If the irrationalities of decisionmakers are sufficiently uncorrelated, they will cancel out, and aggregate data will appear as if stochastically rational." (pdftotext joined "decision-makers" across a line break; the PDF likely reads "decision-makers".)
3. Introduction: "considering only rational representations of RUMs may lead to welfare misjudgments."
4. §7: "normative conclusions, e.g. welfare assessments, are never only data-driven (or theory-free) but always rely on the analyst interpretation".
5. §9: "individuals with correlated mistakes can never be translated into rational aggregate behavior."

## Assumptions

Rationality = maximizing a strict linear order (WARP / Sen's α); finite alternatives; full knowledge of aggregate choice probabilities on all menus.

## Concepts

random utility model (RUM); random non-utility model (RNUM); irrational RUM (I-RUM); correlation bounds; stochastic vs individual rationality; non-falsifiability; Block–Marschak polynomials.
