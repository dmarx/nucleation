# QUESTION-261: When most members of a group privately reject a norm that a vocal minority and widespread silence make look overwhelmingly supported, does correcting information about the actual distribution of opinion dissolve the apparent consensus, or has the public norm by then acquired an institutional force that no longer depends on anyone believing it is supported?

## Why it is a question

A39: "Most members privately oppose a norm, but a vocal minority and
widespread silence make the norm seem overwhelmingly supported. The
collective maintains the practice despite privately held opposition. Does
changing information about the actual distribution of opinion dissolve
the apparent consensus, or has the public norm already acquired
independent institutional force?"

The record holds the mechanisms that make the apparent consensus, and not
what happens when it is exposed.

- **How it forms.** A vocal minority can silence a majority with private
  beliefs held fixed ([THEORY-242](../theory.d/THEORY-242.md)). Network position can make a rare
  attribute look like a local majority to most members ([LIT-1390](../literature.d/LIT-1390.md)). Members
  can misjudge what the others privately hold (pluralistic ignorance,
  [LIT-1370](../literature.d/LIT-1370.md), not reached).
- **What it does to a person.** [CLAIM-415](../claims.d/CLAIM-415.md): a person can be organized by a
  judgement attributed to a collective that does not hold it, and
  [CASE-140](../cases.d/CASE-140.md) would test whether the attributed breadth does that work.
- **What is missing.** Both answers are possible. If the norm is held up
  only by each member's belief about the others, correcting the belief
  should dissolve it. If the norm has been written into rules, roles,
  sanctions or other people's expectations of enforcement, it may persist
  as an institution after no one believes it is popular. Then it is
  [QUESTION-119](QUESTION-119.md)'s case of a stance credited to a population whose effect is
  due to something other than the population as organized.

## What would count as an answer

- An intervention that corrects members' beliefs about the distribution of
  private opinion and measures both the beliefs and the practice after.
  Prentice and Miller's campus studies are the obvious place to start, and
  were not reached ([LIT-1370](../literature.d/LIT-1370.md)).
- A distinction, in cases, between a norm sustained by mutual
  misperception and one sustained by enforcement roles, with the first
  dissolving on correction and the second not. That would answer the
  question by dividing it.
- It would be dissolved, not answered, if correction never changes
  practice at all, so that the apparent consensus was never doing the
  work.
