# ARG-044: The MSF meeting reanalysed by Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy shows that an interaction can make available a way of relating its participants did not hold, and names the condition under which it lasts; it does not show that taking part comes before the reinterpretation

## The inference

In the meeting, two health officials ask MSF to train their physicians ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), pp. 1359–1363).
- **Part 1.** The request offers a role that MSF's operative perspective refuses: "MSF ... not a university" (p. 1362). Robert reaches agreement on an "ad hoc training program", so the interaction made available a way of relating that was not held before.
- **Part 3.** Whether this lasts beyond the encounter is [CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md)'s third part. Tsoukas and Chia name the condition: institutionalization, which may fail ([THEORY-1284](../theory.d/THEORY-1284.md)).
- **Part 2.** Here the reinterpretation is made in talk *so that* taking part becomes possible. That is the reverse of part 2, where taking part comes first.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the conclusion were false?** For part 1, less so. Without the claim's mechanism a refusal was the expected outcome, and the transcript shows the representative beginning one (lines 43–46).
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its rivals?** For part 1, partly. A rival on which Robert simply complied with a request he already accepted is weakened by the marks of resistance. Nothing separates part 2 from its reverse except the order observed, and that order goes against part 2.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** Only in part. The episode is a published excerpt, reread by authors who interpret its pauses by supposition ([NOTE-1484](../notes.d/NOTE-1484.md), Limitations), and the source chapter ([LIT-1914](../literature.d/LIT-1914.md)) is unread. So this is Proposed, and holds for parts 1 and 3 only.
