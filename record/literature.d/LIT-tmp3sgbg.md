# LIT-tmp3sgbg: Neurophenomenal structuralism. A philosophical agenda for a structuralist neuroscience of consciousness

Lyre, H. (2022). Neurophenomenal structuralism. A philosophical agenda for a structuralist neuroscience of consciousness. *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2022(1): niac012. doi:10.1093/nc/niac012.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpg26n5.*

- **The two assumptions.** "Any phenomenal experience is fully
  individuated by its place in a Q-structure. Phenomenal content and
  character are relational, not intrinsic properties of sentient
  subjects." And "Q-structure is mirrored in N-structure", by "a
  surjective homomorphism from N to Q" ("Structure, homomorphisms, and
  structural individuation").
- **Specific, not generic.** Structure fixes which experience a conscious
  state is. Whether it is conscious is left to a general mechanism; the
  view "does not tell us when a mental state is conscious in general"
  (Introduction).
- **Structural individuation.** Relata are "determined up to isomorphy
  only", and even a structure with no symmetries gives them no intrinsic
  properties.
- **Newman.** To avoid the Newman problem "we must specify the nature of
  the relations". Spatiotemporal relations are carried across the
  boundary into the brain, after Russell's reply to Newman.
- **Not structural realism.** "neurophenomenal structuralism is no realism
  about the phenomenal" ("Structural realism"). The Q-structure is reduced
  to the neural structure and has no causal role of its own.
- **Holism and inversion.** Any change in the space can change every
  experience in it. Spectrum inversion is excluded actually by the
  asymmetry of colour space and nomologically by colour's ties to other
  senses. Its metaphysical possibility "cannot be ruled out".

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange cited this paper for individuating phenomenal qualities by their position in quality spaces, and noted that Lyre separates this from structural realism in the philosophy of science. It addresses the character of experiences, not why any process is conscious at all. Bears on CLAIM-tmpdga80 and, by analogy, CLAIM-136.

The reading bears this description out. Lyre states the separation from structural realism himself, and limits the view to the specific character of experiences. Against CLAIM-tmpdga80, Lyre holds the character half of the claim, organization fixing which experience it is, and not its occurrence half. For CLAIM-136, his structural individuation is the claim's strict version in another domain, and his treatment of inversion shows its cost: structurally indiscernible experiences would be one experience.
