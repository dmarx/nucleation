# CLAIM-tmp5lpdu: The potential account of valence is an account of goal-conduciveness valence, not of intrinsic pleasantness: on the component process model's separation of levels of valence, the ordering that prevails ranks states by need satisfaction and goal attainment, the potential's strength tracks 'wanting' and its sign tracks goal conduciveness, while 'liking' is a separate appraisal with its own ordering; so the wanting–liking dissociation is two valence systems coming apart, as that separation predicts, and the objection that the account is not about pleasure is conceded rather than answered

## The claim

[CLAIM-tmpe0iew](CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md) showed that the potential [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) defines tracks
'wanting', not 'liking' ([THEORY-tmp0bbxk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp0bbxk.md)). The component process model
already separates the two, and the separation places the record's account
on one side of it.

**Levels of valence.** Shuman, Sander and Scherer ([LIT-tmpkywoj](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkywoj.md),
[NOTE-tmpogww2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpogww2.md)): "Although intrinsic pleasantness may be rooted in
ultimate evolutionary benefits, it can be differentiated from proximal
goal conduciveness" (p. 2). "The degree of goal conduciveness is directly
determined by the degree of need satisfaction or goal attainment. In
contrast, needs may set thresholds for pleasantness, but cannot predict
pleasantness beyond those thresholds" (p. 2). They rest the separation
on Berridge: "pleasure is independent of goal conduciveness. The sensual
enjoyment of an object and the goal striving toward an object rely on
different brain mechanisms" (p. 2). Scherer and Moors ([LIT-tmpjaacn](../literature.d/LIT-tmpjaacn.md),
[NOTE-tmpmobow](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpmobow.md)) report the dissociation in the body: "Intrinsic valence
had more robust effects on facial EMG ..., whereas only goal congruence
significantly influenced mean skin conductance, forehead temperature, and
finger temperature" (p. 730).

**Where the account falls.** [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md)'s ordering ranks the system's
own states by their bearing on its maintenance, and its sign is progress
against a reference course. That is goal conduciveness, need satisfaction
and goal attainment, not intrinsic pleasantness. So:
- **sign** is goal conduciveness: ahead of course, conducive; behind,
  obstructive;
- **strength of potential** is how much the goal-conduciveness ordering
  overrides the others, and in the dissociation cases it tracks
  'wanting';
- **'liking'** is intrinsic pleasantness, a separate appraisal with its
  own ordering, over stimuli rather than over the system's own states.

The addict who gets the drug and enjoys it less is then consistent: goal
conduciveness positive (the goal is attained), potential strong
(sensitized wanting), pleasantness low. The case that broke the sign
clause, read as hedonic, does not break it read as goal conduciveness.

**What is conceded.** The account is not an account of pleasure.
"Hedonic" in [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) and [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md) now reads
"goal-conduciveness": the owner made the revision on 2026-10-23, and
[CLAIM-tmpe0iew](CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md) is Active as a conceded objection.

## What it does not say

- **That pleasantness has no place in the account.** It has an ordering
  in the CPM, but nothing in the readings has it prevail over others;
  where Shuman et al. name a valence that may override the rest, it is
  moral goodness ("moral goodness may generally dominate other
  micro-valences", p. 9). And as an ordering over stimuli it fails the
  account's kind clause. Whether 'liking' can be potential of some
  ordering is open.
- **That goal conduciveness is incentive salience.** Incentive salience
  is cue-triggered pull; goal conduciveness is an assessment of progress.
  The claim pairs them, strength with one and sign with the other, and
  the third defeater is where that pairing could fail.
- **That the integration of the levels is settled.** Shuman et al. give
  both weighted sums and rules with "no trade-offs between
  micro-valences" (p. 8), which are the compensatory and lexicographic
  regimes of [CLAIM-tmp85p0g](CLAIM-tmp85p0g.md), reached independently.

## Note of 2026-10-23: what incentive sensitization does to this reply

Berridge and Robinson ([LIT-tmpotzm0](../literature.d/LIT-tmpotzm0.md), [NOTE-tmprj162](../notes.d/NOTE-tmprj162.md); [THEORY-tmp7r9j4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7r9j4.md)) put
two pressures on it.
- **The kind-clause asymmetry does not survive.** This reply set 'liking'
  aside as "an ordering over stimuli". But incentive salience "is less
  connected to cognitive goals and more tightly linked to reward cues":
  'wanting' is cue-bound too. Either the kind clause excludes sensitized
  wanting as well, and addiction is then not valence at all, or it is
  loosened, and 'liking' comes back in as a candidate. Which way is
  [QUESTION-tmprsrxs](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmprsrxs.md)'s question.
- **The relapsing addict.** The recovering addict "has a genuine cognitive
  desire to abstain from taking drugs, but still 'wants' drugs, so
  relapses anyway when exposed to drug cues". Read along the person's
  goal of abstaining, relapse is goal-obstructive; read along the
  ordering that won, it is conducive. The reply's reading of the addict
  case above, "goal conduciveness positive (the goal is attained)", holds
  only on the second, which assumes that potential of a proxy is valence
  signed on the proxy. That is the third defeater in plain form, and it is
  open.

Two points support the reply. The same mesolimbic circuitry yields dread
("fearful salience is generated by the same mesolimbic circuitry as
incentive salience"), which fits keeping strength and sign apart. And the
reported case of motivation rising while subjects are "unable to report
any change in subjective feelings" is a candidate, unread first-hand, for
unfelt potential ([CLAIM-tmptd5us](CLAIM-tmptd5us.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-23: the third defeater answered by relativizing sign

The owner's reading ([CLAIM-tmp8ci5a](CLAIM-tmp8ci5a.md)) takes 'wanting' and 'liking' as
saliences relative to different, overlapping processes and makes valence
relative to a perspective. The relapsing addict is then not a choice
between two signs: the prevailing pursuit perspective's valence is
positive, the abstaining perspective's negative, and the system's is the
prevailing one's. The asymmetry of the kind clause goes the same way:
both saliences rank states by their bearing on their own process's
concerns.
