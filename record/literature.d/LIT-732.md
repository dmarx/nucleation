# LIT-732: The Fate of Knowledge

Helen E. Longino (2002), *The Fate of Knowledge*, Princeton: Princeton
University Press — DOI-10.1515/9780691187013

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* Longino's SEP entry ([LIT-725](LIT-725.md)), §5, says that
"in her 2002, Longino argues that the cognitive processes of science, such
as observation and reasoning, are themselves social processes. Thus the
interactions subject to community norms extend not only to discussion of
assumptions in finished research, but to the constructive processes of
research as well." The entry calls the resulting position critical
contextual empiricism, citing this book and [LIT-717](LIT-717.md) together.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request. The claim the essay leans on,
that institutional conditions of criticism "can contribute to the epistemic
capacities of the collective", is closest to this book's thesis rather than
to the 1990 one. That is a reason to read this one first if a copy is
supplied. The account is [THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md).
