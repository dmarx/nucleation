# LIT-tmplzcgc: Translating as a purposeful activity: a prospective approach

Christiane Nord (2005), "Translating as a purposeful activity: a
prospective approach", *TradTerm* 11, 15–28.
DOI 10.11606/issn.2317-9511.tradterm.2005.49673.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpsq37b.*

- **Prospective, not retrospective.** The translator does not reproduce
  "the" source text, which is "as many texts as there are receivers of
  it". The translator adapts it to "a particular target audience from among
  various possible audiences" (pp. 16–17).
- **Four functions.** Phatic, referential, expressive and appellative,
  after Bühler with Jakobson's phatic function added. Most texts carry
  several, one usually dominant (pp. 19–24).
- **Two options when conditions differ.** For each function, either adapt
  the text so the function works for the target audience (option A,
  instrumental), or explain it in a meta-text so that it becomes
  meta-phatic, meta-referential and so on (option B, documentary) (pp.
  24–26).
- **The brief decides.** Nord, unlike Cicero, Schleiermacher, Nida or
  Venuti, does not hold either option better in general. The choice follows
  the translator's reading of the brief: who the audience is and what the
  translation is for (p. 27). Equivalence is often "not required or not
  even desired" (p. 26).
