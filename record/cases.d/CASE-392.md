# CASE-392: The 2005 Washington Nationals' lineage

## The study

[LEAD-099](../leads.d/LEAD-099.md) asked whether the league, the club or its supporters count the
2005 Washington Nationals as the original Senators' continuation, which is
the test [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s defeater names and [CASE-105](CASE-105.md) left open. The run read
public pages on 2026-11-23 and coded each statement by rules written
before reading (scratch/studies/lead-099/CODING.md):

- **Parties**: LEAGUE (MLB's own pages), CLUB (the Nationals organization
  and its officers), SUPPORTERS (fan sites, boards and supporter-writers),
  with the Hall of Fame (HOF) and the press (PRESS) coded apart.
- **Codes**: C-SEN, counts the Nationals the same club or franchise as a
  Senators club (records or franchise years combined, or "the same club
  returned"); H-SEN, claims or honours the Senators as Washington's
  baseball heritage without combining them; C-EX and D-EX, counts or
  disclaims the Expos as the same franchise; D-SEN, disclaims the
  Senators or assigns them to the Twins and Rangers.
- **Verdict per party**: continuation if any C-SEN stands unwithdrawn;
  heritage only if H-SEN and no C-SEN.

The fourteen statements, each with its address, quote and date read, are
scratch/studies/lead-099/statements.tsv; the pages refused, with each
obstacle, are obstacles.tsv beside it.

- **The club at its founding.** Its president in November 2004, as quoted
  in 2020: "a page of records from the old Senators, a page of records
  from the Expos" "and a blank page writing our own records in
  Washington"; "This is a new team." The Senators' records were to be
  displayed, not combined.
- **The club's honours.** The Ring of Honor (2010) took Hall of Famers
  from the Nationals, both Senators clubs, the Homestead Grays and the
  Expos; since 2016 anyone who "made a significant contribution to the game
  of baseball in Washington, D.C." The Homestead Grays, a Negro league
  club that was never in either Senators lineage or the Expos', are
  honoured on the same terms as the Senators, so what the honours track is
  baseball in a place, not a club's line.
- **The standard count.** The Hall of Fame and the statistical reference
  put the Nationals' franchise through Montreal from 1969 and the original
  club's seasons, including the years it was officially the "Nationals",
  with the Twins. The AP counted the 2019 title the franchise's first and
  the city's first since 1924 in the same story.
- **Supporters.** A fan board at the move (2004) assigned the original
  Senators' records to the Twins and the expansion club's to the Rangers,
  and divided over whether the Expos' carried over. A supporter-writer
  (2023) claimed the Senators' legacy for Washington against the Expos'
  and against Minnesota and Texas, as the city's "tradition that has
  existed in Washington since 1901", without claiming their records for
  the club.

No statement read is coded C-SEN. The Nationals took the original club's
official name of 1905 to 1956, and still no party read counts them its
continuation.

## What it can show

That the parties distinguish two units and count them differently: the
franchise, which they trace through Montreal and never through the
Senators, and the city's baseball, which they trace to 1859 or 1901 and
into which the club, the press and supporters put the Senators, the
Homestead Grays and the Nationals alike. This is [CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md)'s row for
narrative and place ("the 2005 Nationals, by claim") with the claim's
content now read: the claim is to a place's history, not a club's.

## What it cannot show

- **The league's own voice.** MLB's pages were refused; the league's
  count is inferred from the Hall of Fame, the reference and the press,
  which report the conventional count but are not the league.
- **How supporters divide.** Two supporter pages, one board thread and one
  opinion piece, cannot say how many supporters count what. A 2023
  r/Nationals thread on the Expos was found and refused (reddit.com).
- **What the club's own site says.** Its history pages (a "Washington
  Baseball Timeline" from 1859, a page of "Nationals' and Expos' all-time
  retired numbers") were seen only as search titles; their content is not
  coded.
- **The Ring of Honor from the club's own announcement.** It is read
  through Wikipedia; the 2010 announcement and its reports were refused or
  gone.
