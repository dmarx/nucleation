# NOTE-tmppca13: Chalmers — The combination problem for panpsychism

## Contribution

The paper gives the combination problem a structure. Before it the problem
was mostly James's subject-summing worry, Seager's name for it and Goff's
sharpening. Chalmers splits it into subject, quality and structure
problems, each with a sharp form, and adds unity, boundary, awareness and
grain. He states each as an explicit argument with numbered premises. He
then classifies every response by its constitutive structure, which shows
that several proposed "solutions" (Rosenberg's high-level individuals,
Gabora, Seager's infusion, IIT as a fundamental law) are emergent or
identity panpsychism and do not combine anything. It does not solve the
problem or prove it unsolvable, and says so (§1).

## Key insight

Panpsychism was attractive because it promised a place for consciousness
in physics without dualism's problem of mental causation. That promise
holds only for *constitutive Russellian* panpsychism, on which
micro-experiences are the quiddities of physical properties and constitute
macro-experience. That view is constrained to build all of our experience
from a handful of micro-properties by necessary relations, with no new
laws: "a little like trying to juggle seven balls in the air with both
hands tied behind one's back" (§9). Every escape from the combination
problem gives up one of those constraints, and with it the motivation.

## Assumptions

- **Terminology from the companion paper** (§2). Micro- and
  macrophenomenal properties; constitutive panpsychism (macrophenomenal
  truths are grounded in microphenomenal truths) versus non-constitutive;
  Russellian panpsychism (microphenomenal properties are quiddities, the
  categorical bases of microphysical dispositions); panprotopsychism
  (protophenomenal properties that are not themselves phenomenal but
  collectively constitute them, by a priori entailment).
- **Causal closure of the microphysical.** It drives every objection from
  mental causation (§§5.1–5.3).
- **Conceivability–possibility links.** Premise (2) of the subject-summing
  and zombie arguments. Chalmers notes that panpsychists who rejected
  physicalism by conceivability arguments are "not in a good position" to
  deny them (§4.2).
- **Structure-preservation** (§8, premise 4): if micro-phenomenal
  structure is isomorphic to micro-physical structure, anything they
  constitute is isomorphic to something micro-physics constitutes.
- **Introspective datum** (§8, premise 5): the structure of the visual
  field is not the structure of the brain.

## Key results

All are informal arguments and classifications. There are no theorems and
no data.

- **The subproblems (§3).**
  - *Subject*: how do micro-subjects yield macro-subjects? Sharp form:
    *subject-summing*. Given 101 subjects, the first 100 do not seem to
    necessitate the 101st.
  - *Quality*: how do micro-qualities yield macro-qualities? Sharp form:
    the *palette* problem.
  - *Structure*: how does micro-experiential structure yield
    macro-experiential structure? Sharp form: *structural mismatch*.
  - *Unity, boundary* (Rosenberg 1998), *awareness* and *grain* (Maxwell,
    Lockwood).
  - A solution "must address all of these problems", and any proposal
    "should indicate which problems it is addressing, and which problems
    it is not".
  - The panprotopsychist has an easier subject problem, since
    protoexperiences need no subjects. In exchange there is a new one: how
    non-experiences constitute experiences.
- **Arguments against constitutive panpsychism (§4).**
  - *Anti-aggregation* (§4.1): from James's nihilism about composites.
    Weak, because nihilism is widely rejected.
  - *Subject-summing* (§4.2): no group of subjects necessitates a further
    subject, supported by a conceivability argument. Called "a
    significant challenge".
  - *Conceivability* (§4.3): a "panpsychist zombie" world, micro-physically
    and micro-phenomenally like ours with no macro-consciousness, is
    conceivable.
  - *Knowledge* (§4.4): Mary told all micro-physical and micro-phenomenal
    facts. "Highly inconclusive", since we know so little about
    micro-phenomenal properties.
  - *Palette* (§4.5): against Russellian versions only.
  - *Revelation* (§4.6): best answered by distinguishing the nature of a
    phenomenal property from the grounds of its instances.
  - *Structural mismatch* (§4.7, §8): an inconsistent tetrad, later a
    five-premise argument. Called "one of the more powerful arguments".
- **Non-combinatorial responses (§5).**
  - *Emergent* panpsychism avoids combination and pays in mental
    causation. Rosenberg, Gabora and IIT read as a fundamental law are
    classed here (§5.1).
  - *Autonomous* panpsychism, including cosmopsychism, faces a
    *decomposition* problem "just as hard" (§5.2).
  - *Identity* panpsychism: the dominant-monad view, identity
    cosmopsychism and *quantum holism*. The last is the most attractive.
    It faces doubt about stable brain-level entanglement and a structural
    mismatch (§5.3).
- **Combinatorial responses (§6).**
  - *Deflating the subject*: eliminativism leaves a "bundle combination
    problem" (§6.1).
  - *Combinatorial infusion* (Seager) is synchronic or diachronic. If
    diachronic it is not constitution. On analysis it is identity or
    emergent panpsychism (§6.2, fn 6).
  - *Phenomenal bonding* (Goff). A phenomenal relation could be the
    quiddity of spatiotemporal or causal relations. If bonding is
    transitive, given ubiquitous causal relations, there is "a single
    giant subject". If not, "far too many subjects". Informational
    integration "along the lines of Tononi's hypothesis" might bound
    subjects, but it is derivative, so a new combination problem opens
    (§6.3).
  - *Deflating awareness*: a functional analysis of awareness (Coleman)
    meets the conceivability objection to functionalism (§6.4).
  - *Panqualityism* avoids micro-subjects but leaves all three problems
    open (§6.5).
  - Footnote 9: Roelofs's mereological inheritance needs strong a
    posteriori necessities.
- **Quality (§7).** Small-palette views need an account of quality
  combination. Large-palette views make most micro-qualities
  epiphenomenal or need a much more complex physics.
- **Structure (§8).** The best reply separates *narrowly* macrophysical
  structure (space, mass, charge) from *broadly* macrophysical structure
  (chemical, biological, computational, informational). Experience
  mirrors the second. Chalmers took this line in *The Conscious Mind*. He
  now says it is "perhaps the only viable line", but it is unclear how
  informational composition could be necessary rather than "brute nomic
  principles".
- **Footnote 7, on exclusion.** Nagel suggested a panprotopsychist
  infusion view: an isolated entity's protophenomenal character fixes a
  subject, while in "the right kind of complex system" it contributes to a
  merged subject. Chalmers likens this to IIT's exclusion postulate, which
  he glosses as "a system is conscious iff it is not part of (and does not
  contain) a system with a higher degree of integrated information".
  "Both views seem to have the counterintuitive consequence that
  consciousness is extrinsic."
- **Verdict (§9).** Most worth exploring: phenomenal bonding or quantum
  holism (subjects), small palettes (qualities), informational composition
  (structure), and a somewhat deflationary account of awareness. It is
  "not at all clear" that they can work together.

## Concepts

- **Constitutive / Russellian panpsychism** — macrophenomenal truths
  grounded in microphenomenal ones; microphenomenal properties as
  quiddities of microphysical roles (§2).
- **Subject-summing problem** — no group of subjects seems to necessitate a
  further subject (§3).
- **Palette problem** — few micro-qualities, many macro-qualities (§3);
  Dainton's "derivation problem".
- **Structural mismatch** — macrophenomenal structure is not isomorphic to
  macrophysical structure (§3, §8).
- **Panpsychist zombie** — a micro-physical and micro-phenomenal duplicate
  of us without macro-consciousness (§4.3).
- **Emergent / autonomous / identity / combinatorial panpsychism** — the
  four classes of response (§5).
- **Quantum holism** — macro-subjects identical to entangled fundamental
  systems (§5.3).
- **Combinatorial infusion** — Seager's merging of subjects (§6.2).
- **Phenomenal bonding** — Goff's phenomenal relations that constitute a
  new subject; co-consciousness is the natural candidate (§6.3).
- **Decomposition problem** — the reverse problem for cosmopsychism (§5.2).
- **Narrowly / broadly macrophysical structure** — physics-level versus
  any structure micro-physics constitutes, information included (§8).

## Connections

James's *Principles of Psychology* is the origin. Chalmers's epigraph is its
twelve-men passage, which ends that "the private minds do not agglomerate
into a higher compound mind". That is the group-mind argument
Schwitzgebel's paper ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md), [NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)) runs against. Schwitzgebel cites
this paper only in his bibliography and fn 25 ([NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md), Composition). Goff
2009 supplies the argument forms of §4, and Seager, Coleman, Rosenberg,
Roelofs and Mørch the responses. The companion paper, *Panpsychism and
Panprotopsychism*, supplies the terminology and the case *for*
panpsychism; this one is the case against. That companion is among the
core consciousness works being filed alongside this reading. The
conceivability argument against physicalism, from Chalmers's core
hard-problem work, is the template for §4.3. IIT appears twice: as emergent
panpsychism (§5.1) and, via the exclusion postulate, in fn 7. The record
holds IIT 3.0 ([LIT-tmpzhi2q](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzhi2q.md), [NOTE-tmpxik42](NOTE-tmpxik42.md)) and a sympathetic assessment of
its panpsychism ([LIT-185](../literature.d/LIT-185.md), [NOTE-122](NOTE-122.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md): supports its structure, adds one argument.**
  - *James's argument as the ancestor of anti-nesting.* Subject-summing's
    premise is that no group of subjects necessitates a further subject.
    It would refuse the upward move to a minded nation as firmly as
    Putnam's stipulation. But it is aimed at *constitutive* panpsychism,
    where the whole's experience is grounded in the parts' experiences.
    The account's functionalist premise grounds it in organisation instead,
    so the argument does not refuse Schwitzgebel's move as stated.
  - *Footnote 7 (C7)* is an objection to exclusion that owes nothing to
    the verdict on groups: exclusion makes consciousness extrinsic. The
    account says exclusion is a postulate justified weakly by the verdict
    it gives. This adds that it carries a cost of its own. It is one line,
    not an argument worked through.
  - *The boundary problem is the account's seam in panpsychist dress.*
    Bonding must bound a human subject without bonding it to everything
    causally connected. The candidate Chalmers names, Tononi-style
    informational integration, is the account's architectural route, and
    he finds it derivative and unprincipled. So the paper arrives,
    independently, at the account's two routes and at its verdict that
    neither is yet principled. It does not discuss groups.
- **[LIT-tmpsygq0](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsygq0.md) / [NOTE-tmp51k1o](NOTE-tmp51k1o.md) (Levin), C8.** Levin's "Chalmers 2013" is
  *Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism* (his reference list, PMC6923654), not
  this paper. Measured against this paper's standard (C1), his remark that
  the model "takes a stand on the combination problem" names no
  sub-problem and no constitution relation, so it is not yet a stand. His
  nested Selves bear on the boundary problem for agency. For experience
  they would need a functional account of awareness, which §6.4 argues
  faces the conceivability objection. [NOTE-tmp51k1o](NOTE-tmp51k1o.md)'s "What the stand is
  is not said" is accurate.
- **[NOTE-131](NOTE-131.md)'s correspondence principle is not here.** Nothing in this
  paper says a conscious system's complex capacities must arise largely
  from relations among subsystems rather than their capacities. The
  nearest idea is §8's informational composition, which concerns
  phenomenal *structure*, not where capacities come from.

## Limitations

- **A map, not a result.** No solution and no impossibility proof (§1).
- **The verdicts are judgements of promise.** §9's shortlist is not shown
  to cohere.
- **Scope.** It treats panpsychism and panprotopsychism; materialist and
  dualist composition are out of scope. Groups of people appear only in
  the James epigraph.
- **Draft, not print.** Read from the April 2015 draft. Footnotes say
  material was added after the first draft, so the published chapter may
  add or move passages, including fn 7 and fn 9.
- **Conceivability premises.** Several arguments stand or fall with the
  conceivability–possibility link. The paper defends it only *ad hominem*
  against panpsychists.

## Open questions

- Is there a bonding relation that is "just nontransitive enough" to
  yield bounded macro-subjects (§6.3)? Any answer would also be a
  criterion for which wholes are subjects, and so would bear on whether a
  nation is one.
- Can informational composition be made constitutive rather than a brute
  law (§8)?
- Does the extrinsicness objection (fn 7) survive IIT 4.0's restatement of
  exclusion? It is aimed at the 2008-era postulate.
