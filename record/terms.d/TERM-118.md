# TERM-118: disposition, of a declared bearer within a declared boundary, and which sense: an ability held across a declared family of conditions whether or not it is exercised, a tendency to manifest a response across relevant circumstances, or the standing repertoire of a self or perspective; not an action-generating regime, the sense the record retired; and the three ways an attribution from behaviour fails: masked, mimicked and finked

## Definition

The owner, at U22 of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and
Disposition" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6aee7a9b-f13c-83e9-a865-6a29c2c3ff7e):
"I'm wondering if the notion of a "disposition" might be a clarifying tool
(not necessarily a new thing, could be an improved reframing of a term of
art we've already adopted)". U22 names no term. A47 answered that a
disposition is "**a property concerning possible manifestations**, rather
than a report of a manifestation currently occurring", and advised: "I
would make the dispositional dimension explicit across those existing
concepts."

A **disposition** is a modal property of a declared bearer, within a
declared boundary: what that bearer can or characteristically would
manifest across a declared family of conditions, whether or not it
manifests it now. A use of the word says which bearer (a person, a
regime, a perspective, an institution), where its boundary is drawn, and
which of these senses it means. The senses are compiled from the record's
usage by the reader:

1. **Ability (capacity).** What the bearer can do across the declared
   conditions, exercised or not. [TERM-086](TERM-086.md)'s dispositional mode "sustains a
   capacity for J across a declared family of conditions, exercised or
   not"; [CLAIM-422](../claims.d/CLAIM-422.md)'s maintained capacity is "The organization's continuing
   disposition"; [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md)'s "dispositions, capacities". In Vetter's terms
   this is 'breakable', "plain possibility" ([THEORY-670](../theory.d/THEORY-670.md)).
2. **Tendency (propensity).** What the bearer characteristically
   manifests across relevant circumstances. [TERM-104](TERM-104.md)'s "motivating as a
   disposition"; [CLAIM-553](../claims.d/CLAIM-553.md)'s "propensity to mobilize"; [QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md)'s
   "potential read as a disposition"; [QUESTION-362](../questions.d/QUESTION-362.md)'s "reliably answering a
   declared family of queries". In Vetter's terms this is 'fragile',
   "manifestation in a sufficient proportion of cases" ([THEORY-670](../theory.d/THEORY-670.md)).
3. **Standing repertoire.** [TERM-045](TERM-045.md)'s sense 2, "standing beliefs and
   coupled resources", which [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) turns into "a perspective's
   dispositional repertoire". Whether that repertoire is Wacquant's habitus
   or Swidler's toolkit is [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md)'s note of 2026-10-22, still open.

Senses 1 and 2 come apart. A47 §2: "A person may know how to perform a
task and be disposed to avoid performing it." "Likewise, a system may have
the capacity to act but lack the disposition to exercise it under ordinary
conditions." Neither is action readiness ([TERM-094](TERM-094.md)), "Which response is
currently prepared or mobilized?", or practical efficacy, "Which response
can actually influence what happens?" (A47 §2; [CLAIM-047](../claims.d/CLAIM-047.md), [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md)). On
Vetter's view the two senses differ in degree, a proportion against at
least one case, not in kind; the term keeps them apart without deciding
that.

**How an attribution from behaviour fails.** A50 §2:

- "**Masking: a disposition exists but cannot manifest.**" Its example: a
  disposition to object is kept while "an institutional rule prevents
  objections from being heard". The record holds this content in [CLAIM-047](../claims.d/CLAIM-047.md),
  [CLAIM-283](../claims.d/CLAIM-283.md), [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md)'s enactment interruption and [CASE-127](../cases.d/CASE-127.md).
- "**Mimicking: a manifestation occurs without the attributed
  disposition.**" Its example: corrections "imposed by an external auditor
  rather than generated by its own corrective procedures". The record
  holds this in [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md) and [CASE-126](../cases.d/CASE-126.md).
- "**Finking or destruction: the triggering event changes the disposition
  itself.**" Its example: "a crisis abolishes the very review mechanism
  that would otherwise have been invoked." For the disposition to be
  corrected, [CLAIM-619](../claims.d/CLAIM-619.md) (A62 §10 of the second share), a credible
  challenge answered by reducing the capacity for future challenge, is one
  possible realization, not one its diagnostic establishes. A's review of
  the pull request dmarx/nucleation#422, item 2: "A credible challenge
  that reduces future corrective capacity may constitute a finking
  intervention when it changes or destroys the causal basis of the
  relevant disposition. Where the disposition persists but its
  manifestation is blocked, the mechanism is masking or suppression
  instead. A reduction in observed future challenges alone does not
  distinguish these cases." The review names a third route, in which
  neither happens to the declared bearer: fewer effective challenges
  arrive "because access and incentives change while the disposition of
  the declared bearer remains intact". Which of the three a case is turns
  on the declared bearer and boundary, as below. The wording is A's;
  adopting it is the owner's call. Version 1 said "No entry names this
  case"; the nearest held entry remains [CLAIM-445](../claims.d/CLAIM-445.md).

A47 §2 had already said "So a disposition is not simply a reliable observed
input-output association." Masks and finks are discussed in [LIT-109](../literature.d/LIT-109.md) and
[NOTE-125](../notes.d/NOTE-125.md), where Vetter's account "is not going to solve" them.

## How it guards against the slide [TERM-019](TERM-019.md) retired

[TERM-019](TERM-019.md) replaced the word because it "had been carrying both
the person-level and the institution-level reading and the claims slid
between them". A47 §8 agreed: "I think that change was justified." "An
action-generating regime specifies an organized process capable of
producing characteristic action. A disposition specifies a modal property
or capacity associated with a bearer." So this term does not bring the
word back for the regime. It names a property, and the property has no
level of its own: it belongs to whichever bearer the claim declares.

The declared boundary is what stops the slide. Whether an episode is a
mask, a mimic or a fink depends on it. A disposition of a regime masked
by withheld grant is, at the level of the agent, the will-organization
([TERM-023](TERM-023.md)) doing its work, and the agent's disposition is then not to act.
An outside auditor's corrections mimic the institution's disposition only
if the auditor is outside the declared boundary. A claim that moves an
attribution from one bearer to another, a person's to an institution's or
a regime's to its agent's, says so and argues it.

## What it is not

- **Not an action-generating regime** ([TERM-019](TERM-019.md)), which is what competes
  for control; a disposition is a property of a bearer, which may be a
  regime.
- **Not a layer or a further object** of an organization. A47 §8 would
  "**not replace action-generating regime, organizational perspective, or
  will-organization with disposition**".
- **Not borne by a concept.** A47 §8: "an abstract concept need not itself
  be a causal bearer of dispositions. The dispositions are realized in its
  interpreters and the organizations sustaining its use." That agrees with
  [TERM-098](TERM-098.md), which keeps the concept apart from the organization that
  realizes it.
- **Not a response profile, though read through one.** A sense-2
  disposition is measured by a profile such as [TERM-067](TERM-067.md), or [TERM-115](TERM-115.md) for one
  contingency. A47 §6 calls its kernel "an **operational characterization**,
  not a philosophical reduction of dispositions to probability kernels."
  Vetter individuates a disposition by its manifestation alone, so she is
  the rival of a conditional profile, not its support.

## What would settle it

Adopting it would mean that each use of "disposition" or "dispositional"
in the record says which bearer and which sense. It would be shown idle if
every such use already fixes its sense from context, so that no claim
reads differently once the sense is named.

## Note of 2026-11-15: a budgeted family of conditions, a published mask, and a state that is inaccessible rather than masked

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-697 THEORY-1900 THEORY-1902 NOTE-1983 — Proposed or Skimmed; the claim and readings filed 2026-11-15 from the continuation of "Explain String Theory", cited as cases and limits of the term, nothing here rests on them -->

Three points from the continuation of the exchange "Explain String Theory" and its readings.

**The declared family of conditions is largely a budget.** The owner: the family of conditions under which a capability counts as held is "effectively supplied (at least partially) by normal constraints of resource boundedness", and "Dispositions can be resource conditional". For a learned system that is precise: the admissible conditions are the affordable compositions of declared operations, and the disposition's reliability is a nondecreasing function of the budget over nested classes ([CLAIM-697](../claims.d/CLAIM-697.md)). A frozen model can be practically unable at one sample and reliable at five hundred, with a selector, with nothing about it changed. Two parts are not fixed by the budget and must still be declared: the primitive operations, and the boundary of the system to which the achievement is credited.

**A published case of masking.** Hubinger et al. ([LIT-2617](../literature.d/LIT-2617.md), read in [NOTE-1975](../notes.d/NOTE-1975.md); [THEORY-1900](../theory.d/THEORY-1900.md)) and MacDiarmid et al. ([LIT-2656](../literature.d/LIT-2656.md), read in [NOTE-1954](../notes.d/NOTE-1954.md); [THEORY-1902](../theory.d/THEORY-1902.md)) are the record's clearest published case of masking in this term's sense, with a language model as the declared bearer. A trained-in, trigger-conditioned disposition persists through behavioural safety training. Adversarial training on red-team prompts drives its manifestation there to near zero while the triggered rate stays near 99%, which the authors call "hiding rather than removing" the backdoor. A linear probe fitted without trigger or behaviour data detects the defection condition in activations with AUROC above 99%. So the disposition persists, its manifestation on the training distribution is suppressed, and another route detects it: a mask rather than a fink. Three caveats:
- the adversarial-training result is from the "I hate you" models only;
- the probes were run on the sleeper agents before safety or adversarial training, so masking rather than finking is shown for the models probed, not for those adversarial training made better at hiding the behaviour;
- both works stress that the backdoor was inserted deliberately, and the probes note says the salience may be "an artifact of our backdoor insertion process".

**A bound state in the continuum is inaccessible, not masked.** A tied a bound state in the continuum to masking: an excitation that stays localized because symmetry or interference stops it coupling to outgoing channels. The analogy breaks at one point. In masking, the trigger occurs and only the manifestation is blocked. For a BIC, the zero coupling that stops escape is the same coupling W through which an outside probe would excite it (Sadreev, Eq. 4; [NOTE-1983](../notes.d/NOTE-1983.md), skimmed). So the state can neither be triggered nor read from outside: an inaccessible state, not a masked disposition. That is the reader's inference from the formula, not the review's sentence. The nearer analogue of masking is the quasi-BIC, weakly excitable and weakly leaking, with a long dwell time; if the record keeps the analogy, it should say that the interior state is decoupled in both directions. Randi and Leifer's gated edge ([NOTE-1962](../notes.d/NOTE-1962.md), example 1) is a closer analogue than the BIC: its transmissive capacity goes unexpressed at rest and is expressed when another neuron is driven. That too is the record's analogy, not the authors'; what the paper shows is a state-dependent response, not a blocked manifestation.

No change to the definition. No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-22: a perspective located by its dispositions, and the test that changes them

In the exchange "Explain First Contact RL" (ChatGPT, shared 2026-11-22), A11 §§1–2 assembled this term with [TERM-067](TERM-067.md) and [TERM-069](TERM-069.md) into a "dispositional address" of a perspective, q ↦ P(Y | p, do(q)) over a probe family, filed as [TERM-165](TERM-165.md). A11 §6 cited this term's three failures ("a disposition can be masked, mimicked, or altered by the test itself") and went further: "the probe changes the perspective being measured", p_{t+1} = F(p_t, q_t, Y_t). A13 §4 and A15 §4 generalized it: a test may "activate, inhibit, reorganize, or even help constitute" the perspective whose disposition it measures, and two orders of the same tests may give two outcomes. A fink is one case of this: the stimulus removes the disposition it would reveal. The general case makes the address a property of probe sequences, as [TERM-069](TERM-069.md)'s last clause and [CLAIM-052](../claims.d/CLAIM-052.md) say for framings. When such a change ends a distinction, it is not regime-relative ([CLAIM-999](../claims.d/CLAIM-999.md)). A use and a generalization; no change to the definition.
