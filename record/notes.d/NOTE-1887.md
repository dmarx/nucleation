# NOTE-1887: Park et al. — Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior

## Contribution

An agent architecture in which long-term memory is an append-only record plus a scored retrieval
function plus LLM-written syntheses with citation pointers, evaluated for believability.

## Key results

- 4.1: "Instead of summarizing, the memory stream described below surfaces relevant memories". The motivating example: summarizing all experiences "produces an uninformative response".
- 4.1: the memory stream "is a list of memory objects, where each object contains a natural language description, a creation timestamp, and a most recent access timestamp."
- 4.1: retrieval score = weighted sum of recency, importance and relevance; "In our implementation, all 𝛼s are set to 1." (Greek alpha in the PDF.) Recency decay factor "0.995".
- 4.2: "Generative agents, when equipped with only raw observational memory, struggle to generalize or make inferences."
- 4.2: the model is prompted "to extract insights and cite the particular records that served as evidence for the insights"; each reflection is stored "including pointers to the memory objects that were cited."
- 4.2: reflections are triggered when summed importance "exceeds a threshold (150 in our implementation)".
- 6.5.1: full architecture best (mu = 29.89) vs. no memory, planning or reflection (mu = 21.21); "𝑑 = 8.16, or eight standard deviations."
- 6.5.2: "Generative agents' memory was not without flaws: they can fail to retrieve the correct instances from their memory." Agents "hallucinated embellishments to their knowledge" but "did not affirmatively claim to have experienced something they had not."
- 6.5.3: "Reflection Is Required for Synthesis."
- 7.2: larger memory posed "a challenge in retrieving the most relevant pieces of information"; agents were "overly cooperative", and "Over time, the interests of others shaped her own interests".
- 8.2: robustness unknown, including "memory hacking—where a carefully crafted conversation could convince an agent of the existence of a past event that never occurred".

## Assumptions

- The target is believability (human-rated), not correctness of beliefs or quality of decisions.
- Memories are never revised or retracted; a later observation is added, not a correction of an
  earlier one. Importance is fixed at creation.
- An LLM judges importance and writes reflections; its errors are inherited (8.2).

## Concepts

Memory stream; observation; retrieval (recency, importance, relevance); reflection and reflection
tree; citation pointers from a reflection to its evidence; planning; believability; ablation.

## Bearing on the record

A cites it for agent memory with retrieval and reflection and sets "remembering information"
against "maintaining an accountable body of claims". **Accurate as far as it goes.** The
architecture is memory for acting believably: no status, no revision or retraction of a memory, no
record of challenge, no governance of what may be written. One qualification the contrast should
carry: reflections do keep evidence pointers to the memories they were inferred from, so the
architecture has a rudimentary support relation; what it lacks is anything that changes a
reflection's standing when its evidence is undercut, or any check on the inference.

- [QUESTION-108](../questions.d/QUESTION-108.md) (does a structured record beat a summary, transcript or posterior because it retains
  more, because what it retains is easier to reach, or because operations are easier on it?):
  **bears on the second mechanism; leaves the question open.** The memory stream retains
  everything and wins by retrieval: sec. 4.1's argument against summarizing is that a summary loses
  the specific memories, and 6.5.2 and 7.2 show that failures come from retrieval, not retention
  (the memory existed but was not surfaced). The ablation removes memory, reflection and planning
  wholesale, so it does not separate retention from access from operations; it does not test the
  question's split. A design that does ([CASE-091](../cases.d/CASE-091.md)) could take this architecture as its "retrieval
  over everything" arm.

## Limitations

Believability rated by human judges over two simulated days; no task with ground truth; one LLM
(gpt-3.5-turbo); costly ("thousands of dollars in token credits"). The memory design is not tested
on revision.
