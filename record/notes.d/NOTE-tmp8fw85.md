# NOTE-tmp8fw85: Curry et al. 2021, moral molecules

## Contribution

The paper ([LIT-tmpps55m](../literature.d/LIT-tmpps55m.md)) extends morality-as-cooperation from a list to a generative
system. The seven domains of the founding statement ([LIT-tmpcsgn6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcsgn6.md)) become
"elements". Each pair is hypothesised to combine into a more complex moral
concept, and an example of each is located in "the professional and popular
literature" (§2). Section 3 counts the possible higher-order and
mixed-valence combinations. Section 4 claims that the theory is "the first
and only theory to make genuine predictions about what moral phenomena there
might be in the world".

## Key insight

Many named virtues and institutions read naturally as two cooperative
solutions applied at once:

- filial piety: deference within the family;
- honour: heroism expressed through revenge;
- queuing: division by first possession;
- arbitration: division settled by deference.

If that holds generally, a small set of cooperative elements could account
for a large and culturally variable moral vocabulary. The paper suggests
that elements may be innate and universal and molecules acquired and
relative (§4).

## Assumptions

- The seven elements are the right basis. The paper's Table 1 restates them
  from [LIT-tmpcsgn6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcsgn6.md) and the MAC-Q ([LIT-tmp0moou](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0moou.md)).
- **Conceptual analysis is the method.** "Not an exact science", and "not
  everyone will be persuaded by all the examples" (§4).
- **The molecules are unordered pairs**, so 7!/(2!·5!) = 21 (footnote 5).
  For each pair the authors hypothesised one candidate, then "tried to find
  examples … that matched our theoretical expectations, and that had a
  positive moral valence".
- **A combination may have unequal parts.** Collegiality could be MR² and
  comradeship M²R (§3), so the number of possible molecules is unbounded.

## Key results

- **Table 2, 21 molecules.**
  - Kinship with: mutualism, fraternity; exchange, blood revenge; hawk,
    family pride; dove, filial piety; division, gavelkind; possession,
    primogeniture.
  - Mutualism with: exchange, friendship; hawk, patriotism; dove, tribute;
    division, diplomacy; possession, common ownership.
  - Exchange with: hawk, honour; dove, confession; division, turn-taking;
    possession, restitution.
  - Hawk with: dove, modesty; division, mercy; possession, munificence.
  - Dove with: division, arbitration; possession, mendicance.
  - Division with possession: queuing.
- **Supporting data, where any is cited.** Blood revenge is a moral
  imperative in 38 of 168 societies and legitimate in 90 (Ericksen and
  Horton 1992). Moral outrage at queue-jumpers is shown experimentally
  (Milgram et al. 1986). The other cells rest on definitions and quotations.
- **Counts** (§3, footnotes 7–8, checked):
  - 2⁷ − 1 = 127 non-empty combinations of the seven, in the proportions
    7 + 21 + 35 + 35 + 21 + 7 + 1.
  - 3⁷ − 1 = 2,186 combinations once each element can be absent, positive or
    negative.
  - The worked ternary example, honour among thieves, 2001100₃ = 1,494, is
    correct.
- **Revision rule** (footnote 9): a new non-cooperative element would force
  the theory "to be revised more fundamentally".

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Each of the 21 pairwise combinations corresponds to a positively valenced moral concept | weak | one example per cell, chosen after the cell was proposed; no protocol, judges or failures reported ("We succeeded in every case") |
| C2 | Morality is a combinatorial system | weak | follows from C1 by analogy with chemistry, DNA, language and music; no test that distinguishes combination from coincidence of description |
| C3 | The framework "has the potential to explain all moral ideas, possible and actual" | assertion | with unequal proportions allowed, the space is infinite (§3), so fitting it is easy and failing it is hard |
| C4 | MAC is "the first and only theory to make genuine predictions about what moral phenomena there might be" | weak | the paper also says the theory "doesn't predict which of these many possibilities will be actualities" (§4) |
| C5 | Elements may be innate and universal, molecules acquired and relative | conjecture | posed as a question for future work |

## Concepts

- **Moral element / molecule.** One of the seven cooperative domains, and a
  concept analysed as a combination of two or more of them.
- **Negative element.** The vice that matches each element: neglect,
  betrayal, cheating, cowardice, disrespect, unfairness, theft. Molecules can
  mix positive and negative elements ("mutiny").
- **Binary and ternary notation** (footnotes 7–8). A seven-digit code for
  which elements are present, and with what sign.

## Connections

- **The founding statement and the MAC-Q ([LIT-tmpcsgn6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcsgn6.md), [LIT-tmp0moou](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0moou.md)).** The
  elements come from the one and the molecule idea from the other's
  limitations section (pp. 40–41 of the manuscript).
- **The sixty-society test ([LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md), [NOTE-034](NOTE-034.md)).** This paper adds no evidence
  on whether the seven are universally valued, or whether non-cooperative
  content is moralized. It cites [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) for the claim that each domain "has
  been considered morally good the world over", and does not discuss the
  objections [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md)'s commentators raised.
- **Purity ([LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md)).** Mendicance is read here as humility plus property.
  It is the one ascetic norm the paper treats. Fitouchi, André and Baumard
  explain ascetic piety and ritual differently, as practices believed to
  train self-control. The record's reading of them ([NOTE-132](NOTE-132.md)) does not
  mention poverty vows. Extended to such vows, the two readings would come
  apart. On this paper's reading, poverty vows should co-vary with
  deference and property norms. On [LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md)'s, they should co-vary with
  other self-control norms such as fasting and chastity. No study in the
  record compares them.
- **The machine-read replication ([LIT-tmpvrv55](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvrv55.md)).** It reports filial piety
  and honour in HRAF paragraphs, as examples rather than counts.

## Bearing on the record

There is nothing here for ML practice, and the anthology does not hold the
paper. For the morality-as-cooperation thread it shows the programme's
ambition and its method, both at their plainest. It should not be cited as
evidence that morality is combinatorial.

## Limitations

- **Confirmatory search.** No record of where the authors looked, what they
  rejected, or whether anyone outside the team agreed with the
  classifications.
- **The elements stretch to fit.** Two molecules combine an element with
  its denial, which the paper's own scheme (§3) counts as mixed valence:
  - mendicance is "disavowing property";
  - common ownership is holding property "in common" rather than
    respecting individual possession.
  Primogeniture reads birth order as "first-come-first-served" possession,
  which is an analogy rather than a combination.
- **No discriminating test.** The paper names none, and does not say what
  outcome would show that morality is not combinatorial.
- **Inconsistent naming.** The abstract and §3 say "mendicancy"; Table 2 and
  §2.20 say "mendicance".

## Open questions

- Given an independent list of moral concepts (a virtue lexicon, the
  eHRAF Ethics index), how many can blind judges place in the scheme, and
  with what agreement? How many fit nothing?
- Is endorsement of a molecule predicted by endorsement of its elements, as
  §4 asks?
- Do poverty vows track deference and property norms, or self-control norms?
