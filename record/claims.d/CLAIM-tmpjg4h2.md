# CLAIM-tmpjg4h2: The cost of making a proposal legible to the authority that controls its resources is not a property of the idea but of how an institution distributes interpretive labour, so an institution can steer which questions are pursued by deciding who must pay to make them legible, with no rule against any idea

## The claim

A56 §4. In the employee–manager case the employee usually pays to make the
idea legible. The cost could be placed elsewhere: the manager trained in
the relevant field, a small exploratory budget for the employee, a
specialist intermediary, or a limited experiment that yields evidence more
cheaply than an advance justification. A proposal that would take ten hours
of specialist explanation can be paid for by the employee's ten hours, by
the manager's prior training, or by a technical group's review. "These
architectures may reach the same authorization decision at different costs,
or they may reach different decisions because different evaluators can
recognize different distinctions."

Hence "the cost of legibility is not simply a property of the idea; it
depends on the institutional distribution of interpretive labor", and "an
institution can effectively control what questions are pursued by
determining who must bear the cost of making them legible". "This may
produce a form of organizational inequality without any explicit rule against
unconventional ideas": a proposal in the language of established strategy is
cheap to approve, one that challenges the governing categories must be
translated before its relevance can be recognized.

## What it does not say

"There is an important caution: unconventionality is not evidence of merit
either." The claim is about the distribution of evaluation costs, "not a
presumption that unfamiliar proposals deserve approval".

## Neighbours

- CLAIM-303's note of 2026-11-19, (b): a bureaucracy cheap for
  administrators because claimants do the work of fitting its categories,
  and a recommender that burdens creators "to remain legible to its
  classifications". Left there as close to definitional; this is that
  observation turned into a claim about which questions get pursued.
- CLAIM-731: the cost of imposing a decision against the cost of
  contesting it. This is the cost of asking, a third. Its note of
  2026-11-23 holds A52 §6's earlier step in the same exchange: "Who must
  spend time to make a demand legible, and who must spend time to answer
  it, are institutional design choices." A56 §4 takes it from a demand on a
  respondent to a proposal to inquire, and adds that the choice steers which
  questions are pursued.
- TERM-064's "direction": James et al.'s reverse onus, legibility demanded
  of the governed (THEORY-857), is a case of the burden placed on the party
  with least to spend.
