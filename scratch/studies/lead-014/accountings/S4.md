## Relations to held entries

- **R1.** THEORY-002 and THEORY-004 (representations identified by their
  kernel up to orthogonal maps): the paper's mechanistic similarity is a
  different, behavioural notion, equality of invariance sets under a
  chosen family of interventions, not similarity of representations. Two
  models can have equal kernels and differ in it only if their readouts
  differ, and can share it with different kernels. **Bears on**; a
  sentence in the NOTE's bearing section, no ARG.
- **R2.** THEORY-018 (identifiability up to an invertible linear map):
  the paper's similarity is relative to the intervention set chosen, so
  "mechanism" is identified only up to what the probes can distinguish.
  **Bears on**; NOTE only.
- **R3.** The eight mode-connectivity LITs just filed: this paper is the
  first on the record to ask *what* connectivity tracks (shared
  mechanisms), not *whether* minimizers connect. A THEORY linking them
  is warranted (C3, C4).

## Ideas of the source (ledger)

- **C1.** Mechanistic similarity defined as equal sets of invariances to
  unit interventions on a generative process with independent, invertible
  latents (Defs. 2–4). **THEORY** (Proposed) and NOTE.
- **C2.** Proposition 1, exhaustiveness: invariance to two unit
  interventions implies invariance to their composition; lack of
  invariance to either precludes invariance to the composition. **NOTE**
  (and gap G3).
- **C3.** Proposition 2: given sufficient overparameterization,
  mechanistically dissimilar minimizers are connected by some continuous
  low-loss path (from Nguyen 2019; Simsek et al. 2021). Empirically, by a
  quadratic path but not a linear one, even after permutation alignment
  (Fig. 4). **THEORY.**
- **C4.** Conjecture 1: lack of linear connectivity (up to symmetries)
  implies mechanistic dissimilarity. Proved for a one-hidden-layer model
  on a simplified process (App. F); supported by fine-tuning experiments
  (Fig. 5). **THEORY**, filed as a conjecture with partial proof.
- **C5.** Naive fine-tuning on clean data can fail to change a pretrained
  model's reliance on a spurious cue: small and medium learning rates stay
  linearly connected and mechanistically similar; large rates break both
  but likely destroy pretrained features. **THEORY** (or within C4's) and
  NOTE.
- **C6.** Exception: under perfect cue–label correlation, fine-tuning does
  change mechanisms, because the pretrained model has no transferable
  mechanism. **NOTE.**
- **C7.** CBFT: a barrier loss on the linear path to the pretrained
  solution plus an invariance loss on class-average representations;
  beats naive fine-tuning, LLR and LPFT on the synthetic benchmarks
  (Table 1). **NOTE**; method detail is ML practice (anthology
  candidate).
- **C8.** Linear connectivity of mechanistically dissimilar models is
  possible when the mechanisms are of similar "complexity" (e.g. both
  linearly separable); hence pretraining should promote a variety of
  expressive mechanisms. **NOTE.**
- **C9.** Mechanistic similarity contrasted with prediction-mismatch
  measures. **NOTE.**

## Gaps in the source's own argument

- **G1. Status inflation.** The abstract ("demonstrate that lack of linear
  connectivity … implies") and §6 ("Our results in §5 demonstrate …")
  state as shown what §5 labels Conjecture 1, proved only in a
  one-hidden-layer simplified setting. Any THEORY must carry the
  conjecture's status, not the abstract's.
- **G2.** Definition 1 as printed is garbled: the bound uses L(θ0), which
  is undefined (θ2 meant), and the weights t and 1−t are attached to the
  endpoints the wrong way round for γ(0) = θ1.
- **G3.** Proposition 1's second half does not hold for invariance defined
  as equality of average loss (Def. 3): a composition can restore what
  one intervention alone breaks only if the model's loss depends on an
  interaction of the two latents; with independent latents set to fixed
  values the claim needs that the composition's loss be at least each
  single intervention's, which Def. 3 does not give. Not checked against
  App. F.1's proof; flagged for checking, not asserted as an error.
- **G4.** All mechanistic evidence is on synthetic cue datasets (CIFAR-10
  and -100 with box cues, Dominoes); the generative-process assumptions
  (independent, invertible latents) are met by construction there and
  not in natural data. The authors say so (future work).
- **G5.** Similarity is relative to the chosen intervention family; two
  models similar under it may differ under an intervention not tried.
  The definition does not say how the family is to be chosen beyond
  "task-relevant vulnerabilities".
- **G6.** CBFT results: three seeds, no variance reported in the text
  extraction; comparison with training from scratch only in App. E.1.
