# CLAIM-tmp0uism: The evaluative space model's positivity and negativity channels are perspectives in the thin sense goal-conduciveness valence uses: each ranks the options by its own activation, the two rankings contest exactly the approach–avoidance pairs, and each holds an authority over action apart from its activation, in its gain, its offset and the override of higher levels; so the model's neutral point, both channels low, is no contest, where valence is undefined, not neutrality, and its coactivation is contest, which a record of conduct shows as ambivalence only when no candidate ordering, a stable compromise included, accounts for the decisions

## The claim

TERM-076 asks three things of a perspective: that it rank the options the
system faces, that its ranking can disagree with another's on a pair (a
contest), and that it hold an authority the system's dynamics set, so that
which ranking wins is the negotiation's outcome and not given by the
rankings (CLAIM-283: the precedence a motive claims and the authority it is
granted differ). It does not ask for sense 1 of TERM-072, a regime of
mutually conditioned interpretation; this claim is about the thin sense
only. The evaluative space model, as Norris, Gollan, Berntson and
Cacioppo restate it (LIT-1438, NOTE-1101), meets each.

1. **Two rankings.** The model's channels are separable positive and
   negative systems with distinct activation functions. Each induces a
   ranking of the options open to the system: positivity ranks them by
   their appetitive activation, negativity by their aversive activation,
   reversed (less aversive is better). The model's evidence for
   separability (uncorrelated positive and negative affect, distinct
   substrates and transmitters; NOTE-1101, C1) is evidence that these are
   two rankings and not one read twice.
2. **Contest is approach–avoidance conflict.** The two rankings disagree on
   a pair exactly when the option that activates positivity more also
   activates negativity more: a target with both channels high against
   withdrawal, the coactivated case. In reciprocal activation the rankings
   agree and nothing is contested; in uncoupled activation one channel is
   indifferent and contests nothing. So the model's three modes of
   activation are, in TERM-076's terms, agreement, one-sided preference and
   contest, and the model's own mark of ambivalence, approach–avoidance
   conflict, is contest by definition.
3. **Authority apart from activation.** Which channel's ranking decides is
   not fixed by the activations. The model gives negativity a higher gain
   and positivity a higher intercept, lets both recalibrate with context,
   and makes evaluation heterarchical, with higher levels able to override
   lower (NOTE-1101). Gain, offset and override are what the negotiation
   grants each channel, and they vary across individuals, contexts and
   levels while the activations stay put. That is CLAIM-283's granted
   authority.

Two consequences follow, and both change how CLAIM-440's consequence 4
reads the model.

- **The model's neutral point is no contest.** Both channels low: neither
  ranks the pair strictly, nothing is contested, and on CLAIM-440's
  consequence 1 valence is undefined there, not neutral. CLAIM-440's
  neutrality is something else: an ordering prevails among contested
  pairs and the system keeps to its reference course along it. The model
  and the claim therefore do not disagree about where neutrality is; they
  name different states, and the record must keep three apart, not two:
  no contest, neutrality, ambivalence.
- **The middling expression splits.** The model says coactivation yields
  "the same middling expression on the affective response surface" as
  neutrality. Read in a record of conduct, a middling output under contest
  is one of two things. Either the system takes the compromise each time
  (in the policy case of CASE-tmpjw1l1, hold), and then a third ordering,
  compromise on top, accounts for every decision and prevails; or it takes
  one channel's top and then the other's, and then no candidate accounts
  for the decisions, which is ambivalence as CLAIM-440 reads it: the same
  pair's outcome reversing. The procedure of CLAIM-tmpkcsp7 tells these
  apart, and tells both from no contest, provided the channels' rankings
  are measured apart from the decisions, as the model's own warning
  requires (NOTE-1101: a record of choices alone cannot tell ambivalent
  from neutral). Its synthetic cases show each verdict
  (scratch/studies/lead-041/test_procedure.py).

So split potential is not the model's coactivation restated, as
NOTE-1101 said. It is what coactivation becomes when the channels are read
as perspectives and the record is read in time: either a compromise
ordering prevails, and the system is not ambivalent in its conduct whatever
it feels, or the decisions alternate and it is. CLAIM-451's narrowing
stands: ambivalence felt with one ordering prevailing in conduct is the
first case, and this claim says nothing against it.

## What it does not say

- That the channels are organizational perspectives in TERM-072's sense 1,
  or represented or phenomenal ones. They meet TERM-076's thin sense:
  ranking, contest, authority.
- That the model's asymmetries (negativity bias, positivity offset) follow
  from anything here. They are read as authorities, not predicted.
- That ambivalence is alternation. Alternation is how ambivalence shows in
  conduct; CLAIM-451's felt ambivalence under a prevailing ordering is not
  excluded.
- That any organism's record has been read this way. The one application
  so far is an organization's (CASE-tmpjw1l1), whose two channels are the
  dual mandate's; no record of an animal's or person's conduct with both
  channels measured has been read.
