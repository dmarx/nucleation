# CLAIM-526: Describing persons and organizations as coexisting levels of one process removes the question of how already-constituted agents add up to a collective, but not the question of levels: whether a collective variable has dynamics of its own, closed against the activity that realizes it, can be decided on a process description that presupposes no agents, is sometimes decided no, and a closed collective variable can be an aggregate that coexists with its realization while keeping none of its members' distinctions

## The claim

Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy ([THEORY-1278](../theory.d/THEORY-1278.md)) write that the problem of
scaling from individual to collective agency "is only relevant ... when
communication is regarded as an interaction between already-existing
agents, operating at different levels, and vanishes the moment
communication is viewed eventfully" (p. 1364). They rest it on Bencherki and
Iliadis ([THEORY-1277](../theory.d/THEORY-1277.md)): entities "are not at different stages of
individuation, but rather different informational levels that preserve
all the characteristics of their organized components. An employee is not
prior to the organization, nor is the organization an abstraction of its
employees; they each exist fully and simultaneously" (ms. p. 21).

The claim has three parts.

1. **What dissolves.** If there are no agents prior to the process,
   there is no question of how their acts are summed into an
   organization's. The record grants this much. [CLAIM-030](CLAIM-030.md) already
   denies that an organized whole's intention is an expectation over its
   members' intentions.
2. **What does not.** Whether a higher-level variable has dynamics of its
   own is a question about a process and a variable, not about agents. In
   the record it is decided by construction. A collective variable can be
   exactly dynamically independent of the activity realizing it
   ([CLAIM-388](CLAIM-388.md), [CASE-120](../cases.d/CASE-120.md)). Generally it is not: the Simon–Ando
   aggregate has a closure defect that vanishes only under stated
   conditions ([CLAIM-386](CLAIM-386.md)). So "does the organization-level description
   run on its own?" can be asked on the eventful description, has
   answers, and some of them are no. That is the strong form's refutation
   condition in [THEORY-1278](../theory.d/THEORY-1278.md)'s promote_when ("scaling-up questions remain
   answerable, and needed, under the eventful description"), met for the
   level question though not for agent aggregation.
3. **Stages and coexistence are not exclusive.** [CLAIM-388](CLAIM-388.md)'s closed
   variable is the *sum* of the units, an aggregate in the plainest sense,
   and it coexists with them: the units go on, and the sum's dynamics are
   closed. It does not "preserve all the characteristics" of its components.
   It keeps none of the distinctions among them, which is why it can be
   closed, and a composite can present at its boundary a constraint that no
   part has ([CLAIM-480](CLAIM-480.md)). Coexistence is what the record's levels already
   have. Preservation of every component characteristic is what they
   characteristically lack.

## What it does not say

- **Not that organizations are aggregates of members.** The record's
  example is a linear model; whether any organizational variable is
  closed is an empirical question for that organization.
- **Not that the agent picture is right.** Part 1 grants the readings'
  point against it.
- **Not that closure is the only criterion of a level.** Slowness
  ([CLAIM-331](CLAIM-331.md)), scale separation ([CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)) and a block's gap
  ([CLAIM-157](CLAIM-157.md)) are others. Which one is meant is fixed by the
  criterion ([CLAIM-405](CLAIM-405.md)), and [CLAIM-474](CLAIM-474.md) holds that they need not agree.
- **Not a reading of Simondon.** It answers his organization-studies
  readers. His own text is held as [LIT-1648](../literature.d/LIT-1648.md), Unavailable.

## Note of 2026-11-01: coexisting levels in the constitution debate

<!-- inactive-ok-block: LIT-tmpk95bs CLAIM-tmp1qybv — Unfetched or Proposed; filed 2026-11-01, cited as context -->
The debate on mechanistic constitution read today holds part 3's picture
of levels, coexisting and without causal relation between them, and adds
two points.

- **Coexistence without inter-level causation is the mechanists' default.**
  If a whole supervenes on its parts and is not caused by them, every cause
  of the whole is a common cause of it and some part ([THEORY-tmp3pqnm](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp3pqnm.md)).
  Romero 2015 ([LIT-tmpk95bs](../literature.d/LIT-tmpk95bs.md), unread) is cited for explaining inter-level
  relations "without inter-level causation". So the claim's "coexists with
  its realization" is the standard picture. What is new in the claim is
  the closure criterion, which the debate lacks.
- **The levels are different levels.** The debate's levels are levels of
  parthood, local to a phenomenon ([NOTE-tmpz7pqv](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpz7pqv.md), footnote 6). The claim's
  are levels of description, picked out by closure. [CLAIM-388](CLAIM-388.md)'s sum is a
  property of the whole, and its units are parts, so the two coincide
  there. In general they need not, which [CLAIM-405](CLAIM-405.md) already allows.
- **Closure is testable where constitution is not.** The debate's
  asymmetry is that the parts can change without the whole changing but
  not the reverse ([NOTE-tmpeljd8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeljd8.md), p. 20). Whether a collective variable is
  closed is decided by exactly those changes, redistributions that leave
  the aggregate fixed, which can be made without touching the aggregate.
  So part 2's "can be decided" survives the debate's negative results,
  which concern constitution, not closure.
