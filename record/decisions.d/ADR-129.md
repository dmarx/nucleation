# ADR-129: An account may take a scope that crosses lines, a theme the record's argument returns to in several lines, beside the accounts of lines and their parts; every cross-line account is reviewed at the synthesis review as a possible sign that the record needs a line it lacks, and a theme that passes the review is put to the owner as a candidate line

## Context

[ADR-083](ADR-083.md) gives an account to "a line or a part of one", with one account per scope. On 2026-11-20 the record filed eight SYNTHESIS accounts for principles P2 and P4 to P10 of the owner's manuscript. An independent audit had found each to be a conjunction of held entries spanning two or three lines (pull request 557; workbench entry of 2026-11-20). The workbench entry and each account noted that their scope "is a theme, not a line or a part of one, which [ADR-083](ADR-083.md)'s one account per scope does not anticipate". Asked whether such accounts are a kind the record wants, the owner ruled as quoted in the summary.

The owner's two reasons pull in different directions. A conjunction that can be formed across lines suggests a line is missing. An account that deliberately crosses lines can carry ideas from one line to another. The ruling keeps both: the account is permitted, and it is also treated as evidence.

## Decision

1. **A scope may be a theme.** Beside a line and a part of a line, an account may take as its scope a theme the record's argument returns to in two or more lines. It says so in its preface and names the lines its components come from. [ADR-083](ADR-083.md)'s other rules apply unchanged: one account per scope, the account adds nothing, and its standing is its components'.
2. **Every cross-line account is audited at the synthesis review.** The review ([ADR-078](ADR-078.md); the `synthesis-review` skill) gains a stage, after its accounts are filed or revised. For each cross-line account, it asks three questions:
   - **An unheld question.** Do the components answer a question that no line's blurb names?
   - **Recurrence.** Does the theme recur: in more than one cross-line account, in a manuscript, or in a claim that rests on components from several of its lines?
   - **Findability.** Would a reader looking for the theme find it more easily under a line of its own than through the accounts of the lines it crosses?
3. **Verdicts.** Three yeses make the theme a *candidate line*. It goes to the owner with a proposed name, a blurb, the accounts and claims it would gather, and what it would take from existing lines. Fewer make it a *bridge*: the account stays cross-line, and the review records why. A candidate line is a decision about the `lines` vocabulary, for the owner, as a split is ([ADR-082](ADR-082.md)). The review proposes it and does not make it.
4. **The audit is recorded** in the review's curation entry, one row per cross-line account.

## Alternatives

- **Forbid cross-line accounts** and require a line first. Rejected by the owner: cross-line accounts can carry ideas between lines.
- **Add a line for every cross-line account.** That makes lines cheap and lets a single integration reshape the vocabulary. The audit's recurrence test guards against it.
- **Treat cross-line accounts as Arm B candidates only**, links to be tested for admission as claims. That is the route that left the eight principles with nowhere to go.

## Consequences

- [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) takes a history note: a scope may be a theme (this decision).
- The `synthesis-review` skill gains stage 4b, "Audit cross-line accounts", and its curation entry a fifth part.
- The eight principle accounts are cross-line accounts under this decision. Their first audit is this decision's demonstration, in the curation entry of 2026-11-21.
