# THEORY-093: A content of mind is conscious when a continuous flow of homeostatic feelings identifies it as the organism's own; the feelings are conscious in themselves and hybrid, partly made of the body state they map, because interoceptive pathways let the body act directly on the neurons that map it

## Source

- Damasio & Damasio (2022), *Homeostatic feelings and the biology of
  consciousness*, [LIT-628](../literature.d/LIT-628.md), in [NOTE-507](../notes.d/NOTE-507.md) (Skimmed): every section,
  Table 1 and the references, but through a fetch tool that returned
  section-by-section accounts, not the text. Nothing below quotes it; the
  essay is paraphrased, and cited by section heading.
- Damasio & Carvalho (2013), *The nature of feelings*, [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md), read in
  [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md): the section on the neural substrates of feeling (C4–C5) and
  the cellular proposal (C7).

## What was actually shown

Nothing was shown in the experimental sense. The essay argues from a
definition, an anatomical contrast and cited clinical observations; the
2013 review argues from lesion cases. What each contributes:

**The identification.** Damasio & Damasio ([LIT-628](../literature.d/LIT-628.md), "Consciousness")
define consciousness as the spontaneous identification of what is
perceived, recalled and thought as belonging to a particular owner, the
organism in its body. They set this against the mere integration of
images, the scale of that integration and attention to some images over
others, which they say it should not be confused with.

**Feelings supply it.** On their hypothesis ("How is consciousness made"),
the continuous flow of homeostatic feelings, salient ones such as hunger,
thirst, pain, well-being and malaise and near-equilibrium ones they call
feelings of existence, provides the identification of a mind with its
organism. Above a threshold, such feelings are generated continuously
while awake. Each is conscious of itself, not by borrowing consciousness
from another process, and they confer consciousness on exteroceptive
images: visual cortex builds the maps, but for those images to be
conscious, the essay proposes, feelings must be present as well.
Self-consciousness is a later step, when perceptions, memories and
reasoning are connected to the organism and its history.

**Why feelings can do this: the hybrid.** Exteroceptive maps cannot
interact with what they map; a map of a landscape does not touch the
landscape. Interoceptive ones can. Table 1 of the essay contrasts the two
kinds of pathway: interoceptive axons poorly or not myelinated (Aδ and C),
non-synaptic as well as synaptic transmission, time scales of seconds to
hours as well as fast ones, a blood–brain barrier absent or with large
gaps, monoamines, acetylcholine and neuropeptides; exteroceptive,
movement and cognitive pathways well myelinated, predominantly synaptic,
very fast, behind a continuous barrier, using glutamate and GABA. So
viscera and circulating molecules can reach the neurons that map them,
and the brain can act back on the source. Map and object interact, and
the authors call the resulting feeling a hybrid of body and neural map
("How do living organisms generate homeostatic feelings?"). Damasio &
Carvalho ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)) had proposed the cellular half nine years earlier, as
speculation: that feeling begins in unmyelinated interoceptive fibres
open to ions and ligands along their length (C7 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)).

**Where it is lost.** The essay places the first integration of body
signals in the posterior upper brainstem (nucleus tractus solitarius,
parabrachial nucleus, periaqueductal grey), and says that small lesions
there abolish consciousness, by compromising integrated body
representations and severing their links to exteroceptive contents, while
even extensive cortical lesions may not ("Impaired consciousness", citing
Parvizi and Damasio 2001 and 2003). Damasio & Carvalho ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)) give the
lesion pattern first-hand from the literature they review: damage to the
posterior half of the upper brainstem gives coma or a vegetative state in
which feeling and sentience are abolished, and damage to the ventral half
gives locked-in syndrome with feeling spared (C5 in [NOTE-330](../notes.d/NOTE-330.md)). The essay
adds that general anaesthetics act at the membrane-level sensing below
feeling, suspending interoception and exteroception together.

What could have come out otherwise: the lesions that abolish
consciousness could have been cortical while brainstem lesions spared it,
or the interoceptive pathways could have been built like the
exteroceptive ones. Neither is the case as the sources report it. Neither
outcome tests the identification itself.

## What this does not say

- **It does not show that feelings make contents conscious.** The
  identification is a definition, and the lending claim is argued from
  it (C1–C2 in [NOTE-507](../notes.d/NOTE-507.md), a definition and weak). A theory of access
  or of integration is not refuted by being told consciousness is
  something else ([NOTE-507](../notes.d/NOTE-507.md), Limitations).
- **Coma and anaesthesia do not discriminate.** Brainstem lesions and
  anaesthetics abolish arousal and every cortical process downstream, so
  their effects are what every theory predicts. The plant evidence shows
  that anaesthetics act on membranes, not where in an animal their effect
  on consciousness lies.
- **The hybrid is an interpretation.** The anatomy of Table 1 is cited
  (to Carvalho and Damasio 2021) with no numbers, and that the anatomy
  makes feeling partly constituted by the body is the authors' reading of
  it (C4–C5 in [NOTE-507](../notes.d/NOTE-507.md)). In 2013 the cellular link was "we propose".
- **It is not checked against the text.** The essay was reached only
  through a fetch tool that would not reproduce it, so every detail here
  is as that tool returned it, and the essay is held Skimmed.
- **It does not include the evolutionary claim.** The essay also holds
  that homeostatic feelings were the first conscious phenomena, selected
  because they let life regulation be guided overtly. That is an adaptive
  story with no comparative evidence (C3 in [NOTE-507](../notes.d/NOTE-507.md)) and is left
  out of this account.

## It extends [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md)

[THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md) holds that felt affect is the experience of the body's
regulatory state, present throughout waking experience, and it leaves
open whether what is felt is a prediction of that state or a map of it.
It explicitly does not claim that consciousness is grounded in feeling.
This account builds on it in two ways. It widens it: the same felt body
state is what makes any content conscious, not only what affect is. And
it refines the open question with a reading [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md) does not list:
neither a prediction nor only a map, but a feeling partly constituted by
the body state it maps. Damasio & Carvalho ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)) are a source of both
accounts. The essay supports [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md)'s "throughout waking experience"
clause with its feelings of existence, continuous while awake. It says
nothing about prediction, so it does not decide against Barrett's version
([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md)); it adds a third option. It is a widening of the same
mechanism, not a rival, so the relation is `extends`.

## It rivals [THEORY-096](THEORY-096.md)

The global-availability account holds that a content becomes conscious
when it ignites a core of long-range cortical hub neurons and is
broadcast, and it takes global availability to be what is experienced.
This account holds that a content becomes conscious when homeostatic
feeling identifies it as the organism's own, and the essay sets
consciousness apart from the attention given to some images over others
and from integration, naming Dehaene's account among those that study
such mechanisms in place of consciousness ("The problem of
consciousness"). The two give incompatible answers to what makes a
content conscious, and incompatible answers about what is lost when it is
lost. The workspace review ([LIT-631](../literature.d/LIT-631.md)) ties the level of consciousness
to frontal-parietal hubs and argues that anaesthetics of different
molecular classes share a disconnection of that network. The essay puts
the critical site in the posterior brainstem, says extensive cortical
lesions may spare consciousness, and puts the anaesthetics' action at
membrane-level sensing below feeling. Neither tests the other; the essay
never engages the workspace's evidence and the review never mentions
interoception. The rivalry holds only on the reading of the workspace
that identifies experience with availability, which is the reading that
account states; read as a theory of access alone, it would answer a
different question.

- **The self-model ([THEORY-064](THEORY-064.md)).** Metzinger likens the transparent
  self-model to Damasio's core self and anchors it in continuous internal
  input. On this account ownership of contents comes first and
  self-consciousness after; the two agree that a felt sense of one's own
  body is what contents are referred to, and differ on whether a model or a
  hybrid feeling does it. Not declared.
- **AI consciousness ([THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md)).** The essay concludes that consciousness
  needs a partnership of a nervous system with the body it serves and is
  absent from inanimate objects of any complexity. On this account a system
  with no body whose state can act on its own maps has no consciousness,
  which is the biological side of the deadlock that account describes,
  stated without an argument the other side would accept.
