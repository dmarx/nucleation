# THEORY-tmplatcg: In Argentina's four main national newspapers, April 1998 to December 2007, a newspaper's monthly front-page coverage of the federal government's corruption scandals was lower in months when it received more government advertising, within newspaper and month and within each newspaper-president pair (about 0.31 of a front page per standard deviation of advertising in the 2010 working paper); the association held for advertising placed through the presidency's agency and not for autonomous agencies, and not for scandals of non-political actors

## Source

Di Tella and Franceschelli, "Government Advertising and Media Coverage of
Corruption Scandals", NBER Working Paper 15402 (October 2009, revised July
2010), published in *AEJ: Applied Economics* 3(4) (2011),
[LIT-tmpa8ani](../literature.d/LIT-tmpa8ani.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmpeytex](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeytex.md) from the working paper.

## What was actually shown

For Clarín, La Nación, Página 12 and Ámbito Financiero, the monthly sum of
the share of each front page given to scandals involving the current
administration (Front Pages) was regressed on the log of that month's
federal advertising in the paper, over 466 newspaper-months. With newspaper
and month fixed effects the coefficient is −3.303 (s.e. 0.682). Adding a
dummy for each newspaper-president pair halves it to −1.652 (0.643). Adding
a linear trend for each pair gives −1.008 (0.589). At the mean of
advertising, one standard deviation (0.26 million 2000 pesos) corresponds to
0.31 fewer front pages a month, 25% of a standard deviation of Front Pages.
Rechecked: 1.652 × [ln(1.49) − ln(1.23)] = 0.317.

Two contrasts could have come out otherwise. Advertising placed by agencies
outside the presidency's agency Télam shows no association (0.115, s.e.
1.454). And front-page coverage of scandals of the police, unions, the
church and piqueteros shows none either (−0.489, s.e. 0.427). Lagged
coverage does not predict advertising (F = 0.96).

## What this does not say

- **Not that advertising bought coverage.** The design removes stable
  paper-president affinity, not shocks within a pair that move both. The
  authors: the findings are "consistent with" newspapers biasing reporting
  in exchange for transfers, and they "do not offer any further
  interpretation". An instrumental-variable estimate appears only in a
  footnote.
- **Not that scandals went unreported.** The timing measure, Hide, shows no
  robust association. Advertising goes with how much space a scandal gets,
  not with whether it is reported.
- **Not robust to every cut.** Without the Kirchner presidency the
  coefficient is −0.531 with a standard error of 1.704.
- **Not about absolute bias.** Coverage is compared within paper and
  across papers. A bias shared by all four would not show.
- **Not the published estimate.** The 2011 article reports 0.23 of a front
  page and 18 percent of a standard deviation.
