# NOTE-1341: Guerreiro et al. — xCOMET

## Contribution

A learned translation metric that predicts, in one model, a sentence-level
quality score and a severity tag (OK, minor, major, critical) for every
token of the translation, under three input modes: translation with
reference only, with source and reference, and with source only (quality
estimation) (§§3–4, pp. 981–983). Its final score mixes the three
regression outputs with an MQM-style score computed from its own predicted
error spans. It is trained by a three-phase curriculum on public WMT data
plus synthetic hallucinations (§4.4, p. 984), released as xCOMET-XL and
xCOMET-XXL, and evaluated against neural metrics and GPT-based evaluators
on WMT22 and WMT23 (§§5–6), on synthetic localized errors and on a
hallucination benchmark (§7), with ablations (§8).

## Key insight

A sentence score and an error-span explanation can be produced by one
encoder and made to agree: "these high correlations with the predicted
scores from each forward pass (ŷSRC, ŷREF, ŷSRC+REF) are obtained despite
no explicit alignment mechanism governing the relationship between the
predictions of the sentence-level and word-level heads. We hypothesize
that it is thus the shared encoder that, during the multi-task training,
aligns the representations between the two tasks" (pp. 986–987). The
reading the record needs is the converse: the agreement between the two
heads is internal coherence, and its size says nothing about how often
either is right.

## Assumptions

- **Supervision is human DA and MQM from WMT 2017–2022**, plus IndicMT,
  DEMETR, MLQE-PE hallucinations and synthetic hallucinations with their
  sentence scores set to 0 (pp. 983–984; Table 1). The test set, WMT22
  News MQM, is excluded from training (p. 983, footnote 5), but its
  annotation protocol, and in part its annotators' conventions, are those
  the model was trained on.
- **The MQM score from spans** is fixed by eqs. (5)–(6):
  e(S) = c_MIN + 5c_MAJ + 10c_CRIT and ŷ_MQM = (25 − e(S))/25, floored at 0.
- **The final score** is ŷ_SL = wᵀ[ŷ_SRC, ŷ_REF, ŷ_SRC+REF, ŷ_MQM] with
  w = [1/9, 1/3, 1/3, 2/9] (eq. 7, p. 983).
- **Severity in evaluation.** Critical errors are converted to major to
  match the test set's guidelines (p. 985, footnote 10).

## Key results

### Agreement with human judgements (§6, pp. 985–987)

- **Segment level, WMT22 MQM** (Table 2a, p. 986): Kendall τ averaged over
  zh-en, en-de, en-ru is 0.421 (XL) and 0.432 (XXL), against METRICX 0.421,
  BLEURT-20 0.365, COMET-22 0.361 and GEMBA-GPT4-DA 0.354. The MQM score
  computed from the predicted spans alone gets 0.402 and 0.395.
- **System level** (Table 2b): pairwise accuracy 0.778 (XL), 0.819 (XXL);
  GEMBA-GPT4-DA 0.825. On WMT23 pooled (Table 3, p. 986), 0.912 and 0.920
  against GEMBA-MQM 0.944, but the best average correlation (0.813, 0.812
  against METRICX-23's 0.808): "Our metrics, at both scales, would win
  the shared task" (p. 986).
- **Error spans** (Table 4, p. 986): character-level F1 averaged 0.269
  (XL) and 0.280 (XXL), against AutoMQM with GPT-4 at 0.262; 0.242 and
  0.255 without a reference.
- **The heads agree** (Table 5, p. 987): Pearson correlation of ŷ_MQM with
  ŷ_SRC, ŷ_REF and ŷ_SRC+REF is 0.73–0.82 per language pair, and with ŷ_SL
  0.90–0.92. The last is partly built in: "The computation of ŷSL,
  contrary to the other scores, makes direct use of ŷMQM" (Table 5
  caption).

### Robustness (§7, pp. 987–989)

- **Localized errors** (SMAUG perturbations of WMT23 synthetic references
  the metric had found error-free, zh-en and he-en). Table 6 (p. 987):
  0.00–17.0% of perturbed translations are predicted error-free, by type,
  size and pair. Predicted spans "overlap with the artificially induced
  perturbations in over 90% of the perturbed samples (98% for XL and
  90.9% for XXL)", but the model also marks spans beyond the perturbation,
  and "When the metric predicts multiple error spans in a sentence, it
  assigns different severity levels to those spans only about 35% of the
  time" (p. 987).
- **The "All" row of that table does not follow from its rows.** Weighted
  by the preprint's per-category counts (its Table 8), the per-type rates
  give 3.76% (zh-en XL), 10.96% (zh-en XXL), 5.53% (he-en XL) and 5.38%
  (he-en XXL), against the printed 2.24, 10.7, 9.81 and 7.00. The he-en XL
  value equals that column's largest entry (Swap NE, 9.81), which no
  average of the column can be unless every example were of that type.
  The preprint's Table 8 also misadds: its zh-en categories sum to 4,562,
  not the printed 4,762. The journal version keeps the table and drops
  the counts.
- **Score sensitivity** (Fig. 4b, p. 988): median decreases of "around 20
  points" for negation and number and named-entity swaps; "In rare cases,
  perturbations may actually lead to an increase in the score.
  Nevertheless, upon closer inspection, for over 90% of cases, the
  increase is smaller than 1 point" (p. 988). (The preprint said
  "decrease" here; the journal corrects it.)
- **Hallucinations** (Table 8, p. 989; de-en benchmark of Guerreiro et al.
  2023, over 3.4k translations): AUROC for ranking hallucinations below
  other translations is 0.865 (XL) and 0.890 (XXL) overall, 0.907 and
  0.964 for fully detached hallucinations, and 0.922 and 0.844 for
  oscillatory ones. Source-only scoring does better overall (0.885,
  0.902), which the authors attribute to "the metric over-relying on the
  reference when it is available" (p. 989). XXL is worse than XL on
  oscillatory hallucinations in both modes, unremarked.

### Ablations (§8, pp. 989–991)

- **Curriculum** (Table 10, p. 990): multi-task training beats single-task
  training at segment level but not at span detection: "The best
  word-level model is obtained by doing Phase II with a word-level only
  objective" (p. 990), with F1 0.285 against the final model's 0.269.
- **Weights** (Table 11, p. 990): including ŷ_MQM raises segment-level τ,
  but "the improvement of our final aggregated score over ŷSRC+REF is not
  substantial" (0.421 against 0.409; p. 991).
- **A typo**: Table 10's last row prints τ = 0.597 for en-de; the same
  model's en-de τ is 0.414 in Table 2a and Table 11, and the row's average,
  0.421, fits 0.414, not 0.597.

## Method

XLM-R XL or XXL encoder; input: a [cls] token, the translation, then the source, the reference or both;
a feed-forward regression head on [cls] and a linear tagger on translation
subwords (Fig. 2, p. 982). Loss per input mode (1 − λ)·MSE + λ·weighted
cross-entropy, summed over the three modes (eqs. 1–4). Phase I DA only
(λ = 0), Phase II MQM with λ = 0.983, Phase III high-quality MQM from
Freitag et al. 2021a with λ = 0.055 (p. 984). At inference the tag
distributions of the three passes are averaged and adjacent error
subwords grouped into spans, each taking its most severe tag (p. 983).

## Concepts

- **MQM** — Multidimensional Quality Metrics: professional annotators mark
  error spans with severity and category (p. 980).
- **DA** — direct assessment: a 0–100 score per translation, from
  non-experts or professionals, z-normalised (pp. 980, 984).
- **Unified evaluation** — one model serving reference-only,
  source-and-reference and source-only modes (§4.2, p. 982).
- **Detached / oscillatory hallucination** — a translation unrelated to the
  source, or one that repeats an n-gram (p. 984).
- **Pairwise accuracy** — the fraction of system pairs a metric orders as
  humans do (p. 985).

## Connections

- Built on COMET (Rei et al. 2020) and the scaled COMETKIWI (Rei et al.
  2023a); its error-span-to-score step follows AUTOMQM (Fernandes et al.
  2023) and INSTRUCTSCORE (Xu et al. 2023); the unified input follows UNITE
  (Wan et al. 2022b) (pp. 980–983).
- The test protocols are WMT's (Freitag et al. 2022, 2023); significance by
  Deutsch et al.'s Perm-Both test (p. 985).
- **[CASE-071](../cases.d/CASE-071.md)** (Kovacs, Deutsch and Freitag, in the record) is the case
  where translations a neural metric selects are rated better by that
  metric and its neighbours and not by human raters. xCOMET is one such
  neighbour; the paper does not test it as a selection criterion, though
  it lists "acting as reward models" among learned metrics' uses (p. 981).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-071](../cases.d/CASE-071.md): consistent; xCOMET does not escape the case's mechanism and
  is not tested against it.** All of xCOMET's evidence is correlation with
  human MQM on held-out segments it did not choose. [CASE-071](../cases.d/CASE-071.md)'s failure
  arises when the metric does the choosing. A metric at τ ≈ 0.43 leaves
  most of the ordering of segment pairs to disagree with experts in some
  part, and nothing here bounds how selection exploits that. The record
  should cite xCOMET as a strong scorer of given translations, not as a
  fidelity criterion for translations optimised against it.
- **[CASE-185](../cases.d/CASE-185.md): a usable baseline, with two qualifications.** The study
  needs a task-independent quality score per route. xCOMET gives one in
  all three modes, and its source-only mode can score a relay hop where no
  reference exists in the pivot language. But its source-only mode is the
  weakest at segment level (Table 11: τ 0.376 against 0.409 with both
  inputs), and its spans' severities are poorly differentiated within a
  sentence (about 35%, p. 987), so span counts are a coarse readout. Its
  hallucination detection is good enough to serve as one of the study's
  negative controls (Table 8).
- **[CLAIM-496](../claims.d/CLAIM-496.md): an instance of coherence mistaken for confirmation.**
  The paper presents the agreement of its score with its own spans
  (Pearson up to 0.92, partly built in by eq. 7) as support for the spans'
  value. That is agreement between two routes through one encoder.
  Against the human annotation the spans reach F1 0.28. The record's
  distinction between path agreement and fidelity applies: the
  explanation is coherent with the score far more than either is correct.
  CLAIM-tmp2um57 states this, and on one scale finds the gap smaller than
  "far".
- **Two THEORYs**: [THEORY-1134](../theory.d/THEORY-1134.md) (agreement with experts against
  agreement between heads) and [THEORY-1151](../theory.d/THEORY-1151.md) (synthetic-error and
  hallucination robustness). Both Proposed when filed; both
  promoted on 2026-10-30, THEORY-1134 narrowed first.
- **Instruction for ML practice: yes, belongs to the anthology.** xCOMET is
  a released evaluation tool, and the paper advises on its use ("under
  computational constraints, one could consider computing a single
  ŷSRC+REF score without the need for three different forward passes and
  error span prediction", p. 991). The LIT keeps `anthology-candidate`.

## Limitations

- **One test domain per year.** WMT22 News for the main tables, WMT23 for
  aggregates; three pairs each, all into or out of English.
- **Synthetic stress tests.** The localized errors are SMAUG perturbations
  applied to English targets only (p. 987); the hallucinations are one
  de-en benchmark. The training data includes synthetic hallucinations of
  the same kinds (p. 984), so the stress tests partly test what was
  trained.
- **The span–score agreement** is presented as interpretability. No user
  study, and part of the agreement is arithmetic (eq. 7).
- **Inconsistent table**: the localized-error "All" row, as above; and a
  typo in Table 10.
- **The authors' own limits**: low-resource pairs and other hallucination
  benchmarks are future work (preprint p. 10; the journal drops the
  sentence); improving differentiation among multiple errors is "an
  interesting avenue for future research" (p. 987).

## Open questions

- Does xCOMET, used to select among candidate translations, show [CASE-071](../cases.d/CASE-071.md)'s
  metric bias? An MBR or reranking experiment with human MQM on the picks
  would settle it.
- How well do xCOMET's spans agree with human spans on the kind of errors
  the record cares about, act and register rather than accuracy? MQM's
  typology has a style category; no result here is broken down by
  category.
- Is the source-only advantage on hallucinations a sign of reference
  over-reliance generally? A test where the reference is paraphrased or
  wrong would say.
