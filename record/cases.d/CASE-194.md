# CASE-194: Three organizations at 50% on one task, output y = c for a fair context c ∈ {−1, +1}, with the same failure from three causes: one shared actuator setting, an observation identical in both contexts, and an authority rule that always selects the process proposing +1; supplying observation, enlarging actuation or revising authority each repairs one and not the others, and the observational one is infeasible only relative to its observation-limited policies

## The construction

The owner's U8 asked for CASEs the record lacks. A8: "This is the proposal I
would prioritize if the goal is to connect the mature frustration account
directly to reflexive agency."

There are two equally frequent contexts, c ∈ {−1, +1}, and the task is to
output y = c. Each of three organizations outputs y = +1 every time, and
so scores 50%.

- **S, structural.** "Its actuator has only one shared output setting,
  which must be used in both contexts. Even if the context is revealed, it
  cannot independently produce both required outputs as one
  context-dependent policy."
- **E, observational.** "It can produce either output, but its observation
  is identical in both contexts."
- **G, governance.** "It already has the required observations and
  actions. Its authority-allocation mechanism nevertheless always selects
  the process proposing +1."

## Results

The organizations are stipulated, so the results follow from the
definitions.

| intervention | S | E | G |
|---|---|---|---|
| reveal the context | still 50% | 100% | already has it |
| enlarge actuation | 100%, given the context | unnecessary | unnecessary |
| revise the authority rule | no effect | no effect | 100% |

A8: "Supplying additional observation does not overcome S's physical
restriction. Enlarging the actuator's capabilities is unnecessary for E,
which already has them. And G needs neither: its failure comes from the use
of capabilities it already possesses."

**E's feasibility depends on the policy class.** A8: "E is also infeasible
when feasibility is defined relative to its currently implementable
observation-limited policies. It is feasible at the plant level but not at
the policy level." This is [CLAIM-535](../claims.d/CLAIM-535.md). Every circumstance has an acceptable
action, but no policy that acts on E's observation is acceptable in both,
because the two circumstances cannot be told apart and share no acceptable
action. So "the experiment must declare the relevant admissible-policy
class, rather than classify the arms by labels alone."

## What it shows and does not

**It shows that equal performance does not locate a limitation.** Here
three of the record's distinctions are set in one task at equal score:

- structural frustration, [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md) and [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md);
- observation-relative infeasibility, [CLAIM-535](../claims.d/CLAIM-535.md);
- governance frustration as against structural frustration, [CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md).

Each organization calls for a different repair. [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)'s three
achievements, legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability, sit
behind them, but they do not map one to one onto S, E and G.

**It is not [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md)'s triple.** A8 said "The individual distinctions
exist in [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md), [CLAIM-357](../claims.d/CLAIM-357.md), and several governance CASEs." [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md)'s
three obstructions are feasibility, aggregation (no coherent social
choice) and epistemic *coordination* (the coordinated-attack result). E's
limitation is observation, not coordination over an unreliable channel.
G's is an authority rule that selects badly, not a failure of aggregation
to yield a choice. S matches [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md)'s feasibility, and that is the
one part of [CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md) this case illustrates.

**Its method is matched behaviour, then intervention.** This is the pattern
of [CASE-076](CASE-076.md), [CASE-125](CASE-125.md), [CASE-126](CASE-126.md) and [CASE-129](CASE-129.md). It is also the first two
evidence grades of [CLAIM-427](../claims.d/CLAIM-427.md), now superseded by [ARG-025](../arguments.d/ARG-025.md).

**It does not show a developing diagnostic capacity.** A8 proposed an
extension: a meta-learner that "can experimentally ask whether better
sensing, expanded actuation, or revised authority removes the failures".
Its achievement would be "a capacity to distinguish different limitations
on effective regulation and construct the corresponding corrective
mechanisms". That is a proposed study with no design yet. It is left for
a later filing, as "a more focused successor to the broad experimental
architecture of [CASE-179](CASE-179.md)".

## Note of 2026-11-05: the constraint-architecture readings

Three readings from the pasted synthesis on constraint architectures qualify the case's actuator cause. Hopkins's doctoral thesis ([NOTE-1596](../notes.d/NOTE-1596.md), skimmed) treats actuation as a constraint of its own: an actuation space outside the 26 types "cannot be actuated with linear actuators alone", whatever the freedom space. Dollar and Howe ([NOTE-1575](../notes.d/NOTE-1575.md)) show one actuator driving eight joints without failing, since a compliant, adaptive transmission lets contact fit the shared command to each object; passive adaptation is a repair the case does not list, and it holds for power grasps only, the hand having no pinch grip. Liu, Zhang and Fang ([NOTE-1558](../notes.d/NOTE-1558.md)) find that sharing an actuator loses little when the required angles are alike and fails when they are not. So whether a shared setting fails depends on how alike the demands on the coupled parts are. All three are mechanical, with the designer outside the system. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the resource-bounded realizability readings

The pasted synthesis on resource-bounded realizability reads this case as observational equivalence that intervention breaks: "Two systems may be observationally equivalent under a given class of tests while being computationally or structurally inequivalent." A adds a layer, "𝔒 ⟼ {distinctions detectable at budget b}", and concludes that "Resource bounds govern both agency and legibility" and that understanding an object can be modeled as "a resource-bounded process of architectural identification". The record already makes legibility relative to the observer's resources ([TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)), and [QUESTION-031](../questions.d/QUESTION-031.md) asks when a family of probes separates realizations; A's budget index is a parameter on those, not a new distinction. [CLAIM-575](../claims.d/CLAIM-575.md), from the complexity readings, adds that over families of reconfiguration problems telling which obstruction blocks a transition can itself be computationally hard, so identification can carry its own cost. Applying that to this case is the filer's connection: the result concerns families of problems, not three fixed organizations. No status changes.
