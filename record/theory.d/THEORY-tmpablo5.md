# THEORY-tmpablo5: In Kemp, Goodman and Tenenbaum's account, people learn to learn causal models by inferring a causal schema, a partition of objects into categories with category-level causal powers, characteristic features and between-category interactions, from contingency and feature data, and the schema lets a causal model for a new object be learned from one trial or from its features alone while still being overruled when the object proves unlike any category

## Source

Kemp, Goodman and Tenenbaum (2010), [LIT-tmp6aw6y](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6aw6y.md); read in [NOTE-tmpvyejl](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpvyejl.md).
Page numbers are the Early View PDF's.

## What was actually shown

- **The model.** Members of a category do not share one causal model; the
  authors "assume instead that members of the same category play similar
  causal roles" (p. 12). Hyperparameters "were not tuned to fit our
  experimental results" (p. 56).
- **One-shot learning** (Exp. 1, 24 adults): training on different schemata
  changes inference from a single trial, except that in one condition the
  human curve "does not match the bimodal curve predicted by the model"
  (p. 19).
- **Overruling** (Exp. 2, 16 adults): a block unlike all others is learned
  faster when the training blocks never activated, as predicted (pp. 22–23).
- **Zero-shot** (Exp. 3, 24 adults): features alone guide inference about
  a new block, and causal data change how blocks are sorted (p. 30).
- **Interactions only** (Exp. 4, 32 adults): categories found from pairwise
  activations alone support one-shot inference; the model is overconfident
  relative to the human means (pp. 37–38).
- **Against baselines** (p. 24): "Overall, neither baseline model can
  account for our results", the baselines being a bottom-up model and an
  exemplar model, compared qualitatively.

## What this does not say

- That it beats rival causal-learning theories: none was fitted, and there
  are no fit statistics.
- That children learn this way: all the experiments use adults, and the
  perceptual-to-causal shift is a simulation of "a simple artificial
  scenario" (pp. 43–44).
- That the schema's form is learned: "the existence of the hierarchy in
  Fig. 1A is known in advance" (p. 7).
- That categories nest or overlap: they partition, and "Our model does not
  incorporate the notion of a causal hierarchy" (p. 49).
- Anything outside one blocks-and-machine paradigm.
