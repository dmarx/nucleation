# LIT-866: Hyperbolic Entailment Cones for Learning Hierarchical Embeddings

Octavian-Eugen Ganea, Gary Bécigneul and Thomas Hofmann (2018),
*Proceedings of the 35th International Conference on Machine Learning*
(ICML 2018), PMLR 80:1646–1655 — [ARXIV-1804.01882](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01882)

## Key takeaways

- **A partial order as nested regions.** Each concept is a point x of the
  Poincaré ball Dⁿ. Its entailment cone S_x is the exponential map of
  the tangent vectors at x within angle ψ(x) of the outward ray from the
  origin through x. The relation "v is in S_u" is the model's "u entails
  v" (v is a subconcept of u). If cones nest, so that v in S_u implies
  S_v ⊆ S_u, the relation is transitive. Points in a cone are farther
  from the origin than its apex, which makes it antisymmetric. That
  second step is my derivation; the paper states only that the cones
  "induce a partial ordering". It generalises Vendrov et al.'s order
  embeddings, where x is more general than every y with yᵢ ≥ xᵢ for all
  i.
- **What nesting forces** (§3, proofs in Appendices E–F). Ask for cones
  that are symmetric about the outward ray, have an aperture depending
  only on the apex's radius, and vary continuously. Then transitivity
  forces ψ ≤ π/2: a cone covers at most half the ball (Lemma 2). It also
  forces h(r) = r sin ψ(r)/(1 − r²) to be non-increasing (Theorem 3).
  Since h → 0 as r → 0, no non-trivial cone can be defined near the
  origin. The cone family is therefore restricted to ‖x‖ ≥ ε, with no
  top element. Keeping h at its largest value K gives the widest
  apertures, sin ψ(x) = K(1 − ‖x‖²)/‖x‖, with K ≤ 2ε/(1 − ε²) (Eqs.
  24–26). Cones narrow toward the boundary, where specific concepts sit.
- **The closed form.** For this ψ, transitivity holds (Theorem 4), but
  the proof is given only as "similar to that of Thm. 3", and there is
  no appendix for it. Membership is the angle test
  Ξ(x, y) = π − ∠Oxy ≤ ψ(x), with Ξ in closed form (Eq. 28, Theorem 5),
  which I checked numerically against the hyperbolic law of cosines. The
  Euclidean analogue has sin ψ = K/‖x‖ and its own Ξ (Eq. 31). The paper
  also derives closed-form unit-speed geodesics and the exponential map
  of the Poincaré ball (Theorem 1, Corollary 1.1). The experiments do not
  use that exact map, because it brought no significant gain over Nickel
  and Kiela's retraction.
- **Several parents are representable; a lattice is not.** Cones of
  incomparable points can overlap, so a point in both lies below two
  apexes that neither entails the other. A DAG with shared descendants
  is therefore expressible, and the method is stated for any DAG. But
  the intersection of two cones is not in general a cone, so the order on
  the ball has no meets or joins. Nothing is proved about which posets
  embed, in what dimension, or with what margin. All of this is my
  analysis; the paper does not discuss it.
- **The evidence is one link-prediction experiment** (§5, Table 1). The
  data is the transitive closure of the WordNet noun hierarchy, with the
  root removed: 82,114 nodes and 661,127 edges. The training set always
  holds the transitive reduction, plus 0%, 10%, 25% or 50% of the other
  closure edges. Test F1 is on held-out closure edges against ten
  corrupted negatives each, at dimensions 5 and 10. Hyperbolic cones
  score 80.1–92.8 (dimension 5) and 85.9–94.4 (dimension 10) from 10% up,
  ahead of every baseline. At 0% every method scores 27–43, and order
  embeddings lead. So a transitive geometry trained on the reduction
  alone did not reconstruct the closure. Euclidean cones initialised
  from Poincaré embeddings perform "on par with" hyperbolic cones (stated
  without numbers). All results are single runs with no variance, and
  there is no full-reconstruction experiment.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-09 at the owner's request. The owner added it to the
batch of six works on hierarchy and hyperbolic geometry, asked for right
after the record opened [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md), once the other six had been read.
That question asks whether attributes that imply or exclude each other in
co-occurrence still give linear attribute directions, and a concept
lattice that is not Boolean. It builds on [THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md) and [LIT-863](LIT-863.md). The six
are Cagnetta et al.'s random hierarchy model ([LIT-877](LIT-877.md)), Krioukov et
al. 2010 ([LIT-865](LIT-865.md)), Sala et al. 2018 ([LIT-874](LIT-874.md)), Lin et al. 2023
([LIT-870](LIT-870.md)), Zhang et al. 2023 ([LIT-876](LIT-876.md)) and Yang et al. 2023
([LIT-871](LIT-871.md)). It was read on its own merits ([NOTE-671](../notes.d/NOTE-671.md)), and the
reading produces [THEORY-189](../theory.d/THEORY-189.md).

Of the batch, it comes nearest the Lattice Representation Hypothesis
([LIT-267](LIT-267.md)), whose picture is implication as containment of regions. Sala et al.
represent a tree by distances. This paper represents a partial order with
multiple parents by asymmetric containment of cones. It does not answer
[QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md). It has no co-occurrence statistics and no attributes: each
concept is a free point trained on WordNet hypernym edges. Its cones are
not half-spaces, and no direction stands for an attribute. Its Euclidean
baseline, order embeddings, is the half-space picture: concepts are
conjunctions of coordinate thresholds. That baseline is the one the cones
improve on once closure edges are in training. For [CLAIM-119](../claims.d/CLAIM-119.md), it shows
only that a non-tree partial order has a natural home in hyperbolic space.
Whether a concept lattice embeds there, with its meets, is not addressed.
