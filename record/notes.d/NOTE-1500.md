# NOTE-1500: Ryan et al. — We know this much is (meta-analytically) true

## Contribution

It collects, in one place, the 60 meta-analyses that test hypotheses of
self-determination theory (SDT), sorts them by the six mini-theories and
by applied domain (Tables 1 and 2), converts their effects to r, and says
which propositions have meta-analytic support and which have none. It is a
map of the evidence, not a new analysis: no effect is re-pooled, and no
included meta-analysis is re-coded or graded for risk of bias.

## Key insight

The audit's own map is uneven, and it says so. Cognitive evaluation theory
(CET), organismic integration theory (OIT) and basic psychological needs
theory (BPNT) "have received strong meta-analytic support", while "support
is much thinner where COT and GCT are concerned", and relationships
motivation theory (RMT) "has no entry in its column within Table 1"
(Summary and Conclusions; Method). Within the supported parts, the support
is mostly for associations: "The most striking feature of these
meta-analyses, when examined collectively, is their typical (though not
invariant) reliance on cross-sectional datasets" (Summary and Conclusions).

## Assumptions

- **Inclusion.** A meta-analysis or systematic review, completed, with SDT
  variables, testing SDT hypotheses, in English (Method, Study Selection).
  Records came first "from the personal databases of the paper's authors"
  and the Center for SDT website, then from five databases searched in
  February and July 2021. Two reviewers screened; 33 full texts were
  excluded (Table S3, not read); 60 were kept, 58 of them published.
- **No re-analysis.** Effects were extracted and converted to r (d to r
  with a = 4, after Borenstein et al.); k, N, I², τ² and Q are listed in
  Table S1 where reported (Data Collection).
- **"This review of meta-analyses was not preregistered"** (Transparency
  and Openness).
- **Effect-size benchmarks.** It reads effects against Gignac and
  Szodorai's percentiles (r = .11, .19, .29) and Funder and Ozer's (r = .10
  small, .20 medium, .30 large), not Cohen's (Why Meta-Analyses?).
- **Authorship.** The first author is SDT's co-founder; several included
  meta-analyses are by the authors (asterisked references include Donald
  et al. 2020, 2021; Bradshaw et al. 2021, 2022; Duineveld 2018; Gillison
  et al. 2019, with Ryan). The review does not discuss this.

## Key results

All r; k = studies or samples as the review gives them. Sections are the
manuscript's.

### CET

- **Choice** raises intrinsic motivation, r = .15 (Patall et al. 2008; 41
  studies, 46 samples); "instructionally irrelevant" choices had the
  largest effects, which "CET does not offer a more specific explanation
  for".
- **Negative vs positive feedback**, r = −.18 (Fong et al. 2019; 78
  studies). Negative vs no or neutral feedback: no significant difference,
  heterogeneous; in the 15 published studies negative feedback raised
  intrinsic motivation, in the 30 unpublished it lowered it.
- **Rewards** (Deci et al. 1999, re-analysing Eisenberger and Cameron's
  data): all rewards −.12; verbal .16; tangible −.17; unexpected tangible
  .00; expected −.18; task-noncontingent −.07 (n.s.); engagement-contingent
  −.20; completion-contingent −.18; performance-contingent −.14.
- **Intrinsic motivation and performance** (Cerasoli et al. 2014; k = 183),
  r = .21; with incentives directly tied to performance .21, indirectly
  .34. Unique prediction of quality: intrinsic β = .35, incentives .06; of
  quantity: incentives .33, intrinsic .24.

### OIT

- **The continuum.** Chatzisarantis et al. (2003; 21 articles, sport)
  found a simplex. Howard et al. (2017; 486 samples, N > 200,000)
  "largely supported a continuum-like structure", but "due in part to both
  too few studies measuring integration and its high correlation with
  identified regulation, results did not support the differentiation of
  integrated regulation from identified regulation", nor Vallerand's three
  intrinsic subtypes (Testing the Simplex Model). Van den Broeck et al.
  (2021; 124 work samples): integrated regulation "was rarely measured …
  and where it was measured it did not show differentiation from
  identified motivation" (Work and Organizations).
- **Graded relations with support.** Lochbaum and Jean-Noel (2016):
  autonomy-supportive PE teaching with intrinsic .54, identified .50,
  introjected .20, external −.15, amotivation −.19. Mossman et al. (2022;
  131 samples): coach autonomy support with integrated .37, intrinsic .32,
  identified .31, introjected .13, external .00, amotivation −.16.
- **Rationale provision** (Steingut et al. 2017; 23 experiments): task
  value .16, perceived autonomy .20, engagement .10, performance .08, and
  perceived competence −.10.
- **Outcomes** (Howard et al. 2021; 344 samples, N = 223,209, education):
  intrinsic and identified with performance and well-being; introjected
  weakly with persistence and negatively with well-being; amotivation
  worst. Osbaldiston (2005, unpublished) found OIT categories did not
  predict pro-environmental outcomes.
- **Prosociality** (Donald et al. 2021; 138 studies): autonomy with
  prosocial outcomes .28, control with antisocial .16; the longitudinal
  effect of control "non-different from zero"; one intervention study.

### COT

- Hagger and Hamilton (2021; 69 studies, 83 samples): the autonomy
  orientation correlates most with intrinsic and identified regulation,
  the control orientation with external and introjected; correlations with
  outcomes "small-to-medium". Impersonal–control r = .27, called "large".
- Murphy and Steel (2021; 30 priming experiments, 33 effects): evidential
  value survives removal of the largest effects.
- The section opens "only two meta-analyses"; its summary says "Only one
  meta-analysis has thus far examined this proposition".

### BPNT

- Need satisfaction with positive affect .39 to .45, with negative affect
  −.30 to −.33 (Stanley et al. 2021; 16 studies), moderated by gender,
  sample and instrument.
- Autonomy with subjective well-being .46, not moderated by East Asian vs
  Western culture (Yu et al. 2018; 36 samples, about 13,000).
- Slemp et al. (2018; 83 samples, N = 32,870): leader autonomy support
  with autonomy .46, competence .34, relatedness .38 satisfaction; not
  moderated by country.
- **"The proposition that frustration of these needs is associated with
  ill-being has yet to be meta-analytically investigated"** (BPNT
  Summary). Van den Broeck et al. (2016): needs predicted positive outcomes
  more robustly than negative ones, and its authors "questioned if they
  should be considered truly basic needs".

### GCT

- Bradshaw et al. (2022; 92 studies, 70,110 people, 27 countries):
  intrinsic aspirations with well-being .24, ill-being −.11; extrinsic
  aspirations with well-being .02 overall. Split by scoring: as a simple
  score .07 with well-being; as a relative-centrality score −.22 with
  well-being and .23 with ill-being. Not moderated by gender, age, region
  or SES.
- Dittmar et al. (2014; 259 samples): materialism with life satisfaction
  −.13, positive affect −.23, compulsive buying −.44; need satisfaction
  mediates. "the mediational effect of need satisfactions has yet to be
  meta-analytically established for intrinsic aspirations".

### RMT and parenting

- No meta-analysis of RMT. Koehn and Kerns (2018; 10 studies, not
  SDT-framed): parental autonomy support with child attachment security
  .18; harsh control −.20. Bradshaw et al. (2021, under review; 211
  reports): parental autonomy support with child well-being .28, ill-being
  −.22; control with ill-being .19, well-being −.12. Duineveld et al.
  (2018): autonomy support and psychological control r = −.50 overall,
  .10 in early childhood, −.72 in emerging adulthood.

### Interventions

- SDT-based health interventions on behaviour, r = .11 (Sheeran et al.
  2020; 65 tests); autonomy-support training effective, r = .30 (Su and
  Reeve 2011); need-support and autonomous motivation changes, not
  controlled motivation, track behaviour change (Ntoumanis et al. 2021).

## Concepts

- **Meta-review** — here, a narrative synthesis of published
  meta-analyses with their effects tabulated, not a second-order pooling.
- **Relative centrality score** — an aspiration's importance minus, or
  controlling for, the mean importance of all aspirations; the "simple
  score" is the raw mean, which the authors read as carrying "a 'general
  striving' component" (GCT).
- **Dual-process model** — need support does more for well-being than
  against ill-being, and control more for ill-being than against
  well-being (Parenting).
- **Six mini-theories** — CET, OIT, COT, BPNT, GCT, RMT, each with formal
  propositions listed in Table S2 (not read).

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md): confirms what A2 said of Howard et al. 2017, and adds a
  second source.** The review reports that Howard et al. did not
  differentiate integrated from identified regulation, partly because too
  few studies measured integration, and that Van den Broeck et al. (2021)
  found the same at work. So [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)'s first promote_when clause, a
  measure of integration separable from identification, is unmet in the
  meta-analytic literature as of 2021. This is second-hand; Howard et al.
  ([LIT-1996](../literature.d/LIT-1996.md)) is not read. Mossman et al.'s r = .37 for integrated
  regulation with coach autonomy support is not evidence of separability,
  since it is no different in kind from identified (.31).
- **[THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md): the review says the needed meta-analysis does not exist.**
  "The proposition that frustration of these needs is associated with
  ill-being has yet to be meta-analytically investigated" (BPNT Summary).
  [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) is about frustration as a transdiagnostic process; the
  review's satisfaction–well-being correlations do not bear on its
  promote_when, which asks for temporal precedence measured without
  shared self-report content.
- **[QUESTION-369](../questions.d/QUESTION-369.md): the review's evidence is of the kind the question
  says cannot settle it.** Needs are supported by correlations of need
  satisfaction with well-being and affect (Stanley et al.; Tang et al.;
  Yu et al.). Van den Broeck et al.'s doubt that needs predicting positive
  more than negative outcomes are "truly basic" is the nearest the review
  comes to the question, and the authors answer it with frustration
  measures, which have no meta-analysis.
- **[THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md) and [THEORY-154](../theory.d/THEORY-154.md).** The review states CET's third
  proposition as a mediation claim, that "experiences of autonomy versus
  control, and of competence versus lack of it, mediate the relations
  between external events and their … effects", and reports no
  meta-analytic test of that mediation. [THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md)'s promote_when stands
  unmet. The review does not engage [LIT-562](../literature.d/LIT-562.md).
- **[THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md).** Steingut et al.'s rationale meta-analysis (23
  experiments) is the nearest meta-analytic evidence to Deci et al. 1994
  ([LIT-1973](../literature.d/LIT-1973.md)): a rationale raises perceived autonomy (.20) and task
  value (.16), and is "most effective" when delivered autonomously. It
  measures present endorsement, not the history-versus-structure contrast
  [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)'s promote_when asks for.
- **RMT, the registration's target for [LIT-1979](../literature.d/LIT-1979.md) and [LIT-1971](../literature.d/LIT-1971.md).**
  The review confirms A1's table: no RMT meta-analysis exists.
- **[QUESTION-066](../questions.d/QUESTION-066.md).** No meta-analysis in the review tests persistence after
  support is removed with valuation held fixed; Howard et al. 2021's
  persistence outcomes are cross-sectional.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **Not independent.** The lead author founded the theory; several
  included meta-analyses are the authors' own. The registration's "the
  nearest thing to an audit" holds only as a map of the evidence; nothing
  in the review grades the quality of the meta-analyses or their primary
  studies.
- **Not preregistered, narrative, English only**, and seeded from the
  authors' own databases.
- **Its own counts disagree.** COT has "only two meta-analyses" in one
  paragraph and "only one" in its summary; GCT has "only one primary
  meta-analysis" in the conclusions after two (Bradshaw et al. 2022,
  Dittmar et al. 2014) were reviewed.
- **A slip in the GCT section.** "When extrinsic aspirations were
  calculated as a simple score their link to well-being was positive but
  very small (r = .07)" is repeated where the sentence is about ill-being.
  The accepted manuscript only; the published text was not checked.
- **"Large" for r = .27** between the impersonal and control orientations,
  against its own benchmark (Funder and Ozer's .30 is large).
- **Heterogeneity is reported, not explained**: "accounts of
  heterogeneity have been far from comprehensive" (Heterogeneity).
- The supplement (Table S1's k, N, I² per effect) was not read, so no
  effect above was checked against its source meta-analysis.

## Open questions

- Is integrated regulation measurable apart from identified regulation at
  all, or is it a theoretical category with no indicator?
- What would a meta-analysis of need frustration and ill-being, with
  frustration measured from contexts rather than self-report, find?
- Would an independent risk-of-bias assessment of the 60, especially of
  the correlational OIT and BPNT literature, change the "meta-analytically
  true" list?
