**Going wrong.** The owner, at 10:36: "this keeps happening repeatedly. it is an expensive mistake and I'd like it if we didn't keep making it." Three things went wrong.

- **The sweep lost its results.** The assistant's own background sweep of the 184 Unfetched works wrote its results file only at the end. It ran under the default background time limit of 30 minutes, and was stopped at 162. Its pace (18 works in 12 minutes) had said two hours. The assistant read the pace, noted "about two hours", and changed nothing.
- **The lead workers were briefed to commit at the end.** Their brief, kept in the scratchpad, read "if you hit an API usage limit, commit and push what you have". A hard stop leaves no turn for that.
- **The briefs and the triage were scratchpad files**, so they would have gone with the container.

**Wrong theory, and how it was found out.** The theory was that [ADR-108](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-108.md) had dealt with this. It had dealt with it for readers only. The loss showed where the rule stopped: everything that was not a reader. The 04:15 note had said, "When parallel workers may be cut off, commit in progress." Nobody put that into a brief, so the same lesson was drawn twice in six hours.

**What to change.** Two alternative decisions are filed: [ADR-tmpvqx5l](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-tmpvqx5l.md), which supersedes [ADR-108](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-108.md) with one general rule, and [ADR-tmpyb409](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-tmpyb409.md), which adds a sibling to it. Both carry the same first application. The lead-worker brief now lives in the repository as the run-lead skill, with pushes after every step, a PROGRESS file, per-item results and a time budget. The running sweep is checkpointed into its branch every ten minutes, and the three workers then running were told to push at once. A lesson in a devlog note should become a step in a brief the same day, or it will be learned again.
