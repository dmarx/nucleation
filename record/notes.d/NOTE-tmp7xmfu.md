# NOTE-tmp7xmfu: Watts, Duncan and Quan — Revisiting the Marshmallow Test

## Contribution

A conceptual replication of the marshmallow test's longitudinal
predictions in a larger, more diverse sample, with controls for family
background, home environment and concurrent cognitive and behavioral
measures, showing the prediction much weaker and largely explained by
those controls.

## Key insight

What a preschooler's waiting predicts may be mostly what the waiting is a
marker of: the child's background and broader capacities.

## Assumptions

- The controls capture confounds, not mediators.
- The 7-minute cap does not drive the conclusions (checked for the
  lower-SES sample only).
- The study's task (bell, chosen treat, 7-minute cap) measures Mischel's
  construct.

## Key results

- **Abstract**: "this bivariate correlation was only half the size of
  those reported in the original studies, and was reduced by two-thirds in
  the presence of controls for family background, early cognitive ability,
  and the home environment."
- **Abstract**: "Most of the variation in adolescent achievement came from
  being able to wait at least 20 seconds."
- **Results**: achievement "fell substantially when controls for earlier
  characteristics were added (β= 0.08, SE= 0.03, p = .016) and became
  non-significant when 54-months controls were added (β= 0.05, SE= 0.03,
  p = .140)".
- **Results**: "we were surprised to find virtually no relationship – even
  in the absence of controls – between gratification delay and the
  composite score of mother-reported internalizing and externalizing".
- **Discussion**: "These results suggest that the Marshmallow Test may
  capture something rather distinct from self-control."; "an intervention
  that altered a child's ability to delay, but failed to change more
  general cognitive and behavioral capacities, would likely have limited
  effects on later outcomes."
- **Limitation**: "variation in our age-54-months gratification-delay
  measure was not exogenous".

## Concepts

Conceptual replication; gratification delay; threshold effect at 20
seconds; controls for concurrent capacities; hypothetical-intervention
framing.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-tmp07hx9](../cases.d/CASE-tmp07hx9.md)**. Confirms what the case says it cannot show, "That
  waiting predicts later life": the prediction is modest and largely
  explained by background. The paper does not test the reliability
  reading; it cites Kidd et al. only among factors affecting delay.
- **[CLAIM-tmpt091d](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt091d.md)**. Indirect: if wait-time is largely a marker of
  background, an environment's reliability is one consistent explanation,
  but trust is not measured here. Not filed as a relation.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Faithful. A51: the association "diminished considerably after accounting
for family background, early cognitive ability, and the home
environment." Two qualifications A does not carry: the headline figures
are for children of mothers without a degree, and there was no bivariate
association with behavior at all.

## Limitations

Observational; not nationally representative; a 7-minute ceiling most
higher-SES children reached; behavior reported by mothers; outcomes at 15
only. The authors flag all of these.
