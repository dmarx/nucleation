# TERM-tmpx0l4b: frame, as the delivery frame: interpretive, enunciative and performative

## Definition

A12: the frame "from which the translated work is **communicated to its
audience**", covering "*who is saying it, to whom, from what position, in what
social context, and with what attitude*". Three interdependent dimensions:

1. **interpretive frame**: what the translator takes the original to be about;
2. **enunciative frame**: who speaks, to whom, from what position and attitude;
3. **performative frame**: what the utterance is doing.

"The delivery frame isn't merely a stylistic wrapper around meaning. It's
partly constitutive of meaning."

## What became of it

At U8 the vocabulary moved to Austin and Goffman: illocutionary force for the
performative frame, footing and stance for the enunciative one (A14). The
three-way typology was not used again, and the split between interpreting the
source and delivering the rendering has no named place in the manuscript. At
A18 "frame" became a coordinate system ([TERM-tmpdb2i3](TERM-tmpdb2i3.md)).
