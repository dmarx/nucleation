# CLAIM-053: The manuscript argues only that preserving the proposition is insufficient; that it is also unnecessary rests on an argument the exchange gave and the manuscript leaves out

## The claim

A107 §1: "preserving a proposition is not sufficient to preserve a communicative
act." Manuscript §1: "The three utterances share a loose situation model while
differing in footing and illocutionary force. Preserving the proposition is
therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for preserving the communicative
event." Three renderings with a shared situation model and different acts show
that sharing the proposition is not sufficient. They do not show that a rendering
can keep the act while changing the proposition.

## Where the missing half is

In the exchange, not the manuscript. A12: "you can alter many of the propositions
while preserving the speaker's attitude, social position, and communicative
intention—and the result may feel more faithful" ([CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md)). A24's compensation
hypothesis ([CLAIM-091](CLAIM-091.md)) gives the mechanism. The manuscript's Abstract ("change almost
every word while preserving the interpersonal relation") is about words, not
propositions. Granted, as an undermining of [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md) in its manuscript form: the claim
may be true, but the manuscript does not support "necessary".
