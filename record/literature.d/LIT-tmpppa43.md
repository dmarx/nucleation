# LIT-tmpppa43: Teleological Theories of Mental Content

Karen Neander and Peter Schulte (2004; substantive revision 2022),
*Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 18 June 2004,
substantive revision 26 May 2022 —
<https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/content-teleological/>

## Key takeaways

- **The shared idea.** Teleosemantic theories derive the norms of
  content (true or false, accurate or not) from functional norms, which
  allow malfunction and are "regarded as descriptive rather than
  prescriptive" (§1). Most take functions to be etiological: "an
  item's function is determined by its history of selection or by past
  selection of things of that type" (§2).
- **Selection is wider than evolution.** Learning, conditioning, meme
  selection and, on Garson's loosening, neural selection by differential
  retention are counted as selection (§2, §4.4). Shea counts a
  system's past contributions to its own survival as stabilizing a
  function (§3.5).
- **Who fixes content.** Millikan's theory is read as consumer-based:
  content is what a representation must map onto for its consumer to
  function in the Normal way (§3.2). Neander's is producer-based: content
  is what the producing system was selected to respond to (§3.4). On the
  frog they give "frog food" and "small, dark, moving thing".
- **Four objections.** Functional indeterminacy, Swampman (a replica
  with no history has no functions and so no content), liberalism (on
  Millikan's theory "all living beings are representational systems")
  and sophisticated concepts (§4).

## Standing in the record

Registered and read for LEAD-018, beside Millikan's own statement. It is
the record's survey of the selection-based route to a norm of content,
the route CLAIM-650 names as "selection history" and the record had not
read. Its §2 also places the systemic and goal-contribution theories of
function, and its §4.2 reports the Swampman objection that Bickhard's
account (THEORY-220) turns against etiology.
