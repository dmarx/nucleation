# THEORY-1277: Persons and organizations can be described not as successive stages, with the collective built up from individuals, but as coexisting levels of ordering through which one action propagates, each preserving its components; on that description the problem of scaling up from individual to organizational action does not arise

## Source

Bencherki & Iliadis (2021), [LIT-1922](../literature.d/LIT-1922.md), proposition 2 and manuscript pp.
14–17, read in [NOTE-1483](../notes.d/NOTE-1483.md). Bencherki (2017), [LIT-1901](../literature.d/LIT-1901.md), p. 780,
read in [NOTE-1482](../notes.d/NOTE-1482.md).

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested.

**Bencherki & Iliadis** argue, after Simondon, that the scaling-up
problem in the communicative constitution of organization "is not a
matter of sequential physical steps (individual people first and then a
collective) nor is it a synthesis". Levels of informational abstraction
coexist, "in the same way that the DNA of a single cell exists and is as
real as the human individual to whom it belongs" (ms. pp. 14–15). One
action can reorder several levels at once: a synaptic chemical deficit
changes a person's behaviour and an organization's human-resources work
(ms. p. 17). Proposition 2 reads: "An employee is not prior to the
organization, nor is the organization an abstraction of its employees;
they each exist fully and simultaneously."

**Bencherki (2017)**: "I am not, as a human being, anterior to society;
at any moment, I am contemporaneous to each of my cells and to the whole
universe" (p. 780).

The illustration is a meeting excerpt in which a subcommittee's
ordering spreads to a council through sheets, a calendar and a website.

**The parent paper** ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md)) draws the conclusion. "individuation
takes place concurrently at different 'levels,' rather than scaling up
from the individual to the collective"; the scaling question "vanishes
the moment communication is viewed eventfully" (p. 1364). It cites
Bencherki & Iliadis for treating cross-level action as "a processual
continuum" (p. 1351).

## What this does not say

- **That there is no aggregation.** It offers a description on which
  aggregation is not the question. Whether some organizational outcomes
  are better explained as aggregates (votes, market totals) is not
  addressed.
- **What fixes a level.** Domains are distinguished "for practical
  purposes" by their internal ordering. Whose purposes, and how two
  analysts would agree, is not said. [CLAIM-405](../claims.d/CLAIM-405.md) holds that an
  individuation is well defined only once its criterion is fixed.
- **That coexistence is observed.** The cell-to-organization example is a
  thought experiment. The meeting excerpt shows one ordering spreading,
  not two levels changing at once.
