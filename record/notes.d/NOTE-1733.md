# NOTE-1733: Greely (2026) — Alabama's LePage decision, the future of ex vivo embryos, and more

## Contribution

A detailed legal and political anatomy of LePage that separates the holding (a remedy question under
a peculiar damages regime) from the personhood debate it was read as advancing.

## Key results

- Introduction: LePage held "frozen embryos are ‘unborn children’ and so covered by Alabama’s Wrongful
  Death statute"; the article "criticizes the new legislation as giving IVF clinics far more
  protection than they deserve."
- I.B / II: "Alabama, apparently alone among the states, does not allow any compensatory damages for a
  person’s death but allows punitive damages routinely in wrongful death cases"; "This decision, in
  itself, only extended one kind of lawsuit, a wrongful death action, to include frozen embryos."
- II: "Louisiana has barred the destruction of ‘viable’ embryos, even if frozen, since 1986"; "Even an
  Alabama ban on destroying embryos, which the LePage decision did not create, should not lead to an
  end of IVF in Alabama."
- III.A: of the press coverage, "Not one emphasized that the decision held only that frozen embryos
  were ‘minor children’ for purposes of the Wrongful Death Act and not, in that opinion at least, for
  any other purpose or in any other context."
- III.B-C: "Nineteen days, including three weekends and a holiday, so 12 business days."; the Act's
  operative clause: "no action, suit, or criminal prosecution for the damage to or death of an embryo
  shall be brought or maintained against any individual or entity when providing or receiving
  services related to in vitro fertilization".
- IV: "I conclude that the Alabama Supreme Court decision was, as written, at least in part a
  provocation; that the reactions it provoked were at least in part overreactions"; on remedies for
  negligently destroyed embryos, "there certainly seems to be no good reason to deny it just to this
  narrow set of medical cases"; "I suspect that any personhood efforts will expressly exclude IVF".
- V: "Because of its strong political support, IVF is largely safe from major attacks in the USA."

## Assumptions

Legal-realist reading of state law; political support as the main predictor of IVF's fate; the
complaint's facts taken as alleged (never tried).

## Concepts

Wrongful Death of a Minor Act (Alabama, 1872); punitive-only wrongful-death damages; "extrauterine
children"; embryo personhood; provider immunity statute; overreaction by interest groups and media.

## Bearing on the record

**How far it stands in.** For the LePage holding, its legal mechanism, the immunity statute and the future of IVF: well. For
Ryan and McMichael's family-law analysis (disposition disputes, parentage) and their proposed
regulatory principles: not at all. Both criticize the immunity statute's sufficiency, from
different directions (Greely: too protective of clinics; Ryan and McMichael, per their abstract: a
fuller framework is needed).

- **[TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)** (moral standing, kept apart from legal personhood). **Illustrates the separation**:
  LePage gave frozen embryos a legal status ("minor children") for one statute and one remedy,
  which Greely shows was neither a judgement of moral standing nor general legal personhood, and
  which the legislature then neutralized by immunity without touching the classification. A worked
  example of [TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)'s point that standing claims must name the claim and what follows; the legal
  analogue is "children for the purposes of the Wrongful Death Act". Candidate `draws_on` is not
  apt (the term does not use the reading); a dated note on the term, or `illustrates`, fits better.
- **[QUESTION-454](../questions.d/QUESTION-454.md)** (legitimate authority over another's body when the other cannot decide):
  weak. The plaintiffs sued as "parents and next friends" of the embryos, and their storage
  contracts had elected destruction or research use; Greely notes the court left that tension
  unaddressed. It touches who speaks for an embryo, without argument.
- **[THEORY-448](../theory.d/THEORY-448.md) / pregnancy mereology** (found in recheck): no bearing; ex vivo embryos are outside
  gestation.
