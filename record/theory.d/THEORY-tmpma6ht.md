# THEORY-tmpma6ht: Causal coupling between a process and something outside it does not by itself make that thing part of the process, so an argument from coupling to extended cognition needs a further premise saying what makes a process cognitive

## Source

Adams & Aizawa (2001), "The bounds of cognition", [LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md), read in full in
[NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md). Adams & Aizawa (2010), "Defending the Bounds of Cognition",
[LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md), read in [NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md) from a draft that predates the published chapter.

## What was actually shown

**The argument ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md) §3.2).** Quoting the coupled-system passage of
Clark and Chalmers ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)), the authors reply that "the mere causal
coupling of some process with a broader environment does not, in general,
thereby, extend that process". The expansion of a thermostat's bimetallic
strip stays in the strip, and filtration stays in the kidney, though both
are coupled to more. Without a mark of the cognitive, they add, coupling
threatens "pancognitivism", which they call "cognitive bloat" or
"cognitive ooze".

**The name and the pattern ([LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)).** The 2010 text names it the
"coupling-constitution fallacy": point to a case where an object or
process is coupled to a cognitive agent, then "slide to the conclusion
that the object or process constitutes part of the agent's cognitive
apparatus or cognitive processing". Its counter-case: the neurons into a
neuromuscular junction are coupled to the muscle but are not part of it,
and neurotransmitter release is coupled to contraction but is not part of
that process. The opening joke carries the point: "Why did the pencil
think that 2 + 2 = 4? Clark's Answer: Because it was coupled to the
mathematician."

**What follows.** An argument for extended cognition needs a premise about
what makes something cognitive, and [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) offers none ("they have
suggested none"; [NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md), C7). [NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md) rates the coupling point "strong
as a general point" (C6). [NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md) rates the logical point moderate,
resting on one counter-example by analogy (C1).

**What could have come out otherwise.** A case could have been found in
which coupling alone, with nothing else about the coupled thing, made it
part of a process. None of the counter-cases is of that kind.

## What this does not say

- **That coupling never makes parthood.** The texts show that coupling
  does not entail it. They give no argument that coupling never
  accompanies it ([NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md), Bearing on the record).
- **That Clark and Chalmers committed the fallacy.** The 2001 paper grants
  that they "are under no illusions" on this and actually claim that the
  external processes are like the internal ones. That further claim is
  what the paper disputes, and it calls the claim question-begging and
  false. Chalmers's reply ([LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md) §2) is that the objection "confuses the
  thesis with the argument for the thesis", which turns on parity.
- **That it is "the most common mistake that extended mind theorists
  make".** That is the 2010 text's charge against five authors in its
  n. 3. It is not argued case by case there ([NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md), C1).
- **What the further premise should be.** The authors' own candidate, two
  marks of the cognitive, is a separate and weaker account
  ([THEORY-tmpnnu03](THEORY-tmpnnu03.md)).
- **Who first printed the name.** [NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md) dates "coupling–constitution
  fallacy" to the authors' later work (2008). The phrase is not in the 2001
  paper, and the 2010 draft that uses it predates December 2005 ([NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md)
  and [NOTE-380](../notes.d/NOTE-380.md), Corrections).

  cognitive ([THEORY-tmpnnu03](THEORY-tmpnnu03.md)) supply the missing premise one way. Rupert's
  embedded cognition ([THEORY-tmpgrmvp](THEORY-tmpgrmvp.md)) reaches a similar point about
  coupling from conservatism. Chalmers's sensorimotor statement of the
  thesis ([THEORY-tmpegs18](THEORY-tmpegs18.md)) rests on parity instead.
- **Mereology.** It offers no account of parthood for processes; it only
  blocks one route to it ([NOTE-368](../notes.d/NOTE-368.md), Bearing on the record).
