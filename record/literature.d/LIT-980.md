# LIT-980: What is nonclassical about uncertainty relations?

Lorenzo Catani, Matthew Leifer, Giovanni Scala, David Schmid and Robert W. Spekkens (2022), *Physical Review Letters* 129, 240401.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-825](../notes.d/NOTE-825.md).*

- **Existence is not the mark.** The qubit stabilizer theory, Gaussian
  quantum mechanics and odd-prime stabilizer theories all have nontrivial
  predictability trade-offs and admit a noncontextual model (the latter
  two cited, the first exhibited).
- **The bound.** If a state s1 has counterparts s2, s3, s4 with the same
  |⟨X⟩| and |⟨Z⟩| and the other sign patterns, and ½s1 + ½s3 = ½s2 + ½s4,
  then every preparation-noncontextual model gives
  |⟨X⟩| + |⟨Z⟩| ≤ 1 (Eq. 12). The proof is analytic, the bound is tight,
  and it needs no perfectly distinguishable states.
- **From a state to a theory.** Where every state has such an orbit
  (A²₁ symmetry), Eq. 12 constrains the uncertainty relation itself. The
  qubit's ⟨X⟩² + ⟨Z⟩² ≤ 1 violates it, by up to √2 at
  |⟨X⟩| = |⟨Z⟩| = 1/√2; the stabilizer octahedron saturates it; the gbit
  reaches 2.
- **Why classical joint predictability is no counterexample.** The
  two-bit simplicial theory has perfectly predictable X and Z, but the
  states that achieve it fail the equivalence, so the bound does not
  apply to them.
- **Three measurements.** |⟨X⟩| + |⟨Y⟩| + |⟨Z⟩| ≤ 1 under the analogous
  condition, verified by computational algebra, not proved.
- **Limits.** Exact operational equivalence is assumed. The noise
  robustness is asserted with references, not shown.

read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-825](../notes.d/NOTE-825.md). It gives that question a worked

preparations and the noncontextuality inequality they imply, in a

conditions [QUESTION-067](../questions.d/QUESTION-067.md)'s readings set, perfectly distinguishable
