# LIT-206: What Does ChatGPT Want? An Interpretationist Guide

Simon Goldstein, Harvey Lederman (2025), *Inquiry (published online 2026-09-04, doi 10.1080/0020174X.2026.2727584); first circulated as a PhilArchive preprint (GOLWDC-2)* — DOI-10.1080/0020174X.2026.2727584

## Key takeaways

- On an interpretationist theory of belief and desire, the right subject of LLM psychology is the per-context "instance agent", not the model. Such instances plausibly have beliefs and desires, and their desires are best described by the "HHH+0" framework: helpful, honest and harmless, plus "zero-shot" intrinsic desires acquired in context.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The authors examine LLMs through interpretationism, the view that an agent has whatever beliefs and desires best predict and explain its actual and counterfactual behaviour. They argue for three claims. The proper object of LLM psychology is the instance agent created at the start of each context, not the underlying model. Given interpretationism, there is a strong case that instance agents have beliefs and desires. Their desires are best captured by the HHH+0 framework: helpfulness, honesty and harmlessness, plus further intrinsic "zero-shot" desires acquired in context. They weigh the main rivals, that LLMs "just" predict the next word and that they merely role-play having attitudes, and discuss the implications for copyright law, AI safety and possible future AI moral status.
