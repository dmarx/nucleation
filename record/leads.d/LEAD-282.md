# LEAD-282: Read the accounts of what scholarly communication's functions are and of their decoupling, and the overlay journals that already run a journal over work hosted elsewhere, to test the owner's split of publisher, journal and editor against them

## What it would do

Register and read, as the record's reading procedure says:

- Roosendaal and Geurts (1997), "Forces and functions in scientific
  communication", the account usually credited with dividing scholarly
  communication into registration, certification, awareness and
  archiving. Not yet registered; its identifier is to be found.
- Van de Sompel, Payette, Erickson, Lagoze and Warner (2004), "Rethinking
  scholarly communication: building the system that scholars deserve",
  D-Lib Magazine, which argues for performing those functions, with
  rewarding, by separate services joined by a protocol. Not yet
  registered.
- An overlay journal's own account of itself, a journal whose articles
  are hosted on a preprint server and which certifies them without
  hosting them: the nearest working case of U4's journal as a feed over
  editors and U6's host apart from publisher.
- A10's two precedents, which the record has not checked: Bluesky's
  separation of feed generators from labelling services, and Harvard's
  Open Access Tracking Project.

## What would count

- A function in those accounts that none of author, host, editor,
  journal, custodian or registry performs bears against [CLAIM-1330](../claims.d/CLAIM-1330.md)
  and [CLAIM-1346](../claims.d/CLAIM-1346.md).
- An account of registration that a host's timestamps do satisfy bears
  against [CLAIM-1346](../claims.d/CLAIM-1346.md)'s reading of registration as left to no party.
- An overlay journal that certifies without hosting is a documented case
  for [CLAIM-1344](../claims.d/CLAIM-1344.md).
