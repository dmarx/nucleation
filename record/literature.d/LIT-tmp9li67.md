# LIT-tmp9li67: Anxiety as a disorder of uncertainty: implications for understanding maladaptive anxiety, anxious avoidance, and exposure therapy

Brown, V. M., Price, R. & Dombrovski, A. Y. (2023). Anxiety as a disorder of uncertainty: Implications for understanding maladaptive anxiety, anxious avoidance, and exposure therapy. *Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience* 23(3): 844–868. doi:10.3758/s13415-023-01080-w. Preprint: PsyArXiv, doi:10.31234/osf.io/4zykv (2022).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmp1dwn4. The manuscript has no page numbers;
places are given by section.*

- **Not excess threat.** Laboratory studies do not show enhanced aversive
  conditioning or overvaluation of aversive outcomes in anxiety; they show
  disrupted learning under uncertainty (Current ideas about the role of
  uncertainty in anxiety).
- **Seven studies, not a survey.** Across the computational studies in
  Table 2, "Other parameters measuring overall learning rate, choice
  consistency, and outcome valuation show little relationship with
  anxiety"; the differences are in how learning adjusts to uncertainty,
  mostly for aversive outcomes. The authors say the selection does "not
  represent a comprehensive, structured literature review following PRISMA
  guidelines".
- **The loop.** In aversive environments avoiding uncertain states is the
  normal policy, and "This avoidance maintains high uncertainty and
  perpetuates avoidance by preventing exploration of more uncertain
  states." Anxiety, by misreading noise as change, is proposed to feed it.
- **Exposure.** Proposed to work by reducing miscalculated uncertainty
  through forced exploration, updated between sessions, rather than by
  extinguishing excess threat.
- **Not habit.** "Regardless, maladaptive avoidance in anxiety does not
  seem to stem from an increased tendency to form habits."
- **Limits the authors state.** Fear-based disorders only; "the function of
  exposure therapy in non-fear based disorders, like generalized anxiety
  disorder or in eating disorders, is not explained by this framework."

The exchange cited it at turn U14 as "Brown and colleagues' 2023 review" arguing that maladaptive anxiety is better understood through disrupted uncertainty learning and explore–exploit decisions than through threat expectancy alone. The abstract matches.
