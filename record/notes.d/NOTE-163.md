# NOTE-163: SEP — Mereology

## Contribution

The entry introduces mereology as the study of part–whole and part–part relations, tracing it from the Presocratics through medieval, Buddhist, Islamic and modern philosophy to Brentano, Husserl, Leśniewski and Leonard–Goodman. It separates principles that fix the meaning of "part" from substantive ones, formalises a minimal theory, then surveys decomposition principles (from whole to parts) and composition principles (from parts to whole), and ends with the question of whether parthood can be indeterminate.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1: "part" in ordinary language is broader than the mereological relation; the entry restricts attention to the general relation.
- §2.1–2.2: the core theory M takes parthood as reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric; each is noted as having dissenters.
- §3 Decomposition: supplementation (weak, strong) and complementation; strong supplementation yields extensionality, contested by cases like statue/clay; §3.4 on atomism vs gunk (no atoms) vs junk.
- §4 Composition: upper bounds and sums, van Inwagen's Special Composition Question (§4.1), several non-equivalent definitions of "sum" (§4.2), infinitary sums (§4.3), unrestricted composition/GEM (§4.4); §4.5 on whether composition is identity and on existence commitments.
- §5: vagueness in parthood, and whether it is ontic or semantic.
