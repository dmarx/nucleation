# NOTE-tmpt1we4: Rakoczy — Foundations of theory of mind and its development in early childhood

## Contribution

An integrative review of the timeline of theory of mind and the
replication problems of implicit tasks.

## Key results

1. **Two transitions.** "Major changes in ToM capacity occur at around 9
   months and 4 years of age." (PDF p. 10).
2. **Early psychology.** "perception–goal psychology involves only the
   signature of difference" (PDF p. 3); infants "form joint intentions to
   act together towards a shared goal" (PDF p. 3).
3. **At four.** "Children younger than four systematically answer
   incorrectly" (PDF p. 4).
4. **Recursion later.** "higher-order recursive meta-representation (for
   example, the ability to represent and understand ‘A thinks that B
   thinks that C desires that…’) develops gradually over childhood,
   adolescence and adulthood" (PDF p. 6).
5. **Replication.** "The basic findings could largely not be replicated in
   direct large-scale replication studies" (PDF p. 8); "no systematic
   correlations have been found between the different types of implicit
   tasks" (PDF p. 10).

## Assumptions

The classic false-belief test is the criterion of meta-representation.

## Concepts

Perception–goal psychology; the four-year revolution; signature of
difference; implicit versus explicit theory of mind.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's question**, an answer: recursive mindreading comes years
  after age 3, so the three-year-olds' equal split cannot rest on "I
  expect you to expect"; but joint attention and joint intention are
  available from the earlier perception–goal psychology.
