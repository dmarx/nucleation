# LIT-2183: Genetic rescue, the greater prairie chicken and the problem of conservation reliance in the Anthropocene

Mussmann, S. M., Douglas, M. R., Anthonysamy, W. J. B., Davis, M. A., Simpson, S. A., Louis, W. & Douglas, M. E. (2017). Genetic rescue, the greater prairie chicken and the problem of conservation reliance in the Anthropocene. *Royal Society Open Science* 4, 160736. doi:10.1098/rsos.160736

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1670](../notes.d/NOTE-1670.md).*

- **Supply without relief of the threat.** Translocations "may avoid
  the costs of restoring connectivity but also invoke long-term intensive
  management (i.e. the perpetuation of conservation reliance)" (§4.4).
- **Gains that did not last.** The authors call the outcome "success
  either ambiguous or transitory, neither of which is sustainable"
  (§4.4); only 73 birds were recorded in 2011.
- **Both sides at once.** "Two factors establish the baseline for
  conservation reliance, and both must be alleviated in tandem" (§4.4,
  fig. 5): the extrinsic threat and the intrinsic limits of a small
  population.
- **Intrinsic fixes alone backfire.** Small-population approaches alone
  "have a low probability of success and, as a consequence, promote
  rather than alleviate conservation reliance" (§5).
