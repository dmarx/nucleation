# LIT-tmpwlsvo: Observed universality of phase transitions in high-dimensional geometry, with implications for modern data analysis and signal processing

David L. Donoho and Jared Tanner (2009), "Observed universality of phase transitions in high-dimensional geometry, with implications for modern data analysis and signal processing", *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 367(1906): 4273–4293 — [ARXIV-0906.2530](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2530), [doi:10.1098/rsta.2009.0152](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2009.0152)

## Key takeaways

*Unread. From the result as it is standardly stated, to be checked against the text.*

- **A sharp boundary.** In the plane of undersampling δ = n/N and sparsity
  ρ = k/n, recovery by ℓ1 minimization succeeds with probability near 1
  below a curve ρ(δ) and fails above it. For Gaussian matrices the curve
  is computed exactly by counting faces of randomly projected polytopes.
- **Universality, observed.** Many non-Gaussian ensembles (random signs,
  partial Fourier, sparse matrices and others) put the transition on the
  same curve. It is an empirical finding, offered with a large simulation
  study, not proved.
