World* (Princeton, 2024). It is filed as [LIT-216](../../record/literature.d/LIT-216.md) and read in full from the
author's final manuscript (June 2022, 349 pp.).

## What the pass established

- **Birch and Seth answer each other.** Birch's v4 names Seth, and its §12
  overlaps his BBS commentary on Seth. They disagree on four things:
  - whether the illusion that an AI is conscious can be blocked at the level
    of belief (Birch: yes; Seth: perhaps not, like Müller-Lyer);
  - how likely consciousness is in current LLMs;
  - whether a link between consciousness and life is testable;
  - what a global workspace found in an LLM would show.

  They agree on Nagel's definition ([LIT-096](../../record/literature.d/LIT-096.md)) and on treating LLMs as
  role-players.
- **Schwitzgebel's arguments are layered.** *The Weirdness of the World*
  collects the earlier arguments. [LIT-159](../../record/literature.d/LIT-159.md) is its chapter 2 in manuscript and
  chapter 3 in print, revised: the IIT footnote is much expanded and now
  engages the expander-graph case that [LIT-185](../../record/literature.d/LIT-185.md) defends against. *AI and
  Consciousness* ([LIT-191](../../record/literature.d/LIT-191.md)) continues its chapters 10 and 11. [LIT-159](../../record/literature.d/LIT-159.md) now says so.
- **Kleiner & Ludwig's published version drops a result.** The finding that
  metric spaces are not structures of experience is only in arXiv v1 §4.
  Cite the preprint for it.
- **Veit now rejects plant consciousness.** That conflicts with the paper he
  co-wrote, [LIT-193](../../record/literature.d/LIT-193.md).
