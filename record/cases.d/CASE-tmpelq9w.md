# CASE-tmpelq9w: The Chagossians, 1965 to now: a people expelled from the Chagos Archipelago between 1967 and 1973 for the Diego Garcia base, dispersed to Mauritius, the Seychelles and Britain, whose practices, kinship and political organization continued or arose in exile while sovereignty over the islands was settled between states

## The case

The owner, at U1: "tell me about the chagossians"; at U2: "this story
feels likely to be relevant to several lines of inquiry" in this record.

The Chagos Archipelago was detached from colonial Mauritius in 1965 as
the British Indian Ocean Territory. Between 1967 and 1973 its
inhabitants, a population descended largely from enslaved and indentured
plantation workers and resident for generations, were removed, and the
largest island, Diego Garcia, became a US military base. Their
descendants live mainly in Mauritius, the Seychelles and Britain. In 2019
the International Court of Justice advised that the decolonization of
Mauritius had not been lawfully completed and treated resettlement as a
human-rights question within its completion ([LIT-tmp2x4se](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2x4se.md)). In May 2025 the UK
and Mauritius signed a treaty under which Mauritius would hold
sovereignty and the base would continue.

The exchange added particulars the record has not checked, kept as the
exchange's: that British officials represented the islands as having no
permanent population while knowing otherwise, which Human Rights Watch
documents (A2, A3; [LIT-tmp5zye9](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5zye9.md)); that UNESCO lists *sega tambour Chagos* as
endangered by displacement, poverty and the deaths of elders (A2, A3);
that Jeffery's work records food and medicinal plants still cultivated
and exchanged across the three countries, older reciprocity strained by
poverty and distance, and songs of exile reworking the homeland into a
standardized narrative that helped mobilization and complicated legal
claims (A3; [LIT-tmpzjf1o](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzjf1o.md), [LIT-tmp9pvlp](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9pvlp.md)); that Chagossians disagree about Mauritian or
British sovereignty, the base and litigation against negotiation (A3;
[LIT-tmp9pvlp](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9pvlp.md)), and a 2025 parliamentary inquiry drew more than 3,000 responses
that do not form a representative sample (A3); that the treaty's Article
6 permits resettlement on islands other than Diego Garcia without
requiring it, and that as of October 2026 the treaty had not entered into
force and the British implementing bill had fallen (A3). A3 reported the
last from news of October 2026 that this filing could not check.

## What it shows

The case separates what usually comes bundled, and it was separated by
decisions, not by stipulation (A2: "these separations are not abstract
edge cases invented to distinguish definitions").

- **Three interruptions at once** ([CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md)). Place-dependent
  knowledge held by island-born elders and no longer exercisable is
  enactment interrupted; practices whose transmission to the young was
  damaged are reproduction interrupted; and a song still known and seldom
  sung is activation. A3: "Restoring access to a location could repair an
  enactment constraint without repairing a broken transmission system. A
  language revitalization program could restore transmission without
  restoring the former territorial community." It is [QUESTION-257](../questions.d/QUESTION-257.md)'s case
  at the scale of a people (note of 2026-10-27 there).
- **Perspective, agency and recognition apart** ([CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md)). Shared
  practices and memory coexist with divergent political judgments, and the
  bodies governments recognize as speaking for the islanders need not
  represent them. A3's four questions, each settled by different evidence:
  is there a historically continuous people, an organizational
  perspective, collective political agency, legitimate representation?
  And: "a people can possess collective rights without meeting a demanding
  criterion for unified collective agency."
- **A settlement adequate to one target** ([CLAIM-348](../claims.d/CLAIM-348.md), [CLAIM-350](../claims.d/CLAIM-350.md)). A
  description recording sovereignty and military access can be constant
  across settlements that differ in whether the expelled can return, so
  the right does not factor through it. A3: "the boundary of the
  negotiating system is not necessarily the boundary of the system bearing
  the consequences." And [CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md): a settlement that drops the return
  claim from the problem has not satisfied it.
- **Restoration is not the inverse of expulsion.** A3: "A policy of
  restitution implemented fifty years later is not the inverse
  transformation of the original displacement", R∘D ≠ id. The formal
  counterpart is [CASE-111](CASE-111.md)'s fold; the institutional one is
  [CLAIM-tmpdhkhm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdhkhm.md).
- **Classification that changes its referent.** The islands were
  described as having no permanent population, and policy then made the
  description true by removing it. A3: "That is not an epistemically
  innocent confirmation of the original description." It extends
  [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md)'s transport to uptake by an institution that can act on what it
  receives.

## What it does not show

- **Not the wrong of the expulsion.** A3: "Understanding the mechanisms by
  which a people survives dispossession does not diminish the wrong of
  dispossessing them." The analysis is a description of mechanisms beside
  the legal and moral one, not a substitute for it.
- **Not one persisting agent, or that cultural survival is political
  self-determination.** A2: neither "should be treated as evidence that the
  other has been achieved." Whether the later communities continue the
  earlier one is [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md)'s question, with [CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md)'s plural
  successors and A3's warning against summarizing them with one coherence
  scalar.
- **Not an innocent misclassification.** A3: the archival history shows
  intentional deception, and "We should not replace a documented history of
  deliberate coercion with a speculative story about inadvertent systems
  failure."
- **Not a natural experiment.** A2: "a historical stress test". Migration
  routes, resources, families and cohorts select who lives where, so
  comparing the three diasporas needs process tracing, not group
  differences (A3).
- **Not that pre-expulsion life was to be restored whole.** It was a
  plantation society with coercive labour relations (A3, A4). What
  restitution owes is a normative question the structural description
  does not settle.
- **Standing.** The expulsion, the advisory opinion and the treaty's
  signature are documented; the ethnographic particulars and the reading
  are the exchange's, and every source is registered unread. The
  University of Edinburgh's archive "CHAGOS: Cultural Heritage Across
  Generations" (archive.sps.ed.ac.uk/chagos), which A3 named, is the
  natural place to check them.
