# LIT-945: Unscrambling the omelette of causation and inference: The framework of causal-inferential theories

David Schmid, John H. Selby and Robert W. Spekkens (2020), *arXiv:2009.03297*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp5ey9r](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5ey9r.md). Read in part; see the NOTE for what was skipped.*

- **Inference and influence are kept apart.** A causal-inferential theory
  pairs a causal process theory (laboratory procedures, or functions on
  classical variables) with Bayesian-Boolean inference. Two processes are
  inferentially equivalent when they give the same predictions in every
  causally closed embedding (Definition V.1).
- **Quotienting scrambles them.** GPTs and ontological models work up to
  inferential equivalence. That erases causal structure: a mixture of the
  two constant bit functions and a mixture of identity and flip are
  causally opposite, yet inferentially equivalent (§V.C).
- **Leibnizianity.** A classical realist representation is Leibnizian if
  it maps inferentially equivalent processes to inferentially equivalent
  ones (Definition VI.5). It is a formalisation of Leibniz's methodological
  principle, generalised to states of knowledge.
- **Generalized noncontextuality, rehabilitated.** It is the same
  preservation demand on a map into the *quotiented* realist theory
  (Definition VIII.2). It holds iff the quotiented operational theory has a
  classical realist representation (Eq. 214). A quotiented theory is
  neither contextual nor noncontextual.
- **The implication runs one way.** A Leibnizian representation implies a
  generalized-noncontextual one, so every noncontextuality no-go is a no-go
  for Leibnizian representations (Eq. 215). The converse is Conjecture 1′.
- **Bell versus noncontextuality.** A Bell-like no-go denies every classical
  realist representation, a weaker assumption. A noncontextuality no-go
  denies only Leibnizian ones. It applies where no Bell-like constraint
  exists, as in a prepare-measure scenario (§VIII.C).
- **The way out it proposes.** Not abandoning Leibnizianity, but a realist
  theory whose causation and inference are nonclassical. Five criteria are
  set for one (§IX.A); none is built.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 from the owner's papers-feed for [QUESTION-067](../questions.d/QUESTION-067.md), and
read in part on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmp5ey9r](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5ey9r.md). It gives the question what
refuting Spekkens's notion would commit the record to. Within classical
realism, it means giving up Leibnizianity, not realism. A Bell-type
failure would be the stronger result. It also sets two conditions on any
pragmatic test. Equivalence must hold over every tester, not only a probe
family. And the test must use the unquotiented description, in which
equivalent renderings stay distinct. It is the source text for the
"Leibnizian methodological principle" that [THEORY-011](../theory.d/THEORY-011.md) cites, and it
restates the positive-quasiprobability criterion of [THEORY-016](../theory.d/THEORY-016.md) ([LIT-003](LIT-003.md)).
It rehabilitates the definition of [LIT-266](LIT-266.md), which it cites, and does not
treat the Kochen–Specker or sheaf notions. A full reading would add the
proofs (Appendices E–G) and the construction of the theories in §II–IV.
