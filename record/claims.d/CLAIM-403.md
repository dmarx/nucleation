# CLAIM-403: A contribution's admission to an organization's inquiry, its recognition in apt terms, its credibility, its uptake into judgements, and its contributor's part in revising the standards of uptake are separable, each possible without the later ones, so an organization can widen access while every channel by which a less authoritative contribution could change the criteria of authority stays closed

## The claim

A24 §6, after the owner's U24 on the perpetuation of privilege: "An
institution may become more inclusive in the sense that previously
excluded people can submit information, while retaining all the same
categories, decision standards, and authority structures." Its table:

| Form of participation | What changes? |
|---|---|
| **Access** | A person's contribution can enter the process |
| **Recognition** | The contribution can be understood in appropriate terms |
| **Credibility** | It can receive an appropriate assessment of its evidential merit |
| **Uptake** | It can influence judgments, questions, or accepted conclusions |
| **Governance participation** | Contributors can help revise the procedures and standards governing future uptake |

"Someone's contribution may be received and politely acknowledged yet
have no mechanism through which it can change institutional
understanding." Its constructive point: "We should give equal attention to
how an organization maintains **channels through which less authoritative
contributions can change the criteria of authority itself**. That is a
potentially important constitutive feature of adaptive self-governance."

Each rung can fail with the earlier ones in place. Recognition without
credibility is where testimonial injustice falls, when the deficit comes
from prejudice; [THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md)'s pre-emptive form fails at the first rung,
since the word is never sought. Credibility without
uptake is A26 §3's dissent that is heard and changes nothing: "An
organization may retain dissenting specialists and allow them to speak
while having no meaningful process through which their criticism affects
established views." Uptake without the last rung is a criticism that can
change a judgement but not the standards that judge the next one.

**What the record holds.** [CLAIM-349](CLAIM-349.md) separates an organization's "access
to a signal, the signal's authority over action, and action on it", for
its own signals. This carries the separation to contributors and adds two
rungs: recognition, which is the legibility of the contribution
([TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)), and governance participation, which is [THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)'s
"tempered equality of intellectual authority" and [QUESTION-041](../questions.d/QUESTION-041.md)'s "channels
from the members" that keep a course correctable. Longino's uptake is
[THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)'s own word. [CLAIM-362](CLAIM-362.md)'s non-domination ("a process that rarely
decides keeps access to the roles in which it is consequential") is the
same concern on the authenticity line.

**It recurs.** A25 §5, from Kwok (held unread as [LIT-1320](../literature.d/LIT-1320.md), Unavailable
on 2026-10-21): "**An institution can make a
contribution intelligible without making it visible to anyone in a
position to respond.** ... Visibility, interpretability, credibility, and
practical uptake should therefore remain separate dimensions." A27 §6, of
consultation: an institution "can satisfy a nominal requirement to consult
affected agents while denying them effective participation in defining the
decision's relevant questions or criteria", and "Legibility, epistemic
standing, and governance participation help identify this difference."
[CLAIM-406](CLAIM-406.md) is the loop by which a closed first rung can persist.

**The readings of 2026-10-21.** James et al. ([NOTE-1041](../notes.d/NOTE-1041.md)) support the
first point, access widening while nothing else changes, and add that
admission can itself be the harm: access is "bidirectional", and access
"down" for powerful players is "politically consequential". Their refusal,
a "possibility to say no", sits near the top rung, asserted rather than
analysed. Kay et al. ([NOTE-1049](../notes.d/NOTE-1049.md)) state access without uptake for model
development: "Participation may not be effective or even possible in the
case of willful hermeneutical ignorance." Collins ([NOTE-1042](../notes.d/NOTE-1042.md),
[THEORY-834](../theory.d/THEORY-834.md)) gives the standing condition before recognition: outsiders
within see anomalies that insiders produced and are not placed to see,
and lack the authority to press them; her recommendation to
institutionalize that perspective is a form of the top rung. Short, Hill and
Brown's process condition, whether regulators elicited and considered
public views, with the merger guidelines exempt from notice and comment,
is the difference between a closed rung and an open one in one
institution ([NOTE-1051](../notes.d/NOTE-1051.md)). Hyzen et al. ([NOTE-1035](../notes.d/NOTE-1035.md), skimmed) parallel
the ladder from the receiver's side, availability to access to knowledge,
but their stages are an individual's acquisition, not an organization's
uptake. A25 said the paper "comes particularly close to our conjunction of
legibility and operational availability"; it is nearer that than this
ladder.

## What it does not say

- **That every contribution is owed uptake.** A24: "This doesn't mean every
  rejected interpretation is correct." A26: "we must also distinguish
  *uptake* from *obedience*. A community need not accept every criticism;
  it needs procedures capable of evaluating and responding to criticisms on
  relevant grounds."
- **That the rungs are stages a contribution passes through in order.**
  They are conditions, and A22 said the same of its five stages: "analytical
  distinctions, not necessarily five sequential processing steps".
- **How to measure any rung**, or that any actual institution has closed
  one.

## Note of 2026-10-26: influence across levels is not additive

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CASE-166 — Proposed; the multilevel study in which the point arose, cited as open -->

This claim's rungs say which pathways from a contribution to an
organization's judgement exist. The Northstar turns of the exchange
"Experimental Infrastructure Review" raised, twice, how influence along
them adds up when the rungs are held at more than one level. T18 §7: "An
engineer's contribution to a team proposal and that team's subsequent
contribution to the company are partly the same causal pathway. Counting
both would inflate the apparent influence of coordinated employees … A
higher-level contribution is generated through lower-level activity; it
isn't an additional independent force simply because we've named the
coalition." T19 §3: a team proposal "is not an additional independent
opinion appended to the employees' opinions. Otherwise, the simulator
would double-count activity merely because we've described it at two
levels." The study built on it is [CASE-166](../cases.d/CASE-166.md).

A linear construction makes it exact. An employee's message e sets a team
proposal p = c·e, and the company's decision is y = a·e + b·p. With
a = 0.3, b = 0.8, c = 0.5 and e moved from 0 to 1, the total effect of
do(e) on y is 0.7, of which 0.3 is direct and 0.4 runs through the team;
do(p = 0.5) alone moves y by 0.4. Adding "the employee's influence" (0.7)
to "the team's influence" (0.4) gives 1.1, but the decision moved by 0.7.
The excess, b·c = 0.4, is the mediated pathway counted twice (checked
numerically; the decomposition is [THEORY-419](../theory.d/THEORY-419.md)'s do-surgery). So attribution
across the rungs is by intervention on specified pathways, an employee's
total effect divided into a direct and a team-mediated part, not by
summing level-specific weights. The lab's exclusion counterfactual
(dmarx/PerspectiveLab@c54617e, experiments/perspective_engine/) measures a
per-process total effect, and per-level exclusion effects would not sum in
a model whose teams are built from employees.

It does not say a team adds nothing. Its records and procedures can open
a pathway no member has (T19: an incident team "might jointly possess the
authority to deploy a mitigation even though no one member has both the
necessary knowledge and authorization"), and that pathway counts once, as
the team's. The claim is unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-29: a step after uptake

Cover's "Violence and the word", [LIT-1601](../literature.d/LIT-1601.md), read in [NOTE-1384](../notes.d/NOTE-1384.md), adds a
step after this claim's last rungs. Uptake here is a contribution's
influence on an institution's judgements, and governance participation
its contributor's part in revising the standards of uptake. Cover asks
whether the institution's judgment then becomes an act, through others
who must be willing to carry it into deeds. A judgment can be taken up
and still not be executed, and the ladder above does not name that
step. The claim is unchanged.
