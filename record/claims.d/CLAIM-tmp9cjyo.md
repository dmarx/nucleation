# CLAIM-tmp9cjyo: Active inference already runs an internal loop: mental action selects among higher-level cognitive states and sets the precision of lower-level observations, structure learning reshapes the generative model without action, and a judgement is behaviour, so a judgement–observation loop may be action and perception one Markov blanket inward

## The claim

The objection, at full strength. Active inference does not confine action
to the body.

- **Mental action.** Sandved-Smith et al. ([LIT-tmpp54zu](../literature.d/LIT-tmpp54zu.md)) "cast mental action
  as policy selection over higher-level cognitive states and add a further
  hierarchical level to model meta-awareness states that modulate the
  expected confidence (precision) in the mapping between observations and
  hidden cognitive states" (read in [NOTE-tmpknfrc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpknfrc.md)). At the attentional
  level, mental action changes the agent's true attentional state, and so
  what it then observes of itself: action and perception one level
  inward. At the perceptual level it does not change content. The stimuli
  are "predetermined, with a deviant stimulus shown every 20th time step",
  and mental action changes only their precision. Parr and Friston
  ([LIT-tmpu6g9m](../literature.d/LIT-tmpu6g9m.md), read in [NOTE-tmpi40rc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi40rc.md)) separate attention, the precision
  given to data, from salience, the epistemic value of an action that
  samples them. They never call attention an action. Covert precision
  changes what is observed only by biasing "planning in favour of an
  overt saccade".
- **Judgement fixing what is observed.** Brouillet and Friston
  ([THEORY-662](../theory.d/THEORY-662.md)): the feeling of fluency is an inference about the
  precision of one's own processing, and because its cause is internal, a
  felt discrepancy is attributed to "the most salient external source", as
  familiarity, liking or a sense of error. No body is in that loop
  ([NOTE-785](../notes.d/NOTE-785.md)).
- **The observer reshaped without action.** Smith et al. ([THEORY-564](../theory.d/THEORY-564.md)):
  a bounded generative model "can learn its own structure with no
  machinery beyond inference and learning". Spare hidden-state slots fill
  when an observation fits none of the learned causes. During that
  learning, reporting policies are disabled ([NOTE-485](../notes.d/NOTE-485.md)). Active inference
  thus has [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md)'s diachronic loop, in which judgement reshapes
  the observer, inside one generative model whose likelihood is learned.
- **Judgement is behaviour.** Chalmers ([THEORY-577](../theory.d/THEORY-577.md)): "Our judgments
  that consciousness poses a hard problem are behaviour." If judgements
  are behaviour, keeping judgement while cutting action relabels the
  action rather than removing it.

In a deep model, a higher level's outputs are a lower level's priors and
its inputs are the lower level's posteriors, so the lower level is,
formally, the higher level's environment. The judgement is mental action
at the upper level, and the observation is what the lower level returns.
"Cutting action out" draws the blanket around the upper level and calls
what crosses it judgement.

## What it does not say

- It does not save the diagram. Every item above runs with no action on
  the world. If the loop is redescribed as mental action, precision and
  structure learning, the redescription concedes [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md)'s point
  that action on the world is not part of it, and the disagreement is
  about whether the diagram should say so.
- Where the blanket goes is a modelling choice, made before the blanket is
  found ([THEORY-073](../theory.d/THEORY-073.md); [NOTE-447](../notes.d/NOTE-447.md): an external intervention has "exactly the
  same formal properties" as an internal cause). So "one blanket inward"
  is always available, and the objection costs little to make and says
  little.
- The readings narrow the objection. In Sandved-Smith et al., mental
  action changes perceptual precision, not perceptual content, which is
  the line [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) draws. Nothing in that model is learned, so the
  structure-learning strand rests on [THEORY-564](../theory.d/THEORY-564.md) alone. Parr and
  Friston give attention no power to change content short of an overt
  saccade, which is action on the world.
