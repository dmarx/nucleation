# NOTE-1981: Ravfogel, Yehudai, Bruna and Bietti — Geometric factual recall

## Contribution

A construction, a lower bound and experiments for a mechanism of factual
recall in which facts live in the geometry of learned embeddings and the
MLP is a generic selector. Earlier accounts (Bietti et al. 2023, Nichani
et al. 2025, neither held) store facts as associative outer products in
weight matrices over fixed near-orthogonal embeddings, at a parameter cost
of Ω̃(NR). This one needs d = O(R log N) and an MLP whose width does not
depend on N, in a shared-attribute setting harder than Nichani et al.'s.

## Key insight

Whether a transformer's MLP is a store of facts depends on whether the
embeddings can learn: when they can, the subject's embedding carries its
attributes and the MLP only reads the queried one out; when they cannot,
the facts have to go into the weights.

## Assumptions

- N subjects, R relations, each relation a random bijection onto a shared
  pool of N attributes; single-token entities; in the single-hop
  experiments, uniform attention.
- No normalization layers in the theory.
- The LM evidence is a probe, correlational, fitted per relation on 80% of
  subjects. The authors: "in real models this structure might emerge at
  intermediate layers".

## Key results

- **Abstract**: "subject embeddings encode linear superpositions of their
  associated attribute vectors, and a small MLP acts as a
  relation-conditioned selector that extracts the relevant attribute via
  ReLU gating, and not as an associative key-value mapping." The view it
  sets against "casts internal weight matrices as associative memories over
  pairs of embeddings, requiring parameter counts that scale linearly with
  the number of facts".
- **Theorem 4.1**: "There is a 1-layer transformer with a 3-layer MLP of
  width R and embedding dimension d = 4R log(N) + 1 that correctly solves
  the single-hop factual recall problem." In G.1 each subject embedding is
  the block vector (v_{g1(i)}; …; v_{gR(i)}), with codes v ∈ {±1}^{4 log N}.
- **Theorem 4.2**: for k-hop recall without chain-of-thought, a counting
  lower bound on W, the bits in the attention and MLP weights, against D,
  the bits in the embedding; if D < R, "The model must store N R facts
  inside its weights"; if D > R^k the MLP can be a selector, "but the
  embedding dimension is exponential in k"; the intermediate regime has a
  gap (Remark 4.1).
- **Theorem 4.3**: in the key–value construction, "an O(N · R) wide MLP
  operates as a brute-force lookup table".
- **Theorem 4.4**: with chain-of-thought, a one-layer transformer with
  d = Õ(R + k) and MLP width Õ(R) solves k-hop recall; chain-of-thought acts
  as "a discrete memory fetch" through the unembedding.
- **Capacity** (§5.2.1; N = 4096, R from 2 to 16, three seeds): "the
  trainable curve scales as dmin ≈ a + b log2 N"; the frozen curve "as a
  power law of N". R = 16 needs d ≥ 128 learned, d ≥ 512 frozen.
- **Selectivity** (§5.2.2): "the MLP correctly follows counterfactual
  changes to the queried relation while leaving other relations
  unaffected", under minimum-norm interventions along the linear readouts.
- **Transfer** (§5.2.2): with subject embeddings re-initialized to encode
  new bijections, "the frozen MLP achieves substantial accuracy without any
  retraining" (no number in the main text); in the frozen-embedding control
  (D, Fig. 8) zero-shot accuracy "is near chance".
- **Multi-hop** (C.1; N = 2048, d = 256, k ∈ {1, 2, 4}): near-perfect with
  chain-of-thought and learned embeddings; without it, "performance
  degrades sharply as k and R increase".
- **Released LMs** (§5.3; Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen3-14B, Llama-3.1-8B,
  Llama-3.2-1B, Phi-4; rank-512 affine probe): best-layer MRR "0.69 on
  people" and "0.44 on films", rising through the middle layers, best
  "roughly 50–80% through the network". Offered as "the missing theoretical
  basis for the linear relational structure observed empirically by
  Hernandez et al."

## Concepts

- **Geometric versus associative memorization** — facts held in where
  embeddings sit, versus as key–value outer products in weights.
- **Superposition** — here the stacking of a subject's R attribute codes
  into disjoint blocks, a direct sum, which learned solutions are
  "well-approximated" by; not superposition under interference.
- **Relation-conditioned selector** — the ReLU MLP that gates out every
  block but the queried relation's.
- **Freeze-and-swap transfer** — re-initializing subject embeddings to new
  bijections under a frozen MLP.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-1030](../theory.d/THEORY-1030.md)**: partly against, in a toy. A construction that gradient
  descent recovers in which the MLP is a generic selector and not a store
  of pattern-to-continuation associations; freeze-and-swap transfer is
  causal evidence within the toy. It is not the test [THEORY-1030](../theory.d/THEORY-1030.md)'s
  promote_when asks for (no LMs, no unit ablations), and it shows that the
  memory regime is what training finds when embeddings are frozen. So "MLP
  as key–value memory" is one regime among others.
- **[THEORY-1011](../theory.d/THEORY-1011.md), [THEORY-1039](../theory.d/THEORY-1039.md)**: an alternative to storage in MLP weights,

- **[THEORY-1024](../theory.d/THEORY-1024.md)**: consonant, by the reader's inference and not the

## Limitations

- One-layer toy, random bijections, single-token entities. The authors
  leave "correlated relations, typed attributes, polysemy, multi-token
  entities, and long-tailed frequencies" untreated.
- The LM evidence is probe-only: linear decodability, not the absence of
  facts stored in weights.
- The lower bound has a gap in the intermediate regime (Remark 4.1).
- The proofs were not checked, and the zero-shot transfer accuracy is not
  given as a number in the main text.
