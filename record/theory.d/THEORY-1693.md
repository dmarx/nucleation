# THEORY-1693: Freer and Nosratabadi (2026) show that under the standard limited-attention model choices reveal almost no preferences, while assuming that a decision-maker compares at least two alternatives restores about a third of the full-attention revealed preferences at almost no loss of descriptive fit

## The account

Freer and Nosratabadi (2026) show that under the standard limited-attention model choices reveal almost no preferences, while assuming that a decision-maker compares at least two alternatives restores about a third of the full-attention revealed preferences at almost no loss of descriptive fit.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-1797](../notes.d/NOTE-1797.md); its key results are quoted there with locations.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Caliari, read

A **parallel from the data side**, no edge (LIT-2363, NOTE-tmphq6zm). This
account shows from theory that under the standard limited-attention model
choices reveal almost no preferences. Caliari's experiment applies the
Masatlioglu et al. method to 145 participants, and it "uniquely identifies
the best alternative only for 1 out of 145 participants" in the Time
domain and 14 in Risk (Appendix D). The finding agrees with this account's
first half on a second data set, by an independent reading; the second half,
the attention floor, is not tested there, so the promote_when is not met.
