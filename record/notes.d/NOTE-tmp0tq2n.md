# NOTE-tmp0tq2n: Tronto — Higher Education for Citizens of Caring Democracies

## Contribution

As a work: a programmatic address urging universities to serve caring
democracies rather than neoliberal "development". As a neighbour: Tronto's
own one-sentence definition of privileged irresponsibility, and her own
four-step summary of Caring Democracy, neither of which the record had in
her words.

## Key results

- **Definition** (p. 8): "a major source of injustice in the world comes
  from the capability of the relatively privileged to refuse to accept
  unpleasant or burdensome responsibilities (privileged
  irresponsibility)."
- **Professionals' narrowness** (p. 8): "what does it mean if we refuse to
  look beyond our own narrow, professional tasks, and refuse to take any
  responsibility for the institution's standing and condition in
  society?"
- **Diffused responsibility excuses** (p. 11): "if France, like all other
  nations, has a responsibility for educational development everywhere,
  then it bears no special responsibility for the underdevelopment that
  occurred in its own colonies."
- **Caring Democracy's steps** (p. 14): first, "to realign a system of
  privileges that make it impossible to discuss how some people take on an
  unfair load of care responsibilities"; second, "everyone's voice must be
  heard in making these adjustments". Neoliberal thinking, "framing all
  'responsibility' as personal responsibility, is one of the great
  roadblocks" (p. 14).

## Assumptions

All societies organize care somehow (p. 13), and care and democracy are
mutually generative.

## Concepts

- **Privileged irresponsibility**: the capability of the relatively
  privileged to refuse unpleasant or burdensome responsibilities.
- **Caring democracy**; **caring with**.
- **Personal responsibility**, in its neoliberal framing, as an obstacle.
- **Development** discourse, whose single-path logic diffuses
  responsibility.

## Bearing on the record

Filed as [THEORY-tmpna7qd](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpna7qd.md).

- **Which of the originals' results it restates.** For Moral Boundaries
  ([LIT-2708](../literature.d/LIT-2708.md)): the term and its sense, in one sentence. Not the book's
  mechanism, its examples, nor the exchange's "because other people
  routinely perform that work on their behalf" (A15) or "socially
  organized inattention" (A17), which no text read contains. For Caring
  Democracy ([LIT-2669](../literature.d/LIT-2669.md)): Tronto's own outline of the programme, which
  bears out A17's "the distribution of care responsibilities as a matter
  of democratic justice" in substance; nothing on which chapters ("chapters
  2 and 4").
- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**, the upstream strand: the privileged can refuse
  burdensome responsibilities (p. 8), and diffused responsibility relieves
  those with most causal responsibility (p. 11). Corroborates; Tronto does
  not address blame or control.
- **[QUESTION-tmpvoufs](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpvoufs.md)**: "everyone's voice must be heard" in reallocating
  care responsibilities (p. 14) is a democratic answer in kind, not a
  principal–agent mechanism.

## Limitations

Short and programmatic, addressed to one audience; the concept is used,
not argued for.
