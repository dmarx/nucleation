# THEORY-tmpe5ngv: Tomasello and colleagues hypothesize that uniquely human cooperation evolved in two steps, first obligate collaborative foraging that made partners interdependent and selected, through partner choice by reputation as a good collaborator, for joint goals, commitment and helping, with reciprocity playing only a minor role, and then group growth and intergroup competition that scaled this into collective intentionality with conventions, norms and institutions

## The account

Obligate collaborative foraging is a Stag Hunt with three problems:
dividing spoils, coordinating and free riding. Humans solved them with
joint goals and commitment, fair sharing, agent-neutral roles and
cooperative communication, policed by choosing partners by their
reputation as collaborators. Interdependence makes helping a partner pay
(sB > C). Growing groups competing with others scaled the joint "we" to
the group, and norms arose as conventionalized social selection, enforced
on others and, through guilt and shame, on oneself.

## Evidence

Comparative experiments with chimpanzees and children, mostly from the
authors' lab, and theoretical argument; no formal model of the partner
market.
