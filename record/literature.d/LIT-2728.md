# LIT-2728: Why the "Haves" Come Out Ahead: Speculations on the Limits of Legal Change

Marc Galanter (1974). "Why the 'Haves' Come Out Ahead: Speculations on the Limits of Legal Change". *Law & Society Review* 9(1): 95–160. DOI-10.2307/3053023.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading ([NOTE-tmpny8gh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpny8gh.md)).*

- Repeat players against one-shotters, "a continuum rather than" a
  dichotomous pair (pp. 97–98); the RP has "low stakes in the outcome of
  any one case" and "the resources to pursue its long-run interests"
  (p. 98).
- "RPs can play for rules as well as immediate gains" (p. 100); precedent
  is "relatively skewed toward those favorable to RP" (p. 102).
- Overload creates pressure to settle "by causing delay (thereby
  discounting the value of recovery)" and "by raising costs (of keeping
  the case alive)", "shields existing rules from challenge" and "tends to
  protect the possessor" (pp. 121–22).
- RPs are not "to be equated with 'haves'" (p. 103).
