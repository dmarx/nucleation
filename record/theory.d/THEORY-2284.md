# THEORY-2284: PTSD gaze lingers on trauma; rest is thin

## Source

Diotaiuti and colleagues (2026), *European Journal of Psychotraumatology*
17: 2703319, [LIT-3179](../literature.d/LIT-3179.md), read in full from PubMed Central's open text in
[NOTE-2382](../notes.d/NOTE-2382.md).

## What was actually shown

A PRISMA-ScR scoping review: 132 records from three databases, 17 studies
included, charted in Table 1 by sample, paradigm, device, measures and
findings, and synthesised narratively by attentional component. Sustained
gaze on negative material recurs across free-viewing, matrix and dot-probe
designs; first-fixation differences do not. Reduced exploration rests on
Blekic and colleagues (2025) and Roy and colleagues (2022), within-trial
avoidance on Thomas and colleagues (2013), delayed attention to context on
Zidda and colleagues (2025) alone. Every included design presents stimuli
without a preceding recall of the participant's trauma, and none is
longitudinal through treatment.

## What this does not say

- **Not that no such study exists.** The search used no recall, script or
  imagery terms, and the corpus misses at least two eligible studies.
- **Not that PTSD reduces sampling.** The review finds heterogeneous
  patterns, and calls the exploration measures "not process-pure".
- **Not an effect size.** Recurrence is a count of studies.
