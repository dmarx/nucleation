# LIT-tmpj6tf3: Delicate memory structure of origami switches

Jules, T., Reid, A., Daniels, K. E., Mungan, M. & Lechenault, F. (2022). Delicate memory structure of origami switches. *Physical Review Research* 4(1): 013128. doi:10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.013128. arXiv:2106.08243.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpwz8jy. Sections are the arXiv v3's.*

- **A Preisach memory.** Four decoupled bistable barrels in series: "we
  can drive the bellows to any of its 16 allowable states by following a
  prescribed sequence of compression and extension" (abstract). The
  graph's topology depends only on the order of the thresholds (§II.A).
- **A partial readout.** By height, "a precise and unequivocal reading of
  the configuration does not arise from our analysis"; stiffness alone is
  "a worse independent identification tool" (§II.B).
- **Defects by hand.** Defect states were reached "by carefully twisting
  parts of a partially compressed bellows" (§II.C); "there is no
  transition from a reachable microstate to a defective configuration".
- **A counted 64.** 39 of the 48 single-defect states were observed before
  the sample was too damaged; two-defect states were not stable (§II.C,
  appendix B).
- **Irreversible exits.** A defect loop is left by erasure or by a shift of
  the defect to a stiffer module (§II.D).

## Standing in the record

Cited in the pasted synthesis on constraint architectures as S4, as four
Kresling units with 16 nominal states, return-point memory, defects
expanding the state space to 64, Garden of Eden states, and states
discriminated by height and stiffness. The reading corrects three of
these: the readout is partial by the authors' own account; the 64 is a
counting convention, not a set of observed states; and the defect states
exist only under a richer control (twisting by hand) than the declared
uniaxial loading. Filed from it: THEORY-tmpop5d8. No instruction for
machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.
