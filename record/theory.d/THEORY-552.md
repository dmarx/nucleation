# THEORY-552: Indeed's US software-development job-postings index rose to 233.8 in February 2022 and fell to 65.2 by mid-February 2025, measured against 1 February 2020 = 100, while all postings stood near 110; it was the deepest fall of any Indeed sector, though not the only one to have more than doubled, and the postings data cannot apportion the fall among interest rates, over-hiring and AI

## Source

Gergely Orosz (2025), "Software engineering job openings hit five-year
low?", *The Pragmatic Engineer* blog, [LIT-089](../literature.d/LIT-089.md), read in full in [NOTE-039](../notes.d/NOTE-039.md).
The finding rests on [NOTE-039](../notes.d/NOTE-039.md)'s check against Indeed Hiring Lab's job
postings tracker (github.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker, data to
2026-09-18), which FRED republishes. The primary series is not held as a
LIT.

## What was actually shown

**The index.** Hiring Lab's index is the percentage change in seasonally
adjusted active postings since 1 February 2020, as a seven-day trailing
average. It counts postings on Indeed.

**The swing.** US software-development postings peaked at 233.8 on
2022-02-28 and were still 228 in May 2022. On 2025-02-13 they stood at
65.2: a 3.59× fall from the peak and −8.3% year on year, against all
postings at 109.5. Those are the post's 65%, "3.5x" and −8%.

**Against other sectors.** Software development had the largest peak-to-
February-2025 fall (next, Data & Analytics, 3.07×) and the lowest level.
Its boom was not unique: Human Resources (243.3), Logistic Support,
Accounting, Civil Engineering, Data & Analytics and Production &
Manufacturing all peaked above 200, above Banking & Finance's 190.2. The
post's claim that only banking came close holds only among the four
sectors it charted.

**Other countries, same date.** Canada 73.6, Great Britain 55.5, France
68.9, Germany 66.7, Australia 111.7. Only Australia is above baseline, as
the post says.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The headline figures could have
failed to reproduce from the source data. They reproduce to within
rounding; several of the post's sector figures do not (hospitality −18%
against −7.0%).

## What this does not say

- **That openings, vacancies or hiring fell by these amounts.** The post
  uses those words interchangeably. The index is postings on one site, and
  the author himself doubts its coverage of Big Tech and startups.
- **Why it fell.** The post ranks interest rates first, over-hiring second
  and GenAI as a hedged "wait and see"; none of this is analysed. Its
  argument that Section 174 cannot explain the 2022 drop rests on dating
  the rule to 2023, which [NOTE-039](../notes.d/NOTE-039.md) believes is wrong (tax years after 2021,
  from background knowledge, unverified); the cross-country argument
  stands either way.
- **Where it stands now.** In the current vintage US software-development
  postings were 77.3 on 2026-09-18, against all postings at 103.5. The
  figures above may differ slightly from the February 2025 vintage the
  author saw, since the series is re-adjusted weekly.
- **Anything about AI's effect on labour.** [NOTE-039](../notes.d/NOTE-039.md) says the post's
  AI-labour remarks are speculation and should not be cited as evidence.
