# CLAIM-tmpf2p3p: The potential account of valence's first consequence, no frustration, no valence, runs together two relations: perspectives' contested rankings, which a record of winning moves registers, and demands that are jointly infeasible, which the compatibility complex registers; rankings can contest while every demand is satisfiable, and demands can be infeasible before any contest is recorded, so the consequence needs one of them named, and the absorbed writer, valenced with nothing contested, tests whichever is chosen

## The objection

[CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md)'s first consequence: "Potential needs something to prevail
over. Where the perspectives never conflict, the compatibility complex of
[CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md) has no minimal non-faces and no ranking wins anything." The
sentence passes from one relation to another.

- **Contested rankings.** [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) needs contested rankings and winning
  moves: two perspectives prefer different outcomes and one prevails.
- **Frustrated demands.** [CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md)'s complex registers demands that
  cannot all be met by any attainable outcome. [CASE-119](../cases.d/CASE-119.md) is the clean case:
  three demands, every pair satisfiable, the triple not.

They come apart both ways. Two perspectives can rank outcomes differently
while every demand either maintains stays satisfiable: the contest is over
which satisfying outcome, not over whether any satisfies. And demands can
be jointly infeasible before any record of contested decisions picks out
a prevailing ordering. [CLAIM-346](CLAIM-346.md) already holds feasibility, aggregation and
epistemic coordination apart as independent obstructions; the first
consequence collapses the first two. The claim needs to say whether valence
presupposes contest or presupposes frustration ([TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)), and the two
choices give different scopes.

**The absorbed writer tests either reading.** [CASE-051](../cases.d/CASE-051.md): the draft goes
well, email stays open and unblocked but does not win, and nothing is
invoked. [CASE-109](../cases.d/CASE-109.md) separates the effort of the task from the effort of
resolving internal conflict. Flow is ordinarily described as positive, yet
there is little to contest and nothing frustrated. [CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md) has a reply:
flow's pleasantness is intrinsic liking, outside goal-conduciveness
valence ([CLAIM-433](CLAIM-433.md)). That reply is available, but then the scope
restriction is doing real explanatory work, and the account cannot also
say that valence broadly requires frustration.

The flow case itself is CLAIM-441's objection, filed in parallel from the
same comparison of the will and affect lines, through CLAIM-162's reading
of flow as synchronization, and with four exits named. This claim does not
duplicate it. What it adds is the prior point: before flow can tell
against the first consequence, the consequence has to say which relation
it requires, contest or frustration, and the absorbed writer is a case of
neither.

## What would settle it

Rewording the first consequence to name one relation, and stating for the
absorbed writer which valence, if any, the account assigns. If the
compatibility complex is the relation, a case with contested rankings and
no infeasibility should come out unvalenced; if contest is, [CASE-119](../cases.d/CASE-119.md) before
any contest should.

## What it does not say

- That [CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md) is wrong about valence needing something to prevail
  over. The objection is to an equivocation in stating it.
