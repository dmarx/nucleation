# LIT-tmpeggnf: Video ergo sum: manipulating bodily self-consciousness

Lenggenhager, B., Tadi, T., Metzinger, T. & Blanke, O. (2007). *Science* 317(5841):1096–1099. doi:10.1126/science.1143439. PMID 17717189.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp9c2fc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md).*

- **The set-up.** A camera 2 m behind the standing participant fed live
  3D video to a head-mounted display, so the participant saw their own back
  2 m ahead. The participant had white noise in their ears. Their back was
  stroked with a pen for 60 s. In the synchronous condition the video was
  live. In the asynchronous condition, Study I replayed the training
  session's video, and Study II added a fixed 200 ms delay. Study II also
  filmed a mannequin and a white box in place of the participant.
- **Two measures, both borrowed from the rubber hand.** The first was a
  seven-item questionnaire scored from −3 to +3, adapted from Botvinick and
  Cohen. The second was "drift": the blindfolded participant was walked
  backwards in small steps and then asked to walk back to the starting
  position.
- **Study I (n = 14).** The synchronous drift was 24.1 ± 9.0 cm (SEM),
  p = 0.02 against zero. The asynchronous drift was 12.5 ± 8.5 cm, which was
  not significant. Synchronous against asynchronous gave paired p = 0.01.
  Q1–Q3 (touch felt where seen, touch caused by the seen pen, "it felt as
  if the virtual body was my body") were positive only after synchronous
  stroking.
- **Study II (n = 14, another group).** Drift was measured against a
  motor-control condition with no visual scene. The synchronous drifts
  were 17.1 cm for the own body, 25.4 cm for the mannequin and 14.3 cm for
  the box (p = 0.07). Asynchronous drifts were not significant. Synchronous
  against asynchronous was significant only for the mannequin. Q3 was
  2.1 for the own body, 0.6 for the mannequin and −0.4 for the box.
- **What it was not.** "None of the participants reported sensations of
  overt disembodiment or a change in visuospatial perspective" (p. 1097).
  The authors call the state at most heautoscopy-like.
- **The authors' conclusion.** "Spatial unity and bodily self-consciousness
  can be studied experimentally" (abstract). Global self-attribution and
  self-location rest partly on the same visuo-somatosensory integration as
  body-part ownership, constrained by knowledge of what a body is.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-18 at the owner's request for body-ownership illusion
material. It is the experiment that [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)'s C6 and Blanke and
Metzinger's review ([LIT-tmpypm1w](LIT-tmpypm1w.md)) both lean on for the claim that whole-body
ownership can be manipulated. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries
no instruction for machine-learning practice.

The reading ([NOTE-tmp9c2fc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9c2fc.md)) places it **Active**, as evidence for a
smaller claim than it is usually cited for. Synchronous visuo-tactile
stroking shifts reported self-attribution of a filmed body and shifts a
walking-error measure, by modest amounts, in two samples of fourteen. Five
things narrow that claim:

- The drift measure was not reported as felt.
- The body-versus-object contrast is weak for drift.
- The mannequin barely reached self-identification.
- Nothing like an out-of-body experience occurred.
- Ehrsson and Petkova's e-letter offers a reading the data do not exclude:
  participants recognised themselves on a CCTV-like display rather than
  feeling they were the body they saw.
