# THEORY-164: In groups that build a new communication system, larger groups make it more systematic, and the greater variety of input they face drives the effect

## Source

Limor Raviv, Antje Meyer and Shiri Lev-Ari (2019), *Proc. R. Soc. B*
286:20191262, [LIT-825](../literature.d/LIT-825.md); read in [NOTE-628](../notes.d/NOTE-628.md) (main text; the
supplementary models were not read).

## The claim

When people who share no language build one by communicating, the size of
the group they communicate in shapes the result. With everyone able to
talk to everyone and the same total amount of interaction, members of
larger groups end with languages whose forms map more systematically onto
meanings (parts of labels for parts of meanings), reach that structure
sooner, and differ less from one group to the next. Members of small groups
can sustain idiosyncratic, holistic labels, because with few partners and
much shared history they can memorise them. The proposed cause is input
variability: more partners means more competing variants to cope with,
which favours forms that are predictable from their meaning.

## What was actually shown

24 groups (12 of four, 12 of eight) of native Dutch adults played a
typed reference game about 23 scenes of moving shapes over 16 rounds.
Structure, the correlation between string distance and meaning distance in
each participant's labels, rose faster in larger groups and ended higher
(β = 0.11, p = 0.006); larger groups varied less from each other. The
result could have gone the other way: the authors also predicted that
larger groups would converge less, and they did not (convergence and
accuracy ended equal), and the cross-linguistic literature they test
attributes simplification to network density or adult learners, which
were absent here. Input variability was higher in larger groups and at
round n predicted the gain in structure at n + 1, with shared history
adding nothing once it was included.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say the languages of large natural communities are
  simpler because of size.** The experiment removes the confounds; it
  does not show that, in natural populations, size rather than network
  density or adult second-language learning carries the effect.
- **It does not establish the mechanism.** Input variability was
  measured, not varied. Its lagged association with structure is
  consistent with the mechanism and does not exclude others that travel
  with group size (less shared history per pair, more partners to
  coordinate with).
- **It does not hold for any size.** Two sizes were tested, four and
  eight. Nothing is shown about how the effect grows, saturates or
  reverses.
- **It is not about transmission across generations.** No one was
  replaced; this is horizontal interaction. It does not bear directly on
  whether transmission chains converge to the learners' prior
  ([THEORY-155](THEORY-155.md)).
- **"Systematic" here is a string–meaning distance correlation** over a
  meaning space designed to have two separable dimensions. It does not
  measure syntax, morphology or regularity in a natural language's sense.
