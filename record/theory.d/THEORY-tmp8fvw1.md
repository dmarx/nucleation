# THEORY-tmp8fvw1: In the ethical questions that seem to turn on whether someone is the same person, among them prudential concern, responsibility, abortion, advance directives and the separateness of persons, the relation that decides is usually not numerical identity but one that comes apart from it, so the metaphysics of persistence constrains these questions and rarely settles them

## Source

Dufner (2025), "Personal Identity and Ethics", *Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy*, [LIT-130](../literature.d/LIT-130.md), read in [NOTE-166](../notes.d/NOTE-166.md). It is a new entry, first published
30 June 2025, replacing David Shoemaker's at the same URL; a citation of
that URL before mid-2025 is to Shoemaker's text.

## What was actually shown

The entry reports positions; the account is the reading's synthesis of
them ([NOTE-166](../notes.d/NOTE-166.md), Corrections).

- **What matters (§3).** Parfit's fission case: both recipients are
  continuous with me, neither is me, yet I should care about their pain as
  my own (Parfit 1984: 254f). Four candidate views follow: numerical
  identity, continuity, connectedness, or both. The entry endorses none.
- **The biological view concedes (§4).** Its proponents generally grant
  that psychological relations are what matter ethically, and that the
  persistence relation and the relation that matters may come apart (Olson
  1997: 70; 2022).
- **Prudence (§5).** Heavy weight on connectedness implies "a succession of
  overlapping multiple selves" and a discount rate on self-concern. McMahan
  holds a late-stage dementia patient the same person but owed somewhat
  reduced prudential concern.
- **Responsibility (§6).** Positions run from identity (Locke, Butler,
  Reid) through continuity to degree-sensitive connectedness. The
  accountability and attributability distinction lets severe dementia
  remove the first and leave some of the second.
- **Abortion (§7).** Psychological relations may matter "irrespective of
  whether we are numerically identical with fetuses". Biological views
  support a prohibition more strongly than psychological ones, but do not
  force it.
- **Death and advance directives (§8).** "What is relevant … is not whether
  someone's identity has been preserved." For advance directives "numerical
  identity is not decisive": Dworkin's critical interests and Jaworska's
  remaining capacity to value do the work. Dresser (1986) is the exception
  that argues from identity.
- **Separateness (§9).** The appeal "is not specific enough to lend positive
  support to a particular ethical view", and may work at best as a negative
  argument against unrestricted utilitarianism.
- **Priority (§§10–11).** The dependence may run from ethics to what
  matters (Scheffler, Darwall, Korsgaard 1989), and the entry ends with
  three open questions: the right theory of identity, whether identity or
  its constituent relations matter, and which comes first.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The applied debates could have
turned, case by case, on which persistence criterion is true. In the entry's
report they turn on continuity, connectedness, critical interests, the
capacity to value or consciousness, each of which can hold or fail while
identity does not.

## What this does not say

- **That identity never matters.** Uniqueness may matter for positional
  goods: sole ownership, a unique partner, a unique title (Johnston, Wolf,
  Unger). Biological views still bear more strongly on abortion than
  psychological ones.
- **That continuity is what matters.** The entry reports Parfit's argument
  and separates four views; it does not endorse the continuity view, and
  the weight of continuity against connectedness is left open.
- **That the metaphysics is idle.** It constrains: §10 shows that ethics may
  drive what matters, but does not assess whether that makes the
  persistence question practically idle.
- **Anything about copies or artificial agents.** The entry has nothing on
  duplication or uploading beyond the fission case, and nothing on AI.
- **That the survey covers the field.** Four-dimensionalism, constitution
  views and non-Western accounts are absent or passing, and social identity
  is excluded (§1).

  show that identity does not decide them.

  Floridi deflates it.

  uses the same distinction for dementia.
