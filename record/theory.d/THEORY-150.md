# THEORY-150: A platform that sits between the two sides of a market and can cheaply change how it allocates value tends to decay in three stages, first good to its users, then abusing them to favour its business customers, then abusing both to claw the value back for itself, each stage made possible by the lock-in the one before it built; how far it can go is set by the cost of leaving, so lowering that cost is the remedy

## Source

Doctorow (2023), [LIT-731](../literature.d/LIT-731.md), read in [NOTE-573](../notes.d/NOTE-573.md). His 2024 DEF CON talk,
[LIT-033](../literature.d/LIT-033.md), read in [NOTE-041](../notes.d/NOTE-041.md), restates it. His 2025 book, [LIT-753](../literature.d/LIT-753.md), is
unread.

## What was actually shown

Doctorow argues the thesis in [LIT-731](../literature.d/LIT-731.md) from cases; nothing is
measured.

- **The schema.** "Surpluses are first directed to users; then, once
  they're locked in, surpluses go to suppliers; then once they're locked
  in, the surplus is handed to shareholders". Amazon, Facebook and Twitter
  are told this way, and TikTok's "heating" of chosen creators is read as
  the second stage beginning.
- **The cause.** The ease of changing how a platform allocates value,
  combined with a two-sided market in which the platform holds each side
  hostage to the other.
- **What sustains it.** Lock-in through DRM, prepaid services and social
  networks no one can leave alone; blocked interoperability, which
  "increases the amount of enshittification users can be force-fed
  without risking their departure"; and monopoly, with rivals bought out.
- **What limits it.** Users' breaking point. The strategy "only succeeds
  if it is pursued in measured amounts", and the equilibrium just short of
  departure is fragile to competitors, scandals and worker revolt.
- **The remedy.** End-to-end delivery and "freedom of exit", so that
  platforms can die without costing users their communities and data.

## What this does not say

- **Not strictly inevitable.** Doctorow calls it "seemingly inevitable"
  and "a nearly irresistible gravity", and in the same essay makes success
  depend on restraint and on the absence of shocks.
- **Not that platforms should be saved.** "Enshittification truly is how
  platforms die. That's fine, actually."
- **Not about organizations in general.** Its scope is platforms in
  two-sided markets.
- **Not shown.** No platform that stayed good to its users is examined,
  and the figures are the author's, unchecked here.

## Connections

- **The DEF CON talk ([LIT-033](../literature.d/LIT-033.md)).** It keeps the lifecycle and recasts its
  checks as four forces (competition, regulation, interoperability,
  tech-worker power). The 2023 essay has competition, interoperability and
  worker revolt as checks, and regulation only as the remedy.
- **The owner's essay.** §6.4 cites it for alien interests: an
  organization coming to serve an interest at odds with those it is made
  of and for. The record also reads the third stage as §6.3's extraction
  from above. Doctorow's lock-in, built so that abuse can follow, is a
  deliberate cousin of §5.4's protective lock-in, which reproduces its own
  justification.
