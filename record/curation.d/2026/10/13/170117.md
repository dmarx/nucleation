The owner asked for a survey of the Mathematics of Meaning corpus for
material this record could use, then said to start with the literature it
turned up. Four parallel readers covered the prose corpus and MoM's own
record. Their reports named works that MoM's survey entries say its corpus
needed and never cited. Some of those works bear on questions this record is
working, and this record did not hold them.

**Filed, all `Deferred` and unread.** Each is filed from its Crossref or
arXiv record, with no NOTE, and is known by what it is standardly cited for.
Each entry's standing section says which question it is for and what a
reading should check.

- **Boundaries** ([QUESTION-035](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-035.md), [CLAIM-194](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)). Smith & Varzi, "Fiat and Bona
  Fide Boundaries" ([LIT-1024](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1024.md)); Casati & Varzi, *Parts and Places*
  ([LIT-1022](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1022.md)); Weber & Varela, "Life after Kant" ([LIT-1018](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1018.md)). The
  entries note that fiat/bona fide is not [CLAIM-194](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s produced/imposed
  distinction. The autopoietic notion of self-production is the nearer
  match.
- **Composition and the problem of the many** ([QUESTION-009](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-009.md),
  [QUESTION-035](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)). These are van Inwagen, *Material Beings* ([LIT-1016](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1016.md)),
  Unger, "The Problem of the Many" ([LIT-1020](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1020.md)), and Lewis, "Many, but
  almost one" ([LIT-1015](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1015.md)). They are the standard literature on the
  question the distributed-agency line answers with a target-relative
  criterion ([CLAIM-152](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-152.md), [CLAIM-157](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)), which none of them uses.
- **Viability and norms** ([CLAIM-079](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-079.md), [CLAIM-099](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-099.md), [CLAIM-075](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)). Di Paolo,
  "Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency" ([LIT-1017](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1017.md)). It is the
  enactive route from viability to norms, which [CLAIM-075](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)'s disclaimer has to
  face.
- **Transport between systems of distinctions** ([CLAIM-098](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-098.md), [CLAIM-011](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-011.md),
  [QUESTION-005](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-005.md)). Barwise & Seligman, *Information Flow* ([LIT-1019](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1019.md)).
- **Composition as coarse-graining** ([QUESTION-026](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-026.md)). Gallego & Orús,
  "Language Design as Information Renormalization" ([LIT-1023](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1023.md)). It is
  held in both records under [ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md): the anthology holds it as
  [ANTH-LIT-695](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-695.md). It carries `anthology-candidate` last. It is the weakest of
  the ten, filed from a bibliography and linked to the question by
  resemblance.
- **Influence without deception** ([QUESTION-070](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-070.md), [QUESTION-071](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-071.md), [CLAIM-050](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-050.md)).
  Kamenica & Gentzkow, "Bayesian Persuasion" ([LIT-1021](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1021.md)). MoM does not
  cite it. The survey came to it through MoM's framing of reflexive control
  as information design. The entry says that whether persuasion is
  manipulation at all is open.

**Checked.** Every citation was checked against Crossref or arXiv on
2026-10-13, except two first-edition dates. *Material Beings* is dated 1990
from the standard citation, because Crossref's record for its DOI is a 1995
issue. "Many, but almost one" is dated 1993 from the standard citation,
because Crossref holds only the 1999 reprint. Both entries say so. The
anthology (clone at d1573d2) was grepped for each author, title and
identifier. Only Gallego & Orús is there. None of the ten had a LIT here.
Some authors are named in notes, but no entry is for these works.

**MoM is not a co-holder.** MoM's LIT entries are survey entries, each
naming a literature its corpus missed. They are not readings of single works.
So [ADR-037](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-037.md)'s "works held in both" does not apply, and each entry cites the MOM
survey entry only as where the pointer came from.

**One citation was caught and replaced.** The Barwise & Seligman entry first
cited [CLAIM-133](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-133.md) for "correspondence fixes what can be compared". The lint's
inactive-citation report showed [CLAIM-133](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-133.md) is Superseded by [CLAIM-139](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-139.md), and
[CLAIM-139](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-139.md) drops that clause. The entry now cites [CLAIM-011](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-011.md) (Active), which
says the same about anchoring a correspondence.

**Named by the survey and not filed here.** These can go in a later batch if
a question needs them:

  2001), which the van Inwagen entry names;

  already held;

  is already held;

  neo-Aristotelian foil for [CLAIM-075](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-075.md);
- Simon & Ando (1961) on aggregation of variables;
- Thomas (2004) on Russian reflexive control.

The survey also suggested promoting Hacking ([LIT-475](../../record/literature.d/LIT-475.md)) by reading it. That is
a reading, not a filing, and no lawful full text was reachable when it was
filed.
