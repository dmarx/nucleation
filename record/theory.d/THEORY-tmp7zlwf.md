# THEORY-tmp7zlwf: Frey and Schneider argue that platforms offer exit and affective voice but little effective voice

## Source

Frey and Schneider, arXiv:2009.12470 (LIT-tmpqws4y), read in NOTE-tmp1p6j3 as a partial stand-in for LIT-tmpdfowr.

## What was actually shown

An argument by examples. Effective voice: "a form of individual or collective speech that brings about a
binding effect according to transparent processes". Online, "Platforms thus tame the business consequences
of user exit", "Exit is not a real option for most community members", and likes and mutes are "affective
pressure valves in the guise of effective acts". Mechanisms proposed: rules binding on administrators,
selection and removal of administrators, binding thresholds, referenda on governance change.

Corroborates CLAIM-523: affective voice without effective voice is legibility without volitional
efficacy, observed (by example) on platforms.

## What this does not say

No data. It does not say effective voice is always better ("we do not regard one as" a priori superior).
Platforms and peer communities, not public administration or algorithmic decision systems; it is not
Kong and Chang's argument.
