# CLAIM-tmphbje3: An encompassing organization's success does not establish the well-being, viability or autonomy of its constituents — and the converse holds too

## The claim

U37 read enshittification ([THEORY-150](../theory.d/THEORY-150.md)) as evidence that corporate "organisms"
are different kinds of things from humans. U39: "the reverse is also true ...
an organism with an auto-immune disease, or cancer". The assistant's version
(A135): "Constitutive dependence does not entail alignment of interests."
Sen's distinction between institutional outcomes and substantive opportunities
([THEORY-025](../theory.d/THEORY-025.md)) was added later.

## Why it is not in the paper

Credence and inclusion differ: the record believes it, and the paper defers
relations among scales (Section 7.9). The owner's stronger U37 claim — that
corporations are "substantively different kinds of things" — was narrowed to
motivational alienness in the drafts without the narrowing being recorded;
this entry holds the narrow version and names the strong one.
