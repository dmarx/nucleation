# THEORY-tmplookm: Deci and Ryan's General Causality Orientations Scale measures autonomy, control and impersonal orientations as three separately scored and largely independent dispositions, reliable and stable over two months, whose validity rests on correlations with self-report measures in small samples, with behaviour its weakest support

## Source

Deci and Ryan (1985), [LIT-tmp7s2t8](../literature.d/LIT-tmp7s2t8.md), *Journal of Research in Personality*
19, 109–134, read in [NOTE-tmpuvza0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuvza0.md).

## What was actually shown

**The instrument.** Twelve vignettes, each with an autonomy, a control and
an impersonal response rated 1–7, summed into three 12–84 subscales; built
from 96 items by factor analysis, five items rewritten "to improve their
loadings" (pp. 117–118). Scores are levels within a person, not types
(p. 113).

**Reliability.** α (n = 636) .744, .694, .741; each item correlates with
its own subscale (means .48–.52) more than with any other (highest mean
.147), with no overlap of the two distributions; retest over two months
(n = 51) .749, .711, .778 (pp. 118–119, Table 1).

**Structure.** A–C r = .034; A–I −.248; C–I .273 (n = 636, p. 119). The
autonomy and control orientations are not poles of one dimension.

**Validity, correlational.** Table 2 (p. 120): A with supporting children's
autonomy .55 (n = 51), ego development .43 (n = 42), self-esteem .35; I
with Rotter's external control .52, social anxiety .58, self-esteem −.61,
depression .28 (n = 87); C with Type-A .26, public self-consciousness .22;
none with social desirability. Behaviour (pp. 126–128): C with exam grades
−.25 (n = 88); fairness ratings and maternal attachment patterns at
p < .07 and p = .06.

This could have come out otherwise: the autonomy and control subscales
could have been strongly negatively related, as two ends of a continuum,
and the impersonal scale could have tracked nothing beyond Rotter.

## What this does not say

- **That orientations cause behaviour or outcomes.** All results are
  correlations; the behavioural ones are weak by the authors' own account
  ("the behavioral relationships were the least strong", p. 130).
- **That orientations parallel informational, controlling and amotivating
  events.** Hypothesized (p. 113); the paper's evidence for it is the
  pattern of correlations and an unpublished interaction (Kernis 1983).
- **That every prediction held.** The paper says "All of the predicted
  relationships appeared" (p. 123); control with ego development (−.22,
  n = 42) was not significant and impersonal did not predict grades.
- **What the scale's items are.** It is not printed.
