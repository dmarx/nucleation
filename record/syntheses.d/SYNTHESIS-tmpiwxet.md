# SYNTHESIS-tmpiwxet: Maintaining an effective regime and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes are different achievements, since stability does not entail viability: a protective regime can keep itself in place by shaping the evidence and authority that would end it, an organization can record a correction without maintaining the capacity it needs, and resolve, release and a learned prior over which processes govern are among the relations that keep or lose that capacity

*A part account of the will-organization line (ADR-083, ADR-084); the line's account is built over it.*

## The account

**The main result.** CLAIM-025 (the paper's, Section 6.2; Active): an operative
regime, the governing organization among regimes and the capacity to
transform that organization can persist or fail separately. It grounds on
CASE-036 (Northstar's self-sustaining emergency, a variant of CASE-023),
CASE-111 and CASE-112, and uses TERM-020, protective lock-in. CLAIM-079 (the
paper's, from viability theory; demonstrated, Active): stability does not
entail viability. ARG-007 (Active) rests on both, grounds on CASE-036 and
concludes CLAIM-099 (Active), the paper's main result: maintaining a regime
of effective action and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes
"are not the same achievement". CLAIM-018 (Active) reads Canguilhem's
capacity for norm-changing as transformation capacity; its summary says
its absence from ARG-007's premises "is how the record says so", that it is
an interpretation and not a premise. CASE-036's note of 2026-10-22 reads the
emergency as a loss of the evidence needed to lift it, and release as a
separate capacity.

**Access, authority and action.** CLAIM-349 (the owner's idea at U42, in A42's
form; Active) rests on CLAIM-025 and CLAIM-027 and uses TERM-020: access to
a signal, its authority over action, and action on it are separable;
overcontrol "is a maladaptive regime and not more control". CASE-127
(United 173 and crew resource management, the owner's case) illustrates it
and CLAIM-424.

**Correction, release and resolve.** CLAIM-422 (A24 and A25 on the owner's
cases; Active) rests on CLAIM-025 and grounds on CASE-138 (Challenger to
Columbia) and CASE-135 (the Brookhollow fire): a corrective act, a
procedure and a maintained capacity are three things. CLAIM-465 (A24's
line, found in the audit of 2026-10-27; Proposed) grounds on CASE-135 and
complements CLAIM-027 and CLAIM-422: flexibility needs release conditions as
well as escalation, and CLAIM-027's relations and TERM-017 "name
suspension, not release". CLAIM-344 (the owner's frame at U37, A37's
definition; Active) rests on CLAIM-099, CLAIM-277 and CLAIM-331: resolve
keeps a policy effective while keeping the capacity to revise it, and mere
persistence is not resolve. CASE-188 (proposed by the owner) illustrates it
as a study separating corrigible resolve from entrenchment. CASE-128 (A18's,
at the owner's U18; Active; authenticity first), a variant of CASE-013,
illustrates it too: a password given to a friend in calm and refused under
temptation, beside the same restriction imposed without consent; its
summary says the warrant for the override "can be the agent's own earlier
authorization, and that it is read from history, not present behaviour". CLAIM-359 (the
owner's question at U41, A41's answer; Active; distributed-agency first)
rests on CLAIM-025 and CLAIM-282: robustness to compromised constituents
lies in the processes that allocate, audit and revise authority, and can
trade off against self-governance.

**Learned authority and governance-maintained anxiety.** CLAIM-443 (the
owner's, 2026-10-23; Active) rests on CLAIM-277: a learned prior over which
processes govern is a candidate mechanism for durable authority, "but
persistence is not amortization". QUESTION-045 (the fourth synthesis pass;
Open) asks whether a disorder that outlasts its trigger is a protective
lock-in at the individual scale; QUESTION-292, split from it by the status
review of 2026-10-27, is Answered by THEORY-053. CLAIM-446 (the owner's,
2026-10-23; Proposed) answers QUESTION-045, resting on CLAIM-443, CLAIM-283
and CLAIM-349 and using TERM-020: some persistent anxiety may be
governance-maintained, since under the avoidance that authority selects
danger and safety predict the same observation. CLAIM-445 (the owner's;
Proposed; distributed-agency first) rests on CLAIM-446 and carries the
mechanism to institutions and states: a protective regime becomes
self-maintaining when its interventions shift the evidence and authority
that judge it.

## Links tested as claims

None is filed as a claim.

- **Governance-maintained anxiety as escalation without release.** That
  CLAIM-446's regime is CLAIM-465's case. Wrong on the components' own terms:
  CLAIM-446's regime has a release condition, disconfirmation, which the
  avoidance it selects keeps from being observed. The narrower link, that a
  release condition is idle where the escalated regime controls the evidence
  that would meet it, is CASE-036's note of 2026-10-22.
