# ARG-tmpj58hf: Simondon's account of individuation as crystallization from a metastable state, propagating from kin to kin, supports the thesis that an argument's later structure can evolve only on structure that persists

## The inference

Simondon models individuation on a crystal growing in a metastable solution. Structure propagates "from kin to kin", each structured region serving as the principle of the next (Simondon 2005, p. 32, as quoted by Bencherki and Iliadis; [THEORY-tmp1g3ve](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp1g3ve.md)). [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md) holds the same of arguments: a persistent structure is available as an anchor, and later structure grows only on anchors. If Simondon's account held of individuation in general, it would hold of arguments too.

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are the cases alike?** In form. Each has structure that propagates from a seed in a medium able to take it up.
- **What transfers?** The vocabulary, and the warning carried by [CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) that a formed crystal can no longer be reorganized.
- **What does not?** The mechanism, and this is the question that fails. A crystal grows because a surface lowers a free-energy barrier, which the record reads in Vekilov and Ostwald. Nothing shows that an argument's anchors work by anything of that kind. Simondon generalizes from the crystal by analogy, as [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md) does, so his account adds a second analogy, not evidence. His book ([LIT-1648](../literature.d/LIT-1648.md)) is unread here, and his readers use the generalization without a test ([NOTE-tmphu9ok](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphu9ok.md)).

Rejected as support. It stays an ancestor of the line's image.
