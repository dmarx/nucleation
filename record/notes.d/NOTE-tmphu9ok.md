# NOTE-tmphu9ok: Iliadis — Informational ontology

## Contribution

A short exegesis that recasts Simondon, then mostly read in English
through Deleuze and through his writing on technics, as a philosopher of
information. It argues that his ontology is an extension of the
mathematical theory of communication and of cybernetics, not a
rejection of them, and names the result an "informational ontology".

## Key insight

Information is not a thing transmitted but what arises between two
disparate realities when an individuation makes them one system. In
Iliadis's translation of Simondon, information is "never a given thing";
it is "a meaning that arises when an operation of individuation
discovers that the two disparate yet real dimensions may be a system of
information" (p. 11).

## Assumptions

- The quotations are the author's own translations from the 2007 Aubier
  reprint of *L'individuation psychique et collective* (fn. 1, fn. 27).
  Nothing is quoted from the first half of the thesis.
- Cybernetics is read through Shannon, Wiener and Osgood, as having
  already acknowledged a "semantic" remainder that Simondon takes up.

## Key results

The results are interpretive.

1. **Simondon extends cybernetics** (pp. 5–9). Where the cyberneticists
   allowed for semantic information "about" and "for" reality,
   Simondon thought "interoperability and indeterminacy" in terms of
   information "as" reality (p. 7, using Floridi's distinction).
2. **Metastability** (pp. 10–11). Information exists "in two ordered
   states of disparation"; it passes from "metastability to stability";
   there is no "unity and identity of information, because information
   is not an end; it requires a system".
3. **Individuation** (pp. 11–12). "The notion of form must be replaced
   by that of information"; "Information is the formula of
   individuation".
4. **Transduction** (pp. 12–13). "Transduction indicates the meeting of
   two disparate informational realms and signals the beginning of the
   process of individuation. It points to the emergence of a new
   informational structure, one that resolves a disparity between
   fields" (p. 13). The example is Simondon's air-cooled engine, where
   one element performs several functions.
5. **Concretization** (pp. 14–16). The technical object's non-additive
   self-integration, "the engine that drives individuation", with
   information "the gas that keeps concretization working". This is
   where the author places his own work.

## Concepts

- **Informational ontology.** Iliadis's name for Simondon's view that
  information, understood as the resolution of disparity, is the
  principle of individuation.
- **Disparation.** The tension between two domains, "the previous realms
  from which the new informational 'entity' emerges" (p. 11).
- **Transduction.** The meeting of disparate informational realms that
  begins an individuation and yields a new structure.
- **Concretization (concrétisation).** A technical object's progress
  toward self-sufficiency by re-purposing its own elements.

## Limitations

- **No argument that could fail.** The claims are interpretive, carried
  by quotation, and several turns are rhetorical (the "clairvoyant"
  foresight, p. 11; the Wayne's World parallax, p. 14).
- **Thin textual base.** Only the second half of the thesis is quoted,
  in the author's translation, with page numbers but often without the
  surrounding sentence.
- **The cybernetics claim is the weakest.** It rests on parallels
  between Simondon's open informational schema and remarks by Osgood and
  Wiener, not on what Simondon wrote about them.
- **Untidy text.** "mathematical theory of commutation" (p. 5) for
  communication; the abstract says "addresses" for "addressed".

## Open questions

- Whether "information as resolution of disparity" can be stated so that
  something counts as a failure to inform. As stated, every change of
  structure qualifies.
- Whether the four concepts are Simondon's ordering or the author's; the
  English translation ([LIT-1648](../literature.d/LIT-1648.md)) would settle it.

### Pragmatic transport

- **CLAIM-tmpeli2w** offers an operational answer to this note's first open question. An action fails to inform a population if, after exposure, neither its response function on held-out items nor the set of questions it can decide changes.

### Distributed agency and individuation

- **QUESTION-285.** Iliadis sets information "exchanged between beings already individuated" against information "within systems to come that produce a new individuation" (p. 11). That parallels a fixed apparatus against one whose discriminations are revised. Same structure only.
- **TERM-088.** The paper's individuation is genetic, which is none of TERM-088's four senses.
- **QUESTION-281.** "metastability to stability" (p. 11) is the only trace of the dynamical sense. Simondon's crystal (LIT-1648) would settle it.

### Nucleating argument and formal apparatus

- **The open question "whether anything counts as a failure to inform"** has an answer in decision-theoretic terms (CLAIM-503, note of 2026-10-31). An experiment within ε of an uninformative one improves no [0, 1]-loss decision problem by more than ε, and this is decided before any outcome. This is a translation, not an identity: Simondon's disparity is not a likelihood.
- **The crystal.** Iliadis does not use it, but it is Simondon's model case (LIT-1648's summary) and the source of this record's own image (QUESTION-037, CLAIM-185).
