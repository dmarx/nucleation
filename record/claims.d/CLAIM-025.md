# CLAIM-025: An operative regime, the governing organization among regimes, and the capacity to transform that organization can persist or fail separately

## The claim

"An organization may preserve the first two while degrading the third ... it
may become increasingly competent at operating inside the emergency regime
while becoming less capable of changing the organization that keeps the regime
in place."

## Note of 2026-10-12: evidence now linked

[THEORY-145](../theory.d/THEORY-145.md) (Argyris and Schön) now grounds this claim: errors are corrected within the governing values while the values themselves go unexamined. Two caveats. Argyris's "governing values" are not exactly this claim's governing organization among regimes. The evidence is from workshops.

## Note of 2026-10-18: a graded form, and three freedoms

- **A9 §9 of the exchange of 2026-10-17.** "**Freedom to endorse an
  alternative is not the same as freedom to make that endorsement
  effective; freedom to make it effective is not the same as freedom to
  sustain it; and freedom to sustain it is not the same as freedom to
  revise it.**" That is this claim's three-way split, with the attitude
  added in front, stated as degrees: "Those are distinct control
  capabilities with potentially different quantitative profiles." A9's
  Candidate C is this claim with endorsement added, and was not filed.
  A9's "endorse" is the reflective sense, not TERM-053's.
- **A11 §4.** "Freedom to commit: The capacity to choose and establish
  constraints", "Freedom under commitment: The viable alternatives
  remaining after the commitment", "Freedom to revise: The capacity to
  reconsider or exit the commitment under appropriate conditions". "None
  can replace the others." The third is this claim's capacity to transform
  the organization. The case is CASE-013.
- A7 §4 and A8 §4 gave models in which a capacity is lost apart from the
  regime it governs (CASE-tmpi1bt1, CASE-tmpiyvbk). The graded thesis built
  on this claim is CLAIM-tmpilfhr.
