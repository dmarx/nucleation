# THEORY-072: Organisms differ from dissipative structures such as a candle flame not by far-from-equilibrium self-maintenance, nor by circular self-maintenance alone, but by organisational differentiation: several distinct constraints that produce one another at different time scales

## Source

- Mossio, Saborido & Moreno (2009), [LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md), read in [NOTE-444](../notes.d/NOTE-444.md):
  §4.1 (closure, with Bénard cells and the candle flame as worked
  examples), §4.2 (organisational differentiation), endnote viii.
- Montévil & Mossio (2015), [LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md), read in [NOTE-468](../notes.d/NOTE-468.md):
  Definitions 1–4, the remark that closure needs both slow and fast
  dependence, footnote 25 (closure versus a cycle of processes), and
  footnotes 4 and 27 (closure does not define the organism).

## The question

[NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md) reports the dispute. Closure accounts, autopoiesis and
teleodynamics are each charged with failing to separate a living system
from a self-organising dissipative structure such as a candle flame or a
tornado. Until this batch the record held no answer. Prigogine ([LIT-516](../literature.d/LIT-516.md),
[NOTE-424](../notes.d/NOTE-424.md)) gives the dissipative-structure criterion: an open system held
far from equilibrium, maintained by flows, whose order is born from an
instability. [NOTE-424](../notes.d/NOTE-424.md) records that a flame meets it on the same terms,
and so, by the lecture's own §1, do living systems. Friston ([LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md))
names the flame and excludes it in one sentence, by a criterion that
[THEORY-073](THEORY-073.md) argues cannot do that work.

## The claim, in three steps

**1. Far-from-equilibrium self-maintenance is not the line.** Organisms
and flames both have it. That is Prigogine's picture as [NOTE-424](../notes.d/NOTE-424.md) reads
it, and Mossio et al. begin from it. A self-maintaining system is one in
which a macroscopic pattern exerts "at least one 'top-down' constraint"
that keeps up the flow enabling its microscopic dynamics ([NOTE-444](../notes.d/NOTE-444.md)).

**2. Closure in the broad sense is not the line either.** Mossio et al.
give the flame as a worked example. The flame keeps the temperature
above the combustion threshold, vaporises wax and drives the convection
that brings oxygen in, and the reactions in turn keep generating the
flame ([LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md), p. 9). That is "organizational closure, i.e. a
circular causal relation between some macroscopic … pattern … and the
microscopic … dynamics". "Minimal self-maintaining systems, such as
flames or hurricanes, do instantiate closure, but their components do
not have functions."

**3. The line is differentiation.** "Dissipative systems make a unique
contribution to their own maintenance." In a flame the many chemical
components "all 'converge' to generate a single macroscopic pattern", so
no component makes a contribution of its own. Functions, and with them
the organism's kind of organisation, need "different and localizable
patterns or structures, each making a specific contribution" (§4.2).
Material complexity is not the same thing: a flame has many molecular
species and is still undifferentiated. Endnote viii describes how a
single-catalyst autocatalytic set becomes differentiated, and so
functional, once it also makes a membrane.

**The 2015 closure says the same thing in another vocabulary.** This is
the record's reading, not either paper's. Montévil and Mossio
redefine closure. A constraint is something that acts on a process while
its relevant aspects stay conserved at that process's time scale
(Definition 1). Closure is a set of constraints in which every member
depends directly on another member and is generative for another
(Definition 4), and such a set "must include" both a slow and a fast
dependence. A single macroscopic pattern cannot satisfy that by itself:
it would have to depend on and generate another constraint in the set,
and there is no other. A cycle of transformations also fails, because
its entities "do not act as constraints on each other" (footnote 25, the
hydrologic cycle). So the 2015 closure already requires several distinct
constraints, each with its own contribution and its own time scale. That
is differentiation in the 2009 sense, built into closure. [NOTE-468](../notes.d/NOTE-468.md)
and [NOTE-444](../notes.d/NOTE-444.md) both reach this reading and both mark it as an
inference. The title states the line in the form common to the two
papers.

## What this does not say

- **It does not define life or the organism.** "Although an organism
  necessarily realises closure, a system realising closure is not
  necessarily an organism" ([LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md), footnote 27). The claim is
  that this condition, which organisms have, is what flame-like
  dissipative structures lack. It is a necessary condition that
  separates the two, not a sufficient condition for being alive. A
  protocell or an autocatalytic set with a membrane may meet it, and the
  account takes no view on whether such a system is alive.
- **No paper shows that a flame fails the 2015 closure.** Neither paper
  analyses one. Montévil and Mossio leave open whether "some specific
  classes of non-biological natural systems (such as, for instance,
  complex chemical systems) might be pertinently said to realise
  closure" (footnote 4). The obvious test is the candle itself. The wick
  is a constraint on the flow of fuel, but the flame does not make it.
  The wax pool is made by the flame's heat, but it is fuel consumed by
  the process, not a constraint conserved through it. On that reading the
  flame has one constraint of its own and fails. The reading is the
  record's, and promote_when asks for it to be done properly.
- **"Differentiation" has no measure.** The 2009 paper leaves "different
  and localizable" informal ([NOTE-444](../notes.d/NOTE-444.md), Limitations). The 2015
  definitions make it checkable in principle, but they have been applied
  only to fragments and toy diagrams.
- **It does not answer Baedke's objection, which is a different
  question.** Baedke ([LIT-167](../literature.d/LIT-167.md), [NOTE-148](../notes.d/NOTE-148.md)) argues that closure and
  persistence criteria are too permissive, because colonies, holobionts
  and ecosystems meet them. That objection is about which living
  individual counts as the organism, not about where life ends and flame
  begins. [LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)'s tendency to closure and nested closures are its
  answer to that question, and they are labelled preliminary.
- **It takes Nahas and Sachs's first check.** [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md) asks any account
  of this kind to say which project it serves. This one is a claim about
  what organisms are, the philosophical project. It does not say that a
  model in biology must demarcate, which Nahas and Sachs argue it need
  not (C3 in [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)).
- **It is not about agency.** Being an organism in this sense is not
  being an agent, and [THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md) adds two further conditions for
  that. Nor is it about any of the four unities of [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md).

## Connections

- **Friston's criterion ([LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md)).** It is an account of the same
  demarcation, so it is this account's natural rival. But the record
  holds it as a LIT corrected by two critiques, with no THEORY stating
  it, so no `rivals` is declared. The two share an ingredient. Friston
  says a blanket needs couplings that "change slowly with respect to the
  states", and Montévil and Mossio say a constraint is conserved at the
  time scale of the process it acts on. Both put the line at a
  separation of time scales. They differ on production. For closure, the
  slow structures must be made by processes that the system's other
  constraints shape. A blanket needs only to persist, and [THEORY-073](THEORY-073.md)
  argues it cannot represent a boundary the system makes.
- **Deacon ([LIT-529](../literature.d/LIT-529.md), unread).** Nahas and Sachs report his teleodynamics
  as the coupling of two mutually limiting dissipative processes
  ([NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md), §2.1). As reported, it also draws the line at more than
  one self-organising process constraining another, and it is the
  nearest neighbour of this account. The book is Deferred, so nothing
  here rests on it.
- **[THEORY-030](THEORY-030.md) (Landauer).** It notes that a structure held away from
  equilibrium "dissipates continually". That standing cost belongs to
  flames and cells alike, which fits step 1. The thermodynamic theories
  in the record ([THEORY-030](THEORY-030.md), [THEORY-026](THEORY-026.md)) price information processing.
  Neither draws or depends on this line, and no relation is declared.
- **[THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md) (minimal agency)** builds agency on an autonomous
  organisation of mutually enabling processes. It does not extend this
  account, and the reason is given there.
- **"What Lives?" ([LIT-211](../literature.d/LIT-211.md), [NOTE-109](../notes.d/NOTE-109.md)).** Its "Dissipative
  Self-Organizing Systems" and "Cognitive Autonomy" clusters map onto the
  two sides of this line, and this account says what separates them.
