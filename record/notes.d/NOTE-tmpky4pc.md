# NOTE-tmpky4pc: Mazinani and O'Connor — An Updated and Revised Dyadic Model of Partner Violence

## Contribution

A conceptual reorganization of existing meta-analytic associations within
Bartholomew and Cobb's (2011) multilevel dyadic model, keeping perpetration
and victimization separate and noting gender differences. It adds no new
data. Its value for the record is the authors' explicit separation of
violence by both partners from symmetry of control, motive or
responsibility.

## Key results

- **Scope** (Abstract): "Eight peer-reviewed meta-analyses published
  between 2000 and 2024 formed the core synthesis."
- **Main pattern** (Abstract): "the most consistent risk markers of IPV
  clustered within the relationship-context level, particularly prior
  partner aggression, emotional abuse, and relationship dissatisfaction."
- **Design** (§3.1): "This article is a selective review and conceptual
  synthesis of meta-analytic evidence, rather than a systematic or
  structured review. It was not preregistered."
- **The term bounded** (§2.3): "In this review, bidirectional violence
  refers only to relationships in which both partners were reported to
  have used violent acts. The term does not imply equivalence in motive,
  coercive control, severity, consequences, or responsibility."
- **Beside Johnson** (§2.2): "Johnson’s framework is primarily a typology
  of violent patterns, while the dyadic model is a multilevel process
  framework"; "The frameworks therefore address different questions and
  can be used together". The dyadic model "should not be interpreted as
  assuming symmetric control, equivalent agency, or shared
  responsibility"; "responsibility for a violent or coercive act lies with
  the person who commits it."
- **What act counts miss** (§7): "Effect sizes based primarily on the
  occurrence of physical acts may not capture motive, coercive control,
  fear, chronicity, severity, injury, sexual violence, or constraints on
  safety and leaving."
- **Resistance** (§7): where intimate terrorism holds, "a victim’s
  resistance or constrained responses should not be treated as equivalent
  to the perpetrator’s controlling violence".
- **Responsibility** (§7): "Dyadic conceptualization does not assume that
  both partners contribute equally or that responsibility for violence is
  shared."
- **Control as a marker** (§5.1, from Spencer et al. 2022): "controlling
  behavior (r=0.30), and greater perpetrator power within the relationship
  (r=0.18)".
- **Self-limitation** (§7): "the present synthesis does not constitute
  empirical validation of the model’s integrated temporal or causal
  pathways."
- **Practice** (§7.4): "Couple-focused approaches should not be assumed
  appropriate in relationships characterized by coercive control or
  substantial safety risk."

## Assumptions

- Placing a marker at a level is "theory-guided conceptual mapping" by the
  first author alone, with no reliability check (§3.3).
- Physical IPV in adult heterosexual relationships stands in for the
  phenomenon.
- The source meta-analyses are treated as associations, and their overlap
  in primary studies is not quantified.

## Concepts

- **Dyadic model of partner violence**: background, relationship-context
  and situational levels feeding the pattern of violence.
- **Risk marker** as against risk factor and predictor.
- **Bidirectional violence**: both partners reported to have used violent
  acts, and nothing more.
- Johnson's intimate terrorism, situational couple violence and violent
  resistance; no fourth type is named.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-709](../claims.d/CLAIM-709.md)**: corroborates the separation, as a methodological
  stance: violent acts by both partners do not imply equivalence in
  "coercive control ... or responsibility", and act-based effect sizes "may
  not capture" coercive control. It is an interpretive guard, not a test.
- **[CASE-279](../cases.d/CASE-279.md)**: "a victim’s resistance or constrained responses should
  not be treated as equivalent to the perpetrator’s controlling violence"
  is the reading of Carol's strikes the case needs. The review adds no
  evidence of how often such cases occur.
- **[TERM-tmp8pnnr](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8pnnr.md)**: the authors' "responsibility for a violent or
  coercive act lies with the person who commits it" is culpability for
  the particular act, kept apart from the control context.

## Limitations

Selective and not reproducible: the authors say their search logs "cannot
now be verified". One coder; no appraisal of the source reviews' quality;
effect sizes from overlapping sources; physical IPV only. The authors
disclose that ChatGPT (GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6) assisted "the drafting and
revision of manuscript text". The prose discusses more meta-analyses than
the eight the abstract counts. Nothing on mutual violent control.
