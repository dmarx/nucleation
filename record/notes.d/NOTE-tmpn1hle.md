# NOTE-tmpn1hle: Isaac — Phenomenal structure: what is it and what is it for?

## Contribution

It tries to say what a structuralist science of consciousness is, at a
grain fine enough to avoid the platitude that every science is
structural. It names three kinds of mathematical structure in experience
(metric quality spaces, groups of transformations under which constancies
are invariant, vector fields of force), gives each a function (meaning,
objectivity, agency), and gives the programme a method (iterative
de-idealization of isomorphisms among experience, world and brain) and an
epistemology (convergence of independently derived models). Its new
element, against Lyre's similarity structuralism, is that geometry is not
enough: the higher layers carry what is distinctive of experience.

## Key insight

"I do not represent the category chair as a set of possible chairs, but as
a group of transformations across which chair-ness remains invariant"
(§2.2). Constancy is invariance under transformations that act *within
experience*, not only in the world, and the gap between the varying
"subjective" level (the shadowed part of the wall looks darker) and the
invariant "objective" level (the wall looks uniformly off-white), both
present at once, is where the subject–object distinction comes from.
Following Cassirer, perception "is a process of objectification, the
characteristic nature and tendency of which finds expression in the
formation of invariants" (§3, quoting Cassirer 1944). On this view the
checker-shadow "illusion" is veridical recovery of surface reflectance,
not error.

## Assumptions

- **Structuralism as a stance.** "structuralism as developed here is not a
  theoretical assertation [sic] or result, but rather a stance" (§1). It is
  judged by the trajectory of its models, not by any one isomorphism.
- **Mathematics by conceptual analysis.** That constancy has
  group-theoretic structure, and felt pushes and pulls vector-field
  structure, is "conceptual" and "not empirical": group theory "just is
  the abstract mathematical formalization of the qualitative concept of
  invariance across transformations" (§2.2), and a vector is "just the
  mathematical object characterized by magnitude and direction" (§2.3).
  The empirical questions are how far experience is actually invariant,
  and whether there are such forces in experience.
- **Isomorphism is used loosely**, as "structure preserving map",
  homomorphisms included. Technical worries such as Newman's objection are
  set aside because the term is "suggestive, metaphorical" at the
  methodological level (§4).
- **Indirect perception.** It takes the gestalt and Cassirer side against
  Gibson and Noë: the world as experienced is distinct from the physical
  environment, and only that distinction makes the search for isomorphisms
  between them coherent (§3).
- **Phenomenal claims by introspection.** Its claims about levels of
  colour experience are offered for the reader to "verify through
  introspection and reflection", with no position on the mechanisms of
  perception (footnote 6).
- **Specific character only.** It addresses phenomenal experience, "one
  facet of consciousness" (§1). It says nothing about what makes a state
  conscious rather than not.

## Key results

Nothing is proved or tested. The paper's claims, in order:

- **Geometric structure (§2.1).** Quality spaces are bounded regions with
  topological and metrical structure, measured by just-noticeable
  differences, ratio and magnitude judgements, matching, bisection and
  semantic descriptors (Dravnieks' 146-dimensional odour atlas). Visual
  space is formally one more quality space. Three dimensions are an
  idealization for colour: asymmetric matching needs more than three
  (Niederée 2010), and surface features such as self-luminosity motivate
  up to five (Evans 1974). Olfaction may need from 6 to thousands.
- **Group-theoretic structure (§2.2).** Constancies (colour, size, shape,
  timbre) are invariants under transformations of perspective and
  context, modelled after Klein's Erlangen programme. They must be
  invariants of transformations *internal to experience*, for two reasons:
  - experienced shape is not a planar projection, since convergence in
    depth is experienced as depth, and measured perceived angles do not
    match those of a linear projection (Erkelens 2015; Wagner et al. 2018);
  - the transformations can be realized with no physical analogue:
    apparent rotation between two static images (Shepard 1984), and
    mental-rotation times linear in angle (Shepard and Cooper 1982).
  Constancy is "at best approximate" (Durgin et al. 2012; Daoust 2021).
  The illumination model is a "rough, toy model", and Krantz's Grassmann
  structures for metamers are strictly groupoids, not groups (footnote 5).
- **Dynamic structure (§2.3).** Gestalt laws of organization, valence
  (Lewin's *Aufforderungscharakter*) and affordance are forces in
  experience, modelled by vector fields. Affordances "must have degrees of
  invitational character which compete, i.e. induce a vector field",
  against Gibson's positive or negative. Quantification is shown only as
  "proof of concept": lever-press rates, Warden's electrified-floor
  obstruction method, Franchak and Adolph's frequencies, questionnaires.
- **Functions (§3).** Contrast within a space makes a quality apt to carry
  information, and an isolated shade is "not apt to play any
  representational role". The similarity structure is antecedent
  structure co-opted for representation, like the arrangement of pixels
  or of silver halide. Constancy turns flux into "a stable representation
  of an objective world". Gestalt segregation into wholes introduces
  "baseline meaning in the form of a normative part-whole structure".
  Valence fields guide action; the hot pot handle invites the grasp
  despite the knowledge that it is hot.
- **Method (§4).** Inner and outer psychophysics (Fechner) become the
  search for isomorphisms among three domains. Following Köhler and
  Shepard, these are "functional" or second-order isomorphisms, of
  relations among representations to relations among their objects
  (Shepard and Chipman 1970). Worked cases: CIELAB, a homomorphism from
  spectra into colour experience that fails through hue
  superimportance (Judd 1970) and context; DKL, an LGN-based space that
  distorts CIELAB distances and misplaces the unique hues (Schloss et al.
  2018; Wuerger et al. 2005). The failures direct de-idealization, on the
  model of residual-phenomena progress in astronomy (Smith 2014).
  Loudness is a straightforward phenomenal model whose neural correlate is
  "mysterious and complex": firing rate turned out not to be isomorphic to
  loudness.
- **Epistemology (§5).** Three answers to how a third-personal science
  targets experience. Relations between experiences, unlike bare
  qualities, are made "behaviorally manifest" and so comparable across
  subjects. Introspection, including phenomenology "front-loaded" into
  design, keeps the target fixed. And, the author's "own considered view",
  the strongest evidence is **model convergence**: CIELAB (matching) and
  OSA-UCS (reflective judgements of equal distance) agree coarsely, with
  some distortion (Oleari 2001), and models derived from different kinds
  of behaviour that converge "target an underlying phenomenal experience,
  because such experience is the least common denominator". Convergence
  is "never perfect", and discrepancies often exceed each method's own
  uncertainty, which others take as evidence that no well-defined metric
  exists (MacLeod and Willen 1995; Decock 2006; Barwich 2019).
- **The closing puzzle (§6).** The models average over subjects, so
  their target is a "standard observer", not any individual's experience.

## Concepts

- **Quality space**: the bounded region of possible phenomenal qualities
  of one modality, with topological (nearness) and metrical (distance)
  structure (Clark, Gärdenfors, Rosenthal).
- **Group-theoretic structure**: invariants of experience under a set of
  transformations with identity, associative composition and inverses.
  Here it is "conceptual", the formalization of invariance under
  transformation, applied loosely (groupoids included).
- **Levels or modes of experience**: the "subjective" level that varies
  with context (aperture mode; the "painter's attitude") and the
  "objective" level that stays constant (surface mode), present together.
- **Dynamic structure**: forces in experience, as against kinematics,
  modelled by vector fields over a space: gestalt organization, valence,
  affordance.
- **Behavioural field**: Koffka's experienced space of objects that invite
  and repel, as against the geographical environment.
- **Second-order isomorphism**: Shepard and Chipman's correspondence
  between relations among representations and relations among their
  objects; first-order isomorphism, where it exists, is a mechanism for
  realizing it.
- **De-idealization**: refining a model of world, experience or brain so
  that the maps among them improve, driven by residual discrepancies.
- **Model convergence**: agreement in structure between models of one
  quality space built from different behavioural evidence.

## Connections

The paper looks back on purpose: Koffka (1935), Köhler, Lewin, Cassirer's
"The concept of group and the theory of perception" (1944) and Shepard's
work of the 1970s and 1980s, through Hatfield on visual space and
Westheimer (2024) on Cassirer. Among contemporary structuralists it cites
Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021) and Lyre (2022) (LIT-1344, read in
NOTE-1060) as the convergence it joins, Loorits (2014) on the hard
problem, and Decock (2006) for the physicalist reinterpretation of
phenomenal spaces. It continues the author's own papers on psychophysics
and the geometry of colour (2013), structural realism for secondary
qualities (2014) and "The allegory of isomorphism" (2019). Its affordance
field is Reed's (1993) and Rietveld and Kiverstein's (2014). Negro
(NOTE-1054) and Aldé (NOTE-1055), read in the same batch,
attack Lyre's version and do not cite this paper, and it does not cite
them.

## Bearing on the record

- **Two THEORYs warranted, both Proposed.** THEORY-tmpt62hk states
  the constancy account (C2–C3) as the paper supports it.
  THEORY-tmpeq5py states the convergence epistemology (C7), with
  the failures of convergence that the paper itself concedes.
- **THEORY-880, THEORY-867 and THEORY-868.** Isaac is not the
  similarity structuralist those objections target. He agrees with Lyre
  that geometry is the first layer, but denies that it is enough ("The
  geometrical structure of phenomenal experience by itself is not enough
  to generate meaning", §3), and never says that an experience is nothing
  but its place in a space. Aldé's point that colour-space geometry does
  not mark the unique hues (THEORY-867) is consistent with Isaac's DKL
  case, where a neural space misplaces the unique hues; Isaac treats it
  as a residual to de-idealize, not as a refutation. His stance is the
  "methods, not metaphysics" structuralism that Negro (THEORY-868)
  allows. The comparison is mine.
- **QUESTION-255.** The question named Isaac's dynamical structures
  as candidate material for a formal object that holds unresolved
  conflicts together. The paper does give one shape for it: a vector field
  of competing valences and affordances over one experienced space, where
  the hot handle's invitation and the remembered heat's repulsion are
  experienced together, as forces in one field. But the paper has no
  cover, no local sections and no gluing condition, and its field
  composes forces by superposition, which is always globally consistent.
  So it is a candidate for a kind-(1) reading of felt conflict (one global
  state that holds both sides), not a model of kind (2). The inference is
  mine.
- **QUESTION-254 and CLAIM-412.** The record filed this paper
  because its group-theoretic layer "induces a distinction between subject
  and object", one candidate for what a perspective adds to an
  interpretation. The reading finds the claim stated, after Cassirer, and
  supported only by the co-presence of a varying and an invariant level
  in experience. It gives no criterion that would separate a system that
  extracts invariants (as any object recognizer does) from one for which
  there is a subject–object distinction. So it does not answer
  QUESTION-254. For CLAIM-412, Isaac's layers are a structural
  analogue of interpretive organization (contrast, invariance, valence
  that gives significance and guides action), and like Lyre he addresses
  only the character of experience, not why any process is conscious
  (CLAIM-411's gap remains).
- **CLAIM-138.** Isaac sets the Newman objection aside, because the
  isomorphisms are "suggestive, metaphorical" at the level of method. His
  relations are in practice fixed by named behavioural procedures
  (matching, equal-distance judgements, descriptor ratings), which is the
  independently specified relations that CLAIM-138 says concede
  non-structural input. He can pay that cost because he makes no claim
  that relations alone fix what an experience is. A further instance for
  the claim's parallel; the comparison is mine.
- **CLAIM-368.** Isaac's valence field and the record's frustration of
  maintained demands are both about competing pulls. The paper's tea
  towel, placed over the handle to remove its affordance from the visual
  field, is a case of managing a demand by altering the experienced field,
  not by satisfying it. A note for the consolidator, not a correction.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- **Programmatic.** Nothing is tested, and the author says the
  group-theoretic and dynamic layers "are mere suggestive sketches" and
  asks "How exactly should they be characterized?" (§6).
- **"Conceptual, not empirical" does too much.** That invariance can be
  formalized by groups is analytic; that perceptual constancy *has* the
  closure, identity and inverse properties of a group is not, and the
  paper's own cases fall short: the illumination toy model's "inverse" of
  adding a light is defined by filtering the white baseline, so it works
  only from that baseline, and Krantz's structures are groupoids
  (footnote 5). "Group-theoretic" is used for invariance in general.
- **The objectivity claim has no test.** Nothing in the paper would show
  that a constancy-extracting process lacks a subject–object distinction,
  so C3 cannot fail on the paper's evidence.
- **Convergence is not shown to beat the rival explanation.** Models
  built from different behaviour could converge because they share the
  stimulus geometry or a common decision process, not experience. The
  paper's "least common denominator" argument does not exclude this, and
  it concedes that discrepancies often exceed stated uncertainties.
- **Isomorphism is loose by design.** By setting the mathematical
  conditions aside, the paper cannot say when a map counts as structure
  preserving enough, or what a failed isomorphism shows, beyond "refine".
- **Averaging.** Its own closing puzzle: the models target a standard
  observer, so whether the programme reaches any individual's experience
  is left open.
- **Specific character only.** Whether a state is conscious is outside
  its scope.

## Open questions

- What property of a transformation structure, beyond invariance, would
  make it a subject–object distinction? A criterion that some
  invariant-extracting systems fail would make C3 testable.
- How much convergence, measured against what null (shared stimulus
  geometry, shared response process), is evidence that models target
  experience? A comparison of model convergence with and without a
  shared stimulus metric would bear on C7.
- Can valence and affordance fields be measured as fields, with
  direction and magnitude at each point of an experienced space, rather
  than as single rates? The paper's citations measure scalar strengths.
- Can de-idealization reach individual experience, or only the standard
  observer (§6)?
