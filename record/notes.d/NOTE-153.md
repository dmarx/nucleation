# NOTE-153: Rizi — What is emergence, after all?

## Contribution

The paper observes that "emergence" is increasingly used, often imprecisely and sometimes mystically, for collective behaviours such as flocking, synchronisation and collective intelligence. As a perspective piece, it recasts emergence as a measurable phenomenon grounded in physics. Using temperature, magnetism and herd immunity on social networks as examples, it shows how collective behaviour arises from local interactions under global constraints, and argues that a properly framed notion yields insight rather than mysticism.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction (p. 1): traces the concept from Mill and Lewes through the British emergentists, multiple realisability and Anderson's "More is different"; Fig. 1 charts the rise of the word in Google Books.
- Levels of description / coarse-graining (pp. 1–2): what counts as emergent is partly epistemic, but emergent behaviour has objective signatures; formalised as lossy compression retaining information relevant to prediction and control.
- A New Ontology (pp. 2–3): temperature as a coarse-grained variable meaningful only in the thermodynamic limit, with LLM capability onset at scale offered as a digital analogue (Fig. 2).
- Criticality, RG and universality (pp. 4–5): order parameters, diverging susceptibility and data collapse locate the onset of emergence; dualities show distinct micro-descriptions yielding identical macrophysics, grounding autonomy "without extra causes".
- Herd immunity on networks (pp. 5–7): an example where emergence is rigorously defined and tied to network geometry.
- Weak vs strong emergence; Conclusion (p. 7): science needs only weak emergence (explanatory autonomy, in-principle derivability); invoking strong emergence for consciousness or society introduces unanchored causal principles; distinguishes explanatory from ontological reduction.

## Open questions

- A compact, citable physicist's position on weak emergence; contrasts with McKenzie's broader survey ([LIT-141](../literature.d/LIT-141.md)) and with philosophical strong-emergence views.
- The LLM "emergent abilities" analogy is asserted, not examined; check it against the metric-artifact critique of emergent abilities (Schaeffer et al. 2023) before relying on it.
- Check whether "information relevant for prediction and control" is given a formal measure or only gestured at via causal-emergence work.
