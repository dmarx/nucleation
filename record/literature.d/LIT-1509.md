# LIT-1509: Open-ended Learning in Symmetric Zero-sum Games

Balduzzi, Garnelo, Bachrach, Czarnecki, Pérolat, Jaderberg & Graepel
(2019), *Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Machine
Learning*, PMLR 97: 434–443 — arXiv:1901.08106

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1143](../notes.d/NOTE-1143.md). Page numbers are the PDF's.*

- **Transitive plus cyclic, relative to a measure.** Every antisymmetric
  φ(v, w) is grad(f) + ψ, with f the mean payoff against agents drawn from
  a chosen probability measure and ψ divergence-free (Theorem 1). The
  split is correct. The paper calls it orthogonal "with respect to the
  inner product on games", but the form it states, ∫φ(v, w)ψ(v′, w′) over
  four independent variables, is zero for every pair of antisymmetric
  functions. The ordinary L² product makes the claim true.
- **Individuals stop meaning anything.** "In a cyclic game, the
  performance of individual agents is meaningless" (p. 5). The paper
  measures populations instead.
- **A population invariant.** The value of the zero-sum game between two
  populations does not depend on which Nash equilibrium is used. Members
  that behave as mixtures of others do not change it, and a population
  inside another's hull cannot beat it (Propositions 2 and 5;
  [THEORY-944](../theory.d/THEORY-944.md)).
- **Algorithms.** Response to the Nash mixture (PSRO_N) strictly enlarges
  the gamescape while some agent beats the Nash, and stalls once the
  game's Nash lies inside it. Response to the rectified Nash (PSRO_rN)
  trains each supported agent against what it beats or ties. In Blotto and
  differentiable Lotto it beats PSRO_N, PSRO_U and self-play on relative
  population performance.
- **Corrections.** Proposition 5(ii) prints max f − max f for monotonic
  games where σ(max f − max f) is meant. Effective diversity depends on
  which Nash is used, and the definition does not fix one.

## Why this is here

Filed 2026-10-24 for the record's governance and cycles lines. The
population value is a scoring invariant that, unlike Nash averaging
([LIT-1508](LIT-1508.md)), needs no uniqueness and covers behavioural mixtures. Its
monotonic-versus-transitive distinction is the record's own
ordinal-versus-cardinal one ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), [TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md)). It bears on
[CLAIM-451](../claims.d/CLAIM-451.md) and [CASE-124](../cases.d/CASE-124.md) ([NOTE-1143](../notes.d/NOTE-1143.md)). It carries
`anthology-candidate` because its algorithms are for training ML agents.
It is filed for understanding, not as an instruction for practice.
