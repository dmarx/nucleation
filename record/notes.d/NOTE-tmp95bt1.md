# NOTE-tmp95bt1: The Emotion Machine

## Contribution

The draft is the late statement of the Society of Mind programme, and it
changes three things in it.

- It renames the earlier book's "agents" as "resources" and gives the
  reason. Readers took an agent to be "a personlike thing (like a travel
  agent)".
- It puts emotion inside the machinery of thinking. An emotional state is a
  large-scale selection of resources, of the same kind as an "intellectual"
  Way to Think.
- It organises the resources into a Critic–Selector loop running at six
  levels of reflection.

The rest is the earlier programme carried further: K-lines, frames,
panalogies, censors and negative expertise, the Single-Self as fiction. The
draft sends the reader back to *The Society of Mind* ([LIT-tmpqepho](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqepho.md)) for the
details.

## Key insight

The mind has no one way of thinking, so it needs ways to notice that the
current way has failed and to switch. A Critic recognises a type of
obstacle: "I've made many attempts with no success", "Achieving goal A made
goal B harder". A Selector then turns on a different set of resources: a
different Way to Think. When that switch is large enough to spread into a
"cascade" that changes goals, perception and priorities together, we call
the result an emotion. "Resourcefulness" is having many such ways and good
Critics for choosing among them. "Intelligence" is a suitcase word for it.

## Assumptions

- **Method.** The arguments are thought experiments, everyday vignettes
  (Joan's knee, Carol's mud cake, Charles in love), dialogues with imagined
  objectors, and appeals to AI programs Minsky's group built (Builder,
  Evans's analogy program, GPS). There is no experiment, and the neural
  claims are few and hedged ("I expect that in the next few years, we'll
  discover structures like those in this diagram").
- **Functionalism.** What matters is "what each separate part does, and how
  those parts are connected up", not what it is made of (§1-5).
- **Complexity first.** The draft rejects parsimony for psychology on purpose:
  "this book will embark on the opposite quest: to find more complex ways to
  depict mental events that seem simple at first" (§1-2; also §5-6, "No
  theory should have more parts than it needs" is answered as having
  "retarded Psychology").
- **Conjecture, marked as such.** The Imprimer theory is "only theories of
  mine… These ideas might be right but they also might not" (chapter 2
  summary). Disruption cascades in suffering are something brain scans
  "should" one day show (§3-2).
- **Levels are a modelling convenience.** "I agree that those boundaries are
  indistinct" (§5-6). The Critic–Selector model and Model Six are two views
  to switch between, not one architecture (§5 summary).

## Key results

The draft proves nothing; these are its main proposals, by chapter.

- **Emotional states (ch. 1).** The thesis: "Each of our major 'emotional
  states' results from turning some set of resources on and turning another
  set of them off" (§1-5). Selectors do the switching. When the switched set
  includes other Selectors, the change spreads into a "cascade" (§1-7).
  Infants switch whole states at once; adults learn to mix them (§1-6).
  "Emotion", "love" and "understand" are suitcase words.
- **Attachments and goals (ch. 2).** Trial and error teaches means.
  Attachment-related praise and blame teach ends: "Pride and Shame… help us
  learn 'ends' instead of 'means'". The people who can do this are
  "Imprimers". Internalised models of them become "conscience". A goal is
  defined operationally: you want G when "some active mental process works
  to reduce the differences between G and your present situation".
- **Pain to suffering (ch. 3).** Pain is the first sensation, hurting its
  early effects, and suffering the large-scale cascade that seizes resources
  and takes away "your freedom to choose what to think about". Critics come
  as Correctors, Suppressors, Censors and Self-Controllers. "Negative
  expertise", knowing what not to do, may be much of competence.
  Creativity, humour, decisiveness, pleasure, beauty and mystical experience
  are each read as Critic suppression (§3-5). One emotion can be used to
  switch off another, as when anger is used against sleep (§3-8).
- **Consciousness (ch. 4).** "Consciousness" is a suitcase word, unpacked
  into 27 listed activities (§4-2). The sense that it is one thing could come
  from a detector C that fires when four things run together: recent
  memory, serial processing, symbolic description and self-models (§4-3).
  The "Immanence Illusion": answers that arrive before the question is
  asked feel instantaneous (§4-3.1). A B-brain watches and corrects an
  A-brain (§4-9). Chalmers's hard problem is called a compression of many
  problems into one word (§§4-8, 9-6).
- **Levels (ch. 5).** Model Six has instinctive reactions, learned
  reactions, deliberation, reflection, self-reflection and self-conscious
  reflection. Deliberation uses If→Do→Then rules chained into plans. Search
  from both ends cuts a 20-step search "several thousand times". Imagination
  works by changing high-level descriptions ("simuli"), not pictures. A
  prediction machine with "suppressor bands" can stop and think (§5-9). Minsky
  says this scheme appears in §6-7 of his 1953 PhD thesis.
- **Common sense (ch. 6).** Knowledge is organised in realms: physical,
  social, dominion, procedural, economic, conversational and more.
  Panalogies link the same item across realms (§6-1). A rough estimate puts a
  person's commonsense knowledge at "a few dozen millions of items", leaning
  on Landauer's figure of about 2 bits per second (§6-2). "Baby machines" are
  said to have stalled for want of new representations, under three
  paradoxes. The Optimization Paradox: the better a system works, the more
  likely each change makes it worse. The Investment Principle. The Parallel
  Processing Paradox. A goal is a Difference-Engine (GPS) process (§6-3).
  "Free will" is "a process we use to stop other processes" (§6-5).
- **Thinking (ch. 7).** The Critic–Selector model (§7-3). Lists of Ways to
  Think and of Critics at each level (§§7-4, 7-5). Emotional embodiment,
  discussed with James (§7-6). Poincaré's preparation, incubation, revelation
  and evaluation. A conjecture that everyday thought alternates "micro-manic"
  generation with "micro-depressive" criticism (§7-7). "Cognitive contexts"
  hold the state to return to after an interruption (§7-8).
- **Resourcefulness (ch. 8).** Distance is judged many ways, so failures
  are tolerated (§8-2). Panalogies explain why rooms and objects stay put as
  we move (§8-3). A "mental animal trainer" copies short-term descriptions
  into long-term memory by repeated difference-reduction (§8-4). A K-line
  remembers a Way to Think by re-activating the resources used (§8-4). Credit
  should go to the earlier choice that selected the winning strategy
  (§8-5). "Genius" is an unusual combination of common traits (§8-6).
  Representations run from numbers, which are "opaque", to semantic
  networks, frames with defaults, trans-frames and scripts. "Micronemes" are
  context signals broadcast widely (§8-7).
- **The Self (ch. 9).** "Whenever you think about your 'Self,' you are
  switching among a network of models" (§9-1). Sub-personalities, traits as
  self-trained regularities, and the Barnum effect as matching some
  self-model (§9-2). Reasons the Single-Self idea is convenient (§9-3).
  Pleasure as a suitcase of satisfaction, exploration, relief and Critic
  suppression (§9-4). Why a mind cannot be a company: its parts are too
  specialised. The Parallel and Pinnacle Paradoxes (§9-5). Qualia as complex
  reflective descriptions, with the "Easy is Hard Paradox" (§§9-6, 9-7).

## Claims

|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Each major emotional state is a selection of resources turned on and others off, and is a "Way to Think" of the same kind as intellectual ones | weak: a design proposal | thought experiments (§§1-1, 1-5, 3-8); no measurement |
| C2 | Attachment figures ("Imprimers") change a child's goals through pride and shame, where trial and error changes only methods | weak, and marked by Minsky as his own untested theory | Bowlby, Harlow, Lorenz and Goodall on attachment, cited (§2-7); no evidence for the goal-elevating mechanism itself, which Minsky says no brain research has shown (§2-3) |
| C3 | Suffering is a cascade in which prolonged pain seizes resources and removes freedom to choose what to think about | weak | phenomenology of complaints; Melzack and Wall quoted on pain's wide brain involvement; "when scanners show more… we should be able to see those cascades" (§3-2) |
| C4 | "Consciousness" names many processes, and the sense of one thing could come from a detector of four co-occurring features | moderate as a conceptual argument (suitcase analysis); weak for the detector | §§4-2, 4-3; argued, not modelled |
| C5 | No resource can know what another is doing "right now", only recent records ("Immanence Illusion") | moderate: a plausible argument from signal delay and caching | §§4-3.1, 4-7; Dennett and Kinsbourne quoted |
| C6 | Purely numerical representations, as in neural networks and statistical learning, are opaque to reflection and limit a system's growth | weak, and a 2005 prediction | argued from the number 12 having many histories (§8-7); no test |
| C7 | The body is one more set of resources; bodily expression may sustain emotional states as an external memory, but emotion starts in the brain | weak: argued against James, with no data | §§3-8, 7-6 |
| C8 | Elliot's decision failures might have caused his emotional flatness, not the reverse, through damage to Critics | speculation, offered as an alternative reading | §7-2; no evidence offered, and the gambling-task data are not discussed |
| C9 | A person's commonsense knowledge is of the order of a few dozen million items | weak: an order-of-magnitude estimate | §6-2, from rough counts and Landauer (1986) |
| C10 | The Self is a network of partial self-models switched among, and a single unified model would be impractical | moderate as an argument | §§1-8, 4-5, 9-1; analogies with physics and car repair |

## Concepts

- **Resource.** Any structure or process the mind uses, from perception to
  bodies of knowledge, used "in a hazy way" (§1-5). It replaces the earlier
  book's "agent".
- **Critic / Selector.** A Critic recognises a problem type and activates a
  Selector, which turns a set of resources on or off. At the lowest level
  they are just an If and a Then (§7-3, note 3).
- **Way to Think.** A set of active resources suited to a type of problem.
  Emotional states are a subclass with popular names.
- **Cascade.** A spreading change in active resources large enough to alter
  goals and perception (§1-7).
- **Imprimer / impriming.** A person a child is attached to, and learning
  values in their presence (§2-3).
- **Suitcase word.** A word that packs many different processes, such as
  "consciousness", "emotion", "pain" or "pleasure" (§§1-1, 4-1).
- **Immanence Illusion.** "For most of the questions you would otherwise ask,
  some answers will have already arrived before the higher levels of your
  mind have had enough time to ask for them" (§4-3.1).
- **Model Six.** Six levels: instinctive and learned reactions,
  deliberation, reflection, self-reflection, self-conscious reflection (ch. 5).
- **Simulus.** "A counterfeit perception caused by changing a mental
  representation" (§5-8).
- **Panalogy.** A "parallel analogy": one structure linking corresponding
  roles in several realms, so a view can switch without starting over (§§6-1,
  8-3).
- **K-line.** A Selector that re-activates the resources used when a problem
  was solved, a "snapshot" of a Way to Think (§8-4).
- **Microneme.** A widely broadcast context signal, often nameless (§8-7).
- **Negative expertise.** Knowing what not to do; carried by Censors and
  Suppressors (§3-5).

## Connections

- **The Society of Mind ([LIT-tmpqepho](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqepho.md)).** The draft carries that book further
  and leans on it for details. It cites its sections throughout: K-lines to
  §8.1, panalogy to chapter 25, verbal expression to §22.10, "Closing the
  Ring" to §19.10, the self as a friend inside to §17.1. Its §9-5, note 24,
  says the earlier book was read as a "community view" against its intent.
  This is the `extends:` relation on the LIT.
- **Singh's exposition ([LIT-tmpxp9b8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxp9b8.md)).** The draft's Introduction cites "Push
  Singh 2003" for how the two books relate, and the draft credits Singh's
  "Reflective Critics" for Critics that reflect (§7-3, note 3). Singh's
  footnotes describe this book's selector K-lines, panalogies and six levels
  before publication, and they agree with the draft.
- **The earlier memos and papers**, being filed by another reader at the
  same time. The draft names its K-line idea as first developed in
  "Plain Talk" (1977) and "K-lines" (1980) (§8-4, note 17). It cites the
  frames memo for default assignments and for the room example (§8-3, note
  7; §6-1, note 2). It cites the jokes essay for jokes as negative expertise
  (§3-5, note xvi). §9-5 quotes a passage on "the paradox-oscillation of
  ultimate purpose", attributed to "Jokes and the Logic of the Cognitive
  Unconscious". That is the heading of the memo as Minsky posted it on his
  home page. The memo (AI Memo 603, November 1980) and its 1981 published
  version are titled "Jokes and their Relation to the Cognitive
  Unconscious".
- **Damasio ([LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md) to [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)).** The draft cites only *Descartes' Error*,
  which is not in this record. The comparison:
  - **What an emotion is.** For Damasio it is a body state or its
    as-if simulation, which marks options ([LIT-384](../literature.d/LIT-384.md)). For Minsky it is a
    selection of resources. The body is "just one more set of resources to
    exploit" (§3-8). "However, we should not too closely identify these
    [somatic effects] with how Anger changes one's Ways to Think." This is
    the disagreement, and it is direct. The Introduction announces that
    chapter 7 "will take the opposite view" to the bodily one.
  - **The body's role.** Minsky grants James that bodily expression may
    keep a state alive as a "dependable external memory device" (§7-6). This
    is near Damasio's body loop. But Minsky makes it optional: "exaggerating
    the body's role in emotions can lead to serious misconceptions".
  - **Elliot.** Damasio infers that reduced emotion degraded decision.
    Minsky proposes the reverse: lost Critics degraded decision, and lost
    cascades followed (§7-2). Minsky gives no evidence. The gambling-task
    result ([LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md)), with anticipatory skin-conductance responses before
    the advantageous strategy is known, is the kind of timing evidence that
    bears on the direction, and the draft does not engage it. The record's
    reading of that report finds its statistics weaker than its abstract on
    this point, so neither side's case is closed.
  - **Feeling.** Damasio: feelings are the experience of body states and
    report homeostatic value ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)), and they are the source of
    consciousness ([LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md)). Minsky: "what we call 'feelings' are attempts (by
    various parts of our minds) to describe large-scale aspects of mental
    conditions" (§3-3). Their objects are brain cascades, and they need
    reflective self-models (§9-6). So the dependence runs the other way
    between the two authors.
  - **Agreement.** Both reject a separable, emotion-free rationality ("a
    myth", Introduction and §7-8). Both put emotion inside decision rather
    than against it.
  - **An unmarked parallel.** Minsky's K-line, re-activating the set of
    resources that were active, has the shape of the recall-as-reactivation
    in Damasio's convergence-zone proposal ([LIT-385](../literature.d/LIT-385.md)). Neither cites the other
    on this; the pairing is mine.
- **Global workspace.** §4-6 quotes Baars and Newman's workspace and accepts
  it as "a good way to start", then says the mind needs "a great deal more
  architectural structure". It is one of the theories Butlin et al. ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md))
  turn into indicator properties.
- **Collective intelligence and group minds.** §9-5 argues a mind is not like
  a company or town, because its parts are too specialised. It asks whether a
  city could have a Self, and answers that it would seem to have more of a
  personality only if its departments' models were linked into panalogies.
  This bears against reading Pilgrim et al.'s collective intelligence
  ([LIT-046](../literature.d/LIT-046.md)) or Schwitzgebel's conscious United States ([LIT-159](../literature.d/LIT-159.md)) as models of
  a single mind. The bearing is mine; the draft names neither.
- **Others the draft quotes that the record holds or lists.** Ryle's *The
  Concept of Mind* ([LIT-318](../literature.d/LIT-318.md), unread here) on unnoticed and mistaken
  sensations (§9-7). Chalmers on the hard problem (§§4-8, 9-6). Dennett
  throughout. The draft's "external representations" Way to Think (§7-4)
  and the body as external memory sit near the Extended Mind ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)),
  which it does not cite.

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-136](NOTE-136.md)** glosses "suitcase word" as "Minsky's term (ref. 22)". The
  reference list of Safron's editorial ([LIT-195](../literature.d/LIT-195.md)), checked on Crossref, gives
  ref. 22 as Minsky, *The emotion machine*, 2007, 400 pp., the paperback.
  So [NOTE-136](NOTE-136.md) already names this work, and the draft confirms the term
  (§§1-1, 4-2.1).
- No THEORY document in this record is about the architecture of emotion
  or of mind, so this reading supports or contradicts none. Should the
  record take a position on whether emotions are body states or brain
  selections, this reading and the Damasio readings would be its two
  sources, and the Elliot case and the gambling-task timing its test.
- **Boundary.** The draft carries no instruction for machine-learning
  practice. It does make claims about machine learning: §§6-2 and 8-7
  argue that numerically represented learners ("Neural Networks,
  Perceptrons, Fuzzy Logic systems, and Statistical Learning Programs") are
  opaque to reflection and stall. These are 2005 predictions, untested in
  the draft, and an anthology reader might want them as historical
  positions on representation. I did not tag the work `anthology-candidate`:
  its subject is the human mind, and the ML claims are a minor thread.

## Limitations

- It is a draft, and it is not the book. Chapter 9 is partial and full of
  editorial brackets ("[edit]", "[This section should begin by…]"). Several
  "§§" cross-references point to sections that do not exist in it
  (§§Embodiment, §§Alarms, §§Duplication, chapter §10).
- The figures are missing from what I read.
- There are no experiments. The neural claims are guesses about what scans
  will show. Its account of emotion does not engage the affective
  neuroscience of its time beyond Damasio's one patient and a footnote on
  Panksepp.
- It names many mechanisms (Critics, Selectors, panalogies, micronemes) but
  specifies none precisely enough to implement or test, as Singh
  ([LIT-tmpxp9b8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxp9b8.md)) says of the earlier book.

## Open questions

- Does the published book keep the draft's chapter structure? The
  Introduction's outline gives chapter 3 as "Emotions are different Ways to
  Think", while the draft's chapter 3 is "From Pain to Suffering". Its
  account of Damasio and its §7-6 should also be checked against the book.
- What evidence would separate Minsky's Elliot reading from Damasio's? One
  candidate: patients with intact anticipatory body responses but impaired
  problem-type recognition, or the reverse.
- Can the Critic–Selector loop be specified tightly enough to fail? Singh's
  later work with Minsky on a commonsense architecture is where that was
  tried. It is not in this record.
