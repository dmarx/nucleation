  Frankfurt (1969), The Journal of Philosophy 66(23):829–839. The
  principle of alternate possibilities (PAP), "a person is morally
  responsible for what he has done only if he could have done otherwise",
  is false. Coercion excuses only when the agent acts because of it (§§II–

  any role in bringing it about (§IV); such a fact "does not help in any

# LIT-tmpwts2i: Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility

Harry G. Frankfurt, "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility",
*The Journal of Philosophy* 66(23), 4 December 1969, pp. 829–839 —
DOI-10.2307/2023833. Reprinted as chapter 1 of Frankfurt, *The Importance
of What We Care About* (Cambridge University Press, 1988), pp. 1–10, "with
minor stylistic changes" ([LIT-tmpwnjgf](LIT-tmpwnjgf.md)).

## Key takeaways

- **PAP is false, and its plausibility is borrowed from coercion.** The
  paper opens by calling PAP "an a priori truth" to some and denies it:
  "A person may well be morally responsible for what he has done even
  though he could not have done otherwise" (pp. 829–830). The doctrine that
  coercion excludes responsibility is not a special case of PAP. Of the
  three ways Jones may face a threat to do what he has already decided
  on, only the stampeded Jones2, who acts "entirely because he was
  terrified", is excused; Jones3, who acts on his prior decision though
  the threat would have moved him, keeps his responsibility "even though
  … he could not have avoided doing what he did" (pp. 831–833).
- **The case.** Black wants Jones4 to perform "a certain action", watches,
  and will intervene by threat, potion, hypnosis or direct manipulation of
  the brain only if Jones4 is about to decide otherwise. Jones4 decides
  "for reasons of his own" to do just that, and Black never acts. Jones4
  "will bear precisely the same moral responsibility" as without Black,
  and it would be "quite unreasonable to excuse Jones4 … or to withhold
  the praise to which it would normally entitle him" (pp. 835–836). The
  case is credited to Nozick's lectures (n. 2). Black's foreknowledge
  comes from a twitch that precedes one decision and not the other, which
  "does not beg the question of determinism" (n. 3). Black can be replaced
  by a machine or by "natural forces involving no will or design" (n. 4).
- **Why PAP fails.** A fact that makes an action unavoidable may play "no
  role whatever in the explanation of why he did it"; then it "could have
  been subtracted from the situation" with nothing changed, and "it seems
  quite gratuitous to assign it any weight in the assessment of his moral
  responsibility" (pp. 836–837). We accept "I could not have done
  otherwise" as an excuse only because we take it to explain the action.
- **The replacement, and the determinism question.** Frankfurt considers
  and rejects "not responsible if he did it *because* he could not have
  done otherwise", noting that with the incompatibilist reading of "could
  have done otherwise" it would make every determined act excused (p.
  838). He proposes instead "not morally responsible … if he did it only
  because he could not have done otherwise", that is, not because "that
  was what he really wanted to do", and says this "does not appear to
  conflict with the view that moral responsibility is compatible with
  determinism" (pp. 838–839).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-04 at the owner's request, to file Harry Frankfurt "to the
best of your ability". It is moral philosophy and the metaphysics of
responsibility, with no instruction for machine-learning practice, so no
anthology topic holds it.

`Active` from a full reading ([NOTE-tmpour9o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpour9o.md)). Until now the record held
this paper only through Robb's SEP entry ([LIT-294](LIT-294.md), [NOTE-269](../notes.d/NOTE-269.md)), which
quotes it through Fischer's retelling, and McKenna and Coates's SEP
*Compatibilism* ([LIT-291](LIT-291.md)), which gives "a close approximation". Three
things in those readings change:

- **The original case is schematic.** It has no vote and no shooting: Black
  and Jones4 and "a certain action". The voting version is Fischer's (1982)
  and the shooting version is McKenna and Coates's.
- **The prior sign is there, in a note.** Robb says the sign is "not always
  explicit in Frankfurt's own FSCs" (§4.3.2). In this paper it is explicit
  in n. 3, the twitch. The dilemma defence that later turns on it is
  therefore aimed at a feature the original case has.
- **Robb's §5.1 citation of this paper needs its context.** Robb cites it
  for the worry that "determinism may still preclude responsibility due to
  the way it blocks alternatives". The paper raises that worry against the
  intermediate revision of PAP and then answers it with "only because".
  That it closes in favour of compatibilism is the paper's own view, not a
  report of an opponent's.

**Its successor.** "Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person"
([LIT-tmpuutyy](LIT-tmpuutyy.md), read) cites this paper (n. 9) for its claim that a
responsible agent need not have been able to do otherwise, and supplies
what "what he really wanted to do" lacks here: a willing addict who acts on
a desire he wants to be his will. That `extends` is declared on that
paper's side.

**The responsibility literature built on it, which the record has not
read.** Fischer's "Responsiveness and Moral Responsibility" ([LIT-tmpavyub](LIT-tmpavyub.md))
and Fischer and Ravizza's *Responsibility and Control* ([LIT-tmp1m893](LIT-tmp1m893.md)) are
reported by the SEP to start from these cases: they move reasons-
responsiveness from the agent to the mechanism that actually issues in
the action. Both are Deferred and unread, so no relation is declared from
here. A reading of either should add `extends` to this paper on its own
side if it confirms the dependence.

**How it bears on the record's theories.** [THEORY-tmpejn3z](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpejn3z.md) is the
account filed from this paper: responsibility does not require the ability
to have done otherwise. Fischer and Ravizza's guidance control
([THEORY-tmpmjijs](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmjijs.md)) is its best-known development. Wolf's asymmetry
([THEORY-tmp6tk5g](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp6tk5g.md)) holds that PAP is false for praise and true for blame;
this paper's case is stated for both ("excuse Jones4 … or … withhold the
praise", p. 836), and its action is not said to be good or bad, so it
claims the symmetric result Wolf denies. Wolf's "Asymmetrical Freedom"
([LIT-tmpbc48b](LIT-tmpbc48b.md)) is unread, and whether she answers a Frankfurt case of
wrongdoing is still open.
