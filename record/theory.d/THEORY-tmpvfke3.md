# THEORY-tmpvfke3: In three cross-sectional studies of undergraduates, most of the variance in adult attachment security lay within persons, across relational partners (56–79%, error included), and relationship-specific satisfaction of the needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness predicted relationship-specific security in every relationship tested, also with the relatedness items removed; relatedness was the strongest unique predictor, then autonomy, then competence

## Source

La Guardia et al. (2000), [LIT-tmpjpszp](../literature.d/LIT-tmpjpszp.md), pp. 371–380; read in [NOTE-tmpo9h31](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpo9h31.md).

## What was actually shown

- Within-person variance 56% (Study 1), 79% (Study 2), about two thirds
  (Study 3).
- HLM slopes of security on within-person need satisfaction, all p < .001, in
  all three studies, and with the relatedness items removed (Table 1);
  residual regressions significant in every specific relationship (Table 3).
- Relatedness strongest in 15 of 18 simultaneous regressions; autonomy stronger
  than competence in 15 of 18 without relatedness (pp. 375–376).

These could have come out otherwise: within-person variance could have been
mostly error unexplained by need satisfaction, or the effect could have
vanished without the relatedness items. Neither happened.

## What this does not say

- **That need satisfaction produces security.** Cross-sectional; the authors
  say the relation is bidirectional and could reflect third variables (p. 381).
- **That the measures separate the needs.** Patrick et al.'s corrected
  correlations among them run to .96 ([NOTE-tmpi2z1s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi2z1s.md)).
