# CASE-150: Economic alignment and political alignment: Kleinman, Liu and Redding find, using China's integration into the world economy and falling air-transport costs as sources of variation, that countries whose welfare becomes more dependent on each other's productivity become more politically aligned

## The case

A6: the authors "develop measures of how one country's economic welfare
responds to productivity changes in another country, capturing the effects
of the broader trade network rather than merely counting bilateral trade
flows." With quasi-experimental variation from China's integration and
falling air-transport costs, "they find that increasing economic alignment
causes increasing political alignment, as measured by outcomes including
UN voting and strategic rivalries." ([LIT-1416](../literature.d/LIT-1416.md), unread. Its abstract confirms the two sources of variation
and the direction of the result, "increased economic friendship causes
increased political friendship", but names no outcome measure, so UN
voting and rivalries are A6's and unchecked. It first appeared as an
NBER working paper in 2020.)

## What it shows

- **A measured coupling.** A6: the exposure measure "is essentially a
  **coupling coefficient**", the elasticity of A's welfare with respect to
  B's productivity: B's flourishing has become good for A. That makes it
  [CLAIM-428](../claims.d/CLAIM-428.md)'s material coupling in a quantity, and an empirical case
  of a change in the coupling, not only the state, of a relationship.
- **A causal claim about alignment.** The variation is meant to be
  exogenous, so the alignment follows the exposure and does not merely
  accompany it.

## What it does not show

- That the countries' interpretive organizations changed. A6: "political
  alignment does not by itself establish that the countries' interpretive
  organizations have changed. Strategic incentives alone could explain
  the alignment." [CLAIM-428](../claims.d/CLAIM-428.md)'s defeater says what would tell them
  apart.
- That alignment makes for peace. UN voting and rivalry are proxies, and
  interdependence can also be organized as leverage.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the outcomes checked

[LIT-1416](../literature.d/LIT-1416.md) is now read ([NOTE-tmpz95lz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpz95lz.md)), in the NBER revision of March 2023,
so "unread" no longer holds, and the flag on the outcome measures can be
lifted. The outcomes are UN voting (S-, π- and κ-scores and ideal-point
distance) and strategic rivalries (any, positional, spatial,
ideological), as A6 said, and also formal alliances, which the case does
not mention. Three things the reading adds.

- **The effect is small** in the panel: 0.094 SD per SD of exposure.
- **Ideological rivalry**, the rivalry nearest to a change of view, is the
  one outcome the instrument does not move.
- **"What it does not show" is sharper than it knew.** The paper's own
  model is the strategic explanation the case sets against changed
  interpretive organization: alignment there is an action chosen for its
  payoff, with preferences held fixed.

The case is otherwise unchanged.
