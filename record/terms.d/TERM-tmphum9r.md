# TERM-tmphum9r: normative governability, of an organized agent or relationship: its maintained, role-appropriate capacity to recognize and respond to justified normative considerations, including considerations that require revising its own governing mechanisms; relational, and not corrigibility, compliance or obedience to challengers

## Definition

A110 §13 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), the last
and most qualified of A's four versions:

> **Normative governability is the maintained, role-appropriate capacity of an organized agent or relationship to recognize and respond to justified normative considerations, including considerations requiring revision of its own governing mechanisms.**
>
> **Its adequacy depends on the legitimacy of the standards and authorities involved, the practical effectiveness of the relevant responses, and the continued availability of capabilities required to discharge the arrangement's justified responsibilities.**
>
> **Deficiencies in normative governability may explain wrongful conduct without excusing every duty arising from it. Responsibility for the deficiency's creation, maintenance, and correction must be evaluated separately.**

The distinction it draws is between an institution that responds to reasons
and one that enforces its own requirements. A108 §7: "**the capacity to
enforce rules and the capacity to be governed by justified reasons are not
the same capability**". The mark of the first is the final clause, what A108
§3 calls responding "to reasons **about its own ways of responding to
reasons**": the second-order responsiveness of [CLAIM-tmp69o4u](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp69o4u.md).

A108 §3 gives the pathway the capacity runs through: a normatively relevant
circumstance, recognition, normative uptake, effective response, and
revision with maintained capability. A110 §9 restates it as representation,
evaluation, authority and execution.

## The earlier versions

- **A106 §18** (the one the owner quoted in U46): "the capacity of an
  organized agent or relationship to have its conduct and governing
  arrangements appropriately responsive to justified normative
  considerations, through mechanisms of recognition, authorization, action,
  and revision, under relevant practical constraints".
- **A108 §3** adds "maintained" and "role-appropriate", and "revise the
  mechanisms governing that responsiveness when justified".
- **A108 §7** adds reasons "concerning the legitimacy of its own rules, the
  allocation of authority, and the adequacy of its procedures".
- **A110 §13** keeps the second-order clause and adds the two qualifying
  paragraphs, the second of which ties the term to [CLAIM-tmpsk25g](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpsk25g.md) and
  [CLAIM-tmp0wx69](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp0wx69.md).

## The link to reasons-responsiveness (the owner's)

The owner's U46: "This feels like it connects nicely to "Reasons
Responsiveness" theories like Susan Wolf's." A108 §2 develops the link
and separates the two theories the owner's phrase joins: Wolf "emphasizes
the agent's capacity to recognize and act on appropriate moral reasons",
while Fischer and Ravizza "develop a more explicit theory of **moderate
reasons-responsiveness**", of a mechanism's receptivity and reactivity. On
the record these are [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md) (Wolf's Reason View, Proposed, read only
second-hand) and [THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md) (Fischer and Ravizza, Active). [THEORY-122](../theory.d/THEORY-122.md)
notes that the survey it relies on counts Wolf among reasons-responsiveness
views in the broad sense, so the owner's phrase is a fair broad usage.

A's institutional reading of each is A's, not the works'. From Wolf, A108 §2
takes: "**An institution's fidelity to its own commitments does not
establish its responsiveness to justified normative reasons.**" From
Fischer and Ravizza, receptivity (can the institution's information and
evaluation discriminate normatively different cases?) and reactivity (can
justified considerations alter its decisions?). A marks the limit: "The
analogy is substantive, but not exact", since theories of responsibility
ask when blame or praise is apt, and this term asks "what makes a governing
arrangement properly responsive to justified claims." That is why the term
is not a responsibility condition: A110 §13 says the definition "is
preferable to one that identifies moral responsibility with
reasons-responsiveness".

## What it is not

- **Not corrigibility.** A106 §18: "This is a substantial refinement of the
  earlier vocabulary of corrigibility. It locates the normative issue in
  the justified allocation of governing relationships rather than in the
  maximal susceptibility of an agent to correction." The record disclaims
  an unconditional duty of corrigibility ([CLAIM-511](../claims.d/CLAIM-511.md), [ARG-058](../arguments.d/ARG-058.md)).
- **Not obedience.** A108 §6: "An institution is not normatively governable
  because it obeys challengers." "The word *appropriately* matters" (A106
  §18).
- **Not universal.** It is relational: "A corporation may be governable by
  regulators but not by its ordinary customers." "**The right question is
  which reasons should be able to govern which actors, through which
  processes, with what authority and practical efficacy.**"
- **Not compliance.** A106 §18 separates justification, compliance and
  "Governability by reasons"; identical decisions can come from different
  capacities ([CLAIM-tmp69o4u](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp69o4u.md)).
- **Not [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s freedom of will.** [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md) (Proposed) is a will's
  governance of its own governability. This term names the same reflexive
  structure for an organized agent or relationship, judged against
  justified standards rather than the agent's own.

## Would replace or add

It adds. No TERM names the institutional capacity; the nearest are
[CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)'s three achievements (Active), which it extends with maintenance
and second-order revision, and [TERM-085](TERM-085.md)'s correctability. It would be
