# SYNTHESIS-099: Responsibility for another's conditions, and moral exploitation: who harmed whom is kept apart from who dominated whom, arranging another's wrong is proposed as a wrong of its own and its true accusation as an instrument of further control, an agent can be governed through its own sound values, and an arrangement can be carried by its members' commitments, credited with what they supply and blame them when it fails, with what is left of its standing to blame them asked

*A part account of the dependence-and-domination line. Version 1 was filed at the interim synthesis review of 2026-11-17, before the readings and before the two filings of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" were reconciled. Version 2 is the follow-up review of 2026-11-18. The first filing (pull request 510, the first share, U1–U17 and A1–A35) and the continuation's (pull request 512, the later share) filed several of these ideas twice; at the reconciliation the first filing's entries kept their codes and took what the continuation's added (the curation entry of 2026-11-17, 20:57:46, its reconciliation section). Each component below is described as it now stands, with the filing each part of it comes from. The readings of 2026-11-16 (the first filing's) and of 2026-11-17 (filed on 2026-11-18) are cited as the support each component has, by the THEORY that carries the relation; a reading is not a component. Where a component cites another, the account says which entry does.*

## The account

**Harm done and power held.** CLAIM-709 (A's; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed). The first filing took it from A3's "Where I land", at the
owner's U1 and U2: equality of wrongdoing and equality of power are
separate questions, so a tally of wrongful acts does not settle who is
responsible for the relationship's coercive structure. The continuation
added, from A3 §3, §7 and A7 §9, why they separate (domination in
TERM-124's sense is a capacity, "used or not", while a tally counts its
exercises) and the converse, now in the title: a finding of that control
does not excuse the controlled party's own wrongful acts. It grounds on
CASE-279, uses TERM-124 and TERM-127, and complements CLAIM-690 (its
body: "the interpersonal version") and CLAIM-708. ARG-113 (the first
filing's, the filer's statement of A's argument; existence, Proposed)
concludes it: CASE-279's Relationship B separates the count of wrongful
acts from control over the terms, which is all the claim's first half
needs; the second half needs a premise the case does not supply, that
one can be responsible for the conditions of another's conduct, and
ARG-113 rests on CLAIM-708 for it.

CASE-279 (A's, A3 §1 and §5; fictional thought experiment, Proposed):
Alice and Bob fight and neither controls the other; Carol may strike Dan
more often, while Dan controls their money, monitors her messages,
isolates her and threatens her if she leaves. The continuation added A3
§5's table and A3 §3's six questions. It `draws_on` THEORY-1961 (Johnson
2006, the interview), Johnson's three types of violence.

*Readings.* Seven THEORYs corroborate CLAIM-709, each for its first half
only, the separation of acts from control: THEORY-1925 (Johnson 2006,
"Conflict and Control": types defined by "the degree of control
present", not by the acts), THEORY-1961 (Johnson's 2006 interview),
THEORY-1991 (Hardesty et al. 2015: a count of control tactics can
misclassify a controlling abuser as low control), THEORY-1943 (Stark
and Hester 2019: "Coercive control is not a type of violence"),
THEORY-1986 (Stark 2015), THEORY-1949 (Cunha et al.: the Conflict
Tactics Scale counts acts and cannot tell initiation from self-defence)
and THEORY-1929 (Mazinani and O'Connor 2026, an interpretive guard, not
a test). The claim's dated note of 2026-11-17 records that none takes
up its second half, that responsibility for the structure goes with
control, or its converse; Johnson allows only that violent resistance
"can sometimes be" justified. Lohmann et al. (THEORY-1922) carries no
relation. CASE-279's dated note places Carol's strikes as violent
resistance in Johnson's terms, and records that mutual violent control,
the fourth type in A's table, is confirmed by nothing read.

**Arranging another's wrong.** CLAIM-708 (A's; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed; lines organizational-normativity and distributed-agency). The
first filing took A7 §8's sentence, which the owner quoted back at U5,
and A9's principle: one who wrongfully structures another's
circumstances so that a wrong becomes likely, hard to avoid or
apparently necessary can answer for that wrong while the one who
commits it still answers for committing it. A9 §7 grades the
structuring party's share by six questions (control, knowledge, the
cost of the actor's alternatives, directness, benefit or weaponizing,
proportionality) and limits it to wrongful interference with another's
capacity to govern their own conduct. The continuation added, from A9
§1, §4, §9 and A11 §6, that the structuring is also a wrong against the
person structured, whether or not they comply, and that responsibility
is not zero-sum. It complements CLAIM-624 (its body: "the organizational
form") and CLAIM-709, bears on QUESTION-070 and QUESTION-449, and uses
TERM-127.

*Readings.* Five THEORYs corroborate it. THEORY-1939 (Santoni de Sio
and van den Hoven 2018) carries responsibility from the operator to the
"choice architect", and qualifies the second half: where the operator's
conditions fail through no fault of hers, tracing relocates
responsibility rather than adding a bearer. THEORY-1912 (the SEP entry
"Blame") gives the complicity condition: one who ordered the wrong "is
not in a position to blame" the subordinate, "even if civilians are".
THEORY-1919 (the SEP entry "Coercion"), partly and second-hand, treats
the coercee's responsibility as its own question. THEORY-1914 (Tessman)
supports the structure where the structured party does no wrong. THEORY-1917
(Keller 2016) supports the relational wrong ("I mistreat you by placing
you in a difficult moral predicament"), but his victim is pressed to do
right, not wrong. Readings without a relation qualify the actor's end:
Nagel's control condition pulls against "still answers"; Strawson's
coercion plea can remove responsibility "or at all", and his
"systematically perverted" mind exempts (THEORY-1973); Watson, through
King (THEORY-1987), reduces accountability-blame where the actor lacked
"a reasonable opportunity"; Miller, through Shaw-Young (THEORY-1958),
puts the remedial burden on the structured party once the coercer is
gone, which the claim's repair clause does not consider.

**The true accusation as an instrument.** CLAIM-726 (A's, A7 §5, A9 §6,
A11 and A13 §8; the owner's U6 brought in reflexive control; filed by
the continuation; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; also
distributed-agency): a party can arrange the conditions of another's
real wrong and use the true accusation, before an audience whose
judgement it anticipated, to justify further control; the truth of the
accusation neither vindicates the arranger nor excuses the doer. It
rests on CLAIM-445 (its summary: the loop's mechanism, "with the
target's own conduct as the shifted evidence"), uses TERM-127 and
TERM-124, and complements CLAIM-701 (in SYNTHESIS-098). Its body says it
uses the wrong CLAIM-708 names "as an instrument", that CLAIM-721
(SYNTHESIS-101) is "the same shape with incapacity in place of a wrong",
that CLAIM-619's inversion differs in whether the thing used is a wrong,
and that the first filing had held the pattern within CLAIM-708 and
CLAIM-707.

*Readings.* None corroborates it. ARG-115 (the assistant's, at the
readings of 2026-11-18; source, Proposed) grounds on THEORY-1917 and
concludes against the inference from Keller: the teachers example A13
§3 relays is Keller's (p. 24), but "The teachers have the right to
strike", so there is no real wrong, and what the official anticipates
is a smear, not a true accusation; Keller carries only the claim's third
move, the anticipated audience. The claim's dated notes add that Harsey
and Freyd's DARVO (THEORY-1926) casts the accusation as false and carries
no relation, and that Gaboriaud et al. (THEORY-1935) vary no upstream
arranger.

**Governed through one's own values.** CLAIM-703 (A's; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; line organizational-normativity). The first
filing took A13, at the owner's U7: one party can wrongfully bring about
circumstances in which another incurs a genuine obligation, so that the
second does right in discharging it and is still wronged, and the
obligation is no larger than the circumstances support. The continuation
added the route in CLAIM-404's terms (A11 §4, A13 §2 and §6): the
manipulator changes the situation, ω, with the target's evidence and
criteria unchanged. It complements CLAIM-708 (its body: "there the
structured party does wrong, here it does right") and CLAIM-404, bears
on QUESTION-472 and QUESTION-071, and uses TERM-127. Its body says what
makes the route a way of being governed is A15 §7's condition, a party
that controls the trigger while another bears the cost, "which is
CLAIM-713's".

*Readings.* THEORY-1917 (Keller 2016) corroborates its central half
and its condition ("if I have the power to change your circumstances,
then I may have the power to change the moral demands you face"); the
dog case is Keller's, the names A's. THEORY-1914 (Tessman, after
Karnein) corroborates it by an argument independent of Keller.

**The moral subsidy.** CLAIM-713 (A's; thesis, interpretive, Proposed).
The first filing took A15 at the owner's U8: a moral commitment is
exploitable as a resource, since whoever controls the circumstances that
trigger it can capture what its fulfilment yields and leave its costs
with the committed party, and an institution can come to run on what it
so avoids paying. The continuation added who bears it, now in the
title (A13 §4 and §10, A15 §4: the burden falls hardest on the most
conscientious), A15 §3's hold-up, and an inference against CLAIM-690
that the body marks as the continuation filer's, "a candidate, not
asserted", kept as QUESTION-472. It rests on CLAIM-703 (its body: "This
claim makes the setting of such circumstances a standing position, not a
single manoeuvre"), complements CLAIM-690 and CLAIM-669, bears on
QUESTION-468, QUESTION-461 and QUESTION-472, and uses TERM-123 and
TERM-127.

*Readings.* THEORY-1917 (Keller 2016) corroborates it, for its
deliberate form only: his law firm can "increase your workload without
having to increase your pay", and caring roles are harder to strike
from; the claim allows reliance without intent. THEORY-1948 (Liedo, Van
Grunsven and Marin) corroborates it from care ethics. Robillard and
Strawser, whom A15 names as a foundation, were not reached.

**Unseen while it lasts.** CLAIM-702 (A's, A17 §6; split out of CLAIM-707
at the first filing's review of 2026-11-16; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed): where members' compensating effort prevents failures the
organization's conditions would produce, the prevention leaves little
trace while its lapse is visible, so the organization can be credited
with outcomes the compensation produced and the members blamed for
failures the conditions produced. It complements CLAIM-707, CLAIM-713
and CLAIM-614, and bears on QUESTION-461. Its body calls it "the
measurement link" CLAIM-700's loop needs. *Readings.* None supports it;
its body says Elish supports the blame mechanism only.

**Control, obligation and blame apart.** CLAIM-707 (A's; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; lines organizational-normativity and
dependence-and-domination). The first filing took A17, at the owner's
U9: an organization can place control over the conditions of action
upstream, the practical obligation downstream, and blame where failure
becomes visible, so those blamed can be those with least control. The
continuation added A17 §1, §4, §9 and §10, A15 §6 and §9 and A49 §11.1:
authority on paper is not actionable control, conscience "does double
duty", the power to set the categories of evaluation, and a crumple zone
"can contain someone who is partly responsible". It rests on CLAIM-708,
grounds on THEORY-1928, complements CLAIM-713, CLAIM-702, CLAIM-614 and
CLAIM-678, bears on QUESTION-461, QUESTION-468 and QUESTION-487, and uses
TERM-127. Its body names CLAIM-715 as one route by which blame reaches
those downstream, and CLAIM-713 as the first half of the double duty.

*Readings.* Its ground, THEORY-1928 (Elish 2019), carries the blame
part, credit to the arrangement and standards set upstream; Elish's
limit (crumple zones "are not a property of every complex and automated
system") is not cited by the claim, so no `bounds` is written.
THEORY-1933 (Ozer, Waggoner and Kennedy) `bounds` its automation
corollary. Corroborating: THEORY-1932 (the NTSB on Tempe, the
control-and-duty part, though the NTSB carries cause upstream, nearer
the second branch of the claim's defeater), THEORY-1916 (Choroszewicz,
allocation before failure), THEORY-1939 (Santoni de Sio and van den
Hoven), THEORY-1946 (Rubel, Castro and Pham, agency laundering, which
needs a misleading ascription and leaves inadvertence open), and
THEORY-1921 and THEORY-1993 (Tronto, for the concept only). Its dated
note records that no reading supports the conscience part. CASE-292
(the Tempe crash, documented; distributed-agency) illustrates it.

**Scrutiny as evidence of ownership.** CLAIM-715 (A's, A23 §7 and A19 §2,
developing the owner's U11, CLAIM-710; filed by the continuation;
thesis, interpretive, Proposed; first line distributed-agency): friction
that makes people think differs from friction that makes the
institution answerable, and the added scrutiny can serve as evidence
that individuals owned decisions whose conditions they could not change.
It rests on CLAIM-349 for the split of access from authority and on
CLAIM-707 for the perverse case, grounds its cognitive half on LIT-2283,
and complements CLAIM-712 (the first filing's, on traceability; outside
this part). *Readings.* THEORY-1916 (Choroszewicz: "human oversight and
responsibility as liability were explicitly codified as a user burden"),
THEORY-1937 (Natali et al.: every designed friction acts on the user)
and THEORY-1931 (the HKMA: approval is meaningful only with "the
authority and practical ability to challenge or reject") corroborate its
first half. None reports scrutiny cited afterwards as ownership: its last
clause is unsupported.

**The hidden-shortfall loop.** CLAIM-700 (A's, A33 §3 and §5 at the
owner's U16; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; lines
organizational-normativity and readiness). The first filing's: where
members' compensation covers a shortfall and the organization budgets to
what it observes, the compensation and its quiet are read as adequacy,
resources are cut toward present need, and the reconfigurations that
would remove the shortfall pass out of reach. The continuation added
the strategic layer with TERM-110's ratchet (where revealing spare
capacity invites its removal, members' best responses feed the loop) and
corrected A's paraphrase of CLAIM-577's rule (last period's peak, not
what was consumed). It rests on CLAIM-679, CLAIM-549, CLAIM-713 and
CLAIM-702, complements CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-707, uses TERM-106 and
TERM-110, and bears on QUESTION-386. Its body says each link is held and
the coupling is not, and names CLAIM-445 and CLAIM-619 as loops "of the
same family". *Readings.* None carries a relation; its notes on
Choroszewicz and the NTSB call them analogues.

**Two constructions.** CASE-288 (A's, A53 §7 at the owner's U24;
stipulated thought experiment, Proposed): three hospitals with the same
output, budget and staff, differing in how extra work is drawn out,
negotiated, competitive or moralized. It illustrates CLAIM-713 and
CLAIM-703, bears on QUESTION-463 and QUESTION-468, and `draws_on`
THEORY-1917: arm C has the mechanism of Keller's third-party moral
blackmail, deliberate in Keller and unspecified in the case. Its body
says arm B shows the same extraction with no appeal to conscience, and
calls arm B's authority over expectations CLAIM-728's residual control
in a workplace. CASE-290 (A's, A33 §3, §6 and §10 B; stipulated
proposed study, not run, Proposed; first line will-organization): with
conditions held fixed, measure acceptance, seen discrepancy, motivation,
protected channel and efficacy, to tell satisfaction from tolerance. It
illustrates CLAIM-553, CLAIM-549 and CLAIM-700, and bears on
QUESTION-474. CLAIM-702's body calls it distinct from that claim: it
would observe CLAIM-700's quiet link, not the visibility of
compensation.

**Four questions.** QUESTION-487 (A's, A17's close; Open) asks what lets
agents hold principals accountable for the conditions they must act
under. It refines QUESTION-461; its body says CLAIM-687, CLAIM-624 and
CLAIM-707 supply the downward half and none the instruments, and that
CLAIM-713 gives one reason dependence may not become authority.
*Readings* bearing on it: THEORY-1921 and THEORY-1993 (Tronto: a
democratic answer in kind), THEORY-1939 (Santoni de Sio and van den
Hoven: a duty on designers, not an instrument for agents) and,
weakly, THEORY-1928 (Elish: certification). Its note: it stays open.

QUESTION-473 (raised by the first filing's third review of 2026-11-16,
ChatGPT's, forwarded by the owner, from A15 and A17; the wording A's;
Open; line organizational-normativity) asks what remains of an
organization's moral standing to blame an agent whose wrongdoing its
conditions substantially produced, as distinct from its authority to
investigate, correct or sanction. Its body cites the complicity
condition from THEORY-1912, names CLAIM-713, CLAIM-702, CLAIM-707 and
CLAIM-708 as the exchange's partial cases, and keeps itself apart from
QUESTION-470 (who must repair) and QUESTION-487 (what the agent can do
to the principal). It has no front-matter relations.

QUESTION-472 (A's, A30 §4 at the owner's U15; Open) asks whether the
ability to activate another's genuine obligations is control over the
terms of their dependence. The continuation added what turns on it for
TERM-124 (if not, CLAIM-703 and CLAIM-713 are "exploitation without
domination"), a second negative answer (a capacity to set terms acts on
options, not reasons) and the sharper test case. *Readings* bearing on
it: THEORY-1917 (Keller: evidence toward yes for fiduciaries, whose
moral cost of striking does the work of a barrier to exit) and
THEORY-1914 (Tessman's case). QUESTION-477 (the owner's, U8, filed at the
owner's request; Open; the wording and its formalization the
assistant's) asks for a resource theory of moral commitments in the
formal sense of THEORY-1397; its body says the economic reading is filed
in CLAIM-703, CLAIM-713 and CLAIM-707, and that a formal answer would
make QUESTION-472 exact.
