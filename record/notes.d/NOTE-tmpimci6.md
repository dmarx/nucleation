# NOTE-tmpimci6: Zaslavsky, Garvin, Kemp, Tishby and Regier 2019 — Evolution and efficiency in color naming: the case of Nafaanra

## Contribution

The first diachronic colour-naming data for one language analysed in the
information-bottleneck framework: a resurvey of a World Color Survey
language four decades on, showing terms added and the older terms'
categories shrunk, with the system staying near the efficiency bound.

## Key results

- **1978.** "The data revealed a 3-term system with terms for
  light/white, dark/black and red."
- **2017.** The resurvey "revealed a 7-term system. The three terms from
  1978 are still used but they now name smaller categories, and there are
  also new terms for (roughly) yellow, green, blue and purple."
- **Efficiency.** "the 2017 Nafaanra system, like the 1978 system, lies
  near the theoretical limit of efficiency, and that this outcome would be
  unlikely without pressure for efficiency."
- **Left open.** "how efficiency interacts with other factors such as
  language contact, are questions for future research."

## Assumptions

- The two surveys, decades apart, measured the same community's naming by
  comparable means. The abstract does not say how; a search snippet of the
  later preprint describes the later study as free-response naming, which
  is unverified here.
- The efficiency measure and need distribution are [LIT-810](../literature.d/LIT-810.md)'s ([NOTE-631](NOTE-631.md)).

## Concepts

- **Information-bottleneck efficiency**: the trade-off between a lexicon's
  complexity and its accuracy, with a theoretical bound ([THEORY-163](../theory.d/THEORY-163.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md), promote_when.** The nearest natural case found: terms
  added to one language's inventory, and the neighbouring older terms'
  extensions re-measured, and they "name smaller categories", which is
  the neighbours yielding space. It does not meet the promote_when: the
  abstract gives no measure, and there is no set of terms outside the
  colour field as a control, so a general shift in how the community
  names (or in how the two surveys elicited names) is not excluded.
  Malik-Moraleda et al. ([LIT-tmpyky5z](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyky5z.md)) add that contact with English may
  explain the change. Bearing: evidence of the shape, without the control
  and, here, without the data.
- **[CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md).** As for [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md): the shape its value account predicts
  ([ARG-tmpcpvnb](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpcpvnb.md)).
- **[THEORY-163](../theory.d/THEORY-163.md).** Extends it from synchronic comparison to one language's
  history, and reports the same near-optimality.

## Limitations

- An abstract: no figures, sample, elicitation method or statistics.
- The year of the resurvey differs between sources (2017 here; 2018 in
  Malik-Moraleda et al.), and the number of terms (seven here; "nine in
  2018" there), unresolved until the journal paper is read.
- A gap in its own argument: "unlikely without pressure for efficiency"
  is asserted against an unstated null model.
