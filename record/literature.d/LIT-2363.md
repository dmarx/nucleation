# LIT-2363: Behavioural welfare analysis and revealed preference: theory and experimental evidence

Daniele Caliari (2026). "Behavioural welfare analysis and revealed preference: theory and experimental evidence." *Theory and Decision*. DOI-10.1007/s11238-026-10164-1.

## Key takeaways

- Every welfare method is "implicitly model-based", since "each welfare
  method, being an inverse mapping, naturally imposes constraints on the
  models of decision-making" (§1).
- Models based on attention, salience, perception, reference points or
  context "violate IR and CNN because they induce a misalignment between
  choice probabilities and the underlying deterministic preference"
  (footnote 8).
- How many observations break an indifference "is a decision variable for
  the researcher" (§2.3.1).
