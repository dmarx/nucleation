# NOTE-tmpgrtgu: Carnap — Logical Foundations of Probability, chapter I on explication

## Contribution

The classic statement of explication as a method, and of the four
requirements an explicatum must meet. It is the source the record's
secondary account (THEORY-1797, from the SEP entry) reports.

## Key results

1. **Explication defined (§2).** It consists "in transforming a given
   more or less inexact concept into an exact one or, rather, in replacing
   the first by the second." The explicatum "must be given by explicit
   rules for its use".
2. **Not right or wrong, but more satisfactory (§2).** "Strictly speaking,
   the question whether the solution is right or wrong makes no good sense
   because there is no clear-cut answer. The question should rather be
   whether the proposed solution is satisfactory, whether it is more
   satisfactory than another one, and the like."
3. **Clarify the explicandum first (§2).** Philosophers ask "'What is
   life?', 'What is mind?'" without checking that the terms are practically
   clear. The remedy: "some examples for its intended use and other
   examples for uses not now intended". These belong "to the formulation of
   the problem, not yet to the construction of an answer."
4. **The four requirements (§3).** "(1) similarity to the explicandum, (2)
   exactness, (3) fruitfulness, (4) simplicity", each to be fulfilled "to a
   sufficient degree":
   - similarity: "in most cases in which the explicandum has so far been
     used, the explicatum can be used; however, close similarity is not
     required, and considerable differences are permitted";
   - exactness: its rules of use given "in an exact form, so as to
     introduce the explicatum into a well-connected system of scientific
     concepts";
   - fruitfulness: "useful for the formulation of many universal
     statements";
   - simplicity: "as simple as the more important requirements (1), (2),
     and (3) permit", and in practice a tiebreaker among concepts that
     "seem to be equally fruitful".
5. **Departures paid for by fruitfulness (§3).** Zoologists replaced Fish
   ("animal living in water") by Piscis, which excludes whales, because
   Piscis "promised to be much more fruitful than any concept more similar
   to Fish". The change "was not a correction in the field of factual
   knowledge but a change in the rules of the language", though "motivated
   by factual discoveries".
6. **Menger's criteria (§3, note).** Cited as a good explicit formulation:
   the explicatum "must include all entities which are always denoted and
   must exclude all entities which are never denoted", may extend use to new
   cases, and "must yield many consequences".

## Assumptions and scope

Carnap's examples are scientific and logical concepts, and fruitfulness
is measured by laws or theorems. The record's terms are mostly concepts in
its own argument, where the analogue of a law is a claim the term makes
statable. That transfer is the record's, not Carnap's.

## Bearing on the record

Gives the explication form (ADR-tmptwlsg) its primary source, and
sharpens two of its critical questions. Similarity is loose: a definition
may exclude paradigm cases of the old word if fruitfulness pays for it,
so "do the paradigm cases fall on the right side?" should ask whether each
departure is paid for. And a separate step comes first: the explicandum,
what "learning" was meant to cover and not cover, must be made clear by
examples before a definition can be judged. Bears on ARG-tmp23khr and
CLAIM-472.
