# THEORY-tmpup8ol: Wenar argues that Scanlon's contractualism cannot, from its principles and their justifications alone, say which principles ascribe rights or to whom obligations are owed, and that because it must also account for rights of authority to change what is owed it must be a multi-level theory, of authority over as well as of owing to

Wenar (2013), [LIT-tmpretfe](../literature.d/LIT-tmpretfe.md).

## The argument

"All of Scanlon's fleshed-out examples of contractualist principles are
principles of wrongness, not of wronging" (p. 385), and "the party whose
interests or complaints are weightiest is not the party we believe to be
the rightholder" (p. 392). "Rights of authority explain who can change
what we owe to each other and how. "What we owe to each other" is a
one-level concept; contractualism must be a multi-level theory" (p. 399).

## What it does not show

- That reasonable rejection cannot supply the second level; Scanlon's
  reply ([LIT-tmplag34](../literature.d/LIT-tmplag34.md)) argues it can, for promises.
- A theory of directionality, promised in n. 46.
