# CLAIM-tmp7xsyj: Formally independent checks can fail together: where a shared concern, such as party alignment, conditions several institutions' dispositions to use their checking powers, their corrective capacities fail in common mode while their legal independence stays intact, so an architecture of checks needs independence of the dispositions to check under the circumstances that call for correction, not only separate offices

## The claim

A36 §6: "Two backup systems are not meaningfully independent if both
depend upon the same vulnerable resource." It draws "the difference
between **formal institutional independence and effective regulatory
independence**". "An institution may possess constitutional authority to
scrutinize another institution while its operative motivations make
exercising that authority less likely." "The relevant question is not
merely whether several institutions possess checking powers. It is
whether their capacities and dispositions to exercise those powers remain
sufficiently independent under the circumstances in which correction is
necessary."

It bounds the claim twice. "Party organization itself is not the
pathology. Parties facilitate representation, coordination and collective
action. The problem arises when particular forms of alignment defeat the
independent corrective functions upon which the architecture relies."
And the other side: "Conversely, divided partisan control can create
strong incentives for scrutiny, but those incentives can also contribute
to obstruction."

A36 cites Levinson and Pildes ([LIT-tmpkhum8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpkhum8.md)): "party competition can be
more explanatory of interbranch behavior than the formal separation of
institutions alone". That is A36's account of the article, unverified
until read. A36 calls them "Political scientists and constitutional
scholars"; both are law professors.

## What it adds to the record

[CLAIM-1002](CLAIM-1002.md) holds that parallel perspectives raise the chance that some
route answers a question only insofar as their failures are independent.
This claim applies that to corrective institutions and names what they
share: not a resource but a disposition to use a power. Its own content is
the distinction between the independence of offices and the independence
of the dispositions to act from them.

## The gap

A36 states the mechanism and gives no measure of correlation or instance.
Whether the use of checking powers varies with party alignment is an old
empirical question in American politics; the assistant recalls that the
findings are mixed (Mayhew's Divided We Govern, [LIT-tmpiqxhs](../literature.d/LIT-tmpiqxhs.md), 1991, found major
investigations about as frequent under unified as under divided
government; later work, such as Kriner and Schickler, [LIT-tmpfn8el](../literature.d/LIT-tmpfn8el.md), found more
investigative activity under divided government). Both are registered, unread; [LEAD-tmps9jkd](../leads.d/LEAD-tmps9jkd.md) lists
them for reading. The second defeater is the confound any such study must
meet. The section is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting
it is the owner's call.
