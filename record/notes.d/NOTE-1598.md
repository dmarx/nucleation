# NOTE-1598: Kuehn — A mathematical framework for critical transitions

## Contribution

Critical transitions ("tipping points") had a working definition, "A
non-mathematical working definition of a critical" transition is an abrupt
change in a dynamical system (§1), and a body of applied early-warning
work. The paper gives them a mathematical definition: "We suggest that
the mathematical theory of fast-slow systems" provides it (abstract). It
adds the recovery exponent, a measure of how slowing down scales with
bifurcation type; an interval for how far the jump goes (Def. 3.1); and,
on generic normal forms, proofs and counterexamples for the standard
early-warning signs. Kuehn offers it as "a mathematical complement" to
Scheffer et al.'s review (§1).

## Key insight

Make the drifting parameter a slow variable, and a tipping point becomes a
geometric object: the place where the attracting slow manifold the system
has been following stops being normally hyperbolic and the trajectory
jumps. Every frozen parameter value is stable until that point; the
transition is in the drift, not in any instantaneous instability.

## Assumptions

- **Small noise.** "There is small noise in the system i.e. the data has a
  major deterministic component" (§1, assumption (5)).
- **Time-scale separation**, 0 < ε ≪ 1, in x′ = f(x, y), y′ = εg(x, y),
  with slow drift g bounded away from zero near the bifurcation and
  mostly g ≡ 1.
- **One slow variable** (n = 1), and mostly one-dimensional fast normal
  forms (fold, transcritical, pitchfork); planar for Hopf.
- **No noise on the slow variable** (σ_g = 0) in the stochastic sections.
- **Fenichel normal form** for the detrended data in §4, which the paper
  itself calls a problem: coordinate changes alter the indicators.

## Key results

1. **Definition (§2, Def. 2.4).** "We say that p is a critical transition
   if there is a candidate" γ₀ with (C1) an attracting normally hyperbolic
   segment, (C2) p a transition point, (C3) the right orientation.
2. **Fold (Prop. 2.5).** "Then the fold bifurcation is also a critical
   transition."
3. **Hopf and pitchfork (Props. 2.6–2.7).** Critical when subcritical; for
   Hopf, "If it is supercritical (l1 < 0) then" the transition is not
   critical, and likewise for the pitchfork.
4. **Transcritical (Prop. 2.8).** "The transcritical bifurcation is a
   critical transition." The jump's distance interval runs from 0 to ∞
   (§3), so it need not go far.
5. **Recovery exponent (Def. 2.9, Prop. 2.10).** If the leading Lyapunov
   exponent on the attracting sheet is O(y^α), "then we suggest to call α
   the recovery exponent."; α = ½ for the fold and 1 for Hopf, pitchfork
   and transcritical. "In particular, we point out that different
   bifurcations can also" lead to different quantitative slowing down.
6. **Delay (§3, Theorems 3.2–3.3).** With slow drift the jump comes
   O(ε^{2/3}) past the fold: "One way to think of Theorem 3.2 is that the
   trajectory of the full system does not jump" at the fold point itself.
   "Let us point out that it is a key new" observation that delay must
   enter prediction.
7. **Variance rises, provably (§4).** "we expect the variance to increase
   as we approach the transition"; for normal forms, "In particular, there
   is no need to refer to heuristic" arguments. Theorem 4.1 bounds how long
   paths stay near the slow manifold; "Theorem 4.1 is reminiscent of the
   classical Kramer’s time to escape from a potential well".
8. **Multiplicative noise (§5).** With "multiplicative noise a rising
   variance early-warning sign can fail"; "P-bifurcation indicators should
   definitely be added to the toolbox". In the Arnold–Boxler example the
   variance is not monotone.
9. **Noise against time-scale separation (§6, Theorem 6.1).** For σ ≫ √ε,
   "critical transitions before the deterministic fold bifurcation occur
   with very high probability."; transitions come early "if the noise level
   is larger than the time scale separation." The escape time over the
   barrier is of order e^{2H/σ²}, citing Freidlin and Wentzell (1998).
10. **Symmetry breaking (§7).** In the pitchfork example, symmetry-breaking
    "and P-bifurcations are not necessarily early-warning signs of critical
    transitions." The Fokker–Planck
    variance rises and then falls near the bifurcation, an artefact of the
    reflecting boundaries chosen.
11. **Single-path variance (§8).** Near a fold, "Hence the sample variance
    will be larger due to geometric considerations and without even"
    considering the noise.
12. **Single-path autocorrelation (§9).** Of two sample paths, one shows
    falling autocorrelation, "but we would make an incorrect prediction
    from the black sample" path.

## Concepts

- **Fast–slow system**, **critical manifold** C = {f = 0}, **normal
  hyperbolicity**, **slow manifold** (Fenichel).
- **Candidate**: a concatenation of singular-limit fast and slow segments;
  a **transition point** is where they switch.
- **Critical transition**: Def. 2.4.
- **Recovery exponent** α (Def. 2.9); the jump-distance interval l^i(p),
  l^s(p) (Def. 3.1).
- **D-bifurcation** (of invariant measures of a random dynamical system)
  and **P-bifurcation** (a qualitative change of the stationary density)
  (§5).
- **Intermediate regime**: σ ≈ √ε for the fold, σ ≈ ε^{3/4} for
  transcritical and pitchfork (§6).

## Connections

It builds on Fenichel's geometric singular perturbation theory ([LIT-1638](../literature.d/LIT-1638.md))
for slow manifolds, on Berglund and Gentz's sample-path estimates for
noise near slow manifolds, on Freidlin and Wentzell (1998 edition; held as
[LIT-2035](../literature.d/LIT-2035.md)) for escape times, and on Arnold's random dynamical systems
for D- and P-bifurcations. It complements Scheffer et al. ([LIT-1956](../literature.d/LIT-1956.md)). Its
companion paper, arXiv 1101.2908 on normal forms, variance and
applications, is not held.

## Limitations

- **The author's own.** Mostly one-dimensional fast normal forms with one
  slow variable; Hopf in detail, global bifurcations, chaos, spatial
  indicators, higher-dimensional systems and noise effects on delay are
  left for later (§10). "Hence we have identified the problem of
  coordinate transformation effects on critical transition indicators as
  a topic for future study." (§4). "we are fully aware that we have not
  maximized the results one" can obtain, and "calculating the precise jump
  time should be the second step of the mathematical analysis."
- **Mine.** Much of it is review; the new results are definitions, short
  propositions and numerics at a single (σ, ε) with 1000 or 10000 paths.
  The §7 variance fall is an artefact of the chosen boundaries. The arXiv
  v2 was read, not the typeset journal version.

## Open questions

- Do the recovery exponents let observed slowing down tell a fold from a
  transcritical or pitchfork loss in data, as §10 suggests? A study fitting
  the scaling on records with known bifurcation type would tell.
