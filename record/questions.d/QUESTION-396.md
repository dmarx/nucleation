# QUESTION-396: Under what conditions does structural memory improve an organization's control over its future behavior, and when does accumulated history instead create hidden states and unexpected transition pathways?

## Why it is a question

A: memory resides in configuration and must be legible, the readout
y = h(s) being possibly non-injective. Programmable reconfiguration,
y = f_z(x) with the program z set from outside, differs from
self-governing reconfiguration, z_{t+1} = g(z, x, y), in which the
system's own history rewrites the program. A also separates the
probabilities that a structure forms, persists and reproduces, P_form,
P_persist and P_reproduce.

The readings show memory doing both things the question names.

- **Memory that extends control.** Jules et al. ([NOTE-1617](../notes.d/NOTE-1617.md); [THEORY-1415](../theory.d/THEORY-1415.md)):
  four bistable Kresling barrels in series form a Preisach memory, all
  16 states stable and reachable, with return-point memory, the
  transition graph fixed by the order of the switching thresholds. Liu et
  al. ([NOTE-1600](../notes.d/NOTE-1600.md); [THEORY-1431](../theory.d/THEORY-1431.md)): a multistable origami reservoir whose buffer
  zone's kept configuration "carries the historic input information".
- **Memory that hides.** In Jules et al. twisting by hand reaches defect
  states the declared loading cannot reach; they sit in loops that the
  loading neither enters nor leaves except by irreversible erasure or a
  shift of the defect. "Garden of Eden states" is the paper's phrase.
  Height and stiffness identify states only partly, and the authors name
  reading precision as the limit on scaling.

The record holds parts. [TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md) gives legibility; [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md) separates
legibility from efficacy and correctability, and Jules et al. separate
stability in time, writability and non-destructive readout in the same
way. [CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md) holds that restoring the conditions of a loss need not
restore the capacity, and [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md) that an intervention can change what
it would take to correct it. None asks when stored history is an asset to
control and when a liability.

## What would answer it

Conditions on the readout h, on the operations that write the stored
configuration, and on the operations declared admissible, under which
stored history enlarges the set of futures the organization can select
and read back; and conditions under which it creates states that the
declared operations reach only by accident or by operations outside them,
or leave only irreversibly, or that the readout cannot tell apart from
others. A mechanical answer in Preisach terms, extended to coupled units,
would answer part.

## What would not answer it

A memory that works, or one that fails, without the conditions that
separate them; or a count of states that includes ones the declared
operations cannot reach, as the paper's 64 does.
