# THEORY-tmp2eczh: Jaber, Jung, Matecki and Petrof review evidence that fully controlled mechanical ventilation weakens, shrinks and injures the human diaphragm as it does in animals: biopsies from brain-dead organ donors ventilated for hours to days show diaphragm fibre atrophy and injury with oxidative stress and raised proteolysis markers, not seen in the pectoralis, the changes growing with the duration of ventilation, and diaphragm twitch pressure in ventilated patients falls by about a third in six days; in animals, keeping spontaneous effort lessens the loss but leaves a residual deficit

## Source

Jaber, Jung, Matecki & Petrof (2011), [LIT-tmp0huzr](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0huzr.md); read in [NOTE-tmpat5nt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpat5nt.md).
It stands in for Levine et al. (2008), [LIT-tmp0xw48](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0xw48.md), of which only the
abstract was read; that abstract agrees with the review's account of it.

## What was actually shown

- **Atrophy in donors.** Levine 2008: 14 donors ventilated 18–69 hours
  against 8 surgical controls ventilated 2–3 hours; atrophy of both fibre
  types (57% and 53% by the abstract), "whereas no such differences were
  observed in the pectoralis muscle" (the review).
- **Dose.** Levine's follow-up (18 donors) and the authors' own study (15
  donors, 24–249 hours, fully controlled): markers, atrophy and injury
  "significantly correlated with the total duration of MV".
- **Force.** Twitch tracheal pressure fell "with a mean reduction of 32 ±
  6% at 6 days" in the authors' patients.
- **Partial support, animals.** Keeping effort "alleviates VIDD", but
  partial modes leave "a residual deficit of diaphragmatic force
  production".

Each human result could have come out otherwise: the pectoralis could have
shrunk too (systemic catabolism), and the changes could have been
unrelated to duration.

## What this does not say

- **That the loss causes failed weaning.** The review asserts diaphragm
  function is "a major determinant" of weaning; no outcome is measured.
- **That ventilation alone is the cause in ICU patients.** Sedation,
  illness and drugs go with duration, and brain death is its own state;
  the authors concede VIDD cannot be diagnosed in a given patient.
- **That partial support protects humans.** That evidence is animal, and
  the optimal effort is unknown.
- **Anything about recovery**, which the authors say is unstudied. So it
  shows no threshold or hysteresis.
- It bears on [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)'s condition that the party did the work throughout,
  and on [CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md) and [CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md), by analogy from tissue; the authors make
  no claim about organizations.
