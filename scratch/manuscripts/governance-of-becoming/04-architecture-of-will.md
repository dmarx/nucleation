# Article 4. The Architecture of Will

## Authority, organizational affect, and the negotiation of incompatible demands

**Abstract.** An organization that maintains incompatible demands still acts, and something settles which of its concerns, proposals and interventions takes effect. This article argues that what settles it is an architecture of practical authority, a will-organization, whose relations (precedence, jurisdiction and suspension, and the authority granted as against the precedence claimed) are distinct from one another and from the mechanisms that realize them. In a model of one stipulated organization, a description in these terms beats five control-only models by more than its complexity cost and alone predicts combinations of changes in authority unseen in training; it loses where authority is idle or idiosyncratic, and a wrongly composed version wins the training evidence and then mispredicts, so such comparisons must be priced on held-out interventions. Against control theory the relational variables are implemented, not replaced; against institutional theory, for an organization, they are not new, and what would be new, one description across realizers, is untested. The article then shows that forbidding self-grants does not stop recursive amplification, that a tolerance is a directed criterion for conferring influence, and that a quiet perspective need not be satisfied; argues that the primitive of regulatory difficulty is frustration, an ordinal relation among demands, not distress; and locates organizational affect, as a conjecture facing an unanswered objection, at the gap between what a motive claims and what it is granted. Learned accommodation can lower the cost of recurring arbitration, but fluent coordination does not show an accommodation adequate, and regimes of authority can outlast what justified them; the saving over recurrence has not been measured.

## 4.1 Introduction

Every organization that maintains more than one demand will sooner or later maintain two that cannot both be met. A firm wants a product shipped and wants it safe; a reliability group objects to a release that management wants out; a person wants to finish a draft and wants to stop. What happens next is not decided by the demands alone. The same proposals, pressed with the same force, can issue in deployment, in caution or in a freeze, depending on who may object, who classifies the severity of a fault and whether anyone may override a halt. The question of this article is what that dependence is: what settles which of an organization's concerns takes effect, and whether a theory of it is anything more than a description of the mechanisms that carry it out.

Two answers are available. On the first, the mechanisms are everything. Control theory and research on executive control describe how a system selects among regimes by their expected value, gates one on and another off, and pays a cost for control; once those mechanisms are written down, nothing is left for a further, agent-level description to do. On the second, which this article defends, there is a structure the mechanisms realize but do not exhaust: a maintained organization of relations that govern when an action-generating regime acquires, keeps, loses or alters practical efficacy. Call it a will-organization. The issue between the two answers can be put as a question with a test attached: when the mechanisms composing a will-organization are described by control theory and executive-control research, does that description implement the account or replace it, and what result would show the agent-level description had become idle (QUESTION-056)? Replacement is shown if the mechanism model predicts as well with no will-organization variables (QUESTION-056).

The argument proceeds in the order its steps depend on each other. Section 4.2 states the vocabulary; 4.3 and 4.4 put it against control-only models and say what the comparison supports; 4.5 and 4.6 give the structure of grants and tolerances; 4.7 argues for frustration as the primitive; 4.8 and 4.9 locate affect at the junction of frustration and authority and test the location on a corporation; 4.10 and 4.11 take up accommodation and entrenchment; 4.12 and 4.13 apply the apparatus to scalar objectives and to misunderstanding; 4.14 states what is not established and the strongest objection.

## 4.2 Differentiated authority

A will-organization is "the maintained organization of relations governing the conditions under which an agent's available action-generating regimes acquire, retain, lose, or alter practical efficacy" (TERM-023). Three of those relations are easily run together: "precedence orders applicable constraints, jurisdiction governs which apply, suspension governs when an applicable one may be disabled" (TERM-017). They can vary independently, as the classification variants of a worked organizational case show (CLAIM-057). The thesis that gives them their point is that which regime becomes effective "depends on maintained relations — accessibility, precedence, persistence, jurisdiction, suspension and transformation — that are not reducible to the contents of the competing regimes, whatever mechanisms realize them" (CLAIM-027). Its characteristic design follows: "changing the conditions of efficacy changes behaviour while the competing preferences stay fixed" (CLAIM-122). If behaviour moves when only the arrangement of authority moves, the arrangement is doing explanatory work that the contents of the regimes are not.

Two distinctions keep authority apart from what it is commonly taken for.

The first is between the precedence a motive claims and the authority it is granted. Frijda's account of emotion gives a motive a claim on behaviour: action readiness with control precedence (Frijda, Ridderinkhof and Rietveld 2014; THEORY-055). But a claim is not a grant. "The precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it are different quantities: a motive can claim control without receiving it, and be granted control it does not claim" (CLAIM-283). The distinction "does not say which wins" (CLAIM-283). A further thesis identifies internal conflict with the gap between the two quantities (CLAIM-484); this article uses only the distinction.

The second separates activation from authority and from efficacy. A threshold on expected benefit, the natural control-theoretic form, would make a process act only when acting is predicted to pay. Yet "an intervention doesn't need to be predicted to be effective to be motivating. consider someone punching a wall in anger." A process's "activation, the authority the organization grants it and the efficacy of what it does are separate quantities, so a threshold on expected net benefit is one special case of an activation threshold and not its general form" (CLAIM-548). The claim's own rival, a goal-directed reading of costly aggression, is stated beside it (NOTE-1117; CLAIM-548).

With these in hand, the question of §4.1 becomes sharper. A control-only model can see the arrangement of authority as an input. What it lacks is a variable for a relation: for who holds which position and what follows from holding it. Whether that lack costs it anything is an empirical question about a model, and the next section answers it for one.

## 4.3 A test against control-only models

The comparison is an executed study of a stipulated organization (CASE-325), built on a worked case of an engineering firm, Northstar (CASE-023), and carrying out a plan to model it "with control and metacontrol mechanisms alone and then with a will-organization over them", price the difference, and set "planning, control and institutional theory" beside the account (LEAD-089). Two criteria were fixed before the run. One is QUESTION-056's, quoted above. The other: "A difference the control-only model cannot produce at matched complexity supports CLAIM-487's gain; none, or one an existing theory already gives, defeats CLAIM-024" (LEAD-089). CLAIM-487 is the thesis that describing these relations as one will-organization across realizers explains something that the separate fields describing their own realizers do not; CLAIM-024 is the objection that nothing in the account is new but a question.

The construction is this (CASE-325). Each episode decides which of three regimes becomes effective: routine deployment, cautious verification or emergency suspension. Authority is three bits, each taken from one of the case's own variants (CASE-002; CASE-003): whether an objection suspends deployment, who classifies severity, and whether senior management may override a freeze. "The proposals are the same under every arrangement", and so are each party's propensities. The will-organization model has four parameters, and in it "Maintained relations decide which proposal becomes effective". Against it stand five control-only models. "Each sees the arrangement of authority as an input. None has a variable for a relation." One of them is a metacontrol gate, "the form of an expected-value-of-control choice among regimes", after the account of control allocation by Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen (2013; THEORY-1110). The control models have 16, 128, 14, 38 and 64 parameters. Training sees "the baseline and each single change of authority"; the test is "2,000 fresh episodes under each of the four combinations never seen in training". In the will-organization's terms, the three bits are a suspension, a jurisdiction and an override of precedence; the study varies them separately, and its result turns on how they compose. It runs four worlds, which differ in how authority actually operates (governed, idle, idiosyncratic and interacting), at four sample sizes and over 20 seeds.

In the governed world, where the organization works as the relational model says, the relational model "beats every control-only model by more than its Occam cost on 80 of 80 seeds". The median log Bayes factor, with its range over seeds, runs from 17.1 (10.0 to 22.2) at 30 episodes to 61.3 (49.1 to 70.7) at 1,000. On the unseen combinations it "is within 0.015 nats per episode of the truth, and every gate is 0.03 to 0.26 nats per episode away" (CASE-325; CLAIM-889). By QUESTION-056's criterion the control models do not predict as well, so replacement is not shown.

The gap is not a matter of data. At a million episodes per training arrangement, the gates' regret on the unseen combinations stays at 0.08 to 0.11 nats per episode for the metacontrol gate and at 0.022 to 0.034 and 0.009 to 0.018 for the two richest gates. The reason is structural: "A log-additive gate cannot represent how the relations compose. The products of two authority bits that would let it are never observed when each change is tried alone" (CASE-325). Nor is a richer gate an escape: "A gate with products of authority bits can represent the composition. It cannot learn those products from single changes without a prior that is the relations again" (CASE-325).

The test can go the other way. In the idle and idiosyncratic worlds, where authority does no work or works case by case, the relational model "loses on every seed, by 0.9 to 2,551 nats". "So the test can come out against the will-organization" (CASE-325).

The fourth world is the instructive one. It has the same relations, "except that where the reliability group classifies it also takes objections over, so an objection no longer suspends by itself", a difference that "shows only in combined arrangements". Here the relational model, which composes the relations the governed way, "wins the training evidence by 10 to 82 nats on every seed, because the misplaced relation never shows in training. It then predicts the unseen combinations worse than the additive gate C2: 0.26 to 0.32 nats per episode against 0.16 to 0.20" (CASE-325). A proposed criterion of willed agency counts a factor as a willed agent when a will-organization over several regimes describes it better than none, priced against an Occam cost (CLAIM-174). Priced on the training arrangements alone, that criterion "also passes a will-organization whose relations compose otherwise than it says" (CLAIM-889). The study's methodological conclusion is therefore part of its result: "The pricing has to be done on held-out interventions" (CASE-325). Evidence on the arrangements a model was trained on cannot tell a right composition of authority from a wrong one; only the combinations it was not trained on can.

These are results about a model in which authority is stipulated to operate in four specified ways. The study says of itself that it cannot show "That any real company is like this", nor "Anything about a person or across realizers" (CASE-325).

## 4.4 Implemented, not replaced; and not new

What the comparison supports depends on which field it is set against, and the reading of it distinguishes three (CLAIM-890).

Against control theory and executive control, the result is a gain. Those fields "give the control-only models, which miss the difference, so against them the will-organization variables are implemented and not replaced". A control model that matched the relational one "has to add a variable for who holds which position and what follows from holding it. That variable is the relation" (CLAIM-890).

Against planning theory, there is nothing to compare: planning theory, "holding the plan fixed as the case does, gives no difference at all" (CLAIM-890). That holds for the planning theory this argument has read. Bratman's planning theory of shared agency (Bratman 2014; LIT-488) has not been read, and "that verdict waits on it" (CLAIM-890).

Against institutional theory, the result is not a gain. Institutional analysis "already writes the relations as rules-in-use" (Polski and Ostrom 1999; THEORY-1809), "so for an organization the difference is one an existing theory gives, and what is left of the explanatory-gain claim is that one description serves across realizers, which a model of one realizer cannot test" (CLAIM-890). The study states the same limit among what it cannot show: "That the gain is the will-organization's rather than the rules'" (CASE-325). For an organization, then, "the agent-level description is not idle, yet not new either. Still open for a person" (QUESTION-056).

This is the article's first result, and it cuts both ways. The objection that the account adds nothing (CLAIM-024) "fails on two fronts in this case and holds on the third" (CLAIM-890). The pre-registered criterion has to be read with that in mind: as written, it says that a difference an existing theory already gives defeats the objection, which "looks inverted"; read the other way, such a difference supports the objection on that front, and that is how the result is graded here (CLAIM-890). Nor does the concession make the account a relabelling. That "would follow only if no second realizer shares the variables. The claim leaves that open" (CLAIM-890).

The concession was anticipated. "The mechanisms composing a will-organization are not novel" (CLAIM-021): what the account claims as novel is "a common explanatory role across realizers, not a mechanism" (CLAIM-021). The study applies that concession to an organization. A second bound comes from a different model: under full-information, independent audits, a learned allocation of influence is no better than a contextual router trained on the same audits, so "whatever governance adds over routing has to be shown where evaluation fails", and no such regime has yet been found (CLAIM-467). How that bound relates to the comparison of §4.3 is not settled; the two models differ in what evaluation can see, and no argument yet joins them.

What would be new is therefore the cross-realizer claim (CLAIM-487), and it needs "a second realizer modelled with the same variables" (CLAIM-890). The natural candidate is a person, such as a writer who wants to finish and wants to stop (CASE-013). That model has not been built; it is planned, as a study to model a second realizer with the Northstar construction's variables and price it against the best model of that realizer's own field by the same log-evidence test (LEAD-tmpb2w1a). The nearest test for a person is a different one: interventions that block or restore the agent model with competing thoughts held fixed, where "Effects of blocking the agent model with thoughts fixed support CLAIM-027" (LEAD-141). A search of the literature found that the frontal stimulation study cited as an early test of this kind (Axelrod et al. 2015) is not one, so the argument that read it as one fails (LEAD-141; ARG-215), and that the intervention wanted "The literature does not supply" (LEAD-141). That intervention is also the across-realizers defeat condition of the thesis of §4.2 (CLAIM-027), "not yet run" (QUESTION-015; SYNTHESIS-036). The question that thesis answers, which maintained relations govern which of an agent's regimes becomes effective, has been answered "on argument, not on that intervention" (QUESTION-015).

## 4.5 Recursive grants

If authority is a relation distinct from its realizers, it can be passed on, and passing it on has a structure of its own. The tempting rule is that no operator may grant authority to itself. It does not suffice. "Forbidding self-granting does not stop an operator from amplifying its own authority: under damped recursive delegation, reciprocal and cyclic grants recycle authority, so an operator's authority depends on its own grants, while one-hop allocation within an epoch, from budgets and baselines fixed independently of outgoing grants, makes it exactly independent of them" (CLAIM-282). These results are proved for the schemes stated.

One-hop allocation has a second virtue. Under it, granting influence to a nearer ally "raises how far its preferred policy is realized while its own authority stays fixed, so the two conditions of the grant scheme hold together" (CLAIM-375): an operator can pursue its ends through grants without its grants feeding back into its own authority. Multi-hop delegation is not ruled out. "Multi-hop delegation can keep an operator's authority exactly independent of its own grants" under leave-one-out damped delegation (CLAIM-502), though that result does not say "that [CLAIM-375]'s guarantee carries over" (CLAIM-502).

Two summaries are not warranted. That only one-hop allocation keeps both conditions is true of the rules examined and open beyond them (SYNTHESIS-087). And none of the three results says "That any rule closes the social route of moving others' grants" (SYNTHESIS-087): an operator who cannot amplify itself through its own grants may still do so by moving what others grant.

A population study of grant schemes extends the point in a fictional model. There, "Role indexing with the critic role scored by relevance leaves no factions and no capture; scalar grants fail either way", and operators bound by both grant conditions "delegate everything, all of it to their own side" (CASE-420). It is "A fictional model, by construction; the numbers are of this model" (CASE-420).

## 4.6 Tolerances as endorsement criteria

Conferral is any effect one process has on another's share of future influence (TERM-103); a grant is a conferral that runs through a maintained relation of granting, delegation, eligibility or support (TERM-101). A tolerance is the criterion by which a perspective confers. "A perspective's tolerance for another process's contribution is its criterion for conferring influence on that contribution, set by its own priorities: tolerances are directed, and the discrepancy each measures is the perspective's own, so inside its tolerance the perspective's interventions raise the other's share of future influence and outside it they lower it; meeting the tolerance is necessary for that conferral where the criterion binds, and sufficient only for a grant, under a rule that grants influence on it" (CLAIM-552).

The scope conditions matter. Meeting a tolerance is necessary for conferral only "where the criterion binds". It is sufficient for a grant, and only "under a rule that grants influence on it". It is not sufficient for influence, which the channel or the rule may fail to carry: the claim is not defeated by "a grant that fails to raise influence because the channel or the rule does not carry it" (CLAIM-552). Endorsement, on this account, is directed and conditional, and it is distinct both from influence and from silence.

Four results surround it, and none makes a quiet perspective a consenting one.

Tolerances come from thresholds. If activation is z = b(c) + α(c)d and the perspective acts with probability σ(κ(z − θ)), the discrepancy it tolerates while acting with probability at most ε is "(θ + κ⁻¹ logit ε − b(c))/α(c)". So one fixed threshold gives different tolerances across contexts, and "raising a threshold enlarges tolerance without relieving any" demand (CLAIM-550). This is the first link of a proposed chain from motivational thresholds to tolerances; whether the chain holds link by link beyond it is open (SYNTHESIS-044; QUESTION-372).

Quiet need not be satisfied. "The quiescent complex … is not the compatibility complex of their requirements … a perspective can be quiet while its requirement is unmet and active while it is met; raising thresholds can only enlarge the quiescent complex and leaves the requirement complex as it was, so it is not relief of frustration" (CLAIM-549). The set of perspectives that are jointly quiet and the set whose requirements are jointly met are different objects, and nothing done to thresholds moves the second.

Not intervening is not endorsing: "a perspective that does not intervene has not thereby endorsed" (CLAIM-553). This one is argued, not derived.

Bounds can be incompatible in a way no local adjustment repairs. On a graph of contexts, an assignment within every tolerance exists "exactly when no cycle's holonomy exceeds the sum of its tolerances", and "local adjustment can move a mismatch among the overlaps of a cycle and never remove it" (CLAIM-538). The feasibility criterion is classical, so the result is granted, not discovered here (CLAIM-538); what the argument adds is its reading as a condition on tolerances. A companion result adds disturbance and what the agent can read (CLAIM-537). These results use symmetric bounds on an edge; the directed tolerance of CLAIM-552 has not been given a compatibility theory of its own.

Nor has a model been built in which tolerances and authority evolve together, so that an organization's acceptance criteria are acquired through its history rather than supplied by the modeler. Such a model would meet a burden stated elsewhere, that norms of function and failure be defined from an organization's own dynamics (QUESTION-128), and that the standard be learned rather than supplied (CASE-239). A forager that learns its own criteria of failure has been built and run (CASE-421), for criteria of failure, not for tolerances. The construction for tolerances, extending that forager to learned tolerances with a fixed-threshold control, is planned and not built (LEAD-tmpobng3).

## 4.7 Frustration among demands

### 4.7.1 The primitive

The relations of §§4.2 to 4.6 decide among demands that conflict. What, then, is the conflict? The answer proposed here is that the primitive for an organization's regulatory difficulty is not distress but frustration: "a relation between maintained, consequential demands and the incompatibilities, constraints or countervailing processes that limit their realization, independent of strain, the burden of sustaining activity, and distinct from distress, a possible integrated response to either" (CLAIM-368). The choice "says what to start from, not that a frustrated organization is distressed or feels anything" (CLAIM-368). Frustration is "the failure of the demands it maintains to be jointly realizable under its admissible transformations and its governance" (TERM-060).

Frustration so defined is ordinal before it is a quantity. One can do a great deal with an ordering without a true measure. "Frustration among an organization's demands is an ordinal fact before it is a quantity: the jointly satisfiable sets of demands form a downward-closed complex whose minimal non-faces are the minimal conflicts, relief is strict enlargement of that complex for the same declared demands, reorganizations that relieve different conflicts are incomparable, and dropping or suppressing a demand is not relief" (CLAIM-347). Relief is judged "against a fixed reference set of its original demands", and "an endogenous revision of what the organization maintains is a transformation of it and not relief" (CLAIM-361). The account of this part of the argument separates a perspective's "activation, tolerance, claims and grants" from the ordinal fact, "so a quiet perspective need not be satisfied and raising a threshold is not relief" (SYNTHESIS-044). This is where §4.6 and the present section meet: the quiescent complex belongs to the side of activation and tolerance, the compatibility complex to the side of frustration, and the two can diverge.

### 4.7.2 Frustration is not thwarting

Frustration should not be identified with thwarting, the operative defeat of a demand by a process. The measures that would be candidates for thwarting, a residual and a regret, "identify none of them as thwarting" (CLAIM-357; CLAIM-631), and they give necessary conditions only (SYNTHESIS-044). A model settles the independence in one setting: "With operative thwarting identified by silencing a process, structural frustration and thwarting are independent in both directions", and thwarting where joint satisfaction is attainable "needs a resolution that weighs authority or preference beyond the demands" (CLAIM-923; QUESTION-131). That clause is the point at which frustration meets authority. A will-organization that weighs authority, or preference, can thwart a demand that could have been met.

An executed model bears on the authority arm. In a coupled model of meaning and influence, "the frustration of satisfiable demands follows the allocation of influence when search and information are held fixed, but a captured allocation acts as a fixed weighting rule, so the model shows the governance-induced kind caused by allocation without showing conferral dynamics causing what no fixed weighting would" (CLAIM-880; CASE-317). That is a partial instance of the thesis that "The governance-induced kind of frustration is not idle" (CLAIM-500): partial, because a fixed weighting rule would do as well.

### 4.7.3 Pathology without duties

Processes in conflict need not stand in moral relations to be in trouble. "Processes need not owe one another moral duties for a system of them to enter a pathological regime of incompatible behaviour, one that takes intervention and a reorganization of their commitments to resolve its systemic frustration" (CLAIM-tmpe6yfm). The claim does not say "that every pathological regime is resolved by reorganizing commitments; some need resources or new capacities" (CLAIM-tmpe6yfm). Its minimal instance is priority inversion (CASE-tmptpna1), which Article 3 (§3.6) reads at length. Here it shows one thing: a relief can move a delay onto a third process instead of removing it.

### 4.7.4 What a demand needs in order to move action

Frustration says what is in conflict; it does not say what lets a demand take effect. Three distinct achievements are needed for that, "and any can fail while the others hold: legibility …; volitional efficacy …; and correctability", and "none establishes that the demand or the revision is justified" (CLAIM-523). The three are necessary conditions. No conjunction of them is sufficient for a demand to be justified, and the claim does not make them sufficient for action either. Volitional efficacy is the will-organization's contribution: the condition that a demand that has been made legible is granted the authority to move what the organization does.

## 4.8 Affect at the junction of claim and grant

### 4.8.1 The functional framing

The argument now has two pieces that a theory of affect could use: an ordinal account of frustration among demands, and a distinction between the precedence a motive claims and the authority it is granted. This section asks whether organizational affect can be located at their junction. Its answer is a conjecture, and every step of it beyond the vocabulary is proposed rather than established.

The framing is functional. "Affective states can be properties of organized systems, characterized functionally as Frijda characterizes emotions, without implying subjective experience" (CLAIM-039). Nothing here says that an organization feels. Whether a state that meets a functional characterization must be felt is an open question (QUESTION-059), and the distinction between "felt" and "functional" is drawn elsewhere (SYNTHESIS-022). The objection the framing has to answer first is Veit's identification of valence with a felt state, on which consciousness began as hedonic valence, a common currency for trading off competing options in action selection (Veit 2025; THEORY-101; CLAIM-039). That objection is not answered here or anywhere in the argument, and it holds back everything in this section and the next that rests on the framing (CLAIM-440; CLAIM-432; CLAIM-355).

### 4.8.2 The elements

Action readiness is Frijda's: "a state of preparation for action, not a representation of a desirable state, that claims control over behaviour by persisting until an end state, resuming after interruption, capturing attention and taking priority over other actions" (TERM-094). "A constructionist can use this term", and using it commits a claim to neither half of Frijda's account (TERM-094). The claimed side of the distinction of §4.2 is this readiness; the granted side is the will-organization's.

A motivating perspective is "an organizational perspective whose interpretations give circumstances a significance relative to concerns it maintains, and whose significance conditions action readiness, a claim to control that the will-organization may or may not grant" (TERM-104). This is a proposed construction, not yet used in a result.

Appraisal, on the conjecture, is negotiation. An organization's appraisal is the process by which its competing perspectives negotiate among themselves, and goal-conduciveness valence is "a regime in which an ordering of the organization's own states by their bearing on its maintenance prevails in the negotiation"; "ambivalence, where no ordering prevails, differs from neutrality" (CLAIM-440; TERM-076). The proposal rests on the ordinal account of frustration, the claim–grant distinction and the functional framing (CLAIM-440).

Two further proposals locate valence relative to perspectives. Wanting and liking are "saliences relative to different, overlapping processes", so "valence is then relative to a perspective" (CLAIM-437). And "an emotion, action readiness claiming control precedence, is a claim, a perspective's valence is read on its claims, and the system's valence is read on what the negotiation grants" (CLAIM-442).

### 4.8.3 What is unreconciled, and what is against it

These proposals are not consistent with each other on one point. One says a change in grants with claims fixed "leaves the perspectives' emotions and valences as they were" (CLAIM-442); the other defines a perspective's valence as "its ranking and its progress against its own reference course" (CLAIM-437), which a change in grants can move. Nothing reconciles the two (SYNTHESIS-020). How perspectives are to be individuated "without appeal to the dissociations they explain" is a defeater of the wanting–liking proposal that has not been met (CLAIM-437; SYNTHESIS-020).

There are objections beside Veit's. On the potential account, valence is read from an ordering of the organization's states that prevails in its contests. Its "no frustration, no valence" "runs together two relations: perspectives' contested rankings … and demands that are jointly infeasible", so "the consequence needs one of them named" (CLAIM-449). That objection is stated on the complex of §4.7.1 and is accepted; the affective proposals have not yet named which relation they mean. An empirical objection holds that the potential "tracks 'wanting', not 'liking'" (CLAIM-438). And the account concedes that it is "of goal-conduciveness valence, not of intrinsic pleasantness" (CLAIM-433).

### 4.8.4 Reading which ordering prevails

If valence is an ordering that prevails in a negotiation, one needs a way to tell when an organization's record of contests has such an ordering and when one cannot be presumed. The formal results here are proved. Some function rises with every winning move "exactly when no chain of winning moves returns to where it began" (CLAIM-434). And "admitting an ordering is strictly weaker than admitting a currency": "the lexicographic order on the unit square has no real-valued representation" (CLAIM-436). An organization can therefore have a coherent ordering of its states without any common scale on which to trade them.

A guard applies to every reading of such a record, and it is an accepted objection to the appraisal proposal: "where an organization's legibility regime selects which contests are admitted or recorded, an acyclic record, even a persistent potential, can be the product of selective contestation, so observed coherence is not evidence that the organization's perspectives are integrated" (CLAIM-448; TERM-078). It has built instances. A chair who admits only two of three cycling contests "gets an acyclic record" (CASE-221). Run as a simulation, a chair "installs any of the three options by leaving one contest off the agenda", so "the recorded outcome and the potential read off it are a function of admission, not of preference" (CASE-366).

One real record has been read by a stated procedure for reading which ordering prevails (QUESTION-268; CLAIM-899): the votes of the Federal Open Market Committee, 1966 to 2026 (CASE-330). It found that "a channel's lead is shown in one window only, on four contests", and that "ambivalence is shown only for Burns's tenure and 1980–81 and only under some readings, and no persistence or resumption can be read" (CASE-330). That is close to nothing, and no second record has been read.

A related study tells registration from endorsement under perturbation, in three synthetic organizations matched in pursuit (CASE-355). Perturbing the evaluator "tells registered from unregistered … and is not fooled by a decoy … while the endorsement relation is told apart only weakly". The classes and the evaluative architecture are stipulated (CASE-355).

## 4.9 Corporate affect and the remorse of institutions

The conjecture of §4.8 has a pointed application. A corporation, on a thesis drawn from Bakan's argument (Bakan 2004; LIT-1430), "lacks remorse, functionally, when harm it does to others is not among the concerns its own appraisals track and gains precedence over what it does only when a regulator or a market turns it into a threat to the corporation's concerns". That profile, "threat without remorse", "is changed by redesigning the corporation's concerns, not its members' characters" (CLAIM-432). The thesis rests on the functional framing of §4.8.1, and inherits its unanswered objection, and on the thesis that a collective's reasons need not be any of its members' reasons (CLAIM-432; CLAIM-039; CLAIM-271). Its documented case is Wells Fargo's Community Bank, 2002 to 2016, in which "signals of the harm reached people who could not act on them" (CASE-151).

The strongest objection to it is direct. "A corporation's functional remorse cannot be told from prudence: any procedure or role duty that makes others' harms its concern was put there for its own ends, reputation, liability or recruitment, so the line between harm as concern and harm as threat … does not hold, and the clinical word adds nothing to a description of its incentives" (CLAIM-431). It has not been answered, and the line between remorse and threat is not shown to be drawable for a corporation (SYNTHESIS-018).

The one case admits two readings, and the argument does not choose between them. On the first, harm reached the bank only as a threat: customer harm was "narrowly construed to mean only financial harm such as fees and penalties", the 2004 investigation's recommendation to cut goals "was not escalated", and the model was abandoned only after the regulators' settlements of September 2016 and the press (CLAIM-432; CASE-151). On the second, an emotion sits on the claim side: "The claim is the emotion whether or not it is granted". If a unit's appraisal under a role duty is the corporation's own, then "CASE-151's 2004 investigation is functional remorse claimed and not granted, where CLAIM-432 reads the bank as without remorse" (CLAIM-442). "The two disagree about the case on that condition", and "neither entry settles whether such an appraisal is the corporation's" (CLAIM-442; CLAIM-432). A contradiction between them is not an integration of them (SYNTHESIS-021). The first reading is further narrowed by the admission condition of §4.8.2: the bank's cross-sell potential "is shown only as the admitted negotiation's, not as the bank's" (CLAIM-432). A third candidate holds that what "the word sociopathy points at is legibility without volitional efficacy for others' claims, not a failure to understand them" (CLAIM-tmptrn75), which reads the profile through the second achievement of §4.7.4. It has not been applied to the case.

The thesis's own test asks whether corporations changed only by sanction keep weighing others' harms once the sanction lifts (LEAD-101). Run on open sources of corporate sanctions, it found that "No case meets the lead's standard", so the thesis's defeater is "neither met nor answered" (CASE-405; CLAIM-432). The test is narrower than a test between the two readings. It "separates remorse from prudence only in the redesigned arm and only with reputation and benefit held fixed" (CLAIM-1169). The run's other findings bear on the question without settling it. A monitor's effect on violations lasts only while it is on site (CLAIM-1136), which fits the remorse thesis, but "the prudence objection (CLAIM-431) predicts the same, so it is no evidence between them". A result on changes of board composition has two readings: as evidence for the redesign half of the thesis (CLAIM-1178), or as "no evidence that redesigned concerns outlast sanction" (CLAIM-1113); the run's own reading leans to the second. Signals without precedence appear inside a firm coded as redesigned, which makes "redesigned" "a matter of degree, not an arm a firm is in or out of" (CASE-405). And the Federal Reserve lifted Wells Fargo's growth restriction on 2025-06-03, so the test "can be run on CASE-151 once a window has passed" (CASE-405).

An argument meeting the felt-valence objection would be the next piece of philosophical work this section needs. It has not been made, and the account of the line names its absence: the objection "holds CLAIM-039 and with it CLAIM-440 and CLAIM-432" (SYNTHESIS-021).

## 4.10 Learned accommodation and what quiet does not show

### 4.10.1 The thesis, in conditional form

A will-organization need not escalate every conflict to a higher process that mediates or overrides. Processes can learn to settle a recurring conflict among themselves, and there is a reason to think this cheaper. "Learned local accommodation can reduce the amortized cost of recurrent arbitration: where a recurring conflict among an agent's processes admits a stable accommodation, settling it among the processes can cost less over repeated encounters than escalating each instance to a higher-level process that mediates or overrides, though local negotiation and the relations it needs are not free" (CLAIM-358). The claim says "can", and its antecedent is a stable accommodation. It does not say "That local settlement is always cheaper" (CLAIM-358). It is argued, not shown. Its illustration is a pair of persons, one who settles conflict by "repeated escalation" and one by "local coordination", with the same effort and the same quality of work; being stipulated, the pair "shows what the claim distinguishes, not that people differ this way" (CASE-109).

Three guards keep the thesis from proving too much. First, a Hebbian prior over authority, a momentum term on who has prevailed, is "a candidate mechanism for durable authority and for amortized governance; but persistence is not amortization, which is shown only when earlier work replaces later computation, communication or recruitment" (CLAIM-443). Second, "Repeated arbitration can fall away from a collaboration by three different mechanisms, valuational concordance, learned practical coordination and externalized governance", and rules that settle recurring conflicts automatically "do not show that the collaborators have learned to coordinate" (CLAIM-1139); of the three, learned practical coordination is the nearest to the route of accommodation. Third, the routes by which a divided agent acts as one, "arbitration, a change in which interest may act, coinciding valuations, local accommodation", "are distinct and differently costly" (SYNTHESIS-037).

### 4.10.2 The price of escalation

The study the thesis needs has two parts: the price of each escalation, and the saving over a recurring conflict. The first has been measured, for single choices. The criterion was fixed beforehand: a fit of the expected-value-of-control model "in which control intensity falls with its cost" would count for the thesis (LEAD-128). The model was fitted to the cognitive-effort-discounting choices of 122 participants in a published, openly licensed study (Scheffel et al. 2023; CASE-382). For about three-quarters of them "the control intensity elected falls as its cost rises, while a need-for-cognition minority seek effort" (CASE-382; CLAIM-981). That supplies "the intrinsic cost that, on THEORY-070, gives escalation the price this claim says a learned accommodation saves — the per-encounter cost this claim's amortization presupposes, not the recurrence" (CLAIM-358; Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen 2013; THEORY-070).

Its limits are plain. It is "single-shot effort pricing", and "Not amortised supervisory cost" (CASE-382). It measures intensity by revealed choice, which is not the operationalization the expected-value-of-control account names (CLAIM-358). The pre-registered criterion would count it on the cost direction alone, but the fit does not price the recurrence, which is what the thesis is about.

### 4.10.3 The recurrence

No executed study prices the recurrence, and the study that bears nearest bears against reading persistence as amortization. Its check was set before its run: "if a Bayesian learner with matched information costs, or a habit model with matched memory, reproduces every intervention response, the extra authority state has not earned its place" (CASE-153; CLAIM-924). In the resulting model, a self-protecting sentry, "under success feedback its update is the habit of protecting, and a habit of matched memory reproduces the responses to belief correction, authority reset and restored evidence" (CASE-346). A persisting allocation of authority, in that model, does no work a habit would not do.

What would count can be stated in advance (LEAD-tmpqn4dk). Take two populations of processes that meet one recurring conflict with a stable accommodation, as the two persons of CASE-109 do: one settles it locally, one escalates each instance at the price of §4.10.2. If the thesis holds as conditioned, the local population's cumulative cost should fall below the escalating one's after a break-even number of encounters that depends on the cost of forming the relation, which is separate from the cost of keeping it: "having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it" (CLAIM-569). Where the recurring conflict has no stable accommodation, the ordering should not appear. The saving has to show up as later arbitration work not done, not as a persisting allocation doing the same work again (CLAIM-443), and it must come from learned coordination, not from an external rule (CLAIM-1139). This is a prediction for a study not yet run.

### 4.10.4 Low conflict does not show adequate accommodation

The converse inference is the one that matters in practice: an organization whose processes rarely conflict and coordinate fluently looks as though it has accommodated well. It does not follow. The point is held in three parts of different firmness, each in its own scope.

First, and proved: a quiet perspective need not be a satisfied one, since "a perspective can be quiet while its requirement is unmet" (CLAIM-549; §4.6).

Second, and accepted under a condition: "where an organization's legibility regime selects which contests are admitted or recorded", "observed coherence is not evidence that the organization's perspectives are integrated" (CLAIM-448; §4.8.4). It holds only under that condition.

Third, and argued: "Processes conditioned never to object coordinate without override at the same behaviour and supervisory cost as processes that negotiate, so the two part only under intervention, when a constituent dissents, or in their history" (CLAIM-364). A quiet subordinate process is not necessarily a satisfied one. What those interventions test is narrowed by a result of Article 1: the two kinds "share their maintenance provenance", so the interventions that part them "test how the constituents' own processes answer dissent, not whose process keeps the arrangement" (CLAIM-tmp9ia3w).

Low supervisory cost, likewise, is not shown to be non-domination (SYNTHESIS-037). Intervention separates registered from unregistered evaluation in a stipulated model, and endorsement only weakly (CASE-355); no record that is not stipulated has been read for it.

## 4.11 Regimes that outlast their reasons

The same structure that makes accommodation possible makes entrenchment possible. Three proposals describe allocations of authority that persist past the conditions that made them fit.

**Anxiety.** "Some persistent anxiety may be governance-maintained rather than belief-maintained", and "the regime can outlast a corrected belief" (CLAIM-446). Its model is the sentry of §4.10.3 (CASE-346). Under restriction, "no run leaves protection in 400 safe steps"; but only "interventions on the evaluation, an independent audit or authority rewarded on independent evidence, tell the two apart" from a habit (CLAIM-924). By the check set before the run (CASE-153), the authority state earns its place only where its evaluation can be reached from outside. The evidence "is model-level and adjacent; nothing here measures authority in patients" (CLAIM-446). The human literature read for it is indirect: reducing safety behaviours lowers symptoms and instructed increases raise them (Goodson et al. 2026; THEORY-903); avoidance learning in healthy humans favours expectancy and inference-based accounts but leaves residual avoidance after the outcome is visibly removed (Mancinelli and Bach 2026; THEORY-900); and patients with OCD or PTSD who avoid a safety cue more "still expected the aversive picture more" (De Kleine et al. 2023; THEORY-912). A rival reading of a corrected belief beside a persisting practice is that extinction "adds a competing inhibitory" association (Craske et al. 2014; THEORY-914). The human tests are open (QUESTION-325; QUESTION-326; QUESTION-327; QUESTION-331).

**Emergency regimes.** "Recruiting an emergency regime, maintaining it and dissolving it are separable capacities", and where entry is cheaper than exit "the regime can outlast its justification" (CLAIM-tmp0gs0t). Its earliest instance is a thought experiment, Northstar's "emergency governance that sustains itself" (CASE-036), read as showing that "Stability does not entail viability" (ARG-007). The release condition is stated: "escalation without a release condition leaves authority with a regime after the demand that justified it has passed" (CLAIM-465).

**Governance heuristics.** "Beside heuristics that select what to do there are governance heuristics, learned priors over which perspective or procedure determines what to do; intuitive expertise and entrenched bias can then be one learned allocation of authority" (CLAIM-tmpn754o). It rests on the Hebbian prior of §4.10.1 (CLAIM-443). Restated as "a compiled contract", it "adds only that the allocation is a protocol among processes" (CLAIM-tmpysg95). Both are offered as candidate explanations, not as established explanations of every instance. A bolder conjecture follows: several cognitive biases may be "observable marks of one organizational constraint", and "the thesis earns standing only if one intervention on such a constraint, measured independently and with the information held fixed, moves several bias measures together" (CLAIM-tmp5atd3). Its premises are mostly proposals, and its leading rival is Hilbert's model of noisy information processing, which predicts co-variation of biases from noise alone (Hilbert 2012; LIT-tmpngp4x; CLAIM-tmp5atd3).

Neither of the last two has been tested. The co-variation study is planned, with the conjecture's defeater as its expectation: it fails if the bias measures move one at a time, or no more together than the noise-based rival predicts (LEAD-tmpr4xwc). The test that tells a governance heuristic from an action heuristic needs human data and has a prediction written before any run: after clear feedback in one of several tasks that share a cue, "the shift toward deliberation generalizes across the tasks that share the cue, including those where intuition is still right", where an action heuristic "is corrected task by task" (CLAIM-tmpn754o; LEAD-tmpotdfw). Because that design gives clear feedback, the identification limit of the sentry model, which needs the protecting posture to mask the outcome, does not carry over to it (CASE-346).

## 4.12 Flow, cycles, and the limits of "scalar"

**Flow.** One conjecture is that in flow "an activity's immediate reward and its longer-run value coincide, so no interest has to be overridden" (CLAIM-162). It is grounded only weakly, since the flow literature has not been read. It meets an objection from the affective conjecture's own side: flow is reported as positive, which "tells against the potential account's condition that valence is undefined where no pair is contested" (CLAIM-441). The objection of §4.8.3, that the potential account runs two relations together, sharpens this: the absorbed writer "tests whichever is chosen" of the two (CLAIM-449). Which of the objection's exits holds is open (SYNTHESIS-020). A more defensible formulation shifts from state to capability: "the achievement is not flow as a state but flow-regulation as an organizational capability: to enter, sustain, interrupt and recover fluent coordination without losing the history of what was learned" (CLAIM-1075).

**Cycles.** If potentials need not exist, cycles in a record of wins need not be pathologies. Three things are kept apart. A cycling record of wins is "organizational nonintegrability". It "becomes organizational tension only when realized as costly reversal against demands the organization maintains, and cognitive dissonance only when that tension is detected and appraised as aversive". Learning can relieve it "by accommodation, by entrenched precedence or by cheap periodic coordination, which observed coherence does not tell apart" (CLAIM-451; CLAIM-434). Each "only when" is a necessary condition; the claim states no sufficient one. Neither is a potential the default and a cycle a pathology: "They are regimes" (CLAIM-451; SYNTHESIS-020). The relief in question is relief of tension in that claim's sense, not relief of frustration in the sense of §4.7.1. Whether entrenched authority relieves frustration by removing demands from contention turns on a free parameter, whether a demand no process presses is removed or only quiet, and is not asserted here (SYNTHESIS-049).

**Scalar objectives.** A natural summary of the article would be that an organizational individual requires an architecture of differentiated authority rather than a single scalar objective. The argument supports less than that, and in several senses of "scalar" that it does not join. An ordering need not be a currency (CLAIM-436). A record of wins need not have a potential (CLAIM-434; CLAIM-451). Where an aggregate revealed preference exists, "it is fitted to one regime of authority, so an intervention on authority that changes the winning moves changes the fitted preference" (CLAIM-482). A will-organization with decisive jurisdictions, unanimity and an unrestricted domain "cannot always choose", and richer information does not escape this (CLAIM-353). In one fictional population, scalar grants "fail either way" (CASE-420). And the gates of §4.3 fail not because they are scalar but because the products of authority bits are never observed in single changes (CASE-325). These concern a currency over options, a potential over outcomes, a preference fitted to a regime, a social choice, grants over operators and the learnability of a gate. One word over six objects is not one result. None of them establishes that an organization requires differentiated authority.

## 4.13 Misunderstanding as an occasion of relief

The apparatus of frustration and relief applies to an ordinary event: two parties find that they read an expression differently. A misunderstanding is productive, on one proposal, "when identifying and clarifying the ambiguity it exposes is the detection of, and a reorganization around, a novel distinction that has become consequential" (CLAIM-tmpupcws). The "when" states a sufficient condition, not a definition. The proposal names misunderstanding as an occasion of self-formation (CLAIM-tmpupcws; TERM-099). It is qualified: a misunderstanding "dates the distinction's relevance rather than its existence", and clarifying "can coarsen as well as refine" (CLAIM-tmpm76n5). And it is opposed: "A misunderstanding can be productive because it is left unresolved" (CLAIM-tmpgx2ej). Article 3 (§3.7) places it among the kinds of ambiguous invocation.

Self-formation driven by frustration has a construction. In it, "an agent's own frustration at two demands one shared output cannot meet drives production of a sensor and a differentiating channel"; below a threshold, "smaller discrepancies are tolerated, at a standing frustration, and form nothing" (CASE-191). The two demands are for different contexts, which the sensor tells apart. An unresolved discrepancy is "an occasion for self-formation … and not a guarantee of it" (CLAIM-532). And an accepted instance of relief by differentiation is niche differentiation: "a strict enlargement of their compatibility complex over viable histories for the same demands", where "the exclusion of a constituent … relieves no structural frustration" (CLAIM-554).

Put together, these give a conditional result. Take each party's reading of an ambiguous expression as a demand on their joint outcomes; that is the result's condition, and nothing independent supports it. A consequential misunderstanding is then a frustrated pair in the sense of §4.7.1, and: "Taking each party's reading of an ambiguous expression as a demand on their joint outcomes, clarifying a consequential misunderstanding relieves frustration by a new distinction only where the readings' demands bear on different situations the expression conflated; where they demand different joint outcomes in the same situation, no distinction relieves them, only an outcome that meets both, and settling on one reading or rewording the demands is not relief" (CLAIM-tmp7rl3d). Given its condition, it is derived.

Clarifying can therefore go four ways. Where the demands bear on situations the expression conflated, a distinction splits the situations and lets each demand be met in its own; that is relief, by the route of CASE-191. Where the demands bear on one situation and require different joint outcomes there, no distinction relieves them, since a distinction lets what is done depend on a feature and cannot make one outcome in one situation take two values; only an outcome that meets both does, which in the terms of CLAIM-532 is a new channel. Settling on one reading drops a demand, which "is not relief" however much quieter the relation becomes (CLAIM-347; CLAIM-549). And restricting or rewording the demands changes what is compared, since "an endogenous revision of what the organization maintains is a transformation of it and not relief" (CLAIM-361).

The result does not say that settling on one reading is unproductive. A joint settlement on one reading, with the new distinction kept for later use, is productive (CLAIM-tmpupcws; CLAIM-tmpisfi1) and is not relief. Productive misunderstanding and relief therefore come apart; they coincide only in the first kind. A misunderstanding productive because left unresolved is not excluded, and coarsening is not classed. The result's caution is that "the same situation" is fixed by the joint outcome the demands bear on: a feature invented after the fact to separate situations, such as who is asking, "absorbs every case and defeats nothing" (CLAIM-tmp7rl3d). Whether settling drops the losing demand or only quiets it is the free parameter of §4.12. An obvious test, that a settling clarification leaves the losing demand pressing again where a refining one does not, cannot tell a dropped demand from a quieted one; it would become a test only if maintenance were measured before the clarification, by withheld probes in which meeting the demand costs nothing (LEAD-tmp8nbac).

The construction is one stipulated agent, and the proposal concerns a dyad or an organization. A discrepancy is an occasion; the sufficient condition for self-formation lies in the agent's own activity producing a maintained capacity, not in the discrepancy (CLAIM-532), and nothing here makes frustration sufficient for a productive misunderstanding or for self-formation. Whether imposed clarification can still be productive is open (CLAIM-tmpro73c; CLAIM-tmpisfi1).

## 4.14 Discussion

### 4.14.1 What the argument does not establish

The argument is firmest in its vocabulary and formal results (§§4.2, 4.5, 4.6, 4.7.1 and 4.8.4), which are definitions, theorems and accepted interpretive theses.

Its central empirical step is a model. The comparison of §4.3 is a simulation of one stipulated organization, and it shows what a relational description gains over control-only models where authority is arranged as stipulated. It does not show that any real organization is so arranged. The only cases about real organizations are a single documented specimen (CASE-151) and a reading of one public record (CASE-330), and the run on open sources of corporate sanctions found no case that met its standard (CASE-405). The one analysis of human data is a fit to published effort-discounting choices (CASE-382), which prices single choices and not recurring conflict. The human avoidance data behind the anxiety proposal are readings of others' studies (§4.11). The argument is about one realizer at a time, and the claim that one description serves across realizers (CLAIM-487) is the claim it cannot test.

The affective part (§§4.8 and 4.9) is a conjecture throughout. Its framing faces an objection it has not answered (THEORY-101), two of its proposals disagree (CLAIM-437; CLAIM-442), an accepted objection requires it to name which of two relations it means (CLAIM-449), and its showcase case admits two readings that the available evidence does not decide (CLAIM-432; CLAIM-442; CASE-405).

The thesis about accommodation (§4.10) is argued, with one measured input. The saving over recurrence is unmeasured, and the one model of persisting authority found it indistinguishable from habit except under intervention on the evaluation (CASE-346; CLAIM-924). The regimes of §4.11 are proposals, one of them modelled.

Finally, the argument does not establish that an organization requires differentiated authority rather than a scalar objective (§4.12). It establishes several separate ways in which a scalar summary fails, and they do not add up to that.

### 4.14.2 The strongest objection

The strongest objection to the thesis is the one the comparison partly concedes: that the will-organization is institutional theory's rules-in-use under another name, and that what the account adds is at most a question (CLAIM-024). For an organization, the comparison grants it. Institutional analysis already writes positions, authority rules and the levels at which rules are changed (THEORY-1809), and the difference the relational model makes over control-only models is one those rules give (CLAIM-890). The study cannot tell the gain from the rules' (CASE-325).

The reply has two parts, and only the first is made good here. First, the concession is local. Against the fields that describe the mechanisms (control theory and executive control) the relational variables are not replaced, and a control model that matched them would have to add them (CLAIM-890). The objection that the mechanisms make the agent-level description idle fails for the organization modelled. Second, what the account claims as new is not a mechanism but a common explanatory role across realizers (CLAIM-021): the same relations, with the same variables, describing a person as well as an organization, where each field has its own vocabulary for its own realizer. That claim would make the account more than a relabelling, and its failure would make it one; the concession "would follow only if no second realizer shares the variables" (CLAIM-890). The decisive study, a second realizer modelled with the same variables and priced against its own field's best model on held-out interventions, is planned and not run (LEAD-tmpb2w1a). Until it is run, the objection stands for the account's novelty and fails for its idleness.

A second objection, specific to the affective part, is that valence is a felt state (Veit 2025; THEORY-101), so that a functional account of organizational affect changes the subject. It is not answered, and the affective sections are offered as a conjecture subject to it.

## 4.15 Conclusion

An organization that maintains incompatible demands acts on some of them because of how practical authority is arranged within it: who may object, who may classify, who may suspend, which claims on behaviour are granted. These relations are distinct from one another and from the contents of the demands. In a model of one organization, describing them as relations beats describing the mechanisms that realize them, and predicts what the mechanism models cannot, provided the comparison is priced on interventions the models were not trained on; against institutional theory, for an organization, the same description is not new, and what would be new is its reach across realizers, which is untested. The structure of authority has formal consequences of its own: self-amplification survives a ban on self-grants, tolerances are directed criteria for conferral, and quiet is not satisfaction. The difficulty authority resolves is frustration, an ordinal fact about which demands can be met together, and dropping a demand does not relieve it. Organizational affect can be located, as a conjecture still facing its first objection, at the gap between the precedence a motive claims and the authority it is granted. Learned accommodation can make recurring conflict cheaper to resolve, but fluent coordination is not evidence that it has been resolved well, and allocations of authority can outlast what justified them. The studies that would carry the argument further are named: a second realizer with the same variables, the saving over recurrent conflict, co-evolving tolerances and authority, and the tests of governance heuristics on human data.
