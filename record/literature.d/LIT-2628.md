# LIT-2628: Do LLMs Really Forget? Hidden-State Leakage in Model Unlearning and How to Fix it

Reisizadeh, H., Ruan, J., Liu, S. & Hong, M. (2026). Do LLMs Really Forget? Hidden-State Leakage in Model Unlearning and How to Fix it. arXiv:2609.36612.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1960](../notes.d/NOTE-1960.md).*

- **Theorem 1** (§2.2): because the two bounds are controlled
  independently, "one can simultaneously achieve I(S; Z | C) = 0 and
  I(S; R) ≥ M for any M > 0 by setting ε = 0". "Suppressing sensitive
  outputs is not the same as erasing sensitive knowledge."
- **Hidden-state leakage** (§3.2, MUSE-News): "all methods appear
  successful under output-level evaluation, with near-zero verbatim
  leakage; however, probe decoders still recover significant verbatim
  information from hidden states across multiple layers."
- **The probe is trained on the forget set** (§3.1): 80% of the forget QA
  set trains it and 20% evaluates it, so what it recovers is what a
  forget-trained readout generalizes to.

## Standing in the record

Filed from A's turn on concept erasure, which says that generative probes
can recover information from internal states when output-level tests
suggest successful forgetting (paraphrased; the exchange was not
rechecked by the filer). The reader found that right, with a caveat A
omits: the probe is trained on forget-set answers, which brings it close
to a relearning attack confined to the head, and the paper does not
separate recovery from relearning. Not to be confused with Wei et al.,
arXiv:2506.05735. [THEORY-1878](../theory.d/THEORY-1878.md) carries its central account.
