# NOTE-tmp60j0q: Neander and Schulte — Teleological Theories of Mental Content (SEP)

## Contribution

The record's map of selection-based teleosemantics. It says what all
these theories share, what "function" and "normative" mean in them, how
the main theories differ on what fixes content, and where they are under
attack.

## Key insight

"What all teleological (or 'teleosemantic') theories of mental content
have in common is the idea that psycho-semantic norms are ultimately
derivable from functional norms" (§3). And functional norms are thin:
"all that is usually meant by saying that the relevant notion of
function is normative ... is that the relevant notion permits the
possibility of malfunction" (§2). The authors place both kinds of norm
"on the is side of any is/ought divide" (§2).

## Assumptions

- Etiological functions are presupposed by most of the theories
  surveyed, and the survey reports the systemic alternative (Cummins)
  and the statistical one (Boorse) as rivals that, on the etiologist's
  view, fail to account for malfunction (§2).
- Teleosemantic theories are "real nature" theories (§1), which is what
  licenses the strongest reply to Swampman (§4.2).

## Key results

- **Two questions** (§1). "the representational status question" (why a
  state has any content) and "the content-determination question" (why
  this content); "there has been a tendency in the debate to focus on
  the second one".
- **Misrepresentation as the mark** (§1). "a capacity for
  misrepresentation is often regarded as essential for genuine
  representation (see esp. Grice 1957)".
- **Function is not prescriptive** (§2). Proponents "may all agree that
  no ought-claim follows from a function ascription without the addition
  of further premises".
- **Etiological function** (§2). "an item's function is determined by
  its history of selection or by past selection of things of that type";
  Neander's 1991 definition is quoted. Modern-history versions take
  recent selection, and "selection does not cease when traits 'go to
  fixation' if on-going maintenance selection is still weeding out fresh
  harmful mutations". Learning, conditioning, meme selection and neural
  selection (Garson, "differential retention without differential
  replication") can count as selection.
- **Error and content go together** (§3). Explaining "how content is
  determined and how the possibility of error are accommodated are not
  separate tasks", contrary to the view, credited to Millikan 2004, that
  function only explains error.
- **Dretske** (§3.1). Indication is factive, so "'misindication' (or
  'misinformation') is impossible"; representations are states with the
  function of indicating.
- **Millikan** (§3.2). Two core claims: representations stand between a
  producer and consumers "designed to cooperate with each other, either
  by natural selection or by a process of individual learning"; and a
  representation must map onto a state of affairs, by a rule, for the
  consumer to perform its proper functions "in the Normal way". On the
  frog, the content is "there is frog food present in l at t". Read as a
  consumer theory, which Millikan rejects (note 12, "Personal
  communication"). Objections: background conditions, over-specific
  content (Neander's infected fly), Pietroski's kimus.
- **Papineau** (§3.3). Desire content first: "that effect which it is
  the desire's biological purpose to produce".
- **Neander** (§3.4). A sensory representation has as content what the
  producing system "was selected for producing Rs in response to", with
  a distality clause. The frog's content is "something small, dark,
  moving".
- **Shea** (§3.5). An outcome must be robust, stabilized "by a process
  of natural selection, a process of individual learning, or by having
  contributed to the survival of S", and produced by components in
  exploitable relations to the world; Shea also allows design functions.
- **Non-etiological teleosemantics** (§3.5). Nanay's modal theory and
  Piccinini's goal-contribution theory of function make functions "much less dependent
  on historical facts", avoiding Swampman.
- **Swampman** (§4.2). A replica with no history has no functions and so
  "no beliefs or desires about anything at all" on standard theories.
  Replies: deny the intuition as false of the real nature (Millikan,
  Neander 1996), or accept it as irrelevant to a claim about the actual
  world (Papineau).
- **Liberalism** (§4.3). Millikan's theory "arguably entails that all
  living beings are representational systems"; Burge objects, Schulte and
  Butlin propose restrictions.
- **Derived and adapted functions** (§4.4). Millikan's derived and
  adapted derived proper functions let learned and novel representations
  have content "whether or not the circumstances obtained during the
  history of our species".

## Concepts

- **Etiological, systemic, modal and goal-contribution functions.** The
  four accounts of function the survey distinguishes (§§2, 3.5).
- **Consumer-based and producer-based teleosemantics.** Whether the
  using or the producing system's function fixes content.
- **Functional indeterminacy.** Distal content, locally co-instantiated
  properties, and "properties mutually implicated in selection" (§4.1).
- **Swampman.** A molecule-for-molecule replica with no history.

## Connections

- **Millikan's own statement** ([LIT-tmpu3jv7](../literature.d/LIT-tmpu3jv7.md), [NOTE-tmpqexrv](NOTE-tmpqexrv.md)) is the
  "2009" compressed version the survey cites. Her chapter's replies to
  Neander and Pietroski are those §3.2 summarizes.
- **Organizational functions.** The survey does not discuss the
  organizational account of Mossio, Saborido and Moreno ([THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md)) or
  Bickhard's ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)). Its nearest neighbours are Shea's
  stabilization by "contributed to the survival of S" and Piccinini's
  goal-contribution functions, both of which tie function to present or
  recent contribution to persistence.
- **Grice.** §1 cites Grice 1957 for misrepresentation as a mark of
  representation; §3.1's factive "indication" is Grice's natural meaning
  ([THEORY-444](../theory.d/THEORY-444.md)) under Dretske's name.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-650](../claims.d/CLAIM-650.md).** It confirms that "selection history" is a developed
  family of answers to question (b), and splits it: consumer-based and
  producer-based theories assign different contents, so selection
  history alone does not settle which content the norm attaches to. The
  claim's text names selection history as a candidate and leaves it
  open; nothing here closes it. Its §3 also says error and content "are
  not separate tasks", which is a second qualification of the claim's
  (a)/(b) split, recorded in [ARG-tmps4eez](../arguments.d/ARG-tmps4eez.md).
- **[CLAIM-648](../claims.d/CLAIM-648.md).** The Swampman objection is the general form of
  [CLAIM-tmp974nn](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp974nn.md): on etiological accounts, a system whose norms are set
  only by its present upkeep has none. Shea's inclusion of past
  contributions to survival, and the non-etiological variants, are the
  routes by which a teleosemanticist could grant the claim. A reported
  objection and two reported escape routes, cited in the objection's
  grounds.
- **[QUESTION-128](../questions.d/QUESTION-128.md).** Evidence on the representational rung, and a caution
  on the rest: the survey's authors hold that functional norms are
  descriptive and yield no ought without further premises (§2). That is
  the question's own separation between system-relative norms and
  normative authority, stated from the etiological side.
- **[THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md).** Bickhard's case against etiology (the lion's
  duplicate) is the Swampman objection; the survey reports two replies
  that [THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)'s text does not meet. A note on [THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md) records it.

## Limitations

- Written by two proponents, one of whom (Neander) is surveyed. Her
  theory gets the most favourable treatment of its weaknesses; Millikan's
  gets the objections.
- A survey: each objection and reply is a paragraph, and the authors
  decline to settle most of them ("a matter for future discussion").
- It is about mental content. Artefact functions get one sentence (§2,
  the trackpad) and are set aside because a theory of original
  intentionality "cannot in turn depend on other intentional phenomena".
  The designed alarm of the record's cases is that set-aside case.

## Open questions

- Whether maintenance selection, learning by differential retention, or
  Shea's survival-stabilization makes etiological function and
  organizational function coincide for a self-maintaining system with a
  history. [CLAIM-tmp974nn](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp974nn.md) names the case where they come apart.
