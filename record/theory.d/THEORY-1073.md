# THEORY-1073: An aggregate variable much slower than the behaviour producing it becomes an environment the components tune to whether or not it summarizes the underlying regularities well, which helps coordination; if it summarizes them poorly the system likely fails in the end, later the larger the separation

## Source

Flack (2017), [LIT-1630](../literature.d/LIT-1630.md), §6; read in [NOTE-1216](../notes.d/NOTE-1216.md).

## What was actually shown

Flack offers time-scale separation as one way a system can overcome the
"inherent subjectivity" of noisy components with partial information (§6):

"If the time scale associated with a coarse-grained or aggregate variable is
very slow compared to the microscopic behaviour producing it, it effectively
becomes the 'social' environment (i say 'social' here to distinguish it
from the exogenous environment, E we started with) and components can tune
to it regardless of whether it actually does a good job of summarizing the
regularities in E or now in M—think of social institutions that are highly
constraining and very hard to change or the power distribution in the
monkey case study. Time scale separation between M and S facilitates
learning and hence coordinated and organized behaviour thereby minimizing
the consequences of subjectivity due to noisy components with different
windows on the world."

The limit, in the next sentences: "it seems likely the system will fail at
some point if the aggregate properties components rely on for prediction do
not capture the regularities in M or E—failure may just take longer if the
time scale separation is very large." The macaque case is the one empirical
anchor: "both the signalling network and power structure have a much slower
time scale than the fight dynamics."

This is an informal argument. No model is given, and no measured time scale
for the slow variables. The claim about failure is hedged ("it seems
likely").

## What this does not say

- That slowness makes an aggregate accurate. The point is the reverse: a
  slow aggregate can be followed when it is inaccurate.
- That separation alone makes the aggregate constrain. In Flack it
  constrains through components tuning to their estimates of it (§1); a
  slow variable no one tunes to is no downward causation.
- That separation is the only mechanism. Flack's second is collective
  estimation (§6), which she calls "a somewhat trivial insight" in its
  averaging form.
- That organization makes separation. Flack's only remark on the converse is
  an open question in §5: whether effective downward causation can
  "increase symmetries at the microscopic level" and make the system more
  amenable to coarse-graining.
