# NOTE-tmpdcebe: Liang and others (2024) — Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale: A Case Study on the Impact of ChatGPT on AI Conference Peer Reviews

## Contribution

A scalable, corpus-level estimator of LLM-modified text and the first large measurement of LLM use in
scientific peer review, with behavioural correlates.

## Key results

- Abstract: "Our maximum likelihood model leverages expert-written and AI-generated reference texts to
  accurately and efficiently examine real-world LLM-use at the corpus level"; "Our results suggest that
  between 6.5% and 16.9% of text submitted as peer reviews to these conferences could have been
  substantially modified by LLMs, i.e. beyond spell-checking or minor writing updates."
- Abstract: "the estimated fraction of LLM-generated text is higher in reviews which report lower
  confidence, were submitted close to the deadline, and from reviewers who are less likely to respond
  to author rebuttals".
- Box 1: "with no significant evidence of ChatGPT usage in Nature portfolio reviews"; "Reviews
  containing scholarly citations are less likely to be AI modified or generated than those lacking
  such citations"; "Higher estimated AI modifications are correlated with homogenization of review
  content in the text embedding space".
- §4.4: "ICLR experienced the most significant increase in estimated α, from 1.6% to 10.6%".
- §5 (homogenization): authors who receive generated text in place of an expert review lose diverse
  expert feedback; the section cites Longino (1990) among studies of the value of reviewer variation
  (not quoted: the two-column extraction interleaves this passage).

## Assumptions

Word-frequency distributions of AI text estimated from the authors' own prompting of particular LLMs
transfer to what reviewers used; α measures "substantial" modification beyond proofreading (checked
by a robustness test); correlations are not causal; corpus-level, not per-review, inference.

## Concepts

Distributional (corpus-level) LLM detection; maximum likelihood mixture estimate α; deadline,
reference, reply-rate, homogenization and low-confidence effects; epistemic homogenization.

## Bearing on the record

**How far it stands in.** For the editorial's review-side finding (more AI-written review text, lower diversity) at another set
of venues: yes, and with a stated method. For the editorial's main findings (42% more submissions,
declining writing quality, the incentive diagnosis "more rather than better"): no.

- **[CLAIM-tmpifp69](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpifp69.md)** (attention-weighted selection in news, clickbait vs investigation). Weak and by
  analogy, through the original: shows a cheap substitute (LLM text) entering a quality-assurance
  practice where effort is unpaid and time-pressed, with lower engagement and more uniform output. It
  says nothing of demand or attention; it is evidence for the capability-displacement theme, not for
  the claim's selection mechanism.
- **[THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md)** (objectivity as the critical practices of a community, which needs diverse,
  responsive criticism). Found in recheck. **Bears directly as an empirical worry**: more LLM text
  correlates with less reviewer engagement with rebuttals ("uptake") and with homogenized reviews
  (less diversity of criticism), which are two of the conditions the theory names. The paper itself
  cites Longino on the value of reviewer variation. Candidate dated note on [THEORY-151](../theory.d/THEORY-151.md) or a
  `bears_on`; it does not test the theory.
- Duplicate check: no LIT on the branch holds arXiv 2403.07183 or the title; "peer review" hits
  ([LIT-014](../literature.d/LIT-014.md), [LIT-036](../literature.d/LIT-036.md) etc.) are other works.

## Limitations

Skimmed; method and validation not checked. ML venues (and Nature as a null), 2023-24 only; estimates
depend on the reference LLMs; α may also capture non-LLM stylistic drift; correlates are observational.
