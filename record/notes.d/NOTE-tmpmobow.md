# NOTE-tmpmobow: The Emotion Process: Event Appraisal and Component Differentiation

## Contribution

A review that reorganises the emotion literature by mechanism. Instead of
asking what distinguishes anger from fear, it asks what each appraisal
criterion does to each response component. Emotion labels become "one
class of dependent variables" (p. 736), placed at the end of the process.

## Key insight

The stable regularities are between appraisal criteria and responses,
not between emotion categories and responses. Kreibig's review of 134
studies found only heart rate and respiration rate stable across
emotions (p. 729), but intrinsic valence reliably moves the zygomaticus
and corrugator, and control reliably moves vocal energy.

## Assumptions

- A process model shared "across many theories" (p. 738): appraisal,
  action tendencies, physiology, expression, feeling, labelling.
- That "it is the nature of the appraisal process that determines the
  nature of the remaining stages in the emotion process" (p. 723).
- Five criteria after Ellsworth and Scherer (2003): novelty, valence
  (intrinsic and extrinsic), agency, control or power, fairness.

## Key results

From the studies reviewed:

- **Valence to action** (p. 725). Positive and negative stimuli prime
  approach and avoidance in compatibility tasks, though semantic overlap
  may explain this. Negative stimuli also prime repair: "conflict stimuli
  (i.e., stimuli that call for two competing actions, such as Stroop
  stimuli), which are incongruent with the goal to perform well on the
  task, instigate the tendency to adjust one's behavior".
- **Intrinsic valence versus goal congruence** (p. 730). In Van Reekum et
  al. (2004), "the manipulation of intrinsic valence had comparatively
  little impact on physiological responses", while goal congruence moved
  heart, skin conductance and finger temperature. In Aue and Scherer,
  "Intrinsic valence had more robust effects on facial EMG (Aue & Scherer
  2011), whereas only goal congruence significantly influenced mean skin
  conductance, forehead temperature, and finger temperature." Goal
  incongruence mobilised more, with less habituation. "The efferent
  effects of the two appraisals combined multiplicatively." In the voice
  (p. 734), goal congruence moved energy, f0 and duration, and intrinsic
  valence moved spectral energy distribution.
- **Sequence** (p. 732). Intrinsic valence acts on the face faster than
  goal congruence (Lanctôt and Hess 2007); goal congruence (about 600 ms)
  precedes prospective control (about 800 ms) (Gentsch et al. 2015).
- **Control** (pp. 726–728). High general control, including social
  power, goes with approach, activity and, depending on salient goals,
  antisocial or prosocial behaviour. Learned helplessness and
  self-efficacy research link low control to passivity. Goal congruence
  effects are "amplified" when control is high and "invariant" when low
  (p. 732).
- **Fairness** (p. 729). In an ultimatum game, "participants' rejection
  rates of these offers were more influenced by goal incongruence than by
  fairness" (Bediou and Scherer 2014).
- **Semantic sedimentation** (sidebar, p. 737). Scherer and Fontaine
  (2018), 24 emotion terms in 28 languages, find "four appraisal factors
  (valence, novelty, prospective control, and other-agency)" and four
  feeling factors, including "strong versus weak".
- **Coherence and awareness** (p. 737). An episode begins with
  "increased synchronization and coherence among the various components
  beyond a certain threshold"; "a person will become aware of an altered
  state after a second threshold of coherence has been reached." The
  mechanism is a central representation that "serves to monitor and
  regulate the degree of system coherence."

## Concepts

- **intrinsic valence** — appraisal of a stimulus "as merely positive or
  negative", independent of goals.
- **goal congruence** — appraisal of a stimulus as serving or obstructing a
  goal; "one form of extrinsic valence".
- **general, situational, retrospective and prospective control** — control
  over stimuli in general, over the current one, over its having come
  about, and the expectation of turning it from negative to positive.
- **component coherence** — synchronisation among components, the
  proposed criterion both for an episode and for its awareness.

## Bearing on the record

**Does the process description already contain "appraisal as negotiation
among perspectives"?** No. It contains most of the parts, and a reader
looking for the record's idea will find places it could go, but the
review does not say it.

What is there:

- **Several criteria, weighted.** Theories "tend to assign different
  weights to the various appraisal criteria" (p. 722). The outcome is "the
  net effect" of a profile, in Grandjean et al.'s phrase.
- **Recursion.** Each component's effect "is expected to feed back to the
  eliciting component, setting off another round of processing likely to
  affect the ultimate outcome" (p. 722).
- **Conflict.** It appears twice. In conflict monitoring, stimuli "call
  for two competing actions" (p. 725), and the response is to adjust. And
  in blends: emotions are mixed in "many cases" (p. 721).
- **One criterion prevailing.** Bediou and Scherer's finding that
  rejection followed goal incongruence more than fairness (p. 729) is,
  read the record's way, a case of one ordering prevailing over another
  in what the agent does.
- **Dominance of stages.** The four functions "each dominate a different
  stage of the process" (p. 721). This is sequence, not contest.

What is missing is the frame. In the review an appraisal criterion is a
dimension on which one evaluator scores an event, and the score profile
drives responses. Criteria do not have rankings of options that compete
for authority over action. Nothing wins, persists or resumes. The
interaction terms (multiplicative valence effects, control amplifying goal
congruence) are statistical interactions of inputs, not the outcome of a
contest. The closer precedents in the same school are Sander and Scherer's
"activation and inhibition thresholds" ([NOTE-tmpsx1uk](NOTE-tmpsx1uk.md)) and Shuman et al.'s
micro-valence integration ([NOTE-tmpogww2](NOTE-tmpogww2.md)). [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md)'s part 1
remains the record's reading of the CPM, not the CPM's self-description.
The record should not cite this review as stating it.

**What the review does give [CLAIM-tmpz8lli](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) and [QUESTION-tmpsqwu5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpsqwu5.md):**

- **Markers that tell the two valences apart.** If the record concedes
  [CLAIM-tmpe0iew](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpe0iew.md) in the way [NOTE-tmpogww2](NOTE-tmpogww2.md) suggests, with the
  ascendancy account covering goal-conduciveness valence and intrinsic
  pleasantness a separate check, the physiology here is how one would
  tell which ordering is driving. Goal congruence shows up
  autonomically, with less habituation when it is negative. Intrinsic
  valence shows up mainly in facial EMG. These are human, individual
  markers, so they bear on the animal side of [QUESTION-tmpsqwu5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpsqwu5.md), not on
  organizations.
- **Control as a gain on the ascendant ordering.** "Amplified goal
  congruence effects when control was high, in contrast to invariant goal
  congruence effects when control was low" (p. 732). If goal conduciveness
  is the ordering that prevails, control modulates how strongly it drives
  the components, which is what [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md) calls intensity. The control
  appraisal concerns the agent's power over its situation. It is not the
  ascendancy of one internal ordering over others, and it is the axis PAD
  calls "dominance" (see [LIT-tmp3deyb](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3deyb.md)). The rename to ascendancy
  keeps the two apart; this finding says how they interact.

**On [CLAIM-tmptd5us](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptd5us.md).** The two-threshold proposal (p. 737) puts
awareness at a degree of the regime itself: a "second threshold of
coherence". That is nearer Veit's identity ([THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)) than to a
self-model condition. But the mechanism offered is a representation: a
central integration that "serves to monitor and regulate the degree of
system coherence." So the review, like the 2009 paper ([NOTE-tmpsx1uk](NOTE-tmpsx1uk.md)),
holds both a threshold view and a model view and does not choose. The
model in question represents coherence, not which ordering wins. It also
adds temporal chunking: feelings come as "temporal chunks with phenomenal
unity" that "might occur when a certain level of component
synchronization persists" (p. 737). Persistence is one of [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md)'s
marks of ascendancy, so this is a point of contact with the record's
account. The review gives it as a hypothesis, not a finding. The
Grandjean et al. reading ([NOTE-tmpt8ltj](NOTE-tmpt8ltj.md)) takes the comparison
further.

## Limitations

A review, restricted to experiments with manipulated appraisals; it
excludes neuroscience and reappraisal "due to space limitations". Paths D
and E of its own model (feeling, labelling) are not reviewed for lack of a
"coherent body of empirical literature" (p. 724), which is where both
[CLAIM-tmptd5us](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptd5us.md) and [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md) would need evidence. The coherence
proposal is untested.

## Open questions

Whether the multiplicative combination of intrinsic valence and goal
congruence is a weighted integration or a gating, which would place it in
one of [CLAIM-tmp83ge2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp83ge2.md)'s two regimes. Whether the two coherence thresholds
can be estimated per episode with the time-series methods it cites.
