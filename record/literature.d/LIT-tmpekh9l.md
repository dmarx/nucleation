# LIT-tmpekh9l: Logic: The Theory of Inquiry

Dewey, J. (1938). *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*. Henry Holt and Company.

Not read. The summary is drawn from what the work is standardly cited
for and from the exchange's description of it. A reading should check it
first.

## Why this is here

A15 §1 named it "the closest predecessor" of the owner's U15 ("inquiry
is a form of specification"): problem formulation is an achievement of
inquiry, not its given starting point. A16 §3 read Dewey's indeterminate
situation as one whose relevant distinctions are not yet established,
and paired Dewey with Simondon as accounts that put a process of
determination before determinate products. The definition quoted in the
summary is the standard one and was not checked against the text. It
bears on CLAIM-tmpalzo4.
