# NOTE-2276: Yang, Steinhardt & Hu — Are Neurons Actually Collapsed? On the Fine-Grained Structure in Neural Representations

## Contribution

An empirical demonstration that collapse as measured, NC1 going to zero,
is compatible with residual within-class variation structured by the
input distribution and recoverable without labels; an ordering in training
(input structure first, label-driven merging later); and a toy theorem for
a one-hidden-layer network.

## Key insight

"Collapse" is a statement about a ratio of variances, and a small residual
can still be faithful to the input: "even when representations apparently
collapse, the small amount of remaining variation can still faithfully and
accurately captures the intrinsic structure of input distribution"
(Abstract).

## Assumptions

- Main runs: ResNet-18, SGD, learning rate 0.1, weight decay 5×10⁻⁴; a 3×3
  sweep of the two in Appendix C. No seeds, repeats or error bars are
  stated.
- Coarsening ỹ = y mod 5 in the main text, random in Appendix F.
- NC1 = (1/C) Tr(Σ_W Σ_B†) and NC2 measured on the coarse labels.
- The Cluster-and-Linear-Probe (CLP) is told the number of original
  classes, uses t-SNE then 2-means within each super-class, and picks the
  label mapping that scores best ("report the highest performance").
- Theorem 7.1: one hidden layer, fixed second layer, unhinged loss, four
  orthogonal-mean Gaussian clusters with two coarse labels.

## Key results

- **Collapse on coarse labels** (§4, Fig. 3): "Neural Collapse does
  happen, i.e. the representations converge to 5 clusters".
- **Fine classes recoverable** (§1, §4.3): CLP can "achieve > 93% accuracy
  on the original CIFAR-10 test set", "matching the standard accuracy of
  ResNet-18, even though only 5 coarse labels are provided the entire
  time."
- **Order in training** (§1, §4.1): "The effect of input distribution
  emerges earlier in training, while the effect of labels appears at a
  later stage"; the diagonal of the class-distance matrix for the original
  classes "emerges earlier in training".
- **Persistence** (§1): "this phenomenon persists even after a very long
  training period (e.g. 1,000 epochs)".
- **Split labels** (§6): with each class split at random into two labels,
  the 10-class relationship "is still preserved, although with a lighter
  color."
- **Semantics** (§5): on CIFAR-100's official super-classes the network
  "is not able to distinguish all sub-classes", but separates them where
  "sufficiently different".
- **Not universal** (§8, App. H): fine structure appears for ResNet and
  DenseNet, "but not for VGG"; VGG "only displays Neural Collapse, and can
  not distinguish different original classes within one super-class, even
  in an early stage of training." "The reason why VGG is abnormal requires
  further exploration."
- **Theorem 7.1**: after gradient descent the hidden features of same-
  sub-cluster points are much closer than those of different sub-clusters
  with the same coarse label.

## Concepts

- **Neural collapse** NC1 (within-class variability relative to between)
  and NC2 (class means toward a simplex ETF).
- **Coarse / fine CIFAR-10**; **super-class and sub-class**.
- **CLP**, Cluster-and-Linear-Probe.
- **The unconstrained-feature (layer-peeled) model**, the authors' foil:
  it "completely ignores the role of the input data".

## Connections

The NC protocol is Papyan, Han and Donoho's ([THEORY-083](../theory.d/THEORY-083.md)), here run on
relabelled data. Its critique of the unconstrained-feature model's
data-blindness is the empirical, feature-learning side of what
Kothapalli and Tirer study in the kernel regime ([NOTE-774](NOTE-774.md), [THEORY-813](../theory.d/THEORY-813.md)). It
contrasts itself with Sohoni and colleagues' subclass work (2020), which
needs a performance gap between sub-classes; that work is not registered
and nothing here relies on it.

## Limitations

- Single runs, no seeds or error bars stated.
- CLP uses t-SNE, nonlinear and stochastic, and chooses the label mapping
  on the test set (2⁵ mappings), a mild optimism.
- CIFAR-scale vision only; architecture-dependent with no explanation.
- The per-epoch evidence is in distances; CLP accuracy is reported at the
  end of training in the main text.
- The theorem is a toy (fixed second layer, orthogonal means, unhinged
  loss) that does not reach the terminal phase of training.
- Appendix H's text swaps the labels of Fig. 4(a) and (b), a typo.
