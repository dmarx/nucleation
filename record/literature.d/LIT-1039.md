# LIT-1039: Agent-initiated socio-technical reconfiguration: a three-level taxonomy of autonomous AI governance and its recursive challenges

Lahiri, R. (2026). Agent-initiated socio-technical reconfiguration: a three-level taxonomy of autonomous AI governance and its recursive challenges. *Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence* 9. DOI 10.3389/frai.2026.1881783

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-732](../notes.d/NOTE-732.md)).

- **Configuration as code.** In the platform studied the agent's
  identity, tools and schedule are files it can read and write, so "the
  coordination architecture got moved inside the technical subsystem's
  own action space."
- **Three levels, by capability.** Constrained (cannot alter timing or
  structure), adaptive (changes when and how often it acts, including
  escalation thresholds), generative (brings a new channel, tool,
  artifact or protocol into being that persists and serves coordination).
  Capability, not permission or observed behaviour, sets the level.
- **Recursive governance.** The social subsystem's authority becomes
  dependent on the agent's acceptance of, or inability to circumvent, its
  constraints. Whitelists and sandboxes within the agent's reach inherit
  the problem; review outside its action space escapes it.
- **Five propositions,** on coupling and control, adaptive and generative
  governance, drift (declared a conjecture), and the insufficiency of
  technical controls. None tested.
- **Evidence.** Two observed vignettes (an interface built for an unplanned
  messaging channel; an unrequested demo) and four public incidents. None
  shows an agent altering a control on itself.

## Why this is here

A11 took it as the closest contemporary case of what the exchange called constitutive influence, which the record holds as [CLAIM-086](../claims.d/CLAIM-086.md) (a constraint may govern conduct without governing the conditions of its own authority). The reading narrows that. The taxonomy's Level 3 counts any new, persisting coordination surface, and a new messaging interface changes nothing about which operations are permitted or how authority is allocated. The constitutive case is in the recursion argument: a whitelist the agent can edit governs its conduct while the condition of its staying in force lies with the agent itself ([THEORY-223](../theory.d/THEORY-223.md)). That is [CLAIM-086](../claims.d/CLAIM-086.md)'s distinction with the governed party, not a rival order, holding authority over the constraint. The paper argues it from architecture and does not observe it. The abstract's talk of the agents' "self-governing capacity" is not examined in the body: every reconfiguration serves the given task, so on Kahl's test ([LIT-1033](LIT-1033.md)) it is adaptation within a frame and says nothing for [CLAIM-284](../claims.d/CLAIM-284.md) beyond showing that endogenous change of coordination architecture occurs. Much of the discussion is advice to organisations deploying agents, not filed here. Flagged `anthology-candidate` because its main example is an AI-agent architecture the anthology's agents-and-environments topic might hold ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)).
