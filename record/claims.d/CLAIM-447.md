# CLAIM-447: Model-based wanting is a pursuit process's ranking of the futures it predicts under candidate interventions, P(S | do(a)) against P(S | do(a₀)), while cue-triggered wanting is learned salience that need not compute those futures; a process's causal model, its wanting and its practical efficacy are separable, so learning to want differently, learning that a want will not pay, and learning not to act on it are three different changes

## The claim

The owner, 2026-10-23: "really this is a do() operation and the future
state that's being anticipated is what might or might not happen given a
candidate intervention."

[CLAIM-437](CLAIM-437.md) makes 'wanting' salience relative to a pursuit process but does
not say what that process evaluates. This claim says. A pursuit process
*i* in state *s* holds a model of what an intervention would bring about,
P_i(S′ | s, do(A = a)), and ranks it against an alternative, often doing
nothing:

    a ≻_i a₀  ⟺  P_i(S′ | s, do(a)) ≻_i P_i(S′ | s, do(a₀)).

The ranking is of predicted distributions over futures, so the record's
ordinal commitment holds: no real-valued utility is assumed. Where one is
available, the familiar case is a positive difference of expected
utilities under the two interventions. `do()` matters because the process
evaluates what the intervention would cause, not what it would expect to
see if the action merely happened ([THEORY-062](../theory.d/THEORY-062.md)'s model-based prediction).
The intervention need not act on the world. It can act on attention, a
retrieval policy or the observer itself ([CLAIM-341](CLAIM-341.md)).

**Two levels.** Model-based wanting computes those futures. Cue-triggered
wanting is learned salience on a state–action pair that need not contain a
prediction at all, which is what incentive sensitization shows: pursuit
that persists against the person's own prediction of the consequences
([THEORY-895](../theory.d/THEORY-895.md), [THEORY-893](../theory.d/THEORY-893.md)). That the cached level is amortized prospective
evaluation is a hypothesis about how it was acquired. It is not a claim
about what it now computes.

**Three components, not one.** Prediction, appraisal of the predicted
future, and motivational salience can each be present without the others.
One can predict that exercise would help and value the help with no urge
to exercise; a cue can move one strongly with no favourable prediction.

**Wanting is not will.** A process can want an intervention and have no
authority to make it. Which proposed intervention becomes effective is the
will-organization's business ([CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)). So there are three separable
questions: what future a process anticipates (its causal model), what
intervention is salient to it (its wanting), and what intervention
becomes effective (its place in the organization). Learning to want
differently, learning that what one wants will not pay, and learning not
to act on it change different ones.

**Wanting need not presuppose conflict.** A single process can favour an
anticipated future that no other process contests. Conflict makes
precedence observable. It is not what makes a want.

The owner's sentence, "I want something because I predict I will occupy a
more desirable state after obtaining it", becomes: I want something, in
the cognitive sense, insofar as I evaluate the futures attainable through
obtaining it as preferable to the relevant alternatives. That keeps
desirability apart from probability: one can want recovery with no
confidence that any treatment achieves it.

## Dated part of 2026-10-24: governance changes what can be wanted

Later in the same exchange (U20), with [CASE-124](../cases.d/CASE-124.md)'s legibility state in
hand: what enters P_i is only what the process can acquire and represent.
Governance therefore reaches wanting by two paths. The practical path is
that authority changes which interventions are feasible or affordable. The
epistemic path is that legibility changes which futures can be represented
credibly enough to be evaluated at all. A process can keep valuing an
outcome, safe engagement for an anxious agent say, while becoming unable
to generate a credible, actionable forecast of reaching it ([CLAIM-446](CLAIM-446.md)).
A desire can lose practical efficacy without losing its evaluative
content, and a change of legibility can make an old desire actionable
without changing the concern behind it.

## What it does not say

- That all wanting is model-based, or conscious, or endorsed.
- That the cached level descends from the model-based one.
- How a pursuit process is individuated apart from the dissociations it is
  invoked to explain. That is still [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md)'s open problem.
