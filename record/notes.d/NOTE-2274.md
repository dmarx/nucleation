# NOTE-2274: Wang, Wu & Fan — Nonlinear spiked covariance matrices and signal propagation in deep neural networks

## Contribution

1. First-order limits and a BBP-type transition for the spikes of nonlinear
   spiked covariance models K = GᵀG, not only weak convergence of the
   bulk: "an exact asymptotic equivalence between spiked spectral
   phenomena in a nonlinear spiked covariance model with those of a
   linearly defined (possibly Gaussian) model" (§3, Theorem 6, formal
   Theorem 12).
2. A deterministic equivalent of the resolvent outside the support, and a
   "no outliers" theorem (Theorem 9).
3. Spike propagation through depth at random weights (Theorem 3,
   Corollary 4).
4. The test-data conjugate kernel's spike and its label alignment after a
   few gradient steps (Theorem 5), proving Conjecture 4 of Ba and
   colleagues ([LIT-3039](../literature.d/LIT-3039.md)).

## Key insight

A spike in the input Gram matrix survives a layer only if it passes that
layer's BBP-type test; each survival multiplies its eigenvector's overlap
with the input by a factor below one. So at random weights depth can only
lose outliers and alignment. Training adds a rank-one piece to the weights
whatever the step size, but an outlier shows only above a threshold.

## Assumptions

- Proportional limit n/dℓ → γℓ, fixed depth L, i.i.d. N(0, 1) weights
  independent of the data (§2.1). Inputs approximately orthonormal, with a
  fixed number r of spikes of bounded size.
- σ twice differentiable with bounded σ′ and σ″; Eσ(ξ) = 0, Eσ² = 1;
  bσ = E σ′(ξ) ≠ 0 and E σ″(ξ) = 0 (eq. 2.5), which holds "for odd
  activation functions σ(·) such as tanh".
- §2.2: isotropic Gaussian inputs, single-index labels plus noise, second
  layer frozen, a fixed number of gradient steps with η of order one (not
  the η = Θ(d) of Dandi and colleagues), independent test data.

## Key results

- **Motivation** (§1): spikes "are often the primary spectral features
  (signal) of interest".
- **Per-layer transition** (§2.1, after eq. 2.7): "The condition zℓ′(si,ℓ−1)
  > 0 describes the 'phase transition' phenomenon for spike eigenvalues in
  this model, where spikes i ∈ Iℓ−1 with zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) > 0 induce spike
  eigenvalues in the CK matrix Kℓ of the next layer, while spikes with
  zℓ′(si,ℓ−1) ≤ 0 are absorbed into the bulk spectrum of Kℓ."
- **Theorem 3(c)**: the squared overlap of layer ℓ's spike eigenvector with
  the input's is a product of per-layer factors φk ∈ (0, 1).
- **Corollary 4** (signal-plus-noise input X = Σθᵢaᵢbᵢᵀ + Z): Kℓ has a
  spike for θᵢ if and only if θᵢ > γ0^(1/4) and i ∈ Iℓ.
- **Curse of depth** (§2.1): the number of spike eigenvalues of Kℓ and
  their alignment with the class labels "are both non-increasing in the
  network depth ... In other words, at random initialization, the input
  signal diminishes as the depth of the NN increases."
- **Remedies, by simulation** (Fig. 2): wider layers keep alignment over
  more layers; "gradient descent training on the weight matrices also
  restores and even amplifies the informative signal in the CK matrix of
  each layer", and after 50 steps the alignment "may increase through
  depth". Simulation, not theorem.
- **Activation conditions** (§2.1): "if bσ = 0, then spiked eigenstructure
  does not propagate across the NN layers in our studied regime of bounded
  spike magnitudes"; E σ″ = 0 "ensures that Kℓ does not have uninformative
  spike eigenvalues; otherwise, as shown in [BP22], Kℓ may have spike
  eigenvalues even when the input K0 has no spiked structure."
- **Trained weights** (§2.2, after Ba and colleagues): W ≈ W₀ +
  (ηbσ/n)Xᵀyaᵀ, a change "approximately rank-1". Proposition 35: "The
  largest singular value smax(W) falls outside the limit of its empirical
  singular value distribution if and only if θ1 > γ0^(1/4)".
- **Theorem 5**: the test-data conjugate kernel "has a spike eigenvalue if
  and only if θ1 > γ0^(1/4) and z′(−1/λ1) > 0", with positive label
  alignment. Fig. 3(b): "above a phase transition threshold, the alignment
  ... increases with the learning rate or number of GD steps; in addition,
  alignment also increases with the training set size n."

## Concepts

- **Conjugate kernel (CK)**: the Gram matrix of a layer's features.
- **Absorbed into the bulk**: a spike failing a layer's test, after which
  the theorems say nothing of what it leaves in the bulk or in
  sub-threshold eigenvectors.
- **Uninformative spike eigenvalues**: outliers of the CK that carry no
  input structure, from non-odd activations (Benigni and Péché,
  [LIT-3047](../literature.d/LIT-3047.md)), assumed away here.
- "Bulk" is used by count throughout ("bulk spectrum").

## Connections

The η of order one regime is the bounded-step sibling of Dandi and
colleagues' maximal-update step ([NOTE-2258](NOTE-2258.md)): here learning shows as one
outlier beside the untrained bulk law, there chiefly as a reshaped bulk.
The paper cites Moniri and colleagues ([LIT-3032](../literature.d/LIT-3032.md)) as "an asymptotic
regime different from ours". Park and colleagues ([NOTE-2260](NOTE-2260.md)) cite it as
prior BBP-in-networks work; their critical time is the analogue of Fig.
3(b)'s threshold in learning rate times steps. It also cites Ben Arous and
colleagues ([LIT-3034](../literature.d/LIT-3034.md)) for spike structure in the Hessian under SGD.
[CASE-301](../cases.d/CASE-301.md) is Corollary 4's input before any layer.

## Limitations

- Random Gaussian weights for the depth result; the training result is
  two-layer, a few steps, η of order one, frozen second layer, Gaussian
  inputs, single-index target.
- Bounded spikes, a fixed number of them, proportional widths.
- bσ = 0 and non-odd activations are outside the theorems.
- First-order limits only: no fluctuations, and nothing on what an
  absorbed spike leaves behind. "The input signal diminishes" is a reading
  of outlier alignment, not a theorem about decodability.
- The width and training remedies of Fig. 2 are simulations.
- The proofs were not checked.
