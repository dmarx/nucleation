# THEORY-tmpfun40: In fear conditioning, extinction does not erase the original threat association but adds a competing inhibitory one, which is why extinguished fear can return with time, a change of context, an unsignalled aversive event or renewed pairing

## Source

Craske, Treanor, Conway, Zbozinek & Vervliet, [LIT-tmpz7j0g](../literature.d/LIT-tmpz7j0g.md), read in full
in [NOTE-tmpqrvri](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqrvri.md) from the NIH author manuscript (PMC4114726): the
sections "Inhibitory Learning Model of Extinction" and
"Reconsolidation".

## What was actually shown

Nothing new. The paper states the model and its evidence: "the original
CS-US association learned during fear conditioning is not erased during
extinction, but rather is left intact as new, secondary inhibitory
learning about the CS-US develops". After extinction the CS "possesses
two meanings". Four procedures uncover the original meaning:
spontaneous recovery (time), renewal (context change), reinstatement
(unsignalled US) and rapid reacquisition (re-pairing). Each is cited to
animal and human studies. The authors note that "additional mechanisms,
such as habituation, are likely to be involved". They also note that
reconsolidation results, which might suggest erasure, "may relate more to
enhancing the retrieval of the extinction learning rather than erasing
acquisition learning".

What could have come out otherwise: extinguished fear could have stayed
gone under all four procedures, as an unlearning account predicts.

## What this does not say

- **That exposure therapy works only this way.** Exposure is "the
  clinical proxy of extinction"; the translation is the paper's
  proposal.
- **That the original association can never be erased.** The
  reconsolidation evidence is described as open.
- **That anxious patients' fear is a conditioned association.** That
  fear conditioning is a valid model of the disorders is assumed, with
  one citation.
