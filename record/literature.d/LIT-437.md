# LIT-437: Gödel, Escher, Bach: an Eternal Golden Braid

Douglas R. Hofstadter (1979), *Gödel, Escher, Bach: an Eternal Golden Braid*,
New York: Basic Books (hardback, ISBN 0-465-02685-0; Library of Congress
78019943, call number QA9.8 .H63; xxi, 777 pp., illustrated, bibliography
pp. 746–756, index). A Vintage Books reprint followed in 1980 (LCCN
80011354), and Basic Books issued a twentieth-anniversary edition in 1999
(LCCN 99192374; trade paperback ISBN 978-0-465-02656-2, on sale 5 February
1999 per the publisher, 824 pp.), which is the edition the publisher sells
now. No source I reached gives a day or month for the 1979 printing, so
`published:` carries the year only, as the first of January.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description and the catalogue record, not a
reading. What follows is what the book and its cataloguers say about it.*

- The publisher's description (Basic Books, for the 1999 edition),
  verbatim: "GEB is a unique insight into the nature of 'I,' self, soul,
  and consciousness, centered on a notion that its youthful author dubbed
  'strange loop,' inspired by the twisty self-referential construction
  invented by logician Kurt Gödel, whereby a sentence asserts its own
  unprovability. The book's chapters alternate with Bach-like contrapuntal
  dialogues between whimsical characters (especially Achilles and the
  Tortoise), and each dialogue's intricate structure exemplifies the notion
  being discussed in it, thus creating indirect self-reference (a fact
  unsuspected by the characters)." The page heads it "A metaphorical fugue
  on minds and machines in the spirit of Lewis Carroll".
- The Library of Congress files it under metamathematics (QA9.8) with the
  subject headings artificial intelligence; metamathematics; symmetry;
  Gödel, Kurt; Escher, M. C.; Bach, Johann Sebastian.
- Contents: the only contents note I could see is the part-level one in a
  catalogue record for the 1980 Penguin printing: "pt. 1. GEB -- pt. 2.
  EGB." No catalogue record, publisher page or open preview I reached lists
  the chapters and dialogues, so I give no chapter-level contents.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for Douglas Hofstadter in this
record: strange loops and the self, and the Fluid Analogies Research
Group's programme that analogy-making is the core of cognition. This is the
first of the strange-loop books. No anthology topic holds it: its subject is
self-reference in formal systems and what that may say about minds, and it
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice. The anthology does
not hold it either (no Hofstadter work is in its literature, checked by
grep).

`Deferred` because it was not read. This record files a book from its
publisher's description and contents when no lawful text can be read, and
does not rebuild a book from what others say about it.

Where it bears on the record:

- Hofstadter's lead essay in the *Philosophical Transactions* theme issue
  on world models is summarised in the issue's editorial, [LIT-195](LIT-195.md), and
  [NOTE-136](../notes.d/NOTE-136.md) records his claim there that passing a consistent-understanding
  Turing test suffices to attribute thinking. That essay, "Is there an 'I'
  in AI?", is being filed at the same time by another reader. Its title
  takes up the question this book's description names first, the nature of
  "I"; the link is drawn from the titles, not from a reading of either.
- The later book in this batch, *I Am a Strange Loop*, is the one its
  publisher presents as Hofstadter's return to this book's themes. No
  `extends:` is declared, because only the publisher, not the later book as
  I could see it, says so.
- The Fluid Analogies Research Group's programme, *Fluid Concepts and
  Creative Analogies* in this batch, is the computational line of the
  same author. Nothing I could see in this book's description names analogy
  as its subject, though the publisher says the book is "filled with
  analogies".
