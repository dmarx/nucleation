# LIT-254: Similarity of Neural Network Representations Revisited

Simon Kornblith, Mohammad Norouzi, Honglak Lee, Geoffrey Hinton (2019), *ICML 2019 (Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR vol. 97); first appeared as arXiv preprint* — [ARXIV-1905.00414](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-743](../notes.d/NOTE-743.md).*

- **Theorem 1.** If s(X,Z) = s(XA,Z) for every invertible A, then
  s(X,Z) = s(Y,Z) whenever rank(X) = rank(Y) = n. Such an index cannot
  compare layers at least as wide as the dataset. The proof is four lines
  (App. A).
- **The right invariances, argued.** §2 argues for invariance to
  orthogonal transformation and isotropic scaling, but not to
  non-isotropic scaling. Three reasons are given:
  - permutation symmetry;
  - the linear-case gradient-descent result of LeCun et al.;
  - networks from different seeds sharing their large principal
    components (Fig. 1).
  No theorem fixes the group.
- **CKA.** HSIC(K,L) = tr(KHLH)/(n−1)², and
  CKA = HSIC(K,L)/√(HSIC(K,K)HSIC(L,L)). Linear CKA is
  ‖YᵀX‖²_F/(‖XᵀX‖_F‖YᵀY‖_F), the RV coefficient. Centring by H removes
  any constant added to a kernel, since H1 = 0.
- **One family.** R²_CCA and R²_LR are CKA of re-weighted Gram matrices
  (Eqs. 11–12). Linear CKA is CCA's overlap Σ⟨u_Xⁱ, u_Yʲ⟩² weighted by the
  eigenvalues λ_Xⁱλ_Yʲ (Eqs. 13–14). Canonical ridge interpolates between
  R²_CCA, linear regression and CKA (App. D.1). Orthogonal Procrustes
  alignment reduces to the nuclear norm ‖YᵀX‖_* (App. D.2).
- **Empirical results.**
  - Linear CKA identifies corresponding layers across ten CIFAR-10 CNNs
    with 99.3% accuracy. Linear regression reaches 45.4%, linear HSIC
    22.2%, and CCA, SVCCA and PWCCA 1.4–15.1%.
  - All indexes pass on Transformer encoders.
  - CKA also shows pathological depth, the effects of width, and early
    layers shared between CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 while pursuing, at the owner's request, the
connection *GNS, unitary equivalence and the convergence of
representations* (see the curation entry of that date). Read on
2026-10-14 in [NOTE-743](../notes.d/NOTE-743.md). It is held as the standard reference for
comparing representations through their kernels, and as the place where
orthogonal invariance is argued to be the right equivalence for
representations. That is the finite-sample counterpart of "determined up
to unitary equivalence". The Platonic hypothesis ([ANTH-LIT-458](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-458.md)) cites it
for that. Its Theorem 1, that an index invariant to every
invertible linear map is degenerate at full rank, is filed as
[THEORY-236](../theory.d/THEORY-236.md).

It is a supporting source of [THEORY-004](../theory.d/THEORY-004.md), for the case for orthogonal
invariance (§2.2). The case is an argument, and the converse that
[THEORY-004](../theory.d/THEORY-004.md) needs, that equal kernels imply an orthogonal relation, is
in [LIT-250](LIT-250.md) and [LIT-259](LIT-259.md), not here. App. D.2's Procrustes identity is the
closer link to [THEORY-004](../theory.d/THEORY-004.md)'s Procrustes–Bures equality. It is a
supporting source of [THEORY-008](../theory.d/THEORY-008.md) only for the family structure: CCA,
regression and CKA as one ridge-indexed family. It says nothing about
readouts or GULP.

Tagged `anthology-candidate` ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)): recommending CKA as the default
similarity index is a tool for ML analysis. It is kept here by the
owner's decision of 2026-09-26 that new work stays in nucleation until a
transfer is judged appropriate ([ADR-010](../decisions.d/ADR-010.md)).
