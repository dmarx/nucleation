# LIT-tmplhywo: Basic psychological need theory: Advancements, critical themes, and future directions

Maarten Vansteenkiste, Richard M. Ryan and Bart Soenens (2020), *Motivation and Emotion* 44(1):1–31 — DOI-10.1007/s11031-019-09818-1

## Key takeaways

- **Criteria for a basic need (Table 2, p. 4).** Five basic (psychological,
  essential, inherent, distinct, universal) and four associated (pervasive,
  content-specific, directional, explanatory). A candidate that appears only
  when another need is frustrated is "a need-substitute or a compensatory
  preference" (p. 5): self-esteem, security and power are examples.
- **Frustration is its own dimension.** "The absence of need satisfaction does
  not necessarily imply the presence of need frustration, whereas the presence
  of need frustration denotes the absence of need satisfaction" (p. 9).
  Frustration predicts ill-being beyond low satisfaction (p. 10).
- **A transdiagnostic claim.** Need frustration predicted adolescents'
  depressive symptoms and eating pathology and "largely accounted for their
  co-occurrence" (Campbell et al. 2018a, p. 12).
- **Universality without uniformity (pp. 17–20).** Moderators change how
  strongly need satisfaction pays off, and which practices satisfy needs, but
  there is "no evidence for cross-over interactions": nobody is found to gain
  from frustration (p. 19).

needs half of SDT. It `extends` Deci and Ryan (2000), [LIT-tmpc4275](LIT-tmpc4275.md): it
