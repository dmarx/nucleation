# LIT-918: Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities

J. J. Hopfield (1982), *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 79(8), 2554–2558

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-819](../notes.d/NOTE-819.md).*

- **Memory as attractors.** "Any physical system whose dynamics in phase
  space is dominated by a substantial number of locally stable states to
  which it is attracted can therefore be regarded as a general
  content-addressable memory."
- **The energy function is derived.** For symmetric couplings,
  E = −½ Σᵢ≠ⱼ TᵢⱼVᵢVⱼ never rises under asynchronous threshold updates
  (Eqs. 7–8). This is the paper's one derivation; the rest is simulation.
- **Storage is Hebbian.** Tᵢⱼ = Σₛ (2Vᵢˢ − 1)(2Vⱼˢ − 1), with Tᵢᵢ = 0. Unlike
  a linear associative net, which answers a mixed cue with a mixed
  output, the network chooses one memory.
- **Basins.** At N = 30 with five memories, a start within 5 bits of a
  memory reaches it more than 90% of the time, falling to 0.2 at 12 bits.
  An ambiguous cue gets "a statistical choice between the memory states
  it most resembles".
- **Capacity.** "About 0.15 N states can be simultaneously remembered
  before error in recall is severe": the load at which about half the
  memories are well retained, at N = 30 and 100.
- **Two different failures.** Memories closer than about a tenth of N
  fuse, at any load. Past capacity, new memories make "all memory states
  irretrievable unless there is a provision for forgetting"; saturating
  synapses keep the recent and lose the distant past.
- **Spurious states at low load.** At five memories in 30 units, 10% of
  random starts end in "stable states of no obvious meaning".
- **Robustness.** The memory survives asymmetric, clipped and one-way
  couplings, failing "softly". With a threshold, a cue that resembles no
  memory falls to a state that signals unfamiliarity.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-11 at the owner's request, with Hebb ([LIT-922](LIT-922.md)) and
Kanerva ([LIT-928](LIT-928.md), [LIT-921](LIT-921.md)), and read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-819](../notes.d/NOTE-819.md). It is
the dynamical source for [CLAIM-180](../claims.d/CLAIM-180.md)'s picture of retrieval by content, and
the reading supports that picture while correcting it in two places:
merging comes from two memories being close, not from load, and the
model has no analogue of a cue "taken for new" or of a code. Its overload
result is the sharper form of the flooding analogy in [CLAIM-179](../claims.d/CLAIM-179.md): past
capacity a memory loses everything unless it forgets. [THEORY-272](../theory.d/THEORY-272.md),
filed from the reading, holds the attractor account with its limits.
Kanerva's report ([LIT-921](LIT-921.md)) puts the same capacity per storage element on
sparse distributed memory. Flagged anthology-candidate.
