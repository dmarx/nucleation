# TERM-038: descriptive, causal and constitutive relations of a communicative act

## Definition

A191 §11 distinguishes "three senses of structure", each with its question:

| Type | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | What relationships are observed? | Two speakers are judged to be aligned. |
| Modal or causal | What would happen under changes in conditions? | Removing shared background knowledge changes whether irony is recognized. |
| Constitutive | Which relationships make this the kind of act it is? | A promise establishes a commitment under relevant conventional and normative conditions. |

A relation of a communicative act is descriptive when it is observed in the
data, causal when it holds under interventions on the conditions, and
constitutive when its alteration changes which act was performed. A191 §4
pairs these with three kinds of identification: categorical identification
("In a specified category, the full representable profile determines an
object up to isomorphism"), operational identification ("A selected family
of measurements can distinguish some communicative realizations from
others") and ontological constitution ("Certain relations partly make a
communicative act the kind of act it is").

A203 §16 restates the table in other words ("Constitutive | Which
relationships partly determine the act's identity?") and adds: "Causal
intervention results can support a constitutive account, but they do not
by themselves prove metaphysical constitution." A218 §2 keeps the distinction as an open
classification: "some observed relations are merely diagnostic, some
causally influential, and others constitutive".

## What it is not

- **Not a chain of entailments.** A191 §11: "These form a useful
  progression in the research, but not an automatic chain of logical
  entailments. Descriptive structure alone does not determine causal
  structure. Causal stability alone does not establish constitution." A191
  §4: "Neither of the first two logically entails the third."
- **Not a ranking of evidence.** Causal evidence is not a stronger grade
  of descriptive evidence for constitution. A constitutive claim needs a
  theory of the act. A191 §6: it "must be grounded in a substantive theory
  of the act and its normative conditions, not only its measured
  classification."
- **Not the distinction between situation and intervention.** That one is
  about what an experiment manipulates ([CLAIM-130](../claims.d/CLAIM-130.md)). This one is about
  what kind of relation a result could establish.
