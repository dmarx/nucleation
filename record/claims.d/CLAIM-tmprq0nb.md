# CLAIM-tmprq0nb: On the BERT anaphora data only Contextuality-by-Default 2.0 gives a single verdict once signalling is allowed: the sheaf figure is that of a sufficient test, and the sheaf-side readings that are exact give other figures, one of them at least as large as the CbD figure, so the comparison supports the thesis that formal contextuality on such data needs Contextuality-by-Default

## The claim

[CLAIM-tmptzmky](CLAIM-tmptzmky.md) shows what each route says on [LIT-844](../literature.d/LIT-844.md)'s support:

- **CbD 2.0's verdict is exact.** A model is noncontextual exactly when a
  multimaximally connected coupling exists. On this support that is
  Δ ≥ 2, and the verdict is checked by linear programming.
- **The sheaf route gives a criterion, not a verdict.** [LIT-844](../literature.d/LIT-844.md) calls
  CF > 2|M|·SF "a criterion of contextuality for signalling models". On
  this support it is sufficient only. Read as "the no-signalling part is
  contextual", the sheaf route calls a model contextual iff SF < 1, which
  covers at least 71.1% of the instances.
- **Distance readings give still other verdicts.** A reading by distance
  to the nearest no-signalling model gives verdicts that depend on the
  norm chosen. In the max norm they cross CbD's in both directions
  ([CASE-tmpskejj](../cases.d/CASE-tmpskejj.md)).

So the 0.148% is a lower bound on one sheaf-side reading, not a competing
finding. Once signalling is allowed, the sheaf side has no single answer
until it fixes how signalling is charged, and each way of charging it is
a further choice. CbD 2.0 makes that choice once, in the definition of a
connection's multimaximal coupling, and gives one answer. Read this way,
the comparison [CLAIM-037](CLAIM-037.md) asks for comes out in its favour: on signalling
data, a sheaf route without a fixed account of signalling does not handle
inconsistent connectedness without loss.

## What it does not say

- That CbD 2.0's choice is the right account of signalling. It says only
  that it is a single, stated choice. [CLAIM-tmpzvz98](CLAIM-tmpzvz98.md) contests that the
  choice is the right one.
- That no sheaf route can handle signalling. [LIT-843](../literature.d/LIT-843.md)'s causal-past route,
  which builds declared signalling into the classical model, is not
  compared here: [LIT-844](../literature.d/LIT-844.md)'s data name no causal order.
