# THEORY-1964: Nickel and Etinson characterize human rights as plural, universal, high-priority rights held independently of whether local law, practice or morality recognizes them, deny that inalienability, minimalism or a political function belongs to the concept, and report that the grounds offered for them vary and that agency and dignity each seem to need supplementing

Nickel & Etinson, "Human Rights" (SEP, 2024 revision), [LIT-2743](../literature.d/LIT-2743.md).

## The account

"People have human rights independently of whether such rights are present
in the practices, morality, or law of their country or culture" (§1). "It
is doubtful that all human rights are inalienable in this sense" (§1). For
agency "some additional ground pertaining to fairness and equality seems to
be needed" (§2.2.1).

## What it does not show

- Which grounding is right: the entry discusses two of many.
- That human rights exist apart from law in any robust sense; it thinks
  the best existence combines the legal and the moral.
