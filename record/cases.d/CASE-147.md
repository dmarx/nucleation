# CASE-147: Conversation against argument: in Kalla and Broockman's field experiments, canvassers who exchanged personal narratives nonjudgmentally reduced exclusionary attitudes durably, while arguments alone did not produce comparable effects

## The case

A4: "In three field experiments, Kalla and Broockman (2020) found that
nonjudgmental exchanges of personal narratives could produce persistent
reductions in exclusionary attitudes, whereas arguments alone did not
produce comparable effects in their immigration-policy experiment."
([LIT-1407](../literature.d/LIT-1407.md), unread; its summary is from the abstract.)

## What it shows

- **The nearest field contrast to [CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md)'s defeater.** The claim
  falls if information of equal favourable content changes attitudes as
  much as taking part does. A conversation in which both parties exchange
  narratives is closer to taking part than an argument heard; the arms
  differ in that, and the narrative arm's effect persisted.
- **A4's distinction**, between "*telling someone what to think*" and
  "*engaging them in an interaction that changes what they can
  meaningfully think*".

## What it does not show

- That the effect runs through a perspective. A4: "Those studies don't
  isolate organizational-perspective formation as the mediator." The arms
  also differ in warmth, attention and length.
- That arguments never work, or that narrative exchange always does. The
  exchange's comparison is to one experiment's argument arm.
- Large effects. [LIT-1407](../literature.d/LIT-1407.md)'s abstract: the narrative arm reduced
  exclusionary attitudes "for at least four months (d = 0.08)", and the
  replications on transphobia gave d = 0.08 and 0.04. Only Experiment 1
  had an argument-only arm.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the paper, read

[LIT-1407](../literature.d/LIT-1407.md) is now read ([NOTE-1396](../notes.d/NOTE-1396.md)), so "unread" and "unchecked against
the paper" no longer hold. The description is right, and can be made
exact.

- **"Arguments alone did not produce comparable effects."** The
  abbreviated arm's pooled effect is d = 0.02 (p = 0.27), and its
  difference from the full arm is 0.06 (p < 0.01). But the arms' difference
  is not significant at the one-month wave or, for prejudice, at three to
  six months.
- **"Persistent."** A pooled effect over waves ending at about four and a
  half months, the last wave's prejudice effect alone being p < 0.08.
- **Length.** The caveat that the arms "also differ in warmth, attention
  and length" is borne out on length by the paper's own footnote 8: the
  full arm was twice as long.

One finding weighs against reading the narrative arm as taking part. In
Experiment 2 a stranger's video narrative, with voters sharing their own
stories in 12% of conversations, did as well as the arm in which both
parties told stories. What the arms shared was a non-judgmental
conversation about a narrative, not the voter's own enactment. The paper
measures no behaviour and no generalization to other groups. The case is
unchanged.
