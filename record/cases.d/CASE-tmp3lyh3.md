# CASE-tmp3lyh3: Data written for a future reader and never read

## The case

Someone stores a message for a reader they anticipate. It might be a note
to a colleague, an instruction for whoever restores a backup, a letter
filed and never sent, or a reminder to their own later self. The writer
chooses words for that reader's expected common ground. The store survives
and nobody ever reads it.

Held fixed against [CASE-tmpjvzb6](CASE-tmpjvzb6.md): nothing receives the event. Varied: the
event was produced for a receiver.

## What it can show

The owner's claim, [CLAIM-tmpdfqoo](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdfqoo.md), that the write fixes the intended
meaning even with no read. Two sub-cases are worth keeping apart:

- **The reader is someone else.** This is the line's usual setting.
- **The reader is the writer's future self.** Otto's notebook ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md))
  is this sub-case. It is the diachronic route to an extended self, which
  [CLAIM-tmptsdgt](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptsdgt.md) names and does not cover: a store is coupled to its user
  across time by a write and a read, not by entrainment.

As a stipulated case it shows only what follows from its description. The
claim that intention is fixed at the write is the claim's to defend, not the
case's.
