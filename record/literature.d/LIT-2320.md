summary: >-
  de Kleer (1986), An assumption-based TMS. De Kleer proposes a truth
  maintenance system that, besides recording the problem solver's
  justifications as a conventional (Doyle-style) TMS does, labels every
  datum with the minimal sets of assumptions ("environments") under which it
  is derivable. Because contexts are explicit, the database need not be
  consistent: mutually contradictory assumptions can be explored at once, a
  contradiction just marks a set of assumptions as a "nogood", context
  switching is a subset test, and most backtracking and all retraction are
  avoided. The ATMS maintains four label properties (consistency, soundness,
  completeness, minimality) incrementally as justifications arrive. It suits
  tasks where many alternative solutions must be explored and compared
  (qualitative reasoning, multi-fault diagnosis), at a cost for tasks that
  want one solution. Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
