# LIT-1035: Stability of nonlinear Hawkes processes

Brémaud, P., & Massoulié, L. (1996). Stability of nonlinear Hawkes processes. *The Annals of Probability* 24(3), 1563–1588. DOI 10.1214/aop/1065725193

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-728](../notes.d/NOTE-728.md)).

- **Linearity and excitation are not what make a Hawkes process settle.**
  With rate φ(∫h(t−s)N(ds)), φ α-Lipschitz and h of either sign,
  α∫|h| < 1 gives a unique stationary law with finite mean intensity, and
  convergence to it from any past whose influence fades (Theorem 1). For
  a network the condition is a spectral radius below one of the matrix
  α_i∫|h_ji| (Theorem 7). In the linear excitatory case these reduce to
  the branching ratio below one of Hawkes and Oakes ([LIT-1028](LIT-1028.md)).
- **A bounded rate needs no size condition.** Bounded Lipschitz φ gives
  the same with kernels of any size (Theorems 2 and 8); bounded rate and
  bounded memory give convergence from any past, exponentially fast
  (Theorems 3 and 5).
- **Inhibition is allowed but costs.** The conditions use |h|, so
  inhibitory weight adds to the bound. Mixed networks with monotone,
  bounded rates and sign-structured cross-connections have a stationary
  law, with uniqueness open (Theorem 6, Remark 9).
- **Sufficient, fixed-kernel, stable side only.** No necessary condition,
  nothing past the threshold, no result on cascade sizes, and kernels do
  not change with time or state.
- The proofs avoid the branching (cluster) representation, which the
  authors say is not available in this generality, and use Poisson
  embedding, a Picard-style iteration and coupling.

## Why this is here

The exchange's model has signed, state-dependent kernels, which A6 and A7
granted do not inherit the linear model's stability guarantees. This is the
standard place where that analysis starts. Method only. The reading
([THEORY-215](../theory.d/THEORY-215.md)) found three things the filing did not say.

- **It covers signed kernels, not state-dependent ones.** The rate is a
  nonlinear function of a fixed linear filter of the past. A6's influence
  canvas, which changes the kernels, is outside every theorem.
- **Its spectral radius is not a branching ratio** except in the linear
  excitatory case. For [QUESTION-091](../questions.d/QUESTION-091.md) that matters: in the nonlinear
  signed case the quantity bounds, and does not measure, how far
  cascades go; with saturating rates there is no criticality to be near.
- **For [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)** a Hawkes network has a coupling, the kernels, and a
  control parameter built from them. It has no order parameter in the
  claim's sense, and this paper proves nothing about transitions. It meets
  part of the demand for cascades of events and none of it for
  valuations, so [QUESTION-091](../questions.d/QUESTION-091.md)'s summary, which calls the branching
  ratio "the coupling and order parameter", says more than this reading
  supports.
