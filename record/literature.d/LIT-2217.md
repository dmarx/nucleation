# LIT-2217: Prolonged weaning from mechanical ventilation: who, what, when and how?

Shah, N. M., Hart, N. & Kaltsakas, G. (2024). Prolonged weaning from mechanical ventilation: who, what, when and how? *Breathe* 20(3): 240122. doi:10.1183/20734735.0122-2024.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1706](../notes.d/NOTE-1706.md). Sections are the review's.*

- **The groups.** Prolonged weaning patients "are those who fail at least
  three weaning attempts or require >7 days of weaning after the first
  SBT" (weaning classification), after Boles et al. (2007).
- **Readiness is underestimated.** "clinicians frequently underestimate
  the ability of patients to successfully wean"; predictors have "a low
  predictive power" (assessing readiness).
- **Delay harms.** Delay from eligibility to a liberation attempt "was
  associated with subsequent weaning failure" (response to a reduction in
  support).
- **Failure is lasting.** Of patients whose weaning fails, "approximately
  50% remain dependent on invasive ventilation at 1 year"; of those
  liberated after prolonged weaning, "42% required reinstitution of
  mechanical ventilation" (pathophysiology).

## Standing in the record

Filed as the stand-in for Boles et al. (2007), [LIT-2213](LIT-2213.md), on the
classification, the definition of weaning failure and the trial as the
readiness test, which it restates from the consensus; its numbers are from
later work. For the record it is a working instance of [QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md)'s
withdrawal measure: a repeated, matched, reversible probe of what removing
support does at the present state. It supports [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)'s requirement to
observe persistence after withdrawal, and its relapse figure is the
clinic's case of slow reversion mimicking persistence. Those who stay
dependent are the case [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) names. Filed from it: [THEORY-1515](../theory.d/THEORY-1515.md).
