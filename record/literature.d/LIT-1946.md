# LIT-1946: Minimal phenomenal experience: Meditation, tonic alertness, and the phenomenology of “pure” consciousness

Metzinger, T. (2020). Minimal phenomenal experience: Meditation, tonic
alertness, and the phenomenology of "pure" consciousness. *Philosophy and
the Mind Sciences*, 1(I), 7. doi:10.33735/phimisci.2020.I.46.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1498](../notes.d/NOTE-1498.md).*

- MPE is defined by subtraction from minimal phenomenal selfhood (Blanke
  and Metzinger 2009, [LIT-1274](LIT-1274.md)). It lacks MPS and every egoic
  self-consciousness, time and a spatial frame (p. 2). It is
  "aperspectival: no epistemic agent model" (p. 11).
- Wakefulness is the phenomenal property. Tonic alertness is the
  functional one, and arousal the physical one. "An organism can be
  tonically alert without knowing that it is alert: Consciousness is
  knowing that one is alert" (p. 33).
- MPE "definitely has representational content" (p. 38). It is "non-egoic
  self-modelling" (p. 36), a model of the organism's epistemic capacities.
  Its epistemic subject "simply is the organism or conscious system as a
  whole" (p. 27).
- MPE lacks even a phenomenal quality of oneness. Its space has "neither
  centre nor periphery" and "lacks the phenomenal quality of singularity
  or numerical identity", so "non-oneness" is added to "non-duality"
  (p. 13).
- A full-absorption episode cannot itself be reported, "because the
  self-referential mechanisms of forming an autobiographical memory are
  suspended". Only entering and leaving it can be remembered (p. 15).
