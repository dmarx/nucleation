# THEORY-tmpsm03n: A we-identity, being one of us, can arise through mutual individualization, in which members grasp one another as non-substitutable and complementary, without depersonalization, as in long friendships and romantic partnerships; so depersonalization is not necessary for a we-identity

## Source

- León (2023), LIT-tmpe36at, read in full in NOTE-tmpuba7m from the
  CC BY 4.0 full text.

## What the account says

A **we-identity** is an identity held as a group member, at times
salient, endorsed, and varying in affective salience. Self-categorization
theory explains it by **depersonalization**: seeing oneself and others as
interchangeable exemplars of an in-group prototype. A **close
relationship** involves robust, reciprocal, non-instrumental concern
that "cannot survive substitution of [its] participants" (Kolodny). In
such a relationship the "we" grows by **mutual individualization**: each
becomes tuned to the other's particularities and so less replaceable.
So there is at least one route to a we-identity that runs opposite to
depersonalization.

## What was actually shown

An argument, not a finding. The source cites relationship research on
we-ness and couple resilience (Skerrett & Fergus 2015; Reid et al. 2006)
and transactive memory in couples (Wegner et al. 1991) as fitting the
account, without analysing it. The record has read none of it. The
claim could fail: if we-ness in couples turned out to track
self-stereotyping as a couple, depersonalization would be doing the work
after all.

## What this does not say

- **Not that self-categorization theory is wrong about large groups.**
  The source grants its use for nations, teams and crowds.
- **Not that close others never act under a group prototype.** A group of
  friends can act under an ad hoc prototype when facing an outsider, and
  shared social identities often enable close relationships.
- **Not that the "we" is a supra-individual subject.** The source assumes
  it is not.
- **Not the socially extended account.** That is the source's positive
  sketch, by analogy to extended-mind criteria, and is not filed.

## Bearing

- **THEORY-tmpo85ke.** Limits its depersonalization clause in scope, not
  its context-dependence claim.
- **THEORY-tmpdftev.** Both deny that similarity or interchangeability is
  necessary for a group. That account puts collective intentionality in
  its place; this one puts mutual individualization.
- **THEORY-126.** A We-mode collective emotion between intimates, as in
  shared grief, would on this account be personalized rather than
  depersonalized. The record has not tested the two together.
