# NOTE-tmp1cbty: Quillien — Luck and intentional action

## Contribution

The paper asks whether an action can be intentional when success was improbable, a case where folk intuitions follow a complex pattern. Quillien proposes a causal theory: people call an outcome intentional when the agent's desire for it was a strong cause of it. When chance decides the outcome, the desire is judged a weak cause, and intentionality ratings drop with it. Across studies, participants who deny that a lucky outcome was intentional also deny that it happened because of the agent's desire. Intentionality judgments follow causal judgments more closely than they follow rival explanatory factors.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Intro, "Our Proposal": builds on Quillien & German (2021) and Davidson. "A did X intentionally" is roughly "X happened because A wanted X". Because causal judgment is graded (Hart & Honoré), a desire can be technically necessary for an outcome and still be judged a weak cause.
- Intro, "Causation and Counterfactuals": counterfactual theories of causal judgment predict sensitivity to necessity (would E have happened without C?) and robustness/sufficiency (would C still lead to E under slightly different background conditions?). Luck undermines robustness.
- Intro, "Other Recent Accounts": the know-how thesis (Pavese & Henne) and the probability-raising thesis (Ericson et al., Doan et al.) are presented as consistent with, and subsumed by, the causal account.
- Overview: Study 1 uses Knobe's expert and novice marksmen. Study 2 tests the Kraemer means/ends effect and gives partial support. Study 3 predicts a new interaction between know-how and probability-raising. Data and code are on OSF.
- General Discussion and Conclusion: luck effects on intentionality mirror luck effects on causal judgment across contexts. One divergence appeared in the morally neutral Kraemer scenario. The folk concept of intentional action is "externalist", like the folk concept of knowledge (Gettier cases).

## Open questions

- Connects the concept of agency to counterfactual and causal-strength models (necessity and robustness). This is relevant wherever intentionality or responsibility is attributed to automated agents.
- Check the effect sizes and the item-level correlations (Fig. 6) to see how much causal judgment adds beyond know-how and probability-raising.
- Check the Study 2 divergence, which may be where the account is weakest.
