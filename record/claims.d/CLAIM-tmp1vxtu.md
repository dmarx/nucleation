# CLAIM-tmp1vxtu: The split of the last eukaryotic common ancestor's central carbon metabolism, archaeal enzymes in the cytosol and alphaproteobacterial ones in the mitochondrion, does not single out syntrophy: any origin in which an archaeal host took up a bacterium with a TCA cycle predicts it, including those where the host was already complex and engulfed the bacterium

## The claim

Santana-Molina and colleagues ([THEORY-1109](../theory.d/THEORY-1109.md), as read in [NOTE-1239](../notes.d/NOTE-1239.md)) find Asgard-archaeal enzymes mainly in cytosolic glycolysis and the pentose phosphate pathway, and alphaproteobacterial ones mainly in the mitochondrial TCA cycle and pyruvate conversion. Any model in which an archaeal host took up a bacterium that brought a TCA cycle predicts that split, whether the partnership began in syntrophy or by engulfment by an already complex host. The authors themselves list what would discriminate the models, "the types of metabolites exchanged, ... the timing of mitochondrial acquisition, the mechanism of mitochondrial uptake", and test none of them.

## What it does not say

Not that syntrophy is wrong. The claim is that this evidence does not tell it from its rivals; the syntrophic thesis is [THEORY-1109](../theory.d/THEORY-1109.md)'s wider form, Proposed.
