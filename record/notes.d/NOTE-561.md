# NOTE-561: Double-Loop Learning, Teaching, and Research

## Contribution

A teaching method (role-playing a failed executive who asks for help) and a
proposal to treat it as a quasi-experiment testing the theory of action.
For this record, its value is a compact first-hand statement of that
theory.

## Key insight

An organization can correct errors indefinitely without ever being able to
question the values that define what counts as an error. The reasoning that
protects those values from embarrassment also hides that they are being
protected.

## Assumptions

- Theories-in-use can be inferred from observed conversation and scored
  reliably (Table 1; a 1965 scoring procedure is cited for inter-rater
  agreement).
- Model I is near-universal, "regardless of gender, race, culture,
  education, wealth, and type of organization", on the strength of
  Argyris's earlier studies.

## Key results

- Definitions of single- and double-loop learning (introduction).
- The Model I governing variables and their consequences: defensiveness,
  misunderstanding, self-fulfilling and self-sealing processes (Part II).
- The four-step logic of organizational defensive routines (Part II).
- Model II's governing values and productive reasoning (Part II).
- The Andy case. The CEOs gave abstract, untestable advice, attributed
  closedness to Andy without testing it, and blamed him as he blamed his
  board (Part I).
- Hypotheses for a quasi-experiment, including the claim that "one
  exception serves to disconfirm the hypothesis" (Part III).

## Claims

|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Single- and double-loop learning are distinct | strong | definitional |
| C2 | Model I is the near-universal theory-in-use | weak | asserted from the author's earlier work; not shown here |

## Method

Facilitated role-play of a published case ("Andy"; Ciampa & Watkins 1999)
with 34 CEOs, recorded and transcribed. The facilitator intervenes when the
dialogue becomes self-reinforcing, then has participants reflect on their
feelings and attributions.

## Concepts

- **governing values (variables)** — what an action strategy is designed to
  keep within acceptable ranges, such as control or winning.
- **theory-in-use** — the design that actually produces action, as opposed
  to the espoused theory.
- **skilled incompetence** — counterproductive action produced fluently,
  without awareness.
- **organizational defensive routine** — a practice that prevents
  embarrassment and the discovery of its causes.
- **productive reasoning** — explicit premises and inferences, conclusions
  testable by independent logic.

## Connections

Restates Argyris & Schön (1974, 1996) and Argyris, Putnam & Smith (1985).
It uses Campbell & Stanley's quasi-experimental design and White's
competence concept. The article-length statement usually cited, the 1977
*HBR* paper ([LIT-739](../literature.d/LIT-739.md)), is unread.

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-145](../theory.d/THEORY-145.md). For the owner's essay:

- **§8.3** describes double-loop learning: the essay's "procedures for
  reconsidering the standards through which policies acquire authority"
  are Argyris's change in governing values.
- **§8.5** says recursive revision does not guarantee normative adequacy.
  Argyris explains a specific failure: the revision procedure itself runs
  on Model I, so the values it should question are undiscussable.
- **§8.2 (Longino)** names uptake of criticism as a norm. Argyris
  describes the mechanism that defeats uptake, criticism that is neither
  illustrated nor testable, met with attributions that are never tested.
- **Rasmussen ([THEORY-144](../theory.d/THEORY-144.md))** has defences eroding unobserved. Argyris
  adds that the observation can be blocked socially as well as
  technically: the inconsistency is made undiscussable.

## Limitations

The evidence is the author's own workshop, interpreted by him, and claims
of universality rest on earlier work not presented. The proposed
quasi-experiment is not reported as run. Argyris's statement that one
exception disconfirms makes the theory testable in principle; no test is
given here.

## Open questions

Whether Model I is as universal as claimed, measured by raters independent
of the theory's authors, and whether Model II learning, once acquired,
persists in organizations whose routines remain Model I.
