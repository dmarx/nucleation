# THEORY-tmpddd8b: Ventromedial prefrontal cortex stores links between kinds of situation and the body states they produced, and re-enacting those states when an option is imagined marks it good or bad, overtly as a feeling or covertly as a bias, pruning the options before reasoning weighs them; without the marks, choice under uncertainty fails despite intact knowledge and intellect

## Source

Damasio (1996), [LIT-384](../literature.d/LIT-384.md) (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. B* 351: 1413–1420),
read in full in [NOTE-332](../notes.d/NOTE-332.md). Bechara, Damasio, Tranel and Damasio (1997),
[LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md) (*Science* 275: 1293–1295), read in full in [NOTE-331](../notes.d/NOTE-331.md). [NOTE-332](../notes.d/NOTE-332.md)
says a THEORY should not rest on the 1996 paper alone, because it reports
its evidence secondhand; [LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md) is the primary report it previews.

## What was actually shown

**The hypothesis ([LIT-384](../literature.d/LIT-384.md), §2b).** Ventromedial prefrontal cortex holds
"dispositional" links between a class of situation and the emotional state
that went with it. When the situation recurs, the link reactivates the
state, through the body ("body loop") or directly in somatosensory cortex
("as if body loop"). The re-enacted state, co-displayed with the imagined
scenario, "marks the scenario as good or bad" (p. 1415): overtly as an alarm
or incentive, covertly as a bias. The marks let some option-outcome pairs
be "rapidly rejected or endorsed", which makes reasoning tractable. The
paper says this is "not ... a general theory for how prefrontal cortices
work" (p. 1414).

**The evidence the 1996 paper summarises (§5), without statistics.**
Ventromedial patients keep IQ, knowledge, language, attention and working
memory and still decide badly in life. They give no skin conductance
responses (SCRs) to emotionally charged pictures, though their SCRs to
startle are normal. In the gambling task (44 controls against 6 patients)
controls drift to the decks with smaller rewards and smaller penalties and
patients do not.

**The 1997 experiment ([LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md), [NOTE-331](../notes.d/NOTE-331.md)).** 10 controls and 6 patients.

- All controls and no patients generated anticipatory SCRs: Fisher's exact
  test, one-sided P < 0.001; group × period F(1,14) = 16.24, P < 0.001
  (note 8). This is the robust result.
- Three patients who reached the stage of stating the right strategy still
  did not switch to the good decks.
- Before the "hunch" stage, controls' preference for the good decks was
  not significant (note 9). It became significant once they reported a
  hunch.
- The pre-hunch SCR effect reported is a group-by-period interaction. No
  bad-deck against good-deck test is reported for that period, and deck
  differences were not significant later either (note 8).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Patients could have developed
anticipatory SCRs and still chosen badly, or controls could have chosen
well without them. Neither happened in these samples.

## What this does not say

- **That the marker causes the choice.** SCRs index a body state. Nothing
  measured shows that it marks options or drives selection ([NOTE-332](../notes.d/NOTE-332.md),
  Limitations). The 1997 model is stated, not tested ([NOTE-331](../notes.d/NOTE-331.md), C7).
- **That people choose well before they know.** The statistics carry
  "before the articulated strategy", not "before any awareness". The
  awareness probe is coarse and starts at card 20 ([NOTE-331](../notes.d/NOTE-331.md), C3–C5).
- **That the deficit is specifically somatic.** The reversed-task evidence
  against reward hypersensitivity and punishment insensitivity is a
  conference abstract (C8). Response inhibition and reversal learning are
  set aside by argument, not experiment.
- **That the body is needed.** The "as if" loop bypasses the body, and the
  author believes it is the usual adult mode, on no evidence offered (C6).
  An argument from this account to "affect needs a body" would have to rest
  on the developmental claim that the body loop came first.
- **That it survives its critics.** Maia and McClelland (2004) and the later
  critiques of the gambling task are not held in the record and were not
  read.
