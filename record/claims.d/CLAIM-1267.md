# CLAIM-1267: Pathological regimes need no moral duties

## The claim

The owner's words, at U14, continuing the proposal of [CLAIM-1293](CLAIM-1293.md): "it
mightnot make sense to describe these processes as having moral duties to
each other precisely, but these systems of processes absolutely can
enterpathological regimes of incompatible behavior requiring intervention
and reorganization of commitments to resolve systemic frustration."

Two parts:

- **A denial, hedged.** It may not make sense to say processes have moral
  duties to one another "precisely". The vocabulary of contracts and
  standing ([CLAIM-1293](CLAIM-1293.md)) is not thereby a vocabulary of moral
  obligation.
- **An assertion, unhedged.** Systems of processes "absolutely can" enter
  pathological regimes of incompatible behaviour, and resolving their
  systemic frustration takes intervention and a reorganization of
  commitments, not only better performance under the commitments they
  have.

## Where the record stands

- Frustration is held: [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md) (the demands an organization maintains
  failing to be jointly realizable) and [CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md) (frustration is ordinal
  first, the jointly satisfiable sets forming a complex). [CLAIM-357](CLAIM-357.md)
  separates structural infeasibility from frustration the governance
  induces; [CLAIM-556](CLAIM-556.md) says dropping a demand is not relief and orders the
  repairs. The claim's "reorganization of commitments" is the repair by
  changing the constraints, and its "pathological regime" a persisting
  configuration of that kind.
- That functional organization does not establish moral standing or
  obligation is held: [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) (viability does not establish what ought
  to persist) and [CLAIM-725](CLAIM-725.md) (a demand's satisfaction condition does not
  carry its warrant). The claim adds that the pathology, and its remedy,
  can be stated without that warrant.

## What A16 made of it

A16 §7 writes the jointly compliant histories as the realizable histories
intersected with every applicable guarantee, and sorts the sources of an
empty set (inconsistent guarantees, insufficient resources, incorrect
assumptions, inadequate scheduling, monitoring, coordination or corrective
capability), each with its own intervention; it separates "Has the
organization become capable of satisfying more of its original
commitments?" from "Has the organization appropriately revised which
commitments it maintains?". A16 §16: "A scheduler may systematically
starve a process. That can constitute a functional pathology without
constituting an injustice to the process itself", and "The relationships
in the two domains can share organizational structure, but the first does
not establish the second." [CASE-427](../cases.d/CASE-427.md) is a minimal regime of this kind,
and [CLAIM-1345](CLAIM-1345.md) A's catalogue of them.

## What it does not say

- It does not say that no system of processes can bear moral duties;
  the hedge is "precisely".
- It does not say that every pathological regime is resolved by
  reorganizing commitments; some need resources or new capacities, which
  A16 §7 lists beside renegotiation.
