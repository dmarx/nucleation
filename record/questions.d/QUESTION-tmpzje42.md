# QUESTION-tmpzje42: Can an empirical model that genuinely fails to glue, under a specified cover and correspondence between its contexts, take part in one phenomenally unified process, given that a unified experience can contain conflicting interpretations or demands and that such conflicts are not by themselves gluing obstructions?

## The question

Take interpretive contexts (vision, touch, interoception, memory, action
readiness) as a cover. Each context carries local interpretive states,
which may or may not agree on overlaps (A7 §10). One answer: an
experience is unified when the local states glue into a global section.
A7 rejected it: "a system can have unified experience while maintaining
incompatible interpretations. A person might simultaneously want to
approach and avoid something", and "the conflict itself can be a unified
experience". Its alternative: "Unity = A realized organization of local
interpretations and their interactions", "a process that *maintains,
negotiates, and responds to* incompatible interpretations."

A7's example does not show what it was taken to show. Wanting to approach
and avoid the same thing is a conflict, but a conflict is not by itself a
gluing obstruction. A global state z = (desire to approach, desire to
avoid) holds both desires with no inconsistency, since the two are not
incompatible assignments to one common observable. A richer global state
space can represent a conflict whose demands cannot be jointly met. So
three things must be kept apart:

1. **Conflicts that glue.** Opposed interpretations or demands that one
   global state represents together. Most felt conflicts may be of this
   kind.
2. **Genuine non-gluing.** An empirical model with no global section under
   a cover and a correspondence between contexts that are fixed
   independently of the verdict, as [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) and [CLAIM-143](../claims.d/CLAIM-143.md) require.
3. **Phenomenal unity.** Whatever makes an experience one.

The question is whether (2) can take part in (3): whether a process whose
local interpretations genuinely fail to glue can still be one unified
experience. A unified experience of conflict does not answer it, unless
the conflict is shown to be of kind (2).
The two readings come apart both ways. A7: "A globally consistent assignment could
exist in a merely descriptive model without any corresponding unified
process. Conversely, a genuinely unified process could contain locally
incompatible states." A6, of Lee-Youngzie et al. ([LIT-tmphb8p5](../literature.d/LIT-tmphb8p5.md)):
"compatible local sections in a specified mathematical model need not
establish that there exists a conscious subject corresponding to their
union. Nor does failure of a global section automatically imply the
absence of experience."

## Why it is a question

The record already uses the global-section reading for a self.
[CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md) (Active): "A global section, one coherent self, exists exactly
when the monodromy Φ around a cycle vanishes." [CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md), [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md)
and [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md) place disunity on overlaps: preference reversal "is not yet
disunity" until a self-prediction creates an overlap. Those claims are
about whether a self holds together across contexts, not about the unity
of one experience. If a genuine gluing failure on an overlap, kind (2),
can be part of one unified experience, then [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s coherent self and
phenomenal unity are different unities. The tag vocabulary already keeps
four unities apart ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)), but no claim says where a gluing failure
falls among them.

It also bears on [CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md), whose unity is a property of a
process, and on Metzinger's "single functional cluster"
([THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)), which is a dynamic criterion, not a sheaf condition.

A7's decomposition of binding names what is at stake. Functional
binding: how distributed processes coordinate. Perspectival binding: when
coordinated processes constitute one frame. Phenomenal binding: "Why, or
whether, does that organization constitute the unity of conscious
experience?" A gluing condition is a candidate for the second, and the
question is whether it can be the third. [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) already records the
exchange of 2026-10-17's five bindings and says that it "does not solve
phenomenal binding".

## What would answer it

Either a showing that the unified experiences offered as cases of
non-gluing are, under the right cover, of kind (1) after all, as the
approach–avoid case may be; or an empirical model of kind (2), with its
cover and correspondence fixed independently, shown to belong to one
unified process, together with a formal object, beside the sheaf, for a process that holds
non-gluing sections together, with a reason to call it the unity of an
experience and not only a functional coupling. Isaac's dynamical
structures ([LIT-tmpwhbpm](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwhbpm.md)) and the record's frustration of maintained demands
([CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md)) are candidate materials for the second.

## Note of 2026-10-22: a candidate answer, and two constraints

A8 proposed the second alternative and gave it a dynamic form: "Unity
consists in jointly consequential interpretation, not necessarily in
agreement among interpretations." Incompatibilities δ_ij enter the update
of a common frame, z_{t+1} = H(z_t, {j_i}, {δ_ij}, v_t). A system keeps a
unified perspective "by preserving unresolved tensions, revising
interpretations, reallocating attention, or shifting its evaluative
priorities". A9 found in the record what any formal version of that must
respect:

- [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) and [CLAIM-143](../claims.d/CLAIM-143.md). A disagreement is a gluing failure only on an
  overlap, and only once the correspondence between the contexts'
  observations is fixed independently. "We cannot simply assign each
  neural module a local interpretation and treat disagreements as evidence
  of contextuality."
- [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s own scope. It models a self's consistency across contexts,
  and A9 noted that it "does not establish a general theory of
  psychological dissociation".
  A9: use sheaf tools "to study the structure of contextual compatibility,
  **not** identify global sections with conscious subjects".

A9 also tied the conflicts to the record's frustration apparatus. Jointly
unrealizable demands form a compatibility complex ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)), and its
minimal non-faces are the minimal conflicts. That is a candidate for the
formal object this question's second answer lacks, beside the sheaf: an
organization of incompatibility, not an absence of it. But infeasible
demands and failure of a global section are different mathematical
facts. A complex with a non-face says that some demands cannot be met
together. It does not say that their local descriptions fail to glue, and
a global state can describe an infeasible set of demands consistently.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the readings

**Lee-Youngzie et al.** ([NOTE-tmpfadjy](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfadjy.md), [THEORY-tmpo2jzk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo2jzk.md)) formalize binding,
not unity, and assume an experience is always unified. They read a sheaf
failure two ways, as disunity in binocular rivalry and as context
dependence inside one unified experience in the Ebbinghaus illusion, and
give no rule for telling the two apart. The reading checked their formal
content.
- The worked Alexandroff topology is wrong as printed.
- By the paper's own gluing definition, rivalry is not a sheaf failure.
- Restriction maps that match trials by label make gluing always succeed,
  so the test has content only as an empirical consistency radius on
  measured data.
- "Global section" there means measured data, not an experience or a
  subject.

The paper gives no support for "unity is a global section".

**A candidate for kind (2).** The paper's Penrose tribar is a presheaf of
local interpretations, compatible at each junction, with no global
section. Yet the figure is seen as one object. If the cover (the
junctions) and the correspondence are accepted as fixed independently,
that is a genuine non-gluing model taking part in one unified percept,
which is what this question asks about. The inference is the record's,
not the paper's. "Seen as one" is report, and whether the percept is
unified phenomenally or only categorized as one object is open. Its
obstruction needs a cycle of three junctions, as [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s does.

**Coherence views.** Laukkonen et al. ([THEORY-tmpw19hw](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpw19hw.md)) and IWMT
([THEORY-tmpopaql](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpopaql.md)) take the coherence side. Laukkonen et al. treat
incoherence as a loss of awareness. Neither addresses a felt conflict.

**Isaac** ([LIT-tmpwhbpm](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwhbpm.md)), whose dynamical structures this question named
as material, could not be reached and is Unavailable.
