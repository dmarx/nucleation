# NOTE-1602: Marsden and Zwart — Quantitative foundations for resource theories

## Contribution

"We present a categorical foundation for quantitative resource theories,
derived from enriched category theory" (abstract). The framework is
parametric twice: in the quantity, by the choice of quantale, and in the
structural features of resources (order, copying, deleting), by a hierarchy
of free constructions. It separates free conversions (the base
categories) from costly ones (profunctors between them).

## Key insight

A quantale is the right algebra for costed conversion because it carries
both operations a path problem needs. "The monoid structure allows us to
combine quantities across the different steps of a process or algorithm,
for example costs, success probabilities or connection strengths", and
"The join semilattice structure is then an optimizer" (§2.1). Changing the
quantale changes the composition law and nothing else.

## Assumptions

- **Commutative quantales** (Def. 1): complete join-semilattices with a
  monoid ⊗ distributing over joins. Small Q-categories.
- **One quantale per theory**: all hom-values in one base, one composition
  law.
- **Static resources**: conversions are quantified, but there is no time,
  rate or state-dependent cost.
- **Proofs sketched** (Theorems 27, 28; Corollary 29).

## Key results

- **The aim** (§1): "In this paper we are interested not in quantifying
  the resources themselves, but in adding the ability to provide
  quantitative data about the conversions that can take place."
- **Against Coecke, Fritz & Spekkens** (§1): "Although some specific
  quantitative elements of resource theories are touched upon towards the
  end of [4], the approach is ad-hoc and no general purpose account of
  quantitative aspects is provided", and "They also fix the structural
  aspects of resources once and for all, rather than identifying this as a
  parameter of their theory."
- **The Lawvere quantale** (Example 4): "The Lawvere quantale L has
  underlying set the extended positive reals [0, ∞] with the join
  semilattice structure given by infima and the monoid structure given by
  addition of real numbers." For an L-category, "A(a, b) can be seen as the
  cost of converting a to b" (Example 9), and "from a resource conversion
  perspective it is entirely natural that two distinct resources could be
  interconvertible."
- **A weakest-link law** (Example 8): "Connection strengths are valued in
  a worst case manner, a composite connection is only as good as its
  weakest link."
- **Q-categories and profunctors** (Defs. 6, 15, 18): k ≤ A(a, a) and
  A(b, c) ⊗ A(a, b) ≤ A(a, c); a profunctor composite is the join over
  intermediates of ⊗.
- **Free constructions** (Theorems 27–28, Corollary 29): monoidal,
  symmetric, deleting, copying and cartesian, with distributive laws over
  the free cocompletion, giving comonads on Prof(Q) whose co-Kleisli
  categories model many-to-one conversions.
- **Closure** (§3; Proposition 37, Corollary 38): "From the point of view of
  resources, an internal monad captures closure under repeated application
  of the available conversions", and "Every endomorphism in our categories
  of interest can be used to construct a free internal monad using the
  reflexive transitive closure construction of proposition 37."
- **A pointer** (§1): "there is clearly a link with Pavlovic’s quantitative
  formal concept analysis [27] that should be explored."

## Concepts

- **Quantale** (Def. 1); deleting, copying and cartesian quantales
  (Def. 25).
- **Q-enriched category** (Def. 6); **Q-profunctor** (Def. 15) and its
  composite (Def. 18).
- **Internal monad** (Def. 22): 1 ⊆ R, R ∘ R ⊆ R. **Bimodule** (Def. 33),
  for freely available multi-conversions. **Free monad** over R (Def. 36),
  the join of the powers Rⁿ.

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization** (Response 3, §2). A: "This is precisely the
  territory of monoidal resource theories and enriched categories.
  Lawvere-style metric enrichment gives the familiar additive shortest-path
  model, while more general ordered monoidal structures allow different
  composition laws." These are A's sentences, not the paper's. Right: the
  paper is that combination; the Lawvere quantale with + and the free
  internal monad give least-cost paths. With a precision A lacks: the
  structures must be quantales, so that joins exist and ⊗ distributes over
  them.
- **Costed composition comes from here, not from Coecke, Fritz &
  Spekkens.** In their framework morphisms are free and carry no cost
  ([NOTE-1591](NOTE-1591.md)); attaching costs to conversions and composing them is
  this paper's quantale enrichment, and Lawvere's.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md)** (threshold = infimum over paths of the peak along the
  path): supports. *The reader's check:* ([0, ∞], inf, max) satisfies
  Definition 1, since max(a, inf_i b_i) = inf_i max(a, b_i). Max along a
  path with the infimum over paths is then this paper's closure
  (Proposition 37) in that quantale, and gives the claim's B* exactly. The
  peak-capacity law is not among the paper's examples (+, min, ×).
- **[CLAIM-585](../claims.d/CLAIM-585.md)** (a Pareto frontier of sufficient budgets): supports,
  and shows why one product quantale is not enough. *The reader's:* a
  product of two quantales is a quantale, but its join is the
  componentwise infimum, which no single path need achieve; for the
  claim's two paths with peaks (3, 1) and (1, 3) it gives (1, 1), which
  suffices for neither. The hom-object must be an up-set of budgets (a
  quantale of up-sets, joined by union) to keep the frontier. Neither A's
  pair law (max, +) nor the paper notes this.
- **A's b-affordable processes.** A: "Thus the collection of b-affordable
  processes need not itself be a category under ordinary composition."
  Correct, and not in this paper. *The reader's derivation:* the morphisms
  costing at most b compose exactly when b ⊗ b ≤ b. That holds for max,
  and for + only when b is 0 or ∞, which is A's "two processes costing at
  most b each may together cost 2b".
- **A's "resource-graded process category, where composing morphisms also
  composes their resource grades".** Not this paper's notion. It matches a
  graded (locally graded) category, with C_x(a, b) × C_y(b, c) →
  C_{x⊗y}(a, c) (Wood, 1976 and 1978, as reported by E. Patterson's
  Topos Institute blog post on graded categories as double functors,
  2025-05-01; Wood not read), and Lawvere's normed category with
  |f| + |g| ≥ |fg| ([NOTE-1585](NOTE-1585.md)). The enriched view keeps only the best
  grade between two objects; the graded view keeps every morphism with its
  grade, which is what A needs when "Budgets then select affordable
  morphisms from that larger structure." A's use matches graded
  categories, not graded monads.
- **Replenishable resources.** A: "For replenishable resources, the cost
  may even depend on the order of operations." Not covered by quantale
  enrichment, which assumes a context-free ⊗; consistent with A's remedy of
  tracking reserves in the state. Bears the same way on **[TERM-105](../terms.d/TERM-105.md)**:
  capacities and cumulative expenditures are quantale bases; reserves and
  deadlines with rates are not context-free laws. Qualifies.
- **[LIT-803](../literature.d/LIT-803.md)** (Seven Sketches §2.5): the textbook account of quantales and
  matrix-multiplication closure; supports, the same mathematics.
- **[LIT-845](../literature.d/LIT-845.md), [LIT-846](../literature.d/LIT-846.md)** (the contextuality resource theory): another
  instance of a resource theory; unrelated in subject.
- Filed: [THEORY-1435](../theory.d/THEORY-1435.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The authors'.** Multi-input-to-multi-output conversions (polycategories)
  left for later; no syntax: "We have focused on models." (§8); proofs
  sketched.
- **Scope.** No dynamics, state-dependent cost or approximation; one
  composition law per theory.
