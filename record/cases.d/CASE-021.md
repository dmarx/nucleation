# CASE-021: Wording, attributed speaker and judgement order: the proposed factorial study

## The case

A21 proposed randomising translations and the attributed speaker (fellow
reveller, moralist, social critic) and eliciting condemnation, solidarity,
amusement and irony in varied order, then comparing a classical latent-variable
model with a quantum one "rather than merely fitting additional parameters".
A24 made it "a factorial study manipulating **wording, attributed speaker
identity, and judgment context/order**, with our Henley translations as
stimuli", comparing "conventional hierarchical probability models, sequential
state-update models, and quantum-probabilistic models, with formal contextuality
tests where the measurement design permits them".

## What it can show

If run, it bears on [CLAIM-052](../claims.d/CLAIM-052.md) (order effects), [CLAIM-009](../claims.d/CLAIM-009.md) (contextuality), [CLAIM-091](../claims.d/CLAIM-091.md)
(wording compensating for speaker) and [CLAIM-005](../claims.d/CLAIM-005.md). It has not been run. The
manuscript's §10 Case I is its descendant, without the order factor, which
moved to Case II's alternating frames.
