# CLAIM-tmpygs06: A platform for institutional alignment must govern development

## The design objective

A15 (https://chatgpt.com/share/6b07d7ae-9434-83ea-888b-9b20796f291c):

> **Enable an institution to develop new collective capacities without
> losing the ability of its legitimate constituents to govern the
> conditions under which those capacities are exercised.**

It "should be applicable to a corporation, a scientific discipline, a
public institution, an open-source community, or a human–AI collective.
AI would be one possible participant, not the organizing assumption." Its
positive conception of alignment is [SYNTHESIS-121](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-121.md)'s harmony ([TERM-162](../terms.d/TERM-162.md)):
"viability, accommodation, and correctability, with constituent demands
retaining access to consequential organizational roles". Its commitments
replace "The institution has an objective" with "The institution
organizes heterogeneous demands", "Performance demonstrates capability"
with "Present performance and future reorganization capacity are
separate", and "Governance constrains development" with "Governance must
itself be capable of development".

## The rest of A15

- **A negative specification**: the platform must not become "A universal
  surveillance infrastructure", "An automated moral authority", "A
  mechanism for converting all human concerns into commensurable scores",
  or "An optimization system that treats all institutional friction as
  inefficiency", since "Some friction constitutes protection. Some
  redundancy constitutes readiness."
- **A data model** of eighteen object types that "should not all share the
  same lifecycle": "A right is not adopted or retired in the same sense as
  a software recommendation." That is Luria's rule for splitting schemes
  ([CLAIM-tmpoh8gm](CLAIM-tmpoh8gm.md)), reached from the other side.
- **Four stages**: institutional memory, answerability, governance of
  possibility, reflexive development. "Interestingly, we would probably end
  up inventing something resembling Luria during the first stage. But we
  would regard it as one component of a much larger institutional
  architecture." This is A's estimate of Luria's scope against the
  account: one stage of four.
- **A formal criterion**, an "authority-sensitive viability kernel", which
  is [TERM-167](../terms.d/TERM-167.md) indexed by an authorized coalition; the note of 2026-11-26 on
  [TERM-167](../terms.d/TERM-167.md) corrects it.
- **A battery of tests**, [LEAD-tmpl9fm6](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpl9fm6.md).

## Admission (ADR-075; the synthesis review of 2026-11-26, the assistant)

*What it adds.* Its premises state mechanisms and, in places, safeguards:
standing that constrains what may be optimized (CLAIM-1252), a metric's two
powers and their separation (CLAIM-1334), the optimizer's interest in
eroding its constraints (CLAIM-1287), the amendable grammar (CLAIM-419),
legibility as a surface for domination (CLAIM-1251), attention revenue against
investigation (CLAIM-656), penalized discovery (CLAIM-1064), reliance holding
obligations (CLAIM-1326) and governed schema change (CLAIM-667). None
states a design objective, and none says that one apparatus must carry
the functions they imply. The claim adds both: an objective, and the
necessity of fourteen requirements under it, with the rule against an
aggregate score derived from CLAIM-1064's outcome ladder. So it is not an
account filed as a claim.

*Route: a separating case, realistic.* Every premise holds, and an
institution meets the design objective with a platform that carries only
some of the requirements, memory, answerability and an amendable grammar,
say, while others are carried outside it: inquiry funded by a body
independent of the criteria it questions (R11), protected opacity secured
by law (R13), dependence governed by a regulator or by contract (R10).
The premises say what must be kept from failing, not where; the record's
own pattern is a division of normative labour, a specialized objective
legitimate only so far as the outside governance it relies on is adequate
(CLAIM-1252), and A15 itself expects the platform to be "one component of
a much larger institutional architecture". The case is not stipulated
and could plainly occur.

*Checked against the claim's own defeater.* Its second clause, that the
record's account "does not imply one of them, so that it is A's design
preference", is met for each requirement that can be carried outside the
platform: the account implies that the function is carried somewhere in
the governing arrangement, not by the platform. So the case separates the
claim from its premises and, as worded, defeats it in part. The narrower
reading survives: the requirements bind the governing arrangement the
platform is part of, and which of them the platform itself carries is a
design choice, with R14 (the platform answerable for itself) binding the
platform in any case. Read so, the separating case no longer defeats it,
and the claim still adds what its premises lack, the objective and the
joint necessity. Revising the statement to that reading is the owner's
call.

*A free parameter, closed.* "Legitimate constituents" would absorb every
case if left open; the claim does not need it settled, since no
requirement's acceptance test turns on who is a constituent except R2 and
R5, which inherit the answer QUESTION-481 and QUESTION-485 are asked for.
The test above holds for any answer.

adopting it is the owner's call.

## Note of 2026-11-26: two tensions the requirements leave open (the assistant)

Gaps.

1. **R13 against R4 and R6.** R4's test asks the platform to detect that
   an overseer is "materially dependent on the activity it oversees",
   which needs the overseer's finances and ties to be legible; R13 asks it
   not to "pursue universal organizational legibility". A names the
   tension in general ([CLAIM-1251](CLAIM-1251.md), the owner's) and does not resolve the
   case its own tests create. A resolution consistent with both: opacity
   protects persons in spheres an authority has no jurisdiction over, and
   legibility is required of offices in the exercise of the authority they
   hold. The overseer's dependence is a fact about an office, so R4 wins
   there; a constituent's exercise of a protected freedom is not, so R13
   wins there. The line between the two is itself a jurisdiction, and so
   falls under R3 and R12.
2. **R14 has no outside.** "Every requirement above must apply
   recursively to the platform" requires something that governs the
   platform, and A's acceptance test answers it only by exit: the
   institution can "govern, replace, or decline the platform without losing
   the capacities it has come to depend on". Exit is R10's remedy applied
   to the platform, so R14 is met only so far as R10 is met for the
   platform's own maintainers ([CLAIM-1326](CLAIM-1326.md): reliance holds obligations past
   their original reasons). It is not a regress, but it makes portability
   the requirement on which the others' recursion depends.

The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.
