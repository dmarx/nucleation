# LIT-494: The Theory of Dyadic Morality: Reinventing Moral Judgment by Redefining Harm

Chelsea Schein, Kurt Gray (2018), *Personality and Social Psychology
Review* 22(1), 32–70; online 14 May 2017 — DOI-10.1177/1088868317698288

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 in the morality-as-cooperation batch ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)), as
the harm-based rival. No anthology topic holds it, and by its abstract it
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.

`Deferred` because no full text could be read (see the status note), so no
NOTE is filed. What is known, to scope it until somebody reads it:

- **From the abstract.** Harm is redefined as "an intuitively perceived
  continuum". The constructionist Theory of Dyadic Morality (TDM) holds that
  acts are condemned in proportion to three elements, norm violations,
  negative affect and perceived harm. Perceived harm is dyadic, an
  intentional agent causing damage to a vulnerable patient. Causation runs
  both ways: harm to immorality ("dyadic comparison") and immorality to
  harm ("dyadic completion"), together the "dyadic loop", which is offered
  to explain moral acquisition and polarization. TDM "argues against
  intuitive harmless wrongs and modular 'foundations', but embraces moral
  pluralism through varieties of values and the flexibility of perceived
  harm".
- **From readings in this batch.** Graham et al. 2013 ([LIT-499](LIT-499.md))
  answer the earlier version of the theory (Gray, Young & Waytz 2012) as
  harm monism that cannot explain disgust's effects. The MFQ-2 paper
  ([LIT-500](LIT-500.md)) lists Gray and Schein as monists. Bloom's commentary on
  [LIT-047](LIT-047.md) (read in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md)) cites Gray, Schein and Ward 2014 for the view
  that sexual morality reflects intuitions about harm. Fitouchi, André and
  Baumard ([LIT-101](LIT-101.md), read in [NOTE-132](../notes.d/NOTE-132.md)) cite Schein et al. 2016 for the
  finding that disgust–condemnation correlations vanish once perceived harm
  is controlled for.

**Why it matters to morality-as-cooperation.** It is the third answer to
Bloom's purity and sex objection, beside the cooperative answers (the
Reply in [LIT-047](LIT-047.md); [LIT-101](LIT-101.md); relationship regulation, [LIT-507](LIT-507.md)): purity
and sex violations are condemned because they are perceived as harmful.
It also bears on Bloom's battery point, since it puts harm, not
cooperation, at the centre. Whether TDM's "perceived harm" is a rival to a
cooperative function, or a proximate mechanism compatible with one, cannot
be said without reading it.

What a reading should check: how "perceived harm" is measured, so that it
is not simply condemnation restated; the evidence for dyadic completion;
and whether the 2018 statement engages morality-as-cooperation or
mutualism at all.
