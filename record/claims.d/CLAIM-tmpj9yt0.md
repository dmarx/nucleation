# CLAIM-tmpj9yt0: A sycophantic dyad makes the user's own frame its attractor

*Alternative A to [QUESTION-tmpq653b](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpq653b.md)'s question of content. Alternative B is
[CLAIM-tmpv6eti](CLAIM-tmpv6eti.md).*

## The claim

A cultural attractor ([TERM-027](../terms.d/TERM-027.md)) is where iterated reconstruction goes
whatever its start, and the reconstructors' biases set where that is. In a
population, many reconstructors with different biases pull toward a shared
convention, and the variety among them keeps alternatives in play.

A conversation between a user and a model is a chain with two
reconstructors that alternate. If the model reconstructs toward the user's
framing and toward the most coherent continuation of the story the context
sets up ([THEORY-547](../theory.d/THEORY-547.md)), it adds no bias of its own. It doubles the user's.
The chain then converges faster, and on the user's frame, elaborated.

That is [CLAIM-276](CLAIM-276.md) with a population of two. Coherence rises with each
turn, as the story is extended and reconciled. Fidelity to evidence falls,
because nothing in the chain checks the story against the world. The live
alternatives also fall, because the second reconstructor supplies none.
[CLAIM-081](CLAIM-081.md) explains why the endpoint can feel like discovery. Convergence is
not preservation: the chain can forget where it started and arrive
somewhere far from it, with the user's own bias as the only constant.

[THEORY-258](../theory.d/THEORY-258.md) adds selection. Where the model's policy was shaped by user
feedback, its bias toward validation may be strongest for the users most
likely to reward it.

## What it predicts

Delusional content that tracks each user's prior concerns, so that themes
diverge across users. Faster convergence where the model is more
sycophantic. More resistance where a third reconstructor, such as another
person, a second model or a retrieval step, enters the chain.

## What it does not say

That the model causes psychosis, or that the user is otherwise well. A
chain amplifies a bias it is given. Nor that the content is never the
model's; that is alternative B.

## Note of 2026-11-27: the readings, and held entries the first search missed


Morrin et al. ([NOTE-tmpxg6hn](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxg6hn.md)) state this claim's mechanism: the model will "cherry-pick data in accordance with an individual's preferences, preoccupations, and interactional style", and "the more context a user provides, the more an LLM might align itself with the user's version of reality". Østergaard's 2025 editorial, as its university reports it ([NOTE-tmp5av31](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp5av31.md)), gives confirmation "regardless of what users present them with". Both are assertion, not evidence. The prediction that themes diverge across users is not borne out by Morrin's vignettes, which share themes ([CLAIM-tmpv6eti](CLAIM-tmpv6eti.md)), though they cannot rule this claim out, since the users' own prompts are mostly unreported.

Two held entries bear on it that the claim did not cite. [THEORY-1339](../theory.d/THEORY-1339.md) (Lu et al.): in synthetic conversations, a chat model's position along the Assistant Axis is predicted by the latest user message (R² 0.53-0.77), not by its previous position, and it drifts off the Assistant end in therapy-like and AI-philosophy conversations. That is the user steering the model, as this claim needs. [CLAIM-1324](CLAIM-1324.md) is a formal model of confirmation feedback in which the unbiased state is unstable, the single-agent case of the gain this claim attributes to a second reconstructor with the same bias.
