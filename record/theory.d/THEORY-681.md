# THEORY-681: If every real idea derives from an impression, there is no idea of a simple self persisting through life; the mind is a bundle of perceptions, and the identity ascribed to it is produced by the imagination passing easily along resemblance and causation, as with a rebuilt ship or a growing oak, though Hume's Appendix concedes that this does not explain what unites the perceptions

## Source

Hume (1739), *A Treatise of Human Nature* 1.4.6, "Of personal identity",
with the Appendix to vol. III (1740), App. 10–21, [LIT-451](../literature.d/LIT-451.md), read in the Hume
Texts Online critical text in [NOTE-386](../notes.d/NOTE-386.md). Paragraph numbers are the Norton
numbering.

## What was actually shown

**No idea of self (1.4.6.1–3).** "It must be some one impression, that
gives rise to every real idea" (1.4.6.2), and no impression is constant and
invariable through life. "Consequently there is no such idea." Introspection
finds only perceptions: "I never can catch myself at any time without a
perception, and never can observe any thing but the perception"
(1.4.6.3). Someone who perceives "something simple and continu'd" may be
right "as well as I", but "we are essentially different in this
particular".

**The bundle (1.4.6.4).** Mankind are "nothing but a bundle or collection
of different perceptions, which succeed each other with an inconceivable
rapidity". The theatre image is withdrawn: "They are the successive
perceptions only, that constitute the mind".

**The mechanism (1.4.6.6–14).** The idea of one invariable object and the
idea of a succession of related objects are distinct, but the acts of the
imagination that survey them are "almost the same to the feeling", so we
"feign" a soul, self or substance "to disguise the variation" (1.4.6.6).
The cases: a mass changed a little, a ship repaired plank by plank, an oak
with no particle the same, a church rebuilt in stone, a river that changes
all its parts within a day (1.4.6.8–14).

**Persons (1.4.6.15–21).** "The identity, which we ascribe to the mind of
man, is only a fictitious one" (1.4.6.15). Memory produces resemblance among
perceptions; causation links them like "a republic or commonwealth"
(1.4.6.18–19). Because we extend our identity past what we remember, memory
"does not so much produce as discover personal identity" (1.4.6.20). Hard
cases have "no just standard" and "are to be regarded rather as grammatical
than as philosophical difficulties" (1.4.6.21).

**The Appendix (App. 10–21).** "I find myself involv'd in such a labyrinth,
that, I must confess, I neither know how to correct my former opinions, nor
how to render them consistent" (App. 10). "If perceptions are distinct
existences, they form a whole only by being connected together. But no
connexions among distinct existences are ever discoverable by human
understanding" (App. 20). "There are two principles, which I cannot render
consistent": that distinct perceptions are distinct existences, and that
the mind never perceives a real connexion among them (App. 21). He pleads
"the privilege of a sceptic" and does not call the difficulty "absolutely
insuperable".

**What could have come out otherwise.** Introspection could have turned up
a constant impression of self. Hume reports that it does not, and grants
that another may report otherwise.

## What this does not say

- **That there is no self in any sense.** The claim is that there is no idea
  of a simple, persisting self. The question is restricted to identity "as
  it regards our thought or imagination"; identity "as it regards our
  passions or the concern we take in ourselves" is left to Book 2
  (1.4.6.5).
- **That the mechanism is shown.** The cases show how we talk about ships
  and churches. That the same passage of the imagination produces belief in
  a self is argued by analogy (1.4.6.15) and from separability (1.4.6.16).
- **That the account of the connexion stands.** The Appendix withdraws it
  without saying which part of 1.4.6 fails. Commentators disagree about
  what exactly the labyrinth is, and the text does not settle it.
- **That memory makes the person.** Hume argues the reverse against a memory
  criterion: memory discovers identity, and causation extends it past what
  is remembered.
- **That the account is free of the subject it replaces.** "We feign" and
  "our propension" presuppose someone doing the feigning. The section does
  not address this ([NOTE-386](../notes.d/NOTE-386.md), Open questions).

## Connections

- **Locke** ([THEORY-546](THEORY-546.md)). Hume takes Locke's analogy between the
  identity of plants and animals and that of persons, holds it for both,
  and makes both fictitious. The Appendix's "most philosophers … personal
  identity arises from consciousness" is Locke's thesis.
- **Dennett** ([THEORY-691](THEORY-691.md)) ends on Hume's failure to find a self and
  answers it with an abstractum of interpretation. Hume's "fictitious"
  identity and Dennett's "theorist's fiction" both make the unity a product
  of a process that tells it, not a thing found; for Hume the teller is the
  imagination, for Dennett the brain's autobiography.
- **Floridi** ([THEORY-650](THEORY-650.md)) quotes the Appendix as the statement of his
  problem of the chariot, what binds a self's information into a unity.
- **Self-models.** Metzinger's account ([THEORY-064](THEORY-064.md)) and relational frame
  theory's self as content ([THEORY-044](THEORY-044.md)) cite Hume as an ancestor. Minsky's
  Single-Self as a convenient fiction ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md), [NOTE-348](../notes.d/NOTE-348.md)) is the bundle
  with an architecture.
- **A candidate connexion.** [NOTE-386](../notes.d/NOTE-386.md) reads Damasio's homeostatic feeling
  ([LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md)) as a candidate for the "real connexion" Hume could not find, one
  supplied by the body and not perceived among the perceptions. That
  reading is the filing reader's, not Damasio's.
- **Hume's commonwealth** (1.4.6.19) is used for persistence through change,
  not for mentality, so it does not bear on whether a commonwealth could be
  a mind ([THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)).
