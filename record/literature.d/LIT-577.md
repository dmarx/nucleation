# LIT-577: Sophisticated Inference

Karl Friston, Lancelot Da Costa, Danijar Hafner, Casper Hesp and Thomas
Parr (2021), *Neural Computation* 33(3):713–763; arXiv:2006.04120, 7 June
2020.

**Authors and year, verified.** The brief gave "Friston et al. (2021;
arXiv 2020)" and asked for a check. arXiv and Crossref agree on the five
authors in this order. The preprint appeared on 7 June 2020, which is the
`published:` date under the record's rule; the journal issue is March 2021.

## Key takeaways

- **The move.** "From simply considering beliefs about 'what would happen if
  I did that' to 'what would I believe about what would happen if I did
  that'." The expected free energy of an action is its own risk and
  ambiguity plus the average expected free energy of subsequent actions
  under the beliefs that each plausible outcome would produce.
- **A tree search over beliefs, pruned.** Paths are followed only while the
  action's prior probability and the outcome's predictive probability stay
  above a threshold (1/16 in the simulations). In the maze, horizons of four
  and six would mean about 1.05 × 10¹⁰ and 1.07 × 10¹⁵ paths; "usually
  several hundred" were evaluated.
- **Bellman, with beliefs.** The recursion is formally like the Bellman
  equation, but over functionals of beliefs rather than functions of states.
  In the zero-temperature limit it recovers Bayes-adaptive reinforcement
  learning; the difference claimed is that expected free energy also
  values information gain.
- **What the simulations show.** In a three-arm T-maze, the sophisticated
  agent visits the informative cue even when the cue is made mildly
  aversive; the one-step agent stays put. In an 8 × 8 maze, a planning
  horizon of four is needed to escape a local minimum behind an aversive
  barrier. With novelty, an agent with no location preferences covers
  nearly every location in 64 moves; a Bayesian-risk agent sits still.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, in the free-energy part of the
agency batch.

**The boundary.** The brief expected none of this batch to belong in the
anthology. This one might. It is an action-selection algorithm, evaluated
in simulated environments, positioned against reinforcement-learning
methods (Bellman, Q-learning, SARSA, Bayes-adaptive RL) and a deep-RL
baseline cited from Tschantz et al., with a co-author from Google Brain. The
anthology's `agents-and-environments` topic ("percept–action loops, world
models … what acting rather than only predicting changes about learning")
could hold it. Against that, the paper is presented as theoretical
neurobiology, its simulations are rats in mazes, and it carries no
instruction a practitioner would follow. It is filed here with the
`anthology-candidate` flag ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)), carried last, so the transfer
question is asked rather than settled. The anthology holds no paper on
active inference.

Where it bears on what the record holds:

- **Friston, "Life as we know it" ([LIT-526](LIT-526.md)).** The paper grounds its "first
  principles" in the statistical physics of systems at nonequilibrium steady
  state and cites [LIT-526](LIT-526.md) for it. The critiques filed beside it bear on that
  grounding, not on the planning scheme. Raja et al. ([LIT-598](LIT-598.md)) argue
  that active-inference models presuppose perception and action in the
  generative model they are given; this paper's rat is handed its A, B, C
  and D matrices, so the critique applies to its simulations as stated.
- **Self-determination theory ([LIT-559](LIT-559.md), [LIT-558](LIT-558.md)).** The paper equates its
  "intrinsic value", expected information gain, with "intrinsic motivation"
  and cites Ryan & Deci (1985). In self-determination theory intrinsic
  motivation is doing an activity for its inherent satisfaction and is tied
  to needs for autonomy and competence; it is not information gain. The
  identification is the paper's, by name, not by argument. It is tagged
  `motivation` because it offers a formal account of curiosity and novelty
  seeking that a reader of that topic would expect to find, not because it
  supports the SDT filings.
- **Constructed emotion ([LIT-542](LIT-542.md)).** The record's other active-inference
  holding, used by [THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md). Nothing here bears on affect.
- **Behavioral integration.** It is tagged for action selection: how an
  agent arbitrates between exploiting preferences and resolving
  uncertainty, and how depth of planning changes the choice.
