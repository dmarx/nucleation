# NOTE-456: Participatory sense-making: An enactive approach to social cognition

## Contribution

Enaction had an account of individual cognition as sense-making by
autonomous systems, and none of social cognition. Embodied critics of
theory-of-mind and simulation theory acknowledged interaction but stayed
"methodologically individualistic". The paper supplies a definition of
social interaction on which the interaction process is itself an autonomous
organization, and the notion of participatory sense-making, so that social
understanding can be partly a property of the interaction rather than of
either individual.

## Key insight

Coordination between coupled systems needs no clever mechanism; pendulum
clocks do it. What is special in social encounters is a double influence:
the pattern of coordination affects whether the encounter continues, and
the encounter affects the likelihood of coordination. That loop gives the
interaction an identity of its own, and since movements are the tools of
sense-making, coordinating movements coordinates sense-making.

## Assumptions

- **The enactive framework,** in five ideas: autonomy, sense-making,
  embodiment, emergence, experience (p. 487). An autonomous system is a
  network of processes with operational closure under precarious conditions
  (isolated processes would run down).
- **Coordination** is "the non-accidental correlation between the
  behaviours of two or more systems that are in sustained coupling, or have
  been coupled in the past, or have been coupled to another, common, system"
  (p. 490). Synchrony is one kind; mirroring, anticipation and imitation are
  others.
- **Scope:** face-to-face, micro-level, dyadic interactions, "not restricted
  to linguistic or even human ones" (p. 486); larger groups are expected to
  extend the account but are not treated.

## Key results

- **Coordination is easy and often hard to avoid (p. 490–491).** Schmidt &
  O'Brien's pendulum-swinging pairs phase-lock when they can see each other.
  Coordination can be absolute (phase-locked) or relative, with phase
  attraction (Kelso 1995, p. 98).
- **Autonomy of the interaction (pp. 492–494).** The double influence makes
  the encounter "an emergent and autonomous process", "typically a fleeting
  one". Interactors "co-emerge as interactors with the interaction". Fig. 1:
  interactional coordination (interaction shapes coordination) and
  functional coordination (coordination serves the interaction).
- **Definition of social interaction (p. 493),** quoted in the LIT. Body heat
  at a bus stop is coupling without regulation, so not social; bumping into
  someone can become social once regulated. Coercion or torture that
  destroys an interactor's autonomy is, "at that point", no longer social
  (p. 495).
- **Participatory sense-making (p. 497),** on a spectrum (Fig. 2). Low end:
  a video-link delay changes the meaning of a pause and A downgrades her
  praise. Middle: orientation (pointing; Stern's "I'm gonna getcha";
  Currie's Janet at the window). High end: joint meaning-making (charades).
- **Perceptual crossing (pp. 501–502).** In Auvray et al.'s minimal
  virtual-tape experiment, recognition of the partner happens because both
  partners search for each other: mutual scanning is the only globally
  stable configuration. "The sense-making that solves the task only happens
  at the level of the collective dynamics."
- **Implications (pp. 502–504).** Social skills are relational; individual
  incompetence should not be inferred from interactional failure (autism is
  named). The other is experienced as neither transparent nor opaque, but as
  shifting with coordination and breakdown.

## Concepts

- **Sense-making.** An autonomous agent's creation and appreciation of
  meaning, grounded in the norms its self-maintenance sets.
- **Coordination; absolute and relative coordination.** As above, after
  Kelso.
- **Interactional and functional coordination.** Interaction shaping
  coordination, and coordination serving the interaction.
- **Participatory sense-making.** As defined in the LIT.
- **Orientation.** One interactor modulating the other's sense-making toward
  a domain of significance.

## Connections

It applies the enactive autonomy of Varela, Thompson and Di Paolo (and the
same group's [LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md)) to social encounters, and takes its dynamics from
coordination studies (Kelso 1995, [LIT-578](../literature.d/LIT-578.md), read here through Kelso
2021, [LIT-601](../literature.d/LIT-601.md); Winfree; Kuramoto). It positions itself against theory
theory and simulation theory and beyond Gallagher's, Hutto's and Thompson's
embodied accounts, and alongside conversation analysis, Trevarthen and
Tronick.

## Bearing on the record

- **Social ontology.** The record's social-ontology holdings are about
  collective intentionality and institutions. This paper offers a different
  route to a social entity: a dynamical organization with its own closure
  and precariousness. It does not discuss collective intentionality.
- **Coordination dynamics.** Its "relative coordination" is the
  metastable regime of the extended HKB model in [LIT-601](../literature.d/LIT-601.md); the record
  can now trace the concept from the hand-movement model to social
  interaction.
- **Psychopathology.** The remark on autism (p. 503) points to a relational
  account of social difficulty; it is a single sentence, and the paper cites
  De Jaegher 2006 for it.
- No THEORY filed: one was declined, because the source does not carry a
  claim beyond the definition itself; the evidence is illustrative.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- Nothing is measured; examples are chosen to illustrate, and the authors
  call the implications "speculative at this stage".
- "Autonomous" for an interaction lasting minutes stretches precarious
  operational closure; how a fleeting relational pattern has processes that
  "would run down" in isolation is not spelled out.
- Groups larger than two are set aside.

## Open questions

- What measurement would show that an interaction, rather than its
  participants, sustains a pattern? The paper points to perceptual-crossing
  paradigms and to measures of coordination but proposes no test.
- How do breakdowns and repairs over many encounters change individual
  capacities, the developmental route the authors name?
