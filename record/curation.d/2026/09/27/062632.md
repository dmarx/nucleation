The owner asked for arXiv 0810.0812 next: Coecke, Pavlovic & Vicary, *A New
Description of Orthogonal Bases* (MSCS 23(3), 2013). [THEORY-017](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-017.md)'s
`promote_when` had named it. It is filed as [LIT-tmpm1wb1](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpm1wb1.md), read in full as
[NOTE-tmpr7p85](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-tmpr7p85.md), and `Active`.

**What it proves.**

- **Thm 5.1.** In FdHilb, commutative †-Frobenius algebras are in bijection
  with *orthogonal* bases, the basis being the vectors the comultiplication
  copies. The bijection is of sets, for each fixed space.
- **§6.** The special ones are exactly the orthonormal bases, with the
  vectors fixed exactly, phases included.
- **Corollaries 7.1–7.2.** These give categorical equivalences with finite
  sets labelled by positive reals, and with FinSet.
- **The proof.** Its key step rests on the cited spectral theorem for
  finite-dimensional commutative C*-algebras.
- **Language and scope.** The paper says "classical data", never "classical
  structure". It says nothing about infinite dimensions, distributions,
  contextuality or projection lattices.

**What changed in [THEORY-017](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-017.md).** It now states the correspondence as proved:
orthogonal bases for commutative †-Frobenius algebras, orthonormal bases for
special ones. It also gains a caveat that the correspondence holds on complex
spaces only.

- **The real-space counterexample.** The reader found it, and I checked it
  numerically: on ℝ², the complex numbers with multiplication scaled by
  1/√2 form a commutative special †-Frobenius algebra with no nonzero
  copyable vector. The associativity, Frobenius, special and commutativity
  laws all hold, and the copy residual shrinks only as the vector goes to
  zero.
- **What it means for the table.** [LIT-273](../../record/literature.d/LIT-273.md), [LIT-272](../../record/literature.d/LIT-272.md) and [THEORY-004](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-004.md) work over
  the reals. A basis there still induces a Frobenius algebra, which is all
  [LIT-272](../../record/literature.d/LIT-272.md) uses, so the table's placements stand. But "basis" and "Frobenius
  algebra" are one notion only in the complex case.

**Why it stays `Proposed`.** The first `promote_when` condition is met. The
second is not. Two placements rest on the readers' own numerical checks,
not on the papers' claims: [LIT-273](../../record/literature.d/LIT-273.md)'s orthogonal covariance and [LIT-272](../../record/literature.d/LIT-272.md)'s
rotation sensitivity. The `promote_when` now says so. Promoting on the
strength of what the readers computed would be exactly the move the field
exists to prevent.

**[NOTE-246](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-246.md) corrected (version bump).** Two sentences overstated this paper.
The spider normal form is not in it, though [LIT-272](../../record/literature.d/LIT-272.md) cites it for that. And
the basis correspondence needs † and a complex Hilbert space. Its claims
table's C3 was accurate and is unchanged.

**Candidate follow-up, not filed.** For infinite dimensions, the reader found
by search, but did not read, Abramsky & Heunen (arXiv 1011.6123). That is
where [THEORY-017](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-017.md)'s finite-dimensional boundary would be tested.
