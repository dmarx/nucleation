# NOTE-2354: Whitmeyer — a concavity in the value of information

## Contribution

It revisits "the classical nonconcavity of the value of information" and
argues the nonconcavity is an artifact of the measure of the amount of
information, not a property of the value of information as such. It offers
a quantification (a convex divergence of the posterior) under which, with
efficient acquisition, the value is concave, and shows the contrasting
inefficient and max-min cases. The results are small and the proofs short;
the contribution is conceptual — separating the phenomenon from its
parametrization.

## Key results

- **Why the nonconcavity happens (§1).** With finitely many undominated
  actions, "the set of beliefs at which an action is optimal is a convex
  body: it is compact, convex, and possesses a non-empty interior.
  Accordingly, for a prior in the interior of one of these regions any
  information that does not move the prior much is worthless." The
  observation "originates with Radner and Stiglitz (1984)", who note that
  "the marginal productivity of information depends, of course, on the way
  the quantity of information is measured."
- **Amount of information (Def. 2.1).** D(F) = ϕ(∫ c(x,µ) dF(x)), with c a
  strictly convex divergence (c(µ,µ)=0) and ϕ weakly convex, strictly
  increasing, ϕ(0)=0. The Shannon-entropy difference is one such c.
- **Efficient value is concave (Prop. 2.3, Cor. 2.4).** Define the
  Efficient Value of Information W(η) = max over Bayes-plausible F with
  D(F) ≤ η of E_F V(x). The amount-of-information constraint binds (Lemma
  2.2), and then "the value of information is concave in its amount, and so
  in non-trivial decision problems the marginal value of information is
  strictly positive at zero." With two undominated actions it is strictly
  concave (Prop. 2.5); three can make it linear on a sub-interval.
- **Inefficient value is almost never concave (Prop. 2.6).** The
  Inefficient Value U(η) = min over F with D(F) ≥ η of E_F V(x). "If we
  take the opposite approach, selecting distributions that are worst for a
  DM's decision problem … the value of information is almost never concave
  in its amount. In particular, the marginal value of information is
  almost always zero at zero" — the set of priors at which U is flat near
  zero is dense in the simplex, because near an interior prior the optimal
  cheap experiment stays within one belief polytope where V is affine.
- **Max-min value is concave (Prop. 3.3).** For a Gilboa–Schmeidler
  max-min decision-maker the value of information W̄ is weakly concave,
  strictly if ϕ is strictly convex; "nature has less strategic freedom",
  strengthening concavity.
- **The engine.** All three follow from replication: the
  amount-of-information constraint binds, so an average of two optimal
  experiments is feasible for the average amount, and linearity of
  expectation gives (super)additivity.

## Assumptions

A single Bayesian decision-maker; finite state set Θ; full-support prior;
compact action set with at least two undominated actions (V not affine);
a convex-divergence measure of information; an upper bound η̄ below the
amount that already yields the maximal decision value. §3 replaces the
Bayesian DM with a max-min one over a full-dimensional convex set of
priors.

## Concepts

- **Value of information** — the gain in expected decision value from
  acquiring a (Bayes-plausible) distribution over posteriors before acting.
- **Amount / quantity of information** — a convex-divergence functional of
  the posterior distribution (Def. 2.1); the nonconcavity lives or dies by
  this choice.
- **Efficient vs inefficient acquisition** — selecting the best, resp.
  worst, experiment that delivers a given amount; W is concave, U almost
  never is.
- **Radner–Stiglitz nonconcavity** — marginal value of information zero at
  zero; the phenomenon this paper dissects.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md), point 4 ("Marginal reasoning fails").** The claim buys
  probes in a *fixed order at fixed cumulative costs* and finds a probe's
  worth can be negative while a later one's is large, so stopping when the
  next probe does not pay misses the optimum. Whitmeyer locates this: a
  fixed, exogenous probe schedule is an *inefficient* quantification of
  the amount observed, which is exactly the regime (Prop. 2.6) where the
  value of information is generically nonconcave and marginal reasoning
  fails. The claim's point 4 is thus the Radner–Stiglitz nonconcavity
  specialized to a committed probe order — it marks a *limit* of that
  result's reach rather than contradicting it. A dated note on the claim
  records this; it is context, not independent proof of the claim's own
  theorem, which stands in its own model.
- **The unchecked citation.** [CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md)'s "What it does not say" says the
  nonconcavity "is usually credited to Radner and Stiglitz … and the
  citation is unchecked." Checked: Radner, R. & Stiglitz, J. (1984), "A
  nonconcavity in the value of information", Bayesian Models in Economic
  Theory, 5:33–52; Whitmeyer confirms the attribution and the content.
- **[QUESTION-374](../questions.d/QUESTION-374.md).** The question asks for the structure of the
  observe/act trade. Whitmeyer's distinction — efficient acquisition gives
  a concave frontier, committed/inefficient acquisition need not — is a
  candidate structural fact for the observing side of that trade.
- **[THEORY-853](../theory.d/THEORY-853.md) (Bergemann–Morris).** A neighbour on the value of
  information through the Blackwell order; Whitmeyer works with
  Bayes-plausible distributions over posteriors in the same tradition but
  asks about concavity in an amount, not comparison of structures. A
  parallel, not evidence.

## Limitations

- The "amount of information" is a modeling choice; the concavity result is
  a statement about one family of measures (convex divergences with
  efficient acquisition), as the paper is careful to say. It does not show
  that any natural cost of observation yields concavity.
- Finite state set and finitely many undominated actions throughout; Prop.
  2.6's density argument uses that V is piecewise affine.
- The paper proves existence of the efficient optimum abstractly; it gives
  no algorithm and no worked numerical example.
- "Efficient" acquisition assumes the decision-maker can choose the best
  experiment of each amount; when the available experiments are fixed (as
  in [CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md)'s probe order) the efficient frontier is not attainable.
