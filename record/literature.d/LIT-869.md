# LIT-869: Multiscale unfolding of real networks by geometric renormalization

Guillermo García-Pérez, Marián Boguñá and M. Ángeles Serrano (2017),
arXiv:1706.00394; *Nature Physics* 14(6):583–589 (2018),
DOI-10.1038/s41567-018-0072-5

## Key takeaways

- **The transformation.** Given a network's S¹ embedding (angles θ and
  hidden degrees κ, inferred by maximum likelihood), cut the circle into
  blocks of r consecutive nodes, merge each block into a supernode, and
  link two supernodes if any member of one was linked to any member of the
  other. Iterating gives a "multiscale shell" of layers, each r times
  smaller, at most O(log N) of them. Coarse-graining composes (r₁ then r₂
  is r₁r₂: an abelian semigroup).
- **The S¹ model is renormalizable, to first order.** With supernode
  hidden degree κ′ = (Σ κ_j^β)^{1/β}, angle a κ^β-weighted power mean of
  the members' angles, μ′ = μ/r, R′ = R/r and β′ = β, the probability that
  two supernodes are linked keeps the S¹ form 1/(1 + χ^β). The derivation
  keeps only the first term of an expansion and treats all member pairs
  of two blocks as equidistant. A synthetic network of about 225,000 nodes
  keeps the connection curve over eight layers (Fig. 3A).
- **Phases of the flow.** The power-law exponent γ of hidden degrees is
  kept if (γ−1)/2 < β. The average degree scales as ⟨k⟩′ = r^ν⟨k⟩, with
  ν = 2/(γ−1) − 1 when (γ−1)/β ≤ 1 and ν = 2/β − 1 when 1 ≤ (γ−1)/β < 2,
  in a power-law approximation the paper says is "not true in general".
  ν > 0 (γ < 3 or β < 2) flows to a fully connected graph; ν < 0 (γ > 3
  and β > 2) to a one-dimensional ring that loses the small world. All six
  real networks lie in the first phase.
- **Self-similarity of real networks.** Rescaled by their average degree,
  the degree distributions, neighbour-degree curves and clustering spectra
  of the layers collapse, and Louvain communities of a layer, mapped back,
  stay high in modularity and in mutual information with those of the
  original. The paper takes this as new evidence that hidden metric spaces
  underlie real networks.
- **Statistical mechanics.** In the ℍ² form, links are non-interacting
  fermions with energy half the hyperbolic distance; the partition
  function factors as Z = ζ^{N/2} Z′, with the intra-supernode links in ζ
  and Z′ the partition function of the renormalized layer.
- **Uses.** "Mini-me" replicas, a layer pruned back to the original
  average degree by lowering μ, reproduce the order-parameter curves of
  Ising, SIS and Kuramoto dynamics at every scale tried. Greedy routing
  that plans in the coarsest layer where source and target differ and
  descends to physical neighbours succeeds more often than single-layer
  routing, at slightly longer paths.

## Standing in the record

Filed at the owner's request on 2026-10-09, in the second part of the
batch on hierarchy and hyperbolic geometry that followed [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md).
It is the direct continuation of Krioukov et al. ([LIT-865](LIT-865.md),

paper equated and asks what it does under a change of scale. Its own
account of the renormalizability is [THEORY-190](../theory.d/THEORY-190.md).

The owner filed it beside Koch-Janusz and Ringel's *Mutual information,
neural networks and the renormalization group* (Nature Physics,
DOI 10.1038/s41567-018-0081-4), which another reader is filing. The two
are not companion papers. They are neighbours in the same issue (14(6),
pp. 578–582 and 583–589), both about the renormalization group, from
different groups, on different systems (lattice spin models with a
learned coarse-graining; networks with a geometric one), and in their
arXiv versions neither cites the other. The issue's contents list no News & Views pairing them.
