# LEAD-109: Settle whether a fixed level and target can have several self-consistent factorizations into agents

## What it would do

Construct a case with two, or argue uniqueness.

Where the record names it:

- [CLAIM-170](../claims.d/CLAIM-170.md): "says itself that whether a fixed level and target can have
  several self-consistent factorizations is the objection's open part"

## What would count

Either closes the objection's open part.

## Note of 2026-11-22: carried out

Carried out as a delegated lead (branch
`claude/lead-109-factorization-uniqueness`). A level and target were
modelled as a fixed coupling matrix over regimes, a factorization as a
partition, and the admission test's first condition
([CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md), [CLAIM-154](../claims.d/CLAIM-154.md)) as a
near-decomposable gap. [CASE-tmprh6pl](../cases.d/CASE-tmprh6pl.md) enumerates it and
[CLAIM-tmpm2589](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpm2589.md) states the result: at a fixed level and target
the self-consistent factorizations form a *nested* hierarchy of
coarsenings of a unique finest partition — several can coexist, but two
never *crosscut* (disagree on co-grouping strongly-coupled regimes), not
even under exact symmetry. So the objection's open part is answered in
the direction that defends [CLAIM-170](../claims.d/CLAIM-170.md): its `defeated_if`,
read as a crosscut, is not met. What remains open is the grain (a regime
as its own agent or part of a larger one), which the level and target as
stated do not fix; three ways to treat that are filed as the owner's call
in [CLAIM-tmpm2589](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpm2589.md). Not covered: directed or time-lagged coupling
(a demonstration there would strengthen or qualify the result), and a
faithful computation of tests 2–3 on dynamics rather than stipulated
per-block verdicts. Status and stage left for the owner.
