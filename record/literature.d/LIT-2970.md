# LIT-2970: Flocks, herds, and schools: A quantitative theory of flocking

Toner & Tu (1998). Physical Review E 58, 4828–4858; arXiv
cond-mat/9804180.

## Key takeaways

- In d = 2 "flocks exhibit a true, long-range ordered, spontaneously
  broken symmetry state", which "does not, in fact, violate the
  Mermin-Wagner theorem, since flocks are a non-equilibrium dynamical
  system" (pp. 2, 4).
- The linear theory's infrared divergence, logarithmic in d = 2, is
  "very reminiscent of the familiar Mermin-Wagner-Hohenberg" theorem
  (p. 27); convective transport "suppresses the very fluctuations that
  give rise to it" (p. 4).
- Density fluctuations "become infinitely bigger than those in a fluid or
  an ideal gas": Cρ(q) diverges as q → 0, as q⊥^(−6/5) for q∥ ≪ q⊥ in
  d = 2 (pp. 7–8, eqs. 1.10–1.11).
- Ordered-phase scaling belongs to "the low temperature fixed point", not
  to the fixed point of the order–disorder transition (p. 64); a compass
  introduces "a "mass"" and leaves "no interesting fluctuations" (p. 63).
