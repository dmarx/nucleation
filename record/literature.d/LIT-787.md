# LIT-787: Design for a Brain: The Origin of Adaptive Behaviour

W. Ross Ashby (1952), *Design for a Brain*, London: Chapman & Hall; second
edition, revised, 1960, as *Design for a Brain: The Origin of Adaptive
Behaviour* — DOI-10.1007/978-94-015-1320-3

## Key takeaways

*Registered from secondary description, not read.* What follows comes from
Crossref's chapter list for the 1960 edition and from standard accounts of
the book.

- **Adaptation as stability.** The book treats the organism as a machine,
  a state-determined dynamic system, and adaptive behaviour as the
  stability of such a system. The 1960 chapters run "The Organism as
  Machine", "Stability", "Adaptation as Stability", "Parameters".
- **Ultrastability.** "The Ultrastable System", "The Homeostat" and
  "Ultrastability in the Organism" follow. An ultrastable system has two
  feedback loops: a fast one that regulates behaviour under fixed
  parameters, and a slow one, through step-functions, that changes those
  parameters when an essential variable leaves its limits, until a
  configuration is found that keeps it in bounds. The homeostat was
  Ashby's machine built to show it.
- **Scaling up.** Later chapters take up the multistable system, temporary
  independence of subsystems, habituation, and "Ancillary Regulations"
  and "Amplifying Adaptation", the question of how adaptation can be fast
  enough in a large system.
