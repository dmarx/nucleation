# NOTE-tmpf6vyn: Remembrance of Brains Past

## Contribution

A short announcement, by its authors, of the result in Shomrat and Levin
2013: a learned familiarity in planarians survives the removal and
regrowth of the head. It explains why the lab built an automated training
device and what it takes the result to mean.

## Key insight

The authors read the result as showing memory held outside the brain and
copied into the new one: "not only can memories be stored somewhere
outside of the brain, but ... they can be imprinted on a naïve
regenerated brain" (paragraph 7).

## Assumptions

- **Feeding readiness measures familiarity.** Recognition is "measured by
  their willingness to eat a piece of liver rather than spend time
  exploring a new environment as controls do" (paragraph 6).
- **A refresher does not create the memory.** The worms "need a brief
  refresher after head regeneration in order to show good recall"
  (paragraph 6). The inference to a surviving memory assumes the refresher
  alone would not give naive worms the same result. The post does not say
  this was tested.
- **Non-neural cells can compute.** The proposed store rests on the lab's
  work on bioelectric signalling in patterning (paragraphs 4 and 8).

## Key results

- **Why automate** (paragraph 3). Hand-run training gave low Ns and weak
  memories, was open to subjective scoring and depended on the operator's
  skill. McConnell's results were "confirmed in some labs but not
  reproduced in some others", and the area was forgotten.
- **The device** (paragraph 5). It tracks planaria and gives individual
  rewards and punishments to 12 worms at a time, "24*7", with
  computer-scored analysis. It also works for *Xenopus* and zebrafish. It
  took "Five years and many engineering problems".
- **The result** (paragraph 6). Worms trained to a chamber with a
  "specifically rough (laser-etched) surface" recognised it after "complete
  head removal and regeneration", given a brief refresher. "A complex
  brain-derived behavior was driven by a memory that survived complete
  head regeneration."
- **What comes next** (paragraph 7). Which tissues hold the memory (are
  neoblasts required?), what pathways imprint it on the brain, and how
  memories are encoded in and decoded from tissue.
- **The hypothesis** (paragraph 8). Memory "could be globally distributed
  throughout the body", in non-neural cells linked by gap junctions into "a
  neural-like network". "At the moment, we do not know where and how the
  memory is encoded in the body."

## Concepts

- **refresher**: a brief re-exposure to training after regeneration,
  needed for good recall.
- **neoblasts**: planarian adult stem cells, from which a lost part
  regrows.
- **bioelectric network**: non-neural cells signalling through resting
  potentials and gap junctions.

## Limitations

- **A blog post.** No Ns, durations, statistics or controls; the reader
  cannot check the result here.
- **The refresher control is not mentioned.**
- **Which fragment regenerated** (tail, trunk) and how long regeneration
  took are not said.
- **Citation oddity.** Sheiman and Tiras 1996, titled "Memory and
  morphogenesis in planaria and beetle", is cited for memory through
  insect metamorphosis.
- **Typos**: a mismatched closing quotation mark in the Chakravarthy and
  Ghosh reference ("'cardiac memory”").

## Open questions

- Do naive worms given only the refresher after regeneration show the same
  feeding readiness? That control decides between recall and savings.
- Which tissue holds the memory, and are neoblasts required? The authors'
  own first question.
