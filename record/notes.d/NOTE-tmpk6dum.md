# NOTE-tmpk6dum: Tomasello — Social cognition and metacognition in great apes: a theory

## Contribution

A review of great-ape social cognition and metacognition, and a theory
joining them: apes have a second, metacognitive tier of control, and
understand others through the states they monitor in themselves.

## Key results

1. **What apes understand.** "if great apes have a theory (or an
   understanding), it is a theory of agentive action" (p. 30).
2. **Rationality.** "For many philosophers, examining one's own knowledge
   critically and adjusting it in the light of new evidence, is best called
   rational decision making." (p. 32).
3. **The tiers.** "Executive monitoring of one’s own behavior is common among
   mammals." (p. 32); apes "attributed the same mental states that they were
   monitoring in themselves to others" (p. 32).
4. **A prediction.** "Across primate species—and perhaps across nonprimate
   species as well—there should be a correlation between self-regulatory
   and social cognitive skills." (p. 33).

## Assumptions

A control-tier architecture of agency (from the 2022 book); competition
for clumped food as the ecological driver, whose conditions the paper
itself calls unknown.

## Concepts

Executive and metacognitive monitoring; agentive action; uncertainty
monitoring; rational decision making; attributing self-monitored states to
others.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-662, CLAIM-439 and QUESTION-266**, a parallel: the debate
  over whether procedural metacognition monitors a feeling or a cognitive
  state; the paper cites Carruthers and Williams, who argue for feelings.
- **QUESTION-054**, as for LIT-tmp8gni8: the metacognitive tier is a
  governing level over the executive.

## Limitations

A review with a theory; the ecology is unknown by its own account; it says
nothing about vertebrates in general or humans.
