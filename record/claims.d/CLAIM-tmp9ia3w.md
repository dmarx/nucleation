# CLAIM-tmp9ia3w: Conditioned and negotiated coordination share their maintenance provenance, since both persist when the supervisor is removed, so the interventions that part them test how the constituents' own processes answer dissent, not whose process keeps the arrangement, and removing the supervisor fails to discriminate because it is the one maintenance-provenance test among them

## Admission ([ADR-075](../decisions.d/ADR-075.md)): a derivation

1. [CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md): conditioned and negotiated coordination behave alike, at the
   same supervisory cost, and "part only under intervention, when a
   constituent dissents, or in their history".
2. [TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md): maintenance provenance is whose process keeps a capacity or
   organization going. Its test is knocking out that process.
3. Removing the supervisor is that knockout for the supervisor's process.
   Both kinds of coordination persist without it, so it does not part them.
   They share maintenance provenance with respect to the supervisor.
4. So the interventions that do part them (a constituent's dissent, and
   how it is answered) probe the constituents' own processes, not whose
   process maintains the arrangement.

The step from 1 to 3 is nontrivial: [CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md) lists intervention among the
ways the two part, and the derivation shows that the maintenance-provenance
intervention is the one that does not.

## What it does not say

- Not that formation provenance fails to part them. Their histories differ,
  which [TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md)'s formation sense reads, during formation ([CASE-191](../cases.d/CASE-191.md)).
- Not that the boundary between inside and outside is given. It must be
  declared ([CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md)).
