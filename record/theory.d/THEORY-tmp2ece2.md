# THEORY-tmp2ece2: Pritchett, Woolcock and Andrews argue that many low-capability states, at their measured pace of improvement, would take centuries to millennia to reach high state capability, and that the persistence is sustained by two techniques: isomorphic mimicry, keeping legitimacy by imitating the forms of functional organizations without their function, and premature load bearing, loading fledgling organizations with tasks too complex too soon

## Source

Pritchett, Woolcock & Andrews (2012; Journal of Development Studies 2013), [LIT-tmphg6vu](../literature.d/LIT-tmphg6vu.md); read in [NOTE-tmpss5e0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpss5e0.md), working-paper pages.

## What was actually shown

- **The timescale, two ways.** Gap from zero over years since independence
  on three 2008 indicators: "For the average of the countries in 2008 in
  the bottom 15 it would take 672 years" (p. 10). ICRG since 1985: "the
  median rate of country improvement for both indicators is zero" (p. 11),
  and most of the bottom 30 have negative rates, "hence the estimated time
  is infinity" (p. 11).
- **Mimicry**, defined (p. 14) and illustrated: Mozambique's de jure PEFA
  scores average B and its de facto scores C (p. 8); a Solomon Islands
  courthouse used twice in its first year (p. 9).
- **Premature load bearing**, defined: "pushing too hard too soon, thereby
  creating a situation we call pre-mature load bearing in which stresses
  exceed capability" (p. 14). No case is analysed as such.
- **A framework**: agents, organizations and systems, with the system's
  readiness to "require, recognize and reward novelty" (p. 16) setting
  whether legitimacy comes from accomplishment or mimicry.

## What this does not say

- That the rates are measured: they are bounds and extrapolations from
  ordinal, perception-based scales.
- That the two techniques cause the slow rates: they are offered as how
  failure persists, not tested against alternatives.
- That cocooned or results-based approaches fail or succeed: Cash on
  Delivery is proposed, unevaluated.
- Anything about capability lost after support is withdrawn.
