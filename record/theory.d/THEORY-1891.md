# THEORY-1891: Gepshtein, Pawar, Kwon, Savel'ev and Albright account for cortical spatial and temporal frequency selectivity, and for lateral effects outside the stimulated region, as the interference of standing, spatially damped neural waves intrinsic to a chain of coupled excitatory–inhibitory motifs

## Source

Gepshtein, Pawar, Kwon, Savel'ev & Albright (2022), [LIT-2633](../literature.d/LIT-2633.md); read in
[NOTE-1984](../notes.d/NOTE-1984.md).

## The result

In a chain of nearest-neighbour-coupled Wilson–Cowan motifs, the
stationary response to a point input "is a standing spatially damped
wave", whose period "is an intrinsic property of the circuit". Under
linearity, the response to an extended stimulus is the superposition of
such waves. Their interference makes the response maximal near the
intrinsic spatial frequency inside the stimulated zone. Outside it, "The
SF of the response in zone 2 is independent of the stimulus SF." Three
predictions were tested and held: lateral modulation of human contrast
sensitivity whose period "does not systematically depend on the stimulus
SF" (two subjects); and, in reanalysed MT data from two monkeys, preferred
TF following "a nonmonotonic function of stimulus SF", and a rise of peak
SF with contrast.

## What it does not show

- Propagating waves. These are standing impulse responses. Travelling
  waves are a stated future direction.
- That cortical waves interfere linearly in general. The contrast effect
  needs the nonlinear regime, and colliding evoked waves in mouse S1 sum
  sub-linearly ([LIT-2654](../literature.d/LIT-2654.md)).
- A tested 2-D model ("whose predictions we have not tested"), or
  single-neuron quantitative fits.
- Anything about artificial networks. The closing paragraph's suggestion
  is speculation.
