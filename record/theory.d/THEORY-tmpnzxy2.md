# THEORY-tmpnzxy2: To ascribe beliefs and desires is to predict a system by assuming it is rational and its beliefs mostly true, so a wholly false belief system has no coherent intentional description and contradictory beliefs no stable one, and the believer, like the person built on that notion, is an idealisation that real creatures only approximate

## Source

Dennett (1971), "Intentional Systems", [LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md), read in [NOTE-350](../notes.d/NOTE-350.md). Dennett
(1980), "Reply to Professor Stich", [LIT-456](../literature.d/LIT-456.md), read in [NOTE-371](../notes.d/NOTE-371.md) from the
author's final draft.

## What was actually shown

**The stance ([LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md) pp. 87–90).** An Intentional system is one whose
behaviour can be predicted by ascribing beliefs and desires and assuming
rationality. Rationality "so far means nothing more than optimal design
relative to a goal or optimally weighted hierarchy of goals" and
constraints (p. 89). Intentional predictions fail on "design weaknesses
and lapses", and consistently failed predictions push the predictor to the
design stance, as with the insane (pp. 93–96).

**Belief's two norms ([LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md) pp. 101–103).** Normally, if x believes p, p
is true; normally, if x avows p, x believes p. Hence "a false belief system
is a conceptual impossibility" (p. 101). The norms break the belief–desire
circle "in the long run and in general" (p. 103). [NOTE-350](../notes.d/NOTE-350.md) rates this
moderate (C7): an evolutionary argument plus a conceptual claim.

**Irrational avowals ([LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md) pp. 103–106).** When avowals are irrational,
leaning on rationality overrides the speaker and leaning on avowal grants
him irrational beliefs: "there is no coherent Intentional description of
such an impasse". Neither phenomenology nor neurophysiology breaks the tie,
since both presuppose a rationality standard. An "Official Tolerance Rule"
for error would confuse "the norm they follow with what gets by in their
world" (C8, weak to moderate).

**Contradiction ([LIT-456](../literature.d/LIT-456.md) p. 74).** Following Quine, evidence for ascribing
the belief that p is the best evidence against ascribing a contradictory
belief that q. Researchers on inference who ascribe contradictory beliefs
uncautiously "will indeed be confused — not 'simply', but subtly".
Ordinary practice tolerates the slack ("he sort of believes that p") at a
cost, notably in judgments of responsibility ([NOTE-371](../notes.d/NOTE-371.md), C3, moderate).

**The concession ([LIT-456](../literature.d/LIT-456.md) p. 75).** Against Stich's charge that a norm of
full rationality would disqualify every natural believer: "The conception
of ourselves as intentional systems is granted to be an unrealizable
idealization", and so is the concept of a person built on it. It is the
idealisation that figures in ethics (C4, stated, not argued).

**What could have come out otherwise.** A stable intentional description of
a creature with contradictory beliefs could have been given. The argument
says it cannot be, from the interdependence of the evidence; no case is
examined.

## What this does not say

- **That people are rational.** The account says ascription assumes it.
  Dennett grants that no one is fully rational.
- **That experiments on reasoning error find nothing.** The reply says they
  find something other than "unproblematic cases of people believing
  contradictions", without saying what ([NOTE-371](../notes.d/NOTE-371.md), Open questions).
- **That a norm-free theory of belief is impossible.** Such a theory is
  "not ruled out", but it would revise our concepts, and Dennett would
  accept it only if it kept most stance-based ascriptions ([LIT-456](../literature.d/LIT-456.md)).
- **That the evolutionary grounding is worked out.** The 1980 reply admits
  it is left "open-ended", "one of those disconcerting bits of
  soft-pedalling of which Stich rightly accuses me". Its reliance is
  minimal: belief structures are extensions of control systems "selected by
  nature because they are benign in the long run".
- **That the stance is observer-relative.** The 1971 paper says so (C1).
  "True Believers" ([LIT-398](../literature.d/LIT-398.md)) withdraws the relativity; this account does
  not depend on either position.
- **Anything about pain or consciousness.** The stance is "unmetaphysical"
  about consciousness ([LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md) p. 100), and the 1980 reply gives pain no
  such protection, offering policy instead of theory.

## Connections

- **The intentional stance itself.** "True Believers" ([LIT-398](../literature.d/LIT-398.md), [NOTE-353](../notes.d/NOTE-353.md)) is
  the record's full reading of the stance as a criterion for having
  beliefs. This account takes only the rationality presupposition from it
  and its 1971 predecessor.
- **Why agency is cheap to agree on.** [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) uses the stance's
  blindness to composition and consciousness ([LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md), C2) to explain why
  every reading it cites grants agency to wholes. The idealisation conceded
  here is part of why that grant is cheap ([NOTE-371](../notes.d/NOTE-371.md), Bearing on the
  record).
- **Behaviour is not enough.** Block's argument ([THEORY-092](THEORY-092.md)) that
  behavioural capacity does not fix intelligence draws on the same
  intentional-system papers for the contrast.
- **Loans of intelligence.** [LIT-440](../literature.d/LIT-440.md)'s other thesis, that any content-
  ascribing theory takes out a loan repaid only by analysing away its
  readers (p. 96), is a methodological rule and is not stated here. Where it
  continues, "Artificial Intelligence as Philosophy and as Psychology"
  ([LIT-393](../literature.d/LIT-393.md)), is filed unread.
