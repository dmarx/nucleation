# CASE-tmpejqc0: The skeptic who delegates: a critic that cannot overturn an emerging consensus gives its authority to a process that can generate alternatives

## The case

A3 §6 set up three regions of a meaning canvas: A, observed evidence; B,
explanatory hypotheses; C, candidate decisions. There are four operators:

| operator | specialization | intervention |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | pattern integrator | stabilizes A → B, coherent explanations |
| O2 | skeptical critic | destabilizes B, unsupported hypotheses |
| O3 | alternative generator | translates neglected patterns in A into new hypotheses in B |
| O4 | decision integrator | stabilizes commitments in C |

O1 and O4 push toward decision a, and O2 disagrees. "Rather than attempting
to increase its own authority directly, O2 could allocate endorsement to O3,
because O3 can generate alternatives likely to undermine the emerging
consensus." The sequence: O2 detects overconfidence in B, O2 grants O3 more
authority, O3 introduces competing hypotheses, and the new hypotheses change
the landscape in C.

## What it can show

A3: "The population can become more epistemically robust precisely because a
dissenting operator successfully delegates authority to a different
operator ... without requiring the dissenting operator to possess sufficient
competence or authority to overturn the consensus by itself." That is
[CLAIM-tmpxuigm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpxuigm.md)'s point, getting one's goals realized through another's
authority. The roles here are fixed by construction. [CLAIM-tmpny1e4](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpny1e4.md)'s role
indexing is what lets O2 back O3 as a generator without backing O3's policy
in C.

## What it cannot show

It is stipulated. Nothing shows that the new hypotheses are better, only
that the consensus is reopened. Whether that helps is [QUESTION-tmplh9fr](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmplh9fr.md).
