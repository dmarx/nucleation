# CLAIM-tmp79lxm: In contested open-source successions an original line that loses recognized legitimacy keeps its productive capability as long as its steward pays its producers: of eight originals only one stopped producing, about six years after its community voted to leave, and the others still release

## The claim

[CASE-223](../cases.d/CASE-223.md) took as its key measure the lag between a loss of recognized
legitimacy and a loss of productive capability, and tied it to
[CLAIM-521](CLAIM-521.md): the official project may keep its code, release machinery and
users "long after contributors begin migrating". In [CASE-tmpntt8e](../cases.d/CASE-tmpntt8e.md) only
Hudson's line ended. Its creator and its community had voted to leave
(2011-01-29), Oracle handed it to Eclipse (2011), and Eclipse wrote that
"Hudson is not maintained anymore" on or before 2017-05-11. Every other
contested original was still releasing or running at the study date,
including the four whose stewards are companies paying their own teams.

So where the steward pays its producers, the loss of legitimacy, measured
from the successor's appearance, does not bring a loss of productive
capability within the record's horizon. Where the producers were the
community (Hudson, freenode's volunteers), the original lost them at once,
and Hudson's end followed.

## What it does not say

- That the originals lost nothing. Contributors and users were not
  counted.
- That freenode kept its capability in any but a nominal sense: it still
  runs, and the study coded no measure of its use.
