# CLAIM-221: The relational-constitution thesis's defeat condition describes a standard rival the record holds no counter for, intention-based accounts of illocution, which plausibly meet it for every act in the four-speakers case except the institutional one

## The objection

[CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)'s defeat condition: "A held account individuates the acts in
question (teasing, reproach, reprimand) by intrinsic features of the
utterance token plus speaker-internal states alone. Relations to audience,
norms and uptake would then be only evidence of the act, not constituents
of it, and that account would predict the [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md) variation as well."

That describes a standard family of accounts of illocution, the
intention-based one: Grice's account of speaker meaning (1957), Strawson's
"Intention and Convention in Speech Acts" (1964), and Bach and Harnish's
*Linguistic Communication and Speech Acts* (1979). On it, a communicative
act is individuated by the speaker's communicative intention, an intention
to be recognized as having a certain attitude or aim. Strawson set apart
the acts that need conventions, such as an umpire's call, from those that
need only the recognition of intention.

Applied to [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md) ("said by a friend, a parent, a judge, a comedian"):

- The friend teasing, the parent reproaching and the comedian entertaining
  differ in what each speaker intends the audience to recognize. An
  intention-based account individuates the three acts by speaker-internal
  states, which is the defeat condition's first clause.
- The judge condemning during sentencing performs an institutional act.
  Its force needs norms and a status, and Searle's status functions and
  deontic powers ([LIT-482](../literature.d/LIT-482.md), read in [NOTE-388](../notes.d/NOTE-388.md)) already hold that part.
  [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) itself names Searle as "the nearer held prior art".

So the rival plausibly meets the defeat condition for three of [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s
four acts, and the part it does not meet is the part Searle covers.

The record's own worked model for [CLAIM-111](CLAIM-111.md) is of this family. The
rational-speech-act listener of [THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md) interprets an utterance "by
inverting a model of a speaker", and [CLAIM-111](CLAIM-111.md) reads its output as "a
posterior over what the speaker meant". [NOTE-634](../notes.d/NOTE-634.md) introduces it as a
formalization of what "Gricean, relevance-theoretic and common-ground
accounts explained ... informally".

Two features of the defeat condition keep this from being noticed.

- **"A held account".** No work of the intention-based line is in the
  record. A search on 2026-10-10 finds Grice only as mentioned in readings
  of other works (the rational-speech-act papers, Block, Brandom, Heim),
  no work by P. F. Strawson or by Bach and Harnish, and no LIT for any of
  them. While no such work is held, the condition cannot fire.
- **"the [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md) variation".** It is an invented variation ([CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md):
  "Invented, so it shows a possibility"), so there is no observed pattern a
  rival could predict worse. [CLAIM-229](CLAIM-229.md) makes this point about the
  case's standing.

## What it does not say

It does not say the intention-based account is right, nor that [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)
is false. Intentions have audience-directed content, and an
externalist can say the relation to the audience enters the act through
that content; that is a reply, and it is not in the record. It says the
standard rival exists, that it plausibly meets the stated defeat condition
for the non-institutional acts, and that the record has neither read it
nor answered it. Austin and Goffman, whom A187 §8 names, are held unread
([LIT-785](../literature.d/LIT-785.md), [LIT-780](../literature.d/LIT-780.md)) and do not settle it either way.

## What would answer it

- A reading of the intention-based line, and an argument that uptake or
  norms are constitutive of teasing and reproach, not only evidence of
  them: for instance a case in which the speaker's intention is held
  fixed and the act changes with the audience's standing.
- Or a narrowing of [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) to institutional and normative acts, where
  Searle already supports it. [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) and [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md), which rest on
  [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md), would narrow with it.

The works named here should be checked against the Anthology of the SOTA
before any is filed.

## Where it stands

Strictly, the defeat condition is not met. It asks for an account that
individuates "the acts in question (teasing, reproach, reprimand)" by
intrinsic features and speaker-internal states *alone*, and this objection
grants that reprimand needs Searle's standing. On 2026-10-10 [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md) was
restated (version 2) with a defeat condition on the acts with standing
conditions, which carry the claim's "can change the act", and on cases in
which the speaker's intention is held fixed. For teasing, the line's central
case, the rival is unanswered and its literature unread here, and
[QUESTION-073](../questions.d/QUESTION-073.md) holds it open. The objection stays open.

## Note of 2026-10-10: a documented case

[CASE-069](../cases.d/CASE-069.md) is the first case the record holds that can be read against
this objection's rival on documented data rather than on [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s
invented variation. In Labov's recorded sounding sessions, whether a remark
is a ritual or a personal insult follows the shared knowledge, before the
audience, that it is untrue. The verdicts of the target and the group
change while the ritual aim that Labov attributes to the speaker stays
fixed.

It supplies what this objection's first answer asks for: a case in which the
act changes with a relation among the parties while the intention is held
fixed. It does so only partly. The intention is the analyst's attribution,
except in one exchange with a use/mention complication. And Labov's rule is
stated in nested beliefs, which is Gricean in shape, so the rival can read
it as the condition for recognising an intention to sound and call the
hearers' uptake a mistake. The case does not settle that dispute. It does
move it from an invented case to recorded transcripts, and names the
elicited test that would settle it. The objection stays open.

## Note of 2026-10-10: an unintended-reading case

[CASE-073](../cases.d/CASE-073.md), the Kia Provo (2013), is a case of the kind this objection's
first answer asks for, an intention held fixed while the audience changes,
and the intention is the speaker's own published statement, not an analyst's
attribution as in [CASE-069](../cases.d/CASE-069.md). What it does to the intentionalist reply is
two-sided. When Kia's intention became known, the offended audience revised
its verdict ("this was a mistake made by the company"), as the intention
account predicts: the audience's reading was evidence, and better evidence
corrected it. But Kia withdrew the name for the UK and Ireland after its
intention was known and accepted, treating it as unusable before that
audience whatever it meant by it. The reply can call that prudence before a
foreseeable misreading. The relational view calls it the act changing with
the audience. So the case removes the multivocal-act escape that
[CASE-061](../cases.d/CASE-061.md) left open, and moves the dispute to what the withdrawal shows.
It does not settle it. The act is a naming, and part of the change is a
lexical collision. The objection stays open.

## Note of 2026-10-12: filed positions on main

The title says the record "holds no counter" for intention-based accounts.
Since PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121) branched, main has filed two positions on the dispute.
[CLAIM-160](CLAIM-160.md), the owner's claim, fixes intended meaning at the write,
relative to the writer's projected receiver. [CLAIM-171](CLAIM-171.md) is its
reception-side counter: "the writer's projected receiver is one more
reading". Both are filed positions, not readings, and neither reads Grice,
Strawson or Bach and Harnish. Whether [CLAIM-160](CLAIM-160.md) is an intention-based
account of the act, or only of intended meaning, is left to the owner.

## Note of 2026-10-13: the line is now held

The four works this objection names are filed. Grice's "Meaning" (1957,
[LIT-tmpvudnt](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvudnt.md), [NOTE-tmpijgfs](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpijgfs.md)) and Strawson's "Intention and Convention in
Speech Acts" (1964, [LIT-tmpyozog](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyozog.md), [NOTE-tmpqe25w](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqe25w.md)) are read in full. Grice's
"Logic and Conversation" (1975, [LIT-tmpswf3g](../literature.d/LIT-tmpswf3g.md)) is read only at pp. 45–47.
Bach and Harnish (1979, [LIT-tmp0vfgl](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0vfgl.md)) is unread, because no lawful copy
was reachable. So the "held account" clause no longer blocks the defeat
condition by default. Read, the line confirms the objection in substance
and corrects it in two places.

- **Confirmed.** Strawson individuates non-conventional illocutions by the
  speaker's wholly overt intention. He separates request from entreaty,
  said with the same words ("Don't go"), by the recognised attitude with
  which the wish is held. That is [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s form. He concedes the
  essentially conventional acts (verdict, sentence, the umpire's call) to
  procedure, as this objection concedes the judge to Searle.
- **Corrected: not "alone".** On Strawson, "the act of communication is
  performed if uptake is secured", so the audience's recognition is a
  condition of the act, not only evidence of it. [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)'s defeat
  condition asks for individuation by speaker-internal states "alone",
  and the line's central text does not supply it. The condition still
  does not fire.
- **Corrected: the reply is in the record.** "What it does not say" calls
  the externalist reply, that the audience relation enters through the
  content of the intention, absent. It is in Strawson's treatment of
  orders. An order's overt intention includes that the utterance be
  recognised as "issued in a certain social context such that certain
  social rules or conventions apply". Strawson grants the scheme holds this
  only "with some strain". A parent's reproach would sit there, between
  the communicative and the institutional ends.
- **Grice (1957)** is about meaning, not acts, and does not separate
  teasing from reproach. Its one case near reproach, cutting someone in
  the street, is speaker-internal in this objection's sense.

The dispute now turns on whether standing that enters through the content
of the speaker's intention counts as a relation in [CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)'s sense.
Neither text decides that. For teasing neither text says anything, and
Bach and Harnish, the work most likely to, is unread. The objection stays
open.
