# ADR-003: Ten more topics, seven of them grouped as philosophy

## Context

The survey of the anthology's journal for work it set aside as out of scope
(curation entry of 2026-09-25, 22:30) found 70 candidates for this record, and
14 of them wanted a word the nine seed topics lack: information-theoretic work
(*The Work Capacity of Channels with Memory*, the one scope decline the
anthology named by title), economics, psychology and anthropology, and
linguistics. The close readings of the day had already strained the
vocabulary from the other side: Bourrat and the coarse-graining account of
individuality are about identity and parts and wholes; the emergence pair is
metaphysics as much as complex systems; Gelman's post is research ethics.

The owner asked for the three missing words and for a philosophy
sub-vocabulary, asking whether luria supports union vocabularies.

## Decision

Add ten terms to `topics`, each with a label and a blurb:
`information-theory`, `social-science`, `linguistics`, and `metaphysics`,
`mereology`, `agency`, `identity`, `ethics`, `consciousness`, `cognition`.

Declare the seven philosophy words as a **tag group** on `LIT.tags`:

    groups:
      philosophy: {require: any, tags: [metaphysics, mereology, agency,
                   identity, ethics, consciousness, cognition]}

**Union vocabularies do not exist in luria 0.31.0**, the latest release; the
nearest construct is the tag group — a named, labelled, blurbed subset of one
field's vocabulary (luria `TagGroup`; `require` may be `any`, `at-most-one` or
`exactly-one`). With `require: any` it is exactly a sub-vocabulary: a label
over its members that constrains nothing. The words live in the one `topics`
table, so there is still a single place each is defined and blurbed.

`cognition` sits in the philosophy group because the survey's cases were
philosophy of mind and cognitive science; a cognition paper measured in tissue
still takes `neuroscience` first.

## Alternatives

- **A separate `philosophy` vocabulary on a second field** — rejected: every
  view reads `tags`, and a work about identity and complex systems would carry
  its subject in two fields that nothing relates.
- **One `philosophy` topic** — rejected: the cases already split into
  metaphysics, mereology, identity and ethics, and a single word would be the
  "nearest wrong tag" the vocabulary's own alert warns against.
- **`philosophy-of-science` and `control-theory`** — held back: one work each
  in the survey. Added when a second arrives.

## Consequences

Existing works retagged where a new word is justifiably appropriate (a
secondary tag; the primary is unchanged): Bourrat (`identity`, `mereology`),
the emergence review and its counterpoint and the theme-issue editorial
(`metaphysics`), Fuchs's thesis (`information-theory`), Gelman's post
(`ethics`). The 70 survey candidates are seeded against the enlarged
vocabulary.
