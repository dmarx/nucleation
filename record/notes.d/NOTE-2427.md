# NOTE-2427: Discovery of Useful Questions as Auxiliary Tasks

Veeriah et al. (2019), NeurIPS 32; arXiv 1909.04607.

## Contribution

1. A learned parameterization of auxiliary-task targets: the question network, with meta-parameters η.
2. Non-myopic meta-gradients to train it, valuing questions by what answering them does to later main-task learning.
3. Evidence on gridworlds and Atari that such discovered questions can build a useful representation on their own.

## Key results

- **GVFs (§1).** "Generalized value functions, or GVFs, generalize the
  standard value function to allow for arbitrary cumulant functions of
  states in place of rewards".
- **Myopic and non-myopic valuation (§2.2).** "Such meta-loss may be
  evaluated after one update (myopic) or k > 1 updates (non-myopic)."
  Changing η "affects not just the immediate update to θ on the next time
  step, but at all future updates".
- **Only training uses the networks (§2.1).** "the question and answer
  networks are only used during training, and neither is needed for action
  selection".
- **Representation-only setting (§4.1).** "the parameters of the encoder
  network are unaffected by gradients from the main-task updates".
  - "in the two gridworld domains the resulting policies were optimal ...
    in the Atari domains the resulting policies were comparable to those
    achieved by the state of the art IMPALA agent after training for 200M
    frames". The reference "learned faster", and the figures run to about
    2e9 frames.
  - The handcrafted auxiliary tasks, including pixel control, "resulted in
    almost no learning".
- **Myopia (Fig. 5).** "the representation learned with k = 1 and k = 2
  was insufficient for the final policy to do anything meaningful". But
  longer is not better: "the largest unroll length k = 50 performing worse
  than k = 25", which the authors attribute to meta-gradient variance. In
  Puddleworld (appendix Fig. 9), k = 10 was best.
- **Number of questions (Fig. 5).** "Too few questions (d = 2) did not
  provide enough signal", and too many "made learning slower".
- **Meta-objective (Fig. 4).** "optimizing area under the curve to be more
  stable".
- **Joint training on 57 Atari games (§4.2).** "None of the auxiliary tasks
  outperformed IMPALA on each and every of the 57 games". On the ten games
  where pixel control gained most, there were "large gains in 6 out of 10
  games". By the plot, the median over all 57 games is not above IMPALA's.

## Assumptions

- **Environments and agents.** Puddleworld and Collect-Objects with A2C;
  Atari with IMPALA. Heads are linear.
- **What a "question" is.** A vector of 128 cumulants and discounts
  computed from future observations, evaluated under the current
  main-task policy. In the gridworlds the discounts are hand-tuned.
- **What "useful" means.** Lowering the summed main-task loss over the
  next k = 10 updates. Nothing else counts: generality, transfer and
  interpretability play no part.
- **Evidence.** Three seeds, with no significance tests.

## Concepts

GVF, cumulant, discount, question network, answer network, myopic and
non-myopic meta-gradient: as quoted above. "Question" and "answer"
follow the value-function sense: "the question is “what is the discounted
sum of future rewards under some policy?” and the answer is the
approximate value function" (§1). The terminology comes from TD networks
(Sutton and Tanner, 2005).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-754](../claims.d/CLAIM-754.md), the inquiry frontier (a parallel only).** The paper's
  "questions" are prediction targets inside a fixed family of 128 GVFs.
  They are chosen for one fixed main task, never pursued, and never used to
  act. What the paper shows is that *which targets a learner predicts* can
  itself be learned, and changes what the learner can later do. That is a
  mechanism for moving something query-like. It is not formulating
  questions in the frontier's sense.
- **[CLAIM-1009](../claims.d/CLAIM-1009.md), query and answer frontiers (operationalizes the split).**
  Question and answer are separate networks. The random-questions baseline
  shows the choice of question matters apart from the capacity to answer:
  answers to random questions can be learned, and the representation still
  fails. Answer accuracy is not reported. [CLAIM-991](../claims.d/CLAIM-991.md) and [CASE-384](../cases.d/CASE-384.md), with
  their bank of TD-learned predictors and their split between readable and
  answerable queries, are the nearest held entries.
- **[CLAIM-1003](../claims.d/CLAIM-1003.md) (qualifies by contrast).** Here bad questions are fully
  detectable, because a declared main task scores them. The method needs
  exactly the downstream signal whose absence is the claim's hard case.
- **[CLAIM-756](../claims.d/CLAIM-756.md) (operationalizes a narrow form, and corroborates).** η is
  valued only by its effect on later learning, and valuing it myopically,
  over one or two updates, fails. But the later learning is of the same
  fixed task, not a different one.
- **[QUESTION-495](../questions.d/QUESTION-495.md), [CLAIM-1197](../claims.d/CLAIM-1197.md) and [CLAIM-1130](../claims.d/CLAIM-1130.md) (sidesteps the optimum).**
  - The valuation is a fixed main-task loss, which is condition 2 of
    [CLAIM-1197](../claims.d/CLAIM-1197.md), though without resource costs.
  - The horizon is truncated at k = 10, which is condition 3 by truncation
    rather than by discounting.
  - Attainment (condition 4) is not addressed.
  - Longer horizons did worse in practice, so a horizon that defines an
    optimum need not be one the method can estimate. Estimability is a
    practical condition the four do not name.
- **[TERM-131](../terms.d/TERM-131.md) (consistent).** The random-questions baseline is a clean
  counterfactual: the same machinery and experience, with a different
  retained organization.
- **[THEORY-2305](../theory.d/THEORY-2305.md) (a parallel).** The joint setting is multi-task learning
  with a learned auxiliary task. The paper has no account of which
  features are reused.

## Limitations

- **The "on their own" test leaks.** In the representation-only setting
  the main-task loss reaches the encoder through η, which is trained on
  it. The paper does not discuss this.
- **Frames.** The representation-only runs, at about 2e9 frames, are
  compared with a 200M-frame reference.
- **Selected subsets.** The Fig. 6 subsets are ranked by pixel control's
  gain, and over all 57 games the method's median is not above IMPALA's.
- **Statistics.** Three seeds and no tests, though the text says
  "significantly".
- **Uninspected questions.** The discovered questions are never examined,
  so nothing shows they are interpretable as questions.
- **Sensitive settings.** d and k are sensitive and non-monotone, and
  their best values differ between the two gridworlds.
- **Scope.** On-policy GVFs only, and the unroll's cost is not reported.
