# NOTE-tmpftsrb: Sen (1985) — The Standard of Living (Tanner Lectures I and II)

## Contribution

An early, open statement of the capability approach to the standard of living, with its valuation method and the device of refined functionings.

## Key results

1. Lecture I: "The standard of living is not a standard of opulence, even though it is inter alia influenced by opulence."
2. Lecture I: "the standard of living is really a matter of functionings and capabilities, and not a matter directly of opulence, commodities, or utilities."
3. Lecture II: three notions distinguished, "(1) agency achievement, (2) personal well-being, and (3) the standard of living."
4. Lecture II, on partial orderings: "There is no great failure in the inability to babble."
5. Lecture II: "A functioning is an achievement, whereas a capability is the ability to achieve."
6. Lecture II: capabilities are "what real opportunities you have regarding the life you may lead".
7. Lecture II: of two equally undernourished people, one is "fasting" and the other is not.

## Assumptions

- What counts as a functioning is a value judgement.
- Partial comparability is acceptable.

## Concepts

Opulence; functionings; capabilities; conversion variation; dominance partial ordering; ranges of weights; agency achievement; refined functionings.
