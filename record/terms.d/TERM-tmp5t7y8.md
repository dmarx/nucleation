# provisional commitment: the costly allocation of a capability to a possible future need before that need is established, under a policy that permits subsequent release or redirection

## Definition

Four things are kept apart:

- **maintaining** a capability without allocating it;
- **provisionally committing** it under uncertainty (this term);
- **exercising** its specialized function;
- **releasing** it after updated information.

A: "The standing capability is an option. Dispatching begins exercising
an option to preposition, and it incurs real costs even if the ultimate
specialized intervention never occurs." The heavy rescue squad sent to a
collision before the need is known (CASE-tmpt6fm7) is provisionally
committed; when it is cancelled it is released, never having been
exercised.

**A necessary condition.** If the commitment cannot be withdrawn or
redirected in response to information, it is not provisional in this
sense. CLAIM-tmp4pk8d's result, that dispatch and cancellation can both
be optimal, depends on it.

**Reversibility is partial.** Fuel, travel time, risk exposure and some
loss of coverage elsewhere cannot be undone by the release.

**Beyond emergency services**, in A's list: speculative computation,
provisional staffing, backup systems, and contingent allocations of
organizational attention.

## What it is not

- **Maintenance.** A maintained capability is held; a committed one is
  allocated to a particular possible need.
- **Use.** The commitment is not the exercise of the specialized
  function, and counting commitments does not count uses.
- **A free option.** The commitment has costs whether or not the need
  arrives; only part of them is recovered on release.
