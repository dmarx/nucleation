# LIT-tmpefjd9: Group decision making in hidden profile situations: Dissent as a facilitator for decision quality

Schulz-Hardt, S., Brodbeck, F. C., Mojzisch, A., Kerschreiter, R., & Frey,
D. (2006). Group decision making in hidden profile situations: Dissent as a
facilitator for decision quality. *Journal of Personality and Social
Psychology*, 91(6), 1080–1093. doi:10.1037/0022-3514.91.6.1080.

Not read: no lawful copy was reached on 2026-10-23 (see the status note).
What follows is the abstract's.

## Key takeaways

*From the abstract only (PubMed 17144766).*

- **Design.** "135 three-person groups worked on a personnel selection case
  with 4 alternatives", with information distributed as a hidden profile:
  the correct option "could be detected only by pooling and integrating the
  members' unique information".
- **Dissent helps, even wrong dissent.** "Whereas groups with homogeneous
  suboptimal prediscussion preferences (no dissent) hardly ever solved the
  hidden profile, solution rates were significantly higher in groups with
  prediscussion dissent, even if none of these individual prediscussion
  preferences were correct."
- **A correct proponent helps more; the amount of dissent does not.** "The
  magnitude of dissent (i.e., minority dissent or full diversity of
  individual preferences) did not affect decision quality."
- **Mechanism.** The effect "was mediated primarily by greater discussion
  intensity and to some extent also by less discussion bias in dissent
  groups."

The abstract gives no solution rates, and does not say how prediscussion
preferences were composed into groups or whether members announced them.

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-23, "Framework Overview Request", cited it
as an external result for QUESTION-089 (when does a process that
destabilizes a whole's prevailing pattern improve the organization) and for
the proposed study CASE-108, whose measures include the suppression of
honest dissenting minorities. It bears on CLAIM-281. It is a
laboratory result about human groups on one task type; how far it carries
to the record's processes is the record's question, not the paper's.

It is to be read with Mojzisch and Schulz-Hardt (2010), LIT-tmp4zxlj,
which by its abstract finds that knowing others' preferences at the start
impairs the same kind of decision. The two are not in conflict as stated:
this one varies whether members actually disagree; that one varies whether
they are told what the others prefer.

The hidden-profile meta-analysis (Lu, Yuan & McLeod 2012, Personality and
Social Psychology Review, doi:10.1177/1088868311417243) is not held in the
record.
