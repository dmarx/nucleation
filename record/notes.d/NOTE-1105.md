# NOTE-1105: Kahneman, Wakker and Sarin — Back to Bentham? Explorations of experienced utility

## Contribution

The paper brings Bentham's hedonic utility back into economics as a
measurable quantity distinct from choice-revealed utility. It sums up the
Peak-End and duration-neglect findings of Kahneman's group as evidence that
the two utilities come apart in normal people, and it gives a normative
axiomatization of the total experienced utility of an extended episode.

## Key insight

Choice is governed by memory of experience, not by experience: "In many
other situations the sovereign masters that determine what people do are
not pleasure and pain, but memories of pleasure and pain" (p. 385).
Because memory summarizes episodes by a "representative moment" (p. 384)
that leaves out duration, preference can be shown to violate dominance,
and utility maximization becomes an empirical claim.

## Assumptions

- **Hedonic states are measurable** as psychophysical magnitudes are, and
  "the variance they share can serve to operationalize the concept of
  instant experienced utility" (p. 380). Here "they" are verbal reports,
  physiological indices and facial measures.
- **A distinctive neutral point** exists and is stable as an experience
  while the stimulus that produces it shifts with context, as in the two
  pails of warm water (p. 380).
- **Instant utility is a sufficient statistic.** Any effect of past or
  anticipated consumption must already be in the moment's rating. This is
  what makes separability (Axiom 3) reasonable (pp. 390–391).
- **No endorsement of hedonism.** The authors disclaim it: "no endorsement
  of Bentham’s view of pleasure and pain as sovereign masters of human
  action is intended" (p. 377).

## Key results

- **The distinction.** "“Decision utility” is the weight of an outcome in a
  decision. “Experienced utility” is hedonic quality, as in Bentham’s
  usage" (p. 375). The amnesic toaster: "The patient’s decision utility for
  using the two toasters is equal, but his experienced utilities are quite
  different" (p. 376).
- **Instant and remembered.** "instant utility is the pleasure or distress
  of the moment; remembered utility is the retrospective evaluation of a
  temporally extended outcome" (p. 379). Instant utility is "a measure of
  hedonic and affective experience, which can be derived from immediate
  reports of current subjective experience or from physiological indices"
  (p. 376).
- **Functions.** "Pleasure is evidently a “go” signal" and "Pain is a
  “stop” signal, which interrupts activities that are causing harm". "The
  common characteristic of the basic forms of pleasure and distress is that
  they regulate the response to the current situation" (p. 379). Hedonic
  value tracks need. Sweet food's hedonic value falls during a meal (citing
  Cabanac 1971), which is alliesthesia under another name (p. 379).
- **Peak-End and duration neglect.** "(1) Peak-End evaluation: the
  remembered utility of pleasant or unpleasant episodes is accurately
  predicted by averaging the Peak (most intense value) of instant utility
  (or disutility) recorded during an episode and the instant utility
  recorded near the end of the experience". "(1) Duration neglect: the
  duration of experiences has little or no independent effect on their
  remembered utility" (p. 381). In the colonoscopy study the Peak-End
  correlation with global rating was .67 and duration's .03 (p. 383).
- **Decisions by remembered utility.** In the cold-water study, subjects
  "simply determined which of the two memories they disliked less", and
  "the choice is made by comparing remembered utilities, without performing
  the explicit comparison that could reveal the Long trial to be a
  dominated option" (p. 387). Shown the two profiles side by side,
  subjects obey dominance (p. 387). Hence "Their decisions did not maximize
  experienced utility ([#9](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/9)), although they may have maximized remembered
  utility ([#10](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/10))" (p. 377).
- **The normative theory.** "Ordinal measurement of instant utility implies
  that any two moments of experience can be compared, to establish which of
  them carries the higher hedonic value" (p. 389). The three axioms hold if
  and only if total utility orders profiles by the integral of a
  nondecreasing value transform of instant utility that assigns 0 to 0, and
  that transform is a ratio scale (Theorem 1, p. 391). Under cardinal
  instant utility, the integral of instant utility itself (Theorem 2,
  p. 392).
- **Measurement.** Real-time measures "avoid the biases of memory and
  evaluation that affect retrospective judgments" (p. 394), and "We believe
  that experienced utility can only be understood when it is directly
  measured" (p. 395).

## Concepts

- **decision utility** — the weight of an outcome in choice, inferred from
  choices.
- **experienced utility** — hedonic quality, in Bentham's sense.
- **instant utility** — the pleasure or distress of a moment, by real-time
  report or physiology.
- **remembered utility** — the retrospective evaluation of an episode.
- **total utility** — normative: the integral over time of transformed
  instant utility.
- **predicted utility** — beliefs about future experienced utility.

## Connections

Berridge and Kringelbach ([LIT-1459](../literature.d/LIT-1459.md)) make the neural version
of the same split, with 'wanting' on the decision side and 'liking' on
the experience side. Their note that 'liking' tracks satiety and KWS's
citation of Cabanac 1971 both point to alliesthesia ([LIT-1467](../literature.d/LIT-1467.md),
unread). Thinking, Fast and Slow ([LIT-1209](../literature.d/LIT-1209.md), Deferred) restates the
experiencing and remembering selves for a general audience.

## Bearing on the record

**Is decision against experienced utility a counterexample to *valence is
potential*? Not directly, but it constrains how potential may be
measured.** The cold-water subjects pull their hands out "as soon as they
were allowed to" (p. 387). In the moment, pain wins its conflict, as the
"stop" signal that "interrupts activities that are causing harm". That is
control precedence, and KWS's functional gloss on instant utility is close
to the record's reading of potential ([TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md), [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)). The
dissociation is elsewhere: between instant utility and choices made later
between remembered episodes. Two things follow for [CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md):

- If potential is read from *deferred* choices between episodes, it
  measures remembered utility, and remembered utility diverges from
  instant utility. So layer (1) must be measured on conflicts won *during*
  the episode, not inferred from later preference. [QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md) should
  say so.
- The dissociation is not a case of strong potential with weak valence, as
  Berridge's is. It is a case of one ordering (the moment's) being
  replaced by another (memory's) across time. On the account that is a
  change of which ordering prevails, and it can be described without
  contradiction.

**For layer (2), instant utility is the record's hedonic marker in
self-report form.** It is measured in real time, apart from choice, and it
agrees across patient, observer and physician in the colonoscopy study
(pp. 383–384). Its weakness for the record is that it is report, which
makes it hard to keep separate from layer (3). KWS's own proposal to add
physiological indices is the better route.

**For layer (3) and the felt-as-self-model candidate, remembered utility
is a warning.** Remembered utility is a system's model of its own past
hedonic states, and KWS show that this model is systematically wrong: it
neglects duration and weights peak and end. If "felt" means the system
models its own potential, and the model is read off by asking the system
retrospectively, it will inherit these biases. So layer (3) cannot be
validated against retrospective report. It needs a concurrent measure, or
a comparison like Hesp et al.'s ([LIT-1468](../literature.d/LIT-1468.md)), where the
self-modelling level is removed and behaviour compared.

**On ordinality.** The record has insisted that an ordering does a lot
without a measure ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md), [CLAIM-436](../claims.d/CLAIM-436.md)). Theorem 1 gives a precise
case. Ordinal instant utility, with a distinguished neutral point and the
three axioms, yields a ratio-scale value whose integral is total utility.
So a neutral zero plus separability turns an ordering into a currency over
time. The sign clause of [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) needs a neutral point too, and the
two neutrals differ. KWS's is a constant *experience* ("neither pleasant
nor unpleasant"). Carver and Scheier's ([LIT-1441](../literature.d/LIT-1441.md)) is a reference
*rate* of progress. KWS's pails example, where the neutral stimulus shifts
with adaptation, is compatible with a reference course. It does not
decide between them.

## Limitations

The experiments are short, passive and hedonically homogeneous episodes, as
the authors say (p. 388). The cold-water sample is small. The normative
theory assumes constant calibration of the instant scale across compared
episodes, which the authors grant fails for radically different situations
(p. 390). The paper does not address non-human animals or organizations.

## Open questions

- Is there a behavioural, non-report measure of instant utility, a
  within-episode analogue of taste reactivity, that could serve layer (2)
  in humans?
- Does a system's remembered utility count as a model of its own
  potential? If so, is the felt-as-self-model candidate a claim about
  remembered or about instant valence?
