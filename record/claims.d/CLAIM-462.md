# CLAIM-462: An arrangement of institutions can keep, or increase, its capacity for effective action while its capacity to be effectively corrected by others degrades, because the two are maintained by different relations, so the first is no evidence of the second

## The claim

A8: "an organization of authority has at least two different capacities:
the capacity to **produce effective actions** and the capacity to **remain
answerable for those actions through other institutions**. These
capacities need not develop together." And: "A complex institutional
system can preserve or even increase its capacity for effective action
while losing its capacity for effective correction." A9 separated four
capacities that "are not different degrees of one quantity": effective
action, effective restraint (preventing a change before it happens),
effective correction (reversing or remedying it once it has) and
effective reconstruction (restoring the capacities, relations and
opportunities it damaged).

It rests on two claims inside one organization. [CLAIM-099](CLAIM-099.md): maintaining
a regime of effective action is not maintaining the capacity to govern
among regimes. [CLAIM-422](CLAIM-422.md): a corrective act, a procedure and a maintained
capacity to correct are three things, so a correction can be recorded and
not maintained. This claim says the capacity to be corrected is also held
in relations the arrangement's effective action does not need: a court's
orders reaching an enforcer who will act on them, information reaching a
reviewer, a foreign partner's cooperation being requirable. A9: "The
institutions are formally continuous. The organization through which they
exercised mutual constraint is not necessarily continuous."

In [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)'s terms, the capacity to be corrected is the correctability, for
corrections of the acting institution's acts, of the arrangement that holds
both it and the institutions that would correct it. The claim is that this
can fall while the institution's capacity for effective action holds.

The cases show the pattern, not its frequency. At USIP ([CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md)) the
executive's capacity to take control was effective, and the court's
capacity to restore control was weighed against the risk the taking had
created. At CECOT ([CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md)) a transfer was coordinated across two
governments, and its reversal had no comparable procedure. At the East
Wing ([CASE-170](../cases.d/CASE-170.md)) demolition was quick and review slow. A9: "the mechanisms
that produce a harmful outcome may operate much faster than the mechanisms
authorized to evaluate and reverse it", which is [CLAIM-423](CLAIM-423.md)'s timescales
deciding who holds authority.

The degradation is hard to see. A8: it "can become visible only when a
court orders an action reversed, a person seeks release from custody, an
institution attempts to recover its former functions, or a community seeks
restoration of rights after displacement", which is [CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md)'s method:
arrangements alike in ordinary operation part under the intervention the
capacity is for. It is the same reason [TERM-069](../terms.d/TERM-069.md)'s probe family must include
the probes that matter: "challenge", "noncompliance" and "restoration" as
well as "ordinary action" (A9).

## What it does not say

- **That self-governance is enough, or that one corrector is the ideal.**
  A9: "An institution can govern itself effectively while resisting every
  attempt by others to hold it answerable", and the ideal "may instead be
  a differentiated arrangement of organizations whose authority over one
  another is limited, conditional, and independently contestable"; a
  government modelled as one agent can hide the distribution that makes
  correction possible ([CLAIM-418](CLAIM-418.md)).
- **Which corrections are owed.** A9: a model of remedial reachability
  "would not decide which institutional actions are legitimate", nor that
  "every legally valid court order should be implemented by any means
  available" ([CLAIM-348](CLAIM-348.md)).
- **That effective answerability means challengers win.** A9: "A
  functioning system can legitimately reject challenges"; the requirement
  is that correction "should depend on appropriately constituted and
  reviewable procedures—not simply on whether the actor exercising power
  consents to being corrected".
- **That it is new.** Cover ([LIT-1601](../literature.d/LIT-1601.md)), read in [NOTE-1384](../notes.d/NOTE-1384.md), holds a
  version of it for courts: a judgment corrects only through the roles
  that carry it into deeds, a different relation from the one that makes
  it right. He does not treat the correction of one institution by
  others. Pettit ([LIT-1614](../literature.d/LIT-1614.md)) on contestability, and Hillebrecht
  ([LIT-1607](../literature.d/LIT-1607.md)) and Rosenberg ([LIT-1615](../literature.d/LIT-1615.md)) on compliance and courts' limits,
  are Unavailable and may hold it already; A9's preferred next step was
  the question first ([QUESTION-278](../questions.d/QUESTION-278.md)), "rather than prematurely declare"
  a result.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Cover, read, holds part of it

Of the four works "What it does not say" lists as unread, Cover, [LIT-1601](../literature.d/LIT-1601.md),
is now read ([NOTE-1384](../notes.d/NOTE-1384.md)); Pettit, [LIT-1614](../literature.d/LIT-1614.md), Hillebrecht, [LIT-1607](../literature.d/LIT-1607.md), and
Rosenberg, [LIT-1615](../literature.d/LIT-1615.md), are Unavailable. Cover holds a version of this claim
for courts. A court's capacity to correct by judgment depends on the role
structure that carries judicial words into deeds through others, which is
a different relation from the one that makes the judgment right. So the
doubt that the claim is new is partly borne out. Cover does not treat the
correction of one institution by others, which is this claim's subject.
His account fits [CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md), one of the claim's grounds, closely: the
police and the security contractor acted on the executive's papers, and a
court's word could become a deed only if they would act on its. The claim
is unchanged and stays Proposed.

## Note of 2026-11-15: restated, and an arithmetic route, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"

this claim three times without it. A30 §10, after reading the record, offered
"one new claim for consideration": "Where an arrangement can foreclose
relevant alternatives before authorized challenges can alter its course, the
arrangement may retain nominal mechanisms of oversight while losing
effective restraint. This loss can occur without any change in the content
of the governing rules or the formal allocation of authority." A itself said
it "would need to be checked against CLAIM-462, [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md), [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)", and
it is this claim with [CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)'s fixed standards. A35's
"strongest formulation", that a system "can retain effective direction
while losing effective self-correction", is the same. Neither is filed.

What the exchange adds is a route. [CLAIM-705](CLAIM-705.md), filed from A35 §3:
when challengeable decisions arrive faster than the corrective process
resolves them, at most a share μ/λ of challenges can be resolved in time,
whatever the order, while every affected party keeps the formal right. The
relations that carry action (the rate of decisions) and those that carry
correction (the rate of resolution) are different, as this claim says, and
raising the first degrades the second without touching it. A35 §8 states
the claim as a shrinking reachable set of corrections,
C(s_{t+1}, B, H) ⊊ C(s_t, B, H), with a required objective dropping out of
it while the standard stays the same; that is [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)
and [CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md) in one line, and is mapped, not filed.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Cuéllar, read

Cuéllar's essay on presidential adjudication ([LIT-2706](../literature.d/LIT-2706.md), [NOTE-2029](../notes.d/NOTE-2029.md)) is a
domain account of this claim's pattern for the United States presidency,
independent of the exchanges the claim came from. In it, the executive's
capacity for effective, particular action grows: more tools, reaching "more
people and organizations, in more specific ways, and in more situations."
(p. 604), with removal power, information technology and DOGE. Meanwhile
the relations that corrected it weaken: norms that "are quickly eroding."
(p. 617), partisan congressional oversight, lost agency independence, and a
presumption of regularity whose premise of dutiful service is in doubt.
Review stays limited "even as the importance and scope of this practice
grows" (p. 599). He also holds, as Cover does, that "courts lack direct
authority to enforce" (p. 647), and he treats discretion and accountability
as separable: they "can coexist" (p. 652). He does not argue that the two
capacities are maintained by different relations; that step remains the
claim's. Relation: [THEORY-1923](../theory.d/THEORY-1923.md) corroborates. No change of status.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Bovens and Zouridis, read

The system-level bureaucracy is "zenith in legal rational authority" (§5.1): its capacity to execute the

## Note of 2026-11-17: oversight pushed past its operating capacity by volume, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-731 CLAIM-730 — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17 -->

A33 §1 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (2026-11-15), after the
owner pointed to the record's resource-boundedness (U16): "**Formal
oversight capacity is not the same as resource-bounded oversight
capacity.**" "An actor need not abolish oversight if it can reliably push
oversight beyond its operational capacity." A33 §4: an agency moving from
a few hundred decisions a week to tens of thousands, with its appeals
unchanged, "has accelerated one timescale without proportionately
increasing another's capacity", so "the institution can generate errors
faster than affected people can discover and challenge them." A23 §3 gives
the human-review form: "If a system generates more requests than humans can
meaningfully evaluate, adding more human checkpoints may simply produce
ceremonial oversight."

That is a route by which this claim's two capacities come apart which the
claim does not name: the volume or rate of action, with no change in
anyone's authority. A33 §1: "That need not even involve deliberate
obstruction. The mismatch can be a structural property of the two
institutions." The claim's edge, that it is about relations and not about
any government's conduct, stands. The cost-side
statement is [CLAIM-731](CLAIM-731.md) (execution friction against contestation
friction); the timescale statement is [CLAIM-730](CLAIM-730.md).

No status changes.
