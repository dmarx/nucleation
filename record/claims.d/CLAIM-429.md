# CLAIM-429: Where interpretation decides the occasions for cooperation and cooperation's outcomes revise interpretation, an intervention can change the loop's state or its coupling: a favourable exception changes the state and leaves the rule that judges exceptions as it was, while a change in how far outcomes may revise the governing interpretation changes the coupling, and where the coupling does not depend on the state, only the second changes how later evidence is taken

## The claim

A5 read prejudice as a loop the record already models. "Their expectation
changes how they interact with those people. They may avoid them,
withhold cooperation, interpret ambiguity negatively, or offer fewer
opportunities to demonstrate trustworthiness. Those behaviors alter the
evidence available in subsequent encounters." That is [CLAIM-297](CLAIM-297.md)'s
coupling of meaning and influence, and [CLAIM-406](CLAIM-406.md)'s structural pre-emptive
injustice is the same loop deciding whose word is sought.

In [CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md)'s linearization the loop has a state (the current biases m
and a) and a coupling (β, γ, how far each shapes the other's update). An
intervention can act on either.

- **On the state.** A friend from the disfavoured group is met and liked.
  The person's response to the friend changes; the rule by which the
  group's members are judged does not. A4: "A prejudiced person can
  befriend a member of a targeted group while preserving their prejudice
  toward everyone else. They simply classify the friend as an exception."
- **On the coupling.** The encounter changes how far what happens in
  cooperation is allowed to revise the interpretation that governs the
  category. Then later evidence from other members is taken differently.
  [CASE-146](../cases.d/CASE-146.md) sets the two side by side.

[CLAIM-330](CLAIM-330.md) says only the loop can destabilize the symmetric state, and that
scoring independently of the interpretation (γ ≈ 0) removes the
instability. In that linearization a change of state alone dies away while
βγ < (1 − α)(1 − δ), and there only a change of coupling lasts. Above the
threshold, a change of state along the growing direction is amplified with
the coupling unchanged. CLAIM-330 holds nothing about the bounded system's
attractors, where a large enough change of state could carry the loop to
another one. So the claim does not say a durable change must act on the
coupling. Whether the durable changes after contact needed one is what the
defeat condition asks.

## What it does not say

- **Not a threshold for prejudice.** A5: the stability result "does not
  establish a threshold for real-world prejudice reduction." The claim
  borrows the model's distinction between state and coupling, not its
  numbers.
- **Not that a coupling change is always good.** A change in how far
  outcomes revise the rule can make a regime more responsive or entrench
  a new one; [CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md) is the case where it is done from outside.
- **Not that the two can always be told apart.** [CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md) says matched
  ordinary trajectories can hide such differences, and the defeater is
  the test.
