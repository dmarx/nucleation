summary: >-
  Friedkin (2011/2012). A formal theory of how power evolves in a group's influence network over a sequence of issues. Each issue is settled by the Friedkin–Johnsen process, with members anchored on their initial positions; by "reflected appraisal", each member's self-weight on the next issue is set to their share of total control over the last, with relative interpersonal weights fixed. In 50 four-person laboratory groups over five issues, prior control predicts the next self-weight. In simulations of 100,000 random networks, over 98% evolve to a single leader whose preferences control outcomes through intermediaries; the rest end with two.

# LIT-1404: A Formal Theory of Reflected Appraisals in the Evolution of Power

Friedkin, N. E. (2011). A formal theory of reflected appraisals in the evolution of power. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 56(4), 501–529. doi:10.1177/0001839212441349.

Read in full on 2026-10-29 ([NOTE-tmpegm16](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpegm16.md)). One account was drawn from
it, with [LIT-1146](LIT-1146.md): [THEORY-tmpbnint](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpbnint.md).

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it as an external result bearing on [CASE-092](../cases.d/CASE-092.md), the minimal coupled meaning–influence model: here influence weights are updated from appraised past control, and the dynamics concentrate power. It is the earlier statement of the mechanism formalized in [LIT-1146](LIT-1146.md). The exchange's description gives no figures to check; the abstract's result, that the loop drives power to concentrate, is what [CASE-092](../cases.d/CASE-092.md) should be compared with.

Note of 2026-10-29, from the reading ([NOTE-tmpegm16](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpegm16.md)): it is the earlier
statement of the update rule, not of [LIT-1146](LIT-1146.md)'s model. Both set a
member's next self-weight to their control over the last issue, but this
paper settles each issue by the Friedkin–Johnsen process, anchored on
initial positions, and [LIT-1146](LIT-1146.md) by DeGroot consensus. The two predict
different end states: here a single leader in almost every group, there
one equilibrium ordered by centrality that is autocratic only for a
star. A re-run on the same random complete networks on 2026-10-29 bore
out the difference; the centrality ordering was not checked.
So the comparison [CASE-092](../cases.d/CASE-092.md) asks for has two baselines, not one.
