summary: >-
  Chorin & Lu (PNAS 2015). Proposes model reduction done entirely in discrete time: the resolved variables are advanced by a fixed numerical scheme plus a discrepancy sequence computed exactly from data, and the discrepancy is fitted as a NARMAX time series (autoregression in itself, lags of functions of the resolved variables, moving average in past innovations). Memory is thereby carried by finitely many lags. The connection to Mori–Zwanzig is drawn by analogy, not derived. On two-scale Lorenz 96 with ε = 0.5, a fit with one autoregressive lag, one or two exogenous lags and at most one moving-average term reproduces the statistics of x₁ better than a polynomial-plus-AR(1) closure, and at observation spacing 0.05 extends the lead time at anomaly correlation 0.6 from 2 to 4. Nothing is proved and no error bound is given.

# LIT-1304: Discrete approach to stochastic parametrization and dimension reduction in nonlinear dynamics

Chorin, A. J. & Lu, F. (2015). Discrete approach to stochastic parametrization
and dimension reduction in nonlinear dynamics. *Proceedings of the National
Academy of Sciences* 112(32): 9804–9809. doi:10.1073/pnas.1512080112.
arXiv:1503.08766 ("A discrete approach to stochastic parametrization and
dimensional reduction in nonlinear dynamics").

Read in full in [NOTE-1028](../notes.d/NOTE-1028.md).

## Key takeaways

- Working in discrete time removes the need to differentiate data: the
  discrepancy between the data and one step of a chosen integrator is exact,
  and it absorbs the integrator's error together with the unresolved
  variables' effect.
- A NARMAX model of that discrepancy is a finite-dimensional Markovian
  augmentation of the resolved variable: its state is a few past values of
  x, of z and of the innovations ξ. The moving-average terms let finitely
  many coordinates carry a memory that is non-zero at every lag.
- On Lorenz 96 without scale separation, one or two lags suffice to
  reproduce the resolved statistics and to improve forecasts
  ([THEORY-818](../theory.d/THEORY-818.md)). That is shown numerically for one system, not proved.
- The relation to Mori–Zwanzig is an analogy. The paper neither derives
  NARMAX from the MZ identity nor bounds the error of the fitted model.

## Standing in the record

Filed for [QUESTION-222](../questions.d/QUESTION-222.md). It is the record's first reading of a discrete,
data-driven closure that carries memory in a finite set of lags, beside the
analytical Mori–Zwanzig readings ([LIT-1245](LIT-1245.md), [LIT-1253](LIT-1253.md)) and the turbulence
closure of [LIT-1259](LIT-1259.md) ([THEORY-760](../theory.d/THEORY-760.md)). It supplies no bound. The record's own
check in [NOTE-1028](../notes.d/NOTE-1028.md) shows, for the linear Gaussian class, why a finite
discrete representation can be exact where the memory kernel never vanishes.
