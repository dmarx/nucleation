# LIT-756: Formality Considered Harmful: Experiences, Emerging Themes, and Directions on the Use of Formal Representations in Interactive Systems

Frank M. Shipman III and Catherine C. Marshall (1999), *Computer Supported Cooperative Work* 8(4):333–352 — DOI-10.1023/A:1008716330212

Read in full on 2026-10-30 (NOTE-tmps2gbq), in the authors' web version
on Shipman's pages, an earlier text than the journal's.

## Key takeaways

*Read in the authors' web version of about 1993–94 (NOTE-tmps2gbq), not
the 1999 journal text.*

- Across five kinds of system for intellectual work, users would not, or
  could not, chunk, label and link as the formalism required, and
  "there is always information that falls between the cracks, no matter
  how well thought out the formal representation is" (§2.2).
- They are often right to resist: formalizing costs cognitive overhead,
  asks for tacit knowledge to be stated, commits people to structure
  before they understand the task, and gives a structure that suits one
  person, group or task and not the next (§3).
- Designers should formalize what the task essentially needs, infer
  structure from informal material, and support incremental
  formalization and restructuring "as tasks are reconceptualized" (§§4–5).
