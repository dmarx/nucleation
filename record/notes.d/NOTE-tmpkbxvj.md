# NOTE-tmpkbxvj: Bickhard — Autonomy, Function, and Representation

## Contribution

A graded account of autonomy for open far-from-equilibrium systems, used
to ground two things in the system's current organisation rather than in
its history or in an observer: normative function, at the level of
self-maintenance, and representation, at the level of recursive
self-maintenance. The representation account ("interactivism") makes
content an implicit presupposition of an indication that some further
interaction will succeed, so error is defined, and detectable, from inside
the system.

## Key insight

What is at stake for a far-from-equilibrium system is its own existence:
if it stops getting what it needs, it goes to equilibrium and ceases. That
asymmetry is enough to make some of its processes count for or against it,
with nobody watching. And a system that has to choose what to do next,
depending on what kind of environment it is in, is in effect betting that
the chosen interaction will work. The bet can fail, and the failure shows
up inside the system. That is the seed of truth value.

## Assumptions

- **Naturalism.** No ad hoc substances or levels "beyond which no further
  questions can be asked".
- **Emergence must be ontological and causally efficacious**, not a
  matter of how an observer talks. A merely ascribed property leads to
  circularity or a regress of glossing agents.
- **Process metaphysics.** Kim's exclusion argument is "valid" but
  "unsound": it assumes particles, and quantum field theory gives only
  processes. A process exists only in some organisation, so organisation
  is a legitimate locus of causal power at every scale.
- **Only current state is causally efficacious.** History acts only
  through its effects on current state. This premise drives every
  objection to etiological accounts.
- **Types.** "Having a function" depends on what type a part and its
  system belong to. The paper says type assignment cannot rest on gloss,
  and defers the full account to Christensen & Bickhard (unread).

## Key results

- **Two forms of stability.** Energy-well stability (an atom, a rock)
  needs no exchange with the environment. Far-from-equilibrium stability
  (a candle flame) requires the process not to reach equilibrium, so such
  processes "are necessarily open processes".
- **A ladder of autonomy.** Autonomy is "actively contributing to their
  own persistence", and graded.
  - Minimal: a chemical bath kept going by external pumps.
  - Self-maintenant: a candle flame keeps itself above combustion
    temperature, vaporises wax and drives convection that brings oxygen.
    It "cannot seek more fuel when the candle is almost gone".
  - Energy-directing infrastructure with no alternatives: primitive cells
    with one metabolism. This is said to be the minimal autonomy of
    Christensen, Collier and Hooker.
  - Recursively self-maintenant: the system deploys "different processes
    depending on differences in the environmental circumstances". The
    bacterium that swims up a sugar gradient and tumbles going down it.
    Stability holds "within certain ranges of changes in conditions".
    This needs switching infrastructure, stable on the switching time
    scale, with energetic and informational aspects.
- **Function from self-maintenance.** Contributions to self-maintenance
  "are, in that sense, functional for that system". Serving a function "is
  necessarily relative to the system whose maintenance is being contributed
  to": "The gut of a parasite will serve functions for the parasite, but be
  dysfunctional for the host." The normative asymmetry between function
  and dysfunction comes from the thermodynamic asymmetry between
  far-from-equilibrium and equilibrium processes.
- **Having a function.** A part has a function if it is of a type that
  tends to serve that function for the type of the whole, so a kidney
  that does not filter blood still has that function. Closeness to being
  able to serve it is graded (a damaged kidney versus scar tissue).
  "Dynamic presupposition" grounds the typing: a process presupposes
  other conditions to succeed, and the component typified as meeting the
  presupposition has the function, "whether or not it actually does so".
- **Against etiological function.** Millikan's swamp lion has no
  functions on her account, though it is molecule for molecule a zoo
  lion; so function "is not constituted by any property of current state",
  and "the etiological approach gives us a model of function that is
  causally epiphenomenal". History can explain why a kidney exists, not
  what having a function is.
- **Representation from recursive self-maintenance.** Switching needs
  differentiations: interactions whose internal outcome (A or B) depends
  on the environment. Differentiations "per se have no content". Content
  belongs to the indication that a further interaction Q is appropriate
  after A. The indication is an implicit predication ("this environment
  … are Q-type environments") that can be false, and "if false, a
  predication whose falsity may be detectable by the system itself".
  Content is the implicitly defined set of conditions under which Q would
  succeed.
- **Against information semantics, Fodor and Dretske.**
  - Informational correspondence cannot be in error: if it holds the
    representation is correct, if not there is none.
  - Fodor's asymmetric dependence is met by a receptor that crank docks in
    as dopamine does; it "captures functional error just as well as it
    captures representational error". It also rests on counterfactuals
    not definable in current state.
  - Dretske's recruitment of C as a cause of M "because of what it
    indicates about F" is explanatory, not causal, so it appeals to an
    explaining observer; and it rests on learning history.
  - None of these allows system-detectable error, which organisms show.
- **More complex representation.** Selection among several indicated
  interactions needs goals; goal conditions can be differentiated without
  being represented (a TOTE organisation). Indicated internal outcomes
  check the indication's truth from inside and so support error-guided
  behaviour and learning. Branching and iterated indications form webs
  said to ground objects and abstractions.
- **Conclusion.** Cognition is grounded in "recursively self-maintenant
  autonomy". Endnote 1: autopoiesis "emphasizes independence from the
  environment, while autonomy emphasizes the ability to make use of the
  environment".

## Concepts

- **Far-from-equilibrium stability.** Persistence of a process that would
  cease at equilibrium, and so needs exchange with its environment.
- **Autonomy.** Active contribution to one's own persistence; graded.
- **Self-maintenance.** Contributing to the conditions of one's own
  far-from-equilibrium existence (a flame).
- **Recursive self-maintenance.** Maintaining the property of being
  self-maintenant across changes in the environment, by switching among
  processes.
- **Infrastructure.** Structure stable on the time scale of switching,
  which carries out the switching and directs energy.
- **Serving a function.** Contributing to the self-maintenance of a
  specified system.
- **Having a function.** Being of a type that is presupposed to serve a
  function for the type of the whole, whether or not it does.
- **Dynamic presupposition.** What a process needs to hold in order to
  succeed.
- **Differentiation.** An internal outcome that depends on the
  environment; it has no content of its own.
- **Indication.** A functional link from a differentiation to a further
  interaction as possible or appropriate; it implicitly predicates
  conditions of success, and that is its content.

## Connections

It builds on Christensen, Collier and Hooker's autonomy theory, Campbell's
evolutionary epistemology (the bacterium) and Moreno & Ruiz-Mirazo on
metabolism, and argues against Millikan, Godfrey-Smith, Fodor, Dretske,
Cummins, the physical symbol system hypothesis and connectionism. Mossio,
Saborido & Moreno ([LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md), [NOTE-444](NOTE-444.md)) name Christensen & Bickhard as
allies they improve on with explicit conditions, and Barandiaran's paper
on malfunction ([NOTE-tmpliz3l](NOTE-tmpliz3l.md)) builds on the same organizational line.

## Bearing on the record

**On [CLAIM-tmpzn7l2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpzn7l2.md): support for its premise, by a different route from
the one it cites.** The objection holds that norms an organization appears
to maintain are the modeller's when its operators maximise objectives the
modeller fixed. Bickhard's account says the same about function. A
function is relative to the system whose far-from-equilibrium maintenance
it serves. A function that exists only in an observer's ascription or
explanation is not natural, by the regress argument, and that is his
objection to Dretske. A reward the modeller writes is not the operator's
or the coalition's persistence condition, so on this account it confers no
function on anything in the simulation. The paper does not discuss
designed systems, and it ends saying the design of artificial cognitive
systems "will have to accommodate these conditions". The application to
reward-maximising operators is the record's.

**On [CLAIM-tmpspyay](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpspyay.md): vocabulary, not evidence.** Recursive
self-maintenance is "maintaining the property of being self-maintenant"
by switching among processes. That is governing among regimes, the second
of [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)'s three layers. The third, transforming the organization that
does the switching, does not appear: the bacterium's switching
infrastructure is fixed. The paper says nothing about selves or about
authority among processes. Like Di Paolo ([LIT-1017](../literature.d/LIT-1017.md)) it supplies a ladder
on which the thesis could be stated, without showing the layers come apart.

**On [QUESTION-tmpiyoc5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpiyoc5.md): a standard internal to the larger organization,
with a limit.**

- The parasite sentence gives the shape of an answer. A sub-system with
  its own self-maintenance has functions for itself that can be
  dysfunctions for the whole that contains it. An authority structure
  that reproduces itself is functional for itself. Whether it is
  dysfunctional for the organization is fixed by the organization's own
  persistence conditions, not by goals assigned from outside.
- Having a function adds malfunction proper: a part typified, by what the
  whole's processes presuppose, as meeting a condition it does not meet.
  An authority structure presupposed to route decisions well and not
  doing so would malfunction in this sense.
- The limit. The only standard is contribution to persistence. Captured
  authority that keeps the organization alive, or makes it more robust,
  is not dysfunctional on this account. That is [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)'s point, and
  Bickhard does not dispute it: nothing in the paper says what ought to
  persist.
- The account of types, which carries malfunction, is deferred to
  Christensen & Bickhard and is the paper's weakest step.

**On [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md): no conflict.** The norms are function and dysfunction
relative to a system's persistence. The paper calls this "normative" and
never treats it as value.

**On [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md): a disagreement about functions, not about the
demarcation.** Mossio, Saborido & Moreno, a source of [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md), hold
that flames "do instantiate closure, but their components do not have
functions". Bickhard gives the flame self-maintenance and says its
contributions are "functional for that system". Both can be read as true
of different notions: Bickhard's flame has serving-a-function but no
infrastructure, so nothing in it has a function as a typed part. His
infrastructure tiers sit where [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md)'s differentiation does. But
the two do disagree on whether self-maintenance alone yields norms, and
[THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md)'s title (that far-from-equilibrium self-maintenance is not
the line between organisms and flames) is not contradicted, since
Bickhard draws no organism line.

**On [THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md) and [LIT-1017](../literature.d/LIT-1017.md).** Bickhard's bacterium is the chemotaxis
case of [LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md) and the case Di Paolo uses. His split between
self-maintenance and recursive self-maintenance is close to Di Paolo's
between robustness and adaptivity, five years earlier. They differ on
where norms start. Di Paolo says conservation gives only an observer's
norm; Bickhard gives the flame function. The difference is smaller than
it looks: on Bickhard's account error the system can detect arrives only
with recursive self-maintenance, which is where Di Paolo puts norms the
system can use. Bickhard has no individuality condition and no
interactional-asymmetry criterion; the system's boundary is taken as
given.

## Limitations

- Conceptual throughout. No model, no formal definition of
  far-from-equilibrium, contribution or switching.
- The anti-Kim argument is a page of assertion about quantum field theory.
- *Contribution to self-maintenance* is not given a measure, and the
  flame's "several active contributions" are listed, not derived.
- Having a function needs types, and the account of typing by dynamic
  presupposition is sketched and deferred.
- The premise that only current state is efficacious is assumed, and
  etiological theorists reject it as a constraint on what function is.
- Representation beyond the primitive level (objects, numbers, language)
  is asserted with references to earlier books.

## Open questions

- Can *contribution to self-maintenance* be stated so that it is
  measurable in a model, for instance as the effect on a viability
  kernel? That would make the parasite criterion applicable to the
  record's authority-capture cases.
- Does a sub-system that switches among self-maintaining processes inside
  an organization (a faction, an entrenched office) have representation
  in Bickhard's sense, and functions of its own that conflict with the
  whole's?
- Is the flame's serving-a-function the same thing Mossio et al. deny it,
  or a weaker notion? A side-by-side reading of Christensen & Bickhard
  (2002) would settle it.
