# LIT-719: Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems

Elinor Ostrom (2009), Prize Lecture, 8 December 2009, *Les Prix Nobel*;
reprinted in *American Economic Review* 100(3):641–672 (2010) —
DOI-10.1257/aer.100.3.641

## Key takeaways

- **Polycentricity.** "Many centers of decision making that are formally
  independent of each other"; whether they function as a system "is an
  empirical question in particular cases" (V. Ostrom, Tiebout & Warren
  1961, quoted §2A). In US metropolitan policing, areas with many
  autonomous producers had more efficient producers, and duplication was
  rare: "complexity is not the same as chaos".
- **Design principles.** The design principles of *Governing the Commons*
  ([LIT-737](LIT-737.md)) are given in an updated form (§4E): clear user and
  resource boundaries; rules congruent with local conditions and costs
  proportional to benefits; collective-choice arrangements; monitoring of
  users and of the resource by users or those accountable to them;
  graduated sanctions; fast, cheap, local conflict resolution; recognition
  of the right to make rules; and nested enterprises. About two thirds of
  over 100 studies reviewed by Cox et al. (2009) confirm them.
- **Experiments.** Without communication, subjects overuse a common pool
  beyond the Nash prediction. Face-to-face "cheap talk" raises joint
  returns. Costly sanctioning is used, and raises gross but lowers net
  returns. Groups that chose their own sanctioning system reached 90% of
  optimum (§5A). An externally imposed rule with weak inspection crowded
  out cooperation in a Colombian field experiment (§5B).
- **Field evidence.** Farmer-governed irrigation in Nepal outperformed
  government systems on all three measures. In forests, local monitoring
  predicts forest condition, and formal protected status does not (§6).
- **No panaceas** (§8). What matters is how an arrangement "fits the local
  ecology, how specific rules are developed and adapted over time, and
  whether users consider the system to be legitimate and equitable".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as the readable source for an
essay's citation of "Ostrom's work on differentiated and polycentric
governance" (§8.2). The account is [THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md).

The essay cites Ostrom for distributed monitoring and authority. Principles
4 and 8 are the most specific support for that. But the principles concern
users governing a resource they depend on. A corporation governing itself
is a further application the essay would need to argue for.
