# CLAIM-728: In an incomplete contract the party holding residual rights of control decides the contingencies the contract did not cover, so a relationship fair in every specified term can still leave one party's terms at the other's discretion, and that discretion is exercised, and seen, only when an uncovered contingency arises

## The claim

The owner's U24: "labor theory, contract theory, and auction theory are
feeling relevant". A53 §2 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b) took
contract theory first. Contracts cannot specify every future
circumstance; Grossman and Hart (1986), then Hart and Moore (1990), argue
that ownership matters because it allocates "**residual control
rights**: authority over uses and decisions not already settled by the
contract." A calls this "almost a direct economic formulation of our
second-order agency thesis", and puts the question:

> **When circumstances arise that the existing agreement doesn't cover, who gets to decide what happens next?**

A's example: an employer and a worker agree compensation, hours and
duties; a staffing emergency arrives. Who decides that the worker must
stay, whether the emergency earns extra pay, whether refusing is
misconduct, and how future emergencies are handled? "An institution can
appear to distribute responsibilities fairly under ordinary conditions
while allocating nearly all residual authority to one party. That
authority may remain invisible until the relationship is perturbed."

**Read in the record's terms.** The holder of residual control has the
capacity to set the terms on which the other party acts in every
contingency the contract left open. Where that capacity is not answerable
to the other party under a declared standard, that is [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)'s
domination, and it is held whatever the specified terms say. A53 §6 takes
it one level up: "**Second-order bargaining:** Who decides which terms can
be offered, which alternatives are available, and how future disputes are
resolved?", and "**Who has the authority to change the bargaining
mechanism after others have invested their labor, commitments, or
resources in reliance on it?**"

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md)** (Proposed): the same magnitude of dependence runs either
  way, and what sorts it is who controls the terms of supply, exit and
  renegotiation. Residual control is one allocation of exactly that
  control, fixed in the contract's form rather than its terms.
- **[CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md)** (Proposed): conditioned and negotiating processes part
  only under intervention, dissent or in their history. Authority that "may remain
  invisible until the relationship is perturbed" is that form: two contracts with the same
  specified terms part at the first uncovered contingency.
- **Hold-up**, which A53 §2 also relays (relationship-specific investment
  weakens the investor's later position), is the dynamic by which an
  allocation of residual control comes to matter more over time. It is
  noted on [CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md), not filed here.
- **[CLAIM-716](CLAIM-716.md)**: A55 §3 keeps the two apart: in breach-induced
  hold-up "the breacher creates a new bargaining position through an
  unauthorized action rather than simply exercising a residual right over
  an unspecified contingency." This claim is the authorized case; that one
  is control acquired by violation.
- **[CLAIM-744](CLAIM-744.md)**: when a deferred reward's delivery is left open,
  residual control over it is control over whether waiting is rational.

## What it does not say

- Not that residual control is domination. It is when it is not
  answerable to the other party under a declared standard ([TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md));
  duties of good faith, labour law or an arbiter can make it answerable.
- Not that Grossman and Hart make this argument. Their question is which
  allocation of control gives efficient incentives to invest; the
  normative reading is A's.
- Not that the works were read. Grossman and Hart, "The Costs and
  Benefits of Ownership", *Journal of Political Economy* 94(4), 1986,
  doi:10.1086/261404; Hart and Moore, "Property Rights and the Nature of
  the Firm", *Journal of Political Economy* 98(6), 1990,
  doi:10.1086/261729 (the part-3 ledger). Neither is held.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Grossman and Hart, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1985 — new theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Grossman and Hart 1986, read ([NOTE-2063](../notes.d/NOTE-2063.md), [THEORY-1985](../theory.d/THEORY-1985.md)), which the claim names as its source and has not read: it corroborates the first clause ([THEORY-1985](../theory.d/THEORY-1985.md) carries `corroborates`): residual rights of control are "the right to control all aspects of the asset that have not been explicitly given away by contract" (§I.B), and §V extends this to employment ("the employer has many of the residual rights of control"). It carries less than the claim's later clauses. (a) In the model the holder does not simply decide the uncovered contingency: the parties renegotiate to the efficient use, and ownership sets the split, "The distribution of ex post surplus, however, will be sensitive to ownership rights" (§I.C). (b) The allocation acts before any contingency arises: investment is distorted "in spite of the fact that the noncooperative outcome never occurs!" (§III.A). On their account the discretion is felt ex ante, through anticipation, not "exercised, and seen, only when an uncovered contingency arises". That is close to the claim's own defeat condition (discretion "exercised and visible in ordinary operation"), though anticipation is not exercise. The claim's "What it does not say" (their question is efficiency) is accurate. An ARG is needed: Grossman and Hart to this claim, saying what the work carries (the definition) and what it does not (unilateral decision; visibility only at the contingency), and whether the last clause should be revised to allow ex ante effects. No status changes here; the ARG decides.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Hart's Nobel lecture, read in part
<!-- inactive-ok-file: NOTE-2052 THEORY-1979 LIT-2742 — new reading (Skimmed) and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed; Hart and Moore Unfetched -->

Hart 2016, read in part ([NOTE-2052](../notes.d/NOTE-2052.md), [THEORY-1979](../theory.d/THEORY-1979.md)), as the neighbour of Hart and Moore 1990 ([LIT-2742](../literature.d/LIT-2742.md), Unfetched): it corroborates the first clause in an author's words, "who has the right to decide about the missing things? We called this right the residual control or decision right" (§2) ([THEORY-1979](../theory.d/THEORY-1979.md) carries `corroborates`). The veto example is a clean case of discretion over an uncovered contingency: "Now that the manager is an employee she has to get permission to implement the idea from her boss: the owner of the power plant has veto power." It qualifies the claim's worker example: property rights theory "emphasizes control over physical (more generally non-human) assets", and "purchasing the mine would not be worth much if the coal mine manager is indispensable", so power over a worker runs through the assets she needs and her replaceability, not the employment label. And "the power plant does anticipate that it will be at the mercy of the coal mine": the discretion shapes conduct before the contingency, as in Grossman and Hart. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-18: what holds it back

Grossman and Hart ([THEORY-1985](../theory.d/THEORY-1985.md)) and Hart's lecture ([THEORY-1979](../theory.d/THEORY-1979.md)), read, say the holder bargains rather than decides and that residual control acts ex ante, through anticipation; that stands against the last clause, "exercised, and seen, only when an uncovered contingency arises", and is unanswered, since the ARG the note of 2026-11-17 asks for is not filed. It is also stated in [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md), which is not adopted.

[ARG-210](../arguments.d/ARG-210.md) is Rejected as an inference from those readings to this
claim as stated: they establish the residual-control definition (clause
1, corroboration not a footing), qualify clause 2 (ownership confers
bargaining power over the assets the other needs, not discretion at will
over a person), and tell against clause 3, "exercised, and seen, only when
an uncovered contingency arises", locating the effect of residual control
*ex ante*, through anticipation, before and apart from any uncovered
contingency. No status changes here; whether clause 3 should be revised is
the owner's call, between two readings:

- **Alternative A (widen the timing).** Revise clause 3 to say that the
  discretion's effect is felt *ex ante*, through the other party's
  anticipation of who will decide the uncovered contingencies, and becomes
  most visible, but not only operative, when such a contingency arises.
  This takes the economic readings as describing the same mechanism the
  claim names and corrects its timing; the claim then no longer separates
  cleanly from an ordinary account of anticipated bargaining power, which
  is the cost.
- **Alternative B (keep the timing, narrow the scope).** Keep clause 3 as
  a claim specifically about the *visible exercise* of residual authority
  at the uncovered contingency, the moment the question "who decides what
  happens next?" is answered in act, and treat the ex ante anticipatory
  effect the readings describe as a distinct, complementary mechanism (a
  separate claim or a note), not part of this one. This preserves what is
  distinctive in the claim, that a fair-looking arrangement's allocation
  of authority shows itself at the perturbation, at the cost of conceding
  that the readings do not ground the claim's own mechanism, only a
  neighbouring one.

The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); choosing between
the alternatives, and adopting either, is the owner's call.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity', A16 §14

A16 §14 cites this claim ("asks who holds residual authority when
circumstances arise that the existing contract does not cover") and
carries it from contracts between persons to contracts between processes:
"If contracts govern relationships among processes, what governs the
contracts themselves?" It orders the governing objects (execution;
coordination and arbitration of commitments; revision of those
arrangements; "Distribution of authority over contract formation and
revision"), notes that "A system might competently resolve ordinary
disputes while being unable to revise the mechanism through which disputes
are resolved", which it ties to CLAIM-719, and concludes: "**The
allocation of authority over unforeseen contingencies is a characteristic
of the organization's constitution, not merely of its current behavior.**"
Bearing: an extension of the claim's residual control to interprocess
contracts, and a reading of it as part of an organization's character
(CLAIM-tmpersgx). The claim is unchanged. The wording of this note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session), reporting A's (ChatGPT's) text; adopting what it reports is the owner's call.
