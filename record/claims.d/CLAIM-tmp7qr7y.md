# CLAIM-tmp7qr7y: Sharing a kind and being a rendering are different relations: kind-sharing is symmetric and says what is to be preserved, while fidelity belongs to a transport from a given source, so an utterance of the same kind made independently of the source shares its kind and has no fidelity to it

## The claim

This is the record's reply of 2026-10-10 to [CLAIM-tmps7va1](CLAIM-tmps7va1.md). It is decisive
and checkable against the record's own texts: it reads the October thesis
and the record's definition of fidelity, and finds that they already keep
apart the two relations the objection runs together.

The objection's cases:

- S: a friend at breakfast says "You blow it all on sex and booze."
- T: a translation of S into French, made from S.
- I: at another table, with no knowledge of S, someone teases a friend in
  French about spending, in an utterance of the same kind.

The objection is right that "both realize K" is symmetric, and that S, T and
I all realize one kind. It concludes that "On the kind criterion I is as good
a rendering of S as T is". That would follow only if the October thesis made
fidelity a matter of kind-sharing. It does not.

## Three relations, and where each lives

- **Kind-sharing** is symmetric and carries no history. [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md)'s invariant
  says what makes two realizations one communicative kind ([TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)). In the
  October thesis it says *what* is to be preserved.
- **Transport** is directed. [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s fidelity clause speaks of
  distinctions that persist "through those transformations": fidelity is
  predicated of a transformation, and a transformation has a source.
  [TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md) defines it so: "A transport T from a source system to a target
  system", and "Its fidelity is the profile of its distortions". [CLAIM-106](CLAIM-106.md)
  makes it "a graded, potentially asymmetric property of transport, not an
  equivalence relation". The October thesis's transport clause,
  "translation transports it", says which transformation is meant.
- **Work identity** is historical. [CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md)'s "continuity of transmission"
  is part of what makes a retelling the *same text* as an original. That is
  a question about the individuation of a work across retellings. [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)
  separates the kind from the token ("Not a token"). A work, with its
  history of transmission, is on the token's side of that line: [CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md)
  individuates it partly by where it came from ("descent is not
  similarity"), not only by what it shares.

Applied to the cases: T stands in a transport from S, so it has a fidelity
profile relative to S, and because it realizes K, the profile can be small
on the distinctions K fixes. I stands in no transport from S. It shares K
with S, and it has no fidelity to S at all: not high, not low, but none,
since the profile is defined only for a transport. So the thesis does say
that T is a rendering of S and I is not. Descent is supplied by the
transport clause, as a fact of provenance about which transformation is
being evaluated. It is not something the kind criterion must recover, and a
criterion of kind would be the wrong place to look for it.

## The residue

One part of the objection holds against [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s wording. Its defeat
condition is stated on "Human judgements of shared communicative kind
across translations, recastings and media", so it tests kind-sharing and
does not test direction. That is a gap in the test, not in the thesis. It is
met by the revised October thesis's transfer disjunct ([CLAIM-tmp3wo5j](CLAIM-tmp3wo5j.md)),
which compares signatures across a transport from one language to another,
and by [CLAIM-106](CLAIM-106.md)'s own condition, which asks whether fidelity judgements are
"symmetric between source and target and transitive along chains".

## What it answers

[CLAIM-tmps7va1](CLAIM-tmps7va1.md), on [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) and [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md). It goes Rejected on this
argument. Its point that [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md)'s identity criterion has no place for
descent is right and harmless: a criterion of kind should not have one.

## What it does not say

- It does not say judges track descent. Whether readers who are not told
  which utterance was made from which judge T more faithful than I is an
  empirical question, and [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md)'s second defeat disjunct ("judges
  reliably privilege one realization, an original") is its nearest test.
- It does not say descent is sufficient for fidelity. A transport can be
  very lossy; T is a rendering of S whatever its profile.
- It does not settle how the work-identity of [CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md) and the kind of
  [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md) relate beyond keeping them apart.
