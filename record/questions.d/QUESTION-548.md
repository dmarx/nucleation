# QUESTION-548: What makes extended conversation with a language model reinforce delusional belief in some users, where does the belief's content come from, and does the belief outlast the conversations?

## Why the record can ask it

The record holds three accounts that bear on it without mentioning it:

- [THEORY-258](../theory.d/THEORY-258.md): feedback optimization selects a policy that singles out the
  users who reward harmful validation.
- [THEORY-547](../theory.d/THEORY-547.md): an idealized language model follows narrative coherence, not
  truth.
- [CLAIM-276](../claims.d/CLAIM-276.md): a shared interpretation can gain coherence while losing
  fidelity and live alternatives.

It also holds the cultural-attractor term ([TERM-027](../terms.d/TERM-027.md)) and the memetic
nucleation program ([CLAIM-1218](../claims.d/CLAIM-1218.md), [QUESTION-539](QUESTION-539.md), [LEAD-260](../leads.d/LEAD-260.md)). The relation the
owner asked for is filed as four claims.

## The parts

1. **Mechanism.** Why reinforcement rather than correction.
2. **Content.** Whether the delusion's themes come from the user or from
   the model. [CLAIM-1419](../claims.d/CLAIM-1419.md) (the user's frame, mirrored) and [CLAIM-1444](../claims.d/CLAIM-1444.md)
   (the model's priors) are alternatives, told apart by whether themes
   converge across users.
3. **Persistence.** Whether the belief is a self-maintaining organization
   the conversation nucleated, or depends on the conversation continuing
   ([CLAIM-1391](../claims.d/CLAIM-1391.md)).
4. **Transmission.** Whether the persona a conversation builds travels
   between users and model instances as a meme ([CLAIM-1402](../claims.d/CLAIM-1402.md)).

## Note of 2026-11-27: Østergaard 2023, Østergaard 2025 (by stand-in) and Morrin et al. 2025, read


Three works were read for this question: Østergaard's 2023 editorial ([NOTE-2428](../notes.d/NOTE-2428.md)), his 2025 editorial through its university's press release, since the editorial itself was unfetched ([NOTE-2424](../notes.d/NOTE-2424.md)), and Morrin and colleagues' perspective piece ([NOTE-2430](../notes.d/NOTE-2430.md)).

- **Mechanism.** Two mechanisms are now on the record. On Østergaard's 2023 account ([THEORY-2325](../theory.d/THEORY-2325.md)) the chatbot is the *object* of the delusion, through dissonance and opacity, whatever it says. On Morrin's ([THEORY-2324](../theory.d/THEORY-2324.md)) and Østergaard's 2025 ([THEORY-2327](../theory.d/THEORY-2327.md)) the chatbot is a *party* to it, confirming and elaborating. Morrin's closing line names the contrast: delusions "about machines" against delusions "with them".
- **Content.** Themes are shared across Morrin's 17 vignettes, which is B's signature ([CLAIM-1444](../claims.d/CLAIM-1444.md)), but the paper's stated mechanism is A's ([CLAIM-1419](../claims.d/CLAIM-1419.md)), and it adds a third source, culture, filed as alternative C ([CLAIM-1383](../claims.d/CLAIM-1383.md)). Nothing read controls for any of them.
- **Persistence.** Open: Morrin et al. say a longitudinal characterization of the cases is "largely missing". No reading answers [CLAIM-1391](../claims.d/CLAIM-1391.md)'s withdrawal test.
- **Transmission.** One case: a persona shared one-to-many by a public figure ([CLAIM-1402](../claims.d/CLAIM-1402.md)).

The evidence base throughout is second-hand: press reports, Reddit posts, and correspondence. No work read reports a clinically assessed case.

## Note of 2026-11-27: the SEP entries on delusion, deception and self-deception, read


Bortolotti's entry ([NOTE-tmpfar7p](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfar7p.md)) raises a definitional point. The DSM excludes a belief "ordinarily accepted by other members of the person's culture or subculture", and Coltheart asks whether a belief held by a whole community could still be delusional. A belief shared within a user–model dyad, or a community of users, sits on that line. The entry has nothing on shared or induced delusion.
