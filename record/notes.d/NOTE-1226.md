  A fact happens at every interaction and is relative to the system it
  affects. A fact relative to F is stable for another system W when W may
  use it as a conditional in the classical total-probability rule, eq. (3);
  interference is the failure of that rule. Decoherence gives stability: if
  F is entangled with an environment E whose branch states overlap by at
  most ε and W does not interact with E, eq. (3) holds for W up to O(ε).

  system coupled differently to F–E may see interference), and a fact can
  be stable for W without being a fact relative to W. The authors base the
  ontology on relative facts, not stable ones, and read the
  Frauchiger–Renner contradiction and the Local Friendliness violation as
  the classical rule used across contexts where facts are not stable.

# NOTE-1226: Di Biagio & Rovelli — Stable facts, relative facts

## Contribution

It gives relational quantum mechanics (RQM) a definition of the two kinds
of fact it had been using informally. A relative fact is defined by an
interaction (§1.1, p. 3). A stable fact is defined by a probability rule:
facts are stable for a system when that system may sum over them
classically, eq. (3). It then derives, in a few lines (eqs. 5–8, p. 4), that
decoherence makes facts stable for a third system to order ε. The paper is
short and mostly conceptual. Its technical content is that derivation. The
rest applies the distinction to the measurement problem, to two no-go
theorems for absolute facts (Frauchiger–Renner, and Bong et al.'s Local
Friendliness inequalities), and to quantum logic.

## Key insight

The classical sum rule P(b) = Σᵢ P(b|aᵢ)P(aᵢ) is the mark of a "stable"
fact, and in quantum theory it holds without condition only when b and the
aᵢ are facts relative to the same system (eq. 2). Used across systems
(eq. 3) it fails, and "The failure of (3) is easily understood in terms of
the standard language of quantum theory: it is the presence of interference
effects" (p. 3). Decoherence restores (3) for a system that cannot reach
the environment. It does so only approximately, and only for that system:
"Stability is only approximate and relational. Approximate, because no fact
is exactly stable for any finite 𝜖. Relational, because it depends on how
the putative 'observer' system couples to the system and the environment"
(p. 6).

## Assumptions

- **Quantum theory is universal.** "There are no exactly classical objects,
  strictly speaking" (p. 2). Collapse models and other limits on quantum
  theory are set aside: "there is no empirical evidence of its failure"
  (p. 2).
- **A fact is fixed by dependence in an interaction** (p. 3): "If an
  interaction affects a variable L_F of F in a manner that depends on the
  value of a certain variable L_S of S, then the value of L_S is a fact
  relative to F." "This is true by definition irrespectively of whether F is
  a classical system." Nothing about F being an observer, macroscopic or
  classical is assumed (p. 4).
- **Eq. (2) holds always.** For facts relative to one system F,
  P(b^(F)) = Σᵢ P(b^(F)|aᵢ^(F))P(aᵢ^(F)), "it is always the case" (p. 3).
  This is stated, not derived; it is RQM's rule.
- **Systems are given.** The definitions start from "two systems S and F"
  and their variables (p. 3). What a system is, or how the world divides
  into them, is not discussed.
- **The two conditions for decoherence** (p. 4): "(a) 𝜖 is vanishing or very
  small and (b) a system W does not interact with E". Both are needed:
  "assumption (b) above is just as crucial as assumption (a) in deriving
  (8)" (p. 5).
- **The quantum state is not ontic.** "The state is not a 'thing', nor a
  condition of a system. Rather, it is what a physicist uses to calculate
  probabilities for relative facts" (p. 7).

## Key results

### §1: stable facts defined (pp. 2–3)

- **Eq. (1), p. 2**: P(b) = Σᵢ P(b|aᵢ)P(aᵢ), for N mutually exclusive facts
  aᵢ. "We take Eq. (1) as a characterisation of stable facts." The
  illustration: after L_z = ℏ/2, the probability of L_θ = ℏ/2 is
  cos²(θ/2) (p. 2).
- **Relative facts (§1.1, p. 3).** The context: "The interaction with F is
  the context in which that variable takes a specific value; we call the
  system F, in this role, a 'context'." Footnote 1 takes "context" from
  Grangier and Auffèves [17], "However we do not require the context to be
  classical", and avoids "observable" because it carries "the ideas of
  observation and a complex observer" (p. 3).
- **Eqs. (2) and (3), p. 3.** With facts labelled by their context, (2)
  holds always; for W ≠ F, "it is in general not the case" that
  P(b^(W)) = Σᵢ P(b^(W)|aᵢ^(F))P(aᵢ^(F)) (3). "If (3) holds, we say that the
  facts aᵢ^(F) are stable for W." "Stable facts are a strict subset of the
  relative facts" (p. 3).
- **Eq. (4), p. 3.** For an isolated S–F, the state
  c₁|a₁⟩⊗|F_a1⟩ + c₂|a₂⟩⊗|F_a2⟩ gives W probabilities with interference
  terms, "because what sums is amplitudes, not probabilities", so the value
  of L_S is not a stable fact for W.

### §1.2: decoherence makes facts stable for a third system (pp. 4–5)

- "the key phenomenon that makes facts stable is decoherence [36, 39, 40]:
  the suppression of interference that happens when some information
  becomes inaccessible" (p. 4).
- **Eqs. (5)–(8), p. 4.** For |ψ⟩ = Σᵢ cᵢ|F_ai⟩⊗|ψᵢ⟩ (5) with normalized
  environment states |ψᵢ⟩, let 𝜖 = max_{i≠j} |⟨ψᵢ|ψⱼ⟩|² (6). Under (a) and
  (b), W's probabilities for facts from an interaction with F can be
  computed from ρ = tr_E|ψ⟩⟨ψ| = Σᵢ|cᵢ|²|F_ai⟩⟨F_ai| + O(𝜖) (7). With
  P(F_ai^(E)) = |cᵢ|², P(b^(W)) = Σᵢ P(b^(W)|F_ai^(E))P(F_ai^(E)) + O(𝜖) (8).
  "Hence the value of the variable L_F is a fact relative to E that is
  stable for W to the extent to which one ignores effects of order 𝜖. In the
  limit 𝜖 → 0, the variable L_F of the system F is exactly stable for W."
  (The bound O(𝜖) is stated, not computed. Since 𝜖 is defined as the
  squared overlap, the off-diagonal elements of ρ are bounded by
  |cᵢcⱼ|√𝜖, not by 𝜖; the paper does not discuss this. A reader's point, not the
  paper's.)
- **Which variables decohere** are "those variables that commute with the
  interaction Hamiltonian", and the decoherence time "is typically extremely
  short for macroscopic variables of macroscopic objects" (p. 5). Both are
  cited as "well understood" [41], not shown.
- **"First, decoherence is not an absolute phenomenon, but a relational one:
  it depends on how the third system W interacts with the combined system
  F − E"** (p. 5). "Another system W′ that interacts differently with F − E
  might be able to detect interference effects."
- **"Second, decoherence implies that an event regarding two systems F and E
  is stable for at third system W. Hence, a fact stable for W is not
  necessarily a fact relative to W"** (p. 5). This is what licenses W to say
  the state of F "has collapsed" before W has interacted with F.
- "decoherence does not imply that there is a perfectly classical world of
  absolute facts, although it does explain why (and when) we can reason in
  terms of stable, hence approximatively classical, facts" (p. 5).
- **Footnote 2, p. 5: stability without decoherence.** If one amplitude has
  |c₁| close to 1 and W does not interact with E, W may use
  ρ′ = |F_a1⟩⟨F_a1| + O(η), η = 1 − |c₁|. "Thus, when a fact relative to a
  system has very high probability, then it is stable for other systems."
  Contrasted with the EPR criterion of reality: it holds only while W
  cannot interact with E, "and there needs to be a fact relative to E in the
  first place".

### §1.3: a measurement in three stages (pp. 5–6)

- If L_S and L_F are entangled and L_F is stable for W, "it follows
  immediately from the definitions that also L_S is stable for W" (p. 5).
- The laboratory measurement, told twice (pp. 5–6). In standard terms: the
  system–apparatus interaction entangles L_S with a pointer L_F; L_F
  correlates with many microscopic variables E inaccessible to W; W reads
  the pointer. In relative facts: "1. A relative fact is established
  between S and F. 2. A relative fact is established between F and E. Since
  W does not interact with E, this stabilises the previous fact for W. 3. A
  relative fact is established between F and W."
- At stage 2, "neither the value of L_S nor that of L_F is a fact for W at
  this stage. Stability simply allows W to "de-label" facts relative to F"
  (p. 6). At stage 3, L_F becomes a fact for W, but "the value of L_S is
  still not a fact relative to W, it is merely a stable fact for W" (p. 6).
- "It is the way that W, F and E couple to each other that makes F a
  measuring apparatus for W" (p. 6).

### §2.1: why the ontology is relative facts, not stable ones (pp. 7–8)

- Stable facts are the textbook link to the world, and "facts stable with
  respect to us are ubiquitous because of the ubiquity of decoherence"
  (p. 7). Two difficulties in taking them as the ontology: "First, stability
  is relational … Therefore one does not avoid relationalism by restricting
  to stable facts. Second, more seriously, stability is only approximate in
  general. At no point the interference terms perfectly vanish" (p. 7).
- "How stable does a fact need to be before it is real? And with respect to
  what systems does it have to be stable, in order to be real? Any answer to
  these questions is bound to be as unsatisfactory as the textbook
  interpretation that requires a classical world" (p. 7).
- "RQM notices that every interaction is in a sense a measurement, in that
  it results in the value of a variable to become a fact … And there is no
  'special context': any system can be a context for any other system"
  (p. 7).
- No universal wavefunction: it "would be a tool to calculate probabilities
  of facts relative to something that does not exist: a system that is not
  part of the universe" (p. 8).
- "the ontology of RQM is realist … Realist in the sense that it is not
  about agents, beliefs, observers, or experiences: it is about real facts
  of the world and relative probabilities of their occurrence. The ontology
  is relational, in the sense that it is based on facts established at
  interactions and are labelled by physical contexts" (p. 8).

### §2.2: the no-go theorems (pp. 8–9)

- **Frauchiger and Renner [12] (§2.2.1).** Their assumption (C), "If W,
  applying quantum theory, concludes that F knows that L_S = a, then W can
  conclude that L_S = a", becomes in relative facts the use of facts
  relative to F as if relative to W, which "holds only if every fact
  relative to F is stable for W" (p. 8). The superobservers have "full
  quantum control" of the friends' labs, so "by definition, what is stable
  for the friends is not stable for W. Hence the contradiction follows from
  inappropriately mixing contexts" (p. 8). That no contradiction follows if
  the friends' labs also decohere for W is credited to [38] and Relaño [26,
  27], not shown. And: "The analysis of how an agent should reason about an
  experiment that will be performed on him has not been done within RQM
  yet" (pp. 8–9), with QBism's analysis [9, 10] commended.
- **Bong et al. [5] (§2.2.2).** Absoluteness of observed events, no
  superdeterminism and Bell locality imply the Local Friendliness
  inequalities, which quantum theory violates. "Absoluteness of observed
  events means that if W deems that F is an observer, then W can use
  (3)—even if W has full quantum control on F" (p. 9), which RQM denies.
  On the photon-friend experiments: dismissing them because "photons are
  not observers" "opens the problem of deciding which systems give rise to
  facts" (p. 9). "The violation of the LF inequalities is no way in tension
  with the relational interpretation. The opposite is true: the result is
  taken as evidence that the facts quantum theory deals with are facts
  relative to systems" (p. 9).

### §2.3: conclusions (pp. 10–11)

- **The measurement problem**: projection and linear evolution "refer to
  facts relative to different systems" (p. 10). "In a slogan: 'Wigner's
  facts are not necessarily his Friend's facts'."
- **Consistency between observers**: after S and F interact they are
  entangled relative to W, "Therefore Wigner will always agree with his
  Friend about the value of L_S once he too interacts with them. In this
  sense, relative facts correspond to real events, they have universal
  empirical consequences" (p. 10), citing [28] (Rovelli 1996). An argument
  from the correlation in the entangled state, not a general proof.
- **Macroreality**: "Such a macroreality only emerges approximately,
  relative to systems for which decoherence is sufficiently strong" (p. 10).
- **Decoherence**: "there is consensus that decoherence alone is not a
  solution of the measurement problem … Decoherence needs an ontology.
  Relative facts provide such a general ontology that is well defined with
  or without decoherence. Decoherence clarifies why a large class of
  relative facts are stable for us" (p. 10).
- **Quantum logic, eqs. (9)–(10), p. 10**: the violation of (1) has been read
  as P(b and (a₁ or a₂)) ≠ P((b and a₁) or (b and a₂)), against
  distributivity; RQM reads it as "forgetting that facts are relative:
  labelled by a context, as Bohr has repeatedly pointed out".
- **Superposition**: "Saying "Friend is in a quantum superposition" does not
  mean anything more than saying that Wigner would be mistaken in using
  (3)"; "Schrödinger's cat has no reason to feel 'superposed'" (p. 11).

## Concepts

- **Relative fact** — the value of a variable L_S, relative to a system F
  whose variable L_F an interaction makes depend on it (p. 3). Written
  a^(F).
- **Context** — the system F in that role (p. 3), from Grangier and Auffèves
  but not required to be classical (footnote 1).
- **Stable for W** — facts aᵢ^(F) for which W may use eq. (3), the classical
  sum rule over them (p. 3).
- **Stable fact** — a relative fact that is stable for the systems in
  question; "those whose relativity can effectively be ignored" (abstract).
  Always stable *for* some system; never absolutely (pp. 6–7).
- **𝜖** — the largest squared overlap of the environment states correlated
  with F's alternatives (eq. 6); the measure of how far a fact is from
  stable for a W that cannot reach E.
- **De-label** — what stability lets W do with facts relative to F (p. 6).
- **Macroreality** — absolute facts, in the Bell literature's sense; here
  emergent "only approximately, relative to systems for which decoherence is
  sufficiently strong" (p. 10).

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-285](../questions.d/QUESTION-285.md): the "stable record" rung is misattributed, and the
  paper supplies a different, sharper rung.** The question's table gives
  this paper as the source for "stable record: the outcome stays accessible
  far longer than the interaction took". Nothing in the paper is about
  duration or accessibility in time. Stability here is a probability rule
  holding for a third system (eq. 3), and it comes from inaccessibility, not
  accessibility: "the suppression of interference that happens when some
  information becomes inaccessible" (p. 4). Persistence and redundancy are
  not discussed. What the paper does give the question is a three-step
  measurement in its own terms (pp. 5–6): a relative fact (S–F), its
  stabilization for W by a further fact (F–E) that W cannot reach, and a
  fact for W (F–W), which W then uses to update its state for S. These come
  apart: at stage 2 L_S is stable for W but not a fact for W, and even after
  stage 3 it is "merely a stable fact for W" (p. 6). And "It is the way that
  W, F and E couple to each other that makes F a measuring apparatus for W"
  (p. 6): apparatus is a role fixed by a three-way coupling, relative to W,
  near [TERM-089](../terms.d/TERM-089.md)'s operational sense. On the question's first rung the
  paper is RQM's answer as the question already states it: "every
  interaction is in a sense a measurement" (p. 7). The exchange was right
  that the paper gives observation without a conscious observer: the
  ontology "is not about agents, beliefs, observers, or experiences" (p. 8),
  and footnote 1 avoids "observable" for its connotation of "a complex
  observer" (p. 3).
- **The exchange's gloss (A19 §3, A23 §VII), checked.** "Facts relative to
  interacting systems" against "stable facts whose relativity can
  effectively be ignored", connected to decoherence: right, nearly verbatim
  (abstract; §1.2). "A relationship established through interaction becomes
  stable enough to support a more nearly observer-independent effective
  description": wrong in two respects. (i) It is not the S–F relation that
  becomes stable by being established. The fact relative to F becomes stable
  *for a third system* because F interacts further with an environment that
  third system cannot reach (p. 4; stage 2, p. 6). (ii) The result is not
  observer-independent, more or less. It is label-independent for a given W,
  and the paper insists on the difference: "decoherence is not an absolute
  phenomenon, but a relational one" (p. 5); "one does not avoid
  relationalism by restricting to stable facts" (p. 7); even macroreality
  "only emerges approximately, relative to systems for which decoherence is
  sufficiently strong" (p. 10). The defensible form is: for a system that
  cannot reach the environment, the relativity of a decohered fact can be
  ignored up to O(ε).
- **[QUESTION-280](../questions.d/QUESTION-280.md): a worked case of approximate absoluteness, whose
  authors decline to promote it to ontology.** The paper is an instance of
  the question's "approximation for ontological claims" for facts, not for
  individuals. A relational feature (facts indexed to contexts) has a limit
  (ε → 0, with W cut off from E) in which it behaves as the absolute,
  label-free facts of classical physics, with a stated error O(ε) (p. 4).
  So it has the quantitative half of what the question asks for: a grade
  (ε) and a limit. But the authors take the question's second horn for the
  ontology: stable facts are not the primary reality, because "How stable
  does a fact need to be before it is real? And with respect to what
  systems does it have to be stable?" have no satisfactory answer (p. 7),
  and "At no point the interference terms perfectly vanish". And they add a
  feature the question does not name: the approximation is itself relative
  to a system (to W, which must not reach E), so even the approximately
  absolute is approximately absolute *for* something. Separable enough, in
  [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)'s terms, is here separable enough for a stated W, and the paper
  says that does not make it independent. It argues this; it does not prove
  that no account of approximate ontology could succeed.
- **[THEORY-621](../theory.d/THEORY-621.md): the stable-fact step is now shown, and the rule and
  consistency gaps stand.** [THEORY-621](../theory.d/THEORY-621.md) records that the decoherence result
  is "cited, not shown" in Rovelli 2021. Here it is derived, at the level of
  a reduced density matrix (eqs. 5–8, p. 4). The promote_when asks for two
  things this paper does not give: eq. (2), the same-system rule, is
  asserted ("it is always the case", p. 3), not derived from independent
  principles; and inter-observer consistency is one sentence, "Wigner will
  always agree with his Friend about the value of L_S once he too interacts
  with them" (p. 10), from the entangled state. The paper also concedes an
  open case: how an agent should reason about an experiment performed on
  himself "has not been done within RQM yet" (pp. 8–9). Its reading of the
  no-go theorems is the same as Rovelli 2021's and has the same limit
  (C8 above). Filed as [THEORY-1075](../theory.d/THEORY-1075.md) and [THEORY-1081](../theory.d/THEORY-1081.md).
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md): a constraint made by inaccessibility, not by slowness.**
  The paper's effective rule, the classical sum (3), appears for W because
  information in E is out of W's reach, not because some variable is slow
  relative to another. Time appears once: the decoherence time, "the time
  needed for 𝜖 to become so small that interference effects become
  undetectable by given observational methods", short for macroscopic
  variables (p. 5). One can read that as a separation (decoherence fast,
  W's later interaction slower), but the paper does not, and its condition
  (b) is about coupling, not rates. So it does not support the claim's
  mechanism. It is a different route to an effective constraint, with a
  graded error (ε), that the claim's "Not one mechanism" list could note.
  The variables that become stable are selected by the interaction
  Hamiltonian (p. 5), which is a case of organization (who couples to what)
  fixing which constraint appears.
- **[THEORY-1071](../theory.d/THEORY-1071.md) and [NOTE-1227](NOTE-1227.md): the systems still precede the facts, and
  the ontology of facts is here by 2020.** [NOTE-1227](NOTE-1227.md) found no ontology of
  events in Rovelli 1996, and [THEORY-1071](../theory.d/THEORY-1071.md)'s note traced Faye and
  Jaksland's "sparse ontology of (relational) quantum events" to Rovelli
  2018. This paper states an ontology of facts outright: "The ontology is
  relational, in the sense that it is based on facts established at
  interactions and are labelled by physical contexts" (p. 8). The word
  "sparse" does not occur. And the definition of a relative fact presupposes
  the systems and their variables ("Let us consider two systems S and F. If
  an interaction affects a variable L_F of F …", p. 3), so Faye and
  Jaksland's "the systems precede the interactions" holds of this text as
  well, again by assumption and without argument. The paper does not take up
  what a system is.
- **[THEORY-1080](../theory.d/THEORY-1080.md): its finding stands for 2021.** Consistency between
  observers is still "the third system predicts correlated outcomes" (p. 10),
  asserted for the general case.
- **[QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md).** Nothing new beyond [NOTE-1227](NOTE-1227.md)'s point: stable facts are
  the nearest thing to an invariant, and they are invariant only for the
  systems that cannot reach the environment.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **The derivation is a sketch.** Eqs. (5)–(8) state the error as O(𝜖)
  without a bound; 𝜖 is a squared overlap, so the residual off-diagonal
  elements of ρ are bounded by |cᵢcⱼ|√𝜖, which the paper does not discuss. Condition (b),
  "W does not interact with E", is all-or-nothing; partial access is not
  treated.
- **The decoherence facts are cited**: the pointer variables commuting with
  the interaction Hamiltonian and the short decoherence times (p. 5) come
  from Zurek [41].
- **The core rule is assumed.** Eq. (2) holding always for same-context
  facts is the interpretation's postulate (p. 3).
- **Consistency between observers is one sentence** (p. 10). Not in this
  text, and not checked in this reading: it is standardly reported that
  later RQM work (Adlam and Rovelli, 2022) added a postulate of
  "cross-perspective links" to secure such agreement. The record holds no
  LIT for it.
- **The no-go analyses rely on cited work**: Relaño [26, 27] and Żukowski and
  Markiewicz [38] for the claim that no contradiction follows when the
  friends' labs decohere for W (p. 8).
- **Typos in the published text**: "phase pace" (footnote 1, p. 3); "stable
  for at third system" (p. 5); the heading "The Link Between the Theory the
  World" (§2.1, p. 7); the conclusions are numbered §2.3 within §2. The
  acknowledgements thank "Eric Wiseman" for a point on [5], whose author
  list has H. M. Wiseman; which person is meant cannot be settled from the
  text.
- The v1 preprint was not read; what changed between v1 and the journal
  text is unknown.

## Open questions

- Is there a quantitative account of partial access, where W interacts
  weakly with E, giving how far (3) fails as a function of both 𝜖 and W's
  coupling?
- If stability is relative to W, is the decomposition into S, F, E and W
  also relative, or given? The paper takes the systems as given.
- How should an agent in RQM reason about an experiment performed on
  himself (pp. 8–9)? The paper names it as not yet done.
- Can the agreement between Wigner and his friend (p. 10) be derived from the
  definitions, or does it need an added postulate?
