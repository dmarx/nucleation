# LIT-tmp8grr0: Nonequilibrium phase transitions in coordinated biological motion: critical fluctuations

J. A. S. Kelso, J. P. Scholz and G. Schöner (1986), *Physics Letters A*
118(6):279–284. doi:10.1016/0375-9601(86)90359-2.

Read in full on 2026-10-30 ([NOTE-tmpegx3g](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpegx3g.md)), from an offprint scan on
Kelso's lab page.

## Key takeaways

- **Fluctuations rise before the switch.** In the wrist experiments the
  SD of the point estimate of relative phase was low and steady for
  three four-cycle segments, rose at the segment before the transition,
  peaked in it, and fell once in-phase was reached (Fig. 2, averaged over
  30 runs on four subjects). The finger experiments, with relative phase
  estimated every 5 ms and frequency raised in 0.25 Hz steps, showed the
  same, more clearly (Fig. 4, ten runs of one representative subject).
- **The asymmetry.** Starting in-phase, neither a shift in mean phase nor
  a growth in fluctuation occurred as frequency rose. Below the
  transition, in-phase was less variable than anti-phase at every
  frequency (p. 283).
- **Not shown here.** Critical slowing down is predicted and backed only
  by "very preliminary evidence" of line narrowing in the power spectrum
  (ref. 13); direct measures of relaxation time by perturbation were left
  to new experiments (p. 283). Hysteresis is not tested.
