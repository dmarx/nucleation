# CLAIM-167: One-way drive rightly passes the threshold criterion: a metronome and its musician, or a nerve and its muscle, form one system relative to a target that needs both, and the direction of coupling gives the system's internal architecture, not its membership

## The claim

The owner's challenge, verbatim, quoting the open objection [CLAIM-173](CLAIM-173.md):
"> a metronome driving a musician, or a nerve driving a muscle, passes —
and why shouldn't they?"

Three reasons they should:

1. **The objection repeats a worry the record had already set aside.**
   Adams & Aizawa's nerve and muscle ([LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)) show that the nerve is not
   part of the *muscle*. That is true, and the threshold criterion
   ([CLAIM-157](CLAIM-157.md)) never says otherwise. It says that nerve and muscle
   together form a system, as the neuromuscular system is one.
   [CLAIM-172](CLAIM-172.md) made exactly this move for entrainment: it individuates
   a new system and infers no parthood in the old one. If the move works
   for timing, it works for strength.
2. **Its intuitions are about agency.** The sense that a metronome and a
   musician "are not one system" is the sense that they are not one
   *agent* or *self*. [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md) asks only whether they are one
   *system* relative to a target, and keeps agency ([QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md)) and
   selfhood ([TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)<!-- ref::uses: TERM-045 -->) ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)) for later. Take the target "predict the musician's
   timing". Then the metronome belongs in the description: cutting it away
   loses the best predictor of the musician. Systems are cheap, and Simon
   expects complexity to be hierarchies of them everywhere ([LIT-905](../literature.d/LIT-905.md)).
3. **Inside or input is a choice relative to the target.** The study design
   for the dancing couple ([CASE-045](../cases.d/CASE-045.md)) takes the music's beat as an
   exogenous input. That is the metronome's structure exactly. Whether a
   one-way driver is a member of the block or forcing on it depends on what
   the description is for. It is not a fact the criterion gets wrong.

**What direction does tell.** It tells the architecture inside a system.
Mutual coupling makes peers. One-way coupling makes a hierarchy: driver and
driven, controller and plant, lead and follow. That asymmetry is normal,
and requiring mutuality would have excluded the record's own paradigm case,
the dancing couple with its lead and follow ([CLAIM-175](CLAIM-175.md)). It is also
information the agency question will need: who drives is part of whether,
and where, there is an agent ([TERM-044](../terms.d/TERM-044.md)<!-- ref::uses: TERM-044 -->).

## What it does not say

- **That everything strongly coupled is one system ([TERM-049](../terms.d/TERM-049.md)<!-- ref::uses: TERM-049 -->) for every purpose.** The
  verdict is relative to a target ([CLAIM-152](CLAIM-152.md)) and to the Occam
  threshold ([CLAIM-154](CLAIM-154.md)).
- **That a driven system is an agent, or that the driver is one.** That is
  the next question.
- **That direction can be read from observation alone.** A symmetrically
  coupled pair reads as "hierarchical" once one member is strongly driven
  ([THEORY-tmpo4fgq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo4fgq.md), [LIT-947](../literature.d/LIT-947.md), [NOTE-tmpjexhc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpjexhc.md), 2026-10-14). Information
  flow follows where the drive enters, not the coupling. So telling peers
  from hierarchy needs an intervention on one member, as [QUESTION-063](../questions.d/QUESTION-063.md)
  asks.
