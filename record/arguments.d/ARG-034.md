# ARG-034: Saussure's account of value grounds the premise that significance is fixed by contrasts rather than by correspondence to a referent

## The inference

Manuscript §2, quoted in [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md): "Saussure's account of linguistic value
makes the significance of a sign depend upon contrasts within a system,
rather than on intrinsic correspondence to a referent." [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md) grounds
on the record's reading of Saussure, [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md), whose source is the Cours,
[LIT-774](../literature.d/LIT-774.md) (read in [NOTE-594](../notes.d/NOTE-594.md)).

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** No. This is the question
  that fails. [CLAIM-072](../claims.d/CLAIM-072.md) (2026-10-09), from Part Two, ch. IV: "dans la langue
  il n'y a que des différences sans termes positifs" holds of the signified
  and the signifier considered separately; the sign that unites them is "un
  fait positif", and signs are related by opposition. "Value, fixed by a
  term's relations to coexisting terms, is distinct from signification, its
  counterpart relation to an idea: French *mouton* and English *sheep* can
  share a signification and differ in value." "Saussure also brackets the
  referent: arbitrariness is between signifier and signified, not between
  sign and thing." [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md) agrees: "it says nothing about how terms
  relate to things".
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** Within one language
  state, yes, for value. Whether the premise reaches across languages and
  to utterances in use is a separate inference, to [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md), and is
  [ARG-033](ARG-033.md).
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than the reading holds?**
  Yes, in two places. "Significance" blurs value with signification, and
  "rather than ... correspondence to a referent" sets up a contrast that is
  not Saussure's. [CLAIM-072](../claims.d/CLAIM-072.md)'s fix: "say value rather than significance, and
  drop the referent contrast, which is not Saussure's."

Rejected as an inference to [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md) as titled. The reading supports a
corrected premise: a term's value is fixed by its oppositions to the
coexisting terms of one language state. [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md) already holds every
point of the correction, so the reading gains nothing. [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md)'s title
still carries the uncorrected contrast, and [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md) rests on it.
