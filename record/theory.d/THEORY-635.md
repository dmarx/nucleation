# THEORY-635: If the quantum state of a Bohmian system is identified with the velocity dispositions it induces on the particles, inequivalent states can share one dispositional history: every energy eigenstate of a free particle in a box induces zero velocity everywhere, so the reading erases differences of energy and of typicality unless the map from states to velocity fields is injective for generic solutions, which is conjectured, not proved

## Source

Belot (2011; *EJPS* 2012), [LIT-100](../literature.d/LIT-100.md), pp. 10–13, read in full in [NOTE-110](../notes.d/NOTE-110.md).

## What was actually shown

**Setting.** Non-relativistic Bohmian mechanics for n spinless particles in
Euclidean 3-space, read as a primitive ontology of n worldlines. Wave
functions are identified up to measure-zero differences and complex
multiples (p. 3, fn. 8). The guidance law is eq. (1).

**The dispositionalist reading.** Belot introduces it himself (fn. 7; the
property idea is credited to Monton 2006). The complete history is q(t)
together with Φt, the system's velocity dispositions, represented by the
vector field X_t on configuration space that the guidance law derives from
ψ(t) (pp. 10–11).

**The counterexample (pp. 11–12).** For a free particle in [0,1], every
energy eigenstate φn(x) = sin(πnx) gives zero velocity everywhere under
the guidance law. Infinitely many inequivalent states share one
dispositional history. Their differences in energy, and the difference in
which configurations are typical (φ1 against φ2), are effaced. Belot calls
this "dismaying" (p. 12).

**The rescue.** Belot does not object to bare or holistic dispositions as
such; content is the problem. He conjectures, crediting Goldstein, that the
map ψ(t) ↦ X_t is injective for generic solutions (fn. 41), and proposes to
accept, elsewhere, that there is no determinate fact about energy or
typicality. The problem arises only for closed systems (fn. 42).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The map from states to velocity
fields could have been injective on stationary states. The box's eigenstates make it
fail.

## What this does not say

- **That the dispositional reading is refuted.** Its rescue rests on a
  genericity conjecture that is unproved either way ([NOTE-110](../notes.d/NOTE-110.md), C6, weak).
- **That ψ is a law, or a field.** Belot prices those readings separately
  and calls the outcome "inconclusive": each option relies on contested
  metaphysics (§3). The costs of the law reading are in [THEORY-669](THEORY-669.md).
- **That the reading also loses determinism by this route.** That is a
  separate cost (fn. 39): (q, Φ) as the instantaneous state is not
  deterministic, possibly repaired by adding time-derivatives of Φ.
- **Anything about GRW or relativistic primitive ontologies.** The case is
  worked only for non-relativistic Bohmian mechanics.
