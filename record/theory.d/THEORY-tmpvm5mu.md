# THEORY-tmpvm5mu: In the ethnographic record of the 60-society HRAF Probability Sample, seven cooperative behaviours are described as good in 961 of 962 valenced mentions, but each is recorded in between 90% and 15% of the societies, and the coding could barely have registered a negative

## Source

Curry, Mullins & Whitehouse (2019), [LIT-047](../literature.d/LIT-047.md) (*Current Anthropology*
60(1), 47–69, with four invited comments and a Reply), read in full in
[NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md), with the online supplement.

## What was actually shown

**The corpus.** eHRAF searches on the 60 societies of the HRAF Probability
Sample Files gave 3,460 paragraphs from 603 sources dated 1704–2009 (median
1967). The two non-blind authors screened them to 1,426 germane paragraphs;
761 of those carry valence, giving 962 observations.

**Valence (Table 2).** Positive against negative: kin 214/0, group 127/0,
reciprocity 151/0, bravery 101/0, respect 133/0, fairness 17/0, property
218/1. The one negative is the Chuuk admiration of open theft as a display
of dominance.

**Presence (Table 3).** Societies with at least one positive observation, of
60: property 54, kin 47, group 44, reciprocity 43, respect 35, bravery 32,
fairness 9. A society shows 4.4 of the seven on average. Fairness is rarer
than every other domain, and property commoner than bravery, respect and
fairness (repeated-measures GLM, Bonferroni, P ≤ .001).

**Coverage.** The number of morals observed per society correlates with its
total eHRAF paragraph count (r = .43, P = .001), so much of the variation in
presence is variation in how much was written.

**What could have come out otherwise.** A society could have been described
as despising one of the seven, and the presence counts could have been
uniform. The first almost never happened; the second did not.

## What this does not say

- **That the seven are universal moral rules.** The behaviours are coded in
  virtue terms ("helping", "respecting", "being fair"), so a negative is close
  to self-contradictory. Cases where one of the seven was condemned for
  costing a larger cooperation (fn. 3), and 18 value conflicts (fn. 11), were
  not coded as negative. The 99.9% mostly measures what the codebook could
  see.
- **That morality is about cooperation and nothing else.** No
  non-cooperative domain (purity, sexual conduct, treatment of the dead, harm
  to strangers) was coded for comparison. Curry 2016 ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md)) names that
  comparison as the theory's own prediction, and this study does not run it.
- **That the seven occur equally in every region.** The regional ANOVAs are
  non-significant at about ten societies per region; that is low power, not
  equality.
- **That the codes are reliable.** The screening from 3,460 to 1,426
  paragraphs was done by the non-blind authors, with no reliability reported.
  The blind rater's agreement on the remainder was κ = .58 overall and .14
  for fairness; the reported κ = 1 is the consensus after discussion.
- **That the mentions record what the society judges.** The judgements are
  those of mostly Western, mostly mid-twentieth-century ethnographers.
