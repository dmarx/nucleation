# THEORY-tmpl13wj: Micropolitics is the modulation of a relational field toward a maximally inclusive co-patterning of contrasting tendencies, in which each realizes itself without curtailing the others; the co-patterning is aesthetic, an affirmation of discord rather than its resolution

## Source

Massumi (2017), *The Principle of Unrest*, [LIT-tmpi0u4n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpi0u4n.md), p. 64, skimmed
in [NOTE-tmpn58uh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpn58uh.md). Earlier, Massumi & McKim (2009), [LIT-tmp8hcfz](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8hcfz.md), p. 12,
read in [NOTE-tmpyf5pn](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpyf5pn.md).

## What was actually shown

A statement of an aim, in an interview. "Micropolitics is the modulation
of this dynamic differential mutual inclusion, tending toward greater
field-intensities. What I mean by that is a maximally inclusive
co-patterning of contrasting tendencies, co-habiting the field in a way
that facilitates each fully realizing itself ... The challenge of the
co-patterning is to facilitate this passage to the limit of tendencies'
intensive becoming in way that they do not oppose and curtail each other"
(p. 64). It is "aesthetic", after Whitehead's beauty, which "requires an
affirmation of discord". In 2009 the same aim is "not to resolve the
tension through a choice, but to modulate it into a symbiosis" (p. 12).

The record checked the quotation that Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy
([LIT-tmp3mudg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3mudg.md), p. 1358) give, and found it exact and on p. 64.

## What this does not say

- **That organizations are like this.** Massumi states what micropolitics
  aims at, and grants that it "sounds utopic". The parent paper says
  "organizational becoming is constituted by the micropolitics of
  attempting to reach" it. That is a descriptive claim about
  organizations, which the source does not make, though "attempting"
  keeps it close.
- **That differences are to be reconciled.** The co-patterning keeps
  discord; it is the opposite of reducing equivocality. On this the
  parent's use is faithful ("rather than denying those differences").
- **How to tell success from failure.** Massumi calls the outcome "always
  as-yet unknowable", so the ideal gives no criterion. That is why this
  stays Proposed.
