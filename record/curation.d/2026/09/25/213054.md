## The pattern

Every one of the five skims carried at least one claim the full text does not
support. Every one of those errors made the paper look **more connected, more
general or more conclusive** than it is. That is the drift the anthology's
`DP-010` warns about: the most citable sentence is often the least supported.
It also held on this record's own most-cited sentence. This record called
Abramsky & Brandenburger "this record's one bridge to the anthology", with
*Topos and Stacks* building on it and sheaf neural networks descending from it.
Belfiore & Bennequin cite it once, in a related-work list, and use nothing from
it. The two sheaf-network papers checked do not cite it at all. The claim was
corrected in place, in this record's reading order and in the anthology's
boundary entry, and each correction is marked as one.

The lesson for the seeding step is simple. A skim is honest about its depth,
but it is not honest about **connections**. A connection named from a skim is a
guess, and it should be written as one.

## What the reading turned up to file

**Rosas et al. 2020, *Reconciling emergences*** (*PLoS Comput. Biol.*), is the
primary source behind `LIT-025`. The review restates its theorem, and every
proof and assumption lives there. It is not held. It is the next filing in this
cluster, and `LIT-025` cannot be cited for the theorem until it is.
