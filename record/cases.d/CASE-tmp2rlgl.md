# CASE-tmp2rlgl: Twin pattern-keepers: two systems keep the same pattern, one by its own responses and one through an external controller, and only removing the controller tells them apart

## The case

A10 §3.3: "System A maintains a stable pattern through its own internally
generated responses. System B maintains the same visible pattern through an
external controller. Their observable outputs may be identical. But
removing the external controller destroys B's stability, while A continues
to regulate itself. This is a clean constitutive contrast: similar behavior,
different organization."

Then the complication: "An internal regulator at one scale is itself
supported by other processes at another scale. The goal is not absolute
independence from an environment ... The issue is whether the system
possesses a distinctive organization of reciprocal enabling and
regulation."

## What it can show

That self-maintenance is not a property of behaviour, which is what
[CLAIM-tmpspyay](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpspyay.md) needs. It is the record's cleanest form of [QUESTION-tmpgjngk](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpgjngk.md):
is there an intervention short of removing the controller that tells A from
B?

## What it cannot show

Where the boundary is. Draw it around B and its controller, and B is A.
[CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md) objects that a coupling criterion cannot settle which boundary is
the system's own. The thermostat and the adaptive controller ([CASE-034](CASE-034.md)) are
the record's earlier pair, which differ in adaptation and not in where the
regulator sits.
