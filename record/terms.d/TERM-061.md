# TERM-061: practical synchronization: an agent's action-generating regimes, relative to a context and a horizon, sustaining mutually compatible courses of activity through their own interactions, without recurrent overriding arbitration by a higher level, whether or not they rank the same course first

## Definition

A45 of the exchange of 2026-10-17, answering the owner's request at U45:

> An agent's constituent action-generating regimes are *intrapersonally
> synchronized*, relative to a context and temporal horizon, insofar as
> their interactions sustain mutually compatible courses of practical
> activity without requiring recurrent overriding arbitration by
> processes operating at a higher level of governance.

It "need not consist in identical preferences, unanimous first rankings,
or the elimination of disagreement. It may arise through aligned
valuations, reciprocal adjustment, negotiated accommodation, or learned
coordination among processes with distinct evaluative perspectives." A
higher level may remain, "for monitoring, exceptional arbitration, and
revision". What marks the condition is that "coherent activity is
ordinarily maintained through the constituent processes' own coupling
rather than repeated imposed resolution of their conflicts."

Three things are relative and are stated with it: the regimes in play,
the context, and the horizon.

**Static compatibility is not synchronization.** That some joint policy
gives every process an acceptable part (A45: "∃π∈Π such that
ρ_i(π)∈A_i(c,t) ∀i") says the course exists. Synchronization is the
dynamics that reach and keep it: local interactions that "generate
compatible joint policies across the relevant horizon while making h_t
unnecessary in ordinary operation", where h_t is supervisory
intervention.

The term is new. The owner asked to generalize [TERM-050](TERM-050.md), and the template
says a changed definition is a new TERM, so that the claims stated in the
old one can be found. Six claims are stated in [TERM-050](TERM-050.md)'s sense ([CLAIM-156](../claims.d/CLAIM-156.md),
[CLAIM-162](../claims.d/CLAIM-162.md), [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md), [CLAIM-169](../claims.d/CLAIM-169.md), [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md), [CLAIM-261](../claims.d/CLAIM-261.md)). It does not
supersede [TERM-050](TERM-050.md): the two are independent dimensions (next section but
one).

## Two dimensions, not a hierarchy

Valuational concordance ([TERM-050](TERM-050.md)) and practical synchronization vary
independently. Regimes can agree about what should happen and have no
working mechanism to bring it about; they can coordinate effectively
while ranking differently.

| | practical coordination absent | practical coordination present |
|---|---|---|
| **valuational concordance absent** | unresolved disagreement | negotiated or coupled coordination |
| **valuational concordance present** | agreement without effective coordination | concordant, coordinated action |

The lower-left cell is what makes them two dimensions. Every regime may
rank the same course first and the agent still fail to take it, because
nothing couples the regimes' activity across the relevant delays, or
because the course needs a coordination none of them supplies. The
upper-right cell is the owner's U45 case: disagreement settled among the
regimes without override. [TERM-050](TERM-050.md) keeps its own role, as the column a
coordination mechanism need not reach. This also meets [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)'s
objection more directly: that objection is to synchronization talk with
no mechanism, and the mechanism belongs to this term's dimension, not to
concordance.

## What it is not

- **Not [TERM-050](TERM-050.md)'s intrapersonal synchronization, and not its
  generalization.** [TERM-050](TERM-050.md) is the case in which every regime ranks the
  course first from every delay, so "there is no contest to win". A45
  called it *valuational concordance*, "the special case captured by the
  previous definition". That inclusion does not follow, as the owner
  pointed out in review of the filing (workbench entries of 2026-10-18).
  Concordance is agreement in rankings and requires no coupling;
  practical synchronization is achieved coordination through the regimes'
  own interactions. See the next section.
- **Not supervisory integration.** A45: "coherent action is achieved
  through repeated higher-level intervention, rather than primarily
  through local coordination". The agent acts as one, as [CLAIM-261](../claims.d/CLAIM-261.md) says a
  divided agent can, but its regimes are not synchronized in either
  sense.
- **Not a test of how the coordination was established.** A45:
  "Behavioral conformity under coercion can resemble synchronization."
  Regimes conditioned never to object can acquire compatible behaviour
  and keep it up through their own local interactions, with no active
  supervisor intervening. They are then practically synchronized. The
  term is descriptive: a dynamical achievement, compatible activity
  sustained by the regimes' interactions. Whether that arrangement is
  non-dominating, that is, how it was established, whether each
  contribution stays consequential and what happens under counterfactual
  dissent, is a further and more demanding condition
  ([CLAIM-362](../claims.d/CLAIM-362.md), with its counter [CLAIM-364](../claims.d/CLAIM-364.md)). Conditioned conformity
  can count as practical synchronization and fail that condition. Two
  agents can be equally synchronized and differ deeply in autonomy. (The
  first filing said the definition excluded conditioned conformity; the
  owner's second review corrected it.)
- **Not authentic integration.** A45 named a stronger condition, in which
  coordination is "also non-dominating, contestable, and maintained
  through the organization's own processes of mutual accommodation"; that
  is the owner's criterion, [CLAIM-362](../claims.d/CLAIM-362.md). This term carries no claim
  about ownership.
- **Not neural phase synchrony**, and not shown to be a mechanism.
  [CLAIM-163](../claims.d/CLAIM-163.md)'s objection stands: the term names a coupling, a candidate
  collective variable (how often supervisory intervention is needed) and
  transitions, but the record has not shown them in a will.
