# THEORY-1439: Dunn and colleagues show that a DNA origami template with several approximately degenerate well-folded shapes can be annealed to different distributions of those shapes by changing only which staples stabilize intermediates: weakening the cooperative long-range seam staples shifts the products strongly toward the fold that short-range staples favour, and strengthening short-range staples suppresses the fold in which one of them must bridge long range, while in their kinetic model strong early seam contacts trap about half of folding runs

## Source

Dunn, Dannenberg, Ouldridge, Kwiatkowska, Turberfield and Bath (2015),
[LIT-2135](../literature.d/LIT-2135.md); read in [NOTE-1643](../notes.d/NOTE-1643.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The system**: a dimer template with about 10^23 staple configurations,
  22 well-folded planar routings in 6 AFM-distinguishable shapes, argued to
  be of roughly equal free energy.
- **Broken seam** (Fig. 4b): halving one staple of each seam pair shifts
  the annealed distribution strongly toward 5:0i, predicted by the model
  and confirmed by AFM (N = 223, 52% of candidates fitted). The authors
  argue the change costs about the same in every well-folded state, so it
  "should not affect their equilibrium populations".
- **Extended staples** (Fig. 4c): "the 4:1 fold is strongly suppressed",
  again without, by the authors' argument, an energetic penalty on
  alternatives.
- **Model** (Extended Data Fig. 8): misfolds in 52% of 1600 runs with the
  full seam set, 1% with broken seams; from the 1:1 intermediate 5:0 is
  inaccessible unless a seam breaks.
- **Could have come out otherwise**: the experimental distributions could
  have stayed put under the pathway-only modifications; they moved as the
  model predicted in three of four variants.

## What this does not say

- That the final folds are measured to be degenerate: that is a design
  argument.
- That the experimental trapping rate is about half: the 52% is the
  model's; experiment reports fitted fractions of candidates, not misfolds.
- That the effect holds at other ramp rates, isothermally or at other
  cation concentrations: one protocol was used.
- That the model is right in general: it is path-dependent in its free
  energies and fails for the long corner staple, having no bending
  penalty.
