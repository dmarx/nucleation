# NOTE-1234: Kay et al. — Dated gene duplications and the assembly of eukaryotes

## Contribution

It puts absolute dates on the order in which eukaryotic features were
assembled. Earlier work (Pittis & Gabaldón, [LIT-1595](../literature.d/LIT-1595.md), among it) ordered
events by the relative lengths of gene-tree branches. This paper dates
individual duplication nodes with a relaxed clock. It carries time
information from a dated species tree of archaea, bacteria and
eukaryotes into each gene tree as priors. Three results follow:
- the eukaryotic stem from the archaeal side (nFECA, 3.05–2.79 Ga) is
  about twice as long as the one from the alphaproteobacterial side
  (mFECA, 2.37–2.13 Ga) before LECA (1.80–1.67 Ga);
- most duplications in archaeal-origin families predate the onset of
  alphaproteobacterial-origin duplications, taken as mitochondrial
  endosymbiosis at about 2.2 Ga;
- most duplications in non-alphaproteobacterial bacterial families come
  after it.

## Key insight

A gene tree has no node for "the mitochondrion arrived". It does have
nodes where a eukaryotic gene duplicated before LECA, and each of those
can be dated. Dating them gives a ceiling for when each paralogue's
function could have begun: "Dating this node provides a maximum estimate
for the timing of origin of their paralogue-specific functions". The
authors read the first burst of duplications among genes from the
alphaproteobacterial partner as the start of its integration. Against
that marker, the host's own families (actin and its ARP2/3 paralogues,
γ-, δ- and ε-tubulin, Golgi and ER trafficking proteins, RNA polymerase
subunits, nuclear-targeted paralogues) were already duplicating. Hence
"complexified archaeon, late mitochondrion" (CALM).

## Assumptions

- **The species tree.** 62 marker genes, concatenated to 15,761
  positions. nFECA branches within Asgard archaea, sister to
  Heimdallarchaeia. mFECA is sister to Alphaproteobacteria excluding
  Magnetococcales. Eighteen fossil calibrations, uniform priors,
  cross-bracing of equivalent nodes (18 calibration instances, 88
  congruent splits), LG + F + G5, four MCMCTree runs.
- **Sequential Bayesian priors.** Pooled species-tree posteriors become
  skew-T priors and maximum constraints on gene-tree speciation nodes.
  Duplications in genes of Asgard (or TACK-sister) origin are
  constrained to lie between nFECA and LECA. Duplications in genes of
  alphaproteobacterial origin are constrained to lie between mFECA and
  LECA. The authors say the hierarchy means "the inferred ages of gene
  duplications are informed by the ages of nodes on the dated species
  tree".
- **Duplication onset marks endosymbiosis.** Gene duplication is much
  more frequent in eukaryotes than prokaryotes, so the first fixed
  duplications in symbiont-derived genes are taken to show the symbiont
  was already inside. This is argued, not tested.
- **Function follows duplication.** A duplication date is a maximum age
  for the paralogue's specific role. For targeting changes, the paper
  says so: "On the assumption that the change in localization occurred
  after gene duplication".
- **Present function stands for ancestral function.** Localization and
  function of each LECA group are taken from human reference proteins.
- **Sample.** Only families of prokaryotic ancestry with dateable
  pre-LECA duplications and alignments good enough for clock analysis.
  Genes novel to eukaryotes are excluded, because their duplication node
  is also the root. That leaves out most of what defines the nucleus,
  such as nuclear pores.

## Key results

- **The timeline (Fig. 1).** nFECA 3.05–2.79 Ga, mFECA 2.37–2.13 Ga, LECA
  1.80–1.67 Ga. The authors give median stem durations of about 1.1 and
  0.6 Gyr. Differences of the interval midpoints give about 1.19 and
  0.52 Gyr, as computed here. The posterior medians are not in the text,
  so the 0.6 could not be checked exactly. The sensitivity analyses
  (Supplementary Note 3, not read) are reported to leave nFECA and mFECA
  and the gap between them robust.
- **The sample.** 135 time-resolved gene trees: 95 of archaeal origin
  and 40 of bacterial origin. 9 of the 95 lack support for Asgard origin.
  19 of the 40 lack strong support for alphaproteobacterial origin.
- **Dating the mitochondrion (Figs 1d, 4a).**
  - Alphaproteobacterial-origin duplications begin soon after mFECA,
    "within 200 Myr", which the authors align to "around 2.2 Ga".
  - Among the earliest are paralogues that work outside the mitochondrion
    (METTL4, 2.24–2.11 Ga; type Y polymerases, 2.24–2.12 Ga; mismatch
    repair, 2.18–2.08 Ga), and parts of the protein import system (TIM14
    and PAM16, 2.24–1.99 Ga; TIM44, 2.22–2.00 Ga; PMPCA and PMPCB,
    2.16–1.94 Ga).
  - "85% of the pre-LECA duplicates of archaeal origin were estimated to
    have been fixed prior to mitochondrial endosymbiosis." The text does
    not say whether the base is 95 or 86 families.
  - 16 of 19 duplications of bacterial but not alphaproteobacterial
    origin have average ages after the inferred endosymbiosis.
- **Cytoskeleton (Fig. 2a).** Actin duplications from about 2.8 Ga. ARP3
  2.90–2.47 Ga and ARP2 2.72–2.06 Ga, a maximum age for branched actin
  and so for phagocytosis "as carried out by extant eukaryotes".
  γ-tubulin 2.84–2.41 Ga, δ 2.75–2.25, ε 2.61–2.07, and the
  filament-forming α and β later, 2.47–1.92 Ga. The authors conclude
  "an organized microtubule network before mitochondrial endosymbiosis,
  probably by 2.7 Ga".
- **Endomembranes (Figs 3, 4b,c).** The oldest trafficking duplications
  are in Golgi–ER–plasma-membrane families (TRAPPC3, 3.00–2.54 Ga;
  YIPF1/2, 2.91–2.55 Ga; COPD, 2.90–2.61 Ga). Endosome, lysosome and
  autophagosome families begin around 2.4 Ga (for example STX7/STX12,
  2.63–2.04 Ga; ARF6, 2.55–1.84 Ga). Bacterial-origin lipid enzymes
  duplicate late (SPTLC1–3, 2.16–1.99 Ga; ACSL, 2.12–1.87 Ga), some not
  from Alphaproteobacteria, which the authors read as "late
  complexification" of the ER by bacterial genes from non-mitochondrial
  sources. Endolysosomal transporters (CLC, SLC, ABCB) date with
  endolysosomal trafficking, and the CLC and SLC families are not of
  alphaproteobacterial origin.
- **Nucleus (Fig. 2b,c).** Sm–LSm duplications span 2.91–1.87 Ga. The
  nuclear localization signals in two subunits arose before the final
  duplication, dated 2.87–1.87 Ga. RNA polymerase I/II/III duplications
  fall in 2.91–2.34 Ga, pol III's at 2.73–2.32 Ga. Nuclear-targeted
  paralogues fall in 2.86–2.08 Ga. The authors note that localization
  signals "might have evolved before the nuclear compartment".
- **Scenarios (Fig. 5, Discussion).**
  - Mitochondria-early (the hydrogen hypothesis, [LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md)) is rejected.
  - Serial endosymbiosis is argued against, because most non-alphaproteobacterial
    genes duplicate after the mitochondrion.
  - Phagocytosis is left open: "our results do not allow us to
    discriminate between the origin of phagocytosis and the timing of
    mitochondrial endosymbiosis".
  - The results are "not fully compatible with any of the competing
    evolutionary scenarios", and fit CALM models best.
- **Environment.** Mitochondrial endosymbiosis is "almost coincident"
  with the Great Oxidation Event (2.43–2.22 Ga), in oceans that stayed
  largely anoxic. The authors say this is "compatible with" syntrophy
  models, while their timeline "precludes a syntrophic association
  between nFECA and mFECA prior to the GOE".
- **The abstract's dates against the body.** The abstract puts the
  cytoskeleton, trafficking, endomembranes, phagocytotic machinery and
  a nucleus "all between 3.0 and 2.25 billion years ago". The body's
  intervals for these features run lower: ARP2 to 2.06, α/β-tubulin to
  1.92, the Sm–LSm signal duplication to 1.87, nuclear-targeted
  paralogues to 2.08. Many overlap the 2.2 Ga marker. The 3.0–2.25 Ga
  range is the span of the older estimates, not of every interval.

## Concepts

- **nFECA and mFECA**: the points where the eukaryotic lineage branches
  from its closest sampled archaea and alphaproteobacteria. They move if
  closer relatives are found.
- **Pre-LECA duplication**: a node uniting two LECA clades in a gene
  tree. Its age is a maximum for the paralogues' distinct functions.
- **Cross-bracing**: fixing equivalent nodes (the same speciation in the
  nuclear and mitochondrial copies of the tree) to the same age.
- **Mitochondria-early, -intermediate, -late**: the authors' grouping of
  scenarios. Early: the hydrogen hypothesis. Late: the phagocytosing
  archaeon, syntrophy and serial endosymbiosis. Intermediate: reverse
  flow, inside-out and E3 ([LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md)'s model).
- **CALM**: "complexified archaeon, late mitochondrion", the class of
  models consistent with the results.

## Connections

The authors place their method against branch-length studies that
"used gene tree branch lengths, expressed in number of substitutions per
site and normalized by relative evolutionary rates post-LECA". That is
the method of Pittis & Gabaldón ([LIT-1595](../literature.d/LIT-1595.md), read in [NOTE-1240](NOTE-1240.md)). The
paper reaches the same order for the mitochondrion. It disagrees on what
came before. Pittis & Gabaldón infer a host already chimaeric with
non-alphaproteobacterial genes, and list serial symbioses among the
explanations. Kay et al. find most non-alphaproteobacterial duplications
after the mitochondrion and argue against serial endosymbiosis. The two
measure different things (duplication against acquisition), so the
disagreement is partial. The comparison is mine.

On Asgard cell biology the paper cites the cultivated isolate of
[LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md) (read in [NOTE-1238](NOTE-1238.md)): cytoskeleton-supported protrusions,
but "in terms of overall size and complexity, the Asgard archaea
cultivated so far resemble typical prokaryotes". It classes E3, that
paper's model, as mitochondria-intermediate and consistent with early
cytoskeletal development. It counts the hydrogen hypothesis ([LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md))
as mitochondria-early and rejected. Tobiasson et al. ([LIT-1558](../literature.d/LIT-1558.md)),
Bernabeu et al. ([LIT-1561](../literature.d/LIT-1561.md)) and Santana-Molina et al. ([LIT-1564](../literature.d/LIT-1564.md)) are not
cited in the text read here.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md), eukaryogenesis as a test.** The question set this
  paper as the "late mitochondrion after a complex host" programme. The
  reading bears that out, with two refinements.
  - **It speaks to one endosymbiotic partner, not a sequence.** The
    paper argues against serial endosymbiosis. Its non-alphaproteobacterial
    genes are explained as transfer, mostly after the mitochondrion. On
    the question's choice between "one two-partner merger or a sequence
    of associations", it takes the first, preceded by a long phase of
    host elaboration with no partner.
  - **What it dates is G and, partly, C.** The host's complexity before
    the mitochondrion is its own gene duplication, not dependence on
    anyone. After the mitochondrion, the earliest dated symbiont-gene
    duplications include the protein import system (TIM14/PAM16, TIM44,
    PMPCA/PMPCB). If the onset marker is right, the machinery for
    importing proteins into the symbiont was being elaborated within
    about 200 Myr. That is the gene-transfer-and-import route of the
    question, early in this association. The inference is mine.
  - **Nothing on D, R or S.** The paper does not address when partners
    became dependent, when they reproduced together, or conflict between
    them.
- **[NOTE-159](NOTE-159.md).** O'Malley and Parke report inferences placing the
  mitochondrion "late" and question whether eukaryogenesis was "driven
  primarily by a transformation in individuality". This paper is one of
  those inferences, dated. It supports the premise. It is silent on
  individuality.
- **[THEORY-141](../theory.d/THEORY-141.md).** No bearing on selection levels. A host that
  complexified for about half a billion years before the endosymbiosis is
  a case where much of the new cell's machinery owes nothing to the
  collective. The inference is mine.
- Filed as [THEORY-1099](../theory.d/THEORY-1099.md). No instruction for machine-learning
  practice.

## Limitations

- **Maximum ages read as presence.** A duplication date bounds a
  paralogue's function from above. To say the host "had" a feature
  before the mitochondrion needs the function to have followed the
  duplication soon after. The paper states this for nuclear targeting and
  leaves it implicit elsewhere.
- **The priors shape the order.** Alphaproteobacterial duplications
  cannot be older than mFECA; archaeal ones can reach back to nFECA. The
  authors grant this and report robustness in Supplementary Note 3,
  which was not read.
- **The marker is an assumption.** Equating the onset of symbiont-gene
  duplication with endosymbiosis is argued from the rarity of
  duplication in prokaryotes.
- **Overlapping intervals.** Many host-feature intervals reach below 2.2
  Ga. The abstract's "all between 3.0 and 2.25" summarizes the older
  ends.
- **A biased sample.** Only well-conserved, dateable families of
  prokaryotic origin. Archaeal families were easier to analyse, which the
  authors note "may simply reflect" more duplications on the archaeal
  stem, or may not. Eukaryote-specific genes, including most of the
  nucleus, are excluded.
- **Function from human orthologues.** Present localization in human
  cells stands for the role a paralogue had at its origin.
- **Not read here:** the supplementary notes, the gene-tree reports and
  the code. The robustness claims rest on them.

## Open questions

- Does the onset of duplication in symbiont-derived genes coincide with
  endosymbiosis, or lag it? A dated case with a known entry time
  (a younger endosymbiosis) would calibrate the marker.
- When were the non-alphaproteobacterial genes acquired, as against
  duplicated? This decides between transfer to the host before the
  mitochondrion and transfer after it.
- Can the endolysosomal system and the mitochondrion be ordered? The
  authors say their data cannot.
- How would placing nFECA closer (a deeper-sampled Heimdallarchaeia
  relative) move the archaeal-stem dates?
