# CLAIM-960: For a whole that is a bare relational arrangement, being a constituent adds no obligation beyond those grounded in the constituent's vulnerability, interests, rights and exposure to power: the part-whole relation locates where an obligation applies without grounding it, and because such an arrangement is not an agent the obligation falls on the agents or roles that maintain, control or benefit from it, not on the whole

## Which sense of "whole"

[TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md) sense 1, the default that [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md) assigns: a
configuration of relations among distinguishable processes, components, roles
or constraints, which "can contain conflicting processes, have no controller,
and be maintained from outside", and which is "a candidate for an agent only
under a stated criterion of agency". The claim is about this bare arrangement.
The parts at issue have standing of their own ([TERM-083](../terms.d/TERM-083.md)); the case as
stated makes them persons. Wholes whose membership carries more than standing
in the relations — a collective institution that is an agent (sense 3), a
cooperative scheme its constituents belong to — are the alternative reading,
[CLAIM-959](CLAIM-959.md).

## Working the case through

[CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md) holds fixed a person's vulnerability, interests, rights and
exposure to the controlling actors' power, and the institutional consequences,
and varies only whether the person is a constituent of the arrangement. Its
question: would the arrangement owe the constituent something it does not owe
the identically placed non-constituent? The three results, and which this claim
takes:

- **The ground is the second result.** In a bare relational arrangement,
  "being a constituent" is standing in the relations and nothing more. It is
  not a shared practice, a mutual recognition or a cooperative scheme; sense 1
  is defined to strip those out. So once the constituent's vulnerability,
  interests, rights and exposure are held fixed, flipping constitution leaves
  no normatively relevant difference between the two persons. Whatever is owed
  to the one is owed to the other, and it is owed because of the vulnerability,
  interests, rights and exposure both share, not because of the membership only
  one has. That is [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)'s second result: "a relational responsibility
  theory that applies across boundaries", in which "the mereological
  relationship would help identify where the obligations apply without
  independently grounding them". It extends [CLAIM-671](CLAIM-671.md)'s vulnerability ground,
  which already locates the strengthened responsibility by exposure rather than
  by membership, and closes the question [CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md) left open when it "holds
  membership fixed and does not deny" the first result.
- **The bearer is the third result.** A sense-1 arrangement is not an agent
  ([CLAIM-418](CLAIM-418.md), Active; [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)). An obligation needs a bearer that can owe
  and can take the alternative a duty weighs. So the arrangement is the ground
  and object of any norm concerning the part, not its bearer; the obligation
  falls on the agents or roles that maintain, control or benefit from the
  arrangement, "each as far as its control reaches". That is [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)'s third
  result, "especially relevant to Nucleation's broad ontology of
  organizations", and the bearer the line already fixes for the duty of
  answerability ([CLAIM-876](CLAIM-876.md)), for domination ([CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)) and for
  accountability ([CLAIM-687](CLAIM-687.md), Active).

The inference, with the contrast's critical questions, is [ARG-207](../arguments.d/ARG-207.md).

## The relation of part to whole, and who bears the obligation

The lead asks which relation of part to whole grounds a norm concerning the
part, and who bears it. For a bare arrangement the answer is: **no relation of
part to whole grounds a norm of its own.** Constitution, causal contribution to
the arrangement, and bare membership ground nothing the part is not already
owed on account of its vulnerability, interests, rights and exposure — grounds
any identically placed non-constituent shares. The part-whole relation does
work, but only locating work: it marks where those obligations bite, not why.
And the bearer is never the whole, which cannot owe; it is the controlling,
maintaining or benefiting agents and roles. Where no agent controls the
arrangement — an organism and its cells, an ecosystem and its populations — the
claim names no bearer, and [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md)'s prior part, whether a non-agent
whole can owe anything beyond the functional, stays open; this claim does not
need it answered, and is defeated only if the record must count such a whole a
bearer.

## What it does not say

- **That vulnerability or exposure grounds obligations.** Whether it does is
  [CLAIM-671](CLAIM-671.md)'s supposition, on a principle (Goodin's) the record has not read
  and does not hold. This claim says only that *whatever* grounds the
  obligation, it is not the bare membership: the ground, if any, is shared by
  the non-constituent.
- **That membership is idle in every whole.** It denies membership a grounding
  role only in the bare arrangement. Where membership is normatively thick,
  [CLAIM-959](CLAIM-959.md) reads the case the other way.
- **That constitution can always be varied with all else held.** [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)'s
  own caution: in some arrangements being a constituent changes the person's
  vulnerability or the consequences, and for wholes whose parts are not persons
  the non-member case may not be coherent. Where the variation cannot be made
  cleanly, the pair does not discriminate, and the claim's scope is the wholes
  for which it can.
- **That the whole is nothing to the norm.** It is the norm's ground and
  object: what is occluding, dominating or depended on. It is not its bearer.

## The alternative reading

[CLAIM-959](CLAIM-959.md) takes [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)'s first result for a normatively thick
membership. The two readings differ in which sense of "whole" the case picks
out and in whether an associative-obligation principle is granted. For the bare
arrangement this claim's reading holds without such a principle; the thick case
needs one the record has not adopted. Which the case is really about, and
whether to adopt the principle, is the owner's call.
