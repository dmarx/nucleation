# CLAIM-tmp4xhdn: A non-eliminative structural realism that keeps relata but denies them any intrinsic identity-fixing feature is the position Chakravartty's dilemma targets, and granting numerical plurality without relational differentiation, as A194 and A198 do, concedes a non-relational ground of identity

## The objection

The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmp8yo01](CLAIM-tmp8yo01.md)) keeps relata and denies them any identity
beyond relational differentiation (U54: "only in so far as they are
differentiated by their relata"). The record already holds the objection to
exactly that position. Chakravartty ([LIT-196](../literature.d/LIT-196.md), read in [NOTE-149](../notes.d/NOTE-149.md)) argues that
one "cannot deny eliminativism and a role for the intrinsic in the analysis
of identity" (p. 200). An object with no intrinsic features "is not an object
at all" (p. 197), so a view that strips every intrinsic feature away is
eliminativism under another name. [THEORY-034](../theory.d/THEORY-034.md) reads the dilemma as well aimed
only at structural realisms that keep relata but deny them every intrinsic
identity-fixing feature, and finds only Floridi's among the record's readings.
The owner's thesis would be the second.

The exchange placed the thesis next to the very view the dilemma names.
A194: "close to the non-eliminative OSR developed by Esfeld and Lam"
([LIT-tmpme4lk](../literature.d/LIT-tmpme4lk.md)). [NOTE-149](../notes.d/NOTE-149.md) records that Chakravartty's fn. 9 lists Esfeld and
Esfeld & Lam among the no-priority views, and that §10.6 applies the same
"what is the what?" question to Esfeld & Lam's spacetime points: "an object
as such is nothing but what bears the relations". None of A191, A194, A198,
A203 or A218 mentions Chakravartty.

## The partial reply the owner's move supplies

[NOTE-149](../notes.d/NOTE-149.md)'s C6 notes a gap in the dilemma. Chakravartty asserts that mass and
charge are intrinsic and fix *identity*, and "the chapter does not engage
group-theoretic readings of them". The owner's character move is such a
reading. Kind-properties become invariants of a symmetry's representations,
as in Wigner's classification of particles by irreducible representations of the
Poincaré group, which [NOTE-149](../notes.d/NOTE-149.md)'s limitations section names.
That removes intrinsic properties from the analysis of *kind*.

It does not reach *identity*. Chakravartty's own line is that citing quantum
non-individuation for OSR "conflate[s] individuation with identity" (p. 204), and
characters do not individuate: isomorphic copies of a representation share
one character and differ only by embedding ([CLAIM-tmp3sn40](CLAIM-tmp3sn40.md)). So the character
move protects the kind claim ([CLAIM-tmpaedgb](CLAIM-tmpaedgb.md)) and leaves this one exposed.

## What it forces

A choice between the two versions of the thesis.

The exchange's examples are the easy case. A194 §5 uses "A symmetric graph …
two distinct vertices exchanged by an automorphism", and A203 §2 two vertices
"connected by an undirected edge". A198 §3's two-person interaction is the same:
in the mutual configuration {a→b, b→a}, a and b are weakly discernible by an
irreflexive symmetric relation. The hard case is Leitgeb and Ladyman's edgeless
two-node graph G′ ([LIT-154](../literature.d/LIT-154.md), read in [NOTE-133](../notes.d/NOTE-133.md)). Its two nodes are not even
weakly discernible, yet graph theory counts two. Two singletons in Set are the
categorical version ([THEORY-032](../theory.d/THEORY-032.md)).

- **The strict thesis** says G′ has one node, or none. That is against
  mathematical practice.
- **The weak thesis** says the plurality is primitive while qualitative
  identity is relational. This is where the assistant went. A194 §5: "Their
  numerical plurality is real; their distinction is not grounded in
  independent intrinsic identities." A198 §3: "a perfectly symmetric
  two-person interaction can have two participants … A non-eliminative
  structural realism can accept the first without requiring the second in
  every case." That splits identity into a numerical part that relations do
  not fix and a qualitative part that they do. U54 allowed no such split. A
  primitive fact of numerical distinctness is a non-relational ground of
  identity, which is the concession the dilemma demands, in a thin form.

Leitgeb and Ladyman take the weak option for mathematical places only. [NOTE-133](../notes.d/NOTE-133.md):
"It does not support the corresponding claim for physics: the authors
explicitly withhold it." Nothing in the record extends it to persons or
utterances.

## What it does not say

- That the dilemma is sound. Its regress step is one the author admits may
  look question-begging (p. 201; [THEORY-034](../theory.d/THEORY-034.md), [NOTE-149](../notes.d/NOTE-149.md) C3).
- That the owner must take the intrinsic horn. The weak thesis is a coherent
  position. The objection is that it is a different one from U54's, and that
  A194 and A198 moved to it without saying so.
- That communicative cases contain relata no relation discerns. Whether two
  act tokens can be relationally undifferentiated is open.
- Anything against the kind claim, whose identity criteria are qualitative by
  construction.
