# THEORY-666: A system that produces itself, a network of production that regenerates the components which produce it and constitute it as a distinguishable unity, has by that fact an intrinsic purpose, the maintenance of its identity, and with it a point of view, valence and a world as distinct from an environment, so that teleology in organisms is real and not an observer's as-if; the account is stated for the cell, and a multicellular organism is not itself autopoietic

## Source

Andreas Weber and Francisco J. Varela (2002), "Life after Kant: Natural
purposes and the autopoietic foundations of biological individuality",
*Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 1(2), 97–125, [LIT-1018](../literature.d/LIT-1018.md), read in
full in [NOTE-720](../notes.d/NOTE-720.md). Published after Varela's death.

## What was actually shown

**Kant (§ 2).** An organism is a natural purpose, "both cause and effect
of itself" (KdU § 64), every part "an organ bringing forth the other
parts" (§ 65). Its purposiveness is intrinsic, unlike an artifact's, which
always points to an external purpose. Kant's own position is unstable: an
"ontological reductionist, but an epistemological anti-reductionist". The
authors grant his stance "may depend on the chosen quotations".

**Jonas (§§ 3.1–3.2).** The organism "keeps its identity exactly by not
keeping the same matter"; "Organisms are things whose existence is their
own achievement". Metabolism is the defining quality of life, and with it
come precariousness, concern and a subjective perspective even in "the
simplest forms of life". We have "the evidence of our intrinsic teleology
in us".

**Autopoiesis (§ 3.3).** "A network of processes of production
(synthesis and destruction) of components such that these components:
1. continuously regenerate the network that is producing them, and
2. constitute the system as a distinguishable unity in the domain in which
they exist" (Varela 1997). For the cell, metabolism and the membrane it
produces are the key terms. "A multicellular organism ... is not in itself
an autopoietic unit of second order"; its autonomy comes through a
"neural identity", deferred.

**The thesis (§§ 3.4, 4).** Self-production "is already and inevitably a
self-affirmation that shows the organism as involved in the fundamental
purpose of maintaining its identity". From it follow a purpose of
sense-creation, the difference between environment ("the physical impacts
it receives") and world (that environment "evaluated from the point of
view established by maintaining an identity"), and valence, "attraction or
rejection". Intrinsic teleology is "an empirical feature of an organism",
objective "only insofar as an organism is a center that organizes matter
into a living being and its Umwelt".

**What could have come out otherwise.** Little; this is exegesis and
synthesis. The authors grant that the metabolism–membrane circle can be
described "entirely from the outside", so what they add is not a
mechanism.

## What this does not say

- **How to tell a produced boundary from an imposed one.** The definition's
  second clause separates a membrane the cell makes from a beam joining two
  pendulums, but the paper applies it only to the cell, where production
  is known from biochemistry, and gives no test ([NOTE-720](../notes.d/NOTE-720.md) for [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)).
- **That combined forms have produced boundaries.** If even an animal is
  not itself autopoietic, a pair, a group or a firm is not.
- **Where autopoiesis differs from a candle flame in organisation.** The
  difference is placed in "concern" and "the instauration of a point of
  view", a phenomenological claim with no observable.
- **That grades of better and worse follow.** Valence here follows from
  self-production directly. A system that only maintains itself or fails
  has no grades, which is the gap Di Paolo's adaptivity fills
  ([THEORY-628](THEORY-628.md)).
- **That persistence is the standard of value.** The paper quotes Jonas:
  "Not duration as such, but 'duration of what?' is the question." But in
  § 3.1 it also calls the "Yes!" to continued existence "the mother-value
  of all values" and runs, in one paragraph, to a "right to the pursuit of
  an undisturbed life". That step is asserted and left to Hösle (note 14),
  and it is not part of this account.
