# LIT-tmpx7zhl: Dynamic Semantics

Rick Nouwen, Adrian Brasoveanu, Jan van Eijck and Albert Visser (2010),
"Dynamic Semantics", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (ed. Edward N.
Zalta). First published 23 August 2010; substantive revision 12 July 2016.
<https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dynamic-semantics/>

## Key takeaways

- **The slogan.** "Meaning is context change potential": a sentence denotes
  an update on a context, and truth is derived from it. The entry calls
  this an abstract framework that makes no empirical predictions by
  itself, and a generalization of truth-conditional semantics rather than
  a rival to it.
- **Dynamic predicate logic.** Groenendijk and Stokhof's DPL interprets a
  formula as a relation between assignments. Predications are tests,
  conjunction is relational composition, and ∃x is a random reset of x.
  So ∃x(φ) ∧ ψ and ∃x(φ ∧ ψ) are equivalent, and "Mary met a student. He
  needed help." gets a compositional analysis that classical logic cannot
  give it sentence by sentence.
- **Donkey sentences.** "If a farmer owns a donkey, he beats it" receives
  its universal reading compositionally. First-order logic can state the
  same truth conditions, but not as the output of a compositional
  translation. DPL defines no set that first-order logic cannot: in a weak
  sense "nothing new happens".
- **Beyond singular anaphora.** Plural and quantificational subordination
  ("Every boy wrote an essay. Each of them submitted it") need contexts
  that are sets of assignments. Modal subordination, vagueness standards,
  at-issue against appositive content, and evidentials get dynamic
  treatments of the same kind.
- **Presupposition.** Heim, after Karttunen, makes local contexts part of
  the meaning of the connectives. Critics such as Soames object that this
  does not explain why unattested connective meanings do not occur.
  Rothschild answers with constraints on definedness, and static accounts
  (Schlenker, Chemla, George, Fox) revived in response. Dynamic epistemic
  logic models announcements and presuppositions as updates on
  multi-agent knowledge states.
- **Typed logic.** Dynamic Montague grammar and its successors inherit
  DPL's destructive reassignment. Muskens's compositional DRT is the
  de facto standard. Stack semantics and van Eijck's incremental typed
  logic avoid destructive reassignment.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-08 at the owner's request and read in full
([NOTE-tmpefif9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpefif9.md)). It is the record's first account of formal semantics as
linguists practise it. Its nearest neighbour is [LIT-208](LIT-208.md), on
distributional semantics and holism. Both make meaning depend on more than
the expression. Distributional semantics takes it from use across a
corpus. Dynamic semantics keeps truth conditions and adds what the
sentence does to the discourse context.
