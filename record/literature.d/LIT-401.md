# LIT-401: What is it like to be a group agent?

Christian List (2016), *Noûs 52(2), 295–319 (2018 issue; online 28 July 2016)* — DOI-10.1111/nous.12162

## Key takeaways

- Agency composes and experience, on this argument, does not. A suitably
  organised collective meets a thin belief–desire definition of agency and
  has "consciousness as awareness" (access, attention, even sleep and
  waking), so all of Schwitzgebel's functional marks can be granted.
  Whether it also has phenomenal consciousness is a separate question,
  settled only by a psycho-physical bridge principle.
- Conditional on IIT, the answer is "(close to) nothing". The argument is
  that group agents are partitionable at low cost in Φ, not that their
  parts are minded. Exclusion is offered as a second, independent route.
- The verdict is asserted from three structural reasons. No group's Φ is
  computed or even estimated, and List allows that a "biomorphic"
  science-fiction collective might score higher.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the works bridging
Minsky's Society of Mind ([LIT-429](LIT-429.md), with the memos [LIT-399](LIT-399.md) and
[LIT-402](LIT-402.md) and Singh's exposition [LIT-444](LIT-444.md)) to Schwitzgebel's
"If materialism is true, the United States is probably conscious"
([LIT-159](LIT-159.md), read in [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md)). [NOTE-340](../notes.d/NOTE-340.md) is the close reading of the
same day, and it placed the work: **Active**.

**Where it sits on the seam.** It sits on the composition side, at the
exact point where agency is separated from experience. It accepts that
agency is built from minded parts and denies that experience comes with
it. Against Schwitzgebel's claims as [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md) numbers them:

- **C3 (a whole of conscious parts can be conscious): contests**, for
  actual group agents only. It does not deny the possibility in principle.
  Its main argument is about coupling structure, not about the parts being
  minded.
- **C4 (Putnam's anti-nesting principle is unjustified): neutral.** Putnam
  is not discussed.
- **C5 (IIT's exclusion postulate has absurd consequences): contests by
  reliance, not by argument.** §6.5 invokes exclusion as an extra reason
  and notes that Schwitzgebel rejects it, but does not answer the
  one-ballot reductio. The main low-Φ argument does not need exclusion.

Schwitzgebel answered it in his book: *The Weirdness of the World*
([LIT-216](LIT-216.md); see [NOTE-188](../notes.d/NOTE-188.md)) adds List 2018 to its IIT footnote and gives the
"Max goes to sleep" case against exclusion. No stand-alone reply to List
was found.

What it gives the bridge, and why it is filed for it:

- **It turns Minsky's architecture into evidence against experience.**
  List's first reason is that a group agent is "decomposable into
  functionally relatively self-contained units" (p. 32). That is the
  society-of-agents picture. Under IIT, decomposability itself lowers Φ,
  so the more a whole looks like a society of nearly independent
  specialists, the less experience the theory gives it. The argument
  applies to a society of mindless agents too, so it would bear on a
  Minsky-style mind exactly as on a nation unless its agents are densely
  recurrently coupled. The paper does not draw that consequence.
- **Its third reason is Chalmers's objection** from Schwitzgebel's §6 (most
  capacities are the members' own, not their relations'), quoted from
  [LIT-159](LIT-159.md). [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md) records Schwitzgebel's reply, that global-workspace
  and "fame" models of human consciousness are also subsystem-driven. List
  does not engage it.
- It is the IIT application that [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md)'s open question asks for ("does
  a formal IIT calculation actually put a polity's Φ above a brain's?"),
  but only in heuristic form, and with the simplified Aaronson Φ rather
  than IIT 3.0 (n. 17). It does not settle that question. [LIT-185](LIT-185.md) and its
  reading [NOTE-122](../notes.d/NOTE-122.md) are where the record holds IIT's current formal
  problems: Barrett et al. argue the measure is undefined for real
  systems, which cuts against any confident low-Φ verdict for a group as
  much as against a high one.

Other neighbours. Dennett's "Real Patterns" ([LIT-220](LIT-220.md)) is the stance List's
thermostat remark leans on (p. 6) without endorsing interpretationism. The
indicator approach of Butlin et al. ([LIT-056](LIT-056.md)) is exposed to List's worry
about global workspace: GWT "simply asserts functionalist conditions for
access consciousness and then stipulates" phenomenal ones (p. 24). List
grants groups a global workspace in principle, which is the case [NOTE-131](../notes.d/NOTE-131.md)
says neither [LIT-056](LIT-056.md) nor [LIT-159](LIT-159.md) addresses. Pilgrim et al.'s collective
intelligence framework ([LIT-046](LIT-046.md)) treats the same groups computationally
and says nothing about experience; List's agency/awareness/experience
split is the vocabulary for keeping those apart. The paper restates and
extends the normative claim of List and Pettit's *Group Agency*
([LIT-391](LIT-391.md), chs 7–8). List says the book asserted an asymmetry between
individual and corporate rights without fully defending it, and this
paper's consciousness argument is offered as the missing ground.

It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice, so nothing
belongs in the Anthology of the SOTA.
