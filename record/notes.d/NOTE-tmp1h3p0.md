summary: >-
  A review arguing that the bidirectional transfer of cells between mother
  and fetus during pregnancy, and their persistence for decades (fetal cells
  in mothers: fetal microchimerism, FMC; maternal cells in offspring:
  maternal microchimerism, MMC), is not accidental but serves reproductive
  fitness: in mice, maternal microchimeric cells sustain offspring's
  regulatory T cell tolerance of non-inherited maternal antigens (NIMAs),
  which protects the offspring's own later pregnancies sired by NIMA-matched
  males (Kinder et al. 2015, ref. 14), and fetal cells retained in mothers
  may analogously protect later pregnancies (supported in humans by
  partner-specific protection against preeclampsia). Microchimeric cells may
  also replace injured tissue or be targets of alloimmunity resembling
  autoimmunity; causal roles in humans are mostly unestablished. The authors
  propose redefining immunological identity to include a "microchiome" of
  familially relevant "extended-self" antigens.

# NOTE-tmp1h3p0: Kinder and others (2017) — Immunological implications of pregnancy-induced microchimerism

## Contribution

A synthesis linking microchimerism to tolerance and reproductive fitness; the
"microchiome" and "extended-self" framing; a table of beneficial and harmful
roles; an agenda (homing, differentiation cues, tools).

## Key results

1. **Not accidental (Abstract).** "these microchimeric cells expressing
   noninherited familially relevant antigenic traits are not accidental
   souvenirs of pregnancy, but are purposefully retained within mothers and
   their offspring to promote genetic fitness by improving the outcome of
   future pregnancies."
2. **Persistence (Maternal memory to fetal antigen persists postpartum).**
   "Y-chromosome DNA can be amplified in maternal PBMCs up to 27 years after
   giving birth to a son".
3. **Intervention in mice (Postnatal tolerance to NIMAs).** "the depletion of
   maternal microchimeric cells from female mice overturned this protection
   against fetal wastage during subsequent pregnancy and eliminated the
   expanded accumulation of NIMA-specific Treg cells".
4. **Caveat on that result (same section).** Protection was shown "when
   complete overlap in MHC haplotype alleles was present ... using defined
   strains of inbred mice"; the degree with partial overlap, as in humans, is
   untested.
5. **Instigators, not beneficiaries (Outlook).** "these genetically foreign
   cells are probably not the beneficiaries, but rather the instigators of
   expanded immune tolerance".
6. **Microchiome and extended self (Redefining immunological identity).**
   "these genetically discordant microchimeric cells can analogously be viewed
   as a biologically active 'microchiome' containing an expanded repertoire of
   familially relevant 'extended-self' antigens". The analogy is explicitly to
   the microbiome's "extended-self" antigens.
7. **Unknowns (Introduction).** "Despite near uniform agreement that all
   individuals contain these microchimeric cells, surprisingly little is known
   regarding their biological function and molecular properties."
8. **Autoimmunity causality speculative (FMC in maternal autoimmunity).** "the
   causative relationship between increased FMC and maternal autoimmunity
   remains speculative."
9. **Grandmaternal transfer unconfirmed (Redefining immunological identity).**
   "direct evidence is still lacking to confirm the existence of grandmaternal
   microchimeric cells".

## Assumptions

Adaptationist reasoning: conservation across placental mammals implies
fitness benefit ("purposefully retained"); mouse inbred-strain results inform
human biology; tolerance can be inferred from Treg accumulation and graft
outcomes.

## Concepts

Fetal and maternal microchimerism (FMC, MMC); non-inherited maternal antigens
(NIMAs); peripherally induced Treg cells; immunological identity; self /
non-self; "microchiome"; "extended self"; maternal–fetal conflict; nature's
allograft; constitutive chimerism.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** A13's account ("proposes that persistent maternal and fetal cells form a
biologically active 'microchiome' and an immunological 'extended self'") is
**accurate**. Two points of precision: "extended self" is a claim about
*immunological identity* (which antigens are tolerated), not about
individuality or selfhood in a wider sense; and the "purposefully retained"
thesis rests mainly on one mouse study (ref. 14) with full MHC overlap.

- **CLAIM-tmpn0ytb** (microchimerism bears on graded or processual
  individuality; separation is not completed at birth at the level of
  cells): **supports the premise, not the gradedness**. It documents
  bidirectional, decades-long persistence and an intervention showing
  maternal cells do work in the offspring's immune regulation, and its
  authors draw an identity conclusion for the immune system ("forces
  reconsideration of how we define immunological identity"). That is A11's
  "immunological boundary" coming apart from the genetic one, exactly; it
  says nothing about organismal individuality being graded. Consistent with
  ARG-tmp51yxc's reading ("the premise, not the gradedness"). The instigators-not-beneficiaries
  line (result 5) is a function claim, in A11's third step (causal
  contribution to host function) for tolerance only.
- **THEORY-443** (evolutionary and physiological individuality come apart):
  **supports, as an instance**. Cells genetically of one lineage (the
  mother's) take part in the physiological (immune) regulation of another
  individual, and the authors' explanation is in evolutionary terms (the
  mother's genetic fitness through her daughter's pregnancies): physiological
  integration with a different evolutionary beneficiary. The paper itself does
  not use the individuality literature.

## Limitations

A 2017 review: later work (the branch's THEORY-tmp6t2l2, THEORY-tmp4rc81)
refines it; adaptationist inference from conservation; the central fitness
result is from inbred mice with full MHC overlap; human evidence is mostly
correlational (partner-specific preeclampsia, transplant outcomes);
tools to manipulate rare cells were lacking.
