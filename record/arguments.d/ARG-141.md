# ARG-141: Mitzenmacher's survey supports the claim that a power law does not identify its mechanism, from one law reached by several mechanisms and from the closeness of the lognormal, though it offers neither a fitting method nor the dynamical tests

## The inference

[CLAIM-803](../claims.d/CLAIM-803.md): a power-law distribution does not identify its mechanism,
nor does a shared law show a shared mechanism; the mechanism has to be read
from the dynamics, after the tail is fitted against the lognormal.

## Critical questions (source)

**Does the work say this, read in context?** The first clause, nearly in
words: "Just because one finds a compelling mechanism to explain a power
law does not mean that there are not other, perhaps simpler,
explanations" (§6, p. 238). It shows the case: Zipf's law from Simon's
attachment (§3), from Mandelbrot's optimization (§4) and from monkeys
typing at random (§6). And a lognormal "may appear linear on a log-log plot
for several orders of magnitude" (§2), so "it is not clear that a
definitive answer is possible" (§8).

**Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** For the negative half,
yes. The positive half, read the dynamics and fit first, is not in the
paper: §2 calls the log-log line "a simple empirical test", and the
dynamical tests the claim names (gain proportional to size, floors and
resets, a cutoff that moves with system size) are not discussed. "Fit first" rests on Clauset, Shalizi and Newman ([LIT-2987](../literature.d/LIT-2987.md), read on
2026-11-22; [ARG-172](ARG-172.md)); the dynamical tests rest on A alone, since
Clauset et al. have none (corrected on 2026-11-22).

**Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** No, so
long as it is cited for the first clause.

## Verdict

Support for the first clause. [LIT-3003](../literature.d/LIT-3003.md) is replaced in the claim's
grounds by [THEORY-2150](../theory.d/THEORY-2150.md).

## Change of 2026-11-22

Corrected: the dynamical tests do not rest on Clauset, Shalizi and Newman,
who have none; only "fit first" does. Version 2.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Clauset, Shalizi and Newman, read

**Qualifies; complementary** ([LIT-2987](../literature.d/LIT-2987.md), [NOTE-2262](../notes.d/NOTE-2262.md), [THEORY-2173](../theory.d/THEORY-2173.md)).
Mitzenmacher supplies the negative clause; Clauset et al. supply the
fitting method this argument notes Mitzenmacher lacks, and they reject
exactly the "simple empirical test" Mitzenmacher's §2 offers: "being
roughly straight on a log-log plot is a necessary but not sufficient
condition" (§4, p. 15). Their support for the claim is [ARG-172](ARG-172.md). The
verdict here is unchanged.
