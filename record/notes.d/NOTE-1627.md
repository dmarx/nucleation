# NOTE-1627: Shirt-Ediss, Torelli et al. — Optimising DNA origami assembly by reducing off-target interactions

## Contribution

Empirical evidence that, with the design held fixed (routing, staple
break points) and every staple a full Watson-Crick complement of its
scaffold, the off-target binding possibilities implicit in the base
sequence can decide whether the target is reached. It also gives a static,
non-simulation selection method and tool ("scaffoldselector"). The authors
set it against the picture of sequence-agnostic nucleation and growth and
against folding models that admit only on-target binding.

## Key insight

The same origami design can be realised by many sequences, and they are
not equivalent: a sequence that offers many partial, unintended bindings
(staple to wrong scaffold site, scaffold to itself, staple to staple,
staple hairpins) folds badly, even though its intended pairing pattern is
identical to that of a sequence that folds well. Removing unintended
compatibilities while keeping every intended one improves yield.

## Assumptions

- **Hypothesis, not mechanism.** "scaffold sequences (and complementary
  staple sets) that lead to a reduced number of off-target reactions are
  statistically less likely to get kinetically trapped during the origami
  self-assembly process" (Introduction). No trap is identified, located or
  observed.
- **What counts as a site.** A binding site counts at ΔG ≤ −7.0 kcal/mol at
  37 °C, from a fast sequence-averaged energy model parameterised on
  NUPACK that allows symmetric interior loops (Fig. 2); M3 and M4 come
  from NUPACK and are maxima, not sums.
- **Equal weights.** "in the absence of better information, we assume that
  the four scoring metrics used in Fig. 1 have equal weightings"
  (Discussion).
- **Manual AFM classes.** "well-folded", "semi-folded", and "misfolded",
  the last being "fragments or aggregates without a specific shape"
  (Results); classified blind.
- **Uncontrolled differences.** The "2" and "3" scaffolds are Lambda DNA
  regions made by PCR and T7 exonuclease digestion; the de Bruijn
  scaffolds are synthesised otherwise, which the authors name as a
  confound. GC content differs between variants (T2 44.8%, T3 59%).
- **Small scaffolds.** 1653 to 2484 nt in vitro; folding at 12.5 mM Mg
  acetate, 10x staples, 1.6 nM scaffold.

## Key results

- **Same design, different sequence (Results, selection of variants).**
  All variants of a shape "had a fully complementary staple set to the
  scaffold sequence, and all made the target shape when fully
  self-assembled"; "The only factor differentiating variants was the
  number of off-target bindings possible in each case."
- **Triangle (Results, AFM; Fig. 4d).** "T1 and T2 variants ... folded
  with the highest frequency into well-folded triangles (65% and 39.3%
  respectively)"; T3 "revealed the lowest percentage of well-folded
  nanostructures (18.7%)".
- **Rectangle, tetrahedron, 6-helix bundle (Figs. 5–6; Supplementary Fig.
  43).** R3 "showed a high percentage (about 90%) of semi-folded and
  misfolded assemblies"; TH1 "gave exceptional assembly yield (74.8%
  well-folded)", while TH3 produced "mis-folded structures that were not
  possible to categorise reliably"; 6HB1 and 6HB2 were "both around 66%
  well-folded" and for 6HB3 "approx 70% were mis-folded or semi-folded"
  (the 6-helix bundle in Bonn only).
- **Summary (Discussion).** The off-target-prone variants "nearly
  completely failed to fold into the intended target shapes in the buffer
  conditions used (only 0–20% well-folded AFM yield)". The one ordering
  that held everywhere: the "2" variants "always assembled in higher
  yield" than the "3" variants.
- **Anomaly (Discussion).** In the Bonn replication, "R2 folded with the
  highest AFM yield, followed by R1 and then R3", so the de Bruijn control
  lost to a Lambda sequence with more off-target sites. The authors raise
  "other, hidden sequence design principles" and DNA synthesis and
  purification as a confound.
- **What failure looks like (Discussion).** T3 and R3 "displayed well
  aggregates when only scaffold strands were present", and T3, R3 and TH3
  did so in the full reactions, "suggesting that for these origamis,
  scaffold strands were hybridised together either directly and/or
  indirectly during assembly". No alternative well-defined structure is
  reported.
- **Trapping despite correction (Discussion).** "our results support the
  view that it is still possible to kinetically trap origami folding if
  off-target reactions are too prevalent", after granting self-correcting
  mechanisms such as strand displacement.
- **Protocol (Results, AGE).** Without initial denaturation, isothermal
  folding at 37 °C gave "similar band patterns" for T1–T3; with
  denaturation "the T3 variant showed aggregates". The fast ramp (95 °C to
  20 °C in 1 h 15 min) was used for AFM; slow-ramp samples showed fainter
  bands. No yield is given per protocol.
- **Persistence (Results, AGE at 4 °C).** After 4 to 5 days, T1 and T2
  kept their band intensity, "indicating higher stability"; for the
  rectangle, "the R3 sample (fast ramp) band disappeared", "underlying the
  assembly instability".
- **Optical tweezers (Fig. 7).** "T3 (GC = 59%) exhibited the highest
  unfolding forces, T2 (GC = 44.8%) the lowest": force tracks GC content,
  not the ranking. Uniformity tracks the ranking: about 75% of T1 traces
  clustered into one group, about 36% of T2 and about 18% of T3 (n = 28 to
  42 traces per variant).
- **Survey of 14 designs (Results).** Biological pools vary most; even for
  8 knt scaffolds there is about a 40% "relative reduction in off-target
  sites" from reverse front to Pareto front. "rotating the scaffold strand
  did little to minimise the already present off-target reactions,
  particularly for larger origamis". GC content and k-mer repeats
  correlate with the metrics "but not tightly enough to substitute any of
  them".
- **What the method cannot see (Discussion).** It "cannot predict classic
  assembly features such as structure melting temperature, speed of
  folding, or potential cooling/re-heating hysteresis".

## Concepts

- **Off-target binding**: any binding other than the intended
  staple–scaffold pairings, in four classes: staple–scaffold,
  scaffold–scaffold, staple–staple, staple hairpin (M1 to M4).
- **Sequence variant**: a scaffold sequence with its complementary staples,
  realising a fixed design.
- **Pareto and reverse fronts**: the best and worst trade-offs among
  variants in the four-metric space.
- **De Bruijn scaffold**: a synthetic sequence in which no window of
  length k recurs.
- **Scaffold rotation**: moving a fixed-sequence scaffold around the
  routing by choosing other staples.
- **Domain-level behaviour**: the authors' term for sequences "where the
  only binding events are the intended on-target bindings between staples
  and scaffold".
- **Positive and negative design**: rewarding intended bindings against
  penalising unintended ones; the method does only the second.
- **Mechanical uniformity**: the non-uniformity metric and trace
  clustering of optical-tweezers unfolding curves.

[THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)), whose 8-nt beads "cannot incorporate strand-displacement
reactions" and whose "kinetic traps are limited to situations where

dissertation, [LIT-2138](../literature.d/LIT-2138.md) ([NOTE-1644](NOTE-1644.md)), agrees in direction (spurious

[LIT-2127](../literature.d/LIT-2127.md) ([NOTE-1639](NOTE-1639.md)), vary design at a fixed shape; together the
two show design and sequence each moving yield. Dunn et al. (2015),

this paper does not vary reversibility.

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization.** In A's third paste, §6: "And a 2026 study of
  DNA origami optimization examines how unintended binding interactions can
  interfere with target assembly, showing why controlling off-target
  interactions remains important." "a 2026 study" is supported (published
  2026-05-26; preprint 2025-01-30). "DNA origami optimization" is
  supported, though the optimisation is of scaffold sequence selection
  specifically, which A does not say. "examines how unintended binding
  interactions can interfere" is partly supported: the paper shows that
  off-target-prone sequences fold worse, by correlating enumerated sites
  with AFM yield, not how; no mechanism, trap or pathway is observed.
  "remains important" is A's paraphrase in its framing: the paper says
  off-target reactions "have so far been largely ignored by the DNA
  nanotechnology community" and offers its result as a "change in
  perspective". A's surrounding lines, "Enough reversibility to correct
  errors" and that the nucleus should stabilize the right relationships
  without prematurely freezing the wrong ones, are A's own: the paper
  varies no reversibility and studies no nucleus, and its only remark on
  correction is the hedged Discussion sentence above. The paper fits A's
  heading for §6, removing constraints as well as adding them, better than
  A uses it: its intervention is purely removing unintended
  compatibilities while keeping every intended one.
- **[CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md) (feasible, reachable, reliably reachable).** Supports, and
  is the cleanest instance on the branch: every variant is feasible by
  construction, and reliable reach under one protocol ranges from about
  65–75% to 0–20%. It does not separate reachable from reliably
  reachable, since some off-target-prone objects are well folded.
- **[CLAIM-583](../claims.d/CLAIM-583.md) (same complex, moves and costs, different reachability).**
  Supports at the domain level, qualifies at the base level. Design,
  routing and intended pairing are identical, nearer to one compatibility
  complex than Majikes et al.'s designs. But the difference is attributed
  to extra off-target compatibilities, so the full base-level complex
  differs: domain-level equivalence does not fix reachability, which is not
  a counterexample within one fully specified complex.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md) (reaching threshold against holding requirement).** Leaves
  open: no melt, barrier or threshold is measured. A weak hint: the
  unfolding force, a proxy for holding, is highest for T3, the worst
  folder, but it tracks GC content.
- **[CLAIM-569](../claims.d/CLAIM-569.md) (construct, maintain, transition).** Weak support:
  construction yield and persistence at 4 °C both rank the variants 1 and
  2 above 3, so here they agree rather than come apart. The vanishing R3
  band is a failure of maintenance, of misfolded product or of the
  structure, unresolved. **[CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md)**: consistent; assembly and multi-day
  persistence are reported separately, and there is no regeneration.
- **[CLAIM-557](../claims.d/CLAIM-557.md), [CLAIM-574](../claims.d/CLAIM-574.md), [CLAIM-570](../claims.d/CLAIM-570.md), [CLAIM-592](../claims.d/CLAIM-592.md) (observing budgets).** A

## Limitations

- **Correlational**, with three variants per shape and one off-target-prone
  exemplar each.
- **The de Bruijn control lost** to R2 for the rectangle in the
  replication, which complicates a monotone reading.
- **Manual AFM classes**, yields as percentages of imaged objects, with no
  counts in the text read.
- **GC content and synthesis route uncontrolled.**
- **No melt, thermodynamic or kinetic data.**
- **Small scaffolds only** in vitro; "validation on larger multilayer
  structures with significant fold-order constraints remains an open
  direction".
- **Equal metric weights**, not fitted to data.
- The Supplementary Information was not read.

## Open questions

- Does the ordering hold with more than one off-target-prone sequence per
  design, and with GC content matched?
- Where in folding do the off-target-prone variants stall: early
  mis-binding, scaffold–scaffold aggregation, or late loss?
- Would weighting scaffold–scaffold binding more heavily, as the authors
  suggest, explain the rectangle anomaly?
