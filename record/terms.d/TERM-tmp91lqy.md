# TERM-tmp91lqy: procedural friction, of a process: a step at which the process's continuation waits on a condition its executor does not control (a person's approval, an elapsed notice period, a check another party runs or a limit another party set), whether or not the step delays it

## The definition

A step in a process is **procedural friction** when the process cannot go
past it unless a condition holds that the executor of the process does not
control. The condition can be a person's act (an approval, a signature, a
hearing's outcome), the passage of time the executor cannot shorten (a
notice period), or a test another party runs or a limit another party set
(an automatic suspension on a threshold the executor cannot change).

The distinction it draws is between **dependence** and **delay**:

| | executor can pass it alone | executor cannot pass it alone |
|---|---|---|
| **takes time** | delay without friction (a slow step the executor could skip) | friction with delay (a review, a notice period) |
| **takes no time** | neither | friction without delay (a standing limit enforced by another's mechanism) |

## Neighbouring senses it must not be confused with

- **Delay.** The ordinary sense, and the one A's objection
  ([CLAIM-tmpwpvbz](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpwpvbz.md)) relies on. A delay the executor controls opens no
  opportunity to intervene.
- **A21's "extractive friction"**, time and effort imposed on those subject
  to a process without influence over it. That is a burden on others, not
  a gate on the executor, and is friction in this sense only where it gates
  the process.
- **Administrative burden** (Herd and Moynihan, [LIT-1609](../literature.d/LIT-1609.md) and
  [LIT-tmp1o7dq](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1o7dq.md)): costs of access borne by claimants. Again a burden, not a
  gate.
- **Mechanical friction**: the metaphor's source, energy lost to motion. The
  term keeps only the resistance and drops the loss.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Cuéllar, read

Cuéllar writes that governors and civil society "can also create friction
and slow down White House edicts" (p. 648), so he speaks of friction as
delay. His instances are dependence, though: states using "their legitimate discretion to decide not" to
cooperate, and litigation that can enjoin. Those gate the
executive and are friction in this term's sense. Mild support for defining
friction by dependence. Relation: none.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Hong Kong Monetary Authority, read

The HKMA's agent harness is this term's "friction without delay". The
agent cannot pass a deterministic check, and it may not modify its own
boundary controls unless the institution has explicitly approved an
arrangement for that (§7.1, pp. 35–36). The limits are held by the
institution's change control and an independent control owner (§5.3).
The paper also supplies a case the term's table does not separate out:
an approval step on which the process waits, and so friction by
dependence, that has become perfunctory. "A formal approval step is not,
by itself, meaningful human oversight." (§7.2, p. 37). Approval counts
only where the approver sees the substance, can reject, "regards rejection
as a legitimate outcome", and has time. So dependence on another's
condition is necessary on this term's reading for an opportunity to
intervene, and not sufficient: a gate can be friction and expose nothing.
This qualifies the reading of CLAIM-tmpkx7ct, where "an opportunity to
intervene is a piece of friction" is close to definitional. The converse,
that a piece of friction is an opportunity, fails. Relation: none (a THEORY
cannot bound a TERM); a note.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Natali, read

The poster defines friction by inefficiency: "deliberate inefﬁciencies designed to foster engagement, critical reason[ing], and user agency", "speed bumps", "intentionally slower algorithms". That is the delay sense the term sets aside; dependence on another party is not mentioned. Two of its mechanisms are nonetheless friction in the term's sense: a pause before AI output, or a required justification before acceptance, is a condition the user cannot pass alone because the designer set it. A neighbouring sense to add: **designed (interaction) friction**, effort or delay a designer imposes on a user's own decision to make the user engage. It is friction in this term's sense only where the user cannot skip it, and then it gates the user's decision for the user's own judgement, not for another party's.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Alfrink, read

The second-system safeguard is a clean case of the term's "friction without delay": every decision's passage depends on a check another party runs, and the process waits on a human only when the check flags. The paper never calls it friction, which supports the term's point that dependence, not delay, is what such safeguards have in common with reviews and approvals.
