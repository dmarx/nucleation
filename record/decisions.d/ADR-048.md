# ADR-048: Nucleation files any work the owner wants here; whether a work is also held in the anthology is the anthology's question

## Context

[ADR-001](ADR-001.md) set a two-part boundary between this record and the Anthology of the
SOTA:

- **(a) Topic.** A work goes wherever a topic can hold it. If an anthology
  topic can hold it, it goes there and not here.
- **(b) Practice.** Material that carries an instruction for machine-learning
  practice belongs in the anthology by definition.

[ADR-013](ADR-013.md) narrowed (a) so that a work could be held in both records when
each reads it for a question of its own. Even so, every filing here began
with a check of the anthology, and dual holding needed a second question,
stated in a curation entry. [ADR-005](ADR-005.md)'s flag marked works whose placement
was in doubt.

On 2026-10-19 the owner asked for the record's gap around the rubber hand
illusion to be filled: Botvinick & Cohen 1998 and the bodily
self-consciousness literature around it. The owner said to assume the
anthology does not hold these works, and that the boundary rule should go.
The check had cost a clone and a grep for each filing. It had also put
nucleation in charge of keeping two records from overlapping, though only
the anthology can say what it holds and why.

## Decision

- **(a) is dropped here.** Nucleation files any work the owner wants here,
  whether or not an anthology topic could hold it. Nothing is checked
  against the anthology before filing.
- **(b) stands.** An instruction for machine-learning practice still belongs
  to the anthology, so the anthology keeps a distinct job. The topic blurbs
  that send ML practice to the anthology (learning theory, compositionality,
  databases, learned structure and others) are unchanged.
- **Cross-reference is optional.** When a filer knows the anthology's code
  for the same work, the LIT cites it (`ANTH-LIT-…`). Not knowing it is no
  defect, and no history note need record a search for it.
- **Overlap is the anthology's question.** Whether a work filed here is also
  held there is for the anthology to find out and reconcile, by its own
  process. Nucleation does not police it.
- **The anthology-candidate flag is still applied** ([ADR-005](ADR-005.md)), after the
  topics and never first. It now means that someone thinks the anthology may
  want this work as well. It no longer marks a work whose filing here is in
  doubt. A transfer, when decided, is still a supersession, as with
  <!-- inactive-ok: LIT-064 — Superseded by ANTH-LIT-726: the worked example of a transfer -->
  `LIT-064`.

[ADR-013](ADR-013.md) is subsumed. A work may be held in both records without a
second question being named. When nucleation reads a work for a question
the anthology's reading does not ask, saying so in the curation entry is
still good practice, but it is not a condition of filing.

## Alternatives

- **Drop (b) as well,** and file ML-practice works here too. Rejected by
  the owner. It would leave the anthology without a job of its own and turn
  nucleation into a mirror, the alternative [ADR-001](ADR-001.md) rejected.
- **Keep (a) and file these works under [ADR-013](ADR-013.md).** That would mean a
  second question and an anthology check for every borderline work. The
  check is the cost the owner asked to stop paying.
- **Retire the anthology-candidate flag.** Without (a), the flag no longer
  records doubt about where a work belongs. The owner kept it, because it
  still tells the anthology which works here it may want.

## Consequences

- CLAUDE.md's boundary section, the topic vocabulary's blurb and alert in
  `luria.yaml`, and the README's opening are rewritten to say what is filed
  here, not what is excluded from it.
- [ADR-001](ADR-001.md), [ADR-005](ADR-005.md) and [ADR-013](ADR-013.md) each take a history note
  pointing here. Their bodies are unchanged.
- Existing LITs whose history notes record an anthology search keep them.
  They were true when written.
- The anthology may find works filed here that it also holds. Reconciling
  them is its business.
