# THEORY-tmpngvdn: Koedinger and Aleven argue that instruction faces an assistance dilemma, help given buying accuracy and efficiency at the cost of shallow processing and help withheld buying generation and memory engagement at the cost of floundering, and that within tutored problem solving, where the learner does the steps and help is added only on failure, the experiments they review favour more help rather than less, support shifting from worked examples toward problems and as-needed hints as competence grows, learners left to ask for help asking badly, and the point at which to switch between giving and withholding unknown

## Source

Koedinger and Aleven (2007), LIT-tmpm7tp8; read in NOTE-tmp607yf.

## What was actually shown

- **The dilemma** is a frame, set out in Table 2 (p. 242), not a result.
- **More help within tutored practice.** In the Lisp tutor, immediate,
  flag and on-demand feedback "led to better and faster learning" than
  none, immediate feedback fastest (p. 252); explanatory error feedback
  beat yes/no feedback (pp. 252–253); mastery learning gave "significantly
  higher learning gains" (pp. 254–255). Each of these could have come out
  for withholding and did not. The authors conclude that "information
  should be withheld very sparingly" in tutored problem solving (p. 255).
- **Fading.** Worked examples faded into problems, "To adjust information
  giving/withholding to learners' growing competence", gave better
  declarative and equal procedural post-tests in less time than the
  standard tutor (Schwonke et al. 2007, p. 257). No effect sizes.
- **Learners ask badly.** Students "frequently use bottom-out hints to
  obtain answers, without reading prior hints", and "even after multiple
  errors on a step, students often do not request help" (pp. 253–254).
- **The switch point** is proposed, not found: give help "when the task
  gets too difficult and thus the probability of error or unproductive
  thinking is high", at a threshold left to future research (p. 260).

## What this does not say

- That help should be given in general: "This result should not be
  construed as supporting information giving in general, or greater
  interactivity in general" (p. 260). Problem solving is itself a form of
  withholding, and the help reviewed comes on failure.
- That withholding never helps: delayed feedback on an intelligent-novice
  model, which lets the learner catch errors, did better on all measures
  (p. 259).
- That the overall tutor gains show interactivity at work; the authors say
  they do not (p. 251).
- That what is learned persists long after the tutor is gone: post-tests
  are nearly all immediate.
- Anything about organizations; the subjects are individual students in
  mathematics, programming and science.
