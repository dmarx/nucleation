# QUESTION-102: Can a family of interpretations each feasible in its own context fail to admit a feasible global integration when a compatible global interpretation exists?

## Why it is a question

A15 §6: "Can a family of locally feasible interpretations fail to admit
a globally feasible integration, even though a mathematically compatible
global interpretation exists?" It distinguished "No joint representation
exists" from "No feasible algorithm constructs or evaluates one", and
said "The second cannot establish the first." [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md) is the record's
formal gluing result. Nothing in it is about cost.

The question is real if some class of local families admits global
sections that are hard to find. Constraint-satisfaction problems with
locally consistent instances suggest that it is. Whether anything
communicative falls in such a class is the open part.

An answer is a model of context-indexed interpretations in which a global
section exists and finding it is provably hard, or an argument that, for
the covers the pragmatic-transport line uses, local consistency makes
gluing cheap.
