# CLAIM-472: Because an apparatus makes some distinctions determinate and leaves others unstatable, choosing and maintaining one is open to evaluation and not only to verification; but that a cut excludes something fixes no obligation, since every distinction excludes, and which exclusions matter is a judgment indexed to a standard, a scale and those affected

## The claim

The owner, at U6, asked A6 to "double click on" Barad's
*ethico-onto-epistem-ology* and on the sentence A4 had given as hers:
"**practices of knowing are simultaneously practices that participate in
the world's becoming.**" A4 §6's example was diagnosis: the boundary
between a diagnosed and an undiagnosed patient "may affect treatment,
institutional access, insurance, personal identity, and subsequent
physiological and social trajectories", constrained by biology but "not
exhausted by the biological facts".

**The first half.** If an apparatus constitutes some of the objects it is
used on ([CLAIM-476](CLAIM-476.md)), then choosing it is not a neutral preliminary.
A6 §4: "the practices by which we establish what counts as knowledge are
themselves consequential interventions in the world." A10 §6: "the choice
and maintenance of an apparatus can itself be an object of ethical
evaluation, rather than a morally neutral preliminary to inquiry." A7 §5
found the norm-constituting case in luria's constitution example, where a
`BOUNDARY` can `override` a `PRACTICE`: "Which distinctions become
first-class entities? Which constraints can override others? Who
authorizes a change? What remains invisible because it doesn't fit a
declared category?" The record's own version of the last is [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md):
whether coding some contributors' moves and leaving others' as unfiled
turns distributes credibility among them.

**The second half.** A6 §4 pressed where Barad does not: "an agential cut
might exclude a possibility, but exclusion is not inherently unjust.
Every useful distinction excludes something. A scientific instrument that
distinguishes one spectral band from another necessarily excludes other
bands from its measurement." So the question "concerns **which exclusions
matter, to whom, according to which standards, and with what
consequences**." That is [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md)'s form, a normative judgment well
defined only once its standard, scale and context are fixed and then
capable of being false, which A6 named as "a possible bridge between
Barad's ethical commitments and a more explicit theory of normative
judgment." A6: "Barad gives us an ontology of consequential
differentiation, while a theory of normativity must still explain how
those differences become grounds for obligations."

## What it does not say

- **Not that Barad holds the second half.** It is A6's pressure on her, and
  her text is unread here ([LIT-1657](../literature.d/LIT-1657.md)).
- **Not that every apparatus choice is a moral question.** It says the
  choice is open to evaluation, which may find nothing that matters.
- **Not which exclusions the record's own grammar makes, or whether any is
  wrong.** [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md) asks one; none is answered here.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (later): the first half's source, at second hand

The two readings of 2026-10-27 give Barad's own statements of the first
half. Faye and Jaksland quote her: "apparatuses … enact what matters and
what is excluded from mattering" (2007, 148; [NOTE-1220](../notes.d/NOTE-1220.md)). Jaksland quotes
the source of the exclusion: an arrangement that makes "position"
determinate "will, by necessity, always produce its constitutive exclusion
(for example, 'momentum')" (Barad 2010, 253; [NOTE-1230](../notes.d/NOTE-1230.md)), and cites Rouse
on Barad's science as "inherently normative" (note 18). So the exclusion
Barad means is complementarity's, generalized. Neither reading addresses
the second half, which remains A6's pressure on her.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (later still): Bowker and Star

A11 found the empirical tradition for this claim's first half in Bowker and
Star ([LIT-1631](../literature.d/LIT-1631.md), unread): classification infrastructures become invisible by
becoming routine, and what they leave residual has consequences. It asked the
claim's question of luria: "**Which distinctions become easier to make because
the apparatus recognizes them, and which distinctions become harder to make
because the apparatus has no place for them?**", and added that "Extensibility
allows a category to be added. It does not guarantee that someone notices the
missing category, has authority to add it, or can do so without costly
consequences". That is [CLAIM-419](CLAIM-419.md)'s limit ("whether every such move is caught is
not shown") made a question of who can act, which is [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md)'s.
