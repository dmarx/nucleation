# NOTE-1805: Zhang et al. — AsynCodeBench: Benchmarking Collaboration of Asynchronous Multi-Agent Systems in Software Engineering

## Contribution

A dependency-level measure of collaboration that separates coordination from
individual coding ability, and the finding that task-level metrics hide
coordination failure.

## Key results

- Abstract: "improvements in coding performance do not necessarily translate
  into stronger collaboration, and task-level metrics can diverge
  substantially from dependency-level collaboration measures."
- §5.2: six of ten models gain under their best multi-agent protocol with
  gains of 7.8–22.7%, while four remain worse than SINGLE; Qwen3.8-27B and
  DeepSeek-V4 reach 93.6% alone and fall to 86.7% and 89.9% at their best
  multi-agent protocol.
- §5.2 (Async-Manager-RO, 190 outcomes): "32 outcomes achieve at least 80%
  Test Pass Rate while resolving fewer than half of their declared
  dependencies, including 26 with zero ADPR; we observe no corresponding
  low-test, high-ADPR cases."
- §5.4: "no collaborative protocol consistently improves dependency
  resolution across all models"; Qwen3.6-27B ranges from −14.9% (Async-Private)
  to +26.3% (Async-Manager).
- §5.4: the "hopping window": 0.50 < x ≤ 0.75 of the trajectory "accounts for
  34.9%–67.8% of eventual closure mass across protocols."
- §4: the manager variants separate "coordination through information
  exchange from direct code intervention."

## Assumptions

Declared dependencies (human-validated) capture what collaboration must
achieve; executable checkers detect their satisfaction; the OpenHands harness
and per-task Docker environment are held fixed; open-weight models only.

## Concepts

Asynchronous collaboration; cross-agent dependency; Dependency Checker;
ADPR; DRS; execution protocols (Single, Serial, Async-Private,
Async-Manager-RO, Async-Manager); hidden dependency failure; hopping window;
quality–cost trade-off.

## Bearing on the record

[QUESTION-459](../questions.d/QUESTION-459.md) records that A31 named AsynCodeBench as an "external test".
Fair: it is an external benchmark of multi-agent coordination. Note: its shared medium is a code repository with no shared notes or record
arm; the protocols vary handoff, concurrency and a manager, not a typed
record, so it would need an added arm to test the question.

- **[QUESTION-459](../questions.d/QUESTION-459.md)** (does a shared, typed, governed record help agents
  holding different parts of the evidence integrate it, beyond a structured
  protocol, and at which stage?): **supports** the question's methodological
  premise that a task-level score can hide integration failure: here tests
  pass while cross-agent dependencies stay unmet, and only a per-dependency
  trace shows it, the analogue of the question's per-clue trace through
  admission, recognition, credibility and uptake. It **qualifies** any
  expectation that adding coordination machinery helps: the read-only and
  repairing manager arms help some models and hurt others. It does not test
  a record; leaves the question open.

- **[CLAIM-346](../claims.d/CLAIM-346.md)** (Active: feasibility, aggregation and epistemic coordination
  are independent obstructions): the paper's split of coding capability from
  collaboration capability is an empirical separation in the same spirit
  (individual competence present, coordination absent); it does not map onto
  the claim's three terms exactly. Illustrative.
- **[CLAIM-403](../claims.d/CLAIM-403.md)** (stages of a contribution's admission, recognition,
  credibility, uptake): the gap between an integrated artifact and a
  satisfied dependency is a case of output admitted without uptake;
  illustrative, no stage-level measurement.
- Duplicate check: arXiv id and title not held elsewhere.

## Limitations

19 tasks; open-weight models only; no limitations section; the authors
declare ChatGPT assistance with drafting, literature discovery, methodology
feedback and code.
