# THEORY-594: Unitarily inequivalent representations in quantum field theory need not refute ontic structural realism: what does physical work in spontaneous symmetry breaking and in the DHR derivation of quantum statistics is structure across representations, so a structural realist who takes structure to be modal and to span them can accept that work, though the modal conception is undeveloped and the removal of Howard's premise rests on Baker's sufficient condition for intertranslatability

## Source

French (2010), [LIT-170](../literature.d/LIT-170.md), read in full in [NOTE-162](../notes.d/NOTE-162.md) from the author's 2010
PhilSci-Archive preprint. The published version (*Studies in History and
Philosophy of Modern Physics* 43(2), 121–136, 2012) was not reached, and
the preprint is unpaginated, so locations are section names.

## What was actually shown

**The objection.** Ladyman's founding case for ontic structural realism
used the Stone–von Neumann equivalence of matrix and wave mechanics. For
infinitely many degrees of freedom that equivalence fails "of necessity".
Howard argues that the realist is then left with non-isomorphic, competing
structures: choosing one is not structural, and keeping all is incoherent.

**Option 1, conventional QFT** ("Option 1"). Following Wallace, a cut-off
QFT treats short-distance inequivalence as an artefact of dropping the
cut-off and long-distance inequivalence as information inaccessible from a
finite region. Fraser's reply, that cut-off QFT lacks "even approximately
the same content" as AQFT, is answered by noting that AQFT has no realistic
interacting models and is also a staging post ("Response" section).

**Option 2, the Swiss Army Knife** (after Ruetsche). Structure spans the
representations, and contingent features pick the relevant one.
- Symmetry breaking (Case 1): broken symmetry is a group–subgroup relation
  (O(3) → O(2); SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) → SU(3)×U(1)), and the ensemble of
  degenerate vacua keeps the full symmetry. If SSB explains asymmetric
  phenomena, the inequivalence serves a symmetry-based ontology; if the
  representations cannot be dismissed, what matters is cross-representation
  structure ("Back to Ruetsche").
- Statistics (Case 2): under the DHR criterion, charge superselection
  sectors correspond to irreducible representations of the global gauge
  group. Abelian groups give Bose and Fermi statistics, non-Abelian ones
  parastatistics. Statistics then "arise naturally from within the (AQFT)
  formulation", and permutation invariance need not be posited. The
  reading grades the physics strong and the moral for OSR moderate.

**Howard's premise.** Ruetsche's identification of unitary equivalence with
physical equivalence rests on Clifton and Halvorson's translation scheme.
French adopts Baker's proposal that a symmetry mapping between
representations suffices for intertranslatability, which "remove[s] the
assumption behind Howard's challenge".

**What could have come out otherwise.** The DHR analysis could have needed
statistics imposed by hand, and SSB could have been explicable only in a
single representation. Neither is the case in the results French imports.
His own steps are arguments, and no step of his could have failed
empirically.

## What this does not say

- **That OSR is vindicated by QFT.** The conclusion that inequivalent
  representations "indicate how that position can be further developed and
  strengthened" is French's reading of others' results.
- **What modal structure is.** French calls it "more profound (and perhaps
  problematic)" and declines to tie it to possible worlds or causation.
- **That every inequivalence is removed.** Baker's condition is sufficient,
  and Baker leaves open whether it is necessary. Representations related by
  no symmetry, such as some infrared-inequivalent ones, are not addressed.
- **That the two options are combined.** The Conclusion sketches a third
  option (modal algebraic structure plus actual Standard Model structure)
  and records Fraser's objection that the representations involved differ
  in kind.
- **That statistics are derived for every field theory.** The DHR criterion
  excludes long-range forces such as electromagnetism.
- **Anything about the published text.** The 2012 version may revise this
  draft, which has visible loose ends ("(see vF?)").
