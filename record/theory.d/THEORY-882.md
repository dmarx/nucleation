# THEORY-882: Consciousness in an inferring system requires three things: a unified generative model of its world; entry to that model by a precision-weighted competition that coherent inferences win; and epistemic depth, the recursive sharing of the model back through the hierarchy, carried by a hyper-model that predicts and sets precision at every level; awareness then varies with epistemic depth, not with the richness of contents, and a first-person perspective, a self-model and agency are not required

## Source

Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria (2025), [LIT-1363](../literature.d/LIT-1363.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1063](../notes.d/NOTE-1063.md) from the PsyArXiv preprint, version 3: sections 2–5 and 7
(pp. 3–20), Table 1 (p. 13) and the discussion (pp. 26–30). Page numbers
are the preprint's.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested. The paper argues by construction and redescription.

- **The conditions.** A world model is "a necessary condition for
  consciousness because it defines what can become conscious" (p. 3).
  Entry is a "precision-weighted competition between possible explanations
  for the causes of sensory data" (p. 7), in which "coherence and
  boundedness are a central criterion" (p. 6). The global model is
  "necessary but not sufficient for conscious experience. It is only when
  this global posterior is reflected back through the underlying hierarchy
  that the conditions for consciousness are met" (p. 9).
- **The formalism.** Table 1 conditions each level of a hierarchical
  generative model on a precision hyper-parameter φ⁽ˡ⁾ under a common prior
  p(Φ), and minimises a free energy over all levels and Φ. The text says
  "It is this recursive aspect that equips belief updating with epistemic
  depth" (p. 11). No simulation is run.
- **The redescriptions.** Inattentional blindness, subliminal priming,
  lucid and non-lucid dreaming, lucid dreamless sleep, minimal phenomenal
  experience, cessation and psychedelic states are each placed by their
  epistemic depth, abstraction and precision (pp. 19–26). Cessation is the
  coherence condition failing: "the world model is not contentless but
  incoherent" (p. 25).
- **What could have come out otherwise.** As stated, little. The paper says
  "The theory is testable and falsifiable at the level of computational
  modeling, but also in terms of neural implementation" (p. 30), and names
  as its test that if the conditions are met "we ought to see evidence of
  awareness or deep and flexible epistemicity, as well as success on any
  Turing-type tests" (p. 30). It names no observation that would count
  against it.

## What this does not say

- **It does not show that the hyper-model knows anything.** A hierarchy
  with learned layer-wise precision is a standard model; the paper itself
  names the hierarchical Gaussian filter (p. 12). That such a model
  "contains the knowledge that it exists" is asserted, not derived.
- **It does not guarantee experience.** Note 11: "Our position does not
  assert that satisfying these conditions guarantees phenomenal experience
  in the subjective sense" (p. 29), though the main text concludes that an
  AI meeting the conditions should be judged conscious.
- **Epistemic depth is defined by awareness.** It is "a capacity or
  continuum (i.e., deepening) of knowing, or awareness" (p. 9), so the
  third condition is not stated independently of what it explains.
- **The contemplative evidence is report.** MPE and cessation enter as
  reported states. Whether meditative "contentless" states lack content is
  disputed by a work the record holds unread ([LIT-1351](../literature.d/LIT-1351.md)).
- **It is not a higher-order theory.** The paper separates its recursion
  from metacognition (p. 20). It is a claim about global, recursive
  precision control.
