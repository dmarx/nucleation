# LIT-tmphhuac: Pushing and Coasting in Dynamic Goal Pursuit: Coasting Is Attenuated in Bipolar Disorder

Daniel Fulford, Sheri L. Johnson, Maria M. Llabre and Charles S. Carver (2010), *Psychological Science* 21(7):1021–1027 — DOI-10.1177/0956797610373372

## Key takeaways

- In daily life, progress on a goal that runs ahead of what a person
  expected is followed by less effort on that goal, and progress that
  falls behind by more (pp. 1024–1025).
- People with bipolar I disorder, in remission, coast less after
  overshooting than controls do, though both groups coast (p. 1025).
- The paper states Carver's (2003) thesis in two sentences (p. 1021):
  positive affect, "a signal of surging ahead", "may mean that the goal to
  which it relates can assume a lower priority". It measures progress and
  effort, not affect and not priority.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-30 as a reading that restates Carver 2003 ([LIT-1445](LIT-1445.md)),
which [CLAIM-468](../claims.d/CLAIM-468.md) and [CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md) cite for the view that positive affect lowers
a goal's priority and which could not be fetched. It is a second-hand
statement of that thesis by Carver's own group, and a test of only its
behavioural consequence.
