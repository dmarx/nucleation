The owner asked how the record stood on the rubber hand illusion. The answer
was that it held nothing directly. Body ownership appeared only as claim C6
of the reading of Metzinger's "Self models" ([NOTE-372](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-372.md)): "body ownership can
be manipulated by multisensory conflict and is the simplest form of
self-awareness". That claim rested on Lenggenhager et al. 2007 and Blanke &
Metzinger 2009, neither of which the record held. The interoception and
active-inference holdings ([LIT-542](../../record/literature.d/LIT-542.md), [LIT-135](../../record/literature.d/LIT-135.md), [LIT-1181](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1181.md)) did not touch
ownership at all.

The owner asked for the gap to be filled, told the filer to assume the
anthology did not hold these works, and dropped the boundary rule in the same
exchange ([ADR-048](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-048.md)). No anthology check was made. Each LIT's first history note
says so.

Five works were filed and each was read in full:

- **Botvinick & Cohen 1998** ([LIT-1277](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1277.md), [NOTE-1006](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-1006.md)). This is the
  original rubber hand illusion. Its one page was read from Nature's typeset
  page on a UC Davis faculty site. That host is a third party, not the
  authors or the publisher, and Unpaywall and OpenAlex both report the work
  closed. The history notes say so. If the owner does not accept that copy,
  the LIT goes to Unavailable and the NOTE is retired.
- **Lenggenhager, Tadi, Metzinger & Blanke 2007** ([LIT-1279](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1279.md),
  [NOTE-999](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-999.md)), the full-body illusion. The paper, its supplementary
  material and the 2008 e-letters were read from EPFL Infoscience. The
  University of Zurich copy OpenAlex points to sits behind a bot check. The
  e-letters carry Ehrsson & Petkova's objection that participants may have
  simply recognised themselves on video.
- **Blanke & Metzinger 2009** ([LIT-1274](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1274.md), [NOTE-1002](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-1002.md)), minimal
  phenomenal selfhood. Read from the publisher PDF on EPFL Infoscience. It
  narrows [NOTE-372](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-372.md)'s "simplest form" to ownership of the whole body, not of
  parts.
- **Apps & Tsakiris 2014** ([LIT-1262](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1262.md), [NOTE-991](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-991.md)), the free-energy
  self. Read from the PMC author manuscript (PMC3848896). It names
  interoception but does not model it, so its tie to the interoceptive
  holdings is thinner than the request assumed.
- **Seth 2013** ([LIT-1263](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1263.md), [NOTE-1000](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-1000.md)), interoceptive inference.
  Read from the publisher PDF in the Sussex repository on figshare. This,
  not Apps & Tsakiris, is the bridge from the interoception cluster to body
  ownership.

The readings were done in parallel. One reader's `luria link --fix`, given
only its own paths, linked across the whole tree: "linked 53 reference(s) in
4040 file(s)". It touched [ADR-048](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-048.md), which another contribution was drafting
at the same time, and the reader's revert broke a link the lint wanted. The
link was restored before commit. **Path arguments to `luria link --fix` do
not confine it in luria 1.1.0.** Concurrent fillers should not run it at
all; the integrator should run it once.

No THEORY was filed. The readers proposed three, which overlap:

1. Synchrony between seen and felt touch suffices for ownership and drift.
2. Ownership is a posterior over the common cause of multisensory input.
3. Ownership is inferred from interoceptive as well as exteroceptive
   signals.

A fourth, on whole-body ownership as the core of minimal selfhood, came
from Blanke & Metzinger. These are left for a later pass, with the
anthology-free filing rule now in place.
