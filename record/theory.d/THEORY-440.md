# THEORY-440: A democracy that breaks down under an elected incumbent need not become a dictatorship: elections, courts and a legal opposition can continue while the incumbent uses state power against opponents and tilts the field, giving competitive authoritarianism, in which competition is real but unfair and the decisive loss is the opposition that withdraws or never forms; Levitsky and Way forecast this for the second Trump term

## Source

Drezner (2025), [LIT-022](../literature.d/LIT-022.md), "Is the United States Still a Liberal
Democracy?", a column dated 13 February 2025, read in [NOTE-001](../notes.d/NOTE-001.md). The account
is Levitsky and Way's ("The Path to American Authoritarianism", *Foreign
Affairs*, February 2025), which the record has not filed and knows only
through Drezner's long quotations. This THEORY rests on that relay.

## What was actually shown

Nothing is shown in the post. It relays and endorses.

- **The concept, as quoted.** Competitive authoritarianism is a system in
  which parties compete in real elections but "the incumbent's abuse of
  power tilts the playing field against the opposition". Competition is
  "real but unfair". Liberal democracy is "full adult suffrage, free and
  fair elections, and broad protection of civil liberties".
- **The mechanisms, as quoted.** IRS targeting of donors; regulatory and
  legal pressure on businesses and media; and the depletion of societal
  opposition, including opposition that "might have materialized … but
  never did".
- **The cases, as quoted.** Fujimori's Peru, Chávez's Venezuela, El
  Salvador, Hungary, India, Tunisia and Turkey.
- **The forecast, as quoted.** US democracy "will likely break down
  during the second Trump administration". There will be no "classic
  dictatorship": "There will be elections in 2028, and Republicans could
  lose them".
- **The indices, second-hand.** Freedom House fell from 92 to 83 over
  2014–2021 (quoted from Levitsky and Way); Polity fell to +5 for 2020
  (quoted from Walter), was back to "full democracy" by 2024, and its front
  page in February 2025 said the US "lies at the cusp of autocracy".
- **Drezner's own additions.** Accommodation by elites who opposed Trump
  in 2017 "sure seems" driven by fear; the administration is laying down a
  "blueprint" for future populists, which he calls "more disturbing".

**What could have come out otherwise.** The forecast could fail either
way: a level field through 2028, or a breakdown that suspends elections.
The post was written three weeks into the term and this reading does not
check it against later events.

## What this does not say

- **That the US has become competitively authoritarian.** It is a
  forecast, endorsed rather than argued ([NOTE-001](../notes.d/NOTE-001.md), C4, weak).
- **That index verdicts are reliable.** Drezner calls Polity's scheme
  possibly "one slightly wonky coding schema". A move across a category
  boundary can change a headline verdict while the coding is in doubt.
- **That accommodation is driven by fear.** Drezner's impression (C5,
  assertion).
- **That the account is Drezner's.** It is Levitsky and Way's, and before
  them Levitsky and Ziblatt's lineage; a THEORY on their own texts would
  replace this source.
