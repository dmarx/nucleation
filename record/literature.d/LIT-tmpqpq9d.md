# LIT-tmpqpq9d: Neurocognitive mechanisms underlying the experience of flow

Arne Dietrich (2004), "Neurocognitive mechanisms underlying the experience of flow", *Consciousness and Cognition* 13(4): 746–761 — [doi:10.1016/j.concog.2004.07.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2004.07.002)

## Key takeaways

*Unread. From secondary descriptions found while filing, to be checked against the text.*

- **Transient hypofrontality.** Flow is proposed to need a temporary suppression of the explicit system's analytic and meta-conscious capacities (frontal and medial temporal), so that a highly practised skill represented in the implicit system (mainly basal ganglia) runs without interference.
- **A theoretical paper.** Its examples are sensorimotor skills such as athletic performance, writing and improvisation. Later work cites it as a hypothesis to test, not a result.
- **Why the record wants it.** It is a two-system account, so it is a society-of-mind reading of flow in miniature: flow is one system yielding, not two systems agreeing. That is the rival reading for the record's claim that in flow the interests agree ([CLAIM-tmpf301t](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpf301t.md)).
