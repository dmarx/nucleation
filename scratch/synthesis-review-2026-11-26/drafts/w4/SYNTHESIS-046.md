# SYNTHESIS-046: Persisting through modes; histories need not glue

*A part synthesis of the realizability line: the part filed from the
exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (2026-11-06 to 07),
with A's question on representability from the paste of 2026-11-05 and
the two readings filed for LEAD-163 on 2026-11-22. Most of its claims are
first in distributed-agency and carry realizability because they bear on
this line's questions; SYNTHESIS-047 sets out the same claims of identity
over time from the side of collectives. The line's synthesis is built over
this one and its sibling parts. It adds nothing. Its standing is its
components'.*

## The question

When an organized individual persists through changes of state and mode,
what is it that persists? And what can the histories such an individual
realizes show about what is realizable for it, and about what a reduced
description of it has left out?

## The argument

**What persists is the organization that keeps the repertoire
realizable.** The premises come from the line's other parts and from
neighbouring lines: a constrained system is characterized by the
repertoire of processes realizable for it (CLAIM-576); feasible, reachable
and reliably reached are three properties (CLAIM-567); that a form
assembles, persists and is regenerated are three (CLAIM-566); stability
does not entail viability (CLAIM-079), and statistics conditioned on
survival can settle while failure is certain (CLAIM-612); the operative
regime, the governing organization and the capacity to transform it fail
separately (CLAIM-025); rate differences make structure (CLAIM-478); and
recovery under interventions on state cannot show where maintenance is
realized (CLAIM-522). On these, A's thesis, corrected by the filer, puts
the locus of persistence through change of mode in what keeps the
repertoire realizable, not in the repertoire, a state, a mode or a
boundary (CLAIM-611). None of the premises says this, and CLAIM-576 reads
the other way, since it characterizes the system by its repertoire. The
claim can fail with every premise holding: swap in an outside controller
unrelated to the system that supplies the same production law, so that
the repertoire and every trajectory are unchanged, and let every criterion
count one individual through the swap (CLAIM-611, "Admission").

**Whether a maintainer outside the individual is part of what persists is
open, and filed two ways.** CLAIM-611 says the organization that persists
"may lie inside the individual or outside it". Read literally, a car that
changes garages is a realistic case of its first defeater: an outside
maintainer replaced by an unrelated one, the same repertoire kept, the
same car counted. The step both readings filed for LEAD-163 share defuses
it: a maintainer that restores a thing from the organization it finds in
the thing transmits that organization through the thing, as a collective's
newcomers take its organization from the collective (ARG-216, by analogy
with CLAIM-329). They part on organization held only by a maintainer. On
one reading a maintainer is a channel and never what is transmitted, and
CLAIM-611's clause is about the processes that carry the organization,
which may lie outside the boundary (CLAIM-983). On the other, an outside
maintainer's organization is transmitted just insofar as the individual
does not carry it and a competent unrelated replacement working from the
individual would not supply it, which keeps CLAIM-611's words and lets a
garage hold none of a car's organization (CLAIM-986, resting on
CLAIM-611). On either, CLAIM-611's own admission case holds only for a
controller whose law was not taken from the system (CLAIM-611, note of
2026-11-22), so the swap that separates the claim from its premises is
stipulated, not realistic. Which reading is the owner's.

**Identity over time is the causal transmission of that organization.**
CLAIM-615, resting on CLAIM-611 and on CLAIM-593's result that isomorphism
is not persistence in either direction, carries the thesis from a moment
of change to a history: identity over time is carried by the causal
transmission of the relations that keep the repertoire realizable, so a
structuralism about individuals has to be historical. Of CLAIM-593's two
directions the first carries over to organized individuals whole and the
second only as consistency (ARG-075, in SYNTHESIS-047). CLAIM-615's own
admission case meets the same slot as CLAIM-611's last clause, and the two
readings close it for both (CLAIM-615, note of 2026-11-22).

**Histories can cohere and still not continue, or continue and not glue.**
Over time intervals, under local dynamics and a pointwise viability
constraint, the viable histories form a sheaf, yet a viable history
extends exactly when its endpoint can stay viable for the time remaining,
so a coherent history can be terminal (CLAIM-625, demonstrated). That is
the distinction CLAIM-611's repertoire needs between what composes and
what can go on. The converse failure comes from reduction: forgetting a
hidden mechanism can turn a sheaf of histories into a presheaf of
realizable observations that is not a sheaf, so a gluing failure need not
show a disagreement on an overlap (CLAIM-623, demonstrated; ARG-074 from
CASE-224, which adds that it need not have an overlap at all). Whether
such a failure identifies the capacity the reduction omitted, and can be
told from modelling error, is open (QUESTION-437), as is the wider
question of when a coherent family of local observations corresponds to
an object the system can construct or sustain within its resources
(QUESTION-379).

**Synchronization is offered as the laboratory.** The owner's proposal is
that synchronization phenomena can serve as a laboratory for the record's
organizational distinctions, and that a drive fixes where control lies,
not whether it is legitimate (CLAIM-616, which CLAIM-611 complements). A
forced oscillator separates dependence for timing, dependence for
performance and suppression of a latent capacity, which only withdrawals
tell apart (CASE-225; ARG-072 concludes CLAIM-616 from it by analogy, and
what transfers is that the distinctions are coherent and separable by
intervention). A network of eight coupled oscillators run for LEAD-098
shows a shared drive and coupling giving the same locking and gluing,
told apart only by displacing one member's drive (CASE-333).

## Where it stands

The results on histories are firm in their models: CLAIM-625 is Active
and CLAIM-623 demonstrated. The persistence thesis and its extension to
identity over time are Proposed and interpretive, and both now wait on
the owner's choice between CLAIM-983 and CLAIM-986. The laboratory is a
proposal; ARG-072 says its defeat condition "waits on the first project".

## Not stated on the record

- **The disjoint failure.** ARG-074 says that, if CLAIM-623 is adopted, it
  could also say that the disjoint failure holds on discrete periods;
  CLAIM-623 does not say it.
- **The laboratory and the persistence thesis beyond the complement.**
  CLAIM-611 names CLAIM-616 and CASE-225 as where its layers "are
  separated in a system small enough to compute". CASE-333 is such a
  computation, but it illustrates CLAIM-474's achievements and CLAIM-616,
  and no entry says whether it separates CLAIM-611's maintenance from its
  repertoire.

## What it does not say

That the repertoire or its maintaining organization constitutes the
individual, which CLAIM-611 and CLAIM-615 leave to QUESTION-284; that
forcing is coercion, or that oscillator results transfer to organizations
without a mapping argued case by case (CLAIM-616); or which reading of
CLAIM-611's last clause is right.
