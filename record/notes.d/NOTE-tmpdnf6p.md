# NOTE-tmpdnf6p: Abrego Garcia v. Noem — Order granting expedited discovery

## Contribution

The first reasoned order on what the Supreme Court's "facilitate"
required of the government, and on what the government had to show about
it. It turns the Supreme Court's "should be prepared to share what it can"
into an enforceable inquiry: a definition of the duty, a benchmark from
the government's own practice, and discovery under the court's control.

## Key insight

The court never asks the government to prove that it cannot. It asks it
to show what it has done, measured against what it has done before in
cases like this, and when the government shows nothing, the court does
not infer inability; it treats the silence as possible noncompliance
and opens the record. The burden of producing the account sits with the
party that holds the information.

## Assumptions

- **The duty is settled.** That Abrego Garcia is entitled to injunctive
  relief is "undisputed", "affirmed without exception" by the Supreme
  Court, which "unanimously affirmed" that the order "properly requires
  the Government to 'facilitate'" his release.
- **The standard for discovery.** Good cause "[w]here significant
  questions regarding noncompliance with a court order have been raised"
  (Cal. Dep't of Social Servs. v. Leavitt, 9th Cir. 2008), assessed on
  seven non-exhaustive factors (Courthouse News v. Harris, D. Md. 2022).
- **Deference.** Footnote 2 accepts the Supreme Court's "due regard for
  the deference owed to the Executive Branch in the conduct of foreign
  affairs", but holds that it does not license "repeated refusal to
  provide even the most basic information".

## Key results

- **The earlier orders (ECF 51, 61).** On 10 April, hours after the
  Supreme Court's order, the court amended its injunction to "take all
  available steps to facilitate the return of Abrego Garcia to the
  United States as soon as possible", dropping "effectuate", and directed
  a declaration "from an individual with personal knowledge" on (1) his
  location and custodial status, (2) what steps had been taken, and (3)
  what further steps would be taken, and when. On 11 April it found that
  the government had "made no meaningful effort to comply", that counsel
  "could not answer these questions" and suggested that the information
  had been withheld from him, and that "nonspecific 'vetting'" was no
  basis for noncompliance; it ordered the same declaration daily.
- **What "facilitate" means (pp. 3–5).** By Black's, Merriam-Webster and
  the OED: to make easier, to assist. So the government is "obligated, at
  a minimum, to take the steps available to them toward aiding,
  assisting, or making easier Abrego Garcia's release from custody in El
  Salvador and resuming his status quo ante." The government's reading,
  "taking all available steps to remove any domestic obstacles", fails
  twice: facilitation of return has in practice meant more, and the
  government "appear[s] to have done nothing" even on its own reading.
- **The benchmark is the government's own practice (n. 1).** In the
  *National Immigration Project* litigation (S.D.N.Y. 2014) the
  government described a policy whose "ultimate objective was '[t]o
  effectuate return'": parole, cables to embassies for travel documents,
  designated points of contact, diplomatic channels, an ICE attaché, a
  Marshals Service flight to Haiti, and a practice of "log[ging] all
  efforts" to facilitate return.
- **The custody claim (n. 3).** A State Department official, Kozak, had
  declared Abrego Garcia "detained … pursuant to the sovereign, domestic
  authority of El Salvador" (ECF 63). The court sets against it a press
  report that the United States paid six million dollars to house the
  detainees "pending the United States' decision on their long-term
  disposition", and holds that plaintiffs may explore "who authorized his
  initial placement there and who presently authorizes his continued
  confinement".
- **The order (pp. 6–8).** Discovery into (1) location and custody, (2)
  steps taken and (3) steps to come: fifteen interrogatories and fifteen
  document requests, answers by 21 April; depositions of four
  government declarants (Cerna, Katz, Kozak, Mazzara) by 23 April, with
  leave to seek two more "of individuals with knowledge and authority";
  supplemental briefing by 28 and 30 April; the rest of the relief, and
  any contempt question, held in abeyance; sanctions available if the
  government does not engage "in good faith".

## Concepts

- **Facilitate**: "to make the occurrence of (something) easier; to
  render less difficult", applied as taking "the steps available to
  them" toward release and the status quo ante.
- **Status quo ante**: Abrego Garcia's case "handled as it would have
  been had he not been improperly sent to El Salvador" (the Supreme
  Court's words).
- **Personal knowledge**: the court's requirement for who must declare
  the steps, against counsel's inability to answer.

## Connections

It applies the Supreme Court's order ([LIT-1606](../literature.d/LIT-1606.md), [NOTE-1376](NOTE-1376.md)) and quotes
Judge Wilkinson's concurring opinion in the Fourth Circuit (7 April
2025), that the order may fairly be read as "requiring that the government
facilitate Abrego Garcia's release, rather than demand it". The
custody question in footnote 3 is the one *J.G.G.* later took up for the
Venezuelans ([CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md)'s constructive-custody particular, kept there as
the exchange's). *El-Masri* ([LIT-1619](../literature.d/LIT-1619.md), [NOTE-1391](NOTE-1391.md)) shifts the burden of
explanation onto a State for facts within its exclusive knowledge; this
order does the same in procedural form, for what a party has done.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-279](../questions.d/QUESTION-279.md).** The triage expected the remand to be the question's
  test case, a court demanding sworn declarations and ordering discovery
  into a claimed inability. The order does that, with two corrections of
  emphasis. First, the government did not here say "we cannot"; it said
  that the duty was narrower than the court's ("remove any domestic
  obstacles"), that the questions touched foreign affairs, and, through
  Kozak, that the custody was El Salvador's. The inability is implied by
  that last premise, not asserted. Second, the court's move is not to
  test a certificate of inability but to refuse to proceed without an
  account. What it requires, in [QUESTION-279](../questions.d/QUESTION-279.md)'s terms:
  - a **report of the search**, sworn by someone with personal knowledge
    (A10's first statement, "we have not found…", required to be made
    and made by the right person);
  - a **benchmark**: the government's own logged practice of facilitated
    returns, which shows that the space of steps is not empty in kind
    (A10's existence and knowability, established from the party's own
    records, not from the party's say-so);
  - an **inquiry into the arrangement** behind the claimed limit, the
    terms on which CECOT held deportees and who authorizes the detention,
    because whether a remedy is available depends on who controls the
    custody;
  - a **legal justification** for whatever cannot be disclosed
    (pp. 5–6).
  The order is a partial answer of the shape the question's note of
  2026-10-29 already found: a party's account of itself is not accepted
  where it alone holds the evidence. It adds that the account must be
  measured against the party's own past capability, which is a
  candidate for what plays the sorted index's part: a record of what the
  arrangement has done before. It does not say what would certify that
  no remedy exists; that question never arose.
- **[CLAIM-457](../claims.d/CLAIM-457.md).** What "facilitate" required of one member of a two-state
  arrangement is its own share: steps "available to them" toward a
  release that El Salvador would carry out, by the means its practice
  shows (requests, embassies, travel documents, transport). The court
  orders nothing of El Salvador. That is the claim's construction: A can
  be ordered to do A's part of a plan that needs B, without anyone
  ordering the outcome. The order presupposes, and does not decide, that
  the coalition can realize the outcome.
- **[CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md).** Two particulars the case can take as documented: the
  amended order of 10 April and the finding of 11 April that the
  government "made no meaningful effort to comply" with the order to
  declare its steps; and, from footnote 3, the conflict between a
  declaration that El Salvador held him on its own sovereign authority
  and the arrangement under which the United States paid for the
  detention pending its own decision. The second bears on the case's
  custody-and-control line ([CLAIM-405](../claims.d/CLAIM-405.md)). The $6 million is the court's
  quotation of a press report.
- **[NOTE-1376](NOTE-1376.md), open question.** On remand, "effectuate" was dropped
  within hours (ECF 51), and "facilitate" was read as above. Whether
  that reading survived is not settled by this order; the later
  proceedings were not read.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **A discovery order.** It finds noncompliance enough to justify
  discovery, not contempt, and nothing about what was in fact possible.
- **Four days of record.** "The record reflects that Defendants have
  done nothing at all" rests on declarations and counsel's answers
  between 10 and 15 April.
- **One citation not traced.** Page 3 cites "ECF No. 54" for the
  required disclosure that ECF 51 and ECF 61 set out; ECF 54 was not
  read, so whether this is a slip for 51 is not known.
- **What followed is outside it**: every later filing, and the return
  of 6 June 2025 that [CASE-175](../cases.d/CASE-175.md) records.

## Open questions

- What did the discovery show about the steps taken, and did the court
  rule on contempt? A later order on the docket would answer it; the
  owner asked for one ruling.
- Would a court accept a sworn, specific "no step is available" if the
  government's past practice showed steps of that kind? This order does
  not reach it, because no such account was offered.
