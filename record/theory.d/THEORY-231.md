# THEORY-231: Within classical realism, a generalized-noncontextuality no-go refutes Leibnizian representations, not realist representations as such; it rests on a stronger assumption than a Bell-like no-go and so applies where none exists, as in prepare-measure scenarios, but only Leibnizianity implying noncontextuality is proved

## Source

Schmid, Selby and Spekkens (2020), [LIT-945](../literature.d/LIT-945.md), arXiv:2009.03297v3, read **in
part** in [NOTE-737](../notes.d/NOTE-737.md), whose status is Skimmed. Read closely: §V
(inferential equivalence and quotienting), §VI (classical realist
representations, Leibnizianity, Definition VI.5, Proposition VI.6), §VIII
(generalized noncontextuality, Definition VIII.2, Proposition VIII.3, Eqs.
214–215, §VIII.C), §IX and Appendices A–B. Not read: §I.A, §§II–IV, where
the theories are constructed, and the proofs in Appendices C–G.
Spekkens (2005), [LIT-266](../literature.d/LIT-266.md), read in full in [NOTE-748](../notes.d/NOTE-748.md), for the
definition rehabilitated here.

## What was actually shown

**Two maps, one demand.** A classical realist representation ξ takes the
unquotiented operational theory into the unquotiented classical realist
theory. It is *Leibnizian* if it maps inferentially equivalent processes
to inferentially equivalent ones (Definition VI.5), a formalisation of an
epistemological generalisation of Leibniz's methodological principle
(§VI.C). A *generalized-noncontextual* representation ζ makes the same
preservation demand of a map into the *quotiented* realist theory
(Definition VIII.2), the counterpart of the ontological models of
[LIT-266](../literature.d/LIT-266.md).

**The chain (Eq. 215).** ∃ Leibnizian ξ ⇒ ∃ generalized-noncontextual ζ ⇔
∃ representation ξ̃ of the quotiented operational theory (Proposition VI.6,
Eq. 200; Proposition VIII.3, Eq. 214). So "Every no-go theorem for
generalized-noncontextual classical realist representations yields a
no-go theorem for Leibnizian classical realist representations." The
converse, that a no-go for Leibnizian representations yields one for
noncontextual ones, is Conjecture 1′. If it fails, Leibnizianity alone may
yield new no-go theorems.

**What the no-go leaves.** The conventional exit from a noncontextuality
no-go "requires a renouncement of Leibnizianity", not of classical
realism (§VIII.D). An experiment that supports a noncontextuality no-go
and no Bell-like one is always consistent with some non-Leibnizian ξ.
The authors' own exit is not to give up Leibnizianity but to seek a
realist theory with nonclassical causation and inference, specified by
five criteria and not constructed (§IX.A).

**Against Bell-like no-goes (§VIII.C).** A Bell-like no-go denies every
classical realist representation consistent with a causal structure:
"Bell-like no-go theorems are based on a weaker assumption."
Noncontextuality no-goes "can be proven for a broader set of
causal-inferential structures—even those involving just a single causal
system". For a prepare-measure structure, "the only restrictions on
F-S-realizable distributions over X, Y, A and B are that Y must be
independent of A and X", so no Bell-like no-go exists there, while
noncontextuality no-goes do.

**A condition on the question asked.** A quotiented theory is neither
contextual nor noncontextual: "it is a category mistake to ask whether ξ̃
is generalized-noncontextual or not." The question can be put only of an
unquotiented description in which equivalent procedures stay distinct.

## What this does not say

- **It rests on a partial reading.** The NOTE is Skimmed. The propositions
  of the chain were read as stated; their proofs, and the construction of
  the causal-inferential theories they are about (§§II–IV), were not. The
  prepare-measure realizability claim is stated without a derivation in
  the text read.
- **It does not say that refuting noncontextuality refutes Leibnizianity
  in the other direction.** Only Leibnizian ⇒ noncontextual is proved. A
  failure of Leibnizianity is not shown to produce a noncontextuality
  no-go.
- **It does not say that a noncontextuality no-go refutes realism.**
  Within classical realism it refutes Leibnizian representations. Denying
  every classical realist representation is the stronger, Bell-like
  conclusion, which needs a causal structure that a single system does not
  supply.
- **It does not say Leibnizianity must be given up.** The authors argue
  for a nonclassical realist theory instead, and build none. Whether one
  exists that represents quantum theory Leibnizianly is open.
- **It does not treat the Kochen–Specker or sheaf notions.** The relation
  of those to generalized noncontextuality is [THEORY-015](THEORY-015.md)'s, from [LIT-266](../literature.d/LIT-266.md),
  and the join to the sheaf form is the record's own.
- **It does not say that [LIT-266](../literature.d/LIT-266.md) appealed to Leibniz.** [LIT-266](../literature.d/LIT-266.md) argues
  from ontological economy. The Leibnizian grounding is this paper's, and
  needs, in the authors' words, an "epistemological generalization" of
  the principle.
