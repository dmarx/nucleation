**What the owner asked.** After pass 20, the owner asked whether readable literature near the unread works could give the held entries what they wait on. Five read-only searches covered 44 works:
- the unread works named in held entries' blocker notes;
- the sources of the seven Proposed readings not read in full;
- Le Cam's randomization criterion;
- Turner 2005.

Each candidate counted only if its full text was reached and quoted. The owner then ruled three borderline copies lawful (ADR-064):
- the SciSpace re-host of Mitra et al.;
- Fischer 2010 on a third-party scholar's site;
- Maitra's draft marked "do not cite".

Everything was filed on one branch.

**What was filed.**
- 30 new LITs read, and 38 NOTEs.
- Five held works turned out to be readable as they stood, from course and lab pages that ADR-064 now admits, and returned to Active: LIT-1728 (Cialdini 1997), LIT-588 (Haken, Kelso and Bunz), LIT-1375 (Putnam's two-level games), LIT-756 (Shipman and Marshall) and LIT-1079 (Mitra et al.).
- Two Active works only skimmed so far are now read in full or by chapter: LIT-981 (Coifman et al.) and LIT-1001 (boyd). NOTE-784 is superseded.
- Works whose copy was located but not fetched are Unfetched, with the address, the obstacle and the date (ADR-053):
  - six held LITs, including LIT-396, which moves from Unavailable;
  - five new ones.
- The obstacles were not the works'. This client fails TLS to PhilSci-Archive and sites.pitt.edu, whose chain ends at an emSign root it does not trust. PhilPapers, PhilArchive, OUP, Wiley and Annual Reviews served bot checks. These are the owner's queue.
- LIT-536's status note said HAL held *Biological Autonomy*. HAL holds only the front matter and the Introduction, which is now read. Mossio and Bich 2014, which THEORY-072's promote_when names, was found readable on HAL and read in full.

Every NOTE ends with "Bearing on the record", a verdict on each entry it bears on. A status-review pass then ruled from those verdicts, checking each against the NOTE's quotations and, where reachable, against the source.

**Two traps for the next person.**
- **Pulling while agents write.** Pulling the bot's repair commit into the shared tree while agents were writing overwrote one agent's uncommitted edit to a file the repair had also touched. The agent caught it and re-applied the edit. From then on, cluster commits waited locally and were pushed only when no agent was writing.
- **A tag fetch, not a release.** The coordinator wrongly told the owner luria 1.5.0 was the latest release, from a local clone that had not fetched tags; PyPI had 1.6.0. Ask PyPI, not a clone.
