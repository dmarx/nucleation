# CLAIM-137: A communicative kind is identified by an invariant that does not depend on any one realization, as a group character identifies a representation up to isomorphism, so that invariant has classificatory priority over any particular realization

## The claim

The owner at U54, quoting A191 §2's contrast with radical OSR: "I'd prefer a
non-eliminative OSR, but in which we effectively invoke character theory.
Character's are "more essential" than the representations that are comprised
of them." The scare quotes are the owner's.

The model is the finite-group case. For a finite group over ℂ, the character
χ_ρ(g) = Tr ρ(g) is unchanged by a change of basis, and it determines ρ up to
isomorphism. Transferred, the spoken, translated and visual realizations of a
teasing remark (A194 §2) are like one representation written in different
coordinates. What makes them one act is a shared invariant, "a common
structure of speaker–audience relations, normative expectations, and permitted
conversational responses", not resemblance to an original. A194 §2: "a
communicative kind could be individuated by an invariant relational signature
shared across its realizations." The kind is a communicative kind
([TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)), and the invariant is a character-like structural signature
([TERM-041](../terms.d/TERM-041.md)), a character in the strict sense only when it is the trace
of a specified representation.

It rests on the constitution claim ([CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)). An invariant of the
act's relations can fix the kind only if those relations are part of what the
kind is.

It answers [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md) without taking either horn. The question asks
whether there is a frame-independent act that translations approximate, or
whether the act is constituted by a family of frame-relative realizations. A
character is frame-independent, since it does not depend on the basis. But it
is not a further object beside the representations. It is the invariant of
their isomorphism class.

The non-eliminative frame is Esfeld's moderate structural realism, which the
record first held through the Stanford survey ([LIT-045](../literature.d/LIT-045.md), read in [NOTE-038](../notes.d/NOTE-038.md)), and now also
from Esfeld and Lam's own paper ([LIT-895](../literature.d/LIT-895.md), read in [NOTE-695](../notes.d/NOTE-695.md)):
"there are objects, but all their properties are relational". The formal
precedent is [THEORY-032](../theory.d/THEORY-032.md). The Yoneda lemma also identifies an object only up
to isomorphism, among objects the category already has, and a character is a
special case of that: it records the dimensions of the hom-sets from the
irreducibles, a counts-only profile that is complete because the category is
semisimple ([CLAIM-132](CLAIM-132.md)). Both identify; neither constructs the relata.

## Priority, stated exactly

A194 §1 corrected the owner's wording: "Strictly, the character is
mathematically derived from a representation, so it is not more fundamental
in every sense. But it is more fundamental *for classification up to
isomorphism*." "More essential" is filed in that sense. A194 §3: "The ordering
here is one of **explanatory and classificatory priority**, not necessarily an
assertion that characters exist as separate physical entities causing
representations." A198 §I: "characters have a certain *classificatory
priority* over representations, while relational organization has
*constitutive priority* over intrinsic labels."

The owner's "comprised of" inverts the mathematics. Representations are not
made of characters. A representation decomposes into irreducible
representations, and its character is computed from it, as the sum of the
irreducible characters with their multiplicities. The owner's priority
survives the correction, and A194 made it. The owner's words are kept above
as written.

A218's Part III restates the position as "the invariant features of its
transformations may be better guides to its kind than a privileged encoding".
That is an epistemic reading of what the owner put as a priority of the
invariant, and the step from priority to "guides" is not argued.

## What it does not say

- That a communicative kind is the trace of a group representation. A194 §2:
  "Here χ is a proposed character-like invariant, not necessarily the trace
  of a group representation." No such invariant has yet been built for any
  communicative case.
- That a complete invariant exists. That is what `defeated_if` tests, and
  even in mathematics completeness needs characteristic zero and
  finite-dimensional representations of a finite or compact group
  ([CLAIM-132](CLAIM-132.md)).
- That a token act is identical to its kind. A194 §3: "an individual object
  is not identical to its character." Characters classify kinds, not tokens
  or positions ([CLAIM-132](CLAIM-132.md)), and token individuation is
  [CLAIM-136](CLAIM-136.md)'s business.
- That classificatory priority is constitution. That inference is disclaimed
  ([CLAIM-150](CLAIM-150.md)).
- That the manuscript's thesis has changed. No draft carries this, and
  [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) stands.
