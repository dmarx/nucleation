# LIT-3002: The Matthew Effect in Science

Merton (1968). Science 159(3810), 56–63. DOI 10.1126/science.159.3810.56.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A names it at A1 for the Matthew effect as the sociological name for cumulative advantage. It bears on the social-science name A gives the rich-get-richer mechanism.

Read on 2026-11-22 in [NOTE-tmp4eogg](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp4eogg.md); the reading is [THEORY-tmp7rz62](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7rz62.md). A
is right on the name and its source and loose on the identity: Merton's
Matthew effect is credit for a given contribution allotted by the
contributor's standing, with a fixed number of top places and a ratchet
among its mechanisms, and cumulative advantage is "one institutional
version" of it, its growth implication credited to Price. So it grounds
the name, not [CLAIM-817](../claims.d/CLAIM-817.md)'s "one feedback" ([ARG-tmpaf6km](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpaf6km.md)), and leaves that
claim's grounds. [TERM-tmpeguzj](../terms.d/TERM-tmpeguzj.md) separates Merton's sense from the formal
one. It also links the effect to Merton's own self-fulfilling prophecy
([LIT-1398](LIT-1398.md)), "a special application" of it (p. 61).
