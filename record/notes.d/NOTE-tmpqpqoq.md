# NOTE-tmpqpqoq: Block — Psychologism and Behaviorism

## Contribution

It gives behaviourism about intelligence its best form, a capacity to
respond sensibly to any input sequence, with "sensible" granted, and refutes
it by a single counterexample. That shows the standard objections to
behaviourism had left the sufficient-condition claim untouched, and it
establishes that the concept of intelligence is partly etiological.

## Key insight

Behaviour is evidence of intelligence only because we assume it was
produced by something that thinks of the responses. Store the responses in
advance and the same behaviour, actual and counterfactual, comes from a
process "of the most elementary sort" (n. 21). Echoes of intelligence are
not intelligence, whether the echo comes from programmers or from a cosmic
accident.

## Assumptions

- **Sense of "intelligent"**: "the possession of thought or reason", not
  the comparative sense (p. 8).
- **Concession**: "sensible" can be defined non-question-beggingly
  (pp. 10–11), "an enormous concession to behaviorism".
- **Finiteness (pp. 19, 23–24)**: a test of bounded length has finitely many
  typable strings; quantised senses make the same true of a whole robot's
  stimuli, so the argument covers intelligence simpliciter. The empirical
  version (analogue-to-digital conversion makes no mental difference) is
  only mentioned; the conceptual version is that our concept allows an
  intelligent being with "dot"-picture eyes (p. 24).
- **Modality (Objection 6)**: behaviourist analyses are conceptual, so
  logical possibility suffices; if the view is a law of psychology instead,
  a world with infinitely divisible matter and the same psychological laws
  still contains the machine (pp. 30–33, n. 25).

## Key results

- **Dispositions vs capacities (pp. 14–19).** Perfect actors and
  super-super-spartans show no disposition is *necessary* for pain or
  intelligence; but "how could an unintelligent system perfectly pretend to
  be intelligent?", so they leave sufficiency standing. Recast as a
  capacity, specified by conditionals with internal states in their
  antecedents, the conception escapes Chisholm–Geach, perfect actors and
  paralytics altogether (p. 18).
- **The string-searcher (pp. 19–21).** Interrogator types A; the machine
  picks a stored sensible string beginning with A and types its next
  sentence B; and so on. Tree-searching variant: one programmed reply per
  branch. Programmers may simulate "my Aunt Bertha". Conclusion: the
  capacity to emit sensible responses "is not sufficient for
  intelligence", and the neo-Turing Test conception, "along with the older
  and cruder Turing Test conceptions", is refuted (p. 21).
- **Conduit, not radio (p. 22).** The programmers' causal efficacy ends
  before the test begins; no signal from the interrogator reaches them. A
  memoriser of Chinese responses, or Luria's mnemonist fed strings by
  philosophers, has the capacity without the intelligence.
- **Objections and replies (pp. 25–43).**
  - O1, every machine's intelligence is its programmers': no; learning
    machines' intelligence "is its own".
  - O2, current events: irrelevant to intelligence.
  - O3, a stipulated new meaning: the two-grandmaster chess trick shows our
    concept already treats echoed performance as misleading; natural-kind
    terms allow stereotypical Xs that are not Xs (Putnam).
  - O3a, chauvinism: the claim is about "richness" of processing, not
    likeness to us; refutation by counterexample needs no definition,
    as a paper clip refutes a definition of life.
  - O4, "what if you were one?": like asking an atheist "what if you turned
    out to be God?" (pp. 28–29).
  - O6, combinatorial explosion: about 10^1500 strings (Miller's estimate,
    n. 24); answered by logical possibility, a law-of-psychology argument,
    Basic English, tree-search and hybrid tricks that "postpone, but do not
    avoid" explosion.
  - O7, unbounded tests: humans cannot pass them either; idealising to
    unbounded competence requires mentalistic talk of memory and motivation
    no behaviourist can use; loop-searchers and ever-extended tapes answer
    the rest.
  - O8, Dennett's "averts exponential explosion" amendment: psychologistic
    already, ad hoc, and caught between postponing and avoiding.
- **Simulators (pp. 40–42).** Machines computing your particle
  trajectories, neurons or psychological mechanisms may avoid explosion and
  are "arguably" unintelligent: manipulating descriptions of cogitation "is
  hardly obvious" to be cogitation. A Chinese-speaking homunculus with a
  manual of your psychology makes the point. Block calls this part
  "incomplete".
- **Note 30.** Lycan ("Form, Function, and Feel", [LIT-419](../literature.d/LIT-419.md)) and Rosenthal objected to
  Block's homunculi-head intuition as a forest-for-the-trees illusion;
  Block answers with a single homunculus attending to a column of the
  machine table. Some symbol-manipulating homunculi-heads are intelligent;
  this one is not because its states' causal relations "do not mirror" ours.

## Concepts

- **Psychologism** — whether behaviour is intelligent depends on the
  character of the information processing that produces it (p. 5).
- **Neo-Turing Test conception** — intelligence is "the capacity to produce
  a sensible sequence of verbal responses to a sequence of verbal stimuli,
  whatever they may be" (p. 18).
- **Disposition vs capacity** — input–output conditionals versus
  conditionals with internal states in the antecedent (pp. 16–17).
- **Sensible string** — a typable string interpretable as a conversation in
  which at least one party is sensible (p. 19).

## Limitations

- "Richness" of processing is named, not defined (p. 28).
- The extension from conversation to all behaviour rests on a quantisation
  claim argued only briefly.
- The simulator cases at the end are explicitly incomplete, and the
  intuition they rely on is the one Block admits is "too easily
  manipulable" (n. 30).
- The machine is a lookup over complete histories; whether its lesson
  transfers to systems that compress or generalise from stored text is not
  addressed.

## Open questions

- What is the minimal processing condition that separates the
  string-searcher from an intelligent system, given that Dennett's
  exponential-explosion condition fails?
- Does the argument transfer to systems that interpolate rather than look
  up, which are neither Block's machine nor the "learning" machines of his
  reply to Objection 1?
