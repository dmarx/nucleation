# THEORY-1352: In Van Lange and Bergmann's model, human cooperation is readily sustained between individuals, above all in dyads, through precise reciprocity, empathy and a partner who cannot be excluded at no cost, and is fragile between groups: categorization alone yields mistrust and in-group favouritism by bounded reciprocity, observed out-group favouritism hardens it into distrust, and chiefly negative interdependence, real or perceived, turns that into costly competition, aggression and hate, so that the processes that build in-group cooperation can fuel intergroup conflict

## Source

Van Lange and Bergmann (2026), [LIT-2002](../literature.d/LIT-2002.md), pp. 547–555. Read in
[NOTE-1537](../notes.d/NOTE-1537.md).

## What was actually shown

Nothing new. The paper assembles results from laboratory social dilemmas,
minimal-group studies and field work, and proposes the stages. Its claims,
with page references, are in the note. Two are worth separating:

- **"need not imply ... any harmful behaviour towards outgroups"** (p.
  553). Favouritism is not harm.
- **"the same processes that can establish ingroup cooperation can also
  fuel intergroup conflict"** (p. 554). Conformity and peer punishment
  push members to invest in a conflict.

## What this does not say

- That groups fail to persist, or go extinct. The model is about
  cooperation and conflict between groups, and [CLAIM-547](../claims.d/CLAIM-547.md)'s
  coexistence of groups is a further target.
- That the cross-group-dyad remedy works. It is proposed, untested.
