# LIT-245: Minimum Description Length and Generalization Guarantees for Representation Learning

Milad Sefidgaran, Abdellatif Zaidi, Piotr Krasnowski (2023), *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 36 (NeurIPS 2023), poster* — [ARXIV-2402.03254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03254)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-742](../notes.d/NOTE-742.md).*

- **The main bound (Theorem 4).**
  E[gen(S,W)] ≤ inf 2√((2E D_KL(P(U,U′|X,X′,Ŵe) ‖ Q) + K + 2)/n) + ε for
  a K-class task. Q is any prior over the 2n latents of a training set
  and a ghost set that is invariant under permutations preserving the
  labels. It may depend on both sets' inputs and labels. The bound
  depends only on the encoder and "is valid for any choice decoder".
  Theorem 7 is the matching tail bound.
- **What it improves on.**
  - With a product prior, the KL term becomes n times a sample-wise
    divergence. With the marginal of Ŷ as Q, that is the empirical mutual
    information (Eqs. 3–4).
  - For threshold classifiers (VC dimension 1), the bound vanishes as
    O(√(log n / n)), while the mutual-information term stays of order
    H(Y).
  - The predicted-label version (Theorem 1) equals I(J; Ŷ^{2n} | Y^{2n}),
    an f-CMI quantity conditioned on labels only. It recovers the VC
    bound d log(2en/d).
- **Why I(X;U) is set aside.** The paper gives four reasons:
  - existing I(X;U) bounds (Shamir et al., Vera et al., Kawaguchi et al.)
    are vacuous or restricted to finite alphabets;
  - generalisation tracks geometric compression instead;
  - I(X;U) is invariant to bijections and so blind to the encoder's
    structure;
  - it is infinite for deterministic continuous encoders.
  Lossy compression keeps the new quantity finite. In the paper's
  example, I(U;X) = ∞ while a lossy Û within distortion 0.05 has
  I(Û;X) = 1 (App. C.3).
- **What is not shown.**
  - The bound is never computed for a trained network.
  - The prior may depend on the ghost data and is only approximated in
    practice.
  - The (K + 2) term sets a floor.
  - The experiment, label-dependent learned Gaussian priors in VIB
    ("CDVIB"), gives 65% against VIB's 62.5% on one small CIFAR-10 CNN.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 at the owner's request, from a list they grouped
under the heading *Relating K-complexity to minimum sufficient
statistics*. Read on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-742](../notes.d/NOTE-742.md). It is held as the
sharpest statement in the record that the IB's I(X;U) is the wrong
complexity for generalisation and a coding rate under a prior is the
right one. Its "MDL" is Shannon-style, a KL to a prior, not Kolmogorov
complexity, and it says nothing about sufficient statistics. Its Theorem 4
is filed as [THEORY-238](../theory.d/THEORY-238.md).

It bears on [THEORY-035](../theory.d/THEORY-035.md), which names it among the formal treatments of
"the IB term controls generalisation". It takes that claim up in order
to replace it, and supplies a third information quantity that does bound
generalisation, beside I(S;W) ([LIT-347](LIT-347.md)) and against I(X;T). It is
`compared_against` Vera et al. ([LIT-240](LIT-240.md)). The paper's case against that bound is
that it is restricted to finite alphabets and vacuous, citing others,
not that it is wrong. The comparison is analytical; a numerical one
against Vera et al. is left to future work (App. C.4). It names its label-dependent priors as a link to
Fischer's CEB ([LIT-226](LIT-226.md)).

Tagged `anthology-candidate` ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)). The CDVIB regulariser is a
training instruction and belongs in the anthology. The bounds are
learning theory. It is kept here by the owner's decision of 2026-09-26
that new work stays in nucleation until a transfer is judged appropriate
([ADR-010](../decisions.d/ADR-010.md)).
