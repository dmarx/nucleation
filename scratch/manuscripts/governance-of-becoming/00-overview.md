# The Governance of Becoming

## Individuation, interdependence, and the organization of possibility

*An introduction to a series of fourteen articles in five movements. How the series is written, and how to read its citations, is in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md); where each article stands, and how it was made, is in the [README](README.md) and the `apparatus/` files.*

## The question and the thesis

Most theories of organization take the organization as given and ask how it behaves, how it should be governed, or what it owes. This series asks the prior question: how organized agency comes to be through the interdependence of processes that were not designed to compose it, how that interdependence comes to carry requirements and standing, and how an organization becomes able, or unable, to govern its own further development.

Its thesis has three parts. First, organizations individuate through maintained relations, and the relations that matter most for governing them (whose processes keep them in being, which relied-upon regularities have become contract-like, what they can still reach and at what cost) are largely invisible to what ordinary observation measures: behaviour, boundaries, coupling and persistence. Second, because cooperation changes its participants, the same success that produces capacities can produce dependence, ignorance and power over others, so that exploitation, domination and agency without answerability can arise from what looks like good performance. Third, the governance of such systems is therefore a matter of what they keep attainable, correctable and answerable over time, and not only of the conduct they constrain now; whether an arrangement is legitimate is a further question, which none of the mechanisms settles.

The series argues for these claims piece by piece. Most of its results are results about models: constructions and simulations whose populations, laws and parameters are stipulated. They show that a mechanism can do what is claimed and that two descriptions can come apart; they do not show that any real organization works that way. Where a step is a conjecture, or rests on a reading of a published study, the article that takes it says so.

## The five movements and the fourteen articles

### Movement I. Organization, possibility, and interdependence

**Article 1. The Identity of Organization: individuation, maintenance provenance, and the limits of behavioural equivalence** (`01-identity-of-organization.md`). *Question:* when are two organizations the same organization, and which of the facts that ordinary criteria miss would matter to anyone governing one? *Thesis:* the criteria of behaviour, boundary, coherence and persistence pick out different things, and the one that matters most for governance, maintenance provenance, is invisible to all of them. "Individuation" has several senses that are not stages, and the achievements from interconnection to autonomy form a partial order. In a simulated population, arrangements with identical ordinary trajectories keep their governing rules in different ways and come apart only under intervention (CASE-161); maintenance is located only by interventions on the maintaining mechanism, relative to a boundary declared in advance (CLAIM-522). Formation is a fact about history that, in the one construction available, no intervention after formation can recover (TERM-100; CASE-191). What persists through the replacement of every part is a lineage carried by causal transmission, not a resemblance (CLAIM-329; CLAIM-615). Individuation in any sense establishes neither agency nor standing.

**Article 2. Regulation as the Governance of Possibility: identity, slow modes, and the temporal organization of adaptive industrial ecosystems.** A separate manuscript, `scratch/manuscripts/governance-of-possibility/`, which is its own work and is read as the series' second article. It argues that what regulation can secure is what it keeps attainable, sustainable and revisable, and that this is a question about time: a hard constraint added to fixed dynamics cannot enlarge what demands can jointly achieve, and ecosystems matched on present output can differ in how soon, and whether without outside money, they can reorganize.

**Article 3. The Contracts We Become: relational invocation, stored coordination, and the formation of interdependence** (`03-contracts-we-become.md`). *Question:* when is a maintained relation among an organization's processes contract-like, and how does a relied-upon regularity become one? *Thesis:* processes can come to stand in contract-like relations that no one stated or agreed to; such a relation is contract-like only if it governs what happens on deviation, conflict and breach (TERM-tmplpplx), and where it is, it can give a process an operational standing that does not presuppose moral consideration (CLAIM-tmpkvzcn). The article separates regularity, expectation, dependence, obligation and contract, so that no reliance is counted as a debt by default. It prices a contract's lifecycle with a cost model of stored work (CASE-326; CLAIM-760), shows that leaving can be unaffordable without any preference for staying and that an obligation bars exit rather than raising its price, and argues that invoking a relationship differs from reusing a stored result because an invocation can change the relation it invokes. The account of contracts is a set of conjectures; the cost geometry is demonstrated in constructions with illustrative values.

### Movement II. Agency, inquiry, and epistemic organization

**Article 4. The Architecture of Will: authority, organizational affect, and the negotiation of incompatible demands** (`04-architecture-of-will.md`). *Question:* when an organization maintains demands that cannot all be met, what settles which takes effect, and is a theory of it more than a description of the mechanisms that carry it out? *Thesis:* what settles it is an architecture of practical authority, a will-organization (TERM-023), whose relations of precedence, jurisdiction, suspension and granted against claimed authority are distinct from one another and from their realizers. In a model of one stipulated organization a description in these terms beats five control-only models on held-out interventions (CASE-325; CLAIM-889); against control theory it is implemented, not replaced, and for an organization it is not new. Forbidding self-grants does not stop recursive amplification, a tolerance is a directed criterion for conferring influence, and the primitive of regulatory difficulty is frustration among demands, not distress. Organizational affect is placed, as a conjecture facing an unanswered objection, at the gap between what a motive claims and what it is granted (CLAIM-039). Quiet coordination does not show an accommodation adequate (CLAIM-364).

**Article 5. Inquiry Changes the Inquirer: costed epistemic frontiers, stored specification, and adaptive readiness** (`05-inquiry-changes-the-inquirer.md`). *Question:* how does inquiry change the frontiers through which questions become askable and answerable? *Thesis:* inquiry is a form of specification, and formulating a question is itself a costed realization, so what an inquirer can later ask, and at what cost, is a product of what its earlier inquiry stored. At one budget, what an agent can ask and what it can answer do not fix which question–answer pairs it can reach together (CLAIM-1115). Authorization and legibility are costs on the same budget and, separately, conditions that bar a transition without pricing it. In executed models, governance changes whether and how much an organization goes on to inquire (CASE-396), and organizations with the same output differ in readiness (CASE-412). Overconsolidation is proposed, against the literature that qualifies it, as a generalization of overfitting (CLAIM-758). Inquiry changes which questions the inquirer can ask and can take its own terms as its object; that it is thereby a way the inquirer learns is not shown.

**Article 6. The Organization of Ignorance: heuristics, reflexive control, self-confirming governance, and protected evidence** (`06-organization-of-ignorance.md`). *Question:* how can an organization be kept ignorant without being kept from information, and what keeps its correction possible? *Thesis:* ignorance can be a property of how evidence, evaluation and authority are arranged, and the arrangements come apart, each with its own mechanism, remedy and way of looking like success from inside. Each in a stipulated model: correcting how evidence was selected is not correcting how it is scored (CASE-162; CLAIM-466); a clean internal record is not compliance (CASE-397); a corrected belief is not a corrected practice (CASE-346; CLAIM-446); and a protected anchor is not a correct one (CLAIM-1185). The general conjecture, that self-correction turns on how evidence, evaluation and authority are coupled (CLAIM-1024), is set beside the objection it has not answered. Familiar biases are placed as candidate manifestations, with tests not yet run on human judgement.

### Movement III. Culture, normativity, and dependence

**Article 7. Memes That Make Worlds: pragmatic transport, cultural nucleation, hyperstition, and operative norm formation** (`07-memes-that-make-worlds.md`). *Question:* can a meme be a seed around which beliefs, practices and relations organize, and how would that be told from contagion? *Thesis:* keeping a relation, rebuilding a distinction, settling on a convention and seeding new organization are different outcomes of transmission, and a theory of memes worth having keeps them apart. A chain can forget its source while staying far from it, so convergence is not preservation (CLAIM-270). On that floor the article states the conjecture that a meme acts less as a replicator than as a seed carrying one characteristic value of a perspective (CLAIM-tmp4mk0t), whose persistent reproduction can seed related but distinct perspectives (CLAIM-tmphi3mx). Three sharper results follow: under reversal of a social attribution, conformity and defiance lie in one component of a response (CLAIM-tmpj4pw3); in a model of hyperstition, circulation amplifies its own realization exactly when the product of gains exceeds the product of decays, and a realization outlasts its representation only with a stable state of its own (CLAIM-tmpvic6m; CLAIM-tmpnws9d); and in a simulation only engagement under randomized exposure told a class that reshaped its referent from one that won on its own measures (CASE-367; CLAIM-954). The step to norms is weaker: restoring a deviation was passed alike by conformity, enforcement and checking against the world (CASE-362; CLAIM-946).

**Article 8. What We Owe Through Cooperation: rights, demands, commitments, and the legitimate governance of relational becoming** (`08-what-we-owe-through-cooperation.md`). *Question:* what, if anything, does what cooperation produces add to what its participants owe, and can it be read off the cooperation's success? *Thesis:* it cannot, and much of it cannot be read off outcomes either. Rights, demands and commitments are distinct and none establishes another (CLAIM-738), so neither joint satisfiability nor viability certifies that a scheme's terms are owed (CLAIM-725). A right that protects a choice constrains how a decision is reached, so two arrangements can reach the same endpoint while one defeats it (CLAIM-741). The proposal that defeating standing to contest is a wrong of its own (CLAIM-723) is set against the instrumentalist objection (CLAIM-1184), and the dispute is located, not settled. Answerability is bounded; the grounds of special obligation are sorted and none is established; repair does not wait on culpability (CLAIM-742); and morality evaluates the terms of cooperation without being confined to cooperators (CLAIM-tmpreipd). Most steps are interpretive proposals.

**Article 9. The Political Economy of Dependence: cooperation, exploitation, domination, and the governance of contractual costs** (`09-political-economy-of-dependence.md`). *Question:* how can reliable provision produce the dependence through which a split of cooperation's product becomes unfair? *Thesis:* where capacity is learned by doing and lost with disuse, supply that does a party's work lowers the capacity it keeps (CLAIM-602), with a deadline after which withdrawal no longer restores it (CLAIM-605). In a model in which the split is re-bargained against the current fallback, such supply lowers the party's terms while it never exits and no price, rule or exit option changes; ending it before the deadline restores the terms, and after it does not (CLAIM-tmp6nzs1). The article proposes that what sorts a dependence is who controls the terms on which it is met, not its magnitude (CLAIM-690; CASE-264), sets beside the exit channel the cost of contesting (CASE-287), the capture of conscientious compensation (CLAIM-700) and the activation of another's obligations, which bars exit without pricing it (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6), and states a presumptive test for domination by an arrangement's controllers (TERM-124). It declares no standard, and so judges no arrangement exploitative or dominating.

## How the articles depend on one another

Each article is written to be read on its own, and none needs the acceptance of another's thesis; they share a source, not a chain of premises. The plan of the series groups them in three streams that can proceed substantially independently:

- **Formal theory, Articles 1 to 5.** Article 1 supplies the separations (maintenance from behaviour, formation from maintenance, lineage from resemblance) that the rest presuppose when they speak of what an organization is. Article 3 builds its contracts on maintained relations of that kind, and its cost model of stored work is the geometry Articles 2, 5 and 9 also use. Article 4's architecture of authority is what Article 3's operational standing operates within, and Article 5's authorization costs and conditions are the epistemic face of Article 3's account of what bars and what prices a transition.
- **Normative theory, Articles 7 to 9.** Article 7's distinction between a regularity, an operative norm and a legitimate one, and Article 3's norm stabilized by the reliance it attracts, are where Article 8 starts: what is operative is not thereby owed. Article 9 takes Article 3's lock-in and exit, its proposal that control sorts a dependence, and the capability loss that Article 5 indexes by actor, into the terms of exchange; like Article 8, it does not read what is owed off what works, and so declines to call any mechanism exploitation without a declared standard.
- **Applied theory, Articles 6 and 10 to 12.** Article 6 applies the separations of Articles 1 and 4 (interventions on mechanism; claimed against granted authority) to evidence and evaluation. Articles 10 to 12 are planned to carry the readiness results of Article 2, the inquiry results of Article 5 and the ignorance results of Article 6 into engineered systems and institutions.
- **Articles 13 and 14**, the constitutional synthesis, are planned to draw on all three streams, with 14 after 13.

A few results recur across articles and bind them more tightly than the streams suggest: the separation of matched ordinary behaviour from response to intervention (Articles 1, 4, 6 and 7), the loss of capacity to supply that does the work (Articles 3, 5 and 9), and models in which governance changes whether an organization goes on looking (Articles 5, 6 and 8).

## What the series does not establish

- **Nothing about real organizations by itself.** Its executed results are models with stipulated parameters, and a model shows what can happen in it. The few studies of real records (coded testimony on one case, a coding of eleven open-source successions, a fit of a cost model to experimental choices) are named where they are used, with their limits.
- **No verdict of exploitation, domination or illegitimacy** on any arrangement. Those are judged only against a standard declared with the judgment, and the series declares none; it gives mechanisms by which such relations can be built while ordinary performance looks fine.
- **Not that the distinctions are one theory.** The series keeps four distinctions explicit throughout: causal dependence is not necessarily contract; operational normativity is not necessarily moral normativity; successful individuation is not necessarily legitimate organization; effective agency is not necessarily answerable agency. Joining the articles' results into a single law is not attempted, and where a join was tempting and failed, the article says the link is open.
- **Many of its central theses are conjectures**: the seed theory of memes, organizational affect, the account of implicit contracts, the coupling conjecture about self-correction, control as what sorts a dependence. Each is argued and stated with the objection it faces; none is offered as shown.
- **The human tests are not run.** Where an article places a familiar bias, an affective state or a habit beside a mechanism, the test on people is named and has not been run.
- **Movements IV and V are not yet written.** What the series says about reliability engineering, legibility, institutional sociopathy and constitutions is so far only the plan.
- **The comparison with neighbouring theories is incomplete.** Transaction-cost and incomplete-contract theory, complex contagion and cultural attraction, and theories of associative obligation are read only in part or not at all, and the articles that need them say so.
