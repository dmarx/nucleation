# NOTE-tmpsll57: Gardner (2012) — The Idea of Justice (critical notice of Sen)

## Contribution

An independent critique of Sen's comparative approach, converging with Valentini (LIT-290), with a new objection to Sen's priority of capability over achievement.

## Key results

1. p. 5: the differences between the two sides "seem more temperamental than strictly philosophical."
2. p. 8, quoting IoJ p. 238: "The central issue ... is the freedom to choose how to live."
3. p. 8, on Sen's healthcare example: "But where is the argument?"
4. pp. 10–11: Sen "still shows anti-perfectionist leanings" in keeping public policy aloof from how capabilities are used.
5. pp. 11–12: to rank injustices one needs to know "the difference between a big departure" and a small one from a standard of justice.
6. p. 14: neither Rawls nor Sen says "what marks a question out as one of justice".

## Assumptions

- Injustice is the breach of a duty of justice.
- Value pluralism; Raz's liberal perfectionism as the point of comparison.

## Concepts

Transcendental institutionalism; realization-focused comparison; capability against achievement; liberal perfectionism and anti-perfectionism.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-434** (Valentini's critique): a second, independent reviewer makes the same objection (results 1, 5).
- **CLAIM-tmp6wuql**: qualifies its framing. The claim's conclusion is stated in Sen's capability terms, and Gardner finds Sen's preference for capability over achievement unargued (results 2–4). ARG-tmpar7tl finds the qualification does not reach the derivation, which holds for achieved functionings too.
- **The exchange's use** of Sen's comparative approach as a theory of change (A10 §VIII, A11 §28, A13 §VIII): Gardner's argument that ranking needs a standard is a live objection to it.

## Limitations

A review; the book itself (LIT-tmpl0haz) was not reached.
