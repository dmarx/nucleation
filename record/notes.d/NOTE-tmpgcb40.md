# NOTE-tmpgcb40: Category error in AI safety: non-sentient moral machines

## Contribution

AI safety discourse increasingly treats AI systems as possible bearers of moral status, calling them "moral machines" and debating their rights and responsibilities. Vieira argues that this conflates functional sophistication with phenomenal consciousness, the sentience he holds is required for moral patiency. Drawing on the philosophical-zombie tradition, AI ethics and critiques of anthropomorphism, he argues that current AI systems are "moral zombies", able to simulate moral behaviour without the experiences that ground moral concern. He takes up objections from relational ethics, from human–AI interaction studies and from global perspectives on moral status, and recasts AI safety as a problem of ontological and ethical clarity rather than value encoding.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (p. 2) states the category-error thesis in Ryle's sense: computational processes are treated as though they had the phenomenal properties that ground moral consideration.
- §3 (p. 3) extends Chalmers' p-zombie to "moral zombies", with an explicit argument. P1: moral status requires phenomenal consciousness. P2: current and foreseeable AI lacks it. The conclusion is that such AI lacks moral status.
- §§4–5 (pp. 3–5) criticise the "moral machines" label and take up the hard question of moral status without sentience.
- §6 (pp. 5–6) draws four safety implications: clarifying priorities, avoiding anthropomorphic errors, keeping responsibility with humans, and building safety metrics on effects on sentient beings rather than on AI "welfare".
- §8 (p. 8) concedes that the question should be revisited if positive evidence of machine consciousness appears.
