# LIT-tmpudm1k: Towards a theory of how the structure of language is acquired by deep neural networks

Francesco Cagnetta and Matthieu Wyart (2024), *Advances in Neural
Information Processing Systems 37* (NeurIPS 2024), pp. 83119–83163 —
[ARXIV-2406.00048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00048), DOI-10.52202/079017-2645

## Key takeaways

- **The task.** The Random Hierarchy Model of [LIT-877](LIT-877.md) (L levels of v
  symbols, each rewriting to m distinct s-tuples from the level below,
  no tuple with two parents), now with an unlabelled root and the last of
  the d = s^L tokens masked. A network sees the d − 1 preceding tokens and
  is trained by cross-entropy to predict the last.
- **Correlations decay with depth of the common ancestor.** The covariance
  C_{i,d}(µ, ν) = P(X_i = µ, X_d = ν) − P(X_i = µ)P(X_d = ν), averaged in
  root-mean-square over the vocabulary, depends only on the level ℓ of the
  two positions' lowest common ancestor, and falls in steps with it:
  C̃^(ℓ) ≈ ((1 − m/v^(s−1))/(v³ m^(2ℓ−1)))^(1/2) (Eq. 8, App. D, large v
  and m). Read against distance t this is roughly a power law,
  t^(−β) with β = ln m / ln s. In the maximal case m = v^(s−1) every
  string occurs and there are no correlations.
- **A finite sample is a finite context.** The empirical estimate from P
  sequences saturates at a noise floor (v²P)^(−1/2) (App. E), so only
  correlations out to t*(P) are resolved; for a power law,
  t*(P) ~ P^(1/(2β)). The thresholds P_ℓ = v m^(2ℓ−1)/(1 − m/v^(s−1))
  (Eq. 12) count the choices that fix two tokens with a level-ℓ ancestor.
- **Hidden variables from correlations.** Given its parent, a tuple is
  conditionally independent of the masked token, so tuples with the same
  parent have the same correlations with it and can be grouped; tuple
  correlations and their noise shrink by the same factor (App. F), so the
  thresholds stand. Counting n-grams needs a sample exponential in the
  context length; a learner that reconstructs the hidden variables needs
  one polynomial in it. This route is argued, not proved for networks
  trained by gradient descent (§6).
- **Measured.** Depth-3 transformers on L = 3 data show a loss step near
  each P_ℓ, to the levels bounded by Eq. 11 (Fig. 2); with the context
  cut to t = s^ℓ − 1 the curve stops after ℓ steps. In depth-4 CNNs matched
  to the tree, a layer's sensitivity to resetting the production rule
  below a level-ℓ symbol, relative to replacing the symbol, drops from 1 to
  0 near P_ℓ (Fig. 3). CNNs reach the third step earlier than P_3, which the
  authors attribute to weight sharing (App. G.1); the first step scales as
  P_1 ∝ (t + 1), which the theory does not capture (App. G.3).
- **Real text.** On character-level tiny-Shakespeare and WikiText-103, the
  token correlation decays as t^(−β) with β ≈ 1.4 and 1.55; the loss of a
  transformer with context t levels off at a P that grows with t, and
  curves for different t collapse when P is rescaled by t^(2β) with β
  taken from the correlations (Figs. 4, 5; Eq. 16). The test bears on the
  conjectured link between correlation range and usable context, not on
  hidden variables, which real text does not expose.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-09 at the owner's request, as one of the works cited by
the batch on hierarchy and hyperbolic geometry ([LIT-865](LIT-865.md) to [LIT-877](LIT-877.md)) that
neither record held. [LIT-877](LIT-877.md), which introduces the Random Hierarchy Model,
names it among the same group's later papers. Read on its own merits
([NOTE-tmpmgc8r](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpmgc8r.md)); the reading produces [THEORY-tmpyaqz7](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyaqz7.md).

It moves the Random Hierarchy Model from labels to language: the
statistics that reveal the hierarchy are now between tokens of the
sequence, which is the kind of object the record's word-embedding readings
work on ([LIT-855](LIT-855.md), [LIT-860](LIT-860.md), [LIT-863](LIT-863.md)). It does not answer [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md). It
studies no embedding, no PMI and no direction; its correlation function is
a root-mean-square over the vocabulary, which says how strong the
dependence between two positions is, not which tokens carry it. What it
adds is how a hierarchy shows in co-occurrence: descendants of one hidden
symbol have identical correlations with what lies outside it, and the
strength falls by a factor of about m per level, so a finite corpus shows
the shallow levels and hides the deep ones. The details are in the NOTE.
