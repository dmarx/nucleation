# CLAIM-1342: In the toy models filed from assistants' exchanges, the verbal conclusion drawn from a model often reads a parameter as the concept it was introduced to stand for, so checking a model's numbers is not enough: each parameter's reading has to be checked before a conclusion is read off it

## The instances

All three are from the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic
Normativity', and in each the numbers were right:

1. **The confirmation threshold** ([CLAIM-1324](CLAIM-1324.md)). A11 presents a
   symmetry that "becomes unstable when κ exceeds 2/3" as a property of
   confirmation-biased sampling. The threshold comes from the
   illustration's decay factor. The sampling bias alone has none.
2. **The sign character** ([CLAIM-1281](CLAIM-1281.md)). A9 reads "I am whatever you say
   I am" as a response with |dr/ds| = 1 under reversal. Oddness under
   reversal, the character the model gives, does not fix the slope:
   r = 0.05s and r = s³ are odd too.
3. **The norm's justification** ([CLAIM-1284](CLAIM-1284.md), note of 2026-11-24). The
   assistant read the model's baseline b as the norm's justification, and
   so reported that a norm in the model could not outlast its
   justification's disappearance. The owner's case ([CLAIM-1245](CLAIM-1245.md)) made
   the assistant recompute: with b read as the tendency to comply, which
   is what it is, the model lets a norm outlast the loss of most of it.

## What follows

Checking a model means checking two things: that its numbers are right,
and that each parameter means what the verbal conclusion takes it to mean.
The second check failed in all three cases above, and the first did not
catch it. Instance 3 is the checker's own error, so the claim is not only
about ChatGPT. The read-and-file skill's step 9a now asks for both checks.
