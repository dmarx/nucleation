# ARG-tmpgjuto: Keller's teachers case does not instance the claim that a party can arrange another's real wrong and use the true accusation against them: in Keller the teachers do no wrong and the publicity the official anticipates is a smear

## The inference

From Keller's case, as A13 §3 relays it, to [CLAIM-tmpciy9q](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpciy9q.md): a party
arranges the conditions under which another commits a real wrong and uses
the true accusation, before an audience it anticipated, to justify further
control.

## Critical questions (form: source)

- **Is the source correctly cited?** Partly. The example is Keller's
  ("Fiduciary Duties and Moral Blackmail", p. 24, read from the author's
  preprint). A's "Administrators" is Keller's single "government official".
- **Does the source say what it is cited for?** No. Keller's official
  expects the teachers' "sense of obligation to their students" to make
  striking "very difficult", and "if they do go on strike, then in the
  publicity war that follows I will have some extra ammunition". Keller
  says "The teachers have the right to strike". The charge the official
  would press is not true, and the act it would attach to is not a wrong.
- **Is the source's case of the claim's kind?** Only in its last move. It
  shows a party exploiting an audience's anticipated judgement of another's
  moral position. That is nearer [CLAIM-619](../claims.d/CLAIM-619.md)'s inversion of a party's
  standing than to the claim's pattern of a real wrong, arranged.

## Verdict

The inference fails. The claim may still be true, and its other grounds
and cases are untouched. The body's paragraph citing Keller should be
revised to drop the example or to mark it as a contrast case.
