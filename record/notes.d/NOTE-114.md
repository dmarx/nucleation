# NOTE-114: SEP — Biological individuals

## Contribution

The entry starts from the question of what biological individuals are and shows how it quickly runs into puzzling cases. It argues for laying out the conceptual space around the question, using the distinction between evolutionary and physiological individuals, and considers groups such as superorganisms and species that have been proposed as individuals. It links individuality to life, reproduction and agency, and closes with recent work on how evolutionary individuals are created and regulated and on the evolution of individuality.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2: puzzle cases — the "humungous fungus" (a genetically uniform Armillaria spread over a large area) and coral reefs.
- §3: three subsidiary questions — Beyond Organisms (microbialism, eliminativism, holobionts), Distinctions (evolutionary vs physiological, after Pradeu), Conceptual Space (from minimal to radical pluralism).
- §4: following Pepper & Herron, biological individuals are organisms, some parts of organisms (placentas, plasmids) and some groups (colonies, lineages), and the categories can overlap (e.g. Buchnera within its host).
- §6: physiological individuals as living agents (metabolism, autopoiesis, Moreno & Mossio's autonomy), reproduction and life cycles, autonomous agency.
- §7 (Fig. 5): nine regions from crossing organism / Darwinian individual / living agent — genes are Darwinian non-agents, honey-bee colonies are Darwinian but agents only metaphorically, organelles such as lysosomes are neither.
- §§8–9: regulation of evolutionary individuals (Queller & Strassmann's "organismality", Clarke) and the evolution of individuality through major transitions.
