# LIT-tmppp40q: Морфология сказки

Владимир Пропп [Vladimir Propp] (1928), *Морфология сказки* [Morphology of
the Folktale], Leningrad: Academia (Вопросы поэтики XII), 151 pp. English:
trans. Laurence Scott, Indiana University, 1958; 2nd ed., University of
Texas Press, 1968 — https://lccn.loc.gov/75204576

## Key takeaways

- **The unit.** In "the tsar gives the hero an eagle, which carries him to
  another kingdom" and "the old man gives Sučenko a horse, which carries
  him to another kingdom", names and attributes vary and actions do not.
  A function is "an act of a character, defined from the point of view of
  its significance for the course of the action"; it is defined
  independently of who performs it and of how, and the same act can be two
  different functions according to its consequences (money given and then
  spent on a magic cat is not money given as a final reward).
- **Four theses** (ch. II): functions are the constant elements of the
  tale; their number is limited; their sequence is always the same; all
  wondertales are of one type in structure.
- **The functions** (ch. III): after an initial situation, 31 functions
  run from absentation, interdiction and violation, through villainy (A)
  or lack (a), mediation, departure, the donor's test, the hero's reaction
  and receipt of a magical agent, guidance, struggle and victory (or
  difficult task and solution), liquidation of the misfortune, return,
  pursuit and rescue, to recognition, exposure of the false hero,
  transfiguration, punishment and wedding. Many come in pairs; A or a is
  the one function every tale has.
- **Seven roles** (ch. VI): functions group into spheres of action of the
  villain, donor, helper, princess (sought-for person) and her father,
  dispatcher, hero and false hero. A sphere can match one character, one
  character can fill several, or one sphere can be split among several.
  Living beings, objects and qualities are morphologically equivalent as
  helpers.
- **The whole tale** (ch. IX): a tale is a move from villainy or lack to
  its resolution; texts can hold several moves. Struggle–victory and
  difficult task–solution almost never share a move (41 and 33 of 100
  tales, together three times); when both occur in a two-move tale, the
  struggle comes first. All the tales fit one scheme with these two as
  alternative branches. Propp admits inversions and some displacements,
  and treats larger departures as humorous decomposition.
- **What follows.** Plot and variant cannot be distinguished; all
  wondertales form one chain of variants, derivable, he conjectures, from
  the tale of the dragon's abduction of a princess. The teller is bound
  in the order of functions and in linked variants, free in omission,
  choice of realisation, naming and language; tales can be generated
  artificially from the scheme. The wondertale's uniformity suggests, as
  a hypothesis for historians, that it is "in its morphological basis a
  myth".
