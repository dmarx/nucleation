# NOTE-1823: Wilie, Cahyawijaya, Ishii, He and Fung — Belief Revision: The Adaptability of Large Language Models Reasoning

## Contribution

- The delta reasoning (∆R) framework: evaluate a model's inference, then its response to an
  added premise, so revision is measured against the model's own prior belief.
- A diagnostic dataset with separate "update" and "maintain" subsets, and the BREU score
  weighting both equally, so that a model cannot score well by always revising or never
  revising.

## Key results

1. Abstract: "LMs generally struggle to appropriately revise their beliefs in response to
   new information. Further, models adept at updating often underperformed in scenarios
   without necessary updates, highlighting a critical trade-off."
2. §1 (Tweety): on learning Tweety is a penguin "we retract the conclusion but not the other
   premises" — the target behaviour is withdrawing a conclusion while keeping its premises.
3. §3.1: the operation must leave beliefs non-contradictory, and new information is either
   incorporated "as long as it does not conflict, thereby justifying the maintenance of the
   reasoner’s prior beliefs" or, if it conflicts, prior conclusions are retracted.
4. Results: "most of the non-API based models perform almost 0% in BU-Acc, indicating their
   inability on performing belief revision."
5. Results: larger LLMs do better "but their performances are still very limited, achieving
   at most ∼50% on BREU."
6. Results: "We discover a trade-off between BU-Acc and BM-Acc: models performing well on
   one subset typically faltered on the other".
7. Discussion: "Better prompting method does not help on belief revision"; plan-and-solve
   improves BREU by about 1%.
8. Discussion: with mental-state effects LLMs "demonstrate tendency towards maintaining
   their beliefs" rather than updating.
9. Future work: integrate LMs "with deterministic symbolic solvers".

## Assumptions

- Ground truth for whether a new premise defeats the prior conclusion is fixed by majority
  vote of human annotators; the "right" answer for update items is a modal "may or may not"
  conclusion.
- "Belief" is used in the dialogue-systems sense: what the system currently treats as true
  in context, not a stored state.
- Revision is single-step and within one prompt context.

## Concepts

belief revision; defeasible inference; suppression task (Byrne 1989); delta reasoning (∆R);
belief update (BU) vs belief maintain (BM); BREU; modus ponens / modus tollens; additional
vs alternative premises.

## Bearing on the record

[CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md)'s note of 2026-11-12 says A39 cited Belief-R for "models that update
readily may perform worse when no update is needed". **Accurate**: result 1 and result 6
say this (the paper's "models adept at updating often underperformed in scenarios without
necessary updates"). A's wording is a fair paraphrase; one should quote the paper,
not A, if the note is upgraded.

- **[CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md)** (Proposed; corrigible resolve vs entrenchment): *supports the design point*.
  Belief-R's paired update/maintain subsets with an equally weighted score are a working
  instance of scoring "when to revise" without rewarding always-revise or never-revise,
  which is what [CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md) asks of its diagnostic ("without building the answer into the
  diagnostic or the score"). Its trade-off finding (result 6) is evidence that readiness to
  revise and resistance to irrelevant change come apart in current models. It is
  single-step, so it does not reach [CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md)'s long-term commitment.
- **[CLAIM-664](../claims.d/CLAIM-664.md)** (Proposed) and **[CLAIM-293](../claims.d/CLAIM-293.md)** (Active): *leaves open*. Belief-R's
  items are logical (additional premise defeats a conditional), the setting where a
  structural rule would suffice; it does not bear on weighted evidence.
- **[THEORY-347](../theory.d/THEORY-347.md)** / **[NOTE-866](NOTE-866.md)** (belief bases, Hansson): result 2 (retract conclusion, keep
  premises) is the base-revision behaviour those entries describe.
- **[LIT-2284](../literature.d/LIT-2284.md)** (Deferred; tool evidence and belief revision) and **[LIT-2329](../literature.d/LIT-2329.md)** (Li et al.,
  read in this batch: LLMs revise theories more but not in proportion to prior error) are
  its neighbours; together they suggest that "how much a model revises" and whether it
  revises when it should are different measures.
