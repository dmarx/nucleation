## 3. Central theses (CLAIMs), by the manuscript's sections

Each entry gives status, warrant and role, then its `rests_on` premises, then a verbatim quotation. "Member" means a member of the work `governance-of-possibility` under ADR-tmpsfjxu.

### 3a. §1–§2: possibility is governed, and fixed-dynamics constraint cannot enlarge it

- **CLAIM-582** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; member). "Under a fixed dynamical model an added hard constraint cannot enlarge the set of admissible trajectories, so it cannot enlarge what some admissible path reaches or lower the least cost of reaching it; what it can improve is a property required of every path a disturbance or a given process may take, such as robustness or reliability, and a constraint that does more has changed the mechanism." Its summary gives A's form: "If K' ⊆ K, then Traj(K') ⊆ Traj(K)".
- **CLAIM-tmpk4bou** (Active since 2026-11-23; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-582, CLAIM-347 and CLAIM-361; member). "A regulation that only adds hard constraints to a fixed dynamical model can only shrink the compatibility complex of the public demands, so it cannot relieve their frustration; a regulation relieves frustration among the original demands only by changing the dynamics, through the technologies, infrastructure or access it brings about". A33 §8 calls this "developmental regulation". The defeater is a showing "that the attainable histories of an ecosystem under a regime are not the histories of the unregulated model that its constraints admit."
- **CLAIM-tmptfpf7** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-tmpk4bou, CLAIM-tmpx9nrj and CLAIM-410; member). "Regulation governs possibility: it shapes which actions, relationships, capabilities and forms of coexistence are admissible, attainable, sustainable and revisable, so it is generative … stabilizing … and reflexive …, and there is no choice between governed and ungoverned possibility, only between organizations of it". It is defeated if it "only restates CLAIM-tmpk4bou …, CLAIM-tmpx9nrj … and CLAIM-410 … with nothing added by the generative, stabilizing and reflexive distinction."
- **CLAIM-410** (Active; interpretive; thesis). Removing a constraint "does not move it along one scale of more or less governance: it changes a profile of who holds governing positions".

### 3b. §2.1: the compatibility complex

- **CLAIM-347** (Active; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-368; member). "the jointly satisfiable sets of demands form a downward-closed complex whose minimal non-faces are the minimal conflicts, relief is strict enlargement of that complex for the same declared demands, reorganizations that relieve different conflicts are incomparable, and dropping or suppressing a demand is not relief". Its body: "𝒦(𝒪; D) = {S ⊆ D : ℛ(𝒪) ∩ ⋂_{i∈S} A_i ≠ ∅}", and with horizon and budget, "F_struct(𝒪, S; H, B) iff ℛ(𝒪; H, B) ∩ ⋂_{i∈S} A_i = ∅". A37, quoted in it: "two reorganizations may relieve different conflicts, with neither compatibility complex containing the other. They would be incomparable rather than ranked by a universal success metric." Also from its body: "merely failing to realize a jointly attainable set of requirements establishes neither structural frustration nor operative thwarting."
- **CLAIM-361** (Active; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-347, CLAIM-284 and CLAIM-287; member). "a demand excised or suppressed has changed the comparison and has not been satisfied, an endogenous revision of what the organization maintains is a transformation of it and not relief".
- **CLAIM-586** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-347; not a member). The budgeted complex "grows with the budget and never exceeds the complex with no budget". A missing face is therefore either "frustrated only relative to b, short of resources" or structurally frustrated.
- **CLAIM-368** (Active; interpretive; thesis). "The primitive for an organization's regulatory difficulty is frustration, not distress".

### 3c. §2.1: existence, reachability and reliability

- **CLAIM-567** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-571; **not a member**: it is in `cost-structure-of-possibility`). "That a target configuration is feasible, that it is reachable from the start under the admissible transformations, and that the process reliably reaches it are three properties, each implied by the next and none implying it". Its summary: "It inserts reachability between the structural infeasibility and realized violation that CLAIM-357 separates."
- **CLAIM-560** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-586 and CLAIM-571; member). "The resource-indexed compatibility filtration and the resource-indexed reachability filtration of one architecture both grow with the budget and neither determines the other". Demonstrated on A's three-state example, where the endpoints need 1 and the intermediate needs 3: "on [1, 3) both endpoints are feasible and neither reaches the other; at 3 they become mutually reachable and the complex does not change."
- **CLAIM-589** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-345 and CLAIM-542; member). "permitted directions, actuated directions and transitions achievable at acceptable cost are three objects". A: "A permitted motion is not necessarily a controllable motion, and a controllable motion is not necessarily inexpensive, reliable, or sustainable."
- **CLAIM-tmpx9nrj** (Active since 2026-11-23; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-347; member). "Possibility and realization are separate dimensions of a regulatory regime … a regime can improve either without the other". A33: "a technology-enabling regulation may expand the compatibility complex without causing firms to adopt the newly available solutions."
- **CLAIM-357** (Active; interpretive; thesis). The residual and regret "separate structural infeasibility, realized violation and minimax suboptimality"; "aggregate performance does not tell infeasibility from realized violation".
- **CLAIM-583** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-347). "compatibility equivalence does not imply equivalence of reachability".
- **CLAIM-591** (Active; demonstrated). "the least budget for going from one configuration to another can differ from that for coming back".

### 3d. §6: demands with deadlines, and readiness curves

- **CLAIM-tmpdvgrf** (Active since 2026-11-23; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-347 and CLAIM-tmpk4bou; **considered and excluded from the work**: "the static against temporal complex is not stated"). "When demands are predicates on histories with deadlines, the sets of them an organization can jointly meet under its response architecture and budget form a downward-closed complex contained in the complex of the same demands without deadlines, so two demands can be compatible statically and incompatible in time". A change of response architecture ("faster actuation, different sequencing, staged capacity or a new method") "can enlarge the temporal complex without weakening any demand". A44's factory example: it can cut emissions and keep production but "lack the capacity to complete the conversion within the regulatory deadline while meeting uninterrupted supply requirements". A44, quoted in the claim, adds that this is "one possible mechanism, not a guarantee of monotone expansion".
- **CLAIM-tmphjm0q** (Proposed; interpretive; **counter**; rests on CLAIM-347 and CLAIM-tmpx9nrj; member). "There is no optimal regulation until the demand specification, the instruments, the horizon and the trade-offs among demands are declared, and a regime is better judged by a profile of possibility, realization, resilience, accountability and cost than by a scalar". A33: "Optimization can proceed once those normative and operational commitments are declared, without pretending that mathematics selected the commitments."
- **CLAIM-tmph25dl** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-478, CLAIM-758 and CLAIM-025; member; §5, §6, §15). "a constraint's adequacy depends on the timescales of the demands it governs and on how fast it can be revised".

### 3e. §8: stability is not viability, and keeping a regime is not governing among regimes

- **CLAIM-079** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; member). "Stability does not entail viability: a system can occupy a stable regime while losing access to trajectories that keep satisfying its constraints". Grounds: THEORY-134 and THEORY-132.
- **CLAIM-099** (Active; interpretive; thesis; member). "Maintaining a regime of effective action and maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes are not the same achievement". ARG-007 concludes it from CLAIM-079 and CLAIM-025. Its note of 2026-11-22 reads TERM-tmpz28l1 as its formalization: "A system can maintain x_t and g_t while c_t degrades." It adds: "This is a parallel and a formalization … the claim's standing is unchanged."
- **CLAIM-025** (Active; interpretive; thesis). "An operative regime, the governing organization among regimes, and the capacity to transform that organization can persist or fail separately".
- **CLAIM-tmpcag0t** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-tmpbzwmr and CLAIM-099; member; §1, §15). Governance "is partly the governance of its temporal response structure … an appropriate architecture preserves the capacity to reshape that spectrum". It is defeated if it "only restates CLAIM-tmpbzwmr and TERM-tmpz28l1".

### 3f. §9: thresholds, budget rules, and reserve against redesign

- **CLAIM-571** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; member). "the resource threshold for reaching a target configuration need not equal the resource requirement of the target: it is the least, over admissible transition paths, of the peak requirement along the path, and it is infinite when no admissible path joins the two". Its scope is "one capacity under one fixed architecture". Grounded by CASE-233.
- **CLAIM-569** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-571; member). "A form has at least three resource requirements, to construct it, to maintain it, and to move into or out of it, and none fixes another … having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it".
- **CLAIM-577** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-571; member). "A budget set each period to the peak resource spent the period before never rises, so the configurations an organization can reach shrink monotonically, and any transition whose threshold exceeds the cost of holding the current configuration is lost after one period spent holding it, unless the budget is raised from outside by at least the difference". Its summary: "It is CLAIM-079's stability without viability, produced by a budget rule. Demonstrated for the pure rule; real budget rules have floors, growth and discretion that weaken it." Its note of 2026-11-22 records A36's three remedies: "reserves, carry-over, and a cheaper transition architecture. Each acts through a different mechanism."
- **CLAIM-679** (Active; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-571; member). Cutting toward the holding cost "removes exactly the transitions whose threshold lies between the old holding and the new". A20: "A society can become more efficient at reproducing its current organization while becoming less capable of transforming that organization." Its note of 2026-11-23: "only a redesign whose threshold is at most the holding cost does" replace a reserve.
- **CLAIM-tmpf71gk** (Proposed; interpretive; thesis; rests on CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-571; member; filed from A48 §9.2–9.4). "Maintaining a reserve and redesigning a transition are distinct routes to readiness: one keeps enough capacity to cross an existing transition barrier, the other lowers the barrier itself". Its own Standing section: "the two are distinct in what they change but not alternatives."
- **CLAIM-tmpg8yl2** (Proposed; demonstrated; **counter**; rests on CLAIM-577, CLAIM-679 and CLAIM-571; a gap fill, so **not a member** under ADR-tmpsfjxu). "Under a budget set each period to the last period's peak use, a redesigned transition is lost like the original unless its threshold is at most the cost of holding the present configuration, so redesign lowers the reserve a transition needs by the fall in its threshold and replaces the reserve only where it leaves no reconfiguration slack". The body: "The table's 'accessible transition capacity 2' for Regime III is a budget held at 2: a reserve of 2 − r(A) = 1 … Otherwise reserve and redesign are complements, not alternatives." It is checked by scratch/formal/redesign_reserve_check.py. Under "What it does not say": "a drill each period at the transition's peak, keeps the route funded by the rule itself, which is a third mechanism."
- **CLAIM-878** (Proposed; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-577; grounds CASE-315). "a capability stays funded exactly as long as every period contains a mobilization at its cost, whether a use, a drill, a false alarm or a cancelled dispatch".
- **CLAIM-921** (Proposed; demonstrated; thesis). For rules with a floor, the loss returns "whenever the base budget alone sustains competence below that level", and "maintenance funded apart from use, at a level that sustains the threshold, has no such state".
- **CLAIM-579** (Active; interpretive; thesis). "an organization can hold ample slack and still be pinned, when none of it can be brought to bear at the bottleneck of the transition it needs".
- **CLAIM-587** (Active; demonstrated; thesis). "the value of preserving one depends on whether the others remain deployable, so individual resource utilization need not track the organization's response capability".
- **CLAIM-632** (Active since 2026-11-20; demonstrated; thesis; rests on CLAIM-587 and CLAIM-558). Records kept per capacity "bound a reserve's readiness … and identify it only when the capacities' dependence is known".
- **CLAIM-558** (Active; interpretive; thesis). "a capability kept for rare events is maintained by practice outside operation, not by operation".
- **CLAIM-561** (Active; demonstrated; thesis). "a cancellation rate alone therefore cannot identify waste".
- **CLAIM-414** (Active; demonstrated; thesis). "Matched ordinary trajectories can conceal differences … a specified intervention exposes the difference that passive observation of the matched trajectories does not". QUESTION-395 cites it.
- **CLAIM-517** (Active; demonstrated; thesis). "fidelity measured in ordinary operation certifies nothing about viability".

### 3g. §10, kept nearby: failures with distinct remedies

## 4. Cases: what was built or run, and what it found

- **CASE-233** (Active). CLAIM-571's barrier: with r(q) = 0.7 + 2.4q(1 − q), "both ends cost 0.7, every path between them passes 1.3 at q = 1/2, and at a budget of 1.0 the feasible set falls into two components".
- **CASE-198** (Proposed). "An architecture-mediated transition: moving between two configurations by first switching to a staging architecture under which the path is cheaper, moving, then switching back, lowers the peak resource need from 1.3 to 1.0". Summary: "The bottleneck of a fixed architecture is not the bottleneck once changing the architecture is a move." This is the record's prior instance of A48's Regime III.
- **CASE-200** (Proposed). "A transition that a budget reaches only by carry-over: a per-period budget of 1.0 holds either configuration at 0.7, the transition needs 1.3, and rolling over the 0.3 left unspent reaches it, while a rule that cuts or expires unspent funds forecloses it". It is the curation entry's Regime II carry-over.
- **CASE-315** (Proposed), LEAD-080's run. It ran A's readiness simulator under spend-matching and under carry-over, 300 runs per configuration over ten years, with "CLAIM-577's capacity of 0.7 to hold and 1.3 with a squad out". Under spend-matching "the squad is lost for good in every one of 300 runs, by a median of month 2 to 8, when it is sent only on confirmation or reports are few". It is lost "in 20 % of runs within nine years when it is sent provisionally to every one of six reports a month". In the other arms: "drilling every month keeps it in every run. Rolling over the unspent part keeps it in every run; rolling over 60 % of it loses it as fast as spend-matching." CASE-200's rule "never loses it for good but, used every month, leaves it reachable in half the months." Caveat: "The model is the assistant's and its rates are invented."
- **CASE-343** (Proposed), LEAD-036's run. Under a floor-plus-use budget, a capability used rarely is lost "in every run or nearly", and "none when maintenance is funded apart from activity".
- **CASE-210** (Proposed). "Two departments perform no extrications during ordinary operation, but only one can satisfy a deadline-constrained extrication demand under intervention".
- **CASE-213** (Active). Provisional dispatch and cancellation, which grounds CLAIM-561.

## 7. The digester's own reading: how this region bears on the manuscript

*This section is the digester's reading, not the record's.*

**What §§1–2 can rest on (Active).** The formal spine of §§1–2 is Active and mostly demonstrated:
- CLAIM-582 shows that a constraint added at fixed dynamics cannot enlarge reach.
- CLAIM-tmpk4bou carries this to the compatibility complex, so relief comes only by changing the dynamics.
- CLAIM-347 supplies the complex, relief as strict enlargement, and incomparability.
- CLAIM-361 holds that deleting a demand is not relief.
- CLAIM-586 holds that a budget enlarges the complex only up to the unbudgeted one.

**What is missing.**
1. **A criterion for how much reserve is warranted.** QUESTION-395 is Open. The break-even parameter (a contingency's probability times its loss against the reserve's cost) is a note, not a claim, and LEAD-tmpnblsf, which would sweep it, is at stage idea.
2. **Any organizational or industrial case.** SYNTHESIS-031 and SYNTHESIS-024 both disclaim application to real organizations. CLAIM-579 says no case in the record shows slack present in total and absent at the bottleneck. CASE-315's rates are invented, and its organization is a rescue squad, not an industry.
3. **Cumulative and multi-resource costs.** The §9 model uses only peak capacity. CLAIM-646 (Proposed) shows that peak thresholds do not fix cumulative costs. CLAIM-585 (Active) shows that several resources give a non-convex frontier "no threshold on a weighted total" describes. A one-resource toy therefore cannot carry §9.5's "multidimensional temporal response profile" without them.
4. **The specification-revision arm of §9.6.** Only CLAIM-361 is held, as a distinction, and LEAD-tmpnblsf would test it. Nothing in this region models a demand family that changes, which §8's 𝔇 presupposes.
5. **Whether a redesign creates or cheapens a capability.** QUESTION-392 is Open, and this is exactly the question Regime III raises.
