# NOTE-1179: Airoldi, Blei, Fienberg and Xing — Mixed membership stochastic blockmodels

## Contribution

It combines the stochastic blockmodel, in which each node belongs to one
block and edges depend on the pair of blocks, with mixed membership, in
which each node has a vector of memberships. The combination keeps
relational dependence by drawing, for each interaction, a block for each
party from its own membership vector. It adds a sparsity parameter
separating rare contact from block structure, a nested variational EM
algorithm whose memory is NK + 2K scalars per cycle, and applications to a
monastery, a school friendship network and a yeast protein-interaction
network.

## Key insight

A node does not have a role. It has a distribution over roles, and in each
interaction it takes one: "the group membership of each node is context
dependent. That is, each node may assume different membership when
interacting to or being interacted by different peers." Marginally, the
probability of an edge from p to q is π_pᵀBπ_q, a bilinear form in the two
nodes' memberships with the block matrix in the middle. That makes the
model a latent-space model with simplex-valued positions and an arbitrary
interaction matrix, which is how §5 relates it to Hoff, Raftery and
Handcock's models.

## Assumptions

- A directed binary graph on N nodes; Y(p, q) ∈ {0, 1} for ordered pairs.
- **Memberships.** π_p ~ Dirichlet(α) on the K-simplex, K fixed.
- **Per-interaction roles.** For each ordered pair, z_{p→q} ~ Mult(π_p) and
  z_{p←q} ~ Mult(π_q), independently across pairs.
- **Edges.** Y(p, q) ~ Bernoulli((1 − ρ) z_{p→q}ᵀ B z_{p←q}), with B a K × K
  matrix of rates in [0, 1] and ρ ∈ [0, 1] the sparsity parameter (§2.1).
- Given the memberships, edges are independent across pairs and across
  replicated graphs (Appendix A).
- **Estimation.** Empirical Bayes for (α, B) by variational EM on a
  mean-field family: a Dirichlet for each π_p and a multinomial for each
  z. K by held-out likelihood on large networks and an approximate BIC on
  small ones.
- No dynamics, no covariates, no interventions.

## Key results

- **Marginal edge probability.** Summing over the two indicators gives
  P(Y(p, q) = 1 | π_p, π_q) = (1 − ρ) Σ_{g,h} π_p(g) B(g, h) π_q(h)
  (Appendix A's mixture form, with Bernoulli f). Re-derived and checked
  numerically.
- **Variational updates (§3, Appendix B).** φ_{p→q,g} ∝ exp(E[log π_{p,g}]
  + Σ_h φ_{p←q,h} f(Y(p, q), B(g, h))), and symmetrically; γ̂_{p,k} =
  α_k + Σ φ_{p→q,k} + Σ φ_{p←q,k}; B̂(g, h) = Σ Y φφ / ((1 − ρ) Σ φφ); α by
  Newton–Raphson. The nested schedule iterates the pair-level updates to
  convergence before partially updating γ and B. The naïve schedule
  "often converged only after many iterations".
- **Sparsity estimator.** ρ̂ = Σ_{p,q} (1 − Y(p, q))(Σ_{g,h} φφ) / Σ_{p,q}
  Σ_{g,h} φφ. Since each φ vector sums to one, Σ_{g,h} φ_{p→q,g}φ_{p←q,h} = 1
  for every pair, so ρ̂ as printed is the fraction of absent edges,
  whatever the φ. Footnote 9 says it equals the density-based ρ̃ "in the
  case of single membership". As printed, the two are always equal.
  Checked numerically.
- **Monks (§2.3).** Sampson's 18 novices. Approximate BIC selects three
  groups, the number Sampson identified, with the Young Turks, Loyal
  Opposition and Outcasts at the simplex's corners and John Bosco and
  Gregory between them. "Amand's uncertain affiliation, however, is not
  captured."
- **Simulations (§4.1).** Graphs of 100, 300 and 600 nodes from MMSB with 4,
  10 and 20 blocks and α = 0.05, 0.1, 0.25. Reordering rows by most likely
  membership "accurately reveals the original blockmodel" (Fig. 6, by
  eye). The nested schedule reached the same likelihood faster than the
  naïve one on 100 nodes and 4 blocks over 26 paired runs (Fig. 7 left).
  In one 300-node example, five-fold held-out likelihood peaked at the
  true K = 10 (Fig. 7 right).
- **School friendship (§4.2).** Add Health, 69 students in grades 7–12.
  BIC suggests K = 6; α̂ = 0.0487, ρ̂ = 0.936, a nearly diagonal B. "Few
  students display mixed membership." Assigning each student to the grade
  of their highest posterior membership, MMSB matches 63 of 69, against 57
  for a mixture blockmodel and 37 for a latent space cluster model
  (Table 1).
- **Yeast proteins (§4.3).** 871 proteins in MIPS's hand-curated
  interactions, each with about 2.4 of 15 top-level MIPS functions.
  - With K = 15, blocks were mapped to functions greedily on 87 proteins,
    memberships thresholded at the 95th percentile, and predicted
    annotations on the other 90% scored: "about 87%" accuracy, against
    "about 70%" for random guessing at the right proportions.
  - With K = 50, chosen by held-out likelihood among K from 2 to 255,
    predicted interactions thresholded at ten levels have higher
    functional precision by gene ontology co-annotation than the MIPS
    collection itself at low recall (Fig. 13, Table 3).
- **The identifiability claim (§5).** "The model decouples the observed
  connectivity patterns into two sources of variability, B, Πs, that are
  apparently in competition for explaining the data, possibly raising an
  identifiability issue. This is not the case, however, as the blockmodel
  B captures global/asymmetric relations, while the mixed membership
  vectors Πs capture local/symmetric relations. This difference practically
  eliminates the issue, unless there is no signal in the data to begin
  with." No argument beyond this is given.
- **A limitation the authors name (§5).** The model "does not readily
  generate hubs" or "networks with skewed degree distributions".

## Concepts

- **Mixed membership vector π_p**: "the expected proportion of times node n
  instantiates the connectivity pattern of group k" (Fig. 1's caption).
- **Interaction-specific membership z_{p→q}**: the single block p takes in
  its interaction with q.
- **Summarizing and de-noising** (§2.2): predicting edges from (B, Π), or
  re-weighting observed edges with (B, Z).
- **Sparsity parameter ρ**: the share of non-interactions attributed to
  "limited opportunities of contact" rather than to blocks.

## Connections

It extends the stochastic blockmodel (Holland and Leinhardt; Wang and
Wong; Snijders and Nowicki) and its Dirichlet-process form (Kemp et al.;
Xu et al.), and the mixed-membership models of Erosheva and of Pritchard,
Stephens and Donnelly, of which LDA is one. It compares itself with
latent space models (Hoff, Raftery and Handcock 2002; Handcock et al.
2007), whose edge probability uses π_pᵀIπ_q with Gaussian positions. A
nonparametric version would need memberships from a hierarchical
Dirichlet process, which the authors propose and do not build. hLDA's
paper ([LIT-1542](../literature.d/LIT-1542.md)) cites it among flat mixed-membership models of
non-text data.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), condition 4.** The LIT says a mixed-membership
  blockmodel of the communication and authority graph is the baseline
  inferred structure must beat. The reading supports that and sharpens
  it. MMSB is a static model of one graph's edges, independent given
  memberships, with no dynamics and no interventions, so it cannot itself
  predict "the effect of changing an authorization pathway". As a baseline
  it has to be extended to the outcome that is to be predicted. And it
  "does not readily generate hubs", which an authorization graph with
  managers has, so a fitted MMSB would misdescribe the graph it is meant
  to summarize.
- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), condition 2 (overlap).** MMSB's overlap is
  proportional: π_p sums to one, so a node's roles compete for its
  interactions. T19's participation without that constraint is a feature
  allocation's kind of overlap, not this one. MMSB's per-interaction role,
  one block per partner, is closer than any of the batch's models to the
  idea of a member taking different perspectives in different
  interactions.
- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), "without their number being fixed".** K is chosen by
  BIC or held-out likelihood, not inferred. In the one simulated example
  the held-out peak was at the true K.
- **[CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md) and [CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md).** The paper's one passage on identifiability
  asserts that B and the memberships do not compete. Given the graph they
  do, along a continuum of changes of block basis, and the sparsity factor
  is not identified at all apart from B's scale ([THEORY-993](../theory.d/THEORY-993.md)). That is
  [CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md)'s gauge in bilinear form, and it puts the paper's readings of
  blocks (factions, grades, protein functions) at [CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md)'s first
  achievement plus whatever the Dirichlet family adds. The PPI mapping
  "estimate[s] a permutation" of blocks to functions, which is exactly the
  part of the gauge the paper admits.
- **[CLAIM-427](../claims.d/CLAIM-427.md).** §4.3.2 is the one place in the batch where inferred
  memberships are checked against labels the model never saw, the MIPS
  annotations. That is the kind of external check [CLAIM-427](../claims.d/CLAIM-427.md) asks for, done
  weakly ([THEORY-1008](../theory.d/THEORY-1008.md), Proposed).
- **[CASE-166](../cases.d/CASE-166.md).** Its analyzer found modes with the number of factors
  fixed. MMSB would find blocks of the interaction graph with K chosen by
  criterion. Neither separates a block of members who interact from a
  block of members who respond alike to a common driver, unless the
  common driver also makes them link. Condition 3 of [QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md)
  is untouched by this paper.
- **[CLAIM-454](../claims.d/CLAIM-454.md).** A block is a pattern of who links to whom. It is
  relational, unlike a feature allocation, so it holds some of what the
  claim says an allocation leaves open (access). It holds no uptake or
  authority. The paper's readings of blocks as factions and functions are
  by correspondence with external labels.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **The identifiability claim is unsupported**, and false for the
  likelihood given the memberships ([THEORY-993](../theory.d/THEORY-993.md)). Whether the
  empirical-Bayes (α, B) is identified in the marginal likelihood rests
  on the Dirichlet family of the memberships, which the paper does not
  examine.
- **ρ is confounded with B's scale.** The edge probability depends on ρ and
  B only through (1 − ρ)B, so ρ is fixed only by the bound B ≤ 1, and the
  printed ρ̂ is the fraction of zeros regardless of the fit.
- **The PPI baseline is weak.** With about 2.4 of 15 functions per protein,
  predicting no function at all would score about 84% if accuracy is per
  protein–function entry, which the paper does not define. Against that,
  "about 87%" is a small gain. Random guessing at the right proportions
  scores about 0.16² + 0.84² ≈ 73%, close to the paper's "about 70%", so
  the per-entry reading is likely. The record's arithmetic, not the
  paper's.
- **Simulations judged by eye.** No recovery error is reported for π or B.
- **No hubs or skewed degrees.**
- **Variational approximation without a check** against MCMC on these
  data; MCMC is called impractical.
- **The arXiv copy was not compared.**

## Open questions

- Under what conditions on B and the membership distribution are B and Π
  identified up to permutation? Results on identifiability of mixed
  membership and latent-structure models (Allman, Matias and Rhodes,
  named in [CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md), not held) may apply; the paper does not ask.
- Does a dynamic MMSB, fitted to an organization's interactions over time,
  predict the effects of interventions on the graph better than the
  graph's own history? That would make it the baseline [QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md)
  needs.
- How does the per-interaction role compare with a feature allocation of
  the same members, on the same data?
