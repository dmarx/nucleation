# CASE-tmpj5tw0: "You blow it all on sex and booze", said by a friend, a parent, a judge, a comedian

## The case

A12: "The same proposition spoken by a friend, a judge, a comedian, a teacher,
or a stranger is not the same communicative event." A18 §6: "imagine it spoken
by a close friend at breakfast, a disapproving parent, a judge during
sentencing, or a comedian on stage. The locutionary content may be essentially
identical. But the *utterance event* is not." Hence "Sentence meaning ≠
Utterance meaning ≠ Communicative effect". A24 made it a transformation of
context, (u, s, a, k, g) → (u, s′, a, k, g): keep the words, change the footing.

## What it can show

The necessity half of [CLAIM-tmphg89g](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmphg89g.md): the same words, and so the same proposition, do not
fix the act. It is the premise of the compensation hypothesis ([CLAIM-tmpqgtmh](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpqgtmh.md)) and of
the quantum move ([ARG-tmp475gh](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp475gh.md)). Invented, so it shows a possibility; whether readers'
judgements shift as described is what [CASE-tmpn2q0k](CASE-tmpn2q0k.md) would test.
