The owner asked for an account of the Yoneda lemma, connected if possible to
ontic structural realism (OSR). Three works were filed and read in full, all
`Active`:

| work | why |
| --- | --- |
| nLab, *Yoneda lemma* ([LIT-221](../../record/literature.d/LIT-221.md)) | the lemma stated and proved, with the embedding as fully faithful and the corollary y(c) ≅ y(d) ⇔ c ≅ d, in a self-contained unit that can be read whole |
| Bain 2013, *Category-theoretic structure and radical ontic structural realism* ([LIT-223](../../record/literature.d/LIT-223.md)) | the main category-theoretic argument for *radical* (eliminative) OSR |
| Lam & Wüthrich 2015, *No categorial support for radical ontic structural realism* ([LIT-222](../../record/literature.d/LIT-222.md)) | the reply. It `corrects` Bain in the record's relation graph. |

## The finding: no source in the core literature names Yoneda

Neither Bain nor Lam & Wüthrich mentions the Yoneda lemma, representable
functors or universal properties. Lal & Teh (arXiv:1404.3049), searched in
full text, does not either. Eva 2016 (EJPS) could not be obtained from a
legitimate free source.

- **Bain stops one step short.** He quotes Awodey on objects "determined …
  by their position in a structure", and defines products by probing them
  from outside, but never takes the step to the lemma.
- **Lam & Wüthrich use two of its consequences without the name.**
  Generalised elements X → A exist in every category, and morphisms "only
  distinguish isomorphism classes of structures".

Two works that do name the lemma were found and **not filed**:

- **Adlam 2025 (arXiv:2507.20313).** One sentence: Yoneda "implies that
  objects may emerge from the 'morphisms'". The source it cites, Yokura 2017,
  was read in full and says nothing of the kind.
- **Hendren, Grasso, Ellia & Tononi 2026 (arXiv:2608.24928).** A box calls
  Yoneda OSR's "mathematical centerpiece". That is an assertion, and the paper
  is about qualia, not physics.

## What the lemma does and does not support

The lemma says an object is determined **up to isomorphism** by its hom-functor,
its relations to every other object. That supports **objects as positions in a
structure**: the non-eliminative OSR of Ladyman 2001 ([LIT-217](../../record/literature.d/LIT-217.md)), and Lam &
Wüthrich's "balanced" OSR. It does **not** support radical OSR, for three
reasons:

1. **The determination presupposes the category's objects.** The hom-functor
   is indexed by them, and the conclusion is an isomorphism between two of
   them.
2. **It needs the composition structure, not merely which hom-sets exist.**
   The nLab page's own counterexample has two non-isomorphic objects whose
   hom-sets are in bijection at every object.
3. **It fixes objects only up to isomorphism.** That is the categorical twin
   of the limit on weak discernibility in Leitgeb & Ladyman ([LIT-154](../../record/literature.d/LIT-154.md)).

So Bain's inference to eliminating objects is overstated. His reliance on
elements as arrows out of a terminal object also fails once generalised
elements are admitted.

## Errors the readings found in the works

- **nLab:** its finite counterexample breaks the identity law as written, and
  its HoTT proof has small errors. It gives only the presheaf form.
  Nat(Hom(A,−),F) ≅ F(A) follows by passing to C^op.
- **Bain:** treats sections of a sheaf of Einstein algebras as maximal ideals,
  and mis-defines a maximal ideal.
- **Both papers:** say Hilb has no products. Direct sums are products.
- **Lam & Wüthrich:** mis-define a topos, and say non-commutative algebras
  have no maximal ideals.
