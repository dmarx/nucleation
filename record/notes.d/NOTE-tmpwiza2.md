# NOTE-tmpwiza2: Jaksland — Naturalized, fundamental, and feminist metaphysics all at once

## Contribution

It identifies the argument that has to hold if Barad's quantum-based
metaphysics is to bear on social and feminist theorizing at all, which
Barad "only ever alludes to" (p. 375): that some features of an ontology
cannot appear or disappear under approximation. It names the conception
of agential realism Barad holds (naturalized metaphysics with an
unrestricted scope) against the two she rejects (analogy, template). And
it shows that the division between fundamental metaphysics and the
metaphysics of the lifeworld, which Ladyman and Ross, Barnes and Mikkola
present as principled, is at most contingent.

## Key insight

The question is not whether quantum effects are large at human scales;
Barad agrees they are not (p. 374). It is whether an ontological feature
can be present only approximately. "If an ontology lacks/contains features
that cannot appear/disappear as the result of approximation then these
features will be absent/present throughout the ontology regardless of
scale" (p. 376). If the subject–object dualism is such a feature and is
absent at the fundamental level, it is absent everywhere, and metaphysics
of the lifeworld must take account of fundamental metaphysics, and the
converse: evidence about the lifeworld would bear on fundamental
metaphysics (p. 378). If no feature is like that, a fundamentally
relational world can approximate a world of individuals, and the
lifeworld "just happen[s] to be autonomous enough from the details of
fundamental metaphysics to be practically independent" (p. 365).

## Assumptions

- **Agential realism is assumed, not assessed, as an interpretation of
  quantum mechanics** (p. 365). Its assessment is Faye and Jaksland's
  (LIT-tmpstodb; note 6, p. 379).
- **One reality, no ontological stratification** (pp. 372–373, 376): the
  macroscopic is "derived from or grounded in the quantum world", which is
  Barad's premise and the paper's for the argument.
- **The quantum-to-classical transition occurs** (note 26, p. 380); how it
  occurs (decoherence) is set aside.
- **"Levels" talk** is kept for the argument, though Ladyman and Ross
  object to it (note 12, p. 380); micro and macro here mark typical length
  scale only.

## Key results

### The problem (introduction, pp. 363–365)

Mainstream metaphysics, naturalized metaphysics included, privileges the
fundamental; feminist metaphysics studies gender and social structure,
which are not fundamental (Barnes 2014; Mikkola 2017). Earlier
reconciliations were permissive: let metaphysics include both (Mikkola,
Bennett, Sider, Schaffer, Hawley, Passinsky). Barad's is different: she
denies the division, by arguing that fundamental metaphysics already bears
on feminist themes. "Barad's agential realism is fundamental, naturalized,
and feminist metaphysics all at once" (p. 365).

### Agential realism (pp. 365–368)

- **Radical relational holism.** "Objects emerge from the whole, what
  Barad (e.g., 2003, 815) denotes 'phenomena'", which "admits multiple
  separations into configurations of objects that have properties only
  relative to the whole" (p. 366). From Barad 2003, 815: "phenomena are
  ontologically primitive relations—relations without preexisting relata"
  (p. 367). It is radical "since only the relational phenomena are
  ultimately real" (p. 367), and note 9 likens it to Schaffer's priority
  monism.
- **The argument from complementarity.** Bohr: complementary properties
  are meaningful only relative to an experimental setup that measures them,
  so the property belongs to the phenomenon, object and setup together
  (pp. 366–367).
- **The agential cut.** "Barad (2007, 140) describes such a separation
  between object and apparatus as an 'agential cut' (more commonly known as
  a Heisenberg cut) and argues that no agential cut is an inherent
  distinction" (p. 367); note 8: it "is thereby different from a Cartesian
  cut where the separation between subject and object is fixed".
- **Meaning and exclusion.** Intra-actions make boundaries and properties
  determinate and concepts meaningful (Barad 2003, 815), so word–world
  dualism goes. Barad 2010, 253: an arrangement that gives determinate
  meaning to "position" "will, by necessity, always produce its
  constitutive exclusion (for example, 'momentum')" (pp. 367–368). The
  exclusion is what makes intra-action "agential", and agency is "cut
  loose from its traditional humanist orbit" (Barad 2007, 177; p. 368).
- **Against OSR.** Both treat individuals as non-fundamental, but OSR
  "focuses on the intrinsic relationality in states of entangled particles
  … that is, on intra-level structures", agential realism on "the
  inseparability between observer and observed, that is, inter-level
  relationality" (p. 368). That shift of emphasis, Jaksland argues, is what
  carries agential realism into philosophical anthropology.

### Feminist and naturalized (pp. 368–373)

- Ladyman and Ross's "truce" with philosophical anthropology ("go in
  peace" to Heideggerians; p. 368) is the division Barad breaks.
- Barad is a moderate naturalist: metaphysics "inspired and constrained" by
  science, with "a distinctive task for philosophers" (p. 371), and with
  science itself held accountable (Barad 2007, 407).
- **Three conceptions of agential realism** (pp. 372–373). (1) *Analogy*:
  quantum concepts used metaphorically outside physics. (2) *Template*:
  "an overarching metaphysical template that is claimed to be instantiated
  by quantum mechanics and which may also be instantiated in other
  domains", "a bowl admitting many different kinds of content", with its
  concepts "content-neutral generalizations" (p. 372). (3) *Naturalized
  metaphysics*: constrained and justified by science "irrespective of the
  context in which this metaphysics is applied". **Barad rejects (1) and
  (2)**: "I am not interested in drawing analogies between particles and
  people" (2007, 24), and the template would be "an illegitimate
  stratification of ontology into separate realms": "the universe is not
  broken up into two separate domains" (2007, 85). Note 21: agential
  realism "is only deployed and applied once, as an account of fundamental
  reality. It thus never leaves physics, but rather extends its scope from
  there"; Hollin et al. 2017 read it as a template.
- Pinch's objection, that Barad situates science but not the physics she
  relies on (p. 373), and Barad's reply by complementarity and
  "experimental meta/physics"; not decided here.

### Metaphysical approximation (pp. 373–377)

- **The challenge.** Newtonian mechanics is a very good approximation
  macroscopically and lacks entanglement, so a quantum metaphysics seems
  irrelevant at lifeworld scales (p. 374).
- **First modification: contingency.** It is "the (apparently) contingent
  size of Planck's constant" that makes quantum metaphysics irrelevant to
  the lifeworld (p. 374). Had it been larger, the autonomy of philosophical
  anthropology and naturalized metaphysics "would have been lost in both
  directions" (p. 374). So the distinction Ladyman and Ross draw "cannot"
  be principled (p. 375).
- **Barad's position.** h/m being non-zero "is the key point"; good
  Newtonian approximation "is not evidence against the new epistemology or
  ontology" (Barad 2007, 416; p. 375). Jaksland reconstructs the argument
  she alludes to: dualisms "are features of an ontology that are either
  there or not there. They cannot differ in magnitude" (p. 375). Adding
  particles to an electron does not reinstate the subject–object split, and
  "Even if the entanglement between agency of observation and object is
  negligible for some practical purpose in the macroscopic domain, the
  entanglement is nevertheless still there" (p. 376).
- **The general form** (p. 376): scale-independent, all-or-nothing features
  of an ontology; candidate examples the dualisms, representationalism and
  "(joint carving) notions". This is "the second and more significant
  challenge to the truce".
- **Individualism is the other way.** "Whether a metaphysics of
  individualism holds could similarly be regarded as a question of all or
  nothing. Either there are individuals, or there are not. Regardless, it
  seems immediately more agreeable to regard this as something that can
  obtain through approximation. Circumstances can be such that a
  fundamentally relational metaphysics can appear to be one of individuals
  (if they are separable enough)" (p. 376). The subject–object dualism or
  representationalism might also obtain approximately, "though the way they
  do so remains more elusive".
- **A no-miracles argument for approximation**: the success of Newtonian
  mechanics would be a miracle if Newtonian metaphysics were not even
  approximately true (pp. 376–377). Denying it "without further
  specification … simply begs the question", and Barad's response is
  unknown since her argument is implicit.

### Consequences (pp. 377–379)

- Barad's contribution is methodological: feminist metaphysics need not
  defend a place beside fundamental metaphysics, since it is fundamental
  metaphysics (p. 377).
- Mutuality runs both ways: "If careful analysis of our lifeworld for
  instance indicates that there is no subject-object dualism, then this
  would, by Barad's argument, be evidence that this dualism is absent at all
  levels of description" (p. 378).
- The method is "experimental entanglement" (Fitzgerald and Callard) and
  demands "a rather extraordinary degree of attentiveness and
  responsibility to the totality of inquiry" (p. 378).
- It cuts both ways: if Bohmian mechanics were vindicated, an ontology of
  individuals with irreducible dispositions would be what flows to social
  theory, perhaps McKitrick's dispositional account of gender (p. 378). The
  method "does not leave us the choice to later recompartmentalize inquiry
  in response to any unwelcome implications" (p. 379).

## Concepts

- **Metaphysical approximation** — a metaphysics (e.g. Newtonian
  individualism) obtaining approximately in a domain where the theory it
  is tied to approximates the more fundamental one.
- **Scale-independent (all-or-nothing) feature** — a feature of an
  ontology that no parameter controls, so it cannot appear or disappear
  between levels (p. 376).
- **Template conception** — agential realism as a content-neutral
  structure instantiated by quantum mechanics and possibly by other
  domains (p. 372); rejected by Barad.
- **Agential cut** — Barad's term for the separation of object and
  apparatus, "more commonly known as a Heisenberg cut", not inherent and
  enacted by specific intra-actions (p. 367); unlike a Cartesian cut, not
  fixed (note 8).
- **Radical relational holism** — Teller's relational holism (inherent
  relations not supervening on the relata's non-relational properties),
  made radical because only the relational phenomena are ultimately real
  (p. 367).

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpl228b compares luria to Barad's apparatus on the conception
  Barad rejects.** The claim says luria is an apparatus "in Barad's sense"
  for a record's objects, taking the notion out of physics and applying it
  to a governed record. That is Jaksland's template conception (p. 372),
  the reading he attributes to Hollin et al. (note 21), and Barad rejects it
  as "an illegitimate stratification of ontology into separate realms". On
  Barad's own conception luria would be an apparatus without any
  comparison, since agential realism applies to all of reality; but that
  holds only if agential realism is correct as quantum mechanics, which
  Faye and Jaksland dispute. So the claim's comparison is the template
  reading and should say so. Its "What it does not say" had it as "the
  exchange's account of her"; the reading shows the account is of a use

  unrestricted" (U6 of 2026-10-27) is an unrestricted template, not a claim

## Limitations

- The approximation argument is reconstructed, by the author's own
  account, from allusions; Barad's response to the no-miracles argument is
  unknown (p. 377).
- No account of what metaphysical approximation is beyond the Newtonian
  analogy; the paper does not say what it would be for an ontological
  feature to be approximately present.
- Agential realism's quantum credentials are assumed throughout, so the
  paper's conclusion is conditional on what Faye and Jaksland contest.

## Open questions

- What is an account of approximation for ontological claims, as there is
  one for theories? (QUESTION-tmpg3qms.)
- Are the dualisms in fact scale-independent? The paper's examples are
  candidates only.
