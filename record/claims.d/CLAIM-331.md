# CLAIM-331: A level of organization has a temporal facet as well as a compositional one: higher-level variables are characteristically slower and longer-lived than the activity that produces them, though slowness is a mark of a level and not its definition

## The claim

The owner, at U8: "I think another important dimension here is
timescale. so like, I'd include that as a facet of 'level of
organization', so 'higher level' doesn't just mean in terms of
abstractions over abstractions, we should also expect 'higher level'
effects to be on slower timescales than 'lower level' effects."

A8 agreed and put it as: "A higher-level organization is not merely a
coarser description of lower-level components. It may be a dynamically
distinguishable organization operating on a characteristic timescale
different from those of its constituents." Its fandom illustration runs
from individual expressions (seconds) through collective responses,
shared attitudes and norms, and cultural reproduction to historical
identity (decades or more), with the slow layers conditioning the fast
ones: "fast interactions reproduce slow variables, and slow variables
condition subsequent fast interactions."

The record already holds the mechanism for the ordinary case. In a nearly
decomposable system ([LIT-905](../literature.d/LIT-905.md), read), strong coupling inside blocks
and weak coupling between them give fast within-block relaxation and
slow aggregate dynamics, which is what [CLAIM-157](CLAIM-157.md)'s block test uses.
What this claim adds is that the timescale is part of what a level is,
not only a consequence of how it was found: the program in
[CASE-102](../cases.d/CASE-102.md) and the rivalry in [CASE-098](../cases.d/CASE-098.md) are levels because their
variables outlast the rosters and supporters that carry them.

## What it does not say

- **Not that higher means slower in every case.** A8: "An economic market
  can undergo a rapid crash while individual participants' preferences
  and habits remain relatively stable. An entire stadium can enter a
  synchronized chant in seconds." Haken's order parameters are slow and
  can still change suddenly near an instability ([LIT-923](../literature.d/LIT-923.md), Unavailable).
  A8 listed four facets that can come apart: compositional scale,
  temporal scale, causal integration and explanatory autonomy. "A quickly
  assembled protest crowd may acquire collective efficacy within minutes.
  A fandom may persist for a century without ever acquiring comparable
  unified agency."
- **Not that a slow variable is a level.** A9 §4: "a constant external
  label can also have a long timescale." What makes a slow variable part
  of the collective's dynamics is [CLAIM-327](CLAIM-327.md)'s question.
- **Not that the slow state and the slow mechanism coincide.** A8: "A
  variable's persistence is not, by itself, evidence of the persistence
  of the organization that generates it."

## Note of 2026-10-18: governance timescales, and two cases of slowness without governing

The exchange of 2026-10-17 used this claim three times without citing it,
and twice confirmed its own limit.

- **Applied to volition.** A20 §4 drew a hierarchy of "effective
  volition" by timescale: momentary action ("Seconds–minutes"), a
  governance regime of habits, plans and control allocation
  ("Days–months"), and organizational continuity ("Months–years"), with
  the caption "Schematic levels, not a demonstrated RG hierarchy". That is
  this claim's temporal facet applied to will-organization.
- **The slower process need not govern.** At U37 the owner pointed to the
  two-timescale update rule of the GAN literature (Heusel et al. 2017,
  [ANTH-LIT-611](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-611.md), held in the anthology for FID; [CASE-041](../cases.d/CASE-041.md)). A37: "the slower
  process in TTUR is the generator, not a higher-order will-controller.
  Slowness alone supplies no authority or motivational hierarchy." And: "A
  slowly moving generator parameter ordinarily does not, merely by being
  slow." That is this claim's "slowness is a mark of a level and not its
  definition", in a case the record had not used.
- **A slow variable no member reads.** Arm A of [CASE-116](../cases.d/CASE-116.md) (U3–U4): a
  register that flips with probability 0.01 per step, second eigenvalue
  0.98, while the members are resampled and never read it. Strong
  timescale separation, no level. It is the construction behind "Not that
  a slow variable is a level" above.

## Note of 2026-10-19: three sources of slowness

A4 of the exchange of 2026-10-19 on coarse-to-fine processes read this
claim against the physics of model reduction and separated three situations
that "timescale hierarchy" groups:

1. **Weakly coupled subsystems.** Slow collective modes follow from
   near-decomposability. That is [THEORY-661](../theory.d/THEORY-661.md), the mechanism this claim cites.
2. **Metastable systems.** Slow transitions run between long-lived
   configurations ([QUESTION-079](../questions.d/QUESTION-079.md), [THEORY-571](../theory.d/THEORY-571.md)).
3. **Turbulent systems.** "Scale-dependent turnover times coexist with
   persistent transfer across scales", with no clean slow–fast split (Eyink
   2005, [LIT-1252](../literature.d/LIT-1252.md), unread).

A4: "CLAIM-331 already avoids defining levels by slowness alone. The
expansion could make that qualification much more precise. It might also
reveal whether its phrase *characteristically slower* is actually justified
outside a restricted class of systems." That is a test of scope, not
evidence against the claim. The third situation is the one this claim's
`defeated_if` would need: variables another criterion individuates as
higher-level, changing as fast as their constituents over ordinary
dynamics. Nobody has yet shown that a cascade has such variables. A5 added
that memory may carry a level's persistence where no instantaneous
variable is slow ([QUESTION-222](../questions.d/QUESTION-222.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-20: graded slowness, and weak coupling without it

Two readings of the coarse-to-fine program bear on the facet this claim
names. Neither defeats it.

- **In turbulence the ordering is graded and set by the cut.** Parish and
  Duraisamy ([NOTE-973](../notes.d/NOTE-973.md)) fit the memory length of the unresolved scales
  at about a fifth of the fastest resolved time scale. It shortens as the
  Reynolds number rises and lengthens as the cutoff is coarsened
  ([THEORY-747](../theory.d/THEORY-747.md), Proposed). The unresolved scales are faster, so the
  ordering this claim expects holds. But it holds by a ratio that moves
  with where the analyst cuts, not across a gap. That is the third
  situation of the note of 2026-10-19, now with numbers.
- **Weak coupling is not slowness.** On Shpak, Stadler, Wagner and
  Altenberg's flat landscape ([NOTE-977](../notes.d/NOTE-977.md)), coupling between blocks is
  entrywise weak at every mutation rate. Yet the block's internal gap and
  the global slow rate stay in a ratio near 0.94, so the aggregate is not
  slower than the activity inside the blocks. Simon's mechanism needs a
  spectral gap, not just small off-block entries. This is a limit on the
  mechanism the claim cites ([THEORY-661](../theory.d/THEORY-661.md)), not on the claim, which already
  says slowness is a mark of a level and not its definition.
- **Slow by construction is not evidence.** Wiskott and Sejnowski's slow
  feature analysis ([NOTE-971](../notes.d/NOTE-971.md), [THEORY-746](../theory.d/THEORY-746.md)) builds a hierarchy
  whose higher stages are slower because the objective requires it, and
  only weakly. On test data the slowness curves across layers cross. The
  slowest outputs include location, which is not an invariant, and nothing
  reads them back, as in [CASE-116](../cases.d/CASE-116.md) arm A. Hierarchies optimized for slowness
  should not be cited as cases of this claim. The probabilistic readings
  of slow feature analysis put slowness in the prior: a random walk in
  Schüler and Wiskott ([NOTE-1015](../notes.d/NOTE-1015.md)), an ordered decay in Turner and
  Sahani as that paper reports it. So they are not evidence either.
