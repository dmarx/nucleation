# CLAIM-971: A centralized self-model matched to an accurate one in parameter count and reproducing the system's behaviour exactly can still systematically misattribute control, so matched capacity and equal behaviour do not force the single owner a unified self-model presents to have a truthmaker in the true distribution of control

## The claim

[CASE-376](../cases.d/CASE-376.md) builds and runs [CASE-114](../cases.d/CASE-114.md): a distributed-control plant
with two self-models, one accurate about the division of labour and one that
attributes control to a single centralized owner. The two are matched in
parameter count, given identical inputs, and both reconstruct the aggregate
outcome exactly (reconstruction error 0 and ~1e-15). On these terms the
centralized self-model still names the wrong component as the cause of an error
about four times in five (localization 0.21 against the accurate model's 0.94,
at chance), spreads attribution toward the owner's global command, and, when
its blame drives governance, strips more legitimate gain from innocent
components to cut the same errors (0.46 against 0.34, on every configuration).

The upshot for the record's argument is narrow and exact. Drayson's objection
([THEORY-646](../theory.d/THEORY-646.md)), as [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md) states it, is that the owner a unified
self-model presents may always have its truthmaker in the organization's own
distribution of control, so that such a self-model never misdescribes who
controls what. The construction exhibits a counterinstance: a unified
(centralized) self-model, matched in capacity to an accurate one and
reproducing behaviour exactly, whose single owner has no truthmaker in the true
distribution of control. So matched capacity and equal behaviour do not, by
themselves, secure the owner a truthmaker. The misdescription is real and sits
in the attribution, which is where [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md) places it.

This corroborates [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md) without its resting on the construction: it is the
case [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md)'s note of 2026-11-06 says the record lacked.

## What it does not say

- It does not say a distributed agent would develop a centralized self-model.
  The modeller writes both self-models ([CLAIM-288](CLAIM-288.md)); the claim is about what
  the centralized content does once present.
- It does not settle Drayson's objection in general — only that the objection's
  universal form (the owner *always* has a truthmaker) is false, shown by one
  matched-capacity construction. Whether a represented owner has a truthmaker in
  a *given* real system is left open.
- It bears on attribution of control, not on phenomenal properties, which
  [CASE-114](../cases.d/CASE-114.md) and [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md) both hold apart.
