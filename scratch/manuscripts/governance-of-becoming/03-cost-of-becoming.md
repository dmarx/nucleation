# Article 3. The Cost of Becoming

### Stored work, contractual lifecycles, and the resource-bounded geometry of possibility

**Abstract.** An organization that has stabilized some of its relations has done work that it need not do again, and it pays to keep that work, to find and check it when it is used, and to change it. This article sets out what the Nucleation record holds about that trade, and with what standing. The owner's thesis, which the record holds as Active, is that a stabilized constraint can be stored work: by replacing later work with cheaper operations, it can move a task within a fixed per-task budget, so that amortization becomes an expansion of capability, with no new degrees of freedom or resources added. The record's demonstrated results then separate what forming, keeping and moving into an organizational form cost; show that the budget needed to reach a configuration is the least peak along a path, and not the cost of holding the configuration; and show that going and coming back can differ. So the affordability of a state does not establish the affordability of reaching it. A cost model run on three cases prices stored work with one set of terms, at illustrative values. It is Proposed, and it holds the store fixed between uses. The article then asks what changes when the store is a contract whose use can change it. The record's answer is a Proposed claim with a test that has not been run, and ChatGPT, whose claim it is, expects the test to come out null. The article separates work saved from work transferred, and keeps four predicates on a transition apart: whether it is physically feasible, affordable, authorized and permissible. The actor-indexed reach that the motivating draft makes the article's formal centre is defined nowhere on the record. It is an open lead, and this article does not use it as a result.

## 1. Organization as stored work

### 1.1 The owner's thesis

The article begins with the owner. At U17 of the exchange "Topological protection explained" (2026-11-18), the owner read ChatGPT's sentence "Selective contraction can expand capabilities when the stabilization of some relationships reduces the resources needed to realize valuable alternatives" as "an excellent formalization of something else Nucleation has tried to develop: a theory of how constraint can relate to amortization of work, and the tradeoff between memory (amortizing a value) and computation (rederiving it just in time)". ChatGPT then stated the thesis (A39 §10), and the record holds it in ChatGPT's wording as the owner's thesis:

> "A stabilized constraint can be stored work: by replacing later work with cheaper operations it changes which activities are realizable within a fixed per-task budget, so amortization becomes capability expansion without any added degrees of freedom or resources; the expansion is conditional on the costs of constructing, keeping, retrieving, verifying and revising the constraint and on the distribution of later demands, and the persistence that buys it can entrench" (CLAIM-761, Active, interpretive).

The record already held the thesis's parts before U17, and three of them are the owner's own:

- **The owner's conjecture.** After reading Hutchins on the cockpit, the owner wrote that "amortized retrieval is actually a kind of amortized computation" (QUESTION-095, Open, 2026-10-15). The question asks whether leaving a result where it can be cheaply retrieved is the same trade as amortizing a computation.
- **The owner's stored inquiry.** "a stabilized specification is past inquiry stored so that later questions become cheaper to ask, and it can make other questions harder" (CLAIM-475, Active, interpretive; the owner's, U17 of the exchange of 2026-10-27).
- **The owner's guard.** "persistence is not amortization, which is shown only when earlier work replaces later computation, communication or recruitment" (CLAIM-443, Active, interpretive; the owner's, 2026-10-23).

The fourth part is ChatGPT's, written at the owner's request for "content to the effect of computation-storage tradeoffs". It states the accounting: "Filing an argument moves work across time: it is paid for now … against reconstruction later, so it is worth its cost only when expected reuse covers it" (CLAIM-303, Active, interpretive). The claim is about the record's own filing. A dated note on it of 2026-11-24 flags a later citation of it for automation as a miscitation, since "this claim is about filing an argument in the record, not about automation" (CLAIM-303). This article cites it only for its accounting. CLAIM-761's summary says what it added: "No claim joined constraint, amortization and capability".

### 1.2 From amortization to capability

CLAIM-303 prices stored work by totals, so stored work is worth having when reuse covers its cost. CLAIM-761's step is different. A question costs 12 units to formulate and investigate from scratch, and 3 with an acquired framework. Under a per-task budget of 5, the first is out of reach and the second is within it. "This is the point where amortization becomes capability expansion" (CLAIM-761, after A39 §1). The stored work moves the task across the budget line "whether or not its construction has yet been repaid". The claim adds: "That is CLAIM-571's point that a threshold is a peak, not a total" (CLAIM-761). Reading a per-task ceiling as a peak in this way is therefore the claim's own step, and the article restates it (J3.1, a restatement of CLAIM-761).

The claim keeps two failures apart, and the article keeps them apart too.

- **An operational failure.** The stored work does not lower the least cost of the task, retrieval and verification included, enough to change what is feasible under the budget. That failure defeats the claim.
- **An economic failure.** The lifetime savings never repay construction and upkeep. That failure "defeats only the stronger reading that the investment was worthwhile, which the claim does not make" (CLAIM-761, defeat condition).

The claim's own example makes the point: "A procedure that costs 100 to acquire and cuts a task from 12 to 3 makes the task feasible under a budget of 5 whether or not the 100 is ever repaid" (ChatGPT's example, at its second review; CLAIM-761).

Two guards limit the thesis.

- **CLAIM-582.** "Under a fixed dynamical model an added hard constraint cannot enlarge the set of admissible trajectories, so it cannot enlarge what some admissible path reaches or lower the least cost of reaching it" (CLAIM-582, Active, demonstrated). A stabilized constraint that lowers a cost has therefore "changed the mechanism: it supplies cheaper operations (a procedure, an index, a framework) and not only a restriction" (CLAIM-761). The thesis is about constraints of that kind. It is not a claim that restriction as such enables.
- **CLAIM-443.** The owner's guard says that a constraint amortizes "only when keeping and using it costs less than re-solving" (CLAIM-761, reading CLAIM-443).

### 1.3 What the thesis does not support

CLAIM-761 names the reading it "invites and does not support": "That every emergent organization is such an investment". ChatGPT's closing sentence at A39 stated that reading, and the owner asked at U18 to develop it. "Some persistent structure saves no work" (CLAIM-761, citing CLAIM-443 and CASE-116's unread slow register). Nor is the expansion shown to be an expansion of freedom. Whether "newly affordable operations widen substantive freedom, and for whom, is CLAIM-352's question" (CLAIM-761; CLAIM-352, Active, interpretive, the owner's).

The record also separates amortization from slowness. "Dynamical separation of time scales … and economic separation (costly formation, cheap repeated use) are different and come apart both ways: a slow variable can save no work, and a result reused cheaply need not be an attractor of any dynamics" (CLAIM-755, Proposed, demonstrated; ChatGPT's, A39 §3). CLAIM-475's summary had identified the two, and its note of 2026-11-19 corrects that identification (SYNTHESIS-106). This article does not read "stored" as "slow".

### 1.4 Instances: accommodation, authorization, and the account

Two entries apply the thesis's logic to coordination and to permission.

**The owner's local accommodation.** "Learned local accommodation can reduce the amortized cost of recurrent arbitration: where a recurring conflict among an agent's processes admits a stable accommodation, settling it among the processes can cost less over repeated encounters than escalating each instance to a higher-level process" (CLAIM-358, Proposed, interpretive; the owner's, U45 of the exchange of 2026-10-17, in ChatGPT's conditional form). Its sharp edge: "it does not say local settlement is always cheaper". ChatGPT later counted a learned coordination structure as "A fourth kind of stored work", one that stores "neither an answer nor an index" (QUESTION-095, note, A39 §2, after CLAIM-358 and CLAIM-443). Reading CLAIM-358 as stored work is therefore a restatement (J3.11, of QUESTION-095's note). It is an amortization in CLAIM-303's sense, a lower total over repeated encounters. It is not one in CLAIM-761's sense: CLAIM-358 names no per-task budget that the accommodation lets a conflict fit within.

**Authorization paid once.** "Authorization granted question by question puts its cost between formulating a question and investigating it, on the same budget, while a standing grant pays it once for a domain before any question in it is formulated" (CLAIM-1016, Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's gap fill; not a member). The claim says it reads the standing grant "as stored work (CLAIM-761) amortizes a cost over the tasks it serves". Section 7 returns to it.

**The account.** The record's account of this region is SYNTHESIS-106 (Proposed), "Stored work and the cost structure of possibility". Its hinge is "the thesis the owner endorsed at U17 (CLAIM-761)", and it says of itself that it "maps the components and adds nothing". Its "Not stated on the record" section keeps one thing open that this article must not close. CLAIM-761 says what stored work does to a budget, but "no entry says that cheap retrieval and amortized computation are the same trade". The owner's conjecture, QUESTION-095, is still Open. The account was written on 2026-11-19, before the cost model of §2 and before the contract entries of §3, and it integrates neither. It also gives CLAIM-761 and CLAIM-760 as Proposed, though both have been Active since 2026-11-20. No account maps the cost model as it now stands (G3.1).

## 2. The general cost model

### 2.1 Three requirements of a form

"A form has at least three resource requirements, to construct it, to maintain it, and to move into or out of it, and none fixes another: a capability can be cheap to keep and costly to acquire, so having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it" (CLAIM-569, Active, demonstrated). The three requirements are ChatGPT's proposal, and the construction that demonstrates them is the filer's. An organization has a budget of 10 a year and spends 7 on what it already does, which leaves a surplus of 3.

- **Capability X** costs 24 to build and 2 a year to keep. X can be sustained, since its upkeep fits the surplus. It cannot be acquired within any one year, since its construction exceeds both the surplus and the whole budget.
- **Capability Y** costs 6 to build and 4 a year to keep. Y can be acquired from two years' surplus. It cannot be sustained, since its upkeep exceeds the surplus.

So "Construction and maintenance rank in opposite orders", and "Moving into a form is not building it": converting from a form whose staff carry over might cost 14, not 24 (CLAIM-569).

The claim's scope needs care, because the motivating draft reads more into it.

- It says "at least three", and it does not say "That these are the only three". Leaving a form "has costs of its own; A counts them with entry" (CLAIM-569, "What it does not say").
- It says that none of the requirements fixes another. It does not say that they move in opposite directions. A note of 2026-11-19 records ChatGPT's addition that "the same investment can lower the first while raising the second" ("An organization can be inexpensive to operate while expensive to revise", A43 §3.3). The same note records that ChatGPT hedged this "under some workloads" and called it "Not a universal inverse relationship". The note concludes: "That fits this claim, which says no requirement fixes another, not that they move oppositely" (CLAIM-569).

### 2.2 One model, three cases

The record's cost model is the result of LEAD-108, run by the assistant on 2026-11-22. The model has two routes for meeting a stream of demands: compute each time, or build a store once, keep it and read it back, falling back to computing when the store does not serve or is wrong. The terms were "fixed before any case was priced" (CASE-326, Proposed):

- F, construction, and λ_F, the price of work at the moment it is paid;
- U, upkeep;
- π, the chance that a demand is one the store serves;
- ρ, retrieval, and "miss", the cost of finding out the store does not serve;
- v, verification, and δ, the chance that verification catches a wrong result;
- σ, L and R: staleness, the loss from using a wrong result undetected, and revision when it is caught;
- ε, erosion of the on-demand route;
- spill, the added retrieval cost the store imposes on other demands;
- c, the on-demand route's cost;
- λ and B, the price of work at the moment of demand and the work available then;
- "party", who pays each item.

A served demand on the store route costs

  ρ + v + σ[δ(c(1+ε) + R + σ_A L_A) + (1 − δ)L].

The model reports three verdicts and keeps them apart. **Feasibility** is the chance that a demand is met correctly within B. **Lifetime** is the priced total. **Distribution** is the same totals broken down by party (CASE-326). The claim drawn from the run is the assistant's: "One cost model prices a crew's speed bugs, a filed argument and a LEGO bin with the same terms, once work is priced by the moment it is done and checked against the budget for each demand, not only summed" (CLAIM-891, Proposed, interpretive; not a member). QUESTION-095's note had listed four terms such an account would need. Those four "alone do not" suffice: "the cockpit is carried by the price of work at the moment it is done, which they lack" (CLAIM-891).

The three cases were not sampled. They are the three that QUESTION-114 (Open) named as the test. The run shows that one set of terms prices those three cases. It does not show that the terms suffice in general, and the case says so: "That the terms are complete" is among what it "cannot show" (CASE-326). Its results, at values that are illustrative except where a source gives them:

- **The cockpit's speed bugs.** As the approach's workload rises relative to the moment the bugs are set, "the bugs lose at 1 and 1.5 and win from 2". So the bugs pay by moving work to a quieter moment, more than by reuse (CASE-326, CLAIM-891).
- **A filed argument.** Pricing staleness "turns a saving of 11 into a loss of 1 at three uses" (CASE-326).
- **A LEGO bin.** For one copy of a brick among 200 similar ones, the bin "pays at its first use". For a brick that stands out, "a bin never pays" (CASE-326).

Three results do not depend on the illustrative values (CASE-326, "What it can show"):

- with π = 1 and a flat price of work, the bugs repay their setting only if F < 4(c − ρ − v);
- erosion is weighted by the chance of falling back, (1 − π) + πσδ;
- a construction at a quiet moment and a construction at the moment of demand are "the same term, λ_F F".

The second is stated as a claim: erosion costs, per demand, "εc[(1 − π) + π(σδ + φ)]", where φ is the share of the skill that checking the stored result repeats. So "it weighs little where demand is predictable, the store rarely wrong and checking cheap, as with a cockpit's speed bugs, and most where reuse cannot be forecast or checking re-derives, as with a filed argument" (CLAIM-892, Proposed, demonstrated; the assistant's; not a member).

Two of the model's limits matter for what follows. "ε is a fixed fraction, not a function of how long the store has been relied on" (CASE-326, its choices). And "Anything about the record's own filings" is outside what it can show: its filing numbers are "A11's construction and illustrative values" (ibid.).

### 2.3 The motivating draft's seven expenditures

The motivating draft lists "Construction, invocation, verification, upkeep, remediation, revision and dissolution" and calls them "independently variable expenditures distributed across participants and timescales" (A49, Article 3, abstract; ChatGPT's draft, not the record). The record holds the list, but not under those names and not with the clause "independently variable" (J3.3):

| The draft's term | Where the record holds it |
|---|---|
| construction | F (CASE-326); the first of CLAIM-569's three requirements |
| upkeep | U (CASE-326); CLAIM-569's maintenance |
| invocation | ρ, retrieval (CASE-326). For a contract, only part of the cost of invoking it is retrieval (§3.3) |
| verification | v and δ (CASE-326) |
| remediation | R, "revision when caught" (CASE-326) |
| revision | R; and, as a change of form, CLAIM-569's transition requirement |
| dissolution | no term of its own: "A counts them with entry" (CLAIM-569) |
| erosion of alternatives (the draft's §2) | ε (CASE-326; CLAIM-892) |
| staleness (the draft's §2) | σ (CASE-326) |
| across participants | "party" and the distribution verdict (CASE-326, from CLAIM-760) |
| across timescales | λ_F and λ, the price of work by moment (CLAIM-891) |

The record demonstrates independence for two pairs only. Construction and maintenance can rank in opposite orders (CLAIM-569). Holding a configuration and reaching it can need different budgets (CLAIM-571, §6). That all seven terms vary independently is not stated on the record, and this article does not claim it.

## 3. Contracts as stateful stored work

### 3.1 The owner's question

The owner's question, at U21 of the exchange "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity", came after the owner's implicit contracts at U14 and U16 (Article 2): "let's integrate contract formation, persistence, and modification in our cost model. It might actually already be in there already under other names like ambiguous invocation of relationship" (QUESTION-tmpb7fx7, Open; the owner's). The owner's phrase, "ambiguous invocation of relationship", is the source of the name "relational invocation", which the motivating draft lists first among the programme's contributions without crediting the owner (curation entry of 2026-11-24 20:56, "U31 and A49", attribution).

The filer checked the owner's suspicion and found it half right. "Formation, persistence and modification: held, for any form." But "Ambiguous invocation of a relationship: not held. No entry uses the phrase or a synonym" (QUESTION-tmpb7fx7).

### 3.2 The lifecycle is already priced

The held half is a restatement (J3.2). Read as a stabilized relational constraint, a contract has CLAIM-569's three requirements: "a construction (formation), a maintenance (persistence) and a transition (modification and exit) requirement, none fixing another". "The exchange supports and applies the claim; it adds nothing to it" (CLAIM-569, note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's).

ChatGPT made the same move for inquiry: "A contract can be a materialized answer to a historically recurring question about how processes should interact" (CLAIM-475, note of 2026-11-24, A18 §8). ChatGPT also read CASE-326's terms contractually (A30 §2): F as formation, U as maintenance of expectations, ρ as identifying and invoking the contract, v and δ as checking applicability and compliance, σ as the inadequacy of inherited arrangements, R as renegotiation, and ε as "the loss of independent capability through reliance". The record holds that reading as a formal application (ARG-tmpa5ped, Proposed; the inference is ChatGPT's, the answers to its critical questions the assistant's). CLAIM-303's break-even then reads n > F/(c − ρ − v − u), which is n > 4.8 at A30's values (F = 24, c = 8, ρ + v = 2, u = 1; checked in ARG-tmpa5ped). Where invocations do not change the arrangement, as with "an explicit, externally enforced agreement", "the assumption holds and the model applies as it stands" (ARG-tmpa5ped).

### 3.3 What is not priced: an invocation that changes the store

ChatGPT's point at A30 §7 is the article's hinge from stored work to contracts: "A cached result normally substitutes for a computation without intentionally changing the underlying problem. Contracts can change the problem." The record holds it as a Proposed claim, in ChatGPT's voice:

> "Invoking a relationship differs from reusing a stored result in that the invocation can change the relationship it invokes, so one operation can reproduce, form or transform a contract, and a stored-work cost model that holds the store fixed between uses prices a contract's lifecycle but not its invocation, which returns an act, a distribution of costs over the parties and the relationship's next state" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp, Proposed, interpretive).

The invocation's formal objects are Article 2's: Invoke(q, C_t) → (a_t, C_{t+1}) and the relational kernel K_C(q). What matters here is the cost model. CASE-326's store "is the same before and after a use, and σ and ε enter as fixed parameters" (ARG-tmpa5ped). For a contract, the erosion of the parties' alternatives and the inadequacy of the arrangement "are produced by its invocations". Where they are, "the per-demand cost is path-dependent and the fixed-parameter formula misprices it" (ibid.). CASE-326's own note of 2026-11-24 calls this "A qualification of scope, not of the case: for a store whose use changes it, σ and ε are not fixed."

The two halves fit together. CLAIM-569's note says the exchange adds nothing to the lifecycle. CLAIM-tmpsf9hp says the model misprices invocation. These claims concern different things, and this article claims only what each says: the lifecycle is held, with CLAIM-569 Active; the pricing of invocation is a Proposed claim.

The cost of resolving *which* relationship an act invokes is a further strain. "When an act could invoke more than one relationship, the uncertainty is of three kinds needing different remedies": epistemic ambiguity, "resolved by information"; relational disagreement, "resolved by negotiation or arbitration"; and constitutive indeterminacy, "resolved only by forming one" (CLAIM-tmp80ny9, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's, at the owner's U21). ChatGPT mapped this cost to ρ. The assistant's answer to the critical question finds the mapping loose: "Only the first of CLAIM-tmp80ny9's three kinds is a retrieval or verification cost" (ARG-tmpa5ped). The other two are a negotiation and a formation.

### 3.4 Expectation, and the test that has not been run

The record holds a test, and it has not been run. LEAD-tmp14n26 (Open, stage idea; ChatGPT's wording, the assistant's design) would make the store "a state updated by its own use", C_{t+1} = Φ(C_t, q_t, a_t, f_t), with ε and σ "functions of the history of invocations rather than fixed parameters". It would then:

1. build two relationships matched in per-use cost and output at the start, one whose invocations leave it unchanged and one whose invocations expand its practical jurisdiction and erode the recipient's alternatives;
2. price each over a horizon, per party, including revision and exit at the end;
3. ask whether "time-varying ε, σ and R suffice" or a term of a new kind is needed.

**The expectation is the null.** ChatGPT, whose claim it is, expects it: "the critical test is whether these require genuinely new cost terms, or merely richer state variables and transition mechanisms within the existing model. I suspect the latter will cover most of the cases" (A30 §15, in CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). The lead's outcomes are stated before any result:

- **CLAIM-tmpsf9hp is defeated** "if the two relationships' later costs never part, or part only by an amount the fixed-parameter formula with a re-estimated ε reproduces";
- **the "richer state" reading is confirmed** "if time-varying ε, σ and R suffice";
- **a new term is needed** "if a per-party cost of resolving or contesting the invocation does not reduce to them" (LEAD-tmp14n26).

If the null holds, an invocation that changes the store is CASE-326 with state-dependent parameters, and "relational invocation" names a reparametrization, not a new cost. The claim's own defeat condition anticipates this, citing A32 §21: "relational invocation" may be "only a vocabulary" (CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). Until the lead is run, this section offers a Proposed claim and a design, and no result.

## 4. Distributed accounting

### 4.1 Work saved, transferred, or capability expanded

"That an organization makes an activity cheaper for those who perform it does not show that work was saved: the work may have been transferred, to an earlier time, to upkeep, or to other parties, and the efficiency an organization appears to have depends on the boundary of the system whose costs are counted; work saved, work transferred and valuable capability expanded are three separate achievements" (CLAIM-760, Active, interpretive). The claim is ChatGPT's (A62 §9), filed at the owner's request after the first filing had left it as a note.

The separating case is a claims office. The office's cost per claim falls from 3 to 1. Each claimant now spends 4 putting their case into the office's categories, where before they spent 1. "Counted at the office, the work fell by 2 per claim; counted over office and claimant, it rose from 4 to 5. No one can do anything they could not do before" (CLAIM-760). CLAIM-303's criterion, reuse covering cost, "is met for the office and says nothing of the claimants" (ibid.). The claim names the stronger reading it does not support: "That organizations which transfer work are wrongful" (ibid.).

The cost model carries the boundary as a term. "party" is CASE-326's term for who pays each item, and it comes from CLAIM-760. The run shows the boundary moving a verdict on the record's own filing. Counting only the session's work, the filing breaks even at 1.7 uses. Counting the owner's review at four times a session's price, it breaks even at 3.3 uses, "and three uses lose". "This is CLAIM-760's boundary, applied to the record" (CLAIM-891, "Choices that are the owner's"; CASE-326). These are alternatives that the run prices and that the owner has not chosen between. They are not theses of the owner's.

### 4.2 The draft's four categories

The motivating draft asks the article to distinguish work "eliminated, transferred, deferred and embodied in acquired capacities" (A49, Article 3, §4). Three of the four map onto CLAIM-760 and CLAIM-761:

- work **eliminated** is CLAIM-760's work saved;
- work **transferred** is CLAIM-760's work transferred, "to an earlier time, to upkeep, or to other parties";
- work **embodied in acquired capacities** is CLAIM-760's "valuable capability expanded", which is "CLAIM-761's capability expansion" (CLAIM-760, "Rests on").

**Deferral** is different. Work moved to a *later* time, not done now and owed later, possibly at a higher price, is not among CLAIM-760's movements. CLAIM-760 lists only movement "to an earlier time". Two entries come close. ChatGPT's gloss reads technical debt as "an increase in the cost or decrease in the reliability of organizational transitions" (CLAIM-569, note of 2026-11-07, A32 §7 of "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"). And deferring a reform imposes on each participant a cost ∫₀ᵀ L_i(t) dt, "substantial even if the decision-making institution incurs little immediate expenditure" (CLAIM-760, note of 2026-11-24, A40 §4.4), which the note reads as transfer to other parties. No entry states deferral as a movement of its own (G3.2).

### 4.3 The costs of contesting and of being heard

Three entries locate costs that an accounting at the organization does not see. Each is a cost the organization passes to those it affects.

- **The owner's friction thesis.** "Automation reduces procedural friction, yet procedural friction is where an opportunity to intervene in a process is exposed and perhaps what such an opportunity consists in" (CLAIM-710, Proposed, interpretive; the owner's, U11 of "Assess Depp Heard Abuse", keeping both the owner's "exposes" and the hedged "characterizes"). ChatGPT developed it at the owner's U12: "The cost of imposing a decision and the cost of questioning, suspending or reversing it are separate … friction moves from the exercise of power to its contestation" (CLAIM-731, Proposed, interpretive). CLAIM-760 itself says that "the contesting cost a transfer imposes is CLAIM-731's subject", so reading contestation cost as part of the accounting is a restatement.
- **The legibility tax.** "an institution can steer which questions are pursued by deciding who must pay to make them legible, with no rule against any idea" (CLAIM-1117, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's, A56 §4 of "Explain First Contact RL"; not a member). ## 5. Timescale separation and readiness

### 5.1 Governance as allocation over time

"Organizational governance is partly the intertemporal allocation of scarce resources among inquiry, response and the upkeep of the structures that make both possible, slow organizational modes arise partly as ways of amortizing inquiry and coordination costs, and adaptive governance must keep the capacity to reallocate between answering existing questions and learning to ask and answer new ones" (CLAIM-1060, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's, A52 §5). The claim's summary says its mechanism is already held, by CLAIM-475 and CLAIM-761. Its step to slow modes is ARG-238 (Proposed), "which CLAIM-755 narrows to the economic sense of slow". By §1.3, "slow" here means costly to form and cheap to use. It does not mean slow to relax.

### 5.2 Rarely exercised capabilities

Readiness is where the three requirements of §2.1 part most visibly. The assistant's result from LEAD-036's study (CASE-343, Proposed) is:

> "Where competence decays without funded maintenance and a capability is committed only when competent enough, a budget that follows observed use has a state in which the capability is never used or funded again whenever the base budget alone sustains competence below that level … so at the same expected funding a rarer capability is lost sooner, while maintenance funded apart from use, at a level that sustains the threshold, has no such state" (CLAIM-921, Proposed, demonstrated).

In the study, at the same expected funding, "the coupled rule lost the capability within thirty years in every run at one occasion in two and a half years and in 39 % at four a year, and the decoupled rule never did" (CLAIM-921). The result is demonstrated within the model, under its stated conditions ("whenever the base budget alone sustains competence below that level"). It is not an observation of any organization.

A capability lost this way is regained at its construction cost, not its upkeep: this is CLAIM-569's X, cheap to keep and costly to build. CLAIM-569 already says so in its own words: "That it is costly to rebuild is one reason to keep it; whether the reason is good depends on what it is for" (CLAIM-569, "What it does not say", pointing to QUESTION-395). This is a restatement (J3.8). The readiness account declines a broader form of it, "The three requirements of a form, each lost by its own mechanism", as "a conjunction" (SYNTHESIS-031, "Not stated on the record").

### 5.3 Slack and the opportunity cost of the present

Reconfiguration slack is "the temporary resources a system needs to move between two configurations it can each hold, beyond what holding either one requires, while keeping specified commitments" (TERM-106, Active). Two demonstrated results say what immediate demands do to it.

- **Cutting toward holding.** "cutting the resource an organization holds toward what holding its present configuration requires leaves that configuration feasible and removes exactly the transitions whose threshold lies between the old holding and the new" (CLAIM-679, Active, demonstrated; ChatGPT's sentence, A20 §IV of "Analyze technological civilization trap": "A society can become more efficient at reproducing its current organization while becoming less capable of transforming that organization").
- **The ratchet.** "A budget set each period to the peak resource spent the period before never rises", so any transition costlier than holding "is lost after one period spent holding it, unless the budget is raised from outside by at least the difference" (CLAIM-577, Active, demonstrated; the assistant's). The claim's own limit: "real budget rules have floors, growth and discretion that weaken it".

The companion manuscript `governance-of-possibility` develops these results at length (its §§9–11). This article uses them only to say that the cost of the present can be paid in future reach, and that no output measure records the payment.

## 6. Reachability and transition asymmetry

### 6.1 The threshold is a peak along a path

"For a fixed architecture and one resource that must be held at each moment, the resource threshold for reaching a target configuration need not equal the resource requirement of the target: it is the least, over admissible transition paths, of the peak requirement along the path, and it is infinite when no admissible path joins the two" (CLAIM-571, Active, demonstrated). Formally, B*(A, B) = inf over γ ∈ Γ(A, B) of max_t r(γ(t)). ChatGPT's example is r(q) = 0.7 + 2.4q(1 − q) on [0, 1]. Both endpoints cost 0.7 to hold, and every path between them passes q = ½, where r = 1.3. At a budget of 1.0, each endpoint is feasible and neither is reachable from the other. The gap of 0.6 is the transition's reconfiguration slack (CLAIM-571; TERM-106).

The claim's scope is "one resource that must be held at each moment". For a cumulative expenditure, "a path costs its integral, not its peak" (CLAIM-571, "What it does not say"). The six resource types "bound different functions of a path and are not one budget" (TERM-105, Active).

### 6.2 Feasible, reachable, reliably reached; going and coming back

Two further results separate the geometry.

- **Three properties.** "That a target configuration is feasible, that it is reachable from the start under the admissible transformations, and that the process reliably reaches it are three properties, each implied by the next and none implying it" (CLAIM-567, Active, demonstrated; not a member).
- **Direction.** Connectivity "does not settle reachability among them: under directed moves a state can share a connected component with a target and have no admissible path to it, and the least budget for going from one configuration to another can differ from that for coming back" (CLAIM-591, Active, demonstrated; not a member). In the filer's construction, with r = 1, 1, 3 on u, v, w and moves u → v, v → w, w → u, "The least budget from u to v is 1; from v back to u it is 3, through w" (CLAIM-591). The claim says "can differ". It does not say that they usually do.

The motivating draft's sentence "the affordability of an organizational state does not establish the affordability of reaching it" restates CLAIM-569's title, "having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it", and CLAIM-571 (J3.6, a restatement).

### 6.3 Contractual lock-in without preference

ChatGPT applied the threshold to contracts at A30 §8. Replacing a critical service provider can require keeping two providers at once, rebuilding internal expertise and moving data, so "The new contract would be cheaper to maintain, but the organization cannot afford the transition. This produces contractual lock-in without requiring any preference for the existing arrangement" (CLAIM-571, note of 2026-11-24: "An application that supports the claim's reading; no change"). The same passage adds that "renegotiation readiness might itself need to be maintained as an organizational capacity", which ties this section back to §5.

A30 §8 also says: "A relationship may be easy to enter, inexpensive to maintain and extremely expensive to leave" (in CLAIM-tmpsf9hp). The record attributes this directedness to TERM-116 (Proposed), the relational profiles of a configuration. The incoming profile is "the cost of producing it from each other configuration", and the outgoing profile is "the cost of turning it into each". "a system may be easy to assemble but hard to change once assembled" (TERM-116, ChatGPT's). TERM-116 is not a directed revision cost. "Directed" appears in a note, "with TERM-116 the revision cost is directed" (CLAIM-569, note of 2026-11-24), and no entry defines R(C → C′). Reading contract exit as CLAIM-591's directed threshold is this article's join (J3.5). The record states the asymmetry for contracts through TERM-116's distances, which add along a path. CLAIM-591's thresholds are peaks along a path. The two are different cost semantics, and no entry joins them for contracts.

## 7. Authority-sensitive cost landscapes

### 7.1 Four predicates, held in three places

ChatGPT set four predicates on a transformation apart, "Physically feasible", "Affordable", "Authorized" and "Normatively permissible", which "need not coincide" (A30 §11, in CLAIM-tmpdu9v6). The record holds each of them, but in different places:

| Predicate | Where the record holds it | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| physically feasible | the architecture's admissible processes P_z(q, q′) (TERM-114) | Active |
| affordable | resource-bounded reachability, q ⇝_b q′ ⟺ ∃p ∈ P_z(q, q′) : κ(p) ⪯ b (TERM-114) | Active |
| authorized | Θ, "whose changes of architecture are authorized, as distinct from which are feasible and at what cost": a note on TERM-114 of 2026-11-17, and not in its definition. "whether to add Θ is the owner's call" | a note on an Active term |
| permissible | Perm_N(p), "its permissibility under a declared normative standard" (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6) | Proposed |

No single object holds the four predicates together. Nor does any object index reach by actor. The motivating draft's formal centre, R_i(S; B, H, W) = {S′ : S′ is reachable by i under B, H, W}, is named in the draft and defined nowhere. The record holds it as LEAD-tmpeo8wq (Open, stage idea): "No entry defines reach per actor, or separates the four predicates on a transition in one formal object". The lead's list of what the record holds is the table above (J3.9, a restatement of LEAD-tmpeo8wq, "The gap").

### 7.2 An obligation is a bar, not a price

"A genuine obligation enters a cost model of exit as a condition on which transitions are permissible, not as a cost: withdrawal can be feasible and affordable yet impermissible until alternatives are arranged or the duty is transferred, so a party who controls the circumstances that keep the restriction in place controls the other's exit without raising its price" (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's, A32 §13). ChatGPT puts it plainly: "The last should not simply be converted into a subjective penalty in a utility function."

The claim agrees with a held result. A normatively permitted path set Γ_N ⊆ Γ gives "b*_N ≥ b*", so a normative restriction on the path "can raise this claim's threshold and never lower it, as CLAIM-582 says of any added hard constraint" (CLAIM-571, note of 2026-11-17; the step is the assistant's, and ChatGPT does not state it).

The claim also has limits. It "does not answer QUESTION-472's second half", which asks what separates such control from ordinary reliance on someone's conscientiousness. "Nor does it say that every obligation is a bar rather than a weight: defeasible obligations would need rules of priority" (CLAIM-tmpdu9v6). Neither it nor CLAIM-571's note supplies such rules.

### 7.3 Authorization: a predicate and a cost

CLAIM-1016 (§1.4) prices authorization as "a third cost on the shared budget" of inquiry, beside formulating and answering. The Θ of TERM-114's note treats it as a predicate instead. The lead combines the two readings: a transition is authorized when "i holding or able to obtain the grant W requires" (LEAD-tmpeo8wq, step 2), and the lead cites CLAIM-1016 among what the record holds. Reading authorization as both predicate and cost is therefore the lead's restatement and not this article's (J3.12). The record has not filed whether obtaining a grant is itself a costed transition.

### 7.4 Collective reach and independent reach

The draft asks "when recurrent successful coordination expands collective reach while contracting the independent reach of particular participants" (A49, Article 3). The record holds three things about this.

- **In prose,** the mechanism: "A capability lost because another reliably supplies it is the Black Queen's loss" (CLAIM-603, Proposed, interpretive; the assistant's, on the owner's remark of 2026-11-05 that "assured help makes recipients lose skill" sounds "closely related to niche differentiation and specialization phenomena"). An arrangement "can produce the dependence through which it exploits" (CLAIM-670, Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's).
- **In values,** the shape. ChatGPT's model at A24 §4 has specialization raise joint production (10 + 16s) while it lowers B's independent capacity, 5(1 − s(1 − p)), where p is the protection of B's fallback. At full specialization and no protection, "the split is 14.75 to 11.25 in A's favour; with full protection, 12.25 to 13.75" (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1, Proposed, interpretive). The bargaining step that turns a fallback into terms is the assistant's: under the equal-weight Nash solution, u_B = (V − o_A + o_B)/2, so each unit of B's outside option moves half a unit of the split "whether or not B ever takes the outside option" (ibid., checked). ChatGPT's own caption limits the model: "Maintaining fallback capacity is not priced in this model, so it cannot establish an optimal degree of protection" (ibid.).
- **In reach,** nothing yet. LEAD-tmpeo8wq would define the coalition reach R_N and the solo reach R_i on TERM-114 with several actors, and test whether "R_N grows while R_i shrinks, and the shrinkage of R_i, not any change in R_N, moves the terms the other can offer". It has a negative control: the same cooperation with the capability kept at its maintenance cost.

The shape the lead asks for is therefore already on the record, in CLAIM-tmp2jhc1's values. The lead itself cites CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 only for its threat point, not for the A24 §4 model, and no entry sets the two side by side (J3.10). This article does not present "collective reach grows while independent reach shrinks" as new. What the record lacks is its statement in terms of reach, with no change in any single transition's price.

**The expectation, before any result,** is the lead's own null: "If R_i cannot shrink without some transition's price changing, the dependence is already priced by TERM-116's profiles, and the index adds notation only" (LEAD-tmpeo8wq). The construction would count as a result only if it gives "R_N growing and R_i shrinking with no change in any single transition's cost" (ibid.). It has not been run.

CLAIM-tmp2jhc1 adds one more thing that bears on §5. A constitutional reserve "can matter without being used", and "efficiency measured on use will tend to remove it" (CLAIM-tmp2jhc1). Reading that removal as CLAIM-921's use-coupled loss is this article's join (J3.7). The carriage is loose. CLAIM-921's capability serves occasions that arise. A fallback's value lies in a disagreement point that, at the solution, is never reached. So the join has no occasions to count, and a use-following budget would fund the reserve at its base rate from the start.

## 8. Neighbouring theories

The motivating draft asks for a comparison "against transaction-cost economics, incomplete-contract theory, dynamic optimization and existing resource-bounded planning frameworks". The record holds little of this:

- **Incomplete contracts.** The record holds readings of Grossman and Hart and of Hart (LIT-2745, LIT-2776, both Active), read as "the right to decide what the contract leaves out, the residual control right, belongs to the owner of the relevant non-human assets" (THEORY-1979, Proposed). Behind them is CLAIM-728 (Proposed, interpretive; not a member): "a relationship fair in every specified term can still leave one party's terms at the other's discretion". CLAIM-tmpro73c sets itself apart from CLAIM-728: "This claim is different in kind: no contingency is uncovered; which arrangement covers the act is left open, and one party decides it case by case and inconsistently" (CLAIM-tmpro73c; Article 2).
- **Institutions.** North's essay (LIT-489) is read as saying that institutions "with technology … fix the costs of transacting" and that "increasing returns to the institutional matrix make frameworks path dependent" (THEORY-429, Proposed).
- **Physical database design.** QUESTION-114's neighbour is held: index selection "is NP-hard", and the tools "choose … by searching configurations costed with the query optimizer's own estimates", "with no term for the one-time cost of building" (THEORY-345, Active). That last clause sets it apart from CASE-326, whose F is exactly that term.
- **Not found.** A search of the record found no reading of Williamson or Coase, and none of dynamic optimization or resource-bounded planning as frameworks to compare with.

The comparison the draft asks for is not on the record, and this article does not supply it from memory (G3.3).
