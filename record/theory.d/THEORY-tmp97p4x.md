# THEORY-tmp97p4x: On computational functionalism, an intervention that changes no neural activity and no actual causal interaction can change consciousness, because a clamp that stays idle on the actual input removes the transitions on every other input

## Source

Albert Gidon, Jaan Aru and Matthew E. Larkum (2025), "Does neural
computation feel like something?", *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 19,
[LIT-1000](../literature.d/LIT-1000.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmpxxz74](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxxz74.md): Results ("The replay results in
a degenerate computation", "Consciousness and nothing", "Counterfactual
erasers"), Figures 3–4, and the Discussion on the Turing-machine model
and on IIT.

## What was actually shown

- **The simulation (Figs. 2–3).** Five Hodgkin–Huxley-type neurons in a
  recurrent ring, driven by two LGN nodes, fire together for green light
  and singly for red or blue. A simulated voltage clamp is given the
  recorded green response as its command voltage.
  - With red light, the clamp supplies exactly the missing LGN input and
    the network fires as for green.
  - With green light, it injects no current: "no intervention was
    needed". Activity is identical to the unclamped run, and so is every
    actual neuron-to-neuron interaction (causation read mechanistically).
- **The computation changes (Figs. 3D, 4).** With the clamp on, state
  transitions no longer depend on the input. On the Turing–Putnam
  definition, where "the inputs, states, transitions, and outputs define
  the functional organization", the clamp becomes part of the transition
  function, and what remains is "a trivial computation consisting of a
  single sequence of states (i.e., universally realizable)".
- **The conditional.** If changing functional organization is changing
  consciousness (cited to Putnam, Shagrir, Block and Lewis), then the
  clamped and unclamped runs on green light differ in consciousness
  although they differ in nothing that occurs: "doing nothing to the
  brain can alter consciousness". The authors call the idle clamp a
  "counterfactual eraser".

What could have come out otherwise is narrow. The simulation could have
shown residual clamp current on the congruent input, so that the clamp
did act; with an ideal clamp in a deterministic model it cannot. The
conditional's force rests on the definitional step, which is where a
functionalist would push back.

## What this does not say

- **Not that functionalism is false.** The authors present a "paradox"
  the functionalist "must contend with", not a refutation. A functionalist
  may accept the verdict: the clamped system *is* a different, trivial
  machine, and its experience may differ. Whether that is absurd is the
  open question.
- **Not that the simulation is evidence about experience.** Its outputs
  are voltages and currents. The verdicts on consciousness are the
  working hypothesis applied by definition.
- **Not how much experience changes.** The authors concede it depends on
  "the proportion of states used in the congruent case compared to all
  other cases", and say only that it might be "severely diminished or
  even abolished".
- **Not about affect.** The paper concerns consciousness in general,
  "the experience … that fades during deep sleep or anesthesia". It says
  nothing of valence or feeling in the affective sense.
- **Not a positive account.** The closing alternative, that "natural
  counterfactuals" embodied in the substrate fix experience, is offered
  in one paragraph and not argued. This account does not include it.
- **Not a challenge to IIT.** The paper says the clamp lowers Φ by
  degrading the transition probability matrix, so IIT gives the same
  verdict without contradiction; Φ is not computed.

## Connections

- **Chalmers on implementation ([LIT-404](../literature.d/LIT-404.md), [NOTE-342](../notes.d/NOTE-342.md)).** Implementation
  needs "reliable, counterfactual-supporting transitions" for every
  formal state; without them a clock and a dial implement any inputless
  automaton. That is the criterion on which the clamped network
  implements only a trivial computation, and the reason a functionalist
  cannot escape by dropping the counterfactuals: dropping them reopens
  the rock problem.
- **Fading qualia ([LIT-392](../literature.d/LIT-392.md), [NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md)).** Chalmers keeps causal
  organization fixed and changes the substrate; this case keeps substrate
  and activity fixed and removes unused organization. Together they show
  the functionalist's experience tracks the organization in both
  directions, including its unexercised part.
- **[THEORY-092](THEORY-092.md).** Block's lookup machine matches every actual and
  counterfactual behaviour and lacks intelligence. The clamped network
  matches every actual activity and lacks the counterfactuals. Both say
  a mental property is not fixed by what actually happens, from
  opposite sides.
- **[THEORY-119](THEORY-119.md).** Frankfurt's device never acts and so cannot change
  responsibility. The counterfactual eraser never acts and, on
  functionalism, can change experience. The paper asks whether Jones,
  held responsible by Frankfurt, may have acted "with altered or
  possibly diminished conscious experience".
- **[THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md).** Whether the clamp is part of "the system" is a
  boundary question of the kind [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) says functionalism has no
  principled answer to yet.
- **[THEORY-101](THEORY-101.md) and [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md).** Veit identifies feeling bad with
  occupying "a coordinated regulatory state". If that is a functional
  role in the Turing–Putnam sense, the identity inherits this
  conditional: the same regulatory activity with its alternatives erased
  is a different feeling or none. This does not answer [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md),
  which asks for a case, not another argument from a definition.
- **[THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md).** The paper takes the biological naturalist's side on AI
  consciousness; this account holds only its conditional and leaves the
  evidential deadlock where [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md) puts it.
