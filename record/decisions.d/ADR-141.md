# ADR-141: Every long-running or delegated unit of work persists its progress in the repository as it goes, on a branch of its own, so that a stop at any moment loses at most the step in hand

## Context

The owner, 2026-11-22, after the third loss of the kind in the session:
"this keeps happening repeatedly. it is an expensive mistake and I'd
like it if we didn't keep making it. the instruction to have readers
commit as they go to their own branches was intended to remedy this sort
of pathology. consider what ADR may need supercesion and how we could
structure an improvement to mitigate this sort of issue future-forward."

The instruction the owner means is [ADR-108](ADR-108.md): a delegated reader keeps
running notes on a branch of its own and commits and pushes after each
step. Its rule is right, and it is scoped too narrowly. It names
readers, and the losses came from work it does not name.

- **Lead workers committed at the end.** The lead-worker brief of
  2026-11-22 (kept in the session's scratchpad) said to check, commit and
  push once the work was done, and "if you hit an API usage limit,
  commit and push what you have". A hard stop leaves no turn in which to
  do that.
- **The assistant's own long job held its results in memory.** The sweep
  of the 184 Unfetched works through OpenAlex, Unpaywall, Europe PMC and
  NCBI wrote its results file only at the end. It ran under the default
  background time limit of 30 minutes, at a pace (18 works in 12
  minutes) that said it needed about two hours. The assistant saw the
  pace and did not act on it. The run was stopped at 162 of 184 and the
  probe details were lost; only a log of outcome labels survived. The
  rerun contacted the services a second time.
- **Working state lived in the scratchpad.** The briefs for readers,
  filers and lead workers, the triage of 208 leads, and drafts of pull
  request descriptions were all scratchpad files, which the container
  takes with it.
- **The lesson had been drawn and not carried out.** The devlog note of
  04:15 the same day, after a usage limit stopped three filers at once,
  ended: "When parallel workers may be cut off, commit in progress."
  Nothing put that into a brief or a decision.

What was not lost is as telling. The workers stopped by the usage limit
were resumed from their transcripts and finished. That works for a usage
limit, but not for a time limit on a process, a reclaimed container, or
a worker that has to be replaced, and a transcript is not in the
repository.

## Decision

1. **Scope.** Every unit of work that runs past one tool call: a
   delegated reader, filer, drafter or lead worker, and the assistant's
   own background jobs and long scripts.
2. **A branch from the first minute.** The brief names the branch. The
   worker cuts it and pushes it before its first substantive step. For a
   lead worker or a filer it is the branch the pull request will come
   from. For a reader it stays `claude/readers/<key>` with no pull
   request ([ADR-108](ADR-108.md), point 2).
3. **Push after every step.** A step is any record file created or
   edited, a computation finished, a copy located, a section read. The
   commit message of a step not yet complete begins "work in progress:".
   A rejected push is followed by a pull and a second push, never a
   force-push.
4. **A PROGRESS file.** Each unit keeps
   `scratch/<kind>/<key>/PROGRESS.md` (for a study, its
   `scratch/studies/<key>/` directory): what is done, what is next, and
   the choices made so far, so that a replacement can continue from the
   branch without the original transcript. It is updated in the same
   push as the step it records.
5. **Computations persist per item.** A computation that runs longer than
   a few minutes writes each item's result as it is produced, to an
   append-only file inside the repository's study directory, and resumes
   by skipping the items already written. It does not hold results in
   memory to write at the end. Checkpoint commits land at least every ten
   minutes while it runs. Results stay small (under about 1 MB); bulk
   output and downloads stay in the scratchpad, as [ADR-108](ADR-108.md), point 3,
   requires.
6. **Time is budgeted, and watched.** A background run is given a time
   limit above twice its estimate. When its measured pace says it will
   not finish inside the limit, it is stopped and restructured at once,
   not left to run.
7. **Briefs and working tables live in the repository.** A standing
   brief becomes a skill under `.claude/skills/`. Working tables, such as
   a triage, go to `scratch/` on the branch of the unit they serve. The
   scratchpad holds downloads and what can be thrown away.
8. **The measure.** A stop at any moment loses at most the step in hand.
   A retrospective that finds more lost names this decision.

From [ADR-108](ADR-108.md), which this supersedes, the reader rules stand unchanged:
notes outside the record in `scratch/readers/<key>.md`, no downloaded
text ever committed, readers not writing to the record, and the reader's
branch removed when the reading is filed. That removal now waits on the
owner, since the session's git proxy refuses branch deletion (403).

## First application

The lead-worker brief is moved from the scratchpad into the repository
as the `run-lead` skill (`.claude/skills/run-lead/SKILL.md`), with
points 2 to 7 as its steps. The same day the sweep was rebuilt on point
5: it writes one line per work to `results.jsonl`, resumes by code, and
is checkpointed into `scratch/studies/unfetched-api-sweep/out/` on its
branch every ten minutes. The three lead workers then running were told
to push at once and after every step.

## Alternatives considered

- **[ADR-142](ADR-142.md): leave [ADR-108](ADR-108.md) standing and add a sibling.** It
  keeps a decision whose rule is right and adds the cases it misses. The
  cost is two decisions saying one thing, and the next uncovered kind of
  work falls between them again. This decision prefers one rule stated
  generally.
- **Resume from transcripts.** It worked for the usage-limit stops. It
  fails for a time limit, a reclaimed container or a replaced worker.
- **Commit at the end, and on a usage limit.** That was the brief, and a
  hard stop gives no turn to do it in.
- **Checkpoint only the assistant's own jobs.** Leaves the workers' briefs
  as they were.

## Consequences

- Branches carry work-in-progress commits. The pull request is opened
  once the unit is done, as now, so CI's repair does not land mid-edit
  ([ADR-139](ADR-139.md)).
- More pushes, and more branches for the owner to clear while the proxy
  refuses deletion.
- Briefs become reviewable and versioned, and a brief's change shows in
  git blame.
