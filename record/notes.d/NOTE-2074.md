# NOTE-2074: Sperber, Vandell, Duncan and Watts — Delay of gratification and adult outcomes

## Contribution

The first preregistered test of the marshmallow test's prediction of a
broad set of adult outcomes in a non-elite cohort, with the control
strategy of Watts et al. (2018), [LIT-2752](../literature.d/LIT-2752.md).

## Key insight

By adulthood the task's predictive power is almost all borrowed from the
early circumstances it is correlated with.

## Assumptions

- The covariates are confounds; models frame a hypothetical intervention
  on delay alone.
- The 7-minute task measures delay ability; categorical models are taken
  to show the ceiling does not seriously bias results.
- Age-26 outcomes index adult functioning.

## Key results

- **Abstract**: "Using a preregistered analysis, Marshmallow Test
  performance was not strongly predictive of adult achievement, health, or
  behavior."
- **Abstract**: "Although modest bivariate associations were detected with
  educational attainment (r=.17) and body mass index (r=−.17), almost all
  regression-adjusted coefficients were nonsignificant."
- **Results**: "After adjusting for child demographic characteristics
  (i.e., Model 2), the educational attainment coefficient declined to
  nonsignificance (β=.04, p=.23)."
- **Exploratory**: "waiting the full period on the Marshmallow Test
  significantly predicted lower BMI when using the binary predictor for
  delay of gratification, even with full covariates (β=−.17, p=.03)."
- **Discussion**: "the task appears to derive much of its predictive
  validity from its apparent associations with other important life
  factors in early childhood (e.g., SES, parenting, etc.)"; it "may
  instead be a screener for broader developmental advantages in early
  childhood."
- **Caveat**: "We did not adjust for multiple comparisons in our
  analyses".

## Concepts

Predictive validity; construct validity; preregistration; stepwise
covariate models; categorical and binary coding of wait-time; moderation
by SES and sex.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-281](../cases.d/CASE-281.md)**. Confirms, to age 26, what the case says it cannot
  show: "That waiting predicts later life".
- **[CLAIM-744](../claims.d/CLAIM-744.md)**. Indirect, as for Watts et al.: the authors trace the
  task's prediction to SES and parenting, consistent with a reliability
  reading but not a test of it; they do not cite Kidd et al.'s result in
  the text read. Not filed as a relation.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Faithful, with one omission. A51: performance "did not reliably predict
adult achievement, health, or behavior once relevant factors were
considered", with 702 participants. Omitted: the exploratory binary BMI
result that survived full controls, flagged by the authors as not
preregistered and uncorrected for multiple comparisons.

## Limitations

Observational; not nationally representative; a 7-minute ceiling;
early-adult outcomes; many tests without correction; the adult waves are
not public. The authors state these.
