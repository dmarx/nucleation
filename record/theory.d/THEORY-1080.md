# THEORY-1080: In relational quantum mechanics as Rovelli first stated it, the relation between two observers' descriptions is itself a quantum fact relative to a third system, so no observer's view can be deduced from another's and there is no description in toto, only a net of partial descriptions; the views are held consistent only in that the third system predicts correlated outcomes, which is shown for a single measurement and asserted in general

## Source

Rovelli (1996), [LIT-1635](../literature.d/LIT-1635.md), arXiv quant-ph/9609002v2; read in
[NOTE-1227](../notes.d/NOTE-1227.md). Page numbers are the preprint's.

## What was actually shown

**No deducible relation.** "The absolute state of affairs of the world is a
meaningless notion; asking about the absolute relation between two
descriptions is precisely asking about such an absolute state of affairs of
the world. … In particular, there is no way of deducing the view of one
from the view of the other" (§II.D, p. 8). The relation "must be addressed
within the view of one of the two observers (or of a third one)", because
"the process of comparison is always a physical interaction, and all
physical interactions are quantum mechanical in nature" (p. 8).

**No description in toto.** A further observer can describe a complex
situation, but that description is "deficient in two directions: upward,
because an even more general observer is needed to describe the global
observer itself, and –more importantly– downward, because the in toto
observer knows the content of the information that the single component
systems possess about each other only probabilistically" (§IV.A, p. 16).
Everett's universal wave function "represents the perspective of a
non-existent observer"; "There is no description of the universe in-toto,
only a quantum-interrelated net of partial descriptions" (§IV.B, p. 19).

**Where the relativity analogy stops.** The paper's analogy is Einstein's
reading of the Lorentz transformations (§I, p. 2) and the relativity of
velocity (p. 6). In relativity a frame's description is obtained from
another's by a transformation. Here it is not: what one observer can have
about another's view is P-relative, probabilistic information that O has
measured (the operator M, eq. 3), not what O found ("I know that you know
the amount of your salary, but I do not know what you know about the amount of your salary", p. 8). What
does not vary between observers is the system's characterization, W(S)
([THEORY-1068](THEORY-1068.md)), not a state of it.

**Consistency.** If P measures q on S and then O's pointer, the two agree,
"as was first noticed by von Neumann, and as is clear from (2)" (p. 9).
That is the case shown. The general claim, that with rules (i) and (ii)
"there is no way in which contradiction may develop", is introduced with
"The reader may convince himself" (§III.D, p. 15). Rovelli also suspects
that this consistency "could be taken as the missing input" for the
reconstruction, and has "no definite result" (§III.C, p. 14).

## What this does not say

- That consistency fails. Nothing in the paper suggests a conflict; the
  gap is that the general case is not proved.
- That there is nothing invariant. The systems and their question sets are
  not observer-relative, and the probabilities a third system assigns are
  given by standard quantum mechanics.
- That this is solipsism. Views can be compared, by interaction (p. 8).
- Anything about communicative acts. Its bearing on [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md) is by
  analogy: a physical theory built on a relativity analogy that keeps a
  frame-independent kind (W(S), compare [CLAIM-137](../claims.d/CLAIM-137.md)) and denies a
  frame-independent state.
- How the later account handles consistency. [THEORY-621](THEORY-621.md) is the 2021
  statement, with the same gap noted in its promote_when.
