# NOTE-1058: Chandia-Jorquera et al. — Neurophenomenological signatures of pure awareness in Transcendental Meditation

## Contribution

The study combines EEG with temporal experience tracing (TET) for one
reported dimension, "pure awareness", in the largest sample of
Transcendental Meditation (TM) practitioners it knows of. It compares them
with matched controls doing mental counting. Its new results are two.
First, the practitioners' retrospective traces of pure awareness are higher
and more variable over a 30-minute session. Second, a multivariate EEG
classifier separates meditation from counting on one family of measures,
and meditation from rest on another. It does not link the reports to the
EEG.

## Key insight

The authors' reading is a "double dissociation" (p. 1). Relative to
counting, the TM period is marked by entropy and aperiodic dynamics.
Relative to the practitioners' own rest, it is marked by low-frequency
phase coherence. Pure awareness, they write, is "both broader and more
variable than ordinary cognition, yet also more stabilized and coherent
than resting baselines" (pp. 11–12). The signature is a spatial pattern
across electrodes, not more or less of any one measure.

## Assumptions

- **Pure awareness is minimal phenomenal experience.** PA is taken, after
  Metzinger (2020), as "a paradigmatic case of minimal phenomenal
  experience" (p. 1). It lacks selfhood, a temporal register and a
  spatial frame, and keeps wakefulness. TM's phenomenology is said to
  match this closely (p. 1). Practitioners describe it as "awake but
  content-free, lacking an intentional object, devoid of deliberate mental
  activity, and minimally engaged with sensory input" (p. 1, citing Travis
  and Pearson 2000).
- **The report scale is the experimenters'.** After the session each
  participant was asked "To what extent do you think that you experienced
  pure awareness during meditation/counting?". The high end was defined
  for them as "an experience free from content defining everyday working
  experiences (e.g., internal thoughts, perception of the external
  world), absence of time, space, and body sensation". The low end was
  "normal waking consciousness with a variety of internal and external
  content" (p. 3). The trace runs from "No content" to "Full of content"
  (Figure 1B).
- **Retrospective tracing is valid.** "The TET instructions were given
  only after the task period had ended", so that monitoring would not
  disturb the practice (p. 3). Traces were hand-drawn on paper and
  digitised.
- **Counting is a fair active control.** Eyes closed, internal, self-paced
  and low in motor demand, while "remaining phenomenologically distinct
  from the reported experience of PA" (p. 13).
- **Feature importance is descriptive.** Random-forest importances are
  model-dependent and affected by collinearity, so they say which
  features were useful to the classifier, not which are mechanistically
  primary (pp. 5–6, 14).

## Key results

- **Sample (p. 2).** 33 TM practitioners (21 male, mean age 40.3, at
  least 4 years of twice-daily practice, mean 12.9 years), recruited by
  the David Lynch Foundation UK with the Maharishi Foundation. 33
  controls new to TM, matched individually on sex and age (±3 years),
  recruited through the University of Cambridge. A power calculation for
  d = 0.70 gave at least 28 per group.
- **Design (pp. 3, 7).** 10 minutes of eyes-closed rest (B1), 30 minutes
  of TM or counting, the TET trace, then 10 more minutes of rest (B2).
  128-channel EEG. Of six experiential dimensions collected, only PA is
  reported.
- **Reports (p. 6, Figure 1).**
  - Two time clusters in which PA was higher for practitioners (8.32–12.53
    min, d = 0.57; 20.00–21.45 min, d = 0.53).
  - Peak PA: TM M = 0.697 (SD 0.209), controls M = 0.523 (SD 0.241);
    t = 3.10, d = 0.771.
  - Variability (SD of the trace): TM 0.154, controls 0.063; t = 5.36,
    d = 1.32.
  - No association between years of practice and PA (r = −0.13,
    p = 0.445; variability r = −0.10, p = 0.550). The authors read this as
    "Consistent with the prediction of the TM framework" that access is
    effortless and independent of experience (p. 6).
- **Meditation against counting (p. 8, Figure 3).** 65% balanced
  accuracy; meditators classified correctly 67% of the time, controls 62%.
  Permutation entropy in delta to alpha led; aperiodic exponent and offset
  were next; phase-lag (WPLI) and symbolic mutual information (WSMI)
  connectivity and Lempel–Ziv complexity were lower. Univariate
  topographies found no corrected electrode-wise difference in the direct
  double-difference contrast (Figure S1D), so the separation is read as
  "distributed multivariate patterns" (p. 8).
- **Meditation against its own rest (p. 9, Figure 4).** Meditation epochs
  were identified with "88% sensitivity". B1 and B2 were confused with
  each other (64% and 55% correct). Low-frequency WPLI (delta, theta) led,
  aperiodic measures next; entropy contributed little.
- **Counting against its own rest (p. 10, Figure 5).** Counting 84%
  correct, B1 84%, B2 81%. Aperiodic measures led. Because B1 and B2 were
  separable in controls but not in practitioners, the authors infer "a
  negligible carry-over effect after TM" (p. 10), and residual change
  after counting.
- **The reports and the EEG (p. 14).** "we did not identify robust direct
  associations between the PA measures and the EEG features". The authors
  do not take this to show that no relationship exists.
- **Against the older TM literature (pp. 13–14).** Alpha coherence,
  earlier reported as TM's hallmark, ranked among the least informative
  features. The authors attribute the difference to volume conduction,
  which coherence does not correct and WPLI does.

## Concepts

- **Pure awareness (PA)**: "an episode of wakeful experiencing stripped of
  customary structural features of consciousness" (p. 1), with no
  self-identification, no explicit time and no spatial frame, keeping a
  sense of wakefulness. Operationally, a hand-drawn trace on the scale
  the participants were given (p. 3).
- **Minimal phenomenal experience (MPE)**: Metzinger's (2020) term, used
  here as the class PA belongs to.
- **Automatic self-transcendence**: TM's own term for effortless access to
  PA, which the null correlation with practice years is said to support
  (pp. 1, 6).
- **Temporal experience tracing (TET)**: retrospective drawing of the
  intensity of a predefined dimension against time (p. 2).
- **Double dissociation**: the authors' term for different feature
  families leading the two classifications. It is a difference between
  feature-importance rankings, not a classic lesion-style dissociation.

## Connections

The study builds on Metzinger's minimal phenomenal experience and on the
TM literature of Travis and colleagues, whose alpha-coherence finding it
reframes. Its method comes from the authors' earlier TET studies of
meditation, breathwork and DMT, and from the neural-marker screening of
Sitt, King and colleagues for disorders of consciousness. It cites
Varela's neurophenomenology as its frame. It does not cite Woods, Windt &
Carter ([LIT-1351](../literature.d/LIT-1351.md)), who argue from expert texts that states like TM's
goal-state are not truly contentless. The record holds no reading of
Metzinger's paper or of the TM physiology literature.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) (experience constituted by interpretive organization).**
  The exchange cited this study as experience with little interpretation,
  which would bar identifying interpretation with rich conceptual content.
  The study is weaker evidence for that than the exchange's description
  suggests. What it measures is a retrospective rating on a scale whose
  top was defined for the participant as "free from content". The ratings
  are an after-the-fact classification of the session in the
  experimenters' categories. They are not observation of an episode with
  little interpretation. The paper's own account of PA keeps wakefulness
  and "alert presence" (p. 1), and the sibling reading of [LIT-1351](../literature.d/LIT-1351.md)
  finds wakefulness, calm, bliss and knowing in TM's expert descriptions.
  On [TERM-073](../terms.d/TERM-073.md)'s broad, nonphenomenal sense of interpretation, nothing
  here shows interpretation absent: the EEG shows a distinct organization,
  not less of one. The study is consistent with the claim's own caveat,
  that interpretation must not be identified with rich conceptualization,
  explicit judgment or self-narration. It does not test the claim.
- **[CLAIM-411](../claims.d/CLAIM-411.md) (much interpretation not shown to be experiential).**
  From the other side, it shows how hard such cases are to use. Phenomenal
  claims about PA rest wholly on report. Even the 88% classification of
  meditation against rest separates a task period from rest, and the
  authors could not tie the reports to the EEG. This is [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)'s
  difficulty in a contemplative setting.
- **[QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md) (what makes an organization experiential).** If PA
  is a real experiential state with little content, it is a case where an
  organization stays experiential while its content is pared down. The
  study's EEG would then be a candidate description of that organization.
  But the link from the EEG to the experience is exactly what was not
  found.
- **[TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md) (perspective, three senses).** PA is described as
  lacking a represented self and a spatial frame while remaining a lived
  point of view. If the description is right, the represented sense of
  perspective (sense 2) comes apart from the phenomenal sense (sense 3).
  The study gives that as definition, not as finding.
- **[THEORY-633](../theory.d/THEORY-633.md) (strong illusionism).** Nothing here bears on it beyond the
  general point: everything phenomenal in the study is a report.
- **THEORY filed.** [THEORY-870](../theory.d/THEORY-870.md) states what the study shows, with the
  missing link between report and EEG as its sharp edge.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the
  anthology.

## Limitations

- **The scale suggests its answer.** Controls were asked whether they had
  experienced "pure awareness" during counting, a term they may not know.
  The authors grant that controls "may lack familiarity with the
  experiential profile of pure awareness, which could potentially
  introduce demand-characteristic effects in their reports" (p. 13). The
  same worry applies to practitioners, recruited through the TM
  organisation, who know what the practice is meant to produce. The paper
  does not raise it for them. Controls' mean peak was still 0.52 of the
  scale while counting.
- **Group, task and state are confounded.** The between-group contrast is
  meditators meditating against non-meditators counting. Trait
  differences, task differences and PA cannot be separated, which the
  authors partly concede (p. 12).
- **No report–EEG link.** The EEG signatures are of the TM period, not of
  reported PA within it (p. 14).
- **A reporting slip.** For peak PA, t = 3.10 is reported with p < 0.001.
  With about 64 degrees of freedom that t gives p ≈ 0.003, and the
  printed means and SDs give t ≈ 3.13. The difference stands at p < 0.01.
  The variability result (t = 5.36) does reach p < 0.001. Recomputed
  here.
- **The years-of-practice claim is a null result.** No association in 33
  people is read as confirming effortless access.
- **Retrospective, single-dimension report.** Five other dimensions were
  collected and not reported. The data are "available from the
  corresponding author upon reasonable request" (p. 14).
- **Funding.** The study was funded by the David Lynch Foundation UK,
  which also recruited the practitioners (pp. 2, 14).
- The version of record was not read.

## Open questions

- Would practitioners asked about a neutral dimension, or about PA with
  the scale defined differently, give the same traces? A design that
  replaced the defined scale with open description would bear on demand.
- Does any EEG feature track moment-to-moment PA within practitioners? The
  authors expect the mapping to be "subtle and distributed" (p. 14).
- Do the EEG patterns separate TM from another effortless practice that
  does not aim at PA, rather than from counting?
- Are the features Woods, Windt & Carter find in TM's expert descriptions
  (wakefulness, calm, bliss, knowing) present in these practitioners'
  reports? The other five TET dimensions might say.
