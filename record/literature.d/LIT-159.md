# LIT-159: If materialism is true, the United States is probably conscious

Eric Schwitzgebel (2014), *Philosophical Studies 172, 1697–1721 (2015 issue)* — DOI-10.1007/s11098-014-0387-8

## Key takeaways

- Any materialist criterion liberal enough to count rabbits and behaviourally human-like aliens as conscious will also count a spatially distributed, richly integrated, self-monitoring and environmentally responsive group entity such as the United States, unless one accepts eliminativism, extreme sparseness or an unprincipled neurochauvinism.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Materialists should think rabbits are conscious, since rabbits are biologically and neurophysiologically much like us. They should also accept consciousness in naturally evolved aliens that behave like us but are built very differently, on pain of "Earthly chauvinism". Schwitzgebel argues that someone who accepts weirdly built aliens should also accept spatially distributed group entities, and someone who accepts rabbits should accept even fairly unintelligent group entities. The United States seems to be such an entity. Once our prejudice against spatially scattered beings is set aside, it has all the kinds of properties materialists usually treat as marks of consciousness.
