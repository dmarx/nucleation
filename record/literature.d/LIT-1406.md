  Seeley et al. (2012). Nest-site scouts deliver stop signals, brief
  vibrations given mostly by head-butting, to scouts dancing for sites
  other than their own while the swarm is choosing between two nest
  boxes, and to all dancers once it has chosen; accumulated signals end
  dances. A mean-field model shows that this cross inhibition, like that
  between neural populations, above a critical rate breaks the deadlock
  between two equal sites, the swarm choosing one at random, and takes it
  to the better site when the two differ enough.
