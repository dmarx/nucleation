# THEORY-tmps476s: Castano, Yzerbyt, Paladino and Sacchi report that in 48 Italian students a reminder of their own death raised identification with Italians, the perceived entitativity of Italians and in-group bias, and propose, without separating it from worldview defence, that a group seen as a real entity lets the self project beyond death

## The account

"Through identification with social groups, the individuals can
project themselves in space and time, beyond their personal death. They
participate in an entity that is not subject to the mortal fate that
characterizes them as human beings" (p. 137). For that the group "must
be perceived as a 'real' entity" (p. 137), so a death reminder should
raise both identification and perceived entitativity. On this account
dying for the group is not selfless but a reasonable choice for a self
that fears annihilation. The authors grant they cannot tell it apart
from terror management's worldview defence (p. 141).

## Evidence

N = 48 (NOTE-1297): identification rose at p ≈ .03 and entitativity at
p < .05 (5.40 against 4.77); the bias was in-group enhancement, not
out-group derogation. Identification is six verbal items; no overlap or
fusion measure. The mortality-salience effect the study builds on
failed to replicate in Many Labs 4 (NOTE-1322).

## What it bears on

QUESTION-301 cites this work for where, on terror management theory, the
temporal and social extensions of the self meet, and its note of 2026-10-29 says the
authors "state the join of the social and temporal self as a
conjecture" and that their one experiment "measures no self". The
reading holds the join as the authors' proposal, not a finding.
