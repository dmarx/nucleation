# LIT-942: Do different prompting methods yield a common task representation in language models?

Guy Davidson, Todd M. Gureckis, Brenden M. Lake and Adina Williams (2025), *arXiv:2505.12075* (NeurIPS 2025).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmppb5ub.*

- **Instructions yield function vectors too.** Built from the 20 heads
  whose patching most helps, they raise zero-shot accuracy from below 20%
  to above 50% in the best models, and work far better in post-trained
  models.
- **Mostly different heads.** Of the top 20, demonstrations and
  instructions share 7 in the Llama-3.2-3B models, 4 in the Llama-3.1-8B
  models, and 2 to 7 elsewhere. Instruction representations are more
  diffuse.
- **The two vectors add.** Adding both beats either alone, without
  interference; part of the gain is the larger intervention.
- **Swapping costs, asymmetrically.** Instruction heads read with
  demonstration activations lose less than the reverse, which the authors
  take as preliminary evidence that demonstration inference is more basic.
- **Post-training builds the instruction route.** It appears at SFT and
  DPO in OLMo-2, and post-trained instruction vectors steer base models;
  demonstration vectors are unchanged by post-training.
- **The limit.** Simple lexical tasks, small models, one extraction method,
  and in the authors' words "we cannot conclusively prove the negative".

## Standing in the record

Filed 2026-10-12 for QUESTION-024 and read 2026-10-14 in NOTE-tmppb5ub. It
is the record's direct evidence that one task given in two prompt forms is
not carried by one internal representation, held as THEORY-tmpgub9t with
LIT-946. It does not test what QUESTION-024 asks, a stance induced from
examples surviving paraphrase. LIT-946 points the same way by another
method, not the opposite way as the triage of 2026-10-14 had it. Its
prompt-design advice belongs to the anthology; the `anthology-candidate`
