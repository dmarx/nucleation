# LIT-tmpyky5z: Concepts Are Restructured During Language Contact: The Birth of Blue and Other Color Concepts in Tsimane'-Spanish Bilinguals

Saima Malik-Moraleda, Kyle Mahowald, Bevil R. Conway and Edward Gibson
(2023), *Psychological Science* 34(12), 1350–1362 —
doi:10.1177/09567976231199742

## Key takeaways

- **Restructuring, not borrowing.** Bilinguals keep Tsimane' words but use
  them with Spanish-like boundaries; they do not import Spanish terms, nor
  Spanish's light-blue/dark-blue split.
- **Every studied term narrows.** The chips selected for each of the six
  modal terms shrink from monolinguals to bilinguals, and the overlap
  (KL divergence) between neighbouring pairs falls. Purple
  (itsidyeisi/morado) did not differ between any groups.
- **A split.** Monolinguals use shandyes and yụshñus for one green-blue
  category (KL 0.10); bilinguals separate them (1.18 in Tsimane', 6.16
  in Spanish; Spanish monolinguals 10.65).
- **The design's limit.** Groups, not a language before and after; colour
  terms only; the bilinguals live in San Borja, more industrialised than
  the monolinguals' communities.

## Standing in the record

Registered by LEAD-056 for THEORY-159's promote_when and CLAIM-082's
defeat condition. It shows a field's terms shifting together when the
field's contrasts change, which is the shape the account predicts, but
with no change in the inventory of Tsimane' words, no terms outside the
field as a control, and a cultural confound the authors name
(NOTE-tmpkl6ez). It also cautions that the Nafaanra change
(LIT-tmpntsdw) may be bilingualism with English rather than efficiency.
