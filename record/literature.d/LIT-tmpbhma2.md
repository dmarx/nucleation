**Where it would sit.** The contents put institutions on the objective side
and socialization and identity on the subjective side. That is the same
ground as Durkheim's chapter ([LIT-tmpl1nlb](LIT-tmpl1nlb.md)), where institutions exist outside
individuals and education imposes them, and the same ground as the social
categories Haslanger ([LIT-tmphg6wn](LIT-tmphg6wn.md)) and Ásta ([LIT-tmprri7i](LIT-tmprri7i.md)) analyse. Whether
the book engages Durkheim, and what it means by "construction", a reading has
to establish; neither is recorded here. Hacking's *The Social Construction of
What?* ([LIT-tmpdaz8m](LIT-tmpdaz8m.md)), also filed unread, asks what such claims are about;
whether it discusses this book was not checked.

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary, as a theory of how institutions and
roles come to be objective. `social-science` holds it as a work of sociology,
and `epistemology` holds the sociology of knowledge, its stated subject.

No instruction for machine-learning practice appears in what was seen, so
nothing here points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.**

- What "social construction of reality" claims: that institutions exist only
  through habitualised and legitimated activity, or something about reality
  in general.
- How "Sedimentation and Tradition" and "Roles" relate to Durkheim's
  consolidated ways of doing and to Ritchie's nodes and roles ([LIT-tmppeiud](LIT-tmppeiud.md)).
- Whether institutions are treated as having anything like agency, which would
  connect it to the group-agency strand ([LIT-401](LIT-401.md)).
