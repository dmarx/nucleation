# QUESTION-tmpao6hx: What do described experiences of delusions with mathematical content have in common, and is their felt certainty the felt quality of mathematical insight?

## The question

Three parts:

1. **The experience.** Do first-person and clinical descriptions of
   delusions with mathematical content share a structure? Candidates from
   the general phenomenology of delusion: a period of perplexity and
   heightened significance, then a sudden "aha" that brings relief and
   makes everything fit (Conrad's Trema and apophany).
2. **The content.** Do they share themes? Candidates: a solution to a
   famous problem, a "world formula" or framework, an invention of great
   importance to mankind (the classical *Erfinderwahn*), hidden numerical
   patterns, and dismissal of standard methods (reported of mathematical
   cranks, a non-clinical population).
3. **The certainty.** Is the felt certainty the same feeling as genuine
   mathematical insight, so that the feeling is no guide to truth? That
   would be CLAIM-276's separation of coherence from fidelity, inside one
   mind.

## Why it bears on the delusion question

Chatbot-associated cases include mathematical ones. The most documented is a
user's "chronoarithmics", a framework built with a chatbot over about three
weeks, whose transcripts were analysed by a former OpenAI researcher. That is
the observation DISTINCTION-tmpwhh30 says the record lacks: logs read
against a delusion co-created with a chatbot (TERM-tmpawees). Studies of
delusional content over time also bear on CLAIM-tmp93fmg, that content
follows the culture.
