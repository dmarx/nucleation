# CASE-tmpxgcq2: Locked-in syndrome: consciousness preserved with anarthria and quadriplegia; in the classic form, vertical eye movement and blinking remain and carry communication, and in the completely locked-in state no motor channel remains

## The case

Locked-in syndrome "consists of anarthria and quadriplegia while
consciousness is preserved. Classically, vertical eye movements or blinking
allow coded communication. Given appropriate medical care, patients can
survive for decades" (Bruno et al. 2011, [LIT-tmp8g2to](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8g2to.md)). The literature
distinguishes classic, incomplete and total forms (Laureys et al. 2005,
[LIT-tmpx97h7](../literature.d/LIT-tmpx97h7.md), not read). In the completely locked-in state (CLIS),
usually reached through ALS, no motor channel remains.

- **Lucid life on one eyelid.** Bauby dictated *Le scaphandre et le
  papillon* ([LIT-tmppvr8q](../literature.d/LIT-tmppvr8q.md)) by blinking his left eyelid as a partner
  read out an alphabet.
- **Lucid over years.** Of 65 chronic patients with usable answers in
  Bruno et al.'s survey, 47 reported happiness, and a longer time in LIS
  correlated with happiness. The survey was self-selected and answered
  through the spared channels.
- **The completely locked-in state.** Kübler and Birbaumer
  ([LIT-tmpxcw33](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxcw33.md)): none of seven CLIS patients regained yes/no
  communication by BCI. They proposed that goal-directed thinking is
  extinguished when contingency between intention and outcome is lost, and
  that "a single CLIS patient who learns to communicate with a BCI or any
  other communication method will disprove our hypothesis". Chaudhary et
  al. ([LIT-tmpzuemc](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzuemc.md)) report such a patient, who spelled phrases with
  intracortical signals after auditory neurofeedback training. The
  authors' earlier CLIS papers were retracted after a DFG misconduct
  finding, and outside researchers urged caution about this one.

## What it can show

- **Classic LIS separates lucid judgement from limb and speech action,
  not from action.** Friston et al.'s action is first of all eye movement:
  their simulations are of oculomotor control ([LIT-tmp670rc](../literature.d/LIT-tmp670rc.md)). So
  classic LIS leaves active inference the action it models first, and
  cannot by itself decide [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md) against it. It can show that
  judgement keeps a built model working, over years, with world-directed
  action reduced to a trickle.
- **CLIS is the test, and it is asymmetric.** Evidence of lucidity in CLIS
  has to cross some channel, and a channel the patient drives is an action
  in active inference's sense. Chaudhary et al.'s patient communicated
  only once neurofeedback restored a contingency between intention and
  outcome. So the case can show the loop running down (Kübler and
  Birbaumer's prediction, [CLAIM-tmpki5gp](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpki5gp.md)), but it cannot show judgement
  running with no action at all, which is [CLAIM-tmpq9do8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpq9do8.md)'s defeater (b)
  turned around.
- **A lead not filed.** Detecting command-following by brain imaging, with
  no motor response at all, would take the patient's output channel to be
  mental action alone. Whether that escapes the asymmetry, or only moves
  the action inward as [CLAIM-tmp9cjyo](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp9cjyo.md) says, is open. No work on it is
  registered.
- **What it cannot show.** Every patient here built their model while
  acting. The case bears on the loop that keeps a model in touch, not on
  the one that builds it.
