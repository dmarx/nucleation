# QUESTION-tmp22a7y: Can a moral norm work among cooperating groups as a stabilizing mechanism does in coexistence theory, limiting a dominant group more than it limits its rivals so that a group reduced to rarity can increase again; and is that what separates norms that keep several groups in being from norms that serve only one group's internal cooperation?

## Why it is a question

Chesson (LIT-1219) separates two ways competitors come to coexist.
Equalizing mechanisms shrink the differences in average fitness. A
stabilizing mechanism makes each type limit itself more than it limits
the others, so a type that falls rare increases again (CASE-118 for two
competitors). CLAIM-tmpjnqe9 says cooperation within groups and the
coexistence of groups can trade off. This question asks whether moral
norms can supply the missing stabilization.

- **A norm that is equalizing** would narrow how much groups gain from
  cooperating internally: a cap, a transfer.
- **A norm that is stabilizing** would bear more on a group the larger it
  is: limits on dominance, protections for minorities, rules against
  exclusion.
- **A loyalty norm** strengthens a group's internal cooperation, and so
  raises its advantage over the others. It is neither.

What would count as an answer: a model of groups competing under norms
in which the invasion growth of a rare group can be computed, with and
without the norm, together with a reason to think real norms have that
form.

## What it is not

- **Not whether such norms are justified.** Viability does not establish
  what ought to persist (CLAIM-075).
- **Not the record's question about processes.** QUESTION-083 asks
  whether processes sharing an update rule can come to hold mutually
  regulated authority. This question is its analogue for groups of
  agents, with norms in place of an update rule.
