# THEORY-tmpc6rib: The human sense of obligation is internalized pressure from a joint agent 'we' to do what 'we' expect of me; it appears around age three inside joint commitments, where children, unlike chimpanzees, protest, repair, take leave and share equally with a committed partner as they do not with others

## Source

Tomasello (2019), [LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md) (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 43, e56, issue
2020), read in [NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md) with 4 of its 32 commentaries in full.

## What was actually shown

**Definition (§3).** The sense of obligation is "the internalized
social/rational pressure from a joint or collective self-regulating agent
'we' … to do what 'we' expect me to". Guilt ("we" think of me) is set
against shame ("they" think of me).

**Joint commitment at about age three (§2.1.2.1).** With a joint commitment,
3-year-olds resist a bribe to defect more than when playing side by side
(Kachel & Tomasello 2019). They wait for, repair for and take over a
partner's role (Gräfenhain et al. 2013). They protest a partner's knowing
failure but not an ignorant one, in generic normative language (Kachel et
al. 2018). They "take leave" when lured away, which 2-year-olds do not
(Gräfenhain et al. 2009).

**Sharing (§2.1.2.2).** 3-year-olds give a peer about one item in four in
dictator games, but split collaborative spoils about equally. The lucky child
equalizes about three-quarters of the time after collaboration and almost
never without it (Hamann et al. 2011). Chimpanzees rarely share equally,
inside or outside collaboration (Melis et al. 2006, 2011).

**Group-minded obligation, 3–5 years (§2.2.2).** In-group favouritism,
conformity overriding preference, protest at norm violations, and
enforcement of self-created norms only on those who agreed to them (Schmidt
et al. 2016b).

**Against reciprocity (§3).** Children who fail to reciprocate do not
apologize (Warneken & Tomasello 2013): tit-for-tat yields gratitude, not
obligation.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Children could have behaved the same
toward partners and non-partners, or chimpanzees could have shared equally
after collaboration. Neither happened.

## What this does not say

- **What anyone ought to do.** The response concedes that "no explanation of
  obligation, scientific or otherwise, can tell us what we ought to do" (R4).
  It is an account of the sense of obligation.
- **That the behaviours are felt obligation.** The alternatives, enhanced
  prosociality, conformity to adult framing and learned scripts, are argued
  against (§2.1.3), not excluded by experiment.
- **That commitment, rather than its surrounding assurances, is the cause.**
  Bratman's commentary (e62) notes that the causal role of commitment is not
  isolated from the "web of assurances".
- **That the sequence is universal.** The samples are WEIRD, as §2.2.2.4
  concedes, and data on cultural differences before age three largely do not
  exist.
- **That obligation is limited to the in-group.** That scoping is contested by
  Killen & Dahl and by Darwall, and by the paper's own account it describes an
  ancestral psychology.
- **That reciprocity cannot generate obligation.** One developmental
  observation and an argument; the author conceded in 2016 that the tie is
  culturally contingent (note 4).
