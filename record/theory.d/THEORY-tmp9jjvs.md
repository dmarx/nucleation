# THEORY-tmp9jjvs: Broersen, Pereira, Jørgensen and Dekkers find, pooling 74 observational studies of adult corticosteroid users, that biochemically suppressed adrenal function after treatment is common, from about 4% after nasal or topical use to about half after oral or intra-articular use; in asthma it rises with dose (2.4%, 8.5%, 21.5%) and with duration (1.4% under a month, 27.4% over a year); it recedes after stopping but incompletely, from 38.7% to 14.9% four weeks after short high-dose courses and from 56.4% to 25.3% six months after long medium-dose use; and they hold that no route, dose or duration excludes it and that no withdrawal scheme has been shown effective or safe

## Source

Broersen, Pereira, Jørgensen and Dekkers (2015), [LIT-tmp5fl4h](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5fl4h.md); read in
[NOTE-tmpjd1tp](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpjd1tp.md). Page numbers are those of the reprint in Broersen's 2019
Leiden thesis.

## What was actually shown

- **Pooling.** Random-effects logistic regression over 74 articles, 136
  study groups, 3,753 adult users tested by insulin tolerance, ACTH, CRH
  or metyrapone test with a cortisol cut-off of about ≤500 nmol/L; 1,190
  insufficient (p. 34).
- **Route** (p. 34, Fig. 1): nasal 4.2%, topical 4.7%, inhaled 7.8%,
  multiple 42.7%, oral 48.7%, intra-articular 52.2%. The intervals for
  nasal and topical use are wide (0.5–28.9%, 1.1–18.5%).
- **Dose and duration** (p. 36, Fig. 3), in asthma patients of all routes:
  2.4%, 8.5%, 21.5% by dose; 1.4%, 11.9%, 27.4% by duration. In
  inhaled-only asthma the same order holds, 1.5%, 5.4%, 18.5% and 1.3%,
  9.0%, 20.3%. The ordering could have come out otherwise; the intervals
  of neighbouring strata overlap.
- **Recovery** (p. 37, Fig. 4): 38.7% to 14.9% (6.8–29.5) at 4 weeks in six
  studies of short high-dose courses; 56.4% to 25.3% (19.4–32.3) at 6
  months in five studies of long medium-dose use, with everyone first
  tested as the denominator. Figure 4 labels the second retest "Re-test
  after 4 weeks"; the text and methods say 6 months.
- **No safe stratum** (p. 28): no form, dose, duration or disease for which
  insufficiency "can be excluded with certainty".
- **No proven withdrawal scheme** (p. 40): asserted from ref. 95 (Dinsen et
  al. 2013), not from data of the paper's own.

## What this does not say

- That longer or heavier supply makes recovery take longer. The two retest
  groups differ in dose, duration and retest interval at once, so they
  cannot separate a slower recovery from a different interval.
- That the share still suppressed at 6 months is permanent, or that it
  reflects a second stable state. The paper shows no threshold or
  bistability; its mechanism, cited not tested, is negative feedback with
  slow recovery.
- That the gradients are causal within patients. They are cross-study
  strata, with route, dose, duration and disease related and not
  disentangled, and study-level exposure for each patient's.
- That suppression is clinically dangerous at these rates. The outcome is
  biochemical, on tests the authors call high in sensitivity rather than
  specificity, and some tests fell within 24 hours of the last dose, when
  residual drug can still suppress.
- That any particular taper works, or fails.
