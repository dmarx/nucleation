  states have been studied by community assembly (species composition,
  competition, priority effects, small scales) and by regime shifts
  (aggregate state, feedbacks through the abiotic environment, large
  scales); the authors argue restoration needs both. A two-species
  Lotka–Volterra model with environmental mortality and facilitation shows
  hysteresis in total abundance, composition changing before the collapse

  fire-suppressed wetland, Japanese lakes after eutrophication, and

# LIT-2165: Merging community assembly into the regime-shift approach for informing ecological restoration

Kadowaki, K., Nishijima, S., Kéfi, S., Kameda, K. O. & Sasaki, T. (2018). Merging community assembly into the regime-shift approach for informing ecological restoration. *Ecological Indicators* 85: 991–998. doi:10.1016/j.ecolind.2017.11.035.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1677](../notes.d/NOTE-1677.md). Page numbers are the journal's.*

- **Restoring the pressure-free condition is not enough when the degraded
  state feeds itself.** In fire-suppressed wetlands "re-introduction of
  fire alone does not restore previous herbaceous communities because
  hardwood species impede the spread of fire, which further facilitates
  hardwood dominance"; restoration adds "mechanical and chemical hardwood
  species removal" (p. 992).
- **Removing the pressure can yield a new undesired state.** After sewer
  systems improved Japanese lakes, "the submerged macrophytes have been
  outcompeted by water chestnut (genus Trapa)", on sludge left by the
  eutrophic phase; removing the sludge locally "succeeded in reducing the
  floating-leaved plants and recovering the submerged plants" (p. 994).
- **Restoring the controller alone did not restore the system.** "Although
  wolves were experimentally reintroduced in 1995, the willows did not
  initially recover", because beaver loss had eroded willow sites (p. 995).
- **Order matters.** In the model, reintroducing the less stress-tolerant
  or slower-growing species first "may fail to restore the community"
  (p. 994).
- **Whether the intervention can then stop is not addressed.** The
  wetland's herbaceous communities "are maintained with prescribed fires"
  (p. 992).

## Standing in the record

Active, as the clearest worked statement held of how a degraded state's
own legacy defeats restoration by removing the pressure, which is the
ecological form of [CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md) and the outage of [CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md). Its
evidence is weak: the cases are narrative, taken from other studies, and
none is tested for hysteresis, so by [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md)'s standard the paper does
not show that any of them has alternative stable states. Filed from it:
[THEORY-1468](../theory.d/THEORY-1468.md).

It stands in for Suding, Gross & Houseman (2004), [LIT-2169](LIT-2169.md), for the
framing only. It cites that paper for sudden transitions, for
regime-shift work on environmental change and for feedbacks as "key
ingredients of alternative stable states and ecological restoration"
(p. 993), and builds on it; it does not reproduce its case list, its
experiments or its argument, and nothing here should be read as Suding
et al.'s finding. No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing
for the anthology.
