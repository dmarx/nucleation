# THEORY-1571: Ramsey argues that a community that lives for ever and does not discount later enjoyments should save at the rate at which saving times the marginal utility of consumption equals the shortfall of present net enjoyment from Bliss, a rule independent of the interest rate, and he calls discounting later enjoyments ethically indefensible, a product of the weakness of the imagination

## The account

"It is assumed that we do not discount later enjoyments in comparison with
earlier ones, a practice which is ethically indefensible and arises merely
from the weakness of the imagination" (p. 543). The community does not
change in numbers, capacity for enjoyment or aversion to labour, and every
generation has the same motives, "so that there is no chance of our savings
being selfishly consumed by a subsequent generation" (p. 544).

If enjoyment can approach a finite maximum, Bliss, any plan that never
approaches it loses an infinite amount, so the problem is to minimize the
total shortfall from Bliss. The rule: "rate of saving multiplied by marginal
utility of consumption should always equal bliss minus actual rate of
utility enjoyed" (p. 547). It is independent of the production function
except through Bliss, and of the interest rate "unless this is actually
zero" (p. 548).

With constant returns, a constant discount rate below the interest rate
gives the same rule for a "modified utility"; one above it runs consumption
down to subsistence (pp. 553–555). Of actual savers, people "are not so
interested in their heirs as in themselves" (p. 557).

## Evidence

Derivation by the calculus of variations, with Keynes's argument beside it,
under strong assumptions: fixed population, unchanging tastes, no
catastrophes, no distribution, additive utility, a finite Bliss. The saving
figure of £300 out of £500 rests on an invented utility scale, "put forward
merely as an illustration" (pp. 548–549). The reading's own check finds the
printed savings share on p. 555 may be a ratio to income consumed, checked
once ([NOTE-1287](../notes.d/NOTE-1287.md), Limitations); that formula is not part of this title.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md) names this work under the impersonal ground: "Ramsey ([LIT-1733](../literature.d/LIT-1733.md))
refused to discount the future merely for being later." Its note of
2026-10-29 files it under "The zero-discount view began as a collective
self's prudence", and calls weakness of imagination "a psychological claim
the question can test". The reading bears on the question's ground of
obligation and on its psychology.
