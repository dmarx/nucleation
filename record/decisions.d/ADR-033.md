# ADR-033: translation joins the topics

## Context

ADR-031 considered a `translation` word and set it aside. Gutt (LIT-784) and
Hofstadter (LIT-766) were then its only works, both unread, and the record's
habit is to wait for a second instance. On 2026-10-09 the owner asked for
the 46 works dropped from the manuscript's bibliography to be registered.
They include five works of translation theory:

- Jakobson's "On Linguistic Aspects of Translation";
- Nida's *Toward a Science of Translating*;
- House's model of translation quality assessment;
- Hatim and Mason's *Discourse and the Translator*;
- Nord's *Translating as a Purposeful Activity*.

The manuscript What Survives Translation? is itself about what a
translation preserves. `linguistics` covers "the study of language itself",
and translation theory is adjacent to that rather than inside it.

## Decision

Add `translation` (Translation): translation and its theory. It covers:

- equivalence and its kinds (formal, dynamic, functional);
- interpretive resemblance;
- register and quality assessment;
- translation as skopos-driven action;
- intersemiotic translation between media;
- what a translation preserves or changes.

The works of translation theory take it first. Gutt and Hofstadter are
retagged with it first. Claims about translation in the argument schemes
may use it.

## Alternatives

- **`linguistics` alone.** It is the nearest word, and that is the problem
  the closed vocabulary exists to prevent.
- **`philosophy-of-language`.** That word fits Gutt's relevance-theoretic
  account and Jakobson's semiotics, but not quality assessment or skopos
  theory, which are applied and professional.
