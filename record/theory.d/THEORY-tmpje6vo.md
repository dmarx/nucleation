# THEORY-tmpje6vo: Umpleby reports that Lefebvre's algebraic model of ethical cognition generates two ethical systems, in the first of which a bad means may not serve a good end and in the second of which it may, and that Lefebvre took the first to dominate in the West and the second in the Soviet Union

## Source

Umpleby (2016), [LIT-tmpmwjhb](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmwjhb.md), a secondary account read as a stand-in for [LIT-tmpor36r](../literature.d/LIT-tmpor36r.md) and [LIT-tmp37qct](../literature.d/LIT-tmp37qct.md). The reading is [NOTE-tmphkehf](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphkehf.md).

## What was actually shown

Umpleby reports the two systems and their cultural reading (pp. 65-66), and that in the first system the compromiser is the saint while in the second the confronter is (p. 66). His evidence for the cultural reading is anecdote and audience polls. Nothing of the model's mathematics is shown.

## What this does not say

- That the cultural differences are established: they are illustrated, not tested.
- The exchange's table (A11 §5), which assigns the systems the other way round.
- Anything about manipulation, or about organizations.
