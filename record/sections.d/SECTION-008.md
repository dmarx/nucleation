# SECTION-008: Recursive maintenance and its limits

## Manuscript text

Recursive individuation becomes relevant when organized processes participate in maintaining or transforming constraints under which those processes operate. Such recursion need not mean independence from the environment. The organization may depend on external materials, energy, and institutions while sustaining a distinctive configuration of causal dependencies within that dependence.

It is nevertheless a mistake to treat any feedback loop as constitutive closure. Reciprocal influences among state variables do not by themselves identify constraints in the organizational sense. A constraint's realization, its effect on activities, its production, and the mechanisms of its repair must be distinguished. Nor does specifying a closure loop establish where the individual begins and ends: maintenance processes may extend across candidate boundaries, or one sustaining practice may reproduce several organizational products.

The organization can also modify its standards of evaluation, but that introduces a further achievement rather than a necessary feature of every individual. A governing rule can be revised without the organization maintaining an effective capacity to enact the revision. An apparent resolution of conflict can be achieved by suppressing one of the requirements whose incompatibility originally motivated change. Endogenous transformation thus does not entail improvement, agency, epistemic warrant, or legitimacy.

These distinctions restrict a proposed universal recursive-individuation theory while preserving a more useful conception. The interesting claim is not that all individuals are self-making. It is that, for some individuals, explaining their persistence requires a theory of how the activities they organize also produce or transform the constraints making those activities possible. Whether this establishes the metaphysical constitution of an individual remains a further, kind-relative question.
