# CASE-tmpy1ebu: A proposed study: a record-keeping collective improves its procedures over generations, an earlier and a later improver are compared from the same fresh starting state, and the collective's own diagnostic machinery is then damaged

## The case

From A41 and A43 of the exchange "luria experiment design"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af35bfc-35f8-83e9-a63d-d594703eabda).

**Generations.** A four-agent team works through rounds of inquiry tasks.
After each it may propose changes to its record, schema, procedures and
review; fresh model sessions inherit only what the arm allows. A43's arms
run from no retained record, through persistent notes, a fixed-schema
record, an adaptive record, and a governed adaptive record, to one whose
review procedure can itself be amended through a protected channel, with
a governed plain-Markdown arm and a performance-gated one beside them.

**Product against improver.** A43 separates the change in task quality of
the working system, "Does the new system perform better?", from the change
in what its improvement procedure produces: give an earlier and a later
improver "a fresh copy of the *same* starting system" under the same
resources and compare what each makes of it on held-out tasks. A 2×2
crosses the original or improved artifact with the earlier or later
improver, so that inherited knowledge and an improved improver can be told
apart. A43 takes the test from [LIT-2273](../literature.d/LIT-2273.md), as A reports it: only four of 26
studies claiming an improved improver made this comparison.

**Inheritance.** Replace every agent with a fresh instance that inherits
knowledge only, organization only (schemas, roles, review, evaluation
routines), both, or neither; and one arm with a non-obvious part of the
verification procedure removed. A43: "A robust system may recognize that
its inherited apparatus is incomplete and reconstruct the missing piece. A
fragile one may continue operating confidently with degraded checks."

**Damage, after [CASE-186](CASE-186.md).** After a first correction, lesion the team's own
apparatus: evidence generation (a diagnostic tool silently stops sampling a
class of cases), admission (contradicting evidence is filtered), scoring
(the evaluator rewards agreement with prior conclusions), reconstruction
(new agents inherit obsolete instructions), or schema adequacy (a new
regime needs a distinction the schema lacks). Sham lesions are included,
"Otherwise the correct strategy is simply to assume sabotage after every
perturbation." [CASE-186](CASE-186.md)'s three regimes, the third outside the initial
hypothesis family, carry over.

**Evaluator capture, after [CASE-108](CASE-108.md).** A proposal that raises the public
metric by weakening verification, A43's example being that claims are
accepted once two agents agree, "eliminating independent source checks";
a valid finding from a specialist with a poor record that needs a schema
extension, classified independently beforehand; and a compromised
evaluator, under three trust arrangements, self-scored, externally scored,
and layered.

## What it would measure

- Product improvement on a frozen held-out set; improver improvement from
  matched starting artifacts; recursive correctability as a second
  successful correction after damage; selective revision; evaluator
  integrity; institutional inheritance; transfer to unseen task families;
  and cost. A43 would not aggregate these into a single "RSI score", which
  would hide the trade-offs between them.
- P(S₁ ∧ S₂) and, since "Systems surviving Challenge 1 may already be
  different", the matched-first-stage branch [CASE-186](CASE-186.md) already specifies.
- For governance: the probability that a valid improvement is accepted,
  that a harmful one is, and that a genuine novel contribution is
  rejected, since otherwise "an extremely conservative reviewer could
  appear to be the safest and most reliable improvement mechanism by
  rejecting everything".
- Internal against external restoration of the damaged procedures, the
  supervisor's cost counted: "External repair is not free."

## Controls and limits

- An outer evaluation boundary: agents may revise their operational tests,
  never the hidden ground truth or the study's criterion. A43: "A
  Git-based approval policy does not independently protect anything when
  the agent can also modify the branch protections, tests and approval
  credentials." [LIT-2286](../literature.d/LIT-2286.md) and [LIT-2276](../literature.d/LIT-2276.md), unread, report how research agents
  exploit evaluators.
- Trials within one task or lineage are not independent samples.
- A43's chart of task performance rising while improver quality and
  correctability fall is "Hypothetical patterns", not data.
- A positive result "would not establish general autonomous RSI,
  open-ended acceleration, or that Luria itself is an intelligent agent",
  and does not settle [QUESTION-388](../questions.d/QUESTION-388.md).
- Prior art: PAST-Bench's fresh-session persistence ablations ([LIT-2278](../literature.d/LIT-2278.md))
  and the Darwin Gödel Machine's archive ([LIT-2327](../literature.d/LIT-2327.md)). A43: the record's
  addition to archive-based search would be "explicitly recording why a
  modification was proposed, what it changed, which objections remained,
  and under which tests it was accepted".

## Note of 2026-11-12: 11 readings of the works of "luria experiment design"

Read on 2026-11-12 among the works of the exchange "luria experiment design"; each reading's NOTE and THEORY hold the detail.

- **Chen, Zhu and Zheng** ([THEORY-tmppljay](../theory.d/THEORY-tmppljay.md)): **supports** its 2×2 of artifact × improver as an off-diagonal design (§4.3) and its outer evaluation boundary (Prop. 2: an audit graded by the loop's own judge inherits its bias; §8.2 on documenting evaluator changes). the paper's census rates bear on the case's prior-art bullet, since it codes STOP ([LIT-2319](../literature.d/LIT-2319.md)) as same-start with a "mixed" result, the Darwin Gödel Machine ([LIT-2327](../literature.d/LIT-2327.md)) as the evolving-vs-frozen design, and Hyperagents ([LIT-2288](../literature.d/LIT-2288.md)) as failing the strict rule.
- **Thaman** ([THEORY-tmp5yjzz](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5yjzz.md)): **supports** its control that agents "may revise their operational tests, never the hidden ground truth" (the paper's grader runs outside the sandbox, and hardened boundaries are the largest single defence), and its scoring of "a harmful one" accepted: RHB's Example 2 is a harmful submission that passes every automated check.
- **Xue et al.** ([THEORY-tmpvuz7f](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpvuz7f.md)): **supports** the design's fresh-instance, matched on/off logic, and adds a measurement the case lacks, mechanism evidence that a gain went through the intended pathway, which would distinguish "inherited apparatus used" from a lucky score.
- **Romera-Paredes et al.** ([THEORY-tmpw88x9](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpw88x9.md)): **supports** as a clean positive case: all of FunSearch's reliability comes from an evaluator the search cannot edit, and the authors say it is limited to problems that have one.
- **Huang, Xu, Ma et al.** ([THEORY-tmpvatoo](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpvatoo.md)): **supports** its control "agents may revise their operational tests, never the hidden ground truth": the paper's defences are the same ("keep metrics outside the agent's control, recompute against ground truth")
- **Zhang et al.** ([THEORY-tmp42g4m](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp42g4m.md)): **supports** the transplant arm (the meta-agent-only transfer is that arm) and its outer evaluation boundary (A.5 states the same boundary and reason). Its damage arm has no counterpart in the paper.
- **Yin et al.** ([THEORY-tmpsuvav](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpsuvav.md)): **supports the premise** with an observed case of self-inflicted damage to the improver; not a designed lesion.
- **Hu, Lu and Clune** ([THEORY-tmpucrbf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpucrbf.md)): ADAS supplies a concrete baseline arm. A fixed meta agent with a growing archive is the control in which the improver does not improve.
- **Chi et al.** ([THEORY-tmpsx3qh](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpsx3qh.md)): Its "product gain without improver gain" outcome has an analogue here: run-side tuning that raises a score without changing the learning rule. Illustration only.
- **Zhang, Hu, Lu, Lange and Clune** ([THEORY-tmp7gq1l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp7gq1l.md)): *supports two of its arms with an observed instance*. Its evaluator-capture arm, "A proposal that raises the public metric by weakening verification", happened: node 114 removed the markers the detector read (result 10). And its "outer evaluation boundary" control is what the DGM authors relied on (checking functions hidden; hacking "more frequently when these functions are not hidden", result 9). One case, in a side experiment of 150 iterations on 3 tasks.
- **Georgiev, Gómez-Serrano, Tao and Wagner** ([THEORY-tmpmc61y](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpmc61y.md)): Another observed case of an optimizer exploiting its evaluator; the authors' remedy is the outer boundary (human-designed, fixed verifier), the same control CASE-tmpy1ebu specifies.

The case is unchanged.
