# QUESTION-337: Does payoff-driven phase synchronisation still form along the strong couplings, and still turn those groups into units that switch as one, when modules overlap or constraints are frustrated, or when entrainment runs through the units' outputs rather than a payoff gradient on phase?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-265](../theory.d/THEORY-265.md) holds Tissot et al.'s result ([LIT-989](../literature.d/LIT-989.md), read in [NOTE-773](../notes.d/NOTE-773.md)) in
the setting they ran: one game of thirty clean, non-overlapping,
consistent modules of ten, couplings 1 within and 0.01 across, one
parameter setting. The authors expect natural interactions to be graded
and frustrated, and they do not claim the mechanism survives that. The
phase rule is a gradient on cycle-averaged payoff, "loosely analogous" to
Kuramoto entrainment, and no run couples units through their outputs.

The sixteenth status review's re-run ([THEORY-265](../theory.d/THEORY-265.md), note of 2026-10-29)
covers part of the scope. With within-module couplings graded, the result
held at across-coupling 0.01. At across-coupling 0.1 or more, random
phases already reach the optimum, so there is no lock-in for timing to
undo. Overlapping modules, frustrated constraints and output coupling were
not run.

## What would answer it

[THEORY-265](../theory.d/THEORY-265.md)'s former promote_when, unchanged:

A replication outside the authors' clean game: modules that overlap,
couplings of graded strength, or frustrated constraints, together with a
sweep of the coupling ratio and the phase rate, showing that payoff-driven
synchrony still forms along the strong couplings and still turns those
groups into units that switch as one. A second route would settle more:
the same result with entrainment through the units' own outputs (a
Kuramoto or HKB coupling) instead of a payoff gradient on phase. It fails
if the effect disappears once modules overlap or the ratio of within to
across coupling falls toward one. What cannot settle it: more runs of the
same 30-by-10 game, or biological synchrony cited by analogy.
