# ARG-113: A stipulated couple in which the partner who strikes more often is the one controlled shows that the count of wrongful acts and control over the terms of the relationship can come apart

## The inference

1. In Relationship B of [CASE-279](../cases.d/CASE-279.md), Carol commits as many wrongful
   violent acts as Dan or more (stipulated: "Perhaps Carol has even struck
   Dan more times than Dan has struck Carol").
2. Dan alone holds control over the terms of Carol's life: her money, her
   messages, her friendships and the cost of her leaving (stipulated).
3. So the party with the larger count of wrongful acts is not the party
   holding control: the two can come apart.
4. Relationship A gives the other direction: mutual wrongful acts with no
   one holding control.
5. If responsibility for the relationship's coercive structure goes with
   control over those terms, a tally of acts does not settle it.

## Critical questions

**Is the case coherent?** Yes. Nothing in it requires that Dan's control
be exercised through violence; A3 §3 gives Stark's point that "the threat of
violence can be enough to enforce control without violence occurring".
Carol's acts and Dan's control are specified independently.

**Does it separate the very things the conclusion says can come apart?**
For the first half of the claim, yes: count of wrongful acts on one side,
control over the terms of the other's life on the other. For the second
half, only with premise 5, which the case does not supply. Premise 5 is
what the same exchange's claim [CLAIM-708](../claims.d/CLAIM-708.md) states, that one can be
responsible for the conditions of another's conduct. Without it, the case
shows a separation in the facts and not yet in responsibility.

## Verdict

Proposed. The existence of the separation is shown; its bearing on
responsibility rests on a second claim filed with this one.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Stark and Hester, read

The argument's answer to "Is the case coherent?" cites A3's attribution to Stark that "the threat of violence can be
enough to enforce control without violence occurring". Read through Stark and Hester (2019), the attribution holds:
coercive control "is not a type of violence", and the review reports entrapment without notable violence in perhaps a
third of cases. No change to the verdict. Relation: none.
