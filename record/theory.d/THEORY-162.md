# THEORY-162: What an utterance does to the context it is used in is part of its meaning, and its truth conditions do not fix it: sentences with the same truth conditions can differ in what they make available to later discourse, in whether they inform or only test, and in whether they describe a state of affairs or bring one about

## Source

Heim (1982), [LIT-838](../literature.d/LIT-838.md), ch. I §1.3 and ch. III, read in [NOTE-608](../notes.d/NOTE-608.md);
Veltman (1996), [LIT-818](../literature.d/LIT-818.md), §1–2, read in [NOTE-617](../notes.d/NOTE-617.md); Krifka (2024),
[LIT-835](../literature.d/LIT-835.md), §2 and §6, read in [NOTE-602](../notes.d/NOTE-602.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Anaphoric potential (Heim).** "I dropped ten marbles and found all of
  them, except for one" and "I dropped ten marbles and found only nine of
  them" are true in the same circumstances, said in the same situation.
  Only the first can be followed by "It is probably under the sofa".
  "John has a spouse" and "John is married" pattern the same way with
  "She is nice". A theory that gives sentences only truth conditions
  (with pragmatic salience on top) must make wording an independent
  factor. Heim's file change semantics explains the contrast because
  the indefinite opens a card. It also shows that two common grounds
  with the same set of worlds can develop differently under the same
  utterance. These are judgement data on constructed examples, and they
  could have come out the other way.
- **Tests (Veltman).** Updates with epistemic "might" (and "presumably")
  pass or fail against the state they meet. They are not persistent, and
  "Maybe it's John… It's Mary… Maybe it's John" is incoherent where the
  same sentences in another order are not. Proposition 1.2 states when a
  dynamic system reduces to a static one: when it is additive. Systems
  with tests are not additive.
- **Performative updates (Krifka).** "Citizens can apply for travel
  abroad" is one proposition. As a declaration it changes every index of
  the context set, as an assertion it adds a guarantee and proposes that
  indices be eliminated. Hedges and question–answer pairs show the two
  readings apart. This is argued from grammatical evidence, not tested.

The common origin is Stalnaker's account of assertion as narrowing a
context set ([LIT-811](../literature.d/LIT-811.md), unread here; known through Heim's quotations
and Krifka's use). Each of the three extends that account where it
falls short.

## What this does not say

- **Not that truth conditions are dispensable.** Each framework recovers
  them: Heim's truth of a file, Veltman's additive fragment, Krifka's
  informative update. The claim is that they underdetermine meaning, not
  that they are wrong.
- **Not that the three formalisms are one theory.** Heim's files,
  Veltman's information states and Krifka's index-changing context sets
  are different objects. Veltman's updates only ever shrink a state;
  Krifka's can add possibilities. Nothing here shows that one framework
  captures all three effects.
- **Not that static rivals have been refuted.** E-type and situation
  accounts of anaphora, and pragmatic accounts of "might", were developed
  after or alongside these works. None was read for this document, and
  the promotion condition asks for exactly that comparison.
- **Not a claim about translation.** None of the sources compares
  utterances in different languages. Applying this to whether a
  rendering preserves an utterance's context change ([CLAIM-061](../claims.d/CLAIM-061.md)) is
  a further step these sources do not take.
- **Not a measurement.** No source gives an observable or an experiment
  that separates the effects. The evidence is intuitive judgement and
  formal construction.
