# CLAIM-tmp4qvx2: That an entry has stopped changing does not show what stopped it: settling is the decay of its contracted features onto the convention, and retention is the start's value on what is copied exactly, so contraction and conservation both settle an entry and are told apart only by retention against a reference fixed outside the record's later restatements, measured as a diagnostic of mechanism and not as a standard of quality

## The claim

CLAIM-tmpvm90n's first test was a count: an entry's revision rate should fall
as its inbound `rests_on` and `grounds` grow. The count is worth making
(LEAD-tmp6do6f), but its result cannot carry the claim alone.

In CLAIM-tmpdx6l8's terms an entry stops changing in two ways. Its
contracted features decay onto the step's fixed point, geometrically
(CLAIM-tmpw6wkt); its conserved features never moved. Both show as a falling
revision rate. They differ in what the settled entry keeps of where it
started: the contracted features keep nothing, and the conserved ones keep
exactly the start's value (CLAIM-635). That is CLAIM-081's distinction,
"convergence is not preservation", between drift and retention as two
profiles of one chain (CLAIM-270).

So a falling rate is predicted both by conservation and by contraction onto
the convention (CLAIM-068). What separates them is retention: how much of
the content at the originating turn the settled entry keeps, judged against
a reference fixed before the record restated it, in a metric fixed in
advance (CLAIM-208's demand). LEAD-tmp84yvk is that measure.

**Relation to ARG-028.** ARG-028's open critical question, carried from
CLAIM-190, is that the anchoring measures are computed in the grammar that
shapes the argument, so they cannot certify that drift fell. This claim
names a mechanism for that worry: contraction onto the grammar's own
convention produces the same settling the measures read as anchoring. Its
remedy is ARG-028's, a reference the record did not supply, applied to
retention rather than to drift.

## What it does not say

- Not that retention is the goal. CLAIM-189 holds that a developing argument
  legitimately drops and changes things, so fidelity is the wrong measure of
  its worth. Here retention is a diagnostic: it says which part of the step
  settled an entry, not whether the settled entry is better.
- Not that the revision-rate count is uninformative. Crossed with retention
  it separates four cases: settled and retained (conserved), settled and not
  retained (contracted), unsettled and retained, unsettled and not retained
  (still copied with error).
