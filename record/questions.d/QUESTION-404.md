# QUESTION-404: Is a failure of information, where a path exists and the inputs could execute it but the system cannot tell which state it is in or which response its context calls for, a fifth obstruction to a transition beside the four obstructions the record's account of failed transitions names, or a kind of control limitation, and what repairs it?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-568](../claims.d/CLAIM-568.md) sorts failed transitions into four obstructions, each with its
remedy: capacity bottleneck, geometric disconnection, control limitation and
dynamic instability. Its body says the four are not claimed exhaustive, and
that a failure from missing information "fits uneasily under control
limitation". [CLAIM-556](../claims.d/CLAIM-556.md) adds compatibility and regeneration and says neither
table has a level for information.

The record holds one construction of the case. In [CASE-194](../cases.d/CASE-194.md), three
organizations fail the same task at 50%; in the observational arm the
observation is identical in both contexts, and supplying observation repairs
it while enlarging actuation does not. [CASE-194](../cases.d/CASE-194.md)'s own title reads it as
infeasible "only relative to its observation-limited policies", which would
make it control limitation over a restricted policy class. Its repair,
though, is none of the four remedies.

## What would answer it

Either of two results. An argument that every failure of information is a
control limitation once the admissible inputs are taken to be the policies
the system's observations allow, with "supply observation" a case of
"change policy". Or a construction in a declared model where a transition
fails for want of information alone, and none of the four remedies touches
the failure while supplying information does. That would make information a
fifth obstruction with its own repair.
