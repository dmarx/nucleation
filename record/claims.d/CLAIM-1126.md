# CLAIM-1126: Integration needs a variable owned as the whole's

## The claim

[CLAIM-1168](CLAIM-1168.md) concedes that a predator–prey oscillation meets [CLAIM-327](CLAIM-327.md)'s
three conditions. If a difference in kind between a pseudo-self and an
ecosystem of pseudo-selves survives, it needs a further condition: that the
members address the whole-level variable as the whole's own. A firm's
employees act on its culture as theirs; a sector's members keep a standard
as the sector's. A predator does not treat the cycle as the community's; it
treats prey as prey.

This brings the criterion near [TERM-166](../terms.d/TERM-166.md)'s requirement of addressable
perspectives: the variable is held from a perspective that takes the whole
as its object.

## Admission

By a discriminating prediction: the integration diagnostic should separate
pluralities whose members address a shared variable as theirs from those
coupled only through a regenerated cycle, at the same timescale and
coupling strength. [CLAIM-1153](CLAIM-1153.md) predicts no such separation at a fixed
regime.

## Its weak point

"Addressed as the whole's own" needs a test of its own, or it is a free
parameter that the integration test would have to supply; the defeater names
this. It also risks making integration depend on representation, which the
record's organizational terms otherwise avoid.
