# NOTE-tmplluya: Kapoor et al., prefrontal decoding of rivalry without report, read for whether its no-report design removes post-perceptual cognition

## Contribution

Direct recordings of prefrontal populations during "a no-report paradigm,
capable of instigating internally generated transitions in conscious
perception, without changes in visual stimulation" (abstract). The
authors set it against the view that prefrontal activity in consciousness
paradigms reflects report or feedforward input, and conclude that
"internally generated changes in the contents of conscious visual
perception are reliably reflected within the activity of prefrontal
populations in the absence of volitional reports or changes in sensory
input."

## Key results

- **Design** (Results, first section). Physical alternation (PA) trials and
  binocular rivalry (BR) trials beginning with binocular flash suppression
  (BFS); upward against downward drifting gratings or dots; the percept
  read from the polarity of the optokinetic nystagmus (OKN). Dominance
  durations were gamma-distributed, as in reporting observers.
- **Units follow the percept.** 987 units across two animals (734 and 253).
  Of stimulus-selective units, 84.21% (BFS) and 76.09% (BR) fired more on
  average when their preferred stimulus was dominant. Units firing more
  during suppression were "a minority in PFC" (2.92% and 4.12%).
- **Decoding and invariance** (Results, "Decoding"). Classifiers trained on
  PA generalised to BR and back "with near-perfect accuracy (levels reaching up to 95%, when computed over data collated across both animals". The authors read the
  generalisation as content, not stimulus: "the population activity
  explicitly encodes the stimulus that enters awareness with minimal
  representation of the suppressed stimulus."
- **Not only eye movements.** Motion content was decoded with the eyes held
  still (Fixation On) and generalised across Fixation On and Off.
- **Against the imaging nulls** (Discussion): "Our findings are in contrast to the conclusions of recent imaging studies suggesting PFC's reduced involvement in conscious perception", which they put down to BOLD's resolution
  and univariate analysis; selectivity for opposite directions sat about
  0.4 mm apart on the array.
- **What was removed.** "we never trained the animals participating in the current study to categorize or discriminate motion direction and report their perception", which the authors say precludes "an expectation that they were mentally generating an action, which was then withheld."

## Assumptions

- That OKN polarity tracks the percept in the monkey as in reporting
  observers (cited, not tested here).
- That removing trained report removes the post-perceptual confound. The
  authors name "decision-making, introspection or preparation and
  execution of motor reports" as the confound and address the motor and
  rule-based parts only.
- That PA-BR generalisation means content rather than a stimulus code that
  happens to follow dominance.

## Concepts

- **No-report paradigm**: perception inferred from a reflex (OKN), with no
  trained report.
- **Cross-trial-type invariance**: a decoder trained on physical
  alternation that classifies rivalrous percepts, taken as the mark of
  conscious content.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md)** (contrast; the key bearing). This is Panagiotaropoulos's
  group read first-hand, as that account's promote_when asked. The
  switches were "unreported but clearly occurring spontaneous transitions
  in conscious perception" (Discussion): conspicuous. So the design
  removes report and trained action, not noticing the switch or thinking
  about the percept, which [THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md) says no-report designs leave in
  place. Its finding, percept-specific prefrontal content without report,
  is what [THEORY-094](../theory.d/THEORY-094.md) predicts such a design will find either way, so it
  neither promotes nor refutes the account. The refutation needs the same
  content "unchanged when the change is made inconspicuous, at matched
  sensitivity". Kapoor et al. have the sensitivity; Brascamp et al.
  ([LIT-tmp5rny4](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5rny4.md), [NOTE-tmprcujk](NOTE-tmprcujk.md)) have the inconspicuous change. No study the
  search found has both (scratch/studies/lead-112/search.md, E3, E8).
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md)** (evidence on the no-report kind). It shows the content
  reaches prefrontal cortex without report. It cannot tell whether that is
  experience or access, since an unreported but noticed percept is
  accessed in the workspace sense. It also extends report-free evidence to
  a non-human primate, which bears on the question's second half only
  within primates.
- **[QUESTION-061](../questions.d/QUESTION-061.md)** (bears, against a compartment here). The rivalrous
  content was present in prefrontal populations as in the temporal lobe
  ("remarkably similar to those observed in the temporal lobe of macaques
  participating in report based BR"), with the suppressed content
  minimally represented. That is uniform presence across these two
  consumers for the dimension tested, direction of motion. It does not
  test a content absent from one consumer and present to another.
- **[THEORY-096](../theory.d/THEORY-096.md)** (consistent) and **[THEORY-098](../theory.d/THEORY-098.md)** (contrast). The result is
  what the global workspace account expects of a conscious content. Against
  Block's account, it places content in the prefrontal machinery of
  access, but only for noticed contents, which that account also allows to
  be accessed.

## Limitations

- Two animals; chronic arrays, so units may repeat across sessions (the
  authors note this and decode within sessions too); generalisation across
  sessions was limited (57.70% to 65.09%).
- The authors concede "the lack of a transient response during BR could
  possibly also result from a jitter of the perceptual changes relative to
  OKN changes across trials."
- Gap in the argument: the step from the absence of trained report to no
  post-perceptual processing is asserted, not shown. Attention to,
  noticing of and thought about a conspicuous switch are not measured or
  manipulated.
- Its claim that stronger decoding elsewhere is "unlikely" is not tested
  in other areas.
