# NOTE-1617: Jules et al. — Delicate memory structure of origami switches

## Contribution

An experimental mechanical memory built from cylindrical Kresling origami,
whose transition graph the Preisach model predicts from the measured order
of the switching thresholds alone; a readout protocol by height and
stiffness, with an account of where it fails; and the discovery and
graph-theoretic analysis of defect states admitted by hidden elastic
degrees of freedom, which are stable but unreachable under the declared
loading and are left only irreversibly.

## Key insight

The state space a memory has depends on the transformations one admits.
Under uniaxial loading with ends that cannot rotate, the stack has 16
states, all reachable. The material's elasticity lets a pair of barrels
collapse half-folded, so a richer control, twisting by hand, reaches
further stable states that no admissible load reaches and that the load,
once applied, can only destroy.

## Assumptions

- **Quasi-static loading** of modules in series under one applied force;
  modules treated as independent hysterons (§II.A).
- **Ends clamped against rotation**, so unit cells of opposite chirality
  collapse in pairs ("barrels"); flat-foldability imposed for every
  pattern, with n = 5 (§I).
- **Crease plasticity and aging** are acknowledged as error, not modelled
  (§II.D).

## Key results

- **All 16 reachable (abstract).** "we can drive the bellows to any of its
  16 allowable states by following a prescribed sequence of compression
  and extension".
- **Order alone (§II.A).** The Preisach graph's topology depends on the
  threshold fields not "per se, but only their relative ordering"; "if
  these two orders are equal, there are no GoE microstates".
- **Readout (§II.B).** By height, "a precise and unequivocal reading of
  the configuration does not arise from our analysis"; "stiffness is a
  worse independent identification tool since we observe more overlap
  between microstates", though it separates some pairs, such as 1001 and
  0110. The number of deployed modules is read unambiguously.
- **Defects by hand (§II.C).** Found "by carefully twisting parts of a
  partially compressed bellows"; elasticity "allows for opposite halves of
  slightly different pattern angle to collapse together".
- **Incomplete coverage (§II.C).** "we only managed to observe 39 out of
  the 48 microstates with a single defect with manual actuation before the
  sample became too damaged to continue"; two-defect configurations "were
  not stable". The 64 counts 16 + 48 (appendix B).
- **Unreachable (§II.C).** "there is no transition from a reachable
  microstate to a defective configuration". Eight isolated GoE states,
  five 2-GoE loops and two 4-GoE loops were found; the counting formula
  predicts three more 2-GoE loops than were recorded.
- **Irreversible exits (§II.D).** "Erasure: The defect disappears, and the
  system shifts to the main cycle." "Shift: The defect moves to a stiffer
  module."
- **Coupling (§II.D, appendix C).** Defects never appear spontaneously in
  the stack: "there is insufficient coupling between modules to drive it".
  In a single coupled pattern (appendix C) they appear and some are
  reachable.
- **Scaling (§III).** "the material we use and our fabrication method limit
  the scalability to only a few barrels before a precise reading becomes
  impossible".

## Concepts

- **Stable microstate**: a configuration that does not transition
  spontaneously over some range of applied force.
- **Reachable microstate**: one reachable from an absorbing state (0000 or
  1111) under arbitrary force protocols; the others are **Garden of Eden
  (GoE)** microstates, which cannot be returned to once left (§II.A; the
  term goes back to Moore and Tukey, via von Neumann's automata).
- **Preisach graph**: the transition graph on reachable states, with
  nested sub-loops by return-point memory.
- **k-GoE loop**: a strongly connected component of k GoE states with
  identical defects; a 1-GoE loop is an isolated GoE state.

## Connections

It applies the Preisach transition-graph analysis of Terzi and Mungan, and
its defect mechanism rests on the hidden elastic degrees of freedom of
Silverberg et al. (2015, its ref. 49). The analysis by strongly connected
components is the same method as [THEORY-1233](../theory.d/THEORY-1233.md)'s for better-response graphs,
in another substrate.

## Bearing on the record

- **The synthesis's description.** The pasted synthesis on constraint
  architectures cites it as S4: four Kresling units, 16 nominal states,
  return-point memory, defects expanding the state space "from 16 to 64
  states", "Garden of Eden states", states discriminated by height and
  stiffness. "Garden of Eden states" is the abstract's phrase; "from 16 to
  64 states" is A's paraphrase (the abstract says the defects "expand the
  configuration space to 64 states"). Three corrections.
  - **The 64 is a counting convention.** It counts 16 Preisach states and
    48 possible single-defect states; only 39 of the 48 were observed
    before the sample failed, and the 6 two-defect states were unstable.
  - **The defects were reached only by hand.** Under the declared control,
    uniaxial force, they are unreachable; they exist under a richer one,
    local twisting. The paper is a clean instance of the synthesis's point
    that impossibility is relative to the admitted transformations, but it
    shows this by an operation outside the protocol, not by loading.
  - **Height and stiffness do not identify states unequivocally.** The
    authors say so themselves (§II.B) and name reading precision as the
    limit on scaling (§III). This supports, better than the synthesis's
    own gloss, its later point that memory must be legible.
- **[TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md) (legibility).** An example: the microstate is a distinction
  the height observer cannot fully recover, while the macrostate, the number
  deployed, is legible.
- **[CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md) (legibility, volitional efficacy, correctability).** Loosely
  qualifies it by analogy: the paper separates stability in time,
  writability by external action and non-destructive readout as three
  properties of a memory (§III), and writing and reading fail separately
  here. Mechanical, not normative; an illustration only.
- **[CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md) and [CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md).** Analogies, no more: leaving a GoE loop by
  erasure is not undone by reversing the load, and a shift enlarges the
  defect loop irreversibly, changing what is reachable afterwards.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md) (composing interactions makes constraints).** Qualifies: the
  series composition of decoupled units adds no new constraint (a pure
  Preisach system); new transitions appear only with coupling between units
  (appendix C).
- **[CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md), [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md), [QUESTION-090](../questions.d/QUESTION-090.md), [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)**, cited in the
  synthesis's part on this work: no bearing beyond metaphor.
- Filed: [THEORY-1415](../theory.d/THEORY-1415.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- **Four barrels**, one sample per configuration; the authors expect
  reading to fail after a few barrels.
- **Crease aging**: some observed transitions contradict the rules ("We
  argue that these discrepancies come from the aging of the creases").
- **Defects by hand**, with incomplete coverage (39 of 48) and a damaged
  sample; three predicted 2-GoE loops unrecorded.
- **Readout error bars** from the uncertainty in the barrel length and the
  equilibrium height do not allow an indisputable identification.
