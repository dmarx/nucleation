# LIT-1382: Event perception: A mind-brain perspective

Zacks, J. M., Speer, N. K., Swallow, K. M., Braver, T. S., & Reynolds, J. R. (2007). Event perception: A mind-brain perspective. *Psychological Bulletin*, 133(2), 273–293. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.133.2.273.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1084](../notes.d/NOTE-1084.md). The manuscript has no journal pagination;
references are to sections.*

- **The theory.** "Perceptual systems continuously make predictions about
  what will happen next. When transient errors in predictions arise, an
  event boundary is perceived" (Abstract). Event models are working-memory
  representations, reset at boundaries.
- **The memory prediction.** "Information presented prior to an event
  boundary should be difficult to retrieve following that boundary",
  supported chiefly by narrative time shifts (Speer & Zacks 2005).
- **Its limits, by its authors.** "The theory in its current form is a
  purely passive perceiver", and the anterior cingulate response it
  predicts at boundaries was not found.

## Why this is here

A37 read the doorway effect through event segmentation: an intention may lose accessibility "because the current situation is being organized through a different event model". The case is [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** The paper does not mention doorways.
Its mechanism, a reset that makes what preceded a boundary harder to
retrieve, differs from the doorway authors' interference account, since the
doorway's cost falls on the object carried across the boundary. And it is a
theory of perceiving others' activity: an intention lost at a boundary is an
extension of it, not a consequence. See [NOTE-1084](../notes.d/NOTE-1084.md) and [THEORY-886](../theory.d/THEORY-886.md).
