# QUESTION-086: Can two systems with matching behaviour and persistence be told apart by whether their own processes generate and revise the constraints that keep them going, and by what intervention?

## Why it is a question

A11 §6: "**Q4. Are organizational persistence and self-constitution
experimentally distinguishable?** Can we construct systems with comparable
behavior and persistence, but different causal capacities to generate and
revise the constraints responsible for their continuing organization?" And:
"I would be particularly careful about keeping Q1 and Q4 separate ... one
concerns the constitutive status of endogenous governance, while the other
concerns whether we can empirically discriminate proposed forms of
self-constitution." [CLAIM-284](../claims.d/CLAIM-284.md) is the record's version of Q1. This is
Q4.

A10 §3.3 proposed the pair. "System A maintains a stable pattern through its
own internally generated responses. System B maintains the same visible
pattern through an external controller. Their observable outputs may be
identical. But removing the external controller destroys B's stability". It
then made the question hard. "An internal regulator at one scale is itself
supported by other processes at another scale." So which side of a boundary
the controller sits on depends on where the boundary is drawn.

What the record holds already:

- [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md) says a gap or coupling criterion cannot tell a membrane the
  cell makes from a beam joining two pendulums. That rules out one family of
  passive tests.
- Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde ([THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md)) require that the system
  itself distinguish itself from its environment, with no observer doing it,
  and they reject statistical and energetic measures of asymmetry as
  sufficient.
- Segura 2026 ([LIT-1030](../literature.d/LIT-1030.md), unread) is reported, unconfirmed (its abstract does not say it), to say that agreement on
  a set of assays implies the same organization only for assay sets that are
  conservative for a class of candidate models. If so, any answer is
  relative to such a class.

An answer would name an intervention short of removing the controller, such
as a perturbation of the maintained constraints or a lesion of one
constituent process, together with the conditions under which it separates
the pair. Or it would argue that nothing short of removal can, which would
make self-constitution a counterfactual property only.

## Note of 2026-10-18: five second appearances

The exchange of 2026-10-17 met this question five times and never cited
it.

- **A5 §3, a maintenance score.** Δ_B(h) = E[L(B_{t+h}^disabled, B*) −
  L(B_{t+h}^intact, B*)], positive when removing the internal maintenance
  pathway worsens recovery toward a target boundary regime B*. Its own
  limit is this question: "A system with externally imposed regulation
  could pass unless the intervention distinguishes maintenance by
  constituents from maintenance by the environment."
- **A7 §3, the pair and four interventions.** Activity x, boundary b and
  repair resources r: ẋ = −x + bφ(x) + d_x, ḃ = −ℓb + q + d_b,
  ṙ = p − cq − d_r. Architecture E is repaired from outside,
  q(t) = q_ext(t); architecture S repairs itself, q(t) = k·r(t)·σ(b* −
  b(t)). "One can tune an external controller so that architectures E and
  S produce approximately the same ordinary trajectories. Consequently,
  **passive observations may not distinguish their causal
  organization**." The design: "damage the boundary, disable internal
  repair, interrupt external support, and restore each pathway
  independently." A7 drafted a demonstrated claim and set its own
  condition: "To count as demonstrated, the paired models need explicit
  parameters and a full input-output equivalence argument for the
  selected observational regime." The exchange did not meet it, so the
  claim is not filed. A7 also named the regress, "where does the internal
  controller itself get maintained?", which is A10 §3.3's scale problem
  above, and the negative control, "an externally programmed controller
  that adapts its own rules", which is [CASE-081](../cases.d/CASE-081.md) and [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md).
- **A24's Proposition 1.** "Construct two systems with the same observed
  linearized interaction structure over a specified operating regime. One
  has a boundary parameter maintained externally; the other has a variable
  representing a boundary maintained through internal processes." Its
  conclusion: "An observational coupling criterion cannot generally
  identify the source of boundary maintenance." Written out with
  parameters, it would demonstrate [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md). A24 only sketches it.
- **A44, the question again.** It proposed "an open question asking
  whether systems with identical outward behavior can be distinguished by
  how their evaluation, influence, and revision mechanisms are organized",
  which is this question.
- **A44's prediction.** "**the way coherence is achieved predicts how
  coherence fails**." "Enforced coordination may fail when its controller
  is disabled. Preference alignment may fail when incentives shift.
  Coupled agreement may collapse after perturbing the feedback network."
  These are candidate answers to "by what intervention": each mechanism
  is exposed by its own perturbation, short of removing anything.
