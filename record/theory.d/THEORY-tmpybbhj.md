# THEORY-tmpybbhj: Mental disorders are maintained by self-reinforcing causal loops rather than expressing a latent common cause, which is how they can outlast their triggers

## Source

- Borsboom (2017), [LIT-547](../literature.d/LIT-547.md), read in full in [NOTE-435](../notes.d/NOTE-435.md): the network theory's
  five principles, of which the fifth is hysteresis.
- Salkovskis, Sighvatsson and Sigurdsson (2024), [LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-428](../notes.d/NOTE-428.md): maintenance by a threat meaning ("Theory A") and the
  behaviour that protects it.
- Kendler (2008), [LIT-539](../literature.d/LIT-539.md), read in full in [NOTE-432](../notes.d/NOTE-432.md): non-aggregative,
  loop-ridden causation across levels.

## What was actually shown

**Borsboom ([LIT-547](../literature.d/LIT-547.md)) states the account as theory.** In general medicine a
tumour is a common cause: remove it and the symptoms go together. For most
mental disorders no such cause has been found ([NOTE-435](../notes.d/NOTE-435.md), C1, cited to
Kendler's later papers). Borsboom's answer is that the symptoms cause one
another, often prosaically ("If you do not sleep, you get tired"), and
that a strongly connected network has two stable states. Principle 5:
"symptoms continue to activate each other, even after the triggering cause
of the disorder has disappeared". Health is the stable state of a weakly
connected network, and disorder the alternative stable state of a strongly
connected one. The evidence for direct symptom–symptom causation is
indirect by the paper's own account: conditional associations, and
patients' and clinicians' reports (C2). Hysteresis rests on cited
simulations of "realistically parameterized" networks (C5), which the
record has not read. Experience-sampling work on critical slowing down is
cited and not read.

**Salkovskis et al. ([LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md)) reach the loop from Beckian CBT.** Problems
are sustained "not by their original causes but by maintaining factors",
because 50 years have produced no real advance on causes ([NOTE-428](../notes.d/NOTE-428.md), C3).
The central maintaining factor is a meaning. The person who reads
palpitations as a heart attack avoids, neutralises or seeks safety, and
that behaviour keeps the meaning from being disconfirmed. They call these
"vicious circles which trap them". Their evidence that cutting the link
helps is that exposure framed as a test of the threat belief beat exposure
alone in three cited trials (C5). All three are co-authored by Salkovskis.

**Kendler ([LIT-539](../literature.d/LIT-539.md)) finds loops across levels.** In his alcohol-dependence
sketch, tolerance raises consumption, temperament selects risky peers, and
offspring of heavy drinkers abstain more often. He also cites a fearful
child's avoidance preventing habituation ([NOTE-432](../notes.d/NOTE-432.md), C3). Loops are one of
his three reasons that psychiatric causation cannot be decomposed and
simply added up.

**What the three share, and where they differ.** Each puts a
self-reinforcing loop where a single latent cause would be, and each says
the loop, not the start, is what keeps the person ill. They differ on what
the loop connects. Borsboom's nodes must be the manuals' symptoms
(Principle 2), so appraisals and safety behaviours have to enter as
constituents of a symptom or of a connection. Salkovskis's loop runs
through exactly those. Kendler's runs across genes, persons and cultures.
That the three converge without citing one another for it is the
account's strength. That none of them measures a loop in an individual
patient is why it is Proposed.

## How it bears on causation, composition and emergence

- **Why "the disorder" is not a cause of its symptoms on this account.**
  On Borsboom's definitions a disorder is a state of the symptom network,
  so it is composed of the symptoms, not something behind them. Hazelwood
  ([LIT-156](../literature.d/LIT-156.md), [NOTE-137](../notes.d/NOTE-137.md)) argues, from Woodward's independent-fixability
  condition, that a whole cannot stand in a causal relation to its own
  parts: an intervention on one is already an intervention on the other.
  Applied here, which is the record's application and not either author's,
  the network theory's refusal to let the disorder cause its symptoms is
  not just an empirical bet. It follows from treating the disorder as
  composed of them. The empirical bet is the other half: that the
  symptoms, which are independently fixable, cause one another.
- **The causation is interventionist.** Borsboom defines a connection by
  what an intervention on one symptom would do to the probability of the
  other (Woodward, Pearl). Symptom-to-symptom links are within one level,
  so the exclusion problem Pernu ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md)) analyses for mental causes does
  not arise for them. It does arise for Kendler's cross-level loops; the
  companion account [THEORY-tmpbzmql](THEORY-tmpbzmql.md) says why his examples survive it.
- **Hysteresis is borrowed from the physics of phase transitions** without
  an order parameter or a measured transition ([NOTE-435](../notes.d/NOTE-435.md), on Rizi,
  [LIT-150](../literature.d/LIT-150.md)). That is the gap `promote_when` asks to close.

## What this does not say

- **Nothing about onset.** Loops explain persistence after a trigger.
  Borsboom himself scopes the theory to episodic disorders with a clear
  onset and sets aside autism, personality disorders and parts of
  schizophrenia ([NOTE-435](../notes.d/NOTE-435.md), C9).
- **Not that there are no common causes anywhere.** A disorder with a
  found common cause would fall outside the account, not refute it
  elsewhere.
- **Not which loop matters.** Whether the important links are among
  symptoms, through meanings, or across levels is open. So is whether CBT
  works by weakening a link (Borsboom's network intervention) or by moving
  a symptom.
- **Not that the loops are psychological rather than biological.** Borsboom
  is agnostic about how a connection is realised. Kendler's include
  pharmacological tolerance.

## Connections

- **Psychological inflexibility ([THEORY-tmpdbe64](THEORY-tmpdbe64.md)) is compatible, not a
  rival.** [NOTE-431](../notes.d/NOTE-431.md) notes that ACT puts one process behind many syndromes
  while Borsboom denies most disorders a common cause. But ACT's process is
  itself a loop: avoidance makes the avoided event more salient and so
  narrows behaviour further ([LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md)). On Borsboom's terms inflexibility
  would be a constituent of many connections, not a latent disease. The two
  conflict only if inflexibility is read as a cause behind the symptoms
  that sustains them whatever their connections, and neither source reads
  it that way.
- **Process-based therapy ([LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md))** treats complex networks as "an
  extension of functional analysis" and an alternative to the latent
  disease model, which is this account's negative half.
- Engel's biopsychosocial model ([LIT-548](../literature.d/LIT-548.md)) and Kendler 2005 ([LIT-552](../literature.d/LIT-552.md)) are
  the unread background both Borsboom and Kendler descend from.
