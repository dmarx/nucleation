  a dynamic discrete-choice model in which loot boxes can have "functional"
  value (better items raise win probability) and "direct" value (utility
  from opening, plus state dependence). Estimating separately for "whales"
  (top spenders) and others, they find regular players value loot boxes

# LIT-2401: What Makes Players Pay? An Empirical Investigation of In-Game Lotteries

Tomomichi Amano and Andrey Simonov (2023). "What Makes Players Pay? An Empirical Investigation of In-Game Lotteries." *SSRN Electronic Journal*, working paper. DOI-10.2139/ssrn.4355019.

## Key takeaways

Skimmed on 2026-11-12 ([NOTE-1747](../notes.d/NOTE-1747.md)). First revealed-preference
(structural) measure of why players buy loot boxes, separating gameplay
complementarity from the thrill of the lottery, with policy counterfactuals.
