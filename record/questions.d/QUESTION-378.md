# QUESTION-378: Under what conditions does coupling two locally rigid organizational subsystems create new adaptive degrees of freedom at their interface?

## Why it is a question

A cites a localized flexible mode "at the interface between two mechanically rigid regions" and proposes that
composition can produce localized freedoms. Asked as A asks it, under
conditions, the mechanical version has answers, and they are
conditional.

- **Distinct phases, and the right orientation.** Chen et al.
  ([NOTE-1624](../notes.d/NOTE-1624.md); [THEORY-1369](../theory.d/THEORY-1369.md)): the flexible interface is a domain wall between
  kirigami of different topological polarization; equal polarizations
  give no such mode, walls of the other orientation are stressed lines
  that carry load, and triangulated origami appears to have zero
  polarization and so cannot host one. Kane and Lubensky ([NOTE-1601](../notes.d/NOTE-1601.md);
  [THEORY-1408](../theory.d/THEORY-1408.md)): at a domain wall zero modes "arise in a location where
  there is no local mismatch", floppy modes or states of self-stress by
  the sides' polarizations.
- **Internal freedom bought with external freedom.** Chen, Choi and
  Mahadevan ([NOTE-1574](../notes.d/NOTE-1574.md); [THEORY-1368](../theory.d/THEORY-1368.md)): a link joining two components
  removes three rigid-body degrees of freedom and adds one internal one,
  (Δ_i, Δ_r) = (+1, −3). The coupled system has a new relative motion and
  fewer motions overall.
- **Coupling, not juxtaposition.** Jules et al. ([NOTE-1617](../notes.d/NOTE-1617.md);
  [THEORY-1415](../theory.d/THEORY-1415.md)): decoupled bistable units stacked in series are a pure
  Preisach memory, and new transitions appear only with coupling between
  units.
- **In FACT, by stacking.** New freedoms come from bodies in series, not
  from added constraints (Hopkins 2010, [NOTE-1596](../notes.d/NOTE-1596.md); [THEORY-1390](../theory.d/THEORY-1390.md)).

[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md) holds that composing interactions can turn an interior into a
constraint, sometimes of a new kind. These readings add the other side:
composition can also turn a boundary into a freedom, under conditions.
[QUESTION-283](QUESTION-283.md) asks the neighbouring question of whether a composite needs a
richer vocabulary than its parts.

## What would answer it

For organizations, an account of what plays the parts of rigidity,
coupling and phase: when two subsystems that each admit no internal
adjustment, joined by a stated kind of link, have joint adjustments that
neither has, and when the same joining instead loads the interface with
conflict that neither side can relieve. A mechanical answer stated in
transferable terms (a local count plus a topological count, as in Kane
and Lubensky) would answer it in part.

## What would not answer it

An instance in which coupled rigid parts gained a freedom, with no
condition on when. The readings show the same coupling can as easily
give a stressed interface.
