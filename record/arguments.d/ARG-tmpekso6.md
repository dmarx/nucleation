# ARG-tmpekso6: Relativistic frames vary descriptions systematically under constrained transformations with invariants, so variations in translation are transformations between pragmatic frames preserving selected invariants

## The inference

A18 took three features of inertial frames: "Frame dependence is systematic",
transformations between frames "are constrained", and "Invariants permit us to
identify the same physical structure". If renderings of one act in different
pragmatic frames ([TERM-tmpdb2i3](../terms.d/TERM-tmpdb2i3.md)) behave alike, translation variation is a change of
frame with selected invariants preserved ([CLAIM-tmpt5uc1](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt5uc1.md)).

## Critical questions

- **In what respect are the cases alike?** Description varies with frame, and
  something is held fixed across the variation.
- **What transfers?** "a research program inspired by relativity's
  architecture: **identify frames operationally, characterize transformations
  between them, and investigate their invariants**."
- **What does not?** The group structure: "Lorentz transformations are
  invertible, compose according to a precise rule, and preserve a specified
  mathematical structure. Real translation operations generally have none of
  those guarantees" ([CLAIM-tmpamojl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpamojl.md)). Nor the source of the invariants: in physics they
  "arise from the mathematical structure of the theory"; in translation "we
  must discover—or negotiate—which communicative properties deserve
  invariance". A18 disclaimed the literal reading: "I would avoid saying that
  speech acts are literally relativistic".

Rejected as an inference to [CLAIM-tmpt5uc1](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpt5uc1.md). A30 left the coordinate picture behind ([TERM-tmppital](../terms.d/TERM-tmppital.md)),
and the manuscript has no relativity in it; what it kept is [CLAIM-tmpse4aa](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpse4aa.md).
