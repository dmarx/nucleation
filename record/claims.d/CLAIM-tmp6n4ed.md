# CLAIM-tmp6n4ed: Enshittification of search is the engine optimizing its own ranking against its users

## The claim

[THEORY-150](../theory.d/THEORY-150.md) gives the three stages of platform decay. The platform is first
good to its users, then abuses them to favour its business customers, then
abuses both to claw the value back, each stage made possible by the lock-in
the last built. In search, the second and third stages are the engine
placing ads, sponsored results and its own properties above what the user
needs.

That is what publishers' optimization does, from the other side. Both put
something the ranking's beneficiary profits from in place of relevance. On
[DISTINCTION-tmp8wqwj](../distinctions.d/DISTINCTION-tmp8wqwj.md)'s test, both are proxy optima. The page optimized for
rank satisfies the ranking. The ranking optimized for revenue satisfies the
engine's objective. Both fail the user's need.

[THEORY-470](../theory.d/THEORY-470.md) says decay follows the weakening of four constraints: competition,
regulation, interoperability and workers' bargaining power. That is [CLAIM-1287](CLAIM-1287.md)
for platforms: a narrow optimizer comes to value weakening its constraints,
with no change in the character of those who run it.

## Why it may not reverse

[THEORY-1701](../theory.d/THEORY-1701.md)'s model has quality content and its absence both stable on a
platform. A long enough period of rewarding cheap content tips the system
into a decline that restoring the original payoff does not undo. Publishers'
optimization and the engine's decay both reward cheap content, and so does
LLM-written content made for retrieval ([CLAIM-tmpejuha](CLAIM-tmpejuha.md)). On this model the
web's quality is not guaranteed to come back when the ranking is fixed.

## What it does not say

That publishers and the engine are equally to blame. The engine holds the
jurisdiction.
