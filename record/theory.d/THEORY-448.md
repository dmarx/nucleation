# THEORY-448: On a substance ontology, a gestated foster that is part of the pregnant organism either is no substance, so that mammals begin at birth and the foster is not the later baby, or is part and substance at once, which leaves no consistent count; a process ontology keeps both parthood and foster–neonate identity, at the price of a count between one and two that it does not define

## Source

Meincke (2021), "One or two? A Process View of pregnancy", *Philosophical
Studies* 179(5): 1495–1521, [LIT-142](../literature.d/LIT-142.md), read in [NOTE-115](../notes.d/NOTE-115.md) from the open-access
text.

## What was actually shown

**Containment (§2).** The view that the foster sits in the gestating
organism as in a container is "logically consistent but unconvincing on
empirical grounds" (§1). Meincke adopts Kingma's negative case: the foster
is entangled by homeostasis, metabolic integration, topological continuity
and immunological tolerance, and cannot move freely in and out.

**Parthood under substance ontology (§3).** Kingma's common-sense parthood
is "unhelpfully vague": fosters are not like kidneys, since organs are
stable and contribute to survival while fosters come and go, and not like
reproductive organs, since "the former produce; the latter are produced"
(§3.1). Two versions remain (§3.2).

- *Parts-of-Substances*: the foster is a part and no substance. Consistent,
  but mammals then begin at birth, and foster–neonate identity is lost.
- *Substances-as-Parts*: the foster is part and substance. Either
  "substance" is deflationary, so that fosters, organs and organisms
  collapse together and identity is stipulated, or it is robust and
  contradicts the parthood claim. It also has no consistent count:
  substancehood from about day 4–5 or day 16 gives two, parthood gives one,
  and possibly three from day 16 (fn. 26). It is "not even a coherent
  option".

**The process view (§§4.1–4.3).** On five premises assumed "for the sake of
argument" and defended elsewhere, among them that organisms are autopoietic
processes whose identity is processual, a pregnant organism is a
"hypercomplex higher-level autopoietic process" bifurcating asymmetrically.
"Necessarily, the foster exists only if an organism that gestates it (a
gravida) exists", but not the converse, and the dependence is on some
gravida, as surrogacy shows. The "How many…?" question "cannot have a
natural number as an answer": the pregnant individual is "something in
between one and two". Birth is itself extended, and through lactation the
bifurcation continues.

**The reconciliation (§5).** The foster is a part, *sui generis*, and
processually identical with the baby, but "not yet a full-blown
individual".

**What could have come out otherwise.** One of the substance versions could
have kept both parthood and identity with a stable count. The dilemma
argument shows neither does, given Kingma's own concessions.

## What this does not say

- **That parthood is wrong.** The skim read it so, and [NOTE-115](../notes.d/NOTE-115.md) corrects
  it: the paper keeps parthood and rejects only parthood under substance
  ontology. It also denies that its view is "just a version of the
  Parthood View", since it puts "transition instead of composition".
- **That the Containment View is incoherent.** Only the Parthood View is
  charged with contradiction; Containment fails on evidence.
- **What a count between one and two is.** No measure, no degree function,
  no account of non-additivity beyond a citation, and no engagement with
  the vagueness literature. The paper runs together "both" one and two with
  "neither strictly one nor strictly two". The river analogy carries the
  claim, and the paper says "Identity is not to be asked of an analogy"
  (fn. 53).
- **That the positive view is worked out.** "Explicating the details is a
  separate task for future work" (fn. 52).
- **Anything about abortion, surrogacy or moral status.** Raised in
  footnotes and not pursued; no normative conclusion is the author's.
- **That it holds beyond placental mammals.** The scope is set for
  heuristic reasons (fn. 7); wider application is asserted in fn. 69.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)** asks when a combined form is one system. This account
  constrains the answer: in a clear case, the right count may not be a
  natural number, so a criterion may need to return a degree rather than a
  verdict of one or two. It does not supply the criterion.
- **Biological individuality.** Wilson and Barker ([THEORY-443](THEORY-443.md)) let one
  organism be part of another and note process ontology as a challenge
  without adopting it; this paper is that challenge applied. Bourrat's
  graded and description-relative individuality ([LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md), [LIT-049](../literature.d/LIT-049.md)) fits its
  rejection of an all-or-nothing count; the parallel is [NOTE-115](../notes.d/NOTE-115.md)'s.
- **Overlap and counting.** Lewis ([THEORY-476](THEORY-476.md)) counts overlapping
  persons by keeping identity sharp and letting them share parts; Meincke
  gives up substance counting instead. Olson's map ([THEORY-417](THEORY-417.md)) lists
  partial twinning under the population question.
- **The ethics survey** ([THEORY-464](THEORY-464.md)) reports that the abortion debate
  assumes each of us was once a fetus, which is what foster–neonate identity
  is about.
- **Organisational closure.** The process view rests on autopoiesis; the
  record's accounts of what makes an organism self-producing are
  [THEORY-221](THEORY-221.md) and [THEORY-072](THEORY-072.md).
- **Mereology.** SEP's mereology entry ([LIT-124](../literature.d/LIT-124.md), §5) separates indeterminate
  parthood, de re or de dicto. Meincke claims an indeterminate count and
  leans de re, "flows naturally from the processual constitution", without
  using that machinery.
