# LIT-tmpb02fe: Process as the Becoming of Temporal Trajectory

Hernes, T. (2016). Process as the becoming of temporal trajectory. In A. Langley & H. Tsoukas (Eds.), *The SAGE Handbook of Process Organization Studies* (pp. 601–606). London: SAGE. doi:10.4135/9781473957954.n38.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from metadata, not a reading.* As the parent paper reports
it, the chapter holds that events gain agency as they connect with other
events while they occur, and so define a temporal trajectory that orients
their becoming.

## Why this is here

Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy 2024 ([LIT-tmp3mudg](LIT-tmp3mudg.md)) take from it:

- that for process scholars agency happens in "streams" that "precede
  actors and not vice versa" ("Hernes, 2016, p. 3", quoted p. 1350);
- that "the event gains agency as it connects with other events while it
  creates itself, thus defining a temporal trajectory that orients its
  becoming" (paraphrase, p. 1353);
- that events, not actors, are "the locus of agency" (p. 1350, with the
  2014 book).

Whether they use it faithfully cannot be judged without the chapter. The
"p. 3" page reference does not fit the published pagination, so the
quoted wording may differ from the printed text.
