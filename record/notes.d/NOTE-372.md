# NOTE-372: Self models

## Contribution

The article states the self-model theory of subjectivity (SMT) in a few
thousand words, which *Being No One* takes seven hundred pages to build.
It turns "there is no self" from a slogan into a definite claim with a
mechanism: what exists is a self-model, and the experience of being a self
is what it is like to run a self-model that one cannot recognise as a
model. It also separates three things often run together: having a
self-model (which a robot can), having a conscious one, and experiencing
oneself as a self.

## Key insight

The self is not what the self-model is a model of; it is what the model
looks like from inside when its construction is invisible. A system that
could see its body image as an image, the way we can see a thought as a
thought, would on this theory stop experiencing itself as a self.

## Assumptions

- **Representationalism and functionalism about the conscious self.**
  "What we call 'the conscious self' ... is a specific form of
  representational content, characterized by specific functional
  properties", multiply realisable.
- **Local supervenience of phenomenal content.** Fix the brain's
  contemporaneous internal properties and the PSM's phenomenology is fixed
  (a brain in a vat could have it), while self-knowledge depends on
  history and environment.
- **A minimally sufficient neural correlate of self-consciousness (NCSC)
  exists** for every biological PSM; if it is nomologically coextensive
  with the PSM, the PSM reduces to it.
- **Consciousness is availability**: "the space of consciousness
  essentially is the space of availability for selective, high-level
  attention". Called "a central working hypothesis ... not yet
  empirically demonstrated".
- **Teleofunctionalism**: the self-model has an evolutionary function, "a
  weapon ... in a 'cognitive arms race'".

## Key results

There are no formal results. The article's claims, by section:

- **SMT.** The questions it is built to answer, from "How, in principle,
  could a consciously experienced self ... emerge in a given
  information-processing system?" to the ethics of machine subjectivity
  and the minimally sufficient neural correlate. Method: "interdisciplinary
  constraint satisfaction".
- **PSM.** The conscious partition of the self-model, meeting further
  constraints (ten in *Being No One*, ch. 3), chiefly a window of presence
  and integration into one world model. A "model" here is a
  variable-free representation by partial structural correspondence that
  can be run offline (after Craik and Johnson-Laird). Functionally, a
  "transient computational module" switched on at waking, absent in
  dreamless sleep, present in dreams. Psychiatric disorders as lost,
  hyperactive or dissociated self-model content. Abstract thought as
  taking the PSM offline. The mirror system as "mutually coupled
  self-models", the basis of perspective-taking and theory of mind.
- **Machine self-models.** The starfish robot (Bongard, Zykov and Lipson,
  2006): random action, 15 candidate self-models fitted by stochastic
  optimisation, exploratory actions chosen to maximise their
  disagreement, convergence, locomotion; after damage the cycle restarts
  and it "learns to limp". Lessons: a self-model can be unconscious,
  a process not a thing, plastic, and non-linguistic, rooted in motor
  control. Owning a body is using a whole-body model to control it,
  "the decisive first step towards becoming an autonomous agent".
- **1E/2E/3E.** First-order embodiment without explicit computation (the
  Tarry II walker); second-order with an explicit self-model used for
  control (the starfish, primitive animals, perhaps sleepwalkers);
  third-order with that model conscious (waking humans, perhaps
  orangutans). Humans always have 1E and 2E, and 3E only in waking and
  dreams. Brugger's subject born without limbs reports vivid phantoms,
  with premotor and parietal activation, as possible evidence of an
  innate body model. Full-body illusions in virtual reality (Lenggenhager
  et al. 2007) shift felt self-location toward a seen virtual body, and
  global body ownership is offered as "the most simple form of
  self-awareness".
- **Transparency.** Transparency is phenomenological, comes in degrees,
  and holds when earlier processing stages are attentionally unavailable;
  "the phenomenology of transparency is the phenomenology of naïve
  realism". The body image is transparent; conscious thought is opaque.
  Central claim and prediction as in the LIT. Humans are special in having
  "a very large opaque partition".
- **PMIR.** The intentionality relation can itself be represented: "we
  also co-represent the representational relation itself". A
  first-person perspective is a transparent PMIR. It lets a system
  discover that it represents and attends, and makes subject–object
  relations globally available, which may be why it was adaptive.

## Concepts

- **self-model** — a model, in Craik's sense, that a system has of itself
  as a whole; may be unconscious.
- **phenomenal self-model (PSM)** — the partition of the self-model that
  is conscious because it meets further functional constraints.
- **transparency** — the attentional unavailability of a representation's
  earlier processing stages, so that it is experienced as the thing
  itself; a matter of degree; its opposite is opacity.
- **PMIR** — phenomenal model of the intentionality relation: a conscious
  representation of oneself as directed at an object or goal.
- **first-, second-, third-order embodiment (1E, 2E, 3E)** — intelligence
  from bodily dynamics alone; with an explicit self-model used for
  control; with that model conscious.
- **NCSC** — minimally sufficient neural correlate of self-consciousness.
- **virtual window of presence** — a system's internal representation of
  an extended now.

## Connections

- **Minsky, *The Emotion Machine*** ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md), [NOTE-348](NOTE-348.md)). The brief asked
  for this one. Points of agreement: both deny a central, substantial
  self; both make self-models the stuff of selfhood; both allow them in
  machines. Points of difference:
  - *One model or many.* Minsky: "Whenever you think about your 'Self,'
    you are switching among a network of models", and a single unified
    model would be impractical ([NOTE-348](NOTE-348.md), C10). Metzinger: a PSM is "a
    coherent self-representation, a consistent internal model of itself
    as a whole", integrated into one world model. Minsky's partial models
    are closer to Metzinger's unconscious self-model layers; what
    Metzinger adds is the claim that the conscious partition is one.
  - *Where the sense of a self comes from.* Minsky explains the
    Single-Self idea by its convenience (§9-3). Metzinger explains the
    experience of selfhood by transparency. Minsky's account says why we
    talk of a self; Metzinger's says why it seems real.
  - *Self-models and consciousness.* Minsky makes self-models one of four
    co-occurring processes whose detection might be what "consciousness"
    names (§4-3). Metzinger makes conscious self-modelling depend on
    separate constraints (presence, a world model, availability), and a
    self-model alone is not conscious. Both reject the inference from
    self-model to experience, by different routes.
  - *Body.* Minsky treats the body as one more resource. Metzinger roots
    the self-model in motor control and body ownership: "in its origins
    it is not based on language or conceptual thought, but very likely on
    an attempt to organize global motor behavior".
- **Dennett** ([LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md), [NOTE-364](NOTE-364.md)). Both are no-self views that keep the
  self as something real of a kind. For Dennett it is an abstractum
  posited by interpretation; for Metzinger it is the content of a
  transparent model. Dennett's self is as determinate as its story;
  Metzinger's self-model is fixed by local brain states.
- **Gallagher** ([LIT-453](../literature.d/LIT-453.md), [NOTE-377](NOTE-377.md)). Metzinger's body-ownership
  work is Gallagher's sense of ownership measured, and his starfish robot
  repeats Gallagher's Box 1 point (Tani's robot) with a self-model that
  persists and learns.
- **Zahavi** ([LIT-463](../literature.d/LIT-463.md), Deferred). By its description, Zahavi's book
  defends the self against the view that it is "a neurologically induced
  illusion", and *Being No One* is that view's main statement.
- **Damasio** ([LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md), [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)). The article cites Damasio's "self in the
  act of knowing" for the relational content of higher self-consciousness,
  and its interoceptive layers (immune system, brainstem, Seth, Suzuki and
  Critchley 2012) are the body Damasio's account of feeling starts from.
- **Seth** ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md), [NOTE-169](NOTE-169.md)). Seth's interoceptive predictive coding is
  cited here as part of the unconscious self-model. Seth's case that
  consciousness belongs to living systems sits uneasily with Metzinger's
  multiple realisability of the PSM, though the article does not say
  whether a machine could have a PSM, only a self-model.
- **Clark and Chalmers** ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md), [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md)). The PSM's phenomenal content
  supervenes locally on the brain, so on this theory an extended self is
  at most an extended self-knowledge, never an extended self-experience.
  The pairing is mine.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) (AI consciousness).** The article gives the record a clean
  three-way split: self-model (the starfish has one), PSM (needs the
  unknown phenomenality constraints), phenomenal self (needs a
  transparent PSM). A system's self-description or self-model is
  therefore not evidence of experience on this theory, and the article
  says the conditions for phenomenality are "presently still unknown".
  That agrees with [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)'s verdict that the question cannot now be
  settled. The article raises "the ethical implications of machine models
  of subjectivity" only as a question; whatever the book argues on it, the
  book was not read, so the record does not hold it.
- **[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) (the bridge).** The PSM is "an instrument for global
  control", one per system, and mutually coupled self-models underlie
  social cognition. That is an architectural criterion of the kind the
  bridge lists: a group of selves linked by coupled self-models would not
  thereby have a single PSM. The application is mine; the article does
  not discuss groups.
- **Levin** ([LIT-439](../literature.d/LIT-439.md), [NOTE-341](NOTE-341.md)). Levin's self is any goal-pursuing
  collection with a cognitive light cone; Metzinger's 2E needs an explicit
  whole-system self-model. So Levin counts selves Metzinger would count as
  1E systems. The comparison is mine.
- **No ML instruction.** The starfish is an illustration from robotics. A
  learned world model that includes the agent would be a 2E system in
  Metzinger's terms; the article says nothing about learned models
  generally, and nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Revised text.** The page was last modified in 2013 and cites 2012
  work; the 2007 citation names a text that is no longer what is served.
- **Editing slips.** The starfish procedure is described twice in nearly
  the same words (once under "Self-models in machines", once under 2E).
  A list of lessons jumps to "Fourth" in a paragraph that has no first to
  third. "Input from the physical boy" for body.
- **Reference gaps.** Cited but not in the list: Bongard, Zykov and Lipson
  2006, the main machine example; "Metzinger 2005a", "2006b" and "2007";
  Weisberg is "2007" in the text and 2005 in the list; Lenggenhager et al.
  has no year in the list; "Metzinger 2003a" appears twice.
- **The central claim is not argued here.** C2 is stated as a definition
  with a prediction; the case for it is in the book, unread.
- **Single cases.** The innate-phantom evidence is one subject.

## Open questions

- Is transparency testable apart from reports of selfhood? The prediction
  that an opaque PSM abolishes the experience of selfhood needs a way to
  make a PSM opaque; the article names none.
- Does a language model's description of itself count as a self-model in
  the article's sense (a variable-free structural model used for global
  control) or only as self-reference in language? The article's
  non-linguistic, motor origin for self-models suggests the latter.
