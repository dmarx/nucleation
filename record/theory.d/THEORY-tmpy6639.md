# THEORY-tmpy6639: Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde propose that minimal agency requires individuality, interactional asymmetry and normativity set by the system's own organisation, each necessary and together, they say, sufficient; that individuality without the other two is not agency; and that a system optimizing an externally fixed function is not a model of agency

## Source

Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde (2009), [LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md), read in full in [NOTE-458](../notes.d/NOTE-458.md):
§2 (the three conditions), Table 1 (the cases), §4 (the generative
definition), footnote 3 (emergent regulation), §6 (the consequences for
models).

## The proposal

Three conditions. Individuality: the system distinguishes itself from its
environment, and an observer does not do it for the system. Interactional
asymmetry: the system is the source of modulations of its coupling with the
environment, defined as the system changing a subset of the constraints on
an otherwise symmetrical coupling (Eqs. 1–3), not as being the bigger
energy source or the stronger statistical influence. Normativity: the
coupling is regulated against a condition that can fail and that the
system's own organisation sets.

Each is proposed as necessary. Individuality is the precondition of the
other two and does not suffice: in Table 1 a cell in passive osmosis, a
kitten warmed by its mother and a person with a Parkinsonian tremor are
individuals, and none is acting; a bacterium in metabolism-dependent
chemotaxis meets all three and acts. Sufficiency is claimed more weakly:
the authors "find it very difficult to conceive of any system that jointly
fulfills these three criteria and it is not an agent".

The generative definition: a system is an agent for a coupling with an
environment iff it is an open autonomous system and modulates that coupling
adaptively, so as to maintain some of its own processes (§4). Footnote 3
requires the modulation to be emergent: subsystems that regulate to a fixed
norm independent of the agent's state "act as external constraints on the
system". Consequence (b) of §6: systems "that only satisfy constraints or
norms imposed from outside (e.g. optimization according to an externally
fixed function) should not be treated as models of agency".

These are the conditions [CLAIM-284](../claims.d/CLAIM-284.md) uses as tests, the line [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md) says a
coupling criterion cannot draw, and the derivation [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md) cites.

## What this does not say

It does not say the proposal is true. The paper argues from cases chosen to
fit the conditions, and its authors call the asymmetry criterion
incomplete: what counts as a parametric modulation and what as a coupling
between variables "is often a matter of choice for the modeler". No
explicit model has been put through the procedure. That test is
[THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md)'s `promote_when`, and [THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md) stays Proposed until the record
holds it. Its "autonomy" and "self" are organisational, not a subject or
self-governance.
