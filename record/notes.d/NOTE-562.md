# NOTE-562: Cancer across the tree of life: cooperation and cheating in multicellularity

## Contribution

A framework that defines cancer by what it breaks rather than by its
human clinical form, so that it can be looked for in any multicellular
lineage, and a survey of the literature using it. It also proposes that
dysregulated differentiation may be a missing hallmark of cancer.

## Key insight

Being multicellular is not only cooperation among cells but continuous
suppression of the cells that would gain by defecting. Cancer is what that
suppression failing looks like, and because complex multicellularity
evolved several times, so did the suppression.

## Assumptions

- Cell-level and organism-level fitness can conflict, and multicellularity
  requires the first to be suppressed in favour of the second.
- "Cheating" is functional: "We do not imply that cheating is a
  pre-adapted or evolved 'strategy'" (Introduction).
- A broad notion of cancer, including neoplastic growth with abnormal
  proliferation or differentiation, is the right unit for comparison
  (Survey).

## Key results

- **Five foundations** and the cheating phenotypes that break them
  (Figure 1 caption).
- **Survey**: bilaterian animals all or nearly all susceptible;
  cnidarians show it, so it was likely a problem for early metazoans;
  galls and calluses in plants and in red and brown algae; masses of
  undifferentiated, sometimes invasive hyphae in fungi; cheating in
  *Volvox* mutants; cooperation and cheating in aggregative
  slime moulds and bacterial biofilms.
- **Generalising** (Discussion): division-of-labour cheating "was also
  present in all forms of multicellularity"; resource and environment
  cheating documented only in animals and plants; metastasis, rare plant
  cases aside, only in animals.
- **Two kinds of cheating**: demographic (proliferation, cell death) and
  economic (resource, labour, environment). Both occurred in every report
  reviewed, and "cheating of both types may be necessary".
- **Suppression**: cell-autonomous, architectural and systemic mechanisms;
  clonal development reduces conflict but is "not strictly necessary";
  proliferative units may break a large suppression problem into
  "more manageable and modular sub-problems".
- **Peto's paradox**: large organisms likely need more cheater detection
  and suppression across all five foundations.
- **Within tumours**: cooperation may re-emerge, with neoplasms
  recapitulating the foundations for the benefit of the cancer cells.

## Concepts

- **cheating** — "Breaking of shared rules, including genetically encoded
  phenotypes or behaviours, that leads to a fitness advantage for the
  cheater" (Box 1).
- **cooperation** — "Transmission of benefits and/or coordination of
  actions that augments fitness of the larger ensemble" (Box 1).
- **five foundations of multicellularity** — the five forms of cellular
  cooperation above.
- **demographic and economic cheating** — the authors' two categories.
- **complex multicellularity** — after Knoll: several cell types,
  intercellular communication, three-dimensional organization, resource
  transport (Endnote).

## Connections

It draws on cooperation theory and the major-transitions literature.
Okasha's overview ([LIT-749](../literature.d/LIT-749.md), read in [NOTE-586](NOTE-586.md)) states the general
principle this paper applies, that a collective is stable only if
mechanisms such as policing align its units' interests. It also gives a
caution this paper partly answers: terms like "cheating" have been
misapplied where no collective action problem was shown. Aktipis et al.
define cheating functionally, not as a strategy, which meets the caution
in part, but the paper does not show that each reported growth is a
conflict of fitness interests; for slime moulds it concedes a cheater
"might be considered a parasite rather than a neoplasm". The levels of
selection literature ([LIT-160](../literature.d/LIT-160.md)) is where its proliferative-unit proposal
would be tested.

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-135](../theory.d/THEORY-135.md). For the owner's essay:

- **§6.3** (capture from below). This is the biological model: units
  gaining at the collective's expense, and a collective that survives only
  by detecting and suppressing them. The paper adds that the environment
  matters as much as the units: "Cancer is not just a matter of cells
  that are inherently cheaters, but also a matter of being in an
  environment that has poor cheater detection and suppression".
- **A positive feedback.** Cheating degrades the local environment, which
  selects for cells that move, which may favour more cheating. The record
  notes the parallel with dysregulation from within (§6.3), without
  claiming it.
- **Modularity.** Proliferative units that contain a defector's spread are
  a structural defence, a candidate parallel for organizational
  subdivision. The paper does not consider organizations.

## Limitations

A review: no model and no new data. The survey depends on what has been
studied, and the authors flag sampling bias and lack of study more than
once. The broad definition of cancer makes the cross-lineage comparison
possible and also makes cheating easier to find in every branch.
Multilevel selection is invoked by citation, not modelled.

## Open questions

Which forms of cheating are necessary and sufficient for cancer-like
growth, which the authors pose; whether proliferative units are a level of
selection; whether bacterial proliferation inhibition serves the
aggregate or the individual cell.
