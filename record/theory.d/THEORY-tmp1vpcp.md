# THEORY-tmp1vpcp: Mossio, Montévil and Longo take organization, as closure of constraints, to be a theoretical principle of biology, posited a priori as physics posits its phase space and defining the domain together with variation, so that every system meeting both is biological by definition and organisms are one class of such systems; on this reading "organisms are organized" is constitutive, not an empirical finding, and the principle is to be judged by what it makes intelligible

## Source

Mossio, Montévil and Longo (2016), LIT-tmph8zl0, §1 (pp. 1–2) and §4
(pp. 6–9) of the authors' HAL manuscript; read in NOTE-tmp1gwqb. The
definitions are LIT-582's.

## What was actually shown

**The status.** "As theoretical principles, organization and variation
constitute overarching hypotheses that frame the intelligibility of the
objects within the biological domain." They play the role space and time
play in physics, or "positing a priori the phase space (i.e. the list of
pertinent observables and parameters)" (p. 2).

**The scope.** "Biology is the science of systems meeting the principles
of organization and variation, organisms being a specific, particularly
relevant, class of biological systems." Ecosystems might qualify
(p. 2).

**The content.** "The principle of organization states that biological
systems realize a closure of constraints" (p. 7). Constraints act on a
process at a time scale at which they are conserved. Closure is a set in
which each constraint depends directly on another member and has another
member depending on it. Only thermodynamically open systems can realize
it, and a closure is always relative to external constraints it does not
include (pp. 6–9).

**The support.** A history from Kant through Bernard, Weiss, Piaget,
autopoiesis, Rosen and Kauffman (§3), and examples: the vasculature and
gut, enzyme synthesis, the Na⁺/K⁺ pump. Nothing is derived from the
principle in this paper beyond the two implications of §5
(THEORY-tmptcl5r).

## What this does not say

- **That closure has been observed in every organism.** "Without
  exceptions" (p. 2) is secured by defining the domain through the
  principle.
- **That only organisms realize closure.** The paper says the opposite:
  the domain is presumably larger than organisms (compare THEORY-514).
- **What would count against the principle.** The paper does not say.
- **Anything formal beyond LIT-582.** The definitions are restated; the
  2015 paper's measure of a tendency to closure is not used.
