# LIT-tmpnhw2v: Dispatched and Canceled en Route

U.S. Fire Administration (2023). Dispatched and Canceled en Route. NFIRSGram, 19 June 2023. https://www.usfa.fema.gov/nfirs/coding-help/dispatched-and-canceled-en-route/

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpain0u](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpain0u.md).*

- **611 only when nobody arrives.** "This incident type should only be
  used when the fire department is dispatched to an incident but does not
  arrive on the scene because they were canceled prior to arrival."
- **Cancelled units are still recorded.** "The use of the
  Apparatus/Personnel Modules can show what resources were available,
  even though they never arrived on scene."
- **Assigned, not only arrived.** In Section G1, all apparatus and
  personnel assigned "should be recorded regardless of whether they
  arrived or were canceled before arrival."
- **What NFIRS cannot see.** A call the department "was aware of ... but
  did not have resources to respond" to is not an NFIRS incident; "it is
  recommended the fire department record them outside of NFIRS in a
  separate database."

## Standing in the record

Registered as the case source the pasted bibliography on emergency
readiness gives for its running example, a heavy rescue squad dispatched
provisionally and cancelled en route. The page has no heavy rescue squad:
its Scenario 2 cancels one tanker/tender and one rescue vehicle after the
first-arriving engine finds a false alarm. Its bearing on A's proposition,
that measuring maintained abilities by observed utilization can destroy
them, runs both ways. NFIRS does record committed but unused units, so its
data are not limited to units that acted on scene; but a failure to
respond for lack of resources, the case closest to the value of
readiness, leaves no NFIRS record at all. No THEORY is filed: the page is
a coding rule, not an account of what is true. It would serve as the
source of a CASE beside [CASE-135](../cases.d/CASE-135.md), the record's other fire-service case.
