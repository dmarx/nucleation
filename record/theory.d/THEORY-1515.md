# THEORY-1515: Shah, Hart and Kaltsakas, following the 2007 international consensus, hold that readiness to come off invasive ventilation is tested by withdrawing support for a spontaneous breathing trial of 30 to 120 minutes, since predictors of readiness have low power; that patients are classed as simple, difficult or prolonged weaning by whether the first attempt succeeds and, if not, whether up to three trials or seven days suffice; and that outcomes worsen across these groups, about half of patients whose weaning fails remaining on invasive ventilation at one year

## Source

Shah, Hart & Kaltsakas (2024), [LIT-2217](../literature.d/LIT-2217.md); read in [NOTE-1706](../notes.d/NOTE-1706.md). It
stands in for Boles et al. (2007), [LIT-2213](../literature.d/LIT-2213.md), on the classification and
the readiness test; the consensus' abstract agrees with it on both.

## What was actually shown

Nothing new: the review reports others' work.

- **The test.** A trial lasting "at least 30 min, increasing up to 120
  min"; predictors, 462 of them in 65 studies, had "a low predictive
  power" (Weaning Task Force).
- **The groups.** Defined as in the title, after the consensus; in WIND,
  57% weaned in under 24 h, 10% in 1 to 7 days, 9% took longer.
- **The gradient.** In Jeong et al. (2015), hospital mortality 21.1%,
  28.7% and 58.6% across the groups, prolonged weaning independently
  associated with death; difficult weaning not.
- **Lasting failure.** "approximately 50% remain dependent on invasive
  ventilation at 1 year" after weaning failure, from worldwide
  observational studies.

## What this does not say

- **That the classification was derived from data.** It was expert
  opinion; outcome gradients are partly built in, since simple weaning has
  no reintubation by definition.
- **That ventilation causes prolonged weaning.** Longer ventilation and
  worse outcomes share severity as a cause; the review asserts a
  load-capacity-drive imbalance without testing it.
- **That a passed trial guarantees success.** 42% of those liberated after
  prolonged weaning needed ventilation again, and in difficult-to-wean
  patients a passed trial "may not be a useful marker".
- For the record it bears on [QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) as a working withdrawal probe, on
  [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)'s requirement to watch persistence after withdrawal, and on
  [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) through the patients who stay dependent.
