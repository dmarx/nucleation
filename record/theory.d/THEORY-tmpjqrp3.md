# THEORY-tmpjqrp3: Grueneisen, Wyman and Tomasello show that six-year-olds use first- and second-order false-belief reasoning to coordinate with a peer without communication, choosing the option their partner falsely believes, or falsely believes they believe, to hold the larger reward, more often than when they choose independently

## The account

In a coordination game without communication, six-year-olds choose not
where the reward is but where their partner believes it is, or believes
they believe it is, so recursive mindreading is recruited for coordination
by 6, earlier than competitive tasks suggested.

## Evidence

One experiment with 52 dyads of 6-year-olds.
