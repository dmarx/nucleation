# NOTE-tmp970xq: Leitgeb and Carus — Rudolf Carnap

## Contribution

An authoritative, recently revised survey that foregrounds explication and framework
choice as engineering, with the current scholarship (Carus, Leitgeb, Brun, Dutilh Novaes)
and the distinction from recent "conceptual engineering".

## Key results

1. §1.1: "philosophical inquiry became, for Carnap, a kind of “conceptual engineering”
   ... rather than a form of inquiry or search for knowledge."
2. §1.1: "the point of philosophical inquiry was not to ask how things are, but rather
   how—within the constraints of our available tools and the knowledge available from the
   sciences—we want things to be".
3. §1.1: recent "conceptual engineering" "has often taken a somewhat different direction,
   and should not be confused with Carnap’s own approach."
4. §1.2: a framework "is meant to reconstruct the conceptual and inferential presuppositions
   of a scientific theory rather than a scientific theory itself."
5. §4 ("The Road to Syntax", the autumn-1932 turn): how to set up a language is "a normative or practical question, has no
   right or wrong answers. One could only try out different ways, and see which ones worked
   better."
6. Supplement D: "Explication in Carnap’s sense is the replacement of a somewhat unclear and
   inexact concept C, the explicandum, by a new, clearer, and more exact concept \(C^*\),
   the explicatum." (The SEP renders C* in MathJax.)
7. Supplement D: the desiderata are exactness, similarity, fruitfulness and simplicity;
   "Each of these four criteria, once again, is to be understood, like exactness, as a
   matter of degree."
8. Supplement D: "even changes of clear-cut applications are acceptable when this serves
   desiderata other than similarity" (whales re-classified as non-fish).
9. Supplement D: explication is "an open-ended process that allows for iterated diachronic
   clarification", "compatible with the synchronic co-existence of different explications".
10. §7: "Internal ones are framework-relative"; external questions make sense only if
    reinterpreted "as a practical (normative) question of deciding among frameworks or
    explications."
11. §1.8: the entry argues Carnap held "a quasi-Kantian view" in which logical reasoning is
    "subordinate to (and narrower in scope than) a purely normative form of reasoning".

## Assumptions

Secondary source: the entry's readings of Carnap (notably §1.8 on values) are the authors'
interpretations, partly from posthumously published drafts.

## Concepts

conceptual engineering; rational reconstruction; explication; explicandum / explicatum;
desiderata of similarity, exactness, fruitfulness, simplicity; linguistic framework;
principle of tolerance; internal vs external questions; voluntarism; practical (normative)
question of framework choice.

## Bearing on the record

CLAIM-472's note of 2026-11-12 says A50 §8, "from Carnap's explication and the
conceptual-engineering literature (LIT-2322, unread)", proposed criteria for a change of the
record's categories: capture the distinction faithfully, support useful inferences, avoid
misleading implications, stay intelligible to collaborators, preserve continuity with
earlier usage, and "these desiderata can conflict". **Partly Carnap's.** "Captures the
intended distinction more faithfully" and "continuity with earlier usage" correspond to
similarity; "useful inferences or operations" to fruitfulness. Carnap's exactness and
simplicity are absent from A's list; A's "avoids misleading implications" and "intelligible
to other collaborators" are not among Carnap's desiderata as the entry gives them (they are
closer to the recent conceptual-engineering literature the entry distinguishes from Carnap,
result 3, which was not read). That the desiderata can conflict is Carnap's: similarity may
be sacrificed for the others (result 8).

- **CLAIM-472** (Proposed; choosing an apparatus is open to evaluation, not only

  normative or practical question" with "no right or wrong answers" (result 5), evaluated by
