# ADR-053: A LIT whose lawful free copy was located but could not be fetched by this client is Unfetched, not Unavailable

## Context

[ADR-041](ADR-041.md) split `Deferred` and gave `Unavailable` to every LIT whose lawful
full text was sought and not reached. It saw that this covered two cases, and
kept them under one word:

> A copy that exists but could not be fetched is a different blocker from a
> copy that does not exist. In both, though, the owner is what unblocks it.

Among its alternatives it rejected "two new words, one for 'none exists' and
one for 'exists, not fetched'", because "eight entries do not justify a sixth
status".

Two things have changed since.

- **The count.** The status notes of all `Unavailable` LITs were triaged on
  2026-10-25. 140 of the 325 record an access obstacle: a bot check, a 403, a
  429, Cloudflare or Incapsula. Each was read and put in one of three buckets.
  - **43: a lawful free copy was located, and this client was blocked
    from it.** The copies are on PhilArchive, SSRN, HAL, bioRxiv, Deep Blue,
    an author's or course page, a Wayback capture of one, or a publisher's
    own free copy (bronze or gold).
  - **80: only a publisher's paywalled copy was found.**
  - **17: a free copy may exist, but none was located.** Some obstacles
    were of another kind, such as a dead site or a request-a-copy form.

  The 43 include five of [ADR-041](ADR-041.md)'s eight. [LIT-137](../literature.d/LIT-137.md) is not among them,
  since only one chapter of that volume has a free copy.
- **The two cases are not cleared alike.** [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) said the owner unblocks
  both. They do, but at very different cost. The owner showed this on
  2026-10-25 by supplying DeGroot 1974 ([LIT-tmpwlnuv](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwlnuv.md)) and Wald 1945
  ([LIT-tmpcevhu](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcevhu.md)), and asked for "a status for content that would be easy for
  me to access where you encountered obstacles". A located free copy takes a
  browser and a minute. A paywalled one takes a library, a subscription or a
  purchase. Under one word, the quick ones are found only by reading every
  note, which is what [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) was written to stop for `Deferred`.

## Decision

- **A new LIT status, `Unfetched`, labelled "Copy found, not fetched".**
  It means a lawful free copy of the work was located at a specific address,
  and this client could not retrieve it. The status note must give that
  address: a URL, a repository handle, or a DOI that resolves to the free
  copy. It must also give what blocked the fetch, and when. As with
  `Unavailable`, the status is no judgement on merit, and a supplied copy
  returns the work to reading.
- **`Unavailable` narrows** to the other cases: no lawful copy was found, or
  only a paywalled one was. Its blurb drops "or one was found that this client
  could not fetch".
- **Where the line falls.**
  - **A free copy of a different version stays `Unavailable`.** A
    translation, a single chapter of a volume, or an abstract does not count.
    The free copy has to be the work itself. A preprint or accepted
    manuscript of the work counts.
  - **A note with no exact address stays `Unavailable`** until a reader
    finds the address. Otherwise the owner's queue would hold searches,
    not clicks.
  - **A copy that is down for everyone is not `Unfetched`.** A server that
    times out or a broken site is no access obstacle peculiar to this
    client. A Wayback capture of the same copy does count.
  - **A copy the owner could not lawfully use stays `Unavailable`.** That
    includes an upload whose uploader is unknown and a PDF whose licence is
    in doubt.
- **The queue is generated.** The owner's list is the status page
  `docs/literature/status/Unfetched.md`, which `luria index` builds for every
  status. Nothing extra needs to be maintained.

Six of the 43 would not move:

- **Wald ([LIT-tmpcevhu](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcevhu.md)):** the owner supplied the copy, and the work was read
  and is Active.
- **[LIT-396](../literature.d/LIT-396.md), [LIT-742](../literature.d/LIT-742.md) and [LIT-1469](../literature.d/LIT-1469.md):** each note names a site but no
  exact address.
- **[LIT-1329](../literature.d/LIT-1329.md):** only a French translation is free.
- **[LIT-1101](../literature.d/LIT-1101.md):** the author's server timed out.

Each moved entry would get a version bump and a history item. The item would
point to this decision and confirm that the note gives the copy's address.
Where a note's address has to be read out of its prose, it would be repeated
in a closing sentence: "Free copy: <address>." Present-tense phrases such as
"stays Unavailable" would change in place, under the same rule
[ADR-041](ADR-041.md) used. The 80 paywalled entries and the 17 unlocated ones would not
change.

The triage was done from the status notes and the last history items. It
could misread a note, so each entry would be checked again by eye when it
moves.

## Alternatives

- **Keep one word and rely on the note.** [ADR-041](ADR-041.md)'s choice. At 43 entries
  out of 325, the owner would have to read about a hundred and forty notes
  to find the quick ones. That is the cost [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) itself pointed to when
  it split `Deferred`.
- **A list kept in a curation entry or a report.** It would go stale the
  first time a work was read or a new one was filed. A status updates the
  generated page with the entry itself.
- **A tag or flag.** Rejected for the reason [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) gives: copy
  availability is not a topic, and a flag would sit beside a status it
  qualifies.
- **Put the paywalled ones in the queue too.** The owner can unblock those
  as well, through a library. But that is a different errand at a different
  cost, and the owner asked for the easy ones. If a library queue is wanted,
  it can be its own decision.
- **Other names.** `Blocked` does not say what blocks the work, and a
  paywall is a block too. `Located` reads as a property of the copy, not of
  the work's standing. `Unfetched` says exactly what is missing.

## Consequences

- **The LIT vocabulary gains a seventh word.** The `lit-statuses` entry in
  `luria.yaml` gains `Unfetched`, and the blurb of `Unavailable` narrows.
  Like `Unavailable`, the new word is not in force, so `inactive-ok`
  annotations that cite a moved work still apply. It falls out of the
  pending-decisions report in the same way.
- **The difference from the anthology grows.** An `Unfetched` LIT moved to
  the anthology would fail its vocabulary, as an `Unavailable` one already
  does ([ADR-041](ADR-041.md)'s consequence).
- **A rule for readers.** A reader who locates a lawful free copy and cannot
  fetch it sets the LIT to `Unfetched`, with the address in the note. The
  LIT template and the reading briefs say so.
- **The owner's queue is a page.** Clearing an entry means supplying the
  copy, which returns the work to reading. The reading then sets its status,
  as with `Unavailable`.
- **Supersession.** On acceptance, [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) stays in force for `Deferred`
  against `Unavailable`. This decision amends its third bullet ("Both cases
  of `Unavailable` take the one word") and its rejected alternative.
  [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) would get a dated note saying so, not a `superseded_by`, because
  its main choice stands.
