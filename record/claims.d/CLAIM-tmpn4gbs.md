# CLAIM-tmpn4gbs: Alternative reading (c) of the constitutivism test: the record's organizational account is not exposed to Leffler's choice point, because it never explains the force of an organization's norms by the organization's constitution; it keeps norms of function and failure apart from their authority, so the test closes a route the record has not taken rather than refuting a position it holds

## The claim

Leffler's explanandum is force: why "we are subject to some possible norms
rather than others" (p. 2). His choice point binds a constitutivist who
explains force by inescapability.

The record does not. Its first two rungs (QUESTION-128) are norms of
function and failure: conditions under which an organization continues
or fails, and under which a component fulfils its role in that. The
record states them as conditions, and refuses the step to authority:

- CLAIM-075 (Active): viability "does not establish what ought to
  persist"; "An unjust institution may be highly viable."
- QUESTION-128, A16: "self-generated normativity and normative
  legitimacy must remain separate"; the practical and moral rungs
  "require considerably more."
- CLAIM-648's profile is a norm "by its own lights", the content of a
  standard of failure, not a claim that the system ought to meet it.
- CLAIM-363 declines A17's fourth row, neither holding nor denying it.

So the record has no position for the test to refute. What the test
does is close one route to the authority the record leaves open: it
shows from the constitutivist side the separation the record already
draws from the organizational side. Its relativism ("exercising their
shmagency is the plight of shmagents") is CLAIM-075's viable unjust
institution restated for agents.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the test is idle: it is independent support for the
  separation, and it removes one candidate answer to QUESTION-129.
- It does not say the record could never take the route; if a later
  entry explains the force of organizational norms by self-maintenance,
  reading (a) applies to it.
- It does not settle whether Leffler's criticizability, which "need not be
  terribly strong", is already implicit in the record's talk of failure
  "by its own lights". If it is, the record holds a weak form of force,
  and reading (b) is the better account.
