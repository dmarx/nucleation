# THEORY-1464: Christie, Marine, French and Blouin show in Hood River steelhead that one generation of hatchery rearing selects for traits that raise reproductive success in captivity and lower it in the wild: first-generation hatchery fish nearly double wild fish's success as hatchery broodstock, and offspring of the wild broodstock most successful in the hatchery have about 71% lower per capita reproductive success in the wild

## Source

Christie, Marine, French and Blouin (2012), [LIT-2185](../literature.d/LIT-2185.md); main text read
in [NOTE-1652](../notes.d/NOTE-1652.md), from the free full text on PMC (PMC3252900).

## What was actually shown

- **Two predictions that could have failed.** Domestication selection
  predicts that captive-born fish do better in captivity than wild-born,
  and that broodstock most successful in captivity leave offspring least
  successful in the wild. Relaxed selection predicts neither: "we would
  expect a flat slope" (Results).
- **The tradeoff** (Results; Figs 2–3). "In 7 of the 8 F1 run-years";
  families of five or more returns, offspring "averaged 71% lower per
  capita reproductive success in the wild"; GLM slope P < 0.001;
  inbreeding at "11 lethal equivalents" cannot explain it.
- **The hatchery advantage** (Results; Fig. 4; Materials and Methods).
  F1 hatchery broodstock "had nearly double the lifetime reproductive
  success ... when spawned in captivity compared with wild fish", "In 4 of
  5 run-years", significant with 1995 excluded; with it, P "equaled 0.1296
  and 0.07".
- **The exception.** 1995: fewer broodstock, "4,600 vs. a mean of 52,700"
  smolts, low-density rearing, larger release size, no selection
  detected.

## What this does not say

- Which traits are selected; growth rate and others are candidates.
- That crowding is the selection pressure: it "points to crowding as a
  possible selection pressure", from one year.
- That the loss persists or grows after supplementation stops, or how
  fast a population recovers: withdrawal is not studied.
- That other taxa respond as fast: "other taxa may not respond in an
  equivalent fashion".
- What the Science paper's ([LIT-2159](../literature.d/LIT-2159.md)) per-generation estimate is.
