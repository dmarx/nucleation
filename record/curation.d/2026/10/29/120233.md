The curation entry of 2026-10-29 10:42 set out how the first pass treated
copies. It read Jasanoff ([LIT-1594](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1594.md)) and PBFT ([LIT-1217](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1217.md)) from copies on
course or group pages. It turned down the same kind of copy for ten other
works, as "of unknown provenance". The owner replied: "let's go find and
read the stuff that they mistakenly turned down."

[ADR-064](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-064.md) records the ruling. A copy of a published article counts as a
lawful copy when it sits on any of these:

- a university course or reading-list page;
- a faculty or research-group page;
- a scholarly third-party site;
- an author's own upload.

Shadow libraries and uploads of whole in-copyright books still do not count.
So the refusals of Kahneman ([LIT-1209](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1209.md), Internet Archive uploads) and of Herd
& Moynihan's whole book ([LIT-1609](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1609.md), a file host) stand. [ADR-053](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-053.md) gets a dated
note.

Three readers went back to the ten works the same day, on a fresh branch.
Each copy was checked against its title, authors and pages before it was
read.

## What the readings settle

- **The coordinated attack problem is Akkoyunlu et al.'s.** It is in the
  appendix of their paper (pp. 73–74), told as gangsters and a mugged
  messenger. They prove that no finite exchange can give both parties
  mutual knowledge that the plan was delivered, even when no message is
  lost. That is three years before Gray (1978). Their goal is mutual
  knowledge of delivery. The step to coordinated action ("both act or
  neither") is Halpern & Moses's ([LIT-1213](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1213.md)). This corrects the first
  pass's note on [CLAIM-346](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-346.md), which said the 1975 attribution "rests on later
  usage".
- **Revonsuo draws the binding distinction himself.** In his terms it is
  stimulus-related against consciousness-related binding, not "functional"
  against "phenomenal", and he credits it to Smythies (1994). The issue
  introduction (Revonsuo & Newman) was not checked: the Santa Fe Institute
  wiki copy returned a 503.
- **"Deprovincialization" is in Pettigrew 1998**, once, on p. 72. Its
  evidence is one unpublished analysis and one laboratory study.

## Errors found in the works

- **Pettigrew & Tropp 2008:**
  - Table 3's Sobel statistic for empathy over all samples is printed as
    4.38. Its own coefficients and standard errors give 12.43, the value in
    the text; this was recomputed.
  - Its "91 independent samples" adds the samples for each mediator. At
    least nine samples measured two mediators, so there are at most 82
    distinct samples.
- **Pettigrew & Tropp 2006:** trim-and-fill is reported as *raising* the
  effect, and the paper does not explain why. "Z" means both the test
  statistic and Fisher's z.
- **Edmondson 1999:**
  - The team-learning ICC is .33 in the text and .27 in Table 3.
  - Table 6 prints p = .21 for t = .78. A two-sided test gives about .44.
  - "Six of eight hypotheses" counts H6, which rests on a p = .06
    coefficient.
- **Nisbett & Wilson:**
  - Storms & Nisbett 1970 is cited as volume 2 for volume 16.
  - Its awareness ratio can exceed 100% (the paper reports 244%), and the
    paper does not remark on it.
- **Seeley et al.:** none. The three χ² tests that can be rebuilt from the
  text reproduce.
- **Akkoyunlu et al.:** one step of the appendix proof is asserted, not
  argued. The conclusion stands.
- **Summaries:** three were wrong in substance and are corrected.
  - [LIT-1408](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1408.md): Pettigrew keeps Allport's four conditions as essential and
    adds a fifth.
  - [LIT-1406](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1406.md): the swarm does not settle on "the best-represented site".
  - [LIT-1214](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1214.md): the problem is in an appendix to an interprocess
    communication paper.
