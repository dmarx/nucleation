# NOTE-tmp01mls: Dzhafarov, Zhang & Kujala, contextuality in behaviour

## Contribution

Most behavioural and social experiments looking for contextuality use cyclic systems of binary measurements, the class that in physics contains KCBS, EPR–Bell and Leggett–Garg systems. Contextuality-by-Default can define and measure contextuality in such systems even when measurements have context-dependent errors or are directly influenced by the context, which is the normal case in behaviour. For binary cyclic systems it gives a necessary and sufficient noncontextuality criterion, known to be violated by some quantum systems. The authors re-analyse several behavioural and social data sets, find no evidence of contextuality in any, and propose as a working hypothesis that behavioural and social systems are noncontextual.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (p. 1–3): a measurement is indexed by object and context, R^c_q; different contexts are stochastically unrelated; a system is noncontextual iff some coupling of all bunches makes each object's measurements across contexts coincide with the maximal probability their own couplings allow (m = m′); consistent connectedness (equal marginals) is the special case the traditional theory assumes.
- §2, Eq. (7): a binary cyclic system of rank n is noncontextual iff ΔC = s1(⟨V1W2⟩,…,⟨VnW1⟩) − (n−2) − Σ|⟨Vi⟩−⟨Wi⟩| ≤ 0 (proved in the cited refs); for consistently connected systems n = 3, 4, 5 give Leggett–Garg, CHSH and KCBS.
- §3 (Eqs. 9–12): Wang–Busemeyer's "quantum question" equality, derived from a double-projection Hilbert-space model, implies ΔC ≤ 0; the question-order effect is inconsistent connectedness, not contextuality; 72 of 73 pairs fit the QQ equality.
- §5: Aerts, Gabora & Sozzo's CHSH violation in conjoint choices (Eq. 15) disappears (ΔC = −3.357) once unequal marginals are counted — CHSH presupposes consistent connectedness.
- §6: Bruza et al.'s primed ambiguous word combinations ("apple chip"): all 23 have ΔC between −2.882 and −0.418; Bruza et al.'s "compositionality" is, in CbD terms, consistent connectedness plus noncontextuality.
- §8: absence of contextuality "does not mean that quantum formalisms are not applicable" — the QQ model predicts it; calling any context effect "contextuality" is "a trivial sense".

## Open questions

- It is the bridge from the physics notions to cognition and semantics, and it corrects a reading common in quantum-cognition and quantum-IR work: order and priming effects are context-dependent marginals, and a Hilbert-space model of them can be exactly noncontextual.
- A deeper reading should get the proof of Eq. (7) from Kujala, Dzhafarov & Larsson 2015 (PRL 115, 150401) and Dzhafarov–Kujala's CbD papers, and check the supplementary computations.
- Later CbD work (e.g. Cervantes & Dzhafarov 2018, human choice experiments) is reported to find contextuality in behaviour; not read here, unverified.
