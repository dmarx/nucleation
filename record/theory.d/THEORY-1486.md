# THEORY-1486: Banerjee, Duflo, Goldberg, Karlan, Osei, Parienté, Shapiro, Thuysbaert and Udry find, in six randomized trials, that a time-limited bundle of a productive asset, temporary consumption support, training and coaching raised the consumption, assets, income and working time of the poorest households, and that these gains held one year after all support ended; they read the durability as consistent with the program having unlocked a poverty trap, while noting that persistent but modest effects also fit a 'poverty flat' without one

## Source

Banerjee, Duflo, Goldberg, Karlan, Osei, Parienté, Shapiro, Thuysbaert &
Udry (2015), [LIT-2160](../literature.d/LIT-2160.md); read in [NOTE-1669](../notes.d/NOTE-1669.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Six RCTs** (Ethiopia, Ghana, Honduras, India, Pakistan, Peru), 10,495
  households chosen as the poorest in their villages; 94% and 91% resurveyed
  at the two endlines, attrition not differential (pp. 1260799-7, -9).
- **Persistence one year after exit** (Table 3, p. -8): 8 of 10 pooled
  indices significant at endline 2; consumption +0.122 SD then +0.120 SD,
  "no evidence of mean reversion" in consumption, food security or assets.
  What could have come out otherwise: gains collapsing once consumption
  support and coaching stopped, as they did in Honduras when the asset died.
- **Working time** (p. -8): +17.5 minutes per adult per day at endline 1 and
  "11.2 min (6.1% increase) at endline 2". The pooled time index is 0.090 and
  0.054 SD, significant at both.
- **The trap reading and its hedge** (p. -14): durability "supports the
  interpretation that the program unlocked a poverty trap. Nevertheless, the
  average effects are not very large"; a "poverty flat", "a world in which
  small changes persist but neither unleash continued improvement" nor
  dissipate, fits as well; the quantile pattern "lends some support" to
  heterogeneous exit, asset ratios do not give "strong evidence".

## What this does not say

- That the program unlocked a poverty trap: the authors offer that as one
  reading and argue against it.
- That any component, or the bundle's size, is necessary: "Whether all the
  ingredients of the program are individually necessary is not tackled
  here".
- That the gains last beyond a year past exit (for India, see [LIT-2170](../literature.d/LIT-2170.md)).
- That it works everywhere: equality across sites is rejected for almost all
  outcomes, and Honduras faded.
- That support never lowers effort: the support here was announced as
  temporary, so it is no test of help the recipient can count on.
- Anything about aid to governments or projects.
