The owner asked for Berniūnas & Dranseika 2016 ([LIT-1308](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1308.md)) to be filed.
It is the reply to Nichols & Bruno ([LIT-1269](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1269.md)) named as an open question in
[NOTE-996](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-996.md).

No lawful full text was reached, so it is filed Unavailable under [ADR-041](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-041.md),
from its abstract, and no NOTE is filed. The status note lists where the
reader looked. One place is unsettled: PhilPapers and PhilArchive returned
403 to this client. Whether PhilArchive holds a copy should be rechecked
from a client it does not refuse, or a copy could be supplied.

From the abstract, the paper argues three things:
- In transformation cases the folk keep "person", "identity" and
  "individual" apart, so an individual can keep its identity while no longer
  counted a person.
- New survey data suggest psychological continuity is necessary for
  neither.
- Folk intuitions are of doubtful use in theorizing about personal
  identity.

If the data bear that out, they are folk-side convergent evidence for the
split between the personhood and persistence questions that [THEORY-417](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-417.md)
draws on conceptual grounds. That is a claim about usage, not about the
distinction's correctness. Nothing is changed in any THEORY until the paper
is read. Whether it retests Nichols & Bruno's first- against third-person
result, which [THEORY-531](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-531.md) now cites, is unknown.
