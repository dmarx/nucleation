# TERM-tmpa0fi1: frame, as inferred from evidence

## Definition

A44 §3A read context as evidence about a latent communicative situation z:
P(y | c, u) = ∫ P(y | z, u) P(dz | c). A45 §10: "**A frame is not necessarily a
coordinate system. It can also be evidence that induces an effective
interpretive model.**" It gave three senses: "Representational: R_f(m);
Inferential: P(z | c_f); Dynamical: K_f(s, ds′). The first changes the
representation of meaning. The second changes beliefs about which communicative
situation obtains. The third changes the interpreter's state and subsequent
dispositions. Our conversational path from relativistic frames to quantum
cognition was, in part, a gradual discovery that these three operations should
not be conflated."

A48 §3.2 gave the same distinction by example: "Being addressed by a friend is
a property of a situation. Being told that a speaker is a friend supplies
evidence about the situation. Being instructed to interpret an utterance
affectionately is an intervention on the interpretive process."

C1 §2, the first full draft (U22), stated all three: a situation *has* a frame,
interpreters *infer* one, and a framing intervention changes their inputs. "In
an LLM experiment, the sentence 'the speaker is a close friend' is evidence
about a supposed situation, not the social relationship itself." 

## What it is not

Not the frame that obtains ([TERM-tmpu9tab](TERM-tmpu9tab.md)), nor an intervention ([TERM-tmpkoyrc](TERM-tmpkoyrc.md)). It adds the
middle term that A39's two-way split lacked. A45 §6: "no unique, identifiable
latent variable z need exist that deserves to be called the pragmatic frame."
Kept in outline v4 (§2.3–2.4); the manuscript has none of the three-way
distinctions.
