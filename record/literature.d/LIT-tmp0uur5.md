# LIT-tmp0uur5: Elements of Information Theory

Thomas M. Cover and Joy A. Thomas (2006), *Elements of Information Theory*,
2nd edition, Wiley-Interscience — DOI-10.1002/047174882X

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* What follows is from the publisher's excerpts only (see
the history note): the table of contents and Chapter 1, the preview.

- **Scope, from the contents.** Seventeen chapters: entropy, relative
  entropy and mutual information (2); the AEP (3); entropy rates (4); data
  compression (5); gambling (6); channel capacity (7); differential
  entropy (8); the Gaussian channel (9); rate distortion theory (10);
  information theory and statistics, from the method of types to Fisher
  information (11); maximum entropy (12); universal source coding,
  including Lempel–Ziv (13); Kolmogorov complexity, including MDL (14);
  network information theory (15); portfolio theory (16); inequalities
  (17).
- **Where the side-information results are.** §15.4 (Slepian–Wolf,
  p. 549), §15.8 (source coding with side information, p. 575) and §15.9
  (rate distortion with side information, p. 580), the textbook treatment
  of the Wyner–Ziv paper, LIT-tmp8aw1m; side information in gambling is
  §6.2 and in investment §16.4.
- **The authors' framing** (Chapter 1). Information theory gives the two
  extreme points of communication: the compression limit, a minimum of
  I(X; X̂), and the transmission limit, capacity, a maximum of I(X; Y).
  They also say they "consider Kolmogorov complexity to be more
  fundamental than Shannon entropy", and that the increase in a gambler's
  doubling rate from side information equals the mutual information
  between the race and the side information.
