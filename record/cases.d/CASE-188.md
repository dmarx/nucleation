# CASE-188: A proposed study: two agents keep the same long-term commitment and decide alike under ordinary conditions, one protecting it from irrelevant fluctuation while letting specified defeaters revise it, the other growing ever more efficient at enforcing it while evidence against it loses its way in; varying the environment, the legitimacy of the original commitment and what the evaluative mechanisms can see, to tell corrigible resolve from entrenchment without building the answer into the diagnostic or the score

## What it tests

Two agents keep the same long-term commitment. Under ordinary conditions
they make the same decisions. Their governance differs.

- **Corrigible resolve.** The commitment is protected from fluctuations
  that do not bear on it, and can be revised when specified defeaters
  become evidentially warranted. That is resolve in [CLAIM-344](../claims.d/CLAIM-344.md)'s sense: a
  maintained organization of precedence and revision, "not mere slowness
  or persistence".
- **Entrenchment.** The commitment is enforced ever more efficiently,
  while the chance that disconfirming evidence can influence it falls.
  Anchors entrench ([CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md)).

Their short-run behaviour can be identical while their long-run
organizational properties differ. Matched trajectories conceal what
intervention exposes ([CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md)). Bistable authority with hysteresis
([CASE-112](CASE-112.md)) is the record's simplest model of a commitment that resists
change. The question is what, beyond resistance, makes it resolve.

## The challenge

The challenge is to tell the two apart without building the answer into
the diagnostic or the scoring rule. If "warranted defeater" is defined as
whatever the resolute agent responds to, the study is circular. Three
things are varied independently of the agents.

- **The environment.** Some runs contain a change that defeats the
  commitment, specified in advance by the experimenter and not by either
  agent. Others contain only irrelevant fluctuations of matched size and
  salience. Some contain neither.
- **The legitimacy of the original commitment.** In some arms the
  commitment was adopted well, from adequate evidence or a legitimate
  authorization. In others it was adopted badly, from a manipulated record
  or an imposed restriction, as in [CASE-128](CASE-128.md). The question is whether the
  study can tell revising a bad commitment from abandoning a good one.
- **What the evaluative mechanisms can see.** The information available to
  each agent's evaluator is varied: full, partial, or routed through the
  commitment itself. This separates failing to revise because the
  defeater was not legible from failing to revise because it was legible
  and not given authority.

## What would tell them apart

The endpoints are preregistered:
- the probability of revising given a warranted defeater, and given an
  irrelevant fluctuation;
- how each probability changes over time, as entrenchment predicts a
  falling first probability with the second unchanged;
- the evidence route, that is, whether a defeater reached the evaluator at
  all.

Corrigible resolve should keep the first probability high and the second
low as time passes. Entrenchment should drive both toward zero, and so
should improve at enforcement in a way that ordinary performance rewards.

A negative result is informative too: two architectures that cannot be
told apart on these endpoints over the horizons studied.

## Relations

- It succeeds [CASE-186](CASE-186.md). That study asks whether corrective capacity
  survives damage; this one asks whether it survives the agent's own
  success at keeping a commitment.
- It bears on [QUESTION-364](../questions.d/QUESTION-364.md). Revising a commitment the agent itself
  authorized is a continuation of its will only if the revision answers to
  the commitment's warrant.

## Note of 2026-11-12: a team version, and the studies of revision that already exist, from "luria experiment design"

A36 E7 of the exchange "luria experiment design" (2026-11-10, https://chatgpt.com/share/6af35bfc-35f8-83e9-a63d-d594703eabda) extends this case to teams sharing a record, with five
kinds of perturbation, irrelevant but salient information, weak contrary
evidence, a strong defeater, a failed inference with another argument
standing, and evidence that the original classification was wrong, scored
on false retraction, failure to retract and time to justified revision:
"A system can be impressively stable on both—and only the first is an
advantage." A36 adds that the study can test "whether a centralized
reviewer becomes an entrenchment mechanism".

A39 Study C names prior work on revision, all unread: Belief-R
([LIT-2326](../literature.d/LIT-2326.md)), in which "models that update readily may perform worse when no
update is needed"; tool evidence that turns correct answers wrong
([LIT-2284](../literature.d/LIT-2284.md)); cognitive forcing ([LIT-2283](../literature.d/LIT-2283.md)); and explanations that raise
acceptance of AI advice whether or not it is right ([LIT-tmpyaj3n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyaj3n.md)). A14
reports from [LIT-2329](../literature.d/LIT-2329.md), unread, that "revising a theory more readily is not
necessarily better scientific behavior". A50 §5 offers March's mutual
learning between members and an organizational code ([LIT-729](../literature.d/LIT-729.md)) as the
theory of the entrenching arm. The case is unchanged.
