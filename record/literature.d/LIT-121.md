# LIT-121: The Meaning of the Wave Function: In Search of the Ontology of Quantum Mechanics

Shan Gao (2016), *arXiv preprint (book draft); published by Cambridge University Press, 2017* — [ARXIV-1611.02738](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02738)

## Key takeaways

- Protective measurements, combined with the ontological-models assumption, show that the wave function is real. What it represents is the state of random discontinuous motion of particles in three-dimensional space, a picture that requires a stochastic, nonlinear collapse term in the dynamics.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Gao asks whether the wave function is ontic, epistemic or something else, and, if ontic, what physical state it represents. Part one reviews the rival views critically, gives a new argument for the ontic view from protective measurements, and derives the free Schrödinger equation from spacetime translation invariance. Part two interprets the wave function as describing random discontinuous motion (RDM) of particles. Part three asks whether this ontology accounts for definite experience and how it fares under relativity.
