# THEORY-tmpfian9: Of 853 national states of emergency declared from 1949 to 2017, 115 were reported as unlawful in declaration, conduct or duration; 97 of those followed political turmoil and 14 natural disasters, democracies were less likely than autocracies to run one, and unlawful emergencies lasted 321 days on average against 115

## Source

Bjørnskov, Voigt & Khesali, [LIT-tmp0s76v](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0s76v.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmp6j0uu](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6j0uu.md) from ILE
Working Paper No. 51 (August 2021): pp. 12–25 and Tables 1–6 and A1. The
version of record (JLS 51(2), 2022) was not read.

## What was actually shown

- **The counts.** 853 national SOEs, 1949–2017; 115 coded unlawful. By
  regime and cause (Table 1): democracies 8 of 271 after natural disasters
  and 41 of 241 after domestic turmoil; autocracies 6 of 68 and 56 of 245.
- **The phase.** Unlawful conduct during the emergency is commonest (65),
  then prolongation (37–39), then declaration (19), overlapping (Table 2,
  p. 22).
- **Duration.** "Lawful SOEs last 115 days on average while the average
  duration of unlawful SOEs is 321 days" (p. 22). 66 per cent of unlawful
  SOEs ran past their constitutional expiry; 63 per cent of lawful ones
  ended exactly at it.
- **Regime.** Democracy has a negative coefficient in every full-sample and
  domestic-events logit (Tables 4–5).

What could have come out otherwise: unlawful emergencies could have
clustered after disasters, where a well-meaning government is surprised by
the scale of the crisis, as the authors allowed (p. 8). Only 14 of 115 did.

## What this does not say

- **That emergency measures outlast emergencies.** The authors name that
  phase and have "no data on this aspect" (p. 6, fn. 2).
- **That the threat had passed.** No measure of threat decline; turmoil is
  partly caused by the government itself, as the authors stress.
- **That one in seven is the rate.** Coding by English-language press and
  NGO reports; the authors expect undercounting, especially in the global
  south and before 1990.
- **Its causes.** The logits are weak (pseudo-R² at most 0.07). The
  abstract's recession and grounds-for-emergency findings are simple
  differences, and its populism finding is an insignificant coefficient.
- **That the duration gap is independent of the coding.** Overlong SOEs
  with no recorded lawful extension are coded unlawful, which builds part
  of the gap in.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-tmp28sti](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp28sti.md).** Supports its political-institutional feedback in
  one place, prolongation: emergencies that escape their legal limits run
  much longer. The authors' untested hypothesis, that executives prolong an
  SOE to postpone review of their emergency conduct (p. 11), is the claim's
  mechanism stated for emergencies. It does not reach the claim's test,
  which needs threat decline measured.
