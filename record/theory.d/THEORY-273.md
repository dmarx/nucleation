# THEORY-273: Inductive and explanatory usefulness is necessary but not sufficient for a natural kind: it holds of classes that share any natural property, and on its own it cannot tell a natural kind from a conventional or pragmatic one

## Source

Alexander Bird and Emma Tobin, "Natural Kinds", *Stanford Encyclopedia
of Philosophy* (2008; substantive revision 28 January 2022), [LIT-244](../literature.d/LIT-244.md),
read in full in [NOTE-769](../notes.d/NOTE-769.md): §1.1.1, §1.2.1 and §3.

## What was actually shown

- **Not sufficient for kindhood (§1.1.1).** Support for inductive
  inference is "a necessary but not sufficient condition", and "does not
  go beyond" the condition that members share a natural property, which
  white things, negatively charged things and things of mass 1 kg meet
  without forming kinds. Participation in laws fails the same way: laws
  "may concern natural qualities and quantities that do not define kinds
  (e.g. Newton's law of gravitation)".
- **Induction is over properties and relations (§1.2.1).** "The real
  answer to 'what tends to confirm an induction?' is not 'natural kinds
  and their instances' but is 'natural properties and their instances'";
  and inductions "often concern relations … But we do not suppose that
  objects that enter into a natural relation form a natural kind."
- **Not sufficient against convention (§3).** Lemeire (2018), as
  reported: epistemic success "is constrained by deference to the world
  not only in cases of natural kind classification, but also in cases of
  conventional and pragmatic classification", so a purely epistemic
  approach "fails to give us a principled way of distinguishing between
  the natural kinds and their conventional counterparts". Kendig and Grey
  (2021), reported likewise: a cluster's stability cannot be assessed
  without the metaphysics that accounts for it.

The counterexamples could have failed: a class of white things or of
charged objects could have turned out to support the projections a kind
supports. That they do not is the survey's case.

## What this does not say

- **Not that usefulness is irrelevant.** Every account in the survey
  keeps inductive support as necessary, and the epistemology-first
  accounts (Slater 2015; Ereshefsky and Reydon 2015) make it central.
- **Not what does suffice.** The survey leaves the "kindhood" condition
  (criterion 4) unclear, and offers candidates without endorsing one:
  Boyd's homeostatic mechanisms, Slater's stability, Hacking's empirical
  discovery of a category's instances, essences.
- **Not a refutation of the epistemic approach.** Lemeire's argument is
  reported, not reproduced, and Slater's stability clause may escape it.
  This is why the account is Proposed.
- **Not about compression or information.** The entry never mentions
  either. The parallel with [THEORY-036](THEORY-036.md) is the record's, not the survey's.
