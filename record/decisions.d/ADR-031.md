# ADR-031: compositionality and mathematical-statistics join the topics

## Context

The manuscript batch of 2026-10-09 was read by several agents working in
parallel. Each reported, without seeing the others' reports, that the closed
vocabulary was missing a word.

- **Compositionality.** GenEval, T2I-CompBench and Composable Diffusion were
  filed with `representation-learning` first. Their readers found the tag
  hard to defend. None of the three papers studies how a model represents
  its data. GenEval and T2I-CompBench ask whether generated images bind
  attributes to objects and get relations right. Composable Diffusion
  composes concepts by adding one guidance term per concept. What the three
  have in common is compositionality. So do [LIT-667](../literature.d/LIT-667.md) and [THEORY-117](../theory.d/THEORY-117.md), which
  are about compositional generalization, and the compositional
  distributional semantics of [LIT-272](../literature.d/LIT-272.md) and [LIT-273](../literature.d/LIT-273.md), which are filed under
  `linguistics`, `logic` and `mathematics`.
- **Mathematical statistics.** Blackwell 1953 ([LIT-781](../literature.d/LIT-781.md)), Torgersen 1991
  ([LIT-778](../literature.d/LIT-778.md)) and the THEORY filed from Blackwell's paper went under
  `probabilistic-modeling`, whose blurb is "Bayesian inference and
  statistical models outside deep learning — nonparametric priors, topic
  models, samplers". The comparison of experiments is none of these things.
  The same mismatch applies to Fritz's theorems on sufficient statistics
  ([LIT-772](../literature.d/LIT-772.md), and the THEORY filed from it), and to Rao 1945 on the
  accuracy attainable in estimation ([LIT-349](../literature.d/LIT-349.md)).

The owner authorised extending the vocabulary on 2026-10-09.

## Decision

Add two topics.

- **`compositionality`** (Compositionality): how the structure and meaning
  of a whole come from its parts and the way they are combined. It covers
  the principle of compositionality and compositional semantics, and the
  emergence of compositional structure in languages under transmission. It
  also covers composition and binding in learned models: compositional
  generalization to unseen combinations, attribute binding, and composing
  distributions or concepts. It is a subject word. A work on how to make a
  model generalize compositionally is still ML practice, and goes to the
  anthology.
- **`mathematical-statistics`** (Mathematical statistics): the theory of
  statistical inference read for itself. It covers comparison and
  sufficiency of experiments (Blackwell, Le Cam deficiency), statistical
  decision theory, the sufficiency theorems (Fisher–Neyman, Basu,
  Bahadur), and the bounds on estimation. Bayesian modelling stays under
  `probabilistic-modeling`.

Retagged now:

- GenEval and T2I-CompBench take `compositionality` first in place of
  `representation-learning`. Both keep `anthology-candidate` last.
- Composable Diffusion takes `compositionality` first and drops
  `representation-learning`, which its reading did not support.
- [LIT-667](../literature.d/LIT-667.md), [THEORY-117](../theory.d/THEORY-117.md), [LIT-272](../literature.d/LIT-272.md) and [LIT-273](../literature.d/LIT-273.md) add `compositionality` after
  their present primary tag.
- Blackwell, Torgersen and the THEORY filed from Blackwell's paper take
  `mathematical-statistics` first and drop `probabilistic-modeling`, the
  nearest wrong word they had been given.
- Fritz and the THEORY filed from Fritz's paper add
  `mathematical-statistics` after `mathematics`.
- Rao 1945 ([LIT-349](../literature.d/LIT-349.md)) takes `mathematical-statistics` first.

Works still being read when this was decided are retagged once their
reading is filed. Kirby, Cornish and Smith is the obvious case for
`compositionality`.

## Alternatives

- **`generative-modeling`.** This is the subject the text-to-image papers
  share. It is also the anthology's topic word, and having it here would
  invite ML practice to be filed in this record. The `anthology-candidate`
  flag already says where those papers may belong.
- **Widening `probabilistic-modeling`.** That would turn a word about
  building models into a word about all of statistics. The blurb would
  then cover everything, and the tag would distinguish nothing.
- **`translation`.** Gutt and Hofstadter are the only two works, and neither
  has been read, because no lawful copy of either was reachable. Under the
  record's habit of waiting for a second instance, a decision can wait until
  one of them has been read.
