# QUESTION-082: When a polity is steered by polling, whose reasons do its decisions carry: its members', its own, or none?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) says an organized whole's operative intention is not the
expectation of its members' intentions. The owner asked what that implies
for a politics that steers by polls, and then put the point in terms of
reasons rather than goods: the whole's reasons need not be its members'
reasons. [CLAIM-271](../claims.d/CLAIM-271.md) carries that answer as a thesis. The question stays open
because three answers are live and the record cannot yet choose:

- **Its members' reasons.** A poll majority is a sum of members' reasons, and
  a decision that follows it carries them. Against this: a majority for one
  conclusion can be made of opposite reasons, so following it may carry no
  reason that any majority holds.
- **Its own reasons.** The polity's reasons are the ones its procedures
  (constitution, platform, deliberation, avowal) make, as [THEORY-131](../theory.d/THEORY-131.md) and
  [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) would have it, and polls are one input to them.
- **None.** A polity that only follows conclusions is aggregating by
  conclusion, and on [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md) its verdicts can then be ones that no set of
  reasons it holds would support. On [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) it is then not conversable:
  it cannot be held to reasons it does not have.

A related point, not yet a claim: a poll's answers are partly produced by
asking. [THEORY-166](../theory.d/THEORY-166.md) finds that question order changes answers across 66 Pew
surveys. If that holds, part of what a poll reports is a property of the
instrument rather than of the members.

What would count as an answer: evidence on whether policy in poll-responsive
democracies tracks aggregated member conclusions, a stated collective
rationale, or neither, and whether outcomes that follow conclusions are less
consistent across linked issues than outcomes reached by deliberation. The
record holds none of that literature yet. Gilens and Page (2014) and Achen
and Bartels' *Democracy for Realists* are candidates, once the anthology has
been checked for them.
