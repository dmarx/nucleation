# LIT-2179: Applied nucleation as a forest restoration strategy

Corbin, J. D. & Holl, K. D. (2012). Applied nucleation as a forest restoration strategy. *Forest Ecology and Management* 265: 37–46. doi:10.1016/j.foreco.2011.10.013.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. What follows comes from the works that stand in for it and
cite it, not from its own text.*

- **The definition.** Holl et al. (2024) cite it for applied nucleation
  as an approach "in which patches of vegetation (referred to variably as
  “nuclei,” “tree islands,” or “woodland islets”) are actively seeded or
  planted to accelerate forest recovery" (p. 2).
- **Strips or nuclei.** Cited for the open question of "whether planting
  in strips, nuclei, or other spatial arrangements is most effective in a
  given system" (Holl et al. 2024, p. 3).
- **Cost.** Schubert et al. (2024) cite it among sources for applied
  nucleation reducing project costs.

## Standing in the record

Unfetched. Two later works from Holl's group stand in for it, and only
so far:

- Holl, Reid, Zahawi, de Siqueira & Brancalion (2024), [LIT-2172](LIT-2172.md), read
  in [NOTE-1676](../notes.d/NOTE-1676.md): an opinion article by the same senior author twelve
  years on, for the mechanism, the comparison with plantation and natural
  regeneration, cost and the minimum nucleus. It gives the authors'
  current position, which may update this review's; its key numbers are
  cited from others.
- Schubert, Zahawi, Oviedo-Brenes, Rosales & Holl (2024), [LIT-2177](LIT-2177.md),
  read in [NOTE-1660](../notes.d/NOTE-1660.md): the 16–18-year census of the Costa Rican
  experiment, which stands in for Holl et al. (2020), [LIT-2145](LIT-2145.md), also
  unread.
