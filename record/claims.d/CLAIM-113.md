# CLAIM-113: Transformations and invariants define one another: translation preserves some structures exactly, carries some equivariantly, preserves some approximately and reorganizes others, and identity is studied through what a class of transformations leaves invariant

## The claim

A105: "we should recognize that **transformations and invariants define one
another**." Given a class G of transformations, study Inv(G); given a structure
F, study Aut(F), the transformations that preserve it. "An expression's identity
can be studied through the transformations under which its selected
communicative structure remains invariant." A more precise distinction "between
**transformations that preserve a structure, transformations that deform it, and
transformations that preserve some structures while changing others**". A102
had already said "structure is not simply what remains invariant under
transformation. Structure is also what makes transformations identifiable,
comparable, and meaningful."

Manuscript §4: "Structural transformations help identify structure through what
they preserve ... We therefore separate (i) exact preservation, (ii)
representation-changing equivariance, (iii) approximate preservation, and (iv)
structural reorganization." §11: "The invariants of a specified transformation
class identify what counts as continuity, while equivariant mappings make sense
of changes of medium."

## What it does not say

That all of this is a group: the manuscript's setting is a semigroup ([CLAIM-098](CLAIM-098.md)).
The Aut(F) direction, studying identity from the structure's side, did not reach
the manuscript.

## Prior statement in Piaget

Piaget, *Structuralism*, p. 11 ([LIT-837](../literature.d/LIT-837.md), skimmed): "all known
structures … are … systems of transformation". The book makes
transformation, not only relation, constitutive of structure.
