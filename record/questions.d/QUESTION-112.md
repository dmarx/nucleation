# QUESTION-112: How should the sufficiency of a retained state be judged when the family of future decisions can itself change in response to what the inquiry finds?

## Why it is a question

A8 ended: "under which decision families and resource constraints does
preserving justificatory history provide additional value beyond
preserving a sufficient current epistemic state?" A10 found the question
mis-posed. If a state is sufficient for a family, no history can improve
ideal risk on it. "So the interesting research problem is not whether
history provides information beyond a sufficient state. Properly
formulated, it cannot." What remains is "Under what conditions does a
state representation that is sufficient for one class of epistemic
decisions cease to be sufficient when the agent's questions, models,
sources, obligations, or computational resources change?"

The hard part is the family. A10 §4: "An observation can do more than
change the answer to an existing question. It can make an entirely new
question intelligible." Allow every conceivable question and lossless
retention is the trivial answer. Allow only today's and the record
discards what tomorrow's question needs. A10 proposed a robust objective
over an uncertainty set of future decision distributions, and named a
restricted class of transformations worth studying: invalidating a
source, revising a likelihood model, introducing an alternative, changing
a concept's scope, auditing an inference.

An answer fixes such a class, states the distribution over it, and says
what a retained state must keep. [CLAIM-295](../claims.d/CLAIM-295.md) is the record's candidate account
of why history would be worth keeping at all.
