# LIT-115: Duality as a category-theoretic concept

David Corfield (2015), *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part B: Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics 59, 55–61 (2017)* — DOI-10.1016/j.shpsb.2015.07.004

## Key takeaways

- Mathematical duality, which philosophers have neglected since Nagel (1939), is best understood category-theoretically as structural reversal organised around adjunction. Philosophers of physics should adopt category theory, because physicists' "dualities" often fall short of duality proper and predicate logic is ill-suited to the sameness questions they raise.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Nagel in 1939 described how projective duality freed mathematicians from thinking they had to describe intuitive elements, but philosophers have largely ignored duality since, even as it spread through twentieth-century mathematics. Corfield argues that a reassessment is overdue. Category theory has much to say about duality, through arrow reversal, dualising objects and adjunctions, alongside changes in how mathematics understands identity and structure. Physicists' use of "duality" does not always match mathematicians', but the mathematical analysis is still useful, and category theory is an important language for philosophy of physics.
