summary: >-
  Chen & Santangelo (2018). A flat triangulated rigid origami is a singular
  point of its configuration space: at first order every vertical
  displacement is a motion, so linearization fails. Expanding the length
  constraints to second order, each self stress of the flat network gives
  one quadratic constraint on vertex heights, and the wheel stress at each
  internal vertex is exactly preservation of its zero Gaussian curvature,
  so a single vertex's near-flat configurations form a double cone, popped
  up or popped down. For patterns with four boundary vertices, numerical
  root-finding on random triangulations finds 2^Vi branches meeting at the
  flat state, two per vertex sign pattern up to global sign, proven for H1
  triangulations. The authors infer that a sheet folded from flat is likely
  to misfold, and that driving forces orthogonal to every wrong branch
  cannot be found for even modest Vi.

# LIT-2021: Branches of Triangulated Origami Near the Unfolded State

Chen, B. G. & Santangelo, C. D. (2018). Branches of Triangulated Origami Near the Unfolded State. *Physical Review X* 8(1): 011034. doi:10.1103/PhysRevX.8.011034. arXiv:1706.03869.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1614](../notes.d/NOTE-1614.md). Sections are the arXiv version's.*

- **Why second order.** "the degeneracy of the unfolded state makes a
  linear approach impossible there" (abstract).
- **Curvature is the constraint.** "the wheel stress constraint is
  precisely Gaussian curvature preservation" (§II B, App. B).
- **Exponentially many branches.** With few exceptions, all 2^(Vi+1)
  Bézout roots of the second-order system came out real, "all the
  solutions of our system are distinct and isolated" (§III A), giving
  2^Vi branches; and "there are exactly two branches with each such pair
  of sign patterns" (§III B). Proven only for H1 triangulations (§IV).
- **Not mountain–valley.** "The branches are not necessarily distinguished
  by the mountain and valley assignments of the folds" (§I).
- **Self-folding.** "once an origami structure begins folding along the
  wrong branch, it is potentially very difficult to return it to the
  desired branch" (§V), and a driving force orthogonal to every wrong
  branch is "impossible" for even modest numbers of internal vertices.

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures cites it for one point,
that second order is needed because linearization fails at the flat state;
that is right, and it is the paper's own motivation. A states none of the
results, and the one that bears most on A's "Feasible target ⇏ reachable
target" is the self-folding consequence: every branch is feasible, and the
difficulty is selecting one. The paper is local to the flat state and did
not compute how branches join globally, so A's distinctions among
existence, continuous reachability, singular passage and branch selection
are A's framing. The record held no rigidity or origami entry before this
filing. Filed from it: [THEORY-1427](../theory.d/THEORY-1427.md). No instruction for machine-learning
practice.
