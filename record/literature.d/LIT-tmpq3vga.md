**What the author's précis says** (JSO 1(1): 1–5, read in full):

- **The aim.** The planning structures central to an individual's
  cross-temporal agency "are also at the core of these important forms
  of sociality". The book seeks sufficient conditions for shared
  intentional activity and leaves open whether they are necessary.
- **The construction.** You and I share an intention to J when:
  1. each intends that we J;
  2. each intends that we J by way of the intentions of each, mutual
     responsiveness, and meshing subplans;
  3. each believes the intentions in 1 are persistence-interdependent and
     will, if they persist, lead to our J-ing;
  4. the intentions in 1 are persistence-interdependent;
  5. all this is common knowledge.
  This requires rejecting the "own-action condition", so that I can
  intend that *we* act.
- **The norms.** Individual planning norms (consistency, agglomeration,
  means–end coherence, stability) induce social counterparts. That is why
  each participant is under rational pressure to coordinate, help and
  mesh.
- **Against Gilbert and Searle.** The précis names them as appealing to
  "basic new elements over and above those involved in individual
  intentional agency". Bratman's approach "is reductive in spirit".
  Persistence interdependence "may, but need not, be based on relevant
  mutual moral obligations". A shared intention "need not involve a group
  subject who intends to J".
- **Shared deliberation.** Shared policies about the weights given to
  reasons form a "common ground" that goes beyond common knowledge. They
  can hold where the participants' evaluative judgments and reasons
  differ.

**What a reading should check.**

- Chapter 5: whether mutual obligation is shown to be optional for
  shared intention, or only for the construction's sufficiency.
- Chapter 6: what "group agents without group subjects" are, and whether
  the account makes room for a corporate agent of List & Pettit's kind.
- Whether the "out in the open" condition, which the précis flags as a
  possible exception, really goes beyond the planning theory's resources.
