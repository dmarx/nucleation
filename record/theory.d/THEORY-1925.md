# THEORY-1925: Partner violence types differ by control context

## Source

Johnson (2006), Violence Against Women 12(11): 1003-1018 ([LIT-2703](../literature.d/LIT-2703.md)), the stand-in for Johnson (2008), [LIT-2715](../literature.d/LIT-2715.md);
read in [NOTE-2028](../notes.d/NOTE-2028.md) (draft).

## What was actually shown

Using Frieze's interviews with 274 women, Johnson classified each violent spouse by whether he or she and the partner
were "violent and controlling". "The distinction lies in the degree of control present." The controlling spouse in
intimate terrorism "is the husband in 94 of the 97 cases"; the general sample "includes almost nothing but situational
couple violence" while court and shelter samples are mostly intimate terrorism. Mutual violent control occurred in
"only five such couples (10 individuals)".

## What this does not say

- That responsibility follows control: the typology is descriptive.
- That the controlled party commits fewer violent acts as a rule in every case, though in its data wives in men's
  intimate terrorism rarely responded with violence.
- Anything quantitative about mutual violent control, which was too rare to describe.
- What the 2008 book adds beyond this article.
