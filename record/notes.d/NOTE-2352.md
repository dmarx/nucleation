# NOTE-2352: Meijer and Rantzer — dual control and exploration-exploitation in linear systems

## Contribution

A compact, recent review of dual control in the sense of active learning
for control: the controller "must balance immediate performance against
the value of new information" (§7). It traces the idea to Feldbaum, who
"introduced the term dual control in the early 1960s … to describe the
need for feedback controllers … to simultaneously learn and regulate the
plant" (§1.1), and organizes the subsequent literature into four strands
(Figure 2): multi-armed bandits (§2), self-tuning regulators (§3),
regret-rate minimization for linear-quadratic control (§4), and minimax
optimal dual control (§5). It is a map of the field, not a new result,
though §5 presents the authors' own minimax line as an active direction.

## Key results

- **The tradeoff and its origin (§1.1).** "The term 'dual control' refers
  to the dual objective of simultaneously balancing exploration and
  exploitation" (abstract). Feldbaum "emphasized that learning often needs
  to be active: Without probing, you will not learn how the system
  responds." "Aggressive probing may deteriorate performance but improve
  parameter estimates. Conversely, cautious control may give better
  short-term performance but fail to gather information" (§1.1). He
  addressed it "using Bellman's work on dynamic programming … by combining
  the physical state with an information state"; Åström (1965) introduced
  POMDPs as a rigorous framework (§1.1).
- **Bandits (§2).** The best-developed theory of the tradeoff: the Gittins
  index gives the optimal Bayesian discounted policy as an optimal-stopping
  computation (§2.1); Lai & Robbins proved regret must grow at least like
  log T and built a policy attaining it (§2.2); the upper-confidence-bound
  rule realizes "optimism in the face of uncertainty" (§2.3).
- **Self-tuning regulators (§3).** A minimum-variance controller with
  recursively estimated parameters (Åström & Wittenmark, 1973). The
  tradeoff "appears" through persistence of excitation: "If only the
  optimal controller is used, then we do not collect information about
  other options" (§3.2).
- **When exploration is fundamentally needed (§4.2).** Logarithmic regret
  is achievable when non-identifiable parameters under the optimal policy
  are unimportant; "if the optimal controller makes essential parameters
  non-identifiable, which is often the case, then exploration needs to
  drive the system away from optimality with higher regret as a result" —
  separating log T from √T problems. The classical certainty-equivalence
  idea of Bar-Shalom & Tse (1974) with added probing attains the optimal
  rate (§4.3).
- **Minimax dual control (§5).** The adaptive control problem is posed as
  a zero-sum dynamic game against an adversary that also picks the unknown
  parameters; past data are compressed into a constant-size matrix Z(t),
  and "with Feldbaum's nomenclature, Z(t) can be viewed as the information
  state, complementing the physical state x(t)" (§5.2.2). A three-step
  value iteration shows "the concave parabola … causing the controller to
  probe the system by applying non-zero actuation even when no prior
  knowledge is available", which "renders the objective … non-convex";
  input randomization recovers convexity (Figure 3, §5.3).

## Assumptions

Linear time-invariant systems x(t+1) = Ax(t) + Bu(t) + w(t) with unknown
(A, B) in a known model class M, continuous state space, quadratic cost.
The strands differ in M and in the disturbance w: stochastic white noise
(self-tuning, regret-rate) versus worst-case/colored disturbances
(minimax). The regret-rate results "rely on idealized conditions"; small
model mismatch generally degrades the rate from log T or √T (§4.2). Several
results assume a pre-stabilizing controller K0 is known; the minimax
approach does not (§4.3, §5).

## Concepts

- **Dual control** — "Feedback control that simultaneously regulates the
  plant and probes it to reduce uncertainty" (margin gloss, §1.1).
- **Information state** — the compressed history (here Z(t)) that, with the
  physical state, makes the optimal dual policy a function of a
  fixed-dimension state (§5.2).
- **Dual effect** — Bar-Shalom & Tse (1974), "Dual effect, certainty
  equivalence, and separation in stochastic control": a control action has
  the double role of regulating and informing.
- **Persistence of excitation** — the input-richness condition under which
  closed-loop estimates stay consistent (§3.2).
- **Certainty equivalence** — "Control strategy that uses current estimates
  as if they were the true parameters" (margin gloss, §4.3).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md) and [CLAIM-535](../claims.d/CLAIM-535.md).** Both set aside "the case where
  acting is itself observing (… dual control (Feldbaum))" and left the
  citation unchecked. This review checks it: Feldbaum, A. A. (1960/1963),
  "Dual control theory", is correctly the origin of the term and of the
  probing/caution duality. The dual-control case is the one where the
  observing and acting budgets of [CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md) are not separable — the same
  action does both — so it is the natural complement of [CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md)'s
  separated-budget model, not a rival to it. A dated note records this.
- **[QUESTION-374](../questions.d/QUESTION-374.md).** The question's third part asks where observing
  and acting draw on one resource; dual control is the limiting case in
  which they draw on the *same action*. The review's closed-loop
  identifiability lesson — being optimal can make the informative
  directions vanish — is a mechanism for why a system must sometimes spend
  performance to learn, parallel to the budget trade the question poses.
- **[THEORY-1254](../theory.d/THEORY-1254.md) (Saligrama).** A direct point of contact: Saligrama's
  "information before commitment" is a safety-constrained dual-control
  limitation, and its NOTE already names "dual control and controlled
  sensing" as the nearest frameworks. This review gives the unconstrained
  dual-control background against which that bound is a restriction.
- **[LIT-729](../literature.d/LIT-729.md) (March).** The review cites exploration/exploitation in
  organizational science (Lavie, Stettner & Tushman, 2010) as one of the
  fields the same tradeoff was rediscovered in; a parallel, not evidence.

## Limitations

- A review, not a source of new theorems (its §5 aside); its account of
  each strand is a summary, and the record should go to the primary works
  for anything load-bearing.
- Scope is linear systems with continuous state; the discrete-state POMDP
  and reinforcement-learning literatures are only pointed to.
- The non-convexity that forces input randomization (Figure 3) is a
  property of the minimax value iteration, not the same object as
  [CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md)'s non-concavity of the observe/act budget frontier; the two
  should not be conflated.
