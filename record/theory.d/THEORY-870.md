# THEORY-870: Experienced Transcendental Meditation practitioners report more pure awareness than matched counting controls, and multivariate EEG separates the meditation period from counting and from rest, but the reports are not shown to track any EEG feature

## Source

Chandia-Jorquera, van Mil, Estarellas, Dauphin, Pascovich &
Canales-Johnson (2026), [LIT-1354](../literature.d/LIT-1354.md), read in full in [NOTE-1058](../notes.d/NOTE-1058.md) from
the bioRxiv preprint, version 3 (2 April 2026): methods (pp. 2–6),
results (pp. 6–10), discussion (pp. 11–14). The version of record in the
Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience was not read.

## What was actually shown

**Reports.** 33 TM practitioners with at least four years' practice and 33
controls matched on sex and age traced, after a 30-minute session, how far
they had experienced "pure awareness", on a scale whose top was defined
for them as "an experience free from content defining everyday working
experiences" (p. 3). Practitioners' peak traces were higher (0.697 against
0.523, d = 0.771) and their traces more variable (d = 1.32) (p. 6). The
peak difference could have come out null: controls, too, rated their
counting at about half the scale.

**EEG.** A random-forest classifier on 65,920 features per trial, tested
on held-out subjects in 100 repetitions of nested cross-validation,
separated meditation from counting at 65% balanced accuracy, with
permutation entropy and aperiodic activity most important (p. 8). Within
practitioners it identified meditation epochs against rest with 88%
sensitivity, with low-frequency phase-lag connectivity most important
(p. 9). It could not separate practitioners' rest before meditation from
rest after it, which it could do for controls around counting (pp. 9–10).

**The missing link.** "we did not identify robust direct associations
between the PA measures and the EEG features" (p. 14). The authors do not
take this to mean there is none.

## What this does not say

- **It is not a neural signature of pure awareness.** The EEG separates a
  meditation period from a counting period and from rest. Within that
  period, no EEG feature was shown to follow the reports. The authors'
  "double dissociation" is a difference between two feature-importance
  rankings.
- **It does not show experience without content.** The reports are
  retrospective ratings on a scale defined for the participant by the
  experimenters. The authors concede possible demand effects for controls
  (p. 13). Practitioners were recruited through the TM organisation and
  know what the practice is said to produce. Woods, Windt & Carter
  ([LIT-1351](../literature.d/LIT-1351.md); [THEORY-866](THEORY-866.md)) find that TM's own teachers describe the
  state as keeping wakefulness, calm, bliss and knowing.
- **It does not separate state from trait or task.** The 65% contrast is
  meditators meditating against non-meditators counting.
- **The null correlation with years of practice is not evidence of
  effortless access.** It is the absence of an association in 33 people.
- **The peak-report p-value is overstated.** t = 3.10 is printed with
  p < 0.001; with about 64 degrees of freedom it gives p ≈ 0.003
  (recomputed in [NOTE-1058](../notes.d/NOTE-1058.md)). The difference stands at p < 0.01.
