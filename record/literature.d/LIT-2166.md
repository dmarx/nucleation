# LIT-2166: The Effect of Wealth on Individual and Household Labor Supply: Evidence from Swedish Lotteries

Cesarini, D., Lindqvist, E., Notowidigdo, M. J. & Östling, R. (2017). The Effect of Wealth on Individual and Household Labor Supply: Evidence from Swedish Lotteries. *American Economic Review* 107(12): 3917–3946. doi:10.1257/aer.20151589. Read as NBER Working Paper 21762 (November 2015), https://www.nber.org/papers/w21762.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1648](../notes.d/NOTE-1648.md). Page numbers are the working paper's
(pp. 1–29), not the AER's.*

- **Two versions, two sets of numbers.** The AER abstract gives lifetime
  marginal propensities to earn (MPEs) "from -0.17 at age 20 to -0.04 at
  age 60"; the working paper read here gives −0.186 and −0.046 (Table 6).
  The working paper also contradicts itself: β is 0.845 in the text
  (p. 21) and 0.855 in Table 5, and γ_h, the maximum annual hours, 1,870.0
  in the text and 1852 in the table. The numbers below are the working
  paper's.
- **Immediate and lasting.** "In the year of the lottery event, labor
  earnings decline immediately and stabilize at a level roughly 1,150 SEK
  lower for each 100,000 SEK won" (p. 13), "roughly constant over time and
  lasting more than 10 years" (p. 3). A 1M SEK prize, about six years of
  median disposable income, cuts pre-tax earnings over ten years by about
  80,000 SEK (p. 3).
- **Hours more than exit.** "a 1M SEK prize reduces hours worked by 3.11%
  of full-time, corresponding to 1.3 hours per week, or 60 hours per year"
  (p. 15); leaving work accounts for about half the response at first
  and a third six years on.
- **Hardly any heterogeneity.** Similar by age at first, sex, education,
  lottery and lump sum against instalments; "the marginal effect of
  lottery wealth is smaller for larger prizes, but the difference is not
  statistically significant" (p. 16).
- **Self-employment falls**, which "contrasts with several studies finding
  that positive wealth shocks increase transition into self-employment"
  (p. 3).
- **Spouses respond less.** Per 100 SEK, married winners −0.981 and their
  spouses −0.458 (Table 7), against unitary household models.
- **Modest, through a model.** "roughly 15% of unearned income is spent
  reducing after-tax labor earnings, with the rest spent increasing
  consumption" (p. 21).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the windfall-and-scarcity batch of 2026-11-05, after the owner
suggested lottery winners as cases. It is the first lottery or windfall
study held. It is the case [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) reserves "slack effort" for:
money that is unconditional, large, permanent and never withdrawn. Effort
does fall, at once and for good, but by little, and there is no sign of a
size threshold in the labour response ([CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md)). Nothing was
withdrawn and skill was not measured, so it says nothing about what is
lost when support stops ([CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)). Set beside [THEORY-1471](../theory.d/THEORY-1471.md) (no
effect of small transfers on work) and [THEORY-1466](../theory.d/THEORY-1466.md) (responses expected
to differ where transfers are dependable and permanent), it is the
dependable, permanent case, with a modest reduction; set beside
[THEORY-1465](../theory.d/THEORY-1465.md), wealth here lowers self-employment rather than lifting
anyone over a threshold, in a population that is not poor.

The paper says that "lottery winners do not squander their wealth" (p. 11),
but the main text supports it only through how wealth is split between
spouses, from appendix tables that were not read; the record does not
assert it. Filed from it: [THEORY-1479](../theory.d/THEORY-1479.md).
