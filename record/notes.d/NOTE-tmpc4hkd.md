# NOTE-tmpc4hkd: The Conversable, Responsible Corporation

## Contribution

A compact restatement and extension of Pettit's case for corporate
responsibility. It joins List and Pettit's group-agency results to an
account of avowal and commitment ("conversability") and to a regulative,
non-libertarian account of what holding responsible is.

## Key insight

What makes an attitude the corporation's is not that members hold it. It
is that the corporation has made it up, through spokespersons whose words
members have authorised and procedures that correct its aggregate. It is
then answerable for living up to those words.

## Assumptions

- A functional account of agency: reliable representation and action in
  normal conditions (after List & Pettit 2011, ch. 1).
- That consistency is a minimal requirement on any agent others can do
  business with.
- That holding responsible is a regulative practice, so a disposition to
  respond to reasons suffices and contra-causal freedom is not needed.

## Key results

Argument, not results:

- Corporations are agents; the "nexus of contracts" objection is a
  fallacy of composition.
- Spokespersons avow and promise for the corporation, and members'
  authorisation makes the words the corporation's.
- The discursive dilemma (p, q, not p&q by majority) means a conversable
  corporation must correct its aggregate, so it has "a mind of its own".
- Three conditions of fitness to be held responsible (grave matter, full
  knowledge, full consent), all met by conversable agents.
- A corporation cannot coherently claim to lack normative judgment
  (performative contradiction), nor, over time, the means to act on it.

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Corporations are agents in the functional sense | moderate | argument from the functional profile; rebuttal of the contracts objection |
| C2 | A corporation that corrects its aggregate has attitudes not fixed by its members' attitudes on each issue | strong | the discursive dilemma, proved elsewhere (List & Pettit) |
| C3 | Conversable agents are fit to be held responsible without contra-causal freedom | moderate | argued from the regulative point of the practice |
| C4 | Corporations cannot excuse themselves by incapacity over the longer haul | moderate | argued: performative contradiction; capacity to acquire capacity |

## Concepts

- **conversable agent** — one that uses words to form, not merely report,
  its attitudes, and commits itself by them.
- **avowal** — expressing an attitude in a way that forgoes the excuse of
  having misread one's own mind.
- **fitness to be held responsible** — grave matter, full knowledge of the
  guilt, full consent of the will.

## Connections

Builds on French's corporate decision structure, List & Pettit's group
agency and judgment aggregation, Dennett's intentional stance, and Hobbes
(against whom the "mind of its own" claim is aimed). Restates
[LIT-tmpoyd9z](../literature.d/LIT-tmpoyd9z.md).

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-tmpe1ql0](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpe1ql0.md). Its group-agency premise is [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md)'s
judgment-aggregation result.

For the owner's essay it addresses two gaps. §7.1 needs corporate
endorsement that is not regressive. Pettit grounds ownership in authorised
avowal and corrective procedure, not in a higher-order attitude. §8
attributes normative competence to an organization, and Pettit's condition
(2) is that attribution, with an argument. The essay should engage both,
and should note that Pettit's corporations need not be *good*. Fitness to
be held responsible is not legitimacy, which is the distinction the essay
draws in §8.5.

## Limitations

Brisk by design. It relies on earlier work for the agency premises, and it
sets aside deception, a corporation masking its real attitudes, "for the
sake of simplicity" (p. 10). It does not address criminal liability.

## Open questions

Whether a corporation's normative judgment is the same capacity as an
individual's or only functionally similar, and what counts as an excusing
condition for a corporation.
