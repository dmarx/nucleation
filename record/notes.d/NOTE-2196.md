# NOTE-2196: Tomasello (interview) — What a Squirrel Knows That Language Models Skip (Michael Tomasello)

## Contribution

Tomasello's own short statement of the agency book's typology and of what
it implies for building artificial agents, which the book itself leaves
mostly aside. The interviewer's contributions (whether agency is reified
like intelligence; whether a market is better treated as an agent; whether
collectives factorize knowledge) are the interviewer's, not Tomasello's.

## Key results

1. **Why agents exist.** "When you need a decision making agent is when the
   environment is unpredictable." (2:54). "So for me, the most
   unpredictable are other agents." (3:21).
2. **Organization, not substance.** By analogy with vitalism, "the living
   things are made of the same basic elements as nonliving things. They're
   just organized in a different way." (20:23). Thermostats and robot
   vacuums show the basic organization, but "I would not call them agents"
   (11:08). "it's really not about complexity" but "about control" (9:42).
3. **Learning as a subprocess.** "learning is only exists to help the agent
   make adaptive decisions." "So learning is a subprocess." (18:25–18:33).
   Reinforcement learning "does have a little agentic quality, but it's
   mainly focused on on learning." (19:06).
4. **What machines lack.** Of self-driving cars and drones: "I think the
   machines have not really embodied that in the right way. It's not just a
   separate little skill." "It's a whole another level of organization. I
   call it a tier of organization." (13:29–13:38). They would need to
   "create novel goals in situations where they don't know what to do"
   (15:41).
5. **Language models.** They "are starting at the pinnacle of human
   achievement" (19:29), while "language is built on all of these other more
   basic cognitive capacities." (19:42). "large language models, to my
   knowledge, don't create linguistic conventions." (43:11). The
   prescription: "try to build something that starts from the bottom up,
   that starts from animals coordinating" (55:31).
6. **The grades, restated.** Reptiles, as models of the first land
   vertebrates, "are organized like a control system" making "only very
   simple decisions" (32:56–33:05); "with mammals, you get an executive layer
   of control" (33:12) and "imaginative representations" (33:46);
   "metacognitive or rational agency, which I see for sure in great apes"
   (35:34); and humans "can form shared agents" (37:14). He ties each grade
   to its main source of unpredictability: prey for reptiles, competing
   group mates for mammals, contest competition for great apes (36:15–36:56).
7. **Influences, not determinism.** Emotions are "nature putting its finger
   on the scale of your decision making." (27:17). Against massive
   modularity, an agent uses "general general processes that cut across all
   domains" (25:54).
8. **Against "intelligence".** He prefers "cognitive capacities, knowledge
   gained from experiencing the world and learning" (31:43) to a construct
   tied to school testing.
9. **Perspective is social.** A child raised alone "wouldn't get the notion
   of perspective" (39:48), following Mead and Davidson; joint attention
   "creates the notion of perspective" (38:55).
10. **Markets and capital.** "I'm not a big fan of talking about inanimate
    things as agents." (1:01:36). Traffic jams, inflation and crashes are
    "phenomena of the third kind" that emerge "from the intentional actions
    of individuals" (1:02:12–1:02:32). Talking of a market as an agent is
    "a useful way to talk", but "If you're saying it is an agent, I I start
    to get a little nervous." (1:04:09–1:04:13).
11. **Lying.** "Lying only works because we're cooperative." (59:26).
12. **Tests.** "a Turing test is not even close to sufficient" (1:05:16);
    yet a machine with all these qualities he "would have to feel like it
    was some kind of an agent" (1:05:35).

## Assumptions

Agency is feedback-control organization; comparative psychology uses
living species as models of ancestors; shared intentionality is unique to
humans. Claims about AI are offered as opinion, with stated uncertainty
about the technology.

## Concepts

Decision-making agent; control versus complexity; executive and
metacognitive tiers; imaginative representations; learning as subprocess;
shared agency and joint commitment; perspective; conventions as
coordination devices; phenomena of the third kind; influences versus
determinism.
