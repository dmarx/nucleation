# THEORY-tmps67up: On Martinez-Garcia and colleagues, dryland vegetation models in which plants only compete over a finite range, and models in which they facilitate nearby and compete farther away, both give the gap–labyrinth–spot sequence along falling rainfall, but the first lose vegetation smoothly and the second collapse abruptly with hysteresis, so the pattern alone does not say which collapse follows; a transplant experiment reading the sign of the net plant interaction under canopy and in bare soil would tell them apart

## Source

Martinez-Garcia et al., LIT-tmp5mwek, read in full in NOTE-tmpbqaec from
the arXiv preprint.

## What was actually shown

The review reruns published models "using the same setup described in the
original publications" (p. 12). In those reruns, the Rietkerk model loses
vegetation abruptly at about R ≈ 0.55 mm/day and recovers only at higher
rain, and the PCF model declines continuously. That both give the same
pattern sequence is shown in the cited works, not here. The transplant
design (Table 2) is proposed, not carried out.

## What this does not say

- That any real dryland is PCF or SDF.
- That facilitation, rather than diffusion, is what makes the SDF collapse
  abrupt. The authors leave this open.
- Anything about two plant types competing, or about bistability between
  two vegetated states.
