**Two more, registered on the way.**

- Harris et al. 2019 (LIT-tmpnfh60), failed replications of the
  selective-accessibility measure, turned up in the search for the 1999
  paper. Read (NOTE-tmpodfzg) from the authors' manuscript on UCL
  Discovery.
- Goldreich 1990 (LIT-tmp5gb4p), which the book cites for the equivalence
  between samplable separations and one-way functions without proving it.
  Filed at the owner's request.

**What the readings changed.**

- CLAIM-tmpvdl53: the separation is unconditional (the book's
  Proposition 3.3); only an efficiently samplable witness needs an
  assumption. Its defeat condition overstated.
- CLAIM-tmpzk2fj and CASE-tmp51hxx: wording tightened to what the book
  proves; postprocessing is an exercise there, not a theorem.
- CLAIM-tmp9snb1: the selective-accessibility account is theory with
  indirect support, its most direct measure does not replicate, and its
  defeat condition no longer assumes retrieval and evaluation separate.
- CLAIM-tmp2rwpl: its full-belief counterpart, Hansson's Abe and Bob.
- QUESTION-tmpga1fv: narrowed. Bases and orderings are one-step answers,
  and neither is provenance.
- LIT-tmp0f102 and LIT-tmpr5312: the first had Hansson's point, the
  second phrases that are A10's paraphrase.

**Worth registering next.** Hutchins's "How a cockpit remembers its
speeds" (Cognitive Science 1995), which he posts himself and which is
closer to the EMT case (CASE-tmp9qlz6) than the book. Mussweiler and Strack
2000a (JPSP 78), the study Harris et al. failed to replicate.
