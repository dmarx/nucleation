  authors attribute the failures to kinetic trapping and scaffold

# LIT-2123: Optimising DNA origami assembly by reducing off-target interactions

Shirt-Ediss, B., Torelli, E., Navarro, S. A., Khamis, H., Kaplan, A., Trewby, W., Elezgaray, J., Moradzadeh, N., Haydell, M., Keppner, D., Famulok, M., Armstrong, K. & Krasnogor, N. (2026). Optimising DNA origami assembly by reducing off-target interactions. *Nature Communications* 17: 6859. doi:10.1038/s41467-026-73387-4. Preprint, under another title: bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2025.01.29.635450.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1627](../notes.d/NOTE-1627.md).*

- **Only the sequence differs.** All variants of a shape "had a fully
  complementary staple set to the scaffold sequence, and all made the
  target shape when fully self-assembled"; "The only factor
  differentiating variants was the number of off-target bindings possible
  in each case."
- **Off-target-prone sequences fail.** The variants scored worst "nearly
  completely failed to fold into the intended target shapes in the buffer
  conditions used (only 0–20% well-folded AFM yield)"; the best reached
  65% (triangle), 74.8% (tetrahedron) and about 66% (6-helix bundle).
- **A heuristic, not a yield predictor.** The metrics are "general
  heuristics for high-yield folding (but not absolute measures of it)",
  and the method "cannot predict classic assembly features such as
  structure melting temperature, speed of folding, or potential
  cooling/re-heating hysteresis".
- **Traps are inferred.** The off-target-prone variants aggregated, and
  the authors conclude "it is still possible to kinetically trap origami
  folding if off-target reactions are too prevalent"; no trap is located.
- **One anomaly.** In the replication, "R2 folded with the highest AFM
  yield, followed by R1 and then R3": the de Bruijn control lost to a
  Lambda sequence for the rectangle.

## Standing in the record

Registered from A's third paste on DNA origami, which cited it as showing
how unintended binding interferes with target assembly. The reading
supports that off-target-prone sequences fold worse; it does not show how,
since no trap or pathway is observed. It is the record's cleanest instance
of one feasible target reached reliably or not according to something
other than the target: it supports [CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md), and it qualifies [CLAIM-583](../claims.d/CLAIM-583.md),
since the variants share the domain-level design but not the full
base-level set of compatibilities. It complements DeLuca et al.
([LIT-2081](LIT-2081.md), [THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)), whose traps are geometric with on-target binding
only, is in tension with Dannenberg et al. (2015), [LIT-2129](LIT-2129.md), which
omits misbinding, and pairs with Majikes et al. (2026), [LIT-2127](LIT-2127.md),
where design rather than sequence moves yield at a fixed shape. Filed from
it: [THEORY-1455](../theory.d/THEORY-1455.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing
for the anthology.
