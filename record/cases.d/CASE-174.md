# CASE-174: Paulinella chromatophora: a photosynthetic organelle in the making, from an endosymbiosis independent of the plastids', its genome reduced to a quarter, more than thirty genes moved to the host nucleus and their proteins imported back

## The case

*Paulinella chromatophora* is a thecate amoeba with photosynthetic
inclusions, chromatophores. They descend from a cyanobacterium acquired in a
primary endosymbiosis independent of, and much later than, the one that gave
plants their plastids: about 60 million years ago, by [LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md)'s estimate.

- **Reduction ([LIT-1621](../literature.d/LIT-1621.md)).** The chromatophore genome is 1.02 Mb with
  867 protein-coding genes, 26% of a free-living relative's. Pseudogenes
  show reduction still under way. It keeps the photosynthesis genes and
  lacks essential amino-acid and cofactor pathways, so it depends wholly on
  its host.
- **Transfer and import ([LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md)).** More than thirty expressed genes
  moved to the amoeba's nucleus. Three photosystem I subunits encoded by
  transferred genes are made in the host cytoplasm, imported into the
  chromatophores, apparently via the Golgi, and assembled with
  chromatophore-encoded subunits.
- **In context ([LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md)).** A 2024 review counts hundreds of imported
  proteins in such recently evolved organelles. It asks whether import
  still separates organelles from endosymbionts.

## What it can show

- **The organelle route, recent and separate.** Gene transfer plus import
  gives the host regulatory control (C) over a former organism. This is the
  route the mealybug ([CASE-171](CASE-171.md)) and aphid ([CASE-173](CASE-173.md)) cases do not
  take ([QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)).
- **A second independent run.** Since it is independent of the plastids,
  it shows which steps recur when a photosynthetic organelle is made.

abstracts until they are read.
