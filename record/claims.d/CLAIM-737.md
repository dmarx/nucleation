# CLAIM-737: That a capability's exercise should be limited and that a given party may limit it are separate conclusions on separate grounds, neither entailing the other; the power to restrict is itself a capability, whose legitimacy turns on how it is bounded and answerable and not on whether the limit it imposes is right

## The claim

A45 §6 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b),
writing a judgment of restriction with its relata fixed, as [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md)
asks ("Actor A's authority to exercise capability C over person B, within
relationship G, should be restricted under normative standard N"), then
splitting it:

> **The grounds of restriction:** Why would the unqualified exercise of C be wrongful, harmful, dominating, or otherwise unjustified?

> **The authority of restriction:** Why may this particular actor or institution determine the limits of C?

"The second question is not answered by the first." A's examples: a
parent who rightly sees a danger is not thereby justified "in imposing
every imaginable restriction"; a government that identifies a harm lacks
"unlimited authority to determine how citizens must live"; an employer
with a legitimate need is not "entitled to eliminate employees' capacity
for collective action". A's summary:

> **The fact that a capability should be limited does not establish that every actor capable of limiting it has the authority to do so.**

**The second half** is A41 §5: "The power to restrict capabilities is
itself a capability", and "often a second-order capability: it governs
which other powers may be exercised." So a theory of restriction "must
also explain **how the authority to restrict is itself organized and
constrained**". A41 answers the regress (who restricts the restrictor?)
without a higher authority: "We need a sufficiently justified arrangement
of mutually constraining powers, rights, and responsibilities." A45 §7
lists the forms: distinct powers held by different actors, joint
authorization, independent review, standing rules the decision-maker
cannot change alone, protected rights of refusal or challenge.

**Three justifications.** A49 §12 adds a third question to the two:
"**Substantive justification** concerns why a particular exercise of
capability ought to be limited. **Jurisdictional justification** concerns
why a particular actor or institution may determine and enforce that
limitation. Neither entails the other." And "**relational
justification**, concerning whether the restriction creates or
reproduces an acceptable arrangement of authority, dependence, and
responsibility". The third is how a protective restriction can turn
dominating while its ground and its holder's authority stay as they
were: "A protective restriction may become dominating if its continuation
depends entirely on unchecked discretion."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-680](CLAIM-680.md)** (Proposed) is the special case: a reasoned judgment that a
  way of living is worthwhile "licenses institutions to cultivate the
  conditions in which people can engage with what is worthwhile, not to
  impose a conception of it". There the ground is a judgment of worth;
  here it is any ground. A45 and A71 §2D relay [CLAIM-680](CLAIM-680.md) faithfully.
- **[CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)** and **[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)** say when another's discretion over a
  dependence is domination. The power to restrict is such a discretion,
  so a restriction regime can dominate the party it protects. A41 §5:
  "restricting a capability can be a means of preventing domination—but
  restricting the wrong capabilities can also constitute domination."
- **[CLAIM-746](CLAIM-746.md)** (F4, from A74 §9 of the same exchange) separates
  normative warrant, jurisdictional legitimacy, organizational adequacy
  and dynamic answerability for any authority. This claim is the first two
  of those for the act of restricting a capability, with the recursion
  that the power to restrict is one more capability to be judged so.
- **[CLAIM-727](CLAIM-727.md)** (F4): authority over a decision is not authority over
  the conditions of its challenge. Both refuse to let one kind of
  authority carry another.
- **A71 §3** states the split for any act: Permitted_N(a, x) against
  Authorized_N(a, b, x), "Neither necessarily entails the other", and
  "**A capability's usefulness does not confer jurisdiction over its
  exercise. Nor does possessing jurisdiction make every possible exercise
  legitimate.**" That is carried in [QUESTION-485](../questions.d/QUESTION-485.md), where the owner
  asked for the grounds of legitimacy in general.
- **[CLAIM-520](CLAIM-520.md)** (Active) and **[ARG-058](../arguments.d/ARG-058.md)** bound the answerability the second
  half asks for. A45 §7 concedes it: "we shouldn't say that legitimate
  restriction requires unlimited corrigibility."

## What it does not say

- Not who has the authority in any case. The claim says the question is
  separate, not how it is answered; [QUESTION-485](../questions.d/QUESTION-485.md) asks that.
- Not that the authority to restrict must be symmetrical or contestable at
  every step. A71 §10 keeps first-order contestability apart from
  answerability (the note of 2026-11-17 on [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)).
- Not that polycentric or divided authority is legitimate. A49 §12, after
  Ostrom: "no organizational form guarantees legitimacy."
