# CASE-tmpq90dm: A fanbase is said to hate its owner or general manager, though it has no procedure for forming a view, some fans approve of the person, and nobody has counted

## The case

Commentators say the fans hate the owner. There are chants, signs,
booing, a boycott call, a hostile tone online. Some supporters defend the
owner, many say nothing, and the fanbase has no procedure by which a
position becomes its own. Nobody has surveyed it.

## What it can show

A2's four readings of "the fanbase hates the owner":

1. **Aggregate.** Most fans individually dislike the owner.
2. **Shared emotional orientation.** Disapproval circulates and becomes
   mutually recognized, the we-mode emotion of [THEORY-126](../theory.d/THEORY-126.md).
3. **Normative position.** Opposing the owner is part of being a loyal
   fan, and defending the owner draws sanction.
4. **Corporate judgment.** An authorized body, such as a supporters'
   trust, takes a position, the group judgment of [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md).

A fanbase can show 2 and 3 strongly and never reach 4. A2: the sentence
can refer to "a public, distributed pattern of communicative behavior"
that forms "a recognizable **public stance**", which is not the
distribution of private attitudes. Preference falsification, unequal
participation and amplification can let a minority fix the stance. A5 §3
judged that the general point, that a collective's attitude can differ
from its members', is already [CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) and [CLAIM-271](../claims.d/CLAIM-271.md), and that what is
new is a collective with no procedure for judgment at all, which makes
the case a possible boundary for [CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md): a public stance without a
collective intention. That is filed as a question, not a claim, as A5
advised.

Given its standing, it cannot show that a majority dislikes anyone, that
the stance is stable, or that the fanbase is in an emotional state in
Huebner's stronger sense ([THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md)). A4's discriminating variation:
measure private attitudes, public expression, perceived norms and media
description over time, and test whether a visible minority holds a stance
the private majority rejects.
