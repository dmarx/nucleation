# THEORY-tmpxi9bt: Across 642 experimental tests to September 2019, forming implementation intentions reliably improved behaviour, cognitive performance, affect regulation and social judgement, but publication bias was substantial: the pooled d = .36 falls to a model-averaged d = .15 under robust Bayesian meta-analysis, with large heterogeneity

## Source

Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024), LIT-1488, read in full in NOTE-1131.
What the meta-analysis measured is THEORY-918, which this entry extends.

## The claim

The review pools 642 experimental tests from 294 reports to September
2019. After a robust Bayesian correction for publication bias it still
finds extreme evidence of an effect, and concludes: "Thus, implementation
intentions appear to have a robust impact on outcomes though the effect
size is small according to conventional guidelines" (p. 170). It reports
that planning helps across four kinds of outcome, behaviour (d = .27),
cognitive performance (.47), affect regulation (.66) and social judgement
(.52), and presents this scope as a finding of the review.

What THEORY-918 holds is narrower on one point. The correction was applied
to the overall estimate only; the four outcome estimates are uncorrected,
and within behaviour vaccination and voting did not differ reliably from
zero. So "reliably", for each domain, is the authors' conclusion carried
past what their corrected analysis tested.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, which QUESTION-310 also asks: an updated or
preregistered set of tests estimated with correction for publication bias
and still reliably above zero, of the same small order. A corrected
estimate per outcome domain would settle the four-domain clause. A larger
uncorrected pooled d cannot answer it.
