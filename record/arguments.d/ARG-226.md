# ARG-226: Zollman's account of transient diversity, by contrast, limits the claim that maintained diversity matters more than transient variety: diversity of pursuit kept indefinitely stops a learning group from settling, so what should be maintained is capacity, not pursuit

## The inference

[THEORY-148](../theory.d/THEORY-148.md)'s contrast: in Zollman's bandit model, the same agents and
methods, with diversity of pursuit made to last a while (by sparse
communication or by stubborn priors) or to last indefinitely (by both),
differ in outcome. A while helps the group survive misleading early
evidence; indefinitely, "the group never settles". Zollman: "both
together make the diversity too stable".

A60 §5 contrasts an organization that lets "many different opinions" be
expressed with no enduring support, which "displays diversity without
maintaining much corresponding capacity", with one that supports "several
small, enduring communities of inquiry", and concludes for the second.

The two do not conflict once their terms are kept apart:

- A60's **transient variety** is unsupported utterance: opinions with no
  practice, method or channel behind them. Zollman's **transient
  diversity** is diversity of what members actually pursue, supported for
  a time and then given up. A60's sense is not Zollman's, and Zollman's
  is a virtue.
- What Zollman shows harmful is **maintained pursuit**: everyone keeps
  acting on their own option. What A60 argues for is **maintained
  capacity**: the competence to take an option up again, kept while the
  ecosystem acts on another. A dormant perspective that can be
  reactivated (A60 §5; [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md)'s interruption of activation) is
  capacity kept without pursuit.

So step 5 of [CLAIM-1038](../claims.d/CLAIM-1038.md) holds of maintained capacity. Read as a call to keep
every perspective's pursuit going, it is bounded by [THEORY-148](../theory.d/THEORY-148.md).

## Critical questions

- **Is everything said to be held fixed really fixed?** In Zollman's model,
  yes: agents, methods and payoffs are fixed and only the sources of
  diversity vary. Between his model and A60's ecosystems, no: Zollman's
  agents learn which of two known options is better, while A60's concern
  is questions not yet posed. The bound therefore applies where an
  ecosystem's perspectives are rival pursuits of one question, not where
  they answer different ones.
- **Does changing the factor change anything else?** Making diversity
  permanent in Zollman's model also delays consensus, and the cost it
  measures is that delay. A60's argument counts no cost of delay; the bound
  is that it should.
- **Is the case realistic, or only coherent?** [THEORY-148](../theory.d/THEORY-148.md) is Proposed, shown
  only in simulation, with Rosenstock and colleagues' finding that
  transient diversity is what holds across the models they test. The
  bound has that standing.

## Verdict

Proposed: the contrast holds within its scope and narrows step 5. Whether
maintaining capacity without pursuit is affordable is [CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md) and
[CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md)'s ground: a capability seldom used decays unless practised.
