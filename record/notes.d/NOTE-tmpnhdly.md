# NOTE-tmpnhdly: Mossio and Moreno (2010) — Organisational closure in biological organisms

## Contribution

The authors' early statement of organisational closure as what separates
organisms from physical self-maintaining systems, with a working definition
and test cases (flame, river bed, water cycle, billiard balls).

## Key results

1. **Organisms are a subset (pdf p. 2).** "whereas all organisms are a kind
   of FFE self-maintaining systems, not all FFE self-maintaining systems are
   organisms".
2. **The flame (pdf p. 5).** It keeps its temperature above threshold,
   vaporizes wax and induces convection, so "flames can be said to be a
   physical example of a self-maintaining system".
3. **Teleology (pdf pp. 5–6).** "self-maintenance provides a naturalized
   grounding for some of the properties usually considered as being proper
   to biological systems, namely their teleology and normativity"; but
   "since in physical or chemical selfmaintaining systems one can hardly
   distinguish organizationally differentiated parts, the present claim is
   rather theoretical than operational".
4. **Single constraint against closure (pdf p. 7).** "dissipative structures
   realize a minimal form of self-maintenance, in the sense of depending on
   the work of a single constraint acting on its own boundary conditions";
   "The mutual dependence between a set of constraints is what we call an
   organisational closure."
5. **The working definition (pdf pp. 7–8).** A set of constraints C1…Cn,
   each with some boundary conditions determined by another Cj "whose
   maintenance depends in turn on Ci as an immediate constraint". Closure
   is partial ("organisational closure cannot be but partial"), holds among
   constraints, not processes (two colliding billiard balls make "a causal
   cycle, but not an organisational closure"), and is direct.
6. **River and bed (pdf p. 9).** "there is a single constraint, and then, no
   organisational closure"; the water cycle likewise.
7. **No growth of complexity in the flame (pdf p. 12).** "the flame will keep
   behaving in the same way in spite of various possible modifications of
   its components"; closure can grow.
8. **Closure is not enough (pdf p. 13).** It "underdetermines, in itself, the
   whole set of requirements"; adaptive control requires that "control
   relationships can be established only between modules whose
   characteristic time scales are sufficiently different".

## Assumptions

That a structure counts as a constraint when it is an emergent property of
a configuration (river bed, water cycle); that the flame's constraint is one,
the flame as a whole.

## Concepts

Far-from-equilibrium self-maintenance; constraint; work-constraint cycle;
organisational closure; partial closure; causal cycle versus closure;
adaptive control; time-scale separation.

  condition, for control (result 8).

## Limitations

- The flame is treated as one constraint without analysis.
- The test for what counts as a constraint is applied case by case, not
  given.
- Time scales enter only for control, not in the definition of closure.
- Teleology is grounded in self-maintenance, which a flame has, so on this
  paper's own grounding the flame is not yet excluded; the book's
  Introduction moves the grounding to closure (NOTE-1437).
