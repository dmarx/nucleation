# THEORY-tmp83tvp: Swann, Gómez, Seyle, Morales and Huici report that a single pictorial item picks out a distinct, group-specific class of Spanish participants fused with Spain, and that challenging or activating a fused person's personal self raised their stated willingness to fight and die for the group while leaving the nonfused unchanged, which they read as personal and social identity being functionally equivalent in the fused

## The account

Identity fusion is a "visceral" oneness with a group in which the
personal self stays salient and joins the group self, against
self-categorization theory's functional antagonism, where the personal
self recedes. The fused "feel that they care as much about the outcomes
of the group as their own outcomes" (p. 996). For them a threat to the
personal self is answered by acting for the group, which the authors
explain by self-verification, the motive to keep one's self-views true.
They call dying for the group "not in the individual's self-interest".

## Evidence

From NOTE-1256:

- Fusion was bimodal (40.5% chose the fully overlapping option),
  correlated .42 to .56 with Mael and Ashforth's identification scale,
  and was uncorrelated with fusion with friends, family, religion, team
  or party (|r| ≤ .10).
- Only fusion predicted saying one would fight (β = .43) or die
  (β = .23) for Spain more than other members would.
- Experiment 1 (N = 602): fusion × challenge on fighting, β = .17; on
  dying, β = .14. Experiment 2 (N = 326) repeated it with the feedback
  from an out-group source.
- Experiment 3 (N = 421): in the fused, personal and social activation
  raised willingness to die equally; in the nonfused only social
  activation did.

All outcomes are agreement with statements, and every sample is
Spanish.
