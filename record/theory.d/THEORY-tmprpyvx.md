# THEORY-tmprpyvx: A neural correlate of conscious report does not require laminar cortex: in crows it is carried by the nidopallium caudolaterale, a nuclear pallial region whose neurons follow the reported percept rather than the stimulus

## Source

- Maldarelli & Güntürkün (2025), [LIT-tmpulpg8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpulpg8.md), read in [NOTE-tmp0a382](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0a382.md): §2
  (sensory awareness), §3 (avian neuroanatomy and the summary of the three
  theories) and §5. The primary studies are Nieder et al. (2020) and
  Wagener & Nieder (2024), which the record does not hold.

## What was shown, and by whom

**The physiology, second-hand.** Maldarelli and Güntürkün, in
[LIT-tmpulpg8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpulpg8.md), report two studies from Nieder's laboratory. Crows performed
a delayed detection task in which a stimulus was sometimes shown and
sometimes not, at intensities around threshold, so that a threshold
stimulus was detected in about half the trials. Single-unit activity in the
NCL "correlated with the subjective report (i.e. when the stimulus was
reported as 'seen'), regardless of the actual presence or absence of the
stimulus", and the neurons "were also silent when the crow reported the
stimulus as 'not seen'". In the second study, where absence was itself a
trained, reportable category, separate NCL populations coded reported
presence and reported absence. The review calls the first study "the first
evidence of an avian brain area linked to subjective experience". The
outcome could have been otherwise: NCL activity could have followed the
stimulus and not the report.

**The anatomy.** Mammalian isocortex is columnar and laminar. The avian
pallium was long seen as nuclear throughout. Stacho et al. (2020), with
three-dimensional polarised-light imaging and tracing, found layers,
columns and repeated canonical circuits in the avian *sensory* pallium.
The NCL "lacks such cortical organization and instead has a nuclear
organization". The authors draw the inference this account states: "If
birds have consciousness and avian consciousness arises (at least in part)
by computations within NCL, consciousness should, in principle, be able to
emerge from nuclear structures". What they take to be shared instead is the
connectome: in the pigeon, NCL is one of five densely interlinked hubs
joined to local modules (Shanahan et al. 2013), the arrangement global
workspace theory requires.

## The claim, and its limits

What the evidence supports is the narrow form in the title: a region whose
activity follows the reported percept rather than the stimulus, the
signature the mammalian literature reads as a correlate of conscious
perception, exists in a vertebrate brain region with no laminar
organisation. So whatever lamination contributes, it is not needed for a
correlate of conscious report. Three limits are part of the claim.

- **The crow evidence is second-hand.** Every physiological result here is
  Nieder's, as one review reports it. The primary papers are not in the
  record, and the two studies come from one laboratory in one species
  (C1 in [NOTE-tmp0a382](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0a382.md), moderate).
- **A report paradigm cannot separate access from phenomenal
  consciousness.** A neuron that follows the "seen" report is a correlate of
  what the bird can report. Whether it is a correlate of what the bird
  experiences, rather than of access, decision or response, is not
  something a report-based task can show, and the review does not discuss
  this. That is why the title says conscious report, not experience. The
  review's own conditional ("if birds have consciousness") marks the same
  gap.
- **"Laminar cortex" is the right contrast, not "pallium".** Birds do have a
  cortex-like laminar sensory pallium. The claim is that the region that
  tracks report is not laminar, not that birds lack layered tissue, and
  whether the percept is formed upstream in laminar sensory pallium and
  only read out in NCL is open.

## How this bears on the record's holdings

- **Global workspace theory.** The review finds GNWT's anatomical
  requirement, a hub-and-module connectome, "largely met" in pigeons, and
  reads the NCL results as consistent with NCL acting as a workspace hub.
  This account makes GNWT's anatomy a property of connectivity rather than
  of cortex, but it does not support GNWT over its rivals: the authors say
  the results "could be explained by any of these theories", ignition has
  not been shown in birds, and the absence-coding result "can potentially
  expand or challenge" GNWT. Butlin et al. ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md)) turn the same
  theory's requirements into indicators for AI systems; this is the same
  move made on a bird.
- **IIT.** The review finds IIT's requirements "not met (yet)", meaning
  untested: no perturbational complexity index has been measured in birds
  and no grid-like lattice of microcolumns has been found. It proposes PCI
  as "a good substitute" for Φ. Barrett et al. ([LIT-185](../literature.d/LIT-185.md)) argue that PCI is
  a proxy capturing at most two of IIT's axioms, so even a positive avian
  PCI would test IIT ([LIT-445](../literature.d/LIT-445.md)) less directly than the review implies.
  Nothing here bears on IIT either way.
- **[THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md).** That account holds that AI consciousness cannot currently
  be settled, and its promote_when asks for a comparative programme in
  which a biological property predicts where consciousness markers fall
  across animals. This is evidence of that kind, and it counts against
  cortical lamination as such a property. It says nothing about life or
  metabolism, the properties Seth's biological naturalism ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md)) turns
  on, and it does not move the deadlock. No relation is declared.
- **[THEORY-tmp8epx6](THEORY-tmp8epx6.md) (ALARM).** Newen and Montemayor ([LIT-tmp1gjg1](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1gjg1.md)) hold
  that general alertness, attention-guided consciousness, needs cortex,
  and they read GNWT as prefrontal. This evidence bears against both
  readings if "cortex" means laminar cortex. The ALARM account as filed
  leaves that clause out, and keeps only the claim that basic arousal is
  subcortical, which this evidence does not touch.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say birds are conscious.** The authors say that "many more
  studies are needed before stronger conclusions can be drawn", and the
  claim here is conditional on report tracking consciousness.
- **It does not say lamination is useless.** The review's own suggestion
  is that a laminar design suits topographic sensory input, which may be
  why the avian sensory pallium has it.
- **It does not rest on the mirror-test evidence.** The review's case for
  graded avian self-awareness (magpies, contested by a failed replication;
  house crows; pigeons and roosters that fail the mark test but treat their
  image differently from a conspecific) is a separate argument.
