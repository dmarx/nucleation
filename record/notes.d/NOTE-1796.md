# NOTE-1796: Rockström and others — Charting a Transformational Course Toward a Safe and Just Future: The Earth Commission's Contribution

## Contribution

A programmatic statement of how the Commission will turn its safe-and-just boundaries into allocated, actor-level budgets and transformation pathways, with justice treated as a structural condition, not an add-on. No new data ("Data were not used, nor created, for this research.").

## Key results

1. Abstract: "multiple safe and just Earth system boundaries have already been transgressed".
2. §2: "Two or more safe and just sub‐global ESBs were found to be transgressed in over 52% of the world's land area, affecting 86% of the global population".
3. §2: "three just boundaries (climate, nitrogen, and aerosols) were stricter than their corresponding safe boundaries."
4. §2: "ensuring minimum access for the poorest would cause further transgressions of the safe and just (harm) boundaries" (under existing inequalities and technologies, ceteris paribus).
5. Box 2: the just assessment uses "the 3 I approach—interspecies justice and Earth system stability, intergenerational justice, and intragenerational justice".
6. §5: ESJ incorporates responsibility "recognizing the rights of future generations to a stable Earth system."
7. §5: "Justice is not a normative add‐on but a structural and strategic condition for planetary stability and resilience."
8. §7: "the first step in cross‐scale translation is transcription"; the second is determining "how these budgets of environmental resources and the relevant pressure reduction burdens ... should be shared among actors."
9. §7: the review will clarify "which approaches risk reinforcing existing injustices."
10. §4: the third access level "addresses the upper bound of consumption, building on emerging frameworks such as limitarianism and sufficiency".

## Assumptions

- Biophysical and social thresholds can be expressed in comparable units and jointly quantified.
- Global boundaries can be "transcribed" into pressure budgets and allocated to cities, companies and countries by stated fairness principles.
- Existing institutions (IPCC, IMF, SBTN) can carry the framework without new institutions.
- Scientific bodies can define who and what counts (future generations, other species) in the "just" boundaries.

## Concepts

Safe and just space; safe vs just Earth system boundaries (ESBs); just access / minimum access; Earth system justice (ESJ: recognition, epistemic, procedural, substantive, structural justice); 3 I (interspecies, intergenerational, intragenerational); Doughnut (Raworth) as origin; transcription and allocation (cross-scale translation); burning-ember diagrams; limitarianism and sufficiency; fair-share principles; TIPMIP; Science Based Targets Network.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** A's account ("the Earth Commission's agenda for allocating environmental budgets and transformation pathways that account for justice") is **accurate**. It should be marked as a commentary that states plans and recaps earlier results (Rockström, Gupta et al. 2023, Nature; Gupta et al. 2024; Rammelt et al. 2023); the substantive findings live in those papers.

- **[QUESTION-456](../questions.d/QUESTION-456.md)** (how the set of demands an organization's compatibility is judged against can be established defensibly, when who counts, how demands are inferred and what they mean are shaped by the organization's governance): **bears directly as a worked instance of a procedure, without answering the question.** The Earth Commission is an attempt to fix a demand set from outside any one organization: a scientific body sets "safe" and "just" boundaries, specifies claimants beyond the organization's own (other species, future generations: the 3 I), and then "transcribes" them into budgets allocated to actors. That is close to what the question says would count as an answer, "a procedure ... whose outputs can be checked against a reference set the organization did not choose". But the paper also shows the question's difficulty: the choice of control variables, of justice criteria (minimum, medium, limitarian levels; "acceptable" and "unacceptable" inequalities) and of allocation principles is itself made by the Commission and left open; it states that its review will ask "which approaches risk reinforcing existing injustices". So: support for the claim that external reference sets are being constructed, and evidence that fixing who counts and what a demand means remains a governance choice one level up. The finding that three just boundaries are stricter than safe ones is a concrete case where adding claimants (people harmed) tightens the acceptance set.
- **[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)** (via [QUESTION-456](../questions.d/QUESTION-456.md) and [LIT-2454](../literature.d/LIT-2454.md); not named in this LIT): the recapped result that meeting minimum access for the poorest "would cause further transgressions" under current inequality is a case of jointly unsatisfiable demands (floor vs ceiling) for given conditions, and the Commission's remedy is reorganization (redistribution, technology), i.e. relief in [CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)'s sense, not dropping a demand. Illustrative only; no ordinal analysis.
- **[LIT-2454](../literature.d/LIT-2454.md)** (Fanning and Raworth 2025, Doughnut monitoring, Deferred; found on recheck): the companion framework the Commission cites as origin ("Donut or well‐being economics"); both bear on [QUESTION-456](../questions.d/QUESTION-456.md) from A42 §5.

## Limitations

A commentary: its results are recapped from the Commission's earlier papers, not shown; its phase-two content is plans; 52 authors from the Commission itself (no outside assessment); the justice framework is stated, not defended against rivals.
