# THEORY-1498: Golosov, Graber, Mogstad and Novgorodsky find, from US tax records of 90,731 state-lottery winners aged 21–64 with wins of at least $30,000 compared with later winners, that a windfall reduces work swiftly and persistently over the following five years, each dollar of annuitized unearned income lowering household pre-tax labour earnings by about 52 cents and raising consumption, imputed from the budget identity, by about 58 cents; that winners save and smooth most of the windfall, as imputed from their capital income; that the per-dollar earnings response rises with pre-win income and falls with prize size; and that wealth raises entry into low-profit self-employment but not into self-employment with profits above $15,000

## Source

Golosov, Graber, Mogstad & Novgorodsky (2024), [LIT-2181](../literature.d/LIT-2181.md); read in
[NOTE-1671](../notes.d/NOTE-1671.md). **Read in the NBER working-paper version (w29000, July
2021); all numbers and pages are that version's.** The QJE abstract drops
the working paper's life-cycle model and its UBI and top-tax bounds and
adds that "existing U.S. studies substantially underestimate wealth and
income effects"; the published tables were not seen.

## What was actually shown

- **Design.** Every W-2G lottery form 1999–2016 linked to tax records;
  winners compared with not-yet winners, cohort by cohort. The earnings
  path could have shown pre-trends or a fade; it shows neither: no
  pre-trends from −7 to −1, and "a sizable, swift, and persistent change
  in wage earnings" (pp. 10–15).
- **The size.** Per dollar of annuitized unearned income, household
  labour earnings −0.52, labour taxes −0.11, implied consumption +0.58
  (Table 4.1, p. 27); per $100 of wealth, "2.3 dollars in each of the five
  subsequent years" (p. 16).
- **Smoothing.** Unearned income per period is $7,497 by assumed perfect
  smoothing and $8,265 from capital income in the data (p. 25); capital
  income rises and then slightly declines, "consistent with lottery
  winners first expanding their savings and then gradually consuming out
  of their new wealth" (p. 16).
- **Gradients.** MPE −0.31 in the bottom income quartile to −0.67 in the
  top; per-dollar effects above 5.5 dollars per 100 for smaller wins and
  below 1 for the largest (pp. 21–23).
- **Business.** Entry into self-employment with profits of $15,000 or less
  +0.47 pp per $100k; above $15,000, −0.02 pp, not significant (Table 6.2,
  pp. 49–51).

## What this does not say

- **That consumption or wealth was observed.** Consumption is the residual
  of the budget identity, and wealth is imputed from taxable capital
  income at an assumed return: "the imputation of wealth using asset
  returns can only be done approximately" (p. 24). That winners save most of
  the windfall rests on two methods agreeing, not on balance sheets, and
  untaxed assets such as a home are invisible.
- **That winners lose capability.** No skill, human-capital or re-entry
  measures; the fall in work is chosen while the stock is kept, which
  bears on the slack-effort clause of [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) but not on loss by
  disuse.
- **That there is a threshold.** Responses are smooth and shrink per
  dollar with prize size; nothing here supports [CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) for
  households, and nothing tests it for structures.
- **That small or means-tested transfers reduce work.** The windfalls are
  large and permanent, in the US; [THEORY-1471](THEORY-1471.md) and [THEORY-1466](THEORY-1466.md)
  concern small transfers in poor countries and are bounded in scope, not
  contradicted.
- **Anything beyond five years**, or about bankruptcy, debt, distress,
  health, scarcity or time pressure.
- **That a UBI would cut earnings by 52 cents a dollar.** That is the
  working paper's model extrapolation (section 5), not observed, and is
  absent from the QJE abstract.
- **That Sweden's smaller effects are wrong.** The authors find theirs
  about twice those of Cesarini et al. (2017) even after adjusting
  measures; the cause of the rest is not established.
