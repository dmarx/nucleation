# CLAIM-tmpcv1pg: Where a content industry reads demand from activity or revenue, the minority of heaviest users comes to set the distribution of content everyone is offered; and if heavy users habituate and seek more extreme material, the mainstream drifts toward it, which may explain why mainstream pornography channels now carry material once niche or extreme

## The claim

The owner, U13, in full: "rich-get-richer effects probably have the knock-on
effect that the entire industry begins to adjust the distribution of its
content towards the demands of the minority of users that generate the most
activity and revenue. this could for example potentially explain why
seemingly mainline pornography distribution channels have become flush
with content that was previously considered niche or extreme: heavy
pornography users have been observed to become desensitized over time,
causing them to pursue more extreme content, and if they're the heaviest
users then from the content providers perspective, the bulk of their
traffic is towards that more extreme content, incentivizing them publishing
more of it."

It has two parts:

1. **Structural.** An industry that reads demand from activity or revenue
   adjusts what it supplies toward its heaviest users. [CLAIM-tmpmz271](CLAIM-tmpmz271.md)
   shows the reading can diverge arbitrarily from what most people prefer,
   and [CLAIM-tmphgjbz](CLAIM-tmphgjbz.md) that exit can widen the gap with no one changing
   their mind.
2. **Escalation.** Heavy users habituate and move to more extreme content,
   so the minority's demand itself moves, and the supply follows it.

A27 §3 notes the decisive structural point for testing: "escalation or
desensitization is not necessary for this loop to operate". A27 §3 also
adds the supply side the owner's mechanism needs, that ranking makes the
recommender part of "the production environment, not merely the
consumption environment" (Adler, [LIT-tmpdak05](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdak05.md); Qian and Jain, [LIT-tmpzsw74](../literature.d/LIT-tmpzsw74.md);
Hu and colleagues, [LIT-tmpgu9ct](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgu9ct.md); all unread).

## Evidence, as A reports it

- For escalation: Wright and Herbenick ([LIT-tmpccpc6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpccpc6.md)), heavier users more
  often report shifts toward more intense content, "small to moderate"
  associations, observational; Ince and colleagues ([LIT-tmpf67mb](../literature.d/LIT-tmpf67mb.md)) and de
  Roos and Willkomm ([LIT-tmp2hne0](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2hne0.md)), cross-sectional, heterogeneous.
- Against a strong industry-wide version: Shor and Seida ([LIT-tmp9goww](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9goww.md)), no
  consistent rise in aggression in popular videos from 2008 to 2016, and
  aggressive videos drew fewer views.
- A27's evidential table rates the full industry-wide escalation story "A
  plausible hypothesis, but not yet demonstrated".

All registered unread; [ARG-tmpshmi5](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpshmi5.md).

## What it does not say

That niche content is harmful: A27 §2 notes that "niche, unconventional,
intense, aggressive, and harmful are not interchangeable categories", and
§5 that catalogue growth can serve previously unserved tastes. Or that the
minority intends anything: A27 §6 calls the result "a governance structure
without an identifiable governor". [LIT-1465](../literature.d/LIT-1465.md)'s taxonomy of addictive design
features (held, read) is the record's nearest reading on the individual
side.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Shor and Seida unavailable, and a later study by the same author

The reading pass of 2026-11-12 could not read Shor and Seida 2018
([LIT-tmp9goww](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9goww.md)): it is closed, and is now Unavailable. Its reader found a
later study by the same first author, Shor and Liu 2025 ([LIT-tmpagy32](../literature.d/LIT-tmpagy32.md),
registered Deferred and unread; only its abstract was seen), which follows
popular videos from 2000 to 2024 and reports, by its abstract, a rise in
mostly consensual aggression. So this claim's use of the 2018 study as
evidence "Against a strong industry-wide version" should not stand alone:
the same group's longer series may point the other way on that measure.
Neither study is read, and nothing here is a finding. The defeater's
parenthesis citing Shor and Seida is kept, marked unread. No status change.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Incentivizing High-Quality Content in Online Recommender Systems (Hu, Jagadeesan, Jordan, Steinhardt), read

From the reading in [NOTE-tmplv2tx](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplv2tx.md) ([LIT-tmpgu9ct](../literature.d/LIT-tmpgu9ct.md)), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **qualifies / leaves open**. It supplies a
demonstrated (formal) supply-side link: platform algorithms change what
producers make, and quality can degrade with no change in anyone's taste,
which matches A27 §3's point that escalation is unnecessary. It does not
model activity-weighted demand, heavy users or extremity, so it gives
nothing to the claim's structural premise (that belongs to [CLAIM-tmpmz271](CLAIM-tmpmz271.md))
or to its escalation half. The CLAIM's citation should describe it as a
supply-side mechanism of quality decline, not as evidence of minority
capture.

## Note of 2026-11-12: de Roos and Willkomm, read

From the reading in NOTE-tmpwj1ap (LIT-tmp2hne0), the reader's assessment as
checked by the filer: **leaves the escalation step open; weakly qualifies
it**. The paper does not measure heavy users, does not observe escalation
over time, and finds escalation "low" and branching rather than a single
drift toward extremity. Its "relative escalation" in S&M, fetish, teen,
group and violent categories is consistent with habituation-driven
exploration but equally with curiosity. Nothing in it bears on the claim's
structural part (supply following heavy users); it agrees with A27 §3 that
the loop does not need escalation. It supports the claim's caveat that
"niche, unconventional, intense, aggressive, and harmful are not
interchangeable categories": the paper separates Non-Traditional Niche from
