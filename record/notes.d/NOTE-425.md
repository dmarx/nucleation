# NOTE-425: Energy flow and the organization of life

## Contribution

The paper joins two lines of Morowitz's and Smith's work into one
statement: the universality of core intermediary metabolism (Smith &
Morowitz 2004) and the statistical mechanics of driven, current-carrying
systems (Smith 1998, 1999, 2005). Its claim is that core metabolism is
not one of many chemistries life happened on, but the channel geochemical
energy flow would open. It adds no new result; its contribution is the
synthesis and a stated research programme.

## Key insight

Read core metabolism as a lightning bolt through the network of possible
geochemical reactions: a structure that forms because it carries a
current, redox electrons from H₂ to CO₂ and phosphate from polymer to
orthophosphate, far better than the near-equilibrium alternatives. Then
the universality and stability of metabolism over four billion years look
like the stability of a phase, not an accident of descent.

## Assumptions

- **Two geochemical "batteries".** Redox couples from geothermal chemistry
  and polyphosphates. The authors flag the second as uncertain:
  polyphosphates are not reported at hydrothermal vents (fn. 5).
- **Metabolism first.** The first living systems were reductive
  chemo-autotrophs, plausibly autotrophic at the level of the individual
  cell. The authors note that the placement of methanogenesis is disputed
  (fn. 6).
- **Jaynes's statistical mechanics.** Statistical physics is statistical
  inference under boundary constraints, so maximum entropy extends to
  driven systems when the entropy is allowed to depend on currents. This is
  taken from Jaynes and from Smith's earlier papers, not derived here.
- **Entropy-production principles fail in general,** because they apply
  the equilibrium expression for entropy to driven systems. Asserted, with
  Onsager and Kondepudi & Prigogine cited as the targets.
- **"Modern metabolism recapitulates the process of biogenesis".** Assumed
  for the cascade argument; §V argues for it from the stability of the core.

## Key results

- **Universality.** The anabolic core of autotrophs is fewer than 500
  molecules under 400 Da, rooted in the eleven carboxylic acids of the
  citric-acid cycle. In the reductive direction the cycle "can duplicate
  its own members from abiotic CO₂ and electrons", which the paper calls
  network autocatalysis.
- **Energy partition.** The reductive cycle captures "about one third" of
  the redox energy from CO₂ to acetate; fatty-acid synthesis "about
  another third"; methanogenesis the rest. These figures are stated, not
  computed here.
- **Light absorption.** Absorption in place of elastic scattering
  "increases heat-transporting capacity by a factor of ∼10⁹ for each atom
  acting as an absorber", citing Hoelzer, Pepper & Smith.
- **The framework (§III).** Current-dependent entropy maximization predicts
  currents (Ohm's and Fourier's laws as the linear cases). With positive
  feedback, thresholds arise that are phase transitions statistically
  equivalent to equilibrium ones, and the ordered state is a better
  "channel" between reservoirs. The entropy principle is "not equivalent to
  maximization of the inter-reservoir current".
- **The collapse (§IV).** The abiotic earth was metastable, and the
  emergence of life was a "collapse" to greater stability, like phase
  separation in a binary alloy, not a new thermodynamic state. Later
  chemical transitions are further collapses and should be irreversible.
- **Ecology versus metabolism (§V).** Metabolism is more universal across
  ecosystems than the division of labour among species. "Universality due
  to descent from common ancestors becomes indistinguishable from
  universality due to evolutionary convergence."

## Concepts

- **Channel (transport channel).** A non-equilibrium structure that carries
  a current (charge, heat, electrons) between reservoirs at different
  potential much faster than diffusion would.
- **Network autocatalysis.** A reaction network that makes more of its
  own members from external inputs, as the reductive citric-acid cycle does
  for carbon.
- **Free-energy stress.** A build-up of free energy (for example H₂ and CO₂
  coexisting) that cannot relax at an appreciable rate without a
  catalytic channel.
- **Collapse to life.** The authors' name for the emergence of life as a
  phase transition of the geosphere.

## Limitations

- No calculation is given. The authors say the thesis is undemonstrated and
  that the framework has "few instances that usefully reach beyond
  equilibrium".
- The case rests on reading the structure of present-day metabolism back
  into its origin, which the paper assumes rather than tests.
- The phosphate half of the energy story is admitted to be uncertain
  (fn. 5).
- Read as a working paper; the journal version was not seen.

## Open questions

- Can the current-dependent maximum-entropy framework predict, for a
  geochemical network with H₂, CO₂ and a phosphate source, that the
  reductive citric-acid cycle forms?
- What observation would tell the "collapse" (convergence) account of
  metabolic universality apart from common descent, given the authors'
  remark that the two become indistinguishable?
