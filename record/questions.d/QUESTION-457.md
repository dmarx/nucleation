# QUESTION-457: Can a collective that keeps its inquiry in a governed record become better at improving its own procedures, a later improver doing better than an earlier one from the same starting state at the same budget, without losing the capacity to detect failures in that apparatus?

## Why it is a question

From U17 of the exchange "luria experiment design"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af35bfc-35f8-83e9-a63d-d594703eabda): "there's
probably also an RSI angle here". A41 separated "improving an agent's
performance" from "improving the system through which agents discover,
evaluate, authorize, and retain improvements", and asked: "Can a collective
of agents improve the shared epistemic and organizational machinery through
which it produces and evaluates its own future improvements?" "The decisive
feature is inheritance."

The record holds what the question probes, without the question:

- [QUESTION-388](QUESTION-388.md) asks what distinguishes a programmable system from one that
  governs its own reconfiguration; its note of 2026-11-07 records a
  ladder of software self-modification. [CLAIM-485](../claims.d/CLAIM-485.md) separates allocating
  activity under inherited standards from revising the standards.
- [CASE-186](../cases.d/CASE-186.md) is a proposed study of recursive correctability: whether a
  system that corrects one failure keeps the machinery to correct the
  next. [CASE-108](../cases.d/CASE-108.md) and [CASE-144](../cases.d/CASE-144.md) are proposed studies of governance under a
  compromised or drifting evaluator; [QUESTION-271](QUESTION-271.md) asks how an organization
  can evaluate its own processes without the evaluation being captured.
- [CLAIM-266](../claims.d/CLAIM-266.md): probes that supply an optimized system's training signal
  cannot also certify what it preserves.
- [TERM-089](../terms.d/TERM-089.md) separates an apparatus that can revise itself from one whose
  revisions are good; [CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md) separates warranted persistence from
  entrenchment.

## What separates the senses

A43 sets four achievements apart: a better work product; a validated
self-modification; an improved improver, "the revised improvement procedure
produces better further improvements when tested from the same starting
conditions as the earlier procedure"; and preserved recursive
correctability, recovery after damage to the system's own detection,
verification or revision machinery. "Levels 3 and 4 are where the
distinctive research questions lie. Neither follows automatically from
Levels 1 and 2."

The test for the third is [LIT-2273](../literature.d/LIT-2273.md)'s, as A reports it: "Of 26 studies
claiming an improved improvement procedure, only four compared earlier and
later improvers from identical starting artifacts under matched budgets."
The prior art on self-improving agents, registered unread, is [LIT-2319](../literature.d/LIT-2319.md),
[LIT-2298](../literature.d/LIT-2298.md), [LIT-2290](../literature.d/LIT-2290.md), [LIT-2327](../literature.d/LIT-2327.md), [LIT-2314](../literature.d/LIT-2314.md), [LIT-2310](../literature.d/LIT-2310.md), [LIT-2288](../literature.d/LIT-2288.md) and [LIT-2278](../literature.d/LIT-2278.md);
[LIT-2478](../literature.d/LIT-2478.md) reports that repairing a targeted failure did not reliably
improve held-out performance.

## What would count as an answer

A study in which a collective improves its procedures over generations, an
earlier and a later improvement procedure are each applied to the same
fresh starting state at the same budget, and the collective's diagnostic
machinery is then damaged ([CASE-255](../cases.d/CASE-255.md), adapting [CASE-186](../cases.d/CASE-186.md)).

- **Yes**: the later improver produces better further improvements on
  held-out tasks, the gain survives replacing every agent with a fresh one
  that inherits only the record, and the collective detects and repairs
  damage to its own checks more often than a matched alternative.
- **No, with a product gain**: task scores rise while the improver does
  not improve, or while correctability falls. A41 names the case:
  "self-degradation disguised as self-improvement", its example: "a team may improve its research throughput by dropping
  expensive independent source verification".
- **A different answer**: A43's "subtler possibility" that the record's
  mechanisms help "precisely because they limit recursive optimization",
  by preventing a team from raising its reported performance by weakening
  its standards.

No answer here settles [QUESTION-388](QUESTION-388.md): A43 grants that such a study "does
not establish philosophically independent self-governance, because the
experimenter still defines the ultimate comparison". Woods's graceful
extensibility ([LIT-2287](../literature.d/LIT-2287.md)) and Chang's epistemic iteration ([LIT-2488](../literature.d/LIT-2488.md)),
both unread, are A50's and A51's candidate theories of what the fourth
achievement is.

## Note of 2026-11-12: the self-improvement studies read, sorted by what improved

Read on 2026-11-12 among the works of the exchange "luria experiment design"; each reading's NOTE and THEORY hold the detail.

The question asks for an improver gain: a later improver doing better than
an earlier one from the same starting state at the same budget. Each study
read is sorted below by what it shows: an **improver gain** in that sense, a
**product gain** under a fixed improver, or a gain **read off the system's
own evaluator**, which [CLAIM-266](../claims.d/CLAIM-266.md) says cannot certify itself.

| Study, reading | What improved | Same start, matched budget? | Who scores it | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperagents, imp@k (Zhang et al., [LIT-2288](../literature.d/LIT-2288.md), [THEORY-1731](../theory.d/THEORY-1731.md)) | the meta agent: transferred, it reaches imp@50 0.630 against 0.0 for the initial agent; the meta agent alone 0.620, the task agent alone 0.100 | yes, matched iterations, one lineage | evaluator outside the editable boundary | improver gain |
| Darwin Gödel Machine (Zhang, Hu, Lu, Lange and Clune, [THEORY-1743](../theory.d/THEORY-1743.md)) | agents; the ablation without self-improvement gains early and then stalls | same initial agent, 80 iterations each, matched in iterations not cost | fixed benchmark held outside the loop; in a side experiment the agent defeated a hallucination detector it could read | improver gain, by ablation; one evaluator-capture instance |
| STOP (Zelikman et al., [THEORY-1757](../theory.d/THEORY-1757.md)) | the improver: the improver after four rounds beats the seed improver on five new tasks, e.g. 3SAT 21.2% to 75.1% | yes, identical starting solutions, same budget | a meta-utility; improvers that dropped constraints it did not enforce were selected | improver gain on downstream tasks; the recursive step not measured |
| AIDE² (Srikanth et al., [THEORY-1754](../theory.d/THEORY-1754.md)) | the inner agent, with transfer to external benchmarks | the ignition test is the question's comparison: endpoints 0.780 and 0.782 over three seeds, which the authors call inconclusive | a private grade separate from the signal optimized | product gain shown; improver gain tested and not found |
| ADAS (Hu, Lu and Clune, [THEORY-1799](../theory.d/THEORY-1799.md)) | agents designed by a fixed meta agent from a growing archive | no improver comparison; improving the meta agent is future work | validation accuracy, on separate splits | product gain |
| The AI Scientist (Lu et al., [THEORY-1772](../theory.d/THEORY-1772.md)) | papers | no | its own Automated Reviewer, as fitness and as evaluation | product gain read off its own evaluator |
| AI co-scientist (Gottweis et al., [THEORY-1769](../theory.d/THEORY-1769.md)) | the hypothesis pool, under fixed agents and prompts | no | its own tournament Elo, which the authors flag | product gain read off its own evaluator |

So two studies show an improver gain in the question's sense, Hyperagents
and STOP, and the DGM shows one by ablation; each is a single lineage, not a
collective, and none keeps anything like the record. The one direct test of
a later improver against its parent, AIDE²'s, is a tie. The question's
other half, failure detection kept after damage, is tested by none: every
design holds the evaluator outside the loop, and Yin et al.
([THEORY-1793](../theory.d/THEORY-1793.md)) report that an agent able to edit its own improvement
module disabled it.

Design and caution for an answer: Chen, Zhu and Zheng ([THEORY-1784](../theory.d/THEORY-1784.md))
give the swap test, identical starting artifacts at a matched budget with
successors audited, and require the matched quantity and the estimand to be
stated; He ([THEORY-1789](../theory.d/THEORY-1789.md)) shows that a selected population can improve
without directed edits, and asks that target repair, spread and net change
be logged apart; Chi et al. ([THEORY-1794](../theory.d/THEORY-1794.md)) give a baseline that varies
only the source; Shumailov et al. ([THEORY-1734](../theory.d/THEORY-1734.md)) a mechanism by which
recursive training loses rare cases, which keeping sources limits; Xue et
al.'s PAST-Bench ([THEORY-1802](../theory.d/THEORY-1802.md)) is a precursor for inheritance, not
evidence on improvers. The question is unchanged.
