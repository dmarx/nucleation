# THEORY-tmpynb4z: Majikes and colleagues find, in a single-fold DNA origami model measured by FRET melting with affine baseline correction, that closing a fold with an already anchored staple is unimolecular and more stable than bimolecular binding of the same domain, and that the fold's melting temperature falls as fold distance grows, by more than 10 °C from short to long, through a configurational-entropy cost; from this they infer, without testing it, that origami folds by nucleation and growth, a staple's longest domain binding first and short, entropically cheap folds then making neighbouring folds cheaper

## Source

Majikes, Patrone, Schiffels, Zwolak, Kearsley, Forry and Liddle (2020),
[LIT-tmp0hqya](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0hqya.md); read in [NOTE-tmperio5](../notes.d/NOTE-tmperio5.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Unimolecular against bimolecular** (Results, Fig. 4A): "The
  hybridization-only sample is less stable than the others as it involves
  a bimolecular reaction, at low concentration, rather than a unimolecular
  reaction."
- **Fold distance** (Results, Fig. 5): "Increasing loop distance depresses
  the Tm, as does increasing persistence length, primarily at short fold
  distances"; "the increase in melt temperature due to a short versus a
  long fold distance can be > 10°C".
- **ΔCp and bending** (Figs. 6–7): curved van't Hoff plots, a <10% change
  in ΔS resolvable, and a ΔH dip at short folds read as a bending penalty,
  resolved for the short persistence-length scaffold only.
- **The inference** (Discussion): "Among the sub-sequences on a staple,
  the longest binds first in a bimolecular reaction, consistent with the
  nucleation seed subsequence concept".

## What this does not say

- That origami folds this way: "confirming this process will require
  additional testing in systems with multiple folds".
- Anything about cooperativity beyond loop entropy: "Here we only explore
  zeroth order cooperative effects, i.e., changes in conformational
  entropy."
- Anything about hysteresis, annealing or kinetics: fluorescence was
  recorded on the melt only, and equilibrium is assumed.
- Anything about global folding traps, the subject of [THEORY-1398](THEORY-1398.md).
