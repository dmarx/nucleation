# CLAIM-689: Whether a desire was formed by social influence and whether its object is worth desiring are separate questions; since every preference is socially formed, a critique of produced wants cannot rest on their social origin as such, and can ask instead whether satisfying them engages people with anything worthwhile

## The claim

A9 §III, after the owner's U7:

> **We should distinguish how a desire originated from whether its
> object is worth desiring.**

A's reason: "virtually every human preference is socially influenced", so "The fact that a preference has been shaped by society cannot, on its own, establish that it is undesirable." Its example: two people with equally intense, equally conditioned commitments, one to preserving an endangered ecosystem, the other to "increasingly elaborate symbols of social superiority". Origin does not tell them apart; worth may.

## Against Marcuse's criterion

Marcuse ([THEORY-1853](../theory.d/THEORY-1853.md), [NOTE-1924](../notes.d/NOTE-1924.md)) defines false needs by origin and function at once: "'False' are those which are superimposed upon the individual by particular social interests in his repression: the needs which perpetuate toil, aggressiveness, misery, and injustice." He adds that however much such needs become the individual's own, "they continue to be what they were from the beginning", products of a society, and that who decides is "the individuals themselves, but only in the last analysis; that is, if and when they are free to give their own answer". The claim keeps the second half of his definition, a need's effects, as a question of worth, and denies that the first half, origin in social interests, settles anything alone. Han's internalized demands ([THEORY-1843](../theory.d/THEORY-1843.md)) are open to the same separation. [ARG-108](../arguments.d/ARG-108.md), on Marcuse against the compulsion claim's measure, notes that his criterion of true and false needs is contested; this claim says where.

## Where it sits

At the boundary [ADR-099](../decisions.d/ADR-099.md) draws. Whose a desire is, and whether it is integrated into a will the agent avows, is the authenticity line's question ([QUESTION-057](../questions.d/QUESTION-057.md)). Whether what the desire aims at is worth a life's engagement is this line's, after Wolf ([THEORY-1836](../theory.d/THEORY-1836.md)). The claim says the second can be asked without first settling the first.

## What it does not say

- Not that origin never matters. Manipulation can wrong a person whatever its object; the claim is about what a critique of the wants themselves needs.
- Not which objects are worthwhile; [CLAIM-680](CLAIM-680.md) holds that no institution may settle it by imposition.

## Note of 2026-11-18: narrowed

The blocker note of this date found the title wider than its argument:
"since every preference is socially formed" answers critiques by social
origin as such, not accounts that discriminate by how a want was formed,
such as [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md)'s introjection. The title now says social origin as
such, and the wider clause is [CLAIM-tmpq1p1x](CLAIM-tmpq1p1x.md), Proposed.
