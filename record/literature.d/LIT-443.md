# LIT-443: Consciousness Explained

Daniel C. Dennett (1991), *Consciousness Explained*, illustrated by Paul
Weiner, Boston: Little, Brown and Co. (1st ed., ISBN 0-316-18065-3; Library
of Congress 91015614, LC class B105.C477 D45 1991; xiii, 511 pp.;
bibliography pp. 469–491, index). The trade paperback (ISBN
978-0-316-18066-5) is now sold under Hachette Book Group's Little, Brown
imprint. The catalogue records give only the year 1991, so `published:`
carries the year, as the first of January.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description and the catalogue record, not a
reading. What follows is what the book and its cataloguers say about it.*

- The publisher's description (Hachette Book Group, for the paperback):
  "*Consciousness Explained* is a full-scale exploration of human
  consciousness. In this landmark book, Daniel Dennett refutes the
  traditional, commonsense theory of consciousness and presents a new
  model, based on a wealth of information from the fields of neuroscience,
  psychology, and artificial intelligence. Our current theories about
  conscious life — of people, animal, even robots — are transformed by the
  new perspectives found in this book."
- The catalogue summary (Open Library's record of the first edition):
  "Advances a new theory of consciousness based on insights gleaned from
  the fields of neuroscience, psychology, and artificial intelligence, and
  clears away obsolete myths about the process of thinking in conscious
  beings."
- The contents note, from the MARC record carried by the Internet Archive's
  catalogue entries: "Prelude: how are hallucinations possible? —
  Explaining consciousness — A visit to the phenomenological garden — A
  method for phenomenology — Multiple drafts versus the Cartesian Theater —
  Time and experience — The evolution of consciousness — How words do
  things with us — The architecture of the human mind — Show and tell —
  Dismantling the witness protection program — Qualia disqualified — The
  reality of selves — Consciousness imagined". (One of the two copies of
  the record reads "Explaining the consciousness" for the second entry; the
  other reads "Explaining consciousness".)

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request for Dennett, as the book his
papers in this record point to. "Time and the Observer" ([LIT-431](LIT-431.md), read
in [NOTE-367](../notes.d/NOTE-367.md)) defers the Multiple Drafts model's wider implications to it
("Dennett 1991b"). "Facing Backwards" ([LIT-397](LIT-397.md), read in [NOTE-346](../notes.d/NOTE-346.md))
quotes its vitalism passage (pp. 281–282). Its "Qualia disqualified" is,
by title, the book-length successor to "Quining Qualia" ([LIT-433](LIT-433.md)).

**What a reading would add to the record's threads.** All of the following
are leads from the contents and from the record's other readings, not
claims about the book:

- *The society-of-mind bridge ([THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)).* The contents' "The
  architecture of the human mind" and "The reality of selves" are where a
  reader would find whether Dennett's mind is a society of agents in
  Minsky's sense, and what kind of whole his self is. Levin's paper
  ([LIT-439](LIT-439.md), read in [NOTE-341](../notes.d/NOTE-341.md)) calls its own view "consistent with
  Dennett's stack of homunculi" and cites a Dennett 1991; I have not checked
  that it means this book.
- *Hofstadter.* Hofstadter's GEB ([LIT-437](LIT-437.md)) and *I Am a Strange Loop*
  ([LIT-423](LIT-423.md)) are filed unread; the record cannot yet compare their selves
  with this book's.
- *The hard problem.* Chalmers's "Facing Up" ([LIT-406](LIT-406.md)) treats the
  Multiple Drafts model as a theory of an easy problem; the book's
  chapters "A method for phenomenology" and "Qualia disqualified" are where,
  by their titles, Dennett's answer to that charge would be.
- *AI consciousness ([THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)).* The publisher's description says the book
  bears on "conscious life — of people, animal, even robots".
