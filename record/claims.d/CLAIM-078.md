# CLAIM-078: The theory succeeds if its observational and decision measures predict changes of communicative identity that text similarity misses and follow its compositional predictions across reconstructions; it needs neither quantum contextuality nor exact Noether laws

## The claim

A110 §X: "A strong result would not require discovering quantum contextuality or
exact Noether conservation laws in ordinary language. It would be enough to show
that **observational and decision-theoretic structural measures predict
transformations of communicative identity that conventional text similarity
misses**, and that their behavior across successive reconstructions follows the
theory's compositional predictions." Its close: "I would not assert that this
framework has already established a new universal mathematical law of meaning.
Its mature contribution at this stage is a **coherent mathematical architecture,
several elementary preservation results, and a testable research program**. The
distinctive results still to be demonstrated are the value of combining
sheaf-structured observations with information-theoretic sufficiency, and the
predictive behavior of those structures under actual ... reconstruction."

## Where it went

Manuscript §10, Case I: "A meaningful success is incremental prediction of
held-out human communicative-fidelity judgments." The compositional half (chains
behaving as [CLAIM-056](CLAIM-056.md) and [CLAIM-059](CLAIM-059.md) predict) and the statement of what remains to be
shown are not in it. It is the success condition the central thesis ([CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md))
needs, alongside its defeat condition.

On 2026-10-10 the criterion came back in the exchange with a new target. A203
§37 (U56) restated it as recognition of "the same communicative kind": "**Do
independently constructed relational signatures and transport-fidelity
profiles predict whether humans recognize two realizations as instances of the
same communicative kind?**" It names three markers of a strong result:

- shared subject matter told apart from shared identity ("the model
  distinguishes shared subject matter from shared communicative identity");
- generalization to realizations, media or contexts not used in fitting;
- prediction of when repeated transformation preserves or destroys the
  relevant distinctions.

The third marker is this entry's compositional half, which the manuscript
dropped. The disclaimer survives too: "None of these requires discovering
quantum contextuality in ordinary language. Nor does the theory need a
universal Noether law for meaning." The owner quoted the bold question at U57
and took collaborative filtering as the laboratory for it ([CASE-tmptm6yt](../cases.d/CASE-tmptm6yt.md)).
A218's Chapter 15 core claim adds factorization baselines to the comparison:
the theory earns support "only if its independently grounded relational
measures predict persistence and transformation beyond standard lexical,
embedding, and factorization baselines". Neither is a draft, so the entry is
not revised.
