# NOTE-955: Fuchs, Mermin & Schack — an introduction to QBism

## Contribution

A short, authoritative statement of QBism by its three main proponents,
written for physics teachers. It adds no formal result. It sets out the
position (personalist probability, measurement as an agent's action whose
outcome is her experience, quantum states as judgements) and uses it to
argue that "quantum nonlocality" comes from three denials: that outcomes
pre-exist experience, that probability-1 assignments are judgements, and
that parameters with no counterpart in any agent's experience have no
place in physics (p. 4).

## Key insight

Quantum mechanics is a single-user tool. An agent assigns states, which
are her judgements; she acts on some external system; the outcome is the
experience the action elicits in her; and she updates. "The notorious
'collapse of the wave-function' is nothing but the updating of an agent's
state assignment on the basis of her experience" (p. 2). Everything
external to her, including other agents, is modelled; her own awareness
of her own experience is not. Correlations are between the experiences
of one agent, which lie on one time-like worldline, so they cannot be
nonlocal.

## Assumptions

- **Personalist probability.** "probabilities are assigned to an event by
  an agent and are particular to that agent" (p. 1); Dutch-book coherence
  is the only constraint, and it applies to one agent's assignments only:
  "It makes no sense to impose Dutch-book coherence on a combination of
  probability assignments made by more than one agent" (p. 2).
- **Agent means actor.** Footnote 1: "'Agent' in the sense of one who
  acts (and not in the sense of one who represents another)" (p. 1).
- **Experience is primitive.** It is not analysed. Its scope is "any and
  all experience" (p. 9).
- **The agent's own awareness is outside the formalism** (p. 2, below).
- **Locality from worldlines.** "No agent can move faster than light: the
  space-time trajectory of any agent is necessarily time-like. Her
  personal experience takes place along that trajectory" (p. 4).

## Key results

The paper has two equations, Bell's factorization conditions (1) and (2)
on p. 8, and no theorem. Its results are positions.

- **Measurement (§I, p. 2).** "A measurement in QBism is more than a
  procedure in a laboratory. It is any action an agent takes to elicit a
  set of possible experiences. The measurement outcome is the particular
  experience of that agent elicited in this way." Then the sentence the
  LIT quotes, verified verbatim: "A measurement does not, as the term
  unfortunately suggests, reveal a pre-existing state of affairs. It is an
  action on the world by an agent that results in the creation of an
  outcome — a new experience for that agent. “Intervention” might be a
  better term, but after more than 80 years of “measurement” the word is
  hard to purge from quantum theory" (p. 2; a reference marker, 12, after
  "term" is dropped here).
- **States are judgements (p. 2).** "Since probabilities are the personal
  judgments of an agent, it follows that a quantum state assignment is
  also a personal judgment of the agent assigning that state."
- **What the agent does not model (pp. 2–3).** "In QBism the only
  phenomenon accessible to Alice which she does not model with quantum
  mechanics is her own direct internal awareness of her own private
  experience. This (and only this) plays the role of the “classical
  objects” of Landau and Lifshitz for Alice (and only for Alice). Her
  awareness of her past experience forms the basis for the beliefs on
  which her state assignments rest." §III repeats it: the "vestige of this
  “classical domain” varies from one agent to another and is limited to
  that agent's directly perceived personal experience" (p. 6).
- **Other agents' reports are measurements (p. 3).** Bob's "personal
  internal awareness" is "inaccessible to Alice", but "verbal or written
  reports to Alice by other agents that attempt to represent their private
  experiences are indeed part of Alice's external world". "When Alice
  elicits an answer from Bob, she treats this as she treats any other
  quantum measurement. Bob's answer is created for Alice only when it
  enters her experience. A QBist does not treat Alice's interaction with
  Bob any differently from, say, her interaction with a Stern-Gerlach
  apparatus." QBism "requires" her to superpose Bob's reports before they
  enter her experience, which Bohr would not have approved (p. 3).
- **Agent-relative reality (p. 3).** "reality differs from one agent to
  another". A "common body of reality can be constructed" through
  communication, "limited only by the inability of language to represent
  the full flavor — the “qualia” — of personal experience."
- **Three precepts (p. 4).** (1) "A measurement outcome does not preexist
  the measurement. An outcome is created for the agent who takes the
  measurement action only when it enters the experience of that agent."
  (2) "Even probability-1 judgments are judgments. They are judgments in
  which the judging agent is supremely confident." (3) Parameters "that do
  not appear in the quantum theory and correspond to nothing in the
  experience of any potential agent can play no role".
- **Wigner's friend (§III, p. 5).** "There is an outcome in the friend's
  experience; there is none yet in Wigner's." The disagreement "is
  resolved the moment he receives her report, i.e. when it enters his own
  experience." In a Bell experiment each party is the other's Wigner.
- **Probability 1 (§IV, p. 6).** Assigning E probability 1 is a betting
  disposition and "does not mean that there is an objective feature of the
  world that makes E happen". Induction is "nothing more than a broadly
  shared personal judgment based on habit. The habit may even be
  hard-wired by evolution, but habit it remains." EPR's error is reading
  p = 1 as an element of reality (p. 7).
- **No influences at all (§V, p. 7).** "Bob's system is not changed by
  Alice's far away intervention in any way whatsoever." The λ of Bell's
  factorization is "undefined", corresponds to nothing in any agent's
  experience, and is "outside the scope of physical science" (p. 8). The
  paper grants that Bell-violating correlations cannot be explained by
  common causes at the source, "an important fact, surprising to one's
  classical intuition", and denies that it implies influence (p. 8).
- **Instruments (§VI, p. 9).** "Instruments are the Copenhagen surrogate
  for experience." QBism replaces "knowledge" by "belief", which "implies
  a believer" (p. 9), and claims it "eliminates the measurement problem"
  (p. 10), asserted rather than shown.

## Concepts

- **Agent.** "one who acts" (footnote 1, p. 1); any user of quantum
  mechanics.
- **Measurement.** "any action an agent takes to elicit a set of possible
  experiences" (p. 2); "Intervention" is offered as a better word.
- **Outcome.** The experience the action elicits in the agent who took
  it; it does not exist for anyone else until reported (pp. 4–5).
- **Judgement.** A probability or state assignment, expressing a betting
  disposition (pp. 2, 6). Judgement and measurement are different things
  in this paper: the first is a belief, the second an action.
- **Single-user theory.** Probability, and so quantum mechanics, binds
  the assignments of one agent (p. 2).
- **Vestigial classical domain.** The agent's own directly perceived
  experience, the one thing she does not model (p. 6).

## Connections

- **[NOTE-121](NOTE-121.md) (Elshatlawy et al., observers).** That chapter defines an
  observer by feedback closure, outputs that "alter the environment, which
  then alters later inputs", and maps the definition onto QBism among
  others. This paper's own account fits that definition: the agent's
  measurement is an action on an external system, and its result is a new
  input to her experience. QBism is therefore an instance of the rival
  [CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md) lists first ("observation and control as one loop
  through the environment"), not only a partial ally. Unlike [NOTE-121](NOTE-121.md)'s
  model, QBism puts no boundary B in the formalism: the cut is drawn by
  the agent's awareness and moves with the agent (p. 6).
- **[NOTE-174](NOTE-174.md) (Rovelli, RQM).** Both deny observer-independent outcomes and
  both handle Wigner's friend by indexing facts. They index differently:
  RQM to any interacting physical system, consciousness playing no
  role (as [NOTE-174](NOTE-174.md) reads it), QBism to an agent's experience, created by her action. RQM's open
  question of inter-observer consistency has a QBist counterpart, answered
  here only by the assertion that language builds "a common body of
  reality" (p. 3). [NOTE-174](NOTE-174.md) records Rovelli's charge that QBism bases its
  ontology on stable facts; this paper does not reply to it.
- **[NOTE-249](NOTE-249.md) (measurement as sheafification).** Both papers say
  nonlocality is an artefact of interpretation. QBism's version is the
  narrower and the more defensible. It concedes the empirical fact that no
  factorizable λ model exists (p. 8), which by Abramsky and Brandenburger's
  theorem, as [NOTE-249](NOTE-249.md) reports it, is the absence of a global section. It
  denies only that this fact implies influence. [NOTE-249](NOTE-249.md)'s paper instead
  says nonlocality disappears, which [NOTE-249](NOTE-249.md) shows it does not. On
  measurement they part: Ghose makes measurement a passage to globally
  communicable, Bohrian classical description; this paper says Bohr's
  ordinary-language reports are themselves subject to quantum mechanics
  for the receiving agent (p. 3), and the only classical domain is each
  agent's own experience.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-341](../claims.d/CLAIM-341.md) (the thesis).** The quotation in its section
  "Judgement is not action" is correct and on p. 2. Three things the
  claim says or implies about QBism need adjusting.
  - *QBism keeps judgement and measurement apart.* In this paper a
    judgement is a state or probability assignment (p. 2, §IV), and a
    measurement is the action that elicits experience. The owner's U1
    phrase "Judgement (qua measurement that generates interpretations)"
    identifies the two; QBism does not. Its loop is judgement (prior) →
    action on an external system → experience (outcome) → updated
    judgement (collapse, p. 2). That is an action–perception cycle with
    Bayesian update, closer to the active-inference diagram the claim
    rejects than to the claim. The claim's "agrees with U1 about
    measurement and disagrees about its target" holds only for the
    creation of the outcome; QBism also disagrees about what a judgement
    is.
  - *The created outcome is already on the agent's side.* What the action
    creates is "a new experience for that agent" (p. 2), and "That
    experience is the outcome" (p. 5); the external system is "not changed
    by Alice's far away intervention in any way whatsoever" (p. 7). So the
    difference from the claim is not "changes the judge, not the world"
    against "changes the world". Both create the outcome on the agent's
    side. The difference is the route: for QBism the outcome is elicited
    by acting on something external, for the claim by the judge's
    operation on itself.
  - *QBism refuses the object the claim needs.* The claim's loop runs
    through a judgement that reshapes the observer. QBism exempts exactly
    that from its formalism: the agent's "own direct internal awareness of
    her own private experience" is the only thing she does not model
    (p. 2). QBism has no measurement of the self, and so no counterpart of
    a judgement whose outcome is a changed judge. That makes it a sharper
    contrast than the claim states: QBism's measurement targets everything
    but the observer, the claim's judgement targets only the observer.
- **[CLAIM-337](../claims.d/CLAIM-337.md) (mental action).** Nothing in this paper supports a
  mental-action reading: its actions are on external systems, and
  "agent" is defined as "one who acts" (p. 1). It does support the
  counter's "judgement is behaviour" step only in the weak sense that a
  probability assignment is a betting disposition (p. 6).
- **[CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md) and [CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) (the anchor).** In QBism the only
  constraint on judgements other than experience is Dutch-book coherence,
  which binds one agent's assignments to each other (p. 2). It is internal
  consistency, not evidence that bypasses the interpretation. What corrects
  judgement is new experience, and on this paper's definition new
  experience as outcome is elicited by action. So QBism, read strictly, is
  on [CLAIM-339](../claims.d/CLAIM-339.md)'s side: no action, no measurement, no outcome. The
  paper does not say whether experience the agent did not elicit (sounds,
  unsolicited reports) counts as a measurement outcome (my observation).
- **[QUESTION-121](../questions.d/QUESTION-121.md), candidate 5 (other people).** The question's gloss,
  "QBism treats another's report as one more measurement outcome entering
  one's own experience", is accurate (p. 3). Two qualifications. In this
  paper the report is something Alice "elicits" by a question, which is an
  action, and Wigner resolves the disagreement only "If Wigner goes on to
  ask his friend" (p. 5). And the report is a measurement on Bob as an
  external quantum system, not unscored testimony. The paper does once use
  a passive form, "Bob's verbal representation of his own experience can
  enter Alice's" (p. 3), which leaves room for unsolicited reports.
- **[CASE-107](../cases.d/CASE-107.md) (locked-in).** My observation: on this paper's
  definition, a completely locked-in agent takes no measurements, since a
  measurement is an action. Whether her experiences are outcomes is not
  addressed.
- **For the anthology.** No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- An essay for a teaching journal: positions, not arguments. "Experience"
  and "action" are not analysed.
- The claim to eliminate the measurement problem (p. 10) is asserted.
- Inter-agent agreement is asserted to come from language (p. 3), with no
  account of why agents' experiences cohere.
- What counts as an action that elicits experience, and whether passive
  experience is an outcome, is not said.

## Open questions

- Whether QBism needs measurement to be an action, or only the eliciting
  of experience; whether unsolicited experience counts. The paper's
  definition says action.
- Whether an agent can apply quantum mechanics to her own future
  judgements, as distinct from her awareness, and so treat a judgement as
  a measurement on herself. The paper excludes her awareness only.
