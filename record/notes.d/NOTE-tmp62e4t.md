# NOTE-tmp62e4t: Bencherki & Iliadis — Organization as informational individuation

## Contribution

The paper finds that the three main CCO schools (the Montreal School,
the Luhmannian and McPhee's Four Flows) each rest on a theory of
communication that takes place between or within pre-existing
entities, so they cannot hold that communicating and organizing are one
process (ms. pp. 8–12). It offers Simondon's informational individuation
as the missing theory, building on Taylor's call to treat
conversations as autonomous, self-reproducing units (ms. pp. 4–5).

## Key insight

Communication is the propagation of action through domains that it
reorders as it passes. So an organization is not where communication
happens but what the propagation leaves ordered: "communication systems
do not pre-exist the communication of information" (ms. p. 7).

## Assumptions

- **Simondon's terms, as read here.** Information is a difference in
  "potentially active energy" between domains, glossed through the Greek
  *ergon* as a difference in action potential (ms. p. 6). This gloss is
  the authors'.
- **Domains** are "similarly-ordered regions of social, physical,
  technical, or cognitive activity" (ms. p. 6), and are identified by
  their internal ordering, not by a boundary (ms. pp. 20–21).
- **Transduction is communication.** "If we equate transduction to
  communication (as was Simondon's intention)" (ms. p. 14).

## Key results

The paper's results are four propositions (ms. pp. 21–22):

1. **Information and communication.** "Information is a differential in
   action potential that emerges from disparity in ordering between
   domains. Communication is the kin to kin propagation of action that
   seeks to resolve disparity and, in doing so, organizes a domain as it
   circulates through it."
2. **Coexisting levels.** "Entities, including organizations and other
   collectives, are not at different stages of individuation, but rather
   different informational levels that preserve all the characteristics
   of their organized components."
3. **Meaning.** "The meaning or signification of any action ...
   corresponds to its participation in the resolution of a disparity of
   ordering."
4. **Knowledge.** "Knowledge of an entity ... should focus on its
   individuation process and should reflect on the way that knowledge
   itself has individuated."

## Method

A theoretical argument, then an illustration (ms. pp. 25–30): a
committee meeting of community organizations in a Montréal district.
Marie, from a subcommittee, asks the Council to join an "Integration
Action Week". The authors read her speech as propagating the
subcommittee's ordering to the Council: through sign-up sheets, a
calendar, a "passport" and a website ("To get on the calendar ... to get
in the network of integration", l. 58–59). The week took place, which
they take to show that the ordering propagated. The first author was
the ethnographer, and he describes his own individuation through the
fieldwork (proposition 4).

## Concepts

- **Domain.** A similarly ordered region of activity within or across
  systems.
- **Disparation.** The difference in action potential between domains,
  which tends toward resolution.
- **Transduction.** Propagation of action "from kin to kin" (*de proche
  en proche*), each structured region serving as the principle of the
  next (Simondon 2005 p. 32, in the authors' translation, ms. pp.
  13–14).
- **Signification.** The reordering an action produces in the domain
  that takes it up; "the structure of compatibility and of viability"
  (Simondon 2005 p. 35, ms. p. 16).

## Limitations

- **Meaning by outcome.** If an action's meaning is the reordering it
  produces, its meaning cannot be known until the reordering is seen. The
  example's evidence that the ordering propagated is that the event took
  place.
- **"Ordering" is not operationalized.** Nothing says how to tell two
  domains apart, or how much reordering counts, independently of the
  analyst's description.
- **The excerpt is chosen to agree.** "The case was selected because it
  offers an example of discourse that follows Simondon's logic" (ms. p.
  25). It illustrates; it could not have disconfirmed.
- **Its Simondon is translated and glossed.** The energy-to-action move
  through *ergon* is the authors', not a quotation.
- **The version is the manuscript.** Changes made in review are unknown.

## Open questions

- Whether "disparity of ordering" can be measured before the encounter
  (for instance, as incompatibility between routines), so that a
  failure to propagate could be predicted.
- What, if anything, distinguishes an organization from any other
  ordered domain on this account. The authors take Sillince's complaint
  that CCO uses "organization" for everything (ms. p. 20) and answer it
  by making "organization" cover any differently ordered group.
