# QUESTION-023: Can the opacity of insider vocabulary be a preservation target in its own right?

## Why it is a question

Henley's rendering of Villon ([CASE-010](../cases.d/CASE-010.md)) is opaque on purpose. A6: "Henley's
opacity is part of its charm. The reader doesn't necessarily know what half the
scams are". The goal it proposed was "to **recreate the experience of
overhearing a world whose inhabitants understand every word, even though you
don't**". A9 put the choice as "whether a modern translation should preserve
the original's *recognizable vulgarity* or its *unfamiliar insider
vocabulary*". The workshop went for vulgarity, and the question was not taken
up again in this stretch.

## Why it is worth keeping

The manuscript's §5 makes audience knowledge K part of the channel: "The same
compact expression may be comprehensible to one audience and opaque to
another." There opacity is a failure of decoding. This question asks whether
the receiver's *not* understanding can itself be among the observables a
rendering preserves, which would put a property of the audience–text relation,
not of the text, among the transport's targets. An answer would say whether
such a target can be stated as an observable in the sense of §2.

A neighbouring distinction, also raised once and dropped (A7): "*what would
Henley write today?* versus *what contemporary line produces the same effect
Henley's produced on his audience?*" The second became the manuscript's notion
of fidelity; the first, a counterfactual-author target, was never weighed.
