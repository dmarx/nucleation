# LIT-tmp9q3ir: Emotional Aperture and Strategic Change: The Accurate Recognition of Collective Emotions

Jeffrey Sanchez-Burks and Quy Nguyen Huy (2009), *Organization Science* 20(1):22–34 — DOI-10.1287/orsc.1070.0347

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's abstract, not read. What follows is what
the abstract says.*

- **Emotional aperture** is defined as "the ability to recognize the
  composition of diverse emotions in a collective (e.g., group or business
  unit)".
- The thesis is that a leader's ability to respond effectively to "patterns
  of shared emotions" in strategic change "and other emotionally turbulent
  organizational processes" depends on using emotional aperture.
- The paper describes "cultural, psychological, and contextual enablers and
  impediments" to the focus and accuracy that aperture needs. It presents
  itself as extending emotional competencies, such as emotional
  intelligence, that "have focused more narrowly on individual-level
  emotions".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-04 at the owner's request, with four works on emotion in
groups and organisations. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice. `Deferred` because it was not
read.

Its place in the batch is the perceiver's side of collective emotion. Von
Scheve & Ismer ([LIT-tmp0fwb0](LIT-tmp0fwb0.md)) define a collective emotion as convergence
in affective responding. Barsade ([LIT-tmpojrfm](LIT-tmpojrfm.md)) shows one way convergence
is produced, through contagion. This paper, by its abstract, asks how an
observer recognises what a group feels when the group's emotions are
*diverse*, a composition rather than one shared state. That makes it the
batch's counterweight to treating group emotion as an average. A group
half anxious and half enthusiastic is not a group that is mildly pleased.
Barsade's closing call for leaders "emotionally attuned to and influenced
by their followers" is the competence this paper names.

For social ontology, the question it raises is whether a collective's
emotion is a distribution over members, an object a perceiver can read off
as a pattern, or a state of the group. The abstract's "collective emotions"
and "patterns of shared emotions" suggest the first two. Whether the paper
commits to any view of group subjects can only be said after a reading.

**Priority for a reading: medium.** The likely contents, a perceptual
skill and its organisational enablers, are known from the abstract. A
reading would say how the paper defines a group's emotional
"composition", and whether it reports a measure or a study.
