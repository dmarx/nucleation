# THEORY-532: Systems pushed from equilibrium develop structures that degrade the applied gradient faster, and living systems and maturing ecosystems are such structures, continuous with convection cells and vortices, so the second law is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for life

## Source

Schneider & Kay (1994), *Mathematical and Computer Modelling*
19(6–8):25–48, [LIT-533](../literature.d/LIT-533.md), read in full in [NOTE-415](../notes.d/NOTE-415.md) from the authors'
formatted text. The typeset version was not seen. [NOTE-415](../notes.d/NOTE-415.md) recomputed the
ratios in Tables 1–3.

[NOTE-415](../notes.d/NOTE-415.md) concludes that "no new THEORY is indicated", since C1 and C4 "are
programmatic and too weakly supported to source one". This document files
the account as Proposed, with that weakness stated.

## What was actually shown

**The principle.** From the Hatsopoulos–Keenan and Kestin result, that an
isolated system reaches a unique equilibrium when constraints are removed,
the authors propose: "as they are moved away from equilibrium, they will
utilize all avenues available to counter the applied gradients. As the
applied gradients increase, so does the system's ability to oppose further
movement from equilibrium." Kestin's proof gives the local stability of
equilibrium. The step to far-from-equilibrium behaviour is not derived
(C1, assertion).

**Bénard convection.** Recalculated from Silveston's data (silicone oil,
6.98 mm deep), heat transfer rises linearly with ΔT and entropy production
and exergy destruction rise nonlinearly. All three jump at the onset of
convection (C2, strong for these data). The authors conclude that the
principle is "not one of maximum entropy production but rather one of
entropy production change being positive semi-definite as you increase the
gradient" (C3, from this one system).

**A vortex.** A bottle drains in about 11 seconds with a vortex and about 6
minutes without.

**Life.** Life is "another means of dissipating the solar induced
gradient"; living systems are "sophisticated mini-tornados, with a memory
(its DNA)". The restated law is "a necessary but not a sufficient
condition for life", and "survival is equally important" (C4, assertion;
C5).

**Ecosystems.** Development should raise energy capture, flow, cycling,
trophic depth, respiration, biomass and diversity.

- *Crystal River marshes* (Table 1). The thermally stressed marsh against
  its control: biomass −34.7%, imports −18.1%, total throughput −20.7%,
  food-web cycles 142 → 69. One pair, no replicate (C6, weak).
- *Oregon forests* (Table 2, Luvall & Holbo). Surface temperature falls
  from 50.7 °C (quarry) and 51.8 °C (clear-cut) to 24.7 °C in a 400-year
  Douglas fir stand. The share of incoming radiation degraded rises from
  62% to 90% (C7, a correlation). The table's net-radiation entry for the
  old stand is inconsistent with its own definition; 90% is 910/1005, and
  the listed 830 is probably the misprint ([NOTE-415](../notes.d/NOTE-415.md)).
- *Regional budgets* (Table 3, Sato et al.). Evapotranspiration takes 70%
  of net radiation over the Amazon and 2% over the Sahara. A model run
  replacing rainforest by grassland lowers it from 63% to 48%.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The stressed marsh could have shown
no loss of flow, and the mature forest could have been warmer than the
clear-cut. Neither happened. But the physics cases were chosen because
structure raises dissipation there, and no counter-case was examined.

## What this does not say

- **That the restated law is a law.** It is a proposal, related to the
  classical second law by analogy. The paper gives no case where it
  predicts something the classical law plus the dynamics does not
  ([NOTE-415](../notes.d/NOTE-415.md)). Le Chatelier's principle, cited as an instance, is also a
  near-equilibrium result.
- **That organized structures must raise dissipation.** The Bénard cell and
  the vortex show that they can.
- **That ecosystems develop because of the second law.** The ecological
  evidence is correlational. The forests and regions differ in water,
  canopy and climate as well as maturity, and the authors say
  evapotranspiration does most of the degrading.
- **Where life begins.** The one marker the paper allows is "memory", which
  lets the process continue "without having to restart" it by chance. That
  is a line drawn by heredity, not by thermodynamics, and the paper's own
  "necessary but not sufficient" says thermodynamics cannot draw it.
- **That it avoids nonequilibrium entropy.** The variables are meant to be
  gradients and exergy, but the Bénard analysis computes an entropy
  production rate.
- **What Schrödinger said.** The paper presents itself as taking up "the
  task proposed by Schrödinger". That reading of him is unchecked; the
  record holds his book unread.

## Connections

- **Maximum entropy production ([THEORY-671](THEORY-671.md)).** The paper rejects MEP
  on the Bénard data. Its own principle does not contradict MEP, though: a
  system can raise its dissipation as the gradient grows and still sit at
  the maximum among its possible steady states. So no `rivals` edge is
  declared.
- **Life forced into existence ([THEORY-492](THEORY-492.md)).** Morowitz and Smith also
  read the biosphere as a channel for energy flow and also reject
  entropy-production principles, but put Jaynesian entropy maximization in
  their place, not a law about gradients. Both use the hurricane as the
  non-living analogue.
- **Dissipative adaptation ([THEORY-412](THEORY-412.md))** makes the neighbouring claim,
  that driven matter favours states that absorb and dissipate work, as
  statistical mechanics.
- **Life and planetary MEP ([THEORY-517](THEORY-517.md)).** Kleidon's argument that
  life raises the Earth's entropy production by absorbing more sunlight is
  the planetary version of the forest comparison here.
- **The demarcation question.** The candle-flame dispute ([LIT-192](../literature.d/LIT-192.md),
  [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)) is the one this paper's tornado and Bénard cell stand in. The
  record's account of where the line falls, [THEORY-072](THEORY-072.md), puts it at
  organisational differentiation and says measures of entropy production
  cannot settle it. This paper's "necessary but not sufficient" agrees that
  thermodynamics alone does not draw the line. [QUESTION-085](../questions.d/QUESTION-085.md) uses the same
  ladder for a social case.
