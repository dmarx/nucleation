summary: >-
  A gerontological essay with a report of the authors' own studies. It reads Erikson's generativity, as McAdams models it, as the psychological form of an ethical category of "joint responsibility", and argues it is a lifelong concern, not a midlife task. It restates McAdams' model: generativity springs from cultural demand and from an inner desire made of a "need to be needed" and a need for symbolic immortality, and passes through concern, commitment and belief in the species to action and a life-story script. It distinguishes generativity from altruism and says generative engagement can be selfish or altruistic in motive. Studies of 3,308 people aged 59 to 108 in Mexico, Spain and Germany found generativity predicted optimism and life satisfaction beyond health, money and family status. An intervention of intergenerational dialogue projects in Belarus, Russia and Ukraine raised Loyola Generativity Scale scores in old and young participants. No test statistics are reported.
