# CLAIM-441: Flow tells against the potential account's condition that valence is undefined where no pair is contested: on the record's own account of flow nothing is overridden because an activity's immediate reward and its longer-run value coincide, yet flow is reported as positive, so either flow's positivity is intrinsic pleasantness and not goal-conduciveness valence, or flow keeps contested pairs its processes settle among themselves, or the positivity comes after the episode and not during it, or the condition is wrong

## The objection

[TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) was revised before merge to add a nonvacuity condition: a
window with no contested pairs has every ordering as its potential, so
valence there is "undefined, not zero or neutral". [CLAIM-162](CLAIM-162.md) holds that in
flow "no interest has to be overridden", because the activity's immediate
reward and its longer-run value coincide. That is intrapersonal
synchronization in [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md)'s sense, every interest ranking the same
course first, and it leaves nothing contested.

So on the record's two claims, a fully synchronized flow episode has
undefined goal-conduciveness valence. Flow is usually described as
enjoyable, and if it is positive during the episode the condition is
wrong about one of the clearest cases of positive engagement there is.

## The exits

1. **Flow's positivity is intrinsic pleasantness.** The account is of
   goal-conduciveness valence only ([CLAIM-433](CLAIM-433.md)); intrinsic pleasantness is
   a separate appraisal with its own ordering. If flow's positivity is
   'liking' and not progress along a prevailing ordering, the condition
   survives and flow is a case where the two levels of valence come
   apart, as [QUESTION-266](../questions.d/QUESTION-266.md) now allows.
2. **Flow keeps contested pairs, settled locally.** [TERM-061](../terms.d/TERM-061.md)'s practical
   synchronization allows rankings to differ while the processes settle
   their conflicts among themselves without override. If flow is
   synchronization in that sense and not [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md)'s, the pairs are still
   contested, the condition is met, and the account reads flow's valence
   off which ordering the local settlements climb. [CLAIM-162](CLAIM-162.md) would then
   need restating.
3. **The positivity comes after the episode.** It is often said, of the
   flow literature, that people in flow are absorbed rather than happy,
   and that the enjoyment is reported afterwards. If so, flow's valence is
   undefined during the episode and positive in retrospect, which is what
   the condition predicts, and the retrospective report measures
   remembered utility, a different ordering ([QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md)'s "when to read
   it"). The record has not read the literature ([LIT-914](../literature.d/LIT-914.md)), so this exit is
   named and not taken.
4. **The condition is wrong.** Valence could be defined where nothing is
   contested, for instance as progress along the shared ordering against a
   reference course, with no conflict needed. That would give up
   [CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md)'s first consequence, "no contest, no valence" (stated as frustration until 2026-10-29; [CLAIM-449](CLAIM-449.md)), and with
   it the account's restriction of valence to systems with conflicts.

## What it does not say

- That flow is valenced. Whether it is, during the episode, is the open
  fact.
- That the exits exclude one another. A flow episode could be positive in
  'liking' and settle contested pairs locally.
