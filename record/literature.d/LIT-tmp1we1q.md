summary: >-
  Greaves & MacAskill. Argue for axiological and deontic strong longtermism: in the most important decisions facing agents today (how a society or an individual spends resources without restriction to a cause, and career choice), every near-best option is near-best for the far future and delivers much larger benefits there than in the near future, and one ought to choose such an option. The argument rests on an expected number of future beings of at least 10^24 (10^14 on a restricted, Earth-only estimate), and on interventions (asteroid detection, pandemic preparedness, AI safety) whose expected far-future benefit per $100 beats the 0.025 lives that bednets save. The deontic thesis follows by a stakes-sensitivity argument that any plausible non-consequentialism should accept. The authors take cluelessness, ambiguity aversion and imprecise credences to fail as objections. They name fanaticism about tiny probabilities as one of the most plausible ways the case could fail. They concede that a positive pure rate of time preference would defeat it.
