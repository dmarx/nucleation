# Article 4. The Architecture of Will

## Authority, organizational affect, and the negotiation of incompatible demands

**Abstract.** For an organization to act together, it has to settle which concerns, proposals and interventions take effect. The Nucleation record calls the relations that settle this a will-organization. It holds that precedence, jurisdiction and suspension are distinct relations of practical authority, and that the precedence a motive claims differs from the authority it is granted. This article sets out that account and its standing. One executed study (CASE-325, Active, with no recorded check by a second session) compares a model of these relations with five control-only models in a stipulated organization. Where the organization is as stipulated, the relational model wins, and it alone predicts combinations of changes in authority that training never showed together. It loses where authority is idle or idiosyncratic. In a world where one relation composes otherwise than the model says, it still wins the training evidence and then mispredicts. The record's own reading of the study (CLAIM-890, Proposed) concedes that for an organization institutional theory already writes these relations, so what the study supports is not a gain over existing theory. The article then takes up the formal results on recursive grants and directed tolerances, the owner's thesis that frustration and not distress is the primitive of regulatory difficulty, and the record's functional account of organizational affect. Every entry in that account is Proposed, and the objection that holds it back is unanswered. Last come learned accommodation, flow, and orderings that cannot be integrated. The record holds that low conflict and fluent coordination do not show that accommodation is adequate. It does not hold that a learned accommodation lowers the cost of arbitration: that claim is argued, not shown.

## 4.1 Why a theory of will-organization beside control theory

A51 leads with CASE-325, and so does this article. Its question is QUESTION-056's (Open): "When the mechanisms composing a will-organization are described by control theory and executive-control research, does that description implement the will-organization account or replace it, and what result would show the agent-level description had become idle?"

### 4.1.1 What was run

LEAD-089 (Open in front matter; run, by its note of 2026-11-22) asked for Northstar (CASE-023) to be modelled "with control and metacontrol mechanisms alone and then with a will-organization over them", for the difference to be priced, and for "planning, control and institutional theory" to be set "beside the account". The assistant carried it out as a delegated worker. The study is CASE-325 (Active, executed study, the assistant's; scratch/studies/lead-089/).

- **Episodes.** Each episode decides which of three regimes becomes effective: routine deployment, cautious verification or emergency suspension.
- **Authority.** Three bits, each one of the case's own variants (CASE-002, CASE-003). The first is whether an objection suspends deployment. The second is who classifies severity. The third is whether senior management may override a freeze. "The proposals are the same under every arrangement", and so are each party's propensities.
- **The models.** The will-organization model (WO) has four parameters, and "Maintained relations decide which proposal becomes effective". Against it are five control-only models, C0 to C4. "Each sees the arrangement of authority as an input. None has a variable for a relation." C2 is a metacontrol gate, "the form of an expected-value-of-control choice among regimes". The control models have 16, 128, 14, 38 and 64 parameters.
- **Training and test.** Training sees "the baseline and each single change of authority". The test is "2,000 fresh episodes under each of the four combinations never seen in training". There are four worlds (governed, idle, idiosyncratic, interacting), four sample sizes and 20 seeds.

### 4.1.2 What was found

The results are CASE-325's, and the finding drawn from them is CLAIM-889 (Proposed, demonstrated, the assistant's).

- **Governed world.** "WO beats every control-only model by more than its Occam cost on 80 of 80 seeds". The median log Bayes factor runs from 17.1 [10.0, 22.2] at n = 30 to 61.3 [49.1, 70.7] at n = 1000. On the unseen combinations "WO is within 0.015 nats per episode of the truth, and every gate is 0.03 to 0.26 nats per episode away".
- **The gap does not close.** At 10⁶ episodes per training arrangement, the gates' regret on the unseen combinations stays at 0.08–0.11 (C2), 0.022–0.034 (C3) and 0.009–0.018 (C4). The case's explanation: "A log-additive gate cannot represent how the relations compose. The products of two authority bits that would let it are never observed when each change is tried alone."
- **Idle and idiosyncratic worlds.** "WO loses on every seed, by 0.9 to 2,551 nats." "So the test can come out against the will-organization."
- **Interacting world.** This is the world A51 leaves out. It has the same relations, "except that where the reliability group classifies it also takes objections over, so an objection no longer suspends by itself", which "shows only in combined arrangements". In this world "WO wins the training evidence by 10 to 82 nats on every seed, because the misplaced relation never shows in training. It then predicts the unseen combinations worse than the additive gate C2: 0.26 to 0.32 nats per episode against 0.16 to 0.20". So a wrongly composed will-organization passes CLAIM-174's test 4 when that test is priced on training data alone. CASE-325 concludes: "The pricing has to be done on held-out interventions." CLAIM-889 puts it into its title: "priced on the training arrangements alone, the same test also passes a will-organization whose relations compose otherwise than it says".

A51 calls the study "considerably better evidence than a demonstration that a relational model can reproduce ordinary behavior". That is accurate about the design: it has a comparison, held-out interventions and worlds in which it can fail. It overstates what the record has done with the result. CLAIM-889 is Proposed. The case has had no second-session check. A51 also omits the interacting world, which shows that the study's headline measure, evidence on the training arrangements, cannot tell a right composition from a wrong one. Only the held-out combinations tell them apart.

### 4.1.3 What the record reads from it

The record's reading is CLAIM-890 (Proposed, interpretive, the assistant's; rests on CLAIM-889). It sets three fields beside the construction:

- **Control theory and executive control.** These "give the control-only models, which miss the difference, so against them the will-organization variables are implemented and not replaced". A control model that matches WO "has to add a variable for who holds which position and what follows from holding it. That variable is the relation".
- **Planning theory.** "holding the plan fixed as the case does, gives no difference at all". This holds for the planning theory the record has read. Bratman's account of shared agency (LIT-488) is Unavailable, and "that verdict waits on it".
- **Institutional theory.** It "already writes the relations as rules-in-use, so for an organization the difference is one an existing theory gives, and what is left of the explanatory-gain claim is that one description serves across realizers, which a model of one realizer cannot test".

QUESTION-056's note of 2026-11-22 sums it up: "for an organization the agent-level description is not idle, yet not new either. Still open for a person." CASE-325 states the same limit among what it cannot show: "That the gain is the will-organization's rather than the rules'."

This is the article's first result, and it is double-edged. Against the control models and the planning theory the record has read, the relational description is not idle (CLAIM-024's objection, Proposed, "fails on two fronts in this case and holds on the third", CLAIM-890). Against institutional theory, for an organization, it is not new. CLAIM-890 does not say the account is a relabelling. That "would follow only if no second realizer shares the variables. The claim leaves that open." CLAIM-890 also finds that LEAD-089's stated criterion "looks inverted", and reads it that way: a difference an existing theory already gives supports CLAIM-024 on that front rather than defeating it.

**What stays open.** The cross-realizer gain (CLAIM-487, Proposed) needs "a second realizer modelled with the same variables, and nothing here does it" (CLAIM-890). LEAD-089's note names one candidate, a person such as the reluctant writer (CASE-013). No lead of its own holds that study. The across-realizers intervention test of CLAIM-027 is also "not yet run" (QUESTION-015; SYNTHESIS-036, "What it does not say"). SYNTHESIS-036 predates CASE-325 and still calls the question open. No account yet maps CASE-325, CLAIM-889 or CLAIM-890 into it.

## 4.2 Differentiated authority

The vocabulary is Active. A will-organization is "The maintained organization of relations governing the conditions under which an agent's available action-generating regimes acquire, retain, lose, or alter practical efficacy" (TERM-023). Three of its relations are "easily conflated: precedence orders applicable constraints, jurisdiction governs which apply, suspension governs when an applicable one may be disabled" (TERM-017). The will paper's central thesis is that which regime becomes effective "depends on maintained relations — accessibility, precedence, persistence, jurisdiction, suspension and transformation — that are not reducible to the contents of the competing regimes, whatever mechanisms realize them" (CLAIM-027, Active, interpretive). In CASE-325 the three authority bits are a suspension, a jurisdiction and an override of precedence. The study varies them separately, and its result turns on how they compose.

Two distinctions keep authority apart from what it is often taken for.

- **Claimed against granted.** "The precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it are different quantities: a motive can claim control without receiving it, and be granted control it does not claim" (CLAIM-283, Active, interpretive; A4 §2, reading Frijda; made Active at the owner's ruling). The claim "does not say which wins". Its first version also identified internal conflict with the gap between the two. That identification is now a separate claim, "Internal conflict is the gap between the precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it" (CLAIM-484, Proposed). This article uses only the distinction.
- **Activation, grant and efficacy.** The owner's U18 objected to an intervention threshold set by expected benefit: "an intervention doesn't need to be predicted to be effective to be motivating. consider someone punching a wall in anger." The record holds that a process's "activation, the authority the organization grants it and the efficacy of what it does are separate quantities, so a threshold on expected net benefit is one special case of an activation threshold and not its general form" (CLAIM-548, Active, interpretive, the owner's point in A's wording). Its rival, a goal-directed reading of costly aggression (NOTE-1117), is cited in the claim.

## 4.3 Recursive grants

A51 names SYNTHESIS-087 (Proposed) as "especially relevant". Its components are Active and demonstrated, and they come from A2's analysis of the owner's U2 design.

- "Forbidding self-granting does not stop an operator from amplifying its own authority: under damped recursive delegation, reciprocal and cyclic grants recycle authority, so an operator's authority depends on its own grants, while one-hop allocation within an epoch, from budgets and baselines fixed independently of outgoing grants, makes it exactly independent of them" (CLAIM-282, Active, demonstrated, role granted).
- Under one-hop allocation, granting influence to a nearer ally "raises how far its preferred policy is realized while its own authority stays fixed, so the two conditions of the grant scheme hold together" (CLAIM-375, Active, demonstrated).
- "Multi-hop delegation can keep an operator's authority exactly independent of its own grants" under leave-one-out damped delegation (CLAIM-502, Active, demonstrated). It does not say "that [CLAIM-375]'s guarantee carries over".

The account declines the tempting summary. "Only one-hop allocation, among the rules on record, keeps both conditions" fails "as adding nothing on record, and is open beyond it" (SYNTHESIS-087, "Not stated on the record"). All three components disclaim "That any rule closes the social route of moving others' grants" (ibid.). A51's phrasing, "forbidding direct self-grants is insufficient to prevent some forms of authority amplification", is CLAIM-282's first clause, correctly scoped.

**Already done.** A population study of grant schemes is CASE-420 (Active, executed study, the assistant's; rerun under a delegated ruling): "Role indexing with the critic role scored by relevance leaves no factions and no capture; scalar grants fail either way". Operators bound by both grant conditions "delegate everything, all of it to their own side". It is "A fictional model, by construction; the numbers are of this model." This article proposes no further grant study.

## 4.4 Tolerances as endorsement criteria

The owner's U12 of "Organizational Nucleation and SLT" (2026-11-04): "i was thinking more along the linesof tolerances expressing endorsement criteria relative to some given perspective's priorities. maybe I'm confusing necessary vs sufficient conditions." The record answers in A's wording, sharpened by the reader:

> "A perspective's tolerance for another process's contribution is its criterion for conferring influence on that contribution, set by its own priorities: tolerances are directed, and the discrepancy each measures is the perspective's own, so inside its tolerance the perspective's interventions raise the other's share of future influence and outside it they lower it; meeting the tolerance is necessary for that conferral where the criterion binds, and sufficient only for a grant, under a rule that grants influence on it" (CLAIM-552, Active, interpretive; promoted under a delegated ruling).

The owner's worry about necessary and sufficient conditions is answered inside the claim. Meeting a tolerance is necessary for conferral "where the criterion binds". It is sufficient only for a grant, and only "under a rule that grants influence on it". It is not sufficient for influence, which the channel or the rule may fail to carry: the defeater excludes "a grant that fails to raise influence because the channel or the rule does not carry it".

Four results surround it, and none of them makes a quiet perspective a consenting one.

- **Where a tolerance comes from.** This is the first link of the owner's U19 chain, "motivational threshold -> motivational tolerances". If activation is z = b(c) + α(c)d and the perspective acts with probability σ(κ(z − θ)), the discrepancy it tolerates while acting with probability at most ε is "(θ + κ⁻¹ logit ε − b(c))/α(c)". So one fixed threshold gives different tolerances across contexts, and "raising a threshold enlarges tolerance without relieving any" demand (CLAIM-550, Active, demonstrated; re-derived at its status review). SYNTHESIS-044 notes that only this first link is given: whether QUESTION-372's chain "holds link by link" is not stated on the record.
- **Quiet is not satisfied.** "The quiescent complex … is not the compatibility complex of their requirements … a perspective can be quiet while its requirement is unmet and active while it is met; raising thresholds can only enlarge the quiescent complex and leaves the requirement complex as it was, so it is not relief of frustration" (CLAIM-549, Active, demonstrated).
- **Not intervening is not endorsing.** The owner's U15, as corrected at U17, gives: "a perspective that does not intervene has not thereby endorsed" (CLAIM-553, Proposed, interpretive).
- **Compatibility of bounds.** The owner's U27 of "Assess SDT Coverage" asked about the "compatibility of tolerance bounds". On a graph of contexts, an assignment within every tolerance exists "exactly when no cycle's holonomy exceeds the sum of its tolerances", and "local adjustment can move a mismatch among the overlaps of a cycle and never remove it" (CLAIM-538, Active, demonstrated). The feasibility criterion is classical, "so the result is granted, not the record's discovery" (ibid.). CLAIM-537 (Active, demonstrated) adds disturbance and what the agent can read.

**Already done, in part.** A49 made a directed tolerance, Q_{i→j} = 1[d_i ≤ τ_{i→j}], its formal centre. The record holds directedness (CLAIM-552), derives a tolerance from a threshold (CLAIM-550), and gives compatibility (CLAIM-538, CLAIM-537), but CLAIM-538 and CLAIM-537 use symmetric bounds on an edge. A model in which directed tolerances and authority evolve together is not on the record. No lead holds it.

## 4.5 Frustration among demands

### 4.5.1 The owner's primitive

At U33 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 the owner wrote: "maybe we should focus less on distress and more on frustration". The record holds:

> "The primitive for an organization's regulatory difficulty is frustration, not distress: a relation between maintained, consequential demands and the incompatibilities, constraints or countervailing processes that limit their realization, independent of strain, the burden of sustaining activity, and distinct from distress, a possible integrated response to either" (CLAIM-368, Active, interpretive, the owner's).

Its sharp edge, in its summary: "it says what to start from, not that a frustrated organization is distressed or feels anything". At U37 of the same exchange the owner corrected A's quantitative turn: "We can do quite a lot with access to an ordering, even if it doesn't give us a true measure." That became:

> "Frustration among an organization's demands is an ordinal fact before it is a quantity: the jointly satisfiable sets of demands form a downward-closed complex whose minimal non-faces are the minimal conflicts, relief is strict enlargement of that complex for the same declared demands, reorganizations that relieve different conflicts are incomparable, and dropping or suppressing a demand is not relief" (CLAIM-347, Active, interpretive, the owner's correction).

The term is "the failure of the demands it maintains to be jointly realizable under its admissible transformations and its governance" (TERM-060, Active). The part account (SYNTHESIS-044, Proposed) gathers these. It separates a perspective's "activation, tolerance, claims and grants" from the ordinal fact, "so a quiet perspective need not be satisfied and raising a threshold is not relief".

### 4.5.2 Frustration is not thwarting

SYNTHESIS-044 declines one reading outright: "That frustration is thwarting: CLAIM-357 and CLAIM-631 give necessary conditions only." The residual and the regret "identify none of them as thwarting" (CLAIM-357, Active, interpretive). A model answers QUESTION-131 (Open) in one setting: "With operative thwarting identified by silencing a process, structural frustration and thwarting are independent in both directions". Thwarting where joint satisfaction is attainable "needs a resolution that weighs authority or preference beyond the demands" (CLAIM-923, Proposed, demonstrated). That last clause is where frustration meets §4.2. A will-organization that weighs authority can thwart a demand that could have been met.

### 4.5.3 Pathology among processes

At U14 of the exchange of 2026-11-24 the owner proposed that processes "absolutely can enterpathological regimes of incompatible behavior requiring intervention and reorganization of commitments to resolve systemic frustration", though "it mightnot make sense to describe these processes as having moral duties to each other precisely". The record holds: "Processes need not owe one another moral duties for a system of them to enter a pathological regime of incompatible behaviour, one that takes intervention and a reorganization of their commitments to resolve its systemic frustration" (CLAIM-tmpe6yfm, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). It does not say "that every pathological regime is resolved by reorganizing commitments; some need resources or new capacities". Its minimal instance is priority inversion (CASE-tmptpna1, Proposed, construction, A16 §4). Article 2 (§2.6) reads that case. Here it shows only that a relief can move a delay onto a third process instead of removing it.

### 4.5.4 What a demand needs in order to move action

CLAIM-523 (Active, interpretive), the owner's synthesis of 2026-10-31, places frustration among the conditions of normative efficacy. "three distinct achievements are needed and any can fail while the others hold: legibility …; volitional efficacy …; and correctability". It adds that "none establishes that the demand or the revision is justified". The three are necessary conditions. No conjunction of them is sufficient for a demand to be justified, and the claim does not make them sufficient for action either.

## 4.6 Appraisal, action readiness and valence

A51's affective sketch has four elements: "frustration, appraisal, action readiness and the difference between a concern's claimed precedence and its practical efficacy". Two of them are Active and the rest are Proposed.

### 4.6.1 What is Active

Action readiness is Frijda's: "a state of preparation for action, not a representation of a desirable state, that claims control over behaviour by persisting until an end state, resuming after interruption, capturing attention and taking priority over other actions" (TERM-094, Active). "A constructionist can use this term", and using it commits a claim to neither half of Frijda's account (TERM-094). The claimed side of CLAIM-283's distinction is this readiness. The granted side is the will-organization's.

### 4.6.2 What is Proposed

- **The motivating perspective.** The owner's U28 of "Organizational Nucleation and SLT": "I'm wondering if the notion of a "motivating perspective" might be a useful construction to make precise". The record's term is "an organizational perspective whose interpretations give circumstances a significance relative to concerns it maintains, and whose significance conditions action readiness, a claim to control that the will-organization may or may not grant" (TERM-104, Proposed).
- **Functional affect.** "Affective states can be properties of organized systems, characterized functionally as Frijda characterizes emotions, without implying subjective experience" (CLAIM-039, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's U19–U20; reopened by the owner on 2026-10-23). "The objection to answer first is THEORY-101's identity of valence with a felt state." CLAIM-432, CLAIM-440 and CLAIM-355 rest on it.
- **Appraisal as negotiation.** The owner, 2026-10-23: "what if the 'appraisal' is the process by which competing perspectives negotiate amongst themselves?" The record holds that an organization's appraisal is that negotiation, and that goal-conduciveness valence is "a regime in which an ordering of the organization's own states by their bearing on its maintenance prevails in the negotiation". It also holds that "ambivalence, where no ordering prevails, differs from neutrality" (CLAIM-440, Proposed, interpretive; rests on CLAIM-347, CLAIM-283 and CLAIM-039; term TERM-076, Proposed).
- **Wanting and liking.** The owner: "I'm not sure if this resolves anything, but I think it least positions the wanting-liking distinction closer to the record's account." The claim is that wanting and liking are "saliences relative to different, overlapping processes", so "valence is then relative to a perspective" (CLAIM-437, Proposed).
- **Emotion as claim, valence as grant.** The assistant's: "an emotion, action readiness claiming control precedence, is a claim, a perspective's valence is read on its claims, and the system's valence is read on what the negotiation grants" (CLAIM-442, Proposed, interpretive; rests on CLAIM-283 and CLAIM-437).

These are not reconciled with each other. The record states the disagreement rather than resolving it: "Whether a perspective's valence stays fixed when grants change. CLAIM-442 says a change in grants with claims fixed "leaves the perspectives' emotions and valences as they were". CLAIM-437 defines a perspective's valence as "its ranking and its progress against its own reference course" … No entry reconciles the two" (SYNTHESIS-020, Proposed, "Not stated on the record"). How perspectives are individuated "without appeal to the dissociations they explain" is CLAIM-437's second defeater, unanswered (ibid.).

### 4.6.3 Reading which ordering prevails

A51 closes this movement with "The record's work on potentials", which "supplies a way to distinguish circumstances in which a unified evaluative representation is available from circumstances in which one cannot simply be presumed". The formal results are Active and demonstrated:

- Some function rises with every winning move "exactly when no chain of winning moves returns to where it began" (CLAIM-434).
- "admitting an ordering is strictly weaker than admitting a currency": "the lexicographic order on the unit square has no real-valued representation" (CLAIM-436).

An interpretive guard is Active too, and it is an objection to CLAIM-440. "where an organization's legibility regime selects which contests are admitted or recorded, an acyclic record, even a persistent potential, can be the product of selective contestation, so observed coherence is not evidence that the organization's perspectives are integrated" (CLAIM-448, Active, interpretive, counter).

**Already done, in part.** One record of conduct has been read by QUESTION-268's procedure (Open): the FOMC, 1966–2026 (CASE-330, Proposed, executed study, the assistant's). ## 4.7 Corporate affect and institutional remorse

A51 §3 corrects its own earlier suggestion. "SYNTHESIS-018 and CLAIM-432 already distinguish harm that becomes an organizational concern from harm that becomes consequential only as an external threat." A51 assigns the argument to "the center of the institutional-sociopathy article", which is A51's Article 12. This article states its standing, which A51 does not.

**The thesis.** The thesis is the record's reading of Bakan (LIT-1430, unread). It was "filed at the owner's request on 2026-10-23 and revised the same day when the owner pointed out that the record's framework assigns affect to corporation-like things". A corporation "lacks remorse, functionally, when harm it does to others is not among the concerns its own appraisals track and gains precedence over what it does only when a regulator or a market turns it into a threat to the corporation's concerns". That profile, "threat without remorse", "is changed by redesigning the corporation's concerns, not its members' characters" (CLAIM-432, Proposed, interpretive; rests on CLAIM-039 and CLAIM-271). Its documented case is Wells Fargo's Community Bank, 2002–2016 (CASE-151, Active, specimen). In that case "signals of the harm reached people who could not act on them".

**The objection, unanswered.** "A corporation's functional remorse cannot be told from prudence: any procedure or role duty that makes others' harms its concern was put there for its own ends, reputation, liability or recruitment, so the line between harm as concern and harm as threat … does not hold, and the clinical word adds nothing to a description of its incentives" (CLAIM-431, Proposed, interpretive, counter). SYNTHESIS-018 lists among what it does not say: "That the remorse–threat line is drawable for a corporation: CLAIM-431 is unanswered".

**Two readings of the one case.** The record holds two readings of Wells Fargo, and it does not choose between them. This article states both.

- *Threat without remorse (CLAIM-432).* Customer harm was "narrowly construed to mean only financial harm such as fees and penalties". The 2004 investigation's recommendation to cut goals "was not escalated". The model was abandoned only after the regulators' settlements of September 2016 and the press. On this reading, harm reached the bank only as a threat.
- *Remorse claimed and not granted (CLAIM-442).* This reading puts an emotion on the claim side: "The claim is the emotion whether or not it is granted". If a unit's appraisal under a role duty is the corporation's own, "as CLAIM-432's part 3 allows", then "CASE-151's 2004 investigation is functional remorse claimed and not granted, where CLAIM-432 reads the bank as without remorse" (CLAIM-442, note of 2026-11-11).

"The two disagree about the case on that condition", and "neither entry settles whether such an appraisal is the corporation's" (ibid.; CLAIM-432's note of the same date states it from its side). The line's account declines to integrate them: "A contradiction between components is not an integration, so nothing is filed" (SYNTHESIS-021, Proposed). A further condition narrows CLAIM-432's own reading. Under CLAIM-440's admission condition, CASE-151's cross-sell potential "is shown only as the admitted negotiation's, not as the bank's" (CLAIM-432, note of 2026-11-11).

**The test, run and inconclusive.** LEAD-101 (Open; run on 2026-11-23 by a delegated lead worker, CASE-405, Proposed) asked whether corporations changed only by sanction keep weighing others' harms once the sanction lifts. Its answer was "**No case meets the lead's standard**", so the defeater is "neither met nor answered" (CLAIM-432, note of 2026-11-23). The run's other findings:

- A monitor's effect on violations lasts only while it is on site. That fits CLAIM-432, and "the prudence objection (CLAIM-431) predicts the same, so it is no evidence between them".
- The board-change result is filed as two alternatives. On one, it is evidence for the redesign half (CLAIM-1178, Proposed). On the other, it is "no evidence that redesigned concerns outlast sanction" (CLAIM-1113, Proposed, counter), and the run leans to this one.
- Signals without precedence appear inside a firm coded as redesigned, which makes "redesigned" "a matter of degree, not an arm a firm is in or out of".
- The Federal Reserve lifted Wells Fargo's growth restriction on 2025-06-03, so "Part 5's test can be run on CASE-151 once a window has passed".

SYNTHESIS-018 dates from 2026-11-10. It still lists part 5's test as unrun. Its next review should take in the run of 2026-11-23.

A51's "Actual remaining work" names "A philosophical argument concerning functional affect … particularly the objection concerning felt valence recorded in SYNTHESIS-018". That objection is THEORY-101. The record holds no answer to it ("An answer to THEORY-101's identification of valence with a felt state, which holds CLAIM-039 and with it CLAIM-440 and CLAIM-432", SYNTHESIS-021, "Not stated on the record"). The answer is open work, not a reported result.

## 4.8 Learned accommodation and its limits

### 4.8.1 The owner's thesis, in conditional form

At U45 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 the owner wrote: "processes which can navigate disagreement amongst themselves require fewer resources from the system than processes which need to activate additional subsystems to resolve disagreement". At the owner's review of the filing, A45's conditional form was adopted:

> "Learned local accommodation can reduce the amortized cost of recurrent arbitration: where a recurring conflict among an agent's processes admits a stable accommodation, settling it among the processes can cost less over repeated encounters than escalating each instance to a higher-level process that mediates or overrides, though local negotiation and the relations it needs are not free" (CLAIM-358, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's).

The claim says "can", and its antecedent is a stable accommodation. It does not say "That local settlement is always cheaper". A49's §5 says such accommodations "are shown to provide" this. That overstates the claim: it is argued, not shown (curation entry of 2026-11-24 20:56, Art. 4 standing). A51's abstract says the claim correctly ("can reduce").

Two Active guards keep the thesis from proving too much:

- The owner's Hebbian prior is "a candidate mechanism for durable authority and for amortized governance; but persistence is not amortization, which is shown only when earlier work replaces later computation, communication or recruitment" (CLAIM-443, Active, interpretive; the owner's question, "what if we just treat this as a kind of momentum term? or like a hebbian prior?").
- The routes by which a divided agent acts as one, "arbitration, a change in which interest may act, coinciding valuations, local accommodation", "are distinct and differently costly" (SYNTHESIS-037, Proposed).

**Not done.** No executed study prices CLAIM-358, and no lead holds the study. The study that bears nearest bears against reading persistence as amortization. In the self-protecting sentry, "under success feedback its update is the habit of protecting, and a habit of matched memory reproduces the responses to belief correction, authority reset and restored evidence" (CASE-346, Active, executed study; rerun by another session). *Expectation, stated before any run, with no lead.* Take two populations of processes that meet one recurring conflict with a stable accommodation: one settles it locally and one escalates each instance. If CLAIM-358 holds as conditioned, the local population's cumulative cost should fall below the escalating one's after some number of encounters, the break-even, that depends on the cost of forming the relation. In a matched population whose recurring conflict has no stable accommodation, the ordering should not appear. CLAIM-443's guard adds a necessary check. The saving has to show up as later arbitration work that is not done. A persisting allocation that does the same work again would not count. This expectation is the drafter's. It is a suggestion for a lead, not a result.

### 4.8.2 Low conflict does not show adequate accommodation

A51's abstract: "low conflict and fluent performance do not establish the adequacy of accommodation". Several entries hold this; the restatement is JR4.1 below.

- "Processes conditioned never to object coordinate without override at the same behaviour and supervisory cost as processes that negotiate, so the two part only under intervention, when a constituent dissents, or in their history" (CLAIM-364, Proposed, interpretive, counter; A45's). Its summary quotes A45: "A quiet subordinate process isn't necessarily a satisfied one."
- "observed coherence is not evidence that the organization's perspectives are integrated" (CLAIM-448, Active, §4.6.3).
- A quiet perspective is not a satisfied one (CLAIM-549, Active, §4.4).
- SYNTHESIS-037 does not say "That low supervisory cost shows non-domination".

**Already done, in part.** Intervention separates registered from unregistered evaluation in a stipulated model, and endorsement only weakly (CASE-355, §4.6.3). A record that is not stipulated has not been read.

### 4.8.3 Regimes that outlast their reasons

Three Proposed entries describe allocations of authority that persist past the conditions that made them fit.

- **Anxiety.** The owner, 2026-10-23: "I think you may have inadvertently landed on a formalization of anxiety". The claim is that "Some persistent anxiety may be governance-maintained rather than belief-maintained", and "the regime can outlast a corrected belief" (CLAIM-446, Proposed, interpretive). Its model is CASE-346 (Active). Under restriction "no run leaves protection in 400 safe steps". But only "interventions on the evaluation, an independent audit or authority rewarded on independent evidence, tell the two apart" from a habit (CLAIM-924, Proposed, empirical, the assistant's). CLAIM-446's evidence "is model-level and adjacent; nothing here measures authority in patients".
- **Emergency regimes.** "Recruiting an emergency regime, maintaining it and dissolving it are separable capacities", and where entry is cheaper than exit "the regime can outlast its justification" (CLAIM-tmp0gs0t, Proposed, interpretive; A34, A36).
- **Governance heuristics.** A's answer to the owner's U11 and U12: "Beside heuristics that select what to do there are governance heuristics, learned priors over which perspective or procedure determines what to do; intuitive expertise and entrenched bias can then be one learned allocation of authority" (CLAIM-tmpn754o, Proposed, interpretive; rests on CLAIM-443). A16 restates it as "a compiled contract", which "adds only that the allocation is a protocol among processes" (CLAIM-tmpysg95, Proposed). A16 offers both "rather than established explanations of every instance". The owner's U10 ("apply our framework to model/explain myriad cognitive biases and empirically observed heuristics") and U11 ("where does organizational affect figure in this picture?") also led to CLAIM-tmp5atd3 (Proposed, A's; not a member): several biases can be "observable marks of one organizational constraint", and "the thesis earns standing only if one intervention on such a constraint, measured independently and with the information held fixed, moves several bias measures together". Its premises are "mostly Proposed", and its rival is Hilbert's noisy processing.

**Not done.** The bias co-variation study is LEAD-tmpr4xwc (Open), the assistant's design. Its expectation is CLAIM-tmp5atd3's defeater: the claim is defeated if the bias measures move one at a time, or no more together than a noise-based rival predicts. The test that tells a governance heuristic from an action heuristic is in CLAIM-tmpn754o's note of 2026-11-24, the assistant's, and no lead holds it. ## 4.11 Misunderstanding as an occasion of relief

This section is a join (J4.1). No entry relates misunderstanding to frustration, and the article marks the step as its own.

The owner's sidebar of 2026-11-24 was: "'productive misunderstanding' - identification and clarification of ambiguity as detection of and reorganization around novel consequential distinctions". The record holds: "A misunderstanding is productive when identifying and clarifying the ambiguity it exposes is the detection of, and a reorganization around, a novel distinction that has become consequential" (CLAIM-tmpupcws, Proposed, interpretive, the owner's). The claim "names misunderstanding as an occasion" of TERM-099's self-formation. The assistant qualified it: a misunderstanding "dates the distinction's relevance rather than its existence", and clarifying "can coarsen as well as refine" (CLAIM-tmpm76n5, Proposed). The assistant also objected: "A misunderstanding can be productive because it is left unresolved" (CLAIM-tmpgx2ej, Proposed, counter). Article 2 (§2.7) places the sidebar among the kinds of ambiguous invocation.

The record's construction of self-formation driven by frustration is CASE-191 (Active, construction, the assistant's). There, "an agent's own frustration at two demands one shared output cannot meet drives production of a sensor and a differentiating channel". Below a threshold, "smaller discrepancies are tolerated, at a standing frustration, and form nothing". CLAIM-532 (Active, interpretive) says that an unresolved discrepancy is "an occasion for self-formation … and not a guarantee of it".

**The join.** Read through CLAIM-347, a misunderstanding that has become consequential is a frustrated pair. Each party's reading carries a demand, and the case that first gives the readings different consequences is one in which the demands cannot be jointly met. Clarifying then does one of three things.

- It can **refine**: it forms a distinction under which each reading governs where it applies. Then the same declared demands become jointly satisfiable, which is relief in CLAIM-347's sense. This is the route of CASE-191's differentiating channel.
- It can **settle for one reading**. That drops the other demand, which by CLAIM-347 "is not relief", however much quieter the relation becomes (CLAIM-549).
- It can **merge readings found to agree** on every case that matters (CLAIM-tmpm76n5's coarsening). That removes no satisfiable face and adds none, so the complex is unchanged.

CLAIM-tmpgx2ej's kept ambiguity, where no case met separates the readings, is not yet a frustration at all. On this reading, a misunderstanding is productive in the owner's sense exactly when its clarification is a relief, and not merely a quieting.

**Limits of the join.** CASE-191 is one stipulated agent, and the owner's claim is about a dyad or an organization. Reading each interpretation as a maintained demand is the join's own step, and no entry holds it. CLAIM-532 makes a discrepancy an occasion, and states a sufficient condition for self-formation in the agent's own activity producing a maintained capacity, not in the discrepancy. Neither it nor this join makes frustration sufficient for a productive misunderstanding, or for self-formation. Whether imposed clarification can still be "productive" is open (CLAIM-tmpro73c; CLAIM-tmpisfi1, Proposed, which Article 2 cites).

## 4.12 What the article claims, and with what standing

1. Precedence, jurisdiction and suspension are distinct relations of practical authority, and claimed precedence differs from granted authority (TERM-017, TERM-023, CLAIM-027, CLAIM-283; Active). Activation, grant and efficacy are separate quantities (the owner's U18; CLAIM-548, Active).
2. In one stipulated organization, a will-organization model beats five control-only models by more than its Occam cost and alone predicts unseen combinations of changes in authority. It loses where authority is idle or idiosyncratic. A wrongly composed one wins on training evidence and loses on transfer (CASE-325, Active, unchecked by a second session; CLAIM-889, Proposed, demonstrated). For an organization, institutional theory already gives the difference. What would be new is one description across realizers, which is untested (CLAIM-890, Proposed).
3. Forbidding self-grants does not stop recursive amplification, and the rules on record differ in what they guarantee (CLAIM-282, CLAIM-375, CLAIM-502; Active, demonstrated).
4. A tolerance is a directed criterion for conferral, necessary where it binds and sufficient only for a grant under a granting rule. A quiet perspective is not a satisfied one, and raising a threshold is not relief (the owner's U12, U19; CLAIM-552, CLAIM-550, CLAIM-549; Active).
5. Frustration, ordinal and not distress, is the primitive, and dropping a demand is not relief (the owner's U33, U37; CLAIM-368, CLAIM-347; Active). Frustration is not thwarting (SYNTHESIS-044; CLAIM-923, Proposed).
6. Organizational affect, characterized functionally, is situated at the claim–grant distinction. Every entry that states it is Proposed (CLAIM-039, CLAIM-440, CLAIM-437, CLAIM-442), THEORY-101's objection is unanswered, and CLAIM-442 and CLAIM-437 are unreconciled.
7. A corporation can be read as lacking remorse when others' harm reaches it only as threat (CLAIM-432, Proposed). The objection that this cannot be told from prudence is unanswered (CLAIM-431). The record's two readings of Wells Fargo disagree (CLAIM-432, CLAIM-442), and the test on open sources was inconclusive (CASE-405).
8. Learned accommodation can lower arbitration cost where a stable accommodation exists (the owner's U45; CLAIM-358, Proposed, argued, unpriced). Low conflict and fluent coordination do not show it is adequate (CLAIM-364, Proposed; CLAIM-448, Active).
9. Regimes of authority can outlast what justified them (CLAIM-446, CLAIM-tmp0gs0t, CLAIM-tmpn754o; Proposed). In the one model run, the authority state cannot be told from a habit except by intervening on the evaluation (CASE-346, Active; CLAIM-924, Proposed).
