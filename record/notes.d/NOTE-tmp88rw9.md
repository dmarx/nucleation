# NOTE-tmp88rw9: Żuradzki (2026) — Ethics-first metaphysics: normative considerations as evidence for ontological commitments

## Contribution

A methodological claim for bioethics: ontological disputes about pregnancy and early human life should be informed by ethical reasoning rather than insulated from it, with two named routes by which normative judgment can bear on ontology.

## Key results

- "Although no particular ontology of pregnancy entails a specific position on abortion or other ethical questions, different views constrain which analogies can plausibly support ethical arguments."
- "where biological evidence underdetermines competing ontologies, considered normative judgments may themselves constitute evidence for choosing between them."
- The two mechanisms: normative considerations "may play a weighting role by influencing which biological features are regarded as metaphysically salient" and "may play a tie-breaking role by providing additional reasons for preferring one framework over another."
- On Lee and Colgrove: "Both authors appeal to essentially the same embryological evidence. Their disagreement concerns which biological features should be regarded as metaphysically salient."
- Conclusion: normative considerations "may legitimately contribute to the justification of ontological commitments in two distinct ways".

## Assumptions

- That biological evidence and standard theoretical virtues do underdetermine the ontologies in question.
- Dasgupta's premise that judgments about harm and wrongdoing can be evidence about essences.

## Concepts

- **Ethics-first metaphysics** (after Dasgupta): considered normative judgments serve as evidence for choosing between metaphysical hypotheses, reversing the "metaphysics-first" order.
- **Weighting** and **tie-breaking**: the two roles normative considerations can play.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** The LIT's title and its place in the commentary cluster on LIT-tmph5uj8 are right. Its stance differs from Dranseika's (LIT-tmpoeqhr): Dranseika allows a commitment to guide the choice of mereology but denies that the resulting fit is independent evidence for it, whereas Żuradzki counts normative judgments as evidence for the ontology, where the biology underdetermines it.

- **THEORY-448**: qualifies. That account takes the metaphysics to constrain the ethics. Żuradzki argues the direction can also run from ethics to metaphysics where the biology underdetermines, so the choice between parthood and containment may itself be partly ethical.
- **THEORY-464**: qualifies, in the same way. It holds that the metaphysics of persistence constrains ethical questions without settling them; Żuradzki, following Dasgupta, takes judgments of prudence and responsibility to guide theorizing about persistence conditions.
- **THEORY-tmp1w39g** (Dranseika): contrasts. Both commentaries let normative commitments shape the choice of mereology; they disagree on whether the fit then counts as evidence.
