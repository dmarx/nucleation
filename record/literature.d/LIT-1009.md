# LIT-1009: Sense of agency in the human brain

Haggard, P. (2017). Sense of agency in the human brain. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 18(4), 196–207. DOI 10.1038/nrn.2017.14.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp7w12y](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7w12y.md).*

- **A narrow construct.** The sense of agency is "the experience of
  controlling one's own motor acts and, through them, the course of
  external events". It is not self-efficacy, and it is not the social
  attribution of an action to an agent. Involuntary movement brings
  ownership without agency.
- **Two kinds of measure.** Explicit judgements overestimate one's own
  agency, more so for good outcomes. Implicit measures, intentional binding
  and interval estimates, correlate only weakly with them. Binding alone is
  not diagnostic, only differences in it between conditions.
- **Prospective as well as retrospective.** On trials where an expected
  tone did not come, action binding was stronger when the tone had been
  likely. Ease of choosing an action raised the sense of control over an
  outcome it did not predict. In schizophrenia, binding depended only on
  whether the tone actually came.
- **Against a single comparator.** The comparator model explains the
  feeling of non-agency but produces no signal for the positive
  experience. The review proposes frontal–parietal connectivity as the
  correlate.
- **Coercion.** Acting on an experimenter's instruction lengthened
  perceived action–outcome intervals and reduced the auditory N1 to the
  outcome, compared with free choice of the same act.
