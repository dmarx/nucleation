# THEORY-410: Brain–computer interface decoding does not show intentions to be causes distinct from their neural realisers: what is many-to-one realised there is a coarse cortical variable over single-neuron patterns, and each intention matches one value of it, so under proportional difference-making the evidence fits identifying the intention with that variable

## Source

Pernu (2018), [LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md) (*Synthese* 195(12), 5159–5174), read in full in
[NOTE-091](../notes.d/NOTE-091.md).

## What was actually shown

**The framework.** C causes E iff C □→ E and ∼C □→ ∼E (p. 5161). Under
multiple realisation, the neural realiser N fails the second condition and
the mental property M meets it, so M and not N is the difference-maker
(p. 5162). That is the List and Menzies result this account presupposes
([THEORY-133](THEORY-133.md)).

**The argument it targets (§4, pp. 5164–5165).** Woodward (2008a) and others
read brain–computer interface work as showing an intention realised by
different neural patterns on different occasions, so citing any one pattern
is "overly specific".

**The diagnosis (§§4–5).** The many patterns are single-neuron realisers of
one cortical-level value N, "the average firing rates of neurons in the
relevant ensembles" (pp. 5167–5168). They do not realise the intention
directly (Fig. 2a). The argument needs an intention realised by distinct
cortical states N and Z (Fig. 2b), and neuroprosthetics does not present
that. The redundancy is micro over macro within the physical, like
microstates under a thermodynamic macrostate.

**The consequence (pp. 5168–5171).** With N read as the cortical variable,
its counterfactual ∼N □→ ∼B holds whenever the mental one does, so the
contrast the argument needs collapses. The mental is "not an epiphenomenon",
but only "because the mental has now been identified with the physical"
(p. 5168). Preferring coarse over fine causes is compatible with physicalism,
since "physicalism is not microphysicalism" (p. 5171).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The decoding evidence could have
shown one intention realised by two distinct ensemble-level states. On
Pernu's reading it does not, but that reading is asserted, not shown.

## What this does not say

- **That intentions are causally idle.** The conclusion is that they are causes
  because they are identical to cortical states: the deflationary defence of
  mental causation, not the substantial one (§6).
- **That the empirical premise is established.** That one intention equals one
  ensemble-average value rests on the research agenda and a quotation from
  Musallam et al. (2004). Decoders are not simple averages, and the mapping is
  not checked across sessions, electrodes or subjects ([NOTE-091](../notes.d/NOTE-091.md), C3: weak).
- **That interventionism in general fails.** Footnote 1 confines the argument
  to difference-making with proportionality. Replies that do without
  proportionality "are not affected".
- **That cross-species multiple realisability fails.** That monkey-to-human
  transfer tells against it is one sentence (p. 5170).
- **That higher-level causes are never autonomous.** It concerns this evidence
  for this case. [THEORY-133](THEORY-133.md)'s criterion stays system by system.

## Connections

- **Presupposes [THEORY-133](THEORY-133.md).** It takes List and Menzies's criterion as given
  and argues that the brain–computer interface case fails it. [THEORY-133](THEORY-133.md)'s
  summary already notes that Pernu disputes its main example.
- **[QUESTION-033](../questions.d/QUESTION-033.md)** asks whether "who drives?" has an answer at a given level.
  This constrains what can count as an answer: many-to-one realisation shows
  that some coarse variable drives, and that variable may be physical, not the
  named higher level. Evidence of degeneracy alone does not put the drive at
  the mental level.
- [THEORY-048](THEORY-048.md) and [THEORY-057](THEORY-057.md) already cite this paper for the same caution
  about inferring a distinct higher-level cause from many-to-one realisation.
