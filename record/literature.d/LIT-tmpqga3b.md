# LIT-tmpqga3b: Nature’s Queer Performativity

Karen Barad (2012). Nature's Queer Performativity (the authorized version).
*Kvinder, Køn & Forskning* no. 1–2, pp. 25–53. DOI 10.7146/kkf.v0i1-2.28067.
An earlier version appeared in *Qui Parle* 19(2) (2011), pp. 121–158.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpynmwh.*

- **The agential cut, in Barad's words.** "A specific intra-action enacts
  an agential cut (in contrast to the Cartesian cut – an inherent
  distinction – between subject and object) effecting a separation between
  'subject' and 'object'. That is, the agential cut enacts a 'local'
  resolution within the phenomenon of the inherent ontological
  indeterminacy" (p. 32). What results is "agential separability – the
  local condition of exteriority-within-phenomena".
- **Phenomena, not things.** What we take for individual entities are
  "(entangled 'parts of') phenomena", and phenomena are "ontologically
  primitive relations – relations without preexisting relata" (p. 32,
  note 28).
- **Cuts are not human.** "Whatever a 'cut' is must not assume a prior
  notion of the 'human'" (p. 31); the analysis must cover differentiating
  done by nonhumans too.
- **The physics is Bohr's, as she reads him.** The which-path and
  quantum-eraser experiments are offered as "direct empirical evidence that
  identity is not fixed and inherent, but performative" (p. 43), on Bohr's
  view that the referent is the phenomenon, the inseparability of object
  and apparatus (p. 42).
- **The ethical claim.** There are no "acts against nature", only "acts of
  nature" (p. 47); accountability is for "how different cuts matter".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-30 as a first-hand reading of Barad for TERM-087, whose
third sense of "cut" had rested on second-hand accounts. It states the
agential cut and its contrast with the Cartesian cut in her own words. It
does not define an apparatus, does not say that cuts are movable, and does
not mention Heisenberg; those parts of TERM-087 still rest on Faye and
Jaksland's quotations from Barad 2007 (LIT-1657). It carries no instruction
for machine-learning practice.
