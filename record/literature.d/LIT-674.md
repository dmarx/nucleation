# LIT-674: Going Beyond Linear Mode Connectivity: The Layerwise Linear Feature Connectivity

Zhanpeng Zhou, Yongyi Yang, Xiaojiang Yang, Junchi Yan and Wei Hu (2023),
37th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2023) —
[ARXIV-2307.08286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08286)

## Key takeaways

- **LLFC co-occurs with LMC.** For pairs that are linearly mode connected,
  1 − cos between f⁽ℓ⁾(αθ_A + (1 − α)θ_B) and αf⁽ℓ⁾(θ_A) + (1 − α)f⁽ℓ⁾(θ_B)
  is near zero in nearly every layer, for α ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}, across an
  MLP on MNIST and CIFAR-10, VGG-16 and ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10, and
  ResNet-50 on Tiny-ImageNet (Figs. 2–3). It is far from zero for
  independently trained pairs (Fig. 4).
- **LLFC is strictly stronger than LMC.** Applied to the output layer, it
  gives an error at most 2ε on the whole linear path if both endpoints have
  error at most ε (Lemma 1).
- **Two conditions imply exact LLFC.** One is weak additivity:
  ReLU(αH̃_A + (1 − α)H̃_B) = αReLU(H̃_A) + (1 − α)ReLU(H̃_B) on the two
  networks' pre-activations. The other is commutativity:
  W_A H_A + W_B H_B = W_A H_B + W_B H_A in every layer. Together they give
  LLFC with scale c = 1 (Theorem 1). Both hold approximately, and far better
  for linearly connected pairs than for independent ones (Figs. 5–7).
- **Git Re-Basin, re-read.** Commutativity can be rewritten as
  (W_A − PW_BPᵀ)(H_A − PH_B) = 0. Weight matching minimises the first
  factor and activation matching the second (§5.2). Enforcing the product
  directly is a quadratic assignment problem (Appendix A.3).
- **Why matching fails for narrow or early networks.** The appendix argues
  that low-rank weights and features make commutativity easy to satisfy,
  and that narrow or barely trained networks lack that low rank (Appendix
  B.5, Figs. 20–22). This is an argument, not a theorem.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 with the owner's mode-connectivity batch ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). It
is the batch's reading of linear mode connectivity from inside the network.
When the path between two minima is flat, the network along it behaves like
a linear blend of the two networks layer by layer, so averaging weights is
roughly averaging features.

It extends two foundations held here. From Frankle et al. ([LIT-654](LIT-654.md)) it
takes the spawning method, training a network for a few epochs and then
splitting it into two runs, with their training recipes, as the first of its
two ways to get linearly connected pairs. From Git Re-Basin ([LIT-661](LIT-661.md))
it takes the permutation method and the 32×-wide LayerNorm ResNet-20
checkpoints, reused directly. It then gives Git Re-Basin's weight and
activation matching objectives a reason to work: each makes one factor of
its commutativity residual small.

Its related work credits Lubana et al. ([LIT-669](LIT-669.md)) with showing that
mechanistically dissimilar models cannot be linearly connected. LLFC is
the positive counterpart: linearly connected models carry the same features
up to a linear blend. The anthology's model soups ([ANTH-LIT-675](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-675.md)) and SWA
([ANTH-LIT-673](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-673.md)) average weights within a basin; this paper is a candidate
explanation of why that averages features too, but it measures
interpolation between two endpoints, not those methods.

No instruction for practice. It is flagged for the anthology because the
anthology holds the permutation and averaging lines it explains.
