# LIT-tmp0qei4: Health and Disease Concepts Cannot Be Grounded in Social Justice Alone

Walter Veit (2025), *Croatian Journal of Philosophy 25(73) (online 2025-04-25); preprint on PhilSci-Archive (item 24933)* — DOI-10.52685/cjp.25.73.7

## Key takeaways

- Kukla's proposal to define health and disease purely by what medicalising would do for social justice defeats itself. Leaving justice undefined turns the concepts into a moral battleground, and without a naturalist or social-constructivist anchor, "medicine" collapses into "whatever promotes justice", which is no definition at all.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Kukla (2014) argued that attempts to define health should drop naturalist and social-constructivist considerations, which fail to capture the concepts' normativity, and define health and disease purely in terms of social justice. Veit argues that this purely normativist project is self-defeating. Health and disease therefore cannot be defined by appeal to social justice alone, although justice may still properly bear on medical decisions.
