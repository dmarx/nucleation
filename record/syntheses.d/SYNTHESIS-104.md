# SYNTHESIS-104: Accountability without blame and normative governability: blame and accountability are proposed to come apart, so that correction does not wait on culpability and an organization may owe repair where no one is to blame, blame traced upstream supplies the provenance of an incapacity, an incapacity an institution brought about in breach of a duty does not excuse it, blame and repair do not substitute for each other, a complete record of who approved an outcome does not settle whether it can fairly be attributed to them, and an institution responsive within its governing mechanism need not be responsive to reasons for revising it

*A part account of the organizational-normativity line, on accountability without blame and on normative governability. It was filed at the interim synthesis review of 2026-11-17, over ten components from the continuation's filing of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (turns U1 to U48, filed 2026-11-17). Version 2, at the follow-up review of 2026-11-18, integrates four entries the first share's filing made (turns U1 to U17, pull request 510, filed 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16), which were in no account, and folds in the readings of the exchange's works (pull request 514). The first filing's entries here come from A's turns and from reviews of that filing which the owner forwarded on 2026-11-16, whose voice is ChatGPT's (the first filing's curation entry, "Review of 2026-11-16"). None of the nine entries reconciled into the first filing's codes is in this part; they are in dependence-and-domination's parts. Most components also carry distributed-agency. Its sibling on rights, demands and commitments is SYNTHESIS-102; the organizational wrong beyond harm and CLAIM-624's duty, which several components here cite, are mapped in SYNTHESIS-094.*

## The account

Each link is stated by the entry named first, in its front matter unless
its body is cited. Every component is Proposed, or Open for a question.
Readings are not components: each is cited as the support a component
has, by the THEORY that carries the relation and the NOTE that reads the
work, or from the component's dated note where the reading qualifies it
without a relation. Every reading named is Proposed; none changed a
status.

**Repair without blame, the question.** QUESTION-470 (A's, from A7 §2 of
the first share, "Someone can owe another person recognition, sympathy,
or efforts at repair without deserving blame for what happened", put as a
question for organizations by the third review of 2026-11-16, ChatGPT's,
forwarded by the owner; filed by the first filing; Open) stores no
relation: under what conditions does an organization's responsibility to
repair a harm survive the absence, uncertainty or dispersion of
blameworthiness among those who contributed, so that causal
responsibility, culpability, accountability and remedial responsibility
come apart? Its body names CLAIM-708, CLAIM-624, CLAIM-671, CLAIM-707
and QUESTION-068 as neighbours, not answers, and Young (LIT-2246,
Deferred) and Williams (LIT-2714, Unfetched; read through Nelkin,
THEORY-1942) as readings, and says an answer would be a principle "from
benefit, from control over the arrangement that produced the harm, or
from capacity to repair", or an argument that the four never come apart.
CLAIM-742, CLAIM-733 and CLAIM-717 bear on it, and QUESTION-476 refines
it (below). No reading carries a relation to it.

**Blame is not accountability.** CLAIM-742 (the owner's, U47; A110's
four questions, usage note, amendment of "unintentionally" to "unwitting"
and three assessments are A's and credited in its body; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; also distributed-agency) uses TERM-127 and bears
on QUESTION-068 and QUESTION-470: an agent's diminished culpability for
the reasons it acts on "does not diminish the wrongfulness of what it
does, the claims of those it harms, or the need for correction, because
the grounds of correction are not exhausted by the grounds of blame". Its
body reads "accountable" in TERM-127's remedial sense, says the claim
leaves open who owes the correction, and says that if the grounds of
correction are not exhausted by those of blame, "the need for correction
can survive diminished culpability, which is one condition" QUESTION-470
asks for.

- *Readings.* Its dated note: Shoemaker 2019, read in part as the
  stand-in for his 2011 paper (LIT-2777; NOTE-2048), confirms the claim's
  correction of A110, that the three-way split of attributability,
  answerability and accountability is Shoemaker's, not Watson's and
  Shoemaker's jointly; King 2013 for Watson (LIT-2718; NOTE-2053) gives
  Watson's accountability a fairness condition, which supports reading
  the owner's "accountable" as not Watson's face. No THEORY carries a
  relation to the claim.

TERM-127 (A's senses, A110 §2 with A5 §5, A9 §5 and A17 §9; the
distinction it keeps is the owner's U47; Proposed; also
distributed-agency) draws on THEORY-147, THEORY-207 and THEORY-1915:
causal, attributability, culpability, remedial and constitutive
responsibility, "a verdict in one sense does not settle another". Its
body says CLAIM-742, CLAIM-733, CLAIM-714 and CLAIM-717 are stated in it.
Six claims of the exchange's first parts, in dependence-and-domination,
also use it.

- *Readings.* Zeiser (THEORY-1915, NOTE-2007) is now a source it draws
  on, for a narrower attributability sense (whose value-judgements a
  decision expresses), and bears out its last clause: "the right person
  to blame didn't actually make the decision". Its dated notes correct it
  from three neighbours: Miller's article is registered (LIT-2755,
  Unavailable), and his 2007 statement, read through Shaw-Young
  (THEORY-1958, NOTE-2064), names six connections, one of them, outcome
  responsibility, with no slot among the term's senses; King's Watson
  (THEORY-1987) has a thicker attributability; and Shoemaker's
  answerability (THEORY-1974) is a type of responsibility the term does
  not list, and not TERM-126's directed answerability. Rubel, Castro and
  Pham (THEORY-1946) give a neighbouring taxonomy, not a source.

**Blame as provenance.** CLAIM-733 (the owner's idea, U47; the label "the
provenance of normative incapacity" and the development are A110 §3's;
its bearing on QUESTION-449 is A's, narrowed by the filer; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; also distributed-agency) rests on CLAIM-742,
uses TERM-127 and TERM-100, and bears on QUESTION-449, QUESTION-071 and
QUESTION-470: responsibility for a harmful act and for producing the
incapacity through which it was done "can come apart and attach to
different actors". Its body says it does not answer QUESTION-449, which
asks whose an organization's counter-normativity is. CASE-291 (A's, A110
§11 Case A, with A110 §7's negligent variant; fictional, Proposed)
illustrates CLAIM-733 and CLAIM-714 and bears on QUESTION-449: two
officials make the same wrongful decision in good faith, and in one
institution a superior removed the review that would have caught it. Its
body says it cannot show that the removal breached a duty, which needs
CLAIM-624 and ARG-114, and that both arms are internal to one
institution, so it does not reach QUESTION-449's own case. No reading
bears on either.

**Self-induced incapacity.** CLAIM-714 (A's, A108 §5 and A110 §7;
thesis, interpretive, Proposed) rests on CLAIM-733, uses TERM-127 and
bears on QUESTION-449: a present incapacity to recognize a violation does
not show the party is not responsible for the incapacity; where it
dismantled the capability in breach of a duty, the incapacity "does not
excuse it", and where no one is culpable "the duty to repair still
stands". Its body says whether the dismantling breached a duty needs
CLAIM-624's maintenance duty, argued in ARG-114, and that it does not
answer QUESTION-449, which is about mechanisms installed by another. No
reading bears on it.

**Blame and repair do not substitute.** CLAIM-717 (A's, A110 §8; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; also distributed-agency) rests on CLAIM-742,
uses TERM-127 and bears on QUESTION-470: assigning blame for a harmful
condition does not discharge present remedial duties, and assigning
remedial duties does not show blame, so treating one guilty official as a
repair is "blame displacement" and loading repair on whoever is nearest
is "remedial scapegoating". Its body names CASE-151 as a documented
instance of blame displacement, CLAIM-707's moral crumple zone as the
mechanism of scapegoating, and QUESTION-476 as the question it bears on
most directly.

- *Readings.* Shaw-Young's report of Miller (THEORY-1958, NOTE-2064,
  skimmed; a weak stand-in for Miller 2001, LIT-2755) corroborates its
  second half; the claim does not rest on it. Miller assigns remedial
  responsibility through six connections, and capacity alone suffices:
  "if I am the only person walking along a river bank when a child falls
  in, then it is my responsibility to rescue the child". The dated note
  says the reading "says nothing about the first half, blame
  displacement".

CASE-284 (A's, A110 §11 Case B; fictional, Proposed; also
distributed-agency) illustrates CLAIM-717 and CLAIM-742: the same wrong
through the same procedure with blameless decision-makers, where one
institution can remedy it and the other cannot but a public authority
can. Its body says it builds only the second half of CLAIM-717, and that
whether jurisdiction grounds a duty rather than a permission is a further
premise. Its dated note: the capacity ground it called unread is now on
the record in Miller's words, as reported (THEORY-1958), and as quoted
capacity grounds a duty when the agent is "uniquely in this position";
whether a public authority with jurisdiction is so placed "the reading
does not settle. The premise stays a premise."

**Blameless culture.** QUESTION-476 (the owner's suggestion, U48, the
exchange's last turn, unanswered; the wording is the filer's; Open; also
distributed-agency) refines QUESTION-470: does blameless post-incident
practice in engineering keep accountability while giving up blame, and
does it keep or lose the upstream provenance blame supplies? Its body
puts it to CLAIM-742 and CLAIM-733, and names CLAIM-717's blame
displacement "turned round" as one possible answer. The candidate sources
it names are the filer's, not the exchange's.

- *Readings.* Three THEORYs bear on it, and its dated notes say how.
  Shoemaker 2019 (THEORY-1974, NOTE-2048, skimmed): corporations
  "blameworthy and blamable in answerability terms, but not ... in
  accountability terms", a worked model of a practice that keeps one kind
  of blame and gives up another; the fit is partial, since blameless
  engineering withholds anger from persons who have the capacities it
  needs, and Shoemaker's accountability is not the remedial sense; it
  says nothing on provenance. The NTSB's Tempe report (THEORY-1932,
  NOTE-2034): a statutory investigation that "does not assign fault or
  blame" still ranks cause and carries it upstream to the company's
  safety culture, which "contributed ... specifically to the vehicle
  operator's extended distraction"; so one blameless practice keeps the
  provenance of an incapacity, by naming contributing factors; "Whether
  this holds of blameless engineering post-mortems ... is not shown".
  Elish (THEORY-1928, NOTE-2019): where no blameless practice intervenes,
  blame lands on the nearest operator. CASE-292, the Tempe crash filed
  from the report (distributed-agency only, in no account of this line),
  bears on the question too.

**Traceability is not fair attribution.** CLAIM-712 (A's, A19 §9 of the
first share: "Traceability and fair attribution are different
achievements"; left unfiled on 2026-11-15 for want of a separating case
and filed on 2026-11-16 once the third review supplied one; thesis,
interpretive, Proposed; reconciled on 2026-11-18 with the continuation's
CLAIM-715 and judged distinct) grounds in CASE-277 and complements
CLAIM-707, CLAIM-708, CLAIM-523 and CLAIM-715: a record can identify
exactly which agent approved an outcome while leaving open whether that
agent had the time, information, authority and alternatives that
responsibility for it requires, so two arrangements with equally complete
audit trails can warrant very different attributions. Its body says a
complete trail can make the last human approval the most visible thing
about a failure, which is how it joins CLAIM-707, that traceability
records CLAIM-708's first kind of responsibility and not the second, and
that CLAIM-715 names one way the trail comes to show the approval and not
the conditions. It says it does not say what fair attribution is.

- *Readings.* No THEORY carries a relation to it. Its body names the
  HKMA paper (THEORY-1931: "A formal approval step is not, by itself,
  meaningful human oversight") and Zeiser's attributability gap
  (THEORY-1915) as "readings in the same neighbourhood".

CASE-277 (the third review's construction, ChatGPT's, forwarded by the
owner on 2026-11-16; stipulated, Proposed) has no relation of its own;
CLAIM-712 and ARG-112 ground in it: two hospitals with the same AI system
and equally complete, accurate audit trails of the same approval before
the same bad outcome, in one of which the clinician had time,
information, authority to override and protected escalation, and in the
other overwhelming volume, too little information and penalties for
overriding. Its body calls hospital B "the moral crumple zone of CLAIM-707
with a perfect paper trail", and says it shows neither how
responsibility should be divided nor that real hospitals are like B.
ARG-112 (the first filing's filer's; form existence, Proposed) rests on
CLAIM-708, grounds in CASE-277 and concludes CLAIM-712: the case is
coherent and separates traceability from the grounds of attribution,
given the ordinary control and epistemic conditions; "The separation is
shown; what fair attribution requires is left open."

**Standing to blame, cited.** QUESTION-473 (A's, raised by the third
review of 2026-11-16, ChatGPT's, forwarded by the owner; first filing;
Open; line organizational-normativity) asks what remains of an
organization's moral standing to blame an agent whose wrongdoing its
conditions substantially produced, as distinct from its authority to
investigate, correct or sanction. It is integrated by
dependence-and-domination's SYNTHESIS-099, beside CLAIM-713, CLAIM-702,
CLAIM-707 and CLAIM-708, which its body names, and SYNTHESIS-038
integrates it for this line. Its body says it is distinct from
QUESTION-470, which asks "who must repair a harm, not who may condemn
for it". It stores no relation to any component here.

**Normative governability.** CLAIM-719 (A's, A108 §6, A110 §9 and §10,
A106 §18; thesis, interpretive, Proposed) uses TERM-128: an institution
can be reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and
unresponsive to justified reasons for revising it, and first-order
responsiveness "is no evidence of responsiveness at the second". Its body
says ρ^(2) "is named, not defined", and that the lack of second-order
responsiveness is a wrong only with CLAIM-624's duty as a further
premise. TERM-128 (A's term, A110 §13's version of four; the link to
reasons-responsiveness is the owner's, U46; Proposed) draws on THEORY-121
and THEORY-122: the maintained, role-appropriate capacity to recognize and
respond to justified considerations, "including considerations that
require revising its own governing mechanisms"; relational, and not
corrigibility, compliance or obedience. Its body says the second-order
clause is CLAIM-719's responsiveness, that A110 §13's last paragraph
("Deficiencies in normative governability may explain wrongful conduct
without excusing every duty arising from it") ties the term to CLAIM-742
and CLAIM-714, and that CLAIM-719 is the first candidate claim stated in
it. No reading of 2026-11-18 bears on either.

## Not stated on the record

- Who owes the correction in any case. CLAIM-742 and CLAIM-717 leave it
  open; CASE-284 says the public authority's duty needs a premise that
  Miller's capacity ground, now read through a neighbour, states for an
  agent "uniquely" placed and does not settle for an authority.
- An answer to QUESTION-470. CLAIM-742, CLAIM-733 and CLAIM-717 bear on
  it, and CLAIM-742's body calls itself "one condition" the question asks
  for. Miller's six connections (THEORY-1958) include two of the grounds
  the question lists as a possible answer, benefit and capacity, but the
  THEORY bears on CLAIM-717, not on the question, and no entry says they
  answer it.
- An answer to QUESTION-449. CLAIM-733, CLAIM-714 and CASE-291 bear on
  it, and each says it does not reach a mechanism installed by another.
- An answer to QUESTION-476, or whether a blameless review is a
  mechanism of second-order responsiveness in CLAIM-719's sense. The
  question does not cite CLAIM-719 or TERM-128. The NTSB reading shows
  one blameless practice that keeps upstream provenance; whether
  engineering post-mortems do is not shown.
- Whether what an organization keeps when it loses standing to blame
  (QUESTION-473, in SYNTHESIS-099) is CLAIM-717's remedial duty or
  something else. QUESTION-473 asks about authority to "investigate, correct or
  sanction"; CLAIM-717 is about blame and repair, and no entry says where
  a sanction falls between them.
- Whether CASE-292, the Tempe crash, is a documented instance of
  CLAIM-712. The NTSB had a complete record of the operator's conduct and
  placed contributing cause upstream; CASE-292 illustrates CLAIM-707, and
  neither it nor CLAIM-712 cites the other.
- Whether CLAIM-719's ρ^(2) can be defined: its body says no case family
  or response set is given.
- Whether the specimen of the sibling part, CASE-289, is an institution
  reliable at the first order and unresponsive at the second. See Arm B:
  with the court's opinion read, the candidate fails.

## Arm B

Integrations tested at the interim review under ADR-075's step 5, and
tested again on 2026-11-18 with the readings and over the four entries
integrated at version 2. None passes, so nothing is filed.

- *CLAIM-742 with CLAIM-743*: an agent's diminished culpability is not an
  exercise of a valid normative power, so the claim to relief persists
  through the agent's excuse. Already stated, in CLAIM-742's body: "The
  imprisoned person's claim to release does not become weaker merely
  because JoJo lacks ordinary moral competence". No reading bears on it.
- *CLAIM-714 with CLAIM-719*: a governability deficiency an institution
  produced does not excuse what is done through it. Already stated, in
  TERM-128's definition (A110 §13's last paragraph) and body.
- *CLAIM-745 with CLAIM-719*: the visa gate is an institution reliable at
  the first order and unresponsive at the second. Route: discriminating
  prediction. At the interim review it failed on a free parameter, which
  standard the gate is indexed to. *With the readings:* the court's
  opinion (THEORY-1952, NOTE-2054) indexes it to the statute, and on that
  indexing the gate is a first-order failure: the officers' required
  assessments were made and were "ultimately irrelevant" to the outcome.
  So the parameter is closed by a reading, and closed against the
  sentence; whether the Department was unresponsive to reasons for
  revising the Policy is not in the opinion (the later events were not
  checked). Fails; recorded under "Not stated".
- *CLAIM-717 and CLAIM-742 with CLAIM-729 and CASE-284*: where the repair
  needs an arrangement no party can reach by permissible,
  resource-bounded means, a claim to correction persists with no one
  bound to complete it. Route: derivation. Fails: the persistence half is
  CLAIM-722's, "complete" is a free parameter, and the "no one bound"
  half meets ARG-114's fallback duties. *With the readings:* Miller's
  capacity ground (THEORY-1958) makes the "no one bound" half weaker
  still, since capacity alone grounds a duty to do what one can.
- *CLAIM-733 with TERM-100*: the provenance blame supplies is not
  descriptive provenance. Already stated, in TERM-127's body ("Not
  TERM-100's provenance") and CLAIM-733's.

Candidates over the new components and the readings, tested on
2026-11-18:

- *QUESTION-476 with CLAIM-733 and the NTSB (THEORY-1932)*: upstream
  provenance can be kept without blame, by naming contributing causes,
  so blame is not needed for the provenance CLAIM-733 says it supplies.
  Already stated, in QUESTION-476's dated note, for one practice and as
  a reading. CLAIM-733 says blame supplies provenance, not that only
  blame does, so the reading does not contradict it.
- *QUESTION-476 with Shoemaker (THEORY-1974)*: a blameless practice is
  answerability-blame without accountability-blame. Already stated, in
  QUESTION-476's dated note, with its limits.
- *QUESTION-473 (SYNTHESIS-099) with CLAIM-717 and THEORY-1912*: an organization that
  loses standing to blame by complicity keeps its remedial duties and
  powers, since blame and repair do not substitute. Route: derivation.
  Fails on a free parameter: QUESTION-473's "sanction" is not placed by
  any component between blame and repair; read as a remedial measure the
  sentence follows from CLAIM-717, read as blame it contradicts the
  question's distinction. Left under "Not stated".
- *CLAIM-712 with CLAIM-742*: an outcome can call for correction where a
  complete record does not show that anyone is fairly blamed for it.
  Route: derivation. Fails as trivial: it conjoins CLAIM-712's
  separation with CLAIM-742's, and adds no inference either lacks.
- *CLAIM-712 with CLAIM-717 and CASE-277*: in hospital B, blaming the
  approving clinician is remedial scapegoating. Already stated, in
  substance: CASE-277's body calls B "the moral crumple zone of CLAIM-707",
  and CLAIM-717's body names CLAIM-707's crumple zone as the mechanism of
  scapegoating.

## What it does not say
