# NOTE-695: Esfeld & Lam — Moderate structural realism about space-time

## Contribution

The paper names and defends a third structural realism. It is ontic, unlike
Worrall's, and it keeps objects, unlike French and Ladyman's (§1). It works
the view out for classical general relativity in the tensor formulation (§2)
and the fibre-bundle formulation (§3). It places the view in the
substantivalism–relationalism debate (§4). It says what survives if points go
(§5).

What it adds is a reply to the question "if there are objects, don't they
require intrinsic properties as identity condition?" (p. 32; fn. 7 credits a
referee and Wüthrich for pressing it). The reply has two parts. Concrete
relations can do whatever identity work intrinsic properties could. And where
nothing qualitative distinguishes the objects, a primitive numerical
distinction is enough.

## Key insight

Read the "master argument" for intrinsic properties (p. 29) as two premises:
(1) relations require relata; (2) "these objects have to be something in
themselves", with intrinsic properties beyond their relations. Epistemic
structural realism accepts both. Radical ontic structural realism rejects
both. Moderate structural realism accepts (1) and rejects (2) (p. 31). "An
object as such is nothing but that what bears the relations" (p. 32). The
remainder, what makes two indistinguishable bearers two, is supplied by
primitive numerical diversity. This is "not a primitive thisness, for it does
not establish an identity in time—or any other sort of an identity" (p. 33).
The paper says this is what the mutual dependence of objects and relations
rests on: "we get the relata and the relations at once as the internal
structure of a whole" (p. 34).

## Assumptions

- **What "intrinsic" means.** Langton and Lewis: "having or lacking an
  intrinsic property is independent of accompaniment or loneliness" (p. 28).
- **"Properties" exclude identity properties by stipulation.** "Properties such
  as being that individual (e.g. being John Smith …) will hence not be
  considered in this paper" (p. 28). So the denial of intrinsic properties
  does not, by itself, deny haecceities. Haecceity is rejected separately, by
  the hole argument for points and by the "spirit of structural realism" (p.
  33).
- **The scope is fundamental intrinsic properties of basic objects.** "It goes
  without saying that there are intrinsic properties of complex systems as a
  whole" (p. 28). The thesis concerns properties that "cannot be traced back
  to more fundamental properties" and that would ground relations.
- **The argument that knowledge is relational is assumed, not argued.**
  Jackson's (1998) argument that science reaches only relations is taken
  "without further discussion" (p. 29).
- **Scientific realism is presupposed.** Structural realism is not used to
  defend it. The paper argues that Worrall's first two theses are in tension,
  so structure is not immune to pessimistic induction (p. 29).
- **Structure means concrete, determinate, first-order relations,**
  particulars rather than universals. "These relations being as determinate
  as intrinsic properties are supposed to be" (p. 32). That is how it claims
  to escape the Newman objection (fn. 8, citing Chakravartty 2004 §3).
- **The physics is classical GR, mostly vacuum.** The paper limits itself
  mainly to "empty" solutions without non-gravitational fields. It asserts
  that this "does not alter the argumentation" (pp. 34–35), and §4 shows that
  matter does change point labelling (p. 43). It treats the metric only, not
  the connection (p. 35). It sets aside the algebraic formulation (p. 34)
  and quantum gravity (fn. 9).
- **Leibniz equivalence is taken from the physics.** Diffeomorphic models
  represent the same physical situation, "in agreement with the common
  practice among working relativists" (p. 36), after Earman & Norton and
  Stachel.

## Key results

This is a philosophy paper with no theorem. Its results are arguments, of
uneven strength.

### §1: the general position (pp. 27–34)

- **Against Worrall.** If science describes only relations, then no line
  separates structure from content within a theory. Theory change is then
  change of structure, and the pessimistic induction applies to structure too
  (p. 29).
- **Three arguments for ontic structural realism** (pp. 30–31): from coherence
  of metaphysics with epistemology, from parsimony, and from quantum physics.
  The authors accept the first two. They reject the quantum argument as
  French and Ladyman give it, on two grounds. Primitive thisness is "a purely
  metaphysical position that one can take anyway", so there is no empirical
  underdetermination (p. 30). And quantum field theory favours fields over
  particles, so the particle/field choice is not underdetermined either
  (Cao 2003a; p. 31).
- **Against radical OSR: relations presuppose relata.** Concrete relations are
  instantiated, and need something that instantiates them. Without relata,
  the position "is not able to distinguish between structure as an abstract
  entity and the structure of the physical world" (p. 31). Fn. 6 cites Cao,
  Chakravartty (2003), Busch and Psillos for this objection.
- **Mutual dependence.** Objects "can neither exist nor be conceived without
  relations in which they stand". Relations "can neither exist in the physical
  world nor be conceived as the structure of the physical world without
  objects" (p. 32).
- **Relations as identity conditions.** "Insofar as intrinsic properties
  account for identity conditions, relations can perform that task as well.
  For instance, if A is bigger than B, heavier than C, etc., these relations
  individuate A" (p. 32). The analogy is the web of belief: beliefs
  individuated by inferential role (Quine 1951; pp. 32–33).
- **The cases where neither suffices.** "Quantum systems of the same kind whose
  states are entangled are indistinguishable … These systems do not have an
  identity in time." And "space-time points can stand in exactly the same
  spatio-temporal relations and, yet, be numerically distinct", "on certain
  symmetry assumptions about space-time" (p. 33). These cases tell against
  bundle theories of both kinds, built of intrinsic properties or of
  relations.
- **Primitive numerical distinction.** Haecceity is rejected (the hole
  argument; it gives objects priority). "One can simply accept a numerical
  distinction (diversity)—among quantum systems or space-time points—as
  primitive (a similar view is held by Pooley, 2005, Sect. 4)" (p. 33). It
  tells us "that there is a number of objects that is greater than one … and
  that is all that it tells us." The motivating situation "is independent of
  structural realism. Any position in the metaphysics of science has to come
  to terms with" it.
- **Why no priority either way** (p. 34). Objects cannot be dispensed with,
  "on pain of running into absurdity". Relations cannot be prior, because
  bundles fail to distinguish entangled systems and symmetric points. Objects
  cannot be prior, because that "would commit us to primitive thisness".
- **Quantum physics.** The formalism "always refers to cases in which there is
  a definite number of quantum systems taken for granted (although these
  systems are not individuals)". "Unless one goes for hidden variables, there
  are no intrinsic properties underlying the entanglement" (p. 34). It
  refers to Esfeld 2004 for the full case.

### §2: GR in the tensor formulation (pp. 34–39)

- **Epistemic SR implies intrinsic individuation of points,** independent of
  the metric (p. 35). That is what the hole argument punishes. Take a
  diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a hole. If points are
  individuated independently of the metric, the two models are distinct
  solutions, and Cauchy data fail to fix the solution in the hole. Two
  space-times "may agree till a time t and then disagree for any time t′ > t"
  (pp. 35–36).
- **Radical SR faces intelligibility.** "What does a space-time relation (of
  the space-time structure) relate? How can the space-time structure be
  represented by the metric alone … given that the metric tensor field itself
  is defined as an assignment of a metric tensor at each manifold point?" (p.
  36).
- **The bare manifold point is ambiguous.** It has no physical meaning, by
  active diffeomorphism invariance, yet it is "conceptually indispensable"
  (pp. 36–37). "There is no bijective correspondence between physical
  space-time points and mathematical bare manifold points; indeed, an
  equivalence class of diffeomorphic manifold points corresponds to a
  space-time point" (p. 36).
- **The moderate reading.** The relata are "space-time points (not bare
  manifold points!)" (p. 37). "The metric tensor field completely determines
  the structural identity of the space-time points" (p. 37). Coordinatization
  is a "purely mathematical" individuation with "no physical meaning" (pp.
  37–38). If p ∈ M were the genuine representative of a space-time point,
  g′(p) ≠ g(p) for a drag-along g′ would break Leibniz equivalence (p. 38).
- **A concrete construction: Bergmann–Komar, as developed by Lusanna and Pauri
  (2005).** Four functions of the four independent eigenvalues of the Weyl
  tensor serve as a non-coordinate "pseudo-coordinatization" of points (pp.
  38–39). Imposed as a gauge fixing in the constrained Hamiltonian framework,
  this labelling "constitutes the structural identity of space-time points"
  (p. 39). The authors concede three limits. It works "for space-times with
  no symmetries". It needs "explicit breaking of active general covariance".
  It holds for "a rather specific class of solutions" (pp. 39). They add
  that moderate SR "is however not tied to this gauge-fixing approach".

### §3: GR in the fibre-bundle formulation (pp. 40–42)

- **The bundle.** The orthonormal frame bundle over the manifold. The metric
  is derived from the frame field, and the Einstein equations can be written
  in it (p. 40).
- **Sections define the points.** Stachel: "the map σ [a section] defines the
  points of space-time … space-time points are not defined before a
  cross-section is given" (Stachel 1986, quoted p. 40).
- **The three claims** (p. 41):
  1. base-to-fibre mappings encode the structural identity of points;
  2. base and fibres are "soldered", so the bare manifold has no independent
     weight;
  3. active general covariance is invariance under fibre-preserving
     automorphisms of the bundle. These determine base diffeomorphisms
     uniquely, and a base diffeomorphism fixes them up to vertical ones. On
     this formulation it is "impossible to even formulate the hole argument"
     (Stachel 2002, quoted p. 41).
- **Against both rivals at once** (p. 42). Nothing in the formalism gives
  points identity independent of the fibres, which tells against epistemic
  SR. Points cannot be eliminated "on pain of running into mathematical,
  physical and conceptual difficulties", which tells against radical SR. That
  the base space can be defined as a quotient does not eliminate points
  (Stachel & Iftime 2005).

### §4: substantivalism and relationalism (pp. 42–43)

- **Three conceptions** (p. 42): (1) Newtonian substantivalism, with
  space-time and matter distinct; (2) Leibnizian relationalism, with
  space-time reduced to non-spatiotemporal relations or properties of matter;
  (3) Cartesian–Spinozan substantivalism, with space-time and matter
  identical.
- **The position fits (1) or (3), not (2).** Its "purely relational" claim is
  non-reductive. It is a form of "sophisticated substantivalism" (p. 43). It
  leaves open whether matter is distinct from space-time.
- **Matter contributes to point identity.** In Lusanna and Pauri's framework,
  matter fields modify the labelling of points and so "also provide
  structural (but non-spatio-temporal) identity to space-time points" (p.
  43). The text cites this as Lusanna and Pauri (2003). The reference list
  has only a 2005 paper.

### §5: conclusion (pp. 43–45)

- **The paper's own summary.** Coherence, parsimony and quantum theory support
  the position, which accounts for active general covariance better than its
  rivals (pp. 43–44).
- **Not tied to points.** The view is not tied to a set-theoretic
  representation by points. In algebraic GR, points correspond to maximal
  ideals, and their identity can be understood structurally at the algebraic
  level (p. 44). "Spelling out the details would obviously require a separate
  paper".
- **The broader claim.** In a broader sense, moderate SR "is not necessarily
  committed to the existence of space-time points". The basic constituents
  "could also stem from a generalization of the notion of a space-time
  point". Quantum gravity "would require a rather radical modification of the
  space-time ontology presented here" while fitting "the main metaphysical
  claim" (pp. 44–45).

## Concepts

- **Master argument for intrinsic properties** (p. 29). (1) Relations require
  relata. (2) Relata must be something in themselves, with intrinsic properties
  beyond their relations. After Langton 1998.
- **Moderate structural realism.** An ontic structural realism accepting (1)
  and denying (2). There are objects, "but instead of being characterized by
  intrinsic properties, all there is to the basic physical objects are the
  relations in which they stand" (p. 31).
- **Radical structural realism.** French and Ladyman (2003): "there is only
  structure in the sense of relations, and there are no objects standing in
  the relations" (p. 30).
- **Numerical distinction (diversity), primitive.** The bare fact that there
  are more than one, "and that is all that it tells us" (p. 33). It
  establishes no identity, in time or otherwise. That is the stated
  difference from primitive thisness.
- **Structural identity.** The identity of a space-time point as fixed by its
  position in the network of chronogeometrical, inertio-gravitational and
  causal relations given by the metric (p. 38), or by a section of the frame
  bundle (p. 41). The paper also calls it "physical identity".
- **Space-time point vs bare manifold point.** A bare manifold point has only
  mathematical identity, through coordinatization. A space-time point is what
  the metric makes of manifold points. It corresponds to an equivalence class
  under diffeomorphism, not bijectively to a manifold point (pp. 36–38).
- **Leibniz equivalence.** Diffeomorphic models represent the same physical
  situation (p. 36).
- **Soldering.** The mutual determination of base-space and fibre geometry in
  the frame bundle (p. 40).

  not its critic. Fn. 6 cites Chakravartty 2003 for the intelligibility

  relations escaping Newman. His 2012 dilemma is aimed at this paper (his fn.
  9 and §10.6, per [NOTE-149](NOTE-149.md)). On his map, the paper takes the horn with no
  intrinsic identity-fixing features. It answers in advance the "what is the
  what?" question that §10.6 asks of its p. 32 sentence. The "what" is a
  bearer numerically distinct from others, with no further identity.
  [NOTE-149](NOTE-149.md) records no engagement by Chakravartty with the primitive-diversity
  reply. I have not reread his chapter to check.
- **Chakravartty's mass-and-charge point ([NOTE-149](NOTE-149.md) C6) is not met here.** The
  quantum discussion concerns entanglement and indistinguishability only.
  State-independent properties such as mass and charge are never discussed,
  so the paper does not say whether they are intrinsic. The p. 28 restriction
  to "fundamental intrinsic properties of basic physical objects" leaves the
  question where Chakravartty presses it.

## Limitations

- **The GR sections argue fit, not entailment.** The tensor and bundle
  formulations "naturally receive" the moderate reading (p. 41), and that is
  as far as they go. Leibniz equivalence and Stachel's bundle reading are
  equally the basis of "sophisticated substantivalism" and of other
  structuralisms. The paper says itself that the endorsement of Leibniz
  equivalence is called sophisticated substantivalism (p. 43).
- **Identity is equivocal across §§1–2.** §1 says symmetric space-times have
  points the relations do not discern, and assigns them primitive diversity
  (p. 33). §2 says the metric "completely determines the structural identity
  of the space-time points" (p. 37). §2 never returns to the symmetric case,
  and the concrete construction it cites works only without symmetries (p.
  39). In those cases, which are the physically familiar exact solutions,
  structural identity does not fix which point is which. The work is done by
  the primitive diversity of §1.
- **The construction it cites breaks the symmetry it relies on.** The
  Bergmann–Komar labelling gives structural identity "through the explicit
  breaking of active general covariance" (p. 39). The paper treats active
  general covariance as what "physically grounds" its view (p. 38). The
  tension is acknowledged in a clause, not discussed.
- **The no-priority argument assumes an exhaustive list.** It runs by
  elimination over bundles, thisness and primitive diversity (p. 34). Other
  options, such as weak discernibility, Morganti-style partial dependence and
  grounding accounts, are not considered.
- **"Space-time point" has no fixed referent.** It is an equivalence class of
  manifold points (p. 36), what a section defines (p. 40), and the values of
  the gravitational degrees of freedom (Dorato & Pauri, p. 39). The paper
  endorses all three without saying which it means.
- **Quantum physics is asserted, not argued.** The paper refers to Esfeld 2004.
  It does not discuss mass, charge or spin. "Unless one goes for hidden
  variables" (p. 34) sets aside Bohmian views, which Esfeld later adopted.

## Open questions

- **Is primitive numerical diversity coherent without thisness?** It counts
  without identifying, and it is "not empirically accessible" in the way
  thisness is said to be (p. 33). A reply from Chakravartty, or a held
  defence of the distinction (Pooley 2005 §4 is the paper's own source),
  would settle whether it escapes the dilemma or is the dilemma's "thin"
  concession, as [CLAIM-134](../claims.d/CLAIM-134.md) has it.
- **What makes a relation concrete?** The paper's answer to Newman is that
  relations are concrete and determinate, but it gives no criterion. Without
  one, the escape from Newman is a stipulation.
- **What do points become in symmetric space-times?** Do points in
  Minkowski or de Sitter space have a structural identity at all, or only
  primitive diversity? The paper's two sections give different answers.
- **Does it survive quantum gravity?** The paper promises that moderate SR
  survives the loss of points in algebraic GR or quantum gravity, but defers
  the details to "a separate paper" (p. 44). Whether Esfeld and Lam wrote it
  (their 2011 and Lam & Esfeld 2012, cited in [NOTE-194](NOTE-194.md)) is unchecked.
