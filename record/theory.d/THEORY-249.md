# THEORY-249: Whether a system has agency can be settled only relative to a reference frame: each of four conditions of agency (a boundary, being the source of action, meaningful goal-directedness and adaptivity) needs a commitment outside the system before it can be decided

## Source

Abel, Barreto, Bowling et al. (2025), "Agency Is Frame-Dependent",
[LIT-969](../literature.d/LIT-969.md), read in full in [NOTE-793](../notes.d/NOTE-793.md): §1 (the four-part account), §2
(Claims 1–4 and the conclusion) and §3 (choosing a frame).

## What was actually shown

An informal argument in four steps, each adapted from a cited result.

- **Boundary (Claim 1).** "Nearly all agents admit many plausible
  boundaries", and quantities such as an RL agent's optimal policy and
  Bellman error change with the boundary (Jiang 2019, Prop. 10).
- **Source of action (Claim 2).** "There exist cases with at least two
  plausible choices of causal variables" on which Kenton et al.'s
  agent-discovery method ([LIT-909](../literature.d/LIT-909.md)) gives opposite verdicts.
- **Goals (Claim 3).** Every input-output system can be described as if
  goal-directed, and the zero reward is consistent with every behaviour,
  so deciding that a goal is "meaningful" needs an extra principle
  (Occam, maximum entropy, Dennett's usefulness).
- **Adaptivity (Claim 4).** Whether a fixed policy is adaptive depends on
  the reference class of behaviour changes counted (Zadeh 1963; Abel et
  al. 2023, Thm 3.1).
- **Conclusion.** Agency, as the conjunction of these conditions given a
  boundary, is "frame-dependent". A frame is the four commitments
  together. No formal definition of a frame is given, and the paper says
  its arguments "are purely philosophical".

Each step could have failed: a condition might have had a single natural
reading that no plausible alternative contests. The paper offers one
contested case or cited result per condition, and no more.

## What this does not say

- **Not that every attribution of agency is frame-relative.** The
  premises are existential ("there exist cases", "many"), and the
  conclusion is not. Systems on which all reasonable frames agree are not
  ruled out.
- **Not that the frame is arbitrary in the sense of unprincipled.** The
  paper asserts arbitrariness but argues only that a choice is needed. Its
  own §3 proposes predictive or explanatory power as a selection
  principle, which, if defensible, would fix the frame.
- **Not that agency is unreal or merely conventional.** Within a frame
  the conditions can be decided, and comparisons can be made.
- **Not a refutation of self-individuating accounts.** Barandiaran et al.
  ([LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md)), on which the four-part account is modelled, require a
  boundary and norms that the system's own organisation produces "without
  an observer". The paper's examples are of observer-drawn boundaries and
  behaviour-inferred goals. It does not engage the organisational claim
  ([THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md)).
- **Not a test.** It gives no criterion that admits some systems as agents
  and refuses others, and no verdict on the thermostat ([CASE-044](../cases.d/CASE-044.md)).
