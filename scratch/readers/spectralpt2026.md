## Report

### Summary
Training is modelled as a random initial weight matrix (Marchenko–Pastur bulk, variance σ_W²) receiving finite-rank gradient perturbations. In a linear teacher–student model with a rank-1 teacher W* = uvᵀ and full-batch GD, the weight covariance after t steps is exactly X(t) = σ_t²X̂⊥ + θ_t²ŝ_tŝ_tᵀ with σ_t² = (1−ε)^{2t}σ_W² (bulk shrinks) and θ_t² = (1−ε)^{2t}rσ_W² + (1−(1−ε)^t)² (signal grows) (eqs. 17–19). A BBP outlier with eigenvector overlap q² > 0 with u appears when θ_t² = σ_t²(r + √r) (eq. 27). Solving gives a critical step size ε_c and, for ε below it, a critical time t_c (eq. 39) at which the outlier appears: a "dynamical BBP transition". A "trainability phase diagram" in (ε, 1/σ_W) is drawn with disordered (no outlier), ferromagnetic (aligned outlier) and paramagnetic (ε > ε_o, unstable or spurious) regions. Extended heuristically to tanh/ReLU (first step, Gaussian equivalence), one hidden layer (first layer only), finite data (load α = P/D deforms the bulk; transient BBP window narrows), and SGD at stationarity (a fluctuation–dissipation/Lyapunov relation for weight fluctuations; a schematic route to heavy-tailed spectra). Empirically, a one- and three-hidden-layer ReLU MLP on UTKFace age regression: final test MAE over (ε, σ_W) shows a central good region; top-k first-layer subspace self-overlap across seeds distinguishes phases; in the convergent large-step regime the learned top features coincide with data PCA components.

### Contribution
A dynamical reading of BBP: the step size and initial variance jointly set both the bulk scale and the spike, so the outlier's emergence is a function of training time and hyperparameters, with a lower and an upper critical branch (a transient window). A teacher-free order parameter, the cross-seed self-overlap q^αβ / q_k, is proposed for real networks.

### Key results (section, verbatim)
1. Mechanism (§I, p.1): "The emergence of informative representations is then realised by isolated eigenvalues detaching from the random spectral bulk of a weight matrix, with the corresponding eigenvectors acquiring nonzero alignment with the learning signal."
2. Step size acts on both sides (§II, p.4): "the step size ε simultaneously controls the spike amplitude and the random bulk, which makes the BBP transition dynamical and induces transient behaviour."
3. Threshold (§II A, eq. 27, p.5): θ_t²(ε_c) = σ_t²(ε_c)(r + √r); after one step ε_c^± = (1 ± 1/(r^{1/4}σ_W))^{−1}, ε_o = 1 (eq. 28).
4. Dynamical transition (§II D, eq. 39, p.8): t_c = −ln(1 + 1/(r^{1/4}σ_W)) / ln(1−ε) for 0 < ε < 1; and "the critical time t_c is always positive and finite, implying that the optimisation always converges to the correct ground state ... gradient descent optimisation is just an interpolation between the initial and the target covariance matrices".
5. Naming caveat (§II B, p.5): "We have adopted the nomenclature of disordered systems, but note that while the boundary ε_c is associated with a spectral phase transition, ε_o is an optimisation threshold."
6. ε_o as spectral reference (§II A, p.4): "interpreted as a spectral reference scale characterising the most efficient suppression of the random bulk, rather than as a general statement about optimal loss or generalisation."
7. Finite data (§IV A, p.13): "For small α, finite-sample fluctuations strongly deform the bulk spectrum and suppress signal extraction"; the ferromagnetic window narrows as α falls (App. B).
8. Heavy tails, schematic (§IV B, eqs. 92–99, pp.15–16): Σ = H⁻¹FH⁻¹ at high temperature, λ_Σ ∼ h⁻², and with ρ_H(h) ∼ h^β near zero, ρ_Σ(λ) ∼ λ^{−(3+β)/2}; "the subsequent analysis is purely schematic and serves only to illustrate a possible route to heavy-tailed spectra" and "the derivation is highly heuristic".
9. Empirics (§V, Fig. 14–16, pp.16–18): disordered phase "q_k ∼ k/D, consistent with random subspaces"; ferromagnetic "develops substantial overlap over an extended range of k"; paramagnetic convergent regime: leading learned feature overlaps PCA-1 at 0.642 / 0.681 versus 0.056 in the ferromagnetic phase, "learning is dominated by the principal covariance directions of the data rather than by task-relevant nonlinear representations".
10. Limits (§VI, p.19): "the precise universality class of the transition in practical deep learning systems remains unclear"; nonlinear and multilayer analyses "mainly restricted to the first optimisation step and the first layer".

### Assumptions
Gaussian i.i.d. inputs (non-Gaussian only at stationarity, App. I); rank-1 (rank-k "following standard methods") teacher; square loss; large N, D at fixed r = N/D; full-batch GD for the exact result; nonlinear case via Gaussian equivalence and self-averaging µ̄1, µ̄2 with u not condensed on a basis vector; multilayer only the first layer, read-out overlap J assumed small or teacher-matched; SGD stationary analysis assumes W near W*, invertible Hessian, regular Fisher; heavy-tail step adds a power-law Hessian density near zero. Empirics: one dataset (UTKFace, 64×64×3 flattened), MLPs, SGD batch 128, 300 epochs, gradient clipping at 10¹².

### Concepts
dynamical BBP transition; critical step size ε_c (two branches); optimal step size ε_o; critical time t_c; trainability phase diagram (disordered I/II, ferromagnetic, paramagnetic); transient BBP; squared overlap q²; self-overlap q^αβ and top-k subspace self-overlap q_k (random baseline k/D); load parameter α = P/D; effective temperature T = ε/|B|; rescaling factors µ1, µ2; fluctuation–dissipation (Lyapunov) relation for weight covariance; superstatistics.

### Verdict on the exchange's characterization
The record's two paraphrases are accurate. LIT-2990's "phase transitions in the weight spectra of networks during training, tied to whether a network can be trained" is right, with one sharpening: the spectrum is that of the weight covariance WWᵀ (in practice the first layer), not of activations or representations, and "trainability" names the phase diagram's regions, of which the spectral boundary is ε_c and the optimisation boundary ε_o (result 5). CLAIM-799's use (A32 §6: "the spectral perspective is not merely an analogy. But they don't establish that DNN representations generally behave as active matter, or that their learning dynamics are critical") holds: the paper establishes a BBP outlier transition in training for a solvable model and supports it empirically, and does not claim active-matter behaviour. On "critical", the paper itself uses the word: its outlook speaks of "the notion of criticality arising during optimisation itself" (§VI, p.19), but what it shows is a BBP threshold in the weight spectrum and it leaves the universality class open (result 10). So A's "don't establish ... critical" is fair if "critical" means a thermodynamic or dynamical critical point, and the filer should note the authors' looser usage.

### Bearing on named codes
- **CLAIM-799: supports, from inside the work, and qualifies the work's own framing.** In the solvable model the signal is in W from the first step (W(t) = (1−ε)^tW(0) + (1−(1−ε)^t)W*, eq. A3), loss falls monotonically and "the optimisation always converges to the correct ground state" for every ε < 1 (result 4), yet the outlier appears only at t_c. That is CLAIM-799's "the signal existed below the threshold ... it is the moment when that signal becomes detectable by this particular spectral estimator", here with the weight matrix itself as the estimator (N rows, D columns) rather than PCA on probe samples. The paper nonetheless reads the outlier as "the emergence of informative representations" (result 1) and names the sub-threshold region a phase that slows "optimisation and information retrieval" (§II B, p.6), which is the conflation CLAIM-799 warns against. Its "phase transition" is a spectral one by its own caveat (result 5). Remove the "could not verify identifier" sentence.
- **TERM-tmpf5ymd: background, an instance of "bulk by count".** "Random spectral bulk" throughout is the RMT continuous part from which "isolated eigenvalues detach"; the informative content sits in the single outlier. An example for the term's sense 1 and TERM-138 sense 3; no new sense.
- **CLAIM-tmp2r6ur: parallel, and qualifies the stop-word half for a different object.** In UTKFace first-layer weights, the top right-singular directions track the data's top principal components (common, high-variance structure: overlap 0.64–0.68) only in the poorly trained, large-step paramagnetic regime; in the well-trained ferromagnetic phase they "capture nontrivial structure in the data that deviates from the principal components" (Fig. 16 caption, overlap ≤ 0.056). So "top directions carry common structure" is regime-dependent here. It concerns first-layer weights of an MLP regressor, not an unembedding, so it neither tests LEAD-tmptgrc7 nor confirms or defeats the claim; it suggests the test should report the training regime.

### Limitations
Exact results only for linear, rank-1, Gaussian, full-batch; the nonlinear, multilayer and finite-data cases are first-step or first-layer; the SGD heavy-tail account is by the authors' word "highly heuristic". Empirics: one dataset, MLPs, test MAE and cross-seed overlaps, no error bars reported in what I read, code not yet released. The "trainability" phases conflate spectral visibility with optimisation quality: in the linear model every ε < 1 converges, so the disordered phase is not untrainable there. The ferromagnetic/paramagnetic names are borrowed, not derived from an order–disorder transition in the thermodynamic sense.

### Candidate THEORY sentence
Park, Bocchi, D'Amico, Lucini and Aarts show, exactly for a linear rank-1 teacher–student model under full-batch gradient descent and heuristically for nonlinear, finite-data and stochastic extensions, that training shrinks the weight matrix's random Marchenko–Pastur bulk while growing a rank-1 spike, so an outlier aligned with the teacher detaches by a BBP transition at a critical step size or a critical time set by the learning rate and the initial weight variance; in that model the student converges for every step size below one whether or not the outlier has yet appeared.
