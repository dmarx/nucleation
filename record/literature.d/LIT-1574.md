# LIT-1574: The Oxymonad Genome Displays Canonical Eukaryotic Complexity in the Absence of a Mitochondrion

Karnkowska, A., Treitli, S. C., Brzoň, O., Novák, L., Vacek, V., Soukal, P., et al. & Hampl, V. (2019). The Oxymonad Genome Displays Canonical Eukaryotic Complexity in the Absence of a Mitochondrion. *Molecular Biology and Evolution* 36(10): 2292–2312. doi:10.1093/molbev/msz147.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1243](../notes.d/NOTE-1243.md).*

- **Not reduced.** About 75 Mb, 16,768 genes and 31,693 introns. It
  shares more ancestral eukaryotic protein families with *Naegleria* (83%
  of its orthogroups) than the parasitic metamonads do.
- **Canonical systems.** DNA replication and repair, transcription,
  translation factors, cytoskeleton, membrane trafficking and autophagy
  look like an ordinary eukaryote's, apart from traits all metamonads
  share.
- **The organelle was emptied in steps.** SUF replaced the mitochondrial
  Fe–S pathway in all examined Preaxostyla. The glycine cleavage system
  and SHMT, still in a relative's organelle, were lost later in this line
  ([THEORY-1098](../theory.d/THEORY-1098.md)).
- **Nothing compensates.** Only mitophagy was lost with the organelle,
  and none of the organism's unique genes is tied to the loss.
- **Caveats.** A draft genome and homology searches. The authors do not
  exclude a highly reduced mitosome below FIB-SEM resolution.

A2's "2019 genomic analysis" of Monocercomonoides, cited without author or title; identified from the exchange's description and the abstract. With the 2016 paper it is A2's negative control.
