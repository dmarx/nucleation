# LIT-413: The Mind's I: Fantasies and Reflections on Self and Soul

Douglas R. Hofstadter and Daniel C. Dennett (composers and arrangers), *The
Mind's I: Fantasies and Reflections on Self and Soul*, New York: Basic Books,
c1981 (hardback, ISBN 0-465-04624-X; Library of Congress 81066099, call
number B29 .H58, Dewey 126; vii, 501 pp., illustrated, 24 cm; bibliography
pp. [465]–482; index). The same year Harvester Press issued it in Brighton
(ISBN 0-7108-0352-4, vii, 501 pp.). Bantam (Toronto and New York, 1982) and
Penguin (Harmondsworth, 1982, vii, 504 pp.) paperbacks followed. Basic Books
sells a trade paperback now (ISBN 978-0-465-03091-0, 512 pp.; on sale 17
January 2001 per the publisher). The catalogue record gives only the year
1981, so `published:` carries the year, as the first of January.

The brief's citation (Basic Books, 1981) is correct. The title page, as the
catalogue transcribes it, says "composed and arranged by" the two, not
"edited by".

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the catalogue record, the publisher's page and a library
contents note, not a reading. What follows is what those sources say.*

- **What it is.** The Library of Congress note: "Includes selected writings
  of Jorge Luis Borges and others with reflections by the authors." The
  subject headings are Self (Philosophy); Intellect; Consciousness; Soul,
  each also as "Literary collections".
- **The publisher's description** (Basic Books, for the current paperback),
  verbatim in part: "With contributions from Jorge Luis Borges, Richard
  Dawkins, John Searle, and Robert Nozick, The Mind's I explores the meaning
  of self and consciousness through the perspectives of literature,
  artificial intelligence, psychology, and other disciplines. In selections
  that range from fiction to scientific speculations about thinking
  machines, artificial intelligence, and the nature of the brain, Douglas R.
  Hofstadter and Daniel C. Dennett present a variety of conflicting visions
  of the self and the soul".
- **Contents.** The 27 selections, in the order of the contents note (MARC
  505) in the catalogue record of the 1982 Bantam printing, which carries
  the Basic Books LCCN 81066099. Spellings are corrected where the note
  misspells ("Whellis", "Statislaw", "espistemological"):
  1. "Borges and I", Jorge Luis Borges
  2. "On Having No Head", D. E. Harding
  3. "Rediscovering the Mind", Harold J. Morowitz
  4. "Computing Machinery and Intelligence", A. M. Turing
  5. "The Turing Test: A Coffeehouse Conversation", Douglas R. Hofstadter
  6. "The Princess Ineffabelle", Stanisław Lem
  7. "The Soul of Martha, a Beast", Terrel Miedaner

  "From Hardware to Software", "Mind as Program", "Created Selves and Free
  Will" and "The Inner Eye". I did not use it to assign selections to
  parts.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request for Daniel Dennett in this
record. The book is where the record's threads were first put side by side
for a general reader: machine minds and the Turing test, consciousness and
what it is like, the self and where it is, and minds built from mindless
parts. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice. The Anthology of the SOTA does not hold it: a
grep of its literature.d for the title, Hofstadter and Dennett found
nothing.

`Deferred` because it was not read. This record files a book from its
publisher's description and contents when no lawful text can be read, and
does not rebuild a book from what others say about it. The selections are
a different matter, since many are reprints with lives of their own, and
the record holds or now files the ones that bear on its threads:

- **Turing**, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (selection 4), is filed
  as [LIT-394](LIT-394.md), read in full from the off-print in the Turing Digital
  Archive.
- **Searle**, "Minds, brains, and programs" (selection 22), is filed as
  [LIT-400](LIT-400.md), read in full from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences
  preprint. The book reprints the target article. The BBS issue also
  carried open peer commentary, and its reference list marks entries cited
  by Dennett and by Hofstadter as commentators, so the editors had answered
  Searle in print before they reprinted him.
- **Nagel**, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (selection 24), is [LIT-096](LIT-096.md),
  read in [NOTE-089](../notes.d/NOTE-089.md).
- **Dennett**, "Where Am I?" (selection 13), is being filed separately in
  this batch, with Dennett's other work. Sanford's "Where Was I?"
  (selection 14) continues its story and was not filed (no lawful copy
  found).
- **Hofstadter**, "Prelude ... Ant Fugue" (selection 11), is reprinted from
  GEB, [LIT-437](LIT-437.md). It is the meeting point [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) names, a
  colony whose ants are mindless and whose organisation is a mind. No
  separate lawful copy exists, so it is not filed apart from GEB.
- **Zuboff**, "The Story of a Brain" (selection 12), probably bears on the
  same bridge. Search summaries describe a brain whose neural patterns are
  induced artificially, as in a vat; I have not read it. PhilPapers records it as first
  published in this book (pp. 202–212) and lists a PDF in PhilArchive,
  uploaded in 2015 by an archiver it does not name. The PDF refused me
  (HTTP 403, a browser challenge) and I could not confirm the upload is the
  author's, so it is not filed. It is the next selection worth filing.

The literary selections (Borges, Lem, Miedaner, Wheelis, Leiber, Rucker,
Smullyan, Nozick) are listed above and none is filed. None has a lawful
copy I could find. Whether any bears on a thread more than as
illustration is a question for a reading of the book.

**The Reflections are unread.** They are the editors' contribution, and
they are what would make the book more than an anthology in this record.
Two traces of them reached me. Dennett's Tufts archive holds "The Myth of
the Computer: An Exchange" (1982, hdl.handle.net/10427/56703), catalogued
under this book with Hofstadter and Searle as names; it is the printed
argument over how the book treats Searle and is being considered with
Dennett's other work. And the BBS commentaries above. Neither is the text
of a Reflection.

Where it bears on the record's accounts, through the selections filed:
[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) holds that behavioural evidence cannot settle AI consciousness.
Turing's paper is the proposal that it should settle machine *thinking*,
and Searle's the argument that no behaviour could. [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) holds
that a mind of mindless parts and a mind of minded parts are one
functionalist move; the Ant Fugue, Zuboff, and Searle's systems reply each
work that seam. Hofstadter's later account of the self, [LIT-423](LIT-423.md), is
the strange-loop line the book's Hofstadter selections begin.
