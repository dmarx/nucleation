# ADR-046: A TERM may be Proposed, and rival, complement or narrow another

## Context

[ADR-032](ADR-032.md) defined a TERM as "a concept or distinction. No credence: it has a
definition, and a changed definition is a new version or a superseding
TERM". The only relation between terms was `supersedes`. That covers a
definition replaced in sequence. It does not cover the other cases the
record has hit:

- **A candidate that is not adopted yet.** In nucleation#162 a replacement
  for [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md) (frustration) was drafted, proposed as a supersession,
  and withdrawn before merge. The open disagreement ended up in a QUESTION
  and a dated note on [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md). Each round had to choose between adopting
  the new definition and leaving it in prose. The owner, afterwards: "I
  think we might currently lack a way to express proposed and/or competing
  definitions of a given term, and if that's the case it might explain the
  tug-of-war we've been experiencing trying to finalize this PR."
- **Two definitions in force for different dimensions.** [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md)
  (intrapersonal synchronization) and [TERM-061](../terms.d/TERM-061.md) (practical
  synchronization) are both Active. [TERM-061](../terms.d/TERM-061.md) says that it does not supersede
  [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md) and that the two are independent dimensions. Only prose said so.
- **A special case that stays in force.** [TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md) `supersedes`
  [TERM-010](../terms.d/TERM-010.md), but [TERM-010](../terms.d/TERM-010.md) is still Active. Its section "Why it is
  still Active" says the weighted sum "survives in that role, as the scalar a
  task-fixed weighting gives". `supersedes` was standing in for narrowing,
  and the `term-revisions` chain showed a retirement that never happened.

The configuration was already half there. TERM takes the shared `statuses`
vocabulary, which includes `Proposed`. But the TERM template listed only
`Active | Superseded | Rejected`, and none of the 63 TERMs was Proposed.

The record has solved the same problem for other schemes. THEORY has
`rivals` ([ADR-022](ADR-022.md)), symmetric, for accounts "that cannot also be right,
where neither yet corrects the other". CLAIM has `complements` ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)),
symmetric, added because three accounts "were wrongly set up as rivals".
QUESTION has `refines` / `refined_by`.

## Decision

**A TERM's status is the standing of its definition, not a credence.**
[ADR-032](ADR-032.md)'s "no credence" holds: whether a definition is adopted is a separate
matter from whether any claim stated in it is true.

| Status | On a TERM |
|---|---|
| `Proposed` | a candidate definition, recorded and not adopted. Its body says what it would replace or add, and what would settle it |
| `Active` | adopted: the argument may be stated in it |
| `Deferred` | a candidate whose adoption is put off |
| `Superseded` | adopted once, then replaced. The successor is named in `superseded_by` |
| `Rejected` | a candidate declined, or an adopted term retired with nothing in its place |

**TERM gains three relations, each borrowed from the scheme that already has it:**

- `rivals` (symmetric). Another definition of the same concept that cannot
  be adopted beside this one for the same use, where neither has yet
  replaced the other. A Proposed candidate `rivals` the Active term it would
  replace. When the question is settled, the winner `supersedes` the loser.
  The loser goes Superseded if it had been adopted, or Rejected if it never was.
  This is [ADR-022](ADR-022.md)'s lifecycle, with `supersedes` in the role `corrects` plays
  for THEORY.
- `complements` (symmetric). Another definition in force beside this one,
  for a different sense or dimension of the same subject.
- `refines` / `refined_by`. This term is a special case of a broader one,
  fixed by further conditions. Both stay in force.

**`supersedes` means an adoption that retires the earlier term.** The
superseded term's status is Superseded. A term that stays Active beside its
successor is not superseded, so the relation it needs is `complements` or
`refines`.

**Applied now:**

- [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md) and [TERM-061](../terms.d/TERM-061.md) `complements` each other, as [TERM-061](../terms.d/TERM-061.md)'s own text says.
- [TERM-010](../terms.d/TERM-010.md) `refines` [TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md), in place of [TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md) `supersedes` [TERM-010](../terms.d/TERM-010.md).
  This removes a supersession the record's own text says did not retire
  anything. [TERM-010](../terms.d/TERM-010.md) stays Active. [TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md)'s prose is unchanged: it still
  says that it replaced the weighted scalar "as the foundation", which is
  true.

Nothing is filed for frustration. [#162](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/162) merged with [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md) Active and the
open relation to thwarting held in a QUESTION. The next candidate definition
of frustration should be filed as a Proposed TERM that `rivals` [TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md).

## Alternatives considered

- **New relation names: `proposes_for`/`proposals` (one-to-one) and
  `alternatives_to`.** These were drafted in the owner's discussion before
  this decision. They say what `rivals`, `complements` and `refines` already
  say elsewhere in the record. A reader who knows THEORY's `rivals` would
  have to learn a second word for the same thing. `proposes_for` also adds
  nothing to "Proposed, and `rivals` the Active term", and making it
  one-to-one rules out a candidate that would replace two terms.
- **A separate CONCEPT scheme**, with definitions pointing at the stable
  concept they define. That is the clean split by luria's own test, when a
  concept's identity needs a standing of its own apart from every definition
  of it. Nothing in the record needs that yet: `line` and the titles already
  group definitions, and every claim `uses` a particular definition. It would
  mean migrating every `uses` edge. Revisit if a concept needs its own status
  or relations.
- **A TERM-only status vocabulary.** The shared `statuses` blurbs already read
  correctly for a definition. A separate vocabulary would add a closed list to
  maintain and nothing that the table above does not give.
- **Status quo: candidates in prose, a QUESTION or the workbench.** That is
  what [#162](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/162) did, and it is what made every proposal a choice between adopting
  and erasing.

## Consequences

- A competing definition can be filed, cited and compared without retiring
  anything. A CLAIM stated in a Proposed TERM is citing something not in
  force. Lint warns on it (`retired-citations`) where the term is cited in
  the body or in a `ref::uses` directive, as the record's claims do. It does
  not warn on the frontmatter `uses:` edge alone. This was checked on
  2026-10-18 by marking [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md) Proposed for one scan: 31 sites in 13 files
  were reported, among them [CLAIM-156](../claims.d/CLAIM-156.md)'s `ref::uses-file` line and not its
  `uses:` field. That is the intended signal: a claim stated in an unadopted
  definition says so with an `inactive-ok` note, or waits.
- `complements` on TERM and CLAIM is a symmetric relation within one scheme,
  which luria reads correctly. Its extension across schemes still waits on
  dmarx/luria#358, as before.
- The `term-revisions` chain no longer shows [TERM-010](../terms.d/TERM-010.md) → [TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md), because that
  was never a revision. Narrowing is visible on the term pages as
  `refines` / `refined_by`.
- [ADR-032](ADR-032.md)'s text is unchanged. Its history notes that this decision refines
  its TERM paragraph.
