# QUESTION-tmp6rtwx: In dialectical behaviour therapy, do skills-homework completion and phone coaching predict falling urges and less frequent target behaviour once baseline functioning and motivation are controlled and patients with missing diary data are kept, and does skill use between sessions mediate the change?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-922 holds that, among 56 of 88 patients in one DBT intensive
outpatient programme, homework completion correlated with falling suicide
and substance-use urges and phone coaching with falling self-harm urges,
uncorrected for multiple comparisons, with no association with how often
the behaviours occurred. Nothing measured skill use between sessions, so
the record's DBT example (CLAIM-443, CLAIM-349), in which practice changes
which process is recruited, has no evidence about what practice changed.

## What would answer it

A larger sample, or a second programme, with the analysis specified in
advance and corrected for multiple comparisons, finding homework completion
associated with falling suicide or substance-use urges and coaching with
falling self-harm urges, with intention-to-treat handling of dropouts and
missing diary days; better, a randomized component design that varies
homework or coaching. The associations are refuted if they vanish with
baseline functioning and motivation controlled, or in the full sample
including those with missing diary data. A causal claim about
generalization needs a measure of skill use as a mediator.
