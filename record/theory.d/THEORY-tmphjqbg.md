# THEORY-tmphjqbg: A treatment's efficacy is not evidence for its theory of change: only change in the theorised process that is specific and comes before the outcome bears on it, and moving the process without moving the outcome counts against the theory

## Source

- Salkovskis, Sighvatsson and Sigurdsson (2024), [LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-428](../notes.d/NOTE-428.md). The logical point is on p. 599. The standard of evidence is
  Kazdin's (2007) six criteria, set out on pp. 608–609.
- Hofmann and Hayes (2018), [LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md), read in full in [NOTE-437](../notes.d/NOTE-437.md), in the
  section "The Rise of Mediation and Moderation Studies".

## What was actually shown

This is an argument, not a finding, and both sources state it in their own
words.

**Salkovskis et al. ([LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md))** put it plainly: "The effectiveness of a
treatment is not evidence for the validity of the theory on which it is
based with one important exception". The exception is a treatment that
demonstrably changes a factor crucial to the theory and does not change
the target problem. That disconfirms the theory. Successful,
theory-informed treatment is only "consistent with" its theory ([NOTE-428](../notes.d/NOTE-428.md),
C1). Their example of the error is exposure. It was adopted as the
mechanism of anxiety treatment because it was common to the treatments
that worked, and then confirmed from the fear reduction it was invoked to
explain. They call this circular, and say it "has in our view held the
field back". What would count instead are Kazdin's criteria: the treatment
changes the mediator, and the mediator then changes the outcome; the
relation is specific; other mediators are ruled out experimentally; and
there is consistency, a gradient and more than one source of data. They
also say mechanism research has mostly failed these criteria, above all
on temporal precedence (C8).

**Hofmann and Hayes ([LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md))** state both directions. "Even if the
intervention works well, if the specified process of change cannot be
shown to be consistently applicable, the underlying model is incorrect and
needs to be set aside." And: "if a procedure fails to alter putatively
critical processes of change ... then the model remains untested even if
the procedure fails". Their admission rule asks for both legs: a procedure
"reliably produces gains and manipulates a process that mediates these
gains". They grant that "a statistically significant mediator is not
synonymous with a generative mechanism because third variables can always
exist" ([NOTE-437](../notes.d/NOTE-437.md), Assumptions).

**Why it is close to logical.** Every theory of change on offer predicts
that the treatment it informs will work. So the treatment's working
cannot tell those theories apart, and a common factor present in every
working treatment cannot either. What can tell them apart is the process
each names: whether it moves, whether it moves first, and whether it
moves the outcome more than the rival's process does. The asymmetry
follows. A treatment that moves the process and leaves the outcome alone
is the case the theory forbids. A treatment that fails to move the process
has not tested it.

The two sources come from the two wings of CBT, Beckian and contextual
behavioural, and they disagree about the answer: one mechanism for all
effective therapy ([LIT-538](../literature.d/LIT-538.md)) against many processes in a hierarchy
([LIT-544](../literature.d/LIT-544.md)). They agree on this standard. That agreement across a rivalry is
why the account is Active.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say mediation proves a mechanism.** Both sources deny it.
  Mediation is necessary evidence and not sufficient. Third variables,
  reverse causation and a process measured after the outcome began to move
  all defeat it. The ACT review ([LIT-555](../literature.d/LIT-555.md), [NOTE-431](../notes.d/NOTE-431.md)) is a worked example:
  its mediation studies are the right kind of test, but its authors note
  that process measures were often taken "after outcomes began to improve
  significantly".
- **It does not say efficacy is unimportant.** Efficacy is evidence that a
  treatment works. It is not evidence about why.
- **Disconfirmation is not automatic.** A process that moved without the
  outcome may have been measured badly, moved too little, or be one of
  several the theory requires. Hofmann and Hayes allow "short delays while
  measurement issues are worked out". This is the ordinary point that a
  test bears on a theory only together with its auxiliary assumptions. The
  record holds no philosophy-of-science reading on confirmation that
  states it, so it is made here without a source.
- **It takes no side on which theory of change is right.** That is
  [THEORY-tmpybbhj](THEORY-tmpybbhj.md)'s and [THEORY-tmpdbe64](THEORY-tmpdbe64.md)'s business, and each is Proposed
  because its own mediation evidence is not yet in.

## How it bears on causation and mind

- **The standard is interventionist.** "The treatment changes the
  mediator, which then changes the outcome" is causation as difference
  made under intervention, the notion Borsboom uses for symptom networks
  ([LIT-547](../literature.d/LIT-547.md)) and that Pernu ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md)) and Hazelwood ([LIT-156](../literature.d/LIT-156.md)) test against
  exclusion and composition. Hazelwood's condition bears on mediation
  directly: a mediator that is partly composed of the outcome, as a
  self-report of distress-related avoidance may be of distress, is not
  independently fixable, and its "mediation" is not causal evidence. That
  application is the record's.
- **It is a mental-causation claim in clinical form.** A theory of change
  names a psychological process (a belief, a meaning, a way of relating to
  thoughts) as what produces change. This account says how such a claim is
  tested. It does not decide whether such processes are causes in their
  own right or by identity with their realisers ([THEORY-tmpbzmql](THEORY-tmpbzmql.md)).
- **A parallel elsewhere in the record.** [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md) finds that behavioural
  evidence cannot settle AI consciousness because every rival theory
  predicts the behaviour. The structure is the same: evidence every
  candidate predicts discriminates none of them.

## Connections

- Macri and Rogge's 2024 meta-analysis of ACT's processes as mediators
  ([LIT-543](../literature.d/LIT-543.md)) is the largest test of this kind in the batch. It is unread.
- Both sources open with Gordon Paul's question as the field's founding
  statement. Hofmann and Hayes use its 1969 form, which adds "and how does
  it come about?"; Salkovskis et al. use the 1967 form without it
  ([NOTE-428](../notes.d/NOTE-428.md)). This account is what that clause demands of an answer.
