# CLAIM-566: That a form assembles, that it persists over a specified horizon and that the system regenerates it after turnover are three properties with separate probabilities, and a structure that assembles and persists without producing the constraints that would regenerate it does not show organizational closure

## The claim

A, Part 5:

- P_form = Pr(desired form initially assembles);
- P_persist = Pr(form persists over a specified horizon);
- P_reproduce = Pr(system regenerates the form after turnover).

"These are not interchangeable." A's example and its limit: "A DNA origami
structure might assemble successfully and remain stable for a time without
participating in the production of the constraints needed to regenerate
itself. So the example does not establish the stronger biological notion of
organizational closure in [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)."

**Assembly is its own question.** DeLuca et al.'s simulations ([NOTE-1561](../notes.d/NOTE-1561.md);
[THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)) separate how assembly goes from how it starts: "faster
coalescence into a proto form early on is not a perfect predictor of
overall folding performance", and larger designs show "frequent trapping in
metastable states". A trapped structure persists, and is not the desired
form. Nothing in the paper concerns regeneration.

**Persistence is not regeneration.** A DNA origami structure, once folded,
persists while nothing takes it apart. Its staples and scaffold are made
outside it. It produces none of the constraints it depends on, so it has
no closure in [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s sense: a set of constraints each depending on
another in the set. Persisting and being regenerated are different facts
about it.

**Where they meet.** [CLAIM-516](CLAIM-516.md): where every constraint capacity turns over
at a positive rate and is produced only by others, with no basal
production, capacities whose production graph is acyclic all decay to
zero. So under turnover, persistence over a horizon much longer than the
turnover times needs regeneration, and the two probabilities converge.
They come apart over horizons shorter than turnover, or where nothing
turns over, which is the DNA origami case. A specified horizon, as A
writes it, is what keeps them distinct.

**Relation to the triad.** [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md) separates an operative regime, its
governing organization and the capacity to transform that organization.
This claim separates phases of one form's life: coming into being,
lasting, being remade. The two cross. A regime can persist while the
capacity to regenerate it is lost, which is [CLAIM-079](CLAIM-079.md)'s stable state
without viable trajectories when regeneration is what viability needs.

## What it does not say

- **That the three are independent.** They need not be: over long
  horizons under turnover, persistence needs regeneration.
- **That regeneration is closure.** A system can be regenerated from
  outside, as a factory remakes a product. Closure needs the regenerating
  constraints to be the system's own; the claim says only that persistence
  without them is not closure.
- **Anything measured for organizations.** The probabilities are A's
  model. The record has measured none of them.

## Note of 2026-11-05: A's synthesis on DNA origami

A restates the claim's three properties, which came from A's earlier synthesis, as P_assemble, P_survive(T) and P_regenerate(T): "A molecular structure can be easy to assemble but chemically fragile. It can be durable but difficult to assemble. And it can possess both properties without regenerating itself." A adds that self-assembly "is not the same as self-maintenance". ## Note of 2026-11-05: the further DNA origami readings

The works cited in A's two syntheses on DNA origami, pasted by the owner on 2026-11-05, read in this pull request.

**Douglas et al., "Self-assembly of DNA into nanoscale three-dimensional shapes".** Douglas et al. (2009), [LIT-2130](../literature.d/LIT-2130.md), support the example. Structures
assemble at a yield and persist through gel, purification and 24 h
incubations, and hierarchical assembly builds polymers and icosahedra from
pre-folded monomers; nothing regenerates. Assembly, persistence and the
absent regeneration are separable here.

**Douglas, Bachelet and Church, "A Logic-Gated Nanorobot".** Douglas, Bachelet and Church (2012), [LIT-2136](../literature.d/LIT-2136.md), support the claim. The
robot assembles, persists, and is designed to change state on cue, yet
never produces the staples or scaffold to make another copy of itself:
persistence and conditional reconfiguration without regeneration. No
status changes.

**Ni, Zhou, Zhu, Bershadsky, Ai, Wang, Sha, Seeman and Chaikin, "Cooperativity, entropy, and effective concentration in DNA origami self-replication".** Ni et al. (2026), [LIT-2124](../literature.d/LIT-2124.md), support the separation and refine it:
- Assembly is measured, as per-cycle yield split into vertical binding and horizontal linking.
- Persistence is engineered by a covalent UV crosslink and not measured over a horizon.
- Regeneration after turnover is absent.
- What the paper shows is a fourth thing, multiplication: more copies of a pattern from supplied parts, restoring no one of them.

Every enabling condition is supplied from outside: tiles, the thermal program, UV and the splitting of samples. The structure makes daughters, but not the constraints its own cycle depends on.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: A's second synthesis on DNA origami

From the second synthesis on DNA origami by another assistant (A), pasted by the owner on 2026-11-05.

A splits the life cycle into yield Y_T, survival S_τ and restoration G_τ by τ after specified component turnover, and notes that templated self-replication "in a supplied chemical environment is still not, by itself, sufficient" for closure. Ni et al. 2026 ([NOTE-1630](../notes.d/NOTE-1630.md)) suggest one more distinction, read by the assistant from their system. Replication, more copies made from fresh supplied parts, is not regeneration after turnover. Their origami copies tile patterns at about 1.9 copies per cycle for dimers. The tiles, the temperature program, the UV crosslink and the splitting of the sample are all supplied from outside, and nothing repairs a damaged copy. So a system can replicate without regenerating, a fourth property beside the claim's three. The wording of that distinction is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on correction, repair, replication and nucleation barriers

Read at the owner's invitation to add readings to the DNA origami filing; the works were chosen by the assistant.

**Winfree and Bekbolatov, "Proofreading tile sets"; Scheckenbach et al., "Self-regeneration and self-healing in DNA origami".** Two readings support the claim's separation of assembling, persisting and regenerating.
- In Winfree and Bekbolatov's kTAM simulations ([LIT-2119](../literature.d/LIT-2119.md)), corrected assembly, persistence of the frozen crystal and refilling of a puncture are distinct behaviours. Refilling is imperfect ("scar tissue").
- Scheckenbach et al. ([LIT-2139](../literature.d/LIT-2139.md)) show folded origami regenerating in the paper's sense. Labels recover to full brightness after bleaching, and a matching staple pool keeps 76 % of nanorulers intact over 11 days in 0.2 % serum, against 12 % without.

In both, every replacement part is supplied from solution and the structure produces none of its constraints. This is regeneration without closure in the sense of [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md). Repair fails above a damage rate (10 % serum) and never reaches the scaffold. Left open: whether either structure keeps its form once the supply is withdrawn, which neither tests.

**Zhou, Sha, Ni, Seeman and Chaikin, "Mutations in artificial self-replicating tiles".**

- Zhou et al. (2021, [LIT-2126](../literature.d/LIT-2126.md), [NOTE-1628](../notes.d/NOTE-1628.md)) stand in for the unread He et al. (2017, [LIT-2132](../literature.d/LIT-2132.md)).
- They show a templated origami replicator multiplying and then selecting among multipliers by growth rate: a mutant reaches parity in five cycles and dominates after nine.
- Nothing in it regenerates a member after turnover. Parents persist as templates, and sterile, untemplated dimers accumulate and are never removed or repaired. Every enabling condition is supplied each cycle: monomers, 48 °C and 22 °C steps, UV.
- This supports the fourth property: replication, even with selection, is not regeneration.
- Left open: whether any templated system can be made to restore its own members.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on aid dependency and self-sufficiency

Read at the owner's request to look to aid and development for theory on how aid-dependent organizations become self-sufficient; the works were chosen by the assistant.

- **Assembly and persistence come apart in aid.** In the Kenya summary ([NOTE-1650](../notes.d/NOTE-1650.md)), "evidence of impact at the time of project exit did not necessarily predict sustainability" two to three years later. Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock's isomorphic mimicry ([NOTE-1664](../notes.d/NOTE-1664.md)) is assembled form without persisting function: "unimplemented laws, unfunded agencies, and unused processes". Moore ([NOTE-1663](../notes.d/NOTE-1663.md)): "we need to distinguish between explaining the origins of collective action and explaining its persistence".
- **A distinction the claim lacks.** Ostrom et al. ([NOTE-1647](../notes.d/NOTE-1647.md)) separate the persistence of a project's effects from that of the project: results can outlast an organization, and a form can outlast its function.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on reintroduction and ecological recovery

Read at the owner's suggestion to look to reintroduction and ecological recovery for what makes support safe or hazardous to remove; the works were chosen by the assistant. The assistant's synthesis is [CLAIM-600](CLAIM-600.md).

- **Assembly and persistence come apart.** Practitioner-judged translocation success was similar for captive-bred and wild-sourced stock, yet captive-bred stock had negative mean population growth (Morris et al., [NOTE-1657](../notes.d/NOTE-1657.md)). Only 24.8 % of reported successes met the standard of persisting "without further augmentation".
- **Persistence without regeneration.** Planted trees persisted for 16–18 years, but few of their seedlings reached the sapling layer ([NOTE-1660](../notes.d/NOTE-1660.md)).
- **A horizon to measure against.** Schröder, Persson and De Roos ([NOTE-1666](../notes.d/NOTE-1666.md)) count a state stable once it lasts a full turnover after the intervention ends, which gives the claim's "specified horizon" a natural length.

No status changes.
