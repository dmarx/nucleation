# QUESTION-tmp1dc6w: Does an independent pre-registered replication, by a team other than the authors, of Shah, Mullainathan and Shafir's Angry Blueberries or a Family Feud borrowing experiment find that poor-budget players borrow a larger share of their budget than rich-budget players and score lower with interest-bearing borrowing than without, while rich-budget players do not, and that poor-budget players spend longer aiming each shot?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-1484 reports that the authors' pre-registered replications kept the
over-borrowing result with smaller effects. They are the authors' own, on
one online pool. The only independent replication, of Study 1, tested the
fatigue effect, which failed.

## What would answer it

A pre-registered replication of a borrowing experiment by another team,
finding the interaction on performance and the longer aiming among the
poor, would answer yes; a null interaction would answer no.

## The condition from THEORY-1458

The working paper's own condition, moved here when the twenty-eighth status review promoted THEORY-1458 under ADR-065. The two readings ask for the same replication, one of the published experiments, the other of the working paper's games, so they share this question.

> An independent pre-registered replication, by a team other than the
> authors, of a borrowing game with budgets of seconds or of guesses
> finds that small-budget players borrow a larger share of their budget
> than large-budget players and score lower when they can borrow with
> interest than when they cannot, while large-budget players do not.
