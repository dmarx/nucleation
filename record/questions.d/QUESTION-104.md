# QUESTION-104: Which partitions of a system remain sufficient, exactly or approximately, when prediction is restricted to a computational budget, and does the budget change which units are worth naming?

## Why it is a question

A14 §3: "The effective units a bounded observer can recognize may
therefore depend on both the target and the observer's resource
constraints." A15 §5: a bounded observer "may have access to a coarse
collective variable that provides reasonably good predictions at low cost",
so under a resource-constrained objective "the collective variable can
become the preferred explanatory unit."

Two things the exchange said should stay out of the answer. A14:
"computational convenience does not establish that the resulting unit
exists independently of the observer." And [CLAIM-154](../claims.d/CLAIM-154.md)'s Occam penalty is a
model-evidence term, not a runtime cost; "We should investigate how those
costs interact, not identify them."

An answer is a system and a family of budgets for which the optimal
sufficient partition provably changes with the budget, or an argument
that it cannot for some class. [CLAIM-302](../claims.d/CLAIM-302.md) supplies the formal reason a
budget can coarsen what is distinguishable at all.
