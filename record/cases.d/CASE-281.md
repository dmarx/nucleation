# CASE-281: Marshmallow task, reliable and unreliable adult

## The case

**The task.** A child may eat one treat now, or wait alone for a larger
reward the experimenter has promised. In the classic experiments by
Walter Mischel and colleagues, A51 §1 relays, children who turned their
attention away from the treat waited longer than those who looked at it,
"demonstrating that waiting depended partly on how the situation was
organized". A names no paper; the attentional finding is Mischel,
Ebbesen and Zeiss, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 21(2),
1972, doi:10.1037/h0032198 (the part-3 ledger).

**The two arms**, A51 §2, after Kidd, Palmeri and Aslin: before the task,
children were given reason to think the experimenter reliable or
unreliable (promised materials were delivered, or not). A's chart: mean
wait 12.03 minutes in the reliable condition, 3.03 in the unreliable, "N
= 28". A's caption: "These are experimentally induced differences in
trust, not evidence of fixed differences in children's self-control."
Unverified by the record until the paper is read.

**Who controls what**, A51 §3:

| Capability | Who controls it? |
|---|---|
| Eat the immediate reward | The child |
| Redirect attention or manage temptation | Primarily the child |
| Establish the terms of the choice | The experimenter |
| Determine when the larger reward becomes available | The experimenter |
| Fulfill the promise of the larger reward | The experimenter |
| Determine whether waiting is worthwhile | Depends partly on both |

**The decision**, A53 §2, at the owner's U24. One reward now, two later,
nothing if the promise fails; p the child's probability that the promise
is kept, δ the value of a delayed reward relative to an immediate one.
Waiting is better when 2pδ > 1. Checked: the expected value of waiting is
2pδ against 1 for eating; at δ = 0.9 waiting pays only above p = 0.556. A:
"This is not a psychological measurement of the child's ability. It's a
model of the decision under uncertainty."

**Three children**, A51 §5, alike in what they do:

- "**The self-governing child:** The child wants the larger reward,
  trusts the promise, and develops a strategy for managing the
  temptation."
- "**The obedient child:** The child waits because they believe the adult
  expects obedience or will disapprove of eating the marshmallow."
- "**The distrustful child:** The child takes the immediate reward because
  they reasonably doubt that the larger reward will materialize."

## What it can show

- **That waiting behaviour does not identify the agency behind it.** A51:
  "these possibilities cannot be distinguished by waiting behavior alone."
  That is [CLAIM-364](../claims.d/CLAIM-364.md)'s form, behaviour alike and organization different,
  with a documented manipulation in place of a stipulation, and it is the
  owner's method ([CLAIM-718](../claims.d/CLAIM-718.md)) applied: A51 §5 calls the task "an
  excellent case for the methodological approach we've been developing".
- **That not waiting can be sound judgment.** "A refusal to delay
  gratification need not indicate a failure of self-government." With the
  inequality, the reliability of the party who controls delivery is a
  term in whether restraint is rational. [CLAIM-744](../claims.d/CLAIM-744.md) grounds on this.
- **That restraint can be organization rather than force** (the Mischel
  arm). [CLAIM-344](../claims.d/CLAIM-344.md)'s resolve, "a maintained organization of precedence,
  commitment and revision", illustrated: A51 §1, "The achievement of
  self-restraint may lie less in the strength of resistance than in the
  organization that makes resistance unnecessary or manageable."

## What it cannot show

- **Anything until read.** The figures are A's relay. Small samples, one
  age group, and a manipulation of the experimenter's reliability, not of
  the child's.
- **That waiting predicts later life.** A51 §6 relays the later work that
  weakened that reading: Watts, Duncan and Quan, *Psychological Science*
  29(7), 2018, doi:10.1177/0956797618761661, and "Sperber and colleagues
  (2024)", 702 participants followed to adulthood, which is Sperber,
  Vandell, Duncan and Watts, *Child Development* 95(6), 2024,
  doi:10.1111/cdev.14129 (the part-3 ledger; A gave no venue). Neither is
  read here.
- **That waiting is the better outcome.** [THEORY-232](../theory.d/THEORY-232.md) reads delayed
  gratification as the mark of a more agential whole. A51 §7 qualifies
  it: "it's important not to equate the later outcome with the better one
  automatically."

## Note of 2026-11-17: The four marshmallow studies, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1963 THEORY-1983 THEORY-1980 THEORY-1989 — new theories of 2026-11-18 and the claim, Proposed -->

Kidd, Palmeri and Aslin 2013 ([NOTE-2036](../notes.d/NOTE-2036.md), [THEORY-1963](../theory.d/THEORY-1963.md)), Mischel, Ebbesen and Zeiss 1972 ([NOTE-2073](../notes.d/NOTE-2073.md), [THEORY-1983](../theory.d/THEORY-1983.md)), Watts, Duncan and Quan 2018 ([NOTE-2041](../notes.d/NOTE-2041.md), [THEORY-1980](../theory.d/THEORY-1980.md)) and Sperber, Vandell, Duncan and Watts 2024 ([NOTE-2074](../notes.d/NOTE-2074.md), [THEORY-1989](../theory.d/THEORY-1989.md)), all read. (1) The reliability arm checks: N = 28, 14 per condition; mean waits "3 min and 2 s (M = 181.57 s)" and "12 min and 2 s (M = 722.43 s)" (§3); the paper's §4 gives "12.03 min" and "3.02 min", so A's 3.03 is the closer rounding of 181.57 s; the ratio is 0.25. A's caption matches the authors' framing, with their caveat that self-control is not shown irrelevant. The first "What it cannot show" bullet, "Anything until read", can be narrowed to the sample: 28 children of one age band, one lab, a 15-minute cap, no bell signal. (2) The attentional arm: the paper is the one the case names, but the attention was redirected by the experimenters (a toy, "think fun"), not by the children, who "chose the place to cover quite randomly before commencing their voluntary delay"; that strengthens the case's "depended partly on how the situation was organized", and the paper adds that the transformations "do not erase or undo the role of the reward contingencies". (3) "That waiting predicts later life": both follow-ups now read, the first finding the association "reduced by two-thirds" with background controls, the second that "Marshmallow Test performance was not strongly predictive of adult achievement, health, or behavior" at 26; the bullet's "Neither is read here" is out of date. The case's `reported_in` may name [LIT-2730](../literature.d/LIT-2730.md) and [LIT-2734](../literature.d/LIT-2734.md), and its standing may move once the owner adopts it. No status changes.
