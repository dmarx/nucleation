# THEORY-446: Organism–environment reciprocal causation, taken diachronically, unrolls into an acyclic sequence of organism, environment and experienced-environment states joined by interventionist arrows; on that representation each niche-construction type has a characteristic causal pattern, and multi-species cases come apart only if organism and environment are kept distinct

## Source

Baedke, Fábregas-Tejeda & Prieto (2021), [LIT-110](../literature.d/LIT-110.md) (*Biology & Philosophy*
36:48), read in full in [NOTE-111](../notes.d/NOTE-111.md). The figures were read from their captions
and the prose walk-throughs.

## What was actually shown

**The diagnosis (pp. 4–12).** Organism–environment reciprocity theories, from
Haeckel, J.S. Haldane, Uexküll and Waddington to current niche construction
theory, fail in two recurring ways: (i) organism and environment are merged,
and (ii) experiential and physical reciprocal causation are not integrated.
Early NCT kept the two apart but ignored experience. Later NCT influenced by
developmental systems theory brought experience in and merged the relata.

**The model (Figs. 2–3, pp. 13–17).** Draw the loop, add causal paths within
each relatum, and unroll it in time. Two ordered state series, O_n and E_n,
are joined by arrows O_n → E_{n+1} and E_n → O_{n+1}. Each arrow is an
interventionist dependency (Woodward 2003). An experienced-environment node
Ex_n sits between E_n and O_n. A change in Ex changes the organism's relation
to its environment without changing the environment's intrinsic properties.

**Characteristic patterns (Figs. 3–4, pp. 17–19).** Constitutive niche
construction (a lion's maturation changes its prey's Ex), mediational
(*Arabidopsis* root architecture), relational (mice piling up in a cold
nest) and relocational (a break in the E-series, for migrating fish) each
get a pattern. Physical niche construction is the coral case.

**Integration (Fig. 5, pp. 20–23).** Bumblebees damaging eggplant leaves is
physical niche construction for the bee and mediational for the plant, so it
needs both types and experience in one diagram. In ruminant herbivory,
microbes do physical niche construction on the rumen and the host does
mediational. Collapse microbe into host, as the holobiont-as-individual view
does, and the case becomes indistinguishable from constitutive niche
construction (p. 22).

**What could have come out otherwise.** A niche-construction type could have
resisted representation in the diagram, or the multi-species cases could have
been drawable with the relata merged. The paper reports neither. It also did
not look for such cases systematically.

## What this does not say

- **That organism and environment are metaphysically separate.** The boundary
  is granted "in order to" trace causes (p. 14). The model "does not develop
  a particular theory of biological boundaries" (fn. 5). An inseparabilist is
  asked to show a practice, not refuted (p. 25).
- **That synchronic co-constitution is wrong.** The model is diachronic and
  Humean by assumption (fn. 3, p. 11). Walsh's (2021) reciprocity is
  reclassified as co-constitution, not answered.
- **That the patterns are complete or unique.** Each type was worked by
  example; there is no proof ([NOTE-111](../notes.d/NOTE-111.md), C5).
- **That selection and niche construction cause each other.** The model has
  no selection node. How a change in Ex alters selection pressures is named
  as open (p. 16). So it neither takes nor answers Hazelwood's objection to
  selection-involving reciprocity ([LIT-156](../literature.d/LIT-156.md)).
- **That the technique is new.** Unrolling a loop into an acyclic graph is
  standard in causal modelling. The novelty is the application and the Ex
  node.
- **That "experience" is analysed.** Plants and microbes experience through a
  sensory–cue interface of "sufficient" specificity (p. 20), left
  unanalysed.

## Connections

- Hazelwood ([LIT-156](../literature.d/LIT-156.md), [THEORY-612](THEORY-612.md)) calls organism–environment reciprocal causation
  "unobjectionable" and targets selection-involving reciprocity. Read
  together, the two papers suggest a division: organism–environment
  reciprocity, unrolled, is tractable; the metaphysical trouble lies where
  selection is a relatum.
- [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md) asks where a combined form's boundary lies relative to a
  target. This account sets the organism–environment boundary relative to a
  causal narrative and gives no criterion that could come out wrong, so it
  does not answer the question. Its ruminant case is a constraint on any
  answer that would merge host and microbes.
- [THEORY-249](THEORY-249.md) also makes a boundary depend on a commitment from outside the
  system. It concerns agency, not causal apportioning.
