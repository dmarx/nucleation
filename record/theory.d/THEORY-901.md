# THEORY-901: As Mancinelli and Bach's systematic review of avoidance learning in healthy humans reports it, rating both A, an avoidance response left available during extinction persists, in some participants for hundreds of unpunished trials, and keeps expectancy of the aversive outcome higher than in people denied it (nine studies); and less effective or costlier responses extinguish faster (six studies, one exception)

## Source

Mancinelli & Bach, [LIT-1477](../literature.d/LIT-1477.md), read in full in [NOTE-1134](../notes.d/NOTE-1134.md) from the
PsyArXiv preprint, version 1 (11 June 2025): pp. 17–20 and 28, and the
table of phenomena (Fig. 4, p. 53). The version of record is closed.

## What was actually shown

The review pools no data; it classifies findings.

- In two older studies with the response available during extinction,
  "42% (Malloy & Levis, 1988) and 47% (Williams & Levis, 1991) of
  participants did not extinguish a unary and costless AR by the 500th
  would-be-unpunished trial" (p. 18).
- Two studies comparing groups with and without the option to avoid
  during extinction found that those allowed to avoid "continue to do so
  and retain higher US expectancies in a follow-up test where avoidance is
  precluded" (p. 18).
- Less effective or costlier responses extinguish faster (Xia et al. 2017;
  Zuj et al. 2020; Rattel et al. 2017, 2020; Dymond et al. 2023, 2025),
  with one exception (Meulders et al. 2016) (p. 19).
- The authors' explanation: "more effective ARs are harder to extinguish
  when the response is not prevented, putatively because effective ARs
  provide fewer opportunities to detect changes in contingency (i.e., the
  absence of punishment when the AR is not performed), causing slower
  learning" (p. 28).

What could have come out otherwise: people could have stopped responding
once the shocks stopped, or their threat expectancy could have fallen as
fast with avoidance available as without.

## What this does not say

- **Anything about anxiety disorders.** Only healthy participants were
  included, mostly students, with shock or loud noise.
- **That the mechanism is unidentifiable evidence.** That is the authors'
  "putatively". The review offers no test that varies the number of
  unavoided trials with effectiveness held fixed.
- **That avoidance strengthens threat belief.** Here it preserves a belief
  that would otherwise extinguish. Raising belief in a stimulus never
  paired with harm is a different phenomenon, [THEORY-920](THEORY-920.md).
- **That the effect is the same with real-world avoidance.** The
  responses are mostly signalled, instructed button presses, which the
  authors rate as their main risk of bias.
