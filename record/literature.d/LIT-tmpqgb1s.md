# LIT-tmpqgb1s: Distributed Morality in an Information Society

Luciano Floridi (2013), *Science and Engineering Ethics* 19(3): 727–743 — DOI-10.1007/s11948-012-9413-4

## Key takeaways

- Morality can be distributed the way knowledge is in epistemic logic. In
  advanced information societies, distributed morality is a feature of
  moral agency that ethics had largely neglected, and "infraethics", the
  morally neutral enablers of moral behaviour, can make good or bad
  behaviour easier or harder.

*Seeded from search-result descriptions of the abstract, not a reading, and not from the abstract itself. What follows is what the work is said to say about itself.*

The paper starts from an analogy with distributed knowledge, a concept from
epistemic logic. It applies the analogy to ethics, and treats distributed
morality as a feature of moral agency that the field had so far neglected.
It considers what distributed morality means for advanced information
societies, and introduces "infraethics" for the morally neutral enablers
that can facilitate or hinder morally good or evil behaviour.

What a deeper reading should check:

- **Distributed knowledge or common knowledge.** The abstract borrows
  distributed knowledge from epistemic logic; faultless responsibility
  ([LIT-tmplkm3o](LIT-tmplkm3o.md)) borrows common knowledge. These are different operators,
  and a reading should say which the analogy needs and whether the later
  paper changed it.
- **Whether distributed morality makes the network an agent.** The record's
  distributed-agency line holds that an organized whole's operative
  intention is produced by systemic mechanisms, not by averaging its
  members' intentions ([CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md)), and leaves open when coordination makes
  an additional agent ([QUESTION-009](../questions.d/QUESTION-009.md)). A reading should say whether this
  paper attributes moral agency to the whole, or only morally loaded
  outcomes to it.
- **Infraethics.** What makes an enabler morally neutral, and whether that
  neutrality survives once the enabler's effects are known.
