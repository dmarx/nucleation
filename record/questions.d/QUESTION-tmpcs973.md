# QUESTION-tmpcs973: Does available avoidance leave threat expectancy uncorrected in proportion to how few unavoided disconfirming trials it allows, in people with anxiety disorders as in healthy controls, and does performing and then losing an unsignalled avoidance response raise threat expectancy for a stimulus never paired with harm, scaling with how much the person avoided?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-901 and THEORY-920 hold what the review reports in healthy
volunteers, with signalled, instructed responses. The authors' mechanism
for protection from extinction is marked "putatively", and every study
of avoidance spreading to the safe stimulus signalled the response's
availability.

## What would answer it

The former promote_when of each, unchanged.

**THEORY-901.** The version of record read and found to rate the same
phenomena; and a study, preferably in people with an anxiety disorder as
well as healthy controls, that varies how effective an available
avoidance response is and measures threat expectancy trial by trial,
showing that more effective avoidance both lasts longer and leaves
threat expectancy less corrected, with the difference carried by the
number of unavoided disconfirming trials. It is refuted if, with
effectiveness varied and response cost and signalling held fixed,
persistence does not depend on effectiveness, or if threat expectancy
falls as fast with avoidance available as without. Further
healthy-volunteer studies with signalled, instructed button responses
cannot settle the clinical reading.

**THEORY-920.** The version of record read and found to report the same;
and a study in which avoidance of a never-punished stimulus is made
available without a signal that could itself indicate danger (a
continuously available, unsignalled response), showing that performing
it, and then losing it, raises threat expectancy for that stimulus, with
the effect scaling with how much the person avoided rather than with
whether the option was offered. It is refuted if, with signalling
removed, offering and then withdrawing avoidance leaves threat
expectancy for a safe stimulus unchanged. Studies that keep the signal
cannot settle it.
