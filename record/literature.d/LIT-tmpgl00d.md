summary: >-
  Nussbaum (2003), DOI-10.1080/1354570022000077926. Nussbaum argues that
  Sen's capability approach yields no theory of social justice until it
  commits to a definite, if revisable, list of central capabilities, each
  owed to every citizen up to a threshold. She sets out her list of ten
  (Life; Bodily Health; Bodily Integrity; Senses, Imagination and Thought;
  Emotions; Practical Reason; Affiliation; Other Species; Play; Control
  over one's Environment). She rejects Sen's talk of freedom as a general
  good because some freedoms are trivial or unjust. And she argues that
  political-liberal respect for pluralism favours a short list of
  capabilities over the promotion of freedom across the board.
