# QUESTION-tmpfw1ib: In a real community whose members' private opinions are measured independently of what they express, does a small, persistently vocal minority go with a large silent share of the majority, and does that silence, rather than conversion or network geometry alone, account for most of the gap between expressed and private opinion?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-242](../theory.d/THEORY-242.md) holds what Vilone and Polizzi's agent-based models show: with private
beliefs fixed, a small hard-core minority can drive 55–60% of agents, nearly
all of the majority, into silence, so that expressed opinion leans far toward
the minority. Whether the mechanism operates in any real community is open.
[QUESTION-261](QUESTION-261.md) asks what happens once such an apparent consensus is exposed; this
asks whether it forms by silence at all.

## What would answer it

An empirical study of a real community that measures members' private opinions
independently of what they express publicly, for example by an anonymous survey
set beside platform posts. It answers yes if a small, persistently vocal
minority goes with a large silent share of the majority, and this silence, not
conversion or network geometry alone, accounts for most of the gap between
expressed and private opinion. Another simulation, with a different decision
rule, cannot settle it: it would show robustness, not that the mechanism
operates. Nor can observational data on expressed opinion alone, which cannot
separate silence from absence.
