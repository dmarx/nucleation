# THEORY-tmppzel6: Harsey and Freyd find, in a vignette experiment with undergraduates, that a perpetrator's DARVO statement, compared with none, leads observers to rate a sexual-assault victim as less believable and more abusive and the perpetrator as more believable, less responsible and less abusive, and lowers their support for punishing him while raising it for punishing her

Harsey and Freyd (2023), [LIT-2710](../literature.d/LIT-2710.md).

## The result

A 2×2 between-subjects design (DARVO or not; insincere apology or not),
235 undergraduates. Every outcome except the victim's rated responsibility
differed between DARVO and no DARVO. Support for punishing the perpetrator
fell from 82% to 60.7%; belief that the victim should not be punished fell
from 92.9% to 66.9%. The effect on the perpetrator's rated responsibility
was small (partial η² = .021).

## What it does not show

- **That DARVO can be recognized from statements.** Ground truth is fixed
  by the design.
- **Anything about a true accusation.** DARVO here calls the accusation
  false.
- **Generality.** One scenario in four versions, one university's
  undergraduates, and a control cell in which the professor said nothing.
