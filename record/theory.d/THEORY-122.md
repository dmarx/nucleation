# THEORY-122: Moral responsibility requires the normative competence to recognise and act on the True and the Good, which identification with one's own motives does not supply

## The evidence is second-hand

**The primary texts are unread.** No lawful copy of Wolf's "Asymmetrical
Freedom", "Sanity and the Metaphysics of Responsibility" or *Freedom Within
Reason* could be found (see their status notes). This account is reported
through the secondary sources named below. It quotes them, and it quotes
nothing from Wolf except where a secondary source quotes her, which is
marked. The owner authorised filing it on this evidence on 2026-10-03.

## Source

- Wolf, "Sanity and the Metaphysics of Responsibility" (1987/88),
  [LIT-690](../literature.d/LIT-690.md), Deferred, unread: the essay in which, by every report, the
  JoJo case and the sanity condition are stated.
- Wolf, *Freedom Within Reason* (1990), [LIT-685](../literature.d/LIT-685.md), Deferred, unread: the
  book-length Reason View.
- McKenna and Coates, SEP *Compatibilism*, [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §4.3, read in [NOTE-270](../notes.d/NOTE-270.md).
- Talbert, SEP *Moral Responsibility*, [LIT-691](../literature.d/LIT-691.md), §§3.1.1 and 3.2, read
  in [NOTE-548](../notes.d/NOTE-548.md).

## What the sources report

**The condition.** McKenna and Coates ([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §4.3) report that for Wolf
"free will concerns an agent's ability to act in accord with the True and
the Good", that "the crucial question for Wolf concerns whether an agent is
able to act upon moral reasons", and that, unlike Frankfurt's, her
conditions "require an anchor external to the agent's internal
psychological states". They cite *Freedom Within Reason* ([LIT-685](../literature.d/LIT-685.md)) and
the papers of 1980 and 1987 ([LIT-688](../literature.d/LIT-688.md), [LIT-690](../literature.d/LIT-690.md)) as where she
develops it.

**Why identification is not enough.** Talbert ([LIT-691](../literature.d/LIT-691.md), §3.1.1) reports
Wolf's case against what she calls Real Self views. The 1987 essay
([LIT-690](../literature.d/LIT-690.md)) calls them deep-self views and names Watson 1975, Frankfurt
1971 and Taylor 1976 as examples.
On such a view an agent is responsible for what issues from her real self,
her will governed by her values or higher-order desires. Wolf grants that
this explains why the hypnotised and the compulsive are excused. Her
objection is that it is silent on how the real self came to be, and that
blame and praise judge the agent herself. That requires, in Talbert's
report of the book (p. 75), not only that she can form her actions on her
values but that she can form her values on the basis of what is True and
Good.

**JoJo.** Talbert ([LIT-691](../literature.d/LIT-691.md), §3.2) gives the case. JoJo, raised by an
evil dictator, becomes the same sadistic tyrant, is "happy to be the sort
of person that he is", and is moved by the desires he wants to be moved by.
He meets the structural conditions. But his upbringing left him "unable to
appreciate the wrongfulness of his behavior", and Talbert says it is "JoJo's
impaired moral competence that does the real excusing work". [LIT-690](../literature.d/LIT-690.md)
records the same reading from Holton's MIT lecture handout, which names
the condition sanity.

**Two conditions, not one.** As [LIT-685](../literature.d/LIT-685.md) records from the openings of
its chapters, the book's account keeps the Real Self View's condition, being
able to govern one's behaviour by one's values, and adds a second, being able
to form and revise those values in the light of the True and the Good. So
identification is necessary on this view, and not sufficient.

Two other read surveys place the view the same way. Dryden's IEP *Autonomy*
([LIT-297](../literature.d/LIT-297.md), [NOTE-266](../notes.d/NOTE-266.md), §2.b) classes Wolf's normative competence as a strong
substantive condition, against content-neutral hierarchical accounts. Buss
and Westlund ([LIT-296](../literature.d/LIT-296.md), [NOTE-267](../notes.d/NOTE-267.md), n. 4) list Wolf, with Fischer and Ravizza,
among reasons-responsive views.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say whether the ability includes being able to do
  otherwise.** Wolf's answer differs for praise and for blame, and that
  answer is [THEORY-118](THEORY-118.md), which carries this account further. This
  account is only the competence condition that both halves share.
- **It does not say identification is unnecessary.** The Reason View adds to
  the Real Self View and does not replace it.
- **It does not locate the defect in JoJo's history.** By the reports, the
  upbringing matters only because it left a present incapacity. That is
  what separates it from a historical condition, and it is what the
  `promote_when` asks a reading to confirm.
- **It does not escape manipulation.** McKenna and Coates ([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §4.3)
  place Wolf "at the same crossroads as is Frankfurt": a manipulator who
  leaves the competence intact produces an agent the view must count
  responsible, or the view must say what is wrong with manipulation. That
  is [THEORY-040](THEORY-040.md)'s forced choice, and this account takes neither branch.
- **It assumes there is a True and a Good to be recognised.** [LIT-690](../literature.d/LIT-690.md)
  reports from Holton that Wolf closes the essay on this worry herself: the
  view assumes a moral objectivity, and may fold rationality too easily into
  truth.
- **It is not a claim about autonomy.** [THEORY-054](THEORY-054.md) places autonomy in
  integration, content-neutrally. JoJo, fully integrated, is the case where
  the two would diverge, but they answer different questions, so no
  `rivals` is declared.

## Relations

**To Fischer and Ravizza ([THEORY-121](THEORY-121.md)): no relation declared.** The two
are close. Both make responsibility a matter of responsiveness to reasons
rather than of identification. Talbert ([LIT-691](../literature.d/LIT-691.md), §2.3) counts Wolf
(1990) among the reasons-responsiveness views, and his §3.2 lists Fischer
and Ravizza (1998) among those who join Wolf in holding that impaired moral
competence undermines responsibility. Fischer and Ravizza's moderate
responsiveness includes receptivity to moral reasons ([LIT-691](../literature.d/LIT-691.md), §2.3),
which is a competence condition of the same kind. I considered three
relations:

- **`rivals`: no.** The scheme's test is that the two cannot both be right.
  This claim and [THEORY-121](THEORY-121.md) can both be true: an agent could need moral
  competence and also need an owned, moderately responsive mechanism. Their
  disagreement is over alternatives for blame, which is the rivalry
  declared between [THEORY-118](THEORY-118.md) and [THEORY-121](THEORY-121.md).
- **`extends`: no.** Neither account is reported to build on the other.
  Fischer's reasons-responsiveness begins in his 1987 chapter ([LIT-687](../literature.d/LIT-687.md)),
  in the same volume as Wolf's "Sanity", and the reports give each its own
  line of descent: Frankfurt cases for Fischer, the critique of deep-self
  views for Wolf. Declaring `extends` would invent a lineage.
- **None.** The kinship is stated here in prose, with its evidence.

**To [THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md): none.** That account says no attitude makes a motive one's
own by its order alone. Wolf's objection is a different one: grant that
JoJo's motives are wholeheartedly his own, and he is still not responsible.
An account could answer the regress and still fail JoJo.

**To [THEORY-040](THEORY-040.md): none.** The Reason View is one of the mesh accounts that
[THEORY-040](THEORY-040.md)'s forced choice covers, as noted above. This claim makes no
assertion about manipulation, so it neither instances nor contradicts
[THEORY-040](THEORY-040.md).

- **Rivals [THEORY-120](THEORY-120.md)**, Frankfurt's hierarchical account, read
  first-hand from 1971 ([LIT-692](../literature.d/LIT-692.md)). On it, JoJo identifies wholeheartedly
  with his desires and so is free. On the Reason View he is not
  responsible, because he lacks normative competence.
