# ADR-064: Copies of published articles on course, faculty and scholarly third-party pages count as lawful copies to read

## Context

[ADR-053](ADR-053.md) lists cases that stay `Unavailable`. One of them is "a copy the
owner could not lawfully use", and that case includes "an upload whose
uploader is unknown". The readers on 2026-10-29 were also briefed to
refuse "uploads of unknown provenance". They applied the rule unevenly:

- **Two works were read from copies of their published versions** on a
  course or group page: Jasanoff ([LIT-1594](../literature.d/LIT-1594.md)), from a University of Colorado
  course page, and PBFT ([LIT-1217](../literature.d/LIT-1217.md)), from MIT PDOS's 6.824 page.
- **Ten works were left `Unavailable` or `Unfetched` because the same kind
  of copy was turned down:** [LIT-1208](../literature.d/LIT-1208.md), [LIT-1214](../literature.d/LIT-1214.md), [LIT-1220](../literature.d/LIT-1220.md), [LIT-1398](../literature.d/LIT-1398.md),
  [LIT-1405](../literature.d/LIT-1405.md), [LIT-1406](../literature.d/LIT-1406.md), [LIT-1408](../literature.d/LIT-1408.md), [LIT-1410](../literature.d/LIT-1410.md), [LIT-1413](../literature.d/LIT-1413.md) and [LIT-1445](../literature.d/LIT-1445.md). The
  copies sat on MIT, University of Washington, Princeton and TU Wien course
  pages, on hedweb.com and rangevoting.org, and on academia.edu.

The curation entry of that day put the inconsistency to the owner. The
owner answered: "let's go find and read the stuff that they mistakenly
turned down."

## Decision

- **These copies count.** Each is a copy of a published article, or of an
  author's manuscript, that a work can be read from:
  - a university course or reading-list page;
  - a faculty or research-group page;
  - a scholarly third-party site that hosts papers for discussion;
  - an author's own upload, as on academia.edu or ResearchGate.

  The reading's NOTE names the exact copy, and the reader checks that it
  is the work: its title, authors and pages.
- **These still do not count.** Shadow libraries and ebook-piracy sites
  (Sci-Hub, LibGen, Anna's Archive, epdf.pub and the like), and uploads of
  whole in-copyright books, such as a trade book on the Internet Archive or
  a press's book on a file host. These are what the earlier refusals of
  Kahneman ([LIT-1209](../literature.d/LIT-1209.md)) and Herd & Moynihan ([LIT-1609](../literature.d/LIT-1609.md)) turned on, and those
  refusals stand.
- **[ADR-053](ADR-053.md) narrows.** Its rule that "a copy the owner could not lawfully
  use stays Unavailable" stands. Its example of "an upload whose uploader is
  unknown" now covers only the cases in the second bullet above.

## Alternatives

- **Keep the strict reading, and send these copies to the owner as
  `Unfetched`.** This is what the first pass did for some of them. It
  makes the owner fetch, by hand, a copy the client already holds. Those
  are the clicks [ADR-053](ADR-053.md) was written to cut down.
- **Accept any copy that can be found.** Rejected. A shadow library or a
  pirated book is not something the record should rest on, and the owner
  asked only for the course-page kind.

## Consequences

- The ten works are read again on 2026-10-29, from the copies the first
  pass turned down. Each moves by status, with a history item citing this
  decision.
- [LIT-1594](../literature.d/LIT-1594.md) and [LIT-1217](../literature.d/LIT-1217.md) stand as read.
- Reading briefs say this, as [ADR-053](ADR-053.md) asks of its own rule.
