  in One- or Two-Dimensional Isotropic Heisenberg Models, Physical Review
  Letters 17, 1133, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.17.1133. Unread; known
  through Halperin (LIT-tmp3zns2), Gelfert and Nolting (LIT-tmpbgf6y) and
  Nachtergaele (LIT-tmp25uib). Proves that quantum Heisenberg ferro- and
  antiferromagnets on one- and two-dimensional lattices (and U(1)
  magnets with in-plane order) have no spontaneous magnetization at any
  T > 0 when the exchange has a finite second moment: Bogoliubov's
  inequality bounds the order parameter by an integral like ∫d^Dk/k²,
  which diverges in D ≤ 2 as the symmetry-breaking field goes to zero.
  Hohenberg's parallel proof for continuum bosons and fermions makes it
  the Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem. Here as the exchange's contrast
  with flocking (A27).

# LIT-2985: Absence of Ferromagnetism or Antiferromagnetism in One- or Two-Dimensional Isotropic Heisenberg Models

Mermin & Wagner (1966). Physical Review Letters 17, 1133. DOI
10.1103/PhysRevLett.17.1133.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws"
(2026-11-20 to 21), where A names it at A27 for the Mermin-Wagner
theorem, "no continuous symmetry breaking in two dimensions at finite
temperature". Not read: Unavailable since 2026-11-22 (paywalled; no
lawful copy found).

That gloss overstates the 1966 paper. By Halperin's account it is "a
similar proof of the absence of long-range order in one and two
dimensions for a Heisenberg ferromagnet or antiferromagnet, with SU(2)
symmetry, or a magnetic system with U(1) symmetry and an order parameter
perpendicular to the symmetry axis"; the statement for "any continuous
symmetry" is the later family (Nachtergaele calls it a gloss of "the
original theorem"), and the family itself excludes orientational order in
a 2D crystal, whose rotation generator "cannot be written as an
unweighted sum of local operators" (Halperin §II).

Three neighbours stand in for it (ADR-114), and how far they reach
matters:

- **Halperin (2018), LIT-tmp3zns2** (NOTE-tmpuwmny, THEORY-tmpqma2p): the
  scope, yes: model, symmetry, the finite-second-moment condition, the
  equilibrium premise and what the theorem does not say
  (quasi-long-range order, T = 0, orientational order). The proof, no.
- **Gelfert and Nolting (2001), LIT-tmpbgf6y** (NOTE-tmppfzap,
  THEORY-tmp1nvjs): the proof's steps, as an outline (Bogoliubov's
  inequality, the operator choice, the logarithmic bound), with the
  final bounds stated "as has been shown by Mermin and Wagner", not
  re-derived; and Cassi's extension to graphs of spectral dimension at
  most 2, at second hand.
- **Nachtergaele (2006), LIT-tmp25uib** (NOTE-tmpl5lm3, THEORY-tmp85ij4):
  only the statement, by a different, more general proof that does not
  need translation invariance.

The paper's own wording, constants and erratum remain unchecked.
CLAIM-841 cites it for the 2D infrared logarithm, which the neighbours
bear out as the integral the proof rests on; its XY row rests on
Kosterlitz and Thouless (LIT-2969) and Kosterlitz (1974), LIT-tmph3tia,
not on this paper, which "sheds no light" on quasi-long-range order
(Halperin). CLAIM-842's graph version is Cassi's (LIT-tmpu2kk5), not this
paper's (ARG-tmpx5evn).
