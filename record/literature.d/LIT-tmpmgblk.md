# LIT-tmpmgblk: Transformation transformed

Philippe Descola (2016), *HAU: Journal of Ethnographic Theory*
6(3):33–44 — DOI-10.14318/hau6.3.005

## Key takeaways

- **Method and doctrine apart.** Structural anthropology is both a method,
  from structural linguistics, and Lévi-Strauss's view of social life
  (exchange of signs, the unconscious, a monist epistemology). The method
  can be used without the doctrine; Descola rejects the semiological
  emphasis (it keeps nonhumans passive) and the deduction of laws of mind
  from institutions.
- **What the method is.** Isolate a set of phenomena; look for relations
  among elements defined "not by intrinsic properties but by their
  position vis-à-vis one another"; order the possible combinations in a
  table of permutations. The model is "a heuristic device which provides
  the syntax of the transformations" from variant to variant, not a
  faithful description. After Pouillon: sets are brought together "in
  virtue of their differences". "No human phenomenon has a meaning in
  itself."
- **Structure versus system.** "A structure is not a system": a structure
  exists only where invariant relations among the elements and relations
  of various sets allow moving from one set to another by a
  transformation. Transformation is "the keystone" of the analysis.
- **Two regimes.** Goethean: the variants are developments of an initial
  form, as the forms of marriage in *Les structures élémentaires* develop
  the principle of reciprocity from restricted to generalized exchange.
  Thompsonian: continuous deformation between already given forms, after
  D'Arcy Thompson's grids, which Lévi-Strauss cites for myth. But myths
  differ from organisms: the group of a myth's transformations is a
  virtual continuum on which the analyst performs arbitrary cuts, choosing
  the mythemes, so "each analyst will trace the path of transformation
  which his or her fancy will suggest".
- **Ontologies as a group.** Descola's animism, totemism, naturalism and
  analogism are the four combinations of similar or different interiority
  and physicality, a Goethean group in which no variant, naturalism
  included, is privileged. Structural comparison is the fairest
  "symmetrization" of observer and observed, though it rests on a demand
  for worldwide data only the West has so far produced.
