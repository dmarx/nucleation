# NOTE-1176: Blei, Griffiths and Jordan — The nested Chinese restaurant process and hLDA

## Contribution

It defines the nested Chinese restaurant process (nCRP), a prior on trees
of unbounded depth and branching, and uses it as the prior of hierarchical
LDA (hLDA), a topic model whose topics are arranged in a tree that is
inferred, not fixed. It gives a collapsed Gibbs sampler that resamples each
document's path as a block, and each word's level, including new branches
and new levels. The finite-depth model was introduced earlier, in Blei,
Griffiths, Jordan and Tenenbaum (NIPS 2003). This paper gives the
infinite-depth version, hyperparameter inference, and the empirical
comparison with LDA.

## Key insight

A tree is a nested sequence of partitions, so a CRP at every node gives a
distribution on trees with the CRP's preferential attachment and
exchangeability. If a document is a path, and the root lies on every
path, the posterior pushes words that every document uses toward the root
and words that a few documents share toward deeper nodes. Abstraction is
not defined. It is read off where in the tree a word's usage is shared:
"the internal nodes are not summaries of their children. Rather, the
internal nodes reflect the shared terminology of the documents assigned to
the paths that contain them." The cost of this is that a document is one
path, so "a document can only access the topics that lie along a single
path in the tree."

## Assumptions

- Bag of words: each document is a multiset of word tokens over a fixed
  vocabulary.
- **nCRP(γ).** At each node a document picks an existing child with
  probability nᵢ/(γ + n − 1) and a new child with γ/(γ + n − 1), where n
  counts the documents through that node before it (Eq. 1 applied at
  every level). Each node carries a topic βₖ ~ Dirichlet(η).
- **One path per document.** cᵈ ~ nCRP(γ); every word of document d is
  drawn from a topic on cᵈ.
- **Levels.** θᵈ ~ GEM(m, π), the stick-breaking distribution with sticks
  Vᵢ ~ Beta(mπ, (1 − m)π), m ∈ (0, 1), π > 0 (§2.4); each word takes level
  zᵈ,ₙ ~ Mult(θᵈ), then w ~ Mult(β at that level of cᵈ).
- Hyperparameters either fixed (§6.2) or given priors m ~ Beta, π ~
  Exponential, γ ~ Gamma, η ~ Exponential and sampled by Metropolis–Hastings
  steps between Gibbs sweeps (§5.4).
- In §6.1 and §6.2 the levels are truncated at three, with a Dirichlet on
  the three proportions instead of the GEM. §6.3 uses the infinite-depth
  model.

## Key results

- **The prior.** After M documents the paths form a random subtree "with a
  branching factor of at most M at all nodes". The CRP's exchangeability
  carries over: the probability of a set of paths does not depend on the
  order in which the documents arrived (§3). Re-checked here for five
  depth-two paths: all 120 orders give the same probability.
- **The sampler (§5).** Collapsed Gibbs over paths cᵈ and levels zᵈ,ₙ, with
  topics and level proportions integrated out. The path conditional (Eq. 4)
  is the nCRP prior times a ratio of Dirichlet normalizers, over existing
  leaves and new branches at every internal node, drawn as a block. Levels
  deeper than any in use are reached by a sequence of Bernoulli steps. No
  convergence-rate result: "it is difficult to say something more
  definitive about the speed of the algorithm". The mode is approximated by
  the sampled state of highest log joint probability (Eq. 5), and restarts
  choose among local modes.
- **Simulated data (§6.1, Fig. 6).** Ten corpora drawn from hLDA itself,
  each 100 documents of 250 words, vocabulary 100, η = 0.005, γ = 1, three
  levels. "We exactly recover the correct hierarchies, with only two
  errors": in one, a single wrongly allocated path; in the other, "the
  inferred mode has higher posterior probability than the true tree
  structure (due to finite data)".
- **Real corpora (§6.2).** With η = (2.0, 1.0, 0.5) by level, γ = 1.0, and
  the GEM parameters "fixed at m = 100 and π = 0.5": JACM, 536 abstracts
  1987–2004, 68K words, a 25-topic tree (Fig. 1) with function words at the
  root and subfields at level two; Psychological Review, 1,272 abstracts
  1967–2003, a 52-topic tree (Fig. 7); PNAS, 12,913 abstracts 1991–2001,
  2.3M words, 56 topics with a smaller γ (Fig. 8). Judged by inspection
  only. The Gibbs sampler took about 0.001 s per document per iteration for
  JACM and Psychological Review, 0.006 s for PNAS.
- **Against LDA (§6.3, Fig. 9).** On JACM, five-fold cross-validation, all
  hyperparameters integrated out, held-out likelihood approximated by the
  harmonic mean of 800 inner samples for each of 100 outer samples. The
  posterior over hLDA's topic count has its mode near 140. At every LDA
  topic count in the bracketing range, hLDA's held-out likelihood was
  higher. The authors expect the reverse for large topic counts: "we
  eventually expect LDA to dominate hLDA for large numbers of topics."

## Concepts

- **Nested CRP**: an infinite collection of CRPs, one per node, each table
  pointing to a child restaurant; a customer's path is the sequence of
  tables chosen on successive nights.
- **Path cᵈ**: the root-to-leaf sequence of nodes a document visits.
- **Level allocation zᵈ,ₙ**: which node on the document's path generated
  word n.
- **GEM(m, π)**: two-parameter stick-breaking with sticks Beta(mπ, (1 − m)π),
  so that m sets the mean stick and π how tightly documents keep to it.
- **Abstraction**: not defined formally; "a statistical procedure that
  allows a system designer to capture notions of abstraction that are
  reflected in usage patterns of the specific corpus at hand".

## Connections

It builds on the CRP (Aldous), stick-breaking and the GEM distribution
(Pitman; Sethuraman), and the Dirichlet process mixture (Antoniak;
Ferguson), and extends LDA (Blei, Ng and Jordan 2003) and its collapsed
Gibbs sampler (Griffiths and Steyvers 2004). It contrasts itself with
hierarchical clustering, whose internal nodes summarize their children,
and with supervised placement of documents into a given hierarchy. It
names as open the variant in which "each document exhibits multiple
paths through the tree", which the nested HDP of [LIT-002](../literature.d/LIT-002.md) ([NOTE-023](NOTE-023.md))
supplies. It names the author-topic model ([LIT-1546](../literature.d/LIT-1546.md)) as adaptable to a
flexible hierarchy, and cites the mixed membership blockmodel
([LIT-1553](../literature.d/LIT-1553.md)) among flat mixed-membership models of non-text data. It
mentions the Indian buffet process (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2006) as the
parallel line of "multiple binary features rather than clusters".

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), condition 2 (overlap).** T21 said that "classical
  hLDA assigns each document one path through a tree", and the paper
  confirms it in its own words ([THEORY-969](../theory.d/THEORY-969.md)). If a document is a
  member's activity, hLDA can place a member under several topics only as
  a chain of ever more specific ones. A member who takes part in two
  sibling branches cannot be represented. That is a partition at every
  level, not an overlap. T21's move to the nested HDP ([LIT-002](../literature.d/LIT-002.md)) is the
  paper's own §4 suggestion.
- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), "without their number being fixed in advance".**
  The nCRP does leave the number of topics open, but the paper is plain
  that the hyperparameters shape the tree, and its readable trees use
  hyperparameters chosen for readability (§6.2). Leaving the number open
  moves the choice into γ, η and the GEM, and does not remove it. That
  bears on [CLAIM-188](../claims.d/CLAIM-188.md)'s price of naming a recovered unit.
- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), "What would not answer it".** §6.1 is exactly the
  test that the question sets aside: recovering the prior's structure from
  data generated under that prior. Eight of ten trees came back exactly
  ([THEORY-1010](../theory.d/THEORY-1010.md)). That shows the sampler can invert the model. It says
  nothing about whether a tree is in data the model did not generate. The
  one failure "due to finite data", a wrong tree with higher posterior
  than the true one, is a small-sample identifiability failure, and the
  authors' own caveat names the condition: "how identifiable the topics
  are". The paper has no identifiability theorem for trees or topics.
  [CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md)'s second step is not reached for hLDA by anything in it.
- **[QUESTION-276](../questions.d/QUESTION-276.md), T21's "allow the hierarchy to lose".** §6.3 is the
  paper's own test of whether the hierarchy earns its constraint, and it
  passes only in a range: near its own topic count on one corpus, with an
  estimator now known to be unreliable ([THEORY-989](../theory.d/THEORY-989.md), Proposed). The
  authors expect a flat model to win when the topic count is free. A
  hierarchy over members' messages would need its own such test against a
  flat mixed-membership model, which is what T21 asked for.
- **[CLAIM-454](../claims.d/CLAIM-454.md).** An hLDA topic is a distribution over words and
  nothing more. The paper's "abstraction" is shared usage, by definition.
  A tree of what members say is at most a constituent in that claim's
  sense, and the paper itself claims no more. T21's falsification test
  (two organizations with identical messages and different authorization
  pathways) follows from the likelihood: hLDA's depends only on the words,
  so it must give both organizations the same posterior.
- **[CASE-166](../cases.d/CASE-166.md).** Its analyzer fixed the number of factors. hLDA does
  not, but the trees of §6.2 were read by eye. The same objection that
  [CLAIM-427](../claims.d/CLAIM-427.md) makes to recovering modes in the framework's own terms applies
  to reading a tree's levels as levels of an organization.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **§5.1 does not match §2.4.** §2.4 defines GEM(m, π) with sticks
  Vᵢ ~ Beta(mπ, (1 − m)π), so E[Vᵢ] = m. §5.1's conditional for a level
  k ≤ max(zᵈ,₋ₙ) is printed with factors ((1 − m)π + #[z = k])/(π + #[z ≥ k])
  and (mπ + #[z > j])/(π + #[z ≥ j]). That is the posterior predictive for
  sticks Beta((1 − m)π, mπ). With §2.4's sticks the factors are
  (mπ + #[z = k])/(π + #[z ≥ k]) and ((1 − m)π + #[z > j])/(π + #[z ≥ j]).
  Checked by Monte Carlo at m = 0.3, π = 4 with seven observed levels: the
  corrected form gives 0.473, 0.241, 0.126, 0.048 for the first four
  levels, against Monte Carlo 0.473, 0.240, 0.126, 0.048, and the printed
  form gives 0.618, 0.262, 0.091, 0.020. The stop probability for new
  levels, printed as (1 − m) times the word likelihood, follows the printed
  convention too.
- **Hyperparameters out of range.** §6.2 fixes "m = 100 and π = 0.5",
  though §2.4 requires m ∈ (0, 1). The text before it says "If we set π to
  be large (e.g., π = 0.5)" and "Setting m to be large (e.g., m = 100)",
  which reads as m and π swapped. The likely intent (mean stick 0.5,
  concentration 100) is the reader's guess. The JACM text may differ.
- **No identifiability result.** Neither for the tree nor for the topics.
  §6.1's success is on sparse, well-separated topics (η = 0.005), and the
  authors say trees "will not be as easy to identify in data sets which
  exhibit polysemy and similarity between topics."
- **No mixing guarantee.** The mode is the best of the sampled states, and
  the authors defend local modes as useful representations: "a local mode
  of the posterior found by Gibbs sampling often provides such a
  representation."
- **The harmonic-mean estimator.** §6.3's held-out likelihoods use it
  (Kass and Raftery). It is known to be unstable and biased, and it can
  have infinite variance, so the margin in Fig. 9 is less secure than it
  looks. One corpus only.
- **Interpretability by eye.** C6 rests on ten LDA topics against the
  hLDA trees, with no protocol, and LDA was run without removing function
  words.
- **Not the typeset paper.** The arXiv v3 text was read; the JACM version
  was not compared.

## Open questions

- Under what conditions on the topics and the data are an hLDA tree and
  its topics identifiable, up to relabelling of siblings? The paper
  supplies a condition in words (separation of topics, enough data) and no
  result.
- Does a multiple-path model (the nested HDP) keep §6.3's predictive
  advantage over a flat model when the topic count is free? [LIT-002](../literature.d/LIT-002.md)'s
  reading ([NOTE-023](NOTE-023.md)) has the variational comparison, not this one.
- With a reliable estimator of held-out likelihood, does the JACM ordering
  in Fig. 9 survive?
