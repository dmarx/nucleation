# CLAIM-328: When the continuities that ordinarily come bundled in a collective (legal franchise, civic role, interpersonal transmission, narrative and place) are separated, several later collectives can each be a genuine successor of the earlier one in a different respect, without any of them, or the earlier collective, having branched in numerical identity

## The claim

A4's H5: "When a collective's institutional, geographical, genealogical,
and cultural continuity relations diverge, multiple later collectives can
possess genuine but different relations of succession to the earlier
collective, without all being numerically identical to it."

The respects, from the Senators case ([CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md)):

| respect | what carries it | successor of the original Senators |
|---|---|---|
| franchise | the league's legal succession | the Minnesota Twins |
| civic role | the city's place in the league | the expansion Senators |
| interpersonal transmission | supporters, families, the press | divided, unknown |
| narrative and place | the city's baseball history | the 2005 Nationals, by claim |

Ordinarily the respects coincide, which hides them; A2: a relocation "acts
as an intervention that separates them". A2 also drew an asymmetry between
them: a league can designate the franchise successor by convention, but
"No comparable authority can compel all supporters to adopt an authorized
successor allegiance". Franchise succession is "conventional and
official"; fandom succession is "distributed and negotiated".

## What it does not say

- **That identity branches.** A4: "Multiple successors do not imply that
  numerical identity itself branches. The weaker and more defensible
  claim concerns plural successor relations." Which successor relation,
  if any, is persistence is [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md); [CLAIM-329](CLAIM-329.md) holds that
  persistence through turnover needs causal transmission of the producing
  organization, which is the interpersonal respect.
- **That resemblance makes a successor.** A1: "two disconnected
  populations might share almost identical practices without constituting
  the same fandom."
- **How supporters actually divided.** A5 §6 advised "Case first", and the
  record holds one documented case. The claim was promoted at the owner's
  word with that standing; a second case, or supporter data on the first,
  is what would move it.
- **The textual case.** [CLAIM-002](CLAIM-002.md)'s family of relations for retellings is
  the analogue for texts, which are copied rather than having their
  members replaced.

## Note of 2026-10-27: a second case of the respects coming apart

The claim's last point says that a second case, or supporter data on the
first, is what would move it. The exchange of 2026-10-26 offered one.

At USIP ([CASE-tmps5qp3](../cases.d/CASE-tmps5qp3.md)), A5 found juridical identity, the building and a
name (later changed) continuing while personnel, leadership, programs,
procedures and partner relations did not: "What persists legally need not
be what persists functionally." A5 cited the claim, and read it in its
weak form: "Nothing requires calling both numerically identical to the
original USIP."

It is a second case of the respects coming apart, by a takeover and not a
relocation. It is not yet a case of plural succession: A5's second
successor, a new body of former staff carrying on the mediation work, was
put "hypothetically". Whether former staff carried the practice elsewhere
is A5's test ("Subsequent employment, collaboration, and peacebuilding work
of former USIP personnel"). The claim is not moved yet. The Chagossian
diaspora ([CASE-tmpelq9w](../cases.d/CASE-tmpelq9w.md)) may be a third, with associations in three
countries each continuing a part.
