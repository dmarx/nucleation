# THEORY-tmp68j1u: In a conservative system a soliton is a solitary wave, held by a balance between nonlinearity and dispersion, that keeps its shape at constant speed and emerges from a collision with another soliton with its amplitude, shape and velocity unchanged except for a phase shift; the name and the numerical discovery come from Zabusky and Kruskal's 1965 solutions of the KdV equation, in which solitons arose from a sine-wave initial condition

## Source

Zabusky & Porter (2010), LIT-tmpun6ep; read in NOTE-tmpb86f1. The read
neighbour of Zabusky & Kruskal (1965), LIT-tmpfx0lb, unread, and cited
beside Kivshar & Malomed (1989), LIT-tmpzah9i, unread.

## What was actually shown

- **The definition** (lead): a solitary wave "arises from a balance between
  nonlinear and dispersive effects"; a soliton "must maintain its shape when
  it moves at constant speed" and "emerges from the "collision" unchanged
  except possibly for a phase shift"; "for a conservative
  (non-dissipative) system", its "amplitude, shape, and velocity are
  conserved after a collision".
- **An illustration** (Figs. 1–2): a two-soliton KdV run from the article's
  own code, with the phase shift visible.
- **The history** (§1, Fig. 3): Zabusky and Kruskal "published their
  numerical solutions of the KdV equation (and invented the term
  "soliton")"; a sine-wave initial condition gives "solitons that arise from
  the initial condition", with near-recurrence. Inverse scattering (1967)
  then solved the KdV initial-value problem.

The evidence is an authored review with illustrative simulations; no proof
or measurement is given in the article.

## What this does not say

- That a solitary wave of a nonintegrable equation keeps its identity in
  collision: the relatives "do not in general have similar interaction
  properties as solitons".
- That a soliton persists under loss: the definition is for a conservative
  system. With loss it decays (Grimshaw, Smyth & Stepanyants 2018).
- Anything about dissipative or topological solitons beyond naming them:
  no thresholds, hysteresis or protection.
- That the soliton's identity is constitutive in TERM-088's sense: it is
  dynamical, the conserved parameters, with no upkeep.
