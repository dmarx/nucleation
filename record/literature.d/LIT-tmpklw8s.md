summary: >-
  Tarsney (2023). The epistemic challenge to longtermism: our ability to predictably affect the future may fade fast enough that far-future effects do not dominate. He models a $1 million longtermist intervention (existential risk reduction) whose effect lasts until an "exogenous nullifying event", arriving at a constant rate r, and compares it with a global-health benchmark. With deliberately conservative inputs, longtermism wins only if r is below about 0.000135 per year when humanity settles the stars, or about 0.000000012 per year when it stays on Earth. Mixing in small credences in space settlement, a low r, fast settlement or very efficient conversion of stars into value makes longtermism win by orders of magnitude. But on some plausible views that win rests on minuscule probabilities of astronomical payoffs, so the case depends either on non-obvious empirical claims or on tolerating Pascalian fanaticism. Even where longtermism wins, most of its expected value may fall within thousands or millions of years.
