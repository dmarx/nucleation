# QUESTION-072: Does a project memory distribute credibility among an argument's contributors, by making some contributors' moves addressable and leaving others' as unfiled turns, so that what gets a code gets cited and what does not is re-derived or lost?

## The question

[CLAIM-182](../claims.d/CLAIM-182.md) says storage is not anchoring and addressability is: a move that
gets a code can be pointed at, cited and built on, and a move that stays a
turn in a transcript cannot. [CLAIM-181](../claims.d/CLAIM-181.md) says the record's closed grammar
decides what can be filed at all. Who files, and in whose terms, then
decides whose moves become anchors.

The question has two parts:

1. **Does the asymmetry occur?** In this record, the owner's words are
   quoted and attributed, and the assistant's readings are marked as
   readings. Is a move's chance of being filed independent of who made it,
   once its content is held fixed? [CLAIM-177](../claims.d/CLAIM-177.md) records one case where
   attribution mattered: the owner, not the assistant, reintroduced an
   analogy that had been rejected.
2. **If it occurs, is it a wrong or only a bias?** Fricker ([LIT-1004](../literature.d/LIT-1004.md))
   defines testimonial injustice as a credibility deficit caused by
   identity prejudice. She defines hermeneutical injustice as a gap in
   shared interpretive resources that traces to marginalisation. A
   contributor whose kind of move fits no scheme is in the second
   position: the move is not disbelieved but cannot be expressed in the
   record's terms. Whether that is injustice, or a design cost that falls
   on no one in particular, depends on whether the gap tracks who the
   contributor is.

## Why the record needs it

- **[CLAIM-179](../claims.d/CLAIM-179.md)** says agent filing moves the cost of capture onto the party
  that would otherwise re-derive. If the filer is also the party whose
  framing the record adopts, the cost moves and so does the standing.
- **[CLAIM-181](../claims.d/CLAIM-181.md)** is the formalization counter. This question asks who bears
  what that counter predicts.
- The record's evaluation counter (anchoring measures computed in the
  record's own grammar) has a social form here: the measures would count
  the filed contributor's moves.

## What would answer it

Two kinds of evidence would bear on it:

- **A count:** in this record's workbench reconstructions, for moves of
  comparable content, the share filed by contributor and by kind of move.
- **A case:** a move that fit no scheme, was lost or re-derived, and
  whose loss traces to how the grammar was chosen, and not to the move's
  merit.

A reading of Fricker would say whether the second case meets her
definition. That reading has not been done.
