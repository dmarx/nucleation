# TERM-023: will-organization

## Definition

> the maintained organization of relations governing the conditions under
> which an agent's available action-generating regimes acquire, retain, lose,
> or alter practical efficacy.

Agent-indexed: it is a role in an agent already warranted as one. Functional,
not additive: not another desire, an inner executive, or a causal substance.
Its dimensions are the efficacy relations (accessibility, precedence,
persistence) and the meta-regulatory ones ([TERM-017](TERM-017.md), plus transformation).

## What it is not

Not the operative will, which is whichever regime currently acts. Not
[TERM-003](TERM-003.md), which it replaced. The manuscript states it three times in
slightly different words (Sections 1, 3.2, 7.10); this entry is the one the
claims cite, so that a fourth wording would be a new version here and not a
silent drift.

## Note of 2026-11-01: Metzinger's M-autonomy, a mental-scale counterpart


Metzinger's work on mental autonomy ([LIT-1942](../literature.d/LIT-1942.md), [LIT-1941](../literature.d/LIT-1941.md),
[LIT-1945](../literature.d/LIT-1945.md)) has this term's shape at the scale of one mind's thinking.
M-autonomy is "the capacity for second-order mental action" ([LIT-1945](../literature.d/LIT-1945.md),
p. 4), and its parts line up with the term's dimensions: veto, "the
intentional inhibition, suspension, or termination of an ongoing process",
is suspension; "goal-permanence" is persistence; frontal control that
"latch[es] onto the best candidate" among the thoughts mind-wandering
offers is precedence; and the ability "to actively sculpt its own internal
affordance landscape" is transformation ([LIT-1941](../literature.d/LIT-1941.md), p. 276;
[LIT-1945](../literature.d/LIT-1945.md), pp. 12–13). Its cyclic loss in mind-wandering is, on the
2015 reading, a change in the efficacy of a capacity the agent keeps, not
in the capacity ([THEORY-1300](../theory.d/THEORY-1300.md)). Same structure, not shown same
mechanism; [ARG-051](../arguments.d/ARG-051.md) holds the verdict, Proposed.

Two points bear on the definition itself.

- **The agent index survives.** Metzinger makes the first-person
  perspective and "certain necessary conditions of personhood" "rare,
  graded, and dynamically variable" ([LIT-1941](../literature.d/LIT-1941.md), p. 295), which might
  seem to remove the agent this term is indexed to during each lapse. He
  keeps it: "We are still persons, because we have the relevant potential"
  (p. 282). The agent is warranted across the lapse; only the efficacy of
  one of its capacities lapses. That is how this term is meant to be read.
- **A condition the term does not list.** Metzinger's condition of efficacy
  is the agent's representation of its own capacity in its self-model
  ([LIT-1941](../literature.d/LIT-1941.md), p. 280). The term's relations hold among regimes. Whether
  such a representation is one of the maintained relations, or only
  accompanies them, is [QUESTION-367](../questions.d/QUESTION-367.md). If it is, a fourth wording of the
  definition would be needed, as a new version here.

No status, title or warrant changes.

## Note of 2026-11-07: will-organization is not a higher-order disposition, from the second share of "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"

A55 §3 of the second share of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (2026-11-06 to 07; https://chatgpt.com/share/6aeea2bc-d280-83e9-ac1f-f6772adf263c), answering the owner's U25, proposes a clarification here: "A will-organization can realize standing dispositions to inhibit, enable, prioritize, suspend, or transform the efficacy of action-generating regimes. But its definition concerns the actual maintained causal organization of efficacy-governing relationships, not merely the existence of higher-order dispositions." That is already this term's "maintained organization", and its "not another desire, an inner executive, or a causal substance". A52 §8's regress, "we should not then insist that every such disposition requires another disposition governing it, indefinitely", is answered in [CLAIM-424](../claims.d/CLAIM-424.md) ("There need not be one reviser"). A55's point that "a latent disposition to veto and an actually effective veto mechanism are different things" is [CLAIM-047](../claims.d/CLAIM-047.md)'s, and [CLAIM-421](../claims.d/CLAIM-421.md)'s for perspectives. Cited rightly; it adds nothing, and the term is unchanged.
