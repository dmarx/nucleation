# THEORY-1496: When two Food for Peace projects in India handed their activities to Government of India programmes, activities continued two years after exit where the receiving programme had dedicated resources, functioning infrastructure and a legal commitment and the project's linkage to it was operating before exit, and stopped where the linkage was begun late; results often varied between states by more than they changed over time

## Source

Rogers, Klemeyer, Bondre & Fierstein (2017), [LIT-2148](../literature.d/LIT-2148.md); read in [NOTE-1655](../notes.d/NOTE-1655.md).

## What was actually shown

- **The receiver's factors** (p. 5): the programmes combined "resources
  dedicated to the programs", "functional infrastructure" (capacity) and
  "a legal commitment to provide services (motivation)".
- **Continued** (pp. 3–4): institutional deliveries in CRS areas "rose
  from 20 percent to 76 percent between baseline in 2002 and the end of
  the POP in 2010, continued to increase to 87 percent at follow-up in
  2012"; irrigated land "from 8.7 percent at POP endline in 2010 to 21.7
  percent at follow-up in 2012".
- **Stopped** (p. 5): "where linkages had not been clearly established",
  "the activities did not continue post-project"; "some schools did not
  start the application process until close to the end of the POP".
- **States** (p. 2): "differences across states were often larger than
  any" endline-to-follow-up "change within a given state".
- It could have come out otherwise: activities handed to well-resourced
  programmes could have lapsed, or the late-linked schools could have
  kept their meals.

## What this does not say

- That the linkage's timing caused the difference: one late case (school
  meals, where boarding schools were also ineligible) against the rest,
  with no comparison group.
- That impact persisted: stunting was never moved by the projects.
- Why states differed: the variance is reported, not explained.
- Anything for receivers without resources, infrastructure or a legal
  mandate, or beyond two years after exit.
