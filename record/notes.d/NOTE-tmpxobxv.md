# NOTE-tmpxobxv: Singh — An ontology for commitments in multiagent systems: Toward a unification of normative concepts

## Contribution

A unification of normative concepts under one directed, context-relative, revocable notion of
social commitment, irreducible to mental states, with explicit operations and policies governing
them.

## Key results

- Sec. 1, A3: "Social commitments cannot be reduced to internal commitments, which apply within an agent".
- Sec. 1, A4: "Commitments are, in general, revocable; the clauses for revoking them are as important as the conditions for satisfying them."
- Sec. 1, A8: "commitments and associated conditions are evaluated in the world, not in the mind of any agent".
- Sec. 2.1, Definition 1: "A commitment is a four-place relation involving a proposition (p) and three agents (x, y, and G)."; "x is the debtor, y the creditor, G the context group, and p the discharge condition".
- Sec. 2.1: the context "provides the court of appeals for adjudicating disputes between the debtor and creditor."
- Sec. 2.2: release "is distinguished from both discharge and cancel, because release does not mean success or failure of the given commitment".
- Sec. 3.1.1: "Explicit commitments are explicitly represented by one or more of the agents; implicit commitments are not."
- Sec. 3.1.1: "Assertives commit the speaker to the statement expressed."
- Sec. 4.3: "commitments—of order greater than 0, i.e., metacommitments—create a society. The metacommitments are the norms of this society."
- Sec. 4.3: "commitments are both the progenitors and the descendants of norms."
- Sec. 6: "We conjecture that named groups and commitments can provide the necessary connections among the agents." Also that as groups become established "their commitments become implicit".
- Sec. 6: no model-theoretic semantics yet: "we leave the development of a semantics to future research."

## Assumptions

A1-A8 (sec. 1), notably: agents recursively composed; social commitments irreducible to mental
ones; revocability; commitments exist in a social context and help create it; semantics separate
from pragmatics; no subjectivist bias. Temporal aspects neglected; no formal semantics.

## Concepts

Social commitment C(x, y, G, p); debtor, creditor, context group, discharge condition; operations
(create, discharge, cancel, release, delegate, assign); sphere of commitment; explicit vs. implicit
commitment; flow down; social policy; i-order commitment and metacommitment; normative vs.
non-normative policy; pledge, ought, taboo, convention, collective commitment; Hohfeldian claim,
privilege, power, immunity.

## Bearing on the record

A cites it for socially recognized commitments, with debtor, creditor, conditions and discharge,
distinct from private intentions, and warns against equating a record's Active status with such a
commitment. **Accurate.** Singh's commitment has exactly these parts and is explicitly irreducible
to mental states (A3, A8, and against reducing to mutual belief in sec. 6). The warning is A's, and
the paper supports it: a Singh commitment is directed from a debtor to a creditor in a context and
has a discharge condition and governed cancellation and release; a record entry's Active status
names no debtor, no creditor and no discharge condition.

- [TERM-tmp8deza](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8deza.md) (shared epistemic state: the publicly inspectable structure of what is on the
  table, its standing, support, contributors and what would need reconsidering, with rules for
  changing it; not shared beliefs, memory or common knowledge): **supports the "not shared beliefs"
  and "public structure" clauses, and qualifies the analogy.** Singh's commitments, like the term's
  structure, are social objects evaluated outside any agent's head, live in a named group, and are
  changed by operations governed by policies (the term's "rules for changing that structure"
  correspond to Singh's social policies on operations). But the term's items carry standing and
  support, not debtor-creditor direction or a discharge condition; so Singh is a predecessor for
  the public, rule-governed character, not for what "standing" means. His "Assertives commit the
  speaker to the statement expressed" gives a route by which a filed claim could create a
  commitment for its contributor, which the term does not say.

## Limitations

Conceptual; no semantics; temporal aspects neglected; definitions given as constraints in a loose
predicate-calculus notation; no empirical or implemented evaluation.
