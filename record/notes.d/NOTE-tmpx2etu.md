# NOTE-tmpx2etu: The impact of place and legacy framing on climate action: A lifespan approach

## Contribution

A replication and extension of Zaval et al.'s (2015) legacy induction in
an age-balanced sample, beside a new place-based induction. It asks
whether older adults, said to care more about legacy and place, respond
more. They do not: both inductions work about equally at every age.

## Key insight

Framings that tie climate action to what outlasts the writer work, but not
by separate routes. The authors expected the legacy task to act through
legacy motivation and the place task through love of nature. Each raised
both, and the authors grant both tasks may have moved "an underlying third
element" (p. 13).

## Assumptions

- **Legacy motivation is what the Legacy Motivation Scale measures.** It
  is described as "how much participants care about their legacy" (p. 4).
  Its eight items (S1 File) are:
  - wanting to be remembered: "I care about what future generations think
    of me"; "It is important to me to leave a positive legacy";
  - self-regard and efficacy: "I have important skills I can pass along to
    others"; "I am good at many things"; "I am well liked by my friends";
    "Others would say that I have made unique contributions to my
    community or society";
  - caring about future people: "I feel a sense of responsibility to
    future generations"; "I feel a connection to future generations".

  Only the total is analysed.
- **A hypothetical donation proxies behaviour.** Participants split a $10
  lottery bonus, which one participant would win, between themselves and
  Trees for the Future, described as helping families "for generations to
  come" (S1 File).
- **Baron and Kenny mediation** with one measured mediator at a time,
  checked by bootstrap (pp. 8–10).

## Key results

- **Sample** (pp. 3–4). 1,005 recruited on TurkPrime in December 2016,
  five quota groups of 200 by age; 17 excluded on attention checks,
  leaving 988. Analysed as young (18–35, n = 327), middle (36–52, n = 324)
  and older (53–87, n = 337). 68% women; 45% called themselves
  environmentalists. Conditions: Place 299, Legacy 295, Control 394.
- **The inductions** (p. 5; S2 File).
  - Legacy: "think about what you want future generations to remember you
    for when you're gone", and about "ways in which you will have a
    positive impact on the environment or on other people", with examples
    of saving water and energy, cutting emissions and supporting
    environmental laws.
  - Place: a place one has an emotional connection to, the impact of
    climate change on it, and "What would you like that place to be like
    for future generations after you're gone?"
- **Manipulation checks** (p. 6).
  - Legacy motivation: induction F(2, 967) = 20.62, η² = .041. Both tasks
    beat control (Place p = .001, Legacy p < .001); Legacy beat Place
    (p = .002). Young adults lower than the rest (η² = .008). No
    interaction (p = .793).
  - Biophilia: induction F(2, 979) = 8.12, η² = .016. Both beat control
    (Place p = .002, Legacy p < .001) and did not differ. Rises with age
    (η² = .041).
- **Intentions** (p. 6; Table 1, p. 7). Induction F(2, 967) = 15.04,
  η² = .030; both beat control, not each other. Older adults higher
  (η² = .012). No interaction (p = .299). Cell means: control 3.27–3.35,
  Legacy 3.64–3.89, Place 3.35–3.85.
- **Donations** (p. 7; Table 2, p. 8). Induction F(2, 979) = 4.30,
  p = .014, η² = .009; Place p = .006, Legacy p = .027 against control.
  Young adults gave less (η² = .010). No interaction (p = .478). Means
  from $2.90 (young, control) to $4.54 (older, Legacy).
- **Mediation, one mediator** (pp. 8–10).
  - Legacy → intentions: c = .203, a = .248, b = .355, c′ = .118 (partial);
    bootstrap indirect 0.180, 95% CI [0.120, 0.245].
  - Legacy → donations: c = .083 (p = .029), b = .139, c′ = .054
    (p = .171, "fully mediated"); indirect 0.069 [0.028, 0.118].
  - Place → donations and intentions through biophilia: both partial.
- **Mediation, both mediators** (S3 File).
  - Legacy → intentions: through biophilia 0.060 [0.016, 0.107], through
    legacy motivation 0.107 [0.065, 0.155]; direct c′ = 0.135, p = .039.
  - Legacy → donations: through biophilia 0.138 [0.038, 0.245], through
    legacy motivation 0.097 [0.014, 0.198]; direct c′ = 0.035, p = .869.
    Total c = 0.270, not significant.
  - Place: no indirect effects through either.
- **Correlations** (S3 File, Table S1). Legacy motivation with biophilia
  .396, with intentions .398, with donation .175.
- **Nulls** (p. 12; S4 File). Neither task changed death-thought
  accessibility (p = .725), hazard belief or worry. Future self-continuity
  on overlapping circles, self now and in five years, did not move with
  induction (p = .201); an age × induction interaction (p = .050) put
  older adults in the Legacy condition highest.

## Concepts

- **legacy motivation**: as scaled here, a mix of wanting to be
  remembered well, believing one has something to pass on, and feeling
  responsible to and connected with future generations.
- **place attachment**: emotional and cognitive bonds with a particular
  place (p. 2); not measured, only induced.
- **biophilia**: affiliation with and care for nature, here five items
  including "Protecting the wellbeing of nature for its own sake is
  important to me".
- **future self-continuity**: felt overlap of self now with self in five
  years, on Ersner-Hershfield et al.'s circles.

## Connections

- **Zaval, Markowitz and Weber 2015 (LIT-tmpetcju)**, unread. The source of
  the induction, the Legacy Motivation Scale, the intentions scale and the
  donation measure. See section 3.
- **Wade-Benzoni (NOTE-tmpcn33u)** never measures legacy concern and
  generosity in one study. This one does, but with a mixed scale.
- **Hurlstone et al. (NOTE-tmpebx4k)** bundle identification with
  generations, a moral appeal and one's death. The legacy task here
  bundles being remembered, one's own impact and examples of the very
  behaviours then measured.
- **Hershfield (NOTE-tmp70rn1).** The future-self circles come from his
  group. Legacy framing did not move them in general.
- **Many Labs 4 (NOTE-tmpzgbo9).** Legacy framing did not raise death
  thoughts, so it gives terror management no foothold here.

## Bearing on the record

- **The legacy question (QUESTION-tmpruhu4).** Its "evidence from the
  legacy experiments" asks whether legacy priming acts through the wish to
  be remembered. This study cannot say. These are inferences from the
  printed items and results.
  - The mediator is a total over eight items. Two are about being
    remembered, four about self-regard, two about responsibility and
    connection to future generations. "Mediated by legacy motivation" is
    mediated by that mix.
  - The legacy task also raised love of nature, and in the two-mediator
    model love of nature carried more of the donation effect (0.138 vs
    0.097). Concern for nature "for its own sake" runs in parallel with
    any self-regarding route.
  - The place task, which asks about a place "for future generations after
    you're gone" and never mentions being remembered, also raised legacy
    motivation against control (p = .001), though not in the two-mediator
    model (a = .032). The score rises without the wish to be remembered
    being prompted.
  - No condition varies whether anyone will know what one did, which is
    the question's test between the thesis's or Pitcher's reading and
    Scheffler's.
- **The triviality objection (CLAIM-tmprudek).** Legacy motive, as
  measured, is not a criterion that can come apart from caring: caring
  about future generations is inside the scale. The one measure of a self
  extended in time, future self-continuity, did not move with legacy
  framing except among older adults, and was not tested as a mediator.
- **The owner's thesis (CLAIM-tmpjylq8).** Neutral. The study shows that
  thinking about what outlasts one raises concern for the future; it
  does not say whose good is served. No dated note on the claim; a dated
  note on QUESTION-tmpruhu4 is proposed (section 4).

## Limitations

- **Self-report and hypothetical giving.** Donations were from a bonus one
  participant might win; nobody's behaviour was observed.
- **Demand.** The legacy prompt lists the behaviours the intentions scale
  then asks about.
- **Mediator measured after the task, with the outcomes.** Order of the
  battery is not reported; causal mediation is not established.
- **No place-attachment check**, as the authors grant (p. 13).
- **Timing.** December 2016, just after the US election; the authors note
  it (p. 13). US participants only.
- **Unequal cells** (Control 394 against about 300) and shifting degrees
  of freedom (967 vs 979) are not explained.
- **Inconsistencies.**
  - The intentions scale is reported as "Range = 0–6, M = 2.73" (p. 4), but
    S1 scores it 1–6 and every cell mean in Table 1 lies between 3.27 and
    3.89. The overall mean cannot sit below every cell; it may be on a 0–5
    coding (an inference). The Legacy and biophilia ranges ("0–6",
    "0–7") likewise differ from S1's 1–6 and 1–7.
  - Each bootstrap indirect effect is about twice the product of the
    stepwise coefficients beside it (intentions: .248 × .355 ≈ .088 against
    0.180). The units are not stated.
  - The Legacy effect on donations is significant in the main text
    (p = .029) and not in S3 (c = 0.270, ns).
  - S3's last paragraph repeats the donation model's direct effect
    (c′ = 0.319, p = .127) for the intentions model, whose figure gives
    0.050.
  - "Bartlett's test indicated sphericity assumptions were adequately met"
    (p. 6) in a between-subjects design.
- **Typos.** Reference 26 gives Zaval et al.'s pages as "26(2):3–19" for
  231–236 (p. 16); "inexorably linked" for "inextricably" (p. 2); "Zaval
  and colleague's" (p. 12); "a $10 dollar lottery" (p. 4); "The potency of
  our findings imply" (p. 12); "hp2" for ηp² (S4 File).

## Open questions

- Does legacy framing work through being remembered? A study that scores
  the two remembering items apart from the two caring items, and adds an
  anonymous condition in which one's actions will never be known, would
  answer QUESTION-tmpruhu4's test.
- Does the legacy task extend the self in time? Future self-continuity
  rose only among older adults; a measure of continuity with one's legacy
  or with future generations, not with one's own self in five years, has
  not been tried.
