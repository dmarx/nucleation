# NOTE-1520: Niemiec, Ryan & Deci — The path taken

## Contribution

The first longitudinal test of goal contents theory's claim about
*attainment*, not pursuit: over one year of post-college life, attaining
intrinsic aspirations (growth, relationships, community, health) tracked
psychological health and attaining extrinsic ones (money, fame, image) did
not, and change in basic need satisfaction mediated the intrinsic link. It
separates intrinsic from extrinsic attainment and well-being from ill-being,
where earlier studies used a relative index and a health composite
("Attainment of Aspirations", "Present Research").

## Key insight

Getting what you aimed for is not uniformly good: what matters is what the
goal was. In the authors' model, intrinsic attainment pays off because it
satisfies the needs, and extrinsic attainment does not satisfy them, so its
success brings no well-being and some ill-being. The need-satisfaction
mediator is what turns "goal contents matter" into a claim about needs.

## Assumptions

- **Intrinsic and extrinsic as two factors**, from Kasser and Ryan 1996's
  factor analysis of seven aspirations; the intrinsic label is given
  "because its goals are expressive of humans' inherent growth tendency and
  are conducive to satisfaction of the basic psychological needs" (Intro).
  The classification already carries the hypothesis.
- **Residualized change.** "Change" from Time 1 to Time 2 is the residual of
  each Time 2 latent construct on its Time 1 value (fn. 6); mediation is by
  Baron and Kenny's steps and MacKinnon et al.'s z′ test.
- **Self-report throughout**: the 21-item Basic Psychological Need
  Satisfaction Scale–General, the 35-item Aspiration Index (importance at
  Time 1, attainment at both), SWLS, six MSEI self-esteem items, PANAS,
  the Pennebaker symptom checklist, six STAI items (Measures).
- **Well-being includes self-esteem**: the latent well-being construct is
  life satisfaction, self-esteem and positive affect (Results).

## Key results

### Sample (Method)

- 246 seniors at two colleges gave permission; 164 answered at one year
  (Time 1), 147 at two years (Time 2); 79.9% Caucasian; unpaid.

### Importance to attainment (Fig. 1)

- Structural model fit χ²(155) = 262.52, CFI = .91, NNFI = .88,
  RMSEA = .05. Time 1 intrinsic importance → Time 2 intrinsic attainment
  β = .51; extrinsic importance → extrinsic attainment β = .67 (both
  p < .01).

### Attainment and psychological health at Time 2 (Fig. 1)

- Intrinsic attainment → well-being β = .77, → ill-being β = −.66.
- Extrinsic attainment → well-being β = .00 (ns), → ill-being β = .38
  (p < .01).

### Change over the year (Figs. 3, 4)

- Well-being: Time 1 → Time 2 β = .67. Change in intrinsic attainment →
  change in well-being β = .63; change in extrinsic attainment β = −.02
  (ns). Change in intrinsic attainment → change in need satisfaction
  β = .44; extrinsic β = .03 (ns). Need satisfaction change → well-being
  change β = .83. With the mediator in, intrinsic attainment's path falls
  from .63 to .16 (ns); z′ = 2.69, p < .01.
- Ill-being: Time 1 → Time 2 β = .65. Intrinsic → ill-being change
  β = −.59; extrinsic β = .27 (p < .07, "marginal"). Intrinsic → need
  satisfaction change β = .45; need satisfaction → ill-being change
  β = −.39; intrinsic's path falls to −.42 (still p < .01), partial
  mediation; z′ = −2.26.
- **Time 1 need satisfaction was unrelated to Time 2 need satisfaction
  (β = −.09, ns)**, in both change models, while well-being (.67),
  ill-being (.65) and attainment (.85, .70) were stable. The paper does
  not comment.

### What the zero-order correlations show (Discussion)

- Extrinsic attainment correlates *positively* with well-being (mean
  r = .19) and with need satisfaction (mean r = .18; competence .21,
  autonomy .17, relatedness .15), and not with ill-being (mean r = .03).
  Intrinsic attainment correlates more strongly with need satisfaction
  (.44, .38, .41). The authors offer a statistical explanation, that the
  extrinsic relations "may have been nullified when both aspiration
  attainments were allowed to compete for variance", alongside the SDT
  one.

## Concepts

- **Aspiration** — a life goal that organizes behaviour over long periods;
  "not isomorphic" with intrinsic or extrinsic *motivation*, which concerns
  the reason for a given behaviour (fn. 1).
- **Intrinsic / extrinsic aspirations** — personal growth, close
  relationships, community involvement and physical health / money, fame
  and image (Intro; Table 1).
- **Absolute continuity** — consistency of an attribute within an
  individual, modelled autoregressively (Analytic strategy).

## Connections

- **Kasser and Ryan 1996** ([LIT-1977](../literature.d/LIT-1977.md)) supplies the two factors; Kasser
  and Ryan 2001 and Ryan et al. 1999 (Russia) the cross-sectional
  attainment findings this extends.
- **Sheldon and Elliot 1999** ([LIT-589](../literature.d/LIT-589.md), [NOTE-448](NOTE-448.md)), the self-concordance
  model, is the regulation half; this paper claims the content half and
  says "the content, in addition to the regulation, of goals matters"
  (Broader Implications). Some data were also used in Study 3 of Sheldon et
  al. 2004 (fn. 4).

## Bearing on the record

- **[NOTE-442](NOTE-442.md)'s summary of Ryan and Deci 2000 says extrinsic aspirations'
  attainment "does not help".** This paper is the later direct test, and it
  qualifies that: the null is a unique-variance result with intrinsic
  attainment in the same model. Alone, extrinsic attainment correlates
  positively with well-being (mean r = .19) and with need satisfaction
  (.18). What the paper shows is that extrinsic attainment adds nothing
  once intrinsic attainment is known, and goes with more ill-being at the
  cross-section.
- **[QUESTION-369](../questions.d/QUESTION-369.md).** The paper uses needs as the mediator that explains
  why some goal successes do not pay, which is one of the "functional"
  uses of need-hood the question asks about. But it inherits the
  question's circularity: intrinsic aspirations are *defined* as those
  "conducive to satisfaction of the basic psychological needs" (Intro), the
  well-being outcome includes self-esteem, and mediator and outcome are
  both self-reported at the same occasions. It does not supply a criterion
  for need-hood that avoids the well-being measure.
- **[THEORY-049](../theory.d/THEORY-049.md) and [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)**, which hold goal contents and regulation
  second-hand: this is primary evidence for the content side, at N = 147.
- **[THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md)** is not addressed: no frustration measure, no clinical
  outcome.
- Filed from it: [THEORY-1319](../theory.d/THEORY-1319.md) (Proposed).
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- Self-report only; "rather homogenous" sample of recent graduates; and
  "our findings were correlational, so they do not provide evidence for the
  direction of effects" (Limitations, the authors').
- **The mediation is weak as causal evidence**: residualized change in the
  mediator and in the outcome are measured over the same interval, so the
  order between them is assumed, not observed.
- **The need-satisfaction measure did not hold its rank order over the
  year** (β = −.09), unlike every other construct. Either need satisfaction
  in post-college life changes almost completely in a year or the measure
  is noisy; either way "change in need satisfaction" here is close to
  "Time 2 need satisfaction".
- Fit indices are marginal by current standards (CFI .90–.91, NNFI
  .86–.88).
- The extrinsic null rests on competition for variance with intrinsic
  attainment, which the authors acknowledge as a possible statistical
  explanation.

## Open questions

- Does the extrinsic pattern hold with outcome measures that are not
  self-reports, and in older or non-student samples (the authors' own
  call)?
- Would a design that changes extrinsic attainment exogenously (a windfall,
  a promotion) show the null on well-being, or the zero-order positive
  relation?
- Why was need satisfaction unstable across the year?
