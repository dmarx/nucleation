# THEORY-2217: Recommenders inflate confidence via fluency

## The account

Dorsch, Coeckelbergh and Vierkant (2026) argue, as an untested mechanism,
that engagement-optimizing recommender systems threaten deliberative
democracy not by isolating users from opposing views but by optimizing
processing fluency. Fluency inflates second-order confidence in one's own
epistemic stance and makes rejecting opponents' views feel warranted, so
beliefs formed there carry reflective epistemic luck the user cannot detect
from within.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-2307](../notes.d/NOTE-2307.md); its key results are quoted there with
locations. The authors call the claim "not yet directly tested empirically"
(p. 9). Held evidence cuts against its motivating link to affective
polarization ([THEORY-1653](THEORY-1653.md)); [THEORY-662](THEORY-662.md) supports its premise that fluency's
cause is not given to introspection.

## Bearing

On [QUESTION-441](../questions.d/QUESTION-441.md)'s second part: a mechanism by which a system reproduces the
conditions under which its harm is not legible to those it harms. It names
no organization as the bearer.
