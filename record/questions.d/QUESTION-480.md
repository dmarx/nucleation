# QUESTION-480: When is answerability required of an authority or governing capability: under what conditions is justification owed, to whom, and why?

## Why it is a question

The owner asked it at U36 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), after
three turns on how an actor can control the conditions under which others
contest it:

> when is answerability required of an authority or governing capability? under what conditions is justification owed, to whom, and why?

It has three parts that need not have one answer:

- **When.** What about an exercise of power, or the holding of it, makes
  an answer owed: interference, office, coercion, dependence, control over
  recourse, or something else.
- **To whom.** Who is owed the answer, and whether the party owed is the
  one who receives it or the one who can demand it.
- **Why.** What grounds the duty, as distinct from what makes it more or
  less demanding.

The record holds pieces and not the question. [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md) states a
second-order duty, to keep violations knowable, contestable and
correctable, and says it is "a verdict only given an independently
justified standard". [CLAIM-687](../claims.d/CLAIM-687.md) says how far accountability should rise
with indispensability. [TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md) makes domination relative to "a declared
standard of answerability", which the record has not defined. [QUESTION-460](QUESTION-460.md)
asks who bears the obligations a whole grounds and on what ground, and
[QUESTION-468](QUESTION-468.md) asks who answers for exploitation. None asks when any actor
owes an answer at all.

## A's answer, and its own sub-question

A83 answers it ("When Is Power Answerable?"). Its account is filed as
[CLAIM-739](../claims.d/CLAIM-739.md) (grounds, roles and the limit), with the term
[TERM-126](../terms.d/TERM-126.md), and its principle that authority over a decision does not
carry authority over the conditions of its challenge as [CLAIM-727](../claims.d/CLAIM-727.md).
A83 §12 restates the owner's question as "**Why should anyone owe another
person reasons at all?**" and admits that the formal account "needs a
moral premise about the standing of agents"; that part stays open here.

A83 closes with a sharper problem that belongs to this question:

> **Can we characterize the minimum organizational conditions under which a justified claim of answerability remains effective without granting the claimant unlimited authority over the actor being challenged?**

It has two sides, as A says: protection against the power-holder, and
protection against "domination through an unrestricted power to demand
answers". The second is the limit [CLAIM-749](../claims.d/CLAIM-749.md) files from part 7 of the
exchange.

## What would count as an answer

- **A trigger with a boundary.** A condition, or a short list of
  conditions, under which an answer is owed, together with cases on both
  sides of it: a private choice that disappoints someone (A83 §9 Case A)
  owes none; an unauthorized beneficial intervention (Case B) owes one.
- **An addressee rule.** Which party is owed, and how the owed party, the
  recipient of reasons and the forum that can demand them come apart.
- **A ground.** A reason the duty holds that is not the actor's capacity
  to be made to answer: second-personal standing, justifiability to those
  governed, or non-domination, as A83 §2 surveys them. Choosing among
  them is [QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md)'s problem in another form.
- **For the sub-question,** a construction in which a claim of
  answerability stays effective, by [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md)'s standard, while the
  claimant's powers over the actor are bounded, and a matched one in which
  widening those powers makes the claimant the dominating party.

A negative answer would also count: an argument that answerability has no
trigger independent of the standard declared with each judgment, so that
the question reduces to choosing a standard ([CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)).

## Note of 2026-11-17: Darwall's answer, through Stern, and Stern's objection
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1975 LIT-2756 LIT-2747 — new or Unavailable; the reading filed on 2026-11-18 and the unread works it bears on -->

Stern 2014, read ([NOTE-2065](../notes.d/NOTE-2065.md), [THEORY-1975](../theory.d/THEORY-1975.md)), standing in for Darwall 2006 ([LIT-2756](../literature.d/LIT-2756.md)). The THEORY now carries `bears_on` this question.

**Darwall's answer, as Stern sketches it.** Obligation "involves the exercise of legitimate authority over the person obliged" (p. 2), and it holds wherever demands "could legitimately" be made, whatever "the actual demands an individual or a community of individuals might make" (p. 3). So justification is owed to those with second-personal standing, the members of the moral community, and because obligation consists in what they can legitimately demand. Giving another that standing "makes you answerable to them" (p. 9). And better guidance does not make authority: "meeting the standards of the normal justification thesis is not in fact sufficient to establish that one person has authority over another" (p. 7), against Raz ([LIT-2747](../literature.d/LIT-2747.md)). That is the point A83 relies on.

**Stern's objection** is the case the answer leaves open: "(1) A person’s suffering does not give a second-personal reason to act (2) Obligations require second-personal reasons (3) The suffering of others can give us obligations" (p. 12). If (3) holds, something can be owed to a party who makes no demand and may have none to make, so wronging, not standing to demand, may ground what is owed. The question's "why" has at least these two candidate answers on the record now, neither read from Darwall himself.

No status changes.
