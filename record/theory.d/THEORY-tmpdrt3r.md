# THEORY-tmpdrt3r: Leffler (2024) argues that of four inescapability proposals only Korsgaard's plight inescapability, reinterpreted as an agent's being continuously positioned to exercise its agency fully absent external limits, captures four necessary features of normative force, and that it then makes force relative to each agent's constitution, so that agents constituted differently, corporate agents included, are bound by different norms

## Source

Leffler (2024), [LIT-2444](../literature.d/LIT-2444.md), read in [NOTE-tmp4a8iu](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp4a8iu.md).

## The account

Constitutivists say that norms implicit in agency bind us, and many say
they bind because agency is inescapable. Leffler separates the question of
why constitutive norms have force (the Arché question) from whether there
could be shmagents, and tests four kinds of inescapability against four
necessary features of force: invariance under mere changes of desire,
ought-implies-can, ought-implies-can-fail and criticizability.

- **Psychological** inescapability (an aim behind every action) fails
  invariance and criticizability.
- **Further-factor** inescapability (social schemata, embodiment) fails
  invariance, risks ought-implies-can, and fails criticizability, since
  "Sometimes the schemata are wrong, not the individuals facing them".
- **Standpoint** inescapability (no standpoint outside agency) fails
  ought-implies-can-fail and criticizability: total failure would put the
  agent outside agency.
- **Plight** inescapability, reconstructed as being "positioned to
  continuously exercise her agency fully, absent external limits", passes
  all four, because what is inescapable is the position, not its
  successful exercise, and success is measured against "the thing to do"
  fixed by the agent's own norms.

The cost: "the standard of success in plight inescapability is set by the
features and commitments of whatever agency one has", so "agents with
different constitutions have different norms applying to them as
forceful". Categoricity as applicability, invariance or convergence does
not avoid this once constitutions vary, and categoricity as constitution
independence is out of reach. Constitutivists must accept relativism or
give up the plight explanation.

## What was actually shown

A conditional, by conceptual argument and cases (the evil demon, the
homophobic society, the failed lie, half a glass of gin). Each verdict
could have gone the other way: a proposal could have captured a feature
Leffler says it misses. The relativism step rests on a premise argued
briefly, that constitutions vary between and within kinds of agent.

## What this does not say

- It does not say constitutivism is false, or that plight inescapability
  is true; the author defends neither.
- It does not say norms have no constitution-independent force; only
  that this explanation cannot provide it.
- It says nothing about organizations that are not agents. Its corporate
  agents are an example of varying constitution, one sentence long. That
  it carries to the record's self-maintaining organizations is the
  record's inference, [ARG-tmpxsh8r](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpxsh8r.md), not the paper's.
