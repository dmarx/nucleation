# CLAIM-tmprfjkk: The BERT anaphora data cannot show which of the two signalling-allowing verdicts is right, because the sentence schema fixes their support and every verdict on them is a threshold on how the three referent choices signal, so the comparison neither meets nor defeats the thesis that formal contextuality on signalling data needs Contextuality-by-Default

## The claim

[CLAIM-037](CLAIM-037.md)'s note of 2026-10-29 says that [LIT-844](../literature.d/LIT-844.md)'s data show the two
verdicts "diverging (0.148% against 71.1%) without showing which is
right". It asks for a comparison of a sheaf route with CbD 2.0 on one
signalling data set. [CLAIM-tmptzmky](CLAIM-tmptzmky.md) gives that comparison on [LIT-844](../literature.d/LIT-844.md)'s
data.

The comparison cannot show which verdict is right. The schema's "same" and
"other" fix the support of every instance to the PR prism's, which
[NOTE-648](../notes.d/NOTE-648.md) and [THEORY-623](../theory.d/THEORY-623.md) already observe. So the possibilistic
obstruction to a global section is present, identically, in all
51,966,480 instances. Each model is fixed by ε, the three numbers that
measure how BERT's referent choices differ from even. Both routes'
verdicts are regions of ε-space:

- the corrected sheaf test is max|ε_i| < 1/6;
- CbD 2.0 is Δ(ε) < 2;
- the readings computed in [CASE-tmpskejj](../cases.d/CASE-tmpskejj.md) are SF < 1, and two distance
  regions.

Choosing between them is choosing how much signalling, and of what
pattern, may sit beside a fixed obstruction before the model stops
counting as contextual. Nothing in the data varies independently of that
choice, so the data cannot favour one choice over another. A data set can
discriminate only if its support is measured, or if its contextuality
varies apart from its signalling, as [NOTE-648](../notes.d/NOTE-648.md)'s open questions already
ask.

**Bearing on [CLAIM-037](CLAIM-037.md).** Its defeat condition, a pragmatic data set on
which the sheaf test handles inconsistent connectedness without loss, is
not met. The sheaf test [LIT-844](../literature.d/LIT-844.md) applies is a sufficient test whose
verdict differs from an exact sheaf-side reading's ([CLAIM-tmptzmky](CLAIM-tmptzmky.md),
item 5). That is a loss. Its promotion condition is not met either: the
comparison exists, but it cannot say which route is right. On this
reading, [CLAIM-037](CLAIM-037.md) stays Proposed, interpretive, until a data set of the
discriminating kind is compared.

## What it does not say

- That the routes are equivalent, or that the choice between them is
  arbitrary in general. On other supports they can differ in ways the
  data could test.
- That CbD 2.0's figure is wrong. It is the verdict of an exact test of a
  definite property, and that is the reading [CLAIM-tmprq0nb](CLAIM-tmprq0nb.md) takes.
