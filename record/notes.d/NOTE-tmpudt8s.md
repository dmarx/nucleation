# NOTE-tmpudt8s: Thine Own Self: True Self-Concept Accessibility and Meaning in Life

## Contribution

The paper turns the old claim that being in touch with one's true self makes
life meaningful into a testable social-cognitive one. It does not measure
the true self, which "might be difficult, if not impossible, to
operationalize directly" (p. 475). It measures the **true self-concept**, "a
cognitive schema representing those aspects of the self that are
considered, by the person, to be most emblematic of his or her true
nature", and its **accessibility**: how readily it comes to mind. It then
asks whether accessibility predicts, and whether manipulating it changes,
reported meaning in life. The actual (everyday, public) self-concept is the
control throughout, so that effects belong to the true self-concept and not
to the self-concept in general.

## Key insight

The true self is proposed as "a wellspring for meaning by exporting
feelings of legitimacy, importance, and value to other aspects of life"
(p. 474). If so, simply activating it should raise felt meaning, "even in
the absence of the phenomenological experience or actual expression of
one's true self-concept" (p. 486). And the data say it does, without the
activation making people like themselves more: the true self's weight lies
in its being taken as who one really is, "regardless of how much we like
it" (p. 486).

## Assumptions

- **Accessibility as speed.** Faster "me" responses to a trait mean the
  trait's self-concept is more accessible (Bargh et al. 2002). Speed is
  residualized on each person's speed for control traits they called "me".
- **The true/actual split is the participant's.** Studies 1–3 define the
  true self as traits one would like to express but can express only
  around those closest to one, and the actual self as traits often
  expressed socially (after Bargh et al. 2002 and Rogers 1951). Studies 4–5
  define the true self as "who you believe you really are, even if you
  sometimes act in different ways" and the actual self as "who you are
  during most of your daily activities".
- **Meaning is presence of meaning, self-reported**: the five-item MLQ
  Presence subscale, with four PIL items added in Studies 3 and 5.
- **Subliminal primes are unseen.** 40 ms (Study 3) or 35 ms parafoveal
  (Study 5) masked presentations; awareness was probed after the task.

## Key results

- **Study 1** (N = 56 after 3 suspicious participants were dropped; traits
  chosen from a 60-word list, true and actual sets forced to differ).
  True-self accessibility predicted meaning (β = .29, p < .05) with
  actual-self accessibility, affect, need satisfaction (β = .37) and the
  traits' social desirability controlled; actual-self accessibility did not
  (p. 477). True-self traits were rated *less* socially desirable than
  actual-self traits, t(55) = 4.14 (p. 477).
- **Study 2** (N = 68; self-generated traits; meaning measured about 1.5
  months later). True-self accessibility β = .24, need satisfaction β = .32
  (p. 479). No desirability difference between the two sets.
- **Study 3** (N = 80; priming one month after trait listing). Participants
  liked their true self more than their actual self (5.81 vs 5.29,
  t(79) = 3.55). True-self primes gave higher meaning than actual-self
  primes (5.59 vs 4.72, t(78) = 4.16), and the difference held in an
  ANCOVA with Time 1 meaning, affect and both liking ratings controlled,
  F(1, 73) = 4.46, p < .05 (p. 481). No participant identified the primes.
- **Study 4** (140 recruited, 123–124 analysed after 17 were excluded for
  not following instructions; overlap between the two sets allowed, mean
  4.05 of 10 shared). In a regression with nine covariates (R² = .56),
  overlap (β = .21), AI-3 authenticity (β = .34) and true-self
  accessibility (β = .19) predicted meaning; actual-self accessibility did
  not (p. 483). Table 4 (N = 124): true- and actual-self accessibility
  correlate r = .70; true-self accessibility correlates .05 with
  authenticity, .19 with state self-esteem and .30 with meaning.
- **Study 5** (155 recruited, 149 analysed; a 2 × 2 of true vs actual self
  by liked vs disliked traits). Estimated marginal means for meaning:
  positive true 5.16, negative true 5.23, positive actual 5.29, negative
  actual 4.91. The planned contrast of negative-actual against the other
  three was F(1, 139) = 5.07, p < .05, and negative-true beat
  negative-actual, F = 4.63. Priming affected neither state self-esteem
  (ps > .40) nor reported authenticity (ps > .34) (p. 485).

## Concepts

- **true self-concept**: the person's avowed true self, a schema of the
  traits they take to be "most emblematic of his or her true nature"
  (p. 475); not the true self of personality theory.
- **actual self-concept**: in Studies 1–3, traits "often able to express to
  others in social settings"; in Studies 4–5, the "everyday self". The
  authors note it "might best be thought of as a public self" (n. 1).
- **true self-concept accessibility (TSCA)**: reversed standardized
  residual of mean log reaction time to true-self traits, regressed on
  reaction time to control "me" traits.
- **meaning in life**: here only presence of meaning (MLQ), not search for
  it.

## Connections

- **Kernis and Goldman ([LIT-tmpc6vlg](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc6vlg.md)).** The paper describes their
  programme as SDT-based and defines authenticity, after them, as "the
  unimpeded functioning of one's true self in daily life" (p. 474). It
  measures their AI-3 (Studies 4–5) and finds it unrelated to TSCA (C4). It
  reads its disliked-trait result as fitting their claim that authenticity
  includes "awareness and acceptance of both one's positive and negative
  attributes" (p. 487).
- **SDT ([LIT-559](../literature.d/LIT-559.md)).** Need satisfaction is the standing rival predictor of
  meaning, and the autonomy items of the Basic Psychological Needs Scale
  include "I feel like I can pretty much be myself in my daily situations"
  (p. 476), an authenticity item inside the autonomy measure.
- **[THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md)** (autonomy as graded integration). The overlap between
  true and actual self-concepts predicted meaning on its own (β = .21) and
  correlated with authenticity, need satisfaction and true-self
  accessibility (Table 4). Overlap is a crude, trait-level index of how far
  who one is every day matches who one takes oneself to be, which is close
  to what SDT means by integration. It is a better candidate for a
  non-endorsement measure of integration than the self-reported reasons
  SDT uses, though the paper does not frame it that way.
- **[THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md)** (ownership cannot come from order alone). The paper's
  "true self" is whatever the participant says it is; it measures the
  psychological weight of avowed ownership, not whether the avowal is
  correct. Its authors say so: the person's "assessment of the contents of
  the true self may not coincide with a person's 'real' true self"
  (p. 475). So it neither supports nor tests the philosophical question.
- **Sheldon & Elliot ([LIT-tmppbyfm](../literature.d/LIT-tmppbyfm.md)).** The same idea, goals that express
  the true self, reached through reasons for goals rather than through a
  self-concept; Sheldon is thanked in the acknowledgements.
- **Meaning ([LIT-183](../literature.d/LIT-183.md)).** Subjective meaning only; Wolf's objective
  condition is outside the measure.
- **Taylor ([LIT-tmprf69i](../literature.d/LIT-tmprf69i.md)).** The paper's own historical framing (the
  "existential vacuum" after shared value bases eroded, pp. 474, 487)
  matches Taylor's account of why authenticity became an ideal; it cites
  Baumeister, Bellah, Becker, Frankl and Fromm for it.

## Bearing on the record

- **Candidate THEORY**, Proposed: "Activating what a person takes to be
  their true self raises their felt meaning in life, independently of
  whether they like those traits", sourced to this paper. It should promote
  on an independent, preregistered replication of the priming effect,
  given that it rests on two subliminal-priming experiments of the
  2005–2009 period, a literature whose replication record has since been
  widely questioned (my judgement, not the paper's), and one of
  them with a misprinted omnibus test. It would be refuted by a
  well-powered failure to replicate Study 5's negative-true vs
  negative-actual contrast.
- **Against conflating authenticity measures.** C4 is the record's first
  evidence that implicit true-self accessibility and self-reported
  authenticity come apart. Any THEORY that treats "authenticity" as one
  construct should cite it.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Self-reported meaning only**, which the authors acknowledge (p. 488).
- **Students in one lab**, one point in life.
- **Small effects and modest samples** (N = 56, 68 in Studies 1–2) for
  regressions with six or seven covariates.
- **Subliminal priming** effects of this vintage have a questioned
  replication record (my judgement); no replication is reported here, and Study 5's main effect of self type was
  null (ps > .18), with the result resting on the interaction contrasts.
- **The control is not neutral.** There is no no-prime condition, so
  footnote 9 uses change scores to argue that true-self primes raised
  meaning and negative actual-self primes lowered it, but conditions
  differed at baseline.
- **Study 4's true and actual accessibility correlate .70** where Studies
  1–2 found them unrelated; allowing the two trait sets to overlap (4 of 10
  words shared on average) makes the two scores partly the same reaction
  times. The regression still separates them, but the independence the
  earlier studies claimed does not hold in this design.

## Open questions

- Does TSCA predict anything other than self-reported meaning, for instance
  behaviour or persistence?
- Is overlap between true and actual self-concepts a usable measure of
  integration in SDT's sense?
- Would a neutral-prime control show that true-self primes raise meaning,
  rather than negative actual-self primes lowering it?

## Corrections

- none to a seeded skim (there was no seed)
- **Study 5 interaction (p. 485).** The page image prints "a significant
  interaction between type of self-concept and valence of self-concept,
  F(1, 139) = 1.63, p < .05". An F of 1.63 on (1, 139) degrees of freedom
  has p ≈ .20. Either the F or the p is misprinted, and the stated
  significance of the omnibus interaction cannot be taken from the paper.
  The planned contrasts (F = 5.07 and 4.63) are reported separately and are
  internally consistent.
- **State self-esteem in Study 4.** The General Discussion says Study 4
  showed that true-self accessibility was "unrelated to state self-esteem"
  (p. 486), but Table 4 gives r = .19, marked p < .05. The experimental
  claim for Study 5 is unaffected.
- **Study 3 table reference.** The Study 3 results refer to "Table 3 … in
  Study 5" (p. 480); Table 3 is Study 3's.
- **Study 4 N.** 140 recruited minus 17 excluded is 123; Table 4 says 124.
