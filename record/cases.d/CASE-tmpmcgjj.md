# CASE-tmpmcgjj: Matched constructions on one courier platform that vary only the capacity to formulate objections, control of review, the erosion of alternatives or the power to revise the rules, with variants that stipulate the protective reasons away

## The common setting

A delivery platform assigns jobs to couriers. Its governing rule R says a
courier is deactivated after three late deliveries in thirty days, lateness
measured against the time the platform quoted when it assigned the job. A
deactivated courier may appeal within fourteen days; an appeal succeeds if
a delivery counted as late was not late under R. The platform's dispatch
office makes deactivation decisions under R.

In every pair below the two worlds share R, the appeal right, the
decisions actually made, and the recorded history: over five years the
same couriers are deactivated, the same appeals are filed, and every
decision that was correct under R stays, every one that was not is
reversed. A pair differs in one stated respect and nothing else.

The test applied to each pair is reasonable rejection as [THEORY-1971](../theory.d/THEORY-1971.md)
states it: an arrangement is wrong to the extent that it is disallowed by
every set of principles for it that no one could reasonably reject, judged
by individuals' reasons for objecting to a principle and its
alternatives. Whether the test explains a pair's difference "without
importing principles of standing or authority" (A102 §12, relayed in
[QUESTION-483](../questions.d/QUESTION-483.md)) is asked of each.

## C1: identical principles, different capacities to formulate objections

- **W1.** Each deactivation notice lists the three deliveries, the quoted
  and actual times, and R's text. The courier can download the delivery
  logs the decision used.
- **W1′.** The notice says only "deactivated under the lateness rule". R
  is published; the logs are not available to the courier. The appeal
  panel sees them.

The courier in W1′ has the same right to appeal and the same reasons
against a wrong decision, but cannot formulate the objection that a given
delivery was not late, since they cannot tell which deliveries counted.
This is A102 §2's split, held as a note on [CLAIM-523](../claims.d/CLAIM-523.md), between reasons
a person has and reasons the person can formulate.

## C2: first-order authority fixed, control of review varied

- **W2.** Appeals go to a panel of three: two couriers drawn by lot each
  month and an arbiter both sides agreed on. The dispatch office cannot
  remove them.
- **W2′.** Appeals go to a panel the dispatch office appoints and may
  remove at will.

The authority to decide deactivations is the dispatch office's in both;
only who controls the review of its decisions differs. On the stipulated
history W2′'s panel reverses exactly the decisions W2's does.

## C2*: C2 with the protective reasons stipulated away

Add to both worlds: deactivation decisions are computed mechanically from
tamper-evident delivery logs by a procedure independently audited as
applying R without error, and this is common knowledge; the panel in
either world has never had a decision to reverse and is known never to
need to. The dispatch office keeps its power over the panel in W2′; it is
known never to need it. Whatever reason a courier had in C2 to want review out of the
dispatch office's control as protection against error, bias or
retaliation is gone. What remains is that in W2 couriers share control of
the body that reviews decisions over them and in W2′ they do not.

## C3: two regimes, one eroding alternatives

- **W3.** Couriers' ratings are portable to other platforms, the platform
  holds no exclusive contracts with restaurants, and its prices are not
  below cost. Couriers' other options stay as they were.
- **W3′.** Over five years the platform, by acts each within the sphere
  its contracts and the law leave to it (exclusive contracts with the
  restaurants that matter, ratings that cannot be exported, prices below
  cost until rivals leave), narrows the couriers' other options. In year
  five it lowers pay to the level W3's platform also sets in year five, and
  answers objection by saying couriers "keep accepting" and have no better
  offer.

The terms in year five are identical. W3′'s narrowness of alternatives is
a product of the relationship the terms govern, as [CLAIM-747](../claims.d/CLAIM-747.md) has it.

## C4: the power to revise the governing rules

Both worlds review as W2 does, and both act and correct well.

- **W4.** R and the appeal procedure can be amended only by a two-thirds
  vote of the couriers together with the platform's consent.
- **W4′.** The platform may amend R and the appeal procedure at will, on
  thirty days' notice; couriers have no say.

On the stipulated history neither platform ever amends R. This is
[QUESTION-481](../questions.d/QUESTION-481.md)'s requested construction: an arrangement that acts and
corrects well while no one subject to it can change its governing rules
(W4′), matched with one that differs only in that capacity (W4).

## C4*: C4 with the protective reasons stipulated away

Add to W4′: the platform is known, as a matter of its settled and verified
disposition and as common knowledge, always to make exactly the
amendments the couriers' vote in W4 would pass, on the same schedule.
Nothing binds it to: it keeps the power to amend otherwise and is known
never to use it so. (A binding device that made its amendments follow the
vote would give the couriers the power by proxy and erase the
difference.) Whatever reason a courier had
in C4 to want a say as protection against unwelcome or unannounced change
is gone; the two worlds produce the same rules at the same times under
every contingency. What remains is that in W4 the couriers hold a share of
the power to revise and in W4′ the platform holds all of it.

## What the construction is for

C1 to C4 are the constructions as stated. C2* and C4* are the assistant's:
they hold fixed not only the recorded outcomes but the dispositions that
make the outcomes safe, which is what a construction must do if it is to
separate a reason about who holds a power from reasons about what a holder
might do with it. The verdicts are filed separately.
