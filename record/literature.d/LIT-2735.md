# LIT-2735: Fuentes v. Shevin, 407 U.S. 67 (1972)

Supreme Court of the United States (1972). *Fuentes v. Shevin*, 407 U.S. 67, decided 12 June 1972. Opinion of the Court by Justice Stewart. Text at the Legal Information Institute.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading ([NOTE-tmpxgail](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxgail.md)).*

- The opportunity to be heard "must be granted at a time when the
  deprivation can still be prevented" (Part IV).
- "no later hearing and no damage award can undo the fact that the
  arbitrary taking that was subject to the right of procedural due process
  has already occurred" (Part IV); bond and damages "are hardly a
  substitute for a prior hearing".
- "The right to be heard does not depend upon an advance showing that one
  will surely prevail at the hearing" (Part V); "a temporary, nonfinal
  deprivation of property is nonetheless a 'deprivation'".
- Extraordinary situations excepted, which "must be truly unusual"
  (Part VI); the holding is "a narrow one" (Part VII).
