# NOTE-498: Block — Précis of The Border between Seeing and Thinking

## Contribution

It gives, in Block's words, the book's main claim and its structure, and
updates it in one direction: since 2022 the working-memory evidence has
made Block doubt that "raw" perceptual representations are used in
cognition at all. The border between seeing and thinking is then a border
of format, policed by recoding when percepts enter working memory.

## Key insight

Read the global workspace not as what makes a percept conscious but as what
puts a percept to cognitive use. Percepts compete in sensory areas;
winners trigger frontoparietal "ignition"; the reverberating front-and-back
coalition is the "cognitive envelope", in which perceptual content is held
in an abstracted, partly conceptual form for reasoning (Figure 1, pp. 274–275).

## Assumptions

- **Perception is constitutively iconic etc.; cognition only
  paradigmatically discursive** (p. 273).
- **Active working memory** is conscious and maintained by broadcasting;
  "activity-silent" retention of unconscious information for up to 4 s is
  set aside as a different phenomenon (pp. 275–276).
- **Content-based reasoning requires working memory**, because premises must
  be held together (p. 276).

## Key results

- **Infant reasoning (p. 276).** Hamlin's helper/hinderer results, including
  preference for "the hinderer of the hinderer", give "some reason" to
  credit 6–8-month-olds with content-based reasoning in working memory,
  though leaner iconic accounts are not ruled out.
- **Nine differences between working memory and perception (pp. 277–279)**,
  listed in the LIT; Block notes they "do not show a change in format", and
  a change could be within iconic representation.
- **Iconicity (pp. 279–280).** Three conditions: representations respond to
  environmental differences by degree; changes in them change what they
  represent by degree; relations among properties are mirrored by analogous
  relations among representations. Burge's notion adds a requirement of
  naturalness, which Block rejects. Gross's upper/lower-case adaptation
  challenge is answered by phonological and lexical association.
- **Iconic thought (pp. 280–281).** Carey's parallel-individuation system
  (capacity about 3 items in infants, 4 in adults) and entropy-based
  same/different matching are iconic, but enclosed in a cognitive envelope,
  so not "entirely non-conceptual or non-propositional"; Block concedes he
  "might have scanted the extent of iconic representation in thought".
- **Attribution and discrimination (p. 281).** Still no fact of the matter
  which is more basic, but now for different reasons, and "perceptual
  discrimination" is "importantly ambiguous".

## Concepts

- **Cognitive envelope** — the links from perceptual representations to
  cognitive faculties in the global workspace (p. 274).
- **Iconic representation** — a family of representations meeting the three
  tracking and mirroring conditions (p. 279).
- **Active working memory** — "active maintenance of representations for use
  in reasoning" (p. 275).

## Limitations

- An update, not a précis: the arguments for the main thesis are not here.
- The working-memory evidence is cited, not assessed.
- The cognitive-envelope reading of the workspace is asserted, not argued
  against the consciousness reading.

## Open questions

- Does recoding in working memory change format (iconic to discursive) or
  only grain within iconic format? Block leaves both open (p. 279).
