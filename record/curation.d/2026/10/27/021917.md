The first pass on the exchange of 2026-10-27 is PR [#337](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/337) and the curation
entry of 00:41. It registered eighteen works and filed QUESTION-277. It
left three things undone: the CASEs, a topic word, and the things the
exchange mentioned without citing a work. The owner then asked for all
three. For the unnamed things, the owner said: "make a good faith effort to
find anything mentioned that wasn't registered. if you can't find it, try
to find adjacent material that at least accounts for the ideas we're hoping
to reference". This entry records that pass.

**The topic.** ADR-055 adds `endosymbiosis`, labelled "Endosymbiosis and
reductive evolution". ADR-028 takes a history note, and `ecology`'s blurb
now points to the new word. The eighteen works of the first pass and
QUESTION-277 are retagged, each with a version bump and history item. All
the works take the new word first. QUESTION-277 keeps `individuation`
first.

**The CASEs.** Six are filed, all `documented`, `specimen` and Proposed,
because every source is unread. Under ADR-051 a case may cite a work the
record has not read. Each case says that what it shows rests on the
abstracts until the works are read.

- Aphid–*Buchnera*, CASE-tmpoa9h4. A1 proposed it.
- The mealybug's nested symbionts, CASE-tmpir76a.
- *Hodgkinia* in cicadas, CASE-tmpx4tix.
- *Paulinella*, CASE-tmpowpq1. A1 proposed it.
- The nitroplast, CASE-tmp30dzl. A1 proposed it.
- *Monocercomonoides*, CASE-tmpa087v.

A1 proposed two "case families", obligate insect–bacterial associations
and recent organelles. A2 then built its argument on three systems and a
negative control. Filing all six keeps each system to one code. The
aphid–*Buchnera* and *Hodgkinia* cases illustrate CLAIM-060. The others
relate to no CLAIM, because the claims they bear on are QUESTION-277's
open dimensions, and a CASE relates only to claims. While drafting, one
statement was removed from the *Buchnera* case: that no symbiont gene is
known to have moved to the host. None of the sources at hand states it.
The case now says only that the abstracts report no transfer.

**Found: works the exchange mentioned without naming.**

- *"Other cicadas show extensive genome fragmentation"* (A2). Its search
  results and the abstracts point to the two *Magicicada* papers: Campbell
  et al. 2015 (LIT-tmpnzwyn) and Campbell et al. 2017 (LIT-tmpdkj4h).
  Łukasik et al. 2018 (LIT-tmp190pe) is registered as well. It shows that
  the *Tettigades* splitting A2 described, from one paper (LIT-1563),
  happened at least six times.
- *Paulinella's independent origin and genome reduction* (A2). These were
  cited to a search result. The primary source is the chromatophore genome
  paper, Nowack et al. 2008 (LIT-tmpzxcpb).
- *Aphid–Buchnera* (A1's case). The founding genome paper is Shigenobu et
  al. 2000 (LIT-tmp98l8f).
- *Host compensation* (A2's mechanism table). Campbell et al. 2018
  (LIT-tmp4u7hy) documents a host compensating at transmission.
- *Endosymbiotic gene transfer* (A1 and A2). The standard review is Timmis
  et al. 2004 (LIT-tmp1u989). No abstract was seen, so its summary is what
  it is standardly cited for.
- *The eukaryogenesis scenarios A1 listed.* For "metabolic syntrophy": the
  hydrogen hypothesis (Martin & Müller 1998, LIT-tmp9k032) and the first
  cultured Asgard archaeon, which lives by syntrophy (Imachi et al. 2020,
  LIT-tmp4y7ls). For "cellular complexity preceding mitochondrial
  acquisition": Pittis & Gabaldón 2016 (LIT-tmp8xhh2), the earlier
  statement of the case LIT-1566 and LIT-1561 continue.

**Adjacent: ideas the exchange named with no work, accounted for by
standard references.**

- *"Population-genetic models of gene loss"* (A1): Kuo, Moran & Ochman 2009
  (LIT-tmpmnpe8) on drift and genome size, and Mira, Ochman & Moran 2001
  (LIT-tmprmgys) on deletional bias.
- *"Metabolic network constraints"* (A1): Pál et al. 2006 (LIT-tmp01c3k).
  It simulates gene loss on a metabolic network and predicts *Buchnera*'s
  gene content.
- *"Mutualism theory"* (A2's answer to why a partnership persists): Sachs
  et al. 2004 (LIT-tmpfubrx). The record held cooperation within species
  (LIT-498, LIT-501) and no synthesis between species.
- *Host control, and within-collective conflict* (A1's "host control of
  symbiont replication"; A2's C and S): Frank's two 1996 models
  (LIT-tmpt00nh, LIT-tmpi201p). The second had no PubMed record that
  matched (the DOI search returned an unrelated paper), so its summary is
  drawn from the title and from what it is standardly cited for.
- *"Multitype branching processes"* (A1): Haccou, Jagers & Vatutin 2005
  (LIT-tmpj3ova), a standard monograph. A search for an endosymbiosis model
  built on multitype branching processes found none. This is the least
  specific registration in the batch.

**How they were checked.** As in the first pass, each work was checked
against Crossref and PubMed, and Crossref was searched for a preprint under
its title. Three take preprint dates under ADR-002: Campbell 2017, Campbell
2018 and Imachi. Five keep month precision because no source gives a day:
Timmis, Mira, Sachs and Frank's *American Naturalist* paper, with the
monograph dated by its print date. Literature.d was searched first for
each surname and title, and none was held.

**Still not registered.** A1 mentioned "multilevel selection" models. The
record holds these already (THEORY-141 and the readings behind it), and the
exchange pointed to them. The exchange named no other work or idea without
something registered for it.
