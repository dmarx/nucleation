# CLAIM-482: An organization's aggregate revealed preference exists exactly when its record of winning moves never cycles, and where it exists it is fitted to one regime of authority, so an intervention on authority that changes the winning moves changes the fitted preference and the preference does not predict the change

## The claim

[CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md) is the redescription objection to [CLAIM-027](CLAIM-027.md), in its
aggregate form: "Northstar simply prefers speed". [CLAIM-122](CLAIM-122.md) answers
the individual-attitude version by holding members' attitudes fixed and
changing authority. It leaves the aggregate version open, because an
organization's revealed preference need not be any member's attitude.

The record has since proved when an aggregate revealed preference exists.
[CLAIM-434](CLAIM-434.md): on a finite set of outcomes, some ordering rises with every
winning move exactly when no chain of winning moves returns to where it
began. That ordering is an ordinal potential ([TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md)). A revealed
preference of the organization, in the only sense its record of wins can
reveal, is such an ordering. So:

1. **Where the wins cycle, the objection has nothing to name.** No
   ordering is climbed by every winning move, so no preference of the
   organization predicts which regime wins.
2. **Where they do not cycle, an ordering exists, and it is fitted to the
   record it was computed on.** The record was produced under one
   arrangement of authority. In [CASE-003](../cases.d/CASE-003.md), Northstar-A and
   Northstar-B have the same policy and the same evidence, and different
   escalation authority, and different contests are won. The ordering fitted
   to A's wins is not the ordering fitted to B's. The redescription can
   follow the change only by refitting after it.
3. **So the revealed preference does not do what [CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md) needs.**
   [CLAIM-027](CLAIM-027.md)'s defeater asks for "a showing that every such relation is
   just another higher-order preference whose content predicts the
   outcome". A preference fitted after each intervention on authority
   describes the outcome. It does not predict it. The relations that
   changed are what predicted the change.

What would carry the objection is a persistent potential ([TERM-078](../terms.d/TERM-078.md)): one
ordering that holds across the windows on both sides of the
intervention, and that the wins follow in each. If changing escalation
authority left the same ordering in force, [CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md) would be right about
that organization, and the change in authority would be idle.

## What it does not say

- **That the aggregate preference is never real.** For an organization
  whose wins follow one persistent potential through changes in authority,
  [CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md)'s reading is the better one. This claim says which records
  decide that, and how.
- **That [CASE-003](../cases.d/CASE-003.md) measured the wins.** [CASE-003](../cases.d/CASE-003.md) is a constructed contrast.
  The claim says what a record of its wins would have to show. A recorded
  pair of windows either side of a change in authority would test it.
- **That it answers [CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md).** Whether the account adds explanatory gain
  over control and institutional theory is a separate objection.
