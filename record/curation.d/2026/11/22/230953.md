LEAD-106 asked for studies of context sampling after cued trauma recall in
PTSD against trauma-exposed controls, and for exposure-matched
intervention contrasts, to advance QUESTION-303. A delegated lead worker
did it on 2026-11-22 on the branch claude/lead-106-ptsd-context-sampling,
following the run-lead and read-and-file skills. It had no subagent tool,
so it read the two works itself rather than delegating a reader per work
(read-and-file step 6); its running notes are
scratch/studies/lead-106/PROGRESS.md.

## Where the record already held it

Nothing. No changelog fragment, dated note or LIT answered LEAD-106 or
QUESTION-303. The record held the model (THEORY-285, Active; its wide form
THEORY-1179, Proposed; both from LIT-983 and NOTE-766) and one human
sampling study on a different task (THEORY-912). After reading, the record
was searched again for hypervigilance, attentional bias, dwell time,
scanpath, context processing, stimulus discrimination, EMDR and
trauma-exposed controls: the nearest held entries were THEORY-907 (anxiety
and exploration in foraging tasks) and CLAIM-446 (governance-maintained
anxiety). Neither holds a gaze study of PTSD.

## The search

Europe PMC REST search, no email parameter, 2026-11-22: eye tracking,
eye movements or gaze, with PTSD or posttraumatic, crossed with (a) script,
recall, trauma reminder, autobiographical or trauma-exposed (102 hits);
(b) context, ambiguous, scene, periphery or hypervigilance (41); (c)
script-driven imagery, trauma film, memory activation or reactivation (9);
(d) stimulus discrimination or context processing with exposure or
therapy (33); (e) exposure with attention guidance, external focus,
safety cues or dismantling (3); (f) information sampling, exploration or
active inference (167). Europe PMC indexes PsyArXiv and bioRxiv preprints,
which appeared among the hits; arXiv was not searched separately, since
the topic is clinical. OpenAlex: LIT-983 has no citing works, and Europe
PMC's record of its preprint none either.

**Result: no study meets the design**, in either half.

## What was left, and why

- **Weaker bearings without a note**: THEORY-907 and CLAIM-446 bear only
  through the Kimble studies, which were not read; a note now would rest
  on abstracts. They are named here so a reading of LIT-tmpizs88 can add
  them.
- **Not registered**: the other eye-tracking studies the scoping review
  includes (Kimble 2010, Thomas 2013, Lazarov 2022, Alon 2023, Lev 2025,
  Veerapa 2023 and others) measure attention to threat, not to context,
  and none follows recall; they are reachable through LIT-tmpogxg7.
  Glass, Neria and Kimble 2026 (sentence stems) and studies of EMDR
  mechanisms with eye movements as the bilateral task were left as off
  the design.
- **Not read though open**: Blekic and colleagues (PMC11823380), Andrews
  and colleagues (PMC10612569), Tyson and colleagues (PMC12498374) and the
  Kimble 2014 manuscript (PMC4211931). The brief asked for one or two
  readings; Blekic is the next.
- **Obstacles**: Europe PMC's fullTextXML for PMC13577267 returned HTTP 500
  twice (2026-11-22); Crossref returned HTTP 429 for 10.1159/000447671,
  not retried, so Europe PMC's metadata was used for that work. No bot
  check was met.

## Gaps

Single-work filings: the gaps are in each NOTE's Limitations. Zidda and
colleagues conclude that controls "processed the context more
extensively" from first-fixation latency alone (left in the NOTE; the
record uses only the latency). The scoping review misses at least two
eligible studies and does not report its pre-2010 check (left in the
NOTE). The gap the record does use, whether a later first look at an
uninformative context is evidence for the sampling account, is the
subject of the two alternative claims and ARG-tmpc0892.

## Offered, not filed (read-and-file step 12)
