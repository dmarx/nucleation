# THEORY-tmpymwd8: In two cross-lagged panel studies of university students, a negatively worded self-report of need frustration predicted later depressive symptoms (standardized β .13 to .18 over three lags) and later negative affect, net of earlier depression and negative affect, while negative affect did not predict later depression; depression also predicted later need frustration at one lag of two

## Source

Levine, Brabander, Moore, Holding and Koestner (2021), LIT-tmpgv3da,
Motivation and Emotion 46(1); read in NOTE-tmpe4mmg.

## What was actually shown

**Study 1**, a Canadian university, September, December and April
2018–19, N = 379 (304 at T3), 85% women. **Study 2**, the same university,
April and late May 2020, N = 235. Need frustration is the mean of nine
negatively worded BMPN items ("I feel lonely", "I feel pressured in my
life"); negative affect five adjectives; depression the CESD-R 10. Fully
cross-lagged observed-variable path models, standardized coefficients.

| path | Study 1, T1→T2 | Study 1, T2→T3 | Study 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| frustration → depression | .13, p = .01 | .17, p = .02 | .18, p = .010 |
| frustration → negative affect | .18, p = .002 | .16, p = .02 | .17, p = .018 |
| negative affect → depression | −.01, p = .85 | .10, p = .25 | .07, p = .287 |
| depression → frustration | .20, p = .007 | .01, p = .88 | −.01, p = .987 |
| negative affect → frustration | −.07, p = .27 | .17, p = .04 | .09, p = .287 |

In Study 2, self-reported frustration predicted family and peers'
reports of depression at β = .14, p = .074, controlling for their
earlier reports.

This could have come out otherwise: had negative affect carried the
prediction, its paths to later depression would have been the significant
ones, as cross-sectional work had suggested.

## What this does not say

- **That frustration causes depression, or comes first.** Without random
  intercepts (tried by the authors, reported only on OSF), lagged paths in
  a cross-lagged panel include stable between-person differences. The
  reverse path at Study 1's first lag (.20) exceeds the forward one (.13).
- **That what predicts is frustration rather than low satisfaction.** The
  BMPN's satisfaction half is not modelled, and the items are negatively
  worded, like the outcomes (THEORY-tmp62iwr's objection in another
  instrument).
- **That frustration is transdiagnostic.** The authors say so; the outcomes
  are one symptom domain and a mood state.
- **Anything about the descriptives of Study 2.** Its Table 3 repeats Study
  1's means and SDs digit for digit, so one of them is a copying error
  (NOTE-tmpe4mmg, Limitations). The path coefficients are reported
  separately and are not affected on their face.
