# LIT-680: Proving Linear Mode Connectivity of Neural Networks via Optimal Transport

Damien Ferbach, Baptiste Goujaud, Gauthier Gidel and Aymeric Dieuleveut
(2024), *Proceedings of the 27th International Conference on Artificial
Intelligence and Statistics* (AISTATS 2024), PMLR 238 — [ARXIV-2310.19103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19103)

## Key takeaways

- **Alignment is optimal transport.** Permuting network B's neurons at layer ℓ
  to minimize Σ_i ‖[W_A]_i − [W̃_B]_i‖² is the Monge problem between the
  empirical distributions of the two layers' weight vectors. By Birkhoff's
  theorem its value is their Wasserstein distance (App. B, Lemma B.3). If
  each layer's weights are i.i.d. draws from one distribution, that distance
  shrinks with width at the known rates for empirical measures, the
  activations of A, permuted B and every network on the line between them
  stay close, and with a convex loss the line stays low-loss.
- **Two-layer networks after training** (Theorem 3.1). Two wide two-layer nets
  trained independently by SGD in the mean-field regime (small step size,
  bounded Lipschitz non-linearity, which excludes ReLU) are, with high
  probability, linearly connected modulo a permutation of the hidden layer,
  with an output-level and a squared-loss version.
- **Deep networks at initialization** (Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2). With Gaussian
  i.i.d. weights and ReLU allowed, LMC modulo permutation holds once the width
  of layer ℓ exceeds m̃_ℓ = Õ((T_ℓ/ε)^{m̃_{ℓ−1}}). For two-layer networks that
  is ε^{−m₀}, against Entezari et al.'s ε^{−(2m₀+4)}. A matching lower bound
  (Theorem 5.3) shows the recursive exponential growth is tight. An
  "effective width" device lets every layer have the same width.
- **Lower-dimensional weights need less width** (Theorem 5.4). If weight
  distributions are approximately supported on a subspace of dimension
  e·m̃_{ℓ−1}, the required width drops until the approximation breaks down. The
  result extends to independent sub-Gaussian weights.
- **Empirically** (§6), across learning rates for a 3×512 MLP on MNIST, a
  lower approximate dimension of the matrices being matched goes with lower
  barriers. A weight matching weighted by the activation covariance beats
  plain weight matching.

## Standing in the record

Filed with the batch [ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md) opened `loss-landscapes` for. It **extends**
Entezari et al. ([LIT-652](LIT-652.md)): it proves their conjecture beyond their single
case, from one hidden layer at random initialization to trained two-layer
nets in the mean-field regime and to deep nets with independent weights. Its
bound is better than theirs in the case they share: ε^{−m₀} against
ε^{−(2m₀+4)} for the hidden width.

It is **compared against** Git Re-Basin ([LIT-661](LIT-661.md)) in §6. It takes that
paper's weight-matching and activation-matching objectives as baselines and
adds a third, weight matching under the activation covariance, which
"constantly and substantially outperforms naive Weight Matching" across
learning rates (Fig. 2). It also gives an account of a tension between the two
earlier papers. Git Re-Basin found no linear connectivity at initialization,
where Entezari et al.'s theorem proves it. The width the bound requires at
initialization is "excessive as compared to the typical width of neural
networks used in practice", so both can hold.

Appendix B.14 relates the result to Kuditipudi et al.'s dropout stability
([LIT-671](LIT-671.md)) in a simplified one-hidden-layer setting: networks with
independent sub-Gaussian neuron weights become dropout stable at the same rate
in width as they become linearly connected. It calls this "a first step"
towards unifying the two views, so no relation is declared.

It carries `mathematics` because its method is the theory of optimal
transport and the convergence of empirical measures, applied to a question
about networks.
