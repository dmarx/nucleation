# NOTE-tmpqarad: Ahmed (2021) — Complaint!

## Contribution

First-person evidence of how an institution keeps its formal machinery of accountability while the machinery fails to carry complaints through, the failure spread over procedures, people and doors rather than one decision.

## Key results

Introduction only; printed pages.

1. p. 1: "To be heard as complaining is not to be heard."
2. p. 5: "making a complaint is never completed by a single action: it often requires you do more and more work."
3. p. 6: "Complaints, it seems, go further the extent to which those positioned higher up in an organization express them or give support to them."
4. p. 22: "complaints procedures function rather like diversity: when offered as solutions to problems, they are problems given new forms."
5. p. 24: "the same complaints procedures used as tools to redress bullying and harassment can be used as tools to bully and to harass."
6. p. 25: "Those who make complaints and those who are heard as complaining are themselves more likely to be complained about, becoming what I call in chapter 4 complaint magnets."
7. p. 19: "I describe complaint as a phenomenology of the institution."
8. p. 10: "I conducted interviews with forty students, academics, researchers, and administrators"; "In addition to interviews, I received eighteen written statements."

## Assumptions

Complainants' testimony is the evidence, with no comparison group and no institutional side; participants mostly approached her and had often paid a high price (p. 10). UK and comparable universities.

## Concepts

Feminist ear; complaint biography; complaint as testimony; institutional mechanics; nonperformatives (a chapter title, unread); complaint magnets; complaint collectives.
