# THEORY-tmpodil0: Hershfield reviews evidence, mostly from his own studies, that people who feel less similar to their future selves treat them more like strangers and choose less patiently, while directly reported liking and caring for the future self, answered near ceiling, did not predict choice

## The account

Prudence is regard for a self who may feel like another person: "If
people tend to consider the future self as a stranger ... then they may
rationally have no more reason to save their money for their future
selves than to give that money to a stranger" (Theoretical
underpinnings). The prediction is graded: "the degree to which an
individual feels disconnected from his or her future self should
correlate with the degree to which that individual discounts future
rewards" (ibid.).

## Evidence

- Similarity to one's self in ten years, chosen from pairs of
  overlapping circles adapted from Aron, Aron and Smollan, predicted
  more larger-later choices in undergraduates and more accumulated
  assets in adults aged 18–86; no coefficients are given (Similarity).
- Bartels and Urminsky raised felt overlap by a passage on stable
  identity, and patience with it, with uncertainty, affective appraisal
  and present bias controlled.
- In fMRI, the larger the current-versus-future-self difference in
  rostral anterior cingulate, the steeper the discounting a week later:
  r = 0.47, P < 0.05 (Figure 2 caption).
- Vividness (age-progressed avatars) and positivity (respect for the
  elderly) each raised hypothetical saving.
- Directly asked liking and caring for the future self: "Neither
  construct, however, mapped robustly onto intertemporal choice." Both
  answers were positively skewed, with little variance (Positivity).
- With age, the future self "may not be confined to just the self, but
  may also include one's children and grandchildren" (Changes in future
  self-continuity with age), a suggestion the review does not test.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md) cites this review because it "measures continuity with
one's future self with overlapping circles ... That makes prudence an
instance of the thesis's shape", and its note of 2026-10-29 says the
self over time "is measured as the thesis needs". The reading is that
evidence, with its limit: the caring null comes from answers near
ceiling.

[QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md) uses the finding as a premise: if concern for future
generations does not fade with delay, then "concern for future people
does not fade with distance as concern for one's own future does
(Hershfield, [NOTE-1259](../notes.d/NOTE-1259.md))".
