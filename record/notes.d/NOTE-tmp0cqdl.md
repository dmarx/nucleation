# NOTE-tmp0cqdl: Illich (1974) — Energy and Equity

## Contribution

Illich's fullest open statement of radical monopoly, with its general law and further cases (shoes, schools), a year after Tools for Conviviality; and the threshold argument that is the transport form of that book's "second watershed".

## Key results

1. p. 2, the 1978 note: "a technology incorporates the values of the society for which it was invented to such a degree that these values become dominant in every society which applies that technology."
2. p. 8, ch. 3: "Past a certain threshold of energy consumption, the transportation industry dictates the configuration of social space."
3. p. 11, ch. 4: "Beyond a certain point, more energy means less equity."
4. p. 14, ch. 6: "Industry dominates traffic as soon as daily life comes to depend on motorized trips." "Because of its hidden, entrenched, and structuring nature, I call this a radical monopoly." It arises "when it becomes the dominant means of satisfying needs that formerly occasioned a personal response", not by scarcity or by driving out competitors, "but rather by virtue of its acquired ability to create and shape the need which it alone can satisfy."
5. p. 14, ch. 6: "The magnitude of voluntary limits is a matter of politics; the encroachment of radical monopoly can be pinpointed by social analysis." Limits come only through "juridical and, above all, to political process", by "provisional" measures. "Beyond some point, compulsory schooling destroys the environment for learning"; at a level of compulsory instruction "learning by seeing and doing declines".
6. pp. 14–15, shoes: "people are compelled to be shod ever since access to schools, jobs, and public services was denied to the barefoot. Teachers or party officials define the lack of shoes as a sign of indifference toward “progress.” Without any intentional conspiracy between the promoters of national development and the shoe industry, the barefoot in these countries are now barred from any office."
7. p. 15, schools: "Only when laws were enacted to make schools both compulsory and free did the educator assume the power to deny learning opportunities on the job to the underconsumer of educational therapies."
8. p. 15: in a free-transport utopia passengers "would be just as equally captive consumers of transport."

## Assumptions

Needs can be met in an autonomous mode (use-values) or an industrial one (commodities), which reinforce each other only while the autonomous mode is protected. Thresholds exist within an order of magnitude that can be identified in theory. Equity is measured in life-time and autonomy, not output.

## Concepts

- **Radical monopoly**: an industry's dominance as the means of meeting a need that formerly had a personal response, through its ability to create and shape that need.
- **Transport and transit**: capital-intensive motion as a commodity, against self-powered motion as a use-value.
- **Socially critical threshold**: the level of energy per person beyond which more energy means less equity.

## Connections

A stand-in for what the excerpt of Tools for Conviviality (LIT-tmp2tehj, NOTE-tmp8ceuj) does not reach. Radical monopoly: carried fully, with more cases than the excerpt, and with the point the excerpt only gestures at ("Cars create distance"): the monopoly creates and shapes the need it serves. The second watershed: carried in substance, not by name (results 3, 5; "transportation smothers traffic"); the word "watershed" does not occur. Counterproductivity: in substance (time scarcity; result 5 on schooling), not by the word. **Not carried**: the burial case (the only "buried" is "more deeply buried costs of transport") and "inaccessible or unimaginable" (the nearest are "previously unimaginable distances", about privilege, and result 6, which is the inaccessibility half in a case). Those still rest on Cayley (THEORY-tmpxwo9k, NOTE-tmpc4m2u). A13 §II's gloss of radical monopoly again loses that the displaced way is the personal, non-industrial response.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpbypt7** (compulsion without a rule; the non-adopter's measure): strong support, and a qualification. Result 4 is compulsion through the dependence of daily life, and result 6 is the claim's mechanism in a case: the non-adopter's options shrink "Without any intentional conspiracy". The harm falls on transit, the non-industrial alternative, which is the claim's measure. But in the shoe and school cases someone does the excluding: teachers and party officials deny access to the barefoot, and laws make schooling compulsory before the educator can deny learning on the job (result 7). "Without any rule requiring it" holds of the technology's adoption, not of the exclusion, which gatekeepers and law can enforce. Corroborates; the qualification is argued in ARG-tmpn3me8.
- **CLAIM-tmpkjv7u** (dependence presumptively dominating by its controllers): bounds it. Radical monopoly is "hidden, entrenched, and structuring", a dependence no one holds, which the claim since version 2 places outside its test (ARG-tmpsh7he). Where gatekeepers exclude the barefoot there are discretion-holders, so Illich's cases straddle the boundary: the industry holds no discretion, the officials do.
- **CLAIM-tmp6wuql**: support in spirit. Result 5's "learning by seeing and doing declines" under compulsory instruction is the claim's capacity-by-doing premise in another domain; no dynamics. No relation.
- **CLAIM-tmpsmg1c**: qualifies it. Limits are political and "provisional", and the threshold can be identified in advance, which favours deciding before lock-in; no burden rule. No relation.
- **The flourishing-and-meaning line**: result 4's need created and shaped by the industry that meets it is the mechanism behind the line's entries on produced needs, filed on 2026-11-13.

## Limitations

The 1978 Pantheon text, with a later note: result 1 is 1978, not 1973 or 1974. Centred on transport; the general law is stated briefly.
