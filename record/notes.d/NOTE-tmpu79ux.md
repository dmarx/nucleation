# NOTE-tmpu79ux: The Definition of Lying and Deception

James Edwin Mahon, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, revised 2015.

## Contribution

A taxonomy of definitions. Most of the entry is on lying: a traditional four-condition definition, objections to each condition, and the split between Deceptionism and Non-Deceptionism over whether lying needs an intention to deceive. The short §3 is on deception: a ladder of definitions D1 to D7. Self-deception is set aside.

## Key results

- **D1** (§3): "To deceive = df to intentionally cause to have a false belief that is known or believed to be false", "the traditional definition of deception, at least in the case of other-deception".
- **The majority** (§3): most "hold that deception, like lying, is intentional. They reserve term 'mislead' to cover cases of causing false beliefs either intentionally or unintentionally (Carson 2010, 47)".
- **Illusion is outside** (§3): on the over-broad dictionary definition, "mere appearances can deceive, such as when a white object looks red in a certain light".
- **The minority** (§3.1): "According to Chisholm and Feehan, however, deception can be unintentional", giving D7, to "cause another person to acquire a false belief", or to keep one, lose a true one, or be prevented from gaining one.
- **Mahon's evidence condition** (D4): the deceiver brings about the evidence on which the false belief rests, so drugging someone is not deceiving them.
- **Negative deception** (§3.1): Mahon doubts it, since "The state of being ignorant is not the same as the state of being mistaken."
- **The one functional definition is of lying** (§1.5.1): "any form of behavior the function of which is to provide others with false information or to deprive them of true information" (Smith 2004, 14). The entry folds it back into intentional deceiving.

## Assumptions

The deceiver is always a believing agent. Systems appear only as targets or channels (robots, an ATM). No functional, biological or selection-based account of deception is surveyed.

## Concepts

Deceiving as a success verb; misleading (Carson); positive and negative deception; deception by omission; paltering; bald-faced lies.

## Bearing on the record

- **Vocabulary for [CLAIM-tmpcv6i6](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpcv6i6.md).** On the majority view, gaming an evaluator with no intent is misleading, not deception. The claim never says "deception", so it stands; glosses of it should say "mislead", or cite Chisholm and Feehan's D7.
- **[DISTINCTION-tmp68xjw](../distinctions.d/DISTINCTION-tmp68xjw.md).** On the intentional definitions only the outside position, an agent who means to, deceives. The trained position deceives only on D7 or a functional definition. The inside position is self-deception, which the entry leaves out.
- **[CLAIM-tmpu6fff](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpu6fff.md).** The entry's illusion case, excluded from deception, matches the counter's analogy of false insight as an optical illusion.
- **Sycophancy** ([CLAIM-tmpsjxip](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpsjxip.md), [CLAIM-tmpvxrj1](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpvxrj1.md)). White lies are "all intentionally deceptive" (§1.4). A trained model's flattery fits Smith's functional definition of lying, not the traditional one.

## Limitations

The 2015 revision predates the work on AI deception. The deception section is short and ends on D7 without a ruling on intention. There are slips: in §1.4 "untruthfulness condition" is written where the intention condition is meant, and a Sorensen quotation is cited to Carson.
