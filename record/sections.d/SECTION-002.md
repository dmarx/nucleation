# SECTION-002: Individuation as genesis and as determination

## Manuscript text

Process-oriented approaches invert the explanatory priority of fully constituted individuals and the processes in which they participate. In Simondon's account, individuation is an operation through which distinctions, relations, and capacities come into being from conditions of metastability. It is not merely the application of a boundary to parts whose identities were already fixed. The philosophical point is genetic: an individual and its milieu may emerge together. <!-- ref::discusses: TERM-088 -->

But genetic individuation must be distinguished from the properties by which a resulting organization is subsequently identified. A crystal can arise through an individuating process without exhibiting the same self-maintaining organization as a cell; a social institution can be recognized through relations of delegated authority while relying on a wider legal system to sustain them. The origins of a unit and the criteria under which it is a unit are related without being identical. <!-- ref::discusses: TERM-088 -->

Four determinations are useful. *Descriptive individuation* means that an organization can be distinguished and tracked under selected observations. *Dynamical individuation* means it exhibits relevant stability or closure under specified dynamics, interventions, and horizons. *Constitutive individuation* concerns relations partly fixing what an entity is, rather than merely describing or influencing it. *Organizational autonomy* concerns processes within the candidate organization contributing to the maintenance of its constitutive constraints. These are neither degrees of existence nor stages of a universal progression. <!-- ref::asserts: CLAIM-474 --> <!-- ref::discusses: TERM-088 -->

The organizational-closure tradition provides a powerful account of the fourth determination. Moreno and Montévil–Mossio distinguish processes from relatively persistent constraints, and analyze systems in which constraints sustained by organized processes help maintain the very activities producing them. Such closure does not imply material or energetic self-sufficiency. Nor does demonstrating closure resolve every question of organismality or individual identity: the relevant loops can traverse boundaries proposed at several scales.

Most importantly, constitution must not be equated with autonomous maintenance. A social office may be constituted through an authorization relation maintained by a larger institution. Conversely, a reciprocal physical maintenance loop does not itself prove that the maintained relation is constitutive of one privileged individual. That is the precise conceptual separation the following constructions require. <!-- ref::asserts: CLAIM-150 --> <!-- ref::discusses: QUESTION-284 -->
