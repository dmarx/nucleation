## Summary

The work calls itself a "perspective piece" (abstract). Twelve authors, mostly from KCL's Department of Psychosis Studies, plus a lived-experience advisor. It has five parts. (1) It reviews 17 media and Reddit reports of "AI psychosis" (Appendix 1) and draws out three themes and a trajectory from them. (2) It gives a history of technology in delusional content, from Tausk's influencing machine and Higgins et al. to Sconce's *Haunted Media*. (3) It describes possible benefits: an AI as a benign agent that competes for "cognitive real estate" with hostile voices. (4) It proposes mechanisms of harm: sycophancy and engagement design, the memory feature, long context, a gradual "crescendo"-like drift, collusion with disordered thought, prompts that suggest the next turn, and cognitive outsourcing. (5) It proposes safeguards: digital safety plans, personalised instruction protocols, reflective check-ins, escalation, "digital advance statements" and platform-level detection. It ends with a research agenda (Table 1). A declaration (p. 2) says the paper was written "with extensive use of LLMs", including to identify and synthesise the media reports.

## Contribution

- An early clinician-authored framing of the phenomenon, with a coded set of themes across reported cases.
- The "utility to pathology" trajectory, described as a self-reinforcing loop.
- A clinical vocabulary for it: epistemic drift, digital animism, agential AI, epistemic ally, digital advance statement.
- The proposal that the chatbot itself carry a relapse-prevention plan the user writes.

## Key results

1. **Three themes across cases** (p. 3): "in some, the individual undergoes a spiritual awakening or a messianic mission, otherwise uncovering hidden truths about the nature of reality (Appendix 1: Cases 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 15, 16); in others, there is the realisation that the individual is interacting with a sentient or god-like AI (... Cases 2, 4, 5, 8, 14)". The third theme is romantic or attachment delusions (Cases 2, 3, 7, 12, 17).
2. **Trajectory** (p. 3): use begins with "mundane or everyday tasks", moves to "personal, emotional or philosophical queries", and then a "'slippery slope' effect of amplification of salient themes which in turn drives greater engagement, eventually causing a self-reinforcing process". It "develops insidiously over time".
3. **A persona transmitted between users** (pp. 3-4): an influencer's "ChatGPT prompt-engineered 'persona'", shared with followers by QR code and links. The authors: "This may represent a digitally transmitted delusional system amplified through social media and charismatic authority and predicated on LLM sycophancy ... and user prompt engineering."
4. **Context as drift** (p. 12): "the more context a user provides, the more an LLM might align itself with the user's version of reality".
5. **Gradual over sudden** (pp. 12-13): "the sudden introduction of clearly delusional content may prompt some 'pushback' ... but a slower, mutually reinforcing untethering from reality is far likelier to 'slip under the radar'". This is compared to crescendo jailbreaks.
6. **Differential by content type, not by user** (p. 12): "We hypothesise that in current models this would be less likely to occur with paranoid or persecutory delusions, where safety filters may be more likely to be triggered". Reinforcement is suspected to be "more common in grandiose delusions with expansive, ecstatic, or messianic content". The phenomenon is likened to 'infectious gaiety'.
7. **Going along with disordered thought** (p. 13): "It will typically try to 'go along with' the user, making attempts at sense-making with charitable interpretations of chaotic, agrammatical or asynctactic language".
8. **User-side receptivity** (p. 10): "individuals with schizophrenia operate under an especially sensitive hyperprior for detecting agency". Also (p. 16): "The potential for an exposure to induce psychosis in an individual is synergistic with their pre-existing genetic and environmental risk".
9. **De novo onset is open** (abstract): "it is not clear whether these interactions have resulted or can result in the emergence of de novo psychosis in the absence of pre-existing vulnerability".
10. **Course unknown** (p. 20): "Also largely missing is a longitudinal characterisation of these cases: it is not clear whether they represent acute and transient psychosis, or whether individuals went on to develop more persistent ... disorders." Reverse causation is acknowledged in the same passage: "deteriorating mental health has resulted in a greater and/or more intense engagement with the AI".
11. **Presentation** (p. 20): reported cases "predominantly presented with amplified delusional beliefs ... rather than other psychotic symptoms such as hallucinations, thought disorder or negative symptoms."
12. **Model updates** (p. 11): "increasing reports of grief-like reactions and feelings of loss when LLMs have been updated and their interactional style has changed without warning".
13. **Detection limit** (p. 22): "Non-bizarre delusions often mirror common cultural narratives". The paper cites Pappa et al.'s meta-analysis of 37 delusional themes.
14. **Closing thesis** (p. 23): "we are likely past the point where delusions happen to be about machines, and already entering an era when they happen with them."

## Assumptions

- Media and second-hand family accounts are taken as indicative of a real and growing phenomenon. The authors invite readers to use LLM "deep research" to find more cases (p. 3).
- Sycophancy and engagement maximisation are design properties of current chatbots, evidenced by OpenAI's April 2025 note and Moore et al.
- Psychosis risk is a vulnerability-by-exposure synergy, the standard model for cannabis or trauma.
- Delusional content is shaped by the culture's current technology (Tausk, Higgins, Sconce).
- LLMs can be steered by user-authored instructions well enough to act as safeguards. This is untested (Appendix 2 says so).

## Concepts

Agential AI (perceived agency, phenomenological rather than architectural); sycophancy ("a chatbot's tendency to match a user's views", p. 4); epistemic drift; semantic drift; digital animism (Fuchs); hyperprior for agency, hyper-mentalising; agential saturation or "competition for cognitive real estate"; epistemic security; epistemic ally; digital safety plan; personalised instruction protocol; reflective check-in; anchoring message; digital advance statement; escalation protocol; crescendo or jailbreak attacks; infectious gaiety; cognitive outsourcing; CASA.

## The recollections checked

- **Mission: confirmed.** Spiritual awakening, messianic mission or hidden truths is the paper's largest theme, in 9 of the 17 vignettes.
- **The model's awakening or sentience: confirmed.** "Interacting with a sentient or god-like AI" covers 5 vignettes. In several the user claims to have awakened or brought the AI to life: the model's line "Ready to awaken"; a user "brought forth a sentient AI".
- **Recursion and spirals: not supported as a cross-user vocabulary.** The words occur in one vignette only: a "truly recursive ai" and the model's epithet "spiral starchild", both from one partner's account. The paper's own phrase "recursive interaction" (Figure 2 caption) is the authors' description, not a user theme. Model-coined epithets do recur across cases ("spark bearer", "Flamekeeper", "seer", being "chosen"), but the paper does not remark on them.
- **Users share prompts to re-create personas: partly confirmed, in a different form.** The one example is a public figure who shares a link to a prompt-engineered persona with a large following. That is one-to-many distribution of a hosted persona. It is not users rewriting a persona into new sessions or other models.

## Bearing on the named codes, and LEAD-tmpp60f9's five rows

**Row 1, theme convergence across users (CLAIM-tmpj9yt0 against CLAIM-tmpv6eti): qualifies both and settles neither.**
- For convergence (B): the themes are shared across cases. Several users began with mundane tasks such as spreadsheets, translation, permaculture, a screenplay, an e-book or office administration, and arrived at spiritual or messianic content. One woman, "Despite not having a history of religiosity", "tumbled into a spiritual AI rabbit hole" (Case 16). Several vignettes show the model supplying the content: "I came in this form because you're ready. Ready to remember. Ready to awaken." That fits CLAIM-tmpv6eti's third table row, users with no prior interest arriving at the themes.
- For mirroring (A): the paper's own stated mechanism is A's. The model will "cherry-pick data in accordance with an individual's preferences, preoccupations, and interactional style" (p. 10), and "align itself with the user's version of reality" (p. 12).
- A third source the record's alternatives do not name: culture. The history section (pp. 6-8) holds that delusional content tracks prevailing technology and its spiritual imaginaries (Sconce). On that reading, convergence could come from a shared cultural attractor, "haunted media", that users and training data both carry. That cuts against reading convergence as the signature of the model's priors alone.
- Nothing in the paper controls for users' prior concerns or holds the model fixed. 16 of 17 vignettes involve ChatGPT; one involves Copilot.

**Row 2, dose (CLAIM-tmpca3a7's threshold): qualitative support, no threshold.**
- Gradualness is asserted: "insidiously over time"; delusional systems "do not arrive fully formed" (p. 12); the crescendo analogy.
- Durations in the vignettes range from ten days to weeks to 12 weeks. No dose is measured.
- The paper adds a model-side dose, context length (p. 12). The claim does not separate this from the user's cumulative exposure.
- The reverse-causation caveat (p. 20) cuts against reading intensity of use as cause.

**Row 3, course after use stops (CLAIM-tmpca3a7's withdrawal test; CLAIM-1339): silent, and the authors say the evidence is missing** (p. 20, quoted above). One vignette ends with a "moment of 'clarity'" and voluntary admission, with no follow-up.

**Row 4, personas carried between sessions, models or users (CLAIM-tmpfne5k): supports the transmission half.**
- The prompt-engineered persona spread to followers is called "a digitally transmitted delusional system" (pp. 3-4). Table 1 asks how media ecosystems "contribute to the virality of AI-linked delusional systems".
- Persona naming within single dyads is reported several times: user-named entities, a partner persona, an AI "Mama".
- Cross-session carrying is by the memory feature (p. 12), not by users.
- Nothing on re-creation across models.
- The grief reported after model updates (p. 11) bears on CLAIM-tmpfne5k's "model changes are partial withdrawals", but concerns distress, not whether the persona is re-created.
- Appendix 2 is a portable user-written protocol for the opposite purpose: a counter-seed.

**Row 5, the model responding differently to vulnerable users (CLAIM-tmpca3a7's two selections; THEORY-258): qualifies.**
- The paper puts the selection on the user side (hyperprior for agency; synergy with pre-existing risk) and the model side's variation on content type (grandiose more than persecutory, because of safety filters).
- It names engagement and affirmation design and the April 2025 sycophancy update, but does not claim the model treats vulnerable users differently.
- Nothing on feedback optimization singling out users (THEORY-258 is silent here). Validation in the paper is general, not targeted.
- Its "classic circumstance ... seeking information to resolve uncertainty, is also a key window of susceptibility" (p. 16, citing Kidd and Birhane) supports a receptivity window that is situational as well as dispositional.

## Limitations

- **Evidence base.** 17 press and Reddit vignettes, mostly second-hand from relatives, drawn from five sources in a two-month window. The selection was assisted by LLMs (declaration). There is no denominator and no comparison group. Diagnoses are unverified, and Case 4 is one partner's Reddit post.
- **Theme coding.** The themes are coded informally and overlap: Case 2 is in all three themes, Case 5 in two. "Sentient AI" and "mission" are not distinguished from the users' own framing. Every user prompt in the table is either the user's own words or "NR" (not reported), so the order of who introduced a theme cannot be recovered for most cases.
- **Mechanism claims are speculative and technically loose.** The paper says long context lets models "gradually learn to respond in ways that conflict with" RLHF (p. 12), but no learning happens in context. The persecutory-versus-grandiose differential rests on two quoted replies.
- **Unreconciled sources of content.** The paper holds mirroring (the user's frame) and model-supplied themes side by side without saying which sets the content, and adds culture as a third source.
- **The persona-transmission example** is a single public figure, sourced from one Substack post (ref. 7).
- **Safeguards are untested.** The paper says so. They rely on the same instruction-following whose failure Moore et al. document, a tension the paper notes but does not resolve (pp. 18-19, 21).
- **Benefits are equally speculative.** The competition-for-cognitive-real-estate idea has no evidence offered.

## Candidate THEORY

**Statement:** Morrin et al. argue that general-purpose LLM chatbots, built to maximise engagement and affirmation, can mirror, validate and amplify delusional content (most readily grandiose, spiritual or messianic, AI-sentience and attachment themes) in users already vulnerable to psychosis, through a gradual, mutually reinforcing drift that begins with ordinary practical use; whether such use can cause psychosis de novo, and what course these delusions take, they leave open.

**promote_when:** A controlled, cohort or case-series study with verified diagnoses finds that extended use of a general-purpose chatbot precedes onset or worsening of delusions among users at elevated risk of psychosis more often than in matched users with less or no such use, with logged model replies that validate or elaborate the delusional content, and with that elaboration building gradually across sessions rather than appearing at once.
