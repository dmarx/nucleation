# THEORY-1488: In ten southern Costa Rican pasture sites where weeding stopped after 2.5 years, planting six tree islands (86 trees per 0.25 ha) or a full plantation (313) still raised later-successional seedling and sapling recruitment and reduced among-site compositional variability relative to natural regeneration after 16–18 years, yet in every treatment the tree layer remained dominated by a few pioneers and the planted cohort, whose own seedlings seldom reached the sapling layer

## Source

Schubert, Zahawi, Oviedo-Brenes, Rosales & Holl (2024), [LIT-2177](../literature.d/LIT-2177.md); read in [NOTE-1660](../notes.d/NOTE-1660.md). A stand-in for Holl et al. (2020), [LIT-2145](../literature.d/LIT-2145.md), unread, for long-term recruitment only.

## What was actually shown

- **Design** (p. 3): three 0.25-ha plots per site, randomized; "313
  trees were planted in plantation, 86 in applied nucleation, and none in
  natural regeneration plots"; all plots "cleared to ground level by
  machete at ~3-month intervals for the first 2.5 years". Natural
  regeneration n = 9, nucleation n = 9, plantation n = 8.
- **Recruitment** (pp. 4–5): richness "increased across the planting
  gradient" (156, 185, 196; reference forest 205); late-successional
  saplings in both planted treatments "having >50% more species than
  natural" regeneration.
- **Variability** (p. 5): "Both seedling and sapling communities showed
  greater variability in species composition under natural regeneration
  than in either planting intervention."
- **Tree layer** (abstract, p. 7): "Trees (≥5 cm dbh) in all restoration
  treatments continued to be dominated by a few early-successional
  species and originally transplanted individuals"; later-successional
  trees "a small fraction (8%)" of reference-forest density.
- **Planted species** (abstract): their seedlings were most abundant
  under nucleation "though few transitioned into the sapling layer".
- What could have come out otherwise: natural regeneration could have
  caught up within two decades, as the authors' unpublished dispersal data
  suggest it may yet.

## What this does not say

- That the planted nucleus is self-maintaining: it persists without
  regenerating; what regenerates is the community it drew.
- That recovery is independent of outside supply: it "is partly a
  function of the extensive seed sources remaining in remnant forests"
  and "would likely be substantially slower in more deforested
  landscapes" (p. 10).
- Anything about island size: the three sizes were not analysed apart.
- That pasture is an alternative stable state: stalling under natural
  regeneration is a risk the authors name, not a result.
- Anything about cost, or about canopy cover, seed rain and birds at 15
  years, which are Holl et al. (2020)'s.
