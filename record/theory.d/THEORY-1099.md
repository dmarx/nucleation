# THEORY-1099: On Kay and colleagues' relaxed-clock dating of pre-LECA gene duplications, most duplications in eukaryotic gene families of archaeal origin, including actin, tubulin, vesicle-trafficking and nuclear-targeted families, predate the onset of duplications in genes of alphaproteobacterial origin at about 2.2 billion years ago, which the authors take as the start of mitochondrial endosymbiosis, so the host was elaborating its cytoskeleton, endomembranes and nuclear machinery before the mitochondrion; each date is a maximum age for its paralogues' functions, many intervals reach below 2.2 billion years, and the order of phagocytosis and the mitochondrion is left open

## Source

Kay, Spang, Szöllősi, Pisani, Williams & Donoghue (2026), *Nature*
650: 129–140, [LIT-1566](../literature.d/LIT-1566.md), read in full in [NOTE-1234](../notes.d/NOTE-1234.md) from the PubMed
Central copy of the version of record. The sections "Establishing the
eukaryogenesis timeline", "Dating mitochondrial endosymbiosis", the four
sections on cell systems, "Testing hypotheses of eukaryogenesis", the
Methods, and the captions of Figures 1–5.

## What was actually shown

- **A dated species tree.** 62 concatenated markers and 18 fossil
  calibrations give nFECA 3.05–2.79 Ga, mFECA 2.37–2.13 Ga and LECA
  1.80–1.67 Ga.
- **Dated duplications.** 135 gene trees (95 archaeal, 40 bacterial)
  were dated with priors taken from the species tree. Duplications in
  Asgard-origin genes were constrained between nFECA and LECA, and those
  in alphaproteobacterial-origin genes between mFECA and LECA.
- **The marker.** Alphaproteobacterial-origin duplications begin within
  about 200 Myr of mFECA. Among the earliest are import-system
  components (TIM14/PAM16, 2.24–1.99 Ga; TIM44, 2.22–2.00 Ga) and
  paralogues that now act outside the mitochondrion. The authors align
  endosymbiosis to this onset, about 2.2 Ga, on the ground that
  duplicate fixation is far more frequent in eukaryotes than in
  prokaryotes.
- **The order.** "85% of the pre-LECA duplicates of archaeal origin were
  estimated to have been fixed prior to mitochondrial endosymbiosis."
  The oldest are in trafficking between Golgi, ER and plasma membrane
  (TRAPPC3, 3.00–2.54 Ga), in γ-tubulin (2.84–2.41 Ga), in ARP3
  (2.90–2.47 Ga) and in RNA polymerase subunits (2.91–2.34 Ga). 16 of
  19 duplications in non-alphaproteobacterial bacterial genes come after
  the marker.
- **What is left open.** Endolysosomal duplications date with the
  earliest alphaproteobacterial ones, and "our results do not allow us to
  discriminate between the origin of phagocytosis and the timing of
  mitochondrial endosymbiosis".

What could have failed: archaeal-origin duplications could have
clustered after the onset of alphaproteobacterial ones, as a
mitochondria-early scenario predicts. Their median ages did not. The
priors allow archaeal duplications to be older than any
alphaproteobacterial one, so they make the result possible but do not
force it.

## What this does not say

- **Not that the host had these features before the mitochondrion.** A
  duplication date is "a maximum estimate for the timing of origin" of a
  paralogue's function. The function may have come later. Many
  intervals (ARP2 2.72–2.06, α/β-tubulin 2.47–1.92, nuclear targeting
  2.86–2.08 Ga) overlap the 2.2 Ga marker.
- **Not that the mitochondrion entered at 2.2 Ga.** That date is the
  onset of symbiont-gene duplication. Equating the two is argued, not
  tested.
- **Not about the nucleus as a compartment.** Its defining
  eukaryote-specific genes, such as nuclear pores, cannot be dated by
  this method. What is dated is nuclear targeting of archaeal paralogues
  and RNA polymerase diversification. The conclusion says "a nucleus (or
  protonucleus), or at the least ... the physical separation of
  translation and transcription".
- **Not that phagocytosis brought in the mitochondrion**, or that it did
  not.
- **Not when non-alphaproteobacterial genes were acquired.** Their
  duplications are dated; their arrival is not. The argument against
  serial endosymbiosis rests on duplication timing.
- **Not about dependence.** The host's elaboration is its own gene
  duplication. Nothing here says when host and symbiont became dependent
  on each other, or reproduced together.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md).** This bears on whether eukaryogenesis was "one
  two-partner merger or a sequence of associations". It favours one
  endosymbiotic partner, after a long host-only phase, with other
  bacterial genes arriving by transfer. It dates G (the host's
  duplications) and gives an early date for the import machinery, the
  start of C on the symbiont side. It says nothing about D, R or S.
- **[LIT-1595](../literature.d/LIT-1595.md) ([THEORY-1102](THEORY-1102.md)).** Pittis & Gabaldón reach the same
  mitochondrion-late order from branch lengths. They infer a host
  already chimaeric with non-alphaproteobacterial genes before the
  mitochondrion. This paper dates most non-alphaproteobacterial
  duplications after it. Duplication is not acquisition, so the two can
  both be right. The comparison is mine.
- **[LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md).** The cultured Asgard isolate is internally simple. This
  paper says so itself and does not take it as a contradiction: a
  modern relative is not nFECA's lineage, and the paper "does not imply
  that a host cell of crown eukaryote size and system-complexity existed
  before mitochondrial endosymbiosis".
- **[LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md).** The hydrogen hypothesis is the paper's example of a
  mitochondria-early model, and is rejected.
