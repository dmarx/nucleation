# CLAIM-677: A technology can become practically compulsory without any rule requiring it: as infrastructures, competitors and expectations adapt to its adoption, the alternatives left to those who decline it shrink, so the freedom an arrangement leaves is measured by what remains open to a non-adopter, not by what it lets adopters do

## The claim

The exchange "Analyze technological civilization trap"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6af6b47c-8b0c-83ea-ae04-3e3eb2649597,
2026-11-13) returned to one mechanism in four turns. A3 §III:

> Something that originally expanded choice gradually becomes a
> prerequisite for ordinary participation.

A13 §II named it "the dependency ratchet" and gave it three parts:

- **Infrastructural adaptation.** Supporting institutions reorganize around
  the technology (roads, fuel and parking for the automobile), which makes
  it more useful and its alternatives less practical. "The crucial point is
  that a technological arrangement can become compulsory without any legal
  requirement to use it."
- **Competitive pressure.** Firms, workers and states adopt because rivals
  do, so adoption is "formally voluntary but strategically compelled".
- **Cultural normalization.** "What initially appears optional becomes
  expected", until the arrangement is hard even to recognize as contingent.

A13 places the point of no return where "the cost of restoring
alternatives exceeds what individual participants can reasonably bear".
A11 §4 gave the measure the claim keeps:

> The relevant measure of technological freedom is not merely what a
> technology allows someone to do, but what possibilities remain available
> when they choose not to use it.

## Which sense of "organization"

Where A speaks of society or institutions "reorganizing" around a
technology, the sense is [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)'s sense 1, a change in a relational
arrangement of infrastructures, practices and expectations, not an act of
a collective institution (sense 3). No one need decide it; that is the
claim's point.

## What the record held

- **Lock-in between competing technologies.** Arthur's model ([THEORY-863](../theory.d/THEORY-863.md)):
  increasing returns make one technology take the market, not necessarily
  the better one, and no small reversal undoes it. That is a choice
  between two technologies, not between adopting and abstaining; what it
  carries to this claim is [ARG-104](../arguments.d/ARG-104.md).
- **Path dependence in politics** ([THEORY-885](../theory.d/THEORY-885.md)), and protective lock-in, a
  regime that shifts the evidence for its own necessity ([CLAIM-445](CLAIM-445.md),
  [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)).
- **An arrangement that produces the dependence it exploits** ([CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md)),
  and dependence organized as opportunity, vulnerability or leverage
  ([CLAIM-428](CLAIM-428.md)). Those are about a party that relies on a provider. This
  claim is about the party who declines: what the adopters' reorganization
  leaves to someone who does not take part.

## What it does not say

- Not that dependence is unfreedom. A says repeatedly that some
  dependencies (medicine, the grid, sanitation) enlarge what people can
  do; the claim measures what is left to a non-adopter, and a technology
  can enlarge that too.
- Not that the mechanisms always run. A13: "A proposed mechanism of
  technological lock-in, not a universal law of innovation."
- Not when compulsory dependence is objectionable. A11 §4: "compulsory
  dependence requires justification"; the threshold A proposed for that is
  [CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md).

## Sources A named

Illich's radical monopoly (Tools for Conviviality, [LIT-2558](../literature.d/LIT-2558.md)), Ellul's
autonomy of technique ([LIT-2576](../literature.d/LIT-2576.md)) and Rosa's dynamic stabilization

## Note of 2026-11-13: the readings of 2026-11-13

Readings registered from the exchange and read, or read through stand-ins, on 2026-11-13. Each that gives evidence carries `corroborates` to this claim.

- **Illich through Cayley** ([THEORY-1862](../theory.d/THEORY-1862.md)): the test of radical monopoly, "whether one can still find an alternative or decently do without", is this claim's measure; the car environment its infrastructural mechanism; alternatives made "unimaginable" its cultural one. **It qualifies the title.** Illich's burial example ends in law: "Now legislation is being passed to make the mortician's ministrations compulsory." A de facto monopoly can later be codified, so "without any rule requiring it" describes how the compulsion can arise, not that it stays unwritten. The title is kept; this note records the qualification.
- **Ellul 1983** ([THEORY-1864](../theory.d/THEORY-1864.md)): outside the technical city "the only choices left" are two. Ellul goes further than the claim: particular choices "no longer have much significance".
- **Rosa** ([THEORY-1835](../theory.d/THEORY-1835.md)): people keep pace "para evitar la pérdida de opciones y conexiones", the non-adopter's loss of options, and standing still means falling behind.
- **Moen** ([THEORY-1867](../theory.d/THEORY-1867.md)): the car city and the arms race, the infrastructural and competitive mechanisms, from a source independent of the exchange.
- **Morozov** ([THEORY-1855](../theory.d/THEORY-1855.md)): once most people self-track, declining is "no longer an autonomous decision"; mechanism and measure, by example.
- **Crawford 2006** ([THEORY-1844](../theory.d/THEORY-1844.md)): Ford "destroyed the possibility of an alternative way of working"; competitive mechanism.
- **Postman** ([THEORY-1840](../theory.d/THEORY-1840.md)): curricula reorganized around tests; technologies become mythic.
- **Kelly** ([THEORY-1838](../theory.d/THEORY-1838.md)): a foil, not evidence. His "More ways, more choices" measures the options technology gives its adopters, the measure this claim sets aside; it neither meets the claim's defeater nor answers it.
- **Lanier** ([THEORY-1858](../theory.d/THEORY-1858.md)) and **Hui** ([THEORY-1865](../theory.d/THEORY-1865.md)): Lanier names lock-in in software without the claim's mechanism; Hui's bifurcated technological futures say lock-in to one path is not necessary, without a mechanism that keeps a non-adopter's options open. Points of contact, no relation.
- **Feenberg** ([THEORY-1841](../theory.d/THEORY-1841.md)): lock-in depends on the prevailing technical code, which shifts the emphasis to contestability; consistent with the claim.

## Note of 2026-11-13: Marcuse and Illich, read

- **Marcuse, One-Dimensional Man, read in full** ([THEORY-1853](../theory.d/THEORY-1853.md),
  [NOTE-1924](../notes.d/NOTE-1924.md)). Mixed support, argued in [ARG-108](../arguments.d/ARG-108.md). The book
  corroborates the mechanism: once a project is operative "it tends to
  become exclusive"; "The initial choice defines the range of
  possibilities open on this way, and precludes alternative possibilities
  incompatible with it"; compliance becomes "a rational technological
  attitude"; the ways out become "unimaginable", the cultural mechanism.
  It argues against the measure: "The range of choice open to the
  individual is not the decisive factor in determining the degree of
  human freedom, but what can be chosen and what is chosen by the
  individual." If the arrangement shapes the wants by which a non-adopter
  chooses as well as the options left to her, two arrangements leaving
  her the same options can differ in freedom. That is the kind of
  argument the claim's second defeater describes. It does not meet the
  defeater as stated, since Marcuse gives no such pair and his criterion
  of true and false needs is contested; it presses the measure toward a
  necessary condition rather than the whole of freedom. Status
  unchanged. Marcuse's mechanism is needs and satisfaction, not
  infrastructure.
- **Illich, Tools for Conviviality, a two-page excerpt** ([THEORY-1861](../theory.d/THEORY-1861.md),
  [NOTE-1921](../notes.d/NOTE-1921.md)). Corroborates, in Illich's own words, the measure and the
  road mechanism. Ordinary monopolies "restrict the choices open to the
  consumer"; radical monopoly is "the dominance of one type of product
  rather than the dominance of one brand", where "one industrial
  production process exercises an exclusive control over the satisfaction
  of a pressing need, and excludes nonindustrial activities from
  competition": the harm falls on the non-adopter's alternative, not on
  adopters' choice. "That motor traffic curtails the right to walk, not
  that more people drive Chevies than Fords, constitutes radical
  monopoly"; cars shape the city, "practically ruling out locomotion on
  foot or by bicycle in Los Angeles". "Cars create distance" is a further
  mechanism the claim does not list: adoption enlarges the very need the
  technology serves, so that people "spend more time for the sake of
  travel". The excerpt does not reach the burial case or "unimaginable";
  the qualification of the title in the note above still rests on Cayley
  ([THEORY-1862](../theory.d/THEORY-1862.md)).

## Note of 2026-11-13: stand-ins, read

At the owner's request to "look for adjacent stand ins for unreachable/unfetched work", neighbours of works the readings could not reach were read.

- **Illich, Energy and Equity** ([THEORY-1863](../theory.d/THEORY-1863.md), [NOTE-1913](../notes.d/NOTE-1913.md)), read in full. Corroborates the mechanism and the measure, and qualifies the title; argued in [ARG-107](../arguments.d/ARG-107.md). "Industry dominates traffic as soon as daily life comes to depend on motorized trips"; radical monopoly arises "by virtue of its acquired ability to create and shape the need which it alone can satisfy". The shoe case is the claim's non-adopter: "Without any intentional conspiracy between the promoters of national development and the shoe industry, the barefoot in these countries are now barred from any office." But in the same passage "access to schools, jobs, and public services was denied to the barefoot" by "Teachers or party officials", and for schooling a law came first: "Only when laws were enacted to make schools both compulsory and free did the educator assume the power to deny learning opportunities on the job". So "without any rule requiring it" holds of the technology's adoption, not of the exclusion of those who decline it, which gatekeepers and law can enforce. With the burial case above, that is a second qualification; the title is kept, and the owner may narrow it. Burial and "unimaginable" are still not reached in Illich's own words.
- **Winner, "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"** ([THEORY-1859](../theory.d/THEORY-1859.md), [NOTE-1941](../notes.d/NOTE-1941.md)), read in full. Corroborates the mechanism, from outside the exchange: Moses's overpasses narrowed the options of those without cars by arrangement, not rule, and the arrangement "becomes just another part of the landscape", the cultural mechanism; the tomato harvester cut growers from about four thousand to about six hundred with no one intending it.
- **Crawford, the 2017 interview** ([THEORY-1839](../theory.d/THEORY-1839.md)): weak. The "captive audience" cannot decline a shared space, the claim's non-adopter applied to attention; no mechanism of adoption. No relation.
- **Vallor, CyCon 2013** ([THEORY-1847](../theory.d/THEORY-1847.md)): weak. Once a practice stops, the expertise to resume or override the machine is gone, which removes the option of returning to unaided practice; not framed as an option set. No relation.
- **Lanier, You Are Not a Gadget, the publisher's excerpt** ([THEORY-1846](../theory.d/THEORY-1846.md)): lock-in named, not argued; leaves it open.

## Note of 2026-11-13: on the flourishing-and-meaning line

The claim takes the line flourishing-and-meaning ([ADR-099](../decisions.d/ADR-099.md)) for its measure: the freedom an arrangement leaves, judged by what remains open to a non-adopter, is a claim about the lives an arrangement leaves open. [CLAIM-674](CLAIM-674.md) complements the measure from the other side: how fully an arrangement satisfies the preferences of those who adopt it does not show that their lives go better. Marcuse's objection to the measure ([ARG-108](../arguments.d/ARG-108.md)), that what is chosen is shaped as well as what can be chosen, now has a claim on this line to press against: [CLAIM-689](CLAIM-689.md), that a want's social origin does not by itself decide its worth. No change to the claim.
