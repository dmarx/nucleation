# LIT-tmp6rglf: Random Perturbations of Dynamical Systems

Freidlin, M. I. & Wentzell, A. D. (2012). *Random Perturbations of Dynamical Systems*, 3rd ed., trans. J. Szücs. Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften 260. Berlin: Springer. doi:10.1007/978-3-642-25847-3. (Russian original 1979; English editions 1984, 1998.)

## Key takeaways

*From the front matter only, NOTE-tmp4cwdr (Skimmed). Page numbers are the book's.*

- **The claim to priority.** "This book was the first monograph in which
  large-deviation theory for stochastic processes was presented" (Preface to
  the Second Edition, p. vii); the quasi-potential is the authors' notion,
  "which we introduced more than forty years ago." (p. v).
- **Rare events decide the long run.** The determination of which unlikely
  events are more or less improbable "serves as a key to the question of
  what the behavior, with probability close to 1, of the process" will be
  on an infinite interval (Introduction, p. xix).
- **The action functional.** Its value "characterizes the difficulty of
  the passage of" the process near a path, and as ε → 0 "only the summand
  with smallest S(ϕ) becomes essential." (p. xx). The exit time from a
  domain then has "an approximately exponential distribution" (p. xx).
- **Rough results only.** "Furthermore, in this book we only consider rough
  results on large deviations," that is, up to logarithmic equivalence
  (p. xxiv).

**The stand-in.** Berglund's survey of Kramers' law, LIT-tmp76lym, read in
full, restates the book's Wentzell–Freidlin action functional,
quasi-potential, exit-time theorem and exit-location concentration, and
points to the hierarchy of cycles. It stands in for Chapter 4's exit
problem, at the level of statements, not proofs. It does not restate the
limit of the stationary distribution among several stable sets (Chapter 6),
averaging (Chapter 7), Hamiltonian systems and graphs (Chapters 8–9),
stability and optimal stabilization (Chapter 10), or perturbations with
jumps (Chapter 5). Kuehn's critical-transitions paper, LIT-tmpc7r2s, cites
the 1998 second edition for the escape time it uses.
