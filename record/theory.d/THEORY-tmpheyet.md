# THEORY-tmpheyet: For moral purposes the body is bounded below by depth: objects whose real definitions are too microphysically committal are not body parts though they are parts in the ordinary sense, and bodily rights and interests concern only events that change the body's high-level properties, so a brain-computer interface user's implemented software can be a body part while the third-party server running it is not

## Source

Christopher Register (2025), "The Depth of the Body", [LIT-138](../literature.d/LIT-138.md), *Philosophers'
Imprint* 25: 47, 1–14, read in full in [NOTE-151](../notes.d/NOTE-151.md).

## What was actually shown

**The problem (§2).** If a BCI is a body part, and its computation runs on a
server a third party owns, then either someone else owns part of a person, or
the user's bodily rights are qualified. The paper rejects qualified rights
(§2.3). A harm condition is too narrow: an unconsented pelvic exam under
anaesthesia violates without harming. An interests condition is too broad.
Autonomy and disrespect conditions presuppose the answer. It adds an
intransience requirement (§2.4): a body part must figure "in a lasting or
irreplaceable way" in bodily processes, which individual molecules fail.

**Depth (§3).** The depth of a description is its degree of microphysical
commitment. The depth of an object is the depth of the primary constituents of
its real definition (after Rosen 2015). A water molecule, defined by atoms, is
deeper than a heart, "the organ that circulates blood in an organism". "Objects
that are too deep are not genuine body parts." The threshold is
context-sensitive, like "tall".

**Two principles (§5).** An event is high-level neutral with respect to an
object "just in case e does not change the high-level properties" of it.
*Bodily interests are high-level* and *bodily rights are high-level*: both
concern events that are not high-level neutral, and high-level-neutral events
"generally" do not bear on them. An unconsented MRI is wrong for other reasons:
disregard of autonomy over medical tests, moving the patient, and extracting
information that is not high-level neutral.

**BCIs (§6).** The software object, defined by algorithms, is a body part. The
server, defined by transistors and circuits, is not. Interference that leaves
the software unaffected is not interference in the body, so maintenance and
replacement are permissible. Interference that changes its high-level
properties is "generally impermissible". Operators acquire duties about risk,
such as backup servers, which the paper calls "an unprecedented species of
moral obligation".

**What could have come out otherwise.** A high-level-neutral intervention could
have seemed a plain violation of the body. The MRI is the paper's hardest case,
and its reply moves the wrong elsewhere.

## What this does not say

- **That BCIs are body parts.** That is assumed (§2.1: "I will assume as much in
  this paper"). The conclusion is conditional on it.
- **That the ethics needs the ontology.** The principles "don't depend on" the
  real-definition claim, which offers "a simple and unified explanation" (§5).
  The abstract's "crucial" is stronger than the body supports.
- **Where the floor is.** No criterion fixes the threshold in a given case.
  Whether brain tissue needs a lower one is left open.
- **That moving software between servers is permitted.** Transfer changes
  location, so it is "not necessarily high-level neutral". The paper leaves its
  ethics open.
- **That moral-body parthood is a mereology.** The heart is a moral body part, a
  molecule is an ordinary part of the heart, and the molecule is not a moral body
  part. So the moral relation is not the transitive closure of ordinary
  parthood. [NOTE-151](../notes.d/NOTE-151.md) makes this observation; the paper does not.
- **Anything about AI systems.** The paper concerns BCI software as part of a
  human body. Where an AI system is "located" is an analogy it does not draw.

- **Mereology.** Register's relation is a restricted "φ-part" in the sense of
  Varzi's survey ([LIT-124](../literature.d/LIT-124.md), [THEORY-tmpnan16](THEORY-tmpnan16.md)), not a rival to parthood.

  restricts parthood by level.

  of the extended mind's reliability and availability conditions.
- **Neuroprosthetics ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md)).** Pernu locates multiple realization at a cortical
  variable; Register locates the body part at the software level above many
  possible servers.

  Register's software body parts both let information-level objects count as
  constituents of a person.
