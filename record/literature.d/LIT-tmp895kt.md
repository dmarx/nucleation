# LIT-tmp895kt: Is It Good to Cooperate? Testing the Theory of Morality-as-Cooperation in 60 Societies

Oliver Scott Curry, Daniel Austin Mullins, Harvey Whitehouse (2019), *Current Anthropology 60(1), 47–69 (followed by invited peer commentaries)* — DOI-10.1086/701478

## Key takeaways

- In the ethnographic records of the 60-society HRAF Probability Sample, seven cooperative behaviours predicted by game theory are morally valenced positively in 961 of 962 observations (99.9%). These are helping kin, helping one's group, reciprocating, bravery, deference to superiors, dividing disputed resources, and respecting prior possession. The authors treat them as candidate universal moral rules.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The theory of morality-as-cooperation says morality is a set of biological and cultural solutions to recurring problems of cooperation, identified through non-zero-sum game theory. It predicts that seven specific cooperative behaviours will be judged morally good in every culture. The authors code the moral valence of these behaviours in the ethnographies of 60 societies. The valence is uniformly positive, most of these morals appear in most societies, and they occur equally often across world regions. The authors propose the seven as universal moral rules and the theory as a unified account of morality for anthropology.

What a deeper reading should check:

- An empirical anchor for game-theoretic accounts of morality. It is relevant to the ethics cluster and to the cooperation thread (c34 ergodicity, c31/c32 collective behaviour).
- The test is lopsided: coding records only the valence of behaviours that appear, so absence of evidence of negative valence is weak evidence. Check how the paper handles the fact that most morals were "observed in most" but not all societies (Table 2 coverage), and what the commentaries criticize.
- Check how independent the coding was (who coded, inter-rater reliability) in the methods and supplement.
