# THEORY-418: A collective becomes a new level of individuality, on this sketch, only when collaboration toward a shared goal, rather than coordination or cooperation, organizes it and some core interactions have become generatively entrenched and, as the paper asserts without deriving it from entrenchment, it has organizational closure; such a community can then be reproduced only by exporting an organized sample of its roles and interactions, not of its members alone

## Source

Griesemer & Shavit (2023), [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md) (*Philosophical Transactions of the
Royal Society B* 378, 20210398), read in full in [NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md) from the Europe
PMC XML. Section numbers stand in for pages.

## What was actually shown

Nothing was shown in the experimental sense; "No data were reported". The
paper states an idealized sequence and argues for parts of it informally.

**The 3Cs (§1, Table 1).**
- *Coordination* in a *collection*: ordering in space and time among
  co-located members; contributions "mainly 'aggregative'".
- *Cooperation* in a *conexus*: a web of binary interactions without a
  required shared goal. Benefit can still be booked per member: "the
  currency of cooperative interaction is the same as for individual
  action" (the lions' meat per lion).
- *Collaboration* in a *community*: heterogeneous multi-way interactions
  for a shared goal. Group benefit "cannot even be recognized as group
  benefits without reference to the special interactions engendered by the
  group", and "reliability is thus an emergent community property".

Table 1 draws "Transition to a new level-of-individuality perspective"
between Stage 2 and Stage 3 only. Collections and conexuses are collectives
but not individuals.

**The mechanism (§2).** Generative entrenchment (after Wimsatt): core
interactions come to be ones "later-evolved interactions will depend on
for their existence and character, so that perturbations of it will tend to
be catastrophic". The transition is "highly contingent, even if
contingently irreversible". A conexus may instead dissolve back to
coordination or persist, unstable, on external scaffolding.

**Closure and reproduction (§3).** Community needs organizational closure
(Moreno & Mossio, adopted not argued), "Hence, community constitutes a new
level of individuality." Following Caporael, it "must be exported as an
organized generative package larger than a single individual member". The
Neolithic thought experiment is conditional: a migrant group "might
require" one of each occupation and the ongoing interaction types.

**What could have come out otherwise.** As stated, nothing yet: there are
no criteria by which an actual group could fail to be a community. That is
why the account is Proposed.

## What this does not say

- **That transitions are described without circularity.** That claim is in
  the abstract only. The among-members against within-members criterion it
  names is used once, to describe the transition (§2), and never as a
  test. Stage 3's shared goal (§1), and §3's "focal goal of every
  participating member" and "cognition about the group itself", presuppose
  the group ([NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md), corrections).
- **That individuality is a special case of collectivity, with explanatory
  benefits.** Abstract only. The body gives a graded scale of collectivity
  and no argument for the benefit.
- **That real transitions follow the sequence.** It is "one possible
  pathway", like an embryologist's normal table. It fits "egalitarian"
  transitions better than "fraternal" ones, which may run in reverse; the
  paper's own discipline-formation example is a reverse case.
- **That inheritance and individuality arise together.** §3's "seem likely
  to evolve in tandem" is hedged, and §2 allows a new level of reproduction
  "but not yet of a transition in inheritance system".
- **That it applies to all life histories.** That is a stated hope. Only
  human cases are discussed, and hominid evolution, named in the abstract,
  is not.
- **That Bourrat's account is wrong.** The paper endorses his scaffolding
  and endogenization view and adds community reliability as a "missing
  ingredient", deferring the model to future work.

## Connections

- [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md) asks when a combined form is one system, relative to a
  target. Entrenchment plus closure is a candidate criterion of the kind it
  wants, but the question asks for one that returns a verdict that can be
  wrong, and this sketch has no measurement. It is not linked as an answer.
- [QUESTION-093](../questions.d/QUESTION-093.md) asks whether organizational closure is needed before a
  coalition is a higher-level agent. This account makes closure the mark of
  a new *individual*, not of an agent, so it does not answer that either.
- Levin's nested agents ([THEORY-583](THEORY-583.md)) also put a shared goal at the
  centre of a higher-level whole, but for Levin any set of parts that
  pursue a goal together is already a larger agent. This sketch asks
  more: collaboration rather than coordination, entrenchment and closure,
  and what it yields is an individual, not an agent.
- [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md) asks what must be transmitted for a collective to persist
  through the replacement of all its members. This sketch's claim that
  such a community is reproduced only by exporting an organized sample of
  its roles and interactions bears on it, though it concerns reproduction,
  not persistence, and gives no criterion separating continuation from
  reconstitution.
- [THEORY-071](THEORY-071.md) makes individuality a necessary but insufficient condition of
  minimal agency; [THEORY-232](THEORY-232.md) compares a whole's agency with its parts'.
  Both start where this account ends, from an individual already given.
- Bourrat's individuality papers, [LIT-157](../literature.d/LIT-157.md), [LIT-049](../literature.d/LIT-049.md) and [LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md), are the
  stated rival emphasis: Darwinian properties plus individualizing
  mechanisms, against community reliability and heterogeneity of
  interactions. Nothing here refutes them.
- The SEP entries on biological individuals ([LIT-168](../literature.d/LIT-168.md)) and on units and
  levels of selection ([LIT-160](../literature.d/LIT-160.md)) are the broader map. O'Malley and Parke
  ([LIT-210](../literature.d/LIT-210.md), [NOTE-159](../notes.d/NOTE-159.md)) report that eukaryogenesis may not have been a
  transition in individuality at all.

## Note of 2026-10-30: the proposal split off

What Griesemer and Shavit propose, as distinct from whether it is right, is
now [THEORY-1247](THEORY-1247.md), Active, and this entry extends it ([ADR-065](../decisions.d/ADR-065.md) §3). The phrase "on
this sketch" in this title asserts the account, as "as X proposes" would.
So this entry keeps the question whether the account holds, and its
promote_when. The entries that test it ([CASE-171](../cases.d/CASE-171.md), [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)) cite this
one.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

The record has no operational criteria that sort real groups into
collection, conexus and community. Nor does it have a worked case showing
the new level of individuality at the conexus–community step, with benefit
that cannot be booked per member and a knockout effect. [NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md) found none
in the paper: "No data were reported". [CASE-171](../cases.d/CASE-171.md) and [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md) name the
mealybug patchwork as a place to test it, and its reports are unread.
