# THEORY-1109: Santana-Molina, Williams, Snel and Spang find that the last eukaryotic common ancestor's central carbon metabolism was chimeric: a few Asgard-archaeal enzymes in cytosolic glycolysis and the pentose phosphate pathway, four placed with Asgard archaea at bootstrap 39–97% and two ambiguously, most alphaproteobacterial ones in the mitochondrial TCA cycle and pyruvate conversion, and others from various bacteria with no third dominant donor, since remodelled by loss, horizontal replacement and retargeting so that compartment no longer follows origin; the authors take the cytosol–mitochondrion split as support for syntrophy

## Source

Santana-Molina, Williams, Snel & Spang (2025), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*
9(4): 613–627, [LIT-1564](../literature.d/LIT-1564.md), read in full in [NOTE-1239](../notes.d/NOTE-1239.md) from the PubMed
Central copy (PMC11976288). The Results, the Discussion, Methods, Figures 1–5
and Extended Data Figures 1–7, read from captions.

## What was actually shown

- **LECA's pathways.** Putative LECA clades for nine of ten glycolytic
  steps, seven of eight pentose phosphate steps and all ten TCA steps, in
  64 hand-curated gene-family trees.
- **Asgard enzymes.** ADPGK (76% bootstrap), APGM (89%), enolase (39%) and
  RPIA (97%) nest with Asgard. GPMI and pyruvate kinase do so ambiguously.
  Except for enolase, they are patchily distributed today, "apparently
  because of independent horizontal replacement events".
- **Alphaproteobacterial enzymes.** Seventeen: two glycolytic (ALDO, TPI),
  one pentose phosphate (PRPS), four pyruvate dehydrogenase subunits and ten
  TCA enzymes. Most are mitochondrial. Some sister groups are mixed with
  Gammaproteobacteria.
- **Other donors.** Individual well-supported trees point to Chlamydia,
  Planctomycetota–Verrucomicrobiota, Fusobacteriota, Dependentiae and
  Chloroflexota. "no additional dominant source of gene donations is
  apparent."
- **Remodelling.** Asgard-derived enzymes were replaced by bacterial ones in
  some lineages. Enzymes of non-alphaproteobacterial origin carry
  mitochondrial targeting signals, and some alphaproteobacterial ones do
  not. Plastid glycolysis comes mostly from duplicated host genes.
- **The reading.** The split "is consistent with symbiogenetic models".
  Then, more strongly: "Specifically, our results support the view of
  syntrophic interactions between host and endosymbiont", the archaeon
  fermenting sugars and passing reducing equivalents to a bacterium "which
  contributed a TCA cycle and an electron transport chain".

What could have failed: carbon metabolism could have been wholly bacterial,
as earlier work proposed, and in four to six trees it is not. Asgard and
alphaproteobacterial contributions could have been mixed across pathways
and compartments. In the main they are not.

## What this does not say

- **Not that syntrophy is singled out.** Any model in which an archaeal host
  took up a bacterium with a TCA cycle predicts archaeal cytosolic enzymes
  and bacterial mitochondrial ones. That includes models where the host was
  already complex and took the bacterium up by engulfment. The authors list
  what would discriminate the models, "the types of metabolites exchanged,
  ... the timing of mitochondrial acquisition, the mechanism of
  mitochondrial uptake", and test none. The inference is mine.
- **Not that glycolysis is mainly Asgard.** Glycolysis has Asgard enzymes,
  two alphaproteobacterial ones (ALDO, TPI) and several from other bacteria
  (HK, PFKA, GAPDH, PGK). The split describes where each partner's
  contributions lie, not each pathway's main origin.
- **Not that the host's metabolism dominated.** In this pathway set,
  alphaproteobacterial contributions outnumber Asgard ones by 17 to six.
- **Not that modern eukaryotes show the trace plainly.** The trace is read
  from LECA's reconstructed set. In living eukaryotes most Asgard enzymes
  have been replaced and targeting has drifted from origin.

## Connections

- **[THEORY-1104](THEORY-1104.md) (Tobiasson et al.): compatible, with a tension.** Both
  find the alphaproteobacterial share centred on the mitochondrion.
  Tobiasson et al. find Asgard leading across most of metabolism, and this
  paper counts more alphaproteobacterial enzymes in carbon metabolism. The
  measures differ.
- **[THEORY-1105](THEORY-1105.md) (Bernabeu et al.): compatible on the presence of the
  other donors, rival on their rank.** The phyla Bernabeu et al. call major
  donors appear here as sisters to a few enzymes. This paper finds no third
  dominant donor. Sixty-four families is too few to rank donors, so the
  rivalry is weak. The two also differ on whether LECA had POR (PFO).
- **[LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md) (Martin & Müller 1998, unavailable).** This paper sets its
  archaeal contributions against "the hypothesis that ... the archaeal host
  metabolism was replaced by counterparts from the endosymbiont", citing
  that work. On the paper's evidence, the replacement was partial.
- **[LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md) (Imachi et al. 2020, unread).** Cited among the syntrophy models
  this paper supports.
- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md).** It bears on G (functions split between partner genomes by
  origin, now all nuclear) and on C (host-derived enzymes imported into the
  mitochondrion). In plastids it suggests that host compensation may
  promote symbiont gene loss. It bears on D only through the syntrophy
  reading, which is inferred.
