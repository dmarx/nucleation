# LIT-3170: Improving immunisation coverage in rural India: clustered randomised controlled evaluation of immunisation campaigns with and without incentives

Banerjee, A. V., Duflo, E., Glennerster, R. & Kothari, D. (2010). Improving immunisation coverage in rural India: clustered randomised controlled evaluation of immunisation campaigns with and without incentives. *BMJ* 340, c2220. doi:10.1136/bmj.c2220.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-2418](../notes.d/NOTE-2418.md) (full text; appendix 2 not read).*

- **Supply alone is modest.** Reliable camps: 18 % fully immunised against
  6 % in control villages; "improving the supply alone might not be
  enough".
- **The demand side doubles it.** With incentives: 39 %, relative risk 2.2
  against camps alone; the incentive "was mainly to reduce the number of
  children dropping out after three injections."
- **Cheaper per child.** "$27.94 ... in the reliable camp with incentives
  and $55.83 ... in the reliable camp without incentives", since the
  nurse's fixed day was spread over 13.4 immunisations instead of 4.5.
- **Not separable.** "we could not introduce an incentive programme
  without simultaneously making the supply more reliable."

## Standing in the record
