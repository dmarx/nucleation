# THEORY-tmpey5s8: Life raises the Earth's entropy production, chiefly by increasing the absorption of sunlight, and if the planet is held at maximum entropy production, the negative feedbacks of that state would give the homeostasis the Gaia hypothesis postulates

## Source

Kleidon (2009), *Naturwissenschaften* 96:653–677, [LIT-519](../literature.d/LIT-519.md), read in full in
[NOTE-423](../notes.d/NOTE-423.md). The model studies the claim rests on (Kleidon 2004a, 2006;
Kleidon & Fraedrich 2005) are not in the record and are taken as the
review reports them.

## What was actually shown

**The budget (Fig. 3).** In mW m⁻² K⁻¹: absorption of sunlight, 555 at the
surface and 258 in the atmosphere; scattering and terrestrial radiative
exchange, about 26 each; the hydrologic cycle, 23; the carbon cycle, about
6, 70% of it at photosynthesis; ground heat diffusion, about 1. Turbulent
processes are small in the budget and matter because they enhance
radiative export. The order of magnitude is strong (C3). Three figures in
the text are printed in W where the inputs give mW ([NOTE-423](../notes.d/NOTE-423.md), Corrections).

**The biotic claim.** Biotic activity raises planetary entropy production
by enhancing the absorption of sunlight (C5). The support is model
studies; the author says it "would still need to be further explored"
(p. 670).

**The step to Gaia.** The implications section proceeds explicitly "under
the presumption that MEP is justified sufficiently well" (p. 670). An MEP
state answers small perturbations with negative feedback; a change in
boundary conditions first gives positive feedback until a new optimum is
reached (Fig. 7, schematic). Planetary MEP mediated by life would then give
Gaia-like homeostasis, and the Earth may have evolved stepwise toward
higher entropy production (C6, weak).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The cited models could have shown
vegetation lowering absorption. They did not, but they are the author's own
sensitivity studies, not tests designed to fail.

## What this does not say

- **That MEP holds.** The account needs it, and the review itself says "no
  one would argue that MEP is well established" (p. 673).
- **That life is a different kind of system.** It treats life as the most
  effective of the Earth's dissipative processes. It gives no criterion
  separating a living dissipative system from a convection cell, and does
  not try to ([LIT-519](../literature.d/LIT-519.md), Standing).
- **That the Earth's history shows the steps.** Stepwise increases at the
  oxidation events are an open question the review raises, not a finding.
- **That the feedbacks have been computed.** Fig. 7 is a schematic of what
  MEP would imply.
- **That life aims at anything.** "Gaia" here is homeostasis as a
  consequence of a selection principle, not regulation by design.
