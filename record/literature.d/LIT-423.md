# LIT-423: I Am a Strange Loop

Douglas Hofstadter (2007), *I Am a Strange Loop*, New York: Basic Books
(hardback, ISBN 978-0-465-03078-1; Library of Congress 2007310563, call
number BD438.5 .H64 2007; xix, 412 pp., [4] pp. of plates, illustrated,
some in colour, references pp. [377]–382, index). The trade paperback (ISBN
978-0-465-03079-8, 436 pp.) followed; the publisher gives its on-sale date
as 8 July 2008. The catalogue records give only the year 2007 for the
hardback, so `published:` carries the year, as the first of January. Open
Library dates it 26 March 2007, which I could not confirm from the publisher
or a library catalogue.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description and the catalogue record, not a
reading. What follows is what the book and its cataloguers say about it.*

- The publisher's description (Basic Books, for the paperback), verbatim in
  part: "Deep down, your brain is a chaotic seething soup of particles. On
  a higher level it is a jungle of neurons, and on a yet higher level it is
  a network of abstractions that we call 'symbols.' The most central and
  complex symbol is the one you call 'I.' An 'I' is a strange loop where
  the brain's symbolic and physical levels feed back into each other and
  flip causality upside down so that symbols seem to have gained the
  paradoxical ability to push particles around, rather than the reverse."
  It asks: "Is our 'I' merely a convenient fiction? Does 'I' exert genuine
  power over the particles in our brain, or is it helplessly pushed around
  by the all-powerful laws of physics?" It calls the book "Douglas
  Hofstadter's first book-length journey into philosophy since his Pulitzer
  Prize-winning Gödel, Escher, Bach", and heads it: "examines where the
  self comes from – and how our selves can exist in the minds of others."
- The catalogue's summary (from an earlier publisher description) calls it
  "Hofstadter's long-awaited return to the themes of Gödel, Escher,
  Bach--an original and controversial view of the nature of consciousness
  and identity", which "argues that the key to understanding selves and
  consciousness is a special kind of abstract feedback loop inhabiting our
  brains."
- The Library of Congress subject headings: consciousness; intellect; self
  (philosophy); soul.
- Contents, from the Library of Congress contents note: Preface: an author
  and his book; An affable locking of horns; On souls and their sizes; This
  teetering bulb of dread and dream; The causal potency of patterns; Loops,
  goals, and loopholes; On video feedback; Of selves and symbols; The epi
  phenomenon; Embarking on a strange-loop safari; Pattern and provability;
  Gödel's quintessential strange loop; How analogy makes meaning; On
  downward causality; The elusive apple of my "I"; Strangeness in the "I"
  of the beholder; Entwinement; Grappling with the deepest mystery; How we
  live in each other; The blurry glow of human identity; Consciousness =
  thinking; A courteous crossing of words; A brief brush with Cartesian
  egos; A tango with zombies and dualism; Killing a couple of sacred cows;
  On magnanimity and friendship; Epilogue: the quandary. The note gives no
  chapter numbers or pages.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for Douglas Hofstadter on
strange loops and the self. This is that account's book-length statement.
No anthology topic holds it, it carries no instruction for machine-learning

`Deferred` because it was not read. This record files a book from its
publisher's description and contents when no lawful text can be read, and
does not rebuild a book from what others say about it.

Its publisher presents it as a return to the themes of *Gödel, Escher,
Bach*, in this batch, and two of its contents entries ("Pattern and
provability", "Gödel's quintessential strange loop") take up that book's
Gödelian construction. No `extends:` is declared: it is the publisher, not
the book as I could see it, that says the later book builds on the earlier.

Where it bears on the record. These are my inferences from the
description and the contents titles, not claims the book was read to make:

- Hofstadter's lead essay in the world-models theme issue, "Is there an 'I'
  in AI?", is being filed at the same time by another reader. The issue's
  editorial ([LIT-195](LIT-195.md)), as [NOTE-136](../notes.d/NOTE-136.md) reads it, reports his claim that passing
  a consistent-understanding Turing test suffices to attribute thinking,
  and that conscious AI may come "perhaps even by 2033". This book's entry
  "Consciousness = thinking" carries the equation that claim needs, and a
  reading should check whether the essay's position is already this
  book's.
- That puts it on one side of the record's open question whether any
  evidence can settle machine consciousness ([THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)). If thinking is
  consciousness, behavioural evidence of thinking is evidence of
  consciousness, which is the step [THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md)'s readings say mimicry
  undercuts. The pairing is mine.
- The description's "symbols seem to have gained the paradoxical ability
  to push particles around", and the entries "The causal potency of
  patterns" and "On downward causality", put the book in the record's
  discussion of levels and emergence: Dennett's real patterns ([LIT-220](LIT-220.md)),
  and Rizi on what emergence is ([LIT-150](LIT-150.md)). Hence `metaphysics`.
- "How we live in each other" and the publisher's selves that "exist in the
  minds of others" are a claim that a self is not confined to one head.
  The record's nearest reading is Clark and Chalmers's extended mind
  ([LIT-097](LIT-097.md)), which infers in one paragraph that the self extends too. Hence
  `identity`, first: the book's question, by its title, its description
  and its catalogue headings, is what an "I" is.
