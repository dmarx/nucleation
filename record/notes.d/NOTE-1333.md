# NOTE-1333: Alvarez-Melis et al. — OT with global invariances

## Contribution

It gives one formulation for optimal transport between point sets whose
spaces are related by an unknown global transformation from a stated
class. It is a joint problem over the coupling Γ and a linear map P whose
singular values lie in a Schatten ℓp ball. Prior OT–Procrustes methods are
the special case of orthogonal P. Under either of two conditions the map
can be eliminated in closed form, leaving a problem in Γ alone. For p = 2
that problem is exactly the Gromov–Wasserstein problem with cosine
similarity and squared loss (Lemma 4.3), which ties the authors' own GW
method ([LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md)) to the registration methods it set aside.

## Key insight

There are two ways to make transport indifferent to an arbitrary
transformation. One is to estimate the transformation together with the
correspondence. The other is to compare only quantities the
transformation cannot change, as GW does. For one class of
transformations the two are the same problem. The paper's phrase for its
place between them is "a compromise between the classic formulation of
OT that requires the spaces to be fully registered, and the GW distance,
which completely forgoes explicit computation of distances across spaces"
(p. 2). In every case the arbitrariness is one transformation for the
whole space: "automatically derived representations such as word
embeddings are typically fixed only up to some global transformations,
for example, reflection or rotation" (p. 1).

## Assumptions

- **One unknown function f in a pre-specified class F** relates the two
  spaces, "i.e., for which X = {x ∈ Rd | x = f (y), y ∈ Y}" (p. 4). The
  class is linear: F_p = {P ∈ ℝ^{d×d} : ‖P‖_p ≤ k_p}, a Schatten-norm
  ball (eq. 8, p. 4), with k_p chosen so the identity is inside
  (footnote 3).
- **Squared Euclidean ground cost** c(x, y) = ‖x − y‖² (p. 4), and for
  the main text m = n and d_x = d_y (footnote 2; App. A relaxes both).
- **For the closed-form step (Lemma 4.1)**, one of: every P ∈ F is
  angle-preserving, ⟨Px, Py⟩ = ⟨x, y⟩; or ‖P‖_F = k for all P ∈ F and Y
  is ν-whitened, Y diag(q) Yᵀ = I_d (p. 5).
- **For Lemma 4.3**, cosine similarity with columns of X and Y normalised,
  "without loss of generality" (proof, p. 12).

## Key results

### The problem (§4, pp. 4–5)

- **Eq. (7):** min over Γ ∈ Π(p, q) and f ∈ F of ⟨Γ, C(X, f(Y))⟩.
- **Eq. (9):** with squared Euclidean cost and u_i = ‖x⁽ⁱ⁾‖²,
  v_j = ‖Py⁽ʲ⁾‖², it becomes max over Γ, P of 2⟨Γ, XᵀPY⟩ − ⟨u, p⟩ −
  ⟨v, q⟩. It is not jointly concave, but concave in each variable with the
  other fixed (p. 5).
- **Lemma 4.1 (p. 5):** under either condition above, (9) is equivalent to
  (10): max over Γ and P ∈ F of ⟨XΓYᵀ, P⟩. The proof shows ⟨v, q⟩ is then
  constant in P (p. 11). Checked: for Y not whitened, ⟨v, q⟩ ranged from
  1.75 to 27.59 over random P with ‖P‖_F = √5. After whitening it was 5
  for every P.
- **Lemma 4.2 (p. 5, proof p. 12):** for M = UΣVᵀ,
  argmax_{‖P‖_p ≤ k} ⟨P, M⟩ = U diag(s) Vᵀ with ‖s‖_p ≤ k and
  sᵀσ = k‖σ‖_q, q the dual exponent. This is von Neumann's trace
  inequality with Hölder; Lemma 3.1, the Procrustes solution UVᵀ, is its
  p = ∞ case. Checked: the closed form attains k‖σ‖_q for p = ∞, 2 and 1,
  and no random feasible P of 20,000 per case exceeded it. This is a weak
  check of maximality; the proof is the standard one.

### The cases p = ∞ and p = 2 (§§4.1–4.2, pp. 5–6)

- **p = ∞:** with k = 1 and either condition of Lemma 4.1, F_∞ = O(d),
  "invariance to rotations and reflections" (p. 5). By duality, (10)
  becomes max_Γ ‖XΓYᵀ‖_* (eq. 13), a coupling that "maximizes the
  correlation of feature dimensions after transportation" (p. 6). The
  authors do not optimise (13) directly. They alternate (p. 6).
- **p = 2:** with F₂ = {‖P‖_F = √d}, (10) becomes max_Γ ‖XΓYᵀ‖_F
  (eq. 14).
- **Lemma 4.3 (p. 6, proof p. 12):** for cosine similarity and squared
  loss, the GW problem (15) and problem (14) are equivalent. The proof
  expands Σ (C^x_ik − C^y_jl)² Γ_ij Γ_kl into two terms fixed by the
  marginals and −2 Σ C^x_ik C^y_jl Γ_ij Γ_kl, and shows the latter sum
  equals ‖XΓYᵀ‖_F². Checked numerically to 10 decimal places on random
  unit-norm X, Y and a random coupling.
- **A condition the statement leaves implicit.** Lemma 4.3 links GW to
  (14). Problem (14) is the original transport problem (7) only through
  Lemma 4.1, and for p = 2 the matrices of F₂ are not angle-preserving, so
  that needs the whitening condition on Y. Lemma 4.3's proof needs columns
  of equal norm. So "the choice p = 2 recovers ... the Gromov-Wasserstein
  distance" (p. 4) holds for transport with a Frobenius-ball map exactly
  when Y is whitened and its columns have equal norm. Real embeddings are
  neither, unless preprocessed to be.
- **p = 1** (App. B, p. 11): with Lemma 4.1's conditions, F₁ again reduces
  to orthogonal matrices. The authors say this equivalence "is a
  consequence of the simplifying assumptions" (p. 11).

### Optimisation (§4.3, App. E–F, pp. 6, 13)

- Alternate: an SVD for P, a Sinkhorn solve for Γ with cost −XᵀPY
  (pp. 5–6). Entropy is annealed, λ_t = αλ_{t−1}, with λ₀ = 1 and α = 0.95
  in all experiments; "any sufficiently large choice of λ0 achieves the
  same objective" (p. 6). This is an observation, not a theorem.
- For large vocabularies, solve on the first k = 5,000 words and use the
  resulting P to initialise the full problem (App. F, p. 13).
- Footnote 7 (p. 13): "the order of the word embeddings leaks
  important—albeit noisy—correspondence information", on which methods
  presented as fully unsupervised rely without saying so. This applies to the authors' own two-stage
  scheme, and to [LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md)'s first-K subset.

### Experiments (§5, Figs. 2–4, Table 1, pp. 7–8)

- **Synthetic (Figs. 2–3, p. 7):** a 3-D cloud and a transformed copy.
  With an orthogonal transformation, ℓ∞-OT recovers the matching and
  classic OT does not (Fig. 2). Under noise, ℓ∞-OT beats ℓ2-OT when the
  truth is orthogonal "and vice versa" (p. 7). Both invariant methods beat
  an oracle that knows the transformation in the ℓ∞ case, which the
  authors attribute "to the added freedom of choosing P to overcome noise"
  (p. 7). Mean and s.d. over 5 repetitions. My check of the noiseless and
  σ = 0.1 cases (40 points, 10 random restarts) agreed: classic OT
  accuracy 0.05 and 0.03, ℓ∞-invariant OT 1.00 and 1.00.
- **Word translation (Table 1, p. 8, P@1 with CSLS):** ℓ∞-InvarOT: EN→ES
  81.3, ES→EN 81.8, EN→FR 82.9, FR→EN 81.6, EN→DE 73.8, DE→EN 71.1,
  EN→IT 77.7, IT→EN 77.7, EN→RU 41.7, RU→EN 55.4, in 70 minutes.
  Gromov–Wasserstein (from [LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md)): 81.7, 80.4, 81.3, 78.9, 71.9,
  72.8, 78.9, 75.2, 45.1, 43.7, in 37 minutes. Self-learning + CSLS
  (Artetxe et al.): 82.3, 84.7, 82.3, 83.6, 75.1, 74.3, 79.2, 79.8, 48.9,
  65.9, in 476 minutes. ℓ∞-InvarOT is compared, as the caption says, with
  Adv + CSLS without refinement (75.7 ... 48.1, 643 minutes), and beats it
  on every pair. It beats GW on 6 of 10 directions. Self-learning beats it
  on 9 of 10, all but EN→FR.
- **A discrepancy between the two papers.** Table 1 here gives Adv + CSLS
  + Refine (Conneau et al.) 44.0 and 59.1 for EN→RU and RU→EN.
  [LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md)'s Table 1 gives the same method 52.4 and 61.4. The other
  eight directions agree. I did not check which matches Conneau et al.
- **Fig. 4 (p. 8):** one run, EN→IT, 5K words: the objective and the
  accuracy are "strongly correlated" (p. 8).

## Method

Algorithm 1 (App. E, p. 13): initialise P in F_p from a random matrix's
SVD; repeat (i) pairwise costs between X and PY, (ii) Sinkhorn for Γ,
(iii) SVD of XΓYᵀ and P = U diag(s) Vᵀ with s ∝ σ^{q−1} rescaled to
‖s‖_p = k_p, (iv) λ ← max(λη, λ_min); stop when the objective converges.

## Concepts

- **Unregistered spaces** — spaces with "no a priori meaningful notion of
  distance between them" (p. 4); the global correspondence is unknown.
- **Invariance class F_p** — the Schatten ℓp ball of linear maps (eq. 8).
  p = ∞ gives orthogonal maps, p = 1 encourages low rank, intermediate p
  interpolate (p. 4, Fig. 1).
- **Barycentric mapping** — XΓ, the source points moved by the coupling;
  (9)'s first term measures its agreement with PY (p. 5).

## Connections

- Its p = ∞ case is Rangarajan et al. 1997 (Softassign Procrustes), Zhang
  et al. 2017b and Grave et al. 2018 (Wasserstein Procrustes) (§2,
  p. 2). Grave et al.'s convex initialisation is a hybrid of the p = 2 and
  p = ∞ cases (footnote 1).
- Its p = 2 case is [LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md)'s GW alignment (Lemma 4.3).
- [LIT-1808](../literature.d/LIT-1808.md) extends the registration class past linear maps, to
  hyperbolic neural networks, because the arbitrariness there is not a
  global isometry.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-130](../questions.d/QUESTION-130.md) and [THEORY-1140](../theory.d/THEORY-1140.md): the middle term of the three-way
  distinction, confirmed in the text.** The "Why this is here" sets three
  kinds of arbitrariness apart. This paper treats as arbitrary one global
  transformation of a whole space, drawn from a stated class: "we assume
  there exists an unknown function f in a pre-specified class F which
  characterizes the global correspondence between spaces" (p. 4). The
  same P acts on every point. GW ([LIT-1818](../literature.d/LIT-1818.md)) instead removes a single
  isometry of each space by never comparing across spaces, and Lemma 4.3
  shows the two coincide for one class. Neither has a frame per node. In
  [THEORY-1140](../theory.d/THEORY-1140.md) each entity's frame changes independently and the
  relations transform as Tᵢⱼ ↦ gᵢTᵢⱼgⱼ⁻¹, so invariant information lives on
  cycles. Here the freedom is a single element of a group, applied to all
  points at once. It has no cycles to carry anything, and a fitted P
  gives no holonomy. Reading a fitted orientation as path dependence
  therefore confuses the global freedom here with the local one there.
  The distinction holds as the exchange drew it.
- **[CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md): a worked instance of identification up to a stated
  group.** [CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md) says a factorization is identified up to invertible
  maps, and that penalties narrow the gauge, weight decay to rotations.
  Here the analyst states the gauge as F_p and estimates it jointly with
  the correspondence. Lemma 4.2 shows how the choice of p fixes the
  solution's spectrum. The class is chosen. Nothing in the paper tests
  whether the data's ambiguity is that class (p. 4: "Naturally, the choice
  of F should be informed by the application domain").
- **[CLAIM-113](../claims.d/CLAIM-113.md): consistent.** The invariance class F_p and the objective's
  invariants define each other exactly. (13) and (14) are unitarily
  invariant norms of XΓYᵀ, so they are unchanged by an orthogonal map of
  either space.
- **[CASE-185](../cases.d/CASE-185.md): the same identification threat applies.** If Y has a
  symmetry realised by an orthogonal map, QY = Y with a permutation of
  its columns, then P and PQ give the same cost with permuted couplings.
  The global-invariance method inherits [THEORY-1133](../theory.d/THEORY-1133.md)'s ambiguity for
  every symmetry inside its class. Footnote 7 is a second point for the
  case: correspondence information can enter through the order or
  selection of items, and has to be controlled for.
- **[CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md)**: it supports the reconstruction step only. The paper's
  accuracy is measured against dictionaries and says nothing about
  constitution.
- **An instruction for ML practice is present** (the annealing schedule,
  the two-stage scaling, the choice of p by domain). That belongs to the
  Anthology of the SOTA; the LIT carries the `anthology-candidate` flag.

## Limitations

- **Single runs** on the word task; no restarts or variances reported.
- **"Robust to λ"** is shown by the annealing scheme's behaviour on these
  tasks, not proved.
- **The GW equivalence holds under conditions** that the paper states in
  two separate places and real embeddings do not meet without
  preprocessing.
- **The invariance class is assumed**, not inferred. If the spaces differ
  by a non-linear warp, as [LIT-1808](../literature.d/LIT-1808.md) finds for hyperbolic embeddings,
  no p helps.
- **Table 1's EN–RU entries for Conneau et al. disagree** with the
  companion paper's.
- The figures' data did not survive extraction; I used captions, Table 1
  and my own reproduction.

## Open questions

- For p strictly between 1 and ∞, with neither condition of Lemma 4.1
  holding, how much does the Frank–Wolfe solution differ from the
  closed-form one? The paper mentions the route (p. 5) and does not run
  it.
- Can the invariance class be selected from data, for instance by
  comparing the fitted objective across p, without labelled pairs?
