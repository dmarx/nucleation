summary: >-
  The SEP survey of the philosophy of death, revised in 2026. Luper gives a compositional account of life (an object is alive when its parts control what composes it, under information they carry) and defines death as the loss of the capacity for vital activities. He reconstructs Epicurus's argument that death is nothing to us. He shows that it fails if welfare is not only pleasure or if things can harm by precluding goods: the deprivationist defense. He surveys answers to when death harms (concurrentism, priorism, subsequentism, indefinitism, atemporalism), Lucretius's symmetry argument, and posthumous harm, where Pitcher's desire-based account locates the harm in the living person. He sides with Williams's critics: immortality need not be tedious.
