# LIT-tmp9iem2: Neurodiversity & evaluation: a defense (or not) of affective fictionalism

Víctor Fernández Castro, Miguel Núñez de Prado-Gordillo (2026), *Philosophical Psychology (online first, pp. 1–25)* — DOI-10.1080/09515089.2026.2638974

## Key takeaways

- Affective fictionalism — reading mental-state attributions as normative evaluations rather than factual reports — is argued to meet three demands the neurodiversity paradigm places on any theory of folk psychology.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The paper connects affective fictionalism about folk psychology with the neurodiversity movement, which treats conditions such as autism and ADHD as natural cognitive variation that can be disabling but is not an inner deficit. The authors hold that fictionalism of this kind, on which attributing mental states is an evaluative act rather than a statement of fact, handles three desiderata that neurodiversity imposes: it recognizes diverse cognitive styles, it allows that practices of attribution can change socially, and it helps reduce the contributory and epistemic injustices neurodivergent people face. Looking at disagreements and resistance within neurodiversity activism, they conclude that the view captures both the evaluative character of mental ascription and its role in shaping social dynamics. The "(or not)" in the title suggests the defence is qualified, which only the full text could confirm.
