summary: >-
  Dias & Ressler (2014). Male mice fear-conditioned to acetophenone or propanol before mating sired offspring (F1) and grand-offspring (F2) with heightened startle to, and lower detection thresholds for, the odour their F0 father was conditioned to, and not the other one. The offspring of acetophenone-trained males also had more M71 (Olfr151-expressing) olfactory neurons and larger M71 glomeruli; the anatomy, but not behaviour, was also tested and replicated in offspring conceived by IVF and carried by surrogates. Conditioning mothers before mating gave the same behavioural and anatomical effects, which survived cross-fostering. Olfr151 was less methylated in the sperm of conditioned F0 males (n = 12 vs 10) and of their naive F1 sons (n = 4 vs 4), but not in F1 or F2 olfactory epithelium. The authors read the effect as inherited sensitivity to a salient cue, not inherited fear, and leave open how an odour reaches sperm.
