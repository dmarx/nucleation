# LIT-2184: The Ticket to Easy Street? The Financial Consequences of Winning the Lottery

Hankins, S., Hoekstra, M. & Skiba, P. M. (2011). The Ticket to Easy Street? The Financial Consequences of Winning the Lottery. *Review of Economics and Statistics* 93(3): 961–969. doi:10.1162/rest_a_00114.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1673](../notes.d/NOTE-1673.md), of the 2009-02-16 working draft. Page
and table numbers are the draft's.*

- **Postponed, not prevented** (pp. 6–7, Table 4). "the bankruptcy rates
  of moderate and large winners fall 0.87 and 1.63 percentage points in
  the first two years"; "These declines are offset, however, by increases
  of 0.5 and 1.21 percentage points three to five years after winning".
  Over five years the large winners' estimate is −0.0042 (se 0.0078): no
  detectable effect, with a 95% interval of about −1.9 to +1.1 points on
  a five-year rate near 5.5%.
- **Enough to clear the debt** (p. 7). The pattern holds "despite the
  fact that the median large winner won a cash prize ($65,000) that was
  sufficient to pay off all of the unsecured debt owed by the most
  financially distressed lottery players ($52,000)". The prize is pre-tax,
  with 25% withheld above $5,000 (p. 2).
- **Not kept** (pp. 9–10, Table 6). Among filers within five years, 47
  who had won $25,000–150,000 and 103 who had won under $5,000, net
  assets and unsecured debt did not differ significantly: "whatever the
  recipients did with their cash, they did not use it to either pay down
  debt or increase their assets."
- **Mechanisms untested** (p. 1). Strategic consumption before a filing,
  high discount rates, mental accounting, "house money" risk-taking and
  low financial literacy are listed as reasons to doubt the cash would
  help; none is tested.

## Standing in the record

Filed as a case of a large, sudden, unconditional windfall to people near
a terminal financial event. A deep buffer bought about two years and no
more, which bears on [CLAIM-601](../claims.d/CLAIM-601.md) (a buffer is a stock of time) and on
[CLAIM-595](../claims.d/CLAIM-595.md)'s distinction between success by a deadline and eventual
success. It qualifies the threshold condition of [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md): a lump
sum larger than the debt did not, by itself, start anything that kept
going, because it was not kept as assets. Set against [THEORY-1465](../theory.d/THEORY-1465.md)
(Balboni et al.), where a one-off productive asset crossed a threshold,
it suggests that what is transferred, and not only how much, matters;
that reading is the assistant's, not the authors'. Filed from it:
[THEORY-1490](../theory.d/THEORY-1490.md).
