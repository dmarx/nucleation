# THEORY-1559: Training forms a task-defined, low-dimensional subregion of prefrontal and premotor population activity (a preparatory subspace, an axis of choice organized as a line attractor) that constrains a monkey's delayed response top down, holding behavior to what the task requires while the trial's cue and evidence select within it

## Source

Juarrero (2023), [LIT-1995](../literature.d/LIT-1995.md), ch. 15, pp. 223–232; read in [NOTE-1532](../notes.d/NOTE-1532.md). Her
reading is reported, Active, in [THEORY-1340](THEORY-1340.md). The primary papers are not
held.

## Where the source asserts it

- The subspace "structures, initializes, and controls the monkeys' delayed
  response on each trial run such that it satisfies the task instructions"
  (p. 223).
- The second study "proves the construction of an organized neural space
  that is contextually generated, task-defined, and multiply realizable"
  and "defines the conditions of satisfaction that will constrain actual
  behavior top down" (p. 229).
- The axis of choice provides "top-down governing constraints for the
  correct response to that trial run" (p. 230).

## What was actually shown

As the chapter reports them: a low-dimensional preparatory subregion whose
end state predicts reaction time and movement, with rotational dynamics
(pp. 225–227); in PFC an axis of choice organized as a line attractor that
persists until the response while the sensory axes are transient; and a
selection vector, from a simulation, that lets the same input move the
state along the attractor in one context and not the other (pp. 227–231).
Prediction, dimensionality reduction and a model fit; nothing reported
intervenes on the pattern.

## What this does not say

- **That the pattern causes behavior over and above its neurons.** The book
  holds governing constraints "not spatiotemporally other than" their
  components (p. 81).
- **What the primary papers say.** Read only through the chapter.
