# CASE-323: A proposed study: members of a group are asked to give to its present cause and to a fund for its members fifty years on, while the self's extent toward the group is varied by salience and measured apart from concern for the group's welfare, and a legacy prime is crossed with whether the gift is credited or known to no one

## The case

### The base: Chan's design, as reported

Spears and Postmes ([NOTE-1280](../notes.d/NOTE-1280.md), p. 39) report that in Chan (2010) church
groups gave more when a call for donations came by email than face to
face, "particularly when Christian identity was salient, and particularly
among low identifiers". The original is unread and its details, sample and
statistics are not in the record. [NOTE-1280](../notes.d/NOTE-1280.md) names what it lacks: "Chan's
design, with a measure of concern for the group's welfare added, would
answer [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)."

### Participants

Members of standing groups that already run collections: congregations,
as in Chan, or member associations. Gifts are real money from a
participation payment, so the outcome is a sacrifice and not an
intention.

### Measured first, in a separate session a week before

- **Concern for the group's welfare**, the caring that [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md) fixes as
  "what the agent is moved to promote": how much the participant wants the
  group and its members to do well, and how moved they are by its needs.
  Asked separately for the group now and for its members fifty years on.
- **The self's extent, by measures caring does not fix.**
  - *Self-definition*: self-stereotyping and in-group homogeneity, kept
    apart from self-investment (satisfaction, solidarity, centrality), as
    Leach et al. separate them ([THEORY-1583](../theory.d/THEORY-1583.md)).
  - *Cognitive inclusion*: slower self-judgements on traits where the
    group differs from oneself, and self–group confusions in memory, the
    signs Branand, Mashek and Aron give of inclusion that the circles do
    not ([THEORY-1625](../theory.d/THEORY-1625.md)).
  - *Continuity in time*: the future-self-continuity items, retargeted at
    the group's members fifty years on, as Simić et al. retargeted them at
    a nation ([THEORY-2212](../theory.d/THEORY-2212.md)), with the overlapping circles Hershfield
    uses for the future self ([THEORY-1609](../theory.d/THEORY-1609.md)).
- **A separability check.** The design is informative only if the
  criterion and concern are not one construct. Leach et al.'s two
  dimensions correlated .61 to .77, and Simić et al.'s continuity and
  attachment .14. Above about .8 the study cannot separate them, and it
  says so instead of reporting a test.

### Manipulated, at the call

1. **Salience of the self's level**, by instruction as in the SIDE
   experiments ([THEORY-1580](../theory.d/THEORY-1580.md)): the call addresses the participant as a
   member of the group, or as an individual with personal reasons to give.
   This fixes the self's level apart from what the participant values.
2. **Medium**, Chan's factor: the call comes by email or face to face.
   Kept so that the replication of Chan's pattern can be checked, and
   dropped if a smaller design is wanted.
3. **The temporal target.** Each participant divides one sum between the
   group's present cause and a sealed fund for its members fifty years on,
   whom the giver will not meet.
4. **Legacy prime × recognition**, the arm [QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md) asks for.
   - *Prime*: before the call, a legacy writing task, how one wishes to be
     remembered by the group, as in Zaval et al.'s task reused by
     Wickersham et al. ([THEORY-1599](../theory.d/THEORY-1599.md)); or a neutral writing task.
   - *Recognition*: the gift to the future fund is either recorded with
     the giver's name in a book the group keeps for its future members, or
     made through a sealed mechanism that tells the participant, credibly,
     that no one, the researchers and the group's officers included, will
     ever learn who gave what. Only the total is released.

### Outcomes

- Money given to each target.
- Measured after the call, and checked against the earlier session:
  concern, the extent measures, and the wish to be remembered, as a
  separate scale, not the Legacy Motivation Scale's total, which mixes
  being remembered with self-regard and responsibility ([NOTE-1316](../notes.d/NOTE-1316.md)).

### Analysis

- **The criterion test.** Regress each gift on the self's extent and on
  concern, together, with the salience manipulation. The thesis's
  prediction, in [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md)'s terms, is a coefficient on the extent measure
  and an effect of salience with concern held fixed. Chan's report
  that salience mattered most among low identifiers is the pattern
  expected if salience, not attachment, moves the gift.
- **The legacy test.** Compare the prime's effect on the future gift under
  recognition and under anonymity.
- **Planning figures**, the assistant's, for a two-sided test at α = .05
  and 80% power: a partial correlation of .15 needs about 350
  participants, and .10 about 780. A difference of 0.3 SD between the
  prime's effect under recognition and under anonymity needs about 350
  per cell, about 1,400 for that 2 × 2. Field groups this large would mean
  pooling many congregations, with group as a random effect.

## What it can show

| result | counts for |
|---|---|
| The gift follows salience and the extent measures with concern held fixed | [CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md) with the independent criterion [CLAIM-492](../claims.d/CLAIM-492.md) asks for; the lead's "What would count" |
| The gift follows concern alone; the extent measures add nothing | against the thesis on a non-trivial reading; for a yes to [QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) if concern also acts where the extent measures show no inclusion |
| The future gift follows continuity with the group's future members, with concern for them held fixed | the thesis's reading of legacy in [QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md), and the psychology [QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md) asks about |
| The legacy prime raises the future gift only when it is credited | the wish to be remembered: the thesis's reading or Pitcher's, which this design does not separate |
| The legacy prime raises the future gift also where no one will know | Scheffler's reading gains, as [QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md) states it |

## What it cannot show

- **Nothing yet.** It is stipulated and not run.
- **Not Pitcher's reading against the thesis's.** Both expect credit to
  matter; telling them apart needs a measure of posthumous interests,
  which this design does not have.
- **Not people with no tie to the giver.** The future members belong to a
  group the giver belongs to; future people in general are a further
  step, toward [QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md).
- **Not the anonymity of SIDE.** Two anonymities are in play. The medium
  is SIDE's "anonymity of" others in the group; the sealed gift is
  "anonymity to" every audience, the strategic dimension, which removes
  reputational reasons. The design keeps them apart on purpose.
- **Chan's result is at second hand.** The base effect is reported by its
  authors' colleagues without statistics; the design does not assume it
  replicates.

## Where it came from

[LEAD-062](../leads.d/LEAD-062.md). Its search (scratch/studies/lead-062/search.md) found
three neighbours and no test. Simić et al. ([LIT-3081](../literature.d/LIT-3081.md)) vary a group's
continuity in time and find its effect only among the attached; its
outcome is concern for present outsiders. Law et al. ([LIT-3083](../literature.d/LIT-3083.md),
[LIT-3082](../literature.d/LIT-3082.md), [LIT-3084](../literature.d/LIT-3084.md)) relate felt connection across time and
continuity with one's own future self to concern for future generations;
all are unread, and none is reported to separate caring.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Syropoulos, Law, Coleman and Young, read

**Draws a measure and a warning**, no edge ([LIT-3082](../literature.d/LIT-3082.md), [NOTE-2315](../notes.d/NOTE-2315.md)).
The paper's overlapping-circles item for "future generations of people", an
adaptation of the future-self item, is a ready measure of continuity toward
a group's future members, though alone it is a self-extent measure only in
form, and the paper's fuller scale adds empathy and perspective-taking. Its
manipulations, writing about one's future self and a framing that
generations "remain the same over time", did not raise identification; a
salience arm built on such tasks may not move the self's extent at all.
