# NOTE-1607: Rocks et al. — Allostery-inspired response tuned into spring networks by bond removal

## Contribution

The question is put plainly: "we ask whether disordered networks, which
generically do not exhibit this behavior, can be tuned to develop a
specific allosteric structural response by pruning bonds." The answer is
yes, easily, and the paper supplies the tool that makes it cheap: an exact
update for the effect of removing any one bond on the source–target
response, computed from a basis of the equilibrium matrix rather than by
re-solving the network for each candidate. The designs were then built.

## Key insight

A generic disordered network can be given a chosen long-range response by
very small structural changes, and many different sets of changes do it
(the response can be reached "in a number of ways by pruning different
bonds"). The bookkeeping that makes the search tractable is the split of
bond tensions into states of self-stress, which produce no node forces, and
states of compatible stress, which do.

## Assumptions

- **Linear response**, athermal, central-force springs with no bond
  bending and no prestress; all springs start unstretched.
- **Rigid to begin with.** Boundary zero modes are removed node by node
  until none remain (Materials and Methods).
- **Networks from jammed packings**: in 2D about 190 nodes and 400 bonds,
  ΔZ ≈ 0.19; in 3D about 240 nodes and 740 bonds, ΔZ ≈ 0.18.
- **Bookkeeping**: a zero-stiffness "ghost" bond between each source and
  target pair.
- **Greedy**, one bond at a time, the best of all candidates; no claim of
  optimality. Success is |η − η*| within 1%.

## Key results

- **Single source and target (Computational Results).** "In two dimensions
  only about 5 bonds out of about 400 were removed on average (∼1%);
  similarly, in three dimensions only about 4 bonds out of about 740 were
  removed on average (∼0.5%)." "The failure rate is less than 2% for strain
  ratios of up to |η| = 1 in two dimensions and less than 1% in three
  dimensions." It rises sharply for |η| ≫ 1.
- **Rigidity limits it.** "In the small ∆Z regime the failure rate is
  higher because very few bonds can be removed without compromising the
  rigidity of the system."
- **Specific, not global.** "the response between the designated target is
  large while the response between other pairs of nodes is essentially
  unaffected by the source strain".
- **Several responses (Fig. 3).** One source drives three targets at
  different ratios; two independent source–target pairs are tuned
  together in one network at ΔZ = 1.0. Failures are more common at lower ΔZ.
- **The stress basis (Theoretical Approach).** From the SVD of the
  equilibrium matrix Q, tensions with zero singular values "are commonly
  known as the states of self-stress (SSS)", and those with positive
  singular values, "We call these the states of compatible stress (SCS)."
  The removal effect of each bond follows in closed form (eq. 15; the
  authors note it also follows from the Sherman–Morrison formula).
- **Experiments (Experimental Results).** Laser-cut 2D and 3D-printed
  networks. On the distances between nodes of removed bonds, the Pearson
  correlation with simulation, over 4 realizations, is "C = 0.98 ± 0.02".
  Despite bond bending and finite strain, the responses survive "in a
  significant fraction of the realizations studied".
- **Degeneracy (Fig. 1).** "some of the removed bonds are the same for both
  η = +1 and η = −1."

## Concepts

- **Strain ratio η = ε_T/ε_S**: the target pair's strain over the source
  pair's, the designed response.
- **States of self-stress (SSS)**: bond tensions in the null space of Q,
  producing no net node force; equivalently incompatible extensions.
- **States of compatible stress (SCS)**: tensions with net node forces;
  extensions compatible with node displacements.
- **Excess coordination ΔZ = Z − Z_iso**, Z_iso being the minimum mean
  number of bonds per node for rigidity.
- **Allostery**, as the authors use it: a local perturbation at one site
  changing the conformation at a distant one.

## Connections

It builds on the jamming and pruning work of the Liu and Nagel groups
(tuning bulk and shear moduli by removing bonds, and rupture control
through rigidity), and on elastic-network models of proteins. Rocks and
Mehta (2024), [LIT-2112](../literature.d/LIT-2112.md), later restate Maxwell–Calladine counting with
energetics and prestress, the extension this paper lists as future work.

## Bearing on the record

- **The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures.** Its sentence that
  "selectively modifying a small number of bonds in disordered spring
  networks can produce specific long-range mechanical responses, including
  multiple designed responses" is A's paraphrase, not the paper's words,
  and a fair one; the paper's word is "removing", of about 1% of bonds.
  Its sentence that this is "designed adaptation ... not yet the same as a
  network autonomously maintaining or revising its own organization" is
  also A's, and the paper bears it out: an external algorithm with a full
  model chooses every bond, and the network does nothing. What A misses:
  the method runs on states of self-stress and compatible stress, A's own
  Maxwell–Calladine vocabulary doing design work; the response is linear
  and athermal; and rigidity is a precondition whose loss at low ΔZ is
  what makes tuning fail, a direct instance of A's trade between freedoms
  and constraints. Nothing A says is wrong.
- **[CLAIM-480](../claims.d/CLAIM-480.md)** (composing interactions turns an interior into a
  constraint). Supports, as a large-scale instance: η is an effective
  relation between boundary pairs produced by the equilibrated interior,
  and the paper retunes it by removing interior bonds. Same physics,
  linear springs; the paper neither cites nor derives the reduction.
- **[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)** (frustration as an ordinal fact). The rising failure of
  multi-response tuning at low ΔZ could someday illustrate joint
  unrealizability of declared responses; the paper does not frame it so.
  [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md), [CLAIM-345](../claims.d/CLAIM-345.md), [CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md) and [CLAIM-365](../claims.d/CLAIM-365.md), which the synthesis cites
  for its Part 2, are unrelated in substance.
- **[THEORY-302](../theory.d/THEORY-302.md)** (coordinated action by tuning coordinative structures).
  Analogy only: a few parameters are set and the network's mechanics
  realizes the response, but the paper says nothing about control
  hierarchies.
- Filed: [THEORY-1425](../theory.d/THEORY-1425.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- The authors': linear response only; athermal, though "proteins are
  thermal whereas our networks are athermal structures"; no
  "temperature, dynamics, pre-stress, bond bending, and nonlinear effects";
  "The limits of our algorithm are not yet known, including the number of
  targets that can be controlled and the number of independent
  responses"; and why the chosen bonds work is not understood.
- Failure rises for |η| ≫ 1 and at very small ΔZ.
- Four experimental realizations, with agreement measured only on the
  removed bonds' node distances.
- This is a reading of the preprint, not of the PNAS text.

## Open questions

- Why do particular bonds work? The authors ask it; a characterization of
  the removed bonds in terms of the stress basis would answer it.
- How many independent responses can one network hold before tuning
  fails, and how does that limit scale with ΔZ?
