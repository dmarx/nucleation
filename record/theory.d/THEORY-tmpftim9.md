# THEORY-tmpftim9: Wickersham et al. report that writing about one's legacy, or about a loved place under climate change, raised pro-environmental intentions and hypothetical donations by small amounts that did not differ by age, and that each task raised both legacy motivation and love of nature, which in a model with both mediators carried at least as much of the legacy task's donation effect

## The account

Framings that tie climate action to what outlasts the writer work, but not
by separate routes. The legacy task was expected to act through legacy
motivation and the place task through love of nature; each raised both, and
the authors grant both tasks may have moved "an underlying third element"
(p. 13).

## Evidence

- Intentions: induction F(2, 967) = 15.04, η² = .030; both tasks beat
  control, not each other; no interaction with age (p = .299).
- Donations: F(2, 979) = 4.30, p = .014; Place p = .006, Legacy p = .027
  against control; no interaction with age (p = .478).
- Legacy motivation rose under both tasks (Place p = .001, Legacy p < .001);
  biophilia rose under both (Place p = .002, Legacy p < .001).
- With both mediators (S3 File), the legacy task's donation effect ran
  through biophilia 0.138 and legacy motivation 0.097.

Self-report and hypothetical giving; the legacy prompt lists the behaviours
the intentions scale then asks about; several reported ranges and effects do
not agree between text and supplement ([NOTE-1316](../notes.d/NOTE-1316.md), Limitations).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md)'s note of 2026-10-29 reads this work: "The effect replicates",
"The scale does not isolate the wish to be remembered", "A parallel route"
through love of nature, and "The question's test is still untried. No
condition varies whether anyone will know what one did." The reading bears
on the question's evidence from the legacy experiments without running its
test.
