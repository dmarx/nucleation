# LIT-1904: The Lesser Existences: Étienne Souriau, an Aesthetics for the Virtual

Lapoujade, D. (2021). *The Lesser Existences: Étienne Souriau, an Aesthetics for the Virtual* (E. Beranek, Trans.). Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press. doi:10.5749/j.ctv1p3xjzq. (Original: *Les existences moindres*, Minuit, 2017.)

## Key takeaways

*Registered from metadata, not a reading.* As it is usually described, the book reads Souriau's
pluralism of modes of existence and his account of how a work, or any
being, is brought into fuller existence. Its title term names beings
whose existence is weak and needs to be taken up by others.

## Why this is here

Bencherki, Brummans & Vézy 2024 ([LIT-1902](LIT-1902.md)) borrow the term "lesser
existences" for their account of responsibility (p. 1366). They define
lesser existences as "those whose process of individuation has not yet
found its way into others and who are still at risk of disindividuation".
They say we have a duty to help them and should "exercise caution in the
way our individuation might affect 'weaker,' 'lesser' existences that
need our solicitude".

They say they "borrow a term". The definition they give is in their own
Simondonian vocabulary of individuation and disindividuation, not in
Souriau's or Lapoujade's. No page of the book is cited. So the parent
uses the book for a term and a moral tone, not for an argument. Whether
the borrowing is faithful cannot be judged without the book.
