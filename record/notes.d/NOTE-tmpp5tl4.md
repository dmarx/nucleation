# NOTE-tmpp5tl4: Chalmers — Extended Cognition and Extended Consciousness

## Contribution

Before this chapter, the extended-mind thesis had no official statement
from its authors. [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) "never quite state[s] a single official thesis"
(§1). After it there is one, and it is stronger than the reading most
critics attacked: external entities partly constitute mental states "in
virtue of the subject's sensorimotor interaction with these entities". The
chapter also turns the foreword's doubt ([LIT-tmpko8t6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpko8t6.md)) into a definite
position on consciousness: perception and action are not a boundary for
cognition, but they are one for consciousness.

## Key insight

What made Otto controversial was never the skin. It was that the notebook
reaches him only through seeing and writing. Make that the thesis, and the
controversy, the strongest objection and the limit on consciousness all
fall out of one feature: belief can tolerate a perceptual step between
store and control, and consciousness, on this account, cannot.

## Assumptions

- **The 1998 thesis, as reconstructed.** "A subject's cognitive processes
  and mental states can be partly constituted by entities that are external
  to the subject, when those entities play the right role in driving
  cognitive processes" (§1). "Partly constituted" is read as partial
  grounding, or as nomological dependence for a property dualist. The body
  is taken as the key boundary, to separate the thesis from embodied-mind
  theses.
- **Extended-circuit cases are accepted by both sides.** Clark's Diva, whose
  damaged division circuit is replaced by an external silicon circuit, is
  extended cognition by the 1998 statement. The chapter takes it that Adams
  and Aizawa (2008) and Rupert (2007, as the text has it; 2009 in the
  references) accept such cases. It does not quote them saying so.
- **Consciousness correlates with direct availability for global control.**
  This is imported from *The Conscious Mind* and "On the Search for a
  Neural Correlate of Consciousness" (§3), with Tye's "poised" states as a
  parallel. It is not argued here.
- **There are no plausible cases of extended consciousness.** The chapter
  takes this as independent evidence and offers directness to explain it.
- **Neutrality on the mind-body problem.** Extended physical states need
  only "nomologically ground (or support)" consciousness for consciousness
  to count as extended, so a dualist can pose the question (§3).

## Key results

- **The 1998 thesis is too weak (§1).** Following Farkas (2012), it is
  "near-obviously true in extended circuit cases" and is accepted by the
  thesis's main opponents. Sticking with it "would be a little akin to the
  US declaring victory in Vietnam and going home."
- **Rejected reformulations (§1).** Adams and Aizawa's modal/nonmodal split
  and the science-fiction/non-science-fiction split both tie the thesis to
  dates ("the 2017 version of the thesis is false but the 2117 version...
  will be true"). Block's quip is reported: false in 1995, true since
  smartphones. Farkas's standing-state thesis misses mental rotation, which
  is not a standing state.
- **The proposed thesis (§1).** First: constitution "in virtue of the
  subject's interacting with these entities via perception and action".
  Then, to finesse doubt that Otto really perceives or acts: "in virtue of
  the subject's sensorimotor interaction with these entities". Chalmers
  calls it "extensionally correct" for paradigm cases and invites Clark to
  accept it "as the retrospectively official statement". It counts Otto
  and Tetris in and Diva out. Brain-computer interfaces and intravenous
  stimulation go with Diva. Telepathy is a borderline case.
- **Five objections answered in one line each (§2).** (1) Different
  psychological profile: "implausible human chauvinism" (cited to Rupert
  2009). (2) Derived content: biological memory's content is derived from
  conscious states too (Adams and Aizawa 2008). (3) Coupling-constitution
  fallacy: "confuses the thesis with the argument for the thesis", which
  turns on parity (Adams and Aizawa 2008). (4) Not conscious: as mental as
  standing beliefs is enough (Gertler 2007). (5) Cognitive bloat: reduce it
  with endorsement-on-retrieval and past-endorsement criteria, accept the
  rest.
- **The perception-action objection (§2).** "By far the strongest
  objection": if the mental lies between perception and action, the
  thesis as now stated is false. It is "not a knockdown", and the reply is
  the foreword's: reject the boundary at "some cost of revisionism". The
  thesis "also overthrows the hegemony of perception and action."
- **The bandwidth explanation is withdrawn (§3).** Vold (2015) is "correct"
  that vision is very high-bandwidth, so low bandwidth cannot be why
  perception fails to carry consciousness.
- **Directness (§3).** Direct access is "not a matter of sheer speed or
  bandwidth" but "a relatively short and unmediated causal pathway", and
  "crucially, direct access requires access that is not mediated by other
  mental states". Standing belief needs two-step availability (to
  consciousness, then to control), which Otto's perception supplies.
  Consciousness needs one-step availability, which perception cannot
  supply. Hence "even if there is extended cognition, there is no extended
  consciousness."
- **Limits stated by the author.** Unconscious perception might escape the
  mediation argument. Then the pathway is still longer and parity fails,
  but a conclusive verdict "would require more detailed criteria for
  directness than I have given here". The author has "no necessary and
  sufficient conditions for directness".

## Concepts

- **extended circuit** — an external component wired or radio-linked into
  the brain so that it plays an internal circuit's role (Diva). Extension
  here is subpersonal.
- **extended circuit thesis** — that such a circuit can partly constitute a
  mental process. The chapter says most opponents accept it.
- **perception-action (or sensorimotor) extension** — constitution in virtue
  of the subject's seeing, reading, writing or pressing. This is the
  chapter's "strong and interesting" thesis.
- **boundary thesis** — that the mental is constituted by processes
  between perception and action.
- **direct availability** — access along a short causal path not mediated by
  other mental states. It is not the same as speed or bandwidth.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md).** The directness criterion is a published
  architectural criterion for consciousness that a reader could apply to
  both a society of mind and a nation. Because directness requires access
  "not mediated by other mental states", and a nation's internal
  information flow runs through its citizens' perceptions and actions, the
  criterion appears to exclude a nation and spare a mind built of mindless
  agents. That is my application, not the chapter's. It also shows that
  the criterion is close to an anti-nesting principle: what disqualifies
  the group is that its parts' mental states sit in the path. The
  criterion's justification is the weakness [THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md) names: it is
  offered to explain an independently held verdict. So it does not meet
  that account's `promote_when`. It is the most promising candidate this
  record now holds for the computation the account asks for.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) and [LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md).** Direct availability for global control is the
  global-availability idea that global-workspace indicators formalise. The
  chapter says nothing about AI systems.
- **[LIT-138](../literature.d/LIT-138.md).** A BCI falls with the extended circuits, outside the
  controversial thesis and possibly able to carry consciousness.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Both main theses are proposals.** The sensorimotor statement is
  defended for fit with paradigm cases, and the consciousness argument
  rests on an imported correlate and on the absence of plausible cases.
- **Directness is left undefined.** The author says so.
- **The opponents' concession is asserted.** The chapter says Adams, Aizawa
  and Rupert accept extended-circuit cases, but quotes none of them.
- **The objections of §2 get one sentence each.** In particular, the reply
  to the coupling-constitution charge assumes that parity, not coupling,
  carries the argument. [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md) found that parity is stated in [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md),
  not argued.
- **Short.** About 5,700 words.

## Open questions

- Does the directness criterion, applied to an explicit society-of-mind
  model and an explicit model of a nation, put them on different sides? If
  so, is "not mediated by other mental states" an anti-nesting principle?
- What becomes of extended belief through unconscious perception, which
  the author concedes his argument does not rule out?
- Does Clark accept the "retrospectively official statement"? Not checked
  here.
