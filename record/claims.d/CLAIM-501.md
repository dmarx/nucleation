# CLAIM-501: Gutt's interpretive resemblance also predicts that judged pragmatic observables beat text similarity, so the central theses' defeat conditions cannot tell the thesis from a relevance-theoretic account either

## The claim

[CLAIM-233](CLAIM-233.md)'s version 2 named Gutt's interpretive resemblance among the rival
accounts that predict a pass on the central defeat test ([CASE-184](../cases.d/CASE-184.md)). The record
knows Gutt only by his book's standard description ([LIT-784](../literature.d/LIT-784.md)): a translation is
to resemble the original interpretively, in the inferences it lets its
audience draw, and direct translation preserves the original's communicative
clues. On that description, judged stance and footing are nearer to what the
account preserves than embedding similarity is, so it predicts the pass.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

No reading of Gutt exists in the record. Translation and Relevance ([LIT-784](../literature.d/LIT-784.md))
is Unavailable. Until a reading is filed, what the account predicts rests on a
catalogue description.
