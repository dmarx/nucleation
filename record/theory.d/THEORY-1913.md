# THEORY-1913: DARVO may be a common abuser response

## Source

Freyd (1997), Feminism & Psychology 7(1): 22-32, pp. 29-30; read in [NOTE-2012](../notes.d/NOTE-2012.md) (draft).

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested. Freyd reports an observation and frames it as a hypothesis: "My proposal, currently very
speculative, is that a frequent reaction of an abuser to being held accountable is the 'DARVO' response" (p. 29). The
attack targets the accuser's credibility, and in the reversal "Figure and ground are completely reversed" (p. 30). She
guards the proposal: "Thus denial is not evidence of guilt" (p. 29).

## What this does not say

- That DARVO changes observers' judgments (that is Harsey and Freyd's later experimental work, [LIT-2710](../literature.d/LIT-2710.md)).
- That denial with counter-accusation identifies an abuser. Freyd's only proposed marker, "overly stated" denial, is
  untested.
- Anything about relationships in which both parties have acted wrongly.
