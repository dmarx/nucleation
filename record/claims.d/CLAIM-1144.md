# CLAIM-1144: Identity stability and adaptability are in tension

## The claim

The owner, U20: "the inherent tension between identity stabilization and
adaptability, and how appropriate response to demands is timescale
dependent". It follows A36's formalization of reorganization readiness
([TERM-167](../terms.d/TERM-167.md)).

## Readings (the assistant's)

"Inherent" can be read two ways. Read strongly, stabilizing identity
always costs adaptability. Read weakly, the two always bear on each other
and must be managed together. A38 rejects the strong reading ("The
tension becomes more interesting if we avoid treating identity and
adaptability as opposing objectives") and keeps the weak one. Its
answer is [CLAIM-1104](CLAIM-1104.md). The second clause, that appropriate response is
timescale-dependent, A38 makes formal in [CLAIM-1010](CLAIM-1010.md). A40 grounds it in
slow modes ([CLAIM-1066](CLAIM-1066.md)).

## Against the record

Identity over time as transmission of organization: [CLAIM-287](CLAIM-287.md),
[SYNTHESIS-046](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-046.md). Rate differences that make constraints: [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md).
Overconsolidation: [CLAIM-758](CLAIM-758.md). Anchors that entrench: [CLAIM-184](CLAIM-184.md).

## Note of 2026-11-23: what holds it back

Which reading of 'inherent' the owner means is unruled, and is already among
the owner's open choices; A38 section 5 tells against the strong reading,
and promoting the wording as it stands would assert both. The review leans
to the weak one: stabilization and adaptability always bear on each other.
