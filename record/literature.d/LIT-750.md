# LIT-750: "Exit, Voice, and Loyalty": Further Reflections and a Survey of Recent Contributions

Albert O. Hirschman (1974), *Social Science Information* 13(1):7–26;
reprinted (1980) in *The Milbank Memorial Fund Quarterly. Health and
Society* 58(3):430–453 — DOI-10.2307/3349733

## Key takeaways

- **The thesis restated** (p. 431). Voice "can and should complement and
  occasionally supersede exit as a recuperation mechanism" when firms,
  public services and other organizations deteriorate. Hirschman now finds
  his advocacy of voice "too timid".
- **Voice as benefit** (pp. 432–434). Voice can become "a highly desired
  end in itself". Public-interest problems, such as a health hazard rather
  than poor taste, call forth voice rather than exit.
- **Information** (pp. 437–438). Voice is "rich and detailed" compared with
  silent exit, and matters most under ignorance and uncertainty shared by
  consumers and producers. Exit can signal nothing when customers churn
  among equally bad suppliers.
- **Exit is not costless** (p. 439). Even without loyalty, switching costs
  can make voice more attractive; integration can institutionalize voice
  rather than suppress it.
- **Floors and ceilings** (p. 441). Beyond some levels exit
  (disintegration) and voice (disruption) end an organization; below some
  floor it deteriorates for lack of feedback.
- **Management's choice** (pp. 441–442). Managers may encourage the
  response "least dangerous to its perpetuation in power". A state, he
  proposes but does not press, can control only two of exit, voice and
  repression.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a lawful substitute for the
1970 book ([LIT-748](LIT-748.md)), which it extends: Hirschman revisits the book's
argument, corrects its assumption that exit is costless, and adds new cases
for voice. For the essay it bears on §6.3 and §6.4, how members respond
when an organization serves them worse, and on §7.2, acting through one's
own organization, which is what voice is. The account is [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md).
