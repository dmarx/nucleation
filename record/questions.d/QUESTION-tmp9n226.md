# QUESTION-tmp9n226: Does reducing safety behaviours lower symptoms, and instructed increases raise them, in diagnosed anxiety disorders, in pre-registered trials with active controls and masked rationale or a larger meta-analysis, once publication bias and expectancy are accounted for?

## Why it is a question

THEORY-903 holds Goodson et al.'s estimates, mostly from young
non-clinical or subclinical samples concerned with appearance or social
anxiety. Egger's test was significant for the reduction effect, the
increase studies could not be tested for bias, and every treatment trial
gave a rationale.

## What would answer it

THEORY-903's former promote_when, unchanged:

The version of record read and found to report the same estimates, and
either a later meta-analysis of standalone safety-behaviour
manipulations with more studies, or pre-registered trials in diagnosed
samples with active controls and masked rationale, finding a symptom
reduction from reducing safety behaviours that survives bias adjustment
and a symptom increase from instructed increases. It is weakened if
bias-adjusted estimates fall to zero as studies accumulate, or if the
reduction effect disappears when expectancy is controlled. Whether
safety behaviours maintain anxiety through belief or through some other
route is not carried by this THEORY.
