# CLAIM-463: Restoring the conditions under which a capacity was lost need not restore the capacity: where the activity that maintains it reinforces itself, the same conditions can sustain both the capacity and its absence, so a capacity lost past a threshold returns only under conditions better than those it was lost under, or by rebuilding the activity directly, and restoring it can cost more than preserving it would have

## The claim

A4 of the exchange of 2026-10-26, on the owner's case of federal science
and education ([CASE-172](../cases.d/CASE-172.md)): "**Resource restoration does not imply
organizational restoration.** When a disruption destroys relationships or
transmission mechanisms necessary for a capability, returning the original
resource conditions need not reinstate that capability." Its formal
ground, as A4 gave it: "[CASE-111](../cases.d/CASE-111.md) demonstrates a possible hysteretic
mechanism."

The demonstration is [CASE-111](../cases.d/CASE-111.md)'s. In ḃ = μb + κb² − b³ with κ > 0, b the
activity that maintains a boundary and μ the conditions that sustain it,
both b = 0 and the maintained state b₊ are stable for −κ²/4 < μ < 0.
Lower μ past −κ²/4 and maintenance collapses to 0. Return μ to any value
it had before, inside that range, and b stays at 0. Maintenance returns
only if μ passes 0, conditions better than any under which it was lost,
or if something outside the dynamics lifts b above the threshold
b₋(μ) = (κ − √(κ² + 4μ))/2. Each step is derived in the case and was
rechecked when it was filed.

Read as a claim, the construction says three things:

- **Conditions do not fix the outcome.** In the bistable range, two
  systems under the same conditions can differ because one kept its
  maintaining activity and the other lost it ([CASE-111](../cases.d/CASE-111.md)'s "Recovery can
  depend on history").
- **Return is not reversal.** Undoing the change in conditions is not the
  inverse of the change's effect. A3 put the same point for the
  Chagossians as R∘D ≠ id.
- **Restoration can cost more than preservation.** Keeping the capacity
  needed μ above −κ²/4; getting it back needs μ above 0 or a direct
  intervention on b. [CASE-111](../cases.d/CASE-111.md) states this consequence in its own words;
  this claim makes it citable.

What makes it a claim about a capacity and not only a state is that b is
an activity that reproduces itself (κb², repair reinforcing repair). That
is [CLAIM-099](CLAIM-099.md)'s distinction, between maintaining a regime of action and
maintaining the capacity to govern among regimes, given a mechanism for
the loss of the second; it is also why the claim complements
[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md), where what the challenged act changes is the arrangement
through which a system can be corrected.

## What it does not say

- **That any institution obeys the normal form.** [CASE-111](../cases.d/CASE-111.md): "This is a
  proposed minimal model, not an empirical fit," and nothing measures μ
  or κ. Whether a laboratory, a people or an institute has a
  self-reinforcing maintenance with a threshold is an empirical question
  for each.
- **That slow recovery shows a threshold.** A4: "The empirical challenge
  is to establish whether particular systems exhibit such path
  dependence, rather than merely labeling any slow recovery as
  hysteresis." [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md): an abrupt change can come from a stepwise
  driver or a simple threshold, and only hysteresis, dependence on the
  initial state, or a lasting shift after a temporary disturbance comes
  close to showing alternative stable states.
- **That resources are idle.** The resource is μ, and μ past 0 does
  restore the capacity. The claim says that returning it to where it was
  may not be enough.
- **What restoration is owed.** Whether a capacity should be rebuilt, and
  by whom, is a normative question; A4: "Lost reachable possibilities are
  not automatically harms."

## Where the record could test it

A4's empirical requirement: "Find documented instances in which resources
were reinstated but independently measured capabilities failed to recover
because the supporting organization had been lost." The candidates on
file, none yet measured: reinstated grants whose teams had dispersed
([CASE-172](../cases.d/CASE-172.md)), the return of USIP's leadership in May 2025 with about 25
staff, funds inaccessible and offices closed ([CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md)), and the
prospect of resettlement on the Chagos islands after half a century
([CASE-169](../cases.d/CASE-169.md)). In each the test is [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md)'s: a lasting shift after
the condition is restored, not a slow return.

## Note of 2026-11-04: Tatel, "A Meta-Analytic Investigation of Procedural Skill Retention and Decay", read

Tatel's dissertation ([LIT-2079](../literature.d/LIT-2079.md)) qualifies the claim's cost asymmetry for individual procedural skills. Its discussion of relearning (p. 124) treats savings as the rule: decayed skills come back faster than they were first learned, so a later retention trial "more accurately captures the essence of relearning rather than retention." In this domain, then, restoring may be cheaper than first acquisition. It leaves the claim's own mechanism, self-reinforcing maintenance activity, untouched. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the constraint-architecture readings

Jules et al. ([NOTE-1617](../notes.d/NOTE-1617.md)), from the pasted synthesis on constraint architectures, give a mechanical analogue. Once erasure returns a stack of Kresling barrels from a defect state to its main cycle, reversing the load does not bring the defect back; the defect states were reached only by twisting parts by hand, a richer control than the uniaxial loading the stack otherwise receives. That has the claim's shape: restoring the conditions does not restore what was lost, and rebuilding it directly does. It is an analogy only, on four barrels; the paper says nothing of self-reinforcing maintenance or of what restoration costs. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on reintroduction and ecological recovery

Read at the owner's suggestion to look to reintroduction and ecological recovery for what makes support safe or hazardous to remove; the works were chosen by the assistant. The assistant's synthesis is [CLAIM-600](CLAIM-600.md).

- **Supported in kind.** Kadowaki et al. ([NOTE-1677](../notes.d/NOTE-1677.md)): reintroducing fire alone does not restore a fire-suppressed wetland, cleaner lake water brought an invader rather than the native plants, and wolves did not bring back Yellowstone's willows once beavers had gone.
- **Qualified in reach.** Of 15 experimental tests in which a pressure was removed, Schröder, Persson and De Roos ([NOTE-1666](../notes.d/NOTE-1666.md)) count 3 that showed alternative states, 5 that recovered and 7 that were inconclusive. Restoring conditions often does restore a capacity.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-07: the legitimacy model's fold and floor, from "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"

A, at A36 §2 of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (2026-11-06 to 07), calls the failure of trust to return after a reversal "a subtle concept we haven't explored enough: **organizational hysteresis**". It is this claim. A's putative instances, both A's reports and unverified as hysteresis rather than other causes: SourceForge's end of bundling under BIZX in February 2016, and Redis's AGPLv3 option of May 2025 without Valkey's return. Both sit in [CASE-223](../cases.d/CASE-223.md).

The reader reran A's schematic model (A36 §6), Ṁ = aLU − dM, L̇ = bM(1−L) − rL − s. On the M-nullcline M = aLU/d it reduces to L̇ = kL(1−L) − rL − s with k = abU/d. The interior equilibria are L = [(k−r) ± √((k−r)² − 4ks)]/(2k), with a fold at s_c = (k−r)²/(4k); with a = U = d = 1, b = 2, r = 0.5, k = 2 and s_c ≈ 0.281, and L* ≈ 0.715 at s = 0.05. Simulated with L floored at 0, ramping s past s_c collapses L and M to 0, and returning s to 0.2, 0.05 or 0.01 leaves them there: at L = 0, L̇ = −s < 0, so the floor is absorbing for every s > 0. Without the floor L goes negative and the integration diverges, so the model as posed needs L ∈ [0, 1] imposed, which A does not say. A's "Depending on model details, nonlinear versions can exhibit multiple attractors, thresholds, or hysteresis" understates it: the model as written is already thresholded and hysteretic. It is this claim's mechanism, and [CASE-111](../cases.d/CASE-111.md)'s fold, in other variables. No status changes.
