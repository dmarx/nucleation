summary: >-
  Damavandi, Hagh, Santangelo & Manning (2022; arXiv 2021). The paper
  separates energetic rigidity, in which every nontrivial global motion
  raises the energy, from structural rigidity, in which no such motion
  preserves the constraints; for Hookean energies they coincide when the
  system is unstressed and can differ under prestress. It classifies when
  the cheap tests imply energetic rigidity: with no states of self-stress,
  first-order rigidity (constraint counting) suffices; with them, the
  spectrum of the prestress matrix decides, second-order rigidity
  sufficing when it is zero or positive semidefinite, and no general
  result holding when it has negative eigenvalues. It argues that
  energetic rigidity is the more useful notion in practice, and that
  dynamics and external tension drive systems to the non-generic
  configurations, with self-stresses, where counting fails.

# LIT-tmp925so: Energetic rigidity. I. A unifying theory of mechanical stability

Damavandi, O. K., Hagh, V. F., Santangelo, C. D. & Manning, M. L. (2022). Energetic rigidity. I. A unifying theory of mechanical stability. *Physical Review E* 105(2): 025003. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.105.025003. arXiv:2102.11310 (v1 2021-02-22).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp8m06t](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8m06t.md). Sections are the arXiv v2's.*

- **Counting needs no self-stress.** "We find that constraint counting
  robustly predicts energetic rigidity only when the system has no states
  of self stress" (abstract).
- **Neither test always suffices.** "there may be systems for which
  neither first nor second-order rigidity imply energetic rigidity"
  (abstract); for a prestress matrix with negative eigenvalues the
  authors "have been unable to derive analytic results" (Sec. II).
- **Where self-stress comes from.** States of self stress "arise from
  redundant constraints" (Introduction); their existence "only depends on
  the geometry of the system and does not imply that the system has to be
  prestressed" (Sec. I C).
- **Dynamics select the bad cases.** Under external tension a system "will
  naturally evolve to a nongeneric configuration having at least one self
  stress" (Sec. I C).
- **Not the shear modulus.** "defining rigidity based on G is not
  equivalent to energetic rigidity" (Sec. I A).

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures cites it, rightly, as
arguing for energy-based rigidity beside the geometric criteria; the paper
puts it more strongly, as "a more useful notion of rigidity in practice".
It is the work in this batch that actually states that states of
self-stress come from redundant constraints, the point the synthesis
attributes to Rocks and Mehta ([LIT-tmpx12go](LIT-tmpx12go.md)). Filed from it:
