# LIT-146: The Moral Status of Animals

Lori Gruen, Susana Monsó (2003), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/>

## Key takeaways

- No candidate capacity yet proposed (species membership, personhood, rationality) picks out all and only humans as morally considerable, so the live questions are which interests animals have and how their claims weigh, not whether they count.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The entry asks whether anything distinctive about humans justifies granting them moral status while denying it to other animals. It reviews the main proposed grounds for moral considerability — species membership, human-exceptional capacities, personhood, sentience and agency — and reports a growing philosophical consensus that human–animal differences do not justify denying animals moral consideration. It then turns to what interests animals have and how their claims are to be counted against competing ones, and closes with approaches (care, relational, empathy-based) that reject the standard argumentative framing altogether.
