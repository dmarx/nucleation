# THEORY-tmprbqew: Frontal anodal transcranial direct current stimulation raises reported mind-wandering without changing rated meta-awareness of where attention is focused, so the stimulation effect on mind-wandering does not pass through a change in that awareness

## Source

Axelrod, Zhu and Qiu (2018), LIT-tmpg3vlm, read in NOTE-tmp36b7e: "our
results indicate ... that at least in some cases
mind-wandering might not be regulated by meta-awareness" (Abstract), and
"the increase in the level of mind-wandering as result of stimulation was
unrelated to the meta-awareness level" (Discussion).

## What was actually shown

Between subjects (30 prefrontal, 28 sham, 27 occipital), 30 minutes of 1 mA
anodal tDCS at F3 during a one-hour SART raised task-unrelated-thought
ratings (F(2,82) = 9.77, p < 0.001; d about 1 against each control). The
second probe, "To what extent have you been aware of where your attention
was focused?", did not differ (F(2,82) = 1.11, p = 0.33; BF 0.25), and the
stimulation did not change the relation between the two ratings
(interaction BF 0.132). Either result could have failed: meta-awareness
could have fallen with stimulation, as Fox and Christoff's letter
(LIT-tmp4vtkl) suggested it might change.

## What this does not say

- **That meta-awareness plays no part in mind-wandering.** Within every
  condition low meta-awareness went with more mind-wandering; the finding
  is only that the stimulation did not act through it.
- **Anything about the agent's model of its capacity to stop a thought.**
  The probe asks where attention was, after the fact; Metzinger's
  epistemic agent model is the conscious model of oneself as able to
  control one's thinking. The two may come apart.
- **That the effect is established.** A pre-registered three-lab
  replication with the same montage found none (THEORY-tmpseev1).
