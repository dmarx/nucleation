# CLAIM-1295: Strong unitary executive theory is deficient where it infers, from the executive's responsibility for executing the laws, an entitlement to govern every process that contributes to, evaluates or constrains that execution: authority over the conditions of correction can leave every corrective function formally in place while their failures become correlated, so the capacity to coordinate correction is not the capacity to remain independently correctable

## The claim

A38's table names the assumption: "Effective unity of executive action
requires hierarchical unity of governing authority." A40 §3: "It is whether
an authority's responsibility for a function establishes entitlement to
govern every process that contributes to, evaluates or constrains that
function." A38 §2, in bold: "The capacity to coordinate corrective activity
is not equivalent to the capacity to remain independently correctable."

## Its argument

- **Seven powers** (A38 §2): direct execution, establish priorities,
  evaluate performance, appoint or remove evaluators, determine admissible
  evidence, suspend investigation, modify oversight arrangements. "These are
  different powers. Their concentration may make the organization more
  efficient under ordinary conditions while increasing its vulnerability
  under circumstances in which its governing authority itself becomes the
  object of correction." A38 calls this "the distinction between first-order
  efficacy and higher-order normative governability" ([TERM-128](../terms.d/TERM-128.md)).
- **Common-mode failure** (A38 §2). If execution controls the appointment,
  resources, criteria and continuation of the monitoring and corrective
  processes, "The organization may retain all three functions formally while
  their effective governance becomes correlated." The model is in
  [CLAIM-1220](CLAIM-1220.md).
- **Reflexive control** (A40 §3.4). If the authority also controls a
  corrective process's resources, evidence, criteria, personnel, occasions
  and continued existence, "The authority relationship becomes reflexive
  because it governs the conditions of its own governability." "These powers
  need not be exercised improperly to have organizational consequences."
  A40 cites [CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md), [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) and [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md); the cites are apt.

## A's own description and qualifications

A38 §2 describes the theory accurately: "Strong versions of unitary
executive theory maintain that the President's constitutional authority
entails extensive control over executive officials, especially their
supervision and removal. Weaker versions permit greater congressional
discretion"; and "The theory is not necessarily a claim that presidential
power overrides Congress, the courts or the law. Its central organizational
concern is the structure of authority within the executive." It concedes
that "Hierarchy can substantially reduce arbitration costs, accelerate
responses and make responsibility more legible", that independence can
become unanswerability (A40 §3.5), and that "The legal question of which
presidential powers the Constitution actually grants remains separate".

## The objection

[CLAIM-1313](CLAIM-1313.md). The table's statement of the assumption mislocates the
theory's warrant, and the correctives that A40 §3.4 worries about are, for
the most part, outside the executive the theory unifies. The critique holds
for correctives inside the executive or dependent on its resources;
[LEAD-280](../leads.d/LEAD-280.md) would test it there.
