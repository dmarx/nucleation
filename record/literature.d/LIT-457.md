# LIT-457: Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies: A Review

Bruce Burns (1995), "Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies: A Review", *AI
Magazine* 16(3), Fall 1995, pp. 81–83 — DOI-10.1609/aimag.v16i3.1155

## Key takeaways

- **What the book is.** A collection of articles, "many already published",
  by Hofstadter and members of the Fluid Analogies Research Group
  (Chalmers, Defays, French, McGraw, Mitchell), held together by
  Hofstadter's prefaces. For "a quick tour", chapters 2 (Jumbo), 4
  (high-level perception) and 5 (Copycat).
- **The computational idea: the parallel terraced scan.** Codelets are
  chosen probabilistically from a coderack; some spot possible structures,
  others build or destroy them, and each run changes the odds for the
  rest. As coherent structure forms, "the temperature is lowered", so it
  becomes harder to tear down: "a coherent structure emerges without the
  need for any top-level control".
- **The psychological idea: cognition is high-level perception**
  ("cognition is recognition"). Representation and processing interact,
  so leaving representation to a later module misses the point; the
  example is BACON, which is handed the data Kepler had to find.
- **Why little impact on AI, criticism 1: microdomains.** Every program
  works on letter strings, table settings, word puzzles or letter fonts.
  Burns grants that real-world programs also restrict their domains, but
  microdomains "can contain hidden assumptions", and it is unclear how far
  the programs succeed by leaving representation to low-level perception.
- **Criticism 2: argument by analogy and intuition.** The symbol-grounding
  objection is pressed against SME but "quickly dismissed" for Copycat's
  SIGMA symbol; Burns turns the same argument back. The evaluation preface
  says AI research is "90 percent" intuition; Burns replies that the other
  10 percent should not be ignored, and that this stance "risks
  irrelevance".
- **Verdict.** Recommended, "because it is eloquently written and
  provocative", and because Burns thinks the basic ideas "might be on the
  right track".

**How it bears on [LIT-428](LIT-428.md).** [LIT-428](LIT-428.md), Hofstadter and FARG's book, is
`Deferred` because no lawful full text could be read, and it already quotes
this review as "the one account of the content I read". This entry is that
review in its own right. The reading checked [LIT-428](LIT-428.md)'s account of the review
against the text, and they agree: the bibliographic details (Basic Books,
New York, 1995, 518 pp., ISBN 0-465-05154-5), the two underlying ideas, the
codelet-and-temperature description and the quotation "without the need for
any top-level control", "cognition is recognition", the two criticisms and
the recommendation. [LIT-428](LIT-428.md) notes that Burns calls the evaluation preface
"Preface 5" where the catalogue's contents note puts it before chapter 9.
The review adds a check on that: it calls the Eliza-effect preface "Preface
4", which agrees with the catalogue if the prefaces are numbered by the
chapter they precede (that preface precedes chapter 4). On that numbering
the evaluation preface would be Preface 9, so the discrepancy is in the
review's "Preface 5", or the book numbers its prefaces another way. The book
is still needed to settle it.

The review is quoted here as the reviewer's account, not as the book's. It
does not make [LIT-428](LIT-428.md) read.

**Tags.** `cognition` for analogy and high-level perception, `complex-systems`
for the emergent, controller-free architecture it describes (as on [LIT-428](LIT-428.md)),
and `philosophy-of-science` for its argument about how AI research should be
evaluated and what intuition can carry.

**Boundary.** It carries no instruction for machine-learning practice. It
compares the terraced scan with neural networks and genetic algorithms, and
suggests learning codelets with genetic algorithms, as a possibility. Its
point about evaluation (the Eliza effect, separating a program's success for
interesting and uninteresting reasons) is about evaluating AI models in
general; an anthology reader on evaluation might want it, as [LIT-428](LIT-428.md) says of
the book's evaluation preface. That is a judgement for the anthology.
