# QUESTION-037: When an argument is developed across sessions with collaborators who keep no memory, what makes it settle into a stable structure, and what can a project memory contribute?

## Why it is a question

The metaphor in the title is that a solution can be supersaturated and
still not crystallize, because crystals need a site to form on. Read that
way, a long exchange with a chat assistant holds more argument than it ever
fixes. Its ideas keep coming back, each time a little redefined, and none
of them settles. Asking for "the crystallized argument" reads out the
terminal state the argument has reached ([TERM-052](../terms.d/TERM-052.md)). The next readout
had a different thesis, and nothing in the exchange said whether the
earlier one had matured into it or been dropped.

This record has been where that happened, twice
([ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md), workbench 2026/10/07 and 2026/10/09–10). Luria was built
against what it calls the rediscovery tax in software projects
([LU-ADR-001](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-001.md)), and was then used unchanged for an argument. The open part is
whether what it supplies is what makes an argument settle, or only
something that helps a reader afterwards. An answer has to separate three
things: the drift, the sites that stop it, and the shape the sites impose.

The candidate answer is [CLAIM-177](../claims.d/CLAIM-177.md), [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md) and [CLAIM-178](../claims.d/CLAIM-178.md) taken
together, with [CLAIM-176](../claims.d/CLAIM-176.md) as the record's own trigger of persistence. The objection to watch is [CLAIM-181](../claims.d/CLAIM-181.md).

## Note of 2026-10-14: IBIS on settling

IBIS's founding paper ([LIT-758](../literature.d/LIT-758.md), read in [NOTE-811](../notes.d/NOTE-811.md)) answers "what makes
it settle" procedurally only: an issue is settled "by convincing the
opponents or decided by a formal decision procedure", or dodged, or
substituted. It offers no account of an argument's structure stabilising
over time, which is what this question asks.
