# NOTE-tmphdvuc: Lohmann et al. — The Trauma and Mental Health Impacts of Coercive Control

## Contribution

The first meta-analysis of coercive control and mental health to keep
coercive control apart from psychological intimate partner violence in
general (PROSPERO CRD42021252071). Its main finding is a null on the
predicted difference: coercive control's associations with PTSD and
depression are no stronger than those of other forms of IPV.

## Key results

- **Scale** (Abstract): "The search identified 68 studies while data from
  45 studies could be included in the meta-analyses."
- **Effect sizes** (Abstract): "moderate associations involving coercive
  control and PTSD (r = .32; 95% confidence interval [.28, .37]) and
  depression (r = .27; [.22, .31])."
- **Comparison** (Abstract): "These associations were comparable to those
  involving psychological IPV and PTSD (r = .34; [.25, .42]) and depression
  (r = .33; [.26, .40])." The discussion sets them beside physical IPV in
  earlier meta-analyses (PTSD r = .34, depression r = .25).
- **Against prediction** (Discussion): "It was unexpected that
  associations of coercive control with PTSD and depression would not be
  clearly stronger than associations involving other types of IPV".
- **The authors' reading** (Discussion): "construct overlap remains likely
  and could explain similar effects"; "many measures may not fully capture
  whether respondents experience a threat that is embedded in a chronic
  pattern of power and control (Johnson, 2008)."
- **Setting** (Subgroup analyses): "a stronger pooled association observed
  in studies of domestic violence support services/shelters settings
  (r = .40; 95% CI [.35, .45]), when compared to community settings
  (r = .26; [.16, .35])."
- **Acts depend on their context** (Background): "verbal threats may or
  may not reflect coercive control, depending on whether or not these occur
  in the context of a broader pattern of controlling, isolating, and
  degrading behaviors."
- **Complex PTSD** (Abstract): "Only one study reported on the relationship
  between coercive control and complex PTSD and meta-analyses could not be
  performed."
- **No causation** (Limitations): "a direct causal link between coercive
  control and mental health could not be established".

## Assumptions

- Subscales such as the PMWI's dominance–isolation subscale measure
  coercive control; studies using only the CTS/CTS2, SVAWS or Danger
  Assessment were excluded as not distinguishing it.
- A standardized β was taken as r where r was not reported.
- Subgroup differences were judged by non-overlapping 95% intervals.

## Concepts

- **Coercive control**, after Stark (2007) and Johnson's intimate
  terrorism, as against situational couple violence.
- **Psychological IPV**, of which coercive control is "both a distinct
  construct and a dimension".
- **Complex PTSD** (ICD-11).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-tmpkivlz](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpkivlz.md)**: neither corroborates nor bounds it, and the record
  should not cite it for more. A cited it as evidence that coercive
  control "has trauma and mental-health effects of its own". The review
  finds associations, not effects, and finds them no larger than those of
  psychological abuse generally. What it does support is the claim's
  premise that an act can mean different things in different patterns
  (the verbal-threats sentence), and the authors explain their own null by
  the limits of act-based measures. That is an explanation they offer, not
  a finding.
- **[CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)**: its "continuing practical freedom" is not what these
  instruments measure.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional data, so no causal inference. Mostly self-report; 76% of
studies recruited only women, 68% were from the United States, and only
English-language studies were included. Heterogeneity is high (I² from
about 72% to 88%). For coercive control and PTSD, Table 2 gives
I² = 71.77% where the text gives "Q(20) = 97.62, I2 = 79.51%". Only 4.44%
of the meta-analysed studies used diagnostic instruments.
