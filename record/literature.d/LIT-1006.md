# LIT-1006: Living without Free Will

Pereboom, D. (2001). *Living without Free Will*. Cambridge University Press. DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511498824.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-823](../notes.d/NOTE-823.md): the Introduction and Chapter 1 pp. 1–10
only.*

- **Hard incompatibilism, not hard determinism.** Pereboom does not claim
  determinism is true. Determinism and quantum-style indeterminacy are
  both incompatible with the freedom basic desert needs. Agent-causal
  libertarianism "may well" be metaphysically possible, but "we have no
  evidence that we are morally responsible".
- **Origination is the core.** Frankfurt-style cases defeat
  alternative-possibilities conditions, but leave standing condition (3):
  a free action is not "produced by a deterministic process that traces
  back to causal factors beyond the agent's control".
- **The manipulation challenge is the best one.** "Causal determination
  presents in principle no less of a threat to moral responsibility than
  does covert manipulation". That argument is Chapter 4, not read.
- **What survives.** Responsiveness to reasons, acting on one's values,
  freedom from coercion, morality, meaning and non-retributive responses
  to crime.
