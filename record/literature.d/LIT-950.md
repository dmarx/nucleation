# LIT-950: All or None: Identifiable Linear Properties of Next-token Predictors in Language Modeling

Emanuele Marconato, Sébastien Lachapelle, Sebastian Weichwald, Luigi Gresele (2024), *AISTATS 2025; arXiv:2410.23501*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-746](../notes.d/NOTE-746.md).*

- **Identifiability (Theorem 5).** For softmax predictors
  p(y | x) ∝ exp(f(x)ᵀ g(y)) of any dimensions, p_{f,g} = p_{f̃,g̃} ⇔
  (f, g) ∼EL (f̃, g̃). This extended linear equivalence is an invertible
  linear map between the projections onto M := Im(P_F P_G) and
  N := ker(P_F P_G)^⊥. Components outside M and N can be distorted
  arbitrarily, even non-linearly (Appendix B.5).
- **Effective complexity.** dim(M) is fixed by the distribution.
  Roeder et al.'s linear identifiability is the special case of equal
  dimensions plus diversity (Corollary 6).
- **Parallelism is a property of the law.** Difference vectors are parallel
  in N exactly when their token pairs have proportional log-odds across all
  contexts (Lemma 8). Only parallelism inside N is shared by equivalent
  models (Theorem 15).
- **All or none (Theorem 14).** Relational linearity of a query q on Γ, and
  the relational linear probing and linear subspaces it implies, holds in
  every equivalent model or in none when Γ ⊆ N and Γ_q ⊆ M. Without the
  second condition an equivalent model can break it (Appendix D.1).
- **What it does not explain (§6).** The parallelism that PCA shows in
  trained models is not shared across the equivalence class. The authors
  conclude that training or architecture selects among equivalent models.
- **Scope.** The results hold for exact equality of distributions over all
  sequences and a shared vocabulary. There are no experiments.

## Standing in the record

Held for [QUESTION-027](../questions.d/QUESTION-027.md), which asks for a signature identified from the
probes when the full latent model is not. The paper proves such a case for
next-token predictors. The linear properties that live in M and N are
constant on each class of distribution-equivalent models, while the
representations themselves are not identified. But its probes are the whole
conditional distribution, so it is an instance of the unrestricted form that
[QUESTION-027](../questions.d/QUESTION-027.md) records as already answered. It is a case of gauge-invariant
functionals ([CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md)) and supports [CLAIM-209](../claims.d/CLAIM-209.md)'s unrestricted sentence
only. It also refines [CLAIM-148](../claims.d/CLAIM-148.md)'s gauge: without diversity, equivalent
models are related linearly only on M and N. Its §6 warns that agreement
between trained models need not reflect identified structure. That bears
on reading cross-model agreement such as [THEORY-175](../theory.d/THEORY-175.md)'s and [LIT-936](LIT-936.md)'s.
Its results are filed as [THEORY-234](../theory.d/THEORY-234.md), which extends [THEORY-018](../theory.d/THEORY-018.md).

Picozzi ([LIT-936](LIT-936.md)) cites it. The follow-up on approximate equivalence by
Nielsen, Marconato, Dittadi and Gresele is [LIT-253](LIT-253.md), held unread. The paper
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice. An anthology topic on
representation learning could hold it, so it keeps the
`anthology-candidate` flag, and it is held here for its own question under
[ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md).
