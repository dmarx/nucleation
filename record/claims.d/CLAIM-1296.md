# CLAIM-1296: Making a process legible to another lowers that other's cost of discriminating among the process's circumstances and intervening on it, whatever the purpose of the intervention, so an investment in observability cheapens coercion as well as cooperation, its savings can go to different parties, and an authority unchanged in law grows more consequential when monitoring cheapens its exercise

## The claim

A42 §2: "It is the coupling of observation, interpretation, intervention and
discretionary authority that creates the potential for domination." It
proposes a **legibility-mediated control surface**: "the set of
distinctions about a process that another process can use to make
consequential interventions upon it", and "The quantity of information is
not the decisive variable. A relatively small amount of strategically
consequential information can confer substantial control."

A42 §3 puts it in the cost model. κ_{A→B}(g; M, W, H) is "the minimum
expenditure required for A to realize goal g concerning B within horizon H,
given monitoring apparatus M and authority structure W", and "Improved
legibility may reduce this cost." The goal g enters only as a goal, so the
saving does not know whether g is assistance or control: "The important
implication is that **investments in observability can simultaneously
amortize the cost of cooperation and the cost of coercion**. And these
savings may accrue to different parties." Its table pairs each investment
with both faces: identity records with reliable attribution and targeted
discrimination, behavioural histories with personalized assistance and
manipulative influence, performance measurement with accountability and
disciplinary pressure. "Legibility is thus a form of stored work whose
value depends upon the authority and objectives of those able to invoke
it", which is [CLAIM-761](CLAIM-761.md)'s stored work, and [CLAIM-761](CLAIM-761.md)'s warning that "the
persistence that buys it can entrench".

A42 states the monotonicity with its limit: "Under fixed dynamics, goals,
costs and authority, additional freely available information cannot
increase the optimal intervention cost if the observer is permitted to
ignore it. But actual monitoring systems impose costs and change behavior,
so this monotonicity does not generally hold for their full lifecycle." The
first sentence is the textbook result that free information has
non-negative value to a single decision-maker; the second is
[CLAIM-1268](CLAIM-1268.md)'s mechanism.

## Consequences A42 draws

- **Drift of the control surface (§11).** "An unchanged formal authority
  may become far more consequential when new monitoring technology reduces
  its intervention costs. The legal specification has not necessarily
  changed, but the control surface has." Carpenter v. United States
  ([LIT-3199](../literature.d/LIT-3199.md), Deferred) is its example of legibility produced by ordinary
  dependence on infrastructure.
- **Asymmetric epistemic enablement (§12).** An informational intervention
  can give ΔF_A > 0 and ΔV_B < 0, the governing process's inquiry frontier
  growing while the dependent's viable reach shrinks; "Surveillance can
  supply a mechanism for maintaining" the asymmetry of [CLAIM-736](CLAIM-736.md)'s contained
  opposition.
- **The domination conditions (§4).** Legibility can intensify all three of
  [CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)'s conditions; that is noted on [CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md).

## What is held and what is added

[CLAIM-678](CLAIM-678.md) holds epistemic power as a dimension separable from operational
and economic power; [TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md) holds direction and "not free to the observed";
[THEORY-857](../theory.d/THEORY-857.md) holds one remedy whose access runs both ways. What this adds is
the purpose-blindness of the saving and its consequence for an unchanged
authority. Who pays for the legibility, which A42 leaves open ("may accrue
to different parties"), is [CLAIM-1117](CLAIM-1117.md)'s and [TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)'s.
