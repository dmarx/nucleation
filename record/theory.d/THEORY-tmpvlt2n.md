# THEORY-tmpvlt2n: Winner holds that artifacts can embody forms of power and authority in two ways, by a design or arrangement that settles an issue in a community and as inherently political technologies that require, or are strongly compatible with, particular political relations, and that the latitude of choice is greatest when a technology is first introduced and vanishes for practical purposes once initial commitments are made

## The account

Winner: Moses's overpasses "embody a systematic social inequality"; the harvester shows "the technological deck has been stacked long in advance to favor certain social interests" without anyone's intent; some systems "actually requires" particular social conditions, others are "strongly compatible with, but does not strictly require" them; "By far the greatest latitude of choice exists the very first time a particular instrument, system, or technique is introduced", and "the original flexibility vanishes for all practical purposes once the initial commitments are made." Innovations are therefore "similar to legislative acts or political foundings".

## Beside Feenberg

Feenberg's account ([THEORY-tmpa59p3](THEORY-tmpa59p3.md)), that design embodies a contingent technical code reflecting dominant values, is Winner's first kind made general: a code is an arrangement that settles issues. The two do not rival. Winner adds what Feenberg's reading here does not: that the openness Feenberg relies on narrows with commitment.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpskck7](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpskck7.md), results 1–6.

## What it does not say

That every technology is inherently political; Winner treats the strong thesis as one version among two and accepts neither wholesale. Nor a rule for how much justification an irreversible choice needs.
