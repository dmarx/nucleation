# LIT-tmp9zqs4: That's my hand! Activity in premotor cortex reflects feeling of ownership of a limb

Ehrsson, H. H., Spence, C. & Passingham, R. E. (2004). *Science* 305(5685):875–877. doi:10.1126/science.1097011. PMID 15232072.

Not read: no lawful full text was reached (see the status note). What
follows is drawn from the PubMed abstract. A reading should check it first.

## Key takeaways

*From the abstract only.*

- **Design, as the abstract gives it.** "A perceptual illusion was used to
  manipulate feelings of ownership of a rubber hand presented in front of
  healthy subjects while brain activity was measured by functional
  magnetic resonance imaging."
- **Result.** "The neural activity in the premotor cortex reflected the
  feeling of ownership of the hand."
- **Interpretation.** "multisensory integration in the premotor cortex
  provides a mechanism for bodily self-attribution."

## Why this is here

Filed in the body-ownership batch as the first neuroimaging study of the
rubber-hand illusion. It is the neural counterpart to the behavioural
classics (Botvinick and Cohen 1998; Tsakiris and Haggard 2005). The abstract
says nothing about how "reflected" was established: whether activity
differed between synchronous and asynchronous stroking, tracked rated
ownership across people or followed the illusion's onset in time. It does
not say how ownership was measured or what the control conditions were.
So the record holds only the claim, not its support.

Apps and Tsakiris ([LIT-tmpetw17](LIT-tmpetw17.md), [NOTE-tmp69t3e](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp69t3e.md)) cite this paper among the
studies in which "the TPJ, IPS, AI and the IFG" are active during the
illusion. Their "core circuit" for recognising oneself does not include
premotor cortex, which is the region this paper's title and abstract name.
From the abstract alone, the record cannot say whether the paper also
reports those regions.

On [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)'s C6, the abstract's "a fundamental aspect of
self-consciousness" is the same move as Metzinger's "the most simple form of
self-awareness". Both are asserted. Neither is shown by a manipulation of
ownership. Carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.
