# THEORY-535: Quantum systems read as bundles of property-instances with no principle of individuality can have their kinds and their number stated in quasi-set theory, so that N indistinguishable particles get a definite quasi-cardinal without labels; the construction covers kinds and counting only, not states, actualization or dynamics

## Source

Federico Holik, Juan Pablo Jorge, Décio Krause and Olimpia Lombardi (2021),
[LIT-107](../literature.d/LIT-107.md), read in full in [NOTE-127](../notes.d/NOTE-127.md) from the authors' preprint "Quasi-set
theory for a quantum ontology of properties" (PhilSci-Archive 19725, 26 pp.).
The *Synthese* version (200(5), art. 401, 2022) was not read and may differ.

## What was actually shown

**The ontology (§§2–3).** General magnitudes are universal U-type-properties.
Observables are their instances, I-type-properties. Eigenvalues are possible
case-properties, and the single realized value is the actual one. A system is
a bundle of I-type-properties, that is, of determinables, with no principle of
individuality. The instances are universals' instances, "only numerically
different", and tropes are rejected because they would reintroduce
distinguishability (p. 5).

**Why standard logic does not suffice (§4).** "An ontological domain populated
exclusively by properties and non-individual bundles … cannot be adequately
apprehended by any language that includes individual constants and
variables" (p. 11). [NOTE-127](../notes.d/NOTE-127.md) rates this a weak-to-moderate philosophical
argument.

**The construction (§6, pp. 19–22).** For each projector-property [Π] there is
a qset U([Π]) of indiscernible instances. R(x) says that x takes exactly one
instance (qc = 1) from each U([M]), U([W]), U([S]) and each U([Π]). The
bundle's kind is Q(B) := {x ∈ P(Q) | R(x)}, and Q's Axiom of Choice gives
Q(B) ≠ ∅. Any two members of Q(B) are indiscernible. N indistinguishable
particles are a subqset N(B) ⊂ Q(B) with qc(N(B)) = N: a definite number with
no labels. Kinds are labelled by (m, w, s) from irreducible representations of
the Galilean group.

**What Q supplies (§5).** Indiscernibility is an equivalence relation but not a
congruence, and Q does not prove substitutivity of indiscernibles: Q ⊬ a ≡ b →
∀z(a ∈ z ↔ b ∈ z) (p. 18). Swapping indiscernibles yields an indiscernible
qset (Theorem 7, p. 19). Both are known results about Q (French & Krause
2006).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The bundles could have needed a
labelling to be counted, or Choice could have had to identify which instance
it picked. The paper's answer is that Choice picks one element from each U
"no matter which one" and that all members of Q(B) are indiscernible (p. 22).

## What this does not say

- **That the ontology is right.** The paper supplies a meta-language for it.
  It presupposes that the algebraic formalism's order of priority, observables
  before states, is a guide to ontology.
- **That the ontology is formalized.** States, the actualization of
  case-properties and dynamics are central to it (propensities, actual
  case-properties) and are not represented in Q. Only kinds and counting are.
- **That the ontology escapes Kochen–Specker or explains entanglement.** The
  "immunity" holds because a system is defined as a bundle of determinables,
  which contextuality never touches. The "mystery vanishes" for EPR because the
  correlations are relocated to properties of one item, not explained.
- **That the Indistinguishability Principle is derived.** It follows only if
  observables are restricted to symmetric ones, which is itself a postulate
  (Messiah & Greenberg). It is moved, not removed. Which symmetry sector is
  then selected is not discussed.
- **That the construction is checked.** Def. 6, Theorem 7's proof and the
  notation on p. 18 contain errors, and no independent consistency check of
  the bundle construction is given. R(x) also quantifies "∀Π" over the
  collection of projector-properties as if it were classical.
- **That it reaches quantum field theory.** The treatment is nonrelativistic,
  and particle number is fixed.

## Connections

- **Leitgeb & Ladyman ([LIT-154](../literature.d/LIT-154.md)).** [NOTE-127](../notes.d/NOTE-127.md) calls the two papers opposite
  answers to one puzzle. Leitgeb and Ladyman keep standard identity and accept
  primitive, non-discernible nodes. Holik et al. drop identity for the items
  and use indiscernibility instead.
- **Moderate structural realism ([LIT-895](../literature.d/LIT-895.md), [THEORY-453](THEORY-453.md)).** Esfeld and Lam
  keep classical identity talk and give entangled systems of one kind a
  primitive numerical diversity. This paper gives them a quasi-cardinal and
  no identity. Both count without identifying.
- **Structural realism.** French (2020) notes the view is close to ontic
  structural realism. The record's structural-realism accounts are [THEORY-032](THEORY-032.md),
  [THEORY-034](THEORY-034.md) and [THEORY-038](THEORY-038.md).
- Wallace ([LIT-151](../literature.d/LIT-151.md)) takes up the same complaint, that non-individual
  ontologies cannot be stated in standard logic, by a math-first route.
