# ADR-041: A LIT whose lawful full text was sought and not reached is Unavailable, not Deferred

## Context

`Deferred` was blurbed "in the corpus, not yet read closely enough to
place". Two situations shared the word:

- **Nobody has tried.** These are works seeded from a feed, an abstract or a
  bibliography, which wait in the queue. On 2026-10-13, 68 of them came
  from the owner's papers-feed alone.
- **Someone tried, and no lawful copy was reached.** The status note says
  where the reader looked: OpenAlex, Unpaywall, the publisher, author pages
  and repositories. It also says why each failed. Many of these notes end
  "It stays Deferred until a copy is supplied."

The two call for different actions. The first is reading work any session
can pick up. The second is blocked until the owner supplies a copy, through
library access, a request to the authors or a browser that passes a bot
check. Until now a reader could only tell them apart by opening each note.
The owner asked for the difference to be a status: "differentiate 'looked for
a legal copy but couldn't find one' from 'we haven't even attempted reading
this yet'".

Luria allows this. A scheme's status vocabulary is the project's own: the
QUESTION scheme already uses Open, Answered and Dissolved ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)). Any
status other than the scheme's `active` one counts as not in force. So a new
word is treated exactly as `Deferred` is in the inactive-citation report.

## Decision

- **A new LIT status, `Unavailable`, labelled "No copy reached".** It
  means a lawful full text was looked for and none could be obtained. That
  covers two cases: none was found, or one was found that this client could
  not fetch. The status note says where and when the reader looked. Like
  `Deferred`, it is not a judgement on merit. A supplied copy returns the
  work to reading, and the reading sets its status.
- **`Deferred` narrows** to a work with no failed search recorded: nobody
  has tried yet, or reading stopped at the abstract or a skim. Its label
  stays "Unreviewed".
- **Both cases of `Unavailable` take the one word.** A copy that exists but
  could not be fetched is a different blocker from a copy that does not
  exist. In both, though, the owner is what unblocks it. The note says
  which case it is (for example [LIT-171](../literature.d/LIT-171.md)'s "failed on access, not
  availability").
- **Where the line falls.** A note counts as recording a search if it names
  at least one place a lawful copy was looked for, even only the publisher.
  Three notes record no search, and those stay `Deferred`:
  - [LIT-746](../literature.d/LIT-746.md) and [LIT-753](../literature.d/LIT-753.md): "no copy was sought, the book being in copyright";
  - [LIT-715](../literature.d/LIT-715.md): "no lawful open copy known", with no place named.

## Migration

All 345 `Deferred` LITs were classified by script from their status notes,
on 2026-10-13, and the borderline ones were read by eye. 177 moved:

- **169 record that no lawful copy was found.** [LIT-475](../literature.d/LIT-475.md) is one.
- **8 record a lawful copy this client could not fetch.** These are
  [LIT-137](../literature.d/LIT-137.md), [LIT-171](../literature.d/LIT-171.md), [LIT-180](../literature.d/LIT-180.md), [LIT-181](../literature.d/LIT-181.md), [LIT-186](../literature.d/LIT-186.md), [LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md), [LIT-536](../literature.d/LIT-536.md) and [LIT-636](../literature.d/LIT-636.md).
  They are the quickest for the owner to clear by hand.

Each merged entry gets a version bump. Its history note gives the reason,
and says that prose written before the change may still say `Deferred`,
which then covered both cases. Two kinds of present-tense phrase were
changed in place: "stays Deferred" and "`Deferred` because no …" (or "…
it could not …"). Prose that gives non-reading as the reason ("`Deferred`
because it was not read") was left as written. The four entries filed
today and not yet merged were changed in place without a bump.

`inactive-ok` annotations elsewhere that cite a moved work still apply,
because `Unavailable` is not in force either. Where their reasons say
"Deferred", they were true when written and are left alone.

## Alternatives

- **Keep `Deferred` and rely on the status note.** That is the state the
  owner asked to leave. The difference can only be found by reading each
  note.
- **A tag instead of a status.** The LIT tag vocabulary is topics plus one
  curation flag ([ADR-005](ADR-005.md)). Copy availability is not a topic, and a flag
  would sit beside a status that contradicts it.
- **Two new words, one for "none exists" and one for "exists, not
  fetched".** That is more precise. But the owner's request named one
  distinction, both cases are cleared the same way, and eight entries do
  not justify a sixth status.
- **Name it `Unobtainable`.** That overstates the case: a copy that exists
  behind a bot check can be obtained by hand.

## Consequences

- **The LIT scheme differs from the anthology's.** [ADR-001](ADR-001.md) copied the
  anthology's LIT, NOTE and THEORY schemes so that a work could move between
  the records as a file. An `Unavailable` LIT moved to the anthology would
  fail its status vocabulary until it is set back to `Deferred` or the
  anthology adopts the word. The anthology may want the same split, since it
  has the same two populations.
- **The pending-decisions report no longer counts `Unavailable` LITs.**
  Luria counts `Proposed` and `Deferred` as undecided. An `Unavailable`
  work is blocked rather than undecided, so dropping out of that queue is
  the intended effect.
- **The rule for new filings.** When a reader looks for a lawful copy and
  fails, the LIT becomes `Unavailable` and the note says where the reader
  looked. A LIT filed without a search stays `Deferred`. The LIT template
  says so.

## Note of 2026-10-25: amended by [ADR-053](ADR-053.md)

[ADR-053](ADR-053.md), accepted by the owner on 2026-10-25, revisits two parts of this
decision: the bullet "Both cases of `Unavailable` take the one word", and
the alternative that rejected two words because "eight entries do not
justify a sixth status". A triage of the `Unavailable` notes found 43 in the
second case, and the two cases are not cleared alike: a located free copy
takes the owner a browser and a minute, a paywalled one a library. A LIT
whose lawful free copy was located at an exact address, and which this
client could not fetch, is now `Unfetched`. Everything else here stands,
including the line between `Deferred` and `Unavailable`. Of the eight
entries named under Migration, seven moved to `Unfetched`. [LIT-137](../literature.d/LIT-137.md) did not,
since only one chapter of that volume has a free copy.
