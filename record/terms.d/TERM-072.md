# TERM-072: perspective, and which sense: organizational, represented or phenomenal

## Definition

The owner's U11 of the exchange of 2026-10-21/22: "under our current
characterization what is a perspective? an interpretation?" A11 answered
with a distinction it said the exchange had needed several times: "We start
by defining perspective organizationally, demonstrate that an organization
possesses one, and then unconsciously begin speaking as though we've
demonstrated the presence of experience. We haven't."

"Perspective" in this record means one of the following, and a claim should
say which:

1. **Organizational.** A11: "A dynamically realized regime of interpretive
   relationships in which the availability, significance, and efficacy of
   local interpretations are mutually conditioned through an identifiable
   organization of context, evaluation, and possible revision." It is the
   organization of interpretation ([TERM-073](TERM-073.md)) one level up: "an
   organization of interpretation that can itself be transformed by
   interpretation". It differs from a single interpretation "of
   organizational level and explanatory role", not of substance. It can be
   studied through dynamics and interventions without assuming
   consciousness.
2. **Represented.** A system's model of its own interpretive standpoint
   or orientation: "I am interpreting events through a threat-oriented
   frame." It can misdescribe the organization it models. It is not the
   same as a **represented owner**, an attribution of the standpoint to a
   subject: "There is a single indivisible subject to whom these
   experiences belong." Both can be mistaken, but in different ways. The
   first can misdescribe how interpretation is organized. The second can
   posit an owner the organization does not contain. [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)'s
   constructed owner is a represented owner, not a represented
   perspective, and [CASE-114](../cases.d/CASE-114.md) varies one while holding the organization
   fixed. So the record's selective fictionalism concerns owners. It does
   not make represented perspectives fictions, and a system can represent
   its perspective accurately while misrepresenting its owner, or the
   reverse.
3. **Phenomenal.** "A lived point of view: what it is like for experience
   to occur as an organized field of significance."

**Self-maintaining** organizational perspective is a subclass of sense 1,
not a fourth sense. In it the organization also takes part in producing or
maintaining the constraints that govern its activity, which is closure in
[TERM-070](TERM-070.md)'s sense. A10 proposed it as the criterion ("self-maintaining
interpretive regime"). A11 withdrew that, since it would import "a disputed
form of enactivism into the account without argument". A person on life
support, a passive experience, or an episode sustained by mechanisms laid
down earlier should not fail by definition. [CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md) shows that
maintenance is a separate, interventional fact.

Interpretations "belong to a common organizational perspective insofar as
their significance is mutually answerable within an identifiable regime of
contextual selection, evaluation, and possible revision" (A11). A8 called
this cross-context answerability. Answering does not mean agreeing: "What
unifies the system is not the absence of disagreement. It is the
organization within which disagreement has consequences" (A8). Which
processes belong to which perspective is a separate question. A11 kept
integration apart from individuation, and "several overlapping, nested, or
partially integrated regimes may exist".

## Why the senses must be kept apart

[CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) says that experience is constituted by interpretive
organization. Stated with this term, the thesis is that certain
organizational perspectives (sense 1) are phenomenal ones (sense 3). That
identity is the claim's bridge, and it cannot be built into the definition
of sense 1. A11: "The identity can't be built into the operational
definition." A thermostat or a firm may have a perspective in sense 1. That
alone settles nothing about sense 3. A system with sense 2 only is the case
[QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md)'s second contrast is about.

A11 named the gap the term leaves open. Sense 1 is defined by "the
conditions governing interpretation". Sense 3 is "the occurrence of
something being present or manifest from a point of view". "We haven't yet
shown that governing interpretation and having something manifest are the
same phenomenon."

## What it is not

- Not a self. A perspective is situated, but "That doesn't mean the system
  must represent an entity to whom everything matters." The senses of
  "self" are [TERM-045](TERM-045.md)'s. A represented perspective (sense 2) need not
  represent a self.
- Not a frame. [TERM-026](TERM-026.md)'s frame is one operation on an interpretive state.
  A11: "A frame specifies how some distinctions are made within a
  particular context." A perspective governs "how frames are selected, how
  interpretations acquire authority, how disagreements become
  consequential, and how this whole organization persists or changes".
- Not a will-organization. [TERM-023](TERM-023.md) governs the efficacy of
  action-generating regimes. A9 found its machinery "surprisingly close",
  but declined to identify the two, which "would exclude possible
  experiences lacking sophisticated agency or governance".
- Not a global section. A perspective in sense 1 can hold conflicting
  interpretations and demands, and a conflict need not be a gluing
  failure. Whether it can hold interpretations that genuinely fail to glue
  is [QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md).

## What would settle it

Adoption is the owner's decision. It would replace the unqualified
"perspective" in [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md), [QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md) and [QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md)
with a numbered sense. It would be shown wanting if a claim needed a
fourth sense, or if sense 1 could not be told from coordinated feedback in
general. A8 called that the decisive test: "If we cannot do that, SIC risks
being a new vocabulary for complex feedback."

## Note of 2026-10-22: the readings

- **Sense 1 without sense 2.** In Gurnee et al. ([NOTE-1069](../notes.d/NOTE-1069.md)) a base
  language model already has the access workspace, and post-training
  installs the assistant's point of view. That separates an
  organizational perspective from a represented one in one system. It
  says nothing about sense 3.
- **Sense 3 without a represented owner.** Meditators report losing the
  sense of self, and of subject and object, while remaining aware
  ([NOTE-1070](../notes.d/NOTE-1070.md), [NOTE-1058](../notes.d/NOTE-1058.md)). That is a reported separation of a
  represented owner from a phenomenal perspective, and it rests on report.
- **Bridges from sense 2 to sense 3.** Higher-order theories
  ([NOTE-1066](../notes.d/NOTE-1066.md)) make representing one's own state what makes it
  experiential. On Graziano's illusionism ([THEORY-873](../theory.d/THEORY-873.md)) experience is
  the represented perspective of a real organization. Both are rivals to
  the bridge [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) proposes from sense 1.
- **Other uses.** Lycan's "perspective" ([NOTE-1068](../notes.d/NOTE-1068.md)) is a mode of
  presentation, nearest sense 2. Laukkonen et al. ([NOTE-1063](../notes.d/NOTE-1063.md)) say a
  first-person perspective is not required, meaning sense 2. Barrett et
  al.'s "conditioned on some perspective" ([NOTE-1067](../notes.d/NOTE-1067.md)) is nearest sense
  1 but does not say. On Ward's enactivism ([NOTE-1065](../notes.d/NOTE-1065.md)), leaving
  self-maintenance out of the definition puts this term on the
  informationalist side.
