summary: >-
  Jung, Rubinstein, Uselis, Yun and Oh (2026), MEME: Multi-entity & Evolving
  Memory Evaluation. MEME organizes agent-memory evaluation on two axes,
  entity scope (single vs. multi-entity) and temporal dynamics (static vs.
  evolving), with six tasks: Exact Recall, Aggregation, Tracking, Deletion,
  and two dependency-reasoning tasks no earlier benchmark scores: Cascade (a
  dependent fact changes by a stated rule after an upstream update) and
  Absence (after an upstream change with no replacement rule, the right
  answer is "Uncertain"). Episodes (~35K tokens) are generated from
  hand-built DAGs of entities, dependency edges and conditional rules in two
  domains, so gold answers are computed by propagation. Six memory systems
  across three paradigms (raw retrieval; LLM-processed memory: Mem0,
  Graphiti; file-based agents: Karpathy Wiki, MD-flat) all fail Cascade (avg
  0.03) and Absence (avg 0.01), although they encode and retain the rule and
  the change event; the failure is at retrieval or answering. Prompt
  optimization, deeper retrieval, less filler and a stronger answering model
  do not close it; only a file-based memory whose internal LLM (Claude Opus
  4.7) writes the propagated value at ingest partly closes it, at about 70×
  the cost. Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
