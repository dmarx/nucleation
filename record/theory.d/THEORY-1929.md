# THEORY-1929: Relationship factors best predict physical IPV

## Source

[LIT-2717](../literature.d/LIT-2717.md), read in [NOTE-2000](../notes.d/NOTE-2000.md) (Europe PMC full text, PMC13603925).

## What was actually shown

Risk markers from eight meta-analyses were mapped onto the dyadic model's levels. "Across the reviewed meta-analyses, the most consistent risk markers of IPV clustered within the relationship-context level, particularly prior partner aggression, emotional abuse, and relationship dissatisfaction." Communication skills and empathy were inversely associated. The authors' stated position: "This relational framing does not imply equivalent agency, mutuality, or shared responsibility; responsibility for a violent or coercive act lies with the person who commits it." This position corroborates [CLAIM-709](../claims.d/CLAIM-709.md) as an independent statement of the same separation. It is the authors' framing, not an empirical result.

## What this does not say

- That relationships cause violence, or that escalation was shown: "the present synthesis does not constitute empirical validation of the model’s integrated temporal or causal pathways".
- That the larger relationship-context associations mean greater causal importance (§7 disclaims this). Some of these markers are other manifestations of the same aggression.
- Anything beyond physical IPV in adult heterosexual relationships.

## From the second reading (2026-11-17)

The second reading adds the authors' bounding of their term, bidirectional
violence "does not imply equivalence in motive, coercive control, severity,
consequences, or responsibility" (§2.3), and two limits:

- **Reproducibility.** A selective, unregistered review with one coder,
  whose search logs "cannot now be verified".
- **Who controls whom in a given couple**, or how often violence by both
  partners coincides with one partner's control.
