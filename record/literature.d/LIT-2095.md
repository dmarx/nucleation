# LIT-2095: Real-Time Ambulance Dispatching and Relocation

Nasrollahzadeh, A. A., Khademi, A. & Mayorga, M. E. (2018). Real-Time Ambulance Dispatching and Relocation. *Manufacturing & Service Operations Management* 20(3): 467–480. doi:10.1287/msom.2017.0649.

## Key takeaways

*From the publisher's abstract only; unverified until read.*

- **The decisions.** Which available ambulance to send, or whether to
  queue the call; and whether to send an idle ambulance to cover the
  location one just left.
- **The method.** A stochastic dynamic program with an unbounded state
  space, approximated by ADP, judged against lower bounds on expected
  response time and on the expected fraction of late calls.
- **The result claimed.** In Mecklenburg County, 12% lower expected
  response time and 30.6% fewer late high-priority calls than the best
  static benchmarks.

## Standing in the record

Registered from the pasted bibliography on emergency readiness, where A
lists it under the value of availability with a gloss ("Formulates
dispatching and relocation as a stochastic dynamic program and develops an
approximate dynamic programming solution") that matches the abstract but
leaves out the queue option, the covering relocation, the lower bounds and
the case-study results. Whether the paper treats provisional dispatch or
cancellation en route, A's running example, cannot be told from the
abstract.

The paper was not read. Its neighbour, Guigues, Kleywegt and Nascimento
(2026), [LIT-2066](LIT-2066.md), read in part in [NOTE-1567](../notes.d/NOTE-1567.md), stands in for it
only weakly: it states the same joint selection-and-reassignment problem,
with the same trade-off between serving a call now and readiness for later
ones, and says of this paper only that it "proposed a number of basis
functions to approximate the value function of the MDP" and compared the
resulting policy with benchmarks and with lower bounds (p. 7). It gives
nothing of this paper's state space, basis functions, bounds or
Mecklenburg results, and its own model, a per-station Markov-chain
preparedness metric, is a different one. Nothing the record holds rests on
this paper.
