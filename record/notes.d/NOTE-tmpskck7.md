# NOTE-tmpskck7: Winner (1980) — Do Artifacts Have Politics?

## Contribution

The standard analytic restatement of Mumford's authoritarian and democratic technics. Winner separates technologies that "actually requires" particular social conditions from those "strongly compatible with, but does not strictly require" them, and internal from external conditions, with worked cases. He adds that choice is widest at introduction and closes after commitment.

## Key results

1. **Mumford, quoted** (opening; Winner's note gives Technology and Culture 5, 1964): "two technologies have recurrently existed side by side: one authoritarian, the other democratic, the first system-centered, immensely powerful, but inherently unstable, the other man-centered, relatively weak, but resourceful and durable."
2. **Arrangements that settle an issue** ("Technical Arrangements as Forms of Order"). Moses's low overpasses kept buses, and those who rode them, off the Long Island parkways; his works "embody a systematic social inequality, a way of engineering relationships among people that, after a time, becomes just another part of the landscape."
3. **No intention needed.** The tomato harvester cut growers from about four thousand in the early 1960s to about six hundred in 1973; "the technological deck has been stacked long in advance to favor certain social interests".
4. **The window closes.** "By far the greatest latitude of choice exists the very first time a particular instrument, system, or technique is introduced"; "the original flexibility vanishes for all practical purposes once the initial commitments are made." Hence innovations "are similar to legislative acts or political foundings that establish a framework for public order that will endure over many generations", and deserve "the same careful attention one would give to the rules, roles, and relationships of politics".
5. **Inherently political technologies** (second part). A system that "actually requires" particular social conditions, or one "strongly compatible with, but does not strictly require" them. "The internal social system of the bomb must be authoritarian; there is no other way."
6. **Practical necessity.** Claims of liberty or equality are "neutralized" by "Fine, but that’s no way to run a railroad"; "once artifacts like nuclear power plants have been built and put in operation, the kinds of reasoning that justify the adaptation of social life to technical requirements pop up as spontaneously as flowers in the spring."

## Assumptions

Politics means "arrangements of power and authority in human associations"; "required" means practical, not logical, necessity; the choice of a design and the choice to adopt are distinct.

## Concepts

- **Inherently political technologies**: those that require, or are strongly compatible with, particular political relations.
- **Technical arrangements as forms of order**: a design that settles an issue in a community, intended or not.
- **Practical necessity**: the reasoning that adapts social life to technical requirements once a system is built.

## Connections

A partial stand-in for LIT-tmpkv5kx. It restates Mumford's distinction in Mumford's own sentence and sharpens it into required against compatible, internal against external; it carries the claim that large systems are compatible with centralized, hierarchical control. It does not restate the megamachine (the machine made of people under central command), the book's history of technics from the Neolithic, the "magnificent bribe" or The Pentagon of Power. U1's glosses of the two technics are close to Mumford's sentence but drop instability and durability, and U1's "requiring ... hierarchy" is the Engels and Chandler version Winner discusses, not Mumford's sentence. Feenberg's contingent technical code (THEORY-tmpa59p3) develops Winner's first kind: a design that settles an issue in favour of some interests.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpsmg1c** (the burden of justification rises with irreversibility): strong support for its premise and its occasion. Result 4 is the premise the claim needs, that choice closes after commitment, and Winner draws a consequence close to the claim's: innovations deserve the scrutiny given to laws. He states no rule that the burden scales with irreversibility. Corroborates; argued in ARG-tmp5juxo.
- **CLAIM-tmpbypt7** (compulsion without a rule; the non-adopter's measure): support with a mechanism. Moses's bridges narrow the options of those without cars by arrangement, not rule, and the arrangement "becomes just another part of the landscape", the cultural mechanism; the harvester narrows the options of small growers with no one intending it. Corroborates.
- **CLAIM-tmpkjv7u** (dependence presumptively dominating by its controllers): consistent with its narrowing, no relation. The bomb's internal "centralized, rigidly hierarchical chain of command" is authority inside a system, not discretion over dependents outside it; result 6 is how accountability erodes, which bears on the third condition. The harvester is concentration no one intended, which the claim since version 2 places outside its test.
- **CLAIM-tmp6wuql**: leaves it open. McCormick's molding machines broke a union through deskilling, a use of power, not a trajectory argument.
- **SYNTHESIS-053** separates dependence, integration, coordination and authority; Winner's required against compatible is a finer cut of the authority side, a point for that account's next revision.
