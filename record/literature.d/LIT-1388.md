# LIT-1388: The Political Legacy of American Slavery

Acharya, A., Blackwell, M., & Sen, M. (2016). The political legacy of American slavery. *The Journal of Politics*, 78(3), 621–641. doi:10.1086/686631.

Read in full ([NOTE-tmpuag3s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuag3s.md)), without the online appendix.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpuag3s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuag3s.md).*

- **Small, consistent effects.** A 20 point rise in 1860 slave share goes
  with "a 2.3 percentage point decrease in the share of whites who
  currently identify as Democrats"; across the CCES outcomes,
  "approximately a 0.09–0.13 standard deviation change" (p. 626).
- **Not racial threat.** Holding present black share fixed leaves the
  direct effect about the same (Table 3).
- **Born after emancipation.** "the data show that regional differences
  emerged after the end of Reconstruction" (p. 634); the 1851 elections on
  secession show little relation to slavery.
- **Attitude, not party.** "it is sociopolitical attitudes, rather than
  partisanship, that are passed down from parent to children" (fn. 23).
- **Transmission is the weak link.** A parent–child correlation in 241
  Southern respondents, not tied to county slavery, and equal effects in
  those born after 1965.

## Why this is here

A29 cited it against the owner's U29 framing, to separate a statistical association from a causal transmission of a perspective. Bears on [CASE-133](../cases.d/CASE-133.md) and [CLAIM-420](../claims.d/CLAIM-420.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** [CLAIM-420](../claims.d/CLAIM-420.md) calls this "the hard case,
where association has to be told from transmission". The reading bears
that out: the association is well supported, transmission much less so.
It adds that the transmitted attitude was formed after slavery's
abolition, as a reaction to it, so the case joins descent from an
institution to transmission of a later perspective. For [CASE-133](../cases.d/CASE-133.md) it shows
in vote data an attitude that stayed in place while its party changed.
See [NOTE-tmpuag3s](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuag3s.md).
