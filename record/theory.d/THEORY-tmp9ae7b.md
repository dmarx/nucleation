# THEORY-tmp9ae7b: A securitised public keeps its routines by not attending to routine security practices, which keeps an anxiety about the threat latent rather than absent; when the security apparatus itself disrupts those routines the anxiety surfaces, and with it blame of the apparatus

## Source

Ku & Finch (2024), LIT-1478, read in full in NOTE-1133 from the version
of record.

## The thesis

Beside the political agency securitisation theory grants an audience,
Ku and Finch propose ontological agency: keeping one's routines going to
keep anxiety bracketed. "ontological agency is about inaction as much as
action in the face of securitising acts" (p. 307). The anxiety is latent,
not absent, and disruption releases it: "Successful and continuous
securitisation thus depends on the extent to which securitising moves fit
in with the audience's routines with minimal disruption" (p. 315).

## What the evidence is

THEORY-898: one afternoon of routine launches in Gangneung, observed by
chance and judged from outward behaviour, and one night of tweets after a
malfunction, filtered on "war" and "North Korea".

## What this does not say

- **That latent anxiety was measured.** "While we cannot access people's
  internal reactions, they seemed unfazed based on their outward
  behaviour" (p. 317, fn. 57).
- **That this is not habituation or low perceived risk.** No alternative
  is tested.
- **That publics resist securitisation.** The authors argue inaction lets
  it reproduce itself (p. 314).
