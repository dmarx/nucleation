# CASE-002: Northstar classification: who decides an incident is severe

## The case

"If an incident is classified as severe, deployment must stop." The product
team controls classification; moving it to an independent reliability group
changes outcomes without changing the rule. Senior management may also
authorize deployment despite a freeze for a "business critical" release.

## What it can show

That precedence, jurisdiction and suspension ([TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md)) come apart: an existence proof,
[ARG-001](../arguments.d/ARG-001.md).

## Note of 2026-11-07: the same classification, different authority, from "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"

In the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition" (2026-11-06 to 07), A43 §3's Institution A and Institution B, which hold one classification and grant it different authority, are this case with a concept in place of an incident's severity, and A43 §10's design (hold interpretation fixed and vary authority) is its test. A did not cite this case. It grounds [CLAIM-627](../claims.d/CLAIM-627.md), which asks whether moving jurisdiction changes not only the outcome but whether a concept's uses are compatible.
