# LIT-982: Theories of consciousness

Anil K. Seth, Tim Bayne (2022), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience 23(7), 439–452*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-849](../notes.d/NOTE-849.md).*

- **Different targets.** Theories differ in whether they explain global
  states, local contents, why a content is conscious, or what it is like;
  GWTs are explicitly theories of access, IIT the most comprehensive in
  coverage. Apparent rivals may not compete.
- **Four families.** Higher-order (meta-representation, prefrontal);
  global workspace (ignition and broadcast, frontoparietal); IIT
  (irreducible integrated information, posterior hot zone, implies some
  non-biological systems are conscious); reentry and predictive
  processing (top-down signalling; PP a theory "for" rather than "of"
  consciousness).
- **Shared data support no one.** Every family can explain why the
  cerebellum is not involved, so that datum favours none unless one
  explanation is better.
- **Front versus back is about access.** Prefrontal and decoding evidence
  favours HOT and GWT; no-report and dream evidence favours IIT and
  reentry; the dispute is over whether access is a proxy for
  consciousness.
- **The report channel limits the test.** Posterior-sufficiency claims
  cannot now be verified because verification relies on report, which
  requires anterior activity.
- **The measurement problem (Box 3).** Markers of consciousness need a
  validation that is neither introspective nor theory-based; none is
  uncontroversial.
- **Moving forward.** Theories need precision, comprehensiveness (affect,
  dreaming, infants, animals, AI) and measurement, with adversarial
  collaborations to test them.
