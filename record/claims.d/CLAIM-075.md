# CLAIM-075: Viability does not establish what ought to persist

## The claim

"We cannot infer from that fact alone that the capacity or agent has moral
value." The owner's U65 pressed for normative statements from viability; the
paper keeps them functional and conditional.

## Note of 2026-10-18: restated three times in the exchange of 2026-10-17

The exchange of 2026-10-17 re-derived this claim without citing it, then
cited it.

- **A16**, on normativity from first principles ([QUESTION-128](../questions.d/QUESTION-128.md)):
  "**self-generated normativity and normative legitimacy must remain
  separate**". Its cases, "A virus can maintain and reproduce its
  organization. A cancer can maintain and expand itself. A destructive
  institution can preserve its own authority relations", are this claim's
  and [CLAIM-060](CLAIM-060.md)'s. A16 cited [CLAIM-060](CLAIM-060.md) and not this.
- **A25**, on a fourth paper about authority between agents: an
  institution can possess "**effective authority**—the ability to make rules
  practically consequential—without necessarily possessing **legitimate
  authority**", which A25 called "one of its non-negotiable distinctions".
  That is this claim's "An unjust institution may be highly viable" for
  authority. A25 cited this claim for "interdependence ⇏ obligation".
- **A38**, on demands in training: "a demand's causal contribution to
  persistence does not establish its moral authority. Organizations can
  maintain harmful purposes." The same guard, uncited.

The claim is also the limit [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md) rests on and [QUESTION-129](../questions.d/QUESTION-129.md)
starts from. Nothing here changes it.

## Note of 2026-10-31: a process ethics that makes the inference this claim disclaims

Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy ([LIT-1902](../literature.d/LIT-1902.md), pp. 1366–1367) answer the
charge that agency without agents is apolitical with a duty "to help
'lesser existences' ... those whose process of individuation has not yet
found its way into others and who are still at risk of
disindividuation". They speak of "the collective duty to foster each
being-in-becoming, regardless of the 'level' on which they are
operating", so "that individuation can continue its course". The duty
rests on dependence: beings "depend on each other to pursue their
individuation" (p. 1366). That is a duty grounded in continuation, the
step this claim disclaims.

The paper's own pages hold this claim's counterexamples. The virus that
infects cells "is itself a process of becoming" (p. 1356), and it is a
being-in-becoming at a "level". In Bencherki (2017; [LIT-1901](../literature.d/LIT-1901.md), pp.
791–792) the army continues its individuation by taking up the spouses'
daycare work, and Bencherki calls that usurpation. "Foster each
being-in-becoming" cannot be meant without exception, and the
exceptions need a standard the paper does not give.

The qualifier "lesser" moves the weight from continuation to precarity:
the duty is to those "at risk of disindividuation". That is closer to
Jonas, whom the paper cites, than to viability. But which existence is
the lesser one is itself a judgment with relata ([CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md)). It has to
be indexed to a standard before it can be true or false. In the MSF
meeting the paper finds disindividuation on both sides, in Robert and
in "the doctors" (p. 1365). The paper also quotes Letiche and Moriceau
that "agency creates and destroys, frees and imprisons, all at once" (p.
1355). Their abstract ([LIT-1915](../literature.d/LIT-1915.md), unread beyond it) warns that
transduction "would seem to escape the individual and/or collective will
– becoming an all-important life-force outside political or ethical
control". The paper does not take up that warning, which points the
same way as this claim.

No status, title or warrant changes.

## Note of 2026-11-12: Lovett and Riedener, and Thompson, read

Two readings of 2026-11-12 corroborate this claim for sense-3 wholes
(TERM-074), on routes of their own. Lovett and Riedener (THEORY-tmpo4ghu,
NOTE-tmp3ebkj): "organizations don’t themselves have an interest in their
own continuation" (p. 230), so an organization's persistence carries no
weight of its own. Thompson (THEORY-tmp3vb9p, NOTE-tmpdkxlk): "Groups are
constructed and maintained by natural persons to serve their purposes"
(p. 12), so a group's persistence must be justified by those purposes.
Wardrope's reading of the Land Ethic (THEORY-tmpsj1yx) is a contrast, an
ethic in which a community's integrity is itself a value, though not
inferred from its viability. Filed as `corroborates` from the first two.
No status change.
