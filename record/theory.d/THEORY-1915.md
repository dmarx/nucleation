# THEORY-1915: Zeiser argues that AI decision support threatens responsibility chiefly as attributability rather than accountability: a decision a human makes with a tool that presupposes outcome-shaping values she has not endorsed does not express her value-judgements, an attributability gap that comes in degrees and that descriptive tools open as well as prescriptive ones

## Source

Zeiser (2024), [LIT-2666](../literature.d/LIT-2666.md), read in full; the reading is [NOTE-2007](../notes.d/NOTE-2007.md).

## What was actually shown

A conceptual argument. The gap: "Attributability-gaps occur when a human decision-maker uses a decision-tool that presupposes values that have a significant influence on the outcome of the decision and that they haven’t endorsed as their own values." Prescriptive tools presuppose aims, means and trade-offs; descriptive tools carry value choices in targets, data and fairness standards, so "descriptive AI systems also don’t provide neutral, value-free information". Attributability "might not be a binary property, but a gradual one". Remedies: endorsement through transparency, calibration to the user's values, and confinement to description (partial).

Bears on [QUESTION-068](../questions.d/QUESTION-068.md): the three faces diverge in ordinary cases, and "responsibility without attributability" is offered as a coherent aim.

## What this does not say

Who should answer for the values a tool embeds, or that users held accountable under an attributability gap are treated unfairly. Nothing empirical about how often the gap occurs.

## From the second reading (2026-11-17)

The second reading adds a relation and a limit. Corroborates [CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md),
which does not rest on it: a decision "technically made by a human" need
not express her judgement, and Zeiser's endorsement remedy is not tested
against manipulated endorsement. Accountability can be kept without
attributability, through "blank check responsibility", though
attributability is "a more robust basis" for it.

- **That endorsement suffices for ownership** is not shown: endorsement
  can itself be shaped, which the paper does not treat.
- It gives [TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md) a source for a narrower attributability sense
  (whose value-judgements a decision expresses).
