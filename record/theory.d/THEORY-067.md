# THEORY-067: Self-control over time needs no faculty of will: interests that dominate at different delays bargain, and resolve is the recursive staking of expected future choices on the present one

## Source

- Ainslie (2005), *Précis of Breakdown of Will*, [LIT-586](../literature.d/LIT-586.md), read in
  [NOTE-443](../notes.d/NOTE-443.md): §§3, 5–6, 8 and 9. The book, [LIT-583](../literature.d/LIT-583.md), is unread.
- Schelling (1980), "The Intimate Contest for Self-Command", [LIT-602](../literature.d/LIT-602.md),
  read in [NOTE-452](../notes.d/NOTE-452.md). His 1984 lecture, [LIT-573](../literature.d/LIT-573.md), is unread.

## What was actually shown

**Temporary preference follows from the curve.** Ainslie ([LIT-586](../literature.d/LIT-586.md))
takes as his one empirical premise that vertebrates discount delayed reward
roughly hyperbolically, value inversely proportional to delay plus a small
constant (Mazur), with many fits cited and none re-analysed. From the shape
alone, a smaller, sooner reward is preferred in the period just before it is
due and dispreferred from a distance (C2 in [NOTE-443](../notes.d/NOTE-443.md)). Animals show
the matching behaviour: pigeons choose in advance to remove the sooner
option, and rats commit to a later, longer shock rather than leave the
choice open.

**Interests, not a governor.** The preferences that dominate at different
delays grow into "interests", each selected by one kind of reward over one
time range, each surviving by forestalling the others. The précis calls
this the "bottom up model": interests "are not options chosen by an
overarching ego, the top down model", and stable choice "has to be
achieved as it is in the kind of markets that don't have governors".

**Will is bargaining by precedent.** Perceiving today's choice as a
predictor of tomorrow's "bundles" the series, and the summed hyperbolic
curves favour the larger, later rewards, which summed exponential curves
would not change (Figures 3–5). That part is mathematics. The cited
experiments impose bundling from outside: students chose the larger, later
money more often when choosing for five weeks at once (Kirby & Guastello
2001), and rats did so when choosing for a block of three trials. Because
each choice now stakes the expected value of the series, a lapse lowers the
credibility of the rule, and "personal rules are a recursive mechanism; they
continually take their own pulse". "Will, in short, is a bargaining
situation, where credibility is power."

**Against a faculty.** The précis compares five models of will and argues
that an "organ" of will "has to be guided by some evaluation process outside
of motivation", and asks "What keeps this strength from being co-opted by
the bad option?". Bargaining is "the only model that explains both temporary
preference and adequate incentive to overcome it from the properties of the
rewards involved". The predicted costs of the mechanism are its further
support: legalism, lapse districts, motivated blindness to lapses, and rules
that favour countable goals. These are fitted to clinical observation, not
tested.

**Schelling's convergent description.** Schelling ([LIT-602](../literature.d/LIT-602.md)) observes
that the willing spirit and weak flesh "frequently alternate rather than
coincide", and that modelling the lapser as a high discounter would need a
rate of "100 percent per hour or per minute". That argument against one
constant rate is what a hyperbolic curve answers. His catalogue of tactics
(bright lines, "zero is a more enforceable limit" than a ration, New Year's
resolutions that raise the stakes) is the catalogue of managing another
person, applied to one's later self. He offers no mechanism and leaves open
which self is authentic. He supplies the phenomena; Ainslie supplies the
mechanism.

## What this does not say

- **It is not a claim about self-governance.** Whether resolve, so
  explained, is the agent governing herself by her own standards is a
  different question ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)). This account says how one control system
  over time comes to act as one, which is `behavioral-integration`'s
  "intertemporal conflict and self-control". The précis does not say which
  interest is the person's own, and Schelling says there is no general
  criterion.
- **"A population of interests" is not a claim about personhood.** The
  précis says the person is "just the resultant" of the interests. In this
  record's terms that is a claim about how behaviour is integrated, and it
  does not say that a person is many persons.
- **It does not deny that other tactics work.** Ainslie lists external
  constraint, attention control and emotion control beside will; the claim
  is that will, the strongest, needs no further faculty.
- **The mechanism is not tested directly.** The précis says so (§8), and
  its BBS commentaries were not read.

## Connections

- **The rival in self-governance.** Bratman's essay on a planning agent's
  self-governance over time ([LIT-567](../literature.d/LIT-567.md)) is declared as the rival of the
  précis among the literature, and the theory built on it, [THEORY-076](THEORY-076.md),
  is declared this theory's rival. The two collide on what resolve is. Bratman treats oneself over
  time as a partner in a shared intention, with a reflexive end of one's own
  diachronic self-governance; Ainslie treats the later self as a bargaining
  opponent and suggests resolute choice "may turn out to be another name for
  intertemporal bargaining". Both explain resolve against a foreseen
  temptation, and the explanations exclude each other.
- **[THEORY-070](THEORY-070.md) (the expected value of control).** That account treats
  patient intertemporal choice as a "default override" needing costly
  control specified by a dedicated system. That is close to the "organ"
  model the précis rejects. For patience, the two cannot both give the
  mechanism, though neither text tests one against the other. No `rivals` is
  declared here, because the EVC account's own claim is about cognitive
  control in general and does not commit to patience; the collision is in
  one of its applications.
- **[THEORY-056](THEORY-056.md)** (regulation as one motive state checking another, with no
  regulator above them). This account supports its architecture from
  outside emotion research, and supplies what [THEORY-056](THEORY-056.md) names as its
  largest gap, which state wins: the one with the greatest discounted
  reward at that moment, with bundled future rewards added by
  self-prediction. It differs in what does the checking: an expectation
  about one's own future choices, not a concurrent emotion. No relation is
  declared, because the two are about different processes.
- **[THEORY-062](THEORY-062.md)** (model-based and model-free control by reliability)
  rejects the same reason-against-impulse picture for a different division:
  there the controllers differ in method, here in delay. They do not
  conflict.
- **[THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md).** The précis's regress against an organ of will has the
  shape of Velleman's regress against hierarchical ownership, posed about
  force rather than ownership. The bearing is the record's.
- **Self-determination theory.** The will's legalism, choices "detached from
  their immediate outcomes", resembles introjected regulation ([THEORY-047](THEORY-047.md)).
  The mechanisms differ, so this is a convergence of phenomena.
