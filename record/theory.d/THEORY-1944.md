# THEORY-1944: de Boer and Raaphorst find, in a survey of 549 Dutch food-safety inspectors, that working with a partially automated verdict system is associated with a more legal and more accommodative self-reported enforcement style and, weakly, with lower perceived discretion, and that perceived discretion does not mediate the association

## Source

de Boer and Raaphorst (2023), Public Management Review 25(1): 42–62 ([LIT-2674](../literature.d/LIT-2674.md)), read in [NOTE-1997](../notes.d/NOTE-1997.md).

## What was actually shown

Cross-sectional survey, NVWA inspectors, 2018 (n = 549, 82 using INSPECT). Automation: legal style
β = 0.097 (p = .044), accommodation β = 0.109 (p ≈ .06), facilitation n.s.; perceived discretion
β = −0.086 (p = .069); no significant indirect effect. "automation (1) increases bureaucrats’ legal and
accommodation style" while "automation does decrease" perceived discretion (Abstract).

No corroborates relation is proposed: the study measures style and perception, not intervention points
or correction.

## What this does not say

Not causal ("no conclusions can be drawn about causality", p. 56). Not about actual (granted) discretion
or observed enforcement outcomes. One regulator, self-reported treatment; several results at p < .10.
