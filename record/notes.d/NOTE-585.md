# NOTE-585: Causation and Manipulability

## Contribution

It replaced the agency theories' appeal to free human action with a
causally defined notion of intervention. That answers the anthropocentrism
objection and turns the circularity objection into a non-vicious
dependence on other causal facts.

## Key insight

What makes a correlation causal is how it behaves when X is changed from
outside in a way that touches Y only through X. A free act does not
guarantee that (the placebo case); an intervention, by definition, does.

## Assumptions

- Causal relata are variables, whose values the same system can take.
- An intervention is defined relative to a target variable Y (Woodward and
  Hitchcock), not, as in Pearl, by leaving the X→Y mechanism intact.
- Causal effect is relative to a background context.

## Key results

- **Intervention, M1–M4** (§5). (M1) I is the only cause of X, setting it
  entirely. (M2) I does not directly cause Y by a route that avoids X.
  (M3) I is not caused by any cause that affects Y by a route that avoids
  X. (M4) I leaves unchanged the values of Y's causes other than those on
  the path from I through X to Y.
- **Causal concepts** (§7). X is a total cause of Y if some interventions
  on X change Y. X is a direct cause of Y if Y changes under an
  intervention on X while all other variables, including those on routes
  from X to Y, are held fixed by interventions.
- **Non-vicious circularity** (§6). The account says which other causal
  facts are needed, not the fact being characterized; "we do sometimes
  learn about causal relationships by performing experiments". It is not
  trivial, because it disagrees with causal-process theories about
  omission and double prevention.
- **Lewis explained** (§9). Lewis's similarity ordering works because it
  roughly picks out relationships "stable under interventions".
- **Setting versus possibility-constrained interventions** (§§5, 10, 12).
  Woodward leans to the setting version: what matters is whether
  well-grounded theory says what would happen, not whether the
  intervention is physically possible. He states both views on
  unmanipulable causes such as race and gender and does not settle it.
- **Reply to operationalism** (§13). The account does not require that
  interventions can be carried out; observational data can confirm claims
  about what interventions would do. Woodward concedes it is
  "mono-criterial".

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Free action does not suffice to identify a cause | strong | placebo counterexample to Menzies & Price |
| C2 | X causes Y iff some intervention on X (M1–M4) would change Y | moderate | definitional proposal, defended against objections |
| C3 | The account is non-reductive but not viciously circular | moderate | argument, §6 |
| C4 | Interventions need not involve human agents | strong | follows from the causal definition; natural experiments |
| C5 | Whenever a macro-level interventionist counterfactual is true there is some more fundamental explanation of why | weak | stated as a plausible expectation, not argued (§13) |

## Concepts

- **intervention** — an exogenous causal process on X meeting M1–M4 with
  respect to Y; "it has no essential connection with human action"
  (preamble).
- **interventionist counterfactual** — what Y would do if an intervention
  set X to a value.
- **setting intervention** — X set to a value by arrow-breaking, with no
  further restriction on possibility.
- **soft intervention** — an exogenous source of variation in X,
  uncorrelated with X's other causes, that leaves those causes acting
  (Eberhardt & Scheines, §14).

## Connections

It builds on Pearl and on Woodward & Hitchcock, and criticizes Menzies &

## Bearing on the record

Source of [THEORY-138](../theory.d/THEORY-138.md). It is the general account behind the
realization-insensitivity test of [THEORY-133](../theory.d/THEORY-133.md).

For the owner's essay (§5.3), it says what an agentive explanation of
Northstar would have to deliver to count as causal: an organizational
variable that the same organization could take in different values, and an
intervention on it that changes the outcome by no other route. The "Role"
supplement's example comes closest to institutions: on "being female causes
one to be discriminated against", intervening on the legal and cultural
framework is "a quite different experiment" from intervening on gender.
That is a model for separating an organization's regime from its members'
attributes. The entry does not discuss organizations, collective agents or
levels, so anything further is the record's application.

## Limitations

It is a survey. It does not adjudicate between rival characterizations of
intervention, only sketches token causation, leaves open whether
fundamental physics is causal, and admits that the notion of a possible
intervention is unclear. Invariance, proportionality and higher-level
causation are not treated in this version.

## Open questions

What, for an organization, counts as an intervention on an organizational
variable that leaves its members' other causal influence on the outcome
unchanged (M4); and what chapter 6 of the book adds on invariance.
