# LIT-tmp0gt3l: Smart cities and technologies: connected or disconnected citizens? Interview with Matthew Crawford

Matthew B. Crawford, interviewed by David Ménascé (2017). "Smart cities and technologies: connected or disconnected citizens?" *Field Actions Science Reports*, Special Issue 16, "Smart Cities at the Crossroads", pp. 8–10. Institut Veolia. ISSN 1867-8521.

## Key takeaways

- Attention as a common resource captured in shared space: "people are a captive audience and that their mental space could be monetized"; the remedy is "an attentional commons".
- "distraction is not a problem of technology in itself. It is rather a problem of political economy."
- Removing the need for skill frees attention for capture: "as we do not need our skills and brains to move in our environment, companies have more of our brain to exploit."

## Standing in the record

A first-hand stand-in for the diagnosis of LIT-tmpchx6j, The World Beyond Your Head, which is Unavailable: attention as a commons, the captive audience, distraction as political economy, environments that eliminate contingency. It does not carry the book's positive account (skilled practice, jigs, affordances, the ecology of attention), for which Dijkema's review (LIT-tmpnno4c) is a second-hand stand-in.
