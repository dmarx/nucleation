# QUESTION-351: Recorded with single units or dense bilateral prefrontal sampling in a no-report or task-irrelevant design, is a clearly seen image's category present in prefrontal cortex while its identity and orientation, decodable posteriorly in the same subjects, are absent there with Bayesian support for the absence, and does prefrontal cortex stay silent at stimulus offset under an equally sensitive test?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-245](../theory.d/THEORY-245.md) holds what the COGITATE consortium measured: prefrontal cortex carried
a clearly seen image's category but not its identity, and a face's orientation
at most weakly, and no prefrontal site showed the preregistered onset-and-offset
response. The signals were iEEG high gamma, MEG source power and BOLD. Whether
prefrontal cortex lacks identity and orientation in other signals, and stays
silent at offset under an equally sensitive test, is open.

## What would answer it

A second recording of the same pattern with a different method or sample:
single-unit or dense bilateral prefrontal recordings, in a no-report or
task-irrelevant design, showing that a seen image's category is present in
prefrontal cortex while its identity and orientation, decodable posteriorly in
the same subjects, are absent there, with Bayesian support for the absence; and
an offset test with the same sensitivity. The answer is no if prefrontal
identity or orientation coding of seen, task-irrelevant images appears in
signals COGITATE did not sample (single units, low frequencies, activity-silent
states probed by perturbation), or a prefrontal response appears at stimulus
offset under matched conditions. More demonstrations that category is decodable
in prefrontal cortex cannot settle it.
