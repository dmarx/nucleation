# THEORY-1327: As Ryan and colleagues' 2022 meta-review maps it, the meta-analytic evidence for self-determination theory is concentrated in cognitive evaluation, organismic integration and basic needs theory, is mostly cross-sectional and heterogeneous, and has gaps: no meta-analysis of relationships motivation theory or of need frustration, one or two of causality orientations and goal contents, and none that separates integrated from identified regulation

## Source

Ryan et al. (2022), [LIT-1967](../literature.d/LIT-1967.md), *Psychological Bulletin* 148, 813–842,
read in [NOTE-1500](../notes.d/NOTE-1500.md) from the authors' accepted manuscript (OSF preprint
gk5cy). The manuscript has no page numbers; sections are named.

## What was actually shown

**The map.** 60 meta-analyses, 58 published, found from the authors'
databases, the SDT website and five databases searched in 2021, sorted
into Table 1 (mini-theories) and Table 2 (applied domains). Relationships
motivation theory "has no entry in its column within Table 1" (Method).

**Where the support is.** "Whereas CET, OIT, and BPNT mini-theories have
received strong meta-analytic support, support is much thinner where COT
and GCT are concerned. In each case, only one primary meta-analysis
exists" (Summary and Conclusions). The COT section itself reviews two
(Hagger and Hamilton 2021; Murphy and Steel 2021), the GCT section two
(Bradshaw et al. 2022; Dittmar et al. 2014): "one or two" in the title
follows the sections.

**What kind of support.** "their typical (though not invariant) reliance
on cross-sectional datasets"; causal evidence comes from the experimental
(Deci et al. 1999) and intervention meta-analyses (Gillison et al. 2019;
Su and Reeve 2011; Sheeran et al. 2020, r = .11 on behaviour). "a high
degree of heterogeneity in many study findings … accounts of heterogeneity
have been far from comprehensive" (Heterogeneity in Effect Sizes).

**The named gaps.**
- Need frustration: "The proposition that frustration of these needs is
  associated with ill-being has yet to be meta-analytically investigated"
  (BPNT Summary).
- Integration: Howard et al. 2017 "did not support the differentiation of
  integrated regulation from identified regulation", partly for "too few
  studies measuring integration"; Van den Broeck et al. 2021 found it
  "rarely measured … and where it was measured it did not show
  differentiation" (OIT; Work and Organizations).
- Goal contents: mediation by need satisfaction "has yet to be
  meta-analytically established for intrinsic aspirations" (GCT Summary).
- CET's mediation proposition, that felt autonomy and competence
  "mediate the relations between external events and their … effects", is
  stated, and no meta-analysis of the mediation is reported (CET).

The map could have come out otherwise: the theory's own founder could
have reported meta-analyses of RMT or need frustration, or of integration,
and did not find them.

## What this does not say

- **That the supported findings are true.** The review re-pools nothing
  and grades no meta-analysis for bias; its "meta-analytically true" is
  the included meta-analyses' conclusions. It is not preregistered.
- **That the map is complete.** English only; seeded from the authors'
  own databases; to July 2021. Later meta-analyses (for example of need
  frustration) are outside it.
- **That it is independent.** The lead author co-founded the theory and
  several included meta-analyses are the authors' own.
- **Anything about the primary studies.** Howard et al. 2017 ([LIT-1996](../literature.d/LIT-1996.md))
  is reported here second-hand.
