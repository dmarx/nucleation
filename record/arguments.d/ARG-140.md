# ARG-140: Mitzenmacher → growth with a floor, power law

## The inference

[CLAIM-824](../claims.d/CLAIM-824.md): i.i.d. multiplicative growth gives a lognormal; the same
growth "with an additive floor" gives a stationary Pareto tail with κ
solving E[A^κ] = 1, even when E[log A] < 0. Read as a source,
[THEORY-2150](../theory.d/THEORY-2150.md) would carry both halves.

## Critical questions (source)

**Does the work say this, read in context?** The first half, yes: "if the
ln Fk are independent and identically distributed variables with finite
mean and variance, then asymptotically Xj will approach a lognormal
distribution" (§5, p. 236). The second half, in another form: "As long as
there is a bounded minimum that acts as a lower reflective barrier to the
multiplicative model, it will yield a power law instead of a lognormal
distribution. The theory of this phenomenon is more fully developed in
[Gabaix 99, Kesten 73]" (§5.1, p. 238). A reflecting minimum is not an
additive B: one stops X from falling below m, the other adds a positive
amount each step.

**Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** Not for the exponent. The
paper gives no equation for κ and no conditions. Its Champernowne example
(F = 1/2 with probability 2/3, 2 with probability 1/3) satisfies the
claim's equation: (2/3)·2^(−κ) + (1/3)·2^κ = 1 at κ = 1, which is its
Pr[X ≥ x] = m/x, and E[ln F] = −0.231 < 0, so it contracts in log terms
as the claim allows. The filer checked both by hand and by simulating the
chain with its floor (python3 -I). That is an instance the paper gives,
not a statement it makes.

**Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** By listing
it as a ground, yes: the claim's exponent and conditions come from Kesten,
whom the paper only cites. The paper corroborates the claim's three-way
account (entry, floor, both): its §8 names two of the routes, "once the
observation time is random or a lower boundary is put into effect".

## Verdict

[LIT-3003](../literature.d/LIT-3003.md) moves from the claim's grounds to `corroborates` on
[THEORY-2150](../theory.d/THEORY-2150.md). The claim's "additive floor" is one kind of floor; a
reflecting barrier is another, and a dated note on [CLAIM-824](../claims.d/CLAIM-824.md) says so.

## Change of 2026-11-22

Kesten's paper ([LIT-3005](../literature.d/LIT-3005.md)) is Unfetched; Kevei's note ([LIT-3043](../literature.d/LIT-3043.md), the
reading [THEORY-2183](../theory.d/THEORY-2183.md)) was read in its place. Two things follow for
this argument, neither changing the verdict:

- **The Kesten half rests on Kevei's restatement**, not on Kesten unread:
  [CLAIM-824](../claims.d/CLAIM-824.md)'s `grounds` now hold [THEORY-2183](../theory.d/THEORY-2183.md) in [LIT-3005](../literature.d/LIT-3005.md)'s place
  ([ARG-183](ARG-183.md)).
- **The reflecting minimum falls under a theorem.** Kevei restates
  Goldie's Theorem 5.2 for X = AX ∨ B: under the same conditions, "there
  is a unique solution X to (12), and P{X > x} ∼ cx−κ with some c ≥ 0,
  and c > 0 if and only if P{B > 0} > 0" (p. 4). Mitzenmacher's
  Champernowne chain with its lowest class is of this max form, with one
  caveat: log F = ±log 2 is arithmetic, outside the theorem's
  nonarithmetic hypothesis, so the chain is the lattice case, whose tail
  holds on the lattice points (Pr[X ≥ x] = m/x there) rather than as a
  smooth asymptotic. Its κ = 1 is the exponent the same equation gives.
  A reflecting minimum and an additive B are different constructions with
  one exponent equation. Version 2.
