# NOTE-1061: Bo et al. — Deconstructing the brain bases of emotion regulation

## Contribution

Earlier imaging work on reappraisal compared regulated with unregulated
viewing and called what went up a "generator" and what went down a
"target". It could not show that a region was *not* engaged, so it could
not say whether the regions active in reappraisal are specific to it, nor
which emotion-related regions reappraisal leaves alone. This study uses
Bayes factors, which can give evidence for a null, in two samples large
enough for that (n = 182 and 176), and requires every effect to replicate in
both. It finds four kinds of region, two of which (reappraisal-only and
non-modifiable) needed the null to be established.

## Key insight

A region can be classified by the pattern of what it does and does not do
across two contrasts, emotion generation [Look Negative − Look Neutral] and
reappraisal [Reappraise Negative − Look Negative], as an axiomatic
definition: a reappraisal-specific region "must (1) respond more strongly
when reappraising negative images than when merely looking at them, and (2)
must not respond differentially to negative vs. neutral images in the
absence of reappraisal demand" (p. 5). Strong evidence for the null in
condition (2) is what turns active during reappraisal into selective for
it.

## Assumptions

- **Two contrasts carry the processes.** Emotion generation is Look
  Negative against Look Neutral; reappraisal is Reappraise Negative against
  Look Negative (p. 5). Look Negative is taken as the unregulated response.
- **Three conditions stated by the authors (p. 23).** "(1) brain activation
  from different conditions reflects different demand on appraisal
  processes, (2) Look negative vs look neutral can cover brain response of
  appraisal processes, (3) Appraisal processes do not introduce activity
  decreases." They say each could fail, and what would follow.
- **Thresholds.** BF > 10 for an effect and BF < 1/10 for a null, with a
  JZS prior (Rouder et al. 2009), computed voxelwise from group t statistics
  (pp. 25–26). BF 10 corresponds to p < 0.0018 here (p. 8). A null at 10:1
  needs at least 120 participants (p. 5; p. 26).
- **Consensus maps.** Each study's binary map was smoothed (3 mm) and the
  two multiplied; a voxel enters the consensus map above a product of 0.01
  (p. 26). The worst consensus voxel lies 4–4.4 mm from the nearest voxel in
  either study's map (p. 27).
- **The sample.** A midlife community sample from the Pittsburgh area,
  mean age 42.7 and 40.1 (p. 21), from two existing projects (AHAB-2 and
  PIP). Each person saw 15 trials per condition of IAPS pictures, a 2 s cue
  ("Look" or "Decrease"), the picture for 7 s, and a 4 s rating of negative
  feeling on a 5-point scale (p. 24).

## Key results

- **Behaviour (p. 7).** Negative pictures raised negative ratings over
  neutral ones (t = 63.8, d = 3.37). Reappraisal lowered them against
  looking (t = −17.5, d = 0.92). "81% of individual participants showed
  regulation effects in the same direction as the group findings." The
  dominant strategies, by McRae et al.'s coding, were changing current
  circumstances, reality challenge and changing future consequences (Fig.
  1D).
- **Reappraisal only (p. 8; Fig. 2A).** Sixteen regions replicated across
  both studies: anterior lateral prefrontal cortex (aDLPFC, aVLPFC), TPJ,
  IPL, posterior middle and anterior inferior temporal gyri, and superior
  cerebellum. By resting-state network, 47% default mode, 31%
  frontoparietal, 16% ventral attention. On the bar plots these regions sit
  at the same level for Look Neutral and Look Negative and rise only under
  reappraisal.
- **Common appraisal (pp. 8–9; Fig. 2B).** Fourteen regions, "the largest
  set of brain regions among system components": DLPFC, VLPFC, SPL, pSTS,
  anterior insula, a dMPFC/aMCC/SMA cluster, precuneus and cerebellum.
  Reappraisal-only regions lie anterior and lateral to them, "suggesting a
  cortical gradient" (p. 9).
- **Non-modifiable emotion generation (p. 10; Fig. 3A).** "Most regions
  responsive to emotional stimuli were non-modifiable, including all
  subcortical emotion-related regions." Thirty-two regions: amygdala, PAG,
  parabrachial nucleus, ventral putamen, hypothalamus, ventral striatum and
  pallidum, and in cortex lateral OFC, medial prefrontal regions,
  midcingulate and retrosplenial cortex, agranular area 6, parietal cortex,
  precuneus and ventral fusiform.
- **Modifiable emotion generation (p. 10; Fig. 3B–C).** Eight regions,
  "located exclusively in the cortex": mainly higher retinotopic visual
  areas (V3, V4, V7, VO, LO, MT), intraparietal sulcus, postcentral gyrus
  and inferior frontal junction. "We did not detect any modifiable emotion
  regions in the subcortical cortex" (sic, p. 10).
- **Amygdala and PAG (p. 11; Fig. 4).** Every amygdala subregion and the
  PAG responded to negative pictures (all p < 0.0001), and none was reduced
  by reappraisal (all BF < 0.1). Basolateral and superficial amygdala were
  "numerically in the opposite direction as predicted by the downregulation
  hypothesis". On the figure the reappraise bars equal or slightly exceed
  the look-negative bars.
- **Reappraisal success (pp. 11–12; Fig. 5).** Success, the drop in rated
  negative affect, correlated with reappraisal-related activity in
  reappraisal-only regions (r = 0.2, adjusted R² = 0.039) and common
  appraisal regions (r = 0.14), negatively with modifiable regions
  (r = −0.14), and not with non-modifiable ones (r = 0.015, p = 0.77). The
  best subset model held reappraisal-only and modifiable together
  (r = 0.26, adjusted R² = 0.063). A whole-brain robust regression (n = 358,
  FDR q < 0.05) added nucleus accumbens, pregenual ACC and posterior insula
  and somatosensory cortex (lower with success), which the four systems
  missed because they fell with negative emotion.
- **Annotation (pp. 13–16; Figs 6–7).** Against 50 Neurosynth topics,
  reappraisal-only maps were most selective for "response inhibition",
  common appraisal for "cognitive conflict", non-modifiable for "emotion
  face" and modifiable for "object recognition". Against PET binding maps,
  reappraisal-only and common appraisal both went with CB1, 5-HT2A,
  mu-opioid, mGluR5 and GABA-A; non-modifiable regions went with 5HTT, NET,
  mu-opioid and D2. Both analyses are called hypothesis-generating.
- **The authors' readings (pp. 17–22).**
  - Reappraisal-only regions are among the most expanded in humans and
    "positioned above unimodal sensory hierarchies and thus in a position to
    regulate them based on conceptual context" (p. 17).
  - Common appraisal regions "are presumably involved in the appraisal
    processes that give rise to negative emotion, and may be involved in
    spontaneous self-regulation in the absence of explicit regulatory
    instructions" (p. 18).
  - Summary: "emotion generation and reappraisal depend on many of the same
    cortical regions, in line with psychological theories that posit overlap
    between affective appraisal and reappraisal. However, reappraisal
    involves a distinct set of brain regions" (p. 20).
  - Subcortical regions "were neither influenced by reappraisal nor
    correlated with reappraisal success", which "might indicate that
    subcortical affective processes are less modifiable by reappraisal than
    previously thought" (p. 20). Two reasons are offered for the difference
    from earlier amygdala findings: reinterpretation strategies dominate
    here, and the sample is older (pp. 20–21).
  - Modifiable visual regions are read as semantic and conceptual
    representation, not only shifted attention, since they are higher-order
    visual areas (p. 21).
  - The closing sentence: reappraisal "is not able to modulate
    subcortical-level affective experience as well as lateral prefrontal and
    orbitofrontal areas related to value construction" (p. 22).

## Concepts

- **Systems identification (axiomatic)**: defining a hypothetical process by
  the effects any signal encoding it must and must not show, after Caplin &
  Dean and Rutledge et al. (p. 5).
- **The four components (pp. 5–6)**: "Reappraisal only" (reappraisal +,
  emotion null); "Common appraisal" (both +); "Non-modifiable
  emotion-generation" (emotion +, reappraisal null); "Modifiable
  emotion-generation" (emotion +, reappraisal −).
- **Reappraisal**: "reinterpreting the meaning of stimuli and events in
  service of a regulatory goal" (p. 3).
- **Reappraisal success**: a person's drop in rated negative affect,
  Look Negative minus Reappraise Negative (p. 11).
- **Bayes factor for the null**: BF < 1/10, read as 10:1 evidence that the
  two conditions do not differ (p. 8).

## Connections

The design and samples come from Gianaros et al. (2014, 2020, 2022). The
axiomatic method is borrowed from Caplin & Dean (2008) and Rutledge et al.
(2010). It sets itself against the meta-analyses that make the amygdala the
main target of reappraisal (Buhle et al. 2014, a Wager co-authored paper;
Frank et al. 2014), and agrees with the meta-analysis finding that
reinterpretation does not reliably lower amygdala activity (Dörfel et al.
2014). Its framing question is Gross & Barrett (2011), "Emotion generation
and emotion regulation: one or two depends on your point of view", which
[THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)'s promotion test names and the record has not filed. It also cites
Mesquita & Frijda (2011) and Barrett 2017b ([LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md)) for the view that
affect generation is widely distributed.

## Bearing on the record

- **The dispute between [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md).** This is the first
  data the record holds on the question that divides them, whether
  regulation is a process apart from generation. The paper gives each side
  a result.
  - **For [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md).** Most of the reappraisal network is also engaged
    by looking at negative pictures without any instruction (C3). The
    authors read these as appraisal regions shared with generation, and
    allow that they "may be involved in spontaneous self-regulation". That
    is what a regulation made of the same stuff as generation looks like,
    and the authors place it "in line with" theories of overlap, naming
    Gross & Barrett and Mesquita & Frijda.
  - **Against [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md).** A separate set of regions is engaged by
    reappraisal and shows evidence of no engagement by emotion generation
    (C2). They are anterior prefrontal and temporo-parietal, annotated as
    response inhibition and executive control, and the best predictors of
    success (C6). The authors describe them as "positioned above unimodal
    sensory hierarchies and thus in a position to regulate them". That is
    close to the distinct regulating system above the motive states that
    [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) denies. [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)'s `promote_when` says in advance that "a
    difference in network activation between emotion and regulation tasks"
    cannot settle it, because one readiness may be built differently from
    another. By that clause the result does not refute it. But the clause
    then excludes the very evidence a separate regulator would leave in
    imaging, and the record should note that it makes [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) hard to
    test with imaging at all.
  - **For [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md), partly.** C2 fits its claim that regulation includes
    processes distinct from generation. C4 and C5 fit its picture of
    reappraisal acting early, on meaning: what reappraisal changes is
    higher visual and parietal representation of the picture, not the
    subcortical response.
  - **Against [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md)'s measurement.** Its `promote_when` asks for each
    person's unregulated response to be estimated independently. Here Look
    Negative is the unregulated baseline, and the authors themselves allow
    that common appraisal regions may carry spontaneous regulation during
    it (p. 18). That is the counterfactual problem [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) already
    names, met in practice. Only one strategy was tested, so nothing
    compares strategies by stage.
  - **Neither promotion test is met.** No lesion, interference or
    developmental dissociation ([THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)); no stage comparison ([THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md)).
    Filed as [THEORY-878](../theory.d/THEORY-878.md).
- **[QUESTION-054](../questions.d/QUESTION-054.md) (is there a governing level?).** The reappraisal-only
  regions are a candidate governing level for deliberate emotion regulation,
  in the anatomical sense: selective, high in the cortical hierarchy and
  predictive of success. It is a partial answer for one deliberate strategy
  only. It says nothing about effortless regulation, which is where
  [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md) differ.
- **[CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) (experience constituted by interpretive organization).**
  The exchange cited this study as a limit on the constitutive reading: not
  every component of an emotion is open to reinterpretation. The reading
  supports a narrower statement. Reinterpretation lowered reported negative
  feeling while amygdala, PAG and hypothalamus responses to the pictures
  were unchanged, with evidence for the null. Whether that limits the claim
  depends on whether those responses are part of the experience.
  - If they are, reported experience changed while part of what
    constitutes it did not, which the claim must explain.
  - If they are not, the result shows what the claim allows: reported
    experience follows the reorganized cortical interpretation, not every
    process the stimulus drives.
  - The study cannot choose. It measures BOLD in regions and one rating per
    trial, and its closing phrase "subcortical-level affective experience"
    (p. 22) treats subcortical activity as experience without evidence. The
    reading that an unchanged subcortical component is the emotion, apart
    from a feeling that changed, is the split Barrett et al. reject
    ([NOTE-1067](NOTE-1067.md)). Filed as [THEORY-871](../theory.d/THEORY-871.md).
  - The exchange's description should be corrected in one respect. The
    unmodulated system is not only subcortical. Most emotion-responsive
    regions were non-modifiable, cortical ones included (lateral OFC,
    medial prefrontal, midcingulate, parietal); the subcortical regions were
    the ones with none modifiable.
- **[CLAIM-411](../claims.d/CLAIM-411.md) (broad interpretation admits processing not shown to be
  experiential).** Indirect support. The common appraisal regions are
  engaged without instruction and "may be involved in spontaneous
  self-regulation", an appraisal that is not reported as such. Nothing
  here shows it is unconscious.
- **[CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md) (four facts that come apart).** A loose parallel, mine.
  Reappraisal is an intervention on the interpretation, and the result
  separates where the interpretation is represented (reappraisal-only and
  common appraisal regions) from where it is effective (modifiable regions)
  and where it is not (non-modifiable ones). It is correlational imaging,
  not the construction the claim rests on.
- **[TERM-073](../terms.d/TERM-073.md) (interpretation).** The paper's "reappraisal" is
  interpretation in the term's sense, a revised significance with
  consequences. Its "appraisal" regions are those engaged whether or not the
  revision is asked for.
- **[LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), [NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md), C8 (Barrett 2017).** Barrett's hypothesis was that
  reappraisal works with the same predictions that construct the emotion,
  and that emotion and regulation are brain modes, not two systems. C3
  supports the overlap. C2 is the part Barrett's hypothesis did not predict.
  [NOTE-434](NOTE-434.md) reports Barrett's Figure 4C overlap (anterior insula, vlPFC, MCC,
  SMA, posterior STS), and those are the common appraisal regions here.
- **[THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md).** Not tested: the study has one category, negative
  affect, and no variation across instances.
- **[THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md).** Not touched. The authors suggest that posterior insula and
  somatosensory regions lower with success "may be related to
  interoception" (p. 13), without a test.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Preprint, not the version of record.** The published paper may differ;
  only the abstract was compared. The supplement was not read.
- **One task, one strategy, one kind of stimulus.** Instructed downregulation
  of negative IAPS pictures, 15 trials per condition. Nothing here says
  whether other strategies, upregulation or real-life emotion behave alike.
- **"Unaffected" is a BOLD null at 2 mm voxels and region scale.** Fast or
  small changes, or changes in pattern without a change in mean, would
  not show. The authors' explanation, that subcortical processes are
  engaged before reappraisal can act, is offered, not tested.
- **The component definitions rest on the authors' three assumptions**
  (p. 23), and they say the key limitation is that "emotion appraisal may
  not be sufficiently defined by current contrast". The non-modifiable set
  is a null on one contrast, so it depends on Look Negative being an
  unregulated baseline.
- **Experience is one rating per trial.** Every claim about experience goes
  through it. C8 goes beyond it.
- **Small individual-difference effects.** The best model explains about 6%
  of the variance in success.
- **Reporting slips.** The discussion says "two large samples of over 200
  participants each" (p. 17), against 182 and 176. "Subcortical cortex"
  (p. 10). The reappraisal condition is named "Reappraise", "Regulate" and
  "Decrease" in different places. The methods give the Neurosynth topics
  as 50 and, a few sentences later, 52 (p. 27). None changes a result.

## Open questions

- Do the reappraisal-only regions do the regulating, or do they only
  accompany it? Interference with them (stimulation, lesion) during
  reappraisal, with reported affect and the modifiable regions measured,
  would tell.
- Does the subcortical null hold for other strategies, for upregulation,
  and with designs sensitive to fast or patterned change?
- Is Look Negative free of regulation? A design that estimates each
  person's unregulated response separately ([THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md)'s test) would show
  whether common appraisal regions include spontaneous regulation.
- Does the subcortical response bear on what is felt? Studies that predict
  trial-by-trial experience from amygdala or PAG activity, with and without
  reappraisal, would show whether the unchanged response is part of the
  experience or apart from it.
