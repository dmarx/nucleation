## Reading of 2026-11-16

Not read. A7 §7, that Williams and Nagel developed moral luck and that
responsibility "appears to depend heavily on factors outside our control",
is fair, though Williams's own position is, in the SEP's words, "a
notoriously difficult task" to extract. A7 §2 cites Williams for "moral
residue" through "cases in which people must make painful choices and may
appropriately experience regret regardless of which option they select".
**That conflates two works.** The dilemma remainder is "Ethical
Consistency" (1965), cited by Nagel for a dilemma that "leaves him with
nothing to do which is not wrong". What "Moral Luck" supplies is
agent-regret for harm caused without fault. That does support A's
"regret need not imply culpability" and "Someone can owe another person
recognition, sympathy, or efforts at repair without deserving blame":
agent-regret's expression includes "the willingness to compensate a person
who was harmed by one’s actions". The source A cites (turn494560search0)
could not be identified.
