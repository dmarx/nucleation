## What the readings found

- **C6 was weaker than it cited, on both halves.** Lenggenhager et al.
  moved self-location by 17–25 cm, which participants did not report
  feeling, and the ownership rating barely survived a mannequin. Kilteni et
  al. class the design as self-location, not ownership. Whole-body
  ownership is shown better by Petkova and Ehrsson, and by agreement seen
  from one's own eyes rather than by conflict. "Simplest form of
  self-awareness" is Blanke and Metzinger's proposal, true by their
  definition, and they restrict it to global ownership and deny it of the
  rubber hand. [NOTE-372](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-372.md)'s C6 strength cell is rewritten, and its Bearing
  section, [LIT-450](../../record/literature.d/LIT-450.md) and [THEORY-064](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-064.md) point here.
- **Expectancy is not separated from multisensory integration.** Lush's
  quasi-control shows that people who are told about the procedure but not
  stroked expect the illusion statements more than the control statements,
  and more under synchronous stroking. The reader found in the posted data
  that they also expect the statement-by-synchrony interaction, and more
  drift under synchronous stroking. Neither result is reported in the paper.
  None of the other works controls for this. It does not show the illusion
  is suggestion, and Lush says so.
- **Two accounts, neither tested.** Kilteni et al.'s causal inference and
  Apps and Tsakiris's free-energy self both put suggestibility or context
  into the prior, so an expectancy finding would be absorbed, not counted
  against them. In both, the self-model is revised by input the
  participant did not select, with action out of play. That bears on
  [CLAIM-342](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-342.md) and [QUESTION-121](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-121.md), and [LIT-1181](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1181.md) now says so. No claim was edited.
