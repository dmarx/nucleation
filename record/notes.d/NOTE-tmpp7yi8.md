# NOTE-tmpp7yi8: Dennett — Quining Qualia

## Contribution

The paper moves the burden of proof. Before it, functionalists answered the
absent- and inverted-qualia arguments by saying they could accommodate qualia.
Dennett says they should have asked "What qualia?" (§2). He gives a
catalogue of what qualia are supposed to be and a set of cases in which the
marks come apart. It ends with an account of why experience seems to have
such properties when it does not. It is the founding statement of qualia
eliminativism, the position later called illusionism.

## Key insight

Whatever is supposed to be the quale, as distinct from the subject's
reactions to it, sits somewhere in the causal stream between stimulus and
judgment. Any change can be placed either upstream or downstream of that
point with the same introspective and behavioural result. So nothing,
neither introspection nor physiology, fixes where the quale is. A property
whose presence nobody could detect, the subject included, cannot be the
intimately known property qualia were supposed to be.

## Assumptions

- **Method.** "Rigorous arguments only work on well-defined materials", so
  the tools are intuition pumps, not formal arguments (§1). The conclusion
  is meant to shift intuitions, not to be derived.
- **Target fixed by the tradition.** The four marks are taken from the
  literature (§2). Dennett treats Shoemaker's milder "qualitative or
  phenomenal features" definition and argues that "phenomenal" either
  means nothing definite or leads back to the four marks.
- **Functional symmetry of the cases.** The two surgeries in pump #6 and the
  two adaptation stories in pump [#8](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/8) are stipulated to produce the same
  introspective effects. The argument needs this stipulation and does not
  defend it.
- **No infallibilism.** Dennett assumes that nobody now holds subjects
  infallible about their qualia (§4), and gives two reasons it would not
  help: infallibility bought by emptying the claim of content
  (Wittgenstein, Malcolm), or qualia as logical constructs of judgments,
  which makes them theorists' fictions.
- **Elimination as tactics.** Endnote 2 says that the choice between
  eliminative and reductive materialism here is "not so much … a doctrinal
  issue as a tactical issue". The paper's "there are no qualia" should be
  read with that endnote.

## Key results

- **Intersubjective comparison is impossible even with ideal technology
  (pump #4, the Brainstorm machine).** A device feeding your experience
  into my brain must be calibrated by our reports, so it returns us to the
  evidential starting point.
- **Intrasubjective comparison is no better (pumps #5–#6).** An "evil
  neurosurgeon" can invert an early channel (qualia inverted) or the
  memory-access links that compare today's hues with those of yore (only
  reactions inverted). On waking the subject should say "either my qualia
  have been inverted or my memory-linked qualia-reactions have been
  inverted. I wonder which!" The memory link plays the role of the cable
  in pump #4.
- **Chase and Sanborn (pump [#7](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/7)).** Both tasters used to like the coffee and
  no longer do. Chase says his taste changed and the coffee tastes the
  same. Sanborn says his tasters changed and his standards did not. Each
  has three readings: one pole, the other, or a mixture. Third-person tests
  (blind retasting, physiology such as the area-18 response to illusory
  contours) can favour one pole in extreme cases. They cannot separate the
  components when the effects are small, and the subject's own judgment is
  evidentially no better than a naive observer's, "like … his own body
  temperature constancy". What he can report with authority is that the
  relation between sipping and judging changed.
- **Adaptation (pump [#8](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/8)).** After surgical inversion of taste and full
  behavioural compensation, the physiology can locate the compensation, but
  not tell whether it lies before or after "the qualia phase".
- **The dilemma (§4).** Either reactions are constitutive of the
  experienced quality (the experienced beer drinker, pump [#9](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/9); the
  cauliflower pill, pump [#11](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/11)), and qualia are relational, not intrinsic;
  or they are not, and qualia are inaccessible to their owner. "We have
  to ask Chase which way he wants to go", and the ordinary concept does not
  answer.
- **Clinical cases (§5).** Hemi-achromatopsia with insight (Damasio et al.
  1980), colour-naming failure without awareness of deficit (Geschwind and
  Fusillo 1966), complaint with residual 50% guessing (Meadows 1974), and
  achromatopsia with prosopagnosia and full insight (Damasio et al. 1980).
  The qualia vocabulary gives no rule for describing them, and Dennett
  reports that philosophers he sampled disagreed "strident[ly]" about each.
- **Replacement (§6).**
  - *Intrinsic*: no accepted definition exists (Lewis 1983 is quoted
    failing to give one). Public redness and bitterness are relational, and
    nothing requires the relation to bottom out in an intrinsic property.
    Monetary value is the analogy.
  - *Ineffable*: practically ineffable, because a first experience
    (pump [#13](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/13), the osprey cry) gives a property detector whose projection
    is untested and whose deliverance is too information-rich for a
    description, as the torn Jello box edge (pump [#14](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/14)) cannot be described
    without becoming a recipe for forging it.
  - *Private*: idiosyncrasy of discrimination profiles, empirically
    discoverable.
  - *Homogeneous or atomic*: functional invariability, close kin to
    Pylyshyn's cognitive impenetrability, and plastic with training (pump
    [#15](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/15), the guitar harmonic).
  - Bieri's "phenomenal information properties" (pips) are offered as a
    respectable successor notion, with the caveat that calling them
    "phenomenal" is unjustified.

## Concepts

- **Quining.** From *The Philosophical Lexicon*: "To deny resolutely the
  existence or importance of something real or significant." The title is
  self-consciously ironic (§1).
- **Intuition pump.** A thought experiment built to elicit an intuition; the
  paper deploys fifteen and numbers them.
- **Chase-type vs Sanborn-type change.** A change in reactive standards vs a
  change in perceptual machinery, as the two poles of any intrapersonal
  experiential shift (pump [#7](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/7)).
- **Autopsychologist.** What Chase and Sanborn are once their claims are
  testable: theorists of their own nervous systems, not introspectors with
  privileged access (§4).
- **M-detector.** A detector of a property that can be named only as "the
  property this detects", like a torn Jello-box edge for its mate (pump
  [#14](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/14)).
- **Pips.** Bieri's "phenomenal information properties": the natural meaning
  an internal perceptual event type carries (§6).

## Connections

- **Block, "Troubles with Functionalism" ([LIT-tmpc6np5](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc6np5.md), [NOTE-tmpxt8ou](NOTE-tmpxt8ou.md)).**
  Quoted twice. The jazz reply to "what are qualia?" is the presumption the
  paper sets out to overthrow, and "immediate phenomenological qualities" is
  quoted as an example of the fourth mark. The wine-tasting machine (pump
  #2) is an absent-qualia case. The paper's answer to Block is not a reply
  to the China brain but a denial that the property in dispute is one that
  anyone could detect. Endnote 1 lists Block's "Are Absent Qualia
  Impossible?" (1980), Shoemaker's reply and Conee and Davis on absent
  qualia as the current literature. None is held in the record.
- **Chalmers, "Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia" ([LIT-tmp0swyc](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0swyc.md)).**
  Both papers turn on a change in experience the subject cannot notice.
  Chalmers takes it as a reductio of absent and inverted qualia under fixed
  organisation. Dennett takes it as a reductio of qualia as properties
  separable from reactions. The comparison is mine.
- **Chalmers, "Facing Up" ([LIT-tmpbxqmy](../literature.d/LIT-tmpbxqmy.md), [NOTE-tmp0mhgd](NOTE-tmp0mhgd.md)).** Its strategy (b),
  "deny the phenomenon", names Dennett among others. This is the paper that
  strategy points to, for qualia.
- **Frankish ([LIT-103](../literature.d/LIT-103.md), [NOTE-143](NOTE-143.md)).** Cites Dennett 1988 for the post-Cartesian
  paradigm. Frankish's "access consciousness is the whole story" is
  stronger than anything this paper asserts: Dennett grants experience its
  properties and denies only the special ones.
- **Nagel ([LIT-096](../literature.d/LIT-096.md)).** Cited for "what it is like to be a bat" among the
  homely examples that tempt us toward qualia (§2).
- **Damasio.** Dennett quotes Damasio et al. (1980) on central
  achromatopsia. That clinical paper is not the Damasio work the record
  holds ([LIT-383](../literature.d/LIT-383.md) to [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)), and the record's Damasio readings do not
  discuss qualia.
- **The Mind's I.** Endnote 7 says Chase and Sanborn first appeared in
  Dennett's reflections on Smullyan in *The Mind's I* (Hofstadter and
  Dennett 1981, pp. 427–428), which another reader is filing now.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-tmp31lxe](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp31lxe.md).** The THEORY's agreed ground is that agency survives
  both directions, and its dispute is over phenomenal consciousness. This
  paper denies that there is a further property for the dispute to be
  about, in the sense Block and List use. On that view the anti-nesting
  question is a question about capacities, and the "two ways to refuse the
  upward move" are refusals of something the paper says is not there. This
  is not support for the THEORY's promotion: the paper offers no criterion
  for separating a modular mind from a nation. It does make one of the
  THEORY's framings, that the dispute "narrows" to experience, a
  contested premise worth naming in its text. My inference; the paper
  says nothing about groups.
- **[THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) (AI consciousness).** Not discussed by the paper. Pump #2,
  the wine-tasting machine, is its only machine, and Dennett's point is
  that the intuition it pumps is the target, not evidence.
- **No new THEORY.** A THEORY that "the absent-qualia intuition has no
  subject-accessible content" would need the record to hold the replies
  (Shoemaker 1981, Block 1980), which it does not.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Intuition pumps.** Each case is stipulated, not built. Pump #6 needs the
  two surgeries to produce identical introspective effects, which a defender
  of qualia can deny.
- **Strawman risk.** Dennett anticipates the charge (§2) and argues against
  Shoemaker's milder definition, but only by asking what "phenomenal"
  means. Later phenomenal realists use definitions by example (as Schwitzgebel
  does in [LIT-191](../literature.d/LIT-191.md), read in [NOTE-104](NOTE-104.md)) that the paper does not address.
- **Clinical cases.** Short quoted excerpts; the paper does not analyse them
  beyond showing that the qualia vocabulary yields competing descriptions.
- **Endnote 2 softens the conclusion.** Elimination and reduction are said to
  differ in tactics, not doctrine. Read with it, C8 is a recommendation about
  vocabulary.

## Open questions

- Can the indistinguishability stipulation of pump #6 be shown or refuted for
  a real system, say by a model in which "early" and "memory-comparison"
  stages are both specified? The paper treats it as obvious.
- Is Bieri's pip notion a candidate for what the absent-qualia debate should
  have been about? The paper half-endorses it and drops it.
- Does the argument extend to experience in general, or only to qualia as the
  four marks define them? The paper says only the latter (§1); later
  illusionism says more.
