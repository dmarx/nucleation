# THEORY-217: A trait malfunctions when its operation does not correlate positively with the normative field, the minimum change its organization's viability requires at each precarious state; malfunction needs no second order of regulatory norms, and regulatory traits can malfunction by the same standard

## Source

Barandiaran (2025), [LIT-1026](../literature.d/LIT-1026.md), read in [NOTE-730](../notes.d/NOTE-730.md): §3 (the
dual-order account and its problems), §4.1–4.4 (regions, the normative
field, the model, the three kinds of dysfunction), §4.5 (attribution and
compensation), §5.1–5.2 (norms without orders, illness as a shrunken
viability space), note 23 (nested autonomies).

## What was actually shown

The paper argues from definitions, counterexamples and a one-variable model
of a metabolite fed by food. It measures nothing.

**Against the dual-order account.** As quoted from Moreno & Mossio (2015,
[LIT-536](../literature.d/LIT-536.md)), a trait malfunctions when it fits first-order norms but fails the
second-order regulation that shifts the system to another regime. That
definition cannot count a stiffening coronary artery at rest, since no
regime switch is involved. It cannot count an autonomic nervous system that
fails to trigger the stress response, since only the regulated trait can
malfunction. And it cannot tell which of the two failed. Barandiaran traces
this to two assumptions: that first-order norms are all-or-nothing, and
that regulation happens only as switching between regimes. Physiological
regulation is mostly continuous modulation of rates.

**The replacement.** Essential variables span a viability space. Holding a
parameter fixed, the space divides into a viable region (the system stays
alive), a precarious region ("(still) alive but will inevitably die if no
parametric change occurs") and a terminal region (beyond recovery). The
normative field is "the minimum constant parametric change, for each point
of the precarious region, necessary to avoid the irreversible
disintegration of the system". A trait functions when "its effects on the
viability space correlate positively with the normative field". The three
failures are subfunction (right direction, too slow), malfunction (wrong or
no direction) and nonfunction (no operation or no effect where operation is
required). Regulatory traits fall under the same standard, and a regime
switch becomes an added dimension of the viability space. One can still be
"a dualist on organizational orders ... but a monist regarding norms".

**Attribution.** Identifying the unmet norm is not locating the failing
part. The paper sketches a recursive search over each trait's feasible
range, and argues that compensation does not hide a dysfunction, because
"compensations have a cost on the viability of the system".

## What this does not say

- **It does not compute a field for any real system.** The author says
  doing so "is extraordinarily complex and may not be feasible".
- **It does not derive the essential variables.** They are given, as K is
  given in viability theory ([THEORY-134](THEORY-134.md)). Which states count as death is an
  input.
- **It is not Aubin's viability theory.** The paper works with Ashby's
  essential variables and bifurcation diagrams. Its "viable region" is
  where the system survives with the parameter held constant, not a viable
  set. Read in Aubin's terms, the terminal region is outside the viability
  kernel of the controlled system, and the precarious region is inside it
  but not invariant at the current parameter. That correspondence is the
  record's.
- **It does not say what ought to persist.** The norms are for the
  organism, relative to its persistence. [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) stands.
- **It does not refute the organizational account of function.** It is an
  organizational account. It disputes only one definition of malfunction,
  taken from the 2015 book. The 2009 paper ([LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md)) is cited as part of
  the standard approach; its dysfunction is a produced trait that fails to
  contribute, and it is not the dual-order definition.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-087](../questions.d/QUESTION-087.md) (answers, in part).** The question asks whether a
  self-reproducing authority structure can be pathological by a standard
  the organization itself supplies. This account supplies one: the
  normative field of the whole organization. Since regulatory traits can
  malfunction, an authority structure, a regulation of other processes, can
  be judged by whether its operations correlate with that field. Two further
  results widen the net. Illness can be a shrinking of the viability space
  rather than a failure to contribute (§5.2), and protective lock-in's
  fifth step ([TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)), reduced capacity to leave the regime, is harm of
  that kind on the record's mapping. And compensation shows up as cost.
  The answer is partial. An authority that perpetuates itself while leaving
  the organization as viable as before is not dysfunctional on this
  account, so it does not separate merited trust from entrenched advantage
  ([CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)) where both keep the organization alive equally well.
  And note 23 allows that an entrenched authority may become an autonomous
  level with its own norms in conflict with the organization's, without
  saying which prevails.
- **[QUESTION-086](../questions.d/QUESTION-086.md) (no relation declared).** The attribution procedure,
  varying one trait within its feasible range, is an intervention design.
  It locates where a norm fails. It does not test whether the norm is the
  system's own, which is what that question needs.
- **[CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md) and [THEORY-134](THEORY-134.md).** A system in the terminal region is alive
  with no trajectory left that keeps it so; that is [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)'s gap
  between occupying a state and keeping access to viable futures, in the
  record's reading.
- **Di Paolo (2005, [LIT-1017](../literature.d/LIT-1017.md)).** The precarious region is where Di Paolo's
  adaptivity acts. This account turns his graded inner norm into a field
  that a part can be judged against.
