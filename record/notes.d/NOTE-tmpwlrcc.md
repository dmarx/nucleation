# NOTE-tmpwlrcc: Cook — How Complex Systems Fail

## Contribution

A compact statement of the view of failure that the "new look" in safety
science, and later blameless review, rests on. Failure is joint, its
attribution to a single cause is a social act, and practitioners both
produce and defend, adapting continuously to keep a flawed system working.

## Key results

Points by number; page of the PDF.

- **Multiple failures** (3, p. 1): "Each of these small failures is
  necessary to cause catastrophe but only the combination is sufficient to
  permit failure."
- **Running broken** (5, p. 1): "The system continues to function because
  it contains so many redundancies and because people can make it
  function, despite the presence of many flaws." "After accident reviews
  nearly always note that the system has a history of prior
  'proto-accidents' that nearly generated catastrophe."
- **No root cause** (7, p. 2): "there is no isolated 'cause' of an
  accident. There are multiple contributors to accidents." Evaluations by
  root cause "do not reflect a technical understanding of the nature of
  failure but rather the social, cultural need to blame specific, localized
  forces or events for outcomes."
- **Hindsight** (8, p. 2): "Hindsight bias remains the primary obstacle to
  accident investigation, especially when expert human performance is
  involved."
- **Two roles, seen at different times** (9, p. 2): practitioners produce
  and defend; "Outsiders rarely acknowledge the duality of this role. In
  non-accident filled times, the production role is emphasized. After
  accidents, the defense against failure role is emphasized."
- **The sharp end** (11, p. 3): "Organizations are ambiguous, often
  intentionally, about the relationship between production targets,
  efficient use of resources, economy and costs of operations, and
  acceptable risks of low and high consequence accidents. All ambiguity is
  resolved by actions of practitioners at the sharp end of the system."
  Afterwards their actions "may be regarded as 'errors' or 'violations'
  but these evaluations are heavily biased by hindsight and ignore the
  other driving forces, especially production pressure."
- **Remedies** (15, pp. 3–4): "Post-accident remedies for "human error"
  are usually predicated on obstructing activities that can "cause"
  accidents. These end-of-the-chain measures do little to reduce the
  likelihood of further accidents", and "usually increase the coupling and
  complexity of the system."
- **Safety is made** (17, p. 4): "People continuously create safety."
  These activities are "for the most part, part of normal operations and
  superficially straightforward."

## Assumptions

Intrinsically hazardous, heavily defended systems; the examples are
transportation, healthcare and power generation. The points are asserted
and argued from the author's field experience; footnote 1 cites
anthropology for "the social construction of the notion of 'cause'". No
data are given.

## Concepts

- **Sharp end**: the practitioners in direct contact with the hazard.
- **Latent failures**: flaws present in normal operation, each
  insufficient.
- **Proto-accidents**: near-catastrophes in a system's history.
- **End-of-the-chain measures**: remedies that block the last act in a
  failure trajectory.

## Bearing on the record

Filed as THEORY-tmpl5x2t.

- **CLAIM-707** (control upstream, duty downstream, blame at visibility).
  Point 11 states the control-and-duty part from safety science:
  organizations leave the trade between production and risk unresolved,
  "often intentionally", and the sharp end resolves it, after which
  hindsight calls the resolution error. Corroborates, by argument rather
  than data.
- **CLAIM-702** (prevention leaves little trace while its lapse is
  visible). Points 5, 9 and 17: the system works "because people can make
  it function"; the defending role is emphasized only "After accidents".
  Corroborates the visibility half. It says nothing about credit going to
  the organization.
- **CLAIM-733** and **QUESTION-476.** Point 7 makes blame a poor carrier of
  provenance: the need to blame looks for "specific, localized" causes,
  which is the opposite of tracing a failure upstream. Blameless review
  starts from this point. It does not show that tracing without blame
  reaches upstream either.
- **CLAIM-tmpx4kjt** and **CLAIM-tmp5g7xc**: point 11's "often
  intentionally" names the upstream decisions a review would have to
  trace; point 7 says blame localizes. Neither claim rests on Cook.
- **CLAIM-719** (the note of 2026-11-18): point 15 warns that completing
  remedies is not good in itself; end-of-the-chain measures add coupling.
- **CLAIM-700** (the note of 2026-11-18): point 5's proto-accidents are
  what near-miss reporting would record.

## Limitations

- A treatise, not a study. Every point is asserted.
- Written for medicine and other hazardous systems, not for software
  operations, though software practice cites it.
- It says how failure is attributed; it says nothing about what a review
  should do instead, or about who in an organization should answer.
