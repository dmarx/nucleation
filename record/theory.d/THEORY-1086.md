# THEORY-1086: A fundamental metaphysics bears on the metaphysics of the lifeworld only through features that admit no approximation; without any, a relational world can approximate one of separable individuals, and the independence of social theorizing from fundamental metaphysics is then real but contingent, on the size of Planck's constant, not principled

## Source

Jaksland (2024), [LIT-1673](../literature.d/LIT-1673.md), pp. 373–377; read in [NOTE-1230](../notes.d/NOTE-1230.md).

## What was actually shown

**The challenge.** Newtonian mechanics approximates quantum mechanics well
at lifeworld scales and lacks entanglement, so a quantum metaphysics seems
idle there (p. 374). Barad agrees the effects are small (2007, 279) and
denies the conclusion: that h/m is not zero "is the key point" (2007, 416).

**The reconstruction.** For that to work, the features agential realism
denies must be all-or-nothing: "If an ontology lacks/contains features that
cannot appear/disappear as the result of approximation then these features
will be absent/present throughout the ontology regardless of scale"
(p. 376). Candidates: the subject–object and word–world dualisms,
representationalism, "(joint carving) notions". The argument for the
dualisms: adding particles to an electron does not reinstall the
subject–object split, and negligible entanglement "is nevertheless still
there" (p. 376).

**The other side.** Individualism "could similarly be regarded as a
question of all or nothing", yet "it seems immediately more agreeable to
regard this as something that can obtain through approximation.
Circumstances can be such that a fundamentally relational metaphysics can
appear to be one of individuals (if they are separable enough)" (p. 376).
A no-miracles argument supports approximation in general: Newtonian
success would be a miracle if Newtonian metaphysics were not approximately
true (pp. 376–377).

**Contingency.** With a much larger Planck constant quantum effects would
pervade "situatedness and practice", and the autonomy of philosophical
anthropology from naturalized metaphysics "would have been lost in both
directions" (p. 374). So the division Ladyman and Ross, Barnes and Mikkola
draw is "a contingent matter of fact" (p. 375). This half is argued.

## What this does not say

- That any feature resists approximation. "Whether there are such
  features remains, in my view, an open question" (p. 365).
- What metaphysical approximation is. The paper gives the Newtonian
  analogy and no account of an ontological feature being approximately
  present.
- That an ontology of individuals is approximately true. That is the
  author's inclination, stated, not argued beyond the analogy.
- That the result needs agential realism. The conditional holds for any
  relational fundamental metaphysics; agential realism is the case.
