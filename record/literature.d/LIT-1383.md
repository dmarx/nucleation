# LIT-1383: A Theory of Structure: Duality, Agency, and Transformation

Sewell, W. H., Jr. (1992). A theory of structure: Duality, agency, and transformation. *American Journal of Sociology*, 98(1), 1–29. doi:10.1086/229967.

Read in full on 2026-10-22 ([NOTE-tmpfw64c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfw64c.md)). No THEORY was filed: the
framework is conceptual and its one empirical conjecture is illustrated
only.

## Why this is here

Cited from A30 on as an account of how structures are reproduced and transformed through activity, which the perspective view must build on. Bears on [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** The exchange's use is accurate. The
reading also bears on [CLAIM-416](../claims.d/CLAIM-416.md): the polysemy of resources makes what a
success shows contestable, and the reading that prevails redistributes
power (the cavalry charge, p. 19). And on [CLAIM-423](../claims.d/CLAIM-423.md) and [CASE-133](../cases.d/CASE-133.md): the
United States kept one constitution through "at least five sharply
distinct party systems", and Sewell conjectures that political structures
last by depth, not power or speed (p. 24). Its critique of habitus is the
one Wacquant ([LIT-1377](LIT-1377.md)) dismisses; the record cites both, and they
disagree. See [NOTE-tmpfw64c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfw64c.md).
