# LEAD-tmpl9349: Build four systems matched on the ordinary course, mere coupling, unilateral dependence, coordinated adaptation and maintained conditional commitment, and test whether the probes for implicit contracts part them as predicted

## What it would do

Build four systems of two or three processes, matched on the ordinary
course (the same behaviour when nothing goes wrong):

1. **Mere coupling**: the assistant's two controllers sharing a room, with a
   deadband and a fixed rule that heating yields ([CLAIM-tmp7hqtl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7hqtl.md)'s note).
2. **Unilateral dependence**: A20's dependency model ([CLAIM-tmp18v1o](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp18v1o.md)).
3. **Coordinated adaptation**: two processes that each give up a capacity
   the other supplies.
4. **Maintained conditional commitment**: the arbitration rules of
   [CASE-tmptpna1](../cases.d/CASE-tmptpna1.md), with a mechanism that revises them.

Apply [CLAIM-tmpersgx](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpersgx.md)'s six probes to each: interruption, a substitute
provider, competing demands, removal of an enforcer, withdrawal of a
discretionary contribution, a request to renegotiate.

## Expectations, stated before any run

- Under interruption, mere coupling and unilateral dependence show no
  correction, only a fall in functioning.
- Coordinated adaptation shows both parties' functioning fall.
- Maintained conditional commitment recruits correction toward its terms.

## What would count against

- The probes do not part the four systems, or part them as well on the
  ordinary course.
- The two controllers respond to a change of terms with the processes kept
  exactly as a maintained commitment does. [CLAIM-tmp7hqtl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7hqtl.md)'s filter would
  then have no test behind it.
