# THEORY-655: Social behaviour evolves as if each individual valued a neighbour's fitness against its own by their coefficient of relationship, and its discriminations evolve to make that coefficient determinate; so kin are favoured and strangers met with hostility, relatives fight less, and haplodiploid sisters, related by 3/4, are predisposed to worker altruism

## Source

Hamilton (1964), "The Genetical Evolution of Social Behaviour. II", [LIT-506](../literature.d/LIT-506.md)
(*Journal of Theoretical Biology* 7: 17–52), read in full in [NOTE-410](../notes.d/NOTE-410.md). It
rests on Part I ([LIT-497](../literature.d/LIT-497.md), [NOTE-401](../notes.d/NOTE-401.md)). Fractions garbled in extraction are
taken from the prose.

## What was actually shown

**The first principle (§3).** "The social behaviour of a species evolves in
such a way that in each distinct behaviour-evoking situation the individual
will seem to value his neighbours' fitness against his own according to the
coefficients of relationship appropriate to that situation." It is offered
as a "generalized unrigorous statement" of Part I. The model is not
extended mathematically.

**The second principle (§4).** The situations a species discriminates "tend
to evolve and multiply in such a way that the coefficients of relationship
involved in each situation become more nearly determinate". Herring gulls
accept strange chicks for the first days and reject them by the end of the
first week, when chicks begin to wander. Ground-nesting Sooty terns learn
their young within four days, and tree-nesting Noddies never do. Strangers
are expected to be attacked or "relegated to the lowest social rank".

**Hymenoptera (§5).** Full sisters by a singly mated mother are related by
3/4, more than a mother to a daughter. That predisposes workers to
altruism. Multiple mating lowers it: with n equally contributing drones the
mean sister relationship is ¼ + 1/(2n), about 0.31 for the honeybee's
n ≈ 8 ([NOTE-410](../notes.d/NOTE-410.md) computes this from the formula the prose describes).

**Fights (§6).** Sibs should fight to the death only if the winner gains
more than 50%. Hymenopteran full sisters need more than 75%. Gregarious
parasitoid larvae, which are siblings or clones, do not fight, and solitary
ones kill rivals.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The natural history could have
shown no link between relatedness and these behaviours. The author reports
cases where it did not fit and explains them away (§7).

## What this does not say

- **That the generalization is proved.** It is an analogy with the
  classical theory, "about as good" as that theory's own principles (§2).
- **That haplodiploidy explains hymenopteran sociality.** The author reports
  multiple mating in *Apis* and *Atta* and Michener's Australian halictines
  as "against our hypothesis", and calls the evidence "somewhat contrary".
- **That anomalies were tested.** §7 handles each by error or
  misinterpretation, with no criterion that could count against the
  principles ([NOTE-410](../notes.d/NOTE-410.md), C5).
- **Anything about display or deference in contests.** §6 concerns
  restraint between relatives, not signalling.
- **Anything about morality.** Fisher's attempt to explain "the heroic
  ideal in barbaric human societies" is reported and not pursued.

## Connections

- It extends Part I ([THEORY-408](THEORY-408.md)), whose model it generalizes.
- Maynard Smith & Price ([LIT-505](../literature.d/LIT-505.md), [THEORY-466](THEORY-466.md)) explain restraint in
  fights between unrelated animals by retaliation. This explains it between
  relatives by relatedness. Their remark that very dangerous weapons "would
  be opposed by kin selection" is where the two meet.
- Trivers ([LIT-510](../literature.d/LIT-510.md), [THEORY-663](THEORY-663.md)) disputes the kin explanation of alarm
  calls given here and offers a self-interested one.
- The second principle is the biological root of the in-group partiality
  that morality-as-cooperation files under family and group loyalty
  ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md)). Hamilton's mechanism is relatedness, though, and that
  account's group domain rests on shared payoffs among non-kin ([NOTE-410](../notes.d/NOTE-410.md)).
- The clones-and-colonies section is an early case of the units-of-selection
  question that Lloyd's entry ([LIT-160](../literature.d/LIT-160.md)) treats.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

Read in full ([NOTE-410](../notes.d/NOTE-410.md)), but its promote_when is unmet. The record holds
no comparative test with relatedness measured and predictions fixed in
advance, whether of kin discrimination, of escalation against measured r,
or of worker altruism against measured sister relatedness. Hamilton's
evidence is anecdotal, and he reports multiple mating and the Australian
halictines as running against the hymenopteran hypothesis.
