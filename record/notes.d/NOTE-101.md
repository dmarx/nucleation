# NOTE-101: Luque — Philosophical analysis of the Price equation

## Contribution

Luque analyses the Price equation's role in evolutionary theory. Traditional population-genetic models build in simplifying assumptions to stay tractable, whereas the Price equation starts from assumptions taken to be true and derives the system's mathematical rules. He argues it is a generalization-sketch whose main job is to give researchers a unifying framework for building specific models, a role it can play because, like other scientific principles, it is abstract, unifying and invariant. Seeing it this way defuses recent disputes about it.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1: frames the controversy — critics (van Veelen, Nowak) treat the equation as a mere identity, not a model; users employ it productively. Luque says both are right.
- §2: population genetics' traditional method — Hardy–Weinberg baselines, simplifying assumptions, diffusion theory.
- §4: Kuhn's "generalization-sketch" (f = ma taking different forms per problem) and the structuralist notion of a theory-net; §§4.1–4.3 argue the Price equation has abstractness, unification and invariance.
- §5: Hamilton's rule as a test case — Birch's special (game-theoretic) vs general (Price-derived) versions; the generalization-sketch reading shows why "almost never holds" and "always holds" talk past each other.
- §§6–7: the Price equation embodies a "fundamentalist" or axiomatic approach, and its kinship with Newton's second law vindicates the forces analogy in evolutionary theory against its critics.
