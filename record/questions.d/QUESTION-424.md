# QUESTION-424: Does a replication of Knack's cross-country estimates, with better-justified instruments and reported first stages, or with country panels and fixed effects, again find that higher aid goes with smaller gains in governance, more so for technical assistance, not depending on initial governance and less so where aid is variable?

## Why it is a question

Knack's estimates rest on one change score per country, instruments whose
exclusion is defended only by overidentification tests, and no reported
first stage ([THEORY-1517](../theory.d/THEORY-1517.md), [NOTE-1707](../notes.d/NOTE-1707.md)). Whether the association holds under
other identification, and whether it is causal, is open.

## What would answer it

An independent test of the same result, as the condition asked. The
condition as it stood on [THEORY-1517](../theory.d/THEORY-1517.md): A replication, with these instruments
or better-justified ones and with first stages reported, on the published
2001 data or an extended ICRG panel, again finds a negative, significant
coefficient of mean aid on the change in governance, a larger coefficient
for technical assistance than for aid overall, a null interaction of aid
with initial governance, and a positive interaction of aid with its
variability. A replication in which the aid coefficient becomes
insignificant once country panels with fixed effects are used would refute
it as a causal reading. Evidence on tax effort or on what follows the
withdrawal of aid does not bear on it.
