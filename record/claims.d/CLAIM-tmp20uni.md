# CLAIM-tmp20uni: On the Tempe failure the criminal process, the process built to assign blame, charged and convicted only the operator and declined to charge the company, while the investigation barred from assigning blame named the company's safety culture and the state's oversight and addressed its recommendations to them

## The claim

Two processes took up the Tempe crash of 18 March 2018.

- **The investigation**, barred by rule from assigning blame and by
  statute from use in damages suits, made the operator's distraction the
  probable cause and named as contributing Uber ATG's risk assessment,
  oversight of operators and lack of countermeasures to automation
  complacency, "all a consequence of its inadequate safety culture", and
  the Arizona Department of Transportation's oversight. Its
  recommendations went to NHTSA, Arizona, AAMVA and Uber ATG, none to the
  operator ([CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md), [THEORY-1932](../theory.d/THEORY-1932.md)).
- **The criminal process** found "no basis for criminal liability for the
  Uber corporation" (March 2019), charged the operator with negligent
  homicide (September 2020), and took her guilty plea to endangerment,
  with "three years of supervised probation" (July 2023). No one else was
  charged in the reports read ([THEORY-tmpfdyu6](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfdyu6.md)).

So on one failure, the process whose business is blame stopped at the
person nearest the event, and the process forbidden to blame carried the
account upward. In court, the defence put the investigation's account:
"Uber should share some blame for the collision". The prosecution put the
other: "The defendant had one job and one job only".

## Where it sits

- **[CLAIM-751](CLAIM-751.md)** complements it. That claim shows, on this failure, that
  provenance can be carried without blame. Its first defeater asks
  whether blame-based processes on the same failures reach upstream
  actors that no-blame investigations do not. On this failure the
  criminal one did not. One failure does not defeat or confirm a rule
  about investigations "as a rule"; it is the instance the defeater names,
  turning the other way.
- **[CLAIM-707](CLAIM-707.md)** is corroborated by it, in one institution. The control
  over the conditions of action lay upstream ([CASE-292](../cases.d/CASE-292.md): Uber removed the
  second operator and the Volvo's own braking), and the criminal law's
  blame lay where failure became visible. The operator's own fault, which
  the NTSB found and she admitted in part by her plea, is within what
  [CLAIM-707](CLAIM-707.md) allows.
- **[CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md)** (the owner's: blame supplies provenance for holding
  upstream actors accountable) is not denied by it. The criminal law is
  one practice of blame, and here it carried no provenance upstream. That
  bears on whether blame usually does, which [CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md) does not assert.

## What it does not say

- That the company was free of every consequence: the victim's family
  settled with it, and the governor suspended its testing. Those are not
  the criminal process ([CLAIM-tmp64hmn](CLAIM-tmp64hmn.md)).
- Why the prosecutors declined to charge the company. The reasons are not
  in the reports read.
- That the operator was blameless. The claim is about where the processes
  stopped, not about whether she was at fault.
