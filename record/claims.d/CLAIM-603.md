# CLAIM-603: A capability lost because another reliably supplies it is the Black Queen's loss, favoured to the one who loses it, and whether it is dependency or division of labour is decided by the supply, not the loss: one-way supply set from outside, as aid is, leaves a dependence that nothing in the dependent's dynamics limits and that withdrawal punishes, while reciprocal loss, each partner supplying what the other gave up, leaves interdependence, so the aim framed as self-sufficiency is better framed as reciprocity

## The same loss in three places

- **Free-living microbes.** On the Black Queen Hypothesis ([THEORY-1048](../theory.d/THEORY-1048.md),
  [NOTE-1212](../notes.d/NOTE-1212.md)), a cell that stops detoxifying peroxide saves the cost and is
  still protected while enough others keep doing it. Loss is favoured by
  ordinary selection on individuals and runs until helpers are rare enough
  that "the benefit of any further loss is negated by its cost". It looks
  like a division of labour, and the paper says it is not one.
- **Endosymbionts.** Buchnera ([THEORY-1103](../theory.d/THEORY-1103.md)) has lost almost all the
  amino-acid genes its aphid host covers and kept those the host lacks.
  Its metabolism interlocks with the host's.
- **Aid.** In Ostrom et al.'s Samaritan's dilemma ([NOTE-1647](../notes.d/NOTE-1647.md)), help
  that is assured makes low effort the recipient's best reply, and the
  recipient "loses skill and motivation over time". In Azam, Devarajan and
  O'Connell's model ([NOTE-1682](../notes.d/NOTE-1682.md)) the recipient "is actually better off"
  in the high-aid, low-capacity state. The loss is favoured to the one who
  loses, as in the microbes.

## The claim

The loss is the same in all three, and it is adaptive while the supply
holds. What makes it dependency or division of labour is the supply.

1. **Who supplies, and what limits the loss.** Black Queen helpers are
   members of the same population, so the loss is frequency dependent and
   stops by itself. Aid is set by a donor's decisions and does not fall as
   the recipient's capacity does. Nothing in the recipient's dynamics stops
   the loss short of what aid covers: in [CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md) capacity falls with
   aid's share to none at full substitution. Moss, Pettersson and van de
   Walle ([NOTE-1661](../notes.d/NOTE-1661.md)) report aid ratios that "have tended not to recede,
   but to grow larger over three decades".
2. **What withdrawal costs.** While the supply holds, the loss is a saving.
   When it stops, the loss is a deficit that must be relearned, at a cost
   that grows with how long the supply ran ([CLAIM-602](CLAIM-602.md)). Aid that is
   meant to be temporary and is not reliable is the case in which a
   favoured loss is most costly.
3. **One-way or reciprocal.** Black Queen dependence runs one way, from
   beneficiaries to helpers, with no collective ([THEORY-1048](../theory.d/THEORY-1048.md)). So does aid.
   Buchnera's runs both ways, since each side has lost what the other
   makes. Reciprocal loss gives interdependence, the mutual dependence of
   West et al.'s transitions in individuality ([THEORY-1351](../theory.d/THEORY-1351.md)). A one-sided
   loss gives a dependant whose supplier needs nothing from it, so the
   supply is neither assured nor bargained over. Moore's tax state
   ([NOTE-1663](../notes.d/NOTE-1663.md)) has the reciprocal form: revenue is exchanged for
   service, and the bargaining over it is where his accountability comes
   from.

So the aid literature's goal of self-sufficiency asks the organization to
regrow every capability it gave up. The specialization reading suggests a
cheaper goal: lose what others supply, provided the organization supplies
something they need in return. Ostrom et al.'s requirement that
beneficiaries contribute their own assets and share the decision to
continue is a step from the one-way form toward the reciprocal one.

## What the gift readings add

Read after the owner said the claim's reciprocal goal reminded them of
the "gift economy" of Burning Man and adjacent communities.

- **One-way supply costs standing.** Mauss ([NOTE-1658](../notes.d/NOTE-1658.md)) names three
  obligations, to give, to receive and to return. "Le don non rendu rend
  encore inférieur celui qui l'a accepté": the gift not returned still
  makes the one who accepted it inferior. To accept without returning, or
  without returning more, "c'est se subordonner, devenir client et
  serviteur". Heins, Unrau and Avram ([NOTE-1679](../notes.d/NOTE-1679.md)) bring this to aid
  through Dillon's study of the Marshall Plan. The French "felt the
  obligation to repay gifts but did not have the possibility to fulfill
  their obligation and thereby to become accepted as equals". The claim's
  cost of one-way supply was capacity. The gift readings add a cost in
  rank that does not end when the aid does.
- **Reciprocity need not be bilateral.** The claim's examples, Buchnera and
  the fiscal contract, return to the supplier. The gift readings have two
  further forms.
  - **Circular.** In Mauss the gift travels through third parties and
    comes back: the hau "veut revenir au lieu de sa naissance", it wants to
    return to its place of origin. Heins et al. call the kula's
    reciprocity "circular, not antagonistic" and its returns "indirect and
    delayed".
  - **Generalized.** Burning Man's own statement of its Gifting principle
    ([LIT-2175](../literature.d/LIT-2175.md), Unavailable, read through St John, [NOTE-1662](../notes.d/NOTE-1662.md)) is
    that "The value of a gift is unconditional. Gifting does not
    contemplate a return or an exchange for something of equal value." St
    John reads this as Sahlins's "generalized reciprocity", with "an
    absence of an obligation on the part of the recipient to reciprocate".
    The gratitude it inspires "keeps the gift in motion", and the return
    goes to the community through later work, not back to the giver.
  What separates these from aid is not reciprocity with the giver. It is
  whether the receiver has any path by which to give.
- **Gifts sit beside self-reliance.** Burning Man's principles put Radical
  Self-reliance beside Gifting, and St John notes that participants bring
  their own supplies. Mauss's conclusion says the same of modern societies:
  "Il faut qu'il soit forcé de compter sur soi plutôt que sur les autres",
  the individual must be made to count on himself rather than on others.
  For him, too much generosity harms as much as egoism. Both keep some
  capabilities self-provided and let the rest circulate. That is the line
  this claim left open: which capabilities can safely be given up.
- **Free riding is limited by the community.** St John's free riders are
  spectators, tourists and camps that sell turnkey participation. The
  organization answered in part by reforming how theme camps are
  registered and placed. That is a limit set by the givers' own community,
  where Black Queen loss is limited by helpers growing scarce and aid by
  nothing in the recipient's dynamics.

## What the counterpoint readings show

Read after the owner asked for a counterpoint, "e.g. the myth of the lazy poor".
- **The Samaritan's-dilemma response is not found in poor households.** Cash transfers barely change work (Banerjee, Hanna, Kreindler and Olken, [NOTE-1685](../notes.d/NOTE-1685.md); Baird, McKenzie and Özler, [NOTE-1684](../notes.d/NOTE-1684.md)). Graduation packages raise work ([NOTE-1669](../notes.d/NOTE-1669.md)). The poorest Bangladeshi women work the most hours (Balboni et al., [NOTE-1654](../notes.d/NOTE-1654.md)).
- **Where the response does appear.** It appears where help is large, permanent and relieves no binding constraint, as with pensions. Ostrom et al.'s repeated donor rehabilitation of state assets is that setting.
- **The scope narrows to match.** The claim's three places of loss stand, but the third is an organizational case. It should not be read down to households.
- **Reciprocity at the level of public services.** In coproduced sewers, the agency's continued part is what kept residents' contribution working (Watson, [NOTE-1659](../notes.d/NOTE-1659.md); Evans, [NOTE-1665](../notes.d/NOTE-1665.md)). That is the reciprocal form at the level of public services.

## What it adds

- **To [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md).** The question lists routes to dependence among
  formerly autonomous partners: selected loss, drift, complementary loss,
  gene transfer. Aid adds a route the question's readings lack: loss under
  supply from a provider outside the population, which neither
  selection's frequency dependence nor reciprocity limits.
- **To [CLAIM-554](CLAIM-554.md).** That claim says specialization can relieve frustration
  where nothing competes. This one adds when specialization is safe: when
  what is given up is matched by something supplied back.
- **To [QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md).** Independence from the founding intervention need not
  mean self-sufficiency. A partner can stay dependent on its supplier for
  good, as Buchnera does, and still be secure, if the supplier depends on
  it as well.

## What it does not say

- **That reciprocity makes a partnership robust.** Interdependence makes
  each partner's survival hang on the other's. An aphid without Buchnera
  fares badly too. Reciprocity changes who bears a withdrawal and whether
  the supply is bargained over. It does not remove the risk.
- **That aid recipients are like microbes in mechanism.** The microbes'
  loss is genetic and selected. The recipient's is learned, and it is
  chosen, as in the Samaritan's dilemma. The claim is about the shape of
  the dependence each produces, not about mechanism.
- **That a state can trade what it gave up.** Some capabilities, such as
  raising its own revenue, may be ones no partner can supply without
  taking over what the state is for. Mauss and Burning Man both keep a
  self-provided core, but neither says how to decide what belongs in it.
- **That generalized reciprocity is secure.** It holds only while enough
  members keep giving, and St John's account is interpretive, with no
  measure of how many return gifts or free-ride. Mauss's own circuits close
  back on their origin, so his work does not show that open-ended giving
  forward is stable.

## Note of 2026-11-05: the readings on assistance and skill

Two qualifications on incentives. Koedinger and Aleven's tutors put a designed cost on asking for help, and some learners game the system anyway ([NOTE-tmp607yf](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp607yf.md)). Macnamara et al. cite a crossover near 70 % automation accuracy, below which relying on the aid stops paying ([NOTE-tmpfgu7k](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfgu7k.md)): the favoured loss depends on how reliable the supply is.
