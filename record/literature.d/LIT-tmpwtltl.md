# LIT-tmpwtltl: Serious Problems With Interpreting Rubber Hand “Illusion” Experiments

Roseboom, W. & Lush, P. (2022). Serious problems with interpreting rubber hand "illusion" experiments. *Collabra: Psychology* 8(1), 32274. DOI 10.1525/collabra.32274. Preprint: PsyArXiv, DOI 10.31234/osf.io/uhdzs.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpj0dh8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpj0dh8.md).*

- **The relationship is large enough to decide outcomes.** The paper
  draws simulated studies of 20 from the 351 reports in Lush et al. 2020,
  splitting them at the median of subjective hypnotisability. Studies drawn
  from the upper half found the illusion (S1–S3 mean significantly above
  zero) 99% of the time, and studies from the lower half 51%. The authors
  conclude that the correlation (rs = 0.26) is not "too small to be of
  concern".
- **Ownership needs the suggestible.** For the one ownership statement
  (S3), lower-half studies found significant agreement about 4% of the
  time. Their p-values were roughly uniform, "the same as the probability
  of obtaining any other extent of 0.05".
- **Referred touch is robust, and the measure is muddled.** S1 was
  agreed with in 95% of lower-half studies. The authors argue that S1 is
  ambiguous and that only S3 concerns ownership. They also note that 12%
  of participants agreed with S1 while disagreeing with S2, though both
  are meant to measure referred touch.
- **Synchronous against asynchronous is set aside.** Following Lush
  (2020), the authors treat the difference as confounded by expectation
  and analyse only the synchronous condition.
- **Three readings, one preferred.** The same people may control their
  experience in the RHI as elsewhere (the authors' preferred reading).
  They may have the RHI for distinct reasons, such as pure multisensory
  integration ("not explicitly ruled out"). Or they may respond as if they
  did for some other reason. The first reading does not exclude a
  multisensory contribution.
- **Implicit and animal measures.** These are linked to ownership only
  through report, so the paper calls the inference from drift,
  neuroimaging or a "rubber tail" in mice to subjective ownership a
  reverse inference with "no direct evidence".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request for experimental work on the
rubber hand illusion that bears on the record's four Proposed THEORYs
about it. Read in full from the accepted manuscript and the authors'
notebooks: **Active**.

It is not new data. It re-expresses the correlation in Lush et al. 2020
(filed alongside) as outcomes for a field of small studies, and it
answers the commentary by Ehrsson and colleagues, the exchange filed
alongside as Ehrsson et al. 2022 and Lush & Seth 2022. The reading in the
NOTE separates two things. The part that holds even at n = 50 is that
agreement with the ownership statement itself depends heavily on trait
phenomenological control. The part that depends on sample size is the
claim that the averaged illusion statements "require" such participants.

What it does to the four THEORYs, set out in the NOTE:

- [THEORY-tmpef8lf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpef8lf.md). It does not meet the `promote_when`: it has no
  asynchronous or drift analysis, and it does not test whether the
  synchrony effect survives trait suggestibility. It bears against the
  ownership clause more than the referred-touch clause.
- [THEORY-tmpom3z2](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpom3z2.md). It suggests that the interoceptive-sensitivity
  result should be re-tested with trait phenomenological control as a
  covariate.
- [THEORY-tmpxdoe8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdoe8.md) and [THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md). It qualifies their evidence
  without testing them.

It also bears on [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md), on reverse inference from implicit and
animal measures.
