# CLAIM-034: Fidelity is one evaluative goal among adaptation, critique and parody, and a deliberately adaptive translation calls for one-way simulation of the source's relevant possibilities rather than equivalence

## The claim

Normative half, A38 §11.5: "Examine whether recreating original speaker footing
is always ethically or socially appropriate. Recognize that the choice of
preserved invariants is itself interpretive and potentially normative." A40
§14.3: "Preserving the original's social effect is not always desirable or
possible. Translators may deliberately change footing, register, or
communicative function. Distinguish fidelity as one evaluative goal from
adaptation, critique, parody, and transformation." A40 §14.2: "Partial
equivalence may be more appropriate than global matching."

Formal half, A40 §7.3: "Consider one-way simulation where target audiences can
reproduce the source utterance's relevant communicative possibilities. Discuss
why asymmetric simulation may be appropriate when translation is deliberately
adaptive."

## Where it went

The bisimulation section was demoted deliberately ([QUESTION-008](../questions.d/QUESTION-008.md)), and the one-way case
went with it. The ethics section did not reach the manuscript. What survives is
directedness: transport "is intentionally directed: compression and
interpretation need not be invertible" (§6), and the normative choice of
observables (§11). Neither says that a rendering may rightly fail to be faithful.
A39 §5.4 made a neighbouring distinction, also dropped: "fidelity as an
explanatory criterion" versus "fidelity as an optimized translation objective".
