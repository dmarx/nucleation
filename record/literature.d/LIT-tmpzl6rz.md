# LIT-tmpzl6rz: European Society of Endocrinology and Endocrine Society Joint Clinical Guideline: Diagnosis and Therapy of Glucocorticoid-induced Adrenal Insufficiency

Beuschlein, F., Else, T., Bancos, I., Hahner, S., Hamidi, O., van Hulsteijn, L., Husebye, E. S., Karavitaki, N., Prete, A., Vaidya, A., Yedinak, C. & Dekkers, O. M. (2024). European Society of Endocrinology and Endocrine Society Joint Clinical Guideline: Diagnosis and Therapy of Glucocorticoid-induced Adrenal Insufficiency. *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* 109(7): 1657–1683. doi:10.1210/clinem/dgae250. Co-published in *European Journal of Endocrinology* 190(5): G25–G51, doi:10.1093/ejendo/lvae029.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmppitgm. Located by recommendation number or section; the copy has no page numbers.*

- **Supraphysiologic supply always suppresses.** Anti-inflammatory doses
  "invariably result in HPA axis suppression" (Introduction, pathophysiology).
- **Mechanism, cited not shown.** Feedback inhibition of CRH and ACTH;
  prolonged use "often leads to a reduction in the overall responsiveness
  of the anterior pituitary gland"; in rodent models, pro-apoptotic
  effects that end in "atrophy of the adrenal cortex" (Introduction, pathophysiology).
- **Recovery is slow, ordered and sometimes incomplete.** ACTH first, then
  cortisol; recovery "from a few months to up to 4 years in some cases"
  (Clinical question I), and "Complete recovery of cortisol production can
  remain impaired in a minority of patients" (R 2.6).
- **Replacement must leave work to do.** The dose "should provide
  sufficient replacement, but also a sufficient stimulus for recovery
  (meaning avoiding any over-replacement)" (R 2.7).
- **The taper is consensus.** Table 4's schedule is good clinical practice;
  of five taper studies "There were no studies identified comparing
  different tapering schemes" (Clinical question II). Courses under 3–4
  weeks need no taper (R 2.1, very-low evidence).

## Standing in the record

Filed with Broersen et al. (2015), LIT-tmp5fl4h, which it uses as its
source for prevalence and recovery, and which it extends with a later
review of recovery (three cohorts, 202 patients), a review of tapering
(five studies), a stated mechanism and the schedule now recommended. For
the record it is the clinical form of CLAIM-602's prescription: supply
that covers only what the party cannot yet do, kept low enough to leave
the party's own work as a stimulus, and withdrawn in steps once the threat
it stood in for has gone, as CLAIM-600 requires. Recovery is gradual,
ordered and read as a continuum, which is slow reversion, not a threshold
(CLAIM-604); only the persistent minority hints at irreversibility, and
its mechanism is atrophy, not a flux loop.

Two errors or limits to carry. Its Table 6 lists Broersen et al.'s dose
strata (2.4%, 8.5%, 21.5%) and duration strata (1.4%, 11.9%, 27.4%) under
"Inhaled glucocorticoids", but Broersen et al. computed them in asthma
patients of all routes; the inhaled-only figures are 1.5%, 5.4%, 18.5% and
1.3%, 9.0%, 20.3%. And its taper schedule rests on consensus, with the
evidence behind its graded taper and testing recommendations very low.
