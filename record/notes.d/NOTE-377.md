# NOTE-377: Philosophical conceptions of the self: implications for cognitive science

## Contribution

The paper gave cognitive science a two-part map of the self that it did
not have: a minimal self, available in present experience without memory
or story, and a narrative self, extended in time. Its sharper move is
inside the minimal self: it separates the sense of ownership from the
sense of agency and connects their dissociation to a specific mechanism,
so that a philosophical principle (immunity to error through
misidentification) gets a candidate neurocognitive basis.

## Key insight

When a schizophrenic patient says an inserted thought is someone else's,
she is right that it is in her mind and wrong about who is thinking it.
That is ownership without agency. If a failed forward model explains the
error, the working forward model is part of what makes ordinary
first-person reference immune to error. A philosophical principle becomes
a claim about a mechanism.

## Assumptions

- **Thinking is a kind of action** with intentions a comparator can check,
  as motor action is (Frith's assumption, adopted).
- **Phenomenology and mechanism align**: the senses of ownership and
  agency map onto sensory feedback and forward prediction respectively.
- **Neonatal imitation is genuine** and requires a self/non-self
  distinction, proprioceptive self-location and recognising a face as
  one's own kind (Meltzoff and Moore).
- **Narrative is language-based** and grounded in episodic memory.

## Key results

There are no new results; the review's moves, in order:

- **Immunity principle.** After Wittgenstein's "I" as subject versus as
  object, and Shoemaker: using "I" as subject involves no identification,
  so cannot misidentify. Deafferentation-with-video experiments show "I"
  as object can.
- **Thought insertion as a counterexample** (Campbell, after Feinberg and
  Frith). Frith's patient: "They come from this chap, Chris. They're his
  thoughts." Whether it is a counterexample is left open (Gallagher 2000
  is cited for doubts), but the explanation of the failure would explain
  the principle.
- **Ownership versus agency.** Coincide in voluntary action, dissociate in
  involuntary movement. Ownership: ecological self-awareness from sensory
  feedback (Neisser; Gallagher and Marcel). Agency: what "precedes
  action", supported by lateralised readiness potential and SMA
  stimulation results (Haggard and Eimer; Haggard and Magno).
- **Frith's comparator model.** A non-conscious forward model compares
  efference copy with intention. Patients with thought insertion and
  delusions of control show deficits in forward monitoring but not in
  feedback-based control; premovement potentials involving supplementary
  motor, premotor and prefrontal cortex are abnormal. Extended to inner
  speech, failed forward monitoring yields thoughts in one's own stream
  that seem not self-generated (Fig. 2).
- **Embodied minimal self.** Bermúdez's non-conceptual first-person
  content and Neisser's ecological self; neonates "less than an hour old"
  imitate facial gestures, implying a primitive embodied self.
- **Reflective minimal self.** Strawson's self as a "single (hiatus-free)
  mental thing", "a bare locus of consciousness, void of personality",
  lasting as long as a period of experience.
- **Box 1, Tani's robot.** Top-down predictive and bottom-up
  sensory-motor processes are both dynamical systems in a shared space;
  when they conflict, arbitration requires the robot to take its own state
  into account, which Tani calls the robotic equivalent of
  self-consciousness. The self is momentary and occasioned by failure.
  This drops Strawson's requirement that the self be a conscious subject.
- **Narrative self.** Hume's bundle "strung together by the imagination"
  as ancestor; Dennett's narrative theory ("we don't spin them; they spin
  us"); Neisser and Fivush on narrative and the self-concept; Gazzaniga's
  left-hemisphere interpreter, whose self is "a bit fictional"; Pribram on
  episodic memory and time sense, and a boy with right-hemisphere and
  frontal damage who cannot form narrative sequence.
- **The extended proposal.** Ricoeur's richer narrative self, entangled
  in others' narratives, extended to "the sum total of its narratives",
  including "equivocations, contradictions, struggles and hidden
  messages": decentred, distributed and multiplex (Fig. 4b).
- **Conclusion.** Damasio's core and autobiographical selves; episodic
  memory registered as "my" memory depends on a reiterated minimal self;
  the neural link between them is still "informed speculation".

## Concepts

- **minimal self** — the self "accessible to immediate and present
  self-consciousness", without temporal extension.
- **narrative self** — the self extended in time through memory,
  intention and narrative; Neisser's "extended self".
- **sense of ownership** — the sense that it is my body moving or my
  experience; "I am the one undergoing it".
- **sense of agency** — the sense that I am the initiator or source of the
  action.
- **immunity principle** — immunity to error through misidentification
  relative to the first-person pronoun (Shoemaker).
- **forward model / comparator** — a non-conscious mechanism that
  compares efference copy of motor (or thought) commands with intentions,
  anticipating sensory feedback.

## Connections

- **Dennett** ([LIT-442](../literature.d/LIT-442.md), [NOTE-364](NOTE-364.md)). Gallagher's summary of the centre of
  narrative gravity matches [NOTE-364](NOTE-364.md)'s reading. His objection, that a
  point cannot hold conflict and self-deception, is answered in Dennett's
  essay by bifurcation into two selves; Gallagher keeps one self and puts
  the conflict inside it.
- **Strawson** ([LIT-452](../literature.d/LIT-452.md), [NOTE-383](NOTE-383.md)). The review presents
  Strawson's 1997–1999 minimal self. "Against Narrativity" later counts
  revision against narrative views, and Gallagher's narrative self
  includes "self-deceptions" by design. So the two disagree about whether
  a self that misdescribes itself is a defect or a feature.
- **Schechtman** ([LIT-468](../literature.d/LIT-468.md)). Not cited, though published four years
  earlier; the narrative side here is Dennett, Ricoeur and the
  psychologists.
- **Minsky** ([LIT-432](../literature.d/LIT-432.md), [NOTE-348](NOTE-348.md)). Minsky's Self as a network of partial
  self-models is closer to Gallagher's "multiplex" narrative self than to
  Dennett's single point. The forward comparator is the kind of mechanism
  Minsky's Critics describe in other words. The pairing is mine.
- **Damasio** ([LIT-387](../literature.d/LIT-387.md), [LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)). Gallagher's concluding remarks report
  Damasio's 1999 core/autobiographical distinction, the precursor of the
  feeling-based account those LITs hold.
- **Clark and Chalmers** ([LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md), [NOTE-180](NOTE-180.md)). The embodied, ecological
  minimal self and the extended mind both locate selfhood partly in
  organism–environment interaction; Gallagher says nothing of external
  resources.
- **Hume**, filed in parallel, is cited for the bundle view as the
  ancestor of narrative theories.

## Bearing on the record

- **Self-models and machines.** Tani's robot, and the published commentary
  "a view from a robot", are early claims that a machine can have a
  minimal self in a functional sense, bought by giving up consciousness.
  That is the record's distinction between self-models and experience
  ([THEORY-023](../theory.d/THEORY-023.md) asks only about the latter). Metzinger's starfish robot
  ([LIT-450](../literature.d/LIT-450.md)) is the same move six years later.
- **[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md).** The multiplex narrative self puts multiplicity inside one
  self, against Dennett's rule of one self per coherent story. That is a
  third option between decomposition and nesting; Gallagher does not
  discuss groups. The pairing is mine.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Read in the preprint.** Wording, figures and possibly references may
  differ in the typeset article.
- **A review, not an argument.** The positive proposal (C7) is two
  paragraphs. The neuroscience is reported from the cited studies.
- **Reference slips in the preprint.** The text cites Meltzoff and Moore
  "1977, 1984"; the references list 1977 and 1983. "Deprati, et al.
  (1997)" in the text is "Daprati, E., et al. (1997)" in the list. Frith,
  Blakemore and "Walpert" is presumably Wolpert. Whether the typeset
  version corrects these I could not check.
- **Two minimal selves.** The embodied (neonatal) and the reflective
  (Strawson) minimal selves differ in whether they are conceptual and
  conscious, and the review does not say which one the ownership/agency
  analysis is about.

## Open questions

- Does the sense of agency for thought really run through the same
  comparator as motor agency? The review takes Frith's extension on trust.
- If the narrative self is the sum of all narratives including false ones,
  what makes any narrative not part of the self?
