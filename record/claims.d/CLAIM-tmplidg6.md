# CLAIM-tmplidg6: Rights organize later justification: once justified, rights and normative powers become part of the structure within which further justification occurs, fixing which later undertakings are valid, by whom and with what effect, so rights can be prior in organization to later exercises of authority even where principles are prior to them in justification

## The claim

A102 §3 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b), headed
"Rights are not merely conclusions of justificatory reasoning; they organize
future justification". A grants Scanlon's position, that contractualism
"can explain Hohfeldian claims, powers, liberties, and immunities without
taking rights as basic elements of moral thinking" (A's report of
Scanlon's 2013 "Reply to Leif Wenar"), and adds:

> But we can argue that once rights and powers have been justified, they become **constitutive elements of the structure within which further justification occurs**.

A separates Justified(P), that a principle is defensible, from
ConstitutedBy(G, P, Θ), that an institution operates under a structure Θ of
of rights, powers, duties and procedures associated with P. Once in
place, Θ "affects what agents can validly undertake, which claims they can
create, and which later decisions count as authorized."

The two examples carry it. A lease: the landlord's power to terminate
under conditions, the tenant's immunity against unauthorized termination,
and the tenant's procedural rights "constrain how a dispute over
termination is decided". A promise: whatever justifies promissory fidelity,
"once a valid promise is made, it changes the normative relationship
between particular people. The promisee may possess a power to release the
promisor. The promisor's later preferences do not automatically extinguish
the obligation." The conclusion:

> Thus, even if principles are justificatorily prior to particular rights, **rights can be organizationally prior to subsequent exercises of justified authority**.

A106 §19 restates it as a chain, from justified principles to rights and
powers, to valid undertakings and institutional acts, to new relationships,
claims and obligations, to "Further questions of justification", and draws
the distinction "**the justification of a normative relation** from **the
organizational processes through which that relation is validly
constituted, maintained, altered, or made practically ineffective**".

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-086](CLAIM-086.md)** (Active) holds that a constraint may govern conduct without
  governing the conditions under which it remains authoritative. This claim
  is the other side: a right, once constituted, governs which later acts
  are valid, and so is among the conditions under which later authority is
  exercised.
- **[CLAIM-tmpnb4v4](CLAIM-tmpnb4v4.md)** (filed from the part before) holds that rights,
  demands and commitments are distinct and that each passage between them,
  a valid undertaking generating a right among them, needs its own
  warrant. This claim says what a right does once the passage is made.
- **[CLAIM-tmpol7nz](CLAIM-tmpol7nz.md)** holds that a right protecting a choice constrains a
  policy, not a realized history. Both claims locate a right's work in
  structure rather than in any one outcome.
- **[QUESTION-tmpk7u3e](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpk7u3e.md)** asks whether the record's account only supplements
  contractualism. This claim is compatible with the supplement answer: A
  says it is "compatible with much of Scanlon's view" and "develops the
  dynamic consequences of his account more thoroughly".

## What it does not say

- Not that rights are basic, or prior to principles in justification. It
  grants Scanlon that they are not.
- Not that every existing allocation of rights is legitimate. A106 §19:
  "We cannot simply infer the agreement's normative authority from the fact
  that the corporation regards it as valid. Nor can we assume that every
  existing legal relationship is morally indefensible until newly justified
  from nothing."
- Not what Wenar's challenge was. A says Wenar "explicitly describes the
  need for a multilevel theory"; the paper is not named, and the record has
  not read it.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Scanlon's reply to Wenar, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmpegc2w — new reading and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Scanlon 2013, "Reply to Leif Wenar", read ([NOTE-tmpzarar](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzarar.md), [THEORY-tmpegc2w](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpegc2w.md)), which the claim relays from A as unread. The premise is Scanlon's: "Contractualism seeks to explain not only first-order claim rights but also powers, as well as the prior liberties" (p. 403), and "I don't think that we need to appeal to the idea of a right to explain why our moral situation has this form" (pp. 403–404); the abstract says contractualism "does not appeal to the notion of a right as a basic element of moral thinking". The claim's addition, that rights once justified "become part of the structure within which further justification occurs", is not in the reply: Scanlon treats the promisee's power as justified once, by Principle F, and stops there. The nearest he comes is that moral rights "place demands on what laws and other institutions must be like. They therefore cannot derive their authority from these institutions" (p. 401). Detail: the claim reports A as saying contractualism explains "claims, powers, liberties, and immunities"; immunities appear in the reply's free-expression section ("an immunity: a limit on the powers of government", p. 402), not in its account of contractualism. The reading carries what the claim cites it for, the premise, and no more; no ARG is needed for that. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Wenar, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmpup8ol — new readings and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

Wenar 2013, "Rights and What We Owe to Each Other", read ([NOTE-tmp3co3x](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp3co3x.md), [THEORY-tmpup8ol](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpup8ol.md)): corroboration, which the THEORY carries. Wenar distinguishes "First-order rights", which "define requirements on conduct", from "second-order rights", which "assign authority to change requirements on conduct" (p. 376), and argues that contractualism "cannot plausibly be only a "first-order" theory that explains the duties and obligations that exist at one moment" (p. 398): "Rights of authority explain who can change what we owe to each other and how" (p. 399). That is the distinction the claim needs between a right's justification and the powers that then govern later changes. Wenar does not take the claim's further step, that justified rights and powers "become part of the structure within which further justification occurs"; he argues only that a theory must account for authority, and his theory of directionality is promised, not given (n. 46). Scanlon's reply ([NOTE-tmpzarar](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzarar.md)) answers that contractualism explains powers, for promises. No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Wenar and Cruft's SEP entry "Rights", read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-tmpxe1we — new reading and theory of 2026-11-18, Proposed -->

SEP "Rights", read ([NOTE-tmp2b9lt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp2b9lt.md), [THEORY-tmpxe1we](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxe1we.md)): corroboration of the claim's structural half, which the THEORY carries. "A has a power if and only if A has the ability to alter her own or another's Hohfeldian incidents", and "Power-rights to alter the authority of others are, as we will see, definitive of all developed legal and political systems" (§2.1.3); "Ordering, promising, waiving, abandoning, consenting, selling, and sentencing are all examples of acts by which a right-holder exercises a power". So rights, once in place, include powers that fix which later acts change what is owed, as the claim says. The entry says nothing about justificatory priority, the claim's "rights organize later justification" in its normative sense. No status changes.
