# LIT-1266: Where exactly am I? Self-location judgements distribute between head and torso

Alsmith, A. J. T. & Longo, M. R. (2014). Where exactly am I? Self-location judgements distribute between head and torso. *Consciousness and Cognition* 24, 70–74. DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2013.12.005.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1008](../notes.d/NOTE-1008.md).*

- **Pointing at oneself, on one's own body.** Earlier studies asked people
  to locate a self on a drawn outline or a cartoon (Limanowski & Hecht
  2011; Starmans & Bloom 2012), or narrowed it down by interview (Bertossa
  et al. 2008). Here people pointed at themselves, adapting the old
  "egocentre" task from the study of binocular vision.
- **Two regions, not one point.** Over 96 trials each, answers fell mostly
  on the upper face or the upper torso (region effect F(4, 36) = 9.70,
  p < .0001). "No single body part is judged as the unique seat of the
  self."
- **Where the pointer starts decides which.** Starting from straight up
  pushed answers to the face, from straight down to the torso (F(4, 36)
  = 24.00). In the visual condition from above, about three answers in
  four were to the upper face (read off Fig. 2).
- **Vision and touch agree.** The haptic answers were less sharply
  bimodal, which the authors read as lower precision, not a different
  judgement.
- **What it can carry is small.** Ten participants; 20.7% of trials
  excluded for a camera delay; and with the lower torso dropped, the
  region effect is F(3, 27) = 2.90, printed "p = .53" and called
  significant, when that F gives p ≈ .053. The start effect, which did
  survive, is also what a "stop at the first plausible place" strategy
  predicts.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request, in the round of experimental
work near the rubber hand illusion. It is an experiment by a philosopher
and a psychologist, tagged "experimental philosophy" by its authors, but
it is not a folk-concept survey: participants judged where they themselves
are, on their own bodies. Read in full: **Active**.

It bears on [THEORY-768](../theory.d/THEORY-768.md), Blanke & Metzinger's minimal selfhood, at its
self-location dimension, defined by them as "a determinate volume in
space, normally localized within the bodily boundaries". The result fits
a volume better than a point, and it gives a measure of self-location
other than the walking-back drift that [THEORY-768](../theory.d/THEORY-768.md)'s promotion test
says cannot measure it alone. It also shows that this measure moves with
task context. It does not meet or bear against that THEORY's promotion
test, which asks for whole-body measures during the rubber hand illusion
and for people without a history of agency. It bears on none of
[THEORY-766](../theory.d/THEORY-766.md), [THEORY-769](../theory.d/THEORY-769.md), [THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md) or [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md): it involves
no ownership manipulation and no action. On the record's self holdings it
is about [TERM-045](../terms.d/TERM-045.md)'s sense 1, the subject, located in space. It carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice.

Filed alongside: Starmans & Bloom 2012, which it criticises and
contrasts ([LIT-1265](LIT-1265.md)).
