# THEORY-144: Under steady cost and effort pressure, an organization's activity migrates toward the boundary of acceptable performance, and redundant defences erode unobserved because a breach of one shows no effect, so that major accidents arise from many locally reasonable decisions rather than from coincident errors

## Source

Rasmussen (1997), [LIT-743](../literature.d/LIT-743.md), read in [NOTE-584](../notes.d/NOTE-584.md).

## What was actually shown

Rasmussen ([LIT-743](../literature.d/LIT-743.md)) argues from investigations of major accidents and
from a model of adaptive behaviour.

- **The pattern.** The court and investigation reports from Bhopal,
  Flixborough, Zeebrügge and Chernobyl show "a systematic migration of
  organisational behaviour toward accident under the influence of pressure
  toward cost-effectiveness". They do not show a coincidence of
  independent failures. At Zeebrügge, decisions in separate departments,
  each sensible locally, together prepared the capsize, and one act
  released it.
- **The mechanism.** Work leaves degrees of freedom that actors close by
  adaptive search. Management supplies a cost gradient and the work an
  effort gradient, and activity moves toward the boundary of acceptable
  performance.
- **Why it is invisible.** Under defence in depth, breaching one defence
  "has no immediate, visible effect". One actor's safe boundary depends on
  what other actors have breached, so the defences "degenerate
  systematically through time".
- **The consequence for control.** Widening margins invites adaptation that
  uses them up, and safety campaigns only work as a permanent
  counter-pressure. Rasmussen proposes making the boundaries visible.

## What this does not say

- **Not that individuals are blameless or that errors do not occur.** The
  claim is that error-centred analysis misses the shaping mechanism.
- **Not quantitative.** The gradients and boundaries are a functional
  description, not measured fields.
- **Not shown in organizations that did not have accidents.** The evidence
  is reconstructions of failures, so drift may be over-represented.

## Connections

- **Viability ([THEORY-134](THEORY-134.md)).** The work space bounded by constraints is
  an informal viability set, and migration is a trajectory heading for its
  edge. Rasmussen's remedy, making the boundary visible, amounts to letting
  the actors see where the viability kernel ends.
- **Resilience ([THEORY-132](THEORY-132.md)).** Eroded margins are lost resilience in
  Holling's sense: the system looks the same and fails under a smaller
  shock.
- **The owner's essay.** It gives §6 an empirical mechanism of
  precariousness, and a contrast for Northstar. Drift erodes a routine
  regime's margins through ordinary optimisation, while protective lock-in
  reproduces an emergency regime. Both degrade viability, by different
  routes.
