# LIT-251: Contrastive learning, multi-view redundancy, and linear models

Christopher Tosh, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Daniel Hsu (2020), *Proceedings of the 32nd International Conference on Algorithmic Learning Theory (ALT 2021), PMLR 132:1179-1206* — [ARXIV-2008.10150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10150)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpj78ol](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpj78ol.md).*

- **The optimum.** The logistic problem with equal priors is solved over
  all functions by the PMI, and g* = exp f* is the joint-to-product
  density ratio (§2.3). The paper states this without derivation.
- **Redundancy.** µ(x) = E[E[Y|Z]|X=x] satisfies
  E[(µ(X) − E[Y|X,Z])²] ≤ εX + 2√(εXεZ) + εZ for any joint law (Lemma 1,
  proved).
- **The change of measure.** µ(x) = ∫ E[Y|Z=z] g*(x,z) p_Z(z) dz, so the
  label enters only through redundancy and g* does not depend on Y
  (§2.4).
- **Landmarks.** m random landmarks give a linear predictor within
  2 var(E[Y|Z]g*(X,Z̃))/⌊m/log₂(1/δ)⌋ of µ (Lemma 2, Theorem 3). For
  bounded labels the variance is at most E[g*²], which is 1 + χ² of the
  joint against the product (the record's identification). In the
  Gaussian model it is (1 + σ²)²/(1 + 2σ²) (Prop. 5).
- **Direct embeddings.** With a finite hidden variable the ratio
  factorizes exactly at rank |S| (§4.1). Otherwise low-dimensional
  factorizations exist (Lemma 7), but the paper does not show that
  minimizing its direct loss finds them.
- **Error propagation.** Downstream risk grows smoothly with excess
  contrastive loss, under a uniform bound g_max on the ratio
  (Theorems 10–11). The logistic loss is minimized by log g*, the direct
  loss by g* itself (§5).
- **No symmetry.** X and Z may be different spaces with any joint law.
