# LIT-199: The Philosophy and Physics of Duality

Sebastian De Haro, Jeremy Butterfield (2025), *Oxford University Press (open-access monograph, CC BY-NC-ND); manuscript also on arXiv* — [ARXIV-2508.01616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01616)

## Key takeaways

- A duality is an isomorphism between models that represent a common core ("bare") theory. Dual models are physically equivalent only when their interpretation maps commute with the duality map, and on the authors' account dualities neither favour structural realism nor create an in-principle underdetermination threat to cautious scientific realism.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

A book-length account of dualities in physics, meaning equivalences between often very different theories. It covers what dualities are, examples from quantum mechanics, electrodynamics, statistical mechanics, QFT and string theory, and the philosophical questions they raise. Part I sets out a "Schema" for duality and relates it to symmetry and interpretation, with simple examples (position–momentum, wave–particle, electric–magnetic, Kramers–Wannier). Part II works through advanced cases up to gauge–gravity duality. Part III takes up theoretical equivalence, the geometric view of theories, emergence, fundamentality, and understanding and explanation.
