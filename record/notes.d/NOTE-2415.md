# NOTE-2415: Lippl and Lindsey — Inductive biases of multi-task learning and finetuning: multiple regimes of feature reuse

## Contribution

The inductive biases of multi-task learning (MTL) and of pretraining then
fine-tuning (PT+FT), written as norms the learned solution minimizes:
implicit regularization for diagonal linear networks (a corollary of
earlier results), and the explicit penalty on weight change for
fine-tuning one-hidden-layer ReLU networks (new). From the norms: a
conservation law trading sparsity against dependence on pretrained
features, a "nested feature selection" regime of fine-tuning, and a
technique, rescaling the weights after pretraining, that moves a network
into it.

## Key results

1. **The fine-tuning penalty (Corollary 2, §3.3).** For a diagonal linear
   network pretrained from infinitesimal weights to zero error, with the
   readout re-initialized at magnitude γ, fine-tuning finds the
   interpolant minimizing Σ_d (|β_d^aux| + γ²) q(2β_d^main / (|β_d^aux| +
   γ²)). The penalty for each dimension is set by the pretrained
   coefficient and by γ.
2. **The two limits (§4.2).** For a feature unused in pretraining "both
   networks therefore have an ℓ1 norm minimization bias, suggesting that
   they tend to learn a sparse set of new features"; for large pretrained
   features both penalties converge to a weighted ℓ2 bias: "This implies
   that the networks preferentially use large pretrained features." PT+FT
   "is more strongly biased toward reusing features from the auxiliary
   task compared to MTL".
3. **A conservation law (Proposition 4, §4.3).** "For the MTL and PT+FT
   penalties derived in both the diagonal linear and ReLU cases,
   ℓ-order + FD = 1", so "an increase in sparsity (i.e. a smaller ℓ-order)
   yields a corresponding decrease in feature dependence".
4. **Nested feature selection, by rescaling (§4.4).** "We can produce this
   behavior in these tasks by rescaling the weights of the network
   following pretraining by a factor less than 1." In ReLU networks,
   "Performing weight rescaling in either direction following pretraining
   uncovers" the rich and the lazy regimes, and "different rescaling values
   may be required to enter the nested feature selection regime". The
   ReLU nested-sparsity result is flagged by the authors as "a postdiction
   rather than a prediction".
5. **Rescaling trades reuse against sparsity (App. D.1, Fig. 9).**
   "weight rescaling by a factor less than 1.0 following pretraining
   reduces the propensity of the network to share features between
   auxiliary and main tasks during finetuning"; "for small rescaling
   values, this benefit becomes smaller".
6. **Correlated features (§4.5).** PT+FT in ReLU networks benefits from
   main-task features correlated with pretrained ones; MTL does not, and
   "if features are identical, MTL outperforms PT+FT in the low-sample
   setting".
7. **Deep networks (§5, App. E, F).** In ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100,
   "rescaling values less than 1 improve finetuning performance", with
   the dimensionality signature of nested feature selection only after
   rescaling; with ImageNet-pretrained networks a single factor of 0.5,
   not swept, "improves finetuning performance, to varying degrees, in all
   of our experiments". For ViTs "rescaling before finetuning (both by
   larger and smaller values) decreases generalization performance".
8. **The symmetry (§3.1).** "the network function is invariant to
   rescaling the hidden weights by α>0 and the readout weights by 1/α."
   The paper uses this for its magnitude-direction parameterization; it
   does not say that its post-pretraining rescaling preserves the
   pretrained function, or report the auxiliary task after rescaling.

## Assumptions

Diagonal linear networks trained by gradient flow to zero error from
infinitesimal initialization, with an artificial step before fine-tuning
(pathway weights set to their sum, readout reset to γ); for ReLU networks,
explicit minimization of the ℓ2 norm of weight change, offered as "a
proxy" for implicit regularization; mean squared error; teacher-student
tasks with inputs drawn from the unit sphere and sparse teachers; deep
experiments with cross-entropy and no theory behind them.

## Concepts

Multi-task learning against pretraining plus fine-tuning; group lasso
(ℓ1,2) and the Q penalty; ℓ-order and feature dependence (FD); lazy, rich
and nested feature selection regimes; weight rescaling after pretraining;
participation ratio and effective number of shared dimensions (ENSD) as
a signature of the regime.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-754](../claims.d/CLAIM-754.md) and [CLAIM-675](../claims.d/CLAIM-675.md).** The lead's question: do its regimes show
  the same present capability with different later learning outside the
  initialization Anguita and colleagues generalize? Yes, by a step the
  paper does not take. Its rescaling of the hidden layer by c, with the
  auxiliary readout scaled by 1/c (the symmetry of result 8), leaves the
  pretrained network's function, and what a linear probe can reach from
  it, unchanged, while changing the per-dimension penalty of result 1
  from |β^aux| to c²|β^aux|, so what full fine-tuning learns, and which c
  is best, depend on the declared main task (results 4 and 5). Filed as
  [CLAIM-1043](../claims.d/CLAIM-1043.md), checked in [CASE-407](../cases.d/CASE-407.md); the reading that it is one
  learner under different procedures is [CLAIM-1013](../claims.d/CLAIM-1013.md), and the objection
  [CLAIM-1089](../claims.d/CLAIM-1089.md). A second instance, uncontested, is γ itself: the same
  pretrained network fine-tuned with different readout scales learns
  under different penalties, a developmental frontier indexed by the
  procedure, as [CLAIM-754](../claims.d/CLAIM-754.md)'s last section says.
- **[CLAIM-1011](../claims.d/CLAIM-1011.md).** Its identity (a function-preserving rescaling is a
  change of the optimizer's per-coordinate step sizes) applies exactly to
  this paper's rescaling technique, which the paper presents as part of
  the fine-tuning method. Background for the alternative reading.
- **[THEORY-2077](../theory.d/THEORY-2077.md) (Anguita and colleagues).** This is the theory they
  extend: infinitesimal initialization, balanced layers, with γ the only
  scale. They add absolute and relative scales at initialization. The
  regimes here are three of their four.
- **[CLAIM-758](../claims.d/CLAIM-758.md) (overconsolidation).** Nothing on training duration:
  pretraining is always to convergence. A parallel only: after pretraining
  the scale of the weights, which the present objective does not see,
  decides whether fine-tuning can still pick sparse new features.
- **[CLAIM-756](../claims.d/CLAIM-756.md).** Feature reuse lowers the sample cost of the main task
  where tasks share features (Fig. 2c-f): an instance of the claim's
  premise that one acquisition lowers the cost of another, nothing on its
  valuation.
- **[CASE-294](../cases.d/CASE-294.md).** The study should record the weight scale of each
  checkpoint and the head's initialization, since by this paper both set
  which regime fine-tuning is in.

## Limitations

- Shallow networks, square loss and teacher-student tasks; the ReLU
  result rests on explicit regularization as "a proxy" for implicit, and
  one of its findings is a postdiction.
- The fine-tuning setup of the diagonal network has an artificial step,
  which the authors call "an idiosyncrasy of the diagonal linear network
  setup".
- In deep networks the rescaling is not described closely enough to say
  which weights were scaled, or whether the pretrained function was kept;
  the ImageNet factor was not swept, and no error bars or significance
  are given in the prose.
- The paper never compares two networks with the same function: that the
  rescaling preserves the function is the filer's inference from result
  8, checked in [CASE-407](../cases.d/CASE-407.md) for the diagonal network only.
