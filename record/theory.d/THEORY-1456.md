# THEORY-1456: Rothemund shows that a 7-kilobase single-stranded scaffold routed through a shape, with about 200 unpurified staple strands in excess designed to bind only the scaffold, folds in one two-hour anneal from 95 °C to 20 °C into designed shapes about 100 nm across, with AFM-judged yields of well-formed structures from about 1% to 90% by design and at least 70% for rigid designs on circular scaffold; he proposes, without testing, that strand invasion, staple excess and cooperative staple binding account for this

## Source

Rothemund (2006), [LIT-2120](../literature.d/LIT-2120.md); read in [NOTE-1631](../notes.d/NOTE-1631.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Folding.** Six shapes (square, rectangle, star, three-hole disk,
  triangle of rectangles, sharp triangle) were folded from M13mp18
  (7,249 nt) with 200–250 staples at a 100-fold excess, annealed from
  95 °C to 20 °C in about two hours, and imaged by tapping-mode AFM.
- **Yields.** Well-formed meant no defect over 15 nm; counts ran from 22
  to 319 structures per design. The bridged rectangle gave 90%, the
  equiangular triangle of rectangles about 1%, the star 11% on a linear
  scaffold and 63% on a circular one. Any design could have failed to
  fold at all; several nearly did.
- **Excess.** 100:1 and 9:1 were indistinguishable; at 1:1 about 1% of
  structures were rectangular.
- **Mechanism.** Strand invasion, excess, cooperativity and staples that
  never bind one another are proposed in the Discussion and S9; no
  experiment tests them.

## What this does not say

- That the folded shape is the equilibrium state, or at what temperature
  it forms or melts: neither was measured.
- That the low yields are folding failures. They may be breakage,
  deposition or tip damage; the paper cannot tell them apart.
- That different staple sets for one shape fold equally well. All merge
  patterns give the same shape, but their yields are not compared.
- That excess staples are unneeded to hold the structure. Recycling them
  is suggested, not done.
