# TERM-045: self, and which sense

## Definition

"Self" in this record means one of the following, and a claim should say
which:

1. **Subject.** Being a subject, with a point of view or experience that is
   its own. This is [ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s `self`, and the sense the dancers objection
   keeps ([CLAIM-165](../claims.d/CLAIM-165.md)).
2. **Dispositional, or extended.** What a cognitive agent is, including its
   standing beliefs and coupled resources. This is Clark & Chalmers'
   extended self ([NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md)).
3. **Persisting.** Being the same over time. Coherence conserved along the
   system's own dynamics ([CLAIM-161](../claims.d/CLAIM-161.md)), or unity across contexts
   ([CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)).
4. **Narrative.** The unity of a life as a story ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s `narrative-unity`).
5. **Self-governing.** Governing oneself by one's own standards ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s
   `self-governance`). A willed agent ([TERM-048](TERM-048.md)) has something like this
   without needing sense 1 ([CLAIM-155](../claims.d/CLAIM-155.md)).
6. **Integrated.** Many control systems acting as one ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s
   `behavioral-integration`). This is the sense of "the self as a
   multi-agent system" and of Ainslie's population of interests that is
   "just the resultant" ([CLAIM-169](../claims.d/CLAIM-169.md)). It is numbered last because it
   was added in version 2.
7. **Presented.** The self a person gives to others: the presentations
   made to each audience, and whether they cohere where audiences meet.
   This is the self of impression management, Goffman's sense as boyd
   uses it ([LIT-1001](../literature.d/LIT-1001.md)), and of MoM's roles as sections over family,
   professional and social contexts ([CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md)). It is
   audience-relative and synchronic. It differs from 3, which is sameness
   over time, and from 4, which is a life's story told whole. A person can
   have it divided while 1 and 6 are intact. Added in version 3.

## What it is not

- **Not one property with several aspects.** The senses come apart. A firm may
  have 5 without 1. A dreamer may have 1 without 5. Otto's notebook bears on 2
  only. A speaker whose audiences are brought together may find 7 divided
  with nothing else changed ([CASE-052](../cases.d/CASE-052.md)).
- **Not "person"** ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s `personhood`).

## Note of 2026-10-18: a missing "modeled" sense

A26 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 argued for "a selective fictionalism about
the substantial ego": what is fictional is "the imagined substantial owner,
not the organized agent". The self that argument turns on is in none of the
seven senses. It is the self as the content of a self-model, which the
system does not recognise as a model: Metzinger's transparent self-model
([THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)) and Dennett's centre of narrative gravity ([THEORY-691](../theory.d/THEORY-691.md)). Call it
"modeled". It differs from 4, narrative, in being a representation the
system uses in governing itself rather than the unity of a life told whole,
and from 1, subject, in that a claim about it says nothing about whether
there is experience.

[CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md), filed from that exchange, is stated in senses 1 and 6 and
names the modeled sense in its body. Adding an eighth sense is a new version
of this term by its own rule. It is flagged here and not done.

## Note of 2026-10-22: the senses as perspectives

At U31 of the exchange "Graph Learning Applications" the owner proposed
that the record's selves are "better described as 'organizational
perspectives'" ([CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md), Proposed). A31 read this term's seven
senses as evidence that "there probably isn't one unified phenomenon
waiting for a single theory of selfhood", and translated all but one:

- 2, dispositional or extended: a perspective's repertoire and coupled
  resources;
- 3, persisting: the persistence of a perspective or of its carrier, in
  [TERM-071](TERM-071.md)'s senses;
- 5, self-governing: reflexive governance of a perspective
  ([CLAIM-424](../claims.d/CLAIM-424.md));
- 6, integrated: the organization of a perspective ([TERM-072](TERM-072.md), sense 1);
- 4, 7 and the "modeled" sense of the note of 2026-10-18: representations
  of a perspective ([TERM-072](TERM-072.md), sense 2).

Sense 1 is left alone: "A self is one subject" is "A separate question".
The senses are unchanged. If the claim is adopted, a claim using senses 2,
3, 5 or 6 should say which perspective object it means instead.

The exchange also filed the case that separates the senses most cleanly
in one person: Clive Wearing ([CASE-137](../cases.d/CASE-137.md)) keeps substantial skilled
competence and short-horizon organization (senses 2 and 6) through a
profound loss of autobiographical memory and narrative continuity (sense
4). The case does not show a failure of sense 3, persistence, in general.
It shows why persistence must not be identified with remembered
continuity, which is the "What it is not" bullet, "The senses come
apart", in a documented person. (Corrected the same day, after a review
the owner relayed: the first version of this note had sense 3 failing,
"as remembered continuity", which is the conflation the case should
prevent.)

## Note of 2026-10-22: selves held by others

All seven senses here are held or given by the person; sense 7, the
presented self, is the self a person gives to others. [CLAIM-415](../claims.d/CLAIM-415.md) needed
the other direction: what others take a person to be, what the person
takes them to take them to be, and who the person takes "them" to be.
Those are filed apart, as [TERM-075](TERM-075.md), and not as an eighth
sense here, because they are not selves the person has. They become part of
the self the person takes themselves to be only when appropriated
([TERM-075](TERM-075.md), sense 4), and can then become content of sense 4, the
narrative self, and sense 7, the presented self; unappropriated, they can
still shape what the person does (CASE-142).
