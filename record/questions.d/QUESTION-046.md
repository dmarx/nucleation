# QUESTION-046: What criterion, applied the same way to a modular mind and to an organized group, would say whether an organized whole is a subject of experience and not only an agent?

## The question

Functionalist reasoning builds a mind downward out of mindless agents and a candidate mind upward out of minded ones. The readings grant agency both ways, and the dispute narrows to experience. The question is what criterion separates a whole that is a subject from one that is only an agent. It must be applied by one procedure to a society-of-mind architecture and to an organized group, and motivated independently of the verdict it gives on groups. [QUESTION-019](QUESTION-019.md) asked a looser version on another line and was reframed away. This question asks for the criterion that framing lacked.

## Why the record needs it

The distributed-agency line assumes such a line exists. [CLAIM-155](../claims.d/CLAIM-155.md) places a willed agent such as a firm between agency and selfhood, "without thereby being a subject". [CLAIM-165](../claims.d/CLAIM-165.md) holds that a combined form is not shown to be a subject, and calls that "a marker", not a verdict. Without a criterion both are stipulations, and the top tier of [CLAIM-155](../claims.d/CLAIM-155.md) cannot be tested the way [QUESTION-032](QUESTION-032.md) tests the lower ones. [CLAIM-003](../claims.d/CLAIM-003.md)'s experiential half, compartmented experience in organizations, waits on the same criterion.

## What the record holds

- [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md): every published way to grant a society of mind experience and refuse it to a group is "an anti-nesting principle or an architectural criterion, neither yet principled". An architectural criterion that marks a group down for loose coupling marks a modular mind down too. Limit: philosophical argument, nothing computed.
- [THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) offers one architectural criterion, motivated within a single brain rather than by groups: unity of experience is "one single area of maximal causal density". Limit: a borrowed prediction, never computed, and "typically" leaves room for the group case.

## What would answer it

An integration or dynamic-core measure, or an anti-nesting rule justified on other grounds, actually applied to a modular-mind model and to a group model. It should put them on different sides for a reason not drawn from the verdict. Another thought experiment, or another intuition that a nation is not conscious, would not answer it.

## Note of 2026-11-01: Metzinger's contraction principle

Metzinger 2024 (LIT-tmp3uosd, NOTE-tmpy472x, pp. 79–82) gives a view
on which the question's "whole that is a subject" is misframed. On it,
phenomenality "is a subpersonal property, a property of certain
functionally integrated brain states". Ordinary brains misrepresent it
"by contracting it into a transparent conscious self-model", so that the
person seems to be what is conscious. State consciousness "can occur
without system consciousness, but not vice versa" (p. 81). "The
subpersonal world-model in the person's head is what is truly conscious"
(p. 82).

If that is right, a criterion of the kind the question asks for would
classify states, not wholes, and a whole would be a subject in the strong
sense only by representing itself as one. Like THEORY-063's
dynamic-core prediction, this is motivated within one brain and not by
groups. It is stated without a measure. The evidence offered is nondual
meditative states, known by report (THEORY-tmp8nd9o). Same structure as
the record's distinction between an organization's state and its
members', not shown same mechanism. It does not answer the question. It
narrows what an answer would have to classify. No status, title or warrant
changes.
