# LIT-tmpu9yys: Rubber hands 'feel' touch that eyes see

Botvinick, M. & Cohen, J. (1998). Rubber hands 'feel' touch that eyes see. *Nature* 391(6669), 756. DOI 10.1038/35784.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpt0l4z](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpt0l4z.md).*

- **The illusion.** Synchronous brushing of a seen rubber hand and the
  subject's hidden hand makes subjects feel "the touch not of the hidden
  brush but that of the viewed brush, as if the rubber hand had sensed the
  touch". Three questionnaire items were affirmed reliably: touch felt
  where the rubber hand was touched, touch caused by the brush on the
  rubber hand, and "I felt as if the rubber hand were my hand".
- **Ownership comes with it.** Subjects who referred touch to the rubber
  hand also reported it as belonging to them, and eight of ten used words
  of ownership unprompted.
- **Felt position moves.** After 30 minutes of exposure, blind
  intermanual reaches were displaced toward the rubber hand, more so the
  longer the illusion had been reported (r² 0.47, p<0.03, Fig. 2).
- **Synchrony is the cause.** With a small asynchrony in the brushing,
  the illusion was reported for 7% of the exposure against 42%, and
  reaches moved 13 mm away from the rubber hand rather than 23 mm toward
  it.
- **The interpretation.** A constraint-satisfaction process among vision,
  touch and proprioception, shaped by their normal correlations,
  reconciles conflicting input by distorting position sense; and
  intermodal matching "can be sufficient for self-attribution".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-19 at the owner's request to fill the record's gap
around the rubber hand illusion and bodily self-consciousness. Before it,
the record held body ownership only at second hand: Metzinger's "Self
models" ([LIT-450](LIT-450.md), [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md)) cites full-body illusions (Lenggenhager et al.
2007) for its claim C6 that ownership can be manipulated by multisensory
conflict, and none of the primary studies had been read. This is the
founding primary report of that kind of manipulation, read in full: **Active**.

It is a single page with ten subjects, so it establishes the effect and
its dependence on synchrony, not its size or mechanism. It bears on
[THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md), which says such illusions show the self-model can be
manipulated but not that it is all there is; on [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md) and the
ownership–agency distinction of Gallagher ([LIT-453](LIT-453.md)) and Haggard
([LIT-1009](LIT-1009.md)), since the illusion is ownership produced in a passive hand
with no agency at stake; and on the precision-weighted multisensory
accounts in the record ([LIT-1181](LIT-1181.md)), whose reconciliation of conflicting
senses it is an early behavioural instance of. The NOTE sets this out.
