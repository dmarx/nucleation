# QUESTION-122: Can a deep network during training have meaningful affect-like states, such as frustration of its demands, and to which unit, the network, the network–optimizer pair or the training process, would they belong?

## Why it is a question

The owner, at U30: "apply this framework to discuss the possibility of
meaningful affective states attributable to a deep neural network
specifically during training". The framework was the regulatory distress
of U28–U29. Training, not inference: the question is about the process
that changes the network.

A30's verdict: "a meaningful, qualified notion of training-time affective
organization—but it does not currently support the conclusion that a
neural network feels distress, suffers, or experiences anything during
training." Its ladder: "one loss ⇏ one integrated representation ⇏ one
affective state ⇏ one experiencer". Gradient conflict, g_iᵀg_j < 0, is "a
possible **ingredient** of functional distress, not distress itself". A
negative inner product is itself only a coordinate-dependent indicator,
not a finding of frustration ([CLAIM-366](../claims.d/CLAIM-366.md)).

The question has three halves, and the record holds them in different
places.

- **Which unit.** U31's correction, granted by A31: individuate first. For
  conventional training, [CLAIM-367](../claims.d/CLAIM-367.md) answers in part: most of the
  governance of updates belongs to the optimizer–model–data system, so a
  condition of training is not thereby the network's. It does not say
  which condition would be affect-like, so it is not written as an answer.
  [CASE-113](../cases.d/CASE-113.md) holds the design that would locate it.
- **Which condition.** A30 put it in "persistent, system-wide conflicts
  among consequential learning and maintenance demands" rather than "the
  magnitude of its loss". U33 moved the primitive from distress to
  frustration, "a failure of joint realizability" ([CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md)), and
  A33 showed that a zero aggregate gradient can hide it ([CLAIM-351](../claims.d/CLAIM-351.md)).
  A31 adds the condition a demand must meet: "frustration normally
  presupposes a relevant demand or commitment belonging to the affected
  system". In conventional training the demands are the trainer's
  ([CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md)).
- **Whether it is felt.** That is [QUESTION-059](QUESTION-059.md), Deferred with its line, and,
  for systems without biology, [QUESTION-048](QUESTION-048.md). [QUESTION-046](QUESTION-046.md) asks for the
  criterion of a subject that would apply the same way to a network as to
  a group. A30: "Illusionism doesn't make any negatively evaluated feedback
  loop a suffering subject."

## What would count as an answer

For the unit: the crossed interventions of [CASE-113](../cases.d/CASE-113.md), run, with the
condition moving or staying when the optimizer is changed and the network
held fixed. For the condition: a training-time state that meets A30's
caution, "If z_t is merely a metric computed by an external experimenter,
it does not establish internally organized affect", by being computed and
used inside the learner, and whose ablation changes how conflicting
demands are resolved. The felt half is not answered by either.
