# CLAIM-190: The anchoring measures are computed in the grammar that shapes the argument, so by themselves they cannot certify that drift fell: drift must also be judged under standards the record did not supply

## The claim

The nucleating-argument line proposes measures for its claims: inbound
references per code, to tell an anchor from a structure that merely
persists ([QUESTION-038](../questions.d/QUESTION-038.md), [CLAIM-179](CLAIM-179.md)), and turns per filed entry, set against
drift ([CLAIM-186](CLAIM-186.md)). Each is computed in the record's grammar: codes,
relations and statuses.

[CLAIM-149](CLAIM-149.md), on the pragmatic-transport line, says "a probe family that
supplies a transformation's training signal cannot also certify what the
transformation preserves: preservation must be shown under probes that
were not used to train it." The record's grammar is the training signal
for the argument. It rewards coding, linking and announcing changes. A
measure built from the same grammar will register more coding, linking and
announcing, whether or not the argument drifted less by the participants'
own standards. [CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md) says observational equivalence is relative to the
probes, and these probes are the record's.

This is distinct from [CLAIM-181](CLAIM-181.md). [CLAIM-181](CLAIM-181.md) is about how the record shapes
what the argument becomes. This is about how the record shapes what its
success looks like.

## What would answer it

Inbound-reference counts and turns per anchor agree with drift judged
blind, by raters using the exchange's own standards, on stretches held out
from the grammar's influence: for example, re-derivations and unannounced
changes of thesis coded before the record existed.

## Note of 2026-10-12: a documented case, and an open dispute about raters

[CASE-071](../cases.d/CASE-071.md), filed in PR [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/121), is now a ground of this claim.
Translations picked by a neural metric are rated better by that metric and
its neighbours, and human raters find them no better. It is [CLAIM-149](CLAIM-149.md)'s
point made by selection rather than training. Its limit: it is machine
translation scored for accuracy, not an argument scored for drift. This
claim's remedy, drift "judged blind, by raters using the exchange's own
standards", treats raters as the criterion. That is [CLAIM-217](CLAIM-217.md)'s
position, [CLAIM-206](CLAIM-206.md) holds that human judgements are one probe family
among others, and the dispute is open.
