# TERM-tmpgqmop: interpretation, as a context-dependent determination of significance: a transformation that differentiates information and establishes or revises its consequences within an operative organization, defined without experience

## Definition

A11's revised form: "A context-dependent transformation that
differentiates information and establishes or revises its significance
within an operative organization of possible consequences." A7's earlier
form: "the context-dependent organization of information into distinctions
that acquire significance through their relationships to a system's
existing models, evaluative standards, and possible state transitions."

Three parts, in both:

1. **A distinction.** Something is differentiated from alternatives.
2. **A context.** The distinction's significance depends on other states
   and constraints. A raised heart rate can take part in several
   interpretations (A7).
3. **A consequence.** The distinction changes what the system expects,
   attends to, regulates or can do next. Significance is not "an
   association supplied by an outside observer" (A7).

In the record's operator model ([CLAIM-336](../claims.d/CLAIM-336.md)) A11 wrote it as
j_{i,t} = J_i(s_{i,t}; x_t, q_t, w_t): a local interpretive
determination of signal s, given the interpretive state x, the operative
context q and the distribution of influence w.

Interpretation is the operation as well as its result. A11: "Applying the
interpretive operation can also change the state on which subsequent
operations act", which is [TERM-026](TERM-026.md)'s point about frames. "An
interpretation can transform its own future conditions." Order can matter,
since framing operations need not commute ([CLAIM-052](../claims.d/CLAIM-052.md)).

Interpretation is not judgement. A1: judgement is "a higher-order
operation within an interpretive system". Most interpretation in this
sense is not propositional, not deliberate and not reportable.

## Why it is defined without experience

A7: "Otherwise the constitutive theory would explain experience using a
definition of interpretation that already contains experience." A11:
"none of this requires consciousness. Unconscious perception, artificial
classifiers, and ordinary regulatory mechanisms may perform
interpretations in this broad sense. That is a feature of the definition,
not a defect." The cost is [CLAIM-tmp1lvwf](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1lvwf.md). Interpretation in this sense is
not sufficient for experience, and whatever is sufficient must be found in
how interpretations are organized ([TERM-tmpe36gg](TERM-tmpe36gg.md), sense 1).

## What it is not

- Not a frame. [TERM-026](TERM-026.md)'s frame is an operator applied to an
  interpretive state; an interpretation is what a frame applied in context
  determines. The two are complements: one names the operation's form, the
  other its result and its consequences.
- Not a perspective. A perspective is the organization through which
  interpretations "acquire, contest, and revise their significance"
  (A11). A perspective is an interpretation only in a broad, temporally
  extended sense, which A11 declined to adopt because it would make the
  two words synonymous.
- Not the "interpretive" of the record's warrant vocabulary ([ADR-043](../decisions.d/ADR-043.md)). A
  claim's warrant is how the claim is argued, not a process in a system.

## What would settle it

Adoption is the owner's decision. The record already uses
"interpretation" in its pragmatic-transport line, for communicative acts and translation
([TERM-005](TERM-005.md), [TERM-007](TERM-007.md), [TERM-035](TERM-035.md)). If adopted, this term is the sense for the
evidence-for-experience line. It complements those uses and does not
replace them. It would be shown wanting if the three parts could not be
told apart in a system without assuming what the system experiences.
