# THEORY-tmpcqo5a: LaVoice finds that, among similarly blighted census tracts in 28 large US cities, black neighbourhoods were two to three times as likely to be cleared under Title I of the Housing Act of 1949, that cleared tracts kept about 12–13% lower population and housing density and a 16% lower black share for decades with rents up about a quarter, and, because rents in uncleared low-income tracts rose with them, reads a spatial-equilibrium model as showing the lowest-income households city-wide made worse off

## Source

LaVoice (2024), [LIT-tmp3jqit](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3jqit.md); read from a short 2021 draft in [NOTE-tmpuu40d](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuu40d.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Selection** (abstract, §4, Figure 1): within quartiles of treatment
  predicted from non-racial characteristics, high-black-share tracts were
  cleared two to three times as often; robust to distance from the city
  centre. It could have come out flat.
- **Direct effects** (pp. 14–17, Table 2, Figure 2): two-way fixed effects
  and an event study, cleared tracts against slums in cities with limited
  participation chosen by k-nearest neighbours: "a decline in population
  density by 13%, a decline in housing density by 12%, and a decline in
  the share of black residents by 16%" with "a 24% increase in median
  rents" (p. 3). Density stays down; the income gain fades.
- **Within-city rents** (p. 18, Figure 3): a negligible rent difference
  between cleared tracts and synthetic controls from uncleared tracts of
  the same city, against 100 placebo assignments, read as rents rising in
  uncleared low-income tracts too.
- **Welfare** (§3, p. 4): in a two-neighbourhood model with income
  sorting and absentee landlords, a supply effect that dominates the
  quality effect leaves "all low-income households" worse off.

## What this does not say

- Any single income effect: the draft gives 18% and 16%.
- That displaced households were worse off as measured: they were not
  tracked, and the welfare conclusion is the model's.
- That clearance destroyed social organization: "a strong sense of
  community" and the Lower Hill testimony are qualitative and secondary.
- That city-level gains were absent: it takes Collins and Shester's as
  given and locates who bore the costs.
- Anything outside 28 large cities, or about projects funded after 1965.
