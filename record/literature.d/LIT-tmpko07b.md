# LIT-tmpko07b: Do Artifacts Have Politics?

Langdon Winner (1980). "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" *Daedalus* 109(1), "Modern Technology: Problem or Opportunity?", Winter 1980, pp. 121–136. JSTOR 20024652.

## Key takeaways

- Mumford, as Winner quotes his 1964 essay: "two technologies have recurrently existed side by side: one authoritarian, the other democratic, the first system-centered, immensely powerful, but inherently unstable, the other man-centered, relatively weak, but resourceful and durable."
- Two kinds of politics in artifacts: arrangements that settle an issue, and technologies that require or are "strongly compatible with, but does not strictly require" particular social relations.
- "By far the greatest latitude of choice exists the very first time a particular instrument, system, or technique is introduced."
