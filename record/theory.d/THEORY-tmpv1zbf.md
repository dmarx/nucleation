# THEORY-tmpv1zbf: A social fact is a way of acting, thinking or feeling that exists outside individual consciousness and can constrain the individual from outside, shown by sanction or by the resistance it offers to violation; such a fact is general in a group because it is collective, not collective because general, so the group is its substrate

## Source

Émile Durkheim (1894), "Qu'est-ce qu'un fait social ?", *Revue
philosophique* 37, chapter I at pp. 466–475; chapter I of *Les Règles de la
méthode sociologique* (1895). [LIT-484](../literature.d/LIT-484.md), read in full in French in [NOTE-391](../notes.d/NOTE-391.md),
in the Alcan 1919 book text. Page numbers are that edition's. English
glosses are [NOTE-391](../notes.d/NOTE-391.md)'s.

## What was actually shown

All of it is definition with illustration. There are no data beyond a
mention of rates.

- **Exteriority (pp. 5–6).** Duties of brother, spouse and citizen are
  defined outside me, "dans le droit et dans les mœurs". Religious beliefs
  are found "toutes faites". Signs, money and credit "fonctionnent
  indépendamment des usages que j'en fais".
- **Constraint, graded (pp. 6–8).** Law prevents, annuls or punishes; moral
  maxims meet public censure; dress conventions meet ridicule; language and
  industrial technique make deviation fail. The force is invisible to the
  one who conforms and shows itself to the one who resists (p. 7).
- **A new species (p. 8).** Social facts consist of representations and
  actions, so they are not organic; they are in no individual
  consciousness, so they are not psychic; so their substrate is the
  political society or one of its partial groups.
- **Not generality (pp. 11–14).** A thought in every consciousness is not
  thereby social. Collective habits precipitate into formulas (rules,
  proverbs, creeds) that can exist unapplied (pp. 12–13). Where there is no
  formula, statistical rates let individual circumstances "se neutraliser
  mutuellement" and isolate the collective current (pp. 13–14).
- **General because collective (pp. 14–15).** "S'il est général, c'est
  parce qu'il est collectif (c'est-à-dire plus ou moins obligatoire), bien
  loin qu'il soit collectif parce qu'il est général." It is in each part
  "parce qu'il est dans le tout".
- **Two criteria, one fact (pp. 15–16).** External coercive power, or
  diffusion within the group plus existence independent of individual
  forms. The second is "une autre forme de la première", since a way of
  conduct external to individuals spreads only "en s'imposant".
- **Against Tarde (n. 3).** Imitation is a consequence of a fact's social
  character, not its definition.
- **Structure is consolidated practice (pp. 17–19).** Roads, housing and
  political divisions are "des manières de faire consolidées", differing
  from free currents only "dans le degré de consolidation".
- **The formula (p. 19).** "Est fait social toute manière de faire, fixée
  ou non, susceptible d'exercer sur l'individu une contrainte extérieure ;
  ou bien encore, qui est générale dans l'étendue d'une société donnée tout
  en ayant une existence propre, indépendante de ses manifestations
  individuelles." Identical in the 1894 text.

**What could have come out otherwise.** A way of acting could have been
general and independent of its instances without any constraint behind
it, which would split the two criteria. The chapter does not look for one;
the equivalence is asserted (C8, weak).

## What this does not say

- **That the elimination is sound.** That social facts are "une espèce
  nouvelle" needs organic and individual-psychic facts to exhaust the
  non-social. That is assumed (C3, weak).
- **That unfelt constraint has been shown.** That constraint shows itself
  under resistance is illustrated. That it is present all along in the
  compliant case is the chapter's main unargued step (C2).
- **That constraint is one property.** Legal sanction, ridicule and market
  failure are all "contrainte". Whether they are one thing is not argued.
  Later accounts of convention would split the coordination case off
  ([NOTE-391](../notes.d/NOTE-391.md), Open questions).
- **That the group is a mind or an agent.** "Âme collective" is used once
  (p. 14), and not in the 1894 text. The claim is that sociology has facts
  of its own whose substrate is the group. It neither supports nor moves
  [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md).
- **That statistics measures collective states without assumptions.** The
  neutralisation argument assumes individual factors are independent of
  one another and of the current.
- **That this is "not a theory".** Durkheim says the definition summarises
  observation (n. 3). Its elimination and its equivalence of criteria are
  theoretical steps (C11).
