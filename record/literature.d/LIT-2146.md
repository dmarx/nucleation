# LIT-2146: Conservation-reliant species and the future of conservation

Scott, J. M., Goble, D. D., Haines, A. M., Wiens, J. A. & Neel, M. C. (2010). Conservation-reliant species and the future of conservation. *Conservation Letters* 3(2), 91–97. doi:10.1111/j.1755-263X.2010.00096.x

## Key takeaways

*Not read. Nothing here is the authors' text; it is only what the neighbours attribute to it, unverified until read.*

- **The label.** Underwood et al. (2013) cite it, jointly with Scott et
  al. (2005) and Reed et al. (2012), for calling species "conservation
  reliant", which they say "implies that they will require active
  management into perpetuity".
- **State listing.** Mussmann et al. (2017) cite it for "the opportunity
  for potential management of state-listed species that can preclude the
  federal listing process".

## Standing in the record

Unavailable. Three open neighbours stand in, and only for the concept of
conservation reliance and for what they themselves assert, not for
anything this paper says:

- **Mussmann et al. (2017)**, [LIT-2183](LIT-2183.md): the greater prairie chicken,
  where translocation without relief of the habitat threat is said to
  perpetuate reliance. Its definition and its figure that 84 % of listed
  species are conservation-reliant are cited by its authors to Goble et
  al. (2012), which was not read (blocked), not to this paper.
- **Brown et al. (2019)**, [LIT-2155](LIT-2155.md): a viability model of the
  Kirtland's Warbler, a recovered species that stays on supplied habitat
  after delisting.
- **Underwood et al. (2013)**, [LIT-2153](LIT-2153.md): Hawai'i's endangered
  waterbirds, held stable by weed control, predator control and water
  management that the authors call perpetual.

None of them carries this paper's own counts, typology or proposed
management agreements. Cite the neighbours for what they assert, and
nothing from this paper as read until a lawful copy is obtained. It bears
on [CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (independent of what founded it, never of what feeds it) and
[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) (when a partial organization becomes independent of the
intervention that established it).
