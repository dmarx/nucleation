# ARG-139: The recent findings that small spectral directions matter, in three spectra, support the claim that a model's metrics can rank directions in opposite orders, Xu's as evidence of disagreement in trained models and the others weakly, but do not show that the hyperribbon account mistook a hierarchy of widths for one of importance, since a hyperribbon is already measured in the metric of the predictions and the findings concern other spectra

## The inference

A41 cites four findings: zeroing the small singular values of transformer
weight matrices damages the model, and after fine-tuning the smallest
decile matters (Staats et al., held as [THEORY-420](../theory.d/THEORY-420.md)); concept directions
sit in the low-variance tail of the unembedding covariance (Acharya et
al., [LIT-2959](../literature.d/LIT-2959.md), [THEORY-tmpklef6](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpklef6.md)); SGD progresses in the loss Hessian's bulk, not its top
subspace (Song et al., [LIT-2973](../literature.d/LIT-2973.md), [THEORY-tmpthmd7](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpthmd7.md)); and perturbing the unembedding's
least-amplified directions moves next-token distributions more than
matched random perturbations (Xu, [LIT-2967](../literature.d/LIT-2967.md), [THEORY-tmpu3iio](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpu3iio.md)). Read, Xu's
perturbations are of the final hidden state along those directions, not
of the unembedding's weights. From these A41 §8
concludes two things. First, "the hierarchy of geometric magnitude can be
very different from the hierarchy of functional significance". Second,
"We originally treated the hyperribbon hierarchy as a hierarchy of
importance. But strictly speaking, a hyperribbon tells us which parameter
combinations are distinguishable under a particular observation metric."

## Critical questions

- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** For the first
  conclusion, in part (revised 2026-11-21). The findings are in three
  spectra, not four: weight singular values (Staats et al.), the logit
  metric WᵀW (Xu, and Acharya et al., whose "unembedding covariance" is
  "the regularized uncentered second moment of the unembedding rows",
  WᵀW up to scale and ridge), and the training-loss Hessian (Song et al.).
  Version 1 gave Acharya et al. "representation variance" against
  "concept separation"; both were wrong, since the paper measures where a
  concept vector's Euclidean energy sits in the logit metric's eigenbasis.
  Xu sets a small quantity in the logit metric against a large effect in
  the pulled-back predictive Fisher, per unit of logit distance: an
  instance of metrics disagreeing, which is [CLAIM-835](../claims.d/CLAIM-835.md), though not an
  end-to-end reversal. Acharya et al.'s own effect measures run with the
  logit metric, so it is at most a weak instance: a direction's place
  depends on which vector of a concept is measured. Song et al.'s is a
  disagreement within one metric, curvature against contribution to
  descent, at a valley floor. For the
  second, no. A hyperribbon is defined in the Fisher metric of the
  model's predictions under its stated noise ([LIT-2977](../literature.d/LIT-2977.md), [LIT-3009](../literature.d/LIT-3009.md),
  read on 2026-11-21; see the note below): its
  thin directions are those that change the predictions little, which is
  what "unimportant for prediction" means. None of the four findings
  measures that spectrum for parameters, with one qualification (added
  2026-11-21): Song et al.'s main loss is MSE, whose Hessian on the
  training set is the Gauss–Newton matrix JᵀJ plus a residual term, close
  to the hyperribbon's parameter-space metric (the reader's and filer's
  reasoning; the paper does not decompose it). Their picture, stiff
  directions fitted first and progress along the flat floor, is the
  sloppy-model picture, so this finding touches the predictive spectrum
  and agrees with the account. Xu's G_pred is a predictive
  Fisher, but pulled back to a hidden state rather than to parameters. So
  the findings do not show a hyperribbon misranking anything. They show
  that other spectra are poor proxies for the predictive one, and the
  hyperribbon account never used them as proxies.
- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** If metrics
  always ranked directions alike, the findings would not occur. They are
  evidence for the disagreement. Read (2026-11-21), Xu's is the one that
  would not be expected if the metrics agreed in trained models; it shows
  the disagreement concentrated at the bottom of the spectrum, in ten
  models, and partial. The other two would be expected either way.
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its rivals?** The rival "small
  means unimportant in every metric" is refuted by any one finding. The
  rival "the predictive metric is the only one that matters" is not
  touched, since the findings measure importance by effects on
  prediction.

## Verdict

Supports [CLAIM-835](../claims.d/CLAIM-835.md): Xu's finding as evidence that the metrics
disagree in trained models, not that they reverse; Song et al.'s and
Acharya et al.'s weakly (revised 2026-11-21). Does not
support A41 §2's "correction to our hyperribbon account". What A41 §8 states
correctly, that a hyperribbon is relative to its observation metric, was
true by the hyperribbon's definition. It is not a lesson of the
small-eigenvalue work. The parts of A41 that conflate representation
variance with a parameter-space Fisher hierarchy are the ledger's gap
at L26, recorded here.

## Note of 2026-11-21: Transtrum, Machta & Sethna (2010, 2011) and Transtrum et al. (2015), read

Supports the argument's premise. The hyper-ribbon is defined by distances
in data space under g = JᵀJ ([NOTE-2240](../notes.d/NOTE-2240.md)), with residuals in units of
their errors in the 2011 paper ([NOTE-2247](../notes.d/NOTE-2247.md); the 2010 letter states no
noise model, so "under its stated noise" is the 2011 paper's). The papers
frame the hierarchy as what the data constrain, not as importance: "only a
few stiff parameter combinations need to be tuned to fit most of the
data" (2010). The 2015 review does call combinations "that have little effect on
model behavior" "unimportant", relative to the fitted behaviour, and
warns that "if even a single parameter remains unknown ... large
predictive uncertainty can still result" ([NOTE-2242](../notes.d/NOTE-2242.md)): importance
relative to observation, as this argument reads it.

## Note of 2026-11-21: Acharya, Rimal & Dhakal, Song, Ahn & Yun, and Xu, read

The revisions above are this note's. In brief: Concepts Whisper's
spectrum is the unembedding's second moment, the logit metric Xu uses
([NOTE-tmpwvltp](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwvltp.md)), so the findings are three spectra; its sign flip between
activation-side and vocabulary-side vectors is predicted by Park, Choe and
Veitch's map ([CLAIM-tmpqy8mq](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpqy8mq.md)). Song et al.'s Hessian is near the
hyperribbon's metric under MSE and agrees with the sloppy picture
([NOTE-tmpw0eql](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpw0eql.md)). Xu's metric is the state-space pullback (App. B), so it
bears on no parameter-space hierarchy, and its predictive metric depends
on the context through Σ_p(x), so its ranking is averaged over documents
([NOTE-tmp454fw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp454fw.md)). The verdict on the hyperribbon stands.
