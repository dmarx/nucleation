# NOTE-tmp3eo5h: Mostrous and Greenwood — Who Trolled Amber?

## Contribution

Journalism: commissioned expert analysis of a tweet corpus, with reported
findings of coordinated and inauthentic activity. The analysts' methods
and data are not disclosed on the pages read.

## Key insight

The apparent weight of public opinion against one party to a trial may
have been partly produced by inauthentic accounts.

## Assumptions

- Inauthenticity can be inferred from account behavior, by methods not
  stated on these pages.
- The likeness of the Arabic-history accounts to known Saudi networks
  indicates their origin; this rests on one interviewee's opinion ("it
  looks like these are totally from the flies").

## Key results (the publisher's pages)

- **The claim** (written piece): "A new Tortoise investigation suggests
  that Heard was trolled by an army of bots, some of them apparently
  operating from Saudi Arabia."
- **The share**: "In Chen's opinion, more than 50 per cent of the tweets in
  the database came from inauthentic accounts."
- **Coordination**: "A coordinated network of Twitter accounts which sent
  more than 100 identical messages at exactly the same time to any brand
  which had worked with Amber"; "Bot networks promoting Depp operating in
  countries including Thailand and Spain."
- **The Saudi set**: "we found hundreds of other deleted messages, all in
  Arabic ... most praise the Saudi regime".
- **Scope**: "Manufactured online hate against Heard could have
  jeopardised her right to a fair trial."
- **The UK trial**: "Depp lost a UK trial in 2020 on similar charges. That
  trial found, on the balance of probability, that he had abused Heard on
  12 occasions." This agrees with the judgment as read (LIT-tmpqju5l,
  para 575).
- **Series page**: "What if the online hate against her was manufactured?"

## Concepts

Inauthentic accounts; bots and trolls; coordinated networks; "electronic
flies"; manufactured online hate.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpciy9q** (a party arranges another's wrong and uses the true
  accusation before an audience whose judgement it anticipated). Weakly
  related, not filed as a relation: Tortoise reports evidence that the
  audience's apparent judgement was partly produced by inauthentic
  accounts. That is shaping the audience, not anticipating it, and the
  pages attribute it to no party to the dispute. It cannot ground the
  claim's first or second move.
- **CLAIM-415** (a judgement attributed to a collective that does not hold
  it) and **QUESTION-261** and **QUESTION-356** (a vocal minority making a
  norm look broadly held) are near: an inauthentic share inflating an
  apparent consensus.
- **THEORY-659** (most recurring manipulation channels are not
  attributed) bears on how far the Saudi attribution can go.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

Faithful as to what Tortoise reports. A2: the investigation "found
evidence of coordinated, inauthentic pro-Depp social-media activity". The
publisher's own wording is hedged, nothing read says Depp organized the
activity, and the jury point appears only as a question.

## Limitations

The episodes were not heard and no transcript was read. The findings are
the publisher's summary of commissioned analyses whose methods and data
were not seen. The page dates are ambiguous between member and public
release.
