# CLAIM-548: A motivational process can become active, and act, without any expectation that its response will succeed: its activation, the authority the organization grants it and the efficacy of what it does are separate quantities, so a threshold on expected net benefit is one special case of an activation threshold and not its general form

## The claim

A17 made a perspective's activation depend on the best net improvement it
expects from intervening:
Δ_i(w, c) = sup_u [J_i(w^{do(u)}, c) − J_i(w, c) − C_i(u)], with
Pr(a_i = 1) = σ(κ_i[Δ_i − θ_i]). Its table glossed θ_i as "How promising
or consequential an intervention must be before i mobilizes".

The owner, at U18: "an intervention doesn't need to be predicted to be
effective to be motivating. consider someone punching a wall in anger."

A18 conceded: "I made activation depend on instrumental rationality when
our framework should allow motivational activation without any expectation
of instrumental success." It separated three quantities:

| Quantity | A18's question |
|---|---|
| Motivational salience | "How strongly is a perspective disposed toward an action or response?" |
| Activation threshold | "How much activation is required for that disposition to recruit action or control?" |
| Practical efficacy | "Does the resulting action actually change what the perspective seeks to change?" |

"None entails the next." In the revised model Pr(a_i = 1) = σ(κ_i[z_i −
θ_i]), and z_i "could arise from anger, urgency, incentive salience, an
experienced discrepancy, a cue, a habit, or some combination". Then "π_i
need not select an action by maximizing expected consequences."

The claim has three parts.

1. **Activation is not an expected-benefit computation.** The instrumental
   rule is kept for what A18 calls "deliberative intervention selection".
2. **Activation, authority and efficacy come apart.** A18: "A perspective
   can be intensely motivated but inhibited. It can become active without
   being effective. And it can accidentally produce an effective result
   without having anticipated it."
3. **Acting is not the same as intervening in governance.** A18: "punching
   the wall is an action, but it is not necessarily an *attempt to revise
   the organization of the will*." A18 therefore keeps "intervention
   threshold" for "the narrower case in which the activated response
   attempts to alter an ongoing process or governing arrangement."

## Where it sits

- **Frijda's action readiness ([TERM-094](../terms.d/TERM-094.md)).** It is "not a representation of
  a desirable state" and claims control precedence. That is the
  individual-scale basis of part 1.
- **[CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md).** Claimed precedence is not granted authority. That is
  part 2's middle step, and the claim rests on it.
- **[CLAIM-459](CLAIM-459.md)** is the converse case. An input to a bistable balance can be
  efficacious on the balance without changing which regime acts.
- **[CLAIM-156](CLAIM-156.md).** Noticing an impulse as an impulse suspends the step from
  its dominance to its control of action. That is the inhibited arm of
  part 2.
- **Wall-punching at four levels**, from A18: the action tendency, its
  activation, the effective will, and the Frankfurtian higher-order
  volition. "The fourth may oppose the first three."

## The rival reading

Moors, Boddez and De Houwer 2017 ([LIT-1434](../literature.d/LIT-1434.md), read in [NOTE-1117](../notes.d/NOTE-1117.md)) make
goal-directed processes the default cause of emotional action. Those
processes weigh options by the values and expectancies of their outcomes.
In [NOTE-1117](../notes.d/NOTE-1117.md)'s words, "Costly aggression, for instance, may serve a
higher-valued outcome such as repaired self-esteem (p. 18)." On that
reading the wall-punch is instrumental after all, toward an outcome other
than the problem.

The record already parts from Moors on the common currency ([CLAIM-440](CLAIM-440.md)).
[NOTE-1117](../notes.d/NOTE-1117.md) adds that "A lexicographic reading of the same cases is open."
The wall-punch is a case on which the two readings divide. This claim
takes the side the owner took. Its defeater is Moors's reading made good
on such cases.

## What it does not say

- That activation is never instrumental. Expected benefit can drive
  activation, and A18 lists "anticipated consequences" among the sources
  of z_i.
- That the expected-benefit model is useless. It is the special case for
  deliberate choice among interventions.
- That an activated, effective impulse is disowned. Whether the person
  endorses it is a further question ([THEORY-120](../theory.d/THEORY-120.md)).
- That an organization can be angry. [QUESTION-044](../questions.d/QUESTION-044.md) asks that, and the claim
  is stated for processes.
- Anything about how often non-instrumental activation occurs.
