# NOTE-tmpwgqyb: Chen — Realism about the Wave Function

## Contribution

Chen surveys how a realist should understand the wave function, the puzzling central object of quantum mechanics. Setting aside instrumentalist and epistemic readings, he sorts realist proposals into ontological interpretations, nomological interpretations, and a sui generis interpretation. The survey is confined to non-relativistic quantum mechanics and ties each option to the main solutions of the measurement problem.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (p. 2): frames the landscape as instrumentalism vs epistemicism vs realism, noting that the PBR theorem (Pusey et al. 2012) rules out a class of epistemic readings; the article restricts itself to realism.
- §3 (pp. 8–13): four ontological readings — a field on high-dimensional configuration space (Albert's "wave function realism", which Chen says is no longer an apt label), a multi-field on 3D space, properties of physical systems, and a vector in Hilbert space.
- §4 (pp. 13–16): strong nomological readings model Ψ on the classical Hamiltonian in Bohmian mechanics (no back-reaction, so law-like); weak nomological readings are flagged (fn. 28) as possibly "too cheap", since the same move would banish classical fields from the ontology.
- §5 (p. 16): the sui generis option, citing Maudlin (2013) — the wave function is neither ontological nor nomological.
- Table 1 (p. 17): a compatibility matrix of each interpretation against BM, GRW0/m/f, S0/Sm and high- vs low-dimensional fundamental space.
- §6 (p. 17): no winner; the list may not be exhaustive; pointers to book-length treatments (Ney & Albert 2013, Lewis 2016, Gao 2017, Ney 2018).
