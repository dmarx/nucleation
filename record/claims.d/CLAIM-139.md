# CLAIM-139: A communicative kind's identity is grounded in its relational organization rather than in any expression or encoding; statistical methods recover aspects of that organization from restricted observations, translation transports it, and fidelity is which independently identifiable, decision-relevant relational distinctions persist

## The claim

The thesis was stated three times on 2026-10-10, each time on request.

- **A203 X** (U56, 02:52, "layout the full argument from top to bottom for
  me"), after the new question ([QUESTION-029](../questions.d/QUESTION-029.md)): "Our answer is that
  identity is relationally grounded, characterizable through appropriate
  invariants, accessible through restricted observations, and preserved only
  selectively under stochastic transformation." Its closing box ends:
  "Fidelity measures which structural distinctions remain recoverable, usable,
  and stable under admissible transport."
- **A214's thesis paragraph** (U59, 03:19, "develop this further, then tie it
  all together"): "Factorization and other statistical methods recover
  candidate organizational kinds from restricted observations; identifiability
  theory specifies when such recovery is warranted. … Fidelity is the
  task-relative persistence of independently anchored relational
  distinctions, evaluated through observations, causal responses, and
  decisions."
- **A218, opening the October proposal** (U60, 03:26, "generate a new proposal
  and outline based on this new synthesis"): "**Concrete objects and
  communicative acts exist as relata within structured systems, but their
  qualitative identities are grounded in their relational organization rather
  than intrinsic representational labels. Statistical methods can recover
  aspects of that organization from restricted observations. Translation,
  adaptation, and generation transport it across representations. Fidelity
  concerns which independently identifiable, decision-relevant relational
  distinctions persist through those transformations.**"

The record files A218's wording, which is the latest and the fullest. It has
four clauses, and each rests on a separate premise:

- **Ontological.** Identity is grounded in relational organization. This is
  partial constitution of the act by its relations to participants, norms and
  uptake ([CLAIM-151](CLAIM-151.md)).
- **Epistemic.** Structure is accessible only through restricted
  observations, and agreement on a restricted probe family is not structural
  identity ([CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md)). A218 §5 separates five objects of inquiry:
  "Observed relational evidence. Latent explanatory representations.
  Statistically identifiable structure. Task-sufficient structural
  information. Philosophically proposed constitutive relations."
  Identifiability reaches the third at most ([CLAIM-135](CLAIM-135.md)).
- **Transport.** Translation transports the organization, by admissible maps
  that keep designated distinctions apart ([CLAIM-077](CLAIM-077.md)).
- **Fidelity.** What persists is judged by decision relevance
  ([CLAIM-050](CLAIM-050.md)), measured as a profile ([TERM-043](../terms.d/TERM-043.md)).

The unit of identity is the communicative kind ([TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)), which A218
uses throughout and never defines. A218 closes on the question this thesis
answers: "**How can a relationally constituted kind remain identifiable and
useful when the representation through which we encounter it changes?**"

## How it differs from [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) and from the v7 thesis

- **The question widened.** [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md) answers [QUESTION-002](../questions.d/QUESTION-002.md). A203 X
  declares that question superseded: "The final question is no longer simply
  *what does a translation preserve?*" Translation becomes one case of the
  identity and persistence of relationally organized things.
- **An ontology is placed above the fidelity clause.** Neither [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)
  nor the v7 thesis ([CLAIM-133](CLAIM-133.md)) makes a claim about what exists.
- **[CLAIM-017](CLAIM-017.md)'s invariants come back as classification.** A203's
  "characterizable through appropriate invariants" sits beside recoverability
  as the criterion. [CLAIM-017](CLAIM-017.md)'s body records that the move from invariants to
  the receiver "decides which of the two is the criterion, and nothing in the
  transcript argues it". A203 assigns the two to different layers. That is a
  layering, not an argument for either.
- **The receiver is dropped at A218.** A203 still has "recoverable, usable",
  and v7 had "appropriately situated observers". A218 has "independently
  identifiable" ([CLAIM-140](CLAIM-140.md)).
- **The fidelity clause is redefined in one sentence.** A214's "task-relative
  persistence of independently anchored relational distinctions" drops
  [TERM-034](../terms.d/TERM-034.md)'s communication constraint and its interpreter. No turn argues
  the change, and the record does not file the sentence as a TERM.
- **Contextuality is demoted by announcement.** A218: "This replaces the
  earlier emphasis on contextuality as the organizing framework. Contextuality
  remains important, but it now occupies a specific role within a more
  coherent theory of observation and transport." No reason is given beyond
  "more coherent". The literary cases are gone from the outline (the workbench
  entry for U57–U60, 2026-10-10).

## What it does not say

A218 §13: "This project does not claim that matrix factorization proves ontic
structural realism." The ontological clause "must be defended through
argument concerning social acts". It is not derived from the models. The
exchange concedes the point that matters for credence (A214 §13): "A
traditional ontology of intrinsically individuated objects plus relational
properties might reproduce the same statistical predictions." So the empirical
programme tests the epistemic, transport and fidelity clauses, not the
ontological one. Identification is not constitution ([CLAIM-150](CLAIM-150.md)).

It is filed for communicative kinds. A203's preamble, A214 and A218 say
"objects" in general, but A203 §35's own ontological thesis is scoped to
"Communicative acts and other structured phenomena", and nothing in the
exchange argues the general version.

It keeps "partly". A214 wavers between "partly constituted" (§14) and
"relationally constituted" without qualification (its thesis paragraph). Only
the partial reading is the premise this entry rests on.
