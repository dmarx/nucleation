# THEORY-tmph4reb: The content of a crowd-built r/place artwork is decided off the canvas, in negotiation through shared design images, where a few central members admit or refuse additions, and carried out by placers who mostly never join the discussion, so the placement record shows the outcome of coordination but not who directed it or who cooperated

## Source

Litherland & Mørch (2021), LIT-1037, read in full in NOTE-733. What the
case study showed for the 2017 Mona Lisa is THEORY-218, which this entry
extends.

## The claim

The paper proposes a three-level framework in which a visual artifact, the
image, and a social artifact, the forum, evolve together. "The decision of
what to add or replace was (for the most part) made at the group
interaction (meaning) level, whereas actions were carried out on the user
action level." Design images linked in the forum carry the decisions;
central members "had the power to allow and/or stop suggestions from being
acted" on; whether an addition is vandalism or part of the image is "a
tradeoff to be negotiated". And the authors argue generally that
"quantitative methods cannot identify whether the visual images were the
result of collaboration or conflict", so that "community affiliation cannot
be identified with quantitative measures alone". They point to similar
negotiated composites elsewhere on the canvas (Fig. 4).

The evidence is one artwork. About 77,000 accounts placed tiles in its
region and the forum had 161, most placers never posted, and how many
followed the designs was not measured: "the causal relationship between
WAD or power and user action remains unclear". THEORY-218 holds that case.

## What would settle it

The promote_when, which QUESTION-306 also asks: a study across many
artworks of one canvas, pairing the placement log with time-stamped
coordination records, showing design revisions leading placements, a small
identifiable core making or approving them, and placements a behaviour-only
method would misassign. More excerpts from single artworks cannot settle
it.
