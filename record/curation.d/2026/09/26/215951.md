A pass over the `ethics` tag, the same shape as the epistemology pass: audit
the tag, then read every unread work on it in full.

## The audit

Thirty-four works carried the tag. Twenty had been read. Five had been tried in
earlier passes and could not be reached, so they were not retried: [LIT-117](../../record/literature.d/LIT-117.md),
[LIT-129](../../record/literature.d/LIT-129.md), [LIT-137](../../record/literature.d/LIT-137.md), [LIT-186](../../record/literature.d/LIT-186.md) and [LIT-197](../../record/literature.d/LIT-197.md). That left eight to read.

One untagged work now carries `ethics`. [LIT-206](../../record/literature.d/LIT-206.md) (Goldstein & Lederman), in its
§7, argues that attributing desires to a chatbot does not settle welfare. It
also argues that on a social-contract view such desires could still warrant
moral consideration. A reader browsing AI moral status would expect it.

One work lost the tag. [LIT-101](../../record/literature.d/LIT-101.md) (Fitouchi, André & Baumard, *moral
disciplining*) is descriptive moral psychology and cultural evolution. It is
explicitly agnostic about whether puritanical norms work, and it draws no
normative conclusion. Its tags are now `social-science` and `cognition`. The
reader suggested `religion` too, but the paper mentions religion only in
passing, so it was not added.

## The readings

Four works were read in full, and all are `Active`:

- [LIT-098](../../record/literature.d/LIT-098.md), Veit, on health concepts and social justice. Read from the
  PhilSci-Archive preprint; the journal version sat behind a bot challenge.
- [LIT-101](../../record/literature.d/LIT-101.md), the moral-disciplining paper. Read from the accepted manuscript on
  OSF.
- [LIT-179](../../record/literature.d/LIT-179.md), Schmauder et al., *Algorithmic Nudging*.
- [LIT-198](../../record/literature.d/LIT-198.md), Hooker's SEP entry *Rule Consequentialism*.

The corrections, as in the epistemology pass, mostly took claims away from the
seeds:

- **Veit.** The skim had him "conceding" that Kukla can hold the institution
  of medicine fixed. That is the second horn of his own dilemma: holding
  medicine fixed makes the account normativist "in name only".
- **Schmauder et al.** The seed said AI systems "can learn nudges that exploit
  biases nobody has documented". Both case studies exploit documented biases.
  The undocumented case is a conjecture.
- **Hooker.** The seed had the view survive by "acceptance by less than
  everyone and a prevent-disaster rule". That misassigns the replies. Ranking
  rules by the expected good of teaching and accepting them answers the
  collapse objection. The prevent-disaster rule answers only rule-worship. The
  90% threshold is there for punishment rules.

## Four works that could not be reached

These stay `Deferred`, and each status note says what was tried:

- [LIT-128](../../record/literature.d/LIT-128.md) and [LIT-214](../../record/literature.d/LIT-214.md): Taylor & Francis refused the requests.
- [LIT-174](../../record/literature.d/LIT-174.md): a book with no open copy.
- [LIT-183](../../record/literature.d/LIT-183.md): Springer closed access. Its publisher abstract does state the
  conclusion, and the status note now carries it.

So of the thirty-four works on the tag, nine remain unread, all for access
reasons. None of the nine is openly available anywhere a legitimate route
found.
