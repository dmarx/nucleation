# CODE: Bound operators delegate to their side's extremes

*A part synthesis within the conferral-and-authority part of the
distributed-agency line (SYNTHESIS-050): the owner's grant design of the
exchange of 2026-10-13, the accounting results that make it precise, and
LEAD-190's game that ran it. SYNTHESIS-050 integrates it; SYNTHESIS-087
(formal-apparatus) holds the accounting results too, and SYNTHESIS-111 holds
CLAIM-1029. Written at the synthesis review of 2026-11-26, when CLAIM-1147
and the run's other results were in no synthesis. It adds nothing. Its
standing is its components'.*

## The question

Can a grant scheme deny each operator control over its own authority and
still make delegation do work, and when it does, to whom do operators
delegate, and what does that do to the population's factions and shared
meaning?

## The argument

**Accounting decides whether an operator controls its own authority.**
Forbidding self-grants does not stop self-amplification: under damped
recursive delegation, reciprocal and cyclic grants recycle authority, while
one-hop allocation within an epoch, from budgets and baselines fixed apart
from outgoing grants, makes an operator's authority exactly independent of
them (CLAIM-282, granted, with the reciprocal pair of CASE-077).
Leave-one-out delegation, counting only paths that have not passed through
the operator, keeps that independence beyond one hop (CLAIM-502).

**With authority fixed, a grant to an ally pays in realized policy.** Under
one-hop allocation and an authority-weighted mean decision, granting to an
ally whose policy is nearer one's own than the decision is moves the
decision toward one's policy while one's authority stays fixed, so both
conditions of the scheme hold together (CLAIM-375). The owner's design is
then that operators rewarded for how far their preferred policies are
realized, not for authority held, delegate to aligned others, and that the
two conditions need not be confined to one hop (CLAIM-285, Proposed, with
the skeptic who delegates of CASE-080).

**Run, bound operators delegate wholly to their own side, and within it to
its extremes.** In LEAD-190's grant game on thirty populations (CASE-420),
operators bound by both conditions delegated their whole budget and only
to their own side, beyond one hop too under leave-one-out; unbound
operators delegated at about chance, and authority-seekers under a bound
rule never delegated (CLAIM-1081). Within its side an operator gains most
by granting to the ally farthest from the decision, since a grant's
marginal value is proportional to p_j − p*, not to the ally's nearness, and
the run's grants correlated 0.88 to 0.92 with the grantee's extremity
(CLAIM-1147, resting on CLAIM-375). CLAIM-375's condition makes the
gradient positive; it does not set the ranking. So CLAIM-285's "aligned"
holds without qualification if it means on one's own side of the decision,
and picks the wrong allies if it means nearest in policy; the two readings
are the owner's to choose between (CLAIM-1147).

**Grants by role keep disagreement inside without capture.** Granting
influence by role lets an operator empower another as a critic while
opposing its policy, so disagreement can work inside an organization
rather than sort into factions (CLAIM-281, Proposed). In the same run,
role-indexed grants with critics scored by observed relevance left neither
factions nor capture, while scalar grants failed one way or the other:
scored by agreement they captured the population or split it, and moved
by the reward for realized policy they split it into factions reading
only their own side (CLAIM-1029). The shared meaning then settles where the
competence-weighted side of the specialists puts it, so it follows where
competence happens to sit and can override a weak imbalance in the
evidence (CLAIM-1150).

What stays open is the line's question of a self-maintaining, internally
differentiated governance that acts coherently without suppressing the
plurality it comes from (QUESTION-090), and when a destabilizing process
improves an organization (QUESTION-089).

## Where it stands

The accounting results and the game's are demonstrated and Active, of
their model; CLAIM-285 and CLAIM-281, the owner's design and A's role
proposal, are Proposed. CLAIM-1147 is about the weighted-mean decision
alone, and in balanced populations the decision stays central; an
unbalanced population was not run.

## Not stated on the record

- **Two routes by which the extremes carry weight.** CLAIM-1147's
  delegation to a side's most extreme members and CLAIM-493's group
  process, in SYNTHESIS-052, by which a fanbase's more extreme members
  become more prototypical against an outside party, both give a
  collective's extremes more weight than their number. No entry joins
  them. Tested at this review, the sentence that joins them is a
  conjunction of two mechanisms with nothing derived and no case in which
  both hold and it fails, so it is not filed.
- **CLAIM-1147 in an unbalanced population.** Its body says the gradient
  would pull the decision toward the larger side's extremes; that is a
  prediction of the model not run, not a result.

## What it does not say

That operators in any real organization behave so; that grants to the
extremes are bad for the organization; or anything about decision rules
other than the weighted mean.
