# CLAIM-tmp7lzto: Mindfulness integrates a population of interests by changing which of them may act, not by changing what they value: noticing an impulse as an impulse suspends the step from its momentary dominance to its control of action, and leaves the discount curves as they were

## The claim

Where an agent's valuations cross ([CLAIM-tmpmf12z](CLAIM-tmpmf12z.md)), there are three things
that can stop the sooner reward from taking control at the moment it
dominates:

- **Outweigh it.** Bundling stakes the series on this choice ([THEORY-067](../theory.d/THEORY-067.md)).
  The curves still cross, and the bargain is fragile: legalism, lapse
  districts.
- **Remove the crossing.** This is flow ([CLAIM-tmpf301t](CLAIM-tmpf301t.md)): there is nothing
  to stop.
- **Leave the crossing and break the step from dominance to control.**
  This claim says mindfulness does this.

ACT describes the mindful stance as holding a thought or urge as a thought
or urge, from a perspective that is "not the content" ([THEORY-051](../theory.d/THEORY-051.md)). On
Metzinger's reading ([THEORY-064](../theory.d/THEORY-064.md)), attending to a content as a construction
makes it opaque, so it is no longer lived simply as oneself. Put in the
will-organization line's terms, the urge stays the most valued option at
that delay. What changes is its standing as the agent's present action. The
urge is still accessible, but its claim to act is suspended ([TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md)) while
it is held as an object. [CLAIM-027](CLAIM-027.md) says which regime is effective depends on
relations like these and not on the competing regimes' contents. Mindfulness
is then a second individual-scale case of that thesis, beside the reluctant
writer's precommitment device ([ARG-005](../arguments.d/ARG-005.md)), and unlike the device it is
internal and reversible.

## What would tell the routes apart

- **Discounting measured before and after.** On this claim it should be
  unchanged, while response inhibition and choice under temptation
  improve. Dixon et al. ([LIT-tmp8dmxy](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8dmxy.md), n = 23) report the opposite: a
  5-minute exercise lowered measured discounting. Ge et al. ([LIT-tmpz3v2y](../literature.d/LIT-tmpz3v2y.md)),
  as summarised, report some impulsive behaviours reduced and discounting
  not improved. Both are unread. If Dixon et al. holds up, part of
  mindfulness's effect is on the flow route instead.
- **No precedent effects.** Bargaining makes lapses costly to credibility.
  Suspension stakes nothing, so a lapse in mindful practice should not
  lower later self-control the way a broken personal rule does.

## What it does not say

- **That mindfulness never changes valuation.** Sustained practice may well
  do so. The claim is about the mechanism by which it integrates a
  population at the moment of temptation.
- **That decentering is a self.** IFS's Self ([LIT-591](../literature.d/LIT-591.md)) leads; ACT's self
  as context does not. This claim needs only the second.
