summary: >-
  Petkova & Ehrsson (2008). Participants in head-mounted displays saw a male mannequin's body from cameras at its eyes while a rod stroked its abdomen and theirs, in time or out of time. In time, they affirmed "It felt like the mannequin's body was my body" (n = 32) and gave larger skin conductance responses when a knife "cut" the mannequin than after asynchronous stroking or a spoon (n = 10). The response spread from stroked hands to the threatened abdomen (n = 13) and was absent for a body-sized box (n = 12). Facing the experimenter through cameras on the experimenter's head and squeezing hands in time, participants responded more to a knife over the experimenter's wrist than over their own, which they could see (n = 20). The samples are small and separate, the questionnaire analysis treats ratings as independent, and the first-person view is not manipulated.

# LIT-tmp4qdyy: If I Were You: Perceptual Illusion of Body Swapping

Petkova, V. I. & Ehrsson, H. H. (2008). *PLoS ONE* 3(12):e3832. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0003832.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpb1ult](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpb1ult.md).*

- **The mannequin illusion (Exp. 1, n = 32).** Two minutes of synchronous
  stroking of the seen mannequin's abdomen and the unseen real one raised
  agreement with three illusion statements (S1–S3) over asynchronous
  stroking. The mean for S3, "It felt like the mannequin's body was my
  body", is about +1.3 on a −3 to +3 scale (Fig. 2).
- **Threat-evoked skin conductance (Exp. 2, n = 10).** A knife "cutting"
  the mannequin after synchronous stroking evoked larger responses than
  after asynchronous stroking or than a spoon.
- **Generalisation (Exp. 3, n = 13).** Stroking the hands in time, like
  stroking the abdomen, raised the response to a threat to the abdomen
  over its asynchronous control. The two sites were not compared with
  each other.
- **Shape (Exp. 4, n = 12).** No synchronous-over-asynchronous effect for
  a body-sized green box (p = .819). The box drew less response than the
  mannequin even after asynchronous stroking (Fig. 5).
- **The swap (Exp. 5, n = 20).** Cameras on the experimenter's head;
  participant and experimenter squeeze hands. After squeezing in time, a
  knife over the experimenter's wrist evoked more response than one over
  the participant's own visible wrist; Hand × Condition interaction
  F(1, 19) = 17.08.
- **Claimed but not tested.** That a first-person view is a "critical
  condition", and that the illusion moves a "centre of awareness". No
  third-person condition and no measure of self-location were run.

## Standing in the record

The record's first primary experiment on body ownership. It is filed
beside the works [NOTE-372](../notes.d/NOTE-372.md) cites for C6, and it covers the half of C6 they
do not: ownership of a whole body seen from where one's own eyes are,
where Lenggenhager et al. 2007 showed a body seen from behind. Its
physiological measure is one way out of reliance on questionnaires, but
the measure is weaker than the paper says ([NOTE-tmpb1ult](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpb1ult.md)). It is
reviewed, and placed among later full-body illusions, by Kilteni et al.
2015 ([LIT-tmpt4bij](LIT-tmpt4bij.md)). Filed as [THEORY-tmpvh682](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpvh682.md). The phrase "body swap"
is a perceptual one. It bears on Williams's thought experiment
([LIT-462](LIT-462.md)) only by name: nothing here swaps memories or character. It
carries no instruction for machine-learning practice.
