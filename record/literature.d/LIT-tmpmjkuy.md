summary: >-
  Ord (2025). A framework for comparing ways present action could change humanity's long-run future. Humanity's "instantaneous value" over time is a curve; the future's value is the area under it, and an intervention's value is the area between the default and the altered curve. Value must be roughly quantitative and separable across time, with no pure time preference. Four idealised changes have values that do not depend on the curve's shape: an advancement (the future reached δt sooner) is worth about (v_τ − v_0)δt, independent of the future's duration τ, and about −v_0δt if the end comes sooner too; a speed-up scales with (v_τ − v̄)τ; a gain (a fixed rise in value) with τ; an enhancement (a proportional rise) with v̄τ. Raising the chance of surviving near-term existential risk by a factor γ_p is worth (γ_p − 1)v̄τ, like an enhancement, and meets the bar of contingency more easily. So it is hard for advancing progress to rival existential risk reduction: on a million-year future, a year's advancement is worth about a one-in-a-million gain in survival chance.
