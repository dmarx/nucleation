# CLAIM-1034: Diversity is generally an accurate measure of an ecosystem's richness, and the account of querying and answering as resource-bounded, jointly feasible activities explains why

## The claim

The owner's words, at U31: "i think the ideas we're touching on also
clarifies why diversity is generally an accurate measure of an ecosystem's
richness".

The ideas in question are the turns just before it: that formulating a
question is itself a resource-bounded realization ([CLAIM-475](CLAIM-475.md)), that what
can be asked and what can be answered compete for shared time, and that
the frontier of inquiry is a joint object of a query side and an answer
side (U30). The thesis has two parts: that diversity is *generally* an
accurate measure of richness, and that this account says why.

## What it does not say

- Not that diversity is always an accurate measure. "Generally" leaves
  room for the cases A60 itself names: redundant participants, and
  diversity that consumes what the existing capabilities need.
- Not which index of diversity (a count of kinds, an evenness, a measure
  sensitive to how different the kinds are). That is [QUESTION-529](../questions.d/QUESTION-529.md).
- Not that diversity is welfare or justice. A60 adds that it is "evidence
  of potential richness, not ... an unconditional measure of welfare or
  justice", resting on [CLAIM-546](CLAIM-546.md).

## Standing

Proposed, the owner's. A60 develops it ([CLAIM-1038](CLAIM-1038.md), with the sense of
richness in [TERM-158](../terms.d/TERM-158.md)). The assistant's check of A60's coverage model
([CLAIM-1042](CLAIM-1042.md)) finds that the model supports the first part only under
stated conditions ([ARG-222](../arguments.d/ARG-222.md)), so whether "generally" holds turns on
how often ecosystems meet them.
