# LIT-714: The Method of Characteristics Revisited: A Viability Approach

Jean-Pierre Aubin (1999), "The Method of Characteristics Revisited: A
Viability Approach. A Mini-Course", lecture notes, Université de
Paris-Dauphine — ARXIV-math/9906178

## Key takeaways

- **Viability is a property of a set under a dynamics.** An evolution is
  viable in K on [0, T] if it stays in K. K is viable under f if from every
  initial state in K at least one solution stays in K, and invariant if
  every solution does (Definitions 1.1.1–1.1.2). Viability "depends only on
  the behavior of f on K"; invariance depends on f outside K too (remark,
  p. 7).
- **Nagumo's theorem.** For locally compact K and continuous f, K is
  locally viable if and only if f(x) lies in the contingent cone to K at
  every x in K (Theorem 1.3.1): at each state, the velocity must not point
  out of K.
- **When K is not viable,** two questions arise: from which states can an
  evolution stay in K as long as possible, and from outside K, which can
  return to it soonest (chapter 2 introduction). The answers are the
  viability kernel (the states of K from which some solution stays in K for
  ever; the largest viable subset of K, Proposition 2.1.5) and the capture
  basin (the states from which some solution reaches a target in finite
  time). Outside the kernel, every evolution leaves K in finite time
  (Proposition 2.3.2): C minus its kernel is a repeller.
- **"Stability" in these notes is not Lyapunov stability.** §2.6, "Stability
  Properties", concerns whether viability survives taking limits of sets.
  The notes do not compare viability with the stability of an equilibrium
  or an attractor; that comparison is not made here.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as the readable source for an
essay's appeal to "Aubin's mathematical theory of viability" (§6.1). The
record's account is [THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md).

What the notes give the essay is a precise vocabulary: a state space,
dynamics, a constraint set K fixed independently of the dynamics, and the
kernel of states that can keep satisfying K. What they do not give it is
the comparison with stability. That is the essay's own step, and it needs
the essay to say what K is for an organization.
