# TERM-156: crosscutting and nested factorizations

## Definition

Let *P* and *Q* be two factorizations (partitions into candidate agents)
of one system at one level and target.

- *Q* is a **coarsening** of *P* (and *P* a **refinement** of *Q*) when
  every block of *P* lies inside some block of *Q*. *P* and *Q* are then
  **nested**: they agree on every co-grouping *P* makes, *Q* only merging
  some of *P*'s agents into larger ones.
- *P* and *Q* **crosscut** when neither is a coarsening of the other.
  Equivalently, there is a pair of regimes *i, j* in the same agent under
  *P* but in different agents under *Q*, and another pair in the same agent
  under *Q* but different under *P*. The two then *disagree* on which
  regimes an agent governs, in a way no change of grain reconciles.

## Why the record needs it

[CLAIM-170](../claims.d/CLAIM-170.md)'s thesis expects *nested* plurality: the room's one agent
and the pair's two are the same system factored at two grains. Its
`defeated_if` — "two equally good factorizations that disagree on which
regimes each agent's will-organization governs, with nothing at that level
to choose" — is a threat only if read as a **crosscut**; a nested pair is
the predicted relativity, not a defeater. [CLAIM-963](../claims.d/CLAIM-963.md) shows that at a
fixed level and target the self-consistent factorizations are always
nested and never crosscut, so the two cases must be named apart.

## What it is not

- **Not the same as fine versus coarse.** Nesting is a relation between two
  given factorizations, not a property of one. Two factorizations at the
  same number of blocks can still be nested-incomparable, i.e. crosscut.
- **Not a measure of distance.** It is a yes/no relation (comparable under
  refinement or not), prior to any metric on partitions.
