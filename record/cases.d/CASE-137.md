# CASE-137: Clive Wearing: a musician left by encephalitis with almost no memory of his past and none of the last minutes, who still plays, sight-reads and conducts, keeps his attachment to his wife, and writes over and over that he has just become conscious for the first time

## The case

Clive Wearing, a British musician and musicologist, developed herpes
simplex encephalitis in 1985. It left him with very dense anterograde
amnesia, so that he retains new experience for seconds to minutes, and
extensive retrograde amnesia for his past. His musical skill survived: he
can play, sight-read and conduct. Wilson, Baddeley and Kapur's case study
([LIT-1369](../literature.d/LIT-1369.md)) documents both. He kept diaries in which entry after entry
records that he has just woken, or just become conscious, crossing out the
earlier entries. Shown such entries, or recordings of himself, he
recognized his handwriting or his playing and denied having been conscious
then (Wilson, Kopelman and Kapur, [LIT-1368](../literature.d/LIT-1368.md)). His attachment to his
wife, Deborah, endured.

Both papers are registered unread. The facts above are those the exchange
gave from them and that the papers are standardly cited for.

## What it shows

A26 drew four continuities and found two intact: "skill continuity",
"short-horizon organization", "autobiographical continuity" and
"experienced diachronic ownership". "His condition does not consist in
every psychological process resetting every few seconds." So:

- **Organization persists where narrative fails.** Against [THEORY-060](../theory.d/THEORY-060.md)'s
  life-story identity, and for Strawson's episodic selves ([NOTE-383](../notes.d/NOTE-383.md)), a
  person persists, acts in a coordinated way and performs skilled work
  with almost no narrative continuity. In [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md)'s terms, the
  perspective's realization continues while its representation fails.
- **Self-report does not track the organization.** A26: "Recognizing
  evidence that an earlier activity occurred is not the same as
  experiencing or accepting that activity as part of one's own remembered
  past." The self-model misdescribes the history of the organization it
  models, the general form of [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md)'s constructed owner.
- **Continuity can be held outside.** His competence was individuated
  "relative to the stable environmental and interpersonal supports" (A26),
  which is [QUESTION-256](../questions.d/QUESTION-256.md)'s question and Otto's notebook ([NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md)) in
  a real person.

## What it does not show

- A26: "it does **not** establish that the unity of conscious experience is
  an illusion". The owner's thesis at U26, that unity of self is an
  illusion, holds here for a single owner of a continuous past, not for
  the unity of present experience ([QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md)).
- A26: "Wearing's inability to remember being conscious is not evidence
  that he wasn't conscious." "The inference from no unified substantial
  owner to no real phenomenal experience is invalid."
- A26: "His ability to learn entirely new musical pieces was not
  established." The case is not one of a better regime learned and then
  lost, which is how the owner first placed it; A26 called it "selective
  disruption of the machinery supporting different timescales of
  psychological continuity".
- A26: "I would not treat clinical cases as interventions on freely
  manipulable independent variables."
