# LIT-tmplumoc: Moving a Rubber Hand that Feels Like Your Own: A Dissociation of Ownership and Agency

Kalckert, A. & Ehrsson, H. H. (2012). Moving a rubber hand that feels like your own: a dissociation of ownership and agency. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 6, 40. DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2012.00040.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp9gagi](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9gagi.md).*

- **A moving rubber hand is owned.** When a model finger moves in time
  with one's own hidden finger, the model hand is rated as one's own and
  the felt position of the real finger drifts toward it (1.73 cm against
  −0.35 cm under asynchrony). Drift correlated with ownership ratings
  (r = 0.56, n = 17) and not with agency ratings.
- **Ownership without agency.** When the experimenter moved both fingers
  together, ownership stayed (1.57) and agency went (−0.61). Drift did not
  differ between active and passive movement.
- **Agency without ownership.** With the model hand rotated 180°, active
  movement still gave agency (2.2) but not ownership (−0.62), and no drift.
  The authors call this the first direct demonstration of a double
  dissociation in one rubber hand paradigm.
- **Synchrony is needed for both.** A ~500 ms asynchrony removed
  ownership and agency together.
- **Not independent.** Agency was higher when the hand was in an
  anatomical position, even in passive conditions, and correlated with
  ownership only when the hand was owned and moved actively. The authors
  read this as a distinct "body agency" beside agency over external
  objects, on indirect evidence.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-20 at the owner's request for experimental work building
on the rubber hand illusion that bears on the batch's accounts of bodily
self-consciousness. Read in full from the open-access version of record:
**Active**.

It is the record's first primary experiment on the sense of agency, and
the first in which ownership and agency are manipulated against each
other. It bears most on [THEORY-458](../theory.d/THEORY-458.md): it shows the two senses coming apart
outside involuntary movement, in the direction involuntary movement cannot
give (agency without ownership), which answers one of that THEORY's
refutation routes, though not its comparator or thought-insertion half.
It extends the synchrony effect of [THEORY-tmpef8lf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpef8lf.md) from brushing to
movement, with drift absent under asynchrony and under an incongruent
posture, but has no expectation control. Its passive condition is the
"adults whose agency is intact but unused" kind of evidence that
[THEORY-tmpuehzc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpuehzc.md) says cannot settle its agency thesis, and it measures
hand ownership only. It gives Haggard's ([LIT-1009](LIT-1009.md), [NOTE-827](../notes.d/NOTE-827.md)) point that
involuntary movement brings ownership without agency an experimental
instance, and its residual agency under asynchrony is weak, explicit-only
support for [THEORY-322](../theory.d/THEORY-322.md)'s prospective component. The NOTE sets this out.

Filed alongside, in the 2026-10-20 batch of experimental work on the
rubber hand illusion, by sibling readers: Lush 2020 and Lush et al. 2020
on demand characteristics and phenomenological control, the exchange
between Ehrsson et al. 2022 and Lush & Seth 2022, Roseboom & Lush 2022,
Rohde, Di Luca & Ernst 2011 on proprioceptive drift, Abdulkarim &
Ehrsson 2015, and Longo et al. 2008 on the questionnaire's structure.
This paper cites Rohde et al. for not relying on drift alone and Longo et
al. for its questionnaire items.
