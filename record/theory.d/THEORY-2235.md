# THEORY-2235: Teleosemantics derives content from function

## Source

Karen Neander and Peter Schulte, "Teleological Theories of Mental
Content", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (first published 2004,
substantive revision 26 May 2022), [LIT-3104](../literature.d/LIT-3104.md), read in full in
[NOTE-2325](../notes.d/NOTE-2325.md).

## What was actually shown

A survey, so what it shows is the field's shape as two of its members
report it:

- **The shared derivation** (§3): "psycho-semantic norms are ultimately
  derivable from functional norms". Functional norms mean only that
  malfunction is possible (§2), and fall "on the is side of any is/ought
  divide".
- **Etiological function** (§2): function is what an item or its type
  was selected for. Recent and maintenance selection, learning,
  conditioning, meme selection and neural selection count.
- **The division** (§§3.2-3.4): Millikan and Papineau fix content by
  what the consumer needs; Dretske and Neander by what the producer was
  selected to indicate or respond to. On the frog the two give different
  contents, so they differ on when it errs.
- **The objections** (§4): indeterminacy, Swampman, liberalism and
  sophisticated concepts, each with replies; none settled.
- **Variants** (§3.5): Shea counts past contributions to a system's own
  survival as stabilizing a function; Nanay and Piccinini give
  non-etiological teleosemantics.

## What it does not show

- That any of the theories is right; the survey reports and leaves open.
- Anything about artefacts, which it sets aside (§2).
- Anything about organizational accounts of function ([THEORY-072](THEORY-072.md),
  [THEORY-220](THEORY-220.md)), which it does not discuss.
