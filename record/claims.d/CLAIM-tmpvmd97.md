# CLAIM-tmpvmd97: A metric that governs allocates two powers, to make processes legible and to decide what follows from what it makes legible, so an accurate metric can still dominate where it gives a controller unanswerable discretion over those who depend on the arrangement; bounding by role which decisions a metric may govern, and keeping the authority to define success apart from the authority to pursue it, are the corresponding safeguards

## The claim

A44 opens: "a metric can become a constitutive constraint upon the
organization it purports to describe", so "The choice of a KPI is
therefore simultaneously a choice about representation and, when it
governs action, a choice about the organization of practical authority."

A44 §11: "A KPI architecture distributes two kinds of power
simultaneously: 1. Power to make processes legible. 2. Power to determine
what follows from that legibility." Its questions about a proprietary
performance score that decides workers' access to work separate
observational jurisdiction, interpretive authority, specification
authority, practical authority, reciprocal legibility, corrective
standing, constitutional authority, and dependence and exit. "A highly
accurate metric can still participate in domination if it gives a
controller inadequately answerable discretion over people who depend upon
the arrangement. Conversely, appropriate measurement can constrain
domination by exposing unfair treatment or arbitrary decisions."

## The safeguards

A44 §12: different metrics should play different roles (diagnostic,
contractual, protective, developmental, allocative, corrective,
reflexive). "A diagnostic metric need not become an individual performance
target. A protective indicator need not automatically establish
culpability." A44 §13: "The authority to define success must be
distinguishable from the authority to pursue it." Its ten requirements
(traceability, distributional fidelity, bounded jurisdiction, independent
validation, protected inquiry, contestability, privacy protection,
lifecycle accounting, capability protection, revisability) "are not a
universal checklist whose satisfaction guarantees justice", and "There are
real tensions among them": disaggregation exposes unequal treatment and
raises privacy risk.

A44 §16 puts the whole in one line: "The tyranny of metrics is ultimately
a failure to distinguish the representation of value from the authority
to determine value."

## What is held

- **Fibres.** A44 §2's J = j∘T criterion is CLAIM-350; its four workers
  are CASE-110's two members; CLAIM-348 is the normative point.
- **Weights.** A44 §3: "Some requirements should operate as constraints
  rather than as weighted preferences." CLAIM-347 holds joint realizability
  before scalarization. Which requirements are constraints A44 does not say;
  that is QUESTION-456's territory, how the demands are established.
- **Goodhart's mechanisms.** A44 §4's taxonomy relabels held entries:
  proxy inadequacy is TERM-108, performative agreement CLAIM-402 and
  Espeland and Sauder's reactivity (NOTE-1050), cost displacement CLAIM-760,
  capability erosion CLAIM-921 and CLAIM-463, epistemic closure
  CLAIM-tmpbbfcj. Its point that metric tyranny needs no deception, "even
  when every measurement is accurate, every participant acts
  conscientiously and every declared target is satisfied", is CLAIM-348's.
- **Who controls the test.** CLAIM-416: "a perspective that gains influence
  over the test can change what later success shows"; CLAIM-931: what keeps
  a loop from locking in is a score its own authority does not set. The
  separation of defining from pursuing success is their institutional form.
- **A documented case.** CASE-151, Wells Fargo's Community Bank: unwanted
  accounts the board's own investigation traced to the sales model and its
  performance management. A44 did not cite it; it is the record's case of
  a governing metric that the governed could not contest.
