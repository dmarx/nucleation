# LIT-1083: An Efficient, Cost-Driven Index Selection Tool for Microsoft SQL Server

Chaudhuri, S. and Narasayya, V. (1997). An efficient, cost-driven index selection tool for Microsoft SQL Server. In *Proceedings of the 23rd International Conference on Very Large Data Bases (VLDB '97)*, pp. 146–155. https://www.vldb.org/conf/1997/P146.PDF

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-900](../notes.d/NOTE-900.md)). The AutoAdmin tool picks at most k indexes (single- or two-column, clustered or not) for a given workload of SQL statements, minimising the unweighted sum of the optimizer's estimated costs of those statements, with hypothetical ("what-if") indexes made visible to the optimizer by loading their statistics into the catalog. Its contribution is search efficiency: candidates are restricted to indexes in some single query's best configuration, enumeration is Greedy(m, k) (an exhaustive seed of m indexes, then greedy additions), costs of most configurations are derived from a few "atomic" ones, and two-column indexes are generated only from winning single columns. On five TPC-D workloads this cut running time to 10–24% of a baseline, with estimated-cost quality 0–7% worse, and Greedy(2, 4) matched the exhaustive optimum on four of five workloads and was within 1% on the fifth. The cost model has no build cost, no storage term and no frequency weights, the budget is a count of indexes, the workload is fixed, there is no guarantee for the greedy step, and quality is never measured by executing anything.
