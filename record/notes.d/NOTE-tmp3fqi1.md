# NOTE-tmp3fqi1: Karpenko — Defending informational structural realism

## Contribution

Karpenko surveys versions of structural realism and proposes an expanded informational structural realism (ISR). He criticises a non-eliminative OSR that takes entities and structures as primitives but denies theoretical objects. He draws on scientific realism, the information interpretation of quantum mechanics, digital ontology and string-theory dualities. He argues that theories built on different spacetime metrics can yield the same description and the same predictions while implying different fundamental pictures. On his view, what is fundamental is the family of equivalent descriptions (information) rather than substantival spacetime, and theoretical objects therefore exist as ontological primitives.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Opening section on scientific realism (pp. 191–194): a history of ESR from Poincaré, Maxwell and Worrall to Ladyman's and French's eliminative OSR, with Chakravartty's "how can there be relations without relata?" objection (p. 193).
- ISR section (from p. 195): grounds ISR in Floridi's semantic information, which he contrasts with Bar-Hillel and Carnap. He then discusses the information interpretation of quantum mechanics via Mamchur, separating Bohr's epistemic reading from Wheeler's informational ontology (p. 198).
- Dualities section (pp. 199–201): the five superstring theories as limits of M-theory, and holographic (AdS/CFT) duality after Maldacena. On this reading, theories that look like descriptions of different worlds, even with different numbers of spatial dimensions, are equivalent and so represent one reality. He explicitly turns Fursov's (2022) antirealist reading of dualities as underdetermination into an argument for ISR.
- Digital ontology (p. 201, notes 8–10): Fredkin and quantum-state changes read as bit flips.
- Conclusion (pp. 202–203): equivalent models have equal ontological status, and spacetime properties derive from the modes of description. He answers the worry that false models would then be equally real: a wrong model still exists as information, possibly describing another possible world, and he explicitly disowns relativism about truth.
