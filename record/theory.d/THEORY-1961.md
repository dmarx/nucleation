# THEORY-1961: Johnson holds that intimate partner violence divides by its control context into situational couple violence, intimate terrorism and violent resistance, and that agency data and general surveys disagree about gender because they sample different types

Johnson, interviewed by Ooms (2006), [LIT-2775](../literature.d/LIT-2775.md).

## The account

Intimate terrorism is violence used "in combination with other control
tactics" to take "complete control" of a partner; violent resistance is
"when a victim of intimate terrorism fights back"; situational couple
violence, the commonest, arises without such a pattern (p. 3). The type is
fixed by "the power and control issues involved" and by fear (p. 7), not by
the acts. Agency samples reach mostly intimate terrorism and violent
resistance, general surveys mostly situational couple violence, so the two
disagree about gender (pp. 3–4).

## What it does not show

- **Evidence.** It is an interview; the sampling claim and the figures are
  asserted, not shown here. Hardesty et al. (2015), [LIT-2771](../literature.d/LIT-2771.md), give the
  operational test.
- **A fourth type.** Mutual violent control, which A's table credits to the
  2008 book, is not mentioned.
- **Culpability.** It types violence; it does not say how each party's acts
  are to be judged, beyond allowing that violent resistance can sometimes be
  justified.
