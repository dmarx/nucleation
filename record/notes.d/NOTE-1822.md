# NOTE-1822: DARPA — Science of Artificial Intelligence and Learning for Open-world Novelty (SAIL-ON)

## Contribution

A programme statement framing open-world novelty as a research target with a staged capability
list and a requirement for generated, graded novelty in evaluation; no results.

## Key results

1. Goals (page): "develop scientific principles to quantify and characterize novelty in open-world domains, create AI systems that react to novelty in those domains, and demonstrate and evaluate these systems in a selected DoD domain".
2. Problem (page): "AI systems aren't very good at adapting to constantly changing conditions" (curly apostrophe on the page).
3. Four steps (release, Senator): "The first thing an AI system has to do is recognize the world has changed. The second thing it needs to do is characterize how the world changed. The third thing it needs to do is adapt its response appropriately," … "The fourth thing, once it learns to adapt, is for it to update its model of the world."
4. Evaluation design (release): performers must "construct software that generates novel situations at distinct levels of a novelty hierarchy in selected domains".
5. Example (release): "Imagine if the rules for chess were changed mid-game".
6. Fields sought (release): include "anomaly detection, fault diagnosis and recovery".

## Assumptions

Novelty can be generated and graded in a hierarchy; adaptation without large-scale retraining is
achievable; military OODA framing.

## Concepts

Open-world novelty; closed-world vs open-world problem solving; novelty hierarchy; detect,
characterise, adapt, update.
