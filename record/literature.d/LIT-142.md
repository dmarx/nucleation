# LIT-142: One or two? A Process View of pregnancy

Anne Sophie Meincke (2021), *Philosophical Studies 179(5):1495–1521 (May 2022)* — DOI-10.1007/s11098-021-01716-y

## Key takeaways

- Because organisms are processes rather than substances, a pregnant individual — an asymmetrically bifurcating hypercomplex autopoietic process — is neither one individual nor two but something in between, which neither the Containment View nor the Parthood View can capture.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Meincke asks how many individuals are present where there is a pregnant individual. Within substance ontology only two answers are available: two, on the dominant Containment View (the foetus sits in the gestating organism as in a container), or one, on Kingma's Parthood View (the foetus is a part of the gestating organism). She proposes a third: a pregnant individual is neither one nor two but something in between, because organisms are better understood as processes than as substances. Focusing on the Parthood View, she argues that a Process View, on which the pregnant individual is a bifurcating hypercomplex process, does better than either substance-based view.
