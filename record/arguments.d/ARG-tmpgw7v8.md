# ARG-tmpgw7v8: Judged pragmatic observables that predict human judgements of fidelity or shared kind better than text similarity support the central theses of the pragmatic-transport line

## The inference

[CASE-tmpu88go](../cases.d/CASE-tmpu88go.md) is the test the four defeat conditions state: human
judgements of fidelity or of shared kind, predicted by the theory's
measures and by a text-similarity score. [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md), [CLAIM-139](../claims.d/CLAIM-139.md), [CLAIM-005](../claims.d/CLAIM-005.md)
and [CLAIM-078](../claims.d/CLAIM-078.md) are each defeated if text similarity predicts the criterion
as well as the theory's measures do. If the theory's measures win, the
theses survive, and the survival is taken as support.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** Yes.
  A pass is what every rival account predicts (the next question), and
  the predictor shares a method with the criterion (the third).
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** No. [CLAIM-233](../claims.d/CLAIM-233.md):
  the defeat conditions "pit them only against embedding, propositional
  or surface similarity, which no rival account holds, so surviving them
  would not tell the thesis from the functional and relevance-theoretic
  accounts the record says it must be told apart from". [CLAIM-023](../claims.d/CLAIM-023.md) says
  the contribution is "not that translation is pragmatic or that speech
  acts change contexts, but a specified, testable account of which
  communicative distinctions a translation preserves". A
  functional-equivalence account, Gutt's interpretive resemblance and
  plain speech-act pragmatics all predict that ratings of stance and
  footing beat embedding similarity on renderings built to dissociate the
  two. So does the rival [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md) itself names, a global object enriched
  with force. The line's distinctive machinery ([CLAIM-105](../claims.d/CLAIM-105.md), [CLAIM-050](../claims.d/CLAIM-050.md),
  [CLAIM-117](../claims.d/CLAIM-117.md), [CLAIM-056](../claims.d/CLAIM-056.md)) has defeat conditions of its own, and the
  thesis-level ones depend on none of them.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** No. [CLAIM-232](../claims.d/CLAIM-232.md):
  "Predictor and criterion in the central defeat tests are both human
  pragmatic judgements of the same renderings while the baseline is a
  machine score, so the theory's measures can win by shared method
  variance alone." Two human judgements of one rendering share the
  rater's construal, scale use, a general impression and the context of
  elicitation, and an embedding score shares none of that. "The theory's
  measures can beat the baseline without carrying any information the
  baseline lacks about what the renderings preserve." The manuscript's
  split annotators (§10, as [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md) reports) remove the shared rater,
  not the shared construal.

Rejected as an inference to the four theses. It does not say the theses
are false, and beating text similarity is the right first test: failing
it would defeat them. [CLAIM-232](../claims.d/CLAIM-232.md) and [CLAIM-233](../claims.d/CLAIM-233.md) undercut this argument with
what they say about the subject matter: what the rival accounts predict,
and what two human ratings share.

The weakness was seen once before, for an earlier thesis: [CLAIM-083](../claims.d/CLAIM-083.md) says
the manuscript's Case I criterion "is weighed against embedding
similarity, not against static pragmatic similarity", and [CASE-005](../cases.d/CASE-005.md)'s
three-way selection design had the stronger comparison.

## The repair

The record conceded both objections on 2026-10-10. [CASE-055](../cases.d/CASE-055.md) restores the
three-way selection with human receivers, adds a proposition-plus-force
rival, a behavioural criterion and predictors rated by other raters. The
revised theses [CLAIM-220](../claims.d/CLAIM-220.md), [CLAIM-202](../claims.d/CLAIM-202.md) and [CLAIM-204](../claims.d/CLAIM-204.md) are stated against it.
[QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md)'s latent act variable, "a latent act variable that adds
nothing once the relational observables are measured", is the test the
record names that would discriminate. [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md)'s and [CLAIM-139](../claims.d/CLAIM-139.md)'s own
defeat conditions are unchanged.
