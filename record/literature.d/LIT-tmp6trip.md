# LIT-tmp6trip: An Explanation of In-context Learning as Implicit Bayesian Inference

Sang Michael Xie, Aditi Raghunathan, Percy Liang and Tengyu Ma (2021),
*ICLR 2022* — ARXIV-2111.02080

## Key takeaways

- **The model of the data, not of the network.** A document is generated by
  drawing a concept θ from a prior and then a sequence from the HMM whose
  transition matrix θ fixes. The LM is assumed to fit this distribution
  exactly, so in-context learning becomes a question about the
  pretraining distribution's own conditional p(output | prompt), which
  marginalises over θ (equation 1). In-context learning is "implicit
  Bayesian inference" in that sense and no other: nothing is claimed about
  how a network computes it.
- **The mismatch is the hard part.** A prompt concatenates independent
  examples, so its transitions at example boundaries are improbable under
  pretraining, and Bernstein–von Mises does not apply. With delimiter
  states that every concept emits alike (Assumptions 1–2) and a prompt
  start distribution close to the pretraining one (Assumption 3), the
  likelihood factorises across examples with O(1) error each.
- **Theorem 1 (distinguishability).** If, for every θ ≠ θ*, the summed
  per-token KL divergence over one example exceeds the boundary error terms
  (Condition 1), the posterior concentrates on the prompt concept θ* and
  the in-context predictor's 0-1 risk tends to the optimum as n → ∞.
  Longer examples carry more signal, so information in the inputs, not
  only in the input-output mapping, drives it.
- **Theorems 2–3 (no distinguishability).** With a second-order Taylor
  expansion of the KL (Fisher information) or test inputs of random
  length, the excess risk is bounded by g⁻¹(O(γ·sup(error)/(k − 1))),
  roughly O(1/k) in the example length.
- **GINC experiments.** Accuracy rises with the number and length of
  examples; pretraining on one concept, or on random transitions, gives no
  in-context learning; prompts from concepts outside the pretraining family
  fail. Larger Transformers do better at the same pretraining loss, LSTMs
  beat Transformers, the 24 orders of the same four examples vary by
  10–40 points, and zero-shot can beat few-shot when transitions are low
  entropy.

`what-survives-translation`), which considered it and dropped it from the

It carries `anthology-candidate`: its subject, how in-context learning
arises from pretraining, is one an anthology topic holds, and the
anthology has deferred it rather than declined it. Here it is read for its
formal content as a statement about inference under misspecification:
when a context of concatenated examples, improbable as a whole, still
identifies the latent variable that generated each example.
