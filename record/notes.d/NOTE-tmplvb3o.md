# NOTE-tmplvb3o: Han et al. — From gut microbiota to host appetite

## Contribution

An update to earlier reviews that adds recently studied microbial
metabolites (succinate, branched-chain amino acids, bacterial proteins) to
the established short-chain fatty acid and bile acid routes. It proposes
manipulating the microbiota as a therapy for appetite disorders.

## Key insight

The microbiome can reach appetite by producing signals the host already
uses. These include metabolites that trigger gut satiety hormones, and
proteins that mimic host appetite peptides: "ClpB, the best studied
bacterial protein, can act as a mimetic of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating
hormone (α-MSH) to result in similar anorexigenic effects".

## Assumptions

- **Rodent-to-human transfer.** It is assumed rather than shown. The authors
  write: "To date, our understanding of the gut microbiota roles in
  modulating appetite is mainly based on in vitro studies and rodent
  models."
- **A mutualist frame.** The abstract speaks of microbiota that "develop the
  intricate interactions to manipulate the metabolic requirements and
  hedonic feelings". No account of microbial fitness interests is given.

## Key results

- **Humans: associations.** Anorexia nervosa patients show lower microbial
  diversity, and bulimia patients higher bacterial ClpB.
- **Humans: interventions.** "acute colonic propionate delivery decreases
  food intake and stimulates the secretion of PYY and GLP-1, while
  long-term colonic propionate delivery has little effects on PYY and
  GLP-1 release in humans". In one inulin-propionate study, "the reward
  processing and hedonic response rather than the secretion of PYY and
  GLP-1 contribute to the inulin-propionate-caused energy intake
  reduction". Prebiotic trials in adults and children conflict.
- **Transplant.** "Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) from a healthy
  individual to an AN patient has led to weight gain". This is a single
  case report.
- **The authors' own verdict.** "it will be essential to conduct
  well-designed clinical trials or assemble clinical data, in order to fill
  the large gaps between clinical and experimental knowledge". And:
  "Although there is as yet no evidence that such treatments would be safe
  and efficient for feeding-related diseases".

## Connections

- **The owner's example** ([CLAIM-tmpkdc9t](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpkdc9t.md), [CASE-tmpcm951](../cases.d/CASE-tmpcm951.md)). It supports the
  existence of routes from gut microbes to hunger, and causal effects in
  rodents. It does not show that the human microbiome drives everyday
  hunger. In humans, the cleanest effect (propionate) is a delivered
  metabolite, not a microbial community.
- **Interoception** ([THEORY-045](../theory.d/THEORY-045.md)). Felt hunger is the experience of a
  regulatory state, and these are routes into that state from a lower-level
  collective. That bearing is the record's.
