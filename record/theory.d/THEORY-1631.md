# THEORY-1631: Sturesteps reports that in 440 Australian adults having a mode of symbolic immortality in one's life, such as children, predicted accepting it as consolation in imagined terminal illness, that accepting the biological, creative and nature modes went with accepting death as final, and that the modes predicted death anxiety and personality only weakly

## The account

The modes work as consolation drawn from what a life already contains,
not as goals pursued. Availability was the strongest predictor of
acceptance, the secular modes were accepted by the same people who
accepted death as final, and Sturesteps concludes that people do not
consciously aim to make the modes part of their lives: a threat raises
the concern and they look for what they have (p. 127).

## Evidence

- 440 usable responses of 610, aged 18 to 88, recruited through
  religious, skeptic, euthanasia and university groups (§5.1.1).
- Total availability correlated .47 with total acceptance; number of
  children .37 with the biological mode (Table 6-12).
- Accepting death as final correlated .30, .31 and .33 with accepting
  the biological, creative and nature modes (Table 6-13, p. 77).
- Death anxiety was lower with acceptance of the afterlife and
  experiential transcendence modes, but symbolic immortality variables
  were never its strongest predictors; neuroticism showed no association
  (pp. 74–75, 82, 117).

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md)'s note of 2026-10-29: "Symbolic immortality, as Sturesteps
measured it (skimmed, [NOTE-1312](../notes.d/NOTE-1312.md)), is consolation that goes with
accepting death as final, not a motive to act for the future." The
reading is that evidence: whatever extension of the self the modes give
is not believed to carry the person past death.
