# ADR-tmpofuo3: Four topics join the vocabulary at the owner's word: cultural-evolution, law, economics and privacy, carved from social-science and society-and-governance

## Context

The topic vocabulary is closed: "a work that needs a word the list lacks
gets the word added by decision, not the nearest wrong one" (ADR-001). In
the filing of the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity' on
2026-11-24, the filers of the memes turns (U1 to U12), of governance and
constitutional theory (U23 to U26), and of legibility, metrics and
shareholder primacy (U27 to U30) each reported a missing word and used a
broader one in its place:

- memes, hyperstition and cultural transmission went under social-science
  or epistemology;
- constitutional interpretation, executive power, corporate and fiduciary
  law and case law went under society-and-governance;
- hold-up, incomplete contracts, the theory of the firm and shareholder
  primacy went under social-science or society-and-governance;
- surveillance, contextual integrity and the law of searches went under
  ethics or social-ontology.

society-and-governance's blurb already names law, and social-science's
names economics. So these works were not mis-tagged, but the broader words
did not say what they were about. The owner, 2026-11-24: "add the topics".

## Decision

Add four words to `topics`:

- **cultural-evolution** ("Cultural evolution and memetics"): how culture is
  transmitted, varies and is selected.
- **law**: law and jurisprudence as a subject. Politics and policy at large
  stay in society-and-governance.
- **economics**: economic theory and institutions, including contracts,
  hold-up, the firm and corporate governance. Psychology and sociology stay
  in social-science.
- **privacy**: privacy and surveillance, including observability and
  legibility as instruments of control.

Each blurb says what stays in the topic it is carved from. The broader
topics' blurbs are unchanged; a work may carry both words.

## Alternatives

- **One topic for law and politics.** society-and-governance already is
  that topic. The question was whether law needs its own word, and the
  constitutional filings say it does.
- **corporate-governance instead of economics.** Narrower than the hold-up
  and incomplete-contracts literature the same filing registered.
- **Leave the vocabulary alone.** The filers' reports show the cost: four
  groups each chose a different nearest word for the same subjects.

## Consequences

- The entries of this filing whose subject the new words name are retagged
  in the same change, with the new word placed by primacy.
- Earlier entries are not swept. A later filing that meets one may retag
  it, saying so.
